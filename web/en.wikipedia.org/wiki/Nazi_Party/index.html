The Nazi Party, officially the National Socialist German Workers' Party (German: Nationalsozialistische Deutsche Arbeiterpartei, NSDAP), was a far-right political party in Germany active between 1920 and 1945 that created and supported the ideology of Nazism. Its precursor, the German Workers' Party (DAP), existed from 1919 to 1920. Under Adolf Hitler's leadership, the party came to power in 1933 and established a totalitarian dictatorship — Nazi Germany — which initiated the Second World War in Europe and perpetrated the Holocaust.

Origins and rise

The party grew out of the German Workers' Party founded by Anton Drexler in Munich in January 1919. Hitler joined in September 1919, quickly became its chief propagandist, and in February 1920 the party was renamed and adopted its 25-point programme, combining extreme nationalism, anti-Marxism and antisemitism. Hitler became party leader in July 1921. The party's paramilitary wing, the SA (Sturmabteilung), was formed the same year. In November 1923 Hitler attempted a coup — the Beer Hall Putsch — in Munich; it failed, the party was banned, and Hitler was imprisoned, during which he dictated Mein Kampf.

Refounded in 1925, the party rebuilt as a mass movement. The Great Depression propelled its breakthrough: in the September 1930 Reichstag election it won 18.3% of the vote, and in July 1932 it became the largest party with 37.3%. On 30 January 1933 President Paul von Hindenburg appointed Hitler Chancellor.

In power

After the Reichstag fire of February 1933, the regime rapidly dismantled democracy. The Enabling Act of March 1933 gave Hitler legislative power; by July 1933 the NSDAP was the only legal party. The Night of the Long Knives (1934) purged the SA leadership; on Hindenburg's death Hitler combined the offices of Chancellor and President as Führer. The Nuremberg Laws of 1935 stripped Jews of citizenship, part of an escalating persecution that culminated in the Holocaust — the genocide of some six million Jews — and the murder of millions of others. Party membership grew from about 850,000 in 1933 to some 8.5 million by 1945.

The party state spawned a vast apparatus: the SS under Heinrich Himmler became a state within a state; affiliated organisations included the Hitler Youth, the League of German Girls, the German Labour Front and the National Socialist Women's League.

Defeat and prohibition

Germany's defeat in the Second World War in 1945 destroyed the regime; Hitler died by suicide in Berlin on 30 April 1945. The Allied Control Council formally dissolved the NSDAP on 10 October 1945, and the International Military Tribunal at Nuremberg declared several of its organisations criminal. In postwar Germany the party remained banned, and neo-Nazi successor organisations were prohibited under the Basic Law.
