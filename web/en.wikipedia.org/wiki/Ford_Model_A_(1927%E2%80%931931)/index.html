The Ford Model A (also colloquially called the A-Model Ford or the A, and marketed simply as the Ford) is the second car model produced by the Ford Motor Company, replacing the venerable Model T, which had been produced for 18 years. Production began in October 1927 and ended in 1931 (with final assemblies into 1932 in some plants).

Development and launch

By the mid-1920s the Model T was losing ground to competitors such as Chevrolet. Henry Ford resisted change for years before finally halting Model T production in May 1927 and retooling for an entirely new car at enormous expense. The Model A was unveiled on 2 December 1927 to huge crowds; Ford received well over 10 million visitors at showrooms in the first days and took hundreds of thousands of orders.

Design

Unlike the idiosyncratic Model T, the Model A had conventional controls: a three-speed sliding-gear transmission, four-wheel mechanical brakes, hydraulic shock absorbers, and a safety-glass windshield. Its 3.3-litre four-cylinder engine produced 40 horsepower, giving a top speed of around 65 mph (105 km/h). A wide range of body styles was offered, from the Tudor sedan and roadster to trucks, in a variety of colours — a sharp break from the "any colour so long as it is black" era.

Legacy

Nearly 4.9 million Model As were built. The model was also produced under licence abroad, including by the Soviet GAZ works as the GAZ-A. Surviving cars are among the most popular vehicles in the vintage-car hobby, supported by an extensive restoration-parts industry.
