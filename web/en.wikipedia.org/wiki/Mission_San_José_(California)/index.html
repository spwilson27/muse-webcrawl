Mission San José is a Spanish mission located in the present-day city of Fremont, California. It was founded on 11 June 1797 by the Franciscan order, under Fermín de Lasuén, and was the fourteenth of the twenty-one Spanish missions in California. The mission is named for Saint Joseph.

History

Situated on the slopes of the East Bay hills near the homeland of the Ohlone people, Mission San José became one of the most prosperous of the California missions, with extensive herds of cattle, vineyards, and orchards. At its peak in the 1830s it was home to roughly 1,900 neophytes. The original church, dedicated in 1809, was destroyed by the 1868 Hayward earthquake.

Restoration

After secularisation in the 1830s the mission buildings fell into ruin. The mission church was faithfully reconstructed in the 1980s, largely using original plans and materials salvaged from the site, and rededicated in 1985. Today Mission San José operates as a museum and an active parish church within the Roman Catholic Diocese of Oakland, and it gives its name to the Mission San José district of Fremont.
