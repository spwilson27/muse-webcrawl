Zori

Zori (Japanese: 草履, zōri) are traditional Japanese sandals. They are flat-thonged sandals made from rice straw, other plant fibres, wood lacquer, leather, or synthetic materials. Zori are a classic form of footwear worn with traditional Japanese clothing such as the kimono, particularly for informal occasions.

History

Zori developed from the geta and other forms of traditional Japanese footwear over the course of the Edo period. Their flat straw sole and thong strap made them comfortable and easy to slip on and off, and they became the standard footwear for informal wear. During the twentieth century, zori came to be made from modern materials including vinyl, leather, and brocade.

Design

The sole of a zori is flat, and the thong (called the hanao) consists of a central post passing between the big toe and second toe, joined to two side straps. Zori may be made with a tatami straw facing, a cork sole covered in leather, or, for formal wear, brocade matching the obi sash. Modern informal zori worn by women may have a slightly raised heel.

Use

Zori are worn with tabi socks. Setta, a related style of sandal, are traditionally worn by men. In formal settings, geta are considered more casual while zori are regarded as the more formal of the two types of traditional flat footwear.
