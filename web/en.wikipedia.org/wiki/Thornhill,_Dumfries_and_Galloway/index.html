Thornhill is a village in the Mid Nithsdale area of Dumfries and Galloway, Scotland, situated on the River Nith about 14 miles (23 km) north of Dumfries. It serves as a local centre for the surrounding agricultural district of Nithsdale.

History

Thornhill was established as a planned burgh by the Earls (later Dukes) of Queensberry, whose seat, Drumlanrig Castle — a great pink-sandstone palace of the seventeenth century — stands a few miles to the north-west. The village's broad main street, cross, and regular layout reflect its planned origins in the eighteenth century. Its railway station, on the former Glasgow and South Western Railway main line, closed in 1965; campaigns for reopening have continued.

Landmarks and amenities

The village has a mercat cross, churches, shops, and Wallace Hall Academy, a secondary school serving Mid Nithsdale. Nearby attractions include Drumlanrig Castle and its gardens, and the walking country of the Lowther and Queensberry hills. Joseph Thomson, the nineteenth-century explorer of Africa after whom Thomson's gazelle is named, was born in nearby Penpont and is commemorated locally.
