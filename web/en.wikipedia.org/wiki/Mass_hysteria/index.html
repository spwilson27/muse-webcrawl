Mass hysteria
Also calledMass psychogenic illness
FieldPsychology, sociology

Mass hysteria, in sociology and psychology, is the rapid spread of illness signs and symptoms, beliefs, or behaviours through a population without an organic cause—a phenomenon now studied as mass psychogenic illness or mass sociogenic illness.

Episodes

Recorded episodes include the dancing plagues of medieval Europe, notably that of 1518 in Strasbourg; the 1962 Tanganyika laughter epidemic; and the 2011–12 outbreak of tic-like symptoms among schoolgirls in Le Roy, New York. Symptoms such as fainting, laughter, or convulsions spread through social groups under stress.

Explanations

Researchers attribute outbreaks to anxiety, social networks, and suggestion rather than toxins, after medical investigations find no physical cause. Media coverage and social media can amplify spread.
