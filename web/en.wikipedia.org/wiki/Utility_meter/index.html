Utility meter

A utility meter is a device that measures the amount of a utility service consumed at a premises — most commonly electricity, natural gas, or water — so that the supplier can bill the customer according to usage. Meters are usually installed at the point where the utility supply enters a building and remain the property of the utility company or a metering provider.

Types

  • Electricity meters measure energy in kilowatt-hours (kWh). Traditional electromechanical induction meters use a rotating aluminium disc driven by eddy currents; electronic meters use digital sampling and may record time-of-use data.
  • Gas meters typically measure volume (cubic metres or cubic feet) using diaphragm, rotary, or ultrasonic mechanisms, corrected for temperature and pressure.
  • Water meters measure volumetric flow, commonly with positive-displacement or velocity (turbine/multi-jet) designs.
  • Heat meters measure thermal energy delivered in district-heating systems by combining flow and temperature measurements.

Meter reading and smart metering

Historically, meters were read manually by visiting meter readers, with estimated bills used between visits. Automated meter reading (AMR) allows one-way collection of readings by radio or power-line communication, while smart meters provide two-way communication, interval data, remote connection and disconnection, and outage reporting. Large-scale smart meter rollouts in the European Union, North America, and elsewhere are intended to support time-of-use tariffs, demand response, and integration of rooftop solar and electric vehicles, though programmes have raised debates over cost, privacy, and data security.

Accuracy and tampering

Meters are regulated measuring instruments subject to accuracy classes and periodic testing under national metrology law. Utilities combat meter tampering and energy theft — a significant source of revenue loss worldwide — with seals, inspection, and consumption analytics.
