Refugee travel document
TypeTravel document
PurposeIdentification and travel for refugees
EligibilityRecognised refugees

A refugee travel document (also called a 1951 Convention travel document or Geneva passport) is a travel document issued to a refugee by the state in which they normally reside, allowing them to travel outside that state and to return there. Refugees are likely to be unable to obtain a passport from their state of nationality (from which they have sought asylum) and therefore need a travel document so that they might engage in international travel.

The 145 states which are parties to the 1951 Convention Relating to the Status of Refugees are obliged to issue travel documents to refugees lawfully resident in their territory.

History

Refugee travel documents grew out of the Nansen passport, an internationally recognised identity document issued by the League of Nations to stateless refugees after the First World War. The 1951 Convention standardised the modern document, whose cover is usually blue and marked with two diagonal stripes in the upper left corner.

Appearance and use

Convention travel documents are booklet documents resembling a national passport. Holders enjoy visa-free travel, or visas on arrival, to a number of countries, although the document does not normally permit travel to the holder's country of origin. Stateless persons may be issued a similar document under the 1954 Convention Relating to the Status of Stateless Persons.
