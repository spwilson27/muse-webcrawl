100 Famous Japanese Mountains

Mount Fuji (3,776 m), the highest and best known of the Hyakumeizan

The 100 Famous Japanese Mountains (日本百名山, Nihon Hyakumeizan) is a list of celebrated peaks in Japan compiled by mountaineer and writer Kyūya Fukada in his 1964 book of the same name. The list, drawn from mountains Fukada had himself climbed, selected peaks for their beauty, historical and cultural significance, and distinctive character as well as their height, generally favouring summits above 1,500 metres.

The list

The Hyakumeizan span the whole Japanese archipelago, from Mount Rishiri and the Daisetsuzan range in Hokkaidō, through the Japan Alps (including Mount Hotaka, Mount Yarigatake and Mount Tate) and Mount Fuji (3,776 m) in Honshū, to Mount Aso in Kyūshū and Mount Miyanoura on Yakushima in the south. Other notable entries include Mount Chōkai, Mount Zaō, Mount Tsukuba — the lowest on the list at 877 m — and Mount Ibuki.

Influence

Fukada's book became a bestseller and transformed Japanese mountaineering culture: completing all 100 peaks became a popular lifetime goal, and the list inspired numerous successor lists, including the 200 and 300 Famous Mountains selected by the Japanese Alpine Club and flower-oriented lists of celebrated peaks. Climbing the Hyakumeizan is covered extensively in Japanese guidebooks, television programmes and hiking media.
