The National Association for Stock Car Auto Racing, LLC (NASCAR) is an American auto racing sanctioning and operating company best known for stock car racing. It was founded by Bill France Sr. in 1948, and the France family has controlled the organisation since; Jim France has served as chairman and CEO since 2018.

NASCAR sanctions three national touring series: the premier Cup Series, the Xfinity Series, and the Craftsman Truck Series, as well as regional and international series in Canada, Mexico and Europe, and local weekly racing at hundreds of short tracks. Its most famous race is the Daytona 500, held each February at Daytona International Speedway and nicknamed "The Great American Race".

History

Stock car racing grew out of the Prohibition era, when bootleggers modified ordinary cars to outrun revenue agents. After the Second World War, France organised meetings at the Streamline Hotel in Daytona Beach in December 1947 that led to NASCAR's incorporation in February 1948. The first Strictly Stock race was held at Charlotte in June 1949; the division became the Grand National Series and, from 1971 under R. J. Reynolds sponsorship, the Winston Cup. Later title sponsors included Nextel, Sprint and Monster Energy, before the "Cup Series" name was adopted with multiple premier partners from 2020.

Cars and racing

Cup Series cars are purpose-built silhouette stock cars. The seventh-generation "Next Gen" car, introduced in 2022, features a single-source chassis, independent rear suspension, a five-speed sequential gearbox and 670/510-horsepower engine packages. Races run mainly on paved oval superspeedways, intermediate tracks, short tracks and a growing number of road courses.

Notable champions

Richard Petty, Dale Earnhardt and Jimmie Johnson share the record of seven Cup championships each. Petty's 200 career wins remain unmatched.
