Pseudocereal

Main examples: Quinoa, amaranth, buckwheat, chia
Distinguishing feature: Seeds used like grains but from broadleaf plants, not grasses
Gluten: Naturally gluten-free

A pseudocereal or pseudo-grain is the edible seed of a plant that is used like a cereal grain but does not belong to the grass family (Poaceae) from which true cereals such as wheat, rice, and maize come. Pseudocereals are typically rich in protein, fibre, and micronutrients, and are naturally gluten-free.

Examples

The best-known pseudocereals are quinoa and amaranth from the Americas, buckwheat from Asia, and chia. Others include kañiwa, wattleseed, and breadnut. Quinoa and amaranth were staple crops of Andean and Mesoamerican civilizations respectively before falling out of favour in the colonial era and being revived as health foods in the late twentieth century.

Nutrition

Unlike most cereals, quinoa and amaranth provide complete protein with all essential amino acids, including relatively high lysine. Their nutritional profile and ability to grow in poor soils or at high altitude have made pseudocereals a focus of food-security research.
