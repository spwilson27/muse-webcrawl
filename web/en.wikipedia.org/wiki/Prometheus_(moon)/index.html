Prometheus (moon)

Discovery1980, from Voyager 1 images
Parent planetSaturn
Dimensionsabout 136 × 79 × 64 km
Orbital period0.613 days

Prometheus is an inner moon of Saturn, discovered in 1980 from photographs taken by the Voyager 1 probe. It is a small, highly elongated icy body, roughly 136 kilometres long, named after the Titan Prometheus of Greek mythology.

Interaction with the F ring

Prometheus acts as a shepherd satellite for Saturn's narrow F ring, which lies just outside its orbit. Because its orbit is slightly eccentric, Prometheus periodically dips into the F ring's inner edge, gravitationally drawing out streamers of ring particles and creating channels and waves — an interaction imaged in detail by the Cassini spacecraft. Its companion moon Pandora orbits just outside the F ring.

Surface

Cassini images show a heavily cratered, lumpy surface with relatively few large craters for its size, suggesting it is a porous rubble-pile body accreted from ring material.
