Chicharrón

TypeFried pork dish / snack
Main ingredientsPork skin (rind), fat
RegionSpain, Latin America, Philippines

Chicharrón (Spanish: [tʃitʃaˈron]) is a dish generally consisting of fried pork belly or fried pork rinds. It is popular in Spain and across Latin America and the Philippines, where it is known as tsitsaron.

Preparation

Pork skin with attached fat is seasoned, sometimes boiled or rendered first, and then deep-fried until it puffs and becomes crisp. Variants are made with pork belly, ribs, or even chicken skin, and are served as a snack, a taco filling, or an ingredient in dishes such as pupusas and mofongo.

Regional varieties

In Mexico, chicharrón is sold in markets as large crisp sheets and also stewed in salsa verde. In Peru and Bolivia, chicharrón often refers to fried pork ribs seasoned with garlic. In the Philippines, chicharon is a common snack eaten with vinegar.
