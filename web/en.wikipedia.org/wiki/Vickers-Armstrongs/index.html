Vickers-Armstrongs

Founded1927 (merger)
IndustryEngineering, shipbuilding, armaments, aircraft
HeadquartersUnited Kingdom

Vickers-Armstrongs Limited was a British engineering conglomerate formed in 1927 by the merger of the assets of Vickers Limited and Sir W. G. Armstrong Whitworth & Company. It became one of Britain's largest manufacturers of armaments, ships and aircraft.

Activities

The company's shipbuilding division at Barrow-in-Furness and on the Tyne built warships for the Royal Navy and foreign customers, including battleships, aircraft carriers and submarines. Its aircraft division, centred at Weybridge, produced famous designs including the Wellington bomber, designed by Barnes Wallis with a geodetic airframe, and after the Second World War the Viscount turboprop airliner and the Valiant V-bomber. The firm also made tanks, artillery, machine guns and the famous Vickers water-cooled machine gun heritage of its predecessor.

Later history

Parts of the conglomerate were progressively nationalised or sold: the aircraft business was merged into the British Aircraft Corporation in 1960, the shipbuilding arm was nationalised into British Shipbuilders in 1977, and the remaining engineering businesses, including the Leeds tank factory that built Challenger tanks, continued as Vickers plc and later passed to Alvis and BAE Systems.
