Wildlife of the Philippines

The wildlife of the Philippines includes a significant number of endemic plant and animal species, reflecting the archipelago's complex geological history of separated islands. The Philippines is recognized as one of the world's megadiverse countries and a global biodiversity hotspot, though its wildlife is under severe pressure from deforestation and habitat loss.

Fauna

The country is home to the Philippine eagle, one of the largest and rarest forest raptors; the tiny Philippine tarsier; the tamaraw, a dwarf buffalo found only on Mindoro; and the Visayan warty pig. Philippine waters hold exceptional marine diversity as part of the Coral Triangle, including whale sharks, dugongs, and hundreds of coral and reef fish species. More than 200 mammal species and over 600 bird species have been recorded, with high rates of endemism in both groups.

Flora

Thousands of plant species, including many orchids and dipterocarp forest trees, occur in the islands, a large proportion of them found nowhere else.
