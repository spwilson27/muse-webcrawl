Torball

Torball is a team sport for blind and visually impaired athletes, developed in Europe in the years following the Second World War as a rehabilitation activity for blinded war veterans. It is governed internationally under the umbrella of the International Blind Sports Federation (IBSA), alongside the related but distinct sport of goalball.

Gameplay

Torball is played on an indoor court 16 metres long and 7 metres wide. Two teams of three players, all wearing eyeshades (oppies) to equalize levels of vision, defend goals that span the full width of the court at each end. The ball — roughly the size of a football but lighter, and containing bells so players can hear it — is thrown underhand along the floor, and must pass under three cords strung across the middle of the court, each hung with small bells. If the ball touches a cord, a penalty is awarded.

Defenders typically lie or kneel across the goal line and use their whole bodies to block the ball; a missed block that lets the ball cross the line is a goal. Matches are played in two halves of five minutes of effective playing time.

Rules

Teams must throw within a few seconds of gaining possession, encouraging a fast counter-attacking game. Faults include throwing the ball too high (over the cords), a long ball, touching the ball with the head in an uncontrolled fashion, or fielding more than the allowed number of players. Penalties are defended by a single player.

History and popularity

The sport developed in Germany in the 1950s and spread across Europe, where it remains most popular — particularly in Germany, France, Italy, Austria, Switzerland and the Netherlands. World and European championships are held under IBSA auspices. Torball is not a Paralympic sport; the Paralympic slot for blind team ball sports is occupied by goalball, which is played on a larger court with a heavier ball and goals of a similar full-width design.
