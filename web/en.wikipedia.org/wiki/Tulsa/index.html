Tulsa is the second-most-populous city in the U.S. state of Oklahoma and the county seat of Tulsa County. Located on the Arkansas River in the northeastern part of the state, in the region known as "Green Country", the city grew from a Creek (Muscogee) settlement in Indian Territory into one of the most important centres of the American petroleum industry, earning the title "Oil Capital of the World" for much of the 20th century.

History

The area was first settled around 1836 by the Lochapoka band of Creek Indians, removed from Alabama, who named the settlement Tallasi ("old town"), from which "Tulsa" derives. The city boomed after oil was discovered nearby — first at Red Fork in 1901 and then with the great Glenn Pool strike of 1905 — attracting oil companies, workers and speculators. Tulsa's wealth financed a remarkable collection of Art Deco architecture in the 1920s. In 1921 the city was the site of the Tulsa race massacre, in which a white mob destroyed the prosperous Black neighbourhood of Greenwood, known as "Black Wall Street".

Economy and culture

Although energy remains important, Tulsa's economy has diversified into aviation, aerospace, finance, technology and health care; the city was also a major stop on U.S. Route 66. Cultural institutions include the Philbrook Museum of Art, the Gilcrease Museum, the BOK Center arena and the Gathering Place riverfront park. Oral Roberts University and the University of Tulsa are located in the city.

Geography

Tulsa lies at the edge of the Great Plains where the rolling, forested hills of eastern Oklahoma begin, astride the Arkansas River. The metropolitan area has a population of roughly one million.
