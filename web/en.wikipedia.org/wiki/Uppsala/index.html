Uppsala

Uppsala

Country: Sweden

County: Uppsala County

Population: approx. 177,000 (city)

Known for: Uppsala University, Uppsala Cathedral

Uppsala is the fourth-largest city in Sweden and the seat of Uppsala County. Located about 71 km north of Stockholm on the Fyris River, it has been a centre of Swedish ecclesiastical, educational and political life for centuries.

History

Originally located at what is now Gamla Uppsala (Old Uppsala), a major religious and royal centre of pre-Christian Sweden associated with the Temple at Uppsala and great burial mounds, the settlement moved downstream to its present site, then called Östra Aros. Uppsala became the seat of Sweden's archbishop in 1164, and its cathedral, begun in the 13th century, is the tallest church in Scandinavia.

University

Uppsala University, founded in 1477, is the oldest university in Scandinavia still in operation. Alumni and faculty include Carl Linnaeus, Anders Celsius and Alfred Nobel, and the university remains one of Europe's leading research institutions. Student life is organised around thirteen student "nations" dating back to the 17th century.

Landmarks

Landmarks include Uppsala Cathedral, Uppsala Castle — begun under Gustav Vasa and site of the abdication of Queen Christina — Gustavianum, the university museum, and the Carolina Rediviva library, home of the 6th-century Codex Argenteus (the "Silver Bible").
