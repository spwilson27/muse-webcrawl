Hellenic Broadcasting Corporation

The Hellenic Broadcasting Corporation (Greek: Ελληνική Ραδιοφωνία Τηλεόραση, Ellinikí Radiofonía Tileórasi), abbreviated ERT, is the state-owned public radio and television broadcaster of Greece. Founded in 1938 as EIR, it is a member of the European Broadcasting Union. ERT operates television channels including ERT1, ERT2 and ERT3, the international channel ERT World, and numerous radio stations centred on ERA.

History

Regular radio broadcasting in Greece began in 1938 under the Radio Broadcasting Service (EIR). Television broadcasts started experimentally in 1966, and ERT in its modern form was established in 1975 after the fall of the military junta. For decades ERT was funded principally by a licence fee collected through electricity bills.

Closure and reopening

On 11 June 2013, as part of austerity measures during the Greek government-debt crisis, the government of Antonis Samaras abruptly shut down ERT, taking its channels off air mid-broadcast and dismissing about 2,600 employees; staff occupied the Athens headquarters and continued streaming programmes online. A replacement broadcaster, NERIT, launched in 2014. Following the election of the Syriza government, ERT was re-established on 11 June 2015, exactly two years after its closure, and most former employees were rehired.

Services

ERT broadcasts general entertainment, news and cultural programming, holds Greek rights to major sporting events, and organises Greece's participation in the Eurovision Song Contest, which it won in 2005. Its orchestras include the ERT National Symphony Orchestra and the Orchestra of Contemporary Music.
