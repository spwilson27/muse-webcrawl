Renewable energy in the European Union

Policy frameworkRenewable Energy Directive; European Green Deal
2030 target42.5% renewable share of gross final energy consumption (RED III)
Leading sourcesWind, hydro, solar, biomass

Renewable energy in the European Union has grown steadily since the 1990s under successive EU directives that set binding targets for the share of energy from renewable sources. The EU aims to be climate-neutral by 2050 under the European Green Deal, with renewables as the central pillar of its energy transition.

Targets

The 2009 Renewable Energy Directive set a 20% target for 2020, which the EU surpassed, reaching roughly 22%. The recast directive (RED II) raised the 2030 target to 32%, and the 2023 revision (RED III) increased it further to 42.5%, with an aspiration of 45%. In electricity alone, renewables supplied over 40% of EU generation by the early 2020s, with wind and solar growing fastest.

Member states

Sweden has the highest renewable share, above 60%, largely from hydropower and biomass, followed by Finland and Denmark — Denmark being notable for generating more than half its electricity from wind. National energy and climate plans coordinate each member state's contribution.
