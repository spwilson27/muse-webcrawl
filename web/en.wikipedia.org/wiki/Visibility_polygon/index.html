Visibility polygon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The visibility polygon of a point (in yellow) among obstacles.

In computational geometry, the visibility polygon or visibility region for a point p among obstacles is the possibly unbounded polygonal region of all points of the plane (or of a polygon) that are visible from p. The visibility polygon is star-shaped, with p in its kernel: every point of the polygon can be connected to p by a line segment lying entirely within the polygon.

Problems

Computing visibility polygons is a fundamental problem with applications in robotics (motion planning and sensor placement), computer graphics (visibility determination and lighting), architectural design, and video game programming, where visibility polygons are used for field-of-view effects and 2D lighting.

Algorithms

For a point inside a simple polygon with n vertices, the visibility polygon can be computed in linear time; optimal algorithms were given by Lee and by Joe and Simpson. For a point among polygonal obstacles (segments or polygons), sweep-line and angular sweep algorithms achieve O(n log n) time, where n is the total number of obstacle vertices. A simple angular sweep approach casts a ray toward each obstacle vertex and constructs the polygon from the nearest intersection points, sorted by angle.

Related concepts

The visibility polygon is closely related to the art gallery problem, which asks for the minimum number of guards needed to see all of a polygon, and to the concept of the kernel of a star-shaped polygon. Three-dimensional analogues are called visibility polyhedra or viewsheds.
