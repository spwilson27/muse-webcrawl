O'Leno State Park

O'Leno State Park is a Florida State Park located on the Santa Fe River near the town of High Springs in Alachua and Columbia counties. Developed by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s, it is one of Florida's first state parks and is notable for the place where the Santa Fe River disappears underground at a large sinkhole known as the River Sink, re-emerging several kilometres away at River Rise Preserve State Park.

LocationAlachua and Columbia counties, Florida, United States
Nearest townHigh Springs
Established1930s (CCC-built)

Features

The park preserves hardwood hammock, sandhill, and floodplain forest along the river. A suspension bridge built by the CCC crosses the Santa Fe, and trails lead past limestone outcrops, sinkholes, and the site of the old town of Leno, a 19th-century mill community from which the park takes its name ("Old Leno"). Wildlife includes deer, gopher tortoises, otters, and a wide variety of birds. The park offers camping, swimming, canoeing, fishing, and an interpretive nature centre.
