Alain Cribier

Alain G. Cribier (25 January 1945 – 16 February 2024) was a French interventional cardiologist renowned for performing the world's first transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI, also called TAVR) in 2002, a procedure that transformed the treatment of severe aortic stenosis in patients unable to undergo open-heart surgery.

Career

Cribier trained in Paris and, after a fellowship in Los Angeles, became head of cardiology at the Charles Nicolle Hospital of Rouen University, where he built a leading catheterisation programme. In 1985 he had already pioneered percutaneous mitral commissurotomy, a balloon technique for rheumatic mitral stenosis widely adopted worldwide. Frustrated that many elderly patients with calcific aortic stenosis were turned down for surgery, he developed — with engineers, and later through the start-up Percutaneous Valve Technologies — a balloon-expandable stent-valve that could be delivered via catheter from the femoral vein.

The first TAVI

On 16 April 2002 in Rouen, Cribier implanted the device in a 57-year-old man in cardiogenic shock who had been refused surgery; the procedure succeeded and the patient recovered. After early scepticism, clinical trials demonstrated TAVI's benefit, first in inoperable and high-risk patients and then progressively in lower-risk groups, making it one of the most consequential advances in modern cardiology. Cribier received numerous honours, including the ESC Gold Medal and election to the French National Academy of Medicine.
