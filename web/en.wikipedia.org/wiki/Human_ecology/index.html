Human ecology

Human ecology is the interdisciplinary study of the relationship between humans and their natural, social, and built environments. The term was popularized by sociologists at the University of Chicago in the 1920s, who applied ecological concepts such as competition, invasion, and succession to the study of urban communities.

History

The field emerged from the work of Robert E. Park and Ernest Burgess, whose "concentric zone model" described how cities grow outward in rings. Later scholars broadened the field to include rural communities, resource use, and the effects of population on ecosystems, linking sociology with anthropology, geography, and biology.

Key concepts

Central ideas include the ecosystem approach to settlements, carrying capacity, adaptation, and the study of how cultural practices shape land use. Contemporary human ecology addresses sustainability, environmental justice, and the health effects of urban design.
