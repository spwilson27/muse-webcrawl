Chakavian or Čakavian (Croatian: čakavski, Chakavian: čakavski, čokavski, cokavski) is one of the three main supradialects of the Croatian language, alongside Kajkavian and Shtokavian. It is named after the interrogative pronoun ča, meaning "what", in contrast to Kajkavian kaj and Shtokavian što. Chakavian is spoken along the Croatian Adriatic coast, in Istria, the Kvarner Gulf, most Adriatic islands, and parts of the Dalmatian hinterland.

History

Chakavian was the first of the Croatian dialects to be written. Medieval Croatian literature in the Glagolitic script was predominantly Chakavian in character, as were many legal documents such as the Vinodol Code of 1288 and the Baška tablet (c. 1100), one of the oldest surviving Croatian texts. Much of Renaissance Croatian literature from Dalmatia and Dubrovnik's hinterland shows Chakavian features mixed with Shtokavian.

With the Ottoman conquests and the migration of Shtokavian speakers westward, the Chakavian area shrank considerably; it once extended much further inland and southward. Today Chakavian is largely confined to the coast and islands.

Classification

Chakavian is conventionally divided into six dialects, based largely on the reflex of the Proto-Slavic vowel yat:

  • Buzet (Upper Mirna) dialect
  • Southwestern Istrian dialect
  • North Chakavian dialect
  • Central Chakavian dialect
  • South Chakavian dialect
  • Southeastern Chakavian dialect

Chakavian dialects vary in their yat reflex: some are Ikavian, some Ekavian, and some preserve mixed Ikavian–Ekavian patterns following Jakubinskij's law.

Features

Characteristic features of Chakavian include the interrogative ča, preservation of older accentual patterns (including the old acute), conditional forms with bin, biš, bimo, bite, and a vocabulary with many loanwords from Italian (Venetian) and, on some islands, remnants of Dalmatian Romance. The Burgenland Croatian language of Austria is based largely on Chakavian.
