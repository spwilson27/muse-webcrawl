Eric Mazur

Eric Mazur (born November 14, 1954) is a Dutch-born physicist and educator at Harvard University, where he is the Balkanski Professor of Physics and Applied Physics and a former Dean of Applied Physics. He is best known for developing Peer Instruction, an interactive teaching method widely used in university science education.

Career

Mazur studied physics at Leiden University, earning his PhD in 1981 with work on optical spectroscopy. At Harvard his research group has worked in nonlinear optics and ultrafast laser physics, including the development of femtosecond laser micromachining ("black silicon"). In the early 1990s, finding that his students performed poorly on conceptual questions despite success on quantitative problems, he redesigned his lectures around short conceptual questions discussed among students—an approach set out in his 1997 book Peer Instruction: A User's Manual.

Peer Instruction

In Peer Instruction, lectures are interspersed with "ConcepTests": students answer individually (often with clickers or cards), discuss their reasoning with neighbours, and answer again, with the instructor addressing remaining difficulties. Studies have shown roughly doubled gains in conceptual understanding compared with traditional lecturing.
