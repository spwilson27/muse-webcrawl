Backlot

A backlot is an area behind or adjoining a movie studio containing permanent exterior sets. Backlots were a defining feature of the classic Hollywood studio system, allowing filmmakers to shoot scenes set in city streets, small towns, Western frontier settlements, and foreign locales without leaving the studio grounds.

History

As film production grew in the early twentieth century, the major studios in and around Los Angeles acquired large tracts of land on which they built standing sets. Famous backlots included those of Warner Bros., Universal Studios, Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, and the RKO Forty Acres lot in Culver City, where exteriors for Gone with the Wind and the television series The Andy Griffith Show were filmed. Sets were frequently redressed and reused: the same street might serve as a New York neighbourhood in one film and a Midwestern town in another.

Decline

From the 1950s onward, the value of the land beneath the backlots rose sharply, and studios sold off large portions for real-estate development. MGM's celebrated backlots were demolished in the 1970s, and Forty Acres was razed in 1976. Surviving backlots, such as the Universal Studios backlot — home to the Back to the Future courthouse square set — remain in use and are popular attractions on studio tours.
