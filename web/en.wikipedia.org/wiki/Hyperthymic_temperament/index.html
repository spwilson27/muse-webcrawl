Hyperthymic temperament

Hyperthymic temperament, or hyperthymia, is a proposed personality type characterised by a persistently upbeat, energetic and confident disposition. People with hyperthymic temperament are described as cheerful, exuberant, warm, extroverted and tireless, with a decreased need for sleep and a high level of productivity and self-assurance. The concept derives from the classification of affective temperaments developed by Emil Kraepelin and later elaborated by Hagop Akiskal.

Hyperthymia is one of the classical affective temperaments.

Characteristics

Traits attributed to hyperthymic individuals include habitual cheerfulness, jocularity, high energy, short sleep duration, overconfidence, risk-taking, and leadership tendencies. Unlike hypomania, hyperthymic temperament is a stable trait rather than an episode, and it is not in itself considered a disorder; many descriptions regard it as adaptive and even protective.

Relationship to mood disorders

Within Akiskal's temperament model, hyperthymic temperament stands alongside depressive, cyclothymic, irritable and anxious temperaments. It has been associated in some studies with bipolar spectrum conditions and with hypomanic episodes under stress, but also with resilience against depression. Its boundaries with hypomania and with narcissistic and histrionic traits remain debated.
