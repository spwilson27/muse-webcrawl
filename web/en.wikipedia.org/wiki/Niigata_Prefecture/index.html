Niigata Prefecture

Capital Niigata City

Region Chūbu (Hokuriku)

Island Honshu

Area 12,584 km²

Niigata Prefecture (新潟県, Niigata-ken) is a prefecture of Japan located in the Chūbu region of Honshu. It has a population of about 2.1 million and its capital and largest city is Niigata. The prefecture faces the Sea of Japan and includes Sado Island; its shape is elongated along the coast, and it is the fifth-largest prefecture by area.

Geography and climate

Niigata is divided by the Shinano River — Japan's longest river — and the Agano River, whose plains form one of the country's great rice-growing regions. The prefecture is famous for heavy snowfall: moist air from the Sea of Japan dumps deep snow on its mountains, making it part of Japan's "snow country" (yukiguni) and a major centre for skiing. This snowmelt water contributes to the reputation of Niigata's koshihikari rice and its sake brewing.

History and economy

Historically the area comprised Echigo Province and Sado Province. The port of Niigata was one of the ports opened to foreign trade in the 19th century. The prefecture suffered major earthquakes, including the 1964 Niigata earthquake and the 2004 and 2007 Chūetsu earthquakes. Beyond agriculture, the prefecture is known for metalworking in the Tsubame-Sanjō area, textiles, and the Kashiwazaki-Kariwa nuclear power plant, among the largest in the world. Echigo-Tsumari's art triennale and the Fuji Rock Festival, held at Naeba ski resort, draw visitors from across Japan.
