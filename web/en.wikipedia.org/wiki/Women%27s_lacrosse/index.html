Women's lacrosse
Governing body: World Lacrosse
First played: 1890, St Leonards School, Scotland
Players: 12 per side (field)

Women's lacrosse is a contact team sport played between two teams using a small rubber ball and a long-handled stick called a lacrosse stick or crosse. The women's game differs substantially from men's lacrosse: it allows less physical contact, uses shallower stick pockets, and is played with twelve players per side including a goalkeeper.

History

The first modern women's lacrosse game was played in 1890 at St Leonards School in St Andrews, Scotland, after its headmistress Louisa Lumsden saw a men's exhibition game in Canada. The sport spread to England and the United States, where it became established in women's schools and colleges. The first Women's Lacrosse World Cup was held in 1982.

Rules

Players wear eye protection, and since 2020 helmets have become more common. Body checking is restricted: physical contact is limited to stick checking and controlled body contact, with strict fouls around the head and the "sphere" around a player's head. Play is governed internationally by World Lacrosse and in the United States by USA Lacrosse; the NCAA version is especially popular.
