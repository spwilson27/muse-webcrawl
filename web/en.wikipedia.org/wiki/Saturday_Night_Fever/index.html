Saturday Night Fever is a 1977 American dance drama film directed by John Badham and starring John Travolta as Tony Manero, a young Italian-American from Brooklyn who finds escape from his working-class life by dominating the dance floor of a local discothèque. The film is credited with popularising disco music and culture around the world and made Travolta a major star.

Tony Manero in his white suit, an enduring image of the disco era.

Plot and themes

Tony, a 19-year-old paint-store clerk, lives for Saturday nights at the 2001 Odyssey disco, where his dancing gives him the status his job and family life deny him. Preparing for a dance contest with the ambitious Stephanie, he is forced to confront the emptiness and violence of his circle of friends, and ultimately resolves to leave Brooklyn for Manhattan. Adapted from Nik Cohn's 1975 magazine article "Tribal Rites of the New Saturday Night", the film mixes a gritty coming-of-age drama with extended dance sequences.

Soundtrack and legacy

The soundtrack album, dominated by the Bee Gees' "Stayin' Alive", "Night Fever" and "How Deep Is Your Love", became one of the best-selling albums of all time, with over 40 million copies sold, and won the Grammy Award for Album of the Year. The film and album defined the global disco phenomenon of the late 1970s. In 2010 the film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry. A sequel, Staying Alive (1983), was directed by Sylvester Stallone.
