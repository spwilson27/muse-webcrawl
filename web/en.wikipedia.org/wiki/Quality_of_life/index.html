Quality of life

Quality of life (QoL) is the degree to which an individual is healthy, comfortable and able to participate in or enjoy life events. The term is used in fields from philosophy and economics to medicine and urban planning, and is broader than "standard of living", which is based primarily on income. Within health care, health-related quality of life is an important outcome measure alongside survival.

Measurement

International bodies measure quality of life with composite indicators: the Human Development Index (life expectancy, education and income), the OECD Better Life Index, and the Economist Intelligence Unit's quality-of-life index. Clinical measurement uses patient-reported instruments such as the SF-36 and EQ-5D questionnaires, and quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) combine length and quality of life into a single figure used in health economics.

Determinants

Research identifies physical and mental health, relationships, work, income security, environment, personal beliefs and independence as central domains. The World Health Organization's WHOQOL project defines quality of life as an individual's perception of their position in life in the context of their culture and value systems and in relation to their goals, expectations, standards and concerns.
