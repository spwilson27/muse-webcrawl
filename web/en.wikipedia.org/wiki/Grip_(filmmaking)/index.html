Grip (filmmaking)

DepartmentGrip department
HeadKey grip
Works withCamera and electrical departments
EquipmentDollies, cranes, stands, rigging, flags, diffusion

In the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom, grips are trained lighting and rigging technicians in the film and video industries. They constitute their own department on a film set and are directed by a key grip. Grips have two main functions: working with the camera department to provide camera support (dollies, cranes, tracks, and rigs) and working with the electrical department to create lighting set-ups for scenes.

Roles

The key grip supervises the grip department and collaborates with the director of photography. The best boy grip is the key grip's deputy, handling scheduling, equipment, and crew management. Dolly grips operate camera dollies and cranes during takes, executing precise, rehearsed moves.

Equipment and techniques

Grips rig cameras to cars, aircraft, and structures; build scaffolding and overhead frames; and shape light using flags, cutters, nets, silks, and diffusion frames. Safety in rigging is a central part of the craft.

Terminology

The origin of the term "grip" is uncertain; one theory traces it to the tool bags ("grips") carried by early stagehands and circus riggers. In some countries the grip and electrical roles are organised differently, and "grip" may refer only to camera-support work.
