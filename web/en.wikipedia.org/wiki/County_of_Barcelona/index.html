County of Barcelona

Comtat de Barcelona; marcher county, 801–1716.

The County of Barcelona (Catalan: Comtat de Barcelona; Latin: Comitatus Barcinonensis) was a marcher county of the Carolingian Empire in the north-eastern Iberian Peninsula, created after the Frankish conquest of Barcelona in 801. It became the nucleus of Catalonia, and its counts eventually led the Catalan counties and, after 1137, entered into dynastic union with the Kingdom of Aragon.

Origins

Barcelona was taken from Muslim rule by Louis the Pious in 801 and incorporated into the Spanish March (Marca Hispanica), a buffer zone of counties governed by counts appointed by the Frankish kings. The first count was Bera, a Visigoth. Through the 9th century the office passed among Frankish and local magnates until Wilfred the Hairy (r. 878–897) united Barcelona with Girona, Osona, and other counties and made them hereditary in his family, founding the House of Barcelona.

Independence and expansion

Frankish royal authority faded in the late 10th century, especially after Count Borrell II received no aid against the sack of Barcelona by Almanzor in 985; by tradition, Catalan independence dates from this period, though ties were never formally renounced. Under Ramon Berenguer I (r. 1035–1076), styled "prince" of Barcelona, the county dominated the Catalan lands, issued the early Usatges de Barcelona law code, and extended influence into southern France. His successors acquired Provence, Roussillon, and other territories.

Union with Aragon and after

In 1137 the betrothal of Ramon Berenguer IV to Petronilla of Aragon united the county with the Aragonese crown; their son Alfonso II inherited both. Catalonia retained its own laws and institutions—the county's legal personality endured within the Principality of Catalonia—until the Bourbon Nueva Planta decrees of 1716 abolished them after the War of the Spanish Succession.
