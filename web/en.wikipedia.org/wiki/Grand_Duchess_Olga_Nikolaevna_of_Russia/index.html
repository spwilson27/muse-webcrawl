Grand Duchess Olga Nikolaevna of Russia

Grand Duchess Olga Nikolaevna of Russia (15 November 1895 – 17 July 1918) was the eldest daughter of Tsar Nicholas II and Tsarina Alexandra, and the eldest of the five Romanov children. She was murdered with her family by the Bolsheviks at Yekaterinburg in 1918.

Early life

Olga was a bookish, thoughtful girl who learned several languages, played the piano, and was known for her quick temper and candour. With her younger sister Tatiana she formed the "Big Pair" among the imperial children, while the younger sisters Maria and Anastasia were the "Little Pair". During the First World War she trained and worked as a Red Cross nurse alongside her mother and Tatiana, an experience that matured and wearied her.

Revolution and death

After Nicholas II's abdication in March 1917, the family was held under house arrest at Tsarskoye Selo, then exiled to Tobolsk and finally to the Ipatiev House in Yekaterinburg, where they were shot in the early hours of 17 July 1918. For decades impostors claimed to be surviving Romanovs, but DNA testing of remains recovered near Yekaterinburg confirmed the deaths of all the children. The Russian Orthodox Church canonised Olga and her family as passion bearers in 2000.
