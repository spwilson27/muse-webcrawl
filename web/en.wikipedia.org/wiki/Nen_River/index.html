Nen River

The Nen River (Chinese: 嫩江; pinyin: Nèn Jiāng), also known as the Nonni River, is a major river in Northeast China, in the provinces of Heilongjiang and Jilin and the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region. It is the largest tributary of the Songhua River. The Nen is about 1,370 kilometres long and drains a basin of roughly 283,000 square kilometres across the western part of the Northeast China Plain.

Course

The Nen rises in the Greater Khingan Range of Heilongjiang and flows generally southward past the city of Qiqihar across a broad, marshy alluvial plain. It receives tributaries including the Gan, Nuomin, Yalu, Chuo'er, and Tao'er rivers, then joins the Second Songhua River near Da'an to form the main Songhua River, which flows on to the Amur (Heilongjiang).

Flooding

The low gradient of its middle and lower course makes the Nen prone to severe flooding; catastrophic floods in 1998, part of the great Yangtze–Songhua flood season, inundated large areas of the Nen valley and prompted major levee construction. Ice breakup floods also occur in spring.

History

The river's historical name, Nonni, is associated with the region contested between Qing China and Tsarist Russia in the 17th century; the Treaty of Nerchinsk (1689) was negotiated not far from its upper basin. The valley later became an agricultural heartland of Manchuria, noted for soybean and grain production.
