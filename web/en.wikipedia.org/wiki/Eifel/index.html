The Eifel is a low mountain range in western Germany and eastern Belgium, lying between the Rhine, Moselle and the Belgian Ardennes, of which it forms the eastern continuation. It is part of the Rhenish Massif.

Geography

Subdivisions include the High Eifel, Vulkaneifel, Schneifel, and the Nordeifel with the Eifel National Park (founded 2004). Towns include Daun, Monschau, Bitburg, Prüm, Mayen and Adenau; the Nürburgring racing circuit lies in the High Eifel near Nürburg.

Volcanism

The Eifel is famous for its maars — volcanic crater lakes such as the Dauner Maare and Laacher See, whose eruption about 13,000 years ago was one of the largest in late-glacial Europe. Volcanism is dormant, not extinct; CO₂ degassing continues and the region shows slight uplift. The Eifel hotspot is linked to a mantle plume.

History and culture

Historically a poor agricultural region of small lordships, castles (such as Eltz, just east in the Moselle hills) and Roman remains along roads to Trier. Today tourism, mineral water (e.g. Gerolstein) and nature conservation dominate.
