Loan modification

A loan modification is a permanent change to the terms of an existing loan agreed between the borrower and the lender, usually to reduce the borrower's payments and help a struggling borrower avoid default or foreclosure. It differs from refinancing, which replaces the loan with a new one, and from forbearance, which is temporary.

Common modifications

  • Interest rate reduction — lowering the rate, sometimes below market for a period.
  • Term extension — stretching the remaining balance over a longer period, such as 40 years for a mortgage.
  • Principal forbearance or forgiveness — deferring part of the balance to the end of the loan, or writing it off.
  • Capitalisation — adding missed payments and arrears to the loan balance.

Mortgage modifications

Loan modification became widespread in the United States during the subprime mortgage crisis. The federal Home Affordable Modification Program (HAMP), launched in 2009 under the Troubled Asset Relief Program, offered servicers incentives to modify eligible mortgages so that payments fell to about 31% of a borrower's gross monthly income. Lenders weigh modification against foreclosure using a net present value test, comparing the expected recovery from each option.
