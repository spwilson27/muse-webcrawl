The Erinyes, known in Roman mythology as the Furies, are female chthonic deities of vengeance in ancient Greek religion and myth. They punish those who have sworn false oaths and committed serious crimes — above all murder, especially the killing of kin — pursuing their victims relentlessly, even beyond death.

Origins and appearance

In Hesiod's Theogony they sprang from the blood of the castrated Uranus. Later tradition names three: Alecto ("unceasing"), Megaera ("grudging") and Tisiphone ("avenger of murder"). They are depicted as winged women with snakes in their hair, holding torches and scourges.

The Oresteia

In Aeschylus's trilogy, the Erinyes hunt Orestes for killing his mother Clytemnestra. In the final play, Eumenides, Athena founds a court on the Areopagus; Orestes is acquitted and the goddesses, appeased, are transformed into the benevolent Eumenides ("Kindly Ones") and given a cult seat in Athens — a mythic transition from blood-vengeance to civic justice.
