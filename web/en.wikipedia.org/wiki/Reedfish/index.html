Scientific nameErpetoichthys calabaricus
FamilyPolypteridae
LengthUp to about 37 cm
Conservation statusNear Threatened

The reedfish (Erpetoichthys calabaricus), also called the ropefish or snakefish, is a species of fish in the bichir family Polypteridae. It is native to slow-moving rivers, estuaries, and lagoons of West and Central Africa, including the Niger Delta and the Cross River basin.

Description

The reedfish has an exceptionally long, eel-like body without ventral fins, reaching about 37 cm in length. Like other bichirs it possesses a pair of lungs and can breathe air, allowing it to survive in oxygen-poor waters and even move short distances over land.

In the aquarium

Reedfish are popular in the aquarium trade because of their unusual appearance and nocturnal habits. They are accomplished escape artists and require a tightly sealed tank; they feed on small invertebrates and fish.
