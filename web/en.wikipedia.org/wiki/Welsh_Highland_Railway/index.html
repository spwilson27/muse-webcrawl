Welsh Highland Railway

The Welsh Highland Railway (WHR; Welsh: Rheilffordd Eryri) is a 25-mile (40 km) restored narrow-gauge heritage railway in Gwynedd, Wales. Running from Caernarfon to Porthmadog through the Snowdonia National Park (Eryri), it is the longest heritage railway in the United Kingdom and connects at Porthmadog with the Ffestiniog Railway, with which it is jointly operated.

History

The original Welsh Highland Railway was formed in 1922 by amalgamating the North Wales Narrow Gauge Railways and the Portmadoc, Beddgelert and South Snowdon Railway. It was never financially successful, fell into receivership, and was leased by the Ffestiniog Railway in 1934 before closing to passengers in 1936 and entirely in 1937. Much of the trackbed survived as a footpath.

Restoration

After decades of dispute, the Ffestiniog Railway obtained powers to rebuild the line. The first section from Caernarfon to Dinas opened in 1997, extended to Waunfawr in 2000 and Rhyd Ddu in 2003. The remaining section through Beddgelert and the Aberglaslyn Pass to Porthmadog was completed with Welsh Government and EU funding, opening fully in 2011 with a new main-line crossing and harbour station shared with the Ffestiniog at Porthmadog.

Route and operation

Trains climb from Caernarfon past Dinas and Waunfawr to Rhyd Ddu beneath Snowdon (Yr Wyddfa), then descend through Beddgelert and the spectacular Aberglaslyn Pass before crossing the Glaslyn estuary into Porthmadog. The line is worked mainly by powerful articulated NGG16 Garratt locomotives imported from South Africa, among the largest engines ever to run on a two-foot-gauge line.
