Paper cutting

Paper cutting
OriginChina, by the 6th century AD
ToolsScissors or knife
Related formsJianzhi, Scherenschnitte, papel picado, wycinanki

Paper cutting is the art of cutting paper designs with scissors or a knife. The art evolved from handcraft in China, where paper was invented under the Han dynasty; the oldest surviving paper cut-out is a symmetrical circle from the 6th century AD, found in Xinjiang. From China the craft spread along trade routes and developed distinct regional traditions worldwide.

Regional traditions

Chinese jianzhi uses red paper cuttings for festivals, weddings, and Lunar New Year decoration, symbolising luck and happiness. Jewish paper cutting flourished in Eastern Europe for mizrah plaques and ketubbot. German Scherenschnitte produced silhouette scenes and love letters, carried by immigrants to Pennsylvania Dutch country. Mexican papel picado — tissue paper punched and cut into banners — decorates Day of the Dead altars and fiestas, while Polish wycinanki and Swiss and Indonesian traditions each developed distinctive styles.

Technique

Designs may be folded and cut for symmetry or worked freely in a single sheet; silhouette portraiture was a popular 18th- and 19th-century branch. Contemporary artists use laser cutting and layered cutting to create large-scale works.
