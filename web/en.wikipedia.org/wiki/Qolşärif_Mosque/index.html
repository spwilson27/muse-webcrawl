The Qolşärif Mosque (Tatar: Колшәриф мәчете, Qolşärif mäçete; Russian: Мечеть Кул-Шариф, Mechet Kul-Sharif) is a mosque located in the Kazan Kremlin in Kazan, Tatarstan, Russia. Named after Qolşärif, the imam and defender of Kazan killed during the Siege of Kazan in 1552, it was for a time reputed to be the largest mosque in Russia and one of the largest in Europe outside Istanbul.

History

The original mosque on the site was built in the Khanate of Kazan period and was destroyed when Ivan the Terrible stormed Kazan in 1552; Qolşärif, a religious scholar and diplomat, died defending the city. Tatar religious revivalists long campaigned for its reconstruction. Construction of the present building began in 1996, funded by donations from across Russia and abroad, and the mosque was inaugurated on 24 June 2005 as part of the millennium celebrations of Kazan.

Architecture

The complex includes the mosque itself, a library, a publishing centre and the Museum of Islamic Culture. The prayer hall is crowned by a 39-metre blue dome ringed by stained-glass windows, and four 57-metre minarets frame the building. Interiors feature marble from the Urals, chandeliers made in the Czech Republic and hand-woven carpets gifted from Iran. The building can accommodate around 6,000 worshippers, with the surrounding square holding a further 10,000.
