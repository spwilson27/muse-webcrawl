Turtle Mountain Band of Chippewa Indians

The Turtle Mountain Band of Chippewa Indians (Ojibwe: Mikinaakwajiw-Ininiwag) is a federally recognised Native American tribe based in Belcourt, North Dakota, centred on the Turtle Mountain Reservation in Rolette County near the Canadian border. The tribe has more than 30,000 enrolled members, many of whom are of mixed Ojibwe (Chippewa) and Métis heritage.

History

The Turtle Mountain Chippewa descend from Plains Ojibwe bands who moved westward from the Great Lakes. The reservation was established by executive order in 1882 and reduced in 1884. The 1892 McCumber Agreement — remembered as the "Ten-Cent Treaty" because it paid roughly ten cents per acre — ceded some 9 million acres of claimed land in one of the most contested land cessions in U.S. history.

Government and community

The tribe is governed by an elected tribal council under a constitution adopted in the Indian Reorganization Act era. Its economy includes the Sky Dancer Casino, Turtle Mountain Community College, and manufacturing enterprises. The community is noted for its fiddle music and Métis-influenced culture, and many members speak Michif or Ojibwe. Belcourt is the principal population centre, and tribal lands include trust parcels in Montana and elsewhere.
