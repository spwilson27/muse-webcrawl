Glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor

Glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) is a protein that in humans is encoded by the GDNF gene on chromosome 5. It potently promotes the survival of many types of neurons, particularly dopaminergic neurons of the midbrain and motor neurons.

GDNF
Gene: GDNF (chromosome 5)
Family: TGF-beta superfamily (GDNF family ligands)
Receptor: GFRα1 / RET

Function

GDNF signals through the GFRα1 co-receptor and the RET receptor tyrosine kinase. It is essential for kidney development and for the enteric nervous system; mutations affecting the pathway are associated with Hirschsprung's disease.

Therapeutic research

Because it protects dopaminergic neurons, GDNF has been investigated as a treatment for Parkinson's disease, with trials delivering the protein directly into the putamen by infusion or gene therapy; results have been mixed.
