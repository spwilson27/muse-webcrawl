The name River Don is given to several rivers in Britain; the principal one is the Don of South Yorkshire, England, which flows through Sheffield, Rotherham, Doncaster and the Humberhead Levels to join the River Ouse at Goole. It gave its name to Doncaster and to the Don Valley. Other British Dons include the Aberdeenshire Don in Scotland; the great Don of Russia, flowing to the Sea of Azov, is a separate and much larger river.

Course

The Yorkshire Don rises in the Pennines west of Dunford Bridge and descends through Penistone to Sheffield, where it is joined by the Sheaf — the river that gives Sheffield its name — and the Loxley, Rivelin and Porter Brook. From Sheffield it flows north-east through the Don Valley past Rotherham and the Magna Centre, then through Doncaster, afterwards meandering across the low-lying Humberhead Levels, partly in artificial channels cut by the Dutch engineer Cornelius Vermuyden in the 1620s for the drainage of Hatfield Chase. Below Stainforth it becomes the tidal Dutch River or joins the New Junction Canal corridor before reaching the Ouse at Goole.

History

The Don powered the cutlery and steel industries of Sheffield and the heavy engineering of the lower valley; its waters were notoriously polluted in the 19th and 20th centuries, when it was described as one of the most polluted rivers in Europe. Substantial clean-up since the 1990s has restored fish, including salmon and grayling, to much of its length. The lower Don was improved for navigation to form the Sheffield and South Yorkshire Navigation. The river flooded Sheffield in June 2007 and Doncaster in November 2019, events which led to major flood-defence investment.

Other Rivers Don

  • The Scottish Don rises in the Grampian Mountains and flows 131 km through Aberdeenshire, past Alford, Inverurie and Kintore, to the North Sea at Aberdeen; the Brig o' Balgownie, dating from the 13th century, spans it in the city.
  • Short rivers named Don also flow in Lancashire (a tributary of the Calder near Burnley) and in Northumberland.
