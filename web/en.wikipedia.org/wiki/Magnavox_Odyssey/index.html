Magnavox Odyssey

The Magnavox Odyssey is the first commercial home video game console. Designed by a team led by Ralph H. Baer at Sanders Associates, it was released by Magnavox in the United States in September 1972, following public demonstrations of Baer's "Brown Box" prototype. The Odyssey launched the video game console industry.

Design and games

The Odyssey is an all-analog, battery-powered unit that connects to a television set and displays three square dots of light. It contains no CPU and produces no sound; game variations are selected by inserting printed circuit "game cards" that rewire the internal circuitry. Because the graphics were so simple, games relied on plastic overlays taped to the television screen to provide colour and context, along with physical accessories such as dice, cards, and play money. Games included Table Tennis, the basis for Atari's Pong, as well as skiing, hockey, and shooting games using a light rifle.

Legacy

Over 300,000 units were sold. The console's commercial success — and Magnavox's aggressive enforcement of Baer's patents against Atari and other companies — shaped the early video game industry and established Ralph Baer's reputation as the "father of video games."
