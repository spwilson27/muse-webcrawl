Cladding (construction)

Cladding, in construction, is the application of one material over another to provide a skin or layer on a wall or roof. Cladding controls the infiltration of weather, protects the structure, and largely determines a building's exterior appearance. It is usually non-structural: the cladding carries only its own weight, transferring wind loads to the frame behind it.

Materials and systems

Common cladding materials include timber boards, vinyl and metal siding, brick and stone slips, fibre-cement panels, ceramic tiles, glass, and composite panels such as aluminium composite material (ACM). A rainscreen system uses an outer panel, a ventilated cavity and an inner weatherproof layer to manage rain and condensation.

Fire safety

Cladding became a major public-safety issue after the Grenfell Tower fire in London in 2017, in which combustible ACM panels and insulation helped a fire spread rapidly up a residential tower, killing 72 people. The disaster prompted building-regulation reforms and a costly national programme in the United Kingdom to remove dangerous cladding from high-rise homes, as well as reviews in many other countries.
