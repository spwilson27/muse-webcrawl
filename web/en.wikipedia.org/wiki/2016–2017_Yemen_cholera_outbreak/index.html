2016–2017 Yemen cholera outbreak

Outbreak details
DiseaseCholera (Vibrio cholerae O1)
LocationYemen
PeriodOctober 2016 – 2018 (major waves 2016–2017)

DeathsOver 2,000 reported during 2017 (estimates vary)

The 2016–2017 Yemen cholera outbreak was the largest documented cholera epidemic in modern history, part of the ongoing seventh cholera pandemic. Beginning in October 2016 amid the Yemeni Civil War, the outbreak exploded in April–May 2017 after the collapse of water, sanitation and health infrastructure. By the end of 2017, more than one million suspected cases had been reported across nearly all of Yemen's governorates.

Background

Yemen's civil war, between the internationally recognised government backed by a Saudi-led coalition and the Houthi movement, destroyed much of the country's infrastructure. By 2016 roughly half of health facilities were closed or only partly functioning, municipal water and sewage systems in major cities had broken down, and unpaid sanitation workers' strikes left uncollected waste in the streets of Sana'a. Cholera is endemic in the region, and the first cases were confirmed in October 2016.

The 2017 wave

After a partial decline over the winter, cases surged dramatically in late April 2017. At the peak in June and July, tens of thousands of new suspected cases were reported each week; in July 2017, Yemen recorded more cholera cases in a single month than any country had ever reported in a full year. Children under 15 accounted for a large share of cases. Malnutrition, widespread among Yemeni children, sharply increased the risk of death.

Response

The World Health Organization, UNICEF, Médecins Sans Frontières and Yemeni health workers established hundreds of cholera treatment centres and oral rehydration points, and delivered supplies by air and sea despite a blockade and damaged ports. A planned oral cholera vaccine campaign was initially diverted in 2017 but vaccines were deployed in later waves. Case fatality rates were kept near 0.2–0.3 per cent — low for such an emergency — through rapid rehydration treatment, even as the absolute number of cases set records. The epidemic receded with the rainy season's end but flared again in subsequent years.
