General Certificate of Secondary Education

The General Certificate of Secondary Education (GCSE) is an academic qualification in a range of subjects taken in England, Wales, and Northern Ireland, having been introduced in September 1986 and first examined in 1988. It replaced the former O-Level (GCE Ordinary Level) and CSE (Certificate of Secondary Education) qualifications. State schools in Scotland use the Scottish Qualifications Certificate instead. Private schools in Scotland may offer GCSEs alongside or as an alternative to the Scottish qualifications.

Structure

GCSEs are studied over two or three years, beginning in Year 9 or Year 10, with examinations taken at the end of Year 11 at the age of 16. Students typically take a range of subjects, including the core subjects of English language, English literature, mathematics, and science, alongside optional subjects such as history, geography, modern foreign languages, and the arts.

Grading

Following reforms introduced in England from 2017, GCSEs are graded on a scale of 9 to 1, with 9 being the highest grade and 1 the lowest; a grade 4 is considered a "standard pass" and grade 5 a "strong pass". This replaced the previous A* to G letter grading, which remains in use in Wales and Northern Ireland (with Northern Ireland also using a C* grade).

Exam boards

GCSE qualifications are offered by several examination boards, including AQA, Edexcel (Pearson), OCR, Eduqas, and WJEC, and are regulated by Ofqual in England, Qualifications Wales, and CCEA in Northern Ireland. An international version, the International GCSE (IGCSE), is offered worldwide.
