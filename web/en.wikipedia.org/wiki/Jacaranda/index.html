Jacaranda is a genus of 49 species of flowering plants in the family Bignoniaceae, native to tropical and subtropical regions of the Americas, especially South America. The genus name is of Tupi-Guarani origin, meaning fragrant. Several species are widely planted as ornamental trees for their showy blue to purple flowers.

Description

Species range from shrubs to large trees 20–30 metres tall. The leaves are bipinnate in most species, resembling fern foliage, and the flowers are produced in conspicuous panicles, each flower having a five-lobed blue to purple-blue corolla. The fruit is an oblong to oval flattened capsule containing numerous slender seeds.

Cultivation

The best-known species, Jacaranda mimosifolia, the blue jacaranda, has been introduced across the tropics and subtropics and in cities such as Pretoria — nicknamed the Jacaranda City — as well as in Australia, Spain, Mexico and California, where streets lined with jacarandas are noted for their spring displays. The trees are also valued, if sparingly, for timber: the Brazilian rosewood once traded as jacaranda came from related legume trees, a source of frequent confusion.
