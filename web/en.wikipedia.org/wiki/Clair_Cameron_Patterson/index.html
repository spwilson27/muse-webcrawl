Clair Cameron Patterson

Clair Cameron Patterson (June 2, 1922 – December 5, 1995) was an American geochemist. Born in Mitchellville, Iowa, he attended Grinnell College and the University of Chicago. Working at the California Institute of Technology, Patterson calculated the age of the Earth in 1953 using uranium–lead dating of the Canyon Diablo meteorite, arriving at 4.55 billion years — a figure that has stood with only minor refinement. His struggle against lead contamination in his laboratory led him to discover how severely industrial lead, especially from leaded gasoline, had polluted the environment, and he became a leading campaigner for its removal.

Patterson pioneered ultra-clean laboratory techniques.

Age of the Earth

Patterson applied the lead–lead dating method developed under Harrison Brown to iron meteorites, reasoning that meteorites are leftover building materials of the planets and share the Earth's age. His 1953 result, 4.55 ± 0.07 billion years, was presented at a 1953 conference and published in 1956. To obtain it he had to invent unprecedented clean-room procedures, because ordinary laboratory environments were saturated with anthropogenic lead.

Lead pollution campaign

Measuring lead in Greenland ice cores and ocean water, Patterson showed that environmental lead levels had risen dramatically with industrialisation and especially with tetraethyl lead in gasoline, contradicting industry-funded expert Robert Kehoe. Despite industry pressure on Caltech and funding bodies, Patterson's work contributed to the Clean Air Act era regulations and the eventual phase-out of leaded gasoline in the United States (completed for road vehicles in 1996) and worldwide.
