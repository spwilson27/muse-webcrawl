Boris Godunov (opera)

Boris Godunov (Russian: Борис Годунов) is an opera by Modest Mussorgsky, composed between 1868 and 1873 in Saint Petersburg. The libretto, by the composer, is based on Alexander Pushkin's 1825 drama of the same name and on Nikolay Karamzin's History of the Russian State. It is Mussorgsky's only completed opera and is regarded as his masterpiece.

Plot and versions

The opera depicts Boris Godunov, regent and then Tsar of Russia (1598–1605), haunted by guilt over the alleged murder of the Tsarevich Dmitry, and the rise of the pretender False Dmitry during the Time of Troubles. Mussorgsky's original 1869 version was rejected by the Imperial Theatres; his expanded 1872 revision, adding the Polish act and the revolutionary Kromy Forest scene, was premiered at the Mariinsky Theatre in 1874. Because Mussorgsky's orchestration was considered rough, the opera long circulated in re-orchestrations by Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov (1896, revised 1908) and later Dmitri Shostakovich; modern performances increasingly restore Mussorgsky's original scores.
