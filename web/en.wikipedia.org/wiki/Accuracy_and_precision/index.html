Accuracy and precision

Accuracy and precision
FieldMetrology, statistics, experimental science
AccuracyCloseness to the true value
PrecisionCloseness of repeated measurements to each other

Accuracy and precision are two measures of observational error. Accuracy is how close a given set of measurements (observations or readings) are to their true value, while precision is how close the measurements are to each other. In other words, precision is a description of random errors, while accuracy incorporates both systematic and random errors.

Definitions

According to ISO 5725-1, accuracy consists of trueness (the closeness of the mean of a large set of measurements to the true value, i.e. the absence of systematic error or bias) and precision (the closeness of agreement among measurements, i.e. low random error). A measurement system can be accurate but not precise, precise but not accurate, both, or neither — commonly illustrated with a target: tight clustering far from the bullseye is precise but inaccurate.

Quantification

Precision is usually quantified by the standard deviation of repeated measurements, and in statistics by related ideas such as repeatability and reproducibility. In information retrieval and binary classification, "precision" has a distinct meaning: the fraction of retrieved instances that are relevant (positive predictive value), traded off against recall.
