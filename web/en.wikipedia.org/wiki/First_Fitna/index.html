First Fitna

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The First Fitna (Arabic: الفتنة الكبرى, al-Fitna al-Kubrā, "the Great Fitna") was the first civil war in the Islamic community, fought from 656 to 661 CE. It led to the permanent overthrow of the Rashidun Caliphate and the establishment of the Umayyad Caliphate, and its outcome contributed to the lasting divisions between Sunni and Shia Islam.

Date656–661 CE
LocationArabian Peninsula, Iraq, Syria, Egypt
ResultMuawiya establishes the Umayyad Caliphate; end of the Rashidun Caliphate

Origins

The conflict began with discontent against the third caliph, Uthman ibn Affan, whose policies — standardizing the Quranic text, appointing Umayyad relatives to governorships, and distributing stipends — provoked opposition especially in the garrison towns of Egypt and Iraq. Rebel delegations besieged Uthman's house in Medina and assassinated him in June 656. Ali ibn Abi Talib, the Prophet Muhammad's cousin and son-in-law, was then elected caliph.

Course of the war

Ali's authority was immediately challenged. Aisha, Talha, and Zubayr demanded vengeance for Uthman and confronted Ali at the Battle of the Camel near Basra in December 656, where Ali prevailed and Talha and Zubayr were killed. Muawiya, Uthman's cousin and governor of Syria, refused allegiance and met Ali at the Battle of Siffin in 657; when fighting turned against him, his troops raised copies of the Quran on their lances, pressing for arbitration. Ali accepted, deeply angering a faction of his followers who seceded as the Kharijites, holding that "judgment belongs to God alone." The arbitration failed to resolve the dispute, and the Kharijites were crushed by Ali at Nahrawan in 658. Muawiya consolidated control of Egypt and raided Ali's territories. In January 661, Ali was assassinated by a Kharijite, Abd al-Rahman ibn Muljam, while praying in Kufa.

Outcome

Ali's son Hasan briefly succeeded and then abdicated in favor of Muawiya under a treaty in 661 — remembered as the "year of the community" — inaugurating the hereditary Umayyad Caliphate based in Damascus. The events of the First Fitna remain foundational to Shia identity, which holds that Ali was the rightful first caliph, and to Sunni traditions of avoiding civil strife within the community.
