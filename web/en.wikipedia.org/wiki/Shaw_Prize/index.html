The Shaw Prize is a set of three annual awards presented by the Shaw Prize Foundation in Hong Kong: in Astronomy, in Life Science and Medicine, and in Mathematical Sciences. Established in 2002 under the auspices of Hong Kong media mogul and philanthropist Run Run Shaw (1907–2014), the prizes were first awarded in 2004. Each prize carries a monetary award, currently US$1.2 million. Because of their scale and the distinction of their laureates, the Shaw Prizes are sometimes dubbed the "Nobel of the East."

The Shaw Prize is presented annually in Hong Kong.

Purpose and selection

The Shaw Prize honours individuals, regardless of race, nationality, gender and religious belief, who are currently active in their respective fields and who have recently achieved distinguished and significant advances, who have made outstanding contributions in academic and scientific research or applications, or who in other domains have achieved excellence. Selection is by invitation only, through committees of international experts; self-nomination is not permitted.

Notable laureates

The Astronomy prize has gone to researchers including Jim Peebles (2004), Michel Mayor (2005), Saul Perlmutter, Adam Riess and Brian Schmidt (2006, later Nobel laureates for the accelerating universe), and future Nobel laureates in several categories have frequently first been recognised by a Shaw Prize. In Life Science and Medicine, laureates include Stanley Cohen and Herbert Boyer (2004) and Ian Wilmut (2008). In Mathematical Sciences, laureates include Shiing-Shen Chern (2004), Andrew Wiles (2005), and Claire Voisin (2017).
