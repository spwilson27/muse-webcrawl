Shishak, also spelled Shishaq or Sisac (Hebrew: שִׁישַׁק), is the name of an Egyptian pharaoh mentioned in the Hebrew Bible, who invaded the kingdoms of Judah and Israel in the fifth year of King Rehoboam (conventionally dated to about 925 BCE) and carried off the treasures of the Temple in Jerusalem. He is widely identified with Shoshenq I, founder of the Twenty-second Dynasty of Egypt — the first pharaoh mentioned by name in the Bible whose existence is independently documented in Egyptian records.

Biblical account

According to 1 Kings 11, Shishak gave refuge to Jeroboam when he fled from Solomon. In 1 Kings 14 and 2 Chronicles 12, after the division of the Israelite monarchy, "Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem" with a large army — Chronicles gives 1,200 chariots, 60,000 horsemen and innumerable infantry, including Libyans, Sukkiim and Ethiopians. He captured the fortified cities of Judah, took the treasures of the Temple and the royal palace, including the gold shields Solomon had made, and withdrew after the prophet Shemaiah's warning and Rehoboam's humbling of himself.

Identification with Shoshenq I

Shoshenq I, a pharaoh of Libyan (Meshwesh) origin who founded the Twenty-second Dynasty around 943 BCE, recorded a major campaign into the Levant on the Bubastite Portal of the temple of Amun at Karnak. The relief's topographical list names over a hundred places, concentrated in the northern kingdom of Israel and the Negev — including Megiddo, where a fragment of a victory stele bearing his name was found. The Karnak list does not name Jerusalem, which has prompted scholarly debate: Jerusalem may have paid tribute to avoid attack (as the Bible's account of the looted treasures suggests), or may appear in a damaged portion of the list. Shishak's campaign is one of the earliest events in the biblical narrative that can be anchored both in foreign records and in conventional Egyptian chronology, and it is often used as a fixed point in debates about the dating of early Iron Age Israel.
