Paul Davis Ryan (born 29 January 1970) is an American politician who served as the 54th Speaker of the United States House of Representatives from 2015 to 2019 and represented Wisconsin's 1st congressional district for twenty years. He was the Republican nominee for vice president in the 2012 election, running with Mitt Romney. As chairman of the House Budget Committee he became the intellectual face of fiscal conservatism in his party, championing entitlement reform, lower taxes and reduced federal spending.

Early life and education

Ryan was born and raised in Janesville, Wisconsin, in an Irish-American family involved in an earthmoving business founded by his great-grandfather. When Ryan was sixteen, his father died of a heart attack; Ryan later cited collecting Social Security survivors' benefits as formative. He studied economics and political science at Miami University in Ohio and worked on Capitol Hill as an aide and speechwriter — including for Jack Kemp, whose supply-side, pro-growth politics strongly influenced him — before winning election to Congress in 1998 at age 28.

House career and policy profile

Ryan rose through the Budget Committee, becoming its chairman in 2011. His budget proposals, notably "The Path to Prosperity" (2011), proposed converting Medicare into a premium-support (voucher) system for future retirees and cutting domestic spending — plans that drew sharp Democratic criticism and made him a polarising national figure. As the Ways and Means chairman in 2015 he championed trade promotion authority and tax reform.

Vice-presidential candidacy

In August 2012 Republican presidential nominee Mitt Romney chose Ryan as his running mate. The ticket emphasised debt reduction; it lost the general election to Barack Obama and Joe Biden, 51 to 47 per cent of the popular vote. Ryan simultaneously retained his House seat under Wisconsin law.

Speakership

After Speaker John Boehner's resignation amid pressure from the conservative Freedom Caucus, Ryan reluctantly accepted the speakership in October 2015, on conditions of party unity. His tenure was dominated by the arrival of President Donald Trump, with whom his relationship was often strained: Ryan criticised several of Trump's statements during the 2016 campaign yet worked with the administration to pass the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 — the signature legislative achievement of his speakership — and to attempt, unsuccessfully, repeal of the Affordable Care Act. In April 2018 Ryan announced he would not seek re-election; he retired in January 2019 and was succeeded as Speaker by Nancy Pelosi.

Later career

After Congress, Ryan joined the board of Fox Corporation, co-founded the American Idea Foundation, taught briefly at the University of Notre Dame, and entered private equity as a partner at Solamere Capital. He has remained a periodic commentator on Republican Party politics, generally critical of populist turns within the party.
