Tournament of Roses Parade

The Tournament of Roses Parade, popularly known as the Rose Parade, is an annual parade held in Pasadena, California, on New Year's Day (or January 2 if January 1 is a Sunday). First held in 1890 and organised by the Pasadena Tournament of Roses Association, it is famous for its floats entirely decorated with flowers and other natural materials, and is a fixture of American New Year celebrations.

The parade

The parade travels 5½ miles (8.9 km) along Colorado Boulevard, featuring floral floats entered by corporations, municipalities and non-profit organisations, interspersed with marching bands from across the United States and equestrian units. By rule, every visible surface of a float must be covered with flowers, petals, seeds, bark or other natural material; elaborate floats use computer-controlled animation and cost hundreds of thousands of dollars. A Grand Marshal leads the parade, and the tournament's Royal Court, headed by the Rose Queen, is chosen from local high-school and college students.

History and the Rose Bowl

The parade began as a promotion of California's mild winter climate by the Valley Hunt Club; chariot races and other games followed, evolving into the Rose Bowl college football game, first played in 1902 and held since 1923 in the Rose Bowl stadium. Televised nationally since the late 1940s, the parade draws hundreds of thousands of spectators to Pasadena and tens of millions of television viewers.
