New York State Archives

The New York State Archives is part of the Office of Cultural Education within the New York State Education Department. It preserves and makes accessible the archival records of New York State government, holding documents dating from the Dutch colonial period of the 17th century to the present.

Holdings

The archives holds more than 270 million documents, including records of the colonial government, Revolutionary War materials, canal and railroad records, court files, land grants, military records, and the papers of state agencies. Notable collections include documents relating to slavery in New York, Native American treaties, and the construction of the Erie Canal.

Location and services

The State Archives is located in the Cultural Education Center in Albany, sharing the building with the New York State Museum and the New York State Library. It provides reference services, a research room, digital collections online, educational programs, and guidance to local governments on records management through its Government Records Services.

History

Although New York kept records from colonial times, a modern state archives was formally established in 1971, and the current facility in the Cultural Education Center opened in 1978. The archives also oversees the State Records Center and administers grants for local government records programs.
