Dromedary

The dromedary (Camelus dromedarius), also called the dromedary camel, Arabian camel, or one-humped camel, is a large even-toed ungulate of the genus Camelus with one hump on its back. It is the tallest of the three species of camel and has been domesticated for thousands of years; it exists only as a domestic animal, with feral populations in Australia descended from released imports.

Adaptations

Dromedaries are adapted to desert life: they tolerate large changes in body temperature, lose little water through sweat or urine, can drink large volumes quickly after dehydration, and store fat in the hump as an energy reserve. Long eyelashes, sealable nostrils, and broad padded feet protect against sand.

Uses

Dromedaries provide milk, meat, wool, and transport across arid regions of North Africa, the Middle East, and South Asia. Camel racing is a popular sport in the Arabian Peninsula.
