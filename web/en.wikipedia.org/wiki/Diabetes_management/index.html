Diabetes management

The term diabetes management refers to the ongoing care and treatment of diabetes mellitus. The main goal is to keep blood glucose levels as close to normal ("euglycemia") as safely possible, usually with the aim of preventing or delaying long-term complications such as cardiovascular disease, kidney failure, retinopathy, and neuropathy, while avoiding dangerous hypoglycemia.

Monitoring

Glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) reflects average glucose over roughly three months; a target below 7% is commonly recommended for many adults, individualized by age and comorbidity. Self-monitoring of blood glucose, with finger-stick meters or continuous glucose monitors (CGM), guides day-to-day decisions, especially for people using insulin.

Treatment

Type 1 diabetes requires lifelong insulin, delivered by injections or pump, increasingly paired with CGM and automated insulin delivery systems. Type 2 diabetes management begins with lifestyle measures — diet, physical activity, and weight management — and medications such as metformin, GLP-1 receptor agonists, and SGLT2 inhibitors, the latter two also valued for cardiovascular and kidney protection; insulin is added when needed. Management also includes blood pressure and lipid control, smoking cessation, vaccination, and regular screening of eyes, kidneys, and feet.
