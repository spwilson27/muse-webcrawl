Adult education

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An adult evening class (image placeholder)

Adult education, distinct from child education, is a practice in which adults engage in systematic and sustained learning activities in order to gain new forms of knowledge, skills, attitudes, or values. It can mean any form of learning adults engage in beyond traditional schooling, encompassing basic literacy to personal fulfillment as a lifelong learner. In particular, adult education reflects a specific philosophy about learning and teaching based on the assumption that adults can and want to learn, that they are able and willing to take responsibility for the learning, and that the learning itself should respond to their needs.

Characteristics

Adult education is driven by the learning needs of adults, often arising from life transitions such as a new job, family responsibilities, or retirement. Programs are frequently part-time and scheduled around work, and learners typically bring substantial prior experience that serves as a resource for learning. The educator Malcolm Knowles coined the term andragogy for the theory and practice of adult learning, contrasting it with pedagogy, the teaching of children.

Forms

  • Formal learning — courses at colleges, universities, and community colleges, including extension and continuing education programs.
  • Non-formal learning — organized courses outside the credential system, such as workplace training, community classes, and literacy programs.
  • Informal learning — self-directed study, reading, and learning from experience.

History

Mechanics' institutes in nineteenth-century Britain, folk high schools in Denmark inspired by N. F. S. Grundtvig, and workers' education movements in Europe and North America laid the foundations of modern adult education. In the twentieth century, mass literacy campaigns, university extension, and later distance and online learning greatly expanded access. The University of the Third Age movement, founded in France in 1973, addressed learning in retirement.

Goals and outcomes

Adult education serves economic goals (employability, productivity), social goals (civic participation, inclusion), and personal goals (wellbeing, self-development). International bodies such as UNESCO promote lifelong learning as a policy framework, and many countries fund adult literacy and basic skills programs.
