The Triple Nine Society (TNS) is a high-IQ society open to people who score at or above the 99.9th percentile on a standardised test of intelligence—that is, roughly the top one person in a thousand. Its name derives from that three-nines threshold. It is thus considerably more selective than the better-known Mensa International, which admits the top 2 percent.

History and organisation

The society was founded in 1978 by former members of the International Society for Philosophical Enquiry. It is run by elected officers and publishes a journal, Vidya, through which members exchange articles and commentary. Members are known as "Niners".

Membership

Qualifying scores are accepted from a list of approved tests, including certain administrations of the SAT, GRE, LSAT and standardised IQ tests such as the WAIS. Membership has numbered in the low thousands, drawn from dozens of countries.
