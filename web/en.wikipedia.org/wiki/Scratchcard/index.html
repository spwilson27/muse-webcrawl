Scratchcard

TypeLottery / gambling product
Introduced1974 (United States)
MediumCard with latex scratch-off panel

A scratchcard (also called a scratch-off, scratch ticket, or instant lottery) is a small card, often made of paperboard, containing concealed information covered by an opaque substance (usually latex) that can be scratched off to reveal whether the holder has won a prize. Scratchcards are a popular form of instant-win lottery sold by state and national lotteries around the world.

History

The modern scratchcard was developed in the United States in the early 1970s; the first scratch-off lottery ticket was sold by the Massachusetts State Lottery in 1974. The technology combined secure printing with a scratch-off coating, and in 1987 a variable-data printing process was patented that greatly improved security and flexibility, allowing each ticket's outcome to be predetermined and verified.

How they work

The outcome of each card is determined at the time of printing and distributed according to a fixed prize structure with published odds. Players scratch off the coating to reveal symbols, numbers or amounts; matching combinations win the corresponding prizes. Because results are instant, scratchcards are sometimes described as a faster, more game-like form of lottery than draw-based games.

Criticism

Scratchcards have been criticised for their high effective takeout rate, low return-to-player percentages compared with many other gambling products, and their appeal to problem gamblers because of the speed and frequency of play. Some jurisdictions restrict their marketing or the denominations that may be sold.
