Deutsche Bank

Deutsche Bank AG is a German multinational investment bank and financial services company headquartered in Frankfurt, Germany. Founded in Berlin in 1870 to finance German foreign trade, it is the largest bank in Germany and one of the largest banks in Europe by total assets. It operates a corporate bank, an investment bank, a private bank and an asset management business (DWS), and is counted among the global systemically important banks supervised by the European Central Bank.

History

Deutsche Bank was founded in 1870 and grew rapidly with German industry, financing projects such as the Baghdad Railway. It survived the upheavals of two world wars, hyperinflation and the Great Depression; during the Nazi period it participated in the "Aryanisation" of Jewish-owned businesses, a history later examined by independent historians commissioned by the bank. After the Second World War the bank was split into regional institutions in West Germany and reunified in 1957. From the 1980s onwards it expanded internationally as an investment bank, acquiring the British merchant bank Morgan Grenfell (1989) and the American firm Bankers Trust (1999), and listing on the New York Stock Exchange in 2001.

Recent decades

The bank weathered the 2007–08 financial crisis without a state bailout but spent the following decade dealing with litigation and regulatory penalties running into many billions of euros, including settlements over mortgage-backed securities, interest-rate manipulation (LIBOR) and money-laundering control failures. In 2019 it announced a major restructuring, exiting equities trading and cutting 18,000 jobs. Christian Sewing has served as chief executive since 2018. The bank also owns a major stake arrangement with Postbank, Germany's largest retail bank by customers, acquired between 2008 and 2015.
