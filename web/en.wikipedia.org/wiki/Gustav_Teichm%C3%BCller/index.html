Gustav Teichmüller

Gustav Teichmüller
Born: 19 November 1832, Braunschweig
Died: 22 May 1888, Dorpat (Tartu)
Occupation: Philosopher
Known for: Perspectivism, personalism

Gustav Teichmüller (19 November 1832 – 22 May 1888) was a German philosopher known for his works on the history of concepts, perspectivism, and a form of personalism that influenced later thinkers, including aspects of Nietzsche's thought.

Life

Teichmüller studied in Göttingen and Berlin, taught at Basel, and from 1871 held the chair of philosophy at the University of Dorpat (now Tartu, Estonia), where he remained until his death.

Philosophy

He distinguished between being and representation, argued that knowledge is perspectival, and developed ideas about the self and immortality. His works include studies on Aristotle, Plato, and the history of philosophical concepts, as well as "Die wirkliche und die scheinbare Welt" (1882).

Influence

Teichmüller's perspectivism and critique of materialism influenced Russian and Baltic philosophy, and scholars have noted parallels with Nietzsche's later perspectivism.
