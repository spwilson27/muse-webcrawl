Cesare Baronio (also Baronius; 30 August 1538 – 30 June 1607) was an Italian cardinal and historian of the Catholic Church. A member of the Oratory of Saint Philip Neri, he is best known for his monumental Annales Ecclesiastici, a twelve-volume history of the Church from the birth of Christ to 1198, written as a Catholic response to the Protestant Magdeburg Centuries.

Life

Born at Sora in the Kingdom of Naples, Baronio studied law at Naples and Rome before joining Philip Neri's Oratorians, by whom he was ordained priest in 1564. Urged by Neri to counter the Lutheran church histories, he spent decades researching in Roman archives and libraries, publishing the first volume of the Annales in 1588. He became confessor to Pope Clement VIII, who made him cardinal in 1596 and Vatican librarian in 1597. At the conclaves of 1605 he was a leading candidate for the papacy but was opposed by the Spanish crown, partly over his defence of papal — against royal — claims in Sicily. He also produced a revised edition of the Roman Martyrology. Baronio is remembered for his remark that Galileo's science showed "how to go to heaven, not how the heavens go" is attributed to him in a related form regarding Scripture and astronomy; he died in Rome in 1607 and was declared Venerable in 1745.

Works

The Annales Ecclesiastici (1588–1607), continued after his death by Odorico Raynaldi, remained a standard work of Catholic historiography for centuries, notable for its extensive use of documentary sources, including many published for the first time.
