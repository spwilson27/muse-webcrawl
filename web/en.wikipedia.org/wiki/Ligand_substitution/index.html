Ligand substitution

Ligand substitution (also called ligand exchange or ligand metathesis) is a type of chemical reaction in which a ligand in a coordination compound is replaced by another ligand. It is one of the most fundamental reaction classes in coordination chemistry and organometallic chemistry.

Mechanisms

Ligand substitution reactions are generally classified by their mechanism, by analogy with substitution at carbon:

  • Associative substitution (A) – the incoming ligand first binds to the metal centre, forming an intermediate of increased coordination number, after which the leaving group departs. The two-step variant in which bond formation is rate-determining is often called an associative interchange (Ia).
  • Dissociative substitution (D) – the leaving group departs first, giving an intermediate of lower coordination number, which is then attacked by the incoming ligand. A dissociative interchange (Id) has bond breaking dominant in the transition state.

Square planar complexes, such as those of Pt(II), typically substitute by associative pathways, whereas octahedral complexes commonly react by dissociative or interchange mechanisms. The rate law for substitution at square planar centres is characteristically two-term: rate = (k1 + k2[Y])[complex], reflecting solvent-assisted and direct-attack pathways.

Factors affecting reactivity

The lability of a complex depends on the metal, its oxidation state and d-electron configuration, and the nature of the ligands already present. The trans effect describes the ability of a coordinated ligand to direct substitution into the position opposite itself in square planar complexes. Chelating ligands are substituted much more slowly because several metal–ligand bonds must break, a consequence of the chelate effect.

Examples

A classic example is the exchange of water ligands in aqua complexes, and the replacement of carbon monoxide by other ligands in metal carbonyls such as the synthesis of substituted derivatives of Ni(CO)4 and Fe(CO)5. In biochemistry, the binding and release of O2 at the iron centre of haemoglobin is a ligand substitution process.
