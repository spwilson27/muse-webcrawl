BMX

BMX, the abbreviation for bicycle motocross, is a cycle sport performed on BMX bikes, either in competitive BMX racing or freestyle BMX, or else as a general recreational pursuit. BMX began during the early 1970s in the United States when children began racing their bicycles on dirt tracks in southern California, inspired by motocross stars of the time.

History

The sport was founded and sanctioned in the United States, and quickly spread internationally. The International BMX Federation was founded in 1981, and the first world championships were held in 1982. BMX racing became a full medal Olympic sport at the 2008 Summer Olympics in Beijing, and BMX freestyle park made its Olympic debut at the Tokyo 2020 Games.

Disciplines

BMX racing is contested on purpose-built tracks of around 350–400 metres, with riders starting from a raised gate and racing over jumps and banked corners. Freestyle BMX covers several disciplines including park, street, vert, flatland, trails and dirt jumping, in which riders perform tricks judged on difficulty, creativity and style.

BMX bikes are characterised by small 20-inch wheels, a single gear, a rear brake only in racing form, and a strong, lightweight frame built to withstand impacts.
