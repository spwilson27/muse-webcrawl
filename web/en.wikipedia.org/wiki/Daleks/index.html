Daleks

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Daleks are a fictional extraterrestrial species of mutants from the British science-fiction television series Doctor Who, created by writer Terry Nation and designed by Raymond Cusick. Encased in tank-like mechanical shells and shrieking 'Exterminate!', they debuted in the serial The Daleks (1963–64) and became the show's most iconic villains — embodiments of hatred, racial purity and total war — and enduring symbols of British popular culture.

Creation and design

Nation, a comedy writer turned dramatist, devised the Daleks for Doctor Who's second serial; designer Cusick, denied the budget for an actor-operated machine, built the pepper-pot shape around a seated operator, gliding on castors (later a tricycle base). Their voices — harsh ring-modulated staccato — and weapons (a gunstick and a manipulator arm resembling a sink plunger) were dictated by practical ingenuity. Within weeks 'Dalekmania' — toys, records, two Peter Cushing films — swept Britain.

In the fiction

Native to the planet Skaro, the Daleks descend from the Kaleds, mutated by their scientist Davros during a thousand-year war with the Thals; Davros, introduced in Genesis of the Daleks (1975) — voted among the series' greatest stories — stripped them of every emotion but hate. They fought the Time Lords in the Last Great Time War that rebooted the series' mythology in 2005, and individual Daleks have occasionally questioned the programme ('Into the Dalek', the lone 'Metaltron').

Cultural impact

The Daleks entered the Oxford English Dictionary and Parliamentary rhetoric; their survival across sixty years — through the show's cancellation (1989) and revival (2005) — is owed in part to Nation's estate, whose copyright negotiations famously complicate their use. Polls routinely name them the greatest screen monsters, ahead of every rival.
