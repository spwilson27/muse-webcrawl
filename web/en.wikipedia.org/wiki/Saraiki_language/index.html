Saraiki (سرائیکی, Sarāikī) is an Indo-Aryan language of the Lahnda group, spoken by the Saraiki people in the southwestern districts of the Pakistani province of Punjab, as well as in neighbouring areas of Sindh and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. Its main centre is the city of Multan and the surrounding region often called the Saraiki belt or Saraikistan.

Saraiki is written in a Perso-Arabic script with additional letters for its distinctive implosive consonants.

Classification and status

Saraiki is closely related to Punjabi and Sindhi, and forms part of the northwestern Indo-Aryan dialect continuum. Whether it should be regarded as a separate language or a dialect of Punjabi has long been debated. The name "Saraiki" became standard in the 1960s as part of a regional cultural and political movement; earlier names for the language included Multani, after its principal city. It is written in an adapted Perso-Arabic script with extra characters representing its characteristic implosive sounds, which distinguish it from standard Punjabi.

Literature

Saraiki has a rich poetic tradition, particularly in the Sufi tradition. Its most celebrated poet is Khwaja Ghulam Farid (1845–1901), whose kafi verses remain central to the region's cultural identity. The language is used in regional media, including television and radio broadcasts from Multan, and is taught in some local schools and universities.
