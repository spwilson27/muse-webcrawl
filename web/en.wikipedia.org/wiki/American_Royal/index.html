American Royal

The American Royal is a livestock show, horse show, rodeo, and barbecue competition held each year in the fall in Kansas City, Missouri. Founded in 1899, it is one of the nation's oldest and largest agricultural exhibitions and a major civic tradition in the Kansas City metropolitan area.

History

The event began in 1899 as the Kansas City Fat Stock Show, modelled on England's Royal Agricultural Society shows, and took the name American Royal in honour of the British Royal Show. It grew alongside the Kansas City Stockyards into a nationally prominent showcase for purebred cattle, swine, sheep, and horses, and its PRCA-sanctioned rodeo became a fixture of the professional circuit.

Barbecue contest

Since 1980 the American Royal has hosted what is billed as the world's largest barbecue contest, the American Royal World Series of Barbecue, drawing hundreds of competing teams and tens of thousands of spectators to the Kansas Speedway grounds. It anchors Kansas City's reputation as a barbecue capital alongside the city's famous sauce and burnt ends traditions.

Modern era

Today the American Royal Association, a non-profit, runs weeks of events each autumn, funds scholarships and agricultural education for youth, and after decades in the West Bottoms has developed a new year-round facility in Wyandotte County, Kansas. The event's name and history were also famously borrowed by the Kansas City Royals baseball team in 1969.
