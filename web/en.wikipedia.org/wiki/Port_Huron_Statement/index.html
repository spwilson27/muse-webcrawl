Port Huron Statement

The Port Huron Statement is the founding manifesto of Students for a Democratic Society (SDS), adopted at the organisation's convention in Port Huron, Michigan, in June 1962. Drafted principally by activist Tom Hayden, the roughly 25,700-word document became the defining statement of the American New Left.

Content

Opening with the famous words "We are people of this generation, bred in at least modest comfort, housed now in universities, looking uncomfortably to the world we inherit", the statement attacked racial discrimination, Cold War militarism, nuclear weapons, and corporate power, and criticised the apathy and conformity of postwar American life. Its central demand was "participatory democracy" — the extension of democratic decision-making into workplaces, universities, and communities.

Legacy

Some 20,000 copies were printed and distributed on campuses. The statement helped set the agenda for 1960s student activism, civil rights organising, and opposition to the Vietnam War, and remains a classic text of American radicalism, though later SDS factionalism moved far beyond its reform-minded tone.
