Irredentism
Origin: Italian Italia irredenta ("unredeemed Italy"), 1870s

Irredentism is a political movement and territorial claim by a state or ethnic group to lands inhabited by people considered ethnically or historically connected but ruled by another state. The term derives from the Italian movement to incorporate Italian-speaking territories of Austria-Hungary — Trentino, Trieste, Istria and Dalmatia — into the Kingdom of Italy after unification.

History

The movement Italia irredenta, popularised by Matteo Renato Imbriani in the 1870s, drove Italian foreign policy toward the First World War and the Treaty of London (1915). After 1918, Gabriele D'Annunzio's seizure of Fiume became a model for expansionist nationalism.

Examples

Historical and contemporary irredentist claims include Greater Germany and the Sudetenland, Hungarian claims after the Treaty of Trianon, Greek Megali Idea, Serbian, Albanian, Somali and Moroccan claims, Argentine claims to the Falkland Islands (Islas Malvinas), and various claims in South Asia and East Asia. Pan-national movements (pan-Germanism, pan-Arabism, pan-Slavism) often contain irredentist elements.

International law

Irredentism conflicts with principles of territorial integrity and uti possidetis in modern international law, though it is often framed in the language of self-determination.
