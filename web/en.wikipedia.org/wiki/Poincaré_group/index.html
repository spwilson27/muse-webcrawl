Poincaré group

The Poincaré group, named after Henri Poincaré, is the group of Minkowski spacetime isometries in special relativity. It is a ten-dimensional non-abelian Lie group consisting of all transformations that preserve the Minkowski interval: translations in space and time, rotations, and Lorentz boosts. Formally it is the semidirect product of the translations of Minkowski space by the Lorentz group.

The Poincaré group combines spacetime translations with the Lorentz transformations of special relativity.

Overview

The Poincaré group has ten generators: four for translations (corresponding to energy and momentum), three for rotations (angular momentum) and three for boosts. Its associated conserved quantities follow from Noether's theorem and express the homogeneity and isotropy of spacetime.

Poincaré algebra

The Lie algebra of the Poincaré group — the Poincaré algebra — is characterised by commutation relations between the momentum operator P, the Lorentz generators M, and rotation and boost generators. Its Casimir invariants are the mass squared, PμPμ = m², and the square of the Pauli–Lubanski pseudovector, which encodes spin.

Role in quantum field theory

In 1939 Eugene Wigner showed that elementary particles correspond to irreducible unitary representations of the Poincaré group, classified by mass and spin (or helicity for massless particles). This classification — massive particles with spin s, massless particles with helicity, and exotic continuous-spin cases — is the foundation of particle taxonomy in quantum field theory. In the limit of infinite mass the group contracts to the Galilean group of Newtonian mechanics.

History

Poincaré identified the group structure of Lorentz transformations in 1905, and Hermann Minkowski elaborated its geometry in 1908. The representation theory was developed by Wigner and, in mathematical form, by George Mackey.
