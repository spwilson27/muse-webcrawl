Francisco de Vitoria

Francisco de Vitoria O.P. (c. 1483 – 12 August 1546) was a Spanish Renaissance Roman Catholic philosopher, theologian, and jurist of Renaissance Spain. He is the founder of the tradition in philosophy known as the School of Salamanca, noted especially for his contributions to the theory of just war and international law. He has in the past been described as the "father of international law", though contemporary academics have suggested that such a description is anachronistic, since the concept of post-Westphalian international law did not truly originate until much later.

Life and thought

Vitoria entered the Dominican Order and studied and taught in Paris before returning to Spain, where he held the Prime Chair of Theology at the University of Salamanca from 1526 until his death. His lectures (relectiones) addressed the pressing questions of the Spanish conquest of the Americas. In De Indis (1539) he denied that conquest could be justified by discovery, papal donation, or the supposed sins of the Indigenous peoples, affirming instead that the Native Americans were rational beings with rights of ownership and self-government. In De iure belli he developed conditions for just war, and in his wider work he argued for a law of nations (ius gentium) grounded in natural reason and binding on all peoples.

Legacy

Vitoria's ideas influenced later thinkers such as Hugo Grotius and remain foundational to modern discussions of international law, human rights, and the ethics of war.
