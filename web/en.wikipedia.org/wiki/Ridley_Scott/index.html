Ridley Scott

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Ridley Scott directing on a smoke-filled set in the 1980s, storyboard in hand — the visual stylist at the height of his powers]

Sir Ridley Scott (born 30 November 1937) is an English film director and producer, one of the most commercially successful and visually influential directors in cinema. His films include Alien (1979), Blade Runner (1982), Thelma & Louise (1991), Gladiator (2000, Academy Award for Best Picture), Black Hawk Down (2001), Kingdom of Heaven (2005), American Gangster (2007), The Martian (2015), Napoleon (2023) and Gladiator II (2024).

Born in South Shields and trained at the Royal College of Art, Scott made some 2,000 television commercials (the 1973 Hovis 'Boy on Bike' advert is Britain's favourite) through his company RSA before his feature debut The Duellists (1977) won at Cannes.

The visualist

Trained as a designer, Scott storyboards everything (his 'Ridleygrams'), lights with smoke and backlight, and builds worlds that feel used: Alien's truckers-in-space Nostromo and Blade Runner's retro-fitted Los Angeles are design's twin testaments. His work rate into his eighties — often a film a year, shot with multiple cameras at speed — is the industry's marvel; his brother Tony Scott (d. 2012) matched him in the producer-director family firm, Scott Free.

Reception

Three Best Director Oscar nominations without a win is the standard trivia of his career; Gladiator revived the sword-and-sandal epic single-handedly and his historical films (Kingdom of Heaven's director's cut especially) have been revalued upward steadily. Knighted in 2003, he remains British cinema's most powerful living director by output and influence.
