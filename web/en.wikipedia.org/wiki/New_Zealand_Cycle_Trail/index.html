The New Zealand Cycle Trail (Māori: Ngā Haerenga) is a national project to build a network of cycle routes across New Zealand. Its flagship 'Great Rides' are mostly off-road trails showcasing the country's landscapes, complemented by 'Heartland Rides' on quiet rural roads.

History

The idea gained prominence at the 2009 Job Summit and was championed by Prime Minister John Key as a tourism and regional development initiative. Government funding built on existing popular trails such as the Otago Central Rail Trail (opened 2000), widely credited as the model for the network.

The Great Rides

The 23 Great Rides range from one-day to week-long journeys: the Alps 2 Ocean trail from Aoraki / Mount Cook to Oamaru, the West Coast Wilderness Trail, the Timber Trail through Pureora Forest, the Great Taste Trail in Tasman, and the Lake Dunstan Trail in Central Otago, among others. Most are graded 1–5 from easiest to expert.

Impact

The trails were designed to draw visitors to regional New Zealand and have become significant local economic drivers for small towns along their routes, supporting accommodation, shuttle and food businesses. Governance is shared between local trail trusts, councils and the Department of Conservation.
