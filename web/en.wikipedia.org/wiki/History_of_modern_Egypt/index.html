History of modern Egypt

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Cairo, founded in 969 CE, is Egypt's capital and the Arab world's largest city.

The history of modern Egypt is conventionally dated from Napoleon's invasion of 1798, which shattered Mamluk rule and opened Egypt to European influence. Under Muhammad Ali (r. 1805–1848) and his dynasty, Egypt was modernised and effectively independent while remaining nominally Ottoman. Debt led to British occupation in 1882; the Kingdom of Egypt (1922) retained heavy British influence until the Free Officers' revolution of 1952 founded the republic of Gamal Abdel Nasser, followed by the presidencies of Anwar Sadat, Hosni Mubarak, the 2011 revolution, and Abdel Fattah el-Sisi.

Muhammad Ali and his dynasty

Muhammad Ali, an Albanian Ottoman officer, seized power after the French evacuation, massacred the Mamluks (1811), conscripted a modern army, conquered Sudan and Arabia, and twice defeated the Ottomans in Syria before European intervention confined him to hereditary rule of Egypt. His successors built railways and the Suez Canal (opened 1869 under Ismail Pasha), whose debts forced the sale of Egypt's canal shares to Britain (1875) and, after the Urabi revolt, British occupation in 1882.

From occupation to revolution

Egypt was declared a British protectorate in 1914; the 1919 revolution led to nominal independence in 1922 under King Fuad, with Britain retaining control of the Canal Zone and Sudan. The Wafd party dominated parliamentary politics, while the Muslim Brotherhood (founded 1928) grew in opposition. Defeat in the 1948 Arab–Israeli war and the Cairo fire of January 1952 discredited the monarchy; the Free Officers under Muhammad Naguib and Nasser seized power in July 1952, and a republic was declared in 1953.

The republic

Nasser nationalised the Suez Canal in 1956, surviving the tripartite Suez Crisis, and pursued Arab socialism and pan-Arab leadership until defeat in the Six-Day War of 1967. Sadat expelled Soviet advisers, launched the October War of 1973, then made peace with Israel at Camp David (1978) and liberalised the economy (infitah); he was assassinated in 1981. Mubarak's three decades ended in the January 2011 uprising; after the Muslim Brotherhood's Mohamed Morsi won elections in 2012, the military under el-Sisi removed him in 2013 and Sisi became president in 2014.
