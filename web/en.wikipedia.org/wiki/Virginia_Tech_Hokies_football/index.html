Virginia Tech Hokies football

The Virginia Tech Hokies football team represents the Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University in the sport of American football. The Hokies compete in the NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision and the Atlantic Coast Conference (ACC). They play home games at Lane Stadium in Blacksburg, Virginia, the largest stadium in the state.

History

The program began in 1892. Its defining era came under head coach Frank Beamer (1987–2015), a Virginia Tech alumnus who built the Hokies into a national power known for tenacious defense and "Beamer Ball" special teams. The 1999 team, led by quarterback Michael Vick, went undefeated in the regular season and reached the BCS National Championship Game, losing to Florida State in the 2000 Sugar Bowl. The Hokies won four ACC championships between 2004 and 2010 and sustained a streak of 27 consecutive bowl appearances (1993–2019).

Traditions

The team's entrance to Metallica's "Enter Sandman" is regarded as one of college football's most electrifying traditions. Virginia Tech's fiercest rivalries are with Virginia (for the Commonwealth Cup), West Virginia (Black Diamond Trophy), and Miami.

Recent years

Following Beamer's retirement, Justin Fuente coached the team from 2016 to 2021; Brent Pry took over in 2022.
