Grinding machine

TypeMachine tool
ProcessAbrasive machining
ToolGrinding wheel (rotating abrasive)
UsesFinishing, sharpening, shaping, material removal

A grinding machine, often shortened to grinder, is a power tool (or machine tool) used for grinding. It is a type of machining using an abrasive wheel as the cutting tool. Each grain of abrasive on the wheel's surface cuts a small chip from the workpiece via shear deformation.

Process

Grinding is used to finish workpieces that must show high surface quality (e.g., low surface roughness) and high accuracy of shape and dimension. Because each abrasive grain removes only a tiny amount of material, grinding can achieve tolerances as fine as ±0.002 mm and very smooth finishes. It can also machine very hard materials, such as hardened steel and carbide.

Types

Common types include surface grinders (flat surfaces), cylindrical grinders (rotating workpieces, including centerless grinding), internal grinders (bores), tool and cutter grinders, bench grinders, belt grinders, and jig grinders. Creep-feed grinding removes material in deep, slow passes.

History

Natural abrasive stones were used for sharpening from prehistory. Precision grinding machines developed in the 19th century — notably the universal cylindrical grinder of Brown & Sharpe (1876) — became foundational to mass-production engineering.
