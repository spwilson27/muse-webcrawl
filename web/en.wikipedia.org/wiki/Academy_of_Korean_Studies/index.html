The Academy of Korean Studies (AKS; Korean: 한국학중앙연구원) is a South Korean research institute dedicated to the study of Korean culture, history, and society. Established on June 30, 1978, under the Ministry of Education, the academy is located in Seongnam, Gyeonggi Province, near Seoul.

History

The academy was founded in 1978 as a national center for Korean studies at a time of rapid modernization in South Korea, when scholars and policymakers were concerned about preserving and systematically studying the country's cultural heritage. Over the following decades it expanded its collections, its graduate program, and its role in compiling encyclopedic resources on Korea.

Activities

The academy's principal activities include:

  • Operation of the Graduate School of Korean Studies, which trains specialists in Korean history, philosophy, literature, anthropology, and related fields, including many international students.
  • Publication of the Encyclopedia of Korean Culture and the digital Encyclopedia of Korean Folk Culture, major reference works on Korean traditions.
  • Compilation and digitization of classical Korean documents, including materials related to the Joseon dynasty.
  • Support for Korean studies programs abroad through grants, textbook development, and academic exchanges.
  • Publication of scholarly journals such as The Review of Korean Studies and Korea Journal.

Collections

The academy's library holds one of the largest collections of pre-modern Korean documents in the country, including royal manuscripts, genealogical records, and old maps. Many of these materials have been digitized and made available to researchers online.
