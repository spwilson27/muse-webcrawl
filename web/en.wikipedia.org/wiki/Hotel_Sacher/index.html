The Hotel Sacher is a five-star luxury hotel in the Innere Stadt district of Vienna, Austria, situated behind the Vienna State Opera. It is famous for the specialty of the house, the Sachertorte, a chocolate cake with apricot filling. There is also an art gallery in the hotel, with works from the 19th century.

History

The hotel was founded in 1876 by Eduard Sacher, son of Franz Sacher, who had created the Sachertorte in 1832 as a young apprentice in Prince Metternich's kitchen. After Eduard's death, his widow Anna Sacher ran the hotel from 1892 to 1930 and made it one of the great social institutions of fin-de-siècle Vienna, famous for its private dining rooms (chambres séparées), its Lipizzaner horses, and Anna herself with her French bulldogs and cigars.

The hotel fell on hard times after the First World War and was taken over in 1934 by the Gürtler family, who still own it. A second hotel, the Hotel Sacher Salzburg (the former Österreichischer Hof), joined the group in the 1980s.

The Sacher-Torte dispute

A long legal battle with Vienna's Demel bakery over the right to call a cake the "Original Sacher-Torte" ended in a settlement in the 1960s: the Hotel Sacher uses a round chocolate seal and one layer of apricot jam under the icing, while Demel's "Eduard Sacher-Torte" has its jam in the middle. The hotel produces hundreds of thousands of the cakes a year, still largely by hand, and ships them worldwide.
