Gaziantep, historically and still informally called Antep, is a major city in the western part of Turkey's Southeastern Anatolia Region, close to the Syrian border. It is the sixth-largest city in Turkey and one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, with settlement reaching back to the Hittite period.

History

Known in antiquity as part of the region of Commagene, near the Roman city of Zeugma, the city passed through Byzantine, Arab, Seljuk, Mamluk, and Ottoman hands. During the Turkish War of Independence, the defence of Antep against French occupation forces in 1920–21 became legendary, and in 1921 the Turkish parliament added the honorific "Gazi" ("the veteran/warrior") to the city's name. In February 2023 the city was severely affected by the Turkey–Syria earthquakes, which damaged its historic castle.

Culture and economy

Gaziantep is famed throughout Turkey for its cuisine — designated a UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy in 2015 — especially its baklava, pistachios, and kebabs. The Zeugma Mosaic Museum, housing mosaics from the Roman city of Zeugma including the celebrated "Gypsy Girl", is among the largest mosaic museums in the world. Industrially, the city is a major centre for textiles, food processing, and machinery exports.
