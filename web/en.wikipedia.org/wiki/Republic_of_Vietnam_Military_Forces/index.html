Republic of Vietnam Military Forces

The Republic of Vietnam Military Forces (RVNMF; Vietnamese: Quân lực Việt Nam Cộng hòa, QLVNCH) were the armed forces of South Vietnam (the Republic of Vietnam) from their formal creation in 1955 until the fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975. At their peak in the early 1970s they numbered over one million regular personnel, making them among the largest military forces in the world.

History

The RVNMF grew out of the Vietnamese National Army of the French-associated State of Vietnam. After the 1954 Geneva Accords partitioned Vietnam, the United States became South Vietnam's principal patron, building up the Army of the Republic of Vietnam (ARVN) for conventional defence while the Viet Cong insurgency expanded. During the American escalation of the Vietnam War, South Vietnamese forces played a supporting role; under "Vietnamization" after 1969, they assumed primary responsibility, fighting the 1972 Easter Offensive with heavy U.S. air support.

Collapse

The 1973 Paris Peace Accords ended the U.S. combat role and aid was progressively cut. In March–April 1975 the People's Army of Vietnam's spring offensive routed the RVNMF: Ban Me Thuot fell, a disastrous withdrawal from the Central Highlands destroyed II Corps, and Da Nang and Hue collapsed. President Nguyễn Văn Thiệu resigned; on 30 April 1975 North Vietnamese tanks entered the Presidential Palace in Saigon and General Dương Văn Minh surrendered unconditionally, dissolving the Republic of Vietnam and its armed forces.

Organisation

The Joint General Staff in Saigon directed four corps tactical zones (I–IV Corps). In addition to the regular services, the Regional Forces and Popular Forces ("Ruff-Puffs") provided local security, and the Civilian Irregular Defense Groups operated among ethnic minorities under U.S. Special Forces tutelage.
