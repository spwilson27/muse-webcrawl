JSTOR (short for Journal Storage) is a digital library of academic journals, books and primary sources, founded in 1994. It provides full-text searches of digitised back issues of hundreds of well-known academic journals, usually behind a "moving wall" that excludes the most recent few years of each title, as well as access to books, images and primary sources. JSTOR is part of ITHAKA, a not-for-profit organisation that also operates Portico and Ithaka S+R.

History

JSTOR was conceived by William G. Bowen, then president of the Andrew W. Mellon Foundation and former president of Princeton University, as a solution to the rising costs libraries faced in storing ever-growing runs of printed journals. Initially funded by the Mellon Foundation, it began with digitised backfiles of ten journals in economics and history and was used by a small group of libraries; it became independent in 1995 and merged with ITHAKA in 2009.

Content and access

The archive grew to include thousands of journal titles across the humanities, social sciences and sciences, along with tens of thousands of open-access and publisher-supplied ebooks and millions of images via Artstor. Most access is through institutional subscriptions held by libraries and universities; individuals may use limited free programmes such as the Register & Read scheme and open content collections. JSTOR also operates JSTOR Labs projects such as Data for Research and the Understanding Series.

The Swartz case

In 2010–2011, computer programmer and activist Aaron Swartz downloaded millions of journal articles from JSTOR using an MIT network connection. Federal prosecutors charged him with wire fraud and computer fraud; he died in January 2013 while facing prosecution. JSTOR, which had settled with Swartz and declined to pursue civil action, subsequently expanded free and open access offerings, and the case became a landmark in debates over open access to scholarship.

JSTOR Daily and education

JSTOR Daily, an online magazine launched in 2014, publishes articles connecting current events with scholarly research. JSTOR also supports secondary education through JPASS and school access programmes, and worked with prison education programmes.
