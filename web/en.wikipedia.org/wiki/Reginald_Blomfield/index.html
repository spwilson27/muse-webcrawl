Sir Reginald Theodore Blomfield (20 December 1856 – 27 December 1942) was a British architect and gardener designer who was responsible for the design of many buildings in England and in several other countries.

Educated at Haileybury and Exeter College, Oxford, Blomfield was articled to his uncle, the architect Sir Arthur Blomfield, and trained at the Royal Academy Schools. He became a leading figure of the Arts and Crafts-influenced country house tradition and a champion of formal garden design, which he promoted in his book The Formal Garden in England (1892).

Work

Blomfield is particularly remembered for his war memorial work for the Imperial (now Commonwealth) War Graves Commission after the First World War: he designed the Cross of Sacrifice, found in Commonwealth war cemeteries around the world, and the Menin Gate memorial in Ypres, Belgium. In London he rebuilt much of the Quadrant of Regent Street, and designed Lambeth Bridge. He was knighted in 1919.
