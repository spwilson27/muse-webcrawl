North American Charging Standard

[Image: NACS connector]

The North American Charging System/Standard (NACS), now standardized as SAE J3400, is an electric vehicle charging connector system originally developed by Tesla, Inc. and used on its vehicles and Supercharger network in North America from 2012. The compact connector handles both AC charging and DC fast charging through the same two pins, supporting up to 1 MW in its specification.

Adoption

Tesla opened the design to other manufacturers in November 2022. In May 2023 Ford announced it would adopt NACS on its future EVs and gain access to the Supercharger network via adapters; General Motors, Rivian, Volvo, Mercedes-Benz, Hyundai, and most other major automakers selling in North America followed. SAE International published the J3400 standard in December 2023, formalising NACS as a public standard.

Technical details

The connector uses the same power pins for AC (up to 80 A at 240 V) and DC charging, communicating via the protocols used by CCS (DIN 70121 and ISO 15118) over a single-wire CAN-based signalling scheme. Its small form factor and lack of moving parts contrast with the larger CCS1 (Combo 1) connector it has largely displaced in North America.
