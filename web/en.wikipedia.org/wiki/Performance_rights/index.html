Performance rights

Performance rights are the rights under copyright law to perform, or to authorise others to perform, a copyrighted work in public. In music, the public performance right covers playing a song live, broadcasting it on radio or television, streaming it, or playing a recording of it in a public place such as a shop, restaurant, or concert venue. The rights in the underlying musical composition are usually administered separately from the rights in a particular sound recording.

Performance rights organisations

Because no individual songwriter could monitor every performance of their works, performance rights organisations (PROs) — also called collecting societies — license public performances on behalf of many rights holders, collect royalties, and distribute them to members according to surveys and reports of what was played. Major PROs include ASCAP and BMI in the United States, PRS for Music in the United Kingdom, SACEM in France, and GEMA in Germany. Venues and broadcasters typically take a blanket licence covering the society's whole repertoire.

Sound recordings

In many countries, performers and record companies also hold rights in sound recordings when they are broadcast or publicly performed ("neighbouring rights"). The United States is a notable exception: terrestrial radio there pays royalties to songwriters and publishers but not, traditionally, to the performers on the recording, although digital and satellite services do pay a recording performance royalty administered by SoundExchange.
