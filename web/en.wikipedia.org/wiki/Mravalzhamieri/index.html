Mravalzhamieri (Georgian: მრავალჟამიერი, literally "many years" or "long life") is a traditional Georgian polyphonic song, a rousing celebratory chant wishing long life and prosperity. It is one of the best-known genres of Georgian folk singing and is closely associated with the festive table — above all the traditional feast, or supra — where it is sung by groups of male singers, often with raised glasses, as a toast to guests, hosts, the newly married, or life itself.

Characteristics

Like much Georgian traditional music, Mravalzhamieri is built on three-part polyphony: a lead voice states the melody, a second voice answers and elaborates above it, and a bass voice sustains the harmonic foundation. The song is noted for its bright, major-mode character, vigorous rhythmic drive and the gleeful repeated invocation of its title word, which functions as a wish of many happy years. Variants exist across Georgia's regions — Kakhetian versions in the east are particularly famous and are often performed with great volume and stamina by the singers of the table.

Cultural significance

Georgian polyphonic singing was proclaimed by UNESCO in 2001 a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity and was later inscribed on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Mravalzhamieri remains a living emblem of Georgian hospitality: it is performed at weddings, birthdays, religious feasts and official welcomes, and has been recorded by leading folk ensembles and arranged for choirs worldwide.
