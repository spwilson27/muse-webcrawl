Secundus of Asti

Diedc. 119 AD, Asti, Italy
Venerated inRoman Catholic Church, Eastern Orthodox Church
Feast29 March
PatronageAsti

Secundus of Asti (Italian: San Secondo di Asti) was an early Christian martyr and saint venerated as the patron saint of the city of Asti in Piedmont, northwestern Italy. According to tradition, he was a soldier or officer of the Theban Legion who was executed for his Christian faith during the persecutions under the emperor Hadrian, around 119 AD.

Legend

Tradition holds that Secundus was a native of Asti who had been secretly converted to Christianity. When the governor Sapricius arrived in the city to enforce the imperial decrees against Christians, Secundus openly professed his faith. He was imprisoned, tortured, and finally beheaded outside the city walls. His relics were preserved by the local Christian community, and a church was later built over the site associated with his martyrdom.

Veneration

Secundus is commemorated on 29 March. The Collegiate Church of San Secondo in Asti, a prominent Romanesque-Gothic building, is dedicated to him, and his relics are kept there. His feast is a major civic celebration in Asti, traditionally marked by a procession and other public observances in the days around the feast of the Ascension.
