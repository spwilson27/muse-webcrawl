2-Acetyl-1-pyrroline (2-AP) is an aroma compound and flavourant that gives freshly baked bread, jasmine rice, basmati rice, and pandan their characteristic appetising smells. It is also a key component of the odour of popcorn. 2-AP is an extremely potent odorant, with an odour threshold in air of about 0.02 nanograms per litre — among the lowest known for any flavour compound.

Occurrence

George Buttery and co-workers identified 2-AP in cooked rice in 1982 and confirmed it as the principal contributor to the "popcorn-like" aroma of fragrant rice varieties such as jasmine and basmati. It occurs in pandan leaves (Pandanus amaryllifolius), bread flowers (Vallaris glabra), tiger scent-marking fluid, and is generated during cooking by reactions involving the amino acid proline and sugars or methylglyoxal — a pathway related to the Maillard reaction. The same compound forms in the crust of baking bread and in freshly popped corn.

Genetics of fragrant rice

In rice, accumulation of 2-AP is controlled largely by the Badh2 gene, which encodes betaine aldehyde dehydrogenase. Fragrant varieties carry loss-of-function alleles of Badh2: without the enzyme, a precursor (Δ¹-pyrroline) is diverted into 2-AP synthesis, and the grain becomes aromatic. This single-gene trait has been a major target of rice breeding and genetic marker selection.

Use

Because 2-AP is volatile and unstable in pure form — it is usually handled as a dilute solution — the flavour industry uses it sparingly in baked-goods and snack flavourings. Its natural presence is also used as a quality marker for premium fragrant rice.
