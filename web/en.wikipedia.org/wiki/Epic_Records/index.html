Epic Records

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Epic Records is an American record label owned by Sony Music Entertainment, a subsidiary of Sony Corporation of America. The label was founded predominantly as a jazz and classical music label in 1953, but has since expanded to include releases across a variety of genres, including pop, rock, R&B, and hip hop.

History

Epic was launched by Columbia Records (CBS) in 1953, with a distinctive yellow, black, and red logo; its early catalog emphasized jazz artists such as Duke Ellington and classical releases. In the 1960s and 1970s the label broadened into rock and pop, signing acts including Donovan, the Yardbirds, Jeff Beck, and Boston, whose 1976 debut became one of the best-selling debut albums of all time.

Epic achieved enormous success in the 1980s with Michael Jackson — whose Thriller (1982), released by Epic, remains the best-selling album of all time — as well as releases by George Michael, Cyndi Lauper, and Sade. In later decades the roster included Pearl Jam, Rage Against the Machine, Fiona Apple, Travis Scott, Future, and 21 Savage. From 2011 to 2017 the label was chaired by L.A. Reid; Sylvia Rhone became chairwoman and CEO in 2019.
