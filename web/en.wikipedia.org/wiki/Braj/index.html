Braj

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Braj, also known as Brij or Brajbhoomi, is a historical and cultural region in northern India, centred on Mathura and Vrindavan on the banks of the Yamuna River. It lies mainly in western Uttar Pradesh, with portions extending into neighbouring Haryana, Rajasthan, and Madhya Pradesh. Braj is revered in Hinduism as the land of the childhood and youth of the deity Krishna, who was born in Mathura and grew up in the villages of Gokul, Nandgaon, and Barsana.

Geography

The traditional Braj region is often defined as the Braj Chaurasi Kos — a pilgrimage circuit of some 84 kos (about 250 km) encompassing twelve forests (vanas) and numerous sacred sites associated with Krishna's life: Mathura, Vrindavan, Govardhan, Gokul, Nandgaon, Barsana, and Radha Kund, among others. The region is bounded broadly by the Yamuna to the east and by the Aravalli foothills toward the west.

Culture

Braj is the homeland of the Braj Bhasha language, a literary form of Hindi that was the dominant medium of Hindi poetry from the 16th to the 19th century, used by poets such as Surdas, whose Sursagar recounts Krishna's childhood. The region is famous for its festivals — notably the boisterous Holi celebrations of Barsana (Lathmar Holi), Janmashtami at Krishna's birthplace, and the Govardhan Puja — and for devotional traditions including Ras Lila dance-drama, kirtan, and the temple culture of the Vallabha and Gaudiya Vaishnava schools. The Braj Yatra pilgrimage, which traces Krishna's legendary wanderings on foot, continues to draw millions of pilgrims annually.

History

Mathura was an important city of ancient India — a centre of trade and of Buddhist and Jain activity as well as early Krishna worship — and flourished under the Kushan and Gupta empires. The devotional geography of Braj was largely re-created in the 16th century, when saints such as Vallabha and Chaitanya and their followers identified and consecrated the sites of Krishna's pastimes and built the great red-sandstone temples of Vrindavan. The region subsequently lay within the Mughal empire, and later came under Jat, Maratha, and British rule.
