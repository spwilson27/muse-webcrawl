Fairfield is a city in and the county seat of Solano County, California, in the North Bay subregion of the San Francisco Bay Area. It is generally considered the midpoint between the cities of San Francisco and Sacramento, approximately 36 miles (58 km) northeast of San Francisco and 36 miles southwest of Sacramento. The population was 119,881 at the 2020 census.

The city is home to Travis Air Force Base and the Jelly Belly Candy Company, whose factory offers public tours. Neighboring cities include Suisun City, Vacaville and Vallejo.

History

The area was inhabited by the Patwin people before European contact. The city was founded in 1859 by clippership captain Robert H. Waterman, who named it after his hometown of Fairfield, Connecticut. Waterman laid out the town and donated land for the county courthouse when Fairfield became the county seat of Solano County in 1858, displacing Benicia. The city was incorporated in 1903.

Travis Air Force Base, established in 1942 east of the city, became a major employer and a principal gateway for United States military airlift to the Pacific.

Geography

Fairfield lies at the northeastern edge of the Bay Area, where the coastal plain meets the rolling hills of the Vaca Mountains. According to the United States Census Bureau, the city has a total area of about 41.4 square miles (107 km²), of which a small portion is water in the Suisun Marsh area to the south.

Economy

Major employers include Travis Air Force Base, Solano County government, the Fairfield-Suisun Unified School District, NorthBay Healthcare, Kaiser Permanente, and the Jelly Belly Candy Company. The Solano Town Center mall anchors retail in the city.

Transportation

Interstate 80 passes through Fairfield, and the Fairfield Transportation Center serves regional bus routes. The Fairfield–Vacaville Hannigan station on the Capitol Corridor line provides Amtrak service between San Jose and Auburn.
