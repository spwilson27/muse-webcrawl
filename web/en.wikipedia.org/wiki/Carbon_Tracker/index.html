Carbon Tracker Initiative

Type: Non-profit financial think tank
Founded: 2009
Headquarters: London, United Kingdom
Focus: Climate risk in capital markets; fossil fuel investment
Known for: "Unburnable carbon" and the "carbon bubble"

The Carbon Tracker Initiative is a London-based non-profit financial think tank that researches the impact of climate change on capital markets and investment in fossil fuels. Founded in 2009, it aims to align global capital markets with the goals of the Paris Agreement by showing investors the financial risks of continued dependence on coal, oil and gas.

Unburnable carbon and the carbon bubble

Carbon Tracker's 2011 report Unburnable Carbon – Are the World's Financial Markets Carrying a Carbon Bubble? calculated that burning all proven fossil fuel reserves would release around 2,795 gigatonnes of CO₂ — far more than the roughly 565 gigatonnes compatible with limiting global warming to 2 °C with reasonable probability. It argued that up to 80% of reserves held by fossil fuel companies were therefore "unburnable" if climate targets were to be met, and that the market value of those companies — and the reserves on their balance sheets — constituted a carbon bubble posing a systemic financial risk.

Work and influence

The organisation's subsequent analyses have examined the economics of individual fossil fuel projects, the risk of stranded assets, the cost curves of solar and wind power, and the point at which clean energy growth begins to displace fossil fuel demand in sectors such as power generation and road transport. Its reports have been widely cited in the financial press, influenced investor engagement and divestment campaigns, and contributed evidence to central banks and regulators concerned with climate-related financial risk, including through the concept of a "carbon budget" for the global economy.

Key figures associated with Carbon Tracker include its founder Mark Campanale and former Bank of England governor Mark Carney, whose 2015 "Tragedy of the Horizon" speech drew on the carbon bubble analysis in framing climate change as a threat to financial stability.
