The Dee Estuary (Welsh: Aber Dyfrdwy) is a large estuary by means of which the River Dee flows into Liverpool Bay in the Irish Sea. It lies on the border between north-east Wales and the Wirral Peninsula in north-west England. Historically, the estuary formed the maritime outlet for Chester, once one of England's principal ports, until silting diverted trade to Liverpool.

Geography

The estuary is funnel-shaped, some 19 km long and up to 9 km wide, with vast intertidal sandbanks and mudflats exposed at low tide, fringed by saltmarshes — notably at Parkgate and around the Neston and Heswall shores. Settlements on the Welsh side include Connah's Quay, Flint and Mostyn; on the English side, Neston, Parkgate, Heswall, West Kirby and Hoylake. The island of Hilbre Island sits at the estuary's mouth.

Ecology

The estuary is one of Britain's most important sites for wildfowl and waders, supporting internationally significant numbers of birds such as pintail, oystercatcher, knot, dunlin and redshank. The whole estuary is a Site of Special Scientific Interest, a Special Protection Area and a Ramsar wetland of international importance.

Industry and crossings

Industry has included coal mining, steelmaking at Shotton, and the docks at Mostyn. The River Dee is crossed near its head by the Flintshire Bridge and by railway and road bridges at Queensferry and Chester.
