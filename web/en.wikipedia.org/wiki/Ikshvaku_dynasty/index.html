Ikshvaku dynasty

The Ikshvaku dynasty (Sanskrit: इक्ष्वाकु, Ikṣvāku), also called the Suryavamsha or Solar dynasty, is a legendary dynasty of ancient India founded by the king Ikshvaku, a son of Vaivasvata Manu. The dynasty ruled from Ayodhya over the kingdom of Kosala, and its most famous descendant in Hindu tradition is Rama, the hero of the Ramayana.

Legendary lineage

Puranic genealogies trace the line from the sun god Surya through Manu to Ikshvaku, and thence through kings such as Sagara, Bhagiratha, Raghu (from whom the line takes the name Raghuvamsha), Aja, Dasharatha, and Rama. The poet Kalidasa celebrated the dynasty in his Sanskrit epic Raghuvamsha. Jain and Buddhist traditions also incorporate Ikshvaku: the first Jain tirthankara Rishabhanatha is said to belong to the house, and Gautama Buddha's Shakya clan claimed descent from Ikshvaku.

Historical Ikshvakus

A historical dynasty also bore the name: the Andhra Ikshvakus ruled the Krishna valley region from Nagarjunakonda in the 3rd–4th centuries CE after the decline of the Satavahanas. Known from their inscriptions and coinage, these Ikshvaku kings performed Vedic sacrifices while their queens patronised Buddhism, endowing the great monastic complexes at Nagarjunakonda.
