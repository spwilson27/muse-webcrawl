Samsa (food)

TypeSavoury pastry
RegionCentral Asia (Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Xinjiang)
Main ingredientsDough, minced meat (lamb or beef), onion, cumin
Similar dishesSamosa, sambusa, börek

Samsa is a savoury pastry popular across Central Asia, including Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and the Uyghur communities of Xinjiang. It belongs to the wider family of filled pastries — related to the South Asian samosa — that spread along the trade routes of Asia under similar names.

Preparation

Traditional samsa is made by wrapping a filling of minced or finely chopped meat — usually lamb or beef mixed generously with onion and seasoned with cumin, black pepper and salt — in dough, often a laminated dough brushed with fat to produce flaky layers. The parcels are folded into triangles, squares or rounds, sprinkled with sesame or nigella seeds, and traditionally baked plastered to the inner wall of a clay tandoor oven, though modern versions are oven-baked.

Variations

Fillings vary by region and season: pumpkin samsa is a beloved autumn variety in Uzbekistan, and potato, greens or cheese fillings are also common. Meat for festive samsa may be hand-diced rather than ground, with pieces of tail fat (kurdyuk) added for juiciness.

Cultural role

Samsa is sold by street vendors and chaikhanas (teahouses) throughout Central Asia and is eaten as a snack, a breakfast item or part of a larger meal with tea. It also appears wherever Central Asian diaspora communities have settled, including Russia and Israel.
