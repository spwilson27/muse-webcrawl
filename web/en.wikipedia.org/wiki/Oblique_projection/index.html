Oblique projection

Oblique projection is a simple type of technical drawing of graphical projection used for producing two-dimensional images of three-dimensional objects. The objects are not drawn in perspective, and the projection rays are parallel to one another but strike the projection plane at an angle other than ninety degrees. The face of the object parallel to the picture plane is shown in its true shape, while depth is drawn at an angle with a chosen foreshortening.

Types

In cavalier projection, the receding axis is drawn at full scale, typically at 45 degrees, which can make objects look elongated. In cabinet projection, named for its use in furniture drawings, the receding lines are drawn at half scale, giving a more natural appearance. Military projection, used historically for fortification plans, uses a 45-degree elevation. Oblique projection was widely used in Chinese and Japanese painting and remains common in technical illustration and video game art because of its simplicity.
