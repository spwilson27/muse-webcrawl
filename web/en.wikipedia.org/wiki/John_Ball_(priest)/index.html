John Ball (priest)

John Ball (c. 1338 – 1381) was an English priest and radical preacher who became a leading voice of the Peasants' Revolt of 1381. His sermons preached social equality, encapsulated in the famous couplet: "When Adam delved and Eve span, who was then the gentleman?"

Life and revolt

Ball, associated with Colchester and York, was repeatedly imprisoned and excommunicated for his unorthodox preaching against the hierarchy of church and state. Freed by rebels from Maidstone prison in June 1381, he preached to the assembled rebels at Blackheath, urging the abolition of lordship and distinctions of rank.

Death

After the revolt's collapse and the death of Wat Tyler, Ball was captured at Coventry, tried at St Albans in the presence of Richard II, and hanged, drawn and quartered on 15 July 1381.
