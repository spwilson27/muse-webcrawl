Moïse Kapenda Tshombe (10 November 1919 – 29 June 1969) was a Congolese businessman and politician. He served as the president of the secessionist State of Katanga from 1960 to 1963 and as prime minister of the Democratic Republic of the Congo from 1964 to 1965. His declaration of Katanga's independence, backed by Belgian mining interests, precipitated the Congo Crisis.

Katanga secession

Days after Congolese independence in June 1960, Tshombe declared the mineral-rich southern province of Katanga independent, with Belgian support motivated by the copper and uranium mines of the Union Minière du Haut-Katanga. The secession defied the central government of Patrice Lumumba and Joseph Kasa-Vubu.

United Nations forces intervened, and after fighting and negotiation the secession ended in January 1963, when Tshombe went into exile in Europe.

Prime minister and exile

In 1964 Tshombe was recalled to head a government of national reconciliation and to suppress the Simba rebellion, employing white mercenaries in the campaign. President Kasa-Vubu dismissed him in October 1965, and after Joseph Mobutu's coup Tshombe again went into exile, this time in Spain.

In 1967 his aircraft was hijacked and diverted to Algeria, where he was held under house arrest until his death in 1969, officially of a heart attack.
