Courtroom sketch

A courtroom sketch is an artistic depiction of the proceedings in a court of law. In many jurisdictions, cameras are not allowed in courtrooms, or are restricted during particular proceedings, so news media rely on sketch artists to provide visual records of trials, arraignments, and hearings. Courtroom artists work quickly, usually in pastel, charcoal, or watercolor, capturing the likenesses of defendants, judges, witnesses, and attorneys along with the atmosphere of the courtroom, often completing a distributable drawing within minutes of leaving the courtroom.

Practice

Sketch artists typically sit among the press or public gallery with a sketch pad, making rapid studies during proceedings; some jurisdictions prohibit sketching in the courtroom itself, requiring artists to work from memory and notes immediately outside. Famous trials documented by sketch artists include the Charles Manson trial, the O. J. Simpson trial, and proceedings at the Supreme Court of the United States, where cameras remain barred from oral arguments. Prominent American courtroom artists include Bill Robles, Jane Rosenberg, and Elizabeth Williams; Rosenberg drew wide attention for her 2015 sketch of NFL quarterback Tom Brady during the "Deflategate" hearings. Historically, engravings after courtroom sketches illustrated newspaper coverage of sensational nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century trials.

Cameras in court

The sketch tradition is tied to restrictions stemming from the media frenzy around the 1935 Lindbergh kidnapping trial, after which the American Bar Association's Canon 35 and Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 53 barred photography and broadcasting from federal courtrooms. U.S. state courts have gradually liberalized camera access since the Supreme Court's 1981 decision in Chandler v. Florida, but the federal judiciary and the Supreme Court retain broad prohibitions, preserving a working niche for the courtroom sketch artist.
