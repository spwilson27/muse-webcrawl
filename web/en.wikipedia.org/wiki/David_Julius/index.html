David Julius

David Jay Julius (born November 4, 1955) is an American physiologist at the University of California, San Francisco, known for his work on the molecular mechanisms of pain sensation and thermosensation. He discovered the TRPV1 receptor, which detects capsaicin (the heat in chili peppers) and noxious heat, and the TRPM8 receptor for cold and menthol. He shared the 2021 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Ardem Patapoutian "for their discoveries of receptors for temperature and touch."

Research

In 1997 Julius's group cloned the capsaicin receptor TRPV1, showing it to be an ion channel activated by temperatures above about 43 °C — the first molecular sensor of painful heat. He subsequently identified TRPM8, the receptor for cold temperatures and menthol, and TRPA1, activated by irritants such as wasabi and tear gas. These discoveries revealed how the nervous system converts thermal and chemical stimuli into signals, and opened new approaches to pain treatment. Julius also used toxins from tarantulas, scorpions, and cone snails as probes of ion-channel function.

Career

Julius studied at MIT and UC Berkeley, did postdoctoral work at Columbia, and joined UCSF in 1989, later chairing its Department of Physiology. He is a member of the National Academy of Sciences and has received the Shaw Prize, the Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences, and the Kavli Prize in Neuroscience.
