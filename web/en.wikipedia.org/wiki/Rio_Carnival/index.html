The Rio Carnival (Portuguese: Carnaval do Rio de Janeiro) is a festival held every year in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, in the days leading up to Lent, ending on Ash Wednesday. Considered the biggest carnival in the world, it is famed for the competitive parades of the city's samba schools in the Sambadrome and for hundreds of street parties, called blocos, that fill neighbourhoods across the city.

History

Carnival celebrations in Rio date to the Portuguese colonial era, when the pre-Lenten entrudo involved rowdy street water fights. In the nineteenth century, elite masked balls and organised societies with floats civilised the festival, while Afro-Brazilian communities in neighbourhoods such as Praça Onze developed the samba traditions that would come to define it. The first samba school, Deixa Falar, was founded in 1928; official competitions began in the 1930s, and in 1984 the parades moved to the purpose-built Sambadrome Marquês de Sapucaí, designed by Oscar Niemeyer.

Samba school parades

The top twelve schools of the Special Group each stage a parade of thousands of costumed performers, elaborate floats, and a thundering drum section (bateria), built around an original theme song and storyline (enredo). Judges score categories including drums, harmony, floats, costumes, and the flag-bearing couple (mestre-sala e porta-bandeira); the champion is crowned on Ash Wednesday, while the lowest-placed school is relegated to the second-tier Série A. Preparing a parade occupies the school communities — many rooted in favelas — for the entire year.

Street carnival

Alongside the ticketed Sambadrome parades, hundreds of free blocos parade through the streets, some drawing hundreds of thousands of costumed followers. Famous blocos include Cordão do Bola Preta, one of the oldest, and the Banda de Ipanema. The 2021 carnival was cancelled because of the COVID-19 pandemic — the first cancellation in over a century — and the 2022 edition was postponed to April.
