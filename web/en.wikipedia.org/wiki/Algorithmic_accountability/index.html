Algorithmic accountability

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

FieldComputer science, law, public policy
Related conceptsAlgorithmic bias; algorithmic transparency; AI governance

Algorithmic accountability refers to the attribution of responsibility for the outcomes of decisions made or assisted by algorithms, and to the mechanisms — audits, impact assessments, transparency requirements, and legal liability — by which organisations that deploy automated decision systems can be held answerable for harms such as discrimination, error, or loss of due process.

Background

As machine-learning systems came to be used in hiring, credit, policing, welfare administration, and content moderation, researchers and journalists documented cases in which automated systems reproduced or amplified social biases, while their opacity made the errors hard to detect or challenge. Investigations such as ProPublica's 2016 analysis of the COMPAS recidivism-risk tool became touchstones in the debate over algorithmic bias and accountability.

Approaches

Proposed tools include algorithmic impact assessments modelled on environmental and privacy impact assessments; independent auditing of training data and model behaviour; documentation standards such as model cards and datasheets; requirements for explanations of individual decisions; and "accountability by design" in procurement. Legal frameworks have begun to impose duties directly, notably the European Union's GDPR provisions on automated decision-making and the EU AI Act, as well as local laws such as New York City's audit requirement for automated employment decision tools.

Criticism and open questions

Scholars debate who should bear responsibility when harm emerges from a chain of data providers, developers, and deployers — the "problem of many hands" — and whether audits can detect substantive unfairness rather than merely procedural compliance. Others warn that transparency alone, without power to contest or refuse automated decisions, is insufficient for accountability.
