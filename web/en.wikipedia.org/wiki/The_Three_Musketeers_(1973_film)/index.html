The Three Musketeers (also known as The Three Musketeers (The Queen's Diamonds)) is a 1973 swashbuckler film directed by Richard Lester, written by George MacDonald Fraser, and based on Alexandre Dumas's 1844 novel. Shot back-to-back with its sequel, The Four Musketeers (1974), it is noted for its blend of adventure, comedy, and unusually realistic, clumsy sword-fighting choreography.

Plot

Young d'Artagnan arrives in Paris to join the King's Musketeers and falls in with Athos, Porthos, and Aramis. When Queen Anne of Austria gives her diamond studs to her lover, the Duke of Buckingham, Cardinal Richelieu schemes to expose her by demanding she wear them at a ball. The musketeers and d'Artagnan race to London to retrieve the studs from Buckingham while Richelieu's agents, Milady de Winter and the Comte de Rochefort, try to stop them.

Production and the "Salkind clause"

The producers, Alexander and Ilya Salkind, shot enough material for a single long film but decided during production to release it as two features, without informing the cast, who had been paid for one film. The resulting lawsuits led the Screen Actors Guild to require contracts to specify the number of films being made — a provision still known as the "Salkind clause". The film was a critical and commercial success, and a third film, The Return of the Musketeers, followed in 1989.
