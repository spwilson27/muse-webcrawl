Lecturer

A lecturer is a person who gives lectures, especially as an occupation at a university or similar institution. In much of the English-speaking world outside North America, "lecturer" is also a specific academic rank below senior lecturer and professor. In the United States and Canada, the term is used more loosely for anyone who teaches at the post-secondary level, often without tenure.

United Kingdom and Ireland

In the UK, lecturer is the first permanent academic post, roughly equivalent to assistant professor in North America. Lecturers typically hold a doctorate, carry out research, teach undergraduate and postgraduate students, and supervise research students. Promotion leads to senior lecturer, reader (at some institutions), and professor.

North America

In the United States and Canada, a lecturer is usually a non-tenure-track instructor hired primarily for teaching, sometimes on renewable contracts. Some institutions use titles such as senior lecturer or principal lecturer for more experienced teaching staff. Adjunct lecturers are employed part-time, course by course.

Other countries

In Germany, university teachers may hold titles such as Dozent or Privatdozent. In Australia and New Zealand, lecturer is graded (Level B) above associate lecturer and below senior lecturer (Level C). In many Commonwealth countries the British ranking system is followed.
