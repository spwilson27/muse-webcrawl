Entertainment for troops

Entertainment for troops refers to the provision of live performance, film, music, and other forms of diversion for military personnel, whether stationed at home, at overseas bases, or in combat zones. Organised entertainment has been regarded as an important element of morale from the world wars onwards, with famous performers volunteering tours of duty behind the lines as well as on leave centres.

History

During the First World War, concert parties and travelling theatre troupes entertained soldiers near the front lines, while organisations such as the YMCA provided huts with music and recreation. In the Second World War, large-scale official schemes appeared: Britain's ENSA (Entertainments National Service Association) and the United States' USO (United Service Organizations) sent thousands of performers to theatres of war around the globe.

The Korean and Vietnam wars continued the tradition, with comedians such as Bob Hope becoming synonymous with Christmas tours for deployed servicemen. In more recent conflicts in Iraq and Afghanistan, troop entertainment has included rock concerts, stand-up comedy, professional wrestling, and celebrity visits coordinated by organisations such as Armed Forces Entertainment in the United States.

Forms

Troop entertainment has included:

  • Variety and revue shows mixing song, dance and comedy
  • Screenings of films, often before their civilian release
  • Concerts by popular musicians and bands
  • Sporting exhibitions and visits by athletes
  • Stand-up comedy tours to remote bases and ships at sea
