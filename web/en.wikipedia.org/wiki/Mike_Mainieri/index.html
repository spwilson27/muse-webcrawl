Mike Mainieri

Michael T. Mainieri Jr. (born July 24, 1938) is an American jazz vibraphonist, composer, arranger, and producer, best known as the founder and leader of the jazz fusion group Steps Ahead and as a pioneer of the amplified vibraphone and the "SynthAxe"-era MIDI vibraphone instrument he called the synth-vibe.

Career

A child prodigy from a musical Bronx family, Mainieri was performing professionally in his teens and joined Buddy Rich's band at 18, touring and recording with him into the early 1960s. He worked with Wes Montgomery, and in the late 1960s joined Jeremy Steig's pioneering fusion group Jeremy and the Satyrs. During the 1970s he became one of New York's most in-demand session musicians and producers, founding the production company that backed the White Elephant big band experiments with musicians including the Brecker brothers and Steve Gadd.

Steps Ahead

In 1979 Mainieri formed the group Steps (later Steps Ahead) with Michael Brecker, Don Grolnick, Eddie Gómez, and Steve Gadd — later line-ups included Peter Erskine, Eliane Elias, and Mike Stern. The band became one of the defining acoustic-electric fusion ensembles of the 1980s, with albums such as Steps Ahead (1983) and Magnetic (1986). Mainieri founded the NYC Records label and continued to record and tour, including his "American Diary" projects blending jazz with world music influences.
