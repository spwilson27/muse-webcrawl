Saraswati

Saraswati (Sanskrit: सरस्वती, romanized: Sarasvatī) is the Hindu goddess of knowledge, music, flowing water, abundance and wealth, language, and learning. She is one of the Tridevi, along with the goddesses Lakshmi and Parvati, and is regarded as the patroness of learning, intellect, creativity, and the arts.

Saraswati is one of the most ancient goddesses. In the Rigveda, she is a river goddess, identified with the great Sarasvati River, and by late Vedic texts she had become associated with speech (Vāc) and learning. She is generally depicted in a white sari, seated on a white lotus or riding a swan (hamsa), holding a veena, a book (the Vedas), a rosary (mala), and a pot of water.

Worship

Saraswati is worshipped throughout India and Nepal, particularly on the festival of Vasant Panchami (also called Saraswati Puja), when students place their books and instruments before her image to receive her blessings. She is also venerated in Jainism and in some schools of Buddhism, and as Benzaiten in Japan.

Iconography

The goddess is usually shown with four arms, representing the four aspects of human personality in learning: mind, intellect, alertness, and ego. Her vehicle is the swan or peacock. The river Saraswati, mentioned in the Rigveda, is believed to have flowed through northwestern India and is held sacred by Hindus.
