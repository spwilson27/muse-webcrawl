Local exchange trading system

A local exchange trading system (also local employment and trading system or local energy transfer system; abbreviated LETS) is a locally initiated, democratically organised, not-for-profit community enterprise that provides a community information service and record transactions of members exchanging goods and services by using the currency of locally created LETS Credits.

A community exchange market

History

The first LETS was founded by Michael Linton in the Comox Valley, British Columbia, Canada, in 1983, using a unit called the "Green Dollar". The idea spread rapidly in the 1980s and 1990s, especially in the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand, France (as SEL), Germany (as Tauschringe), and elsewhere. At their mid-1990s peak there were several hundred schemes in the UK alone, though numbers later declined as administration proved burdensome and internet platforms changed community organising.

How LETS works

Members list offers and requests in a directory and trade with one another in the local unit of account, usually valued at parity with, or pegged to the time value of, the national currency. No physical currency is needed: transactions are recorded in a central ledger (originally paper cheques and ledgers, later software such as Community Exchange System platforms). Balances may go negative — a commitment to offer services in future — and interest is not charged, distinguishing the mutual-credit structure from conventional money. A small levy, in national or local currency, funds administration.

Aims and criticism

Supporters argue LETS keeps wealth circulating locally, values unpaid and informal work, builds social capital, and gives unemployed people a way to trade skills. Critics note that schemes often stay small, depend on a few organisers, may struggle to supply high-demand professional services, and face questions about tax and benefit rules for earnings in local credits. LETS is related to, but distinct from, time banks (where the unit is strictly one hour of time) and local paper currencies such as Ithaca Hours.
