Video poker

Video poker is a casino game based on five-card draw poker, played on a computerized console similar in size to a slot machine. The player wagers, is dealt five cards from a virtual 52-card deck (in most variants), chooses which cards to hold, and receives replacements; the final hand is paid according to a fixed pay table displayed on the machine.

The game was introduced in the late 1970s, when Dale Electronics' "Poker-Matic" (1970) was followed by the SIRCOMA (later International Game Technology, IGT) "Draw Poker" machines that became widespread in Las Vegas casinos around 1979. Video poker grew rapidly because it appealed to players who preferred a game of skill and known odds rather than pure chance.

Play and strategy

Unlike slot machines, video poker outcomes are governed by the mathematics of a standard deck, so the return of a given machine can be calculated exactly from its pay table. Common variants include:

  • Jacks or Better — the original game; a pair of jacks or better is the lowest paying hand
  • Deuces Wild — all twos are wild cards
  • Joker Poker — a joker is added as a wild card
  • Double Bonus and Double Double Bonus — enhanced payouts for four-of-a-kind hands

Optimal strategy charts tell the player exactly which cards to hold in every situation. Full-pay Jacks or Better ("9/6", paying 9 coins for a full house and 6 for a flush) returns about 99.54% with perfect play, among the best odds in a casino; skilled players combined this with slot-club rewards to gain a small edge, making video poker a lasting concern for casino operators and a favorite of advantage players.
