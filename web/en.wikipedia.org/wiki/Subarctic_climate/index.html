Subarctic climate

The subarctic climate (also called subpolar climate, or boreal climate) is a continental climate with long, cold (often very cold) winters, and short, warm to cool summers. It is found on large landmasses, often away from the moderating effects of an ocean, generally at latitudes from 50°N to 70°N, poleward of the humid continental climates. It is one of the dominant climate types in the Northern Hemisphere, characterizing much of Alaska, Canada, Scandinavia, Siberia, and northern Mongolia.

Characteristics

Subarctic regions experience some of the most extreme seasonal temperature variations on Earth. Winter temperatures can fall below −40 °C (−40 °F) for extended periods, and the coldest reliably recorded temperatures outside Antarctica have been measured in the Siberian subarctic, such as at Oymyakon and Verkhoyansk in the Sakha Republic. Summers are short but can be surprisingly warm, with daytime temperatures occasionally exceeding 30 °C (86 °F).

Precipitation is generally low, concentrated in the warmer months, because the cold winter air cannot hold much moisture. Snowfall persists for many months, and permafrost underlies large areas, restricting drainage and producing extensive wetlands and muskeg in summer.

Subtypes

Under the Köppen climate classification, the subarctic climate comprises several subtypes. The most widespread, Dfc, has a short cool summer with fewer than four months averaging above 10 °C. The Dfd variant, found in eastern Siberia, adds extremely cold winters with a coldest-month mean below −38 °C. Monsoon-influenced variants (Dwc, Dwd) occur in northeastern Asia, while dry-summer variants (Dsc, Dsd) are found in parts of Alaska and Siberia at higher elevations.

Vegetation and life

The subarctic is the realm of the taiga, a belt of coniferous forest dominated by spruce, pine, fir, and larch. Trees grow slowly and often remain small because of the short growing season and nutrient-poor soils. Wildlife includes moose, caribou (reindeer), bears, lynx, wolves, and vast numbers of migratory birds that breed in the summer wetlands. Human settlement is sparse, concentrated in mining, forestry, and transport towns.
