Classical test theory

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Classical test theory (CTT) is a body of related psychometric theory that predicts outcomes of psychological testing, such as the difficulty of items or the ability of test-takers. It is the foundation of standard practice in educational and psychological measurement.

The true score model

CTT models each observed score X as the sum of a true score T and random error E:

X = T + E

Assuming errors are uncorrelated with the true score, the reliability of a test is the ratio of true-score variance to observed-score variance:

ρXX′ = σT² / σX²

Applications

The framework underlies common statistics: item difficulty (p-values), item–total correlations, the standard error of measurement, and internal-consistency coefficients such as Cronbach's alpha.

Limitations

CTT statistics are sample-dependent: item difficulty varies with the group tested. These limitations motivated item response theory, which models item and person parameters separately, although CTT remains widely used for its simplicity and robustness.
