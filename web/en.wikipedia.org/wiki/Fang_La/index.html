Fang La (Chinese: 方臘; died 1121) was a rebel leader who led a major uprising against the Song dynasty in the region of modern Zhejiang during the reign of Emperor Huizong. His rebellion (1120–1121) was one of the largest popular revolts of the Northern Song period, briefly seizing Hangzhou and other cities before being suppressed.

Background

Fang La came from Qingxi County (in present-day Chun'an, Zhejiang) and is said to have worked in the lacquer trade. The region suffered under heavy taxes and the court's requisitioning of timber, flowers and rocks for Huizong's imperial gardens, which created widespread resentment. Fang La also drew support from Manichaean religious networks, which the Song state periodically persecuted as heterodox.

Rebellion

The revolt broke out in late 1120. Fang La proclaimed himself a holy lord, organised his followers under a religious hierarchy, and rapidly captured Hangzhou — the rebels' greatest prize — along with much of the southeast. The court, distracted by diplomacy with the Jin dynasty over an alliance against the Liao, diverted the general Tong Guan and troops intended for the northern frontier. After hard fighting through 1121, Song forces retook Hangzhou and captured Fang La in the hills of Qingxi; he was executed at Kaifeng. Remnant bands held out into 1122.

In literature

Fang La appears as a major antagonist in the Ming-dynasty novel Water Margin, in which the Liangshan heroes are sent against him in their final campaign — a fictionalised mission that costs many of the heroes their lives. In the novel his forces are among the most formidable the outlaws face.
