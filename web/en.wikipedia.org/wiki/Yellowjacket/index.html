Yellowjacket

Yellowjacket
Kingdom:Animalia
Phylum:Arthropoda
Class:Insecta
Order:Hymenoptera
Family:Vespidae
Genera:Vespula, Dolichovespula
The German wasp (Vespula germanica), a typical yellowjacket

Yellowjacket or yellow jacket is the common name in North America for predatory social wasps of the genera Vespula and Dolichovespula. Members of these genera are known simply as wasps in other English-speaking countries. Most of these are black and yellow, like the eastern yellowjacket (Vespula maculifrons) and the aerial yellowjacket (Dolichovespula maculata), but some are black and white, and others have red markings. They can be identified by their distinctive combination of black-and-yellow color, small size (slightly larger than a bee), and entirely black antennae.

Characteristics

Yellowjackets are social insects that live in colonies founded each spring by a single fertilized queen. Workers, sterile females roughly 12 mm (0.5 in) long, build paper nests from chewed wood fiber mixed with saliva, either underground in abandoned rodent burrows, in wall voids and attics, or suspended from branches and eaves in the case of aerial-nesting species. A mature colony may contain several thousand workers by late summer. Unlike honey bees, yellowjackets have smooth stingers and can sting repeatedly, and they release alarm pheromones that can provoke group attacks when a nest is disturbed. Their stings can trigger life-threatening allergic reactions in sensitive individuals.

Diet and behavior

Adult yellowjackets feed on carbohydrate-rich foods such as nectar, fruit juices, and sugary human foods, while foraging workers hunt insects and other arthropods, which they chew up and feed to the larvae in the nest. In late summer and autumn, as colonies decline and food becomes scarce, yellowjackets often become aggressive scavengers around picnics, outdoor dining areas, and refuse containers, where they seek out sweet drinks and meat. They are also considered beneficial predators of many pest insects and are important pollinators of some plants.

Distribution

Species of Vespula and Dolichovespula are distributed throughout the Northern Hemisphere. Several European species, most notably the German wasp (Vespula germanica), have been accidentally introduced into North America, South America, South Africa, Australia, and New Zealand, where they can become serious pests; in New Zealand beech forests, invasive yellowjackets reach extremely high densities and compete with native wildlife for food.
