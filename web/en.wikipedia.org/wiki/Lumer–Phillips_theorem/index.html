In mathematics, the Lumer–Phillips theorem, named after Günter Lumer and Ralph Phillips, is a result in the theory of strongly continuous semigroups (C0-semigroups) that characterises the generators of contraction semigroups on a Banach space. It is a special and frequently used case of the Hille–Yosida theorem, and is a standard tool for proving that the operator governing a linear evolution equation—such as a diffusion or transport equation—is well posed.

Statement

Let X be a Banach space and let A : D(A) ⊆ X → X be a linear operator with dense domain. Then A generates a strongly continuous contraction semigroup on X if and only if:

  • A is dissipative, that is, for every x ∈ D(A) and every duality functional x* it holds that Re ⟨Ax, x*⟩ ≤ 0 — in a Hilbert space this is Re ⟨Ax, x⟩ ≤ 0; and
  • the range condition holds: Ran(λI − A) = X for some (equivalently, every) λ > 0.

On a reflexive Banach space, the range condition may be replaced by the weaker requirement that A is densely defined and dissipative with dense range of λI − A appropriately; in Hilbert spaces the theorem takes the particularly clean form that a densely defined operator generates a contraction semigroup if and only if it is dissipative and λI − A is surjective for some λ > 0.

Use

To apply the theorem to a partial differential equation, one defines the spatial differential operator on a suitable function space, checks dissipativity—typically by integration by parts, with boundary terms controlled by the boundary conditions—and verifies solvability of the stationary resolvent equation. This makes the Lumer–Phillips theorem a cornerstone of the semigroup approach to linear PDEs and control theory.
