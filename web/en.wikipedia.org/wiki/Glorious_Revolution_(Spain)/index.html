Glorious Revolution (Spain)

The Glorious Revolution (Spanish: la Gloriosa), also known as the September Revolution (la Septembrina), was a revolution in Spain in September 1868 that deposed Queen Isabella II and began the six-year period known as the Sexenio Democrático (1868–1874), Spain's first experience of democratic government.

Background

By the 1860s, Isabella II's reign was widely discredited by corruption, authoritarian government, and the monopoly on power of the Moderate Party. Progressive and unionist generals, exiled after failed uprisings — including the 1866 revolt led by General Juan Prim — conspired with democrats and republicans in the Pact of Ostend (1866) to overthrow the monarchy.

The revolution

The uprising began on 19 September 1868 at Cádiz, where Admiral Juan Bautista Topete and the fleet pronounced against the queen, and Generals Prim and Francisco Serrano rallied the army under a manifesto promising universal suffrage and civil liberties. Government forces were defeated at the Battle of Alcolea Bridge (28 September), and Isabella fled to France. A provisional government under Serrano proclaimed a liberal programme and called elections to a Constituent Cortes by universal male suffrage.

Consequences

The revolution produced the progressive Constitution of 1869, freedom of the press and religion, and a search for a new king that ended with the election of Amadeo of Savoy in 1870. His abdication in 1873 led to the First Spanish Republic, cantonal rebellions, and eventually the Bourbon Restoration of 1874. The Glorious Revolution is regarded as a landmark of Spanish liberalism and the first attempt at democracy in Spain.
