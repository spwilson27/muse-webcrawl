Avaris (Egyptian: Hut-waret, Greek: Αὔαρις) was the Hyksos capital of Egypt in the eastern Nile Delta, at the modern site of Tell el-Dab'a. Founded as a planned harbour town in the Middle Kingdom, it grew into a large cosmopolitan city and served as the seat of the Fifteenth Dynasty, the "rulers of foreign lands" (Hyksos), during the Second Intermediate Period.

History

The site began in the 12th Dynasty as a royal estate and port on the Pelusiac branch of the Nile. West Asiatic (Canaanite) settlers and soldiers settled there in increasing numbers, bringing Levantine house plans, burials with weapons, and temples of Syro-Palestinian type. During the 14th Dynasty, Asiatic rulers governed from Avaris, and around the mid-17th century BCE the Hyksos kings made it their capital, ruling Lower and Middle Egypt for about a century.

Excavations by Manfred Bietak of the Austrian Archaeological Institute revealed palaces, a major temple precinct, wealthy tombs, and—remarkably—Minoan-style frescoes in a later palace area, evidence of wide Mediterranean contacts. After the Theban pharaoh Ahmose I captured Avaris (c. 1550 BCE), the site declined; a fortress and palace district (later associated with Peru-nefer and then the Ramesside capital Pi-Ramesses nearby) briefly succeeded it.
