The Amur River (Russian: Амур) is the world's tenth longest river, forming part of the border between the Russian Far East and northeastern China (Manchuria). In Chinese it is called the Heilong Jiang (黑龙江), the "Black Dragon River." Measured from the source of its Argun tributary, the system stretches about 4,444 km. The river flows east from the confluence of the Shilka and Argun, past Blagoveshchensk and Khabarovsk — where it receives the Ussuri — and turns northeast to empty into the Strait of Tartary opposite Sakhalin Island.

History

The Amur basin is home to many indigenous peoples, including Tungusic and Mongolic groups. The region was contested between Russia and Qing China: the Treaty of Nerchinsk (1689) first fixed the border, while the Treaties of Aigun (1858) and Peking (1860) ceded the left bank and the Ussuri region to Russia. Border disputes flared as late as the Sino-Soviet conflict of 1969 before a final demarcation was completed in the 1990s–2000s. The first bridge across the river, at Khabarovsk, opened in 1916 as part of the Trans-Siberian Railway.

Nature

The Amur basin is exceptionally diverse, harbouring kaluga sturgeon, Amur leopard and Siberian tiger habitat in its tributary regions, and vast wetlands important for cranes and other migratory birds. Catastrophic floods occurred in 2013. Bridges linking Russia and China — at Blagoveshchensk–Heihe (road, opened 2022) and Nizhneleninskoye–Tongjiang (rail) — have strengthened cross-border trade.
