Fredrikstad

Fredrikstad (Norwegian: [ˈfrɛdrɪkstɑ]) is a city and municipality in Østfold county, Norway, situated where the river Glomma — Norway's longest — meets the Oslofjord. Founded as a fortified town in 1567, it is renowned for its Old Town (Gamlebyen), the best-preserved fortress town in Northern Europe. Together with neighbouring Sarpsborg it forms one of Norway's largest urban areas, and the municipality has a population of roughly 85,000.

History

Fredrikstad was founded by King Frederick II of Denmark-Norway in 1567 after Swedish troops burned nearby Sarpsborg during the Northern Seven Years' War; the citizens were moved to the Glomma's mouth and the new town was fortified against further Swedish attack. The fortifications were greatly expanded in the 17th century, and the town withstood Swedish sieges during the Great Northern War. In the 19th century Fredrikstad became a major timber and shipping port — the "plank town" exporting sawn wood across Europe — and later a shipbuilding centre. In 1997 it merged with surrounding municipalities; in 2015 it was voted Norway's most attractive city.

The Old Town

Gamlebyen, on the east bank of the Glomma and connected to the modern centre by a free city ferry, is laid out on a Renaissance grid inside star-shaped moats and ramparts. It contains well-preserved 18th-century barracks, storehouses and officers' houses, the Kongsten Fort (1685) on a nearby hill, and a popular model railway and museums. Fredrikstad Cathedral (1880) and the modern town centre stand on the west bank. The surrounding skerries and the Hvaler islands are a major summer recreation area.

Economy and sport

Modern Fredrikstad's economy centres on services, chemicals and maritime industries around the Øra industrial area. The local football club, Fredrikstad FK, founded in 1903, is one of Norway's historically successful clubs, with nine league titles and twelve Norwegian Cups, playing at the Fredrikstad Stadion across the river from the Old Town.
