Federal Chancellery of Austria

The Federal Chancellery (German: Bundeskanzleramt, BKA) is the government department that serves the Chancellor of Austria, coordinating the work of the federal government and housing a number of policy portfolios. Its headquarters on Ballhausplatz in Vienna, opposite the Hofburg palace, has been the seat of Austrian heads of government since the eighteenth century, and "Ballhausplatz" is used as a metonym for the Austrian government much as "Downing Street" is in Britain.

Building

The Baroque building was constructed in the 1710s–1720s, to designs associated with Johann Lucas von Hildebrandt, originally for the Court Chancellery. It hosted negotiations during the Congress of Vienna in 1814–15 and was the office of foreign ministers of the Austrian Empire, including Metternich. The Austrian federal chancellors have worked there since 1918, interrupted by the annexation period of 1938–45.

Functions

Beyond supporting the Chancellor and the Council of Ministers, the Chancellery has at various times been responsible for areas such as European Union coordination, women's affairs, civil service, media, national security coordination and the federal archives. Its staff also manages the federal government's legal service and general policy coordination with the federal ministries.
