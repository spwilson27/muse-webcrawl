Rape culture

Rape culture is a term used in feminist theory and sociology for a social environment in which sexual violence is pervasive and normalised through cultural practices, media representations, institutional responses, and attitudes such as victim blaming, trivialisation of assault, and tolerance of sexual harassment.

Origins

The term emerged in second-wave feminist writing of the 1970s, notably in the 1975 documentary film Rape Culture by Margaret Lazarus and Renner Wunderlich, and in works such as Susan Brownmiller's Against Our Will (1975). It was popularised for a wider audience by the 1993 anthology Transforming a Rape Culture.

Discussion

Proponents argue the concept connects individual acts of violence to broader norms — for example, low prosecution and conviction rates, "rape myths" about victims' behaviour, and media framing — and it has informed movements such as SlutWalk and Me Too. Critics dispute the concept's scope and empirical basis, arguing that in many Western legal systems rape is severely punished and that the term overgeneralises. The concept is also applied, with debate, to settings such as prisons and militaries.
