Functional dyspepsia

Functional dyspepsia (FD) is a common functional gastrointestinal disorder characterised by symptoms centred in the upper abdomen — postprandial fullness, early satiety, epigastric pain or burning — that cannot be explained by structural disease on routine investigation, including endoscopy. It is one of the disorders of gut–brain interaction defined by the Rome IV criteria.


The upper gastrointestinal tract

Subtypes and causes

Rome IV divides functional dyspepsia into postprandial distress syndrome (PDS), dominated by meal-related fullness and early satiety, and epigastric pain syndrome (EPS), dominated by pain or burning unrelated to meals. Proposed mechanisms include impaired gastric accommodation, delayed or rapid gastric emptying, visceral hypersensitivity, low-grade duodenal inflammation and eosinophilia, altered gut microbiota, and psychological comorbidity; a subset of cases follows acute gastroenteritis. Helicobacter pylori infection explains symptoms in a minority and is treated when found.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is clinical after exclusion of alarm features (such as weight loss, anaemia or dysphagia) and, where indicated, endoscopy. Management includes reassurance, dietary modification, acid suppression with proton pump inhibitors, prokinetic agents, and neuromodulators such as low-dose tricyclic antidepressants; psychological therapies and fundic-relaxing or centrally acting agents are used for refractory symptoms.

Epidemiology

Functional dyspepsia affects roughly 10–20% of adults in population surveys, depending on the criteria used, and accounts for a large share of gastroenterology consultations, with a substantial impact on quality of life.
