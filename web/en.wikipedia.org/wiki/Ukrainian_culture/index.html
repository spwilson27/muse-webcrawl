Ukrainian culture

The culture of Ukraine encompasses the material and spiritual values of the Ukrainian people, formed over centuries at the crossroads of Eastern and Central Europe. It draws on the heritage of Kyivan Rus', the Cossack era, folk traditions of the countryside, and the influences of neighbouring peoples, while retaining a distinct language, literature, music and art.

Language and literature

Ukrainian is an East Slavic language written in Cyrillic. Modern Ukrainian literature is conventionally dated from Ivan Kotliarevsky's poem Eneida (1798). Its central figure is the poet and artist Taras Shevchenko, whose collection Kobzar (1840) is regarded as a cornerstone of national identity. Other major writers include Ivan Franko, Lesya Ukrainka and Mykhailo Kotsiubynsky.

Folk traditions

Ukrainian folk culture is famous for pysanky (intricately decorated Easter eggs), embroidery (vyshyvanka), folk songs and dances such as the hopak. Religious feasts, harvest rituals and wedding customs preserve pre-Christian and Christian elements. Cuisine features borscht — inscribed by UNESCO in 2022 as intangible cultural heritage in need of urgent safeguarding — varenyky (dumplings), holubtsi and salo.

Arts and music

Ukraine has a rich tradition of church architecture, from Kyiv's Saint Sophia Cathedral to wooden churches of the Carpathians. The bandura and kobzar minstrel tradition shaped its musical heritage, and composers such as Mykola Lysenko and Myroslav Skoryk built a national school. In the twentieth century, artists like Kazimir Malevich, born in Kyiv, influenced world avant-garde art.
