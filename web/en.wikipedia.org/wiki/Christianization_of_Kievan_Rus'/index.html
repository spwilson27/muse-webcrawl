Christianization of Kievan Rus'

The Christianization of Kievan Rus' culminated in 988, when Grand Prince Vladimir the Great was baptized and ordered the mass baptism of Kyiv's inhabitants in the Dnieper River, adopting Orthodox Christianity from Byzantium as the state religion.

Background

Vladimir's grandmother Olga had converted earlier, and Christian communities existed in Kyiv before 988. According to the Primary Chronicle, Vladimir sent envoys to compare Islam, Judaism, Latin and Byzantine Christianity, choosing the latter — a narrative partly legendary but reflecting real Byzantine ties sealed by Vladimir's marriage to Anna Porphyrogenita.

Consequences

Conversion brought Byzantine art, architecture, writing (Church Slavonic), and law, and integrated Rus' into Christian Europe. Pagan resistance persisted in rural areas for centuries.
