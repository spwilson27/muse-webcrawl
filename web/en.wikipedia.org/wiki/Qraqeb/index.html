Qraqeb

Qraqeb (Moroccan Arabic: قراقب, singular qarqaba), also spelled krakeb or qraqeb, are large metal castanet-like musical instruments traditionally used by the Gnawa people of Morocco. They consist of pairs of double-headed iron cymbals, typically held one in each hand, and form the rhythmic backbone of Gnawa music together with the three-stringed guembri bass lute.

Qraqeb: paired iron castanets of the Gnawa tradition.

Description

Each qraqeb is made of two cupped iron discs joined so that the player, holding a pair in each hand between thumb and fingers, can strike them together to produce a sharp, resonant clapping sound. The instruments are roughly 20 to 30 centimetres across. Because the player wears two per hand, intricate interlocking rhythms can be produced by combining strikes of the hands against each other and against the individual discs.

Use in Gnawa music

In Gnawa ceremonial music — notably the all-night lila ritual of healing and spirit invocation — qraqeb players form the percussion section accompanying the maâlem (master musician), who plays the guembri and sings. The metallic clatter of the qraqeb, played in complex cross-rhythms often based on cycles related to West African patterns, is believed to evoke the sound of shackles and chains, recalling the Gnawa's origins among enslaved West Africans brought to Morocco. Qraqeb are also heard in popular street performance and in the music of groups that have carried Gnawa rhythms to international audiences.
