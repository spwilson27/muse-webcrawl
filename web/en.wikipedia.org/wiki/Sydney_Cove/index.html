Sydney Cove

[Image placeholder: Sydney Cove]

Sydney Cove is a bay on the southern shore of Sydney Harbour, in the centre of Sydney, New South Wales, Australia. It was the site of the first European settlement in Australia when the First Fleet landed on 26 January 1788, and it remains the city's symbolic heart, framed by the Sydney Opera House at Bennelong Point and the Sydney Harbour Bridge.

History

The cove was named by Captain Arthur Phillip after Thomas Townshend, 1st Viscount Sydney, the British Home Secretary. Phillip chose the cove over Botany Bay because of its sheltered anchorage and fresh water supply from the Tank Stream. The Aboriginal name for the area is Warrane. The First Fleet's arrival is commemorated at Circular Quay, which was built by reclaiming the head of the cove in the 1840s.

Geography and landmarks

Sydney Cove lies between Bennelong Point to the east and Dawes Point to the west. Its shores host Circular Quay ferry wharves, the Museum of Contemporary Art, The Rocks district, and the Overseas Passenger Terminal. The Tank Stream, now a storm drain beneath the city streets, still empties into the cove.

Events

The cove is the focal point of Sydney's New Year's Eve fireworks and Vivid Sydney festival, and was central to the 1988 bicentennial celebrations.
