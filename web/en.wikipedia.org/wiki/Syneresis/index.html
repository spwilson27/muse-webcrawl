Syneresis

Syneresis (also spelled synæresis or synaeresis) is the extraction or expulsion of a liquid from a gel, as when serum drains from a contracting clot of blood. Syneresis can be observed when the amount of water on top of yogurt increases, or when whey separates from curds in cheese making. The word is also used in linguistics for the contraction of two vowels into a diphthong, and in chemistry for related separation processes.

In food science

In dairy products, syneresis is generally undesirable: the expelled liquid, called whey in the case of yogurt and cheese gels, makes products look watery. It is controlled by adjusting protein content, heat treatment of milk, fermentation conditions and stabilizers. In cheese manufacture, by contrast, syneresis is deliberately encouraged by cutting the curd and stirring, expelling whey to concentrate the solids.

In gels and polymers

Syneresis in polymer gels occurs as the network continues to crosslink or rearrange after gelation, contracting and squeezing out solvent. It is a significant consideration in sol-gel processing, contact lenses, and food gels based on starch, where retrogradation of amylose causes water to be released over time.
