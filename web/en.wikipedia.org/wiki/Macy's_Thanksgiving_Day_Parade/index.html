The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade is an annual parade in New York City presented by the department store chain Macy's, held on Thanksgiving morning since 1924. It is famous for its giant helium character balloons, elaborate floats, marching bands, musical performances, and a closing appearance by Santa Claus that traditionally marks the start of the Christmas shopping season. The three-hour event runs from the Upper West Side of Manhattan down Central Park West and Sixth Avenue to Macy's Herald Square flagship store.

History

The first parade, in 1924, was organized by Macy's immigrant employees, who marched in costume accompanied by animals borrowed from the Central Park Zoo; it ended with Santa Claus unveiled at the store. The giant balloons debuted in 1927, with Felix the Cat among the first, designed in part by puppeteer Tony Sarg. The parade was suspended from 1942 to 1944 for World War II (its rubber and helium were donated to the war effort). It gained national fame after appearing in the 1947 film Miracle on 34th Street, and NBC has broadcast it since 1953; recent audiences exceed 20 million television viewers.

Balloons and incidents

Balloons — which over the years have featured Mickey Mouse, Snoopy, Pikachu, Spider-Man, and many others — are handled by teams of trained volunteers and inflated the day before near the American Museum of Natural History. Wind has caused notable accidents: in 1997, a Cat in the Hat balloon struck a lamppost whose falling arm seriously injured a spectator, leading New York City to impose strict wind limits and balloon-size rules; the 2019 parade nearly grounded the balloons because of high winds. In 2020 the parade was reduced to a television-only event around Herald Square because of the COVID-19 pandemic. Each year also brings new float performances by Broadway casts and pop stars, and the Radio City Rockettes are perennial performers.
