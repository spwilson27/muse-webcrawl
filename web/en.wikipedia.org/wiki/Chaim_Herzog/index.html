Chaim Herzog

Chaim Herzog (17 September 1918 – 17 April 1997) was an Israeli politician, general, lawyer and author who served as the sixth President of Israel from 1983 to 1993. Born in Belfast and raised in Ireland and Mandatory Palestine, he was the son of Rabbi Yitzhak HaLevi Herzog.

Career

Herzog served in the British Army in World War II, in the Haganah and the Israel Defense Forces, heading Military Intelligence twice. After retiring in 1962 he entered law and media, became Israel's ambassador to the United Nations (1975–1978) – famous for denouncing UN Resolution 3379 ("Zionism is racism") – and a Knesset member for the Alignment.

Presidency

Elected president in 1983 and re-elected in 1988, he travelled widely and worked to reduce domestic tensions. He died in Tel Aviv in 1997.
