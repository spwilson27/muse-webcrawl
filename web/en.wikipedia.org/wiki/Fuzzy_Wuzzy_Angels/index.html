Fuzzy Wuzzy Angels

Fuzzy Wuzzy Angels was the name given by Australian soldiers to Papua New Guinean war carriers who, during the Second World War, supplied Australian and Allied troops and carried wounded soldiers over the Kokoda Track and other terrain in Papua and New Guinea. The nickname expresses affection and admiration for their gentleness and care; a famous poem by Sapper Bert Beros, "The Fuzzy Wuzzy Angels", celebrated their work.

The carriers were recruited, often under compulsion, through the Australian New Guinea Administrative Unit (ANGAU). Thousands served, and their contribution was crucial to the Kokoda Track campaign of 1942. Commemorations in both Australia and Papua New Guinea continue to honour them.
