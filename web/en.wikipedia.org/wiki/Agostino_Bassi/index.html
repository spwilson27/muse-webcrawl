Agostino Bassi

Agostino Bassi (25 September 1773 – 8 February 1856) was an Italian entomologist and pioneer of modern pathology. Working decades before Louis Pasteur, he demonstrated experimentally that a disease—the silkworm disease muscardine—was caused by a microscopic living organism, a fungus. His work provided the first experimental proof that a microorganism could cause a contagious disease, laying groundwork for the germ theory of disease.

Life

Bassi was born in Mairago, near Lodi in Lombardy, into a wealthy farming family. He studied law at the University of Pavia—where he also attended lectures by Lazzaro Spallanzani, Alessandro Volta, and Giovanni Antonio Scopoli—and took a degree in law in 1798, pursuing a career in administration. Alongside his official duties he devoted himself to agriculture, medicine, and natural history on the family farms.

The muscardine experiments

Muscardine (in Italian mal del segno or calcino) was devastating the economically vital silk industry. Beginning around 1807, Bassi investigated the disease for some 25 years. He showed that silkworms sickened when inoculated with material from diseased worms, that the contagious agent was a living organism—a parasitic fungus—and that the disease could be prevented by disinfection and by removing infected material. He published his findings in Del mal del segno, calcinaccio o moscardino (1835–1836), one part theoretical and one practical, recommending control measures for silk producers.

The fungus was later named Beauveria bassiana in his honour (originally Botrytis bassiana). The organism is now widely used as a biological insecticide.

Broader ideas and legacy

Bassi generalised from his experiments, suggesting that diseases of plants, animals, and humans—including measles, syphilis, and cholera—might be caused by living parasites. His work preceded and influenced Pasteur's germ theory, and Pasteur himself acknowledged Bassi's priority. Bassi also published on potato cultivation, cheese making, wine, leprosy, and cholera, and advocated effective disinfection practices.
