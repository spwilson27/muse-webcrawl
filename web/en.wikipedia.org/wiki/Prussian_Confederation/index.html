Prussian Confederation

The Prussian Confederation (German: Preußischer Bund; Polish: Związek Pruski) was an organisation formed in 1440 by Prussian cities, towns and nobles to oppose the rule and taxation of the Teutonic Order. In 1454 the Confederation rebelled against the Order, offered allegiance to King Casimir IV of Poland, and thereby sparked the Thirteen Years' War, which ended with western Prussia becoming the Polish province of Royal Prussia.

Formation

After the First Peace of Thorn (1411), the Teutonic Order imposed heavy indemnities and taxes on Prussian towns and landowners, provoking repeated complaints about arbitrary justice and currency debasement. On 14 March 1440, delegates of the Hanseatic cities of Danzig (Gdańsk), Elbing, Thorn and others, together with Prussian nobles, sealed a confederation at Marienwerder to defend their rights "against force and injustice".

Rebellion and the Thirteen Years' War

When Emperor Frederick III declared the Confederation illegal in 1453 and ordered its dissolution, its leaders renounced obedience to the Order in February 1454, seized many of its castles, and sent a delegation to Kraków offering to incorporate Prussia into the Crown of Poland in exchange for confirmation of their privileges. Casimir IV accepted, incorporating Prussia by the Act of Incorporation of 6 March 1454. The ensuing Thirteen Years' War (1454–1466) ended with the Second Peace of Thorn, under which the western territories became Royal Prussia under the Polish crown.
