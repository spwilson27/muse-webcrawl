Book of Daniel

The Book of Daniel is a biblical book combining court tales (chapters 1–6) and apocalyptic visions (7–12) centred on Daniel, a Jewish exile in Babylon. It is written in Hebrew and Aramaic and set in the 6th century BC, though most scholars date its final form to the Maccabean era (2nd century BC).

Contents

Famous stories include Daniel interpreting Nebuchadnezzar's dream, the fiery furnace of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, Belshazzar's feast and the writing on the wall, and Daniel in the lions' den. The visions describe successive kingdoms, the Son of Man, and resurrection.

Themes and reception

Themes include God's sovereignty over empires, faithfulness under persecution, and hope of final deliverance. Daniel is a Ketuvim book in the Hebrew Bible and a prophetic book in Christian Old Testaments, influential on later apocalyptic literature including Revelation.
