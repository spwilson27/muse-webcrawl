Nerve plexus

A nerve plexus is a plexus (branching network) of intersecting nerves, formed where spinal nerve roots merge and split so that the axons arising from several spinal segments are redistributed into different peripheral nerves. Blood or lymphatic vessels may form plexuses in a similar way.

Major plexuses

The four primary nerve plexuses formed from ventral rami of spinal nerves are:

  • Cervical plexus (C1–C4), supplying the neck and giving rise to the phrenic nerve
  • Brachial plexus (C5–T1), supplying the upper limb
  • Lumbar plexus (L1–L4), supplying the lower abdominal wall, groin and anterior thigh
  • Sacral plexus (L4–S4), supplying the pelvis, buttock and lower limb, and giving rise to the sciatic nerve

There are also autonomic plexuses, such as the celiac (solar) plexus, which carry autonomic fibres to the viscera. Because fibres from several spinal levels mix within a plexus, injury at a single spinal root produces a different pattern of deficit from injury to a named peripheral nerve.
