Cub Scouting (Boy Scouts of America)

Cub Scouts are the youngest members of Scouting BSA's family of programs.

Cub Scouting is a program of the Boy Scouts of America (BSA) for children in kindergarten through fifth grade, roughly ages five to ten. It is the largest of the BSA's membership divisions and is designed as a family-centered program of citizenship training, character development and personal fitness.

Organization

Cub Scouts belong to a pack, chartered by a community organization such as a school, church or civic group. Within the pack, boys and girls are grouped by grade level into dens, each led by an adult den leader. Packs meet monthly for pack meetings where achievements are recognized, while dens typically meet weekly or biweekly.

Ranks

Advancement is organized by school grade: Lion (kindergarten), Tiger (first grade), Wolf (second grade), Bear (third grade), and Webelos (fourth and fifth grades, from "We'll Be Loyal Scouts"). Webelos Scouts work toward the Arrow of Light award, the highest rank in Cub Scouting, before crossing over into a Scouts BSA troop.

Activities

Signature activities include the Pinewood Derby, in which Scouts race small wooden cars they build with an adult partner; the Raingutter Regatta; day camps and resident camps; and community service projects. The Cub Scout motto is "Do Your Best," and the program is built around the Scout Oath and Scout Law.

History

Cub Scouting was officially launched by the BSA in 1930, drawing on the Wolf Cub program developed in Britain and on earlier American experiments with programs for younger boys. Girls were admitted to Cub Scouting beginning in 2018.
