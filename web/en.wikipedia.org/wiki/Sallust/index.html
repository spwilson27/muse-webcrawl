Sallust

Gaius Sallustius Crispus (86 – c. 35 BC), usually called Sallust, was a Roman historian and politician, and one of the great Latin prose stylists. His monographs on the Catilinarian conspiracy and the Jugurthine War are the earliest surviving works of Roman history.

Born86 BC, Amiternum, Roman Republic
Diedc. 35 BC, Rome
OccupationHistorian, politician
WorksBellum Catilinae, Bellum Jugurthinum, Historiae (fragmentary)

Life

Born to a plebeian family in Amiternum, Sallust became tribune of the plebs in 52 BC, was expelled from the Senate in 50 BC for immorality, and attached himself to Julius Caesar, who restored him and made him governor of Africa Nova, where he amassed a fortune and built the famous Gardens of Sallust in Rome.

Works

In retirement Sallust wrote Bellum Catilinae (on Catiline's conspiracy of 63 BC) and Bellum Jugurthinum (on Rome's war against Jugurtha of Numidia, 112–105 BC), diagnosing Rome's decline through greed and faction after the destruction of Carthage. His Historiae, covering 78–67 BC, survive only in fragments. His terse, archaic, moralising style — influenced by Thucydides and Cato the Elder — was imitated by Tacitus.
