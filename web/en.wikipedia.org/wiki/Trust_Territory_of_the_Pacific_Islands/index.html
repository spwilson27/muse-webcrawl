The Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands (TTPI) was a United Nations trust territory in Micronesia administered by the United States from 1947 to 1994. It comprised the island groups formerly held by Japan under a League of Nations mandate — the Carolines, the Marshalls, and the Marianas (except Guam) — spread over some 3 million square miles of the western Pacific.

The districts of the Trust Territory in Micronesia

History

The islands had passed from Spanish to German rule before Japan seized them in 1914 and administered them as the South Seas Mandate. United States forces captured them in heavy fighting during the Second World War, including the battles of Kwajalein, Saipan, and Peleliu. On 18 July 1947 the UN Security Council approved a trusteeship agreement making the TTPI the UN's only "strategic trust," answerable to the Security Council rather than the General Assembly. The U.S. Navy administered the territory until 1951, when responsibility passed to the Department of the Interior, headquartered at Saipan.

The territory was divided into six districts: the Marshall Islands, Ponape (Pohnpei), Truk (Chuuk), Yap, Palau, and the Mariana Islands. Parts of the Marshalls, notably Bikini and Enewetak atolls, were used for American nuclear weapons testing between 1946 and 1958, displacing their inhabitants and leaving a lasting legacy of compensation claims and health studies.

Dissolution

From the 1960s the districts negotiated their political futures separately. The Northern Mariana Islands chose commonwealth status in union with the United States in 1975. The Marshall Islands and the Federated States of Micronesia became independent in free association with the U.S. in 1986. Palau, delayed by disputes over its nuclear-free constitution, approved its Compact of Free Association in 1993 and became independent on 1 October 1994, upon which the UN Security Council formally terminated the trusteeship — closing the last of the UN trust territories.
