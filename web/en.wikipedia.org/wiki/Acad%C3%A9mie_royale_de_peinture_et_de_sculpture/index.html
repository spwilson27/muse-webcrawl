Académie royale de peinture et de sculpture

Formation1648
Dissolved1793
TypeRoyal academy of art
HeadquartersParis, France

The Académie royale de peinture et de sculpture (Royal Academy of Painting and Sculpture) was founded in Paris in 1648, modelled on the Accademia di San Luca in Rome. It was the premier art institution of France under the Ancien Régime, controlling artistic training, exhibitions and taste.

History

The Academy was founded by a group of artists led by Charles Le Brun, seeking freedom from the guild of master painters. Under Le Brun's directorship it codified a hierarchy of genres — with history painting at the top — and organized the official Salon exhibitions. It also administered the Prix de Rome, which sent young artists to study in Italy.

Dissolution and legacy

The Academy was abolished by the National Convention in 1793 during the French Revolution. Its functions were later taken up by the Académie des Beaux-Arts of the Institut de France. Its doctrines profoundly shaped academic art throughout Europe and America.
