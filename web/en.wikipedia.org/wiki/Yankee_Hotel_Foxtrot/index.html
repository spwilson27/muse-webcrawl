Studio album by Wilco

Yankee Hotel Foxtrot is the fourth studio album by the American rock band Wilco. It was completed in 2001 but rejected by the band's label, Reprise Records, a subsidiary of Warner Music Group, which dropped Wilco and let the band leave with the album's master tapes for free. Wilco streamed the album on its website in September 2001 and eventually released it on April 23, 2002 through Nonesuch Records — itself another Warner label. The title comes from a voice repeating the phonetic-alphabet phrase in a recording from the shortwave "numbers station" document The Conet Project, sampled in the song "Poor Places."

Recording and dispute

The album was recorded at Wilco's Chicago loft with increasing friction between frontman Jeff Tweedy and guitarist Jay Bennett over mixing; Bennett left the band after recording. Sam Jones filmed the sessions for the documentary I Am Trying to Break Your Heart: A Film About Wilco (2002). The label dispute became a frequently cited case in debates about the music industry's treatment of commercially risky work: the album that Reprise rejected was widely acclaimed, was certified gold in the United States, and placed at or near the top of many critics' year-end lists, including first place in the Village Voice Pazz & Jop poll.

The cover, designed with a photograph of Chicago's Marina City towers, is one of the most recognizable album images of the 2000s. Songs such as "Jesus, Etc." and "Ashes of American Flags" — written before the September 11 attacks — were heard by many listeners as eerily prescient commentary on their aftermath. The album is regularly ranked among the greatest of its decade.
