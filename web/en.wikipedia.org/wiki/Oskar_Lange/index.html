Oskar Ryszard Lange (27 July 1904 – 2 October 1965) was a Polish economist and diplomat. He is best known for advocating the use of market pricing tools in socialist systems and for providing a model of market socialism. He responded to the economic calculation problem proposed by Ludwig von Mises and Friedrich Hayek by claiming that managers in a socialist economy would be able to use a trial-and-error method to set prices equal to marginal cost, much like in competitive markets.

Career

Lange studied law and economics at the Jagiellonian University in Kraków, where he taught before emigrating to the United States in 1934. He taught at the University of Michigan and Stanford, and from 1938 at the University of Chicago, becoming a US citizen in 1943. His 1936–37 essays "On the Economic Theory of Socialism" made him the leading Western advocate of market socialism. During the Second World War he acted as an intermediary between Roosevelt and Stalin on the Polish question, and after 1945 he renounced his US citizenship to serve the new Polish government as ambassador to the United States and delegate to the United Nations. Returning to Poland, he became a professor at the University of Warsaw, chairman of the Economic Council (1957), rector of the Main School of Planning and Statistics, and a deputy chairman of the Council of State.

Contributions

Beyond the socialist calculation debate, Lange made early contributions to econometrics and mathematical economics, including work on price flexibility and monetary theory ("Say's Law and the Theory of Money"), the Lange–Lerner conditions for the optimum under socialism, and multi-sector growth models. In a late essay (1965), "The Computer and the Market", he argued that electronic computers could perform the calculations that his market mechanism had been designed to approximate, while still insisting that real markets remained indispensable for goods not susceptible to such treatment. Milton Friedman, his Chicago colleague, regarded him as a formidable and honourable intellectual opponent.
