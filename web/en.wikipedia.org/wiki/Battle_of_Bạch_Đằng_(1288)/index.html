Battle of Bạch Đằng (1288)

The Battle of Bạch Đằng was a decisive naval battle fought in April 1288 on the Bạch Đằng River near Haiphong in northern Vietnam, during the third Mongol invasion of Đại Việt. Vietnamese forces under Prince Trần Hưng Đạo (Trần Quốc Tuấn) destroyed the supply fleet of the Yuan dynasty in an ambush using concealed, iron-tipped stakes driven into the riverbed — the same tactic that had won the earlier battle on the same river in 938. The defeat forced the Yuan invasion army, already weakened by disease and lack of supplies, to retreat from Vietnam.

Background

Kublai Khan's third invasion of Đại Việt (1287–88) aimed to install a puppet prince and crush Vietnamese resistance. The invasion army under Toghon advanced overland while a great supply fleet under the admiral Omar (Umar) sailed down the coast carrying the provisions the army depended on. Trần Hưng Đạo, commanding the Vietnamese defense, resolved to destroy the supply fleet on the tidal Bạch Đằng River, emulating Ngô Quyền's famous victory of 938 on the same ground.

Battle

Vietnamese engineers drove thousands of iron-tipped wooden stakes into the riverbed at the Chanh River confluence, positioned to be hidden at high tide. A small Vietnamese flotilla lured the Yuan fleet in at high water and then withdrew past the stake field; as the tide fell, the heavy Yuan junks were impaled and immobilized on the stakes. Vietnamese forces attacked with fire rafts and from both banks, destroying or capturing nearly the entire fleet; Omar was taken prisoner.

Significance

Deprived of its supplies, Toghon's army abandoned its gains and retreated to China, harried all the way. The victory ended the Mongol invasions of Vietnam and made Bạch Đằng and Trần Hưng Đạo enduring symbols of Vietnamese resistance — the third famous victory won on that river by the stake tactic, after 938 and the campaigns against the Song in 981.
