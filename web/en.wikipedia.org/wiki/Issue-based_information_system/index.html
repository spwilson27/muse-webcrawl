Issue-based information system

An issue-based information system (IBIS) is an argumentation-based approach to clarifying wicked problems — complex, ill-defined problems that involve multiple stakeholders. IBIS was invented by Werner Kunz and Horst Rittel in the 1960s and described in their 1970 paper "Issues as Elements of Information Systems".

Structure

An IBIS model consists of three kinds of elements:

  • Issues (or questions) that need to be resolved;
  • Positions (or ideas) — possible answers to an issue;
  • Arguments — statements that support (pro) or object to (con) a position.

Each issue may be generalised, specialised, or replaced by other issues, and positions may raise new issues, so the model grows into a network representing the deliberation. The notation is deliberately minimal, intended to capture the rationale of a discussion rather than any single participant's conclusion.

History and applications

Rittel developed IBIS as part of his work on wicked problems and design methodology. In the 1980s and 1990s it was implemented in hypertext systems such as gIBIS (Jeff Conklin and colleagues), which applied graphical IBIS mapping to software design deliberations. IBIS is the basis of the design rationale field and underlies argument mapping tools such as Compendium. Related notations include QOC (Questions, Options, Criteria).

The approach has been applied to urban planning, engineering design, policy deliberation, and collaborative problem solving, and has influenced later work on dialogue mapping.
