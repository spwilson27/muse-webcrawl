Wolfgang Paul

Wolfgang Paul (10 August 1913 – 7 December 1993) was a German physicist who shared the 1989 Nobel Prize in Physics for developing the ion trap technique. He is not to be confused with Wolfgang Pauli.

Career

Paul studied in Munich, Berlin and Göttingen, and became a professor at the University of Bonn. In the 1950s he developed the quadrupole ion trap — the Paul trap — and the quadrupole mass filter, which allow charged particles to be confined by oscillating electric fields.

Legacy

Paul traps are now standard tools in mass spectrometry, atomic physics and quantum computing research. Paul also worked on particle accelerators and served as director of the physics institute at Bonn.
