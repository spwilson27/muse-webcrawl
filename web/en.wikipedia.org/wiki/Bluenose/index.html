Bluenose was a fishing and racing gaff schooner built in 1921 in Lunenburg, Nova Scotia, Canada. A celebrated racing ship and Canadian icon, she became a provincial symbol of Nova Scotia and an important symbol of Canada. She was later commemorated on the Canadian dime, where her image has appeared in various forms since 1937.

Racing career

Designed by William James Roué, Bluenose was intended both to work the Grand Banks fishery and to compete for the International Fisherman's Cup against American schooners. Under Captain Angus Walters, she was undefeated in the races for the trophy between 1921 and 1938, defeating challengers including Gertrude L. Thebaud, and earned the title "Queen of the North Atlantic".

Later life and loss

After her racing career, Bluenose represented Canada at the Silver Jubilee of King George V in 1935. Sold to the West Indies Trading Company in 1942, she worked as a freighter and struck a reef off Île-à-Vache, Haiti, on 28 January 1946 and was wrecked.

Legacy

A replica, Bluenose II, was launched in 1963 and serves as a Nova Scotia goodwill ambassador. The nickname "Bluenose" is also a traditional demonym for a Nova Scotian. Bluenose was inducted into the Canadian Sports Hall of Fame in 1955.
