Václav Havel

Born5 October 1936, Prague, Czechoslovakia
Died18 December 2011, Hrádeček, Czech Republic
OfficesPresident of Czechoslovakia (1989–1992); President of the Czech Republic (1993–2003)
OccupationPlaywright, essayist, dissident, statesman

Václav Havel (5 October 1936 – 18 December 2011) was a Czech statesman, playwright, author, political dissident and former prisoner. He served as the last president of Czechoslovakia from 1989 until its dissolution in 1992, and then as the first president of the Czech Republic from 1993 to 2003.

Early life and theatre

Born into a wealthy Prague family whose property was confiscated by the Communist regime, Havel was barred from formal study and worked as a stagehand and brewery labourer while writing plays. His absurdist dramas, including The Garden Party (1963) and The Memorandum (1965), gained international attention.

Dissident years

After the suppression of the Prague Spring in 1968 his works were banned. He co-founded the Charter 77 human-rights initiative in 1977 and wrote the landmark essay The Power of the Powerless (1978), advocating "living in truth" under totalitarianism. He was imprisoned several times, spending nearly five years in prison in total.

Presidency and later life

As a leader of the Civic Forum during the Velvet Revolution of 1989, Havel became president by unanimous vote of the Federal Assembly. He oversaw the transition to democracy and a market economy, the peaceful dissolution of Czechoslovakia, and the Czech Republic's accession to NATO. After leaving office he continued writing and campaigning for human rights until his death in 2011.
