Doublespeak

Doublespeak is language that deliberately obscures, disguises or distorts meaning, often to make the unpleasant appear acceptable. The term is modelled on Orwell's doublethink and Newspeak from Nineteen Eighty-Four.

Forms

William Lutz identified euphemism, jargon, gobbledygook and inflated language as its main forms, common in politics, bureaucracy and advertising — for example 'collateral damage' for civilian deaths.

Criticism

Critics argue doublespeak erodes democratic debate by concealing responsibility; the Doublespeak Award satirises notable examples each year.
