Bsharri

Bsharri (Arabic: بشرّي, Bsharrī) is a town in the Bsharri District of the North Governorate in Lebanon. Perched at about 1,450 metres on the edge of the Kadisha Valley, it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in Mount Lebanon and a centre of Maronite Christian heritage.

Geography and sights

Bsharri overlooks the Kadisha Valley (the Holy Valley), a UNESCO World Heritage landscape of monasteries carved into cliffs. Nearby stand the Cedars of God, one of the last remnants of the ancient forests of Lebanon cedars, and Lebanon's highest peak, Qurnat as Sawda'. The town is also a gateway to the Cedars ski resort, one of the country's oldest.

Notable people

Bsharri is the birthplace of the poet, painter, and philosopher Khalil Gibran (1883–1931), author of The Prophet. The Gibran Museum, housed in the former Monastery of Mar Sarkis, displays his paintings, manuscripts, and personal effects, and his tomb is located there.
