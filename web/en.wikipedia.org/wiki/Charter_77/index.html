Charter 77 (Charta 77 in Czech and Slovak) was an informal civic initiative in communist Czechoslovakia from 1977 to 1992. Its founding declaration, published in January 1977, criticised the government for failing to honour human rights provisions in the Czechoslovak constitution and the Helsinki Accords of 1975.

Background and reception

The immediate catalyst was the trial of the rock band The Plastic People of the Universe. Principal authors included the philosopher Jan Patočka, playwright Václav Havel, and former foreign minister Jiří Hájek. State media denounced the charter in the orchestrated "Anti-Charter" campaign; signatories faced dismissal, surveillance, arrest, and imprisonment. Patočka died in March 1977 after a police interrogation.

Legacy

Though signatories never exceeded about 1,900, Charter 77 became the moral centre of Czechoslovak dissent, inspired Poland's KOR and similar movements, and its members — above all Havel — became central figures in the Velvet Revolution of 1989. The group formally dissolved in 1992.
