Ipomoea is the largest genus in the morning glory family, Convolvulaceae.

Ipomoea is the largest genus in the plant family Convolvulaceae, with over 600 species. It is a large and diverse group with common names including morning glory, water convolvulus or kangkung, sweet potato, bindweed, and moonflower.

Description

Most species are twining climbing herbs or shrubs with funnel-shaped flowers in shades of blue, purple, pink, or white. The genus occurs throughout the tropical and subtropical regions of the world.

Uses

The most economically important species is the sweet potato (Ipomoea batatas), a major food crop worldwide. Ipomoea aquatica (water spinach or kangkung) is a widely eaten leafy vegetable in Asia. Many species are grown as ornamental garden plants for their showy flowers, while some are significant agricultural weeds. The seeds of certain species, such as Ipomoea tricolor, contain ergoline alkaloids related to LSD and have traditional ritual uses in Mexico.
