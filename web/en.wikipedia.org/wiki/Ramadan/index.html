Overview

Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic calendar and a holy month of fasting, prayer, reflection and community for Muslims. The fast of Ramadan (sawm) — abstaining from food, drink and marital relations from dawn to sunset — is one of the Five Pillars of Islam. Muslims believe the first verses of the Quran were revealed to Muhammad during Ramadan, an event commemorated on Laylat al-Qadr (the Night of Decree), one of its last ten nights.

Practice

Believers eat a pre-dawn meal (suhur) and break the fast at sunset (iftar), often communally and with dates following the Prophet's example. Children, the ill, travellers, pregnant and menstruating women and the elderly are exempt in whole or part. Because the Hijri calendar is lunar, Ramadan cycles through the seasons over about 33 years. The month ends with the festival of Eid al-Fitr; required almsgiving (zakat al-fitr) precedes it. Laylat al-Qadr is marked by night-long worship in the last ten days, especially the odd nights.
