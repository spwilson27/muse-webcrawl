Diaeresis

The diaeresis ( ¨ ), also spelled dieresis, is a diacritical mark consisting of two dots placed over a letter, most commonly a vowel. It has two principal uses: to indicate that two adjacent vowels are pronounced separately (as in naïve or coöperate), and, in languages such as German, to mark umlaut — a change in vowel quality, as in ä, ö and ü.

Use in English

In English the diaeresis is now rare and mostly confined to words borrowed from French such as naïve, Noël and façade (where it appears in Brontë as a surname convention), and in the house styles of a few publications, notably The New Yorker, which retains forms like coöperate and reëlect.

Other uses

In French, the tréma marks separate vowel sounds (Noël, maïs). In Spanish it appears in güe and güi sequences to show the u is pronounced, as in pingüino. In transliteration and phonetics it may mark centralisation of a vowel.
