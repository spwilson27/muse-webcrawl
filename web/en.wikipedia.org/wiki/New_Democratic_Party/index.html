The New Democratic Party (NDP; French: Nouveau Parti démocratique, NPD) is a federal political party in Canada. Widely described as social democratic, it sits on the centre-left to left of the Canadian political spectrum, advocating policies such as universal public health care, workers' rights, environmental protection, and reducing income inequality. Provincial and territorial NDP parties are integrated with the federal party and have formed governments in several provinces.

History

Origins

The NDP was founded in 1961 at a convention in Ottawa through the merger of the Co-operative Commonwealth Federation (CCF) and the Canadian Labour Congress. Tommy Douglas, the former CCF premier of Saskatchewan who had introduced North America's first universal public medicare program, became the party's first leader.

Development

Under leaders including David Lewis, Ed Broadbent, Audrey McLaughlin, and Alexa McDonough, the NDP became a durable third party in federal politics, holding the balance of power in several minority parliaments. Its provincial wings won elections in British Columbia, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Ontario, Alberta, and Nova Scotia.

Official Opposition and recent years

Under Jack Layton, the party achieved a breakthrough in the 2011 federal election, winning 103 seats and becoming the Official Opposition for the first time — the "Orange Wave," driven largely by a surge in Quebec. Layton died later that year and was succeeded by Thomas Mulcair, under whom the party returned to third place in 2015. Jagmeet Singh became leader in 2017, the first person from a visible minority group to lead a major federal party in Canada. From 2022 to 2024 the NDP supported the Liberal minority government through a confidence-and-supply agreement that produced a national dental care program and a start on pharmacare.

Policies

The party supports Canada's universal health care system and its expansion to dental care and prescription drugs, stronger labour protections, action on climate change, affordable housing, Indigenous reconciliation, and progressive taxation.
