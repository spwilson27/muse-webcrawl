John McCarthy (computer scientist)

John McCarthy (September 4, 1927 – October 24, 2011) was an American computer scientist and cognitive scientist. He coined the term "artificial intelligence" in 1955, co-founded the field at the Dartmouth Workshop in 1956, invented the Lisp programming language, and pioneered time-sharing and garbage collection. He received the Turing Award in 1971.

Life and career

Born in Boston to immigrant parents, McCarthy studied mathematics at Caltech and earned a PhD at Princeton. He taught at Dartmouth, MIT — where he co-founded Project MAC — and from 1962 at Stanford, where he founded the Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (SAIL).

Contributions

McCarthy designed Lisp (1958–60), introduced recursion and conditional expressions in programming languages, advocated time-sharing and utility computing foreshadowing cloud computing, and developed formalisms for commonsense reasoning including circumscription and situation calculus. His 1959 paper "Programs with Common Sense" outlined the Advice Taker.
