Itchy feet

Itchy feet is an English idiom that describes an urge or restlessness to travel or move on from one's current place. The phrase is also used colloquially for a literal sensation of itching in the feet, which can be associated with several medical conditions.

Idiomatic usage

To "get itchy feet" is to feel a growing desire to leave a place, take a trip, or make a change. The expression suggests that staying still becomes uncomfortable, much as an itch demands to be scratched. It is closely related to the word wanderlust, the strong impulse to travel and explore the world. The idiom has been in common use since at least the early twentieth century and appears frequently in travel writing and popular song lyrics.

Medical sense

Literally itchy feet may result from athlete's foot (tinea pedis), a fungal infection of the skin of the foot; contact dermatitis or allergic reactions; dry skin; scabies; or circulatory and nerve conditions. Persistent itching accompanied by redness, scaling, or cracking of the skin is commonly treated with antifungal creams, moisturisers, or, where a cause cannot be identified, medical evaluation.

In popular culture

The phrase lends itself readily to titles and lyrics about travelling. Variations such as "itchy feet" appear in folk and popular music describing restless characters who cannot settle in one place.
