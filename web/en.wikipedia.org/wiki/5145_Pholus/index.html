5145 Pholus, provisional designation 1992 AD, is a centaur in an eccentric orbit in the outer Solar System. It was discovered on 9 January 1992 by David Rabinowitz of the Spacewatch programme at Kitt Peak National Observatory. Pholus was the second centaur to be discovered, after 2060 Chiron, and is named after the centaur Pholus of Greek mythology, brother of Chiron.

Orbit

Pholus orbits the Sun at a distance ranging from about 8.7 AU at perihelion — inside the orbit of Saturn — to about 31.9 AU at aphelion, beyond Neptune, completing one revolution every 91.5 years. Its orbit is inclined about 25 degrees to the ecliptic. The orbit is highly unstable on astronomical timescales: numerical integrations show that Pholus will be perturbed by close encounters with the giant planets and is expected to evolve substantially within the next few million years.

Physical characteristics

Pholus is famous for its intensely red colour, among the reddest of any known small body in the Solar System. The redness is attributed to a surface mantle rich in complex organic compounds (tholins) produced by long exposure of methane and other ices to cosmic rays. Infrared spectroscopy has detected water ice, methanol ice and olivine on its surface, along with a dark, carbonaceous component. Thermal measurements put its diameter at roughly 99 kilometres (62 miles), and its very low albedo of about 0.04 means it reflects only about 4 percent of the sunlight that strikes it.
