Achi Baba

Elevation709 ft (216 m)
LocationGallipoli Peninsula, Turkey

Achi Baba (Turkish: Alçıtepe) is a height of about 709 feet (216 m) on the Gallipoli Peninsula in Turkey. It was the main objective of the British and French forces during the Gallipoli campaign of 1915 at the Cape Helles front.

Gallipoli campaign

Achi Baba overlooked the landing beaches at Cape Helles and the village of Krithia. General Sir Ian Hamilton's plan envisaged its capture on the first day of the landings, 25 April 1915, but the advance stalled far short of it. Repeated attempts in the Battles of Krithia (April–June 1915) failed to reach the hill at heavy cost to British, French, Australian, New Zealand, and Indian troops. Achi Baba remained in Ottoman hands for the entire campaign and became a symbol of the Allies' unattainable objective at Helles.

Geography

The hill, now crowned by the village of Alçıtepe, commands views across the southern peninsula to the Dardanelles. It forms part of the battlefield landscape preserved today within the Gallipoli Historic Area, near several Commonwealth War Graves Commission cemeteries.
