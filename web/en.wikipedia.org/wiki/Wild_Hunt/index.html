Wild Hunt
RegionNorthern, Western, and Central European folklore
LeadersOdin (Woden), Herne the Hunter, King Arthur, Frau Holle, others
ComprisesGhostly or supernatural hunters, the dead, hounds, horses

The Wild Hunt is a folklore motif found across Northern, Western, and Central Europe: a phantasmal, spectral procession of huntsmen, horses, and hounds flying through the stormy sky or racing over the ground, often associated with winter nights and the Twelve Days of Christmas. To witness the Hunt was an omen of catastrophe — war, plague, or the death of the witness — and those caught up in it might be swept away to the realm of the dead.

Leaders and regional variants

The leader varies by region: in Germanic traditions the hunt is led by Odin (Woden) or Frau Holle; in Britain by Herne the Hunter, King Arthur, or the Devil; in France by King Arthur or Charlemagne (the Mesnée Hellequin, source of the word "harlequin"). Jacob Grimm, in his Deutsche Mythologie (1835), developed the idea of a common Germanic origin, connecting the Hunt to Woden's army of the dead, the einherjar. Peter Nicolai Arbo's painting Åsgårdsreien (1872) is its most famous depiction.
