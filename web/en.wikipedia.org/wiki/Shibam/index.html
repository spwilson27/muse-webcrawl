Shibam

Shibam (Arabic: شِبَام) is a town in the Hadhramaut region of Yemen, famous for its dense cluster of mud-brick tower houses, often described as "the oldest skyscraper city in the world" or the "Manhattan of the desert". The walled old town, with some 500 towers rising five to eleven storeys — many dating from the 16th century — was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1982 as the Old Walled City of Shibam.

History

Shibam is first mentioned in inscriptions from the 3rd century AD and was at times a capital of the Hadhramaut kingdom. Its wealth came from the frankincense trade and later from remittances of Hadhrami emigrants. The city was repeatedly destroyed by floods from the Wadi Hadhramaut — a devastating flood in 1532–33 led to much of the present rebuilding — and its compact, fortified plan with a single gate protected inhabitants from Bedouin raids while keeping buildings on high ground away from the wadi bed. Modern Shibam has grown outside the walls and the town's population is roughly 30,000.

Architecture

The tower houses are built of sun-dried mud bricks on stone foundations, plastered with mud and lime, with wooden beams and ornately carved doors and windows. Structures must be regularly replastered; buildings rise to around 30 metres, and ground floors traditionally housed animals and storage, with living rooms above and rooftop terraces. The tallest floors contain reception rooms, and narrow bridges once connected neighbouring towers at high level.

Threats

A severe flood in 2008 (cyclone-related) and Yemen's civil war have placed the site on UNESCO's List of World Heritage in Danger (2015). Erosion of mud brick from rain makes continuous community maintenance essential for survival of the fabric.
