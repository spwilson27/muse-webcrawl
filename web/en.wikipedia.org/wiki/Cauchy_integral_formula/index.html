Cauchy integral formula

In mathematics, Cauchy's integral formula, named after Augustin-Louis Cauchy, is a central statement in complex analysis. It expresses the fact that a holomorphic function defined on a disk is completely determined by its values on the boundary of the disk, and it provides integral formulas for all derivatives of a holomorphic function. Cauchy's formula shows that, in complex analysis, "differentiation is equivalent to integration": complex differentiation, like integration, behaves well under uniform limits — a result that does not hold in real analysis.

Statement

Let U be an open subset of the complex plane, f : U → ℂ a holomorphic function, and γ a closed rectifiable curve in U which winds once around a point a inside it. Then:

f(a) = (1 / 2πi) ∮γ f(z) / (z − a) dz

Moreover, f is infinitely differentiable, with derivatives given by:

f(n)(a) = (n! / 2πi) ∮γ f(z) / (z − a)n+1 dz

Consequences

From the formula follow many of the distinctive results of complex analysis: holomorphic functions are analytic (representable by power series), Cauchy's estimates on derivatives, Liouville's theorem, the maximum modulus principle, and Morera's theorem. The formula is proved using the Cauchy integral theorem together with the explicit evaluation of the integral of 1/(z − a) around a circle.
