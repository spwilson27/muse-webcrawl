BBC Radio 1 is a British national radio station owned and operated by the BBC. It specialises in modern popular music, current chart hits and new music throughout the day, with alternative and specialist programming in the evening. The station targets a 15–29-year-old audience and was launched on 30 September 1967, initially as a direct response to the pirate radio stations broadcasting from offshore ships.

History

Radio 1 was created after the Marine Broadcasting Offences Act 1967 outlawed pirate stations such as Radio Caroline and Radio London, whose disc jockeys had proved hugely popular with young listeners. The BBC hired many former pirate DJs — the first voice heard on the station was Tony Blackburn, who opened with "The Sound of Music" programme playing "Flowers in the Rain" by The Move as the first record. For its first years Radio 1 shared content and frequencies with Radio 2 and lacked a full FM allocation, broadcasting on medium wave; it gained a full-time FM network in 1988. Long-serving presenters such as John Peel, whose evening show championed new and underground music for 37 years, became institutions of British music culture.

Programming and events

Flagship shows include the Radio 1 Breakfast Show (presented by Greg James since 2018) and the Official Chart show on Friday afternoons. The station runs major events including Radio 1's Big Weekend, an annual travelling music festival, and the Teen Awards. Its Live Lounge segment, in which artists perform an acoustic cover of another act's song, has become a widely viewed franchise. Radio 1 is also known for its Newsbeat bulletins aimed at younger audiences.
