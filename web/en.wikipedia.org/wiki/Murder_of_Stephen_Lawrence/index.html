Murder of Stephen Lawrence

Stephen Lawrence (13 September 1974 – 22 April 1993) was an 18-year-old Black British student from Plumstead, south-east London, who was murdered in a racially motivated attack while waiting for a bus in Eltham on the evening of 22 April 1993. He was attacked by a group of white youths and stabbed twice; he managed to run over 100 metres before collapsing and bleeding to death.

Investigations and trials

The Metropolitan Police investigation was widely criticised. Five suspects were arrested but charges were dropped in July 1993 for insufficient evidence. The Lawrence family brought a private prosecution in 1996, which failed; one defendant was acquitted by direction of the judge and the case against the others collapsed. An inquest in 1997 returned a verdict of unlawful killing "in a completely unprovoked racist attack by five white youths".

Following a campaign by the family, supported prominently by the Daily Mail, which in February 1997 named the five suspects as "Murderers" on its front page, Home Secretary Jack Straw ordered a public inquiry led by Sir William Macpherson. The Macpherson Report (1999) concluded that the police investigation had been marred by incompetence, a failure of leadership and institutional racism, making 70 recommendations that reshaped British policing and public-sector race relations. The case also led to the abolition of the double jeopardy rule for serious offences in the Criminal Justice Act 2003.

Convictions

Following a cold-case forensic review, Gary Dobson and David Norris were tried on new DNA and fibre evidence. On 3 January 2012 both were convicted of Lawrence's murder at the Old Bailey and sentenced to life imprisonment. The other suspects have never been convicted.

Legacy

The case is considered a watershed in British race relations. Stephen Lawrence Day is marked annually on 22 April, and the Stephen Lawrence Charitable Trust (now Blueprint for All) was founded by his mother, Doreen Lawrence, who became a life peer in 2013. A later review, the Ellison Report (2014), found evidence that undercover police had spied on the Lawrence family campaign.
