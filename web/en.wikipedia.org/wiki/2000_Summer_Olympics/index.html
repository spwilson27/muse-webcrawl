The 2000 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXVII Olympiad and branded as Sydney 2000, were held from 15 September to 1 October 2000 in Sydney, Australia. They were the second Summer Games hosted in Australia and the Southern Hemisphere, after Melbourne 1956, and were widely praised as among the best-organized Olympics to that date.

Highlights

Indigenous Australian sprinter Cathy Freeman lit the Olympic cauldron and, days later, won the women's 400 metres before an enormous home crowd. Sixteen-year-old Australian swimmer Ian Thorpe won three gold medals, while British rower Steve Redgrave won his fifth consecutive Olympic gold. In the pool and on the track, the United States topped the medal table with 37 golds, ahead of Russia and China; host Australia finished fourth with a record 58 medals. Cameroon won a famous football gold medal, and taekwondo and triathlon made their Olympic debuts.

Organization and legacy

The Games centered on Sydney Olympic Park at Homebush Bay, a remediated industrial site. Volunteer numbers exceeded 46,000. IOC president Juan Antonio Samaranch famously declared Sydney "the best Olympic Games ever." The Games also carried controversy over the bidding process and ticket allocation, and later doping revelations — including Marion Jones's return of her medals — altered some results years afterward.
