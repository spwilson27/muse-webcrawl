Nicolas Steno

Born1 January 1638, Copenhagen, Denmark
Died25 November 1686, Schwerin, Germany
Known forFoundations of stratigraphy; Stensen's duct

Nicolas Steno (Danish: Niels Steensen; 1 January 1638 – 25 November 1686) was a Danish scientist and Catholic bishop who pioneered anatomy and geology. His principles of stratigraphy — superposition, original horizontality and lateral continuity — remain foundations of earth science, and he first recognised that fossils such as shark teeth ("tongue stones") were the remains of once-living organisms.

Science

As an anatomist in the Netherlands and Florence, Steno discovered the parotid salivary duct (Stensen's duct) and argued that muscles contract by geometry rather than by influx of spirits. His 1669 Prodromus proposed how solid objects, including fossils and crystals, come to be enclosed in rock layers, laying groundwork for the study of Earth's history. He also stated the law of constancy of interfacial angles in crystals.

Later life

Steno converted to Catholicism, was ordained a priest in 1675 and made a bishop, devoting his remaining years to pastoral work in northern Germany. He was beatified by Pope John Paul II in 1988.
