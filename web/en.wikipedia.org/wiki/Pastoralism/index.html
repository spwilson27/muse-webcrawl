Pastoralism

Pastoralism is a form of animal husbandry where domesticated animals (livestock) are released onto large vegetated outdoor lands (pastures) for grazing, historically by nomadic people who moved around with their herds.

Forms

It includes nomadic pastoralism, semi-nomadic herding and transhumance — seasonal movement between fixed summer and winter pastures. Species include cattle, sheep, goats, camels, yaks and reindeer.

Distribution

Pastoralism persists on steppes, savannas, tundra and deserts among peoples such as the Mongols, Bedouin, Maasai and Sámi, supporting hundreds of millions, though pressured by land enclosure, borders and climate change.
