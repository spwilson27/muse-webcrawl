El Floridita

El Floridita is a historic bar and restaurant in Old Havana, Cuba, on Calle Obispo at the corner of Monserrate. Founded in 1817 as La Piña de Plata, it took its present name in 1914. The bar styles itself "the cradle of the daiquiri" (la cuna del daiquirí) and is famous for its association with the writer Ernest Hemingway.

The bar at El Floridita, with a bronze statue of Hemingway at his customary corner.

The daiquiri

The frozen daiquiri was perfected at El Floridita by the Catalan immigrant bartender Constantino Ribalaigua Vert ("Constante"), who became owner in 1918. Using shaved ice and an early blender technique, Ribalaigua created the smooth frozen version of the rum, lime and sugar cocktail that made the bar internationally famous; several numbered daiquiri variations appeared on its menu.

Hemingway

Ernest Hemingway, who lived in Cuba for long periods, was a devoted regular. His attributed remark — "My mojito in La Bodeguita, my daiquiri in El Floridita" — is reproduced in his handwriting on the wall. The "Papa Doble" (Hemingway Special), a double daiquiri without sugar and with grapefruit juice and maraschino, was created for him. A life-size bronze statue of the writer leans at his corner of the bar, and his bar stool is kept roped off.

Today

El Floridita remains one of Havana's best-known tourist landmarks, serving seafood in its restaurant and daiquiris at the bar, largely preserved in its 1950s appearance.
