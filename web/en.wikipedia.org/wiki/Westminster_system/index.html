Westminster system

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Westminster system (or Westminster model) is a system of democratic government modelled on the Palace of Westminster, the Parliament of the United Kingdom. Originating in England and exported across the British Empire, it now operates in dozens of countries, including Canada, Australia, New Zealand, India, Jamaica and numerous Commonwealth states — each adapting it to local conditions, federalism and written constitutions.

Features

Core features include: a head of state distinct from the head of government; an executive drawn from the legislature and collectively responsible to it (especially its lower house); a prime minister and cabinet government; an organised opposition; bicameralism in most cases; regularly held elections under plurality or other electoral systems; and conventions — unwritten constitutional rules — supplementing law. Parliamentary sovereignty (in the UK form) contrasts with systems where constitutional courts police the legislature, as in India and (in its federal division) Australia and Canada.

Variations

Scholars contrast Westminster "majoritarianism" with consensus democracies (Lijphart's typology): fused power, single-party governments and adversarial politics on one side; coalitions, proportional representation and power-sharing on the other. Actual Westminster systems have adopted consensus features — proportional elections in New Zealand (MMP since 1996), entrenched bills of rights, devolution in the UK — blurring the model while retaining its core of responsible cabinet government.
