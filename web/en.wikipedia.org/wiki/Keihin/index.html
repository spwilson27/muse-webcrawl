Keihin is best known for motorcycle carburettors and fuel systems.

Keihin Corporation (株式会社ケーヒン, Kabushiki-gaisha Keihin) is a Japanese manufacturer of automotive and motorcycle components, long associated with Honda, which held a majority stake. Founded in 1956, the company became famous for its carburettors, fitted as original equipment to motorcycles of Honda and many other makers, as well as to small engines and marine applications.

Products

As fuel injection replaced carburettors, Keihin expanded into electronic fuel-injection systems, throttle bodies, engine control units, air-conditioning compressors and HVAC modules for cars. The Keihin brand is well known in motorsport and tuning, where its FCR and other performance carburettors are widely used.

Merger

In 2019 Honda announced the consolidation of its listed parts subsidiaries. Keihin was merged in 2021, together with Showa and Nissin Kogyo, into Hitachi Astemo, a joint venture between Hitachi Automotive Systems and Honda, ending Keihin's existence as a separate company while the brand continued on certain products.
