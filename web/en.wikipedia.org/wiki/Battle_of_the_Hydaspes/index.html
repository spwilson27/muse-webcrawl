Battle of the Hydaspes

The Battle of the Hydaspes was fought in 326 BC between Alexander the Great and King Porus of the Paurava kingdom, on the banks of the Hydaspes River (the modern Jhelum River) in the Punjab, in what is now Pakistan. It was the last of Alexander's four great pitched battles and his most costly, fought against an army that included war elephants.

Background

After conquering the Persian Empire and campaigning in Bactria and Sogdiana, Alexander invaded India in 327 BC. Having crossed the Indus, he demanded submission from Porus, who chose to fight and camped on the far bank of the flooded, monsoon-swollen Hydaspes.

The battle

After weeks of feints, Alexander crossed the river upstream at night during a storm, leaving Craterus with a holding force. His cavalry outflanked and broke the Indian wings while the phalanx advanced against the elephants, which inflicted grievous casualties before being driven back into their own ranks. Porus, wounded and captured, famously asked to be treated "as a king" and was restored to his kingdom as a subordinate ally.

Aftermath

Alexander founded two cities on the field, Nicaea and Bucephala — the latter named for his horse Bucephalus, who died around this time. But his exhausted army refused to march further east against the Nanda Empire and mutinied at the Hyphasis (Beas), forcing Alexander to turn back; the Hydaspes thus marked the easternmost limit of his conquests.
