Kaprun disaster

The Kaprun disaster was a fire that occurred in a tunnel of the Gletscherbahn Kaprun 2 funicular railway in Kaprun, Austria, on 11 November 2000. It killed 155 people, most of them skiers and snowboarders heading to the Kitzsteinhorn glacier, and is one of the worst cable-car and funicular accidents in history.

The fire

Shortly after 9 a.m., a train ascending the mountainside stopped inside the tunnel about 600 metres from the lower station after a fire broke out in its rear carriage. Investigators later determined that a defective household-style fan heater installed in the (unattended) rear conductor's cab ignited hydraulic oil that had leaked onto it; the flames melted through hydraulic lines, causing the train to lose pressure and stop, and disabling the doors' hydraulic opening mechanism.

The tunnel acted like a giant chimney: hot gases and smoke raced upward. Most victims died of smoke inhalation and carbon monoxide poisoning while trying to climb up the tunnel stairs toward the upper station. Those who fled downward — including the conductor and a small group from the train — survived. Twelve people descending from the train escaped; several people in the upper station and the descending train also died from smoke. The victims included tourists from Austria, Germany, Japan and other countries.

Aftermath

The funicular never reopened and was later replaced by a gondola lift, the Gletscherjet. Criminal proceedings in Austria ended in 2004 with the acquittal of all 16 defendants, a verdict that drew criticism. The disaster led to stricter fire-safety rules for funicular tunnels, and a memorial was erected near the tunnel's lower entrance.
