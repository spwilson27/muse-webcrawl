Gampopa Sönam Rinchen (1079–1153) was a Tibetan Buddhist teacher of the Kagyu school, the foremost student of the yogi Milarepa, and a key figure in the transmission of Indian Mahayana and Vajrayana teachings in Tibet. He founded Daklha Gampo monastery in 1121, from which his name — "the man from Gampo" — derives.

Life

Born in Nyal in southern Tibet, Gampopa trained first as a physician and became known as Dakpo Lharjé, "the physician from Dakpo." After the death of his wife and children in an epidemic, he renounced worldly life and was ordained a monk in the Kadampa tradition. Hearing of Milarepa's fame, he sought him out and received the yogic teachings of mahamudra and the Six Yogas. Gampopa uniquely combined the Kadampa monastic and lamrim ("stages of the path") teachings with Milarepa's oral instructions.

Teachings and legacy

His best-known work, The Jewel Ornament of Liberation, presents a complete gradual path to enlightenment integrating sutra and tantra. He also taught the "Four Dharmas of Gampopa," a summary of the path still recited widely. His four principal disciples — Düsum Khyenpa (the first Karmapa), Phagmo Drupa, Barompa, and Dagpo Gomtsül — founded the "four major" Kagyu sub-schools, making Gampopa effectively the source of all surviving Kagyu lineages.
