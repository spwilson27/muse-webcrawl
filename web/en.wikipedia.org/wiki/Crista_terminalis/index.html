Crista terminalis

The crista terminalis is a vertical ridge of smooth myocardium inside the right atrium of the heart, running from the opening of the superior vena cava to the opening of the inferior vena cava. It marks the boundary between the smooth-walled posterior part of the atrium (the sinus venarum) and the rough, trabeculated anterior part bearing the pectinate muscles.

Development

The ridge corresponds externally to the sulcus terminalis and represents the junction between the embryonic sinus venosus and the primitive atrium.

Clinical significance

The crista terminalis is an important landmark in cardiac electrophysiology: the sinoatrial node lies at its superior end, and the ridge is a common site of origin for focal atrial tachycardias. A prominent crista terminalis can occasionally be mistaken for an atrial mass on imaging.
