A charter school is a school that receives government funding but operates independently of the established state school system in which it is located. Charter schools are granted a charter — a performance contract — by an authorizing body, detailing the school's mission, programme, students served, performance goals, and methods of assessment. In exchange for exemption from many regulations that apply to traditional public schools, charter schools agree to be held accountable for student outcomes; a school whose charter is not renewed may be closed.

History in the United States

The concept is often credited to educator Ray Budde, who proposed in 1974 that groups of teachers be given contracts ("charters") to explore new teaching approaches, and to Albert Shanker, president of the American Federation of Teachers, who popularised the idea in 1988. Minnesota passed the first charter school law in 1991, and the first charter school, City Academy in St. Paul, opened in 1992. By the 2020s there were more than 7,000 charter schools in the United States serving over three million students, in 45 states and the District of Columbia.

Operation and governance

Charter schools are tuition-free and open to all students, typically admitting by lottery when oversubscribed. They may be run by non-profit organisations, universities, or for-profit management companies, and some belong to charter management organisations (CMOs) such as KIPP, Success Academy, and Uncommon Schools. Teachers in charter schools are less frequently unionised than those in district schools.

Debate

Supporters argue charters foster innovation, competition, and options for families in underperforming districts. Critics contend they divert resources from traditional public schools, may "cream-skim" easier-to-educate students, and lack democratic oversight. Research findings on academic performance are mixed: large studies, such as those by Stanford's CREDO, have found results ranging from slightly negative to modestly positive relative to comparable district schools, with wide variation by state and operator.

Around the world

Related models include academy schools and free schools in England, independent public schools in Australia, and charter-like publicly funded independent schools in Canada (notably Alberta) and New Zealand (discontinued in 2018).
