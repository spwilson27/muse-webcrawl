Iraqi insurgency (2003–2011)

The Iraqi insurgency was an uprising against the United States-led coalition and the post-2003 Iraqi government that began shortly after the invasion of Iraq in 2003 and continued through the withdrawal of US combat forces in December 2011.

Origins

The insurgency drew on disbanded Ba'athist soldiers, Sunni Arab nationalists angered by de-Ba'athification and the dissolution of the Iraqi Army, foreign jihadists, and groups such as al-Qaeda in Iraq under Abu Musab al-Zarqawi. Tactics included ambushes, improvised explosive devices (IEDs), suicide bombings, and assassinations.

Escalation and sectarian war

Major episodes included the battles of Fallujah (2004), the bombing of the al-Askari mosque in Samarra (2006), which ignited a sectarian civil war between Sunni and Shia militias, and the US "Surge" of 2007 combined with the Sunni Awakening movement, in which former insurgents allied with coalition forces against al-Qaeda. Violence declined sharply by 2009–2011, though a renewed insurgency later fed into the rise of the Islamic State.
