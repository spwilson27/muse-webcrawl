The Grand Fleet was the Royal Navy's main battle fleet during the First World War. Formed on the outbreak of war in August 1914 from the First Fleet of the Home Fleets and elements of the Second Fleet, it concentrated Britain's modern dreadnought battleships in northern waters to contain the German High Seas Fleet, enforce the distant blockade of Germany, and keep command of the North Sea and the Atlantic approaches.

Organisation and bases

The fleet was based principally at Scapa Flow in Orkney, an anchorage chosen for its remoteness from German submarine and air attack (though its defences were hurriedly improvised in 1914), with the battlecruiser force stationed at Rosyth on the Firth of Forth. At its height the Grand Fleet comprised some 30-plus dreadnought and super-dreadnought battleships in four battle squadrons, the Battle Cruiser Fleet under David Beatty, cruiser and light cruiser squadrons, and flotillas of destroyers — in total over 150 warships, together with the supporting armada of the Northern Patrol and the Dover Patrol elsewhere.

War service

Admiral Sir John Jellicoe commanded the Grand Fleet from August 1914. Its strategy was one of distant blockade and readiness: the fleet made frequent sweeps into the North Sea to tempt or intercept German forces, but a decisive action eluded it for two years, despite scares such as the German battlecruiser raids on Scarborough (1914) and Lowestoft (1916) and continuous danger from mines and U-boats.

The fleets finally met at the Battle of Jutland (31 May–1 June 1916), the largest naval battle of the war. Jellicoe twice deployed across the German line of retreat ("crossing the T"), but the High Seas Fleet escaped under cover of darkness after fierce fighting in which Britain lost more ships and men — including three battlecruisers destroyed by magazine explosions — while Germany lost the battlecruiser Lützow and the pre-dreadnought Pommern. Strategically the result confirmed British sea control: the German fleet scarcely ventured out again in force, and the blockade tightened until it contributed to Germany's collapse in 1918.

In December 1916 Beatty succeeded Jellicoe (who became First Sea Lord). The fleet's later war was dominated by anti-submarine convoy protection and readiness against a final German sortie that never came. On 21 November 1918 the Grand Fleet, joined by American and French squadrons, received the surrender of the interned High Seas Fleet in the Firth of Forth — 70 German warships escorted to Scapa Flow, where their crews scuttled them on 21 June 1919. The Grand Fleet itself was disbanded in April 1919, its ships redistributed to the new Atlantic, Home and Mediterranean fleets.
