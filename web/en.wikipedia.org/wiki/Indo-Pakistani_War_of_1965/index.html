Indo-Pakistani War of 1965

The Indo-Pakistani War of 1965, or the Second Kashmir War, was a seventeen-day armed conflict between India and Pakistan in August–September 1965, arising from their dispute over Kashmir. After border clashes in the Rann of Kutch in April 1965, Pakistan launched Operation Gibraltar in August, infiltrating forces into Indian-administered Jammu and Kashmir to incite an insurgency, followed on 1 September by Operation Grand Slam, a conventional offensive toward Akhnoor. India responded by crossing the international border toward Lahore and Sialkot on 6 September, producing some of the largest tank battles since the Second World War.

Major battles

Key engagements included the Battle of Asal Uttar (8–10 September), where Indian forces in flooded sugarcane fields destroyed or captured large numbers of Pakistani Patton tanks in the "Patton Nagar" graveyard; the Battle of Phillora and the Battle of Chawinda (14–19 September), one of the largest tank battles in history, in the Sialkot sector; and fighting around Haji Pir Pass, which Indian forces captured in late August. The air war saw the Indian Air Force and Pakistan Air Force fight over both fronts, with aircraft such as the Folland Gnat — dubbed the "Sabre Slayer" — gaining fame. Naval hostilities were limited largely to Pakistan's Operation Dwarka bombardment.

End of the war

Under intense diplomatic pressure, including a mandatory UN Security Council ceasefire resolution and arms embargoes by the United States and Britain on both sides, the war ended on 23 September 1965. The subsequent Tashkent Declaration of 10 January 1966, mediated by Soviet premier Alexei Kosygin, restored the status quo ante bellum; India's prime minister Lal Bahadur Shastri died in Tashkent hours after signing. Casualty figures remain contested, and both countries claimed victory, but most neutral assessments regard the outcome as strategically inconclusive.
