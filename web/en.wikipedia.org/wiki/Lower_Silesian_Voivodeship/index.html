Lower Silesian Voivodeship (Polish: województwo dolnośląskie) is one of the 16 voivodeships (provinces) of Poland. It is located in southwestern Poland, within the historical region of Silesia, and borders Germany to the west and the Czech Republic to the south. Its capital and largest city is Wrocław.

History

The region formed part of medieval Silesia and passed through Polish, Bohemian, Habsburg, and Prussian rule. After the Second World War, under the Potsdam Agreement of 1945, the territory east of the Oder–Neisse line became part of Poland, and its German population was largely expelled and replaced by Poles, many displaced from Poland's former eastern territories. The present voivodeship was created on 1 January 1999 from the former Wrocław, Legnica, Wałbrzych, and Jelenia Góra voivodeships.

Geography

The Oder River flows through the region. The southern border follows the Sudetes mountain range, including the Karkonosze, whose highest peak, Śnieżka (1,603 m), is a popular destination. The Karkonosze National Park is a UNESCO biosphere reserve. Other notable places include the spa towns of the Kłodzko Valley and the medieval city of Świdnica, home to the Church of Peace, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Economy

Lower Silesia is one of Poland's most economically developed regions, with copper mining and smelting around Legnica and Głogów operated by KGHM, manufacturing, and a growing technology sector centered on Wrocław.
