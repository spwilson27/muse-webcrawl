Marvel Comics is an American comic book publisher, one of the two largest in the industry alongside DC Comics. Founded in 1939 as Timely Comics by pulp publisher Martin Goodman, it became famous in the 1960s under editor-writer Stan Lee and artists Jack Kirby and Steve Ditko for flawed, relatable superheroes — including Spider-Man, the Fantastic Four, the X-Men and the Hulk — set in a shared universe. Since 2009 Marvel has been owned by the Walt Disney Company, and its characters anchor the Marvel Cinematic Universe, the highest-grossing film franchise in history.

History

Marvel Comics #1 (October 1939), the first Timely publication, introduced the android Human Torch and Namor the Sub-Mariner and sold out its print run. During the Golden Age Timely's stars were the Torch, Namor and Captain America. In the 1950s, as Atlas Comics, the company drifted through war, romance, western and monster comics as superheroes waned.

The Marvel revolution began in 1961 with The Fantastic Four by Stan Lee and Jack Kirby, published under the new Marvel name. Lee and his collaborators created a stream of enduring characters: Spider-Man (1962, with Steve Ditko), the Hulk, Thor, Iron Man, the X-Men, Daredevil, Doctor Strange and the Avengers. Marvel's heroes quarrelled, doubted themselves and faced everyday problems, and the stories emphasised an interconnected continuity with a distinctively informal, fan-engaging editorial voice ("Make Mine Marvel"). Through the 1970s and 1980s Marvel overtook DC in sales, with landmark runs on X-Men (Chris Claremont) and Daredevil (Frank Miller) and events such as Secret Wars.

The 1990s speculator boom and bust drove Marvel into bankruptcy in 1996; it recovered by licensing film rights and then, under Marvel Studios, producing its own films, beginning with Iron Man (2008). Disney acquired Marvel Entertainment in 2009 for about $4 billion. In publishing, Marvel remains the market leader in North American direct-market sales.

The Marvel Universe

Marvel's characters inhabit a shared continuity (the main one designated Earth-616) anchored in a recognisably real New York City. Signature creations also include Black Panther — the first mainstream Black superhero — the antihero Punisher, Deadpool, and the cosmic saga of Thanos. Creators' rights and credit (notably the long-running Kirby estate disputes) have been continuing themes in the company's history.
