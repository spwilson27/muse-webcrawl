Ferran Adrià

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A modernist tasting-menu dish with foams and spheres]

Ferran Adrià Acosta (born 14 May 1962) is a Spanish chef, the creative head of elBulli in Roses, Catalonia, which held three Michelin Guide stars and topped the World's 50 Best Restaurants list a record five times before closing as a restaurant in 2011 to become a research foundation. He is the most influential chef of the modernist or "molecular gastronomy" era.

Adrià's team systematised deconstruction — dishes that look like one thing and taste of another — with techniques such as hot gelatins, airs and foams (with the siphon), spherification (with alginate) and freeze-drying. Each season the restaurant closed for months of laboratory creation, producing a catalogue of some 1,800 dishes. His methods and the elBulli alumni network reshaped fine dining worldwide; his later work focuses on the elBulli Foundation and the Bullipedia food-knowledge project.

1. Criticism

Criticism

Detractors, including Spain's Santi Santamaria, questioned additives and theatricality; others note that much of the modernist toolkit is now ordinary professional technique. Adrià himself resisted the label "molecular gastronomy".
