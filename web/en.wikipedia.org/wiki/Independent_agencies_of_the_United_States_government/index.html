Independent agencies of the United States government

Independent agencies of the United States federal government are agencies that exist outside the federal executive departments (those headed by a Cabinet secretary) and the Executive Office of the President. In a narrower sense, the term refers to agencies that, while constitutionally part of the executive branch, are statutorily insulated from presidential control, notably through protections against removal of their leadership without cause.

Characteristics

Independent regulatory agencies are typically governed by multi-member commissions or boards whose members serve staggered fixed terms and are often subject to partisan-balance requirements. Examples include the Federal Trade Commission, the Federal Communications Commission, the Securities and Exchange Commission, the Federal Reserve Board and the National Labor Relations Board. The constitutionality of removal protections was upheld in Humphrey's Executor v. United States (1935), though later decisions have narrowed those protections for agencies headed by a single director.

Other independent establishments

Beyond the regulatory commissions, the category includes establishments such as the Environmental Protection Agency, NASA, the Central Intelligence Agency and the United States Postal Service, which are independent of Cabinet departments but whose heads generally serve at the President's pleasure.
