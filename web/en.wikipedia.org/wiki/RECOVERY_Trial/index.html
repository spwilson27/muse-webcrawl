RECOVERY Trial

The Randomised Evaluation of COVID-19 Therapy (RECOVERY) Trial is a large-scale, randomized controlled trial of possible treatments for people hospitalized with COVID-19 in the United Kingdom. Established in March 2020 by the University of Oxford and funded principally by UK Research and Innovation and the National Institute for Health Research, it became one of the largest clinical trials of the pandemic, enrolling tens of thousands of participants across NHS hospitals.

Design

RECOVERY used a streamlined, pragmatic design: eligibility criteria were deliberately minimal, consent procedures simplified, and data collection restricted to essential outcomes, principally 28-day mortality. Participants were randomized between usual care and one of several repurposed candidate drugs, with additional arms added or dropped as evidence accumulated.

Key findings

  • Dexamethasone — in June 2020, RECOVERY showed that low-dose dexamethasone reduced mortality by about one third in patients receiving invasive mechanical ventilation and by about one fifth in patients receiving oxygen; this was the first treatment shown to improve survival in COVID-19 and rapidly changed practice worldwide.
  • Hydroxychloroquine, lopinavir–ritonavir, and azithromycin — no mortality benefit; these arms were stopped.
  • Tocilizumab — an interleukin-6 receptor blocker shown to reduce mortality in hypoxic patients with systemic inflammation.
  • Baricitinib — a JAK inhibitor found to reduce mortality modestly.
  • REGEN-COV (casirivimab/imdevimab) — reduced mortality among hospitalized patients who had not mounted their own antibody response.
  • Convalescent plasma and aspirin — no overall mortality benefit in the studied populations.

Legacy

RECOVERY was widely praised as a model for rapid, large, simple trials integrated into routine care during a public-health emergency, and its infrastructure was later adapted to other conditions including influenza.
