West Francia


West Francia was the western third of the divided Carolingian Empire.

West Francia (Latin: Francia occidentalis), also known as the Kingdom of the West Franks, was the kingdom formed by the western part of the Carolingian Empire under the Treaty of Verdun in 843. It was ruled by Charles the Bald and his Carolingian successors, and is generally regarded as the historical nucleus of the later Kingdom of France.

History

The Treaty of Verdun divided Charlemagne's empire among the three sons of Louis the Pious: Charles received the west, Lothair I the middle kingdom, and Louis the German the east. West Francia roughly comprised the territory west of a line along the Meuse, Saône, and Rhône rivers. Its kings struggled against Viking raids — including the sieges of Paris — and against the growing independence of great dukes and counts. In 911 the Viking leader Rollo was granted land that became Normandy. The last Carolingian king, Louis V, died in 987, and the magnates elected Hugh Capet, founding the Capetian dynasty; by this time the realm was increasingly referred to simply as Francia, and in later usage as France.

Territory and legacy

West Francia's borders shifted over time, losing and regaining border regions such as parts of Lotharingia, but its linguistic identity — Romance-speaking, in contrast to Germanic-speaking East Francia — foreshadowed the modern division between France and Germany.
