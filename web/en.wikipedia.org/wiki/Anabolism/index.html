Anabolism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anabolism is the set of metabolic pathways that construct molecules from smaller units. These reactions require energy, known also as an endergonic process. Anabolism is the building-up aspect of metabolism, whereas catabolism is the breaking-down aspect. Anabolism is usually synonymous with biosynthesis.

Energy sources

Anabolic processes are powered by energy harvested in catabolism. In most organisms the energy currency is adenosine triphosphate (ATP); reducing power is supplied by carriers such as NADH, NADPH, and FADH2. In photosynthetic organisms, sunlight drives anabolism directly through photosynthesis, fixing carbon dioxide into sugars.

Stages

Anabolism proceeds in three broad stages: first, the production of precursors such as amino acids, monosaccharides, isoprenoids, and nucleotides; second, their activation into reactive forms using energy from ATP; and third, the assembly of these precursors into complex molecules such as proteins, polysaccharides, lipids, and nucleic acids.

Regulation

Hormones classically regarded as anabolic include insulin, growth hormone, and testosterone, which stimulate synthesis of protein and tissue growth. Synthetic derivatives known as anabolic steroids mimic these effects and are used medically as well as being abused in sport.
