Unity is a cross-platform game engine developed by Unity Technologies, first released in 2005. It allows developers to build 2D and 3D video games and interactive simulations for more than 25 platforms, including Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, major game consoles, WebGL browsers, and augmented and virtual reality headsets. Games are scripted primarily in C#, and the editor provides a visual scene-based workflow supplemented by an asset store marketplace.

Unity became especially popular with independent and mobile developers for its low cost, ease of use, and broad platform support. Notable games built with Unity include Pokémon Go, Monument Valley, Cuphead, Hollow Knight, Ori and the Blind Forest, Hearthstone, and Among Us. The engine is also used outside games, in film previsualization, architecture, automotive design, and training simulations.

History

Unity was founded in Copenhagen, Denmark, by David Helgason, Nicholas Francis, and Joachim Ante; version 1.0 launched at Apple's Worldwide Developers Conference in 2005, initially for Mac OS X. Support for Windows, the web player, and iPhone (2008) fueled rapid growth. Unity Technologies went public on the New York Stock Exchange in 2020. In September 2023 the company announced a controversial per-install "Runtime Fee," prompting a developer backlash; the policy was reversed and the fee was cancelled in September 2024, when the company returned to a seat-based subscription model.

Features

The engine includes a real-time renderer with two scriptable render pipelines (Universal and High Definition), a physics system, animation tools, the DOTS/ECS performance framework, and support for both Mono and IL2CPP scripting backends.
