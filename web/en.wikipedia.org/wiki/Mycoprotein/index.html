Mycoprotein

Mycoprotein is a form of single-cell protein derived from fungi for human consumption. It is produced from the mycelium of a filamentous fungus, most famously Fusarium venenatum ATCC PTA-2684, grown by fermentation.

The best-known commercial product is Quorn, launched in the United Kingdom in 1985. Mycoprotein has a naturally fibrous texture resembling meat, is high in protein and fibre, and low in saturated fat. Its production generally uses less land and water than livestock farming.

Production

The fungus is grown in large fermenters on a glucose medium, harvested, heat-treated to reduce RNA content, then bound, textured and formed into products such as mince, fillets and nuggets.
