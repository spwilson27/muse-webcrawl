The Bibliotheca (Ancient Greek: Βιβλιοθήκη, "Library"), also known as the Bibliotheca of Pseudo-Apollodorus, is a compendium of Greek myths and heroic legends, arranged in three books, generally dated to the first or second century AD.

Authorship

The work was traditionally attributed to Apollodorus of Athens (c. 180 – post-120 BC), but its language and citations of Roman-era authors show it is later; scholars therefore call its anonymous author Pseudo-Apollodorus.

Contents

Book 1 covers the origins of the gods and early heroes; Book 2 the Heracles cycle and other heroes; Book 3 the royal houses of Athens, Thebes and Troy. A later epitome preserves summaries of the lost ending, covering Theseus and the Trojan War. For many myths — including versions of Perseus, Oedipus and the Argonauts — it is the fullest surviving ancient source and a cornerstone of modern handbooks of Greek mythology.
