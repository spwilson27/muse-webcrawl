Gibbs–Duhem equation

The Gibbs–Duhem equation in thermodynamics describes the relationship between changes in chemical potential for the components of a thermodynamic system: Σ Ni dμi = −S dT + V dP, where Ni is the number of moles of component i and μi its chemical potential.

Derivation

The relation follows from the extensivity of the Gibbs free energy: G is a homogeneous first-order function of the mole numbers, so its total differential can be equated with the fundamental equation dG = −S dT + V dP + Σ μi dNi, yielding the Gibbs–Duhem constraint.

Applications

The equation shows that the chemical potentials of a mixture cannot vary independently: for a binary system, knowing how one chemical potential changes with composition determines the other. It underlies methods for testing the consistency of experimental activity-coefficient data and deriving Raoult's and Henry's law relationships.

It is named after Josiah Willard Gibbs and Pierre Duhem.
