Borrowdale is a valley in the English Lake District, running south from Derwentwater towards the high central fells of Cumbria. Drained by the River Derwent, it is celebrated for its rugged scenery, ancient oak woodlands and dark volcanic crags, and forms the heart of the Borrowdale parish. The B5289 road runs its length, ending at Seatoller before the road climbs the Honister Pass.

Geography

The valley is cut into the Borrowdale Volcanic Group of Ordovician rocks. At its head the hamlet of Seathwaite — famously among the wettest inhabited places in England, with around 3,300 mm of rain a year — gives access to the central fells, including Scafell Pike, Great Gable and Glaramara. Side valleys include Watendlath, Langstrath and the Styhead route to Wasdale.

History

In the 16th century rich deposits of wad — pure graphite — were discovered near Seathwaite. The mines were guarded and the material, vital for casting cannonballs and later for pencils in Keswick, was so valuable that smuggling it was a serious offence. Borrowdale's remoteness also sheltered the valley's famous yew trees, four of which — the "Fraternal Four" — were celebrated by Wordsworth.

Tourism

Borrowdale is one of the most visited valleys in the Lake District. The Bowder Stone, a huge balanced boulder near Grange, has been a tourist curiosity since the 18th century, and the valley's oakwoods — among the largest remnants of temperate rainforest in England — are protected as a Site of Special Scientific Interest and a National Nature Reserve.
