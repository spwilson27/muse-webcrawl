Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019

The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 is an act of the Parliament of India that reorganised the state of Jammu and Kashmir into two union territories: Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh. It was passed alongside the revocation of the state's special status under Article 370 of the Indian constitution and came into effect on 31 October 2019.

Background

On 5 August 2019 the President of India issued a constitutional order applying the entire Indian constitution to Jammu and Kashmir and superseding the 1954 order that had underpinned Article 370; a further order declared Article 370 inoperative except as modified. Parliament then considered the reorganisation bill, which the government argued would promote development, security, and full integration of the region.

Provisions

  • The state of Jammu and Kashmir was divided into the union territory of Ladakh, comprising the districts of Leh and Kargil, and the union territory of Jammu and Kashmir, comprising the remaining territory.
  • Ladakh would be administered by a lieutenant governor without a legislative assembly; Jammu and Kashmir would have a legislative assembly on the model of Puducherry.
  • The new union territory of Jammu and Kashmir was allotted 107 assembly seats (later delimited to 90 elected seats), and both territories remained within the jurisdiction of the Jammu and Kashmir High Court.
  • Central laws and the Indian Penal Code framework were extended to the new territories, replacing the Ranbir Penal Code and state-specific statutes; hundreds of state laws were repealed or applied with modifications.

Legislative history and response

The bill was introduced in the Rajya Sabha on 5 August 2019 and passed the same day; the Lok Sabha passed it on 6 August, and it received presidential assent on 9 August 2019. The changes were met with protests and legal challenges in India and criticism from Pakistan and China, while the government maintained they were an internal matter. In December 2023 the Supreme Court of India upheld the abrogation of Article 370 and directed that elections be held in Jammu and Kashmir and its statehood restored at the earliest.
