Bleach bypass

Bleach bypass, also known as skip bleach or silver retention, is a chemical effect in the processing of colour photographic film in which the bleaching step is wholly or partly skipped. The result is that silver is retained in the emulsion along with the colour dyes.

Appearance

Retaining the silver gives images a desaturated, high-contrast look with muted colours and deep blacks, similar to superimposing a black-and-white image over a colour one. The effect also increases grain and can be adjusted by varying how much bleach is skipped.

Use in cinema

The technique gained prominence through films such as Saving Private Ryan (1998), where cinematographer Janusz Kamiński used it for a stark combat look, and Minority Report (2002). Digital equivalents now reproduce the look in colour grading software.
