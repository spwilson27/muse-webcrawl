The Riddler (Edward Nigma) is a supervillain appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics, commonly as an adversary of the superhero Batman. Created by writer Bill Finger and artist Dick Sprang, he first appeared in Detective Comics #140 (1948). The character is known for his green costume decorated with question marks and his compulsive habit of challenging Batman with riddles, puzzles, and death traps.

Fictional character biography

Edward Nigma grew up obsessed with puzzles and cheating his way to victory in contests. As the Riddler he commits crimes that he cannot help but announce in advance with riddle clues, driven by a compulsion to prove his intellectual superiority — and in particular to outwit Batman, one of the few minds he regards as a worthy opponent. His obsession is often depicted as a psychological disorder: even when he wishes to stop, he cannot commit a crime without leaving a riddle.

Following the Hush storyline (2002–2003), in which the Riddler was revealed as the scheme's mastermind and learned Batman's secret identity, the character was reformed for a time as a private detective competing with Batman to solve cases, before eventually returning to villainy. In the 2011 "New 52" continuity he was reimagined as the central antagonist of the Zero Year arc, flooding Gotham and plunging it into a feral state. Later storylines, such as The War of Jokes and Riddles, depict his rivalry with the Joker.

In other media

The Riddler was played by Frank Gorshin (and briefly John Astin) in the 1960s Batman television series, by Jim Carrey in the film Batman Forever (1995), by Cory Michael Smith in the series Gotham, and by Paul Dano in The Batman (2022), where the character was reinvented as a serial-killer figure inspired by the Zodiac Killer. He has also appeared in numerous animated series and video games, notably the Batman: Arkham games, in which he hides hundreds of trophies and riddles for the player to solve.
