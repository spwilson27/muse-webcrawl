Run-round

A run-round (also called a run-around or, in North American usage, a run-round loop) is a railway track layout that allows a locomotive to move from the front of a train to the rear, typically at a terminal station, so that the train can depart in the opposite direction without the locomotive being turned or without needing a second locomotive.

Layout

The simplest run-round consists of a second track running parallel to the arrival platform track, connected by crossovers at the buffer-stop end or at the throat of the station. When a train arrives, the passengers alight, the locomotive is uncoupled, draws forward past the crossover, reverses onto the parallel track, runs back past its train, and then reverses again onto the front of the coaches to haul them out in the opposite direction.

Use

Run-round loops were essential on single-ended terminal stations and branch lines in the era of steam and early diesel haulage, where push-pull working was not possible. Many heritage railways still use run-rounds at their termini because steam locomotives must haul trains chimney-first in one direction. Modern multiple units and driving trailers have largely eliminated the need for run-round facilities on main-line termini.
