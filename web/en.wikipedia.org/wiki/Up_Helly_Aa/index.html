Up Helly Aa

Up Helly Aa is a series of fire festivals held in Shetland, Scotland, in midwinter to mark the end of the Yule season. The largest, in Lerwick, takes place on the last Tuesday in January and culminates in a torchlit procession of about a thousand costumed "guizers" who burn a replica Viking galley. Smaller Up Helly Aa festivals are held in other Shetland communities through February and March.

The Lerwick festival

The Lerwick event is led by the Guizer Jarl, chief of the Jarl Squad, who spends years preparing an elaborate Viking-themed costume. On the festival morning the Jarl Squad appears in costume around the town; in the evening, squads of guizers in fancy dress march in a torchlit procession dragging the full-size wooden galley, which is then set alight with the torches to singing of the Up Helly Aa song. Afterwards the squads visit halls around the town performing acts and dances until morning; the following day is a public holiday in Lerwick.

History

The festival grew out of nineteenth-century Lerwick Christmas and New Year customs of tar barrelling — rolling burning tar barrels through the streets — which were suppressed for safety reasons and replaced in the 1880s by torchlight processions and, soon after, the galley burning with its Viking theme. The event is a highlight of the Shetland winter and draws visitors from around the world; women took part as guizers in the Lerwick procession for the first time in 2024, after rule changes in 2023.
