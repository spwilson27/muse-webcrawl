William Weatherford (c. 1781 – 24 March 1824), also known as Red Eagle, was a Muscogee (Creek) chief and a leader of the Red Stick faction during the Creek War of 1813–1814. Of mixed Creek and Scots ancestry, he became famous for leading the attack on Fort Mims and for his surrender to Andrew Jackson after the Battle of Horseshoe Bend.

Background

Weatherford was born to a Creek mother of the Wind Clan and a Scottish trader father. Raised largely within Creek society, he became a prosperous planter and a respected figure among the Upper Creek towns, though he was initially reluctant to join the Red Stick uprising inspired by Tecumseh's movement.

Creek War

In August 1813 Weatherford led the Red Stick attack on Fort Mims in present-day Alabama, in which hundreds of settlers and their allies were killed — an event that drew the United States fully into the war. After the decisive American victory at Horseshoe Bend in March 1814, Weatherford famously rode alone into Jackson's camp to surrender, reportedly asking that Creek women and children be protected; Jackson, impressed, spared his life.

Later life

Weatherford retired to a plantation in Monroe County, Alabama, where he lived quietly until his death in 1824. He remains one of the most celebrated figures in Alabama history, romanticised in local legend.
