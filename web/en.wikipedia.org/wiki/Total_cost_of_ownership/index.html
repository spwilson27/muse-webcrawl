Total cost of ownership

Total cost of ownership (TCO) is a financial estimate intended to help buyers and owners determine the direct and indirect costs of a product or system over its whole life cycle. It goes beyond the initial purchase price to include the costs of operation, maintenance, support, training, downtime, and eventual disposal or decommissioning.

Method

A TCO analysis typically divides costs into acquisition costs (purchase, installation, implementation), operating costs (energy, consumables, licences, staff time), maintenance and repair, and end-of-life costs, and may discount future costs to present value. The concept was popularised in information technology procurement by the Gartner Group in the late 1980s, when studies showed that the purchase price of a personal computer was only a small fraction of the cost of owning it over several years.

Applications and criticism

TCO is widely used in fleet management and vehicle purchasing (comparing fuel, insurance, tyres and depreciation), in enterprise software decisions, and in comparing cloud services with on-premises systems. Critics note that TCO figures depend heavily on assumptions, can omit benefits and flexibility, and may be manipulated by vendors; it is best used alongside measures of value and return on investment.
