Israeli Intelligence Community

Main agenciesMossad, Shin Bet (Shabak), Aman
OversightPrime Minister; Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee

The Israeli Intelligence Community comprises the intelligence and security agencies of Israel, principally Mossad (foreign intelligence and covert action), Shin Bet (internal security and counter-terrorism) and Aman (military intelligence of the Israel Defense Forces), alongside police intelligence, the National Security Council and research divisions.

Agencies

Mossad, founded 1949, reports directly to the Prime Minister and conducts espionage and covert operations abroad, including the capture of Adolf Eichmann (1960). Shin Bet handles domestic counter-intelligence and protection. Aman provides military intelligence and national assessments; its signals unit is Unit 8200. Other bodies include Lahav 433 (police), the Center for Political Research in the Foreign Ministry, and intelligence branches for the territories.

History

The community grew from pre-state Haganah intelligence (Shai). Major episodes include the Lavon Affair (1954), the Six-Day War intelligence success, the failure to foresee the 1973 Yom Kippur War — prompting the Agranat Commission and reforms — the Pollard affair, and intelligence failures preceding the 7 October 2023 Hamas attack, which prompted major inquiries.

Oversight and criticism

Oversight rests with the Prime Minister, ministerial committees and a Knesset subcommittee. Agencies have been criticised over targeted killings, surveillance and the 1995 failure to prevent the assassination of Yitzhak Rabin (Shin Bet).
