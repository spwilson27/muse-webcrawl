PERCIST (PET Response Criteria in Solid Tumors) is a standardised framework for assessing the response of solid tumors to treatment using 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) positron emission tomography (PET). It was proposed in 2009 by Richard L. Wahl and colleagues in the Journal of Nuclear Medicine ("From RECIST to PERCIST: evolving considerations for PET response criteria in solid tumors") to give metabolic imaging a rigorous, reproducible criteria set analogous to the anatomy-based RECIST criteria used with CT and MRI.

Method

PERCIST is built around the peak standardized uptake value corrected for lean body mass (SULpeak), measured in a roughly 1 cm³ spherical region of interest placed on the most metabolically active part of a tumor. To be measurable, a target lesion's SULpeak at baseline must exceed a threshold defined relative to background uptake in the liver (or blood pool). Imaging conditions are tightly specified: fasting state, blood-glucose limits, consistent uptake time after tracer injection, and consistent scanner and reconstruction parameters between baseline and follow-up scans.

Response categories

  • Complete metabolic response (CMR) — complete resolution of FDG uptake in all lesions to below background levels, indistinguishable from surrounding tissue.
  • Partial metabolic response (PMR) — a reduction of at least 30% (and at least 0.8 SUL units) in the SULpeak of the target lesion, with no new lesions and no progression of non-target disease.
  • Stable metabolic disease (SMD) — neither sufficient decrease for PMR nor sufficient increase for PMD.
  • Progressive metabolic disease (PMD) — an increase of more than 30% (and more than 0.8 SUL units) in SULpeak, a visible increase in the extent of tumor uptake, or the appearance of new FDG-avid lesions typical of cancer.

Use and limitations

PERCIST is widely used in clinical trials of new cancer therapies, where metabolic response on FDG PET often precedes measurable shrinkage on CT, and variants have been adapted for lymphoma (in combination with the Deauville/Lugano framework), immunotherapy and other tracers. Limitations include tumors with low baseline FDG avidity, confounding uptake from inflammation or infection, sensitivity to technical variability between scans, and the fact that the criteria rest largely on a single hottest lesion rather than total tumor burden, though total lesion glycolysis and metabolic tumor volume are also reported.
