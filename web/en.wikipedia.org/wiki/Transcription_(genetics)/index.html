Transcription (genetics)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Transcription is the first step of gene expression: the synthesis of an RNA molecule from a DNA template by the enzyme RNA polymerase. The RNA copy — most famously messenger RNA for protein-coding genes — carries the gene's information to the cell's protein-making machinery, or itself functions as a structural or regulatory RNA.

Process

Transcription begins when RNA polymerase, guided by transcription factors, binds a promoter sequence and unwinds the DNA. The enzyme moves along the template strand, adding ribonucleotides complementary to the DNA, until it reaches a terminator. In bacteria, translation can begin on the RNA while it is still being made. In eukaryotes the initial transcript (pre-mRNA) is processed — a cap added at the front, a poly-A tail at the end, and introns spliced out — before leaving the Nucleus.

Regulation

Cells control which genes are transcribed through transcription factors, enhancer and silencer sequences, chromatin state and epigenetic marks. Regulation of transcription is the main way cells with identical genomes become different cell types. Reverse transcription — RNA back into DNA — occurs in retroviruses such as HIV and is targeted by antiretroviral drugs.
