Head and neck cancer

Head and neck cancer is a group of cancers that start in the mouth, nose, throat, larynx, sinuses or salivary glands. Most are squamous cell carcinomas arising from the mucosal lining of these structures. Symptoms may include a lump in the neck, a sore that does not heal, a persistent sore throat, difficulty swallowing or voice changes.

Risk factors

The major risk factors are tobacco use and alcohol consumption, which act synergistically. Infection with human papillomavirus (HPV), particularly HPV-16, is an increasingly important cause of oropharyngeal cancer. Other factors include Epstein–Barr virus (in nasopharyngeal cancer), betel quid chewing, occupational exposures and poor oral hygiene.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis involves physical examination, endoscopy, imaging and biopsy. Treatment typically combines surgery, radiation therapy and chemotherapy, chosen according to site and stage. HPV-positive oropharyngeal cancers generally have a better prognosis than HPV-negative ones.

Epidemiology

Head and neck cancers collectively account for several hundred thousand new cases worldwide each year and are more common in men than in women.
