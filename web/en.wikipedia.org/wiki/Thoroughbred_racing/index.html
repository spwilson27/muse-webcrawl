Thoroughbred racing is a sport and industry involving the racing of Thoroughbred horses. It is governed by different national bodies; the two main forms are flat racing, in which horses run over a level track, and jump racing (steeplechasing or hurdling), in which they jump obstacles. Thoroughbred racing is one of the most popular spectator sports in the world and is closely associated with gambling, primarily through pari-mutuel betting.

The Thoroughbred breed

All modern Thoroughbreds trace their ancestry to three foundation sires imported into England in the late seventeenth and early eighteenth centuries: the Byerley Turk, the Darley Arabian, and the Godolphin Arabian. Every Thoroughbred's pedigree is recorded in stud books, the first of which, the General Stud Book, was published in England in 1791. The breed was developed for speed and stamina over middle distances.

Types of race

Flat races are run over distances from about five furlongs (1,000 m) for sprinters to two miles and beyond for stayers. The most prestigious flat races are the Classics, restricted to three-year-olds. In Britain the five Classics are the 1,000 Guineas, 2,000 Guineas, the Derby, the Oaks, and the St Leger. In the United States the Triple Crown consists of the Kentucky Derby, Preakness Stakes, and Belmont Stakes.

Jump racing includes steeplechases over fences and hurdle races; the most famous steeplechase is the Grand National at Aintree in England.

Handicapping and betting

In handicap races, horses carry different weights according to their official ratings so as to equalise their chances. Betting on racing takes place on-course through bookmakers or the totalisator (pari-mutuel system), and increasingly online. Owners, trainers, and jockeys share prize money; leading jockeys are among the best-known figures in the sport.

Industry and welfare

The breeding and racing of Thoroughbreds is a major international industry, with important centres in Britain, Ireland, France, the United States, Australia, Japan, and the Middle East. The sport faces continuing scrutiny over animal welfare, including the use of the whip, racing injuries, and the fate of horses after retirement.
