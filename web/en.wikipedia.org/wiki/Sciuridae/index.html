Sciuridae


Scientific name: Sciuridae
Order: Rodentia
Genera: about 58, with some 285 species

Sciuridae is a family that includes small or medium-size rodents: the squirrels. The family includes tree squirrels, ground squirrels, chipmunks, marmots (including groundhogs), flying squirrels, and prairie dogs, amongst others. Squirrels are indigenous to the Americas, Eurasia, and Africa, and were introduced by humans to Australia.

Characteristics

Sciurids are generally slender animals with bushy tails and large eyes, soft silky fur, and sharp claws adapted for climbing or digging; body length ranges from about 10 cm in the African pygmy squirrel to more than 50 cm in marmots. Most are herbivorous, feeding on seeds, nuts, fruits, and fungi, though some supplement their diet with insects or small vertebrates. Their continuously growing incisors are characteristic of rodents.

Classification

The family is divided into subfamilies including Ratufinae (giant tree squirrels), Sciurillinae (Neotropical pygmy squirrel), Sciurinae (tree and flying squirrels), Callosciurinae (Asian ornate squirrels), and Xerinae (ground squirrels, chipmunks, marmots, and prairie dogs). The earliest known sciurids date to the late Eocene, around 40 million years ago.
