Wii

The Wii is a home video game console developed and marketed by Nintendo. It was released on 19 November 2006 in North America and in December 2006 for most other regions of the world. As a seventh-generation console, the Wii competed with Microsoft's Xbox 360 and Sony's PlayStation 3.

Design and controller

Nintendo states that the console targets a broader demographic than its competitors. Its distinguishing feature is the Wii Remote controller, a wireless device that can be used as a handheld pointing device and which detects movement in three dimensions, using internal accelerometers and infrared sensing of a sensor bar. The controller is often paired with the Nunchuk attachment.

Software and reception

The Wii introduced games such as Wii Sports (bundled with the console in most regions), Wii Play, Wii Fit, Mario Kart Wii, and Super Mario Galaxy. Wii Sports became a cultural phenomenon, credited with bringing video games to older and non-traditional audiences. The system also offered the Wii Shop Channel for downloadable games and the Virtual Console service for games from earlier systems.

Legacy

The Wii sold over 101 million units worldwide, making it Nintendo's best-selling home console at the time and the overall winner of its console generation. Motion control was widely imitated by competitors. It was succeeded by the Wii U in 2012, which was designed to be backward compatible with Wii software and peripherals.
