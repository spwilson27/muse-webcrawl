Dedham is a town in and the county seat of Norfolk County, Massachusetts, United States. Located on the southwest border of Boston, it had a population of 25,364 at the 2020 census. Dedham was settled in 1635 and incorporated in 1636, making it one of the oldest towns in Massachusetts, and it was named after Dedham, Essex in England.

History

The settlers who founded Dedham originally petitioned to name it "Contentment". The town is noted for the Dedham Covenant, signed by the first settlers, and for establishing in 1644 the first public school in America supported by public taxes. In 1648, a Dedham resident built a section of the "Fairbanks House," often cited as the oldest surviving timber-frame house in North America. Dedham's pottery, produced by the Dedham Pottery company from the 19th century, is famous for its crackled glaze with a distinctive blue rabbit motif.

Dedham was the site of the 1927 trial of Sacco and Vanzetti, one of the most famous criminal cases of the 20th century, held at the Norfolk County Courthouse. Several other towns were set off from Dedham's original territory, including much of what is now Norwood, Westwood, Dover, and parts of Needham and other communities.

Geography

Dedham covers about 10.6 square miles and is bordered by Boston (West Roxbury and Hyde Park) to the northeast, Needham to the west, Canton to the south, and Norwood and Westwood to the southwest. The Charles River winds through the town, and the Neponset River forms part of its eastern border. The town includes the neighborhoods of Dedham Square, Oakdale, Greenlodge, Riverdale, East Dedham, and the Manor.

Government and education

Dedham has an open town meeting form of government with a town manager and a five-member Select Board. It is served by the Dedham Public Schools, Dedham High School, and private institutions including Noble and Greenough School and the Ursuline Academy. The Mother Brook, often called America's first man-made canal, runs through town and once powered its mills.
