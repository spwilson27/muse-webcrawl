Expungement

In the common law legal system, an expungement proceeding is a type of lawsuit in which a first-time offender of a prior criminal conviction seeks that the records of that earlier process be sealed or destroyed, thereby making the records nonexistent or unavailable to the general public. If successful, the proceedings are concluded and the individual's record is treated, for most purposes, as though the arrest or conviction never occurred.

Eligibility and process

Eligibility for expungement varies widely by jurisdiction and typically depends on the seriousness of the offence, the time elapsed since the sentence was completed, and whether the petitioner has subsequent convictions. Juvenile records, arrests that did not lead to conviction, and minor misdemeanours are the most commonly eligible. The process usually requires filing a petition with the court, notifying prosecutors, and attending a hearing; some jurisdictions have adopted automatic or "clean slate" expungement for certain categories of records.

Effects

Expungement can improve access to employment, housing, and professional licences, from which criminal records often disqualify applicants. It is distinct from a pardon, which forgives the offence but does not erase the record, and from sealing, which restricts but does not destroy access. Private background-check databases may retain copies of expunged information, limiting the practical completeness of relief.
