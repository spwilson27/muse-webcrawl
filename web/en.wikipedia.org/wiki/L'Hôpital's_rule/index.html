L'Hôpital's rule

L'Hôpital's rule (also spelled L'Hospital's rule) is a theorem in calculus that provides a method for evaluating limits of indeterminate forms. It states that, under suitable conditions, if the limit of f(x)/g(x) as x approaches a point takes the indeterminate form 0/0 or ∞/∞, then the limit equals the limit of the ratio of the derivatives, f′(x)/g′(x), provided the latter limit exists.

Statement

If f and g are differentiable near a point c (except possibly at c itself), g′(x) ≠ 0 there, and lim f(x) = lim g(x) = 0 (or both limits are ±∞), then:

limx→c f(x)/g(x) = limx→c f′(x)/g′(x)

whenever the limit on the right exists or is ±∞. The rule is typically proved using Cauchy's mean value theorem. Other indeterminate forms — 0·∞, ∞−∞, 00, 1∞ and ∞0 — can be rearranged into quotients and treated with the rule.

History

The rule is named after the French mathematician Guillaume de l'Hôpital, who published it in his Analyse des Infiniment Petits pour l'Intelligence des Lignes Courbes (1696), the first textbook on differential calculus. However, the rule had been discovered by Johann Bernoulli: l'Hôpital had paid Bernoulli a retainer for his discoveries, and Bernoulli later asserted authorship, a claim supported by correspondence published in the 20th century and now widely accepted by historians.

Examples and caveats

A classic application: limx→0 sin x / x = limx→0 cos x / 1 = 1. The rule may fail when the limit of the derivative ratio does not exist although the original limit does (e.g. (x + sin x)/x as x → ∞), and repeated application can cycle; limits of products or powers must first be rewritten as quotients.
