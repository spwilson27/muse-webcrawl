A parasitic disease is an infectious disease caused by a parasite — an organism that lives on or in a host and derives nourishment at the host's expense. Human parasitic diseases are grouped by agent into those caused by protozoa (single-celled eukaryotes), helminths (parasitic worms) and ectoparasites such as lice, mites and ticks. Together they account for an enormous global burden of illness, concentrated in tropical and low-income regions.

Major diseases

Malaria, caused by Plasmodium protozoa transmitted by Anopheles mosquitoes, remains the deadliest parasitic disease, killing several hundred thousand people annually, most of them African children. Schistosomiasis (blood flukes) affects over 200 million people; soil-transmitted helminths — roundworm, whipworm and hookworm — infect well over a billion. Other major conditions include amoebic dysentery, giardiasis, leishmaniasis, African trypanosomiasis (sleeping sickness), Chagas disease, lymphatic filariasis (elephantiasis) and onchocerciasis (river blindness). Many are classed among the World Health Organization's neglected tropical diseases.

Transmission

Parasites reach humans by many routes: insect vectors; contaminated water and food; skin penetration (hookworm larvae, schistosome cercariae); person-to-person and faecal–oral spread; and congenital or sexual transmission (as with Trichomonas vaginalis). Complex life cycles with intermediate hosts — snails, fish, livestock — make control difficult but also create exploitable weak points.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis traditionally relies on microscopy of blood, stool or tissue, increasingly supplemented by antigen tests and PCR. Treatment uses antiparasitic drugs such as artemisinin combinations (malaria), praziquantel (schistosomiasis), albendazole and mebendazole (intestinal worms) and ivermectin (filariases). Drug resistance — notably artemisinin-resistant malaria in Southeast Asia — is a persistent concern.

Control

Control strategies combine vector management (insecticide-treated bed nets, indoor spraying), safe water and sanitation, snail control, health education and mass drug administration to whole at-risk populations. Guinea worm disease (dracunculiasis) has been brought to the brink of eradication by behavioural and water-filtering measures alone, demonstrating that parasitic diseases can be eliminated without drugs or vaccines.
