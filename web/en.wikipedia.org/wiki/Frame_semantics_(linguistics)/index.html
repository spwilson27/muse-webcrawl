Frame semantics (linguistics)

Frame semantics is a theory of linguistic meaning developed by Charles J. Fillmore that extends his earlier case grammar. It holds that the meaning of a word can only be understood with reference to a structured background of experience, beliefs or practices — a conceptual structure called a frame.

Core ideas

Words evoke frames: to understand buy or sell one must understand the commercial transaction frame, with participants such as buyer, seller, goods and money and a prototypical sequence of events. Different words in the same frame profile different parts of it. Frames are organised by relations such as inheritance, subframe and perspective, and a word's meaning depends on the frame it activates in the minds of speakers and hearers ("words represent categorizations of experience").

Relation to other theories

Frame semantics grew out of Fillmore's work on case grammar in the 1960s–70s and is a cornerstone of cognitive linguistics, related to prototype theory and to script theory in artificial intelligence (Marvin Minsky's and Roger Schank's frames). Its main applied product is the FrameNet lexical database.
