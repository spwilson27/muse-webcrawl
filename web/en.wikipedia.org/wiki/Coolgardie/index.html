Coolgardie

Coolgardie is a small town in Western Australia, 558 kilometres (347 mi) east of the state capital, Perth. It has a population of approximately 850 people. Although now a modest settlement, Coolgardie was once the third largest town in Western Australia, founded after the discovery of gold in 1892 that sparked one of Australia's great gold rushes.

History

Gold was discovered at Fly Flat in 1892 by Arthur Bayley and William Ford, who claimed their reward at Southern Cross. Thousands of prospectors rushed to the field, and the town boomed; it was the terminus of the railway and the starting point for prospectors heading to the even richer finds at Kalgoorlie, discovered by Paddy Hannan in 1893.

Water and the goldfields pipeline

The arid district suffered severe water shortages, with typhoid outbreaks killing many residents. The problem was solved by C. Y. O'Connor's Goldfields Water Supply Scheme, completed in 1903, which pumped water from Mundaring Weir near Perth through a pipeline over 500 kilometres long to the goldfields.

Present day

Today Coolgardie serves travellers on the Great Eastern Highway and retains many grand buildings from its boom years along the unusually wide Bayley Street, including the Marvel Bar and the Coolgardie Goldfields Exhibition Building.
