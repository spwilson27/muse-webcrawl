Leslie Marmon Silko

Leslie Marmon Silko (born 5 March 1948) is an American writer of Laguna Pueblo descent, regarded as a key figure of the Native American Renaissance. Her novel Ceremony (1977) is one of the most widely taught works of Native American literature.

Life and education

Silko grew up at Laguna Pueblo, New Mexico, in a family of Laguna, Mexican and Anglo heritage, and was raised partly by relatives who passed on traditional storytelling. She studied at the University of New Mexico, briefly attending law school before turning to writing.

Works

Ceremony follows Tayo, a World War II veteran of mixed heritage, as he seeks healing through Laguna ritual and story. Her later works include the story collection Storyteller (1981), which weaves poems, photographs and traditional tales; the sweeping novel Almanac of the Dead (1991); and Gardens in the Dunes (1999). She received a MacArthur Fellowship in 1981.
