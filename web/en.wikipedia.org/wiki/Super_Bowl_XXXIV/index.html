Super Bowl XXXIV was the championship game of the 1999 NFL season, played on 30 January 2000 at the Georgia Dome in Atlanta. The St. Louis Rams defeated the Tennessee Titans 23–16 in one of the most dramatic finishes in Super Bowl history, remembered as "One Yard Short".

The game

The Rams, whose high-powered offence under coordinator Mike Martz was dubbed "The Greatest Show on Turf", led 16–0 through three field goals by Jeff Wilkins and a touchdown pass from Kurt Warner to Torry Holt. Warner, a former Arena Football and NFL Europe quarterback who had become the league MVP, threw for a Super Bowl record 414 yards. Tennessee, behind quarterback Steve McNair and running back Eddie George (two touchdowns), stormed back to tie the game 16–16 with 2:12 remaining. On the very next play, Warner hit Isaac Bruce for a 73-yard touchdown to restore the lead.

The final play

The Titans drove to the Rams' 10-yard line in the closing seconds. With six seconds left and no timeouts, McNair completed a pass to Kevin Dyson, who was tackled by linebacker Mike Jones at the one-yard line as time expired — Dyson stretching the ball toward the goal line but falling literally one yard short. The image of "The Tackle" became one of the iconic photographs of the sport.

Legacy

It was the Rams' first Super Bowl victory and remains the franchise's only title won in St. Louis (they later won Super Bowl LVI in Los Angeles). Warner was named MVP, completing a celebrated rise from stocking supermarket shelves a few years earlier. The Titans' final drive, and the Music City Miracle with which they had reached the playoffs, bookended an extraordinary postseason. The game is consistently ranked among the greatest Super Bowls.
