In probability theory and statistics, the exponential distribution is the probability distribution of the distance between events in a Poisson point process, that is, a process in which events occur continuously and independently at a constant average rate. It is the continuous analogue of the geometric distribution, and it has the key property of being memoryless.

Definitions

The probability density function (pdf) of an exponential distribution is f(x; λ) = λe−λx for x ≥ 0, and zero for x < 0, where λ > 0 is the rate parameter. The cumulative distribution function is F(x; λ) = 1 − e−λx. The distribution is sometimes parametrised instead by the scale parameter β = 1/λ, the mean waiting time.

Properties

The mean and standard deviation of an exponentially distributed random variable are both equal to 1/λ, so the variance is 1/λ². The median is ln(2)/λ. The exponential distribution is memoryless: the probability that an event has not occurred by time s + t, given that it has not occurred by time s, equals the unconditional probability that it has not occurred by time t. It is the only continuous probability distribution with this property.

Occurrence and applications

The exponential distribution models the time between arrivals in queues, the lifetime of radioactive particles before decay, the length of telephone calls, and the distance between mutations on a strand of DNA. It arises as the inter-arrival time distribution of a homogeneous Poisson process, and sums of independent exponential variables follow the Erlang distribution. In reliability engineering it describes components whose failure rate is constant over time.
