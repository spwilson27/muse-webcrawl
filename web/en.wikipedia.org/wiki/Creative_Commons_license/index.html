Creative Commons license

A Creative Commons (CC) license is one of several public copyright licenses that enable the free distribution of an otherwise copyrighted work. A CC license is used when an author wants to give other people the right to share, use and build upon a work that the author has created, without negotiating individual permissions. The licenses are issued by the non-profit organisation Creative Commons, founded in 2001 by Lawrence Lessig and others.

License elements

The regular suite of licenses is built from four conditions:

  • BY — Attribution: credit must be given to the creator.
  • SA — ShareAlike: adaptations must be shared under the same terms.
  • NC — NonCommercial: the work may not be used primarily for commercial purposes.
  • ND — NoDerivatives: the work may be shared but not adapted.

Combining these yields six standard licenses, from the most permissive, CC BY, to CC BY-NC-ND. Creative Commons also provides CC0, a public-domain dedication tool for waiving rights altogether, and the Public Domain Mark for old works already free of copyright. The current version of the license suite is 4.0, released in 2013.

Use

CC licenses are used by Wikipedia and other Wikimedia projects (CC BY-SA), open educational resources, scientific publishers practising open access, governments, museums and millions of creators of photographs, music and video on platforms such as Flickr and YouTube. The licenses operate within copyright law — they do not replace it — and are irrevocable once applied to a work, though the licensor may stop distributing the work.
