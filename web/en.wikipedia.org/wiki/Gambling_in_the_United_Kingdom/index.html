Gambling in the United Kingdom
Regulator: Gambling Commission
Governing law: Gambling Act 2005
Minimum age: 18 (most products)
Notable products: National Lottery, betting shops, casinos, online gambling

Gambling in the United Kingdom is regulated by the Gambling Commission on behalf of the government's Department for Culture, Media and Sport, under the Gambling Act 2005. Gambling has a long history in the UK, from horse racing and betting shops to the National Lottery, launched in 1994, and one of the world's largest regulated online gambling markets.


A betting shop on a British high street

History

Gambling was heavily restricted in the nineteenth century, notably by the Gaming Act 1845, which made wagering contracts unenforceable. The Betting and Gaming Act 1960 legalised betting shops, and the Gambling Act 2005 comprehensively modernised the law, permitting gambling contracts to be enforced and creating the Gambling Commission.

Sectors

The industry includes land-based casinos, betting shops (bookmakers), bingo halls, arcades, society and national lotteries, and remote (online) gambling. Fixed odds betting terminals in betting shops were the subject of controversy, leading to a reduction of the maximum stake to £2 in 2019. In 2023 the government published a white paper proposing further reforms, including affordability checks and a statutory levy.
