My Fair Lady (film)

My Fair Lady is a 1964 American musical film directed by George Cukor and starring Audrey Hepburn and Rex Harrison. Adapted from the 1956 Lerner and Loewe stage musical, itself based on George Bernard Shaw's play Pygmalion (1913), it tells the story of Eliza Doolittle, a poor Cockney flower-seller whom phonetics professor Henry Higgins wagers he can pass off as a lady in London high society. The film won eight Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Director, and Best Actor.

Plot

In Edwardian London, phonetician Henry Higgins bets his friend Colonel Pickering that within six months he can transform Eliza Doolittle, a flower girl with a strong Cockney accent, into a lady who can be presented at the Embassy Ball. Through gruelling elocution lessons ("The rain in Spain stays mainly in the plain") Eliza gradually masters Received Pronunciation, triumphs first at Ascot — despite a lapse into Cockney at the climax of a race — and then at the ball itself. Realising Higgins treats her as an experiment rather than a person, she leaves; Higgins, having grown accustomed to her face, belatedly recognises his feelings as she returns in the final scene.

Production

Warner Bros. studio head Jack L. Warner acquired the film rights for a record sum and chose Hepburn over Julie Andrews, who had originated Eliza on Broadway — a casting decision that remained controversial. Most of Hepburn's singing was dubbed by Marni Nixon, though Hepburn recorded her own versions. Rex Harrison reprised his stage role and, unusually for a musical, performed his songs live on set with a hidden microphone. Cecil Beaton's production and costume design, shot in Super Panavision 70, gave the film its sumptuous look.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major commercial success and won eight of its twelve Academy Award nominations, including Best Picture, Best Director (Cukor), Best Actor (Harrison), Best Cinematography, and Best Costume Design. It was the highest-grossing film of 1964 and is preserved in the United States National Film Registry. Songs including "I Could Have Danced All Night", "Wouldn't It Be Loverly", "On the Street Where You Live", and "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face" became standards.
