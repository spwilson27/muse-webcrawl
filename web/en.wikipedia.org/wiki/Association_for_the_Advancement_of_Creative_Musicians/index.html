Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians

The Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians (AACM) is a non-profit organization of musicians that was formed in 1965 in Chicago, Illinois. Founded by pianist Muhal Richard Abrams and others, it is dedicated to nurturing, performing, and recording serious, original music — particularly avant-garde jazz and experimental music by African American composers — and to providing music education in underserved communities.

History

The AACM grew out of Abrams's Experimental Band and was chartered in 1965, at a time when Chicago's Black avant-garde musicians had few venues or recording opportunities. The organisation took matters into its own hands: it produced its own concerts, established a free music school for inner-city youth, and developed a distinctive aesthetic motto, "Great Black Music, Ancient to the Future."

Notable members and influence

Members have included Anthony Braxton, the Art Ensemble of Chicago (Roscoe Mitchell, Lester Bowie, Joseph Jarman, Malachi Favors, Don Moye), Henry Threadgill, Leroy Jenkins, Amina Claudine Myers, Wadada Leo Smith, George Lewis, and Nicole Mitchell. In 1969 many members travelled to Paris, sparking international attention. Chapters were later established in New York and elsewhere. The AACM's model of musician self-determination has been highly influential on creative music worldwide, and its history is documented in George Lewis's book A Power Stronger Than Itself (2008).
