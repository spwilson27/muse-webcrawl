In the Mood for Love

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Su Li-zhen in a floral cheongsam carrying a noodle thermos down the lamplit stairs, passing Chow Mo-wan in the corridor]

In the Mood for Love (Chinese: 花樣年華, Faayeung nin wa, 'the age of blossoms') is a 2000 romantic drama film written, produced and directed by Wong Kar-wai, starring Maggie Cheung and Tony Leung. In the Hong Kong of 1962, two neighbours in a crowded Shanghainese émigré rooming house discover that their spouses are having an affair with each other; their own meetings — to rehearse, to understand, never (in the film's exquisite restraint) to consummate — become cinema's most celebrated portrait of desire deferred.

Leung won Best Actor at Cannes, the first Hong Kong actor so honoured; Christopher Doyle and Mark Lee's cinematography (corridors, clocks, smoke, cheongsam silk) and Shigeru Umebayashi's waltz theme built the film's trance. It placed fifth in the 2022 Sight and Sound critics' poll — the highest film of the twenty-first century in that list.

Making

Shot over 15 months as Wong improvised — the film's ending (Chow whispering his secret into a hole in the ruins of Angkor Wat, sealing it with grass, from a Cambodian coda) was found late — In the Mood for Love distils its director's manner: what is not shown (the spouses' faces, the affair, the consummation that never happens) carries the charge. Cheung wears a different cheongsam in nearly every scene, some thirty dresses marking time's passage like rings on a tree.

Legacy

Together with Days of Being Wild (1990) and 2046 (2004) it forms a loose trilogy. Sofia Coppola thanked Wong in her Oscar speech for Lost in Translation; the film's design language — red, green, steam, rain — saturates contemporary cinema and photography. For many critics it is simply the most beautiful film ever made, a judgement its poll placings keep confirming.
