Dark academia

Dark academia is an internet aesthetic and subculture concerned with higher education, the arts, and classic literature. Popularised on social media platforms such as Tumblr in the mid-2010s and surging on TikTok and Instagram during the COVID-19 pandemic, it romanticises a melancholic, autumnal vision of scholarly life at old European and Ivy League universities.

Aesthetic

The visual palette centres on dark, muted colours — black, brown, deep green, and burgundy — and on settings such as gothic libraries, candlelit studies, stone cloisters, and museums. Fashion within the subculture draws on tweed blazers, turtlenecks, plaid skirts, brogues, and vintage tailoring. Pursuits celebrated by the aesthetic include reading classical literature and poetry, studying Latin and Greek, calligraphy, visiting art galleries, and listening to classical music.

Origins and influences

The aesthetic is heavily influenced by Donna Tartt's 1992 novel The Secret History, in which an elite circle of classics students at a New England college commits murder, as well as by films such as Dead Poets Society (1989). Its roots reach back through the Romantic and gothic traditions and the pre-war Oxbridge world depicted in Evelyn Waugh's Brideshead Revisited.

Criticism

Commentators have criticised dark academia for romanticising Eurocentric, elitist institutions and for glamorising unhealthy behaviours such as sleep deprivation and melancholy. Others note its appeal as a response to the corporatisation of education: an imagined world where learning is pursued for its own sake. A lighter counterpart aesthetic, light academia, shares the scholarly focus with a brighter, softer mood.
