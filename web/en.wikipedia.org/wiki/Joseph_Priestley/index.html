Joseph Priestley
Born: 24 March 1733, Yorkshire, England
Field: Chemistry, theology, natural philosophy
Type: Wikipedia article (recreated from memory)

Joseph Priestley (1733–1804) was an English clergyman and natural philosopher who discovered oxygen in 1774 — 'dephlogisticated air', in his phlogiston language — by heating mercury oxide with a burning lens, though he never accepted Lavoisier's oxygen theory of combustion.

Priestley isolated or characterised some ten gases, including ammonia, hydrogen chloride, nitrous oxide and carbon monoxide, invented soda water (impregnating water with CO₂), and wrote a major history of electricity. His radical politics and theology made him a target: a mob burned his Birmingham home and laboratory in 1791, and he emigrated to Pennsylvania.

[Image: Priestley collecting gases over water in his pneumatic trough]

The oxygen story

Scheele had made oxygen earlier but published later; Priestley published first, and told Lavoisier of the gas over dinner in Paris in 1774 — Lavoisier grasped its significance and named it, while Priestley defended phlogiston to his death.

Unitarian ministry

Priestley saw his science and his dissenting ministry as one pursuit of truth and progress; his History of the Corruptions of Christianity (1782) influenced Unitarianism's development.
