Halloumi (Greek: χαλλούμι; Turkish: hellim) is a traditional Cypriot cheese made from a mixture of goat's and sheep's milk, and sometimes cow's milk. It has a high melting point, so it can readily be fried or grilled — a property arising from its production method, in which the curd is heated in whey before being salted and stored in brine, often with mint.

Production

Halloumi is made by curdling heated milk with rennet, pressing the curd, then cooking the formed cheese in hot whey until it floats. The cheese is folded, salted, and traditionally sprinkled with dried mint leaves before being packed in its own brine. Its resistance to melting and its firm, squeaky texture make it distinctive among cheeses.

Culinary use

In Cyprus, halloumi is eaten fresh, grilled, or fried, served with watermelon in summer, in sandwiches, or with eggs and lountza. It has become popular internationally as a vegetarian grilling and barbecue ingredient. In April 2021 the European Union registered "Halloumi/Hellim" as a Protected Designation of Origin (PDO), covering production on both sides of the divided island according to a traditional recipe requiring a majority of sheep's and goat's milk during transitional arrangements.
