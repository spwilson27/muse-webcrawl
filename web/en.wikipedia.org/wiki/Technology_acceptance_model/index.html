Technology acceptance model

The technology acceptance model (TAM) is an information systems theory that models how users come to accept and use a technology. Developed by Fred Davis in the 1980s, the model proposes that when users are presented with a new technology, a number of factors influence their decision about how and when they will use it.

Core constructs

The two central factors in the original model are:

  • Perceived usefulness (PU) — "the degree to which a person believes that using a particular system would enhance their job performance."
  • Perceived ease of use (PEOU) — "the degree to which a person believes that using a particular system would be free from effort."

These perceptions shape a user's attitude toward using the system, which in turn determines behavioural intention and, ultimately, actual system use. Perceived ease of use also influences perceived usefulness, since a system that is easier to use can be more useful.

Extensions

TAM has been extended in numerous studies. TAM2, developed by Venkatesh and Davis (2000), added social influence and cognitive instrumental processes such as subjective norm, job relevance and output quality. The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT, 2003) integrated TAM with seven other models of user acceptance.

Criticisms

Critics have argued that TAM relies excessively on self-reported intention rather than observed usage, that its constructs explain only part of technology adoption behaviour, and that it oversimplifies organizational contexts. Nevertheless, it remains one of the most widely cited models in information systems research.
