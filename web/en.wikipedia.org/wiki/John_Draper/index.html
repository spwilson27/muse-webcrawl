John Draper

John Thomas Draper (born 1943), better known by his pseudonym Captain Crunch, is an American computer programmer and former phone phreak. He is best known for discovering that the toy whistle distributed in Cap'n Crunch breakfast cereal could reproduce a 2600 Hz tone, giving him control over AT&T's long-distance telephone switching system.

Phone phreaking

Draper, who served in the U.S. Air Force in Alaska, learned from a blind phone phreak that the Cap'n Crunch toy whistle emitted exactly 2600 Hz, the frequency used by AT&T to signal trunk lines. By blowing the whistle into a phone, he could access the operator mode of the telephone network and place free long-distance calls, pioneering the practice of "phone phreaking."

Computing work

Draper later worked in computing, writing EasyWriter, the first word processor for the Apple II, which became a major product distributed by IBM with the original IBM PC. He was briefly associated with Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak in the early 1970s.

Legal troubles

Draper was convicted several times on wire fraud charges related to phone phreaking, and later on other charges. He remains a controversial figure in hacker culture.
