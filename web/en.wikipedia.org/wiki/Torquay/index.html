Torquay is a seaside town in Devon, England, on the north shore of Tor Bay, about 29 kilometres south of Exeter. With the neighbouring towns of Paignton and Brixham it forms the borough of Torbay, marketed as the "English Riviera" for its mild climate, palm trees, and sheltered bay. The town grew from a small fishing and agricultural settlement into a fashionable health and holiday resort in the nineteenth century.

History

The area has been inhabited since the Palaeolithic: Kents Cavern, a famous cave system in the town, yielded some of the oldest evidence of modern humans in Britain, including a jawbone dated to over 40,000 years ago. Torquay expanded rapidly after the Napoleonic Wars, when officers' families visited the fleet anchored in the bay, and again with the arrival of the railway in 1848. Grand Victorian villas, hotels, and gardens were built on its seven hills, and the town became associated with invalids and convalescents seeking its gentle winter climate.

Notable connections

Torquay is the birthplace of crime writer Agatha Christie (1890), whose life is marked by a local "Agatha Christie Mile"; her novel settings often draw on the Devon coast. The town's other cultural claim to fame is fictional: it was the setting of the BBC sitcom Fawlty Towers, in which John Cleese played the irascible hotelier Basil Fawlty — inspired by a real Torquay hotel owner encountered by the Monty Python team. Torre Abbey, founded in 1196, and its gardens and art gallery are among the town's surviving landmarks, alongside the harbour, marina, and the prehistoric collections of Torquay Museum.
