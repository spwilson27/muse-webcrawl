The Goldstone Deep Space Communications Complex (GDSCC), commonly called the Goldstone Observatory, is a satellite ground station located in the Mojave Desert near Barstow, California. Operated by NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, it is one of three stations of the Deep Space Network, the others being near Madrid, Spain, and Canberra, Australia — spaced roughly 120 degrees apart in longitude so that spacecraft can be tracked continuously as the Earth rotates.

History

The site was established in 1958 to support the Pioneer deep-space probes. Its 26-metre antenna tracked Pioneer 4 in 1959. Goldstone received the television signals of Apollo 11's first Moon walk relay in 1969 (via its Honeysuckle Creek and Parkes partners for the first minutes) and has supported nearly every NASA deep-space mission since, including Voyager, Galileo, Cassini, and the Mars rovers.

Antennas

The complex hosts several large dish antennas. The 70-metre (230 ft) DSS-14 dish, commissioned in 1966 as a 64-metre antenna and enlarged in 1988, is one of the largest and most sensitive steerable antennas in the world and is essential for communicating with the distant Voyager spacecraft. Multiple 34-metre antennas, including high-efficiency and beam-waveguide designs, support routine mission traffic, and the antennas are also used for radar astronomy of near-Earth asteroids — the Goldstone Solar System Radar — and for radio science.
