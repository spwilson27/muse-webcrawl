Red Rocks Amphitheatre (also known colloquially as Red Rocks) is an open-air amphitheatre in Morrison, Colorado, built into a red sandstone rock formation. It is owned and operated by the City and County of Denver and is considered one of the most celebrated concert venues in the world.

Geology and design

The venue is flanked by two enormous tilted sandstone slabs, Creation Rock and Ship Rock, part of the Fountain Formation, which provide natural acoustics. The amphitheatre sits at an elevation of about 6,450 feet (1,965 m) and seats 9,525 people.

History

The site was developed with the help of the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s and dedicated in 1941. It has hosted legendary performances by the Beatles (1964), U2 (whose 1983 concert was filmed for Under a Blood Red Sky), and many others, as well as the annual Easter sunrise service.
