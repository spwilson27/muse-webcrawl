Ariège (French pronunciation: [aʁjɛʒ]; Occitan: Arièja) is a department in southwestern France, in the Occitanie region, named after the river Ariège. Bordering Spain and Andorra along the Pyrenees, it is one of the least densely populated departments of metropolitan France.

History

The department was created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790 from the County of Foix and parts of Languedoc. The area is noted for its medieval Cathar castles such as Montségur, its prehistoric painted caves including Niaux, and its Romanesque churches.

Geography and economy

The south is mountainous, rising to peaks above 3,000 metres on the Spanish border, while the north opens into the foothills around Pamiers, the largest town. Tourism, agriculture, and winter sports are central to the economy.
