Safe as Milk is the debut studio album by American rock group Captain Beefheart and his Magic Band, released in June 1967 on Buddah Records. A blues- and R&B-rooted record with psychedelic and garage-rock elements, it is the most accessible album of Captain Beefheart's (Don Van Vliet's) career, predating the radical avant-garde turn of Trout Mask Replica (1969).

Background

Beefheart — so nicknamed, legend says, partly by his friend Frank Zappa — formed the Magic Band in California in 1964, playing raw covers of Howlin' Wolf and other bluesmen. After two A&M singles, the band signed with the newly formed Buddah label. The album was produced by future Warner Bros. president Richard Perry (in his production debut) and Bob Krasnow. The young Ry Cooder, then about 20, played guitar and arranged several tracks, including a celebrated slide-guitar reading of "Grown So Ugly", though he quit the band shortly before a scheduled appearance at the Monterey International Pop Festival, causing its cancellation and scuppering the album's commercial launch.

Music

The album blends gritty Chicago-style blues ("Zig Zag Wanderer", "Call on Me"), Beefheart originals showcasing his wide-ranging, Wolf-inspired roar, and eccentric touches such as the theremin on "Electricity" — a song pointing toward his later experimentalism. Other notable tracks include "Dropout Boogie", "I'm Glad" (a Soul ballad cover), "Abba Zaba" and "Yellow Brick Road".

Reception and legacy

Safe as Milk sold poorly on release and the band was dropped by Buddah, but it has since been reappraised as a classic of 1960s blues-rock and psychedelia. It influenced later artists including the White Stripes — Jack White has cited Beefheart as a formative influence — and its title is echoed in the Chemical Brothers' track and Morrissey's critical assessments. The album has been reissued repeatedly, including an expanded mono edition for Record Store Day.
