Copyright collective

A copyright collective (also known as a collective management organisation, collecting society, or performing rights organisation) is a non-governmental, typically non-profit organisation that licenses the copyrighted works of its members to users, collects royalties for those licenses, and distributes the royalties to the rights holders. Because it is impractical for individual creators to monitor every use of their works and for users to seek permission from every creator, collectives provide blanket licenses covering a large repertoire.

Functions

Collectives monitor usage of works on radio, television, streaming services, concerts and public venues; negotiate tariff rates; enforce rights; and deduct an administrative fee (often 10–20%) from collections before distributing the remainder to members. In the music industry, prominent collectives include ASCAP and BMI (United States), PRS for Music (United Kingdom), SACEM (France) and GEMA (Germany). For reprographic copying, organisations such as the Copyright Clearance Center license photocopying of texts.

Legal frameworks

In many civil-law jurisdictions, collective management is regulated by statute and some licenses are extended by law to non-members (extended collective licensing, common in Nordic countries). Collectives are coordinated internationally through CISAC, the International Confederation of Societies of Authors and Composers.
