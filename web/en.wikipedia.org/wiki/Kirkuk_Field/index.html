The Kirkuk Field is a giant oil field located near the city of Kirkuk in northern Iraq. It is one of the oldest and largest oil fields in the Middle East, and its discovery in 1927 established Iraq as a major petroleum producer. The field is operated by the state-owned North Oil Company.

History

Oil seeps around Kirkuk, most famously the eternal flames of Baba Gurgur, were known since antiquity and mentioned by ancient writers. The field itself was discovered on 15 October 1927, when a well drilled by the Turkish Petroleum Company at Baba Gurgur blew out, flowing uncontrolled for days before being capped. Commercial production began in 1934 with the completion of a pipeline to the Mediterranean ports of Haifa and Tripoli.

The field passed through the hands of the Iraq Petroleum Company before nationalisation in 1972 placed it under Iraqi state control. Production has repeatedly been disrupted by regional conflict, including the Iran–Iraq War, the Gulf War, sanctions, and the 2003 Iraq War. Disputes between the Iraqi federal government and the Kurdistan Regional Government over control of the field and its exports have been a persistent feature of post-2003 politics; federal forces retook the field from Kurdish Peshmerga control in October 2017.

Geology

The Kirkuk structure is a large asymmetric anticline roughly 100 kilometres long. The principal reservoirs are fractured Cretaceous and Tertiary limestones, notably the Shiranish and Kometan formations and the Main Limestone (Oligocene–Miocene). The field originally contained on the order of 25 billion barrels of oil in place, with recoverable reserves commonly estimated around 8.7 billion barrels, though estimates vary and the field has produced many billions of barrels over its long life.

Production

At its peak in the 1970s the field produced over one million barrels per day. Output in recent decades has typically been a few hundred thousand barrels per day, constrained by reservoir damage from water injection practices, ageing infrastructure, and political disputes over export routes, principally the pipeline to Ceyhan in Turkey.
