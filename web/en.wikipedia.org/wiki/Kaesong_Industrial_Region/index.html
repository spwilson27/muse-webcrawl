Kaesong Industrial Region

The Kaesong Industrial Region (KIR) was a special administrative industrial region of North Korea, centred on the Kaesong Industrial Complex, a collaborative economic development with South Korea. Located in the city of Kaesong, about 10 kilometres north of the Korean Demilitarized Zone, it was established in 2002 under the "Sunshine Policy" of inter-Korean engagement.

Operations

At its peak, the complex hosted more than 120 South Korean companies — mostly small and medium-sized firms in labour-intensive industries such as textiles, footwear, watches and kitchenware — employing over 53,000 North Korean workers supervised by several thousand South Korean managers who commuted daily across the border. Wages were paid in US dollars to a North Korean agency, which passed a portion on to workers.

Suspensions and closure

Operations were repeatedly interrupted by political tensions. North Korea temporarily withdrew its workers in 2013 during a period of heightened military tension. In February 2016, following a North Korean nuclear test and rocket launch, South Korea suspended operations entirely and withdrew its personnel; North Korea responded by expelling South Korean workers and freezing assets at the complex. The 2018 inter-Korean summits raised hopes of reopening, but the complex remained closed, and in 2020 North Korea demolished the inter-Korean liaison office located there.

Significance

The Kaesong complex was the most tangible symbol of inter-Korean economic cooperation and a significant source of hard currency for the North, as well as a model some hoped might encourage gradual economic opening in North Korea.
