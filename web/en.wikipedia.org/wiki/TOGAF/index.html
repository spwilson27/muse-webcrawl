TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework) is a widely used framework for enterprise architecture, maintained by The Open Group. First published in 1995 and based in part on the U.S. Department of Defense's Technical Architecture Framework for Information Management (TAFIM), TOGAF provides a method and vocabulary for designing, planning, implementing and governing an organisation's information technology architecture.

Architecture Development Method

The core of TOGAF is the Architecture Development Method (ADM), an iterative cycle of phases: a Preliminary phase, Architecture Vision (A), Business Architecture (B), Information Systems Architectures (C), Technology Architecture (D), Opportunities and Solutions (E), Migration Planning (F), Implementation Governance (G), and Architecture Change Management (H), with Requirements Management running continuously at the centre. The framework also defines an Architecture Content Framework and the Enterprise Continuum of reusable architecture assets.

Adoption and certification

TOGAF is used by a large share of major corporations and government bodies, and The Open Group runs a certification programme for individuals and training providers. The 10th edition (2022) restructured the standard into a fundamental content volume plus modular series guides covering topics such as agile and digital enterprise architecture. Critics describe TOGAF as heavyweight and document-centric if applied without tailoring.
