Vandalic War

The Vandalic War (533–534) was a war fought between the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire under Emperor Justinian I and the Vandal Kingdom of North Africa. The Byzantine general Belisarius defeated the Vandals at the battles of Ad Decimum and Tricamarum, annexed the kingdom, and restored North Africa to imperial rule — the first success of Justinian's renovatio imperii, the reconquest of the West.

Background

The Vandals, a Germanic people, had crossed to Africa in 429 and taken Carthage in 439, founding a kingdom that persecuted its Nicene Christian subjects under its Arian kings. Relations with Constantinople soured when King Hilderic, friendly to the empire, was deposed and imprisoned by his cousin Gelimer in 530. Justinian used this as a pretext for intervention, encouraged by religious grievances and by a favourable strategic moment: a revolt in Sardinia drew Gelimer's best forces overseas.

The campaign

In June 533 a Byzantine fleet of some 500 transports, escorted by warships and carrying about 15,000–17,000 troops under Belisarius, sailed from Constantinople and landed unopposed in Africa. Marching on Carthage, Belisarius won the Battle of Ad Decimum on 13 September 533 and entered the city two days later. Gelimer regrouped but was decisively beaten at Tricamarum in mid-December; his brother Tzazo, recalled from Sardinia, was killed. Gelimer fled to Mount Papua in Numidia, where he was blockaded through the winter and surrendered in March 534.

Aftermath

Gelimer was led in triumph through Constantinople and pensioned off in Galatia. North Africa was reorganised as the praetorian prefecture of Africa. Vandal resistance and Moorish revolts continued for years, and the conquered territory had to be defended by a costly line of fortresses, but the Vandal people disappear from history as a distinct group.

Sources

The principal account is the History of the Wars by Procopius, who accompanied Belisarius as his secretary and was an eyewitness to the campaign.
