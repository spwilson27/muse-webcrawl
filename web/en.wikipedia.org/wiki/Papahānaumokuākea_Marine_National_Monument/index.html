Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument

LocationNorthwestern Hawaiian Islands, Pacific Ocean
Area~1,508,870 km² (582,578 sq mi)
Established15 June 2006
Expanded26 August 2016
Governing bodiesNOAA, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, State of Hawaiʻi, Office of Hawaiian Affairs

Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument is a vast protected area of ocean and islands in the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands, administered by the United States. Covering roughly 1.5 million square kilometres after its 2016 expansion, it is one of the largest marine conservation areas in the world — larger than all U.S. national parks combined. The monument protects an extraordinary ecosystem of coral reefs, atolls, and deep waters home to more than 7,000 marine species, about a quarter of them found nowhere else.

Name

The name Papahānaumokuākea combines Papahānaumoku ("Papa"), the earth-mother figure in Native Hawaiian tradition, with Ākea (Wakea), the sky father; their union is said to have given birth to the Hawaiian islands. The name reflects the deep spiritual significance of the region in Native Hawaiian culture, where the islands to the northwest are regarded as a place of ancestral spirits.

History

President George W. Bush established the monument on 15 June 2006 under the Antiquities Act as the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands Marine National Monument; it was renamed Papahānaumokuākea in 2007. In 2010 UNESCO inscribed it as a mixed (natural and cultural) World Heritage Site. On 26 August 2016, President Barack Obama quadrupled its size by extending the boundary to the limit of the U.S. exclusive economic zone, making it at the time the largest protected area on Earth.

Ecology and culture

The monument shelters endangered Hawaiian monk seals, green sea turtles, nesting seabirds in the millions, and apex predators such as sharks in numbers rare elsewhere. Islands within it, including Midway Atoll and Kure Atoll, hold archaeological sites of Native Hawaiian settlement and shrines. Access is strictly limited by permit, and commercial fishing is prohibited.
