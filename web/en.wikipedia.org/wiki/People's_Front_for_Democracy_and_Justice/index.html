The People's Front for Democracy and Justice (PFDJ) is the founding, ruling and sole legal political party of Eritrea. It is led by Isaias Afwerki, who has served as President of Eritrea since independence in 1993.

History

The PFDJ was formed in February 1994 at the third congress of the Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF), the guerrilla movement that had fought the 30-year Eritrean War of Independence against Ethiopia. At the congress, the movement reconstituted itself as a political party and adopted its present name, announcing a transition from a liberation front to a broad-based political organisation.

The party's National Charter, adopted in 1994, envisioned a democratic, pluralistic Eritrea with a market-oriented economy tempered by social justice. However, the Eritrean–Ethiopian War of 1998–2000, the indefinite postponement of elections, the 2001 crackdown on dissenters (the "G-15") and the closure of the private press shifted the country toward a highly centralised one-party system. The 1997 constitution was ratified but never implemented.

Organisation

The PFDJ operates mass organisations for youth (the National Union of Eritrean Youth and Students), women (the National Union of Eritrean Women) and workers. Party structures extend throughout the military and the state administration, and the party is closely intertwined with the government and the Eritrean Defence Forces. National service, formally 18 months, has in practice been extended indefinitely for many conscripts, a policy critics — including UN investigators — have described as a system of mass forced labour.

Ideology

The party espouses Eritrean nationalism and self-reliance (hagerawi), rejecting dependence on foreign aid. Its programme combines elements of socialism, a developmental state and national unity across Eritrea's nine recognised ethnic groups and two major religions.
