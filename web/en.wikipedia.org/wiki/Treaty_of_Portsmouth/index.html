Treaty of Portsmouth

Signed5 September 1905
LocationPortsmouth Naval Shipyard, Kittery, Maine, United States
PartiesEmpire of Japan; Russian Empire
MediatorTheodore Roosevelt, President of the United States

The Treaty of Portsmouth was a peace treaty signed on 5 September 1905 that formally ended the Russo-Japanese War of 1904–1905. Negotiations took place at the Portsmouth Naval Shipyard in Kittery, Maine, and were mediated by United States President Theodore Roosevelt, who received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1906 for his role — the first American to win a Nobel Prize.

Background

The war between Japan and Russia over influence in Manchuria and Korea had produced costly battles, including the Siege of Port Arthur, the Battle of Mukden, and the decisive Japanese naval victory at the Battle of Tsushima in May 1905. Both sides were financially and militarily exhausted, and Roosevelt offered American mediation, which both belligerents accepted.

Negotiations

The Japanese delegation was led by Foreign Minister Komura Jutarō, while the Russian delegation was headed by former finance minister Sergei Witte. Talks opened in August 1905. Japan initially demanded an indemnity and the whole of Sakhalin Island; Russia categorically refused any indemnity. Roosevelt intervened to break deadlocks, and the final compromise gave Japan the southern half of Sakhalin (Karafuto) but no indemnity.

Terms

Under the treaty, Russia recognized Korea as within Japan's sphere of influence, transferred to Japan the leasehold of the Liaodong Peninsula (including Port Arthur) and the South Manchuria Railway, evacuated Manchuria, and ceded the southern half of Sakhalin south of the 50th parallel. Fishing rights in Siberian waters were also granted to Japan.

Aftermath

The absence of an indemnity provoked anger in Japan, where the public had expected one; riots in Tokyo (the Hibiya riots) forced the government to declare martial law. In Russia, the treaty was regarded as a diplomatic success for Witte given Russia's military defeats. The treaty confirmed Japan's emergence as a major world power and marked a turning point in the balance of power in East Asia.
