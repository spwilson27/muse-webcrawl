Gdansk is the anglicised spelling of Gdańsk, a city on the Baltic coast of northern Poland. With a population of around 486,000, it is the capital and largest city of the Pomeranian Voivodeship and Poland's principal seaport, forming the Tricity conurbation together with Gdynia and Sopot.

History

The settlement is first mentioned in 997 in connection with the mission of Saint Adalbert. Over the centuries Gdańsk passed between Polish, Teutonic Order, Prussian and German rule; as Danzig it flourished as a member of the Hanseatic League and one of Europe's great grain ports. After the First World War it became the Free City of Danzig under the League of Nations.

The first shots of the Second World War in Europe were fired at nearby Westerplatte on 1 September 1939. The largely destroyed city was rebuilt after 1945, when it became part of Poland, its old town painstakingly reconstructed.

In August 1980 strikes at the Lenin Shipyard gave birth to Solidarity, the first independent trade union in the Soviet bloc, led by Lech Wałęsa. See also the Gdańsk Agreement that ended the strike.

Economy and sights

The Port of Gdańsk is Poland's largest, handling container, bulk and energy cargo, supplemented by shipbuilding, petrochemical and IT industries. Landmarks include Long Market (Długi Targ), Neptune's Fountain, St Mary's Church — one of the world's largest brick churches — the medieval Crane (Żuraw), and the modern European Solidarity Centre and Museum of the Second World War.
