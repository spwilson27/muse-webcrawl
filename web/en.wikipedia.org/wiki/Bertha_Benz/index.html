Died5 May 1944, Ladenburg, Germany

Bertha Benz (née Cäcilie Bertha Ringer; 3 May 1849 – 5 May 1944) was a German automotive pioneer. She was the wife and business partner of Karl Benz, inventor of the Benz Patent-Motorwagen, and in August 1888 became the first person to drive an automobile over a long distance, proving the practicality of the motor car.

The 1888 journey

Bertha Benz had invested her dowry in her husband's ventures and strongly supported his work. Frustrated by the lack of public interest in the Patent-Motorwagen, she took the vehicle without Karl's knowledge in early August 1888 and, accompanied by her sons Eugen and Richard, drove from Mannheim to her mother's home in Pforzheim — a journey of about 106 kilometres (66 miles).

Along the way she solved practical problems that arose: she bought ligroin fuel at a pharmacy in Wiesloch (making it arguably the first filling station), cleared a blocked fuel line with a hat pin, used a garter to insulate an ignition wire, and had a blacksmith repair a drive chain. The publicity from the trip generated the first sales of the automobile, and her feedback led to improvements such as additional gears for hills and better brakes.

Legacy

The route she drove is now commemorated as the Bertha Benz Memorial Route. In 2016 she was inducted into the Automotive Hall of Fame.
