The Prime Minister of Bahrain is the head of government of the Kingdom of Bahrain. Under the Constitution, the prime minister is appointed directly by the king and need not be an elected member of the Council of Representatives. The prime minister chairs the Council of Ministers and directs the work of the ministries, while the king remains head of state and retains broad executive authority.

History

The office has been held by only two men. Khalifa bin Salman Al Khalifa, the uncle of the reigning king, became prime minister on 19 January 1970 — before Bahrain's independence from Britain in 1971 — and served continuously for more than fifty years until his death on 11 November 2020, making him the longest-serving prime minister in the world. He was a central, and to critics authoritarian, figure in Bahraini politics, overseeing the country's development into a regional financial centre and its response to the 2011 pro-democracy protests.

Upon his death, King Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa appointed Crown Prince Salman bin Hamad Al Khalifa as prime minister; Salman had served as deputy prime minister since 2013 and has led economic reform programmes including Bahrain's Economic Vision 2030 and post-pandemic fiscal consolidation.

List of prime ministers

1. Khalifa bin Salman Al Khalifa (1970–2020); 2. Salman bin Hamad Al Khalifa (2020–present).
