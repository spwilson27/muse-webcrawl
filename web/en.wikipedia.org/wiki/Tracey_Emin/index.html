Tracey Emin

Dame Tracey Karima Emin
Born: 3 July 1963, Croydon, London
Movement: Young British Artists
Notable works: Everyone I Have Ever Slept With 1963–1995; My Bed; neon texts

Dame Tracey Emin (born 1963) is an English artist known for autobiographical and confessional artwork in drawing, painting, sculpture, film, photography, neon text and sewn appliqué. She was a leading figure among the Young British Artists (YBAs) of the 1990s.

Work

Her tent Everyone I Have Ever Slept With 1963–1995 (1995) — appliquéd with 102 names — and the unmade, detritus-strewn installation My Bed (1998), shortlisted for the Turner Prize, made her famous and controversial; both the tent (destroyed in a 2004 warehouse fire) and My Bed (sold for £2.2m in 2014) became emblems of BritArt. Later work includes large neon statements in her own handwriting and, after treatment for bladder cancer in 2020, raw paintings of the body.

Honours

Emin represented Britain at the 2007 Venice Biennale, was elected a Royal Academician, was appointed CBE in 2013 and made a Dame in 2024. She has founded the Tracey Emin Foundation and artist residencies in Margate, the town where she grew up. See Young British Artists.
