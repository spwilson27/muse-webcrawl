Bill Bowerman

William Jay Bowerman (February 19, 1911 – December 24, 1999) was an American track and field coach and co-founder of Nike, Inc. Over his 24 years as head coach at the University of Oregon, the Ducks won four NCAA team championships and he coached 64 All-Americans and numerous Olympians, including Steve Prefontaine.

Life and career

Bowerman was born in Portland, Oregon, grew up in Fossil, and attended the University of Oregon, where he played football and ran track under coach Bill Hayward, whom he succeeded in 1949. He served in the U.S. Army's 10th Mountain Division during the Second World War. As a coach he was famed for innovative training methods and for handcrafting lightweight shoes for his runners.

Nike and the waffle sole

In 1964 Bowerman and his former athlete Phil Knight founded Blue Ribbon Sports to import Onitsuka Tiger shoes; the company became Nike in 1971. Bowerman famously created the "waffle" outsole by pouring urethane into a waffle iron, leading to the Waffle Trainer of 1974. He also popularised jogging in the United States after encountering the practice in New Zealand with Arthur Lydiard, co-authoring the 1967 bestseller Jogging.
