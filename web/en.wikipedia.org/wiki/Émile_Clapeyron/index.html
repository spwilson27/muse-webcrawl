Émile Clapeyron, engineer and thermodynamicist.

Benoît Paul Émile Clapeyron (26 January 1799 – 28 January 1864) was a French engineer and physicist, one of the founders of the science of thermodynamics.

Clapeyron studied at the École Polytechnique and the École des Mines in Paris. With his classmate Gabriel Lamé he spent a decade in Russia, teaching and helping to build the first Russian railways and bridges, before returning to France to work on railway engineering; he later designed locomotives and became professor of mechanics at the École des Ponts et Chaussées.

Contributions

His most important scientific work was the 1834 memoir Mémoire sur la puissance motrice de la chaleur ("Memoir on the motive power of heat"), which rescued the ideas of Sadi Carnot from obscurity, restated Carnot's principle, and gave the world the first graphic representation of the reversible cycle now known as the Carnot cycle. In the same paper he combined the gas laws of Boyle, Charles and Avogadro into a single statement, pV = nRT — the ideal gas law in its modern form, known as the Clapeyron equation. From his analysis of phase equilibrium came the relation later generalised by Rudolf Clausius as the Clausius–Clapeyron equation, governing the slope of coexistence curves between phases. In structural engineering he is remembered for the theorem of three moments for continuous beams. Clapeyron is one of the 72 names inscribed on the Eiffel Tower.
