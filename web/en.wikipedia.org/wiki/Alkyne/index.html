Alkyne

In organic chemistry, an alkyne is an unsaturated hydrocarbon containing at least one carbon–carbon triple bond. The simplest alkyne is acetylene (ethyne), C2H2. Alkynes have the general formula CnH2n−2 and are traditionally called acetylenes.

Structure

The triple bond consists of one σ and two π bonds; the carbons are sp-hybridised with 180° bond angles, making alkynes linear around the bond. The C≡C bond is shorter (~120 pm) and stronger than double or single bonds.

Properties and reactions

Terminal alkynes are weakly acidic (pKa ≈ 25) and form metal acetylides with strong bases or ammoniacal silver/copper reagents. Characteristic reactions include hydrogenation (to alkenes with Lindlar's catalyst, or fully to alkanes), hydrohalogenation and hydration (Markovnikov addition giving ketones), halogenation, and the copper-catalysed azide–alkyne cycloaddition ("click chemistry"), recognised by the 2022 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

Uses and occurrence

Acetylene is used in oxyacetylene welding and as a chemical feedstock. Alkynes are rare in nature but occur in some fungal and plant metabolites and in drugs such as the contraceptive norethisterone.
