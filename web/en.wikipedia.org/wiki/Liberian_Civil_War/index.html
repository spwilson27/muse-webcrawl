The Liberian Civil War refers to two successive conflicts that devastated Liberia between 1989 and 2003. Together they killed an estimated 250,000 people, displaced much of the population, collapsed the state and economy, and drew in regional peacekeepers and, at the end, the United Nations.

Background

Liberia's politics had long been dominated by Americo-Liberian settlers over the indigenous majority. In 1980 Master Sergeant Samuel Doe seized power in a coup; his regime became increasingly repressive and ethnically divisive, setting the stage for armed revolt.

First Civil War (1989–1997)

On 24 December 1989 Charles Taylor's National Patriotic Front of Liberia (NPFL) invaded from Côte d'Ivoire. The war fragmented into factional fighting; West African ECOMOG peacekeepers intervened, Doe was captured and killed in 1990, and Monrovia was repeatedly besieged. After several failed accords, elections in 1997 — held amid widespread fear of renewed war — made Taylor president.

Second Civil War (1999–2003)

Rebel movements LURD and later MODEL, backed by neighbouring states, fought Taylor's government from 1999. In 2003 rebels besieged Monrovia; Taylor, indicted by the Special Court for Sierra Leone for his role in that country's war, resigned and went into exile in Nigeria in August 2003. The Accra Comprehensive Peace Agreement ended the war, followed by the UN Mission in Liberia (UNMIL) and elections in 2005 won by Ellen Johnson Sirleaf. Taylor was later convicted in The Hague of war crimes and crimes against humanity for aiding rebels in Sierra Leone.
