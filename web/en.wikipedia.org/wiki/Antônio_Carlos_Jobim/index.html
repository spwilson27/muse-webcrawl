Antônio Carlos Jobim

Antônio Carlos Brasileiro de Almeida Jobim (25 January 1927 – 8 December 1994), also known as Tom Jobim, was a Brazilian composer, pianist, guitarist, songwriter, arranger, and singer. Considered the father of bossa nova, he is regarded as one of the great exponents of Brazilian music and one of the most influential composers of the 20th century.

Career

Jobim rose to prominence with music for the play Orfeu da Conceição (1956) and the film Black Orpheus (1959). With lyricist Vinícius de Moraes and singer/guitarist João Gilberto he launched bossa nova, writing classics such as "Garota de Ipanema" ("The Girl from Ipanema"), "Desafinado", "Corcovado" ("Quiet Nights"), "Chega de Saudade", "Águas de Março" ("Waters of March") and "Wave".

International success

His collaborations with Stan Getz, Charlie Byrd, Astrud Gilberto and Frank Sinatra (the 1967 album Francis Albert Sinatra & Antônio Carlos Jobim) carried bossa nova worldwide. "The Girl from Ipanema" became one of the most recorded songs in history. Rio de Janeiro's international airport is named in his honour.
