Ems Ukaz

Ems Ukaz

Date: 18 (30) May 1876

Signed at: Bad Ems, German Empire

Signed by: Alexander II of Russia

Purpose: Restriction of the Ukrainian language in print, education, theatre, and music

Preceded by: Valuev Circular (1863)

The Ems Ukaz (Russian: Эмский указ; Ukrainian: Емський указ) was a secret decree issued by Tsar Alexander II on 18 May 1876 at Bad Ems, Germany, that banned virtually all publication and public use of the Ukrainian language in the Russian Empire. It is regarded as a landmark of imperial Russification policy and a formative grievance of the Ukrainian national movement.

Background

The ukaz followed the Valuev Circular of 1863, in which Interior Minister Pyotr Valuev had asserted that "a separate Little Russian language never existed, does not exist, and cannot exist", while restricting Ukrainian religious and educational publishing. In the 1870s the Kyiv branch of the Imperial Geographical Society and the hromada network of Ukrainian cultural activists — including the historian Mykhailo Drahomanov — alarmed officials, who feared Ukrainian cultural activity was becoming a separatist political movement encouraged from Austrian Galicia.

Provisions

The decree prohibited: the import of Ukrainian-language books from abroad; the publication of original works and translations in Ukrainian, except historical documents and (with restrictions and in Russian orthography) belles-lettres; Ukrainian-language theatrical performances and public readings; the printing of Ukrainian song lyrics with sheet music; and teaching in Ukrainian in elementary schools. It also ordered the closure of the Kyiv newspaper Kievskii Telegraf, the removal of Ukrainophile officials, and the exile of activists including Drahomanov and Mykola Ziber.

Effects and legacy

The ukaz drove Ukrainian publishing abroad — above all to Lviv in Austrian-ruled Galicia, which became the centre of Ukrainian print culture — and pushed activists toward political organisation. Its restrictions were formally relaxed after the Revolution of 1905 but were never fully repudiated by the empire. Together with the Valuev Circular, the Ems Ukaz is frequently cited in Ukrainian history as evidence of a systematic policy to suppress Ukrainian national identity.
