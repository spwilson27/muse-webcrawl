Scavenger

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A scavenger is an animal that feeds on dead organisms — carrion, and also decaying plant matter and waste — rather than (or as well as) hunting live prey. Scavengers are nature's clean-up crew: vultures, hyenas, raccoons, burying beetles and many other species remove carcasses that would otherwise spread disease.

Adaptations and importance

Obligate scavengers such as vultures soar for hours searching by sight and smell, and tolerate stomach acid strong enough to destroy anthrax and botulinum toxins. Facultative scavengers — most predators included — take carrion when available. The collapse of vulture populations in South Asia in the 1990s–2000s, caused by the veterinary drug diclofenac in cattle carcasses, led to surges in feral dogs, rats and rabies — a landmark demonstration of scavengers' public-health value.

Ecology

Scavengers link food webs across ecosystems (salmon carcasses feed forests; whale falls feed deep-sea communities for decades) and compete with microbes and each other for a rich but unpredictable resource.
