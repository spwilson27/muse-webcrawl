Mambo (dance)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mambo is a Latin dance of Cuban origin that corresponds to mambo music. The dance emerged in the 1940s when the Cuban dance and music form of the same name became popularised throughout the Americas, from Havana to Mexico City and New York.

History

The dance developed alongside the music created by Orestes López, Dámaso Pérez Prado and others, blending danzón with swing and jazz elements. In New York, the Palladium Ballroom became the centre of mambo dancing in the late 1940s and 1950s, where bandleaders such as Tito Puente, Tito Rodríguez and Machito performed. The dance is characterised by sharp movements, turns and breaks on the second beat.

Modern form

Ballroom mambo was codified into the syllabus of international Latin dance competitions; its basic pattern is related to salsa, particularly the "on 2" New York style. Mambo remains taught worldwide as part of Latin American ballroom dancing.
