Malcolm Campbell

Sir Malcolm Campbell MBE (11 March 1885 – 31 December 1948) was a British motor racing driver and motoring journalist. He gained the world speed record on land and on water at various times during the 1920s and 1930s using vehicles called Blue Bird. His son, Donald Campbell, continued the family tradition of speed records.

Career

Campbell made a fortune as a Lloyd's insurance underwriter and in the diamond trade, served in the First World War, and raced motorbikes and cars at Brooklands. He named his cars Blue Bird after the play The Blue Bird by Maurice Maeterlinck. Beginning at Pendine Sands in Wales in 1924 (146 mph), he broke the land speed record nine times, raising it through attempts at Pendine and Daytona Beach with successive Napier- and Rolls-Royce-powered Blue Birds. On 3 September 1935 at the Bonneville Salt Flats he became the first person to drive over 300 mph, averaging 301.13 mph in the Campbell-Railton Blue Bird with a Rolls-Royce R engine.

Water records and later life

Turning to water, Campbell set the world water speed record four times in his jet boat Blue Bird K4, reaching 141.74 mph on Coniston Water in 1939. He served again in the Second World War in the military police and was knighted in 1931 for his record achievements. He died at Reigate in 1948 after a series of strokes; his son Donald went on to hold both land and water records simultaneously in 1964, before being killed on Coniston Water in 1967 in the successor tradition that led eventually to jets such as Thrust SSC on land.
