Old Hall Manuscript

The Old Hall Manuscript (British Library, Additional MS 57950) is the largest, most complete, and most important source of English sacred music of the late 14th and early 15th centuries. It contains compositions for the Mass Ordinary and motets by composers associated with the English royal court and the Chapel Royal, including Leonel Power and John Dunstaple, the latter widely regarded as one of the most influential composers of his age.

History and description

The manuscript was compiled in stages, probably between about 1410 and 1420, with contributions by several scribes. Its repertory — more than 140 pieces — spans older styles in mensural notation and newer works, illustrating the transition in English polyphony that would shape the contenance angloise admired across Europe. The manuscript takes its name from Old Hall, near Ware in Hertfordshire, where it was kept at St Edmund's College after its rediscovery in the 19th century; it was acquired by the British Museum in 1973.

Composers represented

Alongside Power and Dunstaple, the manuscript includes works by Pycard, Byttering, Cooke, and other chapel composers, as well as a piece attributed to "Roy Henry" — possibly King Henry IV or Henry V. Settings of the Gloria and Credo predominate, reflecting the manuscript's use in a royal chapel context.
