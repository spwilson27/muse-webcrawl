Mound springs

Mound springs
LocationGreat Artesian Basin, South Australia (also elsewhere in Australia)
TypeArtesian springs with mineral mounds
Notable sitesDalhousie Springs, Wabma Kadarbu (Blanche Cup, The Bubbler)

Mound springs are natural outlets of the Great Artesian Basin in arid Australia where groundwater reaches the surface through faults and fractures. Over thousands of years, evaporation of the discharged water deposits salts and carbonates, building low conical mounds around the spring vents — some reaching several metres high — from which the features take their name.

Ecology and significance

In the desert landscape the permanent spring wetlands are ecological refuges, supporting endemic fish (such as the Dalhousie goby and desert goby), hydrobiid snails, isopods, arachnids and the dense stands of bore sedge and tea-tree around larger pools. Many species occur at only one or a few springs. The springs are also of deep cultural significance to Aboriginal peoples, featuring in songlines and providing water along traditional routes; notable complexes include Witjira-Dalhousie Springs and Wabma Kadarbu Mound Springs Conservation Park near Lake Eyre South.

Threats

Since the late nineteenth century, thousands of free-flowing pastoral bores have lowered aquifer pressure, reducing or extinguishing flows at many springs. Additional pressure comes from extractive industries drawing on the basin, tourism impacts, and invasive species such as date palms and exotic fish. Bore capping and rehabilitation programs, together with heritage listing of major spring groups, aim to restore flows and protect the communities dependent on them.
