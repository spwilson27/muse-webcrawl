Dura mater

The dura mater is a thick membrane made of dense irregular connective tissue that surrounds the brain and spinal cord. It is the outermost of the three layers of membrane called the meninges that protect the central nervous system. The other two meningeal layers are the arachnoid mater and the pia mater. The name, from Latin "tough mother", reflects a medieval Arabic usage referring to its protective role.

Structure

The cranial dura consists of two layers: an outer periosteal layer attached to the skull and an inner meningeal layer. Where they separate, the dura encloses the dural venous sinuses that drain blood from the brain. Inward folds of dura — the falx cerebri, tentorium cerebelli, falx cerebelli and diaphragma sellae — partition the cranial cavity and support parts of the brain. The spinal dura forms a tube, the thecal sac, around the spinal cord.

Clinical significance

Bleeding between the skull and dura produces an epidural hematoma, while bleeding beneath the dura produces a subdural hematoma, both dangerous after head injury. Spinal anaesthesia and lumbar puncture involve the dural sac; a post-dural-puncture headache can follow leakage of cerebrospinal fluid.
