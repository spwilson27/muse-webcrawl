Kangaroo meat

Source animalsRed kangaroo, eastern and western grey kangaroos, wallaroo
OriginAustralia
IndustryRegulated commercial wild harvest; exports to Europe, Asia and North America
CharacteristicsVery lean; high in protein and conjugated linoleic acid

Kangaroo meat is meat from kangaroos harvested in Australia. It has been eaten by Aboriginal Australians for tens of thousands of years and is today produced commercially from abundant species — chiefly the red kangaroo and the eastern and western grey kangaroos — under state management plans and quotas. Most production is exported, while within Australia it is sold in supermarkets, served in restaurants and used in pet food.

Nutrition

Kangaroo is a very lean red meat, typically around 1–2% fat, rich in protein, iron, zinc, B vitamins and conjugated linoleic acid. Because of its low fat content it is usually cooked rare or medium-rare to avoid toughness; typical cuts include fillets, steaks, minced ("kanga banga" sausages) and roasts.

Industry and debate

The commercial harvest is regulated to a fraction of surveyed kangaroo populations and is defended as a sustainable use of a native animal well adapted to the Australian environment, with a lower methane and land footprint than sheep or cattle. It is opposed by some animal-welfare campaigners, and import restrictions have at times been imposed by overseas markets. Promoters in 2008 coined the term "kangatarian" for a diet that excludes all meat except kangaroo on environmental grounds.
