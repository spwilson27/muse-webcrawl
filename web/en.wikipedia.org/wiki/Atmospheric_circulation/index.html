Atmospheric circulation

Atmospheric circulation
CellsHadley, Ferrel, polar
Driven byUnequal solar heating, Earth's rotation

Atmospheric circulation is the large-scale movement of air by which heat is distributed around the Earth. Together with ocean circulation, it redistributes energy from the tropics toward the poles, shaping the planet's climate zones, prevailing winds and major regions of desert and rainfall.

The three-cell model

  • Hadley cell – warm air rises near the equator (the Intertropical Convergence Zone), flows poleward aloft, sinks in the subtropics around 30° latitude, producing high pressure and the great deserts, and returns as the trade winds.
  • Ferrel cell – an indirect mid-latitude circulation associated with the prevailing westerlies and travelling storm systems.
  • Polar cell – cold air sinks over the poles and flows equatorward at the surface as polar easterlies.

Effects of rotation

The Coriolis effect deflects moving air, breaking what would be a single circulation loop per hemisphere into the three-cell pattern and producing the jet streams at cell boundaries. On smaller scales, circulation includes monsoons, land and sea breezes, and the Walker circulation across the Pacific, whose disruption produces El Niño events.
