The Holocaust in Hungary
Date1941–1945 (main deportations May–July 1944)
LocationKingdom of Hungary
PerpetratorsNazi Germany (SS, Eichmann's Sondereinsatzkommando), Hungarian authorities and gendarmerie, Arrow Cross Party
VictimsApprox. 564,000 Hungarian Jews murdered overall; some 437,000 deported in spring 1944

The Holocaust in Hungary refers to the persecution and murder of Jews in the Kingdom of Hungary during the Second World War. Although Hungary was an ally of Nazi Germany and enacted its own anti-Jewish laws from 1938, its government under Regent Miklós Horthy resisted German demands for deportation until Germany occupied the country on 19 March 1944 (Operation Margarethe).

Deportations of 1944

Within weeks, Adolf Eichmann's small SS detachment, working with the Hungarian interior ministry and gendarmerie, organised the ghettoisation and deportation of provincial Jewry. Between 15 May and 9 July 1944, some 437,000 Jews were deported in 147 trains, the great majority to Auschwitz-Birkenau, where most were murdered on arrival. The speed and scale of the operation made it one of the fastest phases of the Holocaust. Under international pressure and with the Red Army advancing, Horthy halted the deportations in July, temporarily sparing Budapest's Jews.

Arrow Cross terror and rescue

After Horthy attempted an armistice, Germany installed the Arrow Cross regime of Ferenc Szálasi in October 1944. Arrow Cross militia murdered thousands of Budapest Jews, shooting many into the Danube — commemorated today by the Shoes on the Danube Bank memorial — and drove tens of thousands on death marches toward Austria. Rescue efforts by diplomats including Raoul Wallenberg, Carl Lutz and Angelo Rotta, who issued protective passes and maintained safe houses, saved tens of thousands. In all, roughly 564,000 Hungarian Jews perished, including those killed in labour service and earlier massacres. The definitive scholarly account is Randolph L. Braham's The Politics of Genocide.
