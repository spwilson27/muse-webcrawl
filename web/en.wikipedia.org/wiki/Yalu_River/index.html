The Yalu River is a river on the border between China and North Korea. Rising on the slopes of Mount Paektu in the Changbai Mountains, it flows southwest for roughly 790 kilometres before emptying into Korea Bay, between the Chinese city of Dandong and the North Korean city of Sinuiju. Its name is said to derive from a Manchu phrase likening its colour to the green of a duck's neck.

History

The river basin was home to the ancient kingdom of Goguryeo, and the Yalu has repeatedly been a strategic frontier. Major battles were fought along it during the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95), including a significant naval engagement at its mouth, and during the Russo-Japanese War (1904–05). In November 1950, during the Korean War, Chinese forces crossed the Yalu in great numbers to intervene against United Nations troops advancing toward the border — one of the turning points of that conflict.

Geography and use

The river is heavily developed for hydroelectric power, notably at the Sup'ung Dam, for decades one of the largest in Asia, whose output is shared by the two countries. Islands in the river's lower course are assigned by treaty largely according to the channel nearest them, and the border zone remains politically sensitive; the Sino-Korean Friendship Bridge at Dandong carries most of the formal trade between the two states.
