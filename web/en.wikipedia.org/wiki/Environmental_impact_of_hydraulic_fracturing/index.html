Environmental impact of hydraulic fracturing

The environmental impact of hydraulic fracturing (fracking) — the technique of injecting fluid at high pressure to fracture rock and release oil and natural gas — is a subject of scientific study and public controversy. Concerns include contamination of drinking water, methane emissions and climate effects, air pollution, induced seismicity, water consumption, and the handling of large volumes of chemical-laden wastewater.

Water contamination

Studies have documented cases of elevated methane and other gases in water wells near drilling sites, in some instances attributed to faulty well casing or gas migration rather than the fracturing process itself. Spills of fracturing fluid and flowback water at the surface are a recognised contamination pathway. The US Environmental Protection Agency's multi-year assessment (finalised 2016) concluded that hydraulic fracturing activities can affect drinking water resources under some circumstances, identifying vulnerabilities at each stage of the water cycle.

Air and climate

Fugitive methane leaks from wells, pipelines and processing equipment reduce the climate advantage of natural gas over coal, since methane is a potent greenhouse gas. Local air pollutants include volatile organic compounds and nitrogen oxides contributing to ozone formation near dense drilling areas.

Seismicity and waste

Injection of wastewater into deep disposal wells has been linked to significant induced earthquakes, notably in Oklahoma, where regulators responded with volume restrictions. The fracturing itself only rarely causes felt earthquakes. Waste management, land disturbance, truck traffic and noise are further local impacts.

Regulation

Responses vary: several jurisdictions, including France and New York State, have banned fracking, while others regulate it through disclosure of chemicals, setback distances and well-integrity rules. The industry maintains that properly conducted operations pose low risk and that shale gas has displaced more carbon-intensive coal generation.
