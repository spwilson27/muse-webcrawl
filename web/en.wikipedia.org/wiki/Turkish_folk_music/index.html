Turkish folk music (Turkish: Türk halk müziği) is the traditional music of the Turkish people of Anatolia and the Balkans. Deeply rooted in the culture of nomadic Turkic peoples who migrated from Central Asia, it blends Central Asian, Middle Eastern, and Balkan influences. Its central instrument is the long-necked lute bağlama, and its repertoire of songs (türkü) treats themes of love, loss, heroism, nature, and daily village life.

Characteristics

Turkish folk music uses the makam modal system in a simpler folk form and rich asymmetric rhythms (aksak), such as 9/8, alongside regular metres. Songs are performed solo or in groups, often accompanied by dance. Narrative and epic songs were historically performed by travelling minstrels called âşıks (aşıks), such as the legendary Köroğlu and the sixteenth-century poet-musician Pir Sultan Abdal, who composed and sang their verse to the bağlama.

Regional styles

Repertoires vary strongly by region: the Black Sea coast is known for the fast fiddle (kemençe) and the horon dance; Central Anatolia for the bozlak long-song style associated with performers such as Neşet Ertaş; the Aegean region for zeybek dances in 9/8; and southeastern Anatolia for music sharing features with Arab and Kurdish traditions. Radio broadcasts by TRT from the 1930s onward, and collectors such as Muzaffer Sarısözen, standardised and archived a national folk repertoire.
