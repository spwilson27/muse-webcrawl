England and Wales Cricket Board

The England and Wales Cricket Board (ECB) is the national governing body of cricket in England and Wales. It was formed on 1 January 1997, taking over the responsibilities of the Test and County Cricket Board, the National Cricket Association and the Cricket Council.

Responsibilities

The ECB oversees all levels of the game, from the England men's and women's national teams to county, minor counties, club and schools cricket. It operates from Lord's Cricket Ground in London and works through 38 county cricket boards and the 18 first-class counties, as well as Cricket Wales and the Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC), which retains responsibility for the Laws of Cricket.

Competitions

Domestic competitions administered under the ECB umbrella include the County Championship, the One-Day Cup, the T20 Blast and, from 2021, The Hundred — a 100-ball franchise competition for men's and women's city-based teams. England's men won the Cricket World Cup in 2019, hosted by England and Wales, and the women's team won their World Cup in 2017.

Governance

The ECB is governed by a board of directors and funded chiefly by broadcast rights, sponsorship and central revenues, which it distributes to the professional and recreational game. It has run programmes to broaden participation, including Chance to Shine in state schools and All Stars Cricket for young children.
