Zazen

Zazen (Japanese: 坐禅, "seated meditation"; Chinese: zuòchán) is the central meditative practice of Zen Buddhism. Practised seated upright on a cushion (zafu), usually facing a wall, zazen aims at calming the body and mind and realising one's true nature. In the Sōtō school it takes the form of shikantaza ("just sitting"), objectless awareness taught by Dōgen; in the Rinzai school it is paired with kōan study. The term is also used generally for seated meditation in Japanese Buddhism.

Practice

The practitioner sits on a round zafu placed on a flat zabuton mat, legs crossed in full or half lotus (or kneeling in seiza on a bench), spine straight, chin tucked, hands resting in the cosmic mudra—left hand on right, thumbs lightly touching—and eyes half-open, cast downward at about 45 degrees. Sessions typically last 30–50 minutes, alternated with walking meditation (kinhin). Beginners often count breaths; in Rinzai training a teacher assigns a kōan, while Sōtō practice, following Dōgen's Fukanzazengi ("Universally Recommended Instructions for Zazen"), emphasises posture itself as the expression of enlightenment—"practice and realisation are one". A monitor may apply the kyōsaku, a flat "encouragement stick", to a drowsy student's shoulders.

Intensive retreats

Zazen is intensified during sesshin, retreats of several days in which practitioners sit for many periods daily, eat formal oryoki meals, and attend talks (teishō) and interviews with the teacher (dokusan/sanzen).
