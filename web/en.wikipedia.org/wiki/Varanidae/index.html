The Varanidae are a family of lizards in the superfamily Varanoidea. The family is best known for the monitor lizards of the genus Varanus, which is the only living genus, and also includes several extinct forms such as the giant Australian megalania (Varanus priscus) and the marine mosasaur relatives are sometimes placed nearby in classification, though mosasaurs form their own family. Varanids are native to Africa, Asia, and Oceania, and one species, the Nile monitor, has become established in Florida as an invasive species.

Characteristics

Varanids range in size from the tiny short-tailed pygmy monitor, about 20 cm long, to the Komodo dragon, which exceeds 3 metres and is the largest living lizard. They are characterized by elongated necks, powerful tails and claws, well-developed limbs, and forked tongues used to sample the air for scent particles, which are delivered to the Jacobson's organ. Monitors are generally carnivorous or omnivorous, active foragers with high metabolic rates for reptiles, and some species have been shown to possess venom glands in the lower jaw.

Evolution

The varanid lineage originated in Asia during the Late Cretaceous, and the family later dispersed to Africa, Australia, and the Pacific. Extinct varanids include the terrestrial Saniwa of Eocene North America and the giant megalania of Pleistocene Australia, which may have reached lengths of over 5 metres. In Australia, where placental carnivores were historically absent, monitor lizards (known locally as goannas) became the dominant terrestrial reptilian predators.

Intelligence and behaviour

Monitor lizards are regarded among the most intelligent of lizards; some species can count small numbers of items, learn routines in captivity, and cooperate in foraging. Many species are semi-aquatic or arboreal, while others are terrestrial burrowers.
