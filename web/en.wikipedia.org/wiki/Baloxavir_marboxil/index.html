Baloxavir marboxil, sold under the brand name Xofluza, is an antiviral medication used to treat influenza A and influenza B. Developed by the Japanese company Shionogi, it was approved in Japan and the United States in 2018. Unlike older neuraminidase inhibitors such as oseltamivir, which require twice-daily dosing for five days, baloxavir is given as a single oral dose.

Mechanism of action

Baloxavir marboxil is a prodrug converted in the body to baloxavir acid, which inhibits the cap-dependent endonuclease activity of the influenza virus polymerase complex. This blocks the virus's "cap-snatching" mechanism — stealing capped primers from host mRNA — and thereby prevents viral messenger RNA synthesis and replication at an early stage of infection.

Clinical use

Trials showed baloxavir shortened the duration of flu symptoms by about a day compared with placebo, comparable to oseltamivir, and reduced viral shedding more rapidly. It is approved for treatment of uncomplicated influenza in patients 12 years and older (later extended to younger children) and for post-exposure prophylaxis. Concerns include the emergence of reduced-susceptibility variants (notably the PA/I38T substitution) during treatment, seen more often in children.

Side effects

Common adverse effects are generally mild and include diarrhoea, bronchitis, nausea and headache. Absorption is reduced by polyvalent cations, so it should not be taken with dairy products, calcium-fortified drinks or certain antacids and supplements.
