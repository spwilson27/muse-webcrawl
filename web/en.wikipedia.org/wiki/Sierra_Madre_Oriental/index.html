Sierra Madre Oriental

The Sierra Madre Oriental is a mountain range in northeastern Mexico, extending about 1,350 kilometres (840 miles) from the Rio Grande near the Texas border south through the states of Coahuila, Nuevo León, Tamaulipas, San Luis Potosí, Hidalgo, and Puebla, where it joins the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt.

Geology and geography

The range consists mainly of folded and faulted sedimentary rocks — limestones, shales, and sandstones of Cretaceous age — uplifted during the Laramide orogeny. Its highest peak is Cerro el Potosí, at about 3,721 metres (12,208 ft). The mountains form the eastern rim of the Mexican Plateau and drop steeply toward the Gulf Coastal Plain.

Ecology

The Sierra Madre Oriental pine–oak forests are a recognised ecoregion of high biodiversity, with cloud forests on the wetter eastern slopes sheltering many endemic species. The range is an important watershed for rivers flowing to the Gulf of Mexico, including the Conchos, San Fernando, and Pánuco systems. Protected areas include the Cumbres de Monterrey National Park and the El Cielo Biosphere Reserve.
