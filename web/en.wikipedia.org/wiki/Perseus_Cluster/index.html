Perseus Cluster

The Perseus Cluster (Abell 426) is a cluster of galaxies in the constellation Perseus, about 240 million light-years from Earth. It is one of the most massive objects in the known universe, containing thousands of galaxies immersed in an enormous cloud of hot X-ray-emitting gas, and has a total mass of roughly 1015 solar masses.

Properties

The cluster's brightest member is the giant elliptical galaxy NGC 1275 (Perseus A), a strong radio and X-ray source at the cluster centre. The intracluster gas reaches temperatures of tens of millions of degrees. Because radiative cooling should cause this gas to condense far faster than is observed, the Perseus Cluster is a key case in the "cooling flow problem," now largely explained by heating from the central active galactic nucleus, whose jets inflate bubbles in the gas.

The Perseus "note"

In 2003, astronomers using the Chandra X-ray Observatory identified pressure waves — ripples in the hot gas generated by the central black hole — corresponding to a pitch of B-flat about 57 octaves below middle C, the deepest "note" ever detected from an object in the universe. In 2017 the Hitomi satellite made landmark measurements of the gas's turbulence, finding it surprisingly quiescent.
