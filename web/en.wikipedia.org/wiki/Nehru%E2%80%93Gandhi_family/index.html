The Nehru–Gandhi family is a prominent Indian political family that has occupied a central place in the politics of India since the independence movement. Members of the family have served as Prime Minister of India for a total of more than three decades and have long led the Indian National Congress party. The family is unrelated to Mahatma Gandhi; the surname entered the family when Feroze Gandhi married Indira Nehru.

Generations

The family's political prominence began with Motilal Nehru (1861–1931), a wealthy lawyer and Congress president. His son Jawaharlal Nehru became India's first prime minister (1947–1964). Nehru's daughter Indira Gandhi served as prime minister from 1966 to 1977 and from 1980 until her assassination in 1984. Her son Rajiv Gandhi succeeded her and served until 1989; he was assassinated in 1991. Rajiv's widow Sonia Gandhi led the Congress party from 1998 to 2017 and again from 2019 to 2022, and their son Rahul Gandhi and daughter Priyanka Gandhi have been prominent party figures. Indira's younger son Sanjay Gandhi was an influential figure during the Emergency of 1975–77.

Criticism and influence

The family's dominance has been both a unifying force for the Congress party and a focus of criticism about dynastic politics in Indian democracy. Family members have headed the governments that shaped much of modern India's political and economic development.
