View from nowhere

The view from nowhere is a term associated with philosopher Thomas Nagel's 1986 book of the same name, describing an ideally objective perspective that abstracts entirely from any particular point of view, as science aspires to do.

In philosophy

Nagel contrasted subjective experience — what it is like to be a particular being — with the objective standpoint, arguing neither can fully reduce to the other. The tension structures debates in philosophy of mind, ethics and epistemology.

In journalism

Media critic Jay Rosen popularised the phrase as a critique of journalistic false balance: presenting contested claims as symmetrically disputed from a pose of detached neutrality, which can conceal where evidence actually lies and undermine audience trust. Rosen contrasts it with transparency about a reporter's standpoint — "the view from somewhere".
