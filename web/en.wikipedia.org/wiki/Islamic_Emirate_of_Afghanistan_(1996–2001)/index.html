Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan (1996–2001)

Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan

Capital: Kabul (de facto); Kandahar (seat of the Supreme Leader)

Supreme Leader: Mullah Mohammed Omar

Established: 27 September 1996

Disestablished: 17 December 2001

Government: Unitary totalitarian Islamic emirate

Recognition: Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, United Arab Emirates

The Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan was the state established by the Taliban after its capture of Kabul on 27 September 1996. It governed most of Afghanistan until it was overthrown by the United States-led invasion and Northern Alliance forces in late 2001, following the September 11 attacks. The Taliban re-established the Islamic Emirate after retaking Kabul in August 2021.

History

The Taliban (Pashto for "students") emerged in Kandahar in 1994 from religious schools amid the civil war that followed the collapse of the communist government in 1992. Promising to end warlord corruption and restore order, and backed by Pakistan, the movement captured Herat in 1995 and Kabul in 1996, executing former president Mohammad Najibullah. By 1998 the Taliban controlled some 90% of the country; the remaining northeast was held by the Northern Alliance under Ahmad Shah Massoud, who was assassinated by al-Qaeda suicide bombers on 9 September 2001.

Government and policies

The emirate was headed by Mullah Mohammed Omar, who took the title Amir al-Mu'minin (Commander of the Faithful) and ruled through a Supreme Council based in Kandahar. The regime imposed a strict interpretation of Sharia derived from the Deobandi tradition combined with Pashtunwali customs. The Ministry for the Promotion of Virtue and Prevention of Vice enforced rules through religious police.

Women were barred from most employment and from education beyond primary level, were required to wear the burqa in public, and needed a male guardian (mahram) to leave home. Music, television, photography and most sports were banned or restricted; punishments included public executions and amputations. The regime also destroyed the sixth-century Buddhas of Bamiyan in March 2001 despite international appeals. Ethnic and religious minorities, particularly the Hazara, suffered persecution and massacres.

International relations

Only three countries — Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates — recognised the emirate; the United Nations continued to seat the Islamic State of Afghanistan (the Rabbani government). The Taliban hosted Osama bin Laden and al-Qaeda from 1996, refusing demands to extradite him after the 1998 embassy bombings and the 2000 USS Cole attack, which led to UN sanctions. After the September 11, 2001 attacks, the United States invaded on 7 October 2001; Kabul fell on 13 November, Kandahar on 7 December, and the Taliban government dissolved days later as its leaders fled.
