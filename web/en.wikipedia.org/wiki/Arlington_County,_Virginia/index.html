Arlington County is a county in the Commonwealth of Virginia, located on the southwestern bank of the Potomac River directly across from Washington, D.C. Though small — the smallest self-governing county in the United States by land area — it is densely urbanised and contains the Pentagon, Arlington National Cemetery, and Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport.

History

The land was part of the original District of Columbia ceded by Virginia in 1790, lying west of the Potomac in what became Alexandria County. Congress returned it to Virginia in the retrocession of 1846–1847. In 1920 the county was renamed Arlington, after Robert E. Lee's Arlington House estate, to distinguish it from the city of Alexandria. Arlington House's grounds became Arlington National Cemetery during the Civil War. Federal employment drove rapid suburban growth in the 20th century; the Pentagon, completed in 1943, is the county's emblematic employer, alongside the headquarters functions clustered in Rosslyn, Crystal City (National Landing), and the Ballston corridor. Amazon's HQ2 campus opened in National Landing in 2023.

Government and demographics

Arlington is governed by an elected five-member County Board and has no incorporated municipalities within its borders; neighbourhoods such as Clarendon, Shirlington, and Pentagon City are unincorporated. It is consistently among the highest-income and most highly educated counties in the United States and is known for its "smart growth" concentration of development around Metro stations.
