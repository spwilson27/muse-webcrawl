Storm cellar

A storm cellar is an underground room, often connected to the outside of a house with an outside entrance, or a purpose-built underground shelter, used to protect occupants from severe weather, especially tornadoes and hurricanes. Storm cellars are most common in the central United States — particularly in "Tornado Alley" states such as Oklahoma, Kansas, and Texas — where they have historically doubled as root cellars for storing food.

Design

Traditional storm cellars are excavated pits lined with concrete, stone, or brick, covered by a sloped or flat steel or concrete door at ground level, often built into a hillside or beneath a house. Modern prefabricated shelters are made of steel or fiberglass and may be installed in garages, closets, or yards. Safety standards for storm shelters in the United States are set out in the FEMA P-320 and P-361 guidelines and the ICC/NSSA 500 standard, which address resistance to extreme wind pressures and wind-borne debris impacts.

Use

Occupants enter the cellar when a tornado warning is issued. Because flooding can occur during storms, cellars in low-lying areas may be equipped with drainage, and authorities advise keeping supplies such as water, flashlights, and a weather radio inside.
