Official development assistance

ODA from Development Assistance Committee members reached record levels in the 2020s, driven in part by aid to Ukraine and in-donor refugee costs.

Official development assistance (ODA) is a category of foreign aid used by the Development Assistance Committee (DAC) of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) to measure official flows of financing for development. ODA consists of grants and concessional loans provided by official agencies of DAC members and multilateral institutions to countries on the DAC list of ODA recipients, with the promotion of economic development and welfare as the main objective.

Definition and measurement

To count as ODA, a flow must be administered by the official sector, be concessional in character, and be directed to an eligible recipient country or multilateral organisation. Since 2018, loans have been measured on a "grant-equivalent" basis rather than by face value. Military aid is excluded, though some peacekeeping and humanitarian costs qualify.

The 0.7% target

In 1970 the UN General Assembly resolved that economically advanced countries should devote 0.7% of gross national income to ODA. Only a handful of donors — among them Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Luxembourg, and Germany (in some years) — have regularly met the target. The United States is the largest donor in absolute terms, while the United Kingdom legislated the 0.7% target in 2015 before reducing its ratio to 0.5% in 2021.

Volumes and debates

Total DAC ODA exceeded US$200 billion annually in the early 2020s, reaching record levels partly because of assistance to Ukraine following Russia's 2022 invasion and the counting of in-donor refugee costs. Debates over ODA concern its effectiveness, the inclusion of items such as debt relief and refugee costs, tied aid, and the growing role of non-DAC providers such as China, whose development finance is reported outside the ODA framework.
