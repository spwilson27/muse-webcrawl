Kurmangazy Sagyrbayuly

Kurmangazy Sagyrbayuly (Kazakh: Құрманғазы Сағырбайұлы; c. 1818–1896) was a Kazakh composer, dombra player and folk artist who is celebrated as the "father of Kazakh music".

Life

Born in the Bukey Horde (in present-day West Kazakhstan), Kurmangazy came from a poor family and taught himself the dombra, the two-stringed Kazakh lute. He became a wandering musician and a kritic of social injustice: his works protested against the Kazakh aristocracy and Russian colonial administration, and he was imprisoned several times for his defiance, including for alleged horse theft. He died in 1896 and is buried near Astrakhan.

Works

Kurmangazy composed numerous kyui — instrumental pieces for the dombra — that remain core repertoire, including "Saryarka" (Golden Steppe), "Adai", "Balbyrauyn", "Akbai" and "Kisen Ashkan". His music, transmitted orally, was later collected and arranged by scholars such as Akhmet Zhubanov. The Kazakh State Orchestra of Folk Instruments and the Kazakh National Conservatory in Almaty bear his name.
