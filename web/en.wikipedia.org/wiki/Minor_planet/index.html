Minor planet

According to the International Astronomical Union (IAU), a minor planet is an astronomical object in direct orbit around the Sun that is exclusively classified as neither a planet nor a comet. The category includes asteroids, most trans-Neptunian objects, centaurs, trojans, and dwarf planets. The first minor planet discovered was Ceres in 1801. More than a million minor planets have been discovered, the great majority in the main asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.

Terminology and classification

The term "minor planet" has been used since the 19th century for bodies too small to be considered planets. The IAU's 2006 definitions introduced "dwarf planet" (for minor planets massive enough to reach hydrostatic equilibrium, such as Pluto, Ceres, Eris, Haumea, and Makemake) and "small Solar System body" for most other objects; in practice, "minor planet" continues to be used as the umbrella term for all such objects catalogued by the Minor Planet Center. Active comets with minor-planet designations can be dual-classified when they display cometary activity.

Populations

  • Main-belt asteroids between Mars and Jupiter, including collisional families.
  • Near-Earth objects whose orbits approach or cross Earth's.
  • Jupiter trojans sharing Jupiter's orbit at its Lagrange points.
  • Centaurs on unstable orbits between the giant planets.
  • Trans-Neptunian objects in the Kuiper belt, scattered disc, and beyond.

Numbering and naming

Once an object's orbit is sufficiently well determined, the Minor Planet Center assigns it a permanent number, and the discoverer may propose a name, subject to IAU approval. Objects receive provisional designations encoding the year and order of discovery. Names have historically been drawn from mythology and, in modern times, from a wide range of honorees and subjects.

Exploration

Spacecraft have visited a number of minor planets, including Galileo's flybys of Gaspra and Ida, NEAR Shoemaker's orbit of Eros, Dawn's missions to Vesta and Ceres, Hayabusa2 at Ryugu, OSIRIS-REx at Bennu, and New Horizons at Pluto and Arrokoth.
