Lee Strasberg (November 17, 1901 – February 17, 1982) was an American actor, director, and acting teacher. He co-founded the Group Theatre in 1931 and served as artistic director of the Actors Studio from 1951 until his death, where he developed and taught his version of Method acting, adapted from Konstantin Stanislavski's system. His emphasis on emotional memory and psychological realism shaped generations of performers and changed the face of American stage and screen acting during and after the Golden Age of Hollywood.

Early life and Group Theatre

Born Israel Strassberg in Austria-Hungary, he emigrated with his family to New York as a child and grew up on the Lower East Side. After studying at the American Laboratory Theatre with disciples of Stanislavski, he co-founded the Group Theatre with Harold Clurman and Cheryl Crawford, directing and acting in productions that introduced Stanislavski-based training to American theatre. The Group's alumni seeded the postwar acting revolution.

The Actors Studio and the Method

At the Actors Studio, Strasberg's private sessions became legendary: exercises in relaxation, sense memory, and "affective memory" asked actors to draw on their own emotional histories. His students included Marlon Brando, James Dean, Marilyn Monroe, Paul Newman, Al Pacino, and Robert De Niro. Monroe studied with him intensively from 1955, attended sessions during production of The Misfits, and left the bulk of her estate to him. Rival teachers Stella Adler and Sanford Meisner, both Group alumni, disputed Strasberg's heavy stress on emotional recall, creating competing schools of American acting.

Late career and legacy

In 1969 he founded the Lee Strasberg Theatre and Film Institute in New York and Los Angeles. As an actor he is best remembered for playing mob financier Hyman Roth in The Godfather Part II (1974), which earned him an Academy Award nomination. He died in New York in 1982. Whatever the disputes among his rivals, Strasberg's name remains synonymous with the Method and with the transformation of acting into a psychologically grounded craft; many of his pupils became enduring cultural icons.
