Barkhor

LocationLhasa, Tibet Autonomous Region, China
TypeCircumambulation route and market street
EnclosesJokhang Temple
LengthApproximately 800 metres

The Barkhor (Tibetan: བར་སྐོར་, Wylie: bar skor) is an area of narrow streets and a public square in the old city of Lhasa, Tibet, and a sacred circumambulation path around the Jokhang Temple. It is one of the most important religious and commercial circuits in Tibetan Buddhism.

The circumambulation circuit

Tibetan pilgrims walk clockwise around the Jokhang on the Barkhor, spinning prayer wheels, counting rosaries, and in some cases performing full-length prostrations along the route. The Barkhor is the middle of three traditional circumambulation circuits in Lhasa: the inner Nangkor inside the Jokhang, the Barkhor around the temple, and the outer Lingkhor around the old city.

History

The circuit developed around the Jokhang Temple, founded in the 7th century by King Songtsen Gampo. As pilgrims came to the temple, shops, workshops, and lodgings grew up along the route, making the Barkhor the commercial as well as religious heart of Lhasa. Four large incense burners (sangkangs) mark the cardinal points of the circuit.

The Barkhor today

The Barkhor remains busy with pilgrims and with market stalls selling religious goods, jewelry, handicrafts, and Tibetan and imported merchandise. The Jokhang Temple and the Barkhor area form part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site of the Historic Ensemble of the Potala Palace.
