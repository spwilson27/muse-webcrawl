Classification of surfaces

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The classification theorem of closed surfaces states that every connected closed surface can be obtained from a sphere by adding handles, cross-caps, or (for surfaces with boundary) removing discs. Two closed surfaces are homeomorphic if and only if they have the same Euler characteristic and the same orientability.

Statement

Every closed orientable surface is homeomorphic to the connected sum of g tori, where g ≥ 0 is the genus. Every closed non-orientable surface is homeomorphic to the connected sum of k real projective planes, with k ≥ 1.

Invariants

  • Euler characteristic: χ = 2 − 2g (orientable); χ = 2 − k (non-orientable).
  • Fundamental group: distinguishes connected sums of tori from cross-cap sums.
  • Orientability: whether a consistent choice of orientation exists (the sphere and tori are orientable; the Möbius band makes a surface non-orientable).

History

The theorem was developed through the 19th century by Möbius, Jordan, and von Dyck, and given rigorous proofs in the early 20th century by Dehn, Heegaard, and Brahana. It is a foundational result in algebraic topology and remains the model for how complete classification results behave.
