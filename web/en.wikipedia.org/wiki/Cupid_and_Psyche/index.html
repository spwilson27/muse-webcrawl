Cupid and Psyche

Cupid and Psyche
Source: Apuleius, Metamorphoses
Genre: Myth, fairy tale (ATU 425)
Themes: Love, trust, trials

Cupid and Psyche is a story from the 2nd-century AD Latin novel Metamorphoses (The Golden Ass) by Apuleius. It tells of the mortal Psyche, whose beauty provokes Venus, her love for the god Cupid, her betrayal of his trust, and the trials she endures before being made immortal and reunited with him.

Story

Ordered by an oracle to wed a monster, Psyche is carried to a palace where an unseen husband visits by night. Urged by jealous sisters, she lights a lamp, sees Cupid, and wakes him with a drop of oil; he departs. Wandering, she serves Venus, completing tasks — sorting grains, gathering golden wool, fetching Stygian water, and descending to the underworld for Proserpina's beauty box. Opening the box plunges her into deathlike sleep, from which Cupid revives her. Jupiter grants her immortality; their child is Voluptas (Pleasure).

Influence

The tale, ancestor of "Beauty and the Beast" type tales, inspired art by Raphael, Canova's famous sculpture, and writers from Keats to C. S. Lewis (Till We Have Faces).
