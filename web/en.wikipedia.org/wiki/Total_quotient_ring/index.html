Total quotient ring

In commutative algebra, the total quotient ring (or total ring of fractions) of a commutative ring R, usually written Q(R), is the localisation of R at the set of all non-zero-divisors. It is the largest ring in which every non-zero-divisor of R becomes a unit, generalising the construction of the field of fractions of an integral domain.

Construction and properties

Elements of Q(R) are fractions a/s with a in R and s a non-zero-divisor, modulo the usual equivalence. The natural map R → Q(R) is injective, and Q(R) is a von Neumann regular ring: every element divides its own square in a suitable sense, and every principal ideal is generated by an idempotent. If R is a domain, Q(R) is its field of fractions; if R is Noetherian, Q(R) is a finite product of fields when R is reduced.

Uses

The total quotient ring describes the generic behaviour of a ring — its associated primes embed in it — and provides a natural home for divisor theory and for the sheaf of rational functions on a scheme.
