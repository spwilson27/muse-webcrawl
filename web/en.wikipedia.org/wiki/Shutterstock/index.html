Shutterstock

Shutterstock, Inc. is an American provider of stock photography, stock footage, stock music, and editing tools. Headquartered in New York City's Empire State Building, it was founded in 2003 by programmer and photographer Jon Oringer. Shutterstock maintains a library of hundreds of millions of royalty-free images, vectors, and videos contributed by photographers and artists worldwide, licensed to businesses, media organizations, and marketers.

Business

Contributors earn royalties on a tiered schedule based on lifetime earnings and downloads. The company pioneered the subscription model for stock imagery and went public on the New York Stock Exchange in 2012 (ticker: SSTK). Acquisitions have included Bigstock (2009), Rex Features (2015), Flashstock (2017), TurboSquid, a 3D model marketplace (2021), Pond5 (2022), and Giphy (2023, acquired from Meta). In January 2025, Shutterstock and Getty Images announced a planned "merger of equals" to create a combined visual content company, subject to regulatory approval.

Criticism

A 2020 change to contributor royalty rates drew protests from contributors. The company has also faced scrutiny over censorship of search results in China and over the use of its library to train generative AI models, for which it has licensing partnerships with firms including OpenAI.
