Refined grain

Refined grains are grains and grain products that have been significantly modified from their natural composition, in particular by milling that removes the bran and germ from the grain kernel, leaving mainly the starchy endosperm. The process gives flour a finer texture and improves shelf life, but strips away dietary fibre, iron and many B vitamins.

Examples

Common refined grains and products include white flour, white bread, white rice (polished rice), degermed cornmeal, most pasta, breakfast cereals made from refined flour, and baked goods such as cakes, biscuits and pastries.

Enrichment

In many countries, refined flour is enriched: some nutrients lost in milling — iron and the B vitamins thiamin, riboflavin, niacin and folic acid — are added back by law or custom. Enrichment does not restore the fibre or the full range of micronutrients and phytochemicals present in whole grains.

Health aspects

Dietary guidelines in many countries recommend making at least half of grain consumption whole grains. High intakes of refined grains have been associated in observational studies with higher risks of type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease and weight gain, partly because refined products tend to have a higher glycaemic index and lower satiety than their whole-grain counterparts.
