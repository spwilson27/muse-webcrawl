Change blindness

Change blindness is a perceptual phenomenon in which a person fails to notice a large change in a visual stimulus, typically when the change coincides with a brief visual disruption such as a flicker, blink, saccade or cut in a film.

Research

Experiments by Simons and Levin (1997) showed observers often fail to notice that the person they are talking to has been swapped behind a passing door. The phenomenon demonstrates that visual awareness is far sparser than subjective experience suggests and that attention is required to encode scene details. It is related to inattentional blindness.
