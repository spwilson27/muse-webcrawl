Lê Lợi

Reign1428–1433
Born1384 or 1385, Thanh Hóa, Đại Việt
Died1433, Thăng Long (Hanoi)

Lê Lợi (1384/1385–1433), temple name Lê Thái Tổ, was the founder of the Later Lê dynasty of Vietnam and one of the country's most celebrated national heroes. He led the Lam Sơn uprising that expelled the Ming dynasty from Đại Việt.

Lam Sơn uprising

Ming China had occupied Vietnam since 1407. In 1418 Lê Lợi raised a rebellion from his home region of Lam Sơn in Thanh Hóa. After a decade of guerrilla warfare, advised by strategist Nguyễn Trãi, his forces won decisive victories at Tốt Động–Chúc Động (1426) and Chi Lăng (1427), forcing a Ming withdrawal. In 1428 he was proclaimed emperor, founding the Later Lê dynasty, which ruled for centuries.

Legacy

Legend tells that a golden turtle of Hoàn Kiếm Lake in Hanoi lent him the magic sword Thuận Thiên and later reclaimed it — the lake's name means "Lake of the Returned Sword".
