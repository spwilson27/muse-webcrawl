Pole and polar

In geometry, pole and polar are respectively a point and a line that have a unique reciprocal relationship with respect to a given conic section. The concept originates in projective geometry.

If a point P lies outside a conic, the two tangents from P touch the conic at points whose joining line is the polar of P, and P is the pole of that line. If the point lies on the conic, its polar is the tangent at that point. The relationship is reciprocal: if point A lies on the polar of point B, then B lies on the polar of A (La Hire's theorem).

Generalization

Polarity extends to higher dimensions, relating points and hyperplanes with respect to quadric surfaces, and underlies duality in projective geometry.
