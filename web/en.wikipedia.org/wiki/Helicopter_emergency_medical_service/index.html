Helicopter emergency medical service

Helicopter emergency medical services (HEMS), also called air ambulance or air medical services, use helicopters to transport critically ill or injured patients and to bring advanced medical teams rapidly to emergency scenes. Modern HEMS developed from military casualty evacuation in the Korean and Vietnam wars and spread into civilian systems from the 1970s.

Operations

Air ambulances are typically staffed by a pilot, a doctor and/or paramedic and a nurse or technician, and carry advanced life-support equipment, blood products and monitoring. They respond to major trauma, transfer patients between hospitals, and reach remote or congested areas faster than ground ambulances. In the United Kingdom most HEMS operators are charities (such as London's Air Ambulance), while elsewhere they may be run by hospitals, governments or commercial providers. Debates continue over cost-effectiveness, safety and the clinical benefit for particular patient groups.
