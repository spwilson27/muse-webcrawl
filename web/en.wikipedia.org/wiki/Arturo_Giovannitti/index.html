Arturo M. Giovannitti (1884–1959) was an Italian-American poet, labour organiser, and editor, celebrated as one of the leading literary voices of the American radical labour movement. A member of the Industrial Workers of the World (IWW), he won national fame in 1912 when he was tried—and acquitted—on a charge arising from the Lawrence textile strike.

Early life

Giovannitti was born in Ripabottoni, in the Molise region of Italy. He emigrated first to Canada, where he studied for the Presbyterian ministry and worked in missions among Italian immigrants, and then to the United States. Losing his faith, he turned to socialism and labour organising, editing Italian-language radical newspapers including Il Proletario, the Italian organ of the IWW.

Lawrence strike and trial

During the 1912 Lawrence textile strike in Massachusetts—the "Bread and Roses" strike—Giovannitti directed Italian relief work alongside Joseph Ettor. After a striker, Anna LoPizzo, was shot dead during a clash (by a policeman's bullet, witnesses said), Ettor and Giovannitti, who had been speaking at a meeting far from the scene, were arrested as accessories before the fact to murder on the theory that their speeches had incited the unknown killer. Held without bail for months, they were tried in Salem in the autumn of 1912, addressing the jury in celebrated speeches of their own. Both were acquitted; the case became a landmark cause célèbre of American labour.

In prison Giovannitti wrote "The Walker," a poem about a prisoner pacing his cell that was published in a national magazine and made his literary reputation among English-speaking readers.

Later career

His first English collection, Arrows in the Gale (1914), carried an introduction by Helen Keller, an admirer. He continued to write poetry in Italian and English, translated and edited, and from 1924 served as secretary of Local 89 of the International Ladies' Garment Workers' Union, part of the Italian dressmakers' union in New York. His later work moved from revolutionary syndicalism toward the more moderate labour politics of the garment unions. He died in New York in 1959.
