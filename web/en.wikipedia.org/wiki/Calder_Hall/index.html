Calder Hall

Calder Hall, on the Sellafield site in Cumbria, England, was the world's first commercial-scale nuclear power station. Queen Elizabeth II officially opened it on 17 October 1956; its first reactor had been connected to the grid on 27 August 1956.

Design and operation

The station comprised four Magnox reactors of about 50 MWe each (later uprated), graphite-moderated and cooled by carbon dioxide, fuelling with natural uranium metal clad in magnesium alloy. Although publicly presented as a power station, Calder Hall's design was also optimized to produce plutonium for the British nuclear weapons programme, a dual purpose acknowledged in official histories. Its reactors supplied electricity to the national grid for nearly 47 years, far beyond their planned lifetimes.

Closure and decommissioning

Calder Hall ceased generation on 31 March 2003. Defuelling was completed in 2019, and the station's four cooling towers — prominent local landmarks — had already been demolished in a controlled explosion on 29 September 2007. The reactor buildings are being decommissioned under the Nuclear Decommissioning Authority as part of the wider Sellafield programme, with Calder Hall's pioneering role commemorated as a milestone in civil nuclear power.
