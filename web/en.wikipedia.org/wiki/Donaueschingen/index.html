Donaueschingen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Donaueschingen is a town in the Schwarzwald-Baar district of Baden-Württemberg, Germany, at the eastern edge of the Black Forest. It is traditionally regarded as the starting point of the Danube: the river is formed here by the confluence of the Brigach and Breg, and a decorated spring in the grounds of the princely palace, the Donauquelle, is presented as the Danube's source.

History and sights

The town was long the residence of the princes of Fürstenberg, whose palace (Schloss Donaueschingen), art collections, and brewery remain prominent. The ornate Donauquelle fountain group and the confluence point, the Donauzusammenfluss, are visitor attractions. Donaueschingen also has a railway junction on the Black Forest Railway.

Music festival

The town hosts the Donaueschinger Musiktage (Donaueschingen Festival), founded in 1921 under the patronage of the Fürstenberg princes, one of the world's oldest and most important festivals of contemporary classical music. It premiered works by composers including Hindemith, Schoenberg, Webern, Boulez, and Stockhausen, and remains a leading venue for new music each October.
