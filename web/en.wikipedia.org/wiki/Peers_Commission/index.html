Peers Commission

The Peers Commission was a United States Army inquiry, formally the "Department of the Army Review of the Preliminary Investigations into the My Lai Incident", established in November 1969 by Secretary of the Army Stanley Resor and Army Chief of Staff William Westmoreland. It was chaired by Lieutenant General William R. Peers and investigated not the My Lai massacre itself but the adequacy of the Army's original handling of the incident and the subsequent suppression of information about it.

Background

On 16 March 1968, soldiers of Charlie Company, 1st Battalion, 20th Infantry — part of Task Force Barker of the Americal Division — murdered hundreds of unarmed Vietnamese civilians at My Lai. Internal Army inquiries in 1968, led by Colonel Oran Henderson, had concluded that only a small number of civilians had been killed incidentally and closed the matter. The massacre became public in November 1969 through the reporting of Seymour Hersh, prompting the Army to open a deeper investigation.

Findings

The Peers panel interviewed some 400 witnesses and produced a report of several thousand pages, completed in March 1970. It found that officers at every level of the Americal Division's chain of command, up to its commander, Major General Samuel Koster, had suppressed or misrepresented information about the massacre, and that the original investigations had been inadequate and misleading. The commission recommended criminal charges or administrative action against 28 officers and other personnel, citing offences ranging from dereliction of duty to murder. In the event, only Lieutenant William Calley was convicted, and most charges against senior officers were dropped or reduced to administrative sanctions; Koster was demoted and stripped of a decoration.

Legacy

The full Peers Report was not released until 1974, after litigation by the ACLU under the Freedom of Information Act, and even then portions were withheld. It remains a central primary source on My Lai and a case study in command responsibility and institutional cover-up.
