Kintyre

Kintyre Country: Scotland
Council area: Argyll and Bute
Length: about 48 km (30 miles)
Main town: Campbeltown
Southern point: Mull of Kintyre

Kintyre (Scottish Gaelic: Cinn Tìre, "headland") is a long, narrow peninsula in western Scotland, in the historic county of Argyll. Stretching roughly 48 kilometres (30 mi) from the Mull of Kintyre in the south to the village of Tarbert in the north, it is washed by the Atlantic Ocean on its western side and by Kilbrannan Sound and the Firth of Clyde to the east.

Geography

The peninsula is mostly hilly moorland, rising to 454 metres at Beinn an Tuirc near Carradale. Its main settlement is Campbeltown near the southern end, once a major centre of Scotch whisky distilling with more than thirty distilleries in the 19th century; three remain in production, including Springbank. Other villages include Tarbert, Carradale, Southend and Machrihanish, the last noted for its golf links and its long sandy beach.

At its closest, the Mull of Kintyre is only about 19 kilometres (12 mi) from the coast of County Antrim in Northern Ireland, and the two coasts are intervisible in clear weather. The strait between them is the North Channel of the Irish Sea.

History and culture

Kintyre was part of the Gaelic kingdom of Dál Riata, which spanned north-eastern Ireland and western Scotland, and later of the Lordship of the Isles. The peninsula's proximity to Ireland shaped its history of settlement and sea-borne trade. The area is celebrated in Paul McCartney's 1977 song "Mull of Kintyre," recorded with Wings, which became one of the best-selling singles in UK history.
