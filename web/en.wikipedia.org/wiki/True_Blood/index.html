True Blood is an American fantasy horror drama television series produced and created by Alan Ball, based on The Southern Vampire Mysteries, a series of novels by Charlaine Harris. The series revolves around Sookie Stackhouse (Anna Paquin), a telepathic waitress living in the fictional rural town of Bon Temps, Louisiana. It is set two years after the invention of a synthetic blood product branded "Tru Blood", which has allowed vampires to "come out of the coffin" and reveal their existence to the world.

Premise

Sookie's world is turned upside down when 173-year-old vampire Bill Compton (Stephen Moyer) walks into Merlotte's, the bar where she works. The series explores vampires' struggle for civil rights and assimilation as an allegory for minority rights movements, alongside a wider supernatural world of shapeshifters, werewolves, fairies, witches, and maenads. Other principal characters include Sookie's brother Jason, her friend Tara Thornton, vampire sheriff Eric Northman (Alexander Skarsgård), and bar owner Sam Merlotte.

Reception

True Blood was HBO's most-watched series since The Sopranos at its peak and received critical acclaim for its early seasons, particularly for its performances, Southern Gothic atmosphere, and theme song, Jace Everett's "Bad Things". Anna Paquin won a Golden Globe for her performance. Later seasons drew more mixed reviews for increasingly convoluted plotting. The series concluded after seven seasons in 2014.
