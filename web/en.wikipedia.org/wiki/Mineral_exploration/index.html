Mineral exploration

Mineral exploration is the process of searching for evidence of deposits of economically valuable minerals, with the goal of discovering an orebody that can be profitably mined. It is a sequential, high-risk activity carried out by mining companies and prospectors, progressing from broad regional reconnaissance to detailed evaluation of individual prospects.

Stages

Exploration typically begins with reconnaissance: study of regional geology, historical production and existing maps to select favorable terrain. Prospecting follows, involving field examination, geological mapping and surface sampling. Promising areas advance to detailed exploration, using geochemical surveys of soils, rocks and stream sediments, geophysical methods such as magnetics, gravity, electromagnetics and induced polarization, and remote sensing. Targets are then tested by trenching and drilling, commonly diamond core drilling, which provides samples for assay and geological logging.

Evaluation

If drilling demonstrates a coherent body of mineralization, the project proceeds to resource estimation and feasibility studies, assessing tonnage, grade, metallurgy, infrastructure, environmental impact and economics. Only a small fraction of exploration targets — often estimated at fewer than one in a thousand early-stage prospects — ultimately become mines.

Industry

Much grassroots exploration is conducted by small "junior" companies financed through equity markets in centers such as Toronto and Perth, while major mining companies focus on advanced projects and acquisitions. Exploration activity is strongly cyclical, tracking commodity prices.
