Ludmila of Bohemia

Born: c. 860, Mělník

Died: 15 September 921, Tetín

Venerated in: Roman Catholic Church, Eastern Orthodox Church

Feast: 16 September

Saint Ludmila (c. 860 – 15 September 921) was a Czech saint and martyr, the wife of Bořivoj I, the first Christian Duke of Bohemia, and the grandmother and tutor of Saint Wenceslaus. She is venerated as the patron saint of Bohemia and is credited with helping to spread Christianity among the Czechs.

Life

Ludmila was the daughter of Slavibor, prince of the Slavic tribe of the Pšovans. She married Bořivoj, the first duke of the Přemyslid dynasty, and together they were baptised — according to tradition by Saint Methodius himself, around 883. After Bořivoj's death her sons succeeded to the dukedom, and Ludmila retired to the castle of Tetín, where she devoted herself to raising her grandson Wenceslaus, teaching him the Christian faith and letters.

Murder and legacy

Wenceslaus's mother Drahomíra, who acted as regent and resented Ludmila's influence over the young duke and her Christian counsel, arranged her murder. On the night of 15 September 921, assassins broke into Ludmila's residence at Tetín and strangled her — according to legend, with her own veil. She was canonised soon after her death; her relics were translated by Wenceslaus to St. George's Basilica in Prague Castle. Saint Ludmila remains a central figure of Czech national and religious identity, commemorated in art and in Antonín Dvořák's oratorio Saint Ludmila.
