Dynamo is a highly available, distributed key–value storage system developed by Amazon to support core e-commerce services such as the shopping cart. It was described in an influential 2007 paper presented at the ACM Symposium on Operating Systems Principles, "Dynamo: Amazon's Highly Available Key-value Store".

Design principles

Dynamo prioritises availability and partition tolerance over strong consistency, offering eventual consistency so that writes are essentially never rejected — a "shopping cart" must always accept items. Its techniques include consistent hashing with virtual nodes to partition data across a ring of servers, quorum-based replication (parameterised by N, R and W), vector clocks to detect conflicting versions, hinted handoff and gossip-based membership, and Merkle trees (anti-entropy) for replica synchronisation.

Influence

Dynamo's design strongly influenced the NoSQL movement: Apache Cassandra, Riak and Voldemort all adopted variants of its architecture. Amazon later offered its ideas as a managed cloud service in Amazon DynamoDB (2012), which differs in several respects, including offering optional strong consistency.
