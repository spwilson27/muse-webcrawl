Alpha Centauri (α Centauri, abbreviated Alpha Cen) is the closest star system and the closest planetary system to the Solar System, at 4.37 light-years (1.34 parsecs) from the Sun. It is a triple star system consisting of three stars: Alpha Centauri A (officially Rigil Kentaurus), Alpha Centauri B (officially Toliman), and Alpha Centauri C (officially Proxima Centauri).

Alpha Centauri A and B are Sun-like stars (classes G2V and K1V) forming the binary Alpha Centauri AB, which to the naked eye appears as a single star, the third-brightest in the night sky. Proxima Centauri, a small red dwarf, lies slightly closer to Earth at 4.24 light-years and is the Sun's nearest stellar neighbor.

Observational history

The system's binary nature was recognized by Father Jean Richaud in 1689. In 1832–33 Thomas Henderson, working at the Cape of Good Hope, measured its parallax — the first successful measurement of a stellar distance, published in 1839 after he became Astronomer Royal for Scotland at Edinburgh. Proxima was discovered by Robert Innes in 1915.

Planets

In 2016 the European Southern Observatory announced Proxima Centauri b, an Earth-sized planet in the habitable zone of Proxima, sparking intense interest in interstellar probes such as the Breakthrough Starshot initiative. Candidate planets Proxima d (confirmed 2022) and the disputed Proxima c, and a possible planet around Alpha Centauri A imaged directly in 2021, make the system a prime target in the search for nearby habitable worlds. Even the sharpest telescopes are limited by the Airy disk diffraction pattern when resolving such companions.

In culture

As the nearest star system, Alpha Centauri features in countless works of science fiction, from Lost in Space to the film Avatar, and in Chinese tradition the star is part of the Southern Gate asterism.
