Personality test

A personality test is a method of assessing human personality constructs. Most are self-report inventories (questionnaires), though performance-based and projective methods also exist. Personality testing is used in clinical psychology, employment screening, counselling and research.

Types

Self-report inventories include the Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory (MMPI), the NEO Personality Inventory measuring the Big Five traits, and the popular but scientifically criticised Myers–Briggs Type Indicator. Projective techniques, such as the Rorschach inkblot test and the Thematic Apperception Test, ask respondents to interpret ambiguous stimuli.

Psychometric evaluation

Tests are judged on reliability, validity, standardisation and norms. Trait inventories grounded in factor analysis generally show stronger evidence than type-based instruments, which often suffer from poor test–retest reliability.

Applications and criticism

Employers use personality testing for selection and team building, but critics raise concerns about faking, cultural bias, privacy and the weak prediction of job performance by some instruments.
