Gimli (Middle-earth)

Gimli is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth, appearing in The Lord of the Rings. A dwarf of the House of Durin, he is the son of Glóin, one of Bilbo Baggins's companions in The Hobbit. Gimli represents the Dwarves as a member of the Fellowship of the Ring, formed to destroy the One Ring.

Race: Dwarf
Family: Glóin (father)
Weapons: Battle axe, walking axe, short axe
Book(s): The Lord of the Rings

Character

Gimli is a fierce warrior, proud of his people and quick to take offence, but also loyal, humorous, and capable of great courtesy. His arc in the novel is one of reconciliation between Elves and Dwarves: initially distrustful of Elves, he is captivated by Galadriel in Lothlórien and forms a deep friendship with the Elf Legolas, with whom he travels after the breaking of the Fellowship. Their contest of Orc-kills at the Battle of Helm's Deep is played as a running joke between friends.

Later life

After the War of the Ring, Gimli founded a colony of Dwarves in the Glittering Caves of Aglarond behind Helm's Deep and became their lord. He is said to have rebuilt the gates of Minas Tirith in mithril and steel. According to the Red Book, in his old age he sailed west with Legolas to the Undying Lands — the only Dwarf ever granted that passage, by grace of Galadriel.

Portrayal

In Peter Jackson's film trilogy, Gimli is played by John Rhys-Davies, with his stature achieved through forced perspective, scale doubles, and Rhys-Davies's own height being offset by the taller actors playing the Elves and Men.
