The British Racing Partnership (BRP) was a British motor racing team founded in 1957 by Alfred Moss, father of Stirling Moss, and Moss's manager Ken Gregory. The team was notable as one of Britain's first teams to be financed by sponsorship — initially from the Yeoman Credit finance company, under which name it raced for a time — an arrangement that anticipated the modern sponsorship era in Formula One.

History

BRP began in Formula Two before entering Formula One in 1959, running Cooper chassis. Stirling Moss drove for the team at selected races in 1959–1961, winning non-championship events. In 1963 BRP attempted to become a constructor in its own right with the BRP Mk1, designed by Tony Robinson and powered by a BRM V8 engine; Innes Ireland gave the car its best results, including fourth place finishes. The team also ran Lotus and BRM machinery for drivers including Lucien Bianchi, Trevor Taylor and Masten Gregory.

BRP never won a World Championship Grand Prix, and with rising costs and a downturn in sponsorship the team withdrew from racing at the end of 1964 and was wound up. Its Innes Ireland-driven Lotus 24 and its own BRP-BRM remain its best-known cars.
