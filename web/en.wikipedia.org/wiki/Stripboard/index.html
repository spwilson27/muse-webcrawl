Stripboard

Stripboard with parallel copper tracks, drilled on a 0.1-inch grid

Stripboard is a widely used type of electronics prototyping board characterised by a regular grid of holes (typically on 0.1 inch / 2.54 mm spacing) with parallel strips of copper cladding on one side, running the length of the board. It is sold under the trade name Veroboard, originally created by the British company Vero Electronics, and is used to build permanent soldered prototypes and one-off circuits.

Use

Components are inserted through the holes and soldered to the copper strips, which act as connecting rails. Connections along a strip are automatic; where a strip must not connect two points, the copper is cut using a special cutter or a drill bit twisted by hand in the hole. Wire links on the component side complete the remaining connections. Because the strips run in one direction only, layout on stripboard requires planning, and several software tools exist to help design layouts before construction.

Comparison

Stripboard differs from perfboard, whose holes have individual copper pads with no connections, and from the solderless breadboard, whose spring contacts allow temporary, reusable assemblies. Unlike a purpose-designed printed circuit board, stripboard needs no etching or fabrication step, but the resulting circuit is bulkier and harder to reproduce in quantity.
