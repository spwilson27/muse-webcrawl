Barsine

Barsine (Greek: Βαρσίνη; c. 363 – c. 309 BC) was a Persian noblewoman, the daughter of Artabazus II, satrap of Phrygia, and the mistress of Alexander the Great, to whom she bore a son, Heracles of Macedon. She was one of the first Persian women connected with Alexander's court during his conquest of the Achaemenid Empire.

Life

Barsine grew up partly in exile at the Macedonian court of Philip II, where her father had taken refuge, and thus knew Greek language and customs. She was married first to Mentor of Rhodes, a Greek mercenary commander in Persian service, and after his death to his brother Memnon, who led Persian resistance in the Aegean. After Memnon's death in 333 BC, Barsine was captured by the Macedonians at Damascus following the Battle of Issus and became Alexander's mistress. Around 327 BC she bore him a son, Heracles.

Later life and death

Barsine retired to Pergamon and raised Heracles away from court. After Alexander's death in 323 BC, the orator Nearchus proposed Heracles as king at Babylon, but the proposal was rejected. In 309 BC, during the wars of the Successors, the regent Polyperchon brought Heracles forward as a claimant, then betrayed and murdered both the youth and Barsine on the orders of Cassander, extinguishing Alexander's last surviving son.
