History of Norway

The history of Norway has been influenced by its terrain and climate, with a long coastline facing the North Atlantic. Small, scattered farming communities developed early maritime trade, and from the late 8th century Norwegians participated in the Viking Age expansion across the North Atlantic.

Unification and the medieval kingdom

Harald Fairhair is traditionally credited with unifying Norway after the Battle of Hafrsfjord around 872. Christianity was introduced in the 10th and 11th centuries under kings such as Olaf Tryggvason and Olaf Haraldsson (St Olaf). The medieval kingdom reached its greatest extent in the 13th century, ruling Iceland, Greenland, and parts of the British Isles, before plague, economic decline, and dynastic accident weakened it.

Unions and independence

From 1397 Norway was part of the Kalmar Union with Denmark and Sweden, and thereafter remained in union with Denmark for over four centuries. In 1814, ceded to Sweden by the Treaty of Kiel, Norway adopted its own constitution at Eidsvoll and entered a looser personal union with Sweden, which was dissolved peacefully in 1905. Norway was occupied by Germany during the Second World War. Post-war Norway became a founding member of NATO and was transformed by North Sea oil discovered from 1969.
