Alberto Díaz Gutiérrez (14 September 1928 – 25 May 2001), better known as Alberto Korda, was a Cuban photographer who took the photograph Guerrillero Heroico of the revolutionary Che Guevara — described by the Maryland Institute College of Art as "the most famous photograph in the world".

Career

Korda began as a fashion and advertising photographer, co-founding the Korda Studios in Havana in 1953 with Luis Antonio Peirce. After the Cuban Revolution of 1959 he became a photographer for the newspaper Revolución and for a decade travelled with Fidel Castro as an official photographer, also pioneering underwater photography in Cuba.

Guerrillero Heroico

On 5 March 1960, at a memorial service in Havana for victims of the La Coubre explosion, Korda briefly captured Guevara's defiant gaze as he appeared on the speaker's platform. The frame was not published at the time; it spread worldwide only after Guevara's death in 1967, when the Italian publisher Giangiacomo Feltrinelli obtained the image and printed it as a poster. Cropped and reproduced on posters, T-shirts, album covers and murals, it became one of the most reproduced photographs in history. Korda, who received no royalties for decades, successfully sued in 2000 to stop the image being used to advertise vodka, donating the settlement to the Cuban health system.

Death

Korda died of a heart attack in Paris in 2001 while an exhibition of his work was running.
