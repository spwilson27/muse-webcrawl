Warden of the Marches

The Warden of the Marches was an official in the Scottish Marches and the English border counties in the late medieval and early modern periods, charged with administering the frontier districts between the kingdoms of Scotland and England. The marches were divided into East, Middle and West districts on each side of the border, each under its own warden.

Duties

Wardens maintained order in a lawless frontier society dominated by the raiding families known as the Border Reivers. Their duties included holding "days of truce" with the opposite warden to settle cross-border claims under the special body of march law, pursuing raiders by "hot trod", keeping watch and ward along the border, and mustering local forces in time of war. Powerful local magnates — the Percys, Cliffords and Dacres in England, and families such as the Kerrs, Maxwells and Johnstones in Scotland — often held the offices.

History

The wardenships developed from the later 13th century as Anglo-Scottish warfare made the border a militarized zone. Their powers included summary justice, and wardens were at times as much a source of disorder as a remedy for it. After the Union of the Crowns in 1603, James VI and I renamed the region the "Middle Shires" and suppressed the reiving system; the wardenries were abolished and replaced by joint commissions, and the office effectively disappeared during the 17th century.
