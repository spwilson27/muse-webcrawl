Burma campaign

The Burma campaign in the South-East Asian theatre of the Second World War was fought between Commonwealth, Chinese, and United States forces on one side and the forces of Imperial Japan and its allies on the other, across the territory of British Burma from 1941 to 1945. Fought in difficult jungle and mountain terrain and complicated by monsoon weather and disease, it was among the longest continuous campaigns of the war.

Overview

The campaign is usually divided into phases: the Japanese conquest of Burma in 1942, which severed the Burma Road supply route to China; a period of stalemate and limited offensives in 1942–1943, including the Chindit expeditions; the defeat of the Japanese offensive against India at Imphal and Kohima in 1944; and the Allied reconquest of Burma in 1944–1945 under the Fourteenth Army, culminating in the recapture of Rangoon.

The China Burma India theater

Burma formed part of the China Burma India (CBI) theater for American forces. American and Chinese troops under General Joseph Stilwell campaigned in northern Burma to protect and rebuild a land supply route to China, constructing the Ledo Road while aircraft flew supplies "over the Hump" of the Himalayas. Merrill's Marauders and the Mars Task Force were among the American units engaged.

Legacy

The campaign is commemorated at sites such as the Kohima War Cemetery, whose epitaph — "When you go home, tell them of us and say, for your tomorrow, we gave our today" — is famous throughout the Commonwealth. The Burma Star was awarded to Commonwealth personnel who served in the campaign.
