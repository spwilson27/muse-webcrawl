Disinvestment

Disinvestment refers to the action of an organisation or government selling or liquidating an asset or subsidiary. It is the opposite of investment. Disinvestment may occur for financial reasons, to raise funds or reduce debt, or as a political and ethical strategy intended to pressure the entity in which the investment is held.

Motivations

  • Financial — disposing of underperforming or non-core assets to improve returns.
  • Political — campaigns urging institutions to withdraw investments from regimes or industries regarded as unethical.
  • Regulatory — sales required by competition authorities or government policy, as in privatisation programmes of public-sector enterprises.

Notable campaigns

The disinvestment campaign against apartheid-era South Africa in the 1970s and 1980s is regarded as a landmark of shareholder activism. More recently, the fossil fuel divestment movement has persuaded universities, pension funds, and other institutions to remove fossil-fuel holdings from their portfolios on climate grounds. Divestment campaigns have also targeted tobacco, weapons manufacturers, and private prisons.
