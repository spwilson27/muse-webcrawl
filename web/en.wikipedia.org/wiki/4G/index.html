4G

A phone connected to a 4G LTE network

4G is the fourth generation of cellular network technology, succeeding 3G and preceding 5G. The International Telecommunication Union's IMT-Advanced specification set peak requirements of 100 Mbit/s for high-mobility and 1 Gbit/s for low-mobility communication, with all-IP packet-switched service.

Technologies

The two main 4G systems deployed commercially are Mobile WiMAX (IEEE 802.16m) and LTE (Long Term Evolution) from the 3GPP. LTE, first launched in Stockholm and Oslo in December 2009 by TeliaSonera, became the dominant global standard. Its successor, LTE Advanced, was the first release to fully meet IMT-Advanced requirements; earlier LTE and WiMAX releases were permitted to be marketed as "4G" despite falling short of the original targets.

Features

4G networks use OFDMA (and SC-FDMA on the uplink), multiple-antenna techniques such as MIMO, and an all-IP core network. Voice service is carried as data via VoLTE (Voice over LTE) rather than on a separate circuit-switched network. Typical real-world speeds range from a few to tens of megabits per second, enabling mobile broadband, video streaming and app ecosystems that defined the smartphone era of the 2010s.

Deployment and succession

4G coverage spread worldwide through the 2010s, reaching the large majority of the world's population. Commercial 5G networks began launching in 2019, but 4G remains the workhorse layer of most mobile networks and is expected to operate well into the 2030s alongside its successor.
