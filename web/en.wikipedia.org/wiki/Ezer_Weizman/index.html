Ezer Weizman

Ezer Weizman (Hebrew: עזר ויצמן; 15 June 1924 – 24 April 2005) was the seventh President of Israel, first elected in 1993 and re-elected in 1998. Before the presidency, he was a veteran pilot and commander of the Israeli Air Force and Minister of Defense.

Born in Tel Aviv to a prominent Zionist family — his uncle was Chaim Weizmann, Israel's first president — Weizman learned to fly with the RAF during the Second World War and flew combat missions. In the 1948 Arab–Israeli War he flew for the nascent Israeli Air Force. He served as commander of the IAF from 1958 to 1966, building it into the force that achieved air superiority in the Six-Day War of 1967.

Political career

Entering politics with the Herut party, Weizman managed the Likud campaign that won the 1977 election and became Minister of Defense under Menachem Begin. He played a key role in the peace negotiations with Egypt that led to the Camp David Accords and the 1979 treaty, then resigned in 1980. He later moved toward the political centre-left, served as a minister in national unity governments, and as a close adviser to Shimon Peres.

Presidency

As president (1993–2000), Weizman was a popular but outspoken figure, supporting the Oslo peace process. His second term ended early: he resigned in July 2000 amid a scandal over large monetary gifts he had received from businessman Edouard Saroussi in earlier years, though he was never charged with a crime. He died in 2005 and is buried in Or Akiva.
