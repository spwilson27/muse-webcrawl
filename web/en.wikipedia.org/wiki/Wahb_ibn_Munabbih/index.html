Wahb ibn Munabbih

Wahb ibn Munabbih (Arabic: وهب بن منبه; c. 655 – c. 728 CE) was an early Muslim historian, traditionist and narrator of Jewish and biblical lore, regarded as one of the earliest authorities on the Isrā'īliyyāt — traditions derived from Jewish and Christian sources that entered early Islamic literature.

Life

Wahb was born in Dhamar, in Yemen, into a family of Persian descent that had settled in South Arabia. He lived during the Umayyad period and is said to have been appointed a judge (qāḍī) in Sanaa. Tradition reports that he studied Torah and other scriptures and became learned in the histories of the prophets and ancient nations. He died around 728 CE, during the reign of the caliph Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik, though some sources place his death a few years later.

Works and influence

Wahb is credited with works on the beginnings of creation, the stories of the prophets (qiṣaṣ al-anbiyā'), and accounts of the Himyarite kings of Yemen, though much of what survives is transmitted through later authors such as Ibn Ishaq and al-Tabari. His reports supplied a large body of narrative material — including accounts of Adam, Noah, Abraham and other biblical figures — that shaped early Qur'anic exegesis and popular storytelling. Later critics debated the reliability of his narrations, but his role in transmitting biblical and South Arabian lore into Islamic tradition is widely acknowledged.
