Air commodore (abbreviated Air Cdre) is a one-star air officer rank used by the Royal Air Force and the air forces of many Commonwealth and other countries influenced by British practice. It ranks immediately above group captain and immediately below air vice-marshal, and corresponds to the NATO rank code OF-6, equivalent to a brigadier in the British Army and a commodore in the Royal Navy.

Origins

The rank was created at the formation of the Royal Air Force on 1 April 1918, when the new service adopted a unique rank structure partly derived from Royal Navy titles combined with the word "air." It was first used in 1919 when the RAF introduced its own officer ranks to replace the Army titles initially used. Holders of the rank are classed as air officers, and it is the most junior rank to carry the "air" prefix among the marshal-family ranks that ascend through air vice-marshal, air marshal, and air chief marshal to marshal of the Royal Air Force.

Use in other air forces

Air commodore is or has been used by the air forces of Australia, India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, New Zealand, Nigeria, Ghana, Zimbabwe, and others. In the Indian Air Force it is likewise a one-star rank between group captain and air vice marshal. The Netherlands air force uses the equivalent commodore. Some air forces have replaced the rank with brigadier or brigadier general titles, but in the RAF it remains the rank typically held by station commanders of large bases, heads of branch, and senior staff officers.

Insignia

The rank insignia consists of a single broad band of light blue braid on a black background worn on the sleeve or shoulder, together with a distinctive cap with a single row of oak-leaf embroidery on the peak for air officers. The command flag for an air commodore is a rectangular air force ensign variant with one star. On appointment, air commodores are members of the air officer cadre and are addressed as "Air Commodore" followed by surname.
