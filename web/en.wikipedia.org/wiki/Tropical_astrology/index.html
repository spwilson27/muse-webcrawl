Tropical astrology is the form of astrology used by most Western astrologers, in which the zodiac is anchored to the cycle of the seasons rather than to the fixed stars. The first degree of Aries is defined as the position of the Sun at the March equinox; Cancer begins at the June solstice, Libra at the September equinox, and Capricorn at the December solstice. The twelve signs are thus equal 30° divisions of the ecliptic measured from the moving equinox point.

History

The tropical framework was codified in the Hellenistic world and given its definitive form by Ptolemy in the 2nd century CE, whose Tetrabiblos argued that the zodiac should begin at the vernal equinox. At that time the equinox point lay within the constellation Aries, so the tropical and sidereal zodiacs roughly coincided. Since then, the precession of the equinoxes — a slow wobble of Earth's axis with a period of about 26,000 years — has shifted the equinox point westward by roughly 24°, so the tropical signs no longer align with the constellations bearing the same names. Most tropical astrologers regard the signs as seasonal and symbolic sectors rather than star patterns and do not consider this a problem.

Comparison with sidereal astrology

In sidereal astrology — standard in Indian (Vedic) astrology and used by a minority of Western astrologers — the zodiac is fixed to the stars, and the difference between the two systems (the ayanamsa) is currently about 24°. As a result, a planet placed in tropical Taurus often falls in sidereal Aries. The two frameworks also differ in some house systems and interpretive traditions. A separate, unrelated use of "tropical" in astrological history concerns the "tropical signs" Cancer and Capricorn, in which the Sun turns at the solstices (from Greek tropos, "turn").

Practice

Tropical astrologers cast horoscopes using planetary positions computed in the tropical zodiac, interpreting the twelve signs, houses, and aspects for natal, predictive, and mundane work. The overwhelming majority of astrology columns, software, and teaching in Europe and the Americas use the tropical system.
