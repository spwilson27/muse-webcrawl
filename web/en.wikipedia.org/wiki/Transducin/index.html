Transducin

Transducin (Gt) is a protein naturally expressed in vertebrate retina rods and cones, where it conveys the signal of a photon absorbed by rhodopsin (or a cone opsin) to the enzyme phosphodiesterase. It is a heterotrimeric G protein: an α subunit (encoded by GNAT1 in rods and GNAT2 in cones) binds GTP when activated, while βγ subunits anchor the complex to the disc membrane.

Phototransduction

When light isomerises retinal in rhodopsin, the activated receptor catalyses GDP–GTP exchange on many transducin molecules, giving large amplification. GTP-bound α subunits activate cGMP phosphodiesterase (PDE6), which hydrolyses cyclic GMP; the resulting closure of cGMP-gated channels hyperpolarises the photoreceptor and reduces glutamate release. The RGS9–Gβ5 complex accelerates GTP hydrolysis to switch the response off.

Clinical relevance

Mutations in transducin subunits cause forms of congenital stationary night blindness and retinitis pigmentosa. A closely related G protein, gustducin, performs the analogous role in taste receptor cells.
