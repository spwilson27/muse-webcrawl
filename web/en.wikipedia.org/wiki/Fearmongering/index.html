Fearmongering

Fearmongering, or scaremongering, is the deliberate use of frightening claims, rumours or imagery — often exaggerated or unfounded — to influence public opinion, sell products, or win political advantage. It is a form of emotional manipulation related to the appeal to fear in rhetoric and propaganda.

Mechanisms

Fearmongering typically works by presenting a threat as imminent and severe while downplaying uncertainty and counter-evidence. Common forms include alarmist media coverage that singles out rare but vivid dangers (sometimes called "mean world syndrome" in cultivation theory), political campaigns warning that an opponent's victory will bring catastrophe, and commercial marketing that manufactures anxiety about health, safety or social status. Because fear narrows attention and increases reliance on authority, it can suppress scrutiny of the proposed remedy — which is usually the point.

Distinctions and criticism

The term is contested: warnings that are well supported by evidence, such as public-health alerts, are not ordinarily considered fearmongering, and the label is itself sometimes used rhetorically to dismiss legitimate concerns. Analysts therefore distinguish fearmongering by the proportionality of the warning to the evidence, the intent to manipulate, and whether the audience is given means of verifying the claim.
