Joe Heaney (Irish: Seosamh Ó hÉanaí; 1 November 1919 – 1 May 1984) was an Irish traditional singer from Carna, County Galway, regarded as one of the greatest exponents of sean-nós ("old style") singing in the Irish language. His repertoire, learned in the oral tradition of Connemara, numbered more than 500 songs, along with stories, lore, and historical anecdotes.

Life

Heaney grew up in the Irish-speaking village of Carna, in the Aird Thoir townland area, where singing was woven into work and social life. He won singing competitions in Ireland, worked in Scotland and London (where he was recorded by folk collectors and broadcast on the BBC), and emigrated to the United States in 1965, settling first in New York.

In America

In the U.S., Heaney became a towering figure of the folk revival, performing at festivals including the Newport Folk Festival and the Smithsonian Folklife Festival. From 1982 he was artist-in-residence in the ethnomusicology program at the University of Washington in Seattle, where his classes and performances were extensively recorded. He received a National Heritage Fellowship from the National Endowment for the Arts in 1982.

Style and legacy

Heaney's sean-nós singing was noted for its emotional restraint, rhythmic freedom, intricate melodic ornamentation, and his insistence that a song's story mattered more than vocal display — "the story is the thing." The annual Féile Chomórtha Joe Éinniú (Joe Heaney Festival) in Carna celebrates his legacy, and his recordings for labels such as Gael-Linn and Topic, plus extensive archival tapes, remain primary documents of the Connemara tradition.
