Muddy children puzzle

The muddy children puzzle is a classic problem in logic, game theory and epistemology illustrating the concept of common knowledge. It is closely related to the Cheryl's birthday problem genre and to the "cheating wives" or "muddy children" formulations used by logicians such as Robert Aumann.

The puzzle

A group of children play outside and some of them get mud on their foreheads. Each child can see the others' foreheads but not their own. Their father tells them: "At least one of you has mud on your forehead." He then repeatedly asks, in rounds, whether any child knows whether they themselves are muddy; a child who knows must step forward. All the children are perfect logicians and answer simultaneously in each round. Although every muddy child can already see that someone is muddy, all the children stay silent for several rounds — and then, if there are n muddy children, all n step forward together on round n.

Explanation

The father's announcement creates common knowledge of a fact everyone may already know. With one muddy child, that child sees no mud, so the announcement tells them it must be themselves, and they step forward in round 1. With two, each sees exactly one muddy child and expects that child to step forward in round 1; when that does not happen, each realises there must be a second muddy child — themselves — and both step forward in round 2. Induction extends the argument to any n. Without the father's public announcement, the nesting of mutual knowledge would not reach arbitrary depth, and the children could never deduce their state.

Significance

The puzzle is a standard illustration of the difference between mutual knowledge and common knowledge, and of inductive reasoning in dynamic epistemic logic, distributed computing (where it relates to the coordinated attack problem) and the theory of conventions.
