Progressive rock (radio format)

The progressive rock radio format, also known as underground rock, was a freeform radio format that flourished on FM stations in the United States in the late 1960s and 1970s. It gave disc jockeys wide freedom to play album tracks, longer songs, and lesser-known artists rather than the tightly formatted singles of Top 40 radio.

History

The format is often traced to Tom Donahue at KMPX in San Francisco in 1967, who began playing album cuts in a freeform style. Stations such as WNEW-FM in New York and KSAN in San Francisco became influential progressive outlets, championing artists like Pink Floyd, Led Zeppelin, and Jethro Tull. The format's name reflected the "progressive" music it favored—longer, more experimental rock album tracks.

Evolution

During the 1970s, the format gradually tightened into the more commercially structured album-oriented rock (AOR) format, with consultants, research, and fixed playlists, ending the era of DJ-driven freeform programming at most major stations.
