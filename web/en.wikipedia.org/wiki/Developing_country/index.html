Developing country

[Image: a busy street market in a rapidly growing city in the developing world]

A developing country is a nation with a lower standard of living, underdeveloped industrial base and low Human Development Index relative to other countries. The term overlaps with 'emerging market', 'less developed country' and the political label 'Global South'; the World Bank, which now classifies economies by gross national income per capita (low, lower-middle, upper-middle and high income), discourages the binary developed/developing split in its own publications.

Characteristics and debates

Common features include lower GDP per capita, larger agricultural and informal sectors, faster population growth, and weaker infrastructure and institutions — though the category spans Chad and China-sized economies as different as Bolivia and Malaysia. Development economics studies how such economies grow: industrialisation and export promotion (the East Asian model), import substitution, microcredit, foreign aid and investment, and the roles of education, health and governance. Successful developers 'graduate' — South Korea and Singapore are now high-income — while the 'middle-income trap' names the difficulty of the final stage. Usage of the term is increasingly contested as imprecise or patronising, but remains standard in trade law (special and differential treatment) and climate negotiations.
