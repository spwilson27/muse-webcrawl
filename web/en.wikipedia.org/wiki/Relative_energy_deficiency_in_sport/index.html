Relative energy deficiency in sport

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Relative energy deficiency in sport (RED-S, pronounced "reds") is a syndrome of impaired physiological functioning in athletes caused by low energy availability — a mismatch between dietary energy intake and the energy expended in exercise, leaving insufficient energy for normal bodily functions. The term was introduced by the International Olympic Committee in a 2014 consensus statement, broadening the earlier concept of the female athlete triad to recognise that male athletes are also affected and that many more systems than the triad's three (energy availability, menstrual function and bone health) are involved.

Health and performance effects

Low energy availability can impair metabolic rate, reproductive function, bone health, immunity, protein synthesis, cardiovascular and gastrointestinal function, and psychological health. Performance consequences include decreased endurance, muscle strength, glycogen stores, concentration and coordination, together with increased injury risk and impaired recovery. Causes may include inadvertent under-fuelling, high training loads, pressure to achieve a particular physique in lean, aesthetic or weight-class sports, and disordered eating or eating disorders.

Management

Assessment is commonly guided by the IOC's RED-S Clinical Assessment Tool, which categorises athletes by risk and determines participation recommendations. Treatment centres on restoring energy availability — increasing intake, reducing exercise load, or both — usually with a multidisciplinary team including sports physicians, dietitians and mental health professionals.
