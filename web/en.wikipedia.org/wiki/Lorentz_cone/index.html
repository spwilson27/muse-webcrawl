Lorentz cone

In linear algebra, the Lorentz cone (also called the second-order cone or ice cream cone, with the convex optimization literature usually preferring the term "second-order cone") is a convex cone defined by

C = { (x, t) ∈ ℝn+1 : ‖x‖ ≤ t },

where ‖·‖ is the Euclidean norm. Equivalently,

C = { (x, t) : xTx ≤ t2, t ≥ 0 }.

The name derives from the analogy with the light cone of Minkowski spacetime in Lorentz's theory of special relativity: under the Lorentz bilinear form, the set of vectors with non-positive "length" and non-negative time component forms exactly this cone.

Properties

The Lorentz cone is a self-dual cone: its dual cone equals itself. It is also a symmetric cone (a homogeneous, self-dual cone), and indeed the Lorentz cones are, along with the cones of positive semidefinite matrices and a few exceptional cases, the building blocks of all symmetric cones. The cone is proper: it is closed, convex, pointed (contains no line), and solid (has nonempty interior).

Second-order cone programming

Optimization problems in which one minimizes a linear function over the intersection of an affine subspace with the Cartesian product of Lorentz cones are called second-order cone programs (SOCPs). SOCPs can be solved efficiently by interior point methods and generalize both linear programs and convex quadratic programs. Many problems in engineering — including robust least squares, antenna design, and portfolio optimization with certain risk measures — can be cast as SOCPs. The Lorentz cone is representable with a self-concordant barrier function, which underpins the polynomial-time guarantees of these methods.
