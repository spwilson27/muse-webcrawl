Tekle Hawariat Tekle Maryam (Amharic: ተክለ ሐዋርያት ተክለ ማርያም; 1884–1977) was an Ethiopian diplomat, government official, and intellectual, regarded as one of the pioneers of modern Amharic literature. Educated abroad and fluent in several languages, he served Emperor Haile Selassie in a succession of senior posts and is best remembered as the author of Fabula: Yawreoch Commedia ("Fable: The Comedy of Animals"), the first European-style play written in Amharic, and as the principal drafter of Ethiopia's first written constitution in 1931.

Early life and education

Tekle Hawariat was born in Zege, on the western shore of Lake Tana. He received a traditional church education before travelling to Russia as a young man in the company of a Russian mission; there he studied military science, agriculture, and languages, gaining exposure to European literature and theatre. He later studied in other European countries, an unusual education for an Ethiopian of his generation that placed him among the small circle of foreign-educated reformers sometimes called the "Japanisers" for their admiration of Meiji Japan's modernisation.

Career

Returning to Ethiopia, Tekle Hawariat held posts including provincial governor, mayor of Addis Ababa, diplomat, and minister. He represented Ethiopia at the League of Nations and worked to modernise administration and agriculture. Emperor Haile Selassie entrusted him with drafting the 1931 constitution, Ethiopia's first, modelled in part on the Meiji constitution of Japan. His reformist zeal sometimes brought him into conflict at court, and his later years were spent partly in internal exile and retirement at Hirna in the east of the country.

Literary work

His play Fabula, written around 1912–13 and published in 1921, uses animal characters in the manner of Aesop and La Fontaine to satirise the ignorance and corruption of the Ethiopian court and nobility. Because of its perceived criticism of the ruling establishment, it was suppressed and could not be performed publicly for decades; it is now recognised as a foundational work of modern Ethiopian drama and a landmark of Amharic letters.
