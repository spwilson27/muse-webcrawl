Hyde Park is a 350-acre (142-hectare) Royal Park in central London, England. One of the largest of London's parks and the largest of the Royal Parks, it is famous for Speakers' Corner and for major concerts and events. The park is contiguous with Kensington Gardens, separated from it by the Serpentine lake and the Serpentine Bridge.

History

The land was acquired by Henry VIII from Westminster Abbey in 1536 and used as a royal hunting ground. Charles I opened the park to the public in 1637. The Serpentine, an artificial lake, was created in the 1730s for Queen Caroline. The park hosted the Great Exhibition of 1851, whose Crystal Palace stood on its southern side before being moved to Sydenham. Hyde Park Corner, at its south-east, features monumental gateways and the Wellington Arch.

Since the 19th century the north-east corner, Speakers' Corner, has been a traditional site for public oratory and protest, associated with figures from Karl Marx to the Suffragettes. An 1872 Act of Parliament protected the right to speak there.

Features and events

Landmarks include the Diana, Princess of Wales Memorial Fountain, the Holocaust Memorial, the 7 July Memorial, and the Serpentine Gallery on the Kensington Gardens side. The park hosts large open-air concerts — including historic performances by the Rolling Stones (1969) and Queen (1976) — and the annual British Summer Time festival. During the 2012 Summer Olympics it hosted the triathlon and open-water swimming. Each winter it hosts the Winter Wonderland fair.
