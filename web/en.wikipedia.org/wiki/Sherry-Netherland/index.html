The Sherry-Netherland is a 38-story apartment hotel located at 781 Fifth Avenue on the corner of East 59th Street in Manhattan, New York City, overlooking Grand Army Plaza and the southeastern entrance to Central Park. Completed in 1927, it is one of the city's landmark pre-war residential hotels.

History and architecture

The building was designed by Schultze & Weaver, with Buchman & Kahn, for Louis Sherry, the restaurateur and hotelier, on the site of the former Netherland Hotel. Its neo-Romanesque and neo-Gothic tower, capped by a steep slate roof and a small spire, is a prominent feature of the Fifth Avenue skyline. At the time of completion it was among the tallest apartment hotels in the world. The ground floor long housed a Louis Sherry restaurant, and the lobby is noted for its vaulted, richly decorated ceilings modelled on the Vatican's Raphael Loggias.

Residences

The upper floors contain a mix of hotel rooms and cooperative apartments; the building was converted in part to a cooperative in the 1950s. Its full-floor and multi-room residences have been home to many prominent figures, and its location at the southeast corner of Central Park places it among the most prestigious addresses in New York.
