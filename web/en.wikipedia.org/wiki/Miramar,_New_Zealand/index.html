Miramar, New Zealand

Miramar is a suburb of Wellington, New Zealand, located on the Miramar Peninsula at the southeastern end of the city. It occupies the isthmus and hills between Evans Bay and Lyall Bay, and is separated from the city's central suburbs by Mount Victoria and the town belt ridge near the suburb of Hataitai.

The Miramar Peninsula was once an island known in Māori tradition as Motu Kairangi; it became joined to the mainland as the land rose following earthquakes, notably the great Wairarapa earthquake of 1855. European settlement followed, and the area became known for industry as well as housing.

Film industry

Miramar is internationally known as the home of director Peter Jackson's film operations, including Weta Workshop and Weta Digital (now Wētā FX), and associated facilities such as Park Road Post Production and Stone Street Studios. The concentration of production companies in the suburb earned it the nickname "Wellywood". Wellington Airport occupies reclaimed flat land immediately south of the peninsula's neck.

Demographics

The suburb has a population of several thousand and is served by Miramar Central School and other local schools, with State Highway 1 passing nearby through the airport corridor.
