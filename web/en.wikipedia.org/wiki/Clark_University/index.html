Clark University

Clark University is a private research university in Worcester, Massachusetts, United States. Founded in 1887 by industrialist Jonas Gilman Clark, it was one of the first modern research universities in the United States, initially offering only graduate study. Clark is known for its programs in psychology and geography, and as the site, in 1909, of Sigmund Freud's only lectures in America.

History

Clark opened in 1889 under founding president G. Stanley Hall, a pioneer of American psychology who also founded the American Psychological Association in 1892. In September 1909, to mark the university's twentieth anniversary, Hall invited Sigmund Freud and Carl Jung to lecture; Freud's five "Clark lectures" introduced psychoanalysis to an American academic audience. Clark's Graduate School of Geography, founded in 1921, is the oldest sustained geography program in the United States. Physicist Robert H. Goddard, the father of modern rocketry, was a Clark professor and performed early rocket research nearby.

Academics

Clark enrolls roughly 2,200 undergraduates and 1,000 graduate students. Its motto in teaching has emphasized liberal education with research engagement, and the university is a member of the Association of American Universities since 2019 in the company of leading research institutions.
