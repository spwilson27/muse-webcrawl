The National Intelligence Service (Spanish: Servicio de Inteligencia Nacional, SIN) was Peru's principal intelligence agency. It became notorious in the 1990s as the power base of Vladimiro Montesinos, the shadowy adviser to President Alberto Fujimori, who used the SIN to run a vast network of surveillance, bribery and repression. It was dissolved in 2001 after the collapse of the Fujimori government.

Under Montesinos

Formally headed by a military chief, the SIN was in practice controlled by Montesinos, who held the title of adviser. From the SIN's headquarters in Lima he directed the fight against the Shining Path and MRTA insurgencies — including support for the Grupo Colina death squad — while simultaneously spying on opposition politicians, journalists and judges, tapping thousands of telephones, and funnelling state money to buy media outlets, congressmen and officials. Montesinos compulsively videotaped his meetings; after one of these "Vladivideos" showing a bribe being paid was broadcast in September 2000, the regime unravelled within weeks. Fujimori announced new elections, sacked Montesinos and deactivated the SIN before fleeing the country.

Dissolution and succession

The transitional government of Valentín Paniagua formally dissolved the SIN in 2001. Montesinos was captured in Venezuela, extradited, and convicted in a long series of trials for corruption, human rights abuses and arms trafficking, receiving an effective sentence of 25 years. Peru's intelligence function was rebuilt in the 2000s under the National Intelligence Directorate (DINI), created with statutory oversight intended to prevent a repetition of the SIN era.
