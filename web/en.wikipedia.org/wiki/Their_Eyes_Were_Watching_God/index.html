Their Eyes Were Watching God

Their Eyes Were Watching God

Author: Zora Neale Hurston

Published: 18 September 1937

Publisher: J. B. Lippincott

Setting: Florida (Eatonville, the Everglades), early 20th century

Their Eyes Were Watching God is a novel by the American writer Zora Neale Hurston, published in 1937. Written in Haiti in about seven weeks while Hurston was conducting anthropological fieldwork, it is now regarded as her masterpiece and a classic of African-American and feminist literature, although it was out of print for decades before its rediscovery in the 1970s.

Plot

The novel is framed as the life story told by Janie Crawford, a Black woman in her forties, to her friend Pheoby. Raised by her grandmother Nanny in Florida, Janie is married off to the farmer Logan Killicks, then runs away with the ambitious Joe "Jody" Starks, who becomes mayor and storekeeper of the all-Black town of Eatonville — but silences and controls her. Widowed, Janie finds love with the younger Tea Cake Woods and works beside him in the Everglades "muck". During the great Okeechobee hurricane of 1928, Tea Cake is bitten by a rabid dog while saving Janie; driven mad by rabies, he attacks her, and she shoots him in self-defence. Acquitted by a jury, Janie returns to Eatonville, at peace and having found her voice and horizon.

Themes and reception

The novel is celebrated for its use of African-American vernacular in dialogue alongside lyrical "free indirect" narration, and for its themes of voice, self-realisation, love and independence. Contemporary reviewers such as Richard Wright criticised it for avoiding social protest, but the feminist and academic revival led by Alice Walker restored it to the canon; it is now among the most widely taught American novels. A television film adaptation produced by Oprah Winfrey, starring Halle Berry, aired in 2005.
