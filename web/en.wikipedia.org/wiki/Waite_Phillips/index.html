Waite Phillips

Born: 19 January 1883, Conway, Iowa, United States

Died: 27 January 1964, Los Angeles, California

Occupation: Oil businessman, philanthropist

Known for: Philmont Scout Ranch, Philbrook Museum of Art

Waite Phillips (19 January 1883 – 27 January 1964) was an American petroleum businessman and philanthropist who built an independent oil company in Oklahoma and is remembered for donating the Philmont Scout Ranch to the Boy Scouts of America and for founding the Philbrook Museum of Art in Tulsa.

Business career

Phillips grew up on an Iowa farm and, with his twin brother Wiate, worked his way west; after Wiate's death in 1902 he joined his brothers Frank and L. E. Phillips in their Bartlesville, Oklahoma banking and oil ventures, which became Phillips Petroleum. Waite later struck out independently, assembling the Waite Phillips Company, an integrated petroleum firm with production, refining and marketing. In 1925 he sold the company to a New York investment syndicate for a sum reported at about $25 million, then in his early forties, and devoted much of the rest of his life to ranching and philanthropy.

Philanthropy

In New Mexico, Phillips assembled a ranch of more than 300,000 acres near Cimarron, building the Villa Philmonte as his summer home. Between 1938 and 1941 he donated some 127,000 acres of the ranch, together with its headquarters, to the Boy Scouts of America, creating what became Philmont Scout Ranch, the largest youth camp in the world, visited by generations of Scouts for high-adventure backpacking treks. In Tulsa he built the Italianate Villa Philbrook (1927), which he donated to the city in 1938 along with its 23 acres of gardens; it opened in 1939 as the Philbrook Museum of Art. Phillips also gave generously to the University of Tulsa and other Oklahoma institutions.
