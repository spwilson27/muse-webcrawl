Pisco sour
Base spiritPisco
IngredientsPisco, lime juice, simple syrup, egg white, Angostura bitters
OriginLima, Peru (disputed with Chile)
IB A categoryNew Era Drink

The pisco sour is a cocktail made with pisco — a grape brandy produced in Peru and Chile — lime juice, simple syrup, egg white, and a dash of bitters, shaken to produce a characteristic frothy top. Both Peru and Chile claim it as a national drink, and its origin is a matter of lively dispute between the two countries.

History

The drink is generally credited to Victor Vaughen Morris, an American bartender who opened Morris' Bar in Lima in 1916 and served a whisky-sour-style drink made with pisco. His Peruvian bartender Mario Bruiget is said to have perfected the modern recipe, adding egg white and bitters, at the Hotel Maury in the 1920s. Chile's claim rests on a similar drink documented in Valparaíso and on Elliot Stubb, an English steward said to have mixed pisco with lime in Iquique in 1872.

Preparation

The Peruvian version uses Peruvian pisco, Key lime juice, syrup, egg white, and Amargo Chuncho or Angostura bitters; the Chilean version typically uses Chilean pisco and pica lime and may omit the bitters. Peru celebrates a national Pisco Sour Day on the first Saturday of February.
