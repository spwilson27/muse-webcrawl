Rugbrød

Rugbrød
Type: Sourdough rye bread
Origin: Denmark
Main ingredients: Rye flour, cracked rye grains, seeds, sourdough

Rugbrød is a traditional Danish sourdough rye bread. It is a dark, dense, rectangular loaf made from rye flour, cracked or whole rye grains and often sunflower, flax or pumpkin seeds, leavened with sourdough and sometimes sweetened slightly with malt syrup.

Characteristics and use

Rugbrød has been a staple of the Danish diet for centuries and is eaten daily by a large share of the population. It forms the base of smørrebrød, the open-faced sandwiches topped with pickled herring, liver pâté (leverpostej), cheese, egg or cold meats that are a hallmark of Danish cuisine. The bread is sliced very thin, has a moist, chewy crumb with a mildly sour, malty flavour, and keeps well for days.

Nutritionally, rugbrød is high in dietary fibre and whole grains and relatively low in fat. Closely related dark rye breads are found across northern Europe, including German Pumpernickel and Roggenbrot and the seeded Sigtebrød found elsewhere in Scandinavia, though Danish rugbrød is distinctive for its sourdough base and abundance of cracked grains.

History

Rye has been cultivated in Denmark since the Iron Age, and sourdough rye bread became the everyday bread of peasants and townspeople alike because rye grows well in the Danish climate. Industrial bakeries in the 19th and 20th centuries standardised the loaf while home and bakery traditions preserved regional variations, including sweetened and seed-rich varieties.
