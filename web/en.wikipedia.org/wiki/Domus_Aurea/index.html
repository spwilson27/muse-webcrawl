Domus Aurea

The Domus Aurea (Latin for "Golden House") was a vast landscaped palace complex built by the emperor Nero in the heart of ancient Rome after the Great Fire of AD 64 destroyed much of the city.

Domus Aurea
Location: Oppian Hill, Rome
Built: AD 64–68
Commissioned by: Nero
Architects: Severus and Celer
Type: Imperial palace

Description

The complex covered perhaps 100–300 acres between the Palatine and Esquiline hills, with vineyards, woods, an artificial lake (later the site of the Colosseum), and a pavilion whose rooms were sheathed in gold, ivory, gems and mother-of-pearl. Suetonius describes a rotating dining room and a colossal 35-metre bronze statue of Nero, the Colossus Neronis. Painters such as Fabullus covered walls with the delicate grottesche frescoes that later inspired Renaissance artists.

After Nero

After Nero's death in 68, his successors sought to erase his memory: the lake was drained for the Flavian Amphitheatre, and Trajan built his baths over the palace, filling its rooms with earth — which preserved the frescoes. Rediscovered in the Renaissance when artists lowered themselves through holes in the vaults (calling them grotte), the palace has suffered water damage and remains under long-term conservation, with limited guided access.
