Church of the Light

The Church of the Light (Japanese: 茨木春日丘教会, Ibaraki Kasugaoka Kyōkai) is a small Christian church in Ibaraki, Osaka Prefecture, Japan, designed by architect Tadao Ando and completed in 1989. Built for a congregation of the United Church of Christ in Japan on a very small budget, it is one of the most celebrated works of modern sacred architecture, famous for the cruciform slit of light cut into its concrete east wall.

Design

The church consists of a rectangular concrete box, about 6 by 6 by 18 metres, intersected diagonally by a freestanding wall set at a 15-degree angle that slices through the volume and creates the entrance sequence. Behind the altar, a cross-shaped opening is cut through the thick concrete wall; daylight streaming through this void in the otherwise dark interior forms a glowing cross that changes with the hour and the seasons — Ando's "church of light". The floor slopes gently down toward the altar, and furnishings such as the pews and floorboards were made from inexpensive scaffolding planks, in keeping with the congregation's limited funds.

Ando initially proposed leaving the cross open to the weather without glass, but the congregation insisted on glazing. The stark, unornamented concrete and the dramatic contrast of darkness and light express Ando's belief that architecture should heighten the perception of nature and the sacred through abstraction.

Later additions and significance

In 1999 Ando added a small Sunday school annex beside the church, designed in a similar language. The Church of the Light, together with the Church on the Water (1988) in Hokkaido, established Ando's international reputation in sacred architecture and remains among the most visited and studied small buildings of the late 20th century, a canonical example of minimalist concrete architecture.
