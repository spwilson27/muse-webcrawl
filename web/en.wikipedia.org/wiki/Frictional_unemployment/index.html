Frictional unemployment

Frictional unemployment
TypeUnemployment
CauseTime spent between jobs; matching process
RelatedNatural rate of unemployment

Frictional unemployment is a form of unemployment reflecting the time period between jobs when a worker is searching for, or transitioning from one job to another. It is sometimes called search unemployment and can be based on the circumstances of the individual. Frictional unemployment is always present in an economy, even at full employment, because workers are constantly moving between jobs, entering the workforce for the first time, or re-entering it.

Unlike structural unemployment, which results from a mismatch between workers' skills and the skills demanded by employers, frictional unemployment arises even when suitable vacancies exist, simply because job search and matching take time. Unlike cyclical unemployment, it is not caused by downturns in the business cycle.

Causes

Workers may leave jobs voluntarily to seek better opportunities, relocate, or change careers. New graduates enter the labour market and spend time searching. Information about vacancies and candidates is imperfect, so matching workers to appropriate jobs is not instantaneous. Seasonal work also contributes, as workers in industries such as agriculture, tourism and construction move between short-term engagements.

Policy

Policies intended to reduce frictional unemployment include improving the flow of information about vacancies — for example through job centres, employment agencies and online job boards — thereby shortening search times. Unemployment insurance has an ambiguous effect: it cushions the cost of job loss but may lengthen search duration by reducing the urgency of accepting a job offer.
