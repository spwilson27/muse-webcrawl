Computational science

Computational science, also known as scientific computing, technical computing or scientific computation (SC), is a division of science that uses advanced computing capabilities to understand and solve complex physical problems. It combines computer science, applied mathematics, and domain sciences, and is often described as a third pillar of scientific inquiry alongside theory and experiment.

Methods

Computational scientists construct mathematical models, develop numerical algorithms, and run simulations on computers ranging from workstations to supercomputers. Core techniques include numerical solution of differential equations, Monte Carlo methods, optimisation, and linear algebra on large systems. Results are checked through verification and validation against theory and experiment.

Applications

Applications are found across the sciences and engineering: computational fluid dynamics for aircraft and weather prediction; molecular dynamics and quantum chemistry; climate modelling; astrophysical simulation; computational biology and drug discovery; and computational finance. The growing role of data has linked computational science closely with data science and machine learning.

Education

Because the field is inherently interdisciplinary, computational science programmes typically combine training in a scientific discipline with numerical methods, programming, and high-performance computing.
