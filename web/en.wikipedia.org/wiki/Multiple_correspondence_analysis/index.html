Multiple correspondence analysis

Multiple correspondence analysis (MCA) is a data-analysis technique for exploring patterns in categorical data, generalizing correspondence analysis from two variables to many. It was developed largely within the French school of data analysis associated with Jean-Paul Benzécri.

Method

MCA operates on an indicator matrix (or equivalently a Burt matrix) recording, for each individual, the categories taken on each variable. Applying correspondence analysis yields a low-dimensional "factorial map" in which individuals and categories are plotted together: categories frequently chosen by the same individuals appear near one another, and dimensions of the map summarize the main structures of association, measured as inertia.

Applications

MCA is widely used in the social sciences, notably in the work of Pierre Bourdieu, who used its maps to display "social space" in Distinction (1979). It is also used in marketing, epidemiology, and survey research to visualize associations among many categorical variables and to identify clusters of respondents.
