Bunched logic

A "bunch" — a tree structure of contexts in bunched logic

Bunched logic, or the logic of bunched implications (BI), is a substructural logic introduced by Peter O'Hearn and David Pym in the 1990s. It combines the multiplicative connectives of linear logic with the additive connectives of intuitionistic logic in a single system, using proof contexts called "bunches" that are trees whose internal nodes are of two kinds.

Bunches and connectives

In BI there are two conjunctions and two implications. The multiplicative conjunction (∗), read "star", combines resources that must be kept separate; its implication (−∗), the "magic wand", is the hallmark connective of separation logic. The additive conjunction (∧) and implication (→) behave as in ordinary intuitionistic logic, sharing context. Bunches are built from formulas and units using a multiplicative comma and an additive semicolon, and structural rules allow coherent rearrangement within each kind of node.

Semantics

BI has both algebraic and categorical semantics, notably in terms of resource monoids and Day convolution, and Kripke-style semantics over partial commutative monoids, in which truth of A ∗ B means the resource can be split into a part satisfying A and a part satisfying B.

Applications

BI is the logical foundation of separation logic, the program logic used to reason about heap-manipulating programs, which underlies industrial verification tools such as Facebook's Infer. BI has also been applied to reasoning about memory, information flow, and access control.
