Old Dongola (Old Nubian: ⲧⲟⲩⲅⲅⲟⲩⲗ, Tungul; Arabic: دنقلا العجوز) is a deserted town in what is now Northern State, Sudan, on the eastern bank of the Nile opposite the modern town of Dongola. Founded in the 5th century as a fortress, it became the capital of the Christian Nubian kingdom of Makuria.

History

Dongola reached its zenith between the 9th and 11th centuries as a wealthy trading city of churches, monasteries and palatial houses with wall paintings of outstanding quality. The famous Throne Hall of the Makurian kings, a monumental two-storey building, was converted into a mosque in 1317 after the Mamluk-backed Muslim takeover — it is regarded as the oldest preserved mosque in Sudan. The city declined through the 13th and 14th centuries and was abandoned in favour of the new town of Dongola downstream.

Archaeology

Polish archaeological missions from the Polish Centre of Mediterranean Archaeology have excavated Old Dongola since 1964. Discoveries include the cruciform church, the Monastery of St Anthony with its cycle of wall paintings, royal tombs, and extensive domestic quarters; the site is considered the most important record of medieval Christian Nubia.
