Delville Wood

Delville Wood (Bois d'Elville) is a wood near the village of Longueval in the Somme department of northern France. It was the scene of one of the most famous and costly engagements of the Battle of the Somme in July 1916, in which the South African Brigade held the wood against repeated German attacks.

The battle

During the Somme offensive, British forces needed to capture Delville Wood to protect the flank of the advance on High Wood. The 1st South African Infantry Brigade, about 3,150 strong, took most of the wood on 15 July 1916 and held it for five days under intense shellfire and counter-attacks, at times fighting hand to hand among the shattered trees. When relieved on 20 July, only about 750 men remained unwounded; the wood's rides and clearings were named after streets in London and Edinburgh by the troops who fought there.

Memorial

The site now contains the Delville Wood South African National Memorial, designed by Sir Herbert Baker and unveiled in 1926, together with a museum and the Delville Wood War Cemetery. Of the wood's trees, only one original hornbeam survived the war; its descendants and replanted oaks grown from South African acorns now cover the site.
