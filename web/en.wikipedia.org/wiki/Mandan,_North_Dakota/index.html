Mandan, North Dakota

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mandan is a city in and the county seat of Morton County, North Dakota, United States. The population was 24,206 at the 2020 census. Mandan was founded in 1879 on the west bank of the Missouri River, across from Bismarck, the state capital, with which it forms the Bismarck–Mandan metropolitan area.

History

The city is named for the Mandan Native American people who lived in earth-lodge villages along the Missouri for centuries; Lewis and Clark wintered near the Mandan villages at Fort Mandan in 1804–05. The town grew with the arrival of the Northern Pacific Railway, whose bridge across the Missouri opened in 1882.

Geography and economy

Mandan has a continental climate with cold winters and warm summers. Its economy is based on agriculture, an oil refinery, rail transport and services for the state government in Bismarck. Nearby attractions include Fort Abraham Lincoln State Park, site of a reconstructed Mandan village and Custer's last command post.
