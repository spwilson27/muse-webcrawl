The 2010 FIFA World Cup was the 19th FIFA World Cup, the world championship for men's national football teams. It took place in South Africa from 11 June to 11 July 2010 — the first time the tournament was held on the African continent. Spain, the European champions, defeated the Netherlands 1–0 after extra time in the final to win their first world title, becoming the eighth nation to win the tournament and the first European team to win a World Cup held outside Europe.

Tournament summary

The final at Soccer City in Johannesburg was decided in the 116th minute by Andrés Iniesta, who dedicated the goal to his late friend Dani Jarque. The match was notorious for the Netherlands' physical play, with English referee Howard Webb issuing 14 yellow cards, a World Cup final record. Germany beat Uruguay 3–2 in the third-place play-off; Uruguay's Diego Forlán was voted the tournament's best player.

The tournament was marked by the sound of the vuvuzela, the official Jabulani ball — criticised by players for its unpredictable flight — and by several refereeing controversies, including Frank Lampard's disallowed "goal" against Germany that crossed the line, which helped spur FIFA's eventual adoption of goal-line technology. Hosts South Africa became the first host nation eliminated in the group stage, despite beating France, whose squad had mutinied against coach Raymond Domenech. Defending champions Italy also exited in the group stage. Paul the Octopus, a German aquarium octopus, achieved celebrity by "predicting" the results of eight matches, including the final.

Squads and legacy

Spain's victory — built on the tiki-taka possession style of a golden generation including Xavi, Iniesta, Iker Casillas, and David Villa — completed a run of three consecutive major titles (Euro 2008, World Cup 2010, Euro 2012). The tournament cost South Africa an estimated R33 billion in stadium and infrastructure investment and is generally judged to have been an organisational and symbolic success for the host nation.
