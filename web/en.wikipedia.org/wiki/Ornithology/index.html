Ornithology

Ornithology is a branch of zoology that concerns the study of birds — their evolution, anatomy, physiology, behaviour, ecology, distribution and taxonomy. The word derives from the Greek ornis ("bird") and logos ("study"). Ornithology has a long history and studies on birds have helped develop several key concepts in evolution, behaviour and ecology, such as the definition of species, speciation, instinct, learning, ecological niches, island biogeography and conservation biology.

Bird ringing (banding) is a core ornithological technique for studying movement and survival.

History

Observations of birds date to antiquity — Aristotle's History of Animals recorded migration and anatomy — but ornithology became a science in the 18th and 19th centuries with systematic collectors and describers such as John Ray, Carl Linnaeus, John Gould and John James Audubon, whose Birds of America (1827–1838) combined art and natural history. Charles Darwin's study of Galápagos finches informed his theory of evolution by natural selection. In the 20th century the field shifted from specimen collecting to studying living birds, exemplified by Margaret Morse Nice's song sparrow studies, Konrad Lorenz's ethology, David Lack's work on ecology and evolution, and Ernst Mayr's systematics. Learned societies such as the British Ornithologists' Union (founded 1858) and the American Ornithologists' Union (1883) publish leading journals including Ibis and The Auk.

Techniques

Field methods include observation with binoculars and telescopes, mist-netting and ringing (banding) to track individuals, nest monitoring, and census methods such as point counts and territory mapping. Laboratory and modern techniques include DNA analysis, stable-isotope studies of migration, satellite telemetry and geolocators, bioacoustics, and radar ornithology. Large-scale citizen-science programmes — the Christmas Bird Count (begun 1900), the Breeding Bird Survey and the eBird platform — supply data on population trends used in conservation.

Applications

Ornithology informs conservation of threatened species, management of game and pest birds, assessment of environmental change (birds are widely used indicator species), and aviation safety. Economic and cultural connections range from falconry and aviculture to ecotourism and birdwatching, one of the most popular wildlife pastimes in many countries.
