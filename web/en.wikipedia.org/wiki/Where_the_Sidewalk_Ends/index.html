Where the Sidewalk Ends

Where the Sidewalk Ends is a 1974 children's poetry collection by American author and illustrator Shel Silverstein. It was his first collection of poetry for children and has become one of the best-selling children's books of all time.

Contents

The book contains more than 130 poems and drawings, written in Silverstein's loose, playful verse and illustrated in his distinctive black-and-white line style. Its poems celebrate imagination, mischief and the child's point of view: children who refuse to take out the garbage, a girl who eats a whale, a boy who turns into a television set, and the title poem, in which the sidewalk ends and the world of imagination begins.

Well-known poems in the collection include "Sarah Cynthia Sylvia Stout Would Not Take the Garbage Out", "Sick", "Hug O' War", "The Boa Constrictor", and "Where the Sidewalk Ends" itself.

Reception

The book has sold millions of copies and is a staple of school and home libraries. Some of its poems have been challenged for supposedly encouraging disobedience, but critics have generally praised its humour, inventiveness and respect for children's intelligence. It was followed by A Light in the Attic (1981) and Falling Up (1996).
