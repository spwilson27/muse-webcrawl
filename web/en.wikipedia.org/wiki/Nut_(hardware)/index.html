Nut (hardware)

A nut is a type of fastener with a threaded hole. Nuts are almost always used in conjunction with a mating bolt to fasten multiple parts together. The two partners are kept together by a combination of their threads' friction, a slight stretching of the bolt, and compression of the parts to be joined.

Types

The most common shape is the hex nut, with six flat sides that can be gripped by a wrench or spanner. Other common types include:

  • Wing nuts, with projections that allow tightening by hand;
  • Lock nuts, designed to resist loosening under vibration, including nylon-insert nuts;
  • Cap nuts (acorn nuts), which cover the exposed end of a bolt;
  • Square nuts, an older form with four sides;
  • Tee nuts and cage nuts, used in woodworking and sheet-metal enclosures respectively.

Materials and sizes

Nuts are most commonly made of steel, often zinc-plated or galvanised to resist corrosion; stainless steel, brass, and nylon nuts are used where corrosion resistance, appearance, or electrical insulation matter. Thread sizes follow standards such as the ISO metric thread system and the Unified Thread Standard used in North America.
