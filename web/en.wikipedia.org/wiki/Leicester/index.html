Leicester is a city, unitary authority area and the county town of Leicestershire, in the East Midlands of England, lying on the River Soar and on the western edge of the National Forest. It is one of the oldest cities in England, with a continuous history extending back some two thousand years. In the 2021 census the city's population was about 369,000, making it the largest city in the East Midlands by some measures when its urban area is considered, and among the most ethnically diverse cities in the United Kingdom.

History

The Roman town of Ratae Corieltauvorum occupied the site, of which the Jewry Wall and extensive mosaics survive. In the Anglo-Saxon and Viking periods Leicester was one of the Five Boroughs of the Danelaw. In the medieval era it was associated with Simon de Montfort, Earl of Leicester, and with the Parliament of Bats of 1426. Richard III was killed at the Battle of Bosworth Field nearby in 1485 and was buried in Greyfriars Church; his remains were rediscovered beneath a city car park in 2012 and reinterred in Leicester Cathedral in 2015, drawing international attention.

Economy and culture

Leicester grew wealthy in the nineteenth century on hosiery, knitwear, boots and shoes, and engineering; the hosiery magnate Thomas Cook organised his first railway excursion from Leicester in 1841, founding the travel firm that bore his name. After the Second World War, immigrants from South Asia transformed the city's textile industry and culture; the Golden Mile on Belgrave Road is a centre of Indian jewellery and food businesses, and Leicester's Diwali celebrations are among the largest outside India. The city is home to the University of Leicester, De Montfort University, the National Space Centre, and the King Power Stadium, home of Leicester City F.C., whose 5,000-to-1 Premier League title win in 2016 is considered one of the greatest sporting upsets. Leicester Tigers are one of England's leading rugby union clubs.
