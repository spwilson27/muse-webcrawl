Tipler cylinder


A rotating massive cylinder drags spacetime around it

The Tipler cylinder, also called a Tipler time machine, is a hypothetical object theorized to allow time travel into the past. It is a solution to the equations of general relativity proposed by Willem Jacob van Stockum in 1936 and rediscovered and analyzed by Frank J. Tipler in 1974. The object is an infinitely long, extremely dense cylinder rotating around its longitudinal axis.

Mechanism

Rotation of the cylinder produces frame-dragging: spacetime near the cylinder is twisted in the direction of rotation. For a sufficiently massive and rapidly rotating cylinder, the light cones in the surrounding region tip over so far that some paths through spacetime become closed timelike curves — trajectories that loop back to their own past. An object following such a path could, in principle, return to an earlier time.

Feasibility

The classical solution requires a cylinder of infinite length, since a finite cylinder was shown by Tipler himself to involve singularities, and the needed densities approach those of neutron stars with surface speeds exceeding half the speed of light. Stephen Hawking's chronology protection conjecture argues that quantum effects would destroy any would-be time machine before it could be used, making practical construction impossible.
