Libertador Building

Libertador Building
Location: Buenos Aires, Argentina
Completed: 1943
Function: Headquarters of the Argentine Ministry of Defense and Army

The Libertador Building (Spanish: Edificio Libertador) is a monumental government building in the Monserrat neighbourhood of Buenos Aires, Argentina. It houses the Argentine Ministry of Defense and the headquarters of the Argentine Army.

History and architecture

Designed in the French neoclassical style by architect Carlos Pibernat, the building was constructed between 1938 and 1943 during the presidency of Roberto María Ortiz and Agustín Pedro Justo's era of military building programmes. Its vast limestone-clad façade, mansard roof, and symmetrical wings were modelled on French public architecture, and it occupies an entire block facing Paseo Colón avenue near the Casa Rosada.

Name

Originally named for the Ministry of War, the building was renamed in 1950 to honour General José de San Martín, the Libertador (Liberator) of Argentina, Chile, and Peru, on the centenary of his death.
