Hypocorism

A hypocorism is a word or pet name used to show affection for a person or object, typically a shortened or diminutive form of a name — for example, "Bob" from Robert, "Becky" from Rebecca, or "doggy" for a dog. The term derives from Greek hypokorisma, from hypokorizesthai, "to use child-talk".

Formation

Languages form hypocorisms by shortening (Sam from Samuel), adding affectionate suffixes (English -y/-ie, Spanish -ito/-ita, Russian diminutive suffixes), reduplication, or altering sounds. Chinese, Japanese and Korean pet names often repeat a syllable of the given name.

Use

Hypocorisms signal intimacy and informality and are common within families and between friends. They differ from ordinary abbreviations and from pejorative nicknames, though the boundary can shift with context.
