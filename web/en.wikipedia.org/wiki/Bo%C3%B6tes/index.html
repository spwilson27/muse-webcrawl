Boötes is a constellation in the northern sky, located between 0° and +60° declination and 13 and 16 hours of right ascension. Its name comes from the Greek Βοώτης, "herdsman" or "ploughman". It contains Arcturus, the fourth-brightest star in the night sky.

Features

Arcturus, an orange giant, anchors the constellation's distinctive kite- or cone-shaped asterism. Other notable stars include Izar (ε Boötis), a well-known double star, and Muphrid (η Boötis). Boötes also contains the globular cluster NGC 5466 and several galaxies, though it is relatively sparse in deep-sky objects.

Boötes Void

The constellation gives its name to the Boötes Void, an enormous roughly spherical region of space about 330 million light-years in diameter containing very few galaxies, discovered in 1981. It is one of the largest known voids in the universe.

Meteor showers and notable objects

The January Bootids and the Quadrantids (named for the obsolete constellation Quadrans Muralis, once part of this region) radiate from Boötes. The nearby star Tau Boötis hosts one of the first exoplanets discovered around a sun-like star, a hot Jupiter.
