Central Bank of Kuwait

The Central Bank of Kuwait (CBK; Arabic: بنك الكويت المركزي) is the central bank of the State of Kuwait. It issues the Kuwaiti dinar, conducts monetary policy, and supervises the country's banking and financial system. Its headquarters are in Kuwait City.

History

The Central Bank of Kuwait was established by Law No. 32 of 1968 and began operations on 1 April 1969, succeeding the Kuwait Currency Board, which had been set up in 1960 to issue the national currency following Kuwait's independence. The CBK initially took over the issuance of the Kuwaiti dinar, which had replaced the Gulf rupee in 1961.

Functions

Under its establishing law, as amended, the bank's principal functions include issuing and managing the national currency, directing monetary and credit policy to support economic growth, regulating and supervising banks, Islamic financial institutions, exchange companies and other financial entities, managing the state's foreign reserves, and acting as banker and financial adviser to the government. The Kuwaiti dinar is conventionally pegged to an undisclosed weighted basket of currencies; between 2003 and 2007 it was pegged to the US dollar before reverting to the basket arrangement.

Governance

The bank is headed by a governor, appointed by Amiri decree for a renewable five-year term, and a board of directors that includes representatives of the Ministry of Finance, the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, and members drawn from the banking and commercial sectors.

Headquarters

The bank's current headquarters, a landmark tower in the Sharq district of Kuwait City topped by a distinctive pyramid-like structure, was inaugurated in 2017.
