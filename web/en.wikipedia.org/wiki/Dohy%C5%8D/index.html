Dohyō

A dohyō (土俵) is the ring in which sumo wrestling bouts are held. Modern dohyō are made of a raised square platform of clay, 6.7 m on a side and about 60 cm high, with a circle 4.55 m (15 shaku) in diameter formed by partially embedded rice-straw bales (tawara) in the centre.

Construction and ritual

The dohyō is rebuilt for each professional tournament by the yobidashi (ushers), who pack the clay and embed the bales; sacred items — salt, chestnuts, dried squid, kelp, rice and nutmeg — are buried in the centre in a Shinto consecration ritual, and the ring is purified with salt and sake. Two white lines (shikiri-sen) in the centre mark the wrestlers' starting positions.

The tsuriyane

Above the dohyō of a professional venue hangs a Shinto-style roof, the tsuriyane, from whose corners tassels in four colours represent the four seasons and the four guardian deities of Chinese cosmology.
