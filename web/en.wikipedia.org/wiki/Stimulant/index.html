Stimulant

Stimulants (also called psychostimulants or "uppers") are a class of psychoactive drugs that increase activity in the central nervous system, producing heightened alertness, energy, attention, confidence and euphoria, along with raised heart rate and blood pressure. They are used medically, recreationally and, in the case of caffeine, as an almost universal social drug.

Mechanism

Most stimulants act by increasing signalling of the monoamine neurotransmitters dopamine, norepinephrine and serotonin — by promoting their release, blocking their reuptake, or both. Amphetamine reverses monoamine transporters and floods synapses with dopamine and norepinephrine; cocaine blocks reuptake; caffeine works differently, by antagonising adenosine receptors and thereby disinhibiting arousal systems.

Medical uses

Prescription stimulants including methylphenidate and mixed amphetamine salts are first-line treatments for attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and are also used for narcolepsy and, occasionally, treatment-resistant depression. Ephedrine and related compounds serve as decongestants and pressor agents. Modafinil, a wakefulness-promoting agent with a distinct mechanism, treats excessive daytime sleepiness.

Risks

Adverse effects include insomnia, loss of appetite, anxiety, tremor and, at high doses or with chronic misuse, cardiovascular events, hyperthermia and stimulant psychosis. Many stimulants have significant dependence liability, and tolerance develops rapidly. Nicotine, though also sedating at some doses, is classed with the stimulants. Stimulants are banned in competitive sport by the World Anti-Doping Agency except where therapeutic exemptions apply.
