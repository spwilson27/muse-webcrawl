Bale Mountains National Park is a national park in the Oromia Region of southeastern Ethiopia. Covering about 2,200 square kilometres, it protects one of Africa's largest areas of Afroalpine habitat, centred on the Sanetti Plateau at over 4,000 metres elevation, as well as the Harenna Forest, one of Ethiopia's last large cloud forests. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2023.

Geography and habitats

The park spans five zones: northern grasslands and woodlands, juniper–Hagenia forests, the ericaceous belt, the Afroalpine Sanetti Plateau, and the Harenna Forest on the southern escarpment. Mount Tullu Demtu (4,377 m), Ethiopia's second-highest peak, rises from the plateau. The road across Sanetti is among the highest all-weather roads in Africa.

Wildlife

The park is the world's most important stronghold of the Ethiopian wolf, the rarest canid, which hunts giant mole-rats and grass rats on the plateau. Other endemics include the mountain nyala, Menelik's bushbuck, Bale monkey, and numerous birds such as the blue-winged goose, spot-breasted lapwing, and Abyssinian catbird. The Harenna Forest harbours lions, leopards, African wild dogs, and giant forest hogs. More than 1,300 plant species have been recorded, many endemic, including giant lobelias of the Afroalpine zone.

Conservation

Threats include livestock grazing, settlement, and disease transmitted to Ethiopian wolves from domestic dogs, against which vaccination programmes are run. Tourism focuses on trekking, horse riding, and wildlife watching from the park headquarters at Dinsho.
