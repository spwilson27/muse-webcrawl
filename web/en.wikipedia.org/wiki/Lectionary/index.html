A lectionary is a book or listing that contains a collection of scripture readings appointed for Christian worship on a given day or occasion. In many Christian denominations, the readings appointed for Sundays and feast days follow a prescribed cycle, so that over one or more years the congregation hears a substantial, representative portion of the Bible in a fixed order shaped by the seasons of the liturgical year.

History

The practice of appointed readings derives from synagogue worship, in which the Torah was read in a set cycle. Early Christian communities read from the Law, the Prophets, apostolic letters and Gospels; by the fourth and fifth centuries, fixed systems of readings for the great feasts and seasons had developed in Jerusalem, Rome, Constantinople and other centres. Medieval Western lectionaries (such as the comes and Gospel books called evangelistaries) supplied separate readings for Mass (Epistle and Gospel) and the Divine Office. After the Reformation, the Book of Common Prayer provided a daily and Sunday lectionary intended to take worshippers through nearly the whole Bible.

Modern lectionaries

The Second Vatican Council called for a richer selection of readings; the resulting Roman Catholic Ordo Lectionum Missae (1969) established a three-year Sunday cycle (Years A, B and C, centred on Matthew, Mark and Luke respectively, with John read in each year) and a two-year weekday cycle. Many Protestant churches adapted this model in the Revised Common Lectionary (1992), used by Anglicans, Lutherans, Methodists and many others, while the Church of England adopted it in Common Worship. Each Sunday typically has an Old Testament reading, a Psalm, a New Testament (epistle) reading and a Gospel.

Byzantine and other rites

The Byzantine Rite uses its own lectionary system, centred on the Gospel book (Evangelion) and the Apostol (Epistles and Acts), with readings determined by the movable Paschal cycle and the fixed calendar of feasts; the Old Testament is read mainly at Vespers during Lent. Jewish lectionaries similarly order the weekly Torah portion (parashah) and accompanying haftarah readings through the year.
