Omasum

The omasum, also known as the bible, the fardel, the manyplies and the psalterium, is the third compartment of the stomach in ruminants. It lies between the reticulum and the abomasum, on the right side of the abdominal cavity.

Function

During the first stages of digestion, food moves between the rumen and the reticulum; material that has been sufficiently broken down passes into the omasum. The omasum's interior is lined with many broad, leaf-like folds — the laminae — which give the organ its book-like appearance and the name "manyplies". These folds greatly increase surface area.

The omasum absorbs water, volatile fatty acids, and electrolytes such as sodium and potassium from the digesta, and it helps reduce particle size before material passes into the abomasum, the "true stomach" where enzymatic digestion takes place. Studies suggest the omasum may absorb a substantial share of the water and minerals entering it, regulating the consistency of material delivered to the abomasum and small intestine.

In cuisine

Omasum is one of the kinds of tripe used in cooking; beef tripe from the omasum ("book tripe" or "bible tripe") appears in dishes and dim sum preparations.
