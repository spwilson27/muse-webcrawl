Napoléon Louis Bonaparte (11 October 1804 – 17 March 1831) was the second son of Louis Bonaparte, King of Holland, and Hortense de Beauharnais, and a nephew of Emperor Napoleon I. For thirteen days in July 1810, following his father's abdication, he was nominally King of Holland as Louis II, before France annexed the kingdom outright. He was the elder brother of Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte, the future Napoleon III.

Life

After the death of his elder brother Napoléon Charles in 1807, Napoléon Louis became heir to the Dutch throne. When Louis Bonaparte abdicated on 1 July 1810, the five-year-old prince succeeded him, with his mother as regent; Napoleon I, however, annexed Holland to the French Empire days later, ending the reign. The family lived in exile after 1815, chiefly in Italy. Napoléon Louis married his first cousin Charlotte Bonaparte, daughter of Joseph Bonaparte, and settled in Florence.

Death

Devoted to the Italian cause of national unification, he and his younger brother joined the 1831 uprisings in the Papal States against Austrian and papal rule. Fleeing a campaign in the Romagna, he contracted measles and died at Forlì on 17 March 1831, aged 26. His death left his younger brother Louis-Napoléon as the family's principal pretender and eventual head of the Bonapartist cause.
