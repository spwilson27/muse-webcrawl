Butterfly Lovers

The Butterfly Lovers is a Chinese legend of a tragic romance between Liang Shanbo and Zhu Yingtai, sometimes regarded as the Chinese equivalent of Romeo and Juliet. The story, set during the Eastern Jin dynasty (266–420), is one of China's Four Great Folktales, alongside the Legend of the White Snake, Lady Meng Jiang, and The Cowherd and the Weaver Girl. It has been retold in operas, films, television series, and music — most famously in the Butterfly Lovers' Violin Concerto (1959).

The story

Zhu Yingtai, the daughter of a wealthy family in Zhejiang, longs to study, though women are barred from scholarship. Disguised as a young man, she sets out for an academy in Hangzhou; on the way she meets Liang Shanbo, a fellow student, and they swear brotherhood. They study together for three years, during which Zhu falls in love with Liang, who remains unaware she is a woman. When Zhu is summoned home, she hints at her identity and asks Liang to come and propose. He arrives too late: her father has betrothed her to Ma Wencai, a rich man's son. Heartbroken, Liang falls ill and dies.

On her wedding day, Zhu stops the procession at Liang's grave and weeps. The grave splits open in a storm; Zhu leaps in to join him. The two then emerge as a pair of butterflies, flying away together, united at last.

Adaptations

The tale is a staple of Chinese opera, especially Yue opera, and was filmed many times, including the 1954 film celebrated as the first colour feature from the People's Republic of China. The Butterfly Lovers' Violin Concerto, composed by He Zhanhao and Chen Gang in 1959 while students at the Shanghai Conservatory, is among the most popular Chinese orchestral works. In 2006, several Chinese cities jointly sought UNESCO recognition for the legend as intangible cultural heritage.
