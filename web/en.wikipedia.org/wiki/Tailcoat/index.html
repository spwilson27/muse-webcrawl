Tailcoat

A tailcoat is a knee-length coat characterised by a waist-length front and side panels and two long tails reaching to the back of the knees. It is worn as part of the most formal evening dress code, white tie, and in a shorter daytime form as part of morning dress.

History

The tailcoat evolved in the late 18th century from military and riding coats whose skirts were cut away in front for ease in the saddle. By the early 19th century it had become standard fashionable dress for men, popularised in Britain by Beau Brummell. Through the Victorian era it gradually retreated to formal evening wear as the lounge suit took over everyday dress.

Modern use

Today the evening tailcoat is worn with a white waistcoat, white bow tie, and wing-collar shirt at state banquets, formal balls, and some weddings. It is also standard dress for orchestra conductors and classical musicians, hotel staff in grand establishments, and butlers. The daytime variant, the morning coat, is cut with tails that curve away gradually and is worn at formal weddings and Royal Ascot.
