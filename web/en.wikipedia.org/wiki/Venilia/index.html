Venilia

In Roman mythology, Venilia (or Venelia) was a sea nymph associated with the winds and the sea. She was said to be the sister of Amata and the wife of Daunus, king of the Rutulians, by whom she was the mother of Turnus and Juturna. In other traditions she was the consort of Janus, to whom she bore Canens, and was also linked with Faunus.

Mythology

Venilia appears in Virgil's Aeneid, where her sister Amata is queen of the Latins, and in Ovid's Metamorphoses, where she intercedes in the story of Picus and Canens. Like the Greek sea deities she resembles, Venilia is a minor figure whose importance lies chiefly in her genealogical connections to the peoples of ancient Italy — the Rutulians and Latins who oppose and then join Aeneas's Trojans.
