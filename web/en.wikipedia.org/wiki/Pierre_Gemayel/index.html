Pierre Gemayel

Pierre Gemayel (Arabic: بيار الجميّل; 6 November 1905 – 29 August 1984) was a Lebanese political leader and founder of the Kataeb Party (Phalanges). A Maronite Christian from the village of Bikfaya, he became one of the most influential figures in modern Lebanese politics and the patriarch of the Gemayel political family.

Career

A pharmacist by training and captain of Lebanon's national football team, Gemayel attended the 1936 Berlin Olympics and, inspired by disciplined youth movements, founded the Kataeb Party that year as a nationalist youth organisation that grew into a major political party. He played a leading role in Lebanon's independence from France in 1943 and later served repeatedly as a government minister.

During the Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990), the Kataeb's militia, the Lebanese Forces, became a major combatant. Gemayel lived to see his son Bachir elected president in 1982, only to be assassinated before taking office; another son, Amin, then served as president (1982–1988). His grandson, also named Pierre Gemayel, a minister, was assassinated in 2006.
