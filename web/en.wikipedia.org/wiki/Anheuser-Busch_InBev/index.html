Anheuser-Busch InBev SA/NV, known as AB InBev, is a multinational drink and brewing company based in Leuven, Belgium, and the largest brewer in the world, accounting for roughly a quarter to a third of global beer volume. It was formed in 2008 when the Belgian-Brazilian brewer InBev acquired the American company Anheuser-Busch for about $52 billion.

AB InBev operates hundreds of breweries in nearly 50 countries.

History

The company's roots include the Stella Artois brewery of Leuven (whose origins are traced to 1366), the Brazilian brewers Brahma and Antarctica (merged as AmBev in 1999), and Anheuser-Busch of St. Louis, Missouri, founded in the 19th century by Eberhard Anheuser and Adolphus Busch, maker of Budweiser. Interbrew and AmBev combined in 2004 to form InBev. The 2008 takeover of Anheuser-Busch followed a hostile bid resisted by the Busch family and St. Louis interests. In 2016 AB InBev acquired its nearest rival, SABMiller, for about $107 billion — one of the largest acquisitions in corporate history — divesting many brands to satisfy regulators.

Brands and operations

Its global brands are Budweiser, Corona Extra (outside the United States), and Stella Artois, alongside international brands such as Beck's, Hoegaarden, and Leffe and hundreds of local brands including Skol, Brahma, Bud Light, Michelob, Jupiler, and Victoria. The company is known for aggressive cost management (including zero-based budgeting associated with 3G Capital) and is listed on Euronext Brussels with a secondary listing in Johannesburg.
