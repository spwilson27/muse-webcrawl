Computational fluid dynamics

Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) is a branch of fluid mechanics that uses numerical analysis and data structures to analyse and solve problems involving fluid flows. Computers perform the calculations required to simulate the free-stream flow of a fluid and its interaction with surfaces defined by boundary conditions.

Methodology

CFD discretises a domain into a mesh of small cells and solves the governing equations — the Navier–Stokes equations, with conservation of mass, momentum and energy — iteratively at each cell. Turbulence is handled by models ranging from Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes (RANS) to large eddy simulation (LES) and direct numerical simulation (DNS). Results are visualised in post-processing as velocity, pressure and temperature fields.

Applications

CFD is used in aerospace and automotive aerodynamics, weather prediction, turbomachinery, ship design, building ventilation, biomedical flows and combustion. It complements wind-tunnel and experimental testing, allowing many design variants to be evaluated quickly, though accuracy depends on mesh quality, models and validation.
