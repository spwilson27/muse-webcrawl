Torsion bar suspension

A torsion bar suspension is a vehicle suspension system that uses a torsion bar as its main weight-bearing spring. One end of a long steel bar is anchored to the vehicle chassis, while the other end is attached to a lever connected to the wheel's suspension arm; as the wheel moves vertically, the bar twists about its axis, and its resistance to twisting provides the springing action.

Characteristics

Torsion bars are compact and can be packaged along the frame rails, leaving room for a low, flat floor. Ride height is easily adjusted by re-indexing the bar's anchor or turning an adjusting bolt that pre-loads the bar. The spring rate depends on the bar's diameter, length, and the material's shear modulus.

Applications

The layout was popularised by the Citroën Traction Avant and the Volkswagen Beetle, and used on many Chrysler products, the Porsche 356 and 911 (until 1989), and numerous pickup trucks and SUVs. Torsion bars are also the standard suspension of most main battle tanks, including the Leopard 2 and M1 Abrams families, where their simplicity and internal packaging are advantages, though some modern designs use hydropneumatic suspension instead.
