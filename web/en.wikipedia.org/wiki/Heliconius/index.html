FamilyNymphalidae
GenusHeliconius Kluk, 1780

Heliconius is a genus of brush-footed butterflies (family Nymphalidae) native to the tropical Americas, commonly called longwings or heliconians. The genus comprises around 40 species with brightly coloured, boldly patterned wings.

Biology

Heliconius caterpillars feed on passion-flower vines (Passiflora), from which they sequester cyanogenic compounds, making both larvae and adults toxic; their bright colours advertise this unpalatability, and many species are Müllerian mimics of one another. Unusually among butterflies, adults feed on pollen as well as nectar, giving them lifespans of several months. Heliconius has become a model system for studying speciation, mimicry and the genetics of wing patterning, including the supergenes controlling colour patterns in species such as H. melpomene and H. erato.
