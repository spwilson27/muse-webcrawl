George W. Bush

George Walker Bush (born July 6, 1946) is an American politician and businessman who served as the 43rd president of the United States from 2001 to 2009.

George W. Bush
43rd President of the United States, 2001–2009
Vice President: Dick Cheney
Born: New Haven, Connecticut
Spouse: Laura Bush
Previous role: Governor of Texas (1995–2000)

Early life

The eldest son of George H. W. Bush, he grew up in Texas, attended Yale and Harvard Business School, worked in oil, and co-owned the Texas Rangers baseball team. As Texas governor he championed education reform and tax cuts.

Presidency

Bush narrowly won the disputed 2000 election against Al Gore, decided by the Supreme Court in Bush v. Gore. After the September 11 attacks (2001), he launched the War on Terror: invading Afghanistan and, in 2003, Iraq on claims of weapons of mass destruction that proved unfounded — the Iraq War became deeply controversial. Domestically he signed major tax cuts, No Child Left Behind, Medicare Part D, and PEPFAR (a large AIDS relief program for Africa). His second term was marked by criticism over Hurricane Katrina (2005) and the 2008 financial crisis, met with the TARP bank rescue.

Post-presidency

Bush retired to Texas, founded the George W. Bush Presidential Center in Dallas, paints portraits (including of veterans and world leaders), and has generally avoided partisan commentary, though his reputation has partially softened over time.
