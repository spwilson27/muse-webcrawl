Bayraktar Akıncı

The Bayraktar Akıncı (Turkish for "raider") is a high-altitude long-endurance (HALE) unmanned combat aerial vehicle (UCAV) developed by the Turkish defence company Baykar. Larger and more capable than the earlier Bayraktar TB2, the Akıncı is designed for air-to-ground and air-to-air combat missions, strategic strike, and intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance (ISR).

Development

Development of the Akıncı was announced in 2018. The prototype, powered by two Ukrainian Ivchenko-Progress turboprop engines, completed its maiden flight on 6 December 2019 at Çorlu Airport. Following trials and weapons-integration tests, the aircraft entered service with the Turkish Armed Forces in August 2021.

Design

The Akıncı has a maximum take-off weight of roughly 6 tonnes and can carry about 1.5 tonnes of payload across internal and six external hardpoints. It features satellite communication for beyond-line-of-sight control, redundant avionics, and can be fitted with an AESA radar, electro-optical/infrared sensors, and electronic warfare systems. Its armament includes guided bombs, the SOM-J class of stand-off cruise missiles, and air-to-air missiles. Variants powered by different engines, including 450, 750 and 850 horsepower turboprops, have been developed for different mission profiles, giving the aircraft an endurance of more than 24 hours and a service ceiling around 40,000 feet.

Operational use and exports

The Akıncı has been used by Turkish forces in counter-insurgency and cross-border operations, and in May 2024 a Turkish Akıncı helped locate the crashed helicopter of Iranian president Ebrahim Raisi, streaming the search to a large online audience. The type has been exported to a number of countries, and in 2023 Baykar signed a major agreement with Saudi Arabia described as the largest defence export contract in Turkish history.
