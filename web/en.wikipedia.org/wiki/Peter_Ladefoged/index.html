Peter Ladefoged

Born: September 17, 1925, Sutton, London, England

Died: January 24, 2006, Tromsø, Norway

Known for: Phonetics, fieldwork on the world's languages, The Sounds of the World's Languages

Peter Nielsen Ladefoged (September 17, 1925 – January 24, 2006) was a British phonetician based in the United States, widely regarded as the leading figure in experimental and field phonetics of his generation.

Life and career

Ladefoged was educated at Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge, and the University of Edinburgh, where he earned his Ph.D. in phonetics in 1959. After teaching at Edinburgh and working in Nigeria at the University of Ibadan, he moved to the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) in 1964, where he founded and directed the UCLA Phonetics Laboratory and remained until his retirement, continuing as professor emeritus.

Work

Ladefoged traveled extensively to record and analyze the sounds of endangered and little-studied languages, examining over a hundred languages in the field. His textbook A Course in Phonetics (first published 1975, many later editions) became the standard introduction to the subject. The Sounds of the World's Languages (1996, with Ian Maddieson) synthesized decades of laboratory and field research into a comprehensive account of the phonetic structures attested across languages, and Vowels and Consonants (2001) presented the field for general readers. He also served as a consultant on the film My Fair Lady (1964), based partly on the phonetician Henry Higgins, and contributed to the development of the International Phonetic Alphabet's framework for describing speech sounds.

Honors

Ladefoged was a fellow of the British Academy and the Royal Society of Edinburgh, and president of the International Phonetic Association. He died in Tromsø, Norway, while returning from a fieldwork trip to India.
