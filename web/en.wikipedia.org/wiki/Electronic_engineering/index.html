Electronic engineering

Electronic engineering is a sub-discipline of electrical engineering concerned with the design and application of electronic circuits, devices and systems. It uses active electrical components such as semiconductor devices — diodes, transistors and integrated circuits — together with passive components, to process signals and information rather than bulk power.

History

The field emerged from radio engineering in the early twentieth century, built on the thermionic valve (vacuum tube) used in radio, telephony, radar and early computers. The invention of the transistor in 1947 and the integrated circuit in 1958–59 transformed the discipline, enabling miniaturised, reliable and inexpensive electronics and driving the growth of computing, telecommunications and consumer electronics.

Subfields

Electronic engineering branches include analogue and digital electronics, embedded systems, signal processing, telecommunications, control systems, instrumentation, microelectronics and VLSI design, power electronics, and radio-frequency and microwave engineering. Electronic engineers design products from smartphones and medical instruments to avionics and industrial automation, working across the full chain from semiconductor devices to complete systems.

Education and practice

Degree programmes cover circuit theory, signals and systems, electromagnetics, semiconductor physics, digital logic and programming. In many jurisdictions, engineers working on systems affecting public safety require professional licensure or accreditation.
