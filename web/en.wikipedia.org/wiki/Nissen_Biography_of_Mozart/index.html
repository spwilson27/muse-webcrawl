Nissen Biography of Mozart

The Nissen Biography of Mozart (Biographie W. A. Mozarts, Leipzig 1828) is the first full-length biography of the composer Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, written by Georg Nikolaus von Nissen (1761–1826), a Danish diplomat and the second husband of Mozart's widow Constanze. Published two years after Nissen's death, edited by Constanze and completed with the help of Johann Heinrich Feuerstein and Anton Jandel, it remains a fundamental primary source for Mozart scholarship.

Content and sources

The biography was compiled largely from family documents: the letters of Mozart and his father Leopold, Constanze's recollections, and earlier published material including Friedrich Schlichtegroll's obituary (1793), Franz Xaver Niemetschek's biography (1798), and Rochlitz's anecdotes. Nissen's aim — deliberately apologetic — was to counter the image of Mozart as an irresponsible, improvident genius and to present him, in the spirit of Biedermeier morality, as a diligent and virtuous family man.

Reception and reliability

Because Nissen and Constanze had financial and reputational interests in Mozart's posthumous image, modern scholars treat the work critically: letters were sometimes altered, passages deleted, and anecdotes selected to flatter. Constanze is known to have excised passages she considered damaging. Nevertheless, the biography preserves hundreds of letters and documents, many otherwise lost, and it established the framework — and several enduring myths, including the romanticised story of the Requiem commission — within which Mozart's life was understood throughout the 19th century.

Editions

A facsimile edition appeared in 1964, and the complete text is included in the standard edition of Mozart's letters, Mozart: Briefe und Aufzeichnungen. An annotated English translation by Seymour Eisen was published in 2014.
