YAP1

YAP1 (Yes-associated protein 1), also known as YAP or YAP65, is a protein that in humans is encoded by the YAP1 gene on chromosome 11. YAP1 is a transcriptional co-activator and the principal downstream effector of the Hippo signalling pathway, which controls organ size by regulating cell proliferation and apoptosis.

Function

When the Hippo pathway is active, kinases MST1/2 and LATS1/2 phosphorylate YAP1, causing it to be retained in the cytoplasm by 14-3-3 proteins and targeted for degradation. When the pathway is off, unphosphorylated YAP1 enters the nucleus and binds TEAD-family transcription factors, switching on genes that promote cell growth, proliferation and survival. YAP1 contains WW domains that mediate protein–protein interactions and was originally identified by its binding to the SH3 domain of the Yes tyrosine kinase. It also responds to mechanical cues such as substrate stiffness and cell density through mechanotransduction.

Clinical significance

The YAP1 locus is amplified in several cancers and YAP1 hyperactivity drives tumour growth, metastasis and drug resistance, making it an attractive drug target; inhibitors of the YAP–TEAD interaction are under investigation. Conversely, loss-of-function mutations in YAP1 cause a rare syndrome featuring coloboma, hearing loss, cleft lip/palate and intellectual disability. YAP1 also plays roles in tissue regeneration, including in the liver and heart.
