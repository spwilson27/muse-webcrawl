Tommaso Buscetta (Italian pronunciation: [tomˈmaːzo buʃˈʃetta]; 13 July 1928 – 2 April 2000) was an Italian Mafia boss, a member of the Sicilian Mafia (Cosa Nostra), who became the most important pentito (state's witness) in Italian history. His revelations to investigating judge Giovanni Falcone in 1984 exposed the internal structure of Cosa Nostra for the first time and provided the evidential backbone of the Maxi Trial of 1986–87.

Early life and Mafia career

Buscetta was born in Palermo, the youngest of 17 children of a glassmaker. He became involved in organised crime as a young man and was made a "man of honour" in the Porta Nuova Mafia family in the mid-1940s. He rose through the ranks as a cigarette smuggler and heroin trafficker. After the First Mafia War and the Ciaculli massacre of 1963, which provoked a state crackdown, Buscetta fled Sicily and spent years in North and South America — earning the nickname "the Boss of Two Worlds" — participating in international drug networks, including links to the Pizza Connection heroin ring.

Arrested in Brazil in 1972, he was extradited to Italy and imprisoned, but escaped in 1980 and returned to Brazil. During the Second Mafia War (1981–83), the rising Corleonesi faction of Salvatore Riina exterminated Buscetta's allies and murdered many of his relatives, including two of his sons, who disappeared and were never seen again.

Cooperation with Falcone

Recaptured in São Paulo in 1983 and extradited to Italy, Buscetta attempted suicide in July 1984 and, on recovering, told police he wished to talk. Over some 45 days of interrogation in Rome, he gave Falcone an insider's account of Cosa Nostra: its initiation ritual, its pyramid structure of soldiers, capos, families and districts, and the ruling Commission — the "Buscetta theorem" that Falcone used to indict hundreds of Mafiosi. Buscetta insisted he was not an informer out of self-interest but a traditionalist who believed Riina's Corleonesi had betrayed the Mafia's own code.

Later testimony and death

Buscetta was the star witness at the Maxi Trial, and his evidence was upheld by the Court of Cassation in 1992. In the 1990s he gave further evidence, including testimony implicating former prime minister Giulio Andreotti in Mafia association (Andreotti was ultimately acquitted on definitive appeal rulings for the period after 1980, with earlier allegations time-barred). Protected under the Italian pentiti programme and later the United States witness protection system, Buscetta lived his final years in Florida under a new identity, giving masked television interviews and cooperating with biographers. He died of cancer on 2 April 2000.
