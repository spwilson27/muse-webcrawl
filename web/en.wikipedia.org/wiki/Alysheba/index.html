Alysheba

Alysheba (March 3, 1984 – March 27, 2009) was an American Thoroughbred racehorse and sire. He won two legs of the American Triple Crown in 1987 — the Kentucky Derby and the Preakness Stakes — and as a four-year-old in 1988 won the Breeders' Cup Classic, earning Eclipse Awards including American Horse of the Year. He was inducted into the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in 1993.

Racing career

Trained by Jack Van Berg and ridden principally by Chris McCarron, Alysheba was a maiden winner only once in his two-year-old season but emerged in 1987, winning the Kentucky Derby in a stretch duel and then the Preakness. His Triple Crown bid ended at Belmont, where Bet Twice — his rival throughout the season — beat him in the Belmont Stakes. At four he dominated older horses, winning the Santa Anita Handicap, the Woodward Stakes, and the Meadowlands Cup before defeating Seeking the Gold and others in a fast Breeders' Cup Classic at Churchill Downs.

Stud and later life

Alysheba stood at stud in Kentucky and later in Saudi Arabia before being returned to the United States in 2008 to live at the Kentucky Horse Park's Hall of Champions. He was euthanised on 27 March 2009 after falling in his stall and injuring his leg.
