Solar thermal energy

Solar thermal energy (STE) is a technology for harnessing solar energy as heat. Unlike photovoltaics, which convert light directly into electricity, solar thermal systems collect the Sun's radiation to heat a fluid or material for uses ranging from domestic hot water to industrial process heat and electricity generation.

Low-temperature applications

The largest market is water and space heating. Flat-plate and evacuated-tube collectors on rooftops supply domestic hot water worldwide — China accounts for the majority of installed collector area, and solar water heating is mandatory in some jurisdictions. Related applications include solar space heating, swimming-pool heating, solar dryers, solar cookers, and thermal desalination.

Concentrated solar power

For electricity, concentrated solar power (CSP) uses mirrors to focus sunlight: parabolic troughs heat oil flowing along a focal line; solar power towers heat a receiver (often molten salt) atop a tower; dish-Stirling systems drive engines at the focus; and linear Fresnel reflectors use flat mirrors. Molten-salt thermal storage lets plants such as Spain's Gemasolar and Morocco's Noor complex generate electricity after sunset — a key advantage over photovoltaics, though CSP costs have fallen more slowly and new construction is limited mainly to China, the Middle East, and a few other sunny regions.

Other uses

Solar thermal energy also drives absorption cooling, high-temperature furnaces for materials research (such as the Odeillo solar furnace in France), and emerging solar fuels research, in which concentrated heat splits water or carbon dioxide. Low- and medium-temperature solar process heat for industry is a growing niche.
