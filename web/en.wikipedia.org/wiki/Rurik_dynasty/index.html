Rurik dynasty

Heraldry of the Rurikid princes

The Rurik dynasty, or Rurikids, was the ruling dynasty of Kievan Rus' and its successor principalities, founded by the Varangian prince Rurik in the 9th century. It ruled for more than seven centuries — one of the longest-reigning dynasties in European history — until the death of Feodor I of Russia in 1598, and its cadet branches produced numerous princely families of Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus.

History

Rurik's descendants — Oleg, Igor, Sviatoslav, and Vladimir the Great, who Christianised Rus' in 988 — built Kiev into the centre of a loose federation of trading cities. Under Yaroslav the Wise the state reached its height, but the rota (ladder) system of lateral succession generated chronic feuding among proliferating branches, and after the Mongol invasion of 1237–1240 power shifted toward the northeast: Vladimir-Suzdal, then Moscow, whose Daniilovich line of Rurikids gathered the Rus' lands. Ivan III threw off Mongol suzerainty, Ivan IV ("the Terrible") was crowned the first Tsar in 1547, and with his son Feodor I's death in 1598 the Moscow line ended, opening the Time of Troubles and the accession of the Romanovs in 1613.

Branches and legacy

Cadet branches, named from their appanage seats — the Olgovichi of Chernigov, the Iziaslavichi of Polotsk, the Rostislavichi of Smolensk, the Yurievichi of Suzdal, and many others — supplied princes across the Rus' lands, and families such as the Shuiskys, Rostovskys, and (in other traditions) the Gediminids' rivals descend from Rurikid stock. Genetic studies of modern representatives have been used to investigate the dynasty's Scandinavian origins, most finding Y-chromosome haplogroups consistent with a north-west European source. The Rurikid trident symbol influenced the modern coat of arms of Ukraine.
