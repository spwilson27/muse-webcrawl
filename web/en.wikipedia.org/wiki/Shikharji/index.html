Shikharji

Shikharji (Śikharjī), also known as Sammed Shikharji or Sammet Shikhar, is one of the holiest pilgrimage sites (tirthas) in Jainism. It is located on Parasnath Hill in the Giridih district of Jharkhand, India, the highest mountain in the state at about 1,365 metres (4,478 ft).

Significance

According to Jain tradition, twenty of the twenty-four Tirthankaras attained moksha (liberation) at Shikharji, including Parshvanatha, the 23rd Tirthankara, after whom the hill is named. Because so many Tirthankaras achieved nirvana there, the site is revered by both major Jain traditions, Digambara and Shvetambara, and is sometimes called the "king of pilgrimages" (Tirthraj).

The pilgrimage

The pilgrimage route climbs roughly 9 km from the town of Madhuban at the foot of the hill to the summit temples, with shrines (tonks) along the ridge marking the spots where each Tirthankara attained liberation. Pilgrims traditionally make the ascent on foot, often beginning before dawn, and may complete a circuit of about 27 km covering the hilltop temples. The main temple at the base in Madhuban and the jal mandir are important stops.

Temples and administration

Dozens of temples crown the ridges, administered by Jain trusts. Disputes have periodically arisen over the site's management and over government tourism development plans, with Jain communities protesting to preserve its sanctity as a purely religious site.
