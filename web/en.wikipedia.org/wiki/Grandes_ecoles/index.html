Grandes écoles

The grandes écoles are a group of elite higher education institutions in France, distinct from the main body of French public universities. They are highly selective, admit students through competitive national examinations (concours) after two years of preparatory classes (classes préparatoires), and traditionally train France's senior civil servants, engineers, military officers and business leaders.

History

The system dates to the late 18th century, with institutions such as the École Polytechnique (founded 1794) created to supply the state with engineers and artillery officers. The model was expanded under Napoleon and has remained largely intact, producing much of France's political and administrative elite.

Types

Grandes écoles include engineering schools (École Polytechnique, École des Mines, CentraleSupélec), business schools (HEC Paris, ESSEC, ESCP), the écoles normales supérieures for research and teaching, and schools of public administration — the role once held by the École nationale d'administration (ENA), replaced in 2022 by the Institut national du service public (INSP).

Criticism

The system is frequently criticised for reproducing social privilege, since access via the preparatory classes favours students from well-off backgrounds, despite the schools' small size and outsized influence.
