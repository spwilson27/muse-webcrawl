The Johnstown Flood National Memorial is a United States national memorial in Cambria County, Pennsylvania, commemorating the Johnstown Flood of 31 May 1889, in which the failure of the South Fork Dam sent a wall of water down the Conemaugh Valley and killed more than 2,200 people.

The flood

The South Fork Dam impounded Lake Conemaugh, a private reservoir maintained by the South Fork Fishing and Hunting Club, whose members included Pittsburgh industrialists such as Andrew Carnegie and Henry Clay Frick. The dam had been poorly maintained, its spillway obstructed and its crest lowered. After days of exceptionally heavy rain, the dam failed on the afternoon of 31 May 1889. Some 20 million tons of water swept through the valley, destroying the town of South Fork, Woodvale and much of Johnstown, with debris piling against the Pennsylvania Railroad's Stone Bridge and catching fire. The death toll of 2,209 made it one of the deadliest disasters in American history. Relief efforts, prominently including Clara Barton and the American Red Cross in its first major peacetime relief operation, followed.

The memorial

Authorised by Congress in 1964 and administered by the National Park Service, the memorial preserves the remains of the South Fork Dam — its two abutments flanking the breach — together with the dry lakebed, the clubhouse of the South Fork Fishing and Hunting Club, and a visitor center overlooking the site. Exhibits include photographs, the film Black Friday, and interpretive trails along the former dam.
