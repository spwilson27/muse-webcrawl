Goalball

Goalball is a team sport designed specifically for athletes with visual impairments. Two teams of three players attempt to throw a ball containing bells into the opposing team's goal, relying on the sense of hearing to track the ball and on tactile court markings for orientation. All players wear blackout eyeshades so that athletes with different levels of sight compete equally. Goalball is a Paralympic sport, governed internationally by the International Blind Sports Federation (IBSA).

History

The game was devised in 1946 by the Austrian Hans Lorenzen and the German Sepp Reindle as a rehabilitation activity for blinded Second World War veterans. It was demonstrated at the 1976 Toronto Paralympics, became a medal event for men in 1980 and for women in 1984, and world championships have been held since 1978.

Rules

Matches are played on an indoor court 18 metres long and 9 metres wide, with goals spanning the full width at each end. The ball, about the size of a basketball and weighing 1.25 kg, contains bells so players can locate it by sound; spectators must remain silent during play. Players throw underarm, and the ball must bounce in the thrower's team area and the neutral zone before crossing into the opponents' area. Defenders dive to block with their bodies. Games last two halves of 12 minutes of actual playing time; most players have some residual vision outside competition and are classified B1–B3, but the eyeshades level the field entirely.
