Genome editing

Genome editing (or gene editing) is a group of technologies that allow targeted changes to the DNA of an organism — inserting, deleting or replacing genetic material at specific locations in the genome.

Methods

Major tools include zinc-finger nucleases, TALENs, and CRISPR–Cas systems, the latter developed into a practical editing platform by Emmanuelle Charpentier and Jennifer Doudna, who received the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Newer approaches include base editing and prime editing, which change DNA without double-strand breaks.

Applications and ethics

Genome editing is used in research, agriculture and medicine; the first CRISPR therapy, for sickle cell disease and beta thalassemia (Casgevy), was approved in 2023. Germline editing in humans remains controversial, particularly after the 2018 case of genome-edited babies announced by He Jiankui.
