Neon Genesis Evangelion

Neon Genesis Evangelion is a Japanese mecha anime television series produced by Gainax and Tatsunoko Production, directed by Hideaki Anno and broadcast on TV Tokyo from October 1995 to March 1996. Set fifteen years after a worldwide cataclysm called Second Impact, the story follows teenager Shinji Ikari, who is recruited by his father into the paramilitary organization NERV to pilot a giant biomechanical mecha called an Evangelion in combat against beings known as Angels.

Themes and reception

The series blends mecha action with psychoanalysis, religious symbolism drawn from Kabbalah, Christianity and Judaism, and an increasingly introspective exploration of its characters' depression and trauma. Its final episodes, constrained by budget and schedule, take an abstract, psychological form that proved controversial; the 1997 film The End of Evangelion provided an alternate, more concrete conclusion.

Evangelion was a critical and commercial success and is widely regarded as one of the most influential anime series ever made, credited with revitalizing the medium in the late 1990s. It spawned a large franchise of films, manga, video games and merchandise, including the later Rebuild of Evangelion film tetralogy (2007–2021).
