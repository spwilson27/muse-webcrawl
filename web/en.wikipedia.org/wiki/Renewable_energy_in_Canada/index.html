Renewable energy in Canada

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Hydroelectricity dominates Canada's supply

Renewable energy in Canada accounts for roughly two-thirds of the country's electricity generation, one of the highest shares among major economies. Canada is a world leader in the production and use of energy from renewable resources, thanks primarily to its abundant hydroelectric resources, which supply about 60% of national electricity output.

Hydroelectricity

Canada is the world's third-largest producer of hydroelectricity, with major complexes in Quebec (Hydro-Québec's James Bay Project), British Columbia, Manitoba, Newfoundland and Labrador (Churchill Falls and Muskrat Falls) and Ontario. Hydroelectric dams provide low-carbon, dispatchable power and large storage reservoirs.

Wind, solar and other sources

Wind power is the second-largest renewable source and the fastest-growing for much of the past two decades, concentrated in Ontario, Quebec and Alberta. Solar power remains a smaller contributor, concentrated in Ontario following its feed-in tariff program. Biomass from the forestry industry, geothermal heating, and small tidal developments (notably in the Bay of Fundy) also contribute.

Policy

Energy policy is shared between federal and provincial governments. Federal regulations require the phase-out of conventional coal-fired electricity by 2030, and a clean electricity standard aims for a net-zero grid by 2035. Ontario completed its coal phase-out in 2014, the first large jurisdiction in North America to do so.
