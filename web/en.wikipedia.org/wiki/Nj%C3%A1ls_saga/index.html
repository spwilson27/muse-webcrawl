Njáls saga

AuthorUnknown (13th century)
LanguageOld Norse / Icelandic
GenreFamily saga
SettingIceland, c. 950–1020
Also known asBrennu-Njáls saga; Njála

Njáls saga is a 13th-century Icelandic family saga, generally considered the greatest of the sagas of Icelanders. Written by an unknown author, probably in the late 13th century, it recounts events between roughly 950 and 1020 in southern Iceland, weaving together legal disputes, blood feuds, and the coming of Christianity.

Plot summary

The first part centers on the friendship between the wise, peace-loving lawyer Njáll Þorgeirsson and the heroic Gunnar Hámundarson, whose downfall follows his marriage to the proud Hallgerður. The second part follows Njáll and his sons, escalating feuds, and culminates in the burning of Njáll in his home at Bergþórshvöll — the episode that gives the saga its alternate name, Brennu-Njáls saga ("The Saga of Burnt Njál"). The final part follows Kári's long quest for vengeance across the Norse world.

Significance

At some 280,000 words in modern editions, Njáls saga is the longest of the family sagas. It is celebrated for its characterisation, its intricate foreshadowing through dreams and prophecies, and its meditation on law and violence. It survives in numerous vellum and paper manuscripts and remains a cornerstone of Icelandic literature.
