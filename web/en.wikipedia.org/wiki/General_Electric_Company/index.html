General Electric Company

[Image placeholder: GEC branding]

The General Electric Company (GEC) was a major British industrial conglomerate involved in consumer and defence electronics, communications, and engineering. Headquartered in Coventry and later London, it was at its height one of the United Kingdom's largest private employers and manufacturers. It should not be confused with the American General Electric (GE), to which it was historically connected but legally separate.

History

GEC's origins lie in an electrical goods wholesaler founded in London in the 1880s by Gustav Binswanger and Hugo Hirst; the company expanded into manufacturing lamps, telephones, and electrical equipment, and was incorporated as the General Electric Company Ltd in 1900. Under Lord Hirst it became a vertically integrated electrical giant. From 1961, under managing director Arnold Weinstock, GEC grew by acquiring Associated Electrical Industries (AEI), English Electric, and Elliott Automation, and later Plessey (jointly with Siemens), building a formidable cash reserve and a dominant position in British defence electronics and telecommunications.

Decline and breakup

After Weinstock's retirement in 1996, his successor George Simpson reoriented the company toward telecommunications, renaming it Marconi plc in 1999 and selling the defence arm, Marconi Electronic Systems, to British Aerospace (forming part of BAE Systems). Heavy spending at the peak of the dot-com boom left Marconi exposed when the telecom bubble burst in 2001; its share price collapsed, and most of the business was eventually sold to Ericsson in 2006, with the remainder becoming telent.
