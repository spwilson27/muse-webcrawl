Wolf–Rayet star

Wolf–Rayet star
Spectral types: WN, WC, WO
Mass: typically 10–25 M☉ (initially >25 M☉)
Temperature: 30,000–200,000 K

Wolf–Rayet stars, often abbreviated as WR stars, are a rare heterogeneous set of stars with unusual spectra showing prominent broad emission lines of ionised helium and highly ionised nitrogen or carbon. The spectra indicate very high surface enhancement of heavy elements, strong stellar winds, and rapid mass loss. Wolf–Rayet stars are thought to represent a brief late stage in the evolution of very massive stars, after their outer hydrogen layers have been stripped away.

Classification

WR stars are divided into the WN sequence (nitrogen-dominated spectra), the WC sequence (carbon-dominated) and the extremely rare WO sequence (oxygen-dominated), reflecting successive layers of fusion products exposed as the star loses mass. A separate population of central stars of planetary nebulae shows similar spectra and is designated [WR].

Evolution

Stars with initial masses above roughly 25 solar masses are expected to pass through a Wolf–Rayet phase, driven by radiation-driven winds, eruptions or binary stripping. They end their lives in core-collapse supernovae, in some cases leaving black holes; Wolf–Rayet stars are leading candidates for the progenitors of long gamma-ray bursts.

Notable examples

Gamma Velorum (γ² Velorum) is the brightest and closest Wolf–Rayet star, about 1,100 light-years away. WR 104 is a famous dust-producing binary whose spiral "pinwheel" nebula has been extensively imaged.
