Fatah (Arabic: فتح, romanised Fatḥ), formerly known as the Palestinian National Liberation Movement, is a Palestinian nationalist and social democratic political party. It is the largest faction of the confederated multi-party Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) and the second-largest party in the Palestinian Legislative Council. Its name is a reverse acronym of Ḥarakat al-Taḥrīr al-Waṭanī l-Filasṭīnī ("Palestinian National Liberation Movement"); fatḥ also means "conquest" in Arabic.

History

Fatah was founded in 1959 by members of the Palestinian diaspora, principally Yasser Arafat and Khalil al-Wazir (Abu Jihad), then working in Kuwait and Qatar. It launched its first armed operation against Israel on 1 January 1965 through its military wing, al-Asifa. After the 1967 Six-Day War and the 1968 Battle of Karameh, Fatah's standing surged; in 1969 it took control of the PLO, with Arafat as chairman. Expelled from Jordan after Black September (1970), Fatah relocated to Lebanon and later, after the 1982 Lebanon War, to Tunisia.

From armed struggle to the Palestinian Authority

Following the 1993 Oslo Accords, Fatah became the dominant party of the new Palestinian Authority, and Arafat served as its president until his death in 2004. Mahmoud Abbas succeeded him and won the 2005 presidential election, but Fatah lost the 2006 legislative elections to Hamas, and Hamas's takeover of Gaza in 2007 left Fatah governing only the West Bank. Repeated reconciliation attempts between the two movements have failed, and Fatah — criticised by rivals and many Palestinians for corruption, authoritarianism and the failure of the peace process — remains the internationally recognised governing party of the Palestinian Authority.
