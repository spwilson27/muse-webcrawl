Case IH

Case IH is known for red agricultural tractors and harvesting equipment.

Case IH is an American brand of agricultural machinery, part of CNH Industrial. It was created in 1985 when Tenneco’s Case division acquired the agricultural equipment business of International Harvester, combining the heritage of the J. I. Case Company with International Harvester’s Farmall and tractor lines.

History

The J. I. Case Company traced its roots to Jerome Increase Case’s 1842 threshing-machine business in Wisconsin. International Harvester had been formed in 1902 from McCormick, Deering, and other harvester manufacturers. After the 1985 merger, the combined Case IH brand adopted International Harvester’s red colour and sold Magnum tractors, combines, planters, sprayers, and hay tools. In 1999, Case Corporation merged with New Holland to form CNH, later CNH Industrial.

Products

Case IH product lines include Steiger and Magnum row-crop tractors, Puma and Maxxum utility tractors, Axial-Flow rotary combines, Early Riser planters, and Patriot sprayers. Its precision-farming offerings are marketed under the AFS (Advanced Farming Systems) name.
