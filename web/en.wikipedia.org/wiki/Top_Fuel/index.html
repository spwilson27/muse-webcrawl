Top Fuel is a type of drag racing whose dragsters are the quickest-accelerating racing cars in the world, widely considered the "kings of the sport." Modern Top Fuel dragsters are about 25 feet (7.6 m) long, run on a mixture of up to 90 percent nitromethane and methanol, and produce roughly 11,000 horsepower from a supercharged 500-cubic-inch Hemi V8. They cover the standing 1,000-foot race distance in about 3.6 seconds at speeds over 338 mph (544 km/h), subjecting the driver to forces of around 5 g.

The cars

The engine sits behind the driver, driving enormous rear slicks some 36 inches tall and 17 inches wide, while downforce from the front and rear wings — generating several tonnes at speed — keeps the car planted. There is no transmission in the conventional sense; a timer-controlled multi-disc clutch progressively locks up the direct drive during the run. Engines are rebuilt after nearly every pass: a single run consumes around 15 gallons of fuel, and the spark plugs are largely consumed by the run's end, the engine sometimes running on compression ignition alone for the final metres.

History and safety

The class grew out of postwar American drag racing; Don Garlits' rear-engined Swamp Rat 14 of 1971, built after a clutch explosion severed part of his foot, set the layout still used today. Following the death of Scott Kalitta in 2008, NHRA shortened Top Fuel and Funny Car races from the traditional quarter mile to 1,000 feet to improve safety, and extensive measures — burst panels, parachutes, containment — remain central to the class.
