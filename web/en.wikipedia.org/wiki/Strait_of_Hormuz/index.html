The Strait of Hormuz is a strait between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman, on the border between the Arabian Peninsula and Iran. It provides the only sea passage from the Persian Gulf to the open ocean and is one of the world's most strategically important chokepoints: tankers carrying crude oil and liquefied natural gas from Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar, the UAE and Iran must pass through shipping lanes only a few kilometres wide.

Strategic significance

By widely cited estimates, roughly 20% of the world's petroleum liquids and a similar share of LNG trade pass through the strait, making any closure a threat to the global economy. During the Iran–Iraq War (1980–88), both sides attacked shipping in the "Tanker War", and the United States deployed naval escorts. Iran has periodically threatened to close the strait in response to sanctions or attack, while the US Fifth Fleet, based in Bahrain, patrols to keep it open. Incidents — seizures of tankers, attacks attributed to limpet mines in 2019, and the shooting down of a US drone the same year — periodically raise tensions and oil prices.
