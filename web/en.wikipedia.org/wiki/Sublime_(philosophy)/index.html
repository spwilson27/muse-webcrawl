Sublime (philosophy)

Sublime (philosophy)
FieldAesthetics
Key figuresLonginus; Edmund Burke (1757); Immanuel Kant (1790)
[Image: A ship in a stormy sea beneath towering cliffs, evoking the sublime in nature]

In aesthetics, the sublime is the quality of greatness — in nature, art or ideas — that exceeds calculation and overwhelms the mind, mixing awe with terror. Edmund Burke's Enquiry (1757) opposed the sublime (vast, dark, powerful) to the beautiful (small, smooth, delicate); Immanuel Kant located sublimity not in objects but in the mind's discovery of its own rational vocation when imagination fails before immensity (the mathematical sublime) or might (the dynamical sublime).

Legacy

The sublime shaped Romantic painting and poetry — Turner's storms, Friedrich's wanderers — and returned in 20th-century thought (Lyotard's postmodern sublime) and in discussions of technology and ecological catastrophe, where human power again confronts scales beyond comprehension.
