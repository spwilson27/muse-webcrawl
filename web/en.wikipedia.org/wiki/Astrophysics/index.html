Astrophysics

Astrophysics is a science that employs the methods and principles of physics and chemistry in the study of astronomical objects and phenomena. As one of the founders of the discipline, James Keeler, said, the subject matter of astrophysics includes the positions and motions of celestial bodies studied in astronomy, but astrophysics asks "what they are" rather than "where they are".

Among the subjects studied are the Sun, other stars, galaxies, extrasolar planets, the interstellar medium and the cosmic microwave background. Emissions from these objects are examined across all parts of the electromagnetic spectrum, and the properties examined include luminosity, density, temperature, and chemical composition.

History

Although astronomy is as ancient as recorded history itself, it was long separated from the study of terrestrial physics. The modern field emerged in the 19th century with spectroscopy: Fraunhofer's observations of spectral lines in sunlight, and the work of Kirchhoff and Bunsen, showed that the chemical elements in the Sun could be identified from Earth. Stellar classification, the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram, and the discovery of nuclear fusion as the energy source of stars followed in the early 20th century.

Areas of study

Astrophysics divides broadly into observational and theoretical work. Major areas include stellar structure and evolution, galactic dynamics, cosmology — the study of the origin and large-scale structure of the universe — high-energy phenomena such as black holes and supernovae, and planetary science. Because astrophysics is a very broad subject, astrophysicists apply concepts and methods from many disciplines of physics, including classical mechanics, electromagnetism, statistical mechanics, thermodynamics, quantum mechanics, relativity, and nuclear and particle physics.
