South Ossetia, officially the Republic of South Ossetia – State of Alania, is a partially recognised state in the South Caucasus, on the northern border of Georgia. It declared independence from Georgia in the early 1990s, following the collapse of the Soviet Union, and its independence has been recognised by Russia, Nicaragua, Venezuela, Nauru and Syria. The United Nations and the great majority of states consider South Ossetia to be part of Georgia, whose official designation for the territory is the Tskhinvali region.

The region is inhabited predominantly by Ossetians, an Iranian-speaking people who trace their descent to the medieval Alans and the ancient Scythians and Sarmatians. Its capital and largest city is Tskhinvali.

History

During the Soviet period, the South Ossetian Autonomous Oblast was created within the Georgian Soviet Socialist Republic in 1922. As the USSR dissolved, ethnic tensions escalated into the 1991–92 South Ossetia War, which ended with a Russian-brokered ceasefire and a de facto independent government in Tskhinvali controlling most of the former oblast.

In August 2008, fighting between Georgian forces and South Ossetian militia escalated into the Russo-Georgian War. Russia recognised South Ossetia (and Abkhazia) as independent states on 26 August 2008 and maintains a significant military presence in the territory, including the 4th Guards Military Base near Tskhinvali.

Geography

South Ossetia lies on the southern slopes of the Greater Caucasus range, covering about 3,885 square kilometres of mountainous terrain. The Liakhvi River flows south through Tskhinvali toward Georgia's Shida Kartli region. The Roki Tunnel, under the Caucasus watershed, connects the territory to North Ossetia–Alania in Russia and is its principal transport link to the outside world.

Politics

South Ossetia functions as a presidential republic. Its politics are dominated by the question of its status: a 2006 referendum endorsed independence, while many residents and politicians favour eventual unification with North Ossetia within the Russian Federation. Georgia maintains a rival administration in exile and regards the territory as occupied.

Demographics

Before the 2008 war, the population was roughly 70% Ossetian and 30% Georgian. The war displaced most ethnic Georgians from the region, and the population has since declined through emigration to Russia and Georgia proper.
