Endless knot

ReligionsBuddhism, Hinduism, Jainism
MeaningInterconnection, eternity, wisdom

The endless knot (Sanskrit: śrīvatsa; Tibetan: དཔལ་བེའུ་, dpal be'u) is an ancient symbolic knot and one of the Eight Auspicious Symbols (Ashtamangala) in Buddhism, Hinduism and Jainism. It is a decorative knot representing the interweaving of the spiritual path, the flowing of time and movement within that which is eternal, and the interconnectedness of all phenomena.

Symbolism

The endless knot has no beginning and no end, and is understood to symbolise the endless wisdom and compassion of the Buddha, the interdependence of religious doctrine and secular affairs, and the union of wisdom and method. In Tibetan Buddhism it is frequently depicted on thangkas, and it appears in Chinese art as one of the Eight Treasures. Because the knot has no loose ends, it is also taken as a reminder that all events in the universe are linked in a cycle of cause and effect.

Forms and usage

The symbol takes several stylised forms: an angular, geometric version common in Tibetan and Mongolian art, and a more flowing, curvilinear version that resembles a woven ribbon. It is found on temple walls, prayer flags, carpets, metalwork and amulets throughout South, Central and East Asia. A mathematically similar closed knot figure also appears in Celtic art, though its origin there is independent.
