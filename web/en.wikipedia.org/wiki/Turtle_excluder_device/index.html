Turtle excluder device

A turtle excluder device (TED) is a specialized grid of bars fitted into a shrimp trawl net that allows sea turtles and other large animals to escape while retaining most of the shrimp catch. Shrimp pass through the bars into the cod end of the net, while a turtle striking the grid is deflected to an escape opening, usually covered by a flap of netting.

Background

Incidental capture in shrimp trawls was identified as a major cause of sea turtle mortality, with thousands of endangered turtles drowning annually in nets in the Gulf of Mexico and elsewhere. The TED was developed in the 1970s–1980s, with early designs arising from the work of shrimp fisherman Sinkey Boone and subsequent refinement by the US National Marine Fisheries Service.

Regulation

The United States required TEDs on most shrimp trawlers in US waters by the early 1990s. Under Section 609 of Public Law 101-162, shrimp imports are restricted to countries with comparable turtle-protection programmes, a measure upheld by the WTO Appellate Body in the Shrimp-Turtle case subject to good-faith negotiation requirements. Australia and several other shrimping nations also mandate TEDs, often combined with bycatch reduction devices for fish.

Effectiveness

Properly installed hard TEDs exclude about 97% of turtles caught in trawls, though effectiveness depends on grid spacing, escape opening size, and maintenance.
