Cold rolling

A tandem cold rolling mill reducing steel strip thickness at room temperature.

Cold rolling is a metalworking process in which metal strip or sheet is passed between rollers at a temperature below its recrystallisation temperature—usually at room temperature—to reduce its thickness, improve its surface finish and tighten dimensional tolerances. It is contrasted with hot rolling, which is performed above the recrystallisation temperature.

Process

Coils of hot-rolled, pickled steel are fed through a series of rolling stands (a tandem mill) or repeatedly through a reversing mill, each pass reducing thickness; total reductions of 50–90% are possible. Rolling increases the metal's strength and hardness through strain hardening, so intermediate annealing may be required. A final light "skin pass" (temper rolling) sets surface texture and flatness.

Products and properties

Cold-rolled sheet and strip are used for automobile body panels, appliances, furniture, electrical laminations and tinplate for cans. Compared with hot-rolled product, cold-rolled steel offers a smoother, lightly oiled surface, closer tolerances and higher yield strength, at higher cost.

Other metals

Aluminium foil, copper strip and stainless steels are likewise cold rolled, often with lubricants to control friction and surface quality.
