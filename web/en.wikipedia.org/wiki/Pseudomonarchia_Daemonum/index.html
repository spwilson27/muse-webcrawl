Pseudomonarchia Daemonum

The Pseudomonarchia Daemonum (Latin for "The False Monarchy of Demons") is a catalogue of demons written by the Dutch physician and occultist Johann Weyer (1515–1588). It was published as an appendix to his De praestigiis daemonum ("On the Illusions of Demons") in the 1577 edition of that work.

Contents

The text lists 69 demons of the infernal hierarchy, giving each one's rank (king, duke, prince, marquis, count, president, knight), appearance, powers, the number of legions of spirits under its command, and the hours and rituals by which it may be summoned. Although Weyer — often regarded as a founder of psychiatry — argued in De praestigiis daemonum that accused witches were mentally ill rather than in league with the Devil, the Pseudomonarchia presents the demonic hierarchy in the traditional manner of the grimoires.

Sources and influence

Weyer drew on an earlier manuscript tradition, including a source he called the Liber officiorum spirituum. The Pseudomonarchia is the principal ancestor of the Ars Goetia, the first section of the seventeenth-century grimoire The Lesser Key of Solomon, whose 72 demons largely follow Weyer's catalogue with additions, omissions and reordering. Reginald Scot translated and adapted parts of the hierarchy in his sceptical The Discoverie of Witchcraft (1584).
