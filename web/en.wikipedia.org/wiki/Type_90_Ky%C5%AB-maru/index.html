Type 90 Kyū-maru

The Type 90 tank (90式戦車, Kyū-maru-shiki-sensha) is a main battle tank (MBT) of the Japan Ground Self-Defense Force (JGSDF). It was designed and built by Mitsubishi Heavy Industries as a replacement for the Type 61 and to supplement the then-current fleet of Type 74 tanks, and entered service in 1990.

Development

Almost immediately after the Type 74 entered service, the Japanese High Command was already looking for a superior, completely indigenous tank design to defeat the Soviet T-72. Development of prototypes, the TKX, began in 1976. The resulting Type 90 adopted the German Rheinmetall 120 mm smoothbore gun, built under license by Japan Steel Works, and used an automatic loader developed by Mitsubishi, reducing the crew to three.

Design

The Type 90 uses modular ceramic and steel composite armour, giving it a distinctive angular profile. Its Mitsubishi 10ZG32WT 10-cylinder two-stroke diesel engine produces 1,500 hp, driving the 50-tonne vehicle to a top speed of about 70 km/h. The fire-control system includes a laser rangefinder and thermal imaging sights, and the tank is fitted with laser warning receivers. With the exception of the 120 mm gun, which is licensed from Rheinmetall of Germany, the Type 90 and its subsystems were all designed and built in Japan.

Service

A total of 341 Type 90 tanks were produced between 1990 and 2009. They are deployed mainly with units in Hokkaido, where the terrain suits heavier armour. The Type 90 was supplemented, and partially succeeded, by the lighter Type 10, which entered service in 2012 and is better suited to transport across Japan's road network.
