United States Army Special Operations Command

The United States Army Special Operations Command (USASOC) is the command charged with overseeing the various special operations forces of the United States Army. Headquartered at Fort Liberty, North Carolina, it is the Army service component of United States Special Operations Command. USASOC was activated on 1 December 1989.

Subordinate forces

The command includes the 1st Special Forces Command (Airborne), which controls Special Forces groups, psychological operations, civil affairs and sustainment formations; the 75th Ranger Regiment; the 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment (Airborne), known as the Night Stalkers; and the John F. Kennedy Special Warfare Center and School, which trains Army special operations personnel. Other elements include the 528th Sustainment Brigade and specialized support organizations.

Mission

Army special operations forces conduct special warfare and surgical strike missions across the spectrum of conflict. Their tasks include unconventional warfare, foreign internal defence, special reconnaissance, direct action, counterterrorism, counterinsurgency, security force assistance, civil affairs operations and military information support operations. Selection and training emphasize language, regional knowledge, maturity and the ability to work with partner forces and interagency organizations.

History

USASOC consolidated communities whose modern roots lie in Second World War organizations such as the Office of Strategic Services, Rangers and the First Special Service Force, and in Cold War Special Forces, civil affairs and psychological operations units. Since 2001 its soldiers have been continuously deployed in Afghanistan, Iraq, Syria, Africa and other regions, generally forming the largest share of American special operations forces.
