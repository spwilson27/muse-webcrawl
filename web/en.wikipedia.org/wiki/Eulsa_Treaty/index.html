Eulsa Treaty

The Eulsa Treaty, also known as the Japan–Korea Treaty of 1905 or Second Japan–Korea Convention, was concluded on 17 November 1905 between the Empire of Japan and the Korean Empire. It deprived Korea of its diplomatic sovereignty and made it a protectorate of Japan.

Signed17 November 1905, Seoul
PartiesEmpire of Japan; Korean Empire
EffectKorea becomes a Japanese protectorate

Background and signing

After Japan's victory in the Russo-Japanese War, Japanese minister Itō Hirobumi pressured Emperor Gojong and his ministers to accept the convention. Five Korean ministers who signed became notorious in Korea as the "Five Eulsa Traitors". Gojong refused to sign, and later sent emissaries to The Hague in 1907 to protest; Japan responded by forcing his abdication.

Aftermath

A Resident-General (Itō was the first) controlled Korean foreign affairs and increasingly internal government. Resistance, including righteous army guerrilla activity, followed, and in 1910 the Japan–Korea Annexation Treaty fully annexed Korea. South Korea regards the treaty as null and void, in part due to coercion and lack of imperial ratification.
