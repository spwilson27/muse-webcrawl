Brief Encounter is a 1945 British romantic drama film directed by David Lean, based on Noël Coward's one-act play Still Life (1936). It stars Celia Johnson and Trevor Howard as a married woman and a doctor who meet by chance at a railway station refreshment room and fall into a brief, intense but unconsummated love affair. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest films of all time.

Plot

Laura Jesson, a respectable middle-class housewife, meets Dr Alec Harvey in the refreshment room of Milford Junction station after he removes a piece of grit from her eye. Their weekly meetings develop into love, but both are married with families, and guilt and social convention lead them to part: Alec accepts a post in South Africa, and their final farewell at the station is interrupted, leaving their goodbye unsaid. Laura returns home to her husband's quiet kindness.

Production and reception

The film was shot largely at Carnforth railway station in Lancashire, and uses Rachmaninoff's Second Piano Concerto throughout as its emotional underscore. It shared the Grand Prix at the 1946 Cannes Film Festival, and Celia Johnson was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Actress. The British Film Institute ranked it the second-greatest British film of the 20th century. Its restrained portrayal of temptation and renunciation has made it an enduring symbol of a certain strain of British romantic cinema.
