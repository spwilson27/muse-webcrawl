Joaquín Guzmán

Born: Joaquín Archivaldo Guzmán Loera, 4 April 1957, La Tuna, Sinaloa, Mexico

Other names: El Chapo

Criminal status: Incarcerated at ADX Florence, United States

Conviction: Drug trafficking, money laundering, murder conspiracy (2019)

Joaquín Archivaldo Guzmán Loera (born 4 April 1957), commonly known as "El Chapo" (Spanish for "Shorty"), is a Mexican former drug lord who headed the Sinaloa Cartel, one of the world's most powerful criminal organisations. He was considered the most powerful drug trafficker in the world for much of the 2000s and 2010s.

Rise and escapes

Born into poverty in rural Sinaloa, Guzmán entered the drug trade as a young man and rose through the Guadalajara Cartel before founding his own organisation. Arrested in Guatemala in 1993 and extradited to Mexico, he escaped from a maximum-security prison in 2001, reportedly hidden in a laundry cart. Recaptured in 2014, he escaped again in July 2015 through a tunnel dug into his cell shower. He was captured a third time in January 2016 after a raid in Los Mochis.

Trial and imprisonment

Guzmán was extradited to the United States in January 2017. Following a high-profile trial in Brooklyn, he was convicted in February 2019 on all counts, including engaging in a continuing criminal enterprise, and sentenced to life in prison plus 30 years. He is held at ADX Florence, the federal supermax prison in Colorado.
