Tandem mass spectrometry

Tandem mass spectrometry, also known as MS/MS or MS2, is a technique in instrumental analysis in which two or more stages of mass analysis are coupled together to examine selected ions. A first mass analyser isolates a precursor ion of a particular mass-to-charge ratio; that ion is then fragmented, and a second mass analyser records the product ions, yielding structural information about the original molecule.

Method

Fragmentation is most commonly induced by collision with an inert gas (collision-induced dissociation), though methods such as electron-transfer dissociation are used for fragile biomolecules. Instruments include triple quadrupole, quadrupole–time-of-flight, and ion trap systems; tandem analysis can also be performed "in time" within a single trap. Scanning modes include product-ion, precursor-ion, and neutral-loss scans, and multiple reaction monitoring for targeted quantification.

Applications

Tandem MS is central to proteomics, where fragmentation spectra of peptides are matched against sequence databases to identify proteins; to metabolomics and drug metabolism studies; and to clinical screening such as newborn testing for metabolic disorders. Coupled with liquid chromatography (LC–MS/MS), it provides highly sensitive and selective measurement of compounds in complex mixtures.
