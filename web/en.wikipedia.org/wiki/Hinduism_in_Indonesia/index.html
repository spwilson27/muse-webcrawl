Overview

Hinduism in Indonesia is practised mainly by the Balinese people of Bali, where it is the majority religion, and by smaller communities in Java, Kalimantan, Sulawesi and among the Indian diaspora. Hindu influences arrived from India in the first centuries CE and shaped the great Indonesian kingdoms — Srivijaya (Buddhist), Mataram, Kediri, Singhasari and Majapahit — whose courts built Prambanan and whose culture spread as far as the Philippines and mainland Southeast Asia.

Balinese form and recognition

After Islam spread through Java, Hindu practice survived in Bali, blending Vedic and Shaivite theology with indigenous ancestor and nature worship: it organises around the Tri Hita Karana philosophy (harmony of gods, humans, nature), the fivefold Panca Yadnya sacrifices, temple festivals such as Galungan, and the silence day Nyepi. To fit Indonesia's monotheistic state doctrine, Balinese leaders from the 1950s articulated Agama Hindu Dharma around the supreme god Ida Sang Hyang Widhi Wasa, gaining official recognition in 1959–62. Dayak Kaharingan and Javanese Hindu revivals federate under the Parisada Hindu Dharma Indonesia council.
