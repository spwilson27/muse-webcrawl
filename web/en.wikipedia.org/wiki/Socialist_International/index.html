Socialist International

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Socialist International (SI) is a worldwide organisation of political parties that seek to establish democratic socialism, most of them social-democratic, socialist and labour parties. Founded in its present form at Frankfurt in 1951 (reviving the pre-1914 Second International and the interwar Labour and Socialist International), it adopted the Frankfurt Declaration rejecting both capitalism's injustices and communist totalitarianism, positioning democratic socialism as a third force of the Cold War.

At its height the SI united governing parties on several continents (its presidents included Willy Brandt, 1976–92) and shaped the post-colonial left; prominent members included European social democracies, Israel's Labor, India's Congress (later departed), and Latin American and African parties. Strains over members with authoritarian records and financial difficulties led prominent European parties to found the Progressive Alliance (2013) as a parallel network, and several parties reduced SI involvement without formally leaving. The SI continues to hold congresses and campaigns on climate, democracy and inequality, as the institutional memory of a century of socialist internationalism.
