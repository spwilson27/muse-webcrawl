Normal form (mathematics)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, a normal form is a standard or canonical way of presenting mathematical objects. Often, it means the object is represented in the simplest or most efficient way possible, so that two objects are equal precisely when their normal forms coincide.

Examples

The normal form of a matrix under row operations is its reduced row echelon form; under similarity transformations, rational canonical form and Jordan normal form serve as normal forms. A rational number is most naturally written in lowest terms, a polynomial may be written in descending order of degree, and a positive integer may be written in decimal or prime factorization — each is a normal form of the respective object.

Logic and computation

In mathematical logic, formulas may be transformed into conjunctive normal form or disjunctive normal form, which are useful in automated theorem proving and in the analysis of SAT problems. In the lambda calculus and rewriting systems, a term is in normal form if no rewrite rule applies to it; the study of whether every term has a unique normal form (confluence and termination) is central to the Church–Rosser theory.

Databases

In database theory, normal forms (first, second, third, Boyce–Codd, and higher) are criteria for structuring relational tables so as to reduce redundancy and avoid update anomalies. Although the sense is somewhat different — a design standard rather than a canonical representation — the term shares the idea of a preferred standard shape.
