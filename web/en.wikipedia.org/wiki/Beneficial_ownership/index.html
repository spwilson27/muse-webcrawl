Beneficial ownership

Beneficial ownership is a term in domestic and international commercial law referring to any natural person who, directly or indirectly, ultimately owns or effectively controls a legal entity or arrangement, such as a company, a trust, or a foundation. The beneficial owner is distinguished from the legal owner or title holder: a nominee shareholder or a corporate director may appear on paper as the owner, while another person — the beneficial owner — enjoys the actual economic benefits and control.

Definitions

Definitions vary by jurisdiction, but most share two elements: an ownership stake above a threshold (commonly 25 percent of shares or voting rights) and the exercise of control by other means. The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) defines a beneficial owner as the natural person or persons who ultimately own or control a customer and/or the natural person on whose behalf a transaction is being conducted, including persons exercising ultimate effective control over a legal person or arrangement.

Transparency and registration

Because opaque corporate structures and shell companies can facilitate money laundering, tax evasion, corruption, and sanctions evasion, many jurisdictions have introduced beneficial ownership registers requiring companies to disclose their beneficial owners. The United Kingdom established a public register of "persons with significant control" in 2016. The European Union's Fourth and Fifth Anti-Money Laundering Directives required member states to maintain central registers. In the United States, the Corporate Transparency Act of 2021 required most companies to report beneficial ownership information to the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), with reporting beginning in 2024.

Criticism and challenges

Transparency advocates argue that public registers deter illicit finance, but implementation faces challenges: verifying submitted information, protecting privacy and personal safety, defining thresholds that cannot be gamed, and covering trusts and complex cross-border structures. Disputes over public access — including a 2022 European Court of Justice ruling restricting general public access to EU registers on privacy grounds — illustrate the tension between transparency and data protection.
