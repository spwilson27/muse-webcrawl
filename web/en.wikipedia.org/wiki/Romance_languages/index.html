The Romance languages, also known as the Latin or Neo-Latin languages, are the languages that evolved from Vulgar Latin between the third and eighth centuries. They are a subgroup of the Italic languages in the Indo-European language family. The five most widely spoken Romance languages by number of native speakers are Spanish (489 million), Portuguese (240 million), French (80 million), Italian (67 million), and Romanian (25 million).

Origins

Romance languages descend from the spoken Latin of the Roman Empire. As the empire expanded, soldiers, settlers and administrators carried colloquial Latin across Europe. After the empire's fall, regional varieties diverged, influenced by substrate languages and later invasions, eventually becoming distinct languages. The earliest recognisably Romance texts include the Strasbourg Oaths (842 CE), written in an early form of Old French.

Classification

Romance languages are traditionally divided geographically. Western Romance includes Spanish, Portuguese, Catalan, French and the Gallo-Italic languages; Eastern Romance includes Italian (in some schemes) and Romanian; Sardinian is often treated as its own early-branching group. Scholars debate classifications based on shared innovations such as plural formation (vowel-based in the east, -s based in the west).

Characteristics

Common features include two or three grammatical genders, loss of most Latin noun cases (retained partially in Romanian), definite and indefinite articles developed from Latin demonstratives and the numeral unus, and a rich system of verb conjugations preserving tense, aspect and mood distinctions. Vocabulary derives largely from Latin, though each language absorbed words from neighbouring tongues.

Geographical distribution

Through colonisation, Romance languages spread worldwide: Spanish and Portuguese dominate Latin America, French is spoken across Africa and in Canada and the Caribbean, and Romanian is the official language of Romania and Moldova. Catalan, Galician, Occitan, Romansh, Sardinian and many smaller languages survive in Europe, some endangered.
