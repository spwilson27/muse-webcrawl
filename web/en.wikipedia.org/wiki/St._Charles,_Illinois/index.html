St. Charles, Illinois

St. Charles, Illinois
State: Illinois
Counties: Kane and DuPage
Settled: 1833–1834
Nickname: "Pride of the Fox"

St. Charles is a city in DuPage and Kane counties in the U.S. state of Illinois, located about 40 miles (64 km) west of Chicago on the Fox River. It is a suburb in the Chicago metropolitan area, with a population of roughly 33,000 at the 2020 census. Its official slogan is "Pride of the Fox," after the Fox River that runs through its downtown.

History

The area was home to Potawatomi communities before European-American settlement began in 1833–34, following the Treaty of Chicago. Originally named Charleston, the town was renamed St. Charles to avoid confusion with Charleston, Illinois. A dam and mills on the Fox River powered early industry, and the city developed as a manufacturing and agricultural centre. In the 1920s it became a popular resort and entertainment destination for Chicagoans, drawing visitors to riverfront hotels, ballrooms and theatres such as the Arcada Theatre (opened 1926).

Geography and attractions

St. Charles forms part of the "Tri-City" area with Geneva and Batavia. Its riverwalk, historic downtown, and the Art Deco municipal building on the river are local landmarks. Annual events and the Kane County Fairgrounds and Q Center conference facility contribute to the local economy.

Education and transport

The city is served mainly by St. Charles Community Unit School District 303. Metra commuter rail is available in neighbouring Geneva, and Illinois Route 64 (Main Street) crosses the Fox River through the centre of the city.
