Jamal al-Din al-Afghani

Born: 1838/1839, Asadabad (claimed); possibly Iran

Died: 9 March 1897, Istanbul, Ottoman Empire

Known for: Pan-Islamism, Islamic modernism, anti-imperial activism

Jamal al-Din al-Afghani (1838/1839 – 9 March 1897), also known as Sayyid Jamāl ad-Dīn Asadābādī, was a political activist and Islamic ideologist who travelled throughout the Muslim world during the late 19th century. He is regarded as one of the founders of Islamic modernism and a leading advocate of Pan-Islamic unity against European imperialism.

Life and travels

Afghani's origins are disputed: he claimed Afghan Sunni roots, while other evidence suggests he was born in Asadabad, Iran, into a Shia family. He spent periods in Afghanistan, India, Egypt, Paris, and Istanbul, advising rulers and agitating against British influence. In Egypt from 1871 to 1879 he gathered a circle of disciples, including Muhammad Abduh, before being expelled.

Ideas

In Paris, Afghani and Abduh published the journal Al-Urwah al-Wuthqa ("The Firmest Bond"), calling for Muslim unity and resistance to colonialism. He famously debated the French philosopher Ernest Renan on Islam and science in 1883, defending Islam's compatibility with reason while criticizing religious dogmatism. He argued that Muslims needed to embrace science and constitutional government to resist European domination.

Legacy

Afghani's influence extended across the Muslim world, shaping later movements from Islamic reformism to anti-colonial nationalism. He died in Istanbul in 1897, under watch of Sultan Abdul Hamid II, whose pan-Islamic policies he had both served and criticized.
