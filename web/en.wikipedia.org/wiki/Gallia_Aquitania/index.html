Gallia Aquitania

Gallia Aquitania was a province of the Roman Empire, together with Gallia Lugdunensis and Gallia Belgica one of the three provinces of Gaul created by the emperor Augustus. It occupied the southwest of modern France, roughly from the Pyrenees to the river Loire, and took its name from the Aquitani, a people of the western Pyrenean foothills whose language was related to Basque.

Under Julius Caesar, "Aquitania" had denoted only the territory between the Pyrenees and the Garonne. Augustus greatly enlarged it northward and eastward to the Loire and the Cévennes, incorporating many Celtic peoples such as the Arverni, Bituriges and Pictones. The provincial capital was first Mediolanum Santonum (Saintes) and later Burdigala (Bordeaux), a major Atlantic port.

Later history

In the reforms of Diocletian the province was divided into Aquitania Prima (capital Bourges), Aquitania Secunda (Bordeaux) and Novempopulania ("land of the nine peoples") in the south. In the 5th century the region passed to the Visigoths, whose kingdom was centred on Toulouse, before being conquered by the Franks after the Battle of Vouillé in 507. The historic duchy of Aquitaine inherited its name and much of its extent.
