Jacques Cousteau

Born11 June 1910, Saint-André-de-Cubzac, France
Died25 June 1997, Paris, France
OccupationNaval officer, explorer, conservationist, filmmaker
Known forCo-inventing the Aqua-Lung; The Undersea World of Jacques Cousteau

Jacques-Yves Cousteau (11 June 1910 – 25 June 1997) was a French naval officer, ocean explorer, conservationist, filmmaker and author who brought the undersea world to a mass audience. With Émile Gagnan he co-developed the Aqua-Lung, the first successful open-circuit scuba apparatus, in 1943.

Exploration and film

From 1950 Cousteau sailed the converted minesweeper Calypso as a floating laboratory and film platform. His book and film The Silent World (1953/1956) won the Palme d'Or at Cannes and an Academy Award, and his television series The Undersea World of Jacques Cousteau ran from 1968 to 1976, defining marine documentary for a generation. He pioneered underwater habitats in the Conshelf experiments and the diving saucer SP-350.

Conservation

In later life Cousteau became a leading environmental advocate, founding the Cousteau Society in 1973, campaigning against ocean dumping and whaling, and promoting the protection of Antarctica. He was elected to the Académie française in 1988.
