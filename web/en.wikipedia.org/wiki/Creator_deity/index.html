Creator deity

A creator god portrayed in religious art

A creator deity or creator god is a deity responsible for the creation of the Earth, the world, or the universe, found in many religions and mythologies. Creator deities range from supreme beings who create everything from nothing (creatio ex nihilo), to craftsman-like figures who shape the cosmos from pre-existing material, to beings who emerge from a primordial chaos and bring order to it.

Monotheism

In monotheistic traditions such as Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, a single God is the creator of all things. The opening of the Book of Genesis describes God creating the heavens and the Earth; Christian and Jewish theology generally developed this into the doctrine of creation from nothing. In Islam, Allah is described in the Quran as the Creator (al-Khaliq) of everything.

Polytheistic traditions

In many polytheistic religions, creation is the work of one god among many, or of several gods in succession. In ancient Egyptian religion, creator gods included Ptah, who created by speaking, and Atum and Amun-Ra in other traditions. In Mesopotamian mythology, Marduk fashions the world from the body of the slain goddess Tiamat. In Hinduism, Brahma is the creator within the Trimurti, alongside Vishnu the preserver and Shiva the destroyer, while Vishvakarman is the divine architect and craftsman.

Creation from chaos and craftsmanship

Many creation myths begin with a formless void, waters, or chaos from which order is drawn. The Greek concept of the demiurge, described in Plato's Timaeus, is a benevolent craftsman who shapes the cosmos from pre-existing matter according to eternal forms — influential in later philosophy and in Gnostic traditions, where the demiurge was sometimes cast as ignorant or malevolent.

Other examples

In Norse mythology the world is formed from the body of the primordial giant Ymir. In Chinese tradition, Pangu emerges from a cosmic egg and separates heaven and earth. Many Indigenous traditions of the Americas feature creator figures such as the Great Spirit, or trickster-creators like Raven and Coyote. In Yoruba religion, Obatala is credited with shaping human beings and the Earth.
