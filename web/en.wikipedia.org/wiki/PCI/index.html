PCI

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

PCI most commonly refers to Peripheral Component Interconnect, a computer bus standard for attaching hardware devices in a computer, introduced by Intel in 1992 and widely used for expansion cards before being largely superseded by PCI Express.

Peripheral Component Interconnect

Conventional PCI is a parallel bus, typically 32-bit at 33 MHz (133 MB/s), found on PC motherboards of the 1990s and early 2000s. Variants included 64-bit and 66 MHz slots, PCI-X for servers, Mini PCI and CardBus-family successors in laptops, and the serial PCI Express (PCIe), which is now the dominant expansion standard.

Other meanings

PCI may also refer to the Italian Communist Party (Partito Comunista Italiano), the Payment Card Industry (as in PCI DSS, the payment-card security standard), the Postural Control Index, or per-capita income (usually abbreviated PCI in economics reports).
