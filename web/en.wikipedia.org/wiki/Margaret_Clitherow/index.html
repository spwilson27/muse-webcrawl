Margaret Clitherow (c. 1556 – 25 March 1586) was an English saint and martyr of the Roman Catholic Church, canonized in 1970 as one of the Forty Martyrs of England and Wales. Known as "the Pearl of York", she was pressed to death for refusing to plead to the charge of harbouring Catholic priests, an offence under the Jesuits, etc. Act 1584.

Life

Margaret was born in York, the daughter of Thomas Middleton, a prosperous wax-chandler and Sheriff of York. At fifteen she married John Clitherow, a wealthy butcher and chamberlain of the city, and had three children. Although raised in the Church of England, she converted to Roman Catholicism around 1574. Her husband remained Protestant but tolerated her faith; she was fined and imprisoned several times for recusancy, for failing to attend Anglican services.

Harbouring priests

Under the penal laws, Catholic priests were forbidden to minister in England on pain of death, and those who sheltered them faced capital punishment. Clitherow nevertheless converted part of her house in The Shambles into a hiding place for priests, with a secret hiding-hole, and arranged for Mass to be celebrated there. Her home became one of the most important Catholic refuges in northern England.

Arrest and death

In March 1586 her house was raided and a frightened boy revealed the hiding place. Clitherow was arrested and brought to trial at the York assizes. She refused to enter a plea, knowing that a trial would implicate her children and servants, and that refusal to plead carried the punishment of peine forte et dure — being pressed to death. On 25 March 1586 she was executed by pressing beneath a door loaded with heavy stones.

Veneration

Clitherow was beatified by Pope Pius XI in 1929 and canonized by Pope Paul VI on 25 October 1970 as one of the Forty Martyrs of England and Wales. Her shrine is at the Bar Convent in York, and her relics, including her hand, are preserved there. Her former home in The Shambles is also a shrine.
