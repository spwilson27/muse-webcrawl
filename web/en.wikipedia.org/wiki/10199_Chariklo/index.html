10199 Chariklo

10199 Chariklo is the largest confirmed centaur — a class of icy minor planets orbiting among the giant planets. Discovered on 15 February 1997 by the Spacewatch survey, it has a mean diameter of roughly 250 kilometres. Chariklo became famous in 2014 as the first minor planet found to possess a ring system, consisting of two narrow, dense rings.

Orbit and characteristics

Chariklo orbits between Saturn and Uranus, with an orbital period of about 63 years. Its orbit is more stable than those of many centaurs, lying in a resonance-influenced region near Uranus. Observations show a dark, reddish surface bearing water ice, and evidence of cometary activity in the past. The body is named after Chariclo, wife of the centaur Chiron in Greek mythology.

Rings

On 3 June 2013 Chariklo passed in front of a distant star; telescopes across South America recorded, besides the expected dimming, two brief dips before and after — revealing two rings about 7 km and 3 km wide, separated by a gap of roughly 9 km, orbiting some 400 km from the body's centre. The rings were formally named Oiapoque and Chuí in 2023, after rivers that form Brazil's northernmost and southernmost coastal points. Their origin is debated: they may be debris from a collision, material shed by the body, or remnants of a disrupted moon, possibly confined by small shepherd satellites.
