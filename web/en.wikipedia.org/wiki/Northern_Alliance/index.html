Northern Alliance

United Islamic Front (Northern Alliance)
Active1996–2001 (as a unified front)
RegionNorthern Afghanistan
Key leadersAhmad Shah Massoud, Burhanuddin Rabbani, Abdul Rashid Dostum
OpponentsTaliban, al-Qaeda

The Northern Alliance, formally the United Islamic Front for the Salvation of Afghanistan, was a military alliance of Afghan factions formed in 1996 to resist the Taliban, which had captured Kabul that year. It drew mainly on Tajik, Uzbek and Hazara forces and controlled roughly 10 per cent of the country, centred on the northeast, by 2001.

Composition and leadership

The front's internationally recognised political leadership rested with Burhanuddin Rabbani, president of the Islamic State of Afghanistan, while its most effective commander was Ahmad Shah Massoud, the "Lion of Panjshir". Other major components included Abdul Rashid Dostum's Uzbek Junbish forces and Hazara parties.

Role in 2001

Massoud was assassinated by al-Qaeda operatives on September 9, 2001, two days before the September 11 attacks. In the ensuing US-led intervention, Northern Alliance ground forces, supported by American air power and special forces, captured Mazar-i-Sharif, Kabul (November 2001) and Kandahar, bringing down the Taliban regime. Its leaders then took prominent roles in the post-2001 Afghan government. Veterans of the alliance were also central to the National Resistance Front formed in the Panjshir after the Taliban returned to power in 2021.
