Fan Dance (exercise)

The Fan Dance is a load-bearing march exercise used in the selection process of the United Kingdom's Special Forces, including the Special Air Service (SAS) and Special Boat Service. It is one of the test marches held during the hills phase of selection in the Brecon Beacons of Wales.

Description

The exercise takes place over Pen y Fan, the highest peak in South Wales at 886 metres (2,907 ft). Candidates ascend and descend the mountain twice by the Roman Road route, covering about 24 kilometres (15 miles) in total, while carrying a rifle, webbing and a bergen weighing approximately 45 pounds (20 kg), in addition to food and water. The march must be completed within a set time, conventionally around four hours, regardless of weather conditions, which on Pen y Fan can be severe.

Context

The Fan Dance is the culminating test of the first week of the hills phase and precedes further test marches of increasing length, including the "Long Drag". Success depends on endurance, navigation and mental resilience. The exercise has been part of SAS selection since the 1950s, and the route also hosts an annual civilian charity event over the same course.
