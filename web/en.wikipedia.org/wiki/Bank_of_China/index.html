The Bank of China (simplified Chinese: 中国银行; BOC) is a state-owned commercial bank headquartered in Beijing, China. Founded in 1912, it is the oldest bank in mainland China still in operation and one of the country's "Big Four" state banks, alongside ICBC, China Construction Bank, and the Agricultural Bank of China. It is consistently classified by the Financial Stability Board as a global systemically important bank.

History

The bank traces its origin to the Daqing Bank of the Qing dynasty, converted into the Bank of China in 1912 by the new Republic of China; it issued banknotes and acted as the country's central bank until 1928. After 1949 it became the People's Bank of China's specialist foreign-exchange and trade bank, handling China's external finance for three decades. It was spun off as a separate commercial bank in 1979 and restructured as a joint-stock company in 2004, listing in Hong Kong and Shanghai in 2006.

Operations

Bank of China has the most extensive international network of the Chinese banks, with operations in dozens of countries, and is a major clearer of renminbi transactions abroad. Its Hong Kong subsidiary, Bank of China (Hong Kong), is one of the territory's three note-issuing banks. The group provides corporate and personal banking, investment banking through BOC International, insurance, and asset management. Its Hong Kong skyscraper, the Bank of China Tower (1990), designed by I. M. Pei, is a landmark of modernist architecture.
