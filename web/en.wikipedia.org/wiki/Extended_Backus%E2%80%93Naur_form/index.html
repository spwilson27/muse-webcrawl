Extended Backus–Naur form

In computer science, extended Backus–Naur form (EBNF) is a family of metasyntax notations used to express a context-free grammar. EBNF extends the basic Backus–Naur form (BNF) with optional parts, repetitions, and grouping, making grammars shorter and easier to read.

Overview

EBNF was originally developed by Niklaus Wirth for describing the syntax of Pascal. Because many incompatible variants existed, the International Organization for Standardization published ISO/IEC 14977 in 1996 to standardise the notation.

Syntax

In ISO EBNF, a rule is written as name = definition ;. Terminals are quoted, concatenation uses commas, alternation uses vertical bars, square brackets mark optional symbols, braces mark repetition (zero or more times), and parentheses group expressions. Special sequences are enclosed in question marks.

Variants and use

Variants are used in the specifications of many programming languages, data formats, and parser generators. The W3C uses its own EBNF dialect for XML and related specifications, while the IETF's ABNF (Augmented BNF) is common in internet standards.
