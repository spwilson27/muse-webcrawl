James II of England

James II and VII (14 October 1633 – 16 September 1701) was King of England and Ireland as James II and King of Scotland as James VII from 1685 until he was deposed in the Glorious Revolution of 1688. The last Catholic monarch of England, Scotland and Ireland, his attempts to promote religious tolerance by decree and to build an absolutist state alienated the political establishment.

James II
Reign: 6 February 1685 – 11 December 1688
Predecessor: Charles II
Successor: Mary II and William III
Born: 14 October 1633, St James's Palace, London
Died: 16 September 1701, Saint-Germain-en-Laye, France
Spouses: Anne Hyde; Mary of Modena

Early life

Second son of Charles I and Henrietta Maria, James escaped during the English Civil War to the Netherlands, served with distinction in the French and Spanish armies, and as Duke of York commanded the Royal Navy during the Second and Third Anglo-Dutch Wars. The colony of New York was named for him. He converted to Catholicism around 1668, prompting the Test Acts and the Exclusion Crisis attempts to bar him from the succession.

Reign

James succeeded his brother Charles II smoothly in 1685 and crushed Monmouth's Rebellion, followed by the Bloody Assizes of Judge Jeffreys. He expanded the standing army, appointed Catholics to office, prosecuted the Seven Bishops, and issued Declarations of Indulgence suspending penal laws against Catholics and dissenters.

Deposition and exile

The birth of a Catholic son, James Francis Edward Stuart, in June 1688 displaced the Protestant heir Mary. Seven leading politicians invited William of Orange to invade; James fled to France in December 1688. His attempt to recover Ireland was defeated at the Battle of the Boyne (1690). He lived at Saint-Germain under Louis XIV, the focus of Jacobitism, and died in 1701.
