Joseph Stilwell

Joseph Warren "Vinegar Joe" Stilwell (19 March 1883 – 12 October 1946) was a United States Army general who served in the China Burma India Theater during the Second World War. A fluent speaker of Chinese and one of the U.S. Army's most experienced China hands, he commanded Chinese and American forces in the defence and reconquest of Burma, while his abrasive feud with Chiang Kai-shek became one of the war's most consequential command disputes.

Early career

Stilwell was born in Palatka, Florida, and graduated from West Point in 1904. He served in the Philippines and France in the First World War, and in a series of postings in China — as language officer, military attaché, and regimental commander — that made him deeply familiar with the country and sceptical of the Nationalist government's competence. His nickname "Vinegar Joe" reflected a famously caustic wit and an acid pen in his private diaries.

China Burma India Theater

Sent to China in 1942 as Chiang Kai-shek's Allied chief of staff and commander of U.S. forces in the theatre, Stilwell led the retreat from Burma before the Japanese advance — the "walk out" through the jungle into India, at the end of which he bluntly declared "we got a hell of a beating". He then built up Chinese divisions in India (X Force), directed the construction of the Ledo Road to reconnect China by land, and in 1944 commanded the Northern Combat Area Command in the campaign that captured Myitkyina in northern Burma.

His insistence on reforming and actually using the Chinese army collided with Chiang's determination to husband his forces, and in October 1944, amid crisis over command of all Chinese forces, President Roosevelt recalled Stilwell at Chiang's demand. He finished the war commanding the Tenth Army on Okinawa and the Sixth Army in the United States, dying of stomach cancer in 1946. His diaries, published as The Stilwell Papers, and Barbara Tuchman's Pulitzer-winning Stilwell and the American Experience in China, kept his outspoken judgements famous.
