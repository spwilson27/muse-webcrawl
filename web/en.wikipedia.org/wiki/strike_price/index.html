Strike price

In finance, the strike price (or exercise price) of an option is a fixed price at which the owner of the option can buy (in the case of a call option) or sell (in the case of a put option) the underlying security or commodity. The strike price is a key variable in a derivatives contract between two parties and is set when the contract is written.

Moneyness

The relationship between the strike price and the current market price of the underlying asset determines an option's moneyness. An option is in the money if exercising it would be profitable at current prices, at the money if the strike is close to the current price, and out of the money if exercise would not be profitable. For a call option, being in the money means the market price exceeds the strike; for a put, the reverse.

Payoff

At expiration, a call option pays max(S − K, 0) per share and a put pays max(K − S, 0), where S is the price of the underlying and K is the strike price. The strike also anchors models of option pricing such as the Black–Scholes model.
