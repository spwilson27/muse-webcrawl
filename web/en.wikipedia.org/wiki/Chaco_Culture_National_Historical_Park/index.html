Chaco Culture National Historical Park

Chaco Culture National Historical Park is a United States national historical park in northwestern New Mexico, preserving the largest and best-studied collection of ancient ruins north of Mexico. Between roughly AD 900 and 1150, Chaco Canyon was the ceremonial, administrative and economic centre of a far-reaching Ancestral Puebloan culture, notable for monumental public buildings known as great houses. The park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and an International Dark Sky Park.

Geography

The park lies on the Colorado Plateau at about 6,200 feet (1,900 m) elevation, centred on Chaco Canyon, cut by the seasonal Chaco Wash. The climate is high desert: cold winters, hot summers and scant rainfall, making large-scale ancient settlement there a striking achievement of water management and regional organisation.

History

Ancestral Puebloan people built enormous masonry great houses in the canyon from the mid-ninth century. The largest, Pueblo Bonito, rose four to five storeys and contained some 600 rooms and dozens of circular ceremonial chambers called kivas. Construction consumed an estimated 200,000-plus timbers hauled from mountain forests dozens of miles away. A network of engineered roads radiated from the canyon to outlier communities across the San Juan Basin. Construction declined after the mid-twelfth century amid prolonged drought, and Chaco's population dispersed to other Pueblo centres.

Archaeology

Excavations beginning in the 1890s — notably the Hyde Exploring Expedition under George Pepper, and later Neil Judd's Smithsonian work in the 1920s — recovered rich assemblages of turquoise, pottery, macaw feathers and other exotics attesting to trade reaching into Mexico. Modern research treats Chaco as a ritual and redistributive centre whose great houses may have housed relatively few permanent residents. Petroglyphs in the canyon include the famous "Sun Dagger" solstice marker on Fajada Butte.

Protection

Theodore Roosevelt proclaimed Chaco Canyon a national monument in 1907. It became a national historical park in 1980 and was inscribed on the World Heritage List in 1987. Contemporary Pueblo peoples regard Chaco as an ancestral place of deep spiritual significance, and tribal consultation shapes park management.
