FlixBus is a German brand that offers intercity bus service in Europe, North America, South America and Asia. It is owned by Flix SE (formerly FlixMobility GmbH), which also operates FlixTrain rail services. FlixBus operates an asset-light model: it plans the network, sets prices, markets tickets and handles customer service, while the buses themselves are owned and driven by regional partner companies, painted in the distinctive green FlixBus livery.

A FlixBus coach in green livery.

History

FlixBus was founded in Munich in 2011 by Daniel Krauss, Jochen Engert and André Schwämmlein, and launched its first routes in February 2013, immediately after Germany liberalised its long-distance bus market, ending a decades-old protection of the railways. The company expanded rapidly across Europe, merging with its main German rival MeinFernbus in 2015 and acquiring competitors including the European operations of Megabus (2016) and the Austrian Hellö network (2017).

In 2018 FlixBus launched services in the United States, initially on the West Coast. In 2021 FlixMobility acquired Greyhound Lines, the largest intercity bus operator in North America, for about US$172 million. The company rebranded as Flix SE in 2022 and expanded into markets including India and Chile.

Business model

FlixBus does not own its buses. Local and regional bus companies operate services under the FlixBus brand and receive a share of ticket revenue, while Flix controls scheduling, dynamic pricing, the booking platform and quality standards. This model has been criticised by some partner operators over revenue shares, and the company has faced regulatory and competition scrutiny in several markets, but it enabled rapid international scaling with low capital costs.
