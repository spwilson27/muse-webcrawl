Seven Wonders of the Ancient World

[Image: The seven wonders imagined together in a 16th-century engraving by Maerten van Heemskerck]

Overview

The Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, also simply known as the Seven Wonders, is a list of remarkable constructions of classical antiquity given by various authors in guidebooks or poems of popular tourist attractions. The canonical list — compiled in the Hellenistic era and finalised in the Middle Ages in its familiar form — comprises the Great Pyramid of Giza, the Hanging Gardens of Babylon, the Statue of Zeus at Olympia, the Temple of Artemis at Ephesus, the Mausoleum at Halicarnassus, the Colossus of Rhodes and the Lighthouse of Alexandria.

Survival and legacy

Only the Great Pyramid survives today (and the Hanging Gardens may never have existed). The others fell to earthquakes and fire: the Colossus stood only about 56 years, and the Mausoleum survived into the medieval period before crusaders quarried it. The list has spawned successors — medieval wonders, wonders of the modern world (ASCE), and the New7Wonders of 2007 chosen by global vote — and remains the template for ranking human achievement.
