A command sergeant major (CSM) is a senior non-commissioned rank and position in the United States Army. The CSM is the senior enlisted advisor to a unit commander at battalion level and above, responsible for the welfare, discipline, training, and professional development of enlisted soldiers, and serves as the commander's eyes and ears within the formation. The rank is in pay grade E-9, equivalent in grade to a sergeant major, but the command sergeant major carries a leadership position of greater responsibility.

Duties

The CSM advises the commander on all matters affecting enlisted personnel, carries the unit's colours (and is traditionally custodian of them), oversees the non-commissioned officer support channel, and represents the enlisted force at ceremonies and official functions. Command sergeants major are selected for command positions after serving as first sergeants and sergeants major; those in the most senior posts serve at brigade, division, corps, and Army level, culminating in the Sergeant Major of the Army, the senior enlisted advisor to the Chief of Staff.

History and insignia

The position was established in 1967 to give commanders a senior enlisted counterpart distinct from staff sergeants major. The CSM insignia is that of a sergeant major — three up chevrons, three down rockers, and a five-pointed star — with the star encircled by a wreath, denoting the command role. The holder is addressed as "Sergeant Major".
