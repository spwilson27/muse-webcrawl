Victoria Falls is a waterfall on the Zambezi River in southern Africa, forming the border between Zambia and Zimbabwe. It is one of the world's largest waterfalls: although neither the highest nor the widest single falls, its combined width of 1,708 metres and height of 108 metres form the largest sheet of falling water on Earth. In the local Lozi language it is Mosi-oa-Tunya, "The Smoke That Thunders", for the spray that rises hundreds of metres and can be seen from far away.

Geography and visits

The falls occur where the full width of the Zambezi plunges into a narrow chasm cut in basalt, then zigzags through a series of gorges. Scottish explorer David Livingstone was the first European to record a sighting, in 1855, naming the falls for Queen Victoria. The Victoria Falls Bridge (1905) carries road and rail between the two countries. Flow varies hugely with season, peaking around April after the rains; at low water the Eastern Cataract may nearly dry up, while at high water — as much as 500 million litres a minute — the spray causes a permanent rainforest on the opposite bank, one of the few places in the region with constant moisture. The falls and surrounding national parks are a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Africa's great tourist attractions, lying near the countries of Tanzania's wider region and the Zambezi basin.
