The Northern Ireland Executive (Irish: Feidhmeannas Thuaisceart Éireann) is the devolved government of Northern Ireland, an administrative branch of the legislature, the Northern Ireland Assembly. It was established under the Good Friday Agreement of 1998 as a power-sharing body in which unionist and nationalist parties govern together in a mandatory coalition.

Structure

The Executive is headed jointly by the First Minister and deputy First Minister — offices of equal authority, held by the largest party of the largest political designation (unionist or nationalist) and the largest party of the second-largest designation respectively. Other ministerial posts are allocated to parties proportionally using the d'Hondt method, based on their strength in the Assembly. The Executive Office coordinates government; other departments include Health, Education, Finance, Infrastructure, Justice, Communities, Agriculture/Environment, and Economy.

History

The first Executive took office on 2 December 1999 with David Trimble (UUP) as First Minister and Seamus Mallon (SDLP) as deputy First Minister. The institutions were repeatedly suspended amid the Troubles' aftermath (notably 2002–2007). After the St Andrews Agreement, a DUP–Sinn Féin executive under Ian Paisley and Martin McGuinness took office in May 2007. Subsequent executives were led by Peter Robinson, Arlene Foster and Paul Givan (First Ministers) with McGuinness and Michelle O'Neill as deputies.

The Executive collapsed in January 2017 over the Renewable Heat Incentive ("cash for ash") scandal and was restored in January 2020 under the New Decade, New Approach deal. It collapsed again in February 2022 when the DUP resigned in protest at the Northern Ireland Protocol. Following the May 2022 Assembly election, in which Sinn Féin became the largest party for the first time, the Executive was restored on 3 February 2024 with Michelle O'Neill as First Minister — the first nationalist to hold the office — and Emma Little-Pengelly (DUP) as deputy First Minister.

Responsibilities

The Executive administers transferred (devolved) matters including health, education, agriculture, infrastructure and policing/justice (devolved in 2010), while Westminster retains excepted and reserved matters such as defence, foreign affairs and most taxation.
