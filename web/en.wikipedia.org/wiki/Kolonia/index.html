Kolonia

Kolonia is a coastal town on the island of Pohnpei in the Federated States of Micronesia. It is the capital of Pohnpei State and, with a population of roughly 6,000, one of the largest settlements in the country. The national capital, Palikir, lies about 8 kilometres to the southwest.

The name Kolonia derives from the town's origin as a colonial administrative post — first under Spain, then under Germany, whose word Kolonie ("colony") gave the settlement its lasting name.

History

Spain established a settlement on the site in 1887 as the administrative centre of its Caroline Islands colony, surrounding it with a defensive wall near the harbour. After the Spanish–American War, Germany purchased the Carolines in 1899 and developed Kolonia as its district headquarters. The town was the focus of the Sokehs rebellion of 1910–1911, an uprising of Sokehs islanders against German forced labour, after which the rebels were exiled and their island repopulated by other Micronesian groups.

Japan administered Kolonia from 1914 until 1945, expanding its port and grid of streets. After the Second World War the town passed to the United States–administered Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands, and in 1986 it became part of the independent Federated States of Micronesia.

Geography and sights

Kolonia sits on Pohnpei's northern coast beneath Sokehs Ridge, whose summit offers views over the harbour and the town. Notable landmarks include the ruined Spanish Wall, the Catholic mission church with its freestanding bell tower built in the German era, and the causeway to Sokehs Island. The town serves as the main commercial and transport hub for Pohnpei, with the island's hospital, college campus (College of Micronesia-FSM), and the dock used by inter-island shipping.
