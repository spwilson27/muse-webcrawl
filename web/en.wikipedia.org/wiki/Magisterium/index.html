Magisterium

The magisterium of the Catholic Church is the Church's authority, or office, to give an authentic interpretation of the Word of God, whether expressed in Scripture or in sacred tradition. According to Catholic teaching, the task of authentically interpreting the deposit of faith belongs solely to the living magisterium — that is, the Pope together with, or separately from, the bishops in communion with him. The word derives from the Latin magister, "teacher" or "master".

Ordinary and extraordinary magisterium

Catholic theology distinguishes the extraordinary magisterium — solemn definitions by an ecumenical council or by the Pope speaking ex cathedra — from the ordinary magisterium, the day-to-day teaching of the bishops and the consistent teaching of the Pope in encyclicals and other documents. Definitions of the extraordinary magisterium, such as the dogmas of the Immaculate Conception (1854) and the Assumption (1950), are held to be infallible. The First Vatican Council's constitution Pastor Aeternus (1870) defined the conditions for papal infallibility.

Foundations

The doctrine is grounded in passages such as Matthew 16:18–19 (the keys given to Peter) and the promise at the Last Supper that the Spirit would guide the apostles into all truth. The Second Vatican Council's dogmatic constitution Dei Verbum (1965) teaches that Scripture, tradition and the magisterium are so linked that "one cannot stand without the others". Catholics owe responses ranging from the assent of faith (to defined dogma) to "religious submission of mind and will" (to authoritative but non-definitive teaching).

Criticism and other traditions

Protestant traditions generally reject the magisterium in favour of sola scriptura, holding Scripture as the only infallible rule of faith, while Eastern Orthodox theology locates teaching authority in the whole Church and its councils without a universal ordinary magisterium of the Roman type. Within Catholicism, the degree of assent due to particular teachings remains a subject of theological discussion.
