Pilgrims' Way

The Pilgrims' Way is the historical route in southern England, traditionally followed by pilgrims from Winchester in Hampshire to the shrine of Saint Thomas Becket at Canterbury in Kent.

Route

The route, about 120 miles (190 km) long, follows much of the ancient North Downs Way along the chalk escarpment of the North Downs, passing through Guildford, Dorking, and the villages of Surrey and Kent. The name was popularised in the nineteenth century; in practice the trackway system along the Downs is far older, in places prehistoric, and medieval pilgrims used many parallel routes.

History

After Becket's murder in 1170 and canonisation in 1173, Canterbury became one of Christendom's great pilgrimage destinations, and the road from Winchester — and a branch from London — carried pilgrims for centuries until the shrine's destruction in 1538 during the Reformation. The modern long-distance path, the North Downs Way National Trail, opened in 1978, follows much of the traditional line.
