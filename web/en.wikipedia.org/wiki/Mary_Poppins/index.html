Mary Poppins is a fictional character and the protagonist of P. L. Travers's Mary Poppins books and their various adaptations. A magical English nanny, she is blown by the east wind to Number 17 Cherry Tree Lane, London, where she takes charge of the Banks children. She is firm but fair, vain, and possessed of remarkable magical abilities: she slides up banisters, unpacks impossible items from her carpet bag, and carries on conversations with animals.

The books

Travers published the first book, Mary Poppins, in 1934, followed by Mary Poppins Comes Back (1935), Mary Poppins Opens the Door (1943), and several further volumes through Mary Poppins in Cherry Tree Lane (1982). In the books, Poppins is sterner and more mysterious than in the films, and she never explains or apologises for her magic.

Adaptations

Walt Disney's 1964 musical film Mary Poppins, starring Julie Andrews and Dick Van Dyke, softened the character and became one of Disney's most acclaimed productions; the making of the film and Travers's resistance to it were dramatised in Saving Mr. Banks (2013). Emily Blunt played the role in the 2018 sequel Mary Poppins Returns. A stage musical opened in London's West End in 2004 and on Broadway in 2006.

Character

Poppins is characterised by her prim appearance, her parrot-head umbrella, her carpet bag, and her catchphrases, including "Spit-spot!" and "Practically perfect in every way." She arrives when the wind changes and departs without farewell, a pattern repeated across the series.
