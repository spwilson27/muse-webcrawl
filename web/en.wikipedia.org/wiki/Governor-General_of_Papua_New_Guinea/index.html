Governor-General of Papua New Guinea

The Governor-General of Papua New Guinea is the representative of the monarch of Papua New Guinea (currently Charles III) and performs the functions of head of state under the country's constitution. Papua New Guinea is a Commonwealth realm that became independent from Australia on 16 September 1975.

Appointment

Uniquely among Commonwealth realms, the Governor-General is nominated by a simple majority vote of the National Parliament and then appointed by the monarch, rather than being chosen on the advice of the prime minister. The term is six years, with the possibility of one further term; parliament may also vote to dismiss a Governor-General, subject to the monarch's assent.

Role

The Governor-General's duties are largely ceremonial and constitutional: appointing the prime minister after an election, opening parliament, assenting to bills, receiving ambassadors and representing the country. Executive authority is exercised on the advice of the National Executive Council (cabinet). The first Governor-General was Sir John Guise; Sir Bob Dadae has held the office since 2017.
