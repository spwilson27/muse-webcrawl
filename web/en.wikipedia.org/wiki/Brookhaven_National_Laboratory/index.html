Brookhaven National Laboratory (BNL) is a United States Department of Energy national laboratory located in Upton, New York, on Long Island. Founded in 1947 on the site of the former Camp Upton army base, it is operated by Brookhaven Science Associates, a partnership of Stony Brook University and Battelle Memorial Institute. Research at Brookhaven spans nuclear and particle physics, materials science, energy and environmental science, nanoscience, neutron science, and computational science. Seven Nobel Prizes have been awarded for work conducted wholly or partly at the laboratory.

Facilities

The laboratory's flagship facilities include the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC), which collides heavy ions and polarised protons to study the quark–gluon plasma; the National Synchrotron Light Source II (NSLS-II), one of the brightest X-ray light sources in the world; and the Center for Functional Nanomaterials. The site will also host the future Electron–Ion Collider, selected in 2020 to be built largely around the existing RHIC complex.

Discoveries

Notable work at Brookhaven includes the discovery of the J/ψ particle's sibling charm quark evidence (Samuel Ting's share of the 1976 Nobel Prize), the solar neutrino experiment of Raymond Davis Jr. (Nobel Prize 2002), the discovery of CP violation by Lederman's collaborators and the structure of the first transuranium complexes; the laboratory also hosted one of the earliest research reactors and the Brookhaven Graphite Research Reactor.
