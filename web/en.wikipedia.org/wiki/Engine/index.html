An engine (or motor) is a machine designed to convert one or more forms of energy into mechanical energy or work. Humanity's earliest engines exploited water, wind and animals; the steam engine powered the Industrial Revolution, and the internal combustion engine went on to dominate transport.

Types

A heat engine converts heat into work through a temperature difference, following the limits set out by Carnot. Other engines include electric motors, jet and rocket engines, Stirling engines and hydraulic machines. Engineers measure performance by efficiency, power output and torque, and simple machines are the elementary mechanical devices from which complex engines are built.
