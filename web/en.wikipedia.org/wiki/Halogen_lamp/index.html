A halogen lamp (also called a tungsten halogen, quartz-halogen or quartz iodine lamp) is an incandescent lamp consisting of a tungsten filament sealed in a compact transparent envelope that is filled with a mixture of an inert gas and a small amount of a halogen, such as iodine or bromine. The combination of the halogen gas and the tungsten filament produces a halogen-cycle chemical reaction, which redeposits evaporated tungsten on the filament, increasing its life and maintaining the clarity of the envelope.

This process allows the lamp to run at a considerably higher temperature than a traditional gas-filled lamp of similar power and operating life; this in turn produces light of a higher luminous efficacy and colour temperature. These lamps may reach colour temperatures of about 3,000–3,400 K, noticeably whiter than ordinary incandescent light.

History

The halogen cycle was patented in 1959 by General Electric engineers Elmer Fridrich and Emmett H. Wiley, who developed a working lamp using iodine as the halogen. Bromine-based lamps followed. Quartz (fused silica) envelopes are used because ordinary glass cannot withstand the high operating temperatures and pressures.

The halogen cycle

In a conventional incandescent lamp, evaporated tungsten deposits on the inside of the envelope, blackening it and thinning the filament. In the halogen lamp, the halogen combines with the evaporated tungsten vapour to form a tungsten halide. When this gas reaches the hot filament, it decomposes, redepositing tungsten onto the filament and releasing the halogen to repeat the cycle. For the cycle to work, the envelope must be kept very hot — typically above 250 °C (482 °F) — so the envelope is small and made of quartz.

Safety and phase-out

Because halogen lamps run extremely hot, they pose a burn and fire risk and can shatter if touched with bare skin, whose oils create hot spots in the quartz. Many jurisdictions, including the European Union, have phased out most general-purpose halogen lamps in favour of more efficient LED lamps.
