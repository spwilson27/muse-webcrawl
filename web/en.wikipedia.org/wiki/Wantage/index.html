Wantage

Country: England
County: Oxfordshire (historically Berkshire)
Population: about 13,000
Known for: Birthplace of Alfred the Great

Wantage is a historic market town and civil parish in the Vale of White Horse district of Oxfordshire, England, at the foot of the Berkshire Downs. It is celebrated as the birthplace of King Alfred the Great, who was born at the royal palace there in 849.

History

Wantage was a royal estate in Anglo-Saxon times; Alfred's birth is recorded in Asser's Life of King Alfred, and a statue of the king, unveiled in 1877 in the market place, commemorates the association. The town later appears in connection with a law code issued there by Æthelred the Unready. For centuries Wantage was a modest agricultural market town, its fortunes lifted in the nineteenth century by the Wilts & Berks Canal (which ran through the town) and by the nearby Great Western Railway, whose nearest station was at Wantage Road in Grove. The town was in Berkshire until the 1974 boundary changes transferred the Vale of White Horse to Oxfordshire.

The town today

The parish church of St Peter and St Paul contains notable monuments and a famed set of medieval effigies; the Vale and Downland Museum in the market place interprets local history. Poet laureate John Betjeman lived in Wantage from 1951 to 1972 and wrote affectionately about the town. Modern growth has expanded the housing estates toward Grove, and the town serves commuters to Oxford, Swindon, and the Harwell Campus science complex.
