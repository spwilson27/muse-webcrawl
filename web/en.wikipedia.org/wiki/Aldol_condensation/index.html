Aldol condensation

An aldol condensation is a condensation reaction in organic chemistry in which two carbonyl moieties (of aldehydes or ketones) react to form a β-hydroxyaldehyde or β-hydroxyketone (an aldol reaction), and this is then followed by dehydration to give a conjugated enone.

Mechanism

The reaction typically proceeds under acidic or basic conditions. Under base catalysis, an enolate ion formed by deprotonation at the α-carbon attacks the carbonyl carbon of a second molecule. The resulting β-hydroxy carbonyl compound (the aldol) readily loses water on heating or under the reaction conditions, producing an α,β-unsaturated carbonyl compound.

Types

When the two reacting carbonyl compounds are different, the reaction is called a crossed aldol condensation; controlling such reactions was historically difficult because of competing self-condensation and multiple products. Directed aldol reactions using preformed enolates (for example, lithium enolates or silyl enol ethers in the Mukaiyama aldol reaction) allow much greater selectivity. The intramolecular version is important in ring-forming strategies, such as the Robinson annulation.

Significance

Aldol condensations are a fundamental method of carbon–carbon bond formation and are used in the synthesis of many natural products and pharmaceuticals. Biochemically, aldol-type reactions catalysed by aldolase enzymes are central to glycolysis and gluconeogenesis.
