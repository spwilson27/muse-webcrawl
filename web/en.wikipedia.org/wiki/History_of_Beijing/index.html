History of Beijing

The city of Beijing has a long and rich history stretching back over 3,000 years. Ji, a city of the states of Ji and Yan, stood in the area of modern Beijing during the Zhou dynasty. Nearby Peking Man fossils show human habitation in the area hundreds of thousands of years ago.

Early and imperial history

After periods as a frontier commandery under the Han, Tang, and Liao dynasties (when it was called Youzhou and later Nanjing), the Jin dynasty made the city its central capital, Zhongdu, in 1153. The Mongol Yuan dynasty rebuilt it as Khanbaliq (Dadu) under Kublai Khan. The Ming Yongle Emperor moved the capital there in 1420, built the Forbidden City, and gave the city the name Beijing ("northern capital"). The Qing dynasty retained it as capital.

Modern era

Beijing was occupied during the Boxer Rebellion in 1900, became secondary to Nanjing under the Republic, was occupied by Japan from 1937 to 1945, and in 1949 became the capital of the People's Republic of China. It hosted the 2008 Summer Olympics and the 2022 Winter Olympics, the first city to host both.
