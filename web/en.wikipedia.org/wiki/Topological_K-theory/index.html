Topological K-theory

Topological K-theory

OriginatorsMichael Atiyah, Friedrich Hirzebruch

In mathematics, topological K-theory is a branch of algebraic topology that studies vector bundles on topological spaces by assembling them into abelian groups. It was introduced by Michael Atiyah and Friedrich Hirzebruch, adapting Alexander Grothendieck's construction of algebraic K-theory (the Grothendieck group of algebraic vector bundles) to the topological setting. Topological K-theory became one of the first examples of a generalised (extraordinary) cohomology theory.

Definitions

For a compact Hausdorff space X, K0(X) is defined as the Grothendieck group of isomorphism classes of complex vector bundles over X: formal differences [E] − [F], with addition given by direct sum. The group K1(X) can be defined as K0 of the suspension of X (or of X × S1 when X has nontrivial structure), and higher groups are defined by iterating suspension. There are real (KO) and complex versions of the theory.

Bott periodicity and applications

The theory is governed by the Bott periodicity theorem: Kn+2(X) ≅ Kn(X) for complex K-theory, so there are really only two distinct groups, K0 and K1; real K-theory is periodic with period 8. Atiyah and Hirzebruch used the theory to give elegant proofs of results about vector fields on spheres and divisibility properties of manifolds. Topological K-theory underlies the Atiyah–Singer index theorem through the K-theoretic formulation of elliptic operators, and it has found applications in condensed matter physics, where K-theory and KO-theory classify topological insulators and superconductors, and in string theory, where it classifies D-brane charges.
