Eucalyptus grandis

Eucalyptus grandis, commonly known as the flooded gum or rose gum, is a tall eucalypt tree native to eastern Australia. It is one of the most widely planted eucalypts in the world, valued for fast growth and versatile timber, and is a major species in plantation forestry in South America, Africa, and Asia.

KingdomPlantae
FamilyMyrtaceae
GenusEucalyptus
SpeciesE. grandis
Common namesFlooded gum, rose gum

Description

The tree grows to 40–60 metres tall with a straight bole and smooth, pale bark that sheds in ribbons, often with a stocking of rough bark at the base. Lance-shaped adult leaves are glossy green, and white flowers appear in clusters. In its natural range it occurs in wet sclerophyll forests and rainforest margins from near Newcastle in New South Wales to southeast Queensland.

Cultivation and uses

Planted extensively for pulpwood, sawn timber, poles, and essential oil production, E. grandis is commonly hybridized with E. urophylla to produce fast-growing plantation clones suited to tropical climates. In some regions it has naturalized and is regarded as invasive, particularly near watercourses in South Africa.
