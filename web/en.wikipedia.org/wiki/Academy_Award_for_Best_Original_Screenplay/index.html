Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay

The Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay is presented by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences to the writer or writers of the best screenplay not based on previously published material. It was first awarded at the 13th Academy Awards in 1941, when Preston Sturges won for The Great McGinty. The Academy's writing awards were reorganized several times in the early decades; since 1957 the permanent categories have been Original Screenplay and Adapted Screenplay.

Records and notable winners

Woody Allen holds the record for nominations in this category, with 16, and won three times (Annie Hall, Hannah and Her Sisters, Midnight in Paris). Other multiple winners include Billy Wilder, Paddy Chayefsky, and the Coen brothers' collaborators. Charles Chaplin won for The Great Dictator in the category's first year of competitive writing honors for original material. Ben Affleck and Matt Damon famously won for Good Will Hunting (1997), and Jordan Peele became the first Black winner of the category for Get Out (2017).

Selected winners

  • 1940: The Great McGinty — Preston Sturges
  • 1950: Sunset Boulevard — Charles Brackett, Billy Wilder, D. M. Marshman Jr.
  • 1974: Chinatown — Robert Towne
  • 1976: Network — Paddy Chayefsky
  • 1977: Annie Hall — Woody Allen, Marshall Brickman
  • 1994: Pulp Fiction — Quentin Tarantino, Roger Avary
  • 2004: Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind — Charlie Kaufman et al.
  • 2017: Get Out — Jordan Peele
  • 2019: Parasite — Bong Joon-ho, Han Jin-won
  • 2022: Everything Everywhere All at Once — Daniel Kwan, Daniel Scheinert
