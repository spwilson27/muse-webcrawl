Allan Urho Paivio (March 29, 1925 – June 19, 2016) was a Canadian psychologist at the University of Western Ontario and a former bodybuilder. He is best known as the originator of dual-coding theory, which holds that human cognition uses two distinct but interconnected systems — a verbal system and a nonverbal (imagery) system — for representing and processing information.

Biography

Paivio was born in Thunder Bay, Ontario, to Finnish-Canadian parents. An accomplished athlete, he won the "Mr. Canada" bodybuilding title in 1948. He earned his bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees from McGill University, completing his PhD in 1959, and spent his career at the University of Western Ontario, where he became professor emeritus.

Dual coding

Paivio's experiments showed that concrete words and pictures are remembered better than abstract words, an effect he explained by their encoding in both the verbal and imagery systems — the "picture superiority effect" and "concreteness effect." His books Imagery and Verbal Processes (1971) and Mental Representations: A Dual Coding Approach (1986) developed the theory, which has been applied to education, memory research, sports psychology, and the design of instructional materials.

Honours

Paivio received numerous distinctions, including fellowship in the Royal Society of Canada and awards from the Canadian Psychological Association. He published more than 200 articles, chapters, and books.
