Jim Bede

James R. Bede (April 17, 1933 – July 9, 2015) was an American aircraft designer notable in the homebuilt aircraft and experimental aviation community. He founded the Bede Aircraft Company and designed a series of kit aircraft, most famously the tiny BD-5 Micro, once promoted as the world's smallest jet.

Bede with a BD-5, his best-known design.

Early life and career

Bede was born in Cleveland, Ohio, and worked as an aeronautical engineer before setting up his own company in the 1960s. His first design, the BD-1, was a two-seat light aircraft whose rights were sold and which became the American Aviation AA-1 Yankee. He then turned to kitplanes, marketing affordable designs for amateur builders.

The BD-5 and later designs

The BD-5, announced in 1971, was a sleek single-seat aircraft sold with propeller or jet options. Thousands of kits were ordered, but engine problems — especially the failure of the intended Hirth engine and supply difficulties — meant many kits were never completed, and the company went bankrupt in 1979. Bede continued designing aircraft through later ventures, including the BD-10 jet and the BD-4 and BD-6 piston designs, and remained a fixture at EAA AirVenture in Oshkosh.

Death

Bede died in Cleveland in 2015 at the age of 82. His BD-5J jet version remains famous as one of the smallest jet aircraft ever flown, appearing in the James Bond film Octopussy.
