The Special Reconnaissance Regiment (SRR) is a special forces unit of the British Army, part of United Kingdom Special Forces. It was formally established on 1 April 2005 to provide specialist covert surveillance and special reconnaissance capabilities, particularly in support of counter-terrorism operations. The SRR is based at Stirling Lines in Hereford, alongside the Special Air Service.

Role

The regiment conducts close-target reconnaissance, covert surveillance (including human and technical surveillance), and the tracking of individuals of interest, often working alongside the police and security services. It absorbed personnel and experience from the 14 Intelligence Company ("the Det"), which had operated in Northern Ireland during the Troubles, where plain-clothes surveillance against paramilitary groups was developed to a high degree.

Personnel

The SRR recruits from across the British armed forces, including women — it was the first UK special forces unit to draw operators from all services and both sexes from its formation, reflecting the value of female operators in covert surveillance. Candidates undergo selection and specialist training in surveillance, photography, communications, and close-quarter skills. Members wear the uniform of their parent units or civilian clothing as required.

Operations

Details of SRR operations are rarely disclosed. Members have served in Iraq and Afghanistan; Corporal Sarah Bryant, attached to the SRR, was killed in Afghanistan in 2008 and was the first British servicewoman killed in that conflict. The regiment was also publicly linked to the surveillance operation that preceded the shooting of Jean Charles de Menezes at Stockwell tube station in London in July 2005, in which SRR personnel were reportedly involved in monitoring.
