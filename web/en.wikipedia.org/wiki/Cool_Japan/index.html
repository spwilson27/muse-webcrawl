Cool Japan

Cool Japan (クールジャパン, Kūru Japan) refers to the global cultural influence of Japanese popular culture — including anime, manga, video games, fashion, cuisine, and design — and to the Japanese government's strategy of promoting that influence as a form of soft power and economic policy. The phrase was popularised by journalist Douglas McGray's 2002 Foreign Policy article "Japan's Gross National Cool", which argued that Japan's cultural reach was expanding even as its economy stagnated.

Akihabara, Tokyo — a symbolic centre of Japan's pop-culture exports.

Government strategy

From the 2000s, Japanese ministries — particularly the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) — adopted "Cool Japan" as an official brand strategy to promote creative industries abroad. A Minister of State for Cool Japan Strategy was appointed, and in 2013 the public–private Cool Japan Fund (later reorganised as part of overseas demand development bodies) was established to invest in ventures exporting Japanese food, media, fashion, and regional products.

Cultural content

The phenomenon built on the worldwide spread of Japanese products and media since the 1970s and 1980s — consumer electronics, cars, video game consoles, anime such as Pokémon and Studio Ghibli films, manga, karaoke, sushi, and kawaii aesthetics. Analysts connect Cool Japan to Joseph Nye's concept of soft power, though scholars debate how far state branding, as opposed to fan-driven global circulation, accounts for the culture's appeal.

Media

NHK World aired a long-running programme titled Cool Japan (2006–2023), in which foreign residents discussed aspects of Japanese daily life that surprised them. The concept has also been invoked in tourism campaigns, including the "Cool Japan" branding tied to the inbound-tourism boom of the 2010s.

Criticism

Commentators have criticised the strategy as bureaucratic and vague, questioning measurable returns on public investment and noting tensions between official promotion and the grassroots, fan-translated circulation that originally spread the culture.
