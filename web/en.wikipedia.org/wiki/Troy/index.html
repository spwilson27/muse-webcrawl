Troy

Troy (Greek: Τροία, Troia; Latin: Ilium) was a city in the northwest of Asia Minor, in present-day Turkey, best known as the setting of the Trojan War described in Homer's Iliad.

Archaeology

The site at Hisarlik was excavated by Heinrich Schliemann from 1870 and later archaeologists, revealing at least nine superimposed cities, Troy I to IX. Troy VI–VIIa, destroyed around 1180 BC, are candidates for the Homeric city. Hittite texts mentioning Wilusa are widely identified with Troy.

Legend

In myth, Troy under King Priam was besieged for ten years by Greeks after Paris abducted Helen, and fell to the Trojan Horse stratagem. See Trojan War and Iliad.
