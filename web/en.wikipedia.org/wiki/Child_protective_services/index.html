Child protective services

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Child protective services (CPS) is the name given to government agencies in the United States responsible for investigating reports of child abuse and neglect and for protecting children at risk. CPS agencies, typically organised at state or county level, receive referrals, assess child safety, provide or arrange services to families, and, where a child cannot remain safely at home, arrange foster care or other out-of-home placements subject to court oversight.

History

Organised child protection in the US traces to the 1874 case of Mary Ellen Wilson in New York, which prompted the founding of the New York Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Children. Public agencies expanded through the 20th century. The federal Child Abuse Prevention and Treatment Act (CAPTA) of 1974 provided funding and a framework for state reporting systems, and the Adoption and Safe Families Act of 1997 emphasised child safety and timely permanency, including timelines for terminating parental rights in some circumstances.

Process

Cases usually begin with a report to a hotline, often by mandated reporters such as teachers, doctors, and police. Screened-in reports are investigated or receive a family assessment; caseworkers interview the child, parents, and others, and assess risk. Possible outcomes include closing the case, providing voluntary services, court-ordered in-home services, or removing the child to foster care, kinship care, or group care. Substantiated cases may place a caregiver's name on a central registry of abuse and neglect.

Criticism and reform

CPS systems have been criticised from several directions: for racial and socioeconomic disproportionality in investigations and removals; for failures that left children in dangerous homes, sometimes with fatal results; and for interventions experienced by families as punitive rather than supportive. Reform efforts have included differential response models, family preservation services, kinship-care preferences, and the federal Family First Prevention Services Act of 2018, which funds preventive services intended to keep children safely with their families.
