Old Norse name

Old Norse names were the personal names used by speakers of Old Norse in Scandinavia and its overseas settlements during the Viking Age and into the Middle Ages. Most given names were dithematic — compounded of two elements, such as Þór- ("Thor") and -steinn ("stone") in Þórsteinn — though monothematic names were also common. Many names invoked the gods, animals associated with battle such as wolves and ravens, or weapons and armor, and were believed to convey something of those qualities to the bearer.

Naming customs

Children were often named after a deceased relative, in the belief that the ancestor's fortune (hamingja) would pass to the child; naming a child after a living person was generally avoided. Alongside given names, individuals were identified by patronymics — son or dóttir added to the father's name in the genitive — and by nicknames (often unflattering) referring to appearance, habits, or deeds, such as "the Red" or "Skull-splitter." Bynames of this kind are abundantly recorded in the Icelandic sagas.

Legacy

Many modern Scandinavian names descend directly from Old Norse ones, including Björn, Astrid, Sigrid, Harald, and Leif. The patronymic system survives in everyday use in Iceland, where surnames in the European sense remain rare.
