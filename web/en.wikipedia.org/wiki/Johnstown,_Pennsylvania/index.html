Johnstown, Pennsylvania

Johnstown is a city in Cambria County, Pennsylvania, United States, at the confluence of the Stonycreek and Little Conemaugh rivers. Founded in 1800 by Joseph Johns, it grew as a centre of steel production served by the Cambria Iron Works and later Bethlehem Steel.

The city is famous for the Johnstown Flood of 31 May 1889, when the failure of the South Fork Dam sent a wall of water through the city, killing more than 2,200 people — one of the deadliest disasters in American history. Major floods also struck in 1936 and 1977. The Johnstown Inclined Plane, opened in 1891, is among the steepest vehicular inclined planes in the world. The population was about 18,000 at the 2020 census.
