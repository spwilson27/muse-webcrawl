The Lumbricina are a suborder of the order Opisthopora, comprising most of the familiar earthworms — the large terrestrial oligochaete annelids. The group includes more than 5,000 described species in families such as the Lumbricidae (which includes the common earthworm Lumbricus terrestris), Megascolecidae, Glossoscolecidae, and Eudrilidae.

Characteristics

Members of the Lumbricina have a fully peristome-bearing prostomium, setae arranged in eight regular bundles per segment (lumbricine arrangement), and a well-developed clitellum. Their gizzards and calciferous glands vary by family and are important taxonomic characters. Most are terrestrial burrowers feeding on decaying organic matter in soil, although some are semi-aquatic.

Ecology

Earthworms of this suborder are keystone soil engineers: their burrowing aerates the soil, their feeding mixes organic matter into the mineral soil, and their casts are rich in plant-available nutrients — a role famously documented by Charles Darwin in his final book, The Formation of Vegetable Mould Through the Action of Worms (1881). They are conventionally grouped ecologically into epigeic (surface-dwelling), endogeic (soil-feeding), and anecic (deep-burrowing) categories.

Distribution

The suborder is distributed worldwide. The Lumbricidae are native to the Palaearctic but have been spread by human activity across North America and elsewhere, where some introduced species are transforming forest floor ecology.
