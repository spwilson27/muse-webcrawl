Swissair Flight 111 was a scheduled international passenger flight from New York's John F. Kennedy Airport to Geneva, Switzerland, that crashed into the Atlantic Ocean off Nova Scotia, Canada, on 2 September 1998, killing all 229 people on board. It remains Swissair's deadliest accident.

Accident

About an hour into the flight the crew smelled smoke in the cockpit and diverted toward Halifax. While dumping fuel and preparing for landing, an in-flight fire — fed by flammable insulation blankets and wiring associated with the in-flight entertainment network — spread through the ceiling above the cockpit, disabling flight instruments and controls. The MD-11 struck the sea near Peggy's Cove at high speed about 70 minutes after takeoff.

Investigation and legacy

The Transportation Safety Board of Canada's investigation, one of the largest in aviation history, recovered some 98% of the aircraft and issued sweeping recommendations: flammable materials were removed from aircraft fleets worldwide, wiring inspection regimes tightened, and entertainment-system certification rules changed. Victims included UN official Jonathan Mann and his wife, AIDS researcher Mary Lou Clements-Mann. Memorials stand at Peggy's Cove and Bayswater, Nova Scotia.
