Peshwa

The Peshwa was the prime minister of the Maratha Empire in western India. Originally one of the Ashta Pradhan council of ministers created by Shivaji in 1674, the office grew during the eighteenth century until the Peshwas, based at Pune, became the hereditary de facto rulers of the Maratha Confederacy, with the Chhatrapati reduced to a ceremonial figurehead at Satara.

Rise of the office

The first Peshwas served Shivaji and his successors as chief ministers. The turning point came with Balaji Vishwanath (1713–1720), a Chitpavan Brahmin who consolidated power amid civil war and negotiated with the Mughals. His son, Baji Rao I (1720–1740), one of India's most celebrated cavalry commanders, made the office hereditary in the Bhat family and expanded Maratha power across the Deccan and into Malwa, Gujarat and Bundelkhand. Baji Rao's son Balaji Baji Rao ("Nanasaheb") presided over the empire's greatest extent, ended by the catastrophic defeat at the Third Battle of Panipat in 1761.

Later Peshwas and fall

Under Madhavrao I the Maratha state recovered and reasserted itself in the north, but internal rivalries after his death in 1772 — including the murder of Narayan Rao, the Barbhai regency for Madhavrao II, and the intrigues of Nana Fadnavis — weakened the confederacy. The last effective Peshwa, Baji Rao II, signed the Treaty of Bassein (1802) with the British, and after the Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817–1818) he was deposed and pensioned off to Bithur; the Peshwa's office was abolished and his territories annexed by the East India Company.

Legacy

The Peshwa period saw Pune flourish as a political and cultural capital, the building of the Shaniwar Wada palace, and the consolidation of the great Maratha houses — Scindia, Holkar, Gaekwad and Bhonsle — that survived as princely states into the twentieth century.
