H-1B visa

The H-1B is a visa category in the United States under the Immigration and Nationality Act that allows U.S. employers to employ foreign workers in specialty occupations — jobs requiring the theoretical and practical application of a body of highly specialized knowledge and at least a bachelor's degree or its equivalent. It is the principal temporary work visa used by American technology and professional-services employers.

Eligibility and cap

The H-1B programme was created by the Immigration Act of 1990, which split the former H-1 category. Congress set an annual cap of 65,000 new visas, with a further 20,000 available since 2004 to holders of U.S. master's degrees or higher; universities and certain non-profit research organisations are exempt from the cap. Because registrations routinely exceed supply, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services allocates cap-subject visas by lottery. Status is initially granted for up to three years and can be extended to a total of six, and longer in some cases where a permanent-residence application is pending.

Debate

The programme has been the subject of long-running debate over its effects on U.S. workers, wages and employers' dependence on foreign labour, and proposals to reform its allocation — for example by wage level rather than lottery — recur in Congress and in executive policy.
