Israel Gelfand

Israel Moiseevich Gelfand (2 September 1913 – 5 October 2009) was a prominent Soviet and American mathematician who made fundamental contributions to group theory, representation theory, functional analysis, and mathematical biology.

Born2 September 1913, Okny, Kherson Governorate, Russian Empire
Died5 October 2009, New Brunswick, New Jersey, US
FieldsMathematics, mathematical biology
InstitutionsMoscow State University, Rutgers University

Biography

Gelfand never completed a conventional undergraduate degree; he was admitted directly to graduate study at Moscow State University and earned his doctorate under Andrey Kolmogorov. He founded a famous seminar at Moscow that shaped generations of mathematicians.

Work

His name is attached to the Gelfand representation of commutative Banach algebras, the Gelfand–Naimark theorem, the Gelfand–Kirillov dimension, and Gelfand triples (rigged Hilbert spaces). In later decades he applied mathematical methods to cell biology and physiology.

Honours

Gelfand received the Order of Lenin, the Wolf Prize, and a MacArthur Fellowship, and was a foreign member of many national academies.
