Treaty of Perth

The Treaty of Perth, signed on 2 July 1266 at Perth, ended the Scottish–Norwegian war over the Hebrides and the Isle of Man. Under it, Norway ceded the islands to the Kingdom of Scotland.

Background

The Hebrides had been under nominal Norwegian sovereignty since the Viking Age, while Scottish kings pressed their own claims. The conflict culminated in King Haakon IV's great expedition of 1263, whose inconclusive clash at the Battle of Largs and Haakon's death in Orkney that winter opened the way to negotiation.

Terms

In exchange for the islands, King Alexander III agreed to pay Norway 4,000 marks sterling, plus an annuity of 100 marks — the "Annual of Norway" — in perpetuity. Scotland recognized Norwegian sovereignty over Orkney and Shetland, which remained Norwegian (later Danish) for two more centuries. The annuity was eventually abandoned and was formally renounced when Orkney and Shetland were pledged to Scotland in 1468–1469.
