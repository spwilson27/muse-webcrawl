Chemometrics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Principal component analysis scores plot of spectroscopic data]

Chemometrics is the science of extracting information from chemical measurement systems by means of mathematics, statistics, and formal logic. It applies multivariate and data-driven methods to design experiments, calibrate instruments, and interpret the large, complex datasets produced by modern analytical chemistry — spectra, chromatograms, and sensor arrays — where many correlated measurements are made on each sample.

Methods

Core chemometric techniques include principal component analysis (PCA) for exploratory analysis and dimensionality reduction; partial least squares (PLS) regression, the workhorse method for building calibration models that predict composition from spectra; multivariate curve resolution; experimental design (DoE); and pattern-recognition methods such as cluster analysis, SIMCA, and, increasingly, machine learning algorithms.

Applications

Chemometrics underpins near-infrared spectroscopy in agriculture and food analysis, process analytical technology (PAT) in pharmaceutical manufacturing, metabolomics and other -omics research, forensic analysis, and environmental monitoring. The field's name was coined in the early 1970s by the Swedish scientist Svante Wold, by analogy with econometrics and psychometrics, and the International Chemometrics Society was founded in 1974.
