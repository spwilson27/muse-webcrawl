Grazer (zoology)

In zoology, a grazer is an herbivore that feeds on plants such as grasses (or, in aquatic settings, on algae and phytoplankton), as opposed to a browser, which feeds on leaves, shoots and fruits from taller woody plants. Grazing is a major mode of feeding in animals as disparate as cattle, sheep, horses, geese, grasshoppers and sea urchins.

Ecology

Grazers shape plant communities: repeated grazing can maintain grasslands, suppress shrub encroachment and create lawns of short, nutrient-rich regrowth that attract further grazers, while overgrazing can cause erosion and desertification. Many grazers have specialized digestive systems — such as the multi-chambered stomachs of ruminants and the hindgut fermentation of horses — allowing them to extract energy from fibrous, silica-rich grass. In aquatic ecosystems, grazers such as parrotfish and sea urchins control algal growth and can shift reefs between coral- and algae-dominated states.
