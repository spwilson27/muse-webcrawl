Cooperative Program

The Cooperative Program (CP) is the unified funding method of the Southern Baptist Convention (SBC), established in 1925, through which local churches pool contributions to support state Baptist conventions and the national and international ministries of the denomination.

How it works

Member churches voluntarily give a portion of their undesignated receipts to their state or regional Baptist convention. Each state convention retains a share for ministry within the state and forwards the remainder to the SBC Executive Committee, which distributes it according to budgets approved by messengers at the annual meeting. Recipients include the International Mission Board, the North American Mission Board, six seminaries, and the Ethics & Religious Liberty Commission.

History

The program replaced the earlier "75 Million Campaign" (1919–1924), a pledging drive that fell short amid postwar economic difficulties, leaving agencies in debt. Advocates such as M. E. Dodd promoted a unified, ongoing budget plan — "one program" of giving — which the convention adopted at its 1925 meeting in Memphis. Since its inception, Southern Baptists have contributed billions of dollars through the Cooperative Program, making it one of the largest coordinated mission-funding efforts in Protestantism.

Significance

Because Southern Baptist polity affirms the autonomy of the local church, participation is voluntary and percentages vary widely among congregations. Debates over allocations, designated giving, and declining percentages of church receipts directed to the program have been recurring themes in SBC life.
