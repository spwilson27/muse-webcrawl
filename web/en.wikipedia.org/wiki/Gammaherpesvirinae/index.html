Gammaherpesvirinae

Gammaherpesvirinae
Family: Orthoherpesviridae (Herpesviridae)
Order: Herpesvirales
Host cells: Lymphocytes (latency)
Notable members: Epstein–Barr virus (HHV-4), Kaposi's sarcoma-associated herpesvirus (HHV-8)

Gammaherpesvirinae is a subfamily of viruses in the family Herpesviridae (Orthoherpesviridae). The gammaherpesviruses are distinguished by their tropism for lymphoid cells, in which they establish latency, and by variable reproductive rates in cultured cells. Several members are associated with lymphoproliferative disease and cancer.

Human pathogens

Two gammaherpesviruses infect humans. Epstein–Barr virus (human gammaherpesvirus 4) causes infectious mononucleosis and is associated with Burkitt lymphoma, Hodgkin lymphoma and nasopharyngeal carcinoma. Kaposi's sarcoma-associated herpesvirus (human gammaherpesvirus 8) is the causative agent of Kaposi's sarcoma and primary effusion lymphoma.

Taxonomy

The subfamily contains genera including Lymphocryptovirus (which includes Epstein–Barr virus), Rhadinovirus (which includes KSHV), Macavirus and Percavirus. Many animal viruses, such as murine gammaherpesvirus 68, serve as models for human infection.
