Võro language

Võro (Võro: võro kiil) is a Finnic language spoken in southeastern Estonia, in the historic region of Võromaa (the counties of Võru, Põlva, and parts of Valga and Tartu). It belongs to the South Estonian group of dialects/languages and is distinct from standard Estonian, which is based on northern dialects. Võro has roughly 75,000 speakers, and since the late 20th century there has been a movement to recognise and revitalise it as a regional language with its own standard written form.

History

South Estonian was once a language of literature in its own right: the first book printed in Estonian, a Lutheran catechism of 1554–1555, and the New Testament translation of 1686 were in the southern variety. As the northern-based standard gained dominance in the 19th and 20th centuries, written South Estonian fell out of use, and during the Soviet period its use was discouraged. A modern Võro standard, developed from the 1980s onward, is taught optionally in some local schools and used in newspapers, literature, and a Võro-language Wikipedia.

Features

Võro preserves features lost in standard Estonian, most notably vowel harmony, which it shares with Finnish. It also has a distinctive glottal stop (written with the letter q), a three-way quantity distinction in sounds, and vocabulary and morphology that differ markedly from the standard language. The closely related Seto variety is spoken to the east of the Võro area.
