Mahathir Mohamad

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mahathir bin Mohamad (born 10 July 1925) is a Malaysian politician, author and physician who served as the fourth and seventh Prime Minister of Malaysia, holding office from 1981 to 2003 and again from 2018 to 2020. He is the country's longest-serving prime minister, with over 24 years in total, and became the world's oldest serving head of government when he returned to office at the age of 92.

First premiership

During his first tenure, Mahathir presided over rapid modernisation and economic growth, pursuing major infrastructure projects including the Petronas Towers, the North–South Expressway and the new administrative capital of Putrajaya, as well as the "Look East" policy and heavy industrialisation through ventures such as the national car maker Proton. His government weathered the 1997 Asian financial crisis by imposing capital controls and rejecting an IMF programme, a decision that made him a prominent voice among developing nations. His rule was also marked by centralisation of power, the 1988 judicial crisis, and the 1998 sacking and imprisonment of his deputy Anwar Ibrahim.

Return and later years

Mahathir returned from retirement to lead the Pakatan Harapan coalition to victory in the 2018 general election, the first change of government in Malaysia's history. He resigned in February 2020 amid a political crisis. He remains a towering and controversial figure in Malaysian politics, credited with transforming Malaysia's economy and criticised for authoritarian tendencies.
