Emily Kame Kngwarreye (c. 1910 – 2 September 1996) was an Aboriginal Australian artist from the Utopia community in the Northern Territory. She is one of Australia's most significant contemporary artists, remarkable for beginning painting on canvas at about the age of 80 and producing an estimated 3,000 works in the eight years before her death.

Life

Kngwarreye was born at Alhalkere in the Utopia region, northeast of Alice Springs, and worked for much of her life as a stockhand on cattle stations. She learned batik in the 1970s through community craft programs before turning to acrylic on canvas in 1988–89 as part of the Utopia women's painting movement.

Art

Her work draws on Anmatyerre ceremonial knowledge and her country, Alhalkere, depicting the roots and seeds of the pencil yam and other bush foods through fields of dots, sweeping lines, and saturated color. Major works include Earth's Creation (1994), one of the largest Aboriginal paintings ever made. Her style evolved rapidly through distinct phases, from fine dot grids to broad gestural "dump dump" color fields late in life.

Recognition

Kngwarreye represented Australia at the Venice Biennale in 1997 (posthumously, with Yvonne Koolmatrie and Judy Watson). Her works command record prices for a female Australian artist and are held by the National Gallery of Australia, the National Gallery of Victoria, and the Tate, which staged a major retrospective exhibition in 2025.
