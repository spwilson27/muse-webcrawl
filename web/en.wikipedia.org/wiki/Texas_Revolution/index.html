Texas Revolution

The Texas Revolution (October 2, 1835 – April 21, 1836) was a rebellion of colonists from the United States and Tejanos (Hispanic Texans) in putting up resistance against the Centralist Republic of Mexico's government in Texas. The revolution began with political and cultural clashes between the Mexican government and the Anglo-American settlers who had immigrated to Mexican Texas under the empresario system, and was precipitated by Mexico's shift from a federalist to a centralist system under President Antonio López de Santa Anna, who abrogated the Constitution of 1824 and dissolved state legislatures.

Course of the war

Hostilities opened at the Battle of Gonzales on 2 October 1835, when Texians refused to surrender a cannon to Mexican troops—the "Come and Take It" flag. Texian forces soon captured San Antonio de Béxar (December 1835). Santa Anna marched north with several thousand troops and besieged the Alamo, which fell on 6 March 1836 after a 13-day siege; its roughly 200 defenders, including William B. Travis, James Bowie and Davy Crockett, were killed. Weeks later, some 350 Texian prisoners under James Fannin were executed in the Goliad Massacre. On 2 March 1836, delegates at Washington-on-the-Brazos had declared Texan independence, adopting a Declaration of Independence and a constitution for the Republic of Texas, with David G. Burnet as interim president.

San Jacinto and aftermath

The revolution ended decisively at the Battle of San Jacinto on 21 April 1836, where General Sam Houston's army attacked Santa Anna's forces during their afternoon siesta and routed them in about eighteen minutes, shouting "Remember the Alamo! Remember Goliad!" Santa Anna was captured the next day and signed the Treaties of Velasco, agreeing to withdraw Mexican troops south of the Rio Grande. Mexico never ratified the treaties nor recognized Texan independence, but the Republic of Texas functioned as an independent state until its annexation by the United States in 1845, a move that helped trigger the Mexican–American War (1846–1848).
