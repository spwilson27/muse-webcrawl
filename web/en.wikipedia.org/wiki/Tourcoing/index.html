Tourcoing (French pronunciation: [tuʁkwɛ̃]; Dutch: Toerkonje) is a city in northern France on the Belgian border. It is designated municipally as a commune within the department of Nord. Tourcoing is situated near the cities of Lille and Roubaix and the Belgian city of Kortrijk, with which it forms the Franco-Belgian Eurometropolis of Lille–Kortrijk–Tournai. The city was once a major centre of the French textile industry.

History

The first mention of Tourcoing dates to 1080, in a charter relating to the abbey of Saint-Amand. For centuries the town remained a modest market community under the counts of Flanders, and later passed through Burgundian, Spanish and finally French hands, being definitively annexed to France by the Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle in 1668, which ended the War of Devolution.

During the French Revolutionary Wars, Tourcoing was the scene of the Battle of Tourcoing on 17–18 May 1794, in which French revolutionary forces under Jean-Charles Pichegru and Joseph Souham defeated a coalition army of Austrians, British and Hanoverians commanded by Prince Frederick, Duke of York and Albany. The victory helped secure France's northern frontier.

In the nineteenth century Tourcoing grew rapidly as a textile town, specialising in wool combing and worsted spinning, alongside neighbouring Roubaix. The two cities became known as centres of the mail-order industry in the twentieth century; the retailer La Redoute was founded in Roubaix and the mail-order firm 3 Suisses in nearby Croix. The decline of the European textile industry from the 1970s hit the city hard, and it has since worked to diversify its economy.

Geography

Tourcoing lies in the plain of Flanders, a few kilometres south of the Belgian border. It is part of the Métropole Européenne de Lille and is served by the Lille Metro (Line 2), trams and the Lille–Mouscron railway line. The Tourcoing canal connects the city to the Deûle river network.

Main sights

Notable buildings include the Hôtel de Ville (town hall), built in the Second Empire style in 1885; the church of Saint-Christophe, a neo-Gothic church dating mainly from the 15th and 16th centuries with nineteenth-century additions; the Hospice d'Havré, a former convent founded in the seventeenth century now used as a cultural centre; and the MUba Eugène Leroy, the municipal museum of fine arts, whose collection is centred on the Tourcoing-born painter Eugène Leroy. The botanical garden and the former railway station are also of interest.

Notable people

Tourcoing was the birthplace of the painter Eugène Leroy (1910–2000), the singer and actor Brigitte Fossey was born there in 1946, and the city produced several notable sportsmen, including the footballer Yohan Cabaye, born in Tourcoing in 1986. The clarinettist and composer Charles Draeger and the Resistance figure Joseph Duthoit are also associated with the town.

International relations

Tourcoing is twinned with Bottrop in Germany, Mühlhausen in Germany, Biella in Italy, Jastrzębie-Zdrój in Poland, Guimarães in Portugal and Rochdale in the United Kingdom. It is also closely linked across the border with Mouscron in Belgium.
