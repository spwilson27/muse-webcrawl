Sophie Lancaster
Born: 26 November 1986, England
Died: 24 August 2007, Salford Royal Hospital
Cause of death: Head injuries from an assault in Bacup, Lancashire
Legacy: Sophie Lancaster Foundation; campaign on hate crime against subcultures

Sophie Lancaster (26 November 1986 – 24 August 2007) was a 20-year-old English woman who was murdered in Bacup, Lancashire, in an attack motivated by hostility toward her goth appearance. Her death prompted national discussion about prejudice against members of alternative subcultures.

The attack

In the early hours of 11 August 2007, Lancaster and her boyfriend Robert Maltby were walking through Stubbylee Park when they were set upon by a group of teenage boys. Maltby was beaten unconscious; when Lancaster cradled and tried to protect him, she was kicked and stamped on repeatedly. Both were left with severe head injuries. Maltby survived with lasting effects; Lancaster never regained consciousness and died thirteen days later.

Trial and aftermath

Five teenagers were charged; two, aged 15 and 16, were convicted of murder in March 2008 and sentenced to life imprisonment. The trial judge described the attack as "feral thuggery". Lancaster's mother, Sylvia Lancaster, founded the Sophie Lancaster Foundation, whose slogan is S.O.P.H.I.E. — "Stamp Out Prejudice, Hatred and Intolerance Everywhere". The case led Greater Manchester Police in 2013 to become the first UK force to record attacks on goths and other subcultures as hate crimes, and the story was dramatised in the BBC film Murdered for Being Different (2017).
