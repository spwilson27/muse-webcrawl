South Dakota v. Dole

South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987)
Decided: June 23, 1987
Chief Justice: William Rehnquist
Holding: Congress may condition federal funds on state action, within limits.

South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987), was a case in which the Supreme Court of the United States considered the limitation on the Spending Power of Congress. The case involved a federal law (the National Minimum Drinking Age Act of 1984) that withheld a percentage of federal highway funds from states whose legal drinking age was below 21. South Dakota, which allowed 19-year-olds to purchase certain beer, challenged the law.

Background

In 1984 Congress enacted 23 U.S.C. § 158, directing the Secretary of Transportation to withhold 5 percent (later 10 percent) of federal highway funds otherwise allocable to states in which the purchase or public possession of alcoholic beverages by persons under 21 was lawful. South Dakota sued Elizabeth Dole, the Secretary of Transportation, arguing the condition exceeded Congress's spending power and violated the Twenty-first Amendment.

Decision

By a 7–2 majority, in an opinion by Chief Justice Rehnquist, the Court upheld the law. The Court set out the now-canonical limits on conditional federal spending: (1) the spending must be in pursuit of the general welfare; (2) conditions must be unambiguous, so states can exercise their choice knowingly; (3) conditions must be related to the federal interest in the particular national project or program; (4) conditions must not violate any other constitutional provision; and (5) the financial inducement must not be so coercive as to pass the point at which pressure turns into compulsion.

Justices Brennan and O'Connor dissented, each arguing in different terms that the Twenty-first Amendment reserved the regulation of drinking ages to the states.

Significance

The "Dole factors" remain the standard framework for evaluating conditional spending. The anti-coercion principle sketched in the case was given real force for the first time in National Federation of Independent Business v. Sebelius (2012), where the Court held that the threatened loss of all existing Medicaid funds was unconstitutionally coercive.
