Jay Berwanger

John Jacob "Jay" Berwanger (March 19, 1914 – June 26, 2002) was an American college football player. He was the first winner of the Downtown Athletic Club Trophy in 1935, which became known the following year as the Heisman Trophy. He was also the first player selected in the inaugural National Football League draft in 1936, though he never played professional football.

Career

Berwanger was a star halfback for the University of Chicago Maroons under coach Clark Shaughnessy, excelling as a runner, passer, kicker, and defensive player. In 1935 he received the first Downtown Athletic Club Trophy as the outstanding college player east of the Mississippi, an award renamed the Heisman Trophy the next year after coach John Heisman's death.

In February 1936, the Philadelphia Eagles chose Berwanger with the first overall pick of the first NFL draft and traded his rights to the Chicago Bears. Unable to agree on salary with Bears owner George Halas — Berwanger demanded $1,000 per game, an unheard-of sum — he chose instead a business career, working in rubber manufacturing and later running his own plastics company. He served in the U.S. Navy during World War II.

Legacy

Berwanger's trophy was famously used for years as a doorstop by an aunt before being donated to the University of Chicago, where it is displayed today. He was inducted into the College Football Hall of Fame in 1954.
