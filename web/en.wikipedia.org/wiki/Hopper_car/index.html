Hopper car

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A covered hopper car used for grain

A hopper car (US) or hopper wagon (UIC) is a type of railroad freight car used to transport loose bulk commodities such as coal, ore, grain, ballast, and aggregates. Hopper cars are distinguished by their sloped floor and discharge openings — the hoppers — at the bottom, which allow the load to be emptied by gravity.

Types

Open hopper cars carry loads that tolerate weather, such as coal and stone, and are loaded from the top and unloaded through bottom gates, sometimes on rotary dumpers that invert the entire car. Covered hopper cars have a fixed roof with loading hatches and carry weather-sensitive cargo such as grain, cement, sand, plastic pellets, and fertiliser. Covered hoppers are often pressure-assisted or vacuum-unloaded for fine powders. Common North American designs include two-bay, three-bay, and large grain hoppers with capacities around 100 tons.

Operation

Hopper cars are among the most common freight cars in North America, moving in unit trains between mines, elevators, power plants, and ports. Bottom gates may be manual, pneumatic, or electric, and rapid-discharge cars can unload while moving slowly over a receiving pit. The Association of American Railroads classifies hopper cars by their construction and discharge mechanisms.

History

Early hopper cars of the nineteenth century were wooden coal jimmies with simple drop bottoms. Steel construction, air brakes, and standardized designs followed in the twentieth century, and aluminium-bodied cars reduced tare weight for coal service in the late 1900s.
