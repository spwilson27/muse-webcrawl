Instacart is an American retail company, operating as Maplebear Inc., that operates a grocery delivery and pick-up service in the United States and Canada. Customers order groceries through a mobile app or website, and personal shoppers select and deliver the items.

History

Instacart was founded in 2012 by Apoorva Mehta, a former Amazon engineer, together with Max Mullen and Brandon Leonardo. The company participated in the Y Combinator accelerator program. Demand surged during the COVID-19 pandemic as consumers shifted to online grocery shopping. Instacart went public on the Nasdaq in September 2023 under the ticker CART.

Business model

The company partners with major grocery chains and retailers, earning revenue from delivery fees, memberships through its Instacart+ subscription, and advertising. It has expanded into advertising technology and enterprise software for grocers.

Criticism

Instacart has faced disputes over the classification and pay of its gig-economy shoppers, including lawsuits and regulatory settlements concerning tips and worker benefits.
