Remix

A remix is a piece of media which has been altered or contorted from its original state by adding, removing, or changing pieces of the item. In music, a remix is a new version of a song created by rearranging, extending, or transforming its recorded elements — typically by a producer or DJ other than the original artist — and remix practice has spread from music into film, literature, video games, and internet culture generally.

History

Dub and disco roots

Remixing began in Jamaican sound-system culture, where producers such as King Tubby created "versions" of reggae hits by stripping out vocals and rebuilding tracks with echo and reverb — the origin of dub and of producers like Scientist and Mad Professor. In 1970s New York, disco DJs and mixers including Tom Moulton and Walter Gibbons created extended 12-inch mixes for dancefloors, while hip-hop DJs such as Grandmaster Flash turned two copies of the same record into entirely new performances.

Mainstream era

By the 1980s and 1990s the remix was a standard commercial tool: labels commissioned multiple mixes for clubs, radio, and different territories, and remixers such as Shep Pettibone, David Morales, and Todd Terry became stars in their own right. Some remixes — from Soft Cell's extended medleys to Puff Daddy's hip-hop reworkings — outsold or eclipsed the originals.

Techniques and forms

Remixers work from the multitrack "stems" of a recording, changing tempo, key, arrangement, instrumentation, or genre: a ballad may become a house track, or a rap verse may be placed over an unrelated beat (the mashup). Related practices include the edit, the dub version, the cover version, and sampling-based production.

Remix culture

Scholar Lawrence Lessig popularized the term "remix culture" for a society that encourages derivative creativity — fan edits, memes, modded games, and transformative works — and argued that copyright law should accommodate it. Bootleg and unofficial remixes remain a legal gray area, though official remix contests and stem-sharing have made the practice a routine part of music marketing in the streaming era.
