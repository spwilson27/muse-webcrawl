Topological order

FieldCondensed matter physics
Introduced1989–1990 (Wen, Niu)
Key featuresGround-state degeneracy, anyons, long-range entanglement
ExampleFractional quantum Hall states

In physics, topological order is a kind of order in the zero-temperature phase of matter (also known as quantum matter). Macroscopically, topological order is defined and described by robust ground state degeneracy and the fractionalised statistics and braiding of quasiparticles called anyons. Microscopically, it is a pattern of long-range quantum entanglement.

Braiding anyons in a topologically ordered state

Topological order emerged from the study of the fractional quantum Hall effect, whose phases — such as the Laughlin state at filling fraction 1/3 — cannot be distinguished from one another by any local order parameter, defying the Landau paradigm of symmetry breaking that had organised the classification of phases of matter. Xiao-Gang Wen and collaborators introduced the concept around 1989–1990.

Characteristics

Topologically ordered states exhibit several characteristic properties. Their ground state degeneracy depends on the topology of the space on which the system is placed — for example, a system on a torus has more ground states than the same system on a sphere — yet this degeneracy is robust against local perturbations. Their quasiparticle excitations can carry fractional charge and obey fractional or non-Abelian statistics, neither bosonic nor fermionic. They also possess a constant term in their entanglement entropy, the topological entanglement entropy, which serves as a diagnostic of the order.

Examples

Beyond fractional quantum Hall states, examples include quantum spin liquids such as the toric code model introduced by Alexei Kitaev, whose excitations and error-correcting properties made it a paradigm of a gapped topologically ordered phase. The toric code also underlies proposals for topological quantum computation, in which quantum information is stored non-locally and manipulated by braiding anyons, making it intrinsically resistant to local noise.
