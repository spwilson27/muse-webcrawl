A Percht (plural Perchten) is a figure of Alpine folklore associated with the goddess or spirit Frau Perchta (Berchta), whose retinue they form. Perchten appear in noisy processions (Perchtenlauf) during the midwinter nights — especially between Christmas and Epiphany, the so-called Rauhnächte — and again around carnival. They are traditionally divided into the beautiful, bright Schönperchten, who bring blessing and fertility, and the hideous Schiachperchten ("ugly Perchten"), whose frightening carved wooden masks, horns, furs, bells and switches are meant to drive out winter and evil spirits.

Origins

The custom is usually connected with pre-Christian winter rites and with the cult of Perchta, a female figure of southern German and Alpine tradition who leads a host of the dead and rewards the diligent while punishing the lazy during the Twelve Nights. Church authorities repeatedly condemned the masked processions from the Middle Ages onward, but the tradition survived in remote Alpine valleys, particularly in Salzburg's Pinzgau and Pongau regions, Tyrol, Carinthia, Styria and Bavaria.

The Perchtenlauf

In a Perchtenlauf, groups of masked performers — traditionally young men of the village — process through the streets at dusk, dancing, cracking whips, ringing large cowbells and leaping to frighten away winter daemons. The masks (Larven), often carved from pine or larch wood by local craftsmen, may depict devils, animals, witches or grotesque human faces; costumes are assembled from furs, straw, corn husks and feathers. Famous runs include those of St. Johann im Pongau, Gastein, Altenmarkt and Kaprun. The related figure of Krampus, companion of St Nicholas on 5 December, belongs to the same broad complex of Alpine masked traditions.

Modern practice

Today Perchtenläufe are organised attractions drawing thousands of spectators, and mask-carving has become a recognised folk art. Debates continue about the balance between tradition and spectacle, and about safety, since the largest costumes with bells can weigh 30 kg or more and the January runs are physically demanding.
