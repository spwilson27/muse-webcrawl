The Khojaly massacre (Azerbaijani: Xocalı soyqırımı) was the mass killing of Azerbaijani civilians by Armenian forces on 25–26 February 1992 during the First Nagorno-Karabakh War, as the town of Khojaly was captured and its inhabitants fled across snow-covered terrain toward Azerbaijani-held Aghdam. It was the largest single massacre of the conflict.

Background

Khojaly, a town of several thousand Azerbaijanis in the Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast, held the region's only airport and had been under blockade and intermittent shelling as the war escalated in late 1991 and early 1992. In the weeks before the assault, Armenian forces took surrounding villages, leaving Khojaly encircled. Azerbaijani authorities and townspeople later stated that warnings of an impending attack were given but that a secure evacuation corridor was not in fact provided.

The attack

On the night of 25–26 February 1992, Armenian forces, supported by armoured vehicles of the former Soviet 366th CIS Motor Rifle Regiment stationed in Stepanakert, assaulted the town. The defenders were overwhelmed, and thousands of civilians fled on foot through mountain passes in freezing weather. Large numbers were shot at close range in fields and forests near the village of Nakhichevanik and along the escape route. Survivors described bodies lying in the snow for days; journalists and aid workers who reached the area documented the scene.

Casualty figures and investigations

Human Rights Watch, which investigated the event, concluded that Armenian forces were directly responsible for the deaths of at least 200 Azerbaijani civilians, and possibly many more, and described the killings as a grave breach of the rules of war; its reporting effectively corroborated hundreds of deaths. Azerbaijani official figures put the toll at 613 civilians killed, including 63 children, 106 women and 70 elderly people, with hundreds more wounded, missing or taken hostage. The Armenian side acknowledged that civilians were killed but attributed responsibility partly to Azerbaijani forces for failing to evacuate residents and for mixing armed fighters with fleeing columns — claims rejected by human-rights investigators.

Commemoration

The massacre is commemorated annually in Azerbaijan on 26 February, and memorials stand in Baku and in a number of foreign cities. The Azerbaijani government and diaspora campaign "Justice for Khojaly" seeks international recognition of the killings as a crime against humanity or genocide; several national and regional legislatures have passed commemorative resolutions, while Armenia disputes the Azerbaijani characterisation and casualty figures.
