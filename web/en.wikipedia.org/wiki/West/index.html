West


West is one of the four cardinal directions.

West is one of the four cardinal directions or points of the compass. It is the opposite direction from east and is perpendicular to north and south. By convention, the left side of a map is west. On Earth, the sun sets toward the west because the planet rotates eastward; a point moving in the direction opposite to Earth's rotation moves west. The word derives from Old English west, related to a term for "evening" or the going-down of the sun.

Navigation and geography

In standard orientation, west corresponds to an azimuth of 270 degrees. Lines of longitude converge at the poles, and by international convention longitudes west of the prime meridian are measured up to 180 degrees. The "Western Hemisphere" is the half of Earth west of the prime meridian and east of the antimeridian.

Cultural meanings

Beyond geography, "the West" commonly denotes Western civilisation and, in geopolitics, the industrialised democracies of Europe, North America, and their allies — a usage sharpened during the Cold War, when "the West" was contrasted with the communist East. In the United States, "the West" also refers to the western region of the country, with its associations of frontier history and the American Old West. Many languages and religious traditions attach symbolic significance to the west as the direction of sunset, death, or the afterlife.
