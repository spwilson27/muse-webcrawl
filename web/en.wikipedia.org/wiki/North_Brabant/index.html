North Brabant

North Brabant (Dutch: Noord-Brabant) is a province in the south of the Netherlands, bordered by Belgium to the south, Limburg to the east, South Holland and Gelderland to the north, and Zeeland to the west. Its capital is 's-Hertogenbosch (Den Bosch), and its largest city is Eindhoven. With roughly 2.6 million inhabitants it is among the most populous Dutch provinces.

History

The province formed the northern part of the historic Duchy of Brabant. During the Dutch Revolt the north of Brabant was conquered by the Dutch Republic and administered as "States Brabant", a generality land directly under the States General, while the south remained Spanish. It became a full province after the Batavian Revolution, and the name North Brabant dates from the period of the United Kingdom of the Netherlands (1815), when the old duchy was divided into North, South and Antwerp provinces.

Geography and economy

The province is drained by the Meuse (Maas) and its tributaries. Historically agricultural and Catholic, North Brabant industrialised rapidly in the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries around textiles, and around the Philips electronics company in Eindhoven, which grew into a major technology hub. The province is known for its carnival celebrations, traditionally strong in the Catholic south.

Municipalities

Major cities include Eindhoven, Tilburg, Breda, 's-Hertogenbosch, Helmond, Oss and Roosendaal.
