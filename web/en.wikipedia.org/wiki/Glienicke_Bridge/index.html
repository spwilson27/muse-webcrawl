The Glienicke Bridge (German: Glienicker Brücke) is a steel bridge across the Havel river in Germany, connecting the Wannsee district of Berlin with the city of Potsdam in Brandenburg. During the Cold War, when it straddled the border between West Berlin and East Germany, it became famous as the site of exchanges of captured spies between the United States and the Soviet Union, earning it the nickname the "Bridge of Spies".

History

A wooden bridge first crossed the Havel at this point in 1660, serving the hunting lodge and later Schloss Glienicke built for Prince Carl of Prussia. A brick-and-stone bridge designed under the influence of Karl Friedrich Schinkel replaced it in 1834. The present steel truss bridge was completed in 1907 to carry growing road traffic on the highway between Berlin and Potsdam, passing the palaces and parks of the Potsdam cultural landscape (now a UNESCO World Heritage site).

Cold War and spy exchanges

After 1945 the bridge lay on the boundary between the American sector of Berlin and the Soviet occupation zone, later East Germany. Because it was reserved for Allied military and diplomatic traffic, it became a controlled crossing used for high-profile prisoner exchanges:

  • 10 February 1962 — Soviet intelligence officer Rudolf Abel was exchanged for American U-2 pilot Francis Gary Powers, shot down over the USSR in 1960 — the first and most famous exchange.
  • June 1985 — 23 American agents held in Eastern Europe were exchanged for Polish officer Marian Zacharski and three other Eastern agents.
  • 11 February 1986 — dissident Anatoly Shcharansky (Natan Sharansky) and three Western agents were exchanged for five Eastern agents, including Karl and Hana Koecher.

When the Berlin Wall fell on 9 November 1989, the bridge reopened to pedestrians the following day, and it now carries the Bundesstraße 1 road and is crossed by thousands of cyclists and tourists. Steven Spielberg's 2015 film Bridge of Spies, dramatising the Powers–Abel exchange, was partly shot on the bridge itself and renewed its global fame.
