"The Little Mermaid" (Danish: Den lille Havfrue) is a literary fairy tale written by the Danish author Hans Christian Andersen, first published in Copenhagen on 7 April 1837 in a collection of fairy tales told for children. The story follows the journey of a young mermaid who is willing to give up her life in the sea as a mermaid to gain a human soul. It is one of Andersen's most famous tales and has been adapted into numerous works, including ballets, musicals, an iconic Copenhagen statue, and the 1989 Disney animated film.

Plot

The youngest daughter of the Sea King lives in an undersea kingdom. Mermaids live for 300 years but dissolve into sea foam at death, having no immortal soul. At fifteen the little mermaid rises to the surface, watches a shipboard birthday celebration for a handsome prince, and saves him from drowning in a storm — but he never learns it was she who rescued him. Longing for the prince and for an immortal soul, she visits the Sea Witch, who gives her a potion that will turn her tail into legs: every step will feel like walking on knives, and she will lose her voice, which the witch takes by cutting out her tongue. If the prince marries another, she will die at the next dawn.

The prince loves her like a child but marries a neighbouring princess, whom he mistakenly believes rescued him. The mermaid's sisters rise from the sea with their hair shorn — sold to the witch — offering her a knife: if she kills the prince before sunrise she will become a mermaid again. She refuses, throws herself into the sea, and instead of dissolving becomes a "daughter of the air", granted the chance to earn an immortal soul through good deeds.

Themes

The tale treats self-sacrifice, unrequited love, the desire for a soul, and transformation. Scholars have noted its origins in a letter of Andersen's following the marriage of his beloved Edvard Collin, and have read it as reflecting Andersen's own sense of outsiderhood; unlike many adaptations, the original ends in bittersweet transcendence rather than marriage.

Statue and adaptations

Edvard Eriksen's bronze statue of the Little Mermaid, unveiled in 1913 on the Langelinie promenade in Copenhagen, has become a symbol of Denmark, despite repeated vandalism. The tale inspired the ballet by Léo Delibes's contemporary Fini Henriques, Soviet and Japanese animated films, the Broadway musical, and Disney's 1989 animated film and its 2023 live-action remake, both of which give the story a happier ending.
