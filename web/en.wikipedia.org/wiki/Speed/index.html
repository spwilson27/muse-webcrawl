Speed

In everyday use and in kinematics, the speed (commonly referred to as v) of an object is the magnitude of the change of its position over time or the magnitude of the change of its position per unit of time; it is thus a scalar quantity. The average speed of an object in an interval of time is the distance travelled by the object divided by the duration of the interval; the instantaneous speed is the limit of the average speed as the duration of the time interval approaches zero.

v = |Δx / Δt|

Definition

Speed is the magnitude of velocity, the vector quantity that specifies both rate of motion and direction. An object moving in a circle at constant speed is nevertheless accelerating, because its velocity vector continually changes direction. The SI unit of speed is the metre per second (m/s); other common units include kilometres per hour (km/h) and miles per hour (mph). In nautical and aviation contexts, speed is often measured in knots.

Examples of speeds

A human walks at roughly 1.4 m/s (5 km/h) and sprints near 10 m/s. Sound travels at about 343 m/s in air at 20 °C. Light in vacuum travels at exactly 299,792,458 m/s, the universal speed limit denoted c. Escape velocity from Earth is about 11.2 km/s.

Concepts

Related notions include tangential speed for rotating bodies, average versus instantaneous speed defined via calculus as the derivative of position with respect to time (taking magnitude), and special usages such as speed in signal propagation and data transfer. Hypersonic and supersonic regimes classify speeds relative to the speed of sound, measured in Mach numbers.
