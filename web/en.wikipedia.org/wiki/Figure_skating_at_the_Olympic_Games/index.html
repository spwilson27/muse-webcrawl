Figure skating at the Olympic Games

Figure skating was first contested in the Olympic Games at the 1908 Summer Olympics in London. Since 1924, the sport has been a part of the Winter Olympic Games. On April 6, 2011, the International Skating Union voted to admit a team event starting in 2014, as the president of the ISU Ottavio Cinquanta stated, with the purpose of boosting the discipline's popularity and in response to public criticism that the International Skating Union (ISU) had not awarded team medals.

History

Figure skating was contested at the 1908 London Games, with events in men's singles, ladies' singles, special figures, and pairs. It returned at Antwerp 1920 before becoming a permanent Winter Olympic event at Chamonix 1924. Ice dance was added at Innsbruck 1976, and the team event debuted at Sochi 2014.

Events

The modern programme consists of men's singles, women's singles, pairs, ice dance, and the team event. Medals are awarded by the International Skating Union (ISU), which sanctions the competition.

Medal summary

The United States and Russia (including its predecessors) have historically been the most successful nations in Olympic figure skating. Famous champions include Katarina Witt, Yuzuru Hanyu, Kristi Yamaguchi, and Alina Zagitova.
