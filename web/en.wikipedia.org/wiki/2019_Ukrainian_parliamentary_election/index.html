The 2019 Ukrainian parliamentary election was held on 21 July 2019 to elect members of the Verkhovna Rada, Ukraine's parliament. Originally scheduled for late October, the election was brought forward by newly inaugurated President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who dissolved parliament in his inaugural address in May. Zelenskyy's party, Servant of the People, won a landslide victory with 254 of the 424 seats filled, becoming the first party in independent Ukraine's history to hold an outright parliamentary majority.

Electoral system

Of the Rada's 450 seats, 225 are elected by nationwide proportional representation with a 5 percent threshold, and 225 in single-member constituencies. Twenty-six constituency seats in Crimea and the parts of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts outside government control were left vacant because voting could not be held there amid the war in Donbas and Russia's annexation of Crimea.

Results

Servant of the People won 43.16 percent of the party-list vote and dominated the single-member districts, many of its candidates political newcomers. The pro-Russian Opposition Platform — For Life finished second with 43 seats; former president Petro Poroshenko's European Solidarity won 25; Yulia Tymoshenko's Batkivshchyna won 26; and the new party Holos (Voice), led by rock musician Svyatoslav Vakarchuk, entered parliament with 20 seats. Turnout of about 49 percent was the lowest ever recorded for a Ukrainian parliamentary election.

Aftermath

The new Rada convened on 29 August 2019 and appointed Oleksiy Honcharuk, aged 35, as prime minister — Ukraine's youngest — along with a reform-focused cabinet. The single-party majority allowed Zelenskyy to pass legislation rapidly in the parliament's first months, including measures lifting parliamentary immunity and reopening the land market, though the government's popularity declined over the following year.
