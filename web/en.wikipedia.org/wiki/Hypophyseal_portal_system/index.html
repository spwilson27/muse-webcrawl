Hypophyseal portal system

The hypophyseal portal system (or hypothalamo-hypophyseal portal system) is a system of blood vessels in the microcirculation at the base of the brain, connecting the hypothalamus with the anterior pituitary. It transports releasing and inhibiting hormones from the hypothalamus to the anterior pituitary rapidly and in high local concentration, allowing small quantities of hypothalamic hormones to control pituitary secretion without first being diluted in the systemic circulation.

Portal veins carry hypothalamic releasing hormones down the pituitary stalk.

Structure

Blood from the superior hypophyseal arteries forms a primary capillary plexus in the median eminence, into which hypothalamic neurons secrete their hormones. Long portal veins descend along the pituitary stalk (infundibulum) to a secondary plexus in the anterior pituitary, where the hormones act on endocrine cells. A set of short portal veins also connects the posterior and anterior lobes.

Function

Hormones conveyed include thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH), corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH), gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH), growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH), somatostatin and dopamine (the prolactin-inhibiting factor). Pulsatile delivery, especially of GnRH, is essential to normal pituitary responses. The portal vessels lie outside the blood–brain barrier at the median eminence, a circumventricular organ.
