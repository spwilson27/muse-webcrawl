The Civil Aviation Administration of China (CAAC; Chinese: 中国民用航空局) is the civil aviation authority of China. It is an administrative agency under the Ministry of Transport, responsible for regulating civil aviation safety, airworthiness certification, route and market oversight, and the investigation of aviation accidents and incidents within mainland China.

History

The CAAC was established in 1949, shortly after the founding of the People's Republic of China, and for decades functioned not only as regulator but also as China's monopoly airline operator, running the entire national air transport system under the name "CAAC Airlines". As part of economic reforms, the operating functions were separated from the administration in 1987–88: six regional airlines were spun off (including Air China, China Eastern, and China Southern), and the CAAC became primarily a regulatory body. Further restructuring in the 2000s devolved airport management to local governments and placed airlines under separate state asset supervision.

Functions

The CAAC drafts civil aviation regulations, certifies aircraft (including the domestically built COMAC ARJ21 and C919), licenses airlines, pilots, and maintenance organizations, manages airspace policy alongside the military, and supervises seven regional administrations. Its Air Traffic Management Bureau oversees the national air navigation system. The agency's accident-investigation and safety-supervision roles follow the framework of ICAO conventions, of which China is a member state.
