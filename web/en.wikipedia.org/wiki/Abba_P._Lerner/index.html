Abba Ptachya Lerner (28 October 1903 – 27 October 1982) was a Russian-born British and American economist. A student at the London School of Economics under Friedrich Hayek and Lionel Robbins, Lerner became one of the most effective popularisers of Keynesian economics and a major contributor to welfare economics and international trade theory.

Contributions

Lerner's name attaches to several durable concepts. The Lerner index (1934) measures monopoly power as the markup of price over marginal cost, (P − MC)/P. The Marshall–Lerner condition states when a currency depreciation improves the trade balance. In his 1944 book The Economics of Control he developed the theory of "functional finance", arguing that fiscal policy should be judged by its effects on employment and inflation rather than by budget balance. He also contributed the Lerner symmetry theorem in trade and, late in life, the Market Anti-Inflation Plan (MAP) with David Colander.

Career

Lerner taught at the London School of Economics, and after emigrating to the United States held posts at the New School, the University of Missouri–Kansas City, UC Berkeley, Michigan State, and Florida State University, where he remained until his death. He advised the Israeli government in the 1950s and co-authored an economic plan for Israel.

Legacy

Lerner was elected a fellow of the British Academy and served as president of the Atlantic Economic Society. Robert Heilbroner counted him among the "worldly philosophers" who shaped modern economic thought.
