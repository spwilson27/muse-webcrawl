Strake

A strake is a longitudinal course of planking or plating on the hull of a vessel, running from stem to stern. In wooden shipbuilding, strakes are the rows of planks that form the hull's skin; named strakes include the garboard strake, adjacent to the keel, and the sheer strake at the top. In steel shipbuilding, the term applies to a continuous line of shell plates.

Other uses

In aviation, a strake is a small aerodynamic surface, typically a slender extension of the wing root or fuselage, that generates a vortex at high angles of attack, energising airflow over the wing and improving controllability. Leading-edge root extensions (LERX) on fighter aircraft such as the F/A-18 are prominent examples; strakes are also used on missiles and on the nacelles of airliners to direct airflow.

In automotive and other vehicle contexts, the word describes a continuous longitudinal strip or moulding. In armour, strake-like plates may add protection, and on tracked vehicles raised grousers are sometimes called strakes. On helicopter fuselages, strakes along the tail boom improve directional stability in the rotor downwash.

Shipbuilding detail

In clinker (lapstrake) construction, each strake overlaps the one below; in carvel construction the strakes are laid edge to edge for a smooth surface. The wale is a thicker strake providing longitudinal strength, historically used as a rubbing surface alongside quays.
