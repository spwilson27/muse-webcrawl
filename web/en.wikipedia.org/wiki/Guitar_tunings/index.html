Guitar tunings

Guitar tunings are the assignment of pitches to the open strings of guitars, including acoustic guitars, electric guitars, and classical guitars. Tunings are described by the particular pitches that are made by notes in Western music. By convention, the notes are ordered and arranged from the lowest-pitched string (i.e., the deepest bass note) to the highest-pitched string, or the opposite, for a six-string guitar.

Standard tuning

The most common tuning, known as standard tuning, assigns the pitches E2–A2–D3–G3–B3–E4 to the strings of a six-string guitar. The interval pattern is a series of perfect fourths, with a single major third between the G and B strings, which simplifies the fingering of chords and scales.

Alternative tunings

Alternative tunings ("alternate tunings") include:

  • Drop tunings, such as drop D (D–A–D–G–B–E), in which the lowest string is tuned down, allowing power chords to be played with a single finger.
  • Open tunings, such as open G (D–G–D–G–B–D) and open D, in which the open strings form a chord; these are popular for slide guitar and folk music.
  • Regular tunings, in which all strings are tuned at the same interval, such as major thirds tuning and all-fourths tuning, favoured by some jazz and experimental players.
  • DADGAD, a modal tuning associated with Celtic music.
  • New standard tuning, developed by Robert Fripp, using mostly perfect fifths: C–G–D–A–E–G.

Scordatura and historical tunings

The deliberate mistuning of an instrument, known as scordatura, has been used since the Renaissance. Lutes and viols employed a variety of tunings, and early guitars with four or five courses used tunings adapted from the lute tradition. Baroque guitar tuning often included re-entrant strings, in which the order of pitches does not rise monotonically.
