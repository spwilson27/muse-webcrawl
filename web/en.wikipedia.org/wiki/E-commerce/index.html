E-commerce

[Image: a delivery courier handing a parcel to a customer at a front door]

Electronic commerce (e-commerce) is the buying and selling of goods and services over electronic networks, principally the internet — through web shops, marketplaces, mobile apps and social platforms. It covers business-to-consumer (B2C) retail, business-to-business (B2B) trade, consumer marketplaces (eBay, Etsy), digital goods and services, and the payments and logistics systems beneath them.

Growth and effects

From pioneers of the mid-1990s (Amazon, founded 1994; eBay, 1995; Alibaba, 1999), e-commerce grew to several trillion dollars of annual sales worldwide — China's is the largest market — accelerated sharply by the COVID-19 pandemic. Its economics feature low marginal distribution costs, near-perfect Price comparison, long-tail assortment, and powerful network effects concentrating trade on platforms run by multinational corporations. Consequences include pressure on physical Retail, vast delivery and warehousing employment, new tax and regulatory questions (marketplace liability, digital services taxes), and data-driven Marketing. Live shopping, quick commerce and cross-border platforms mark its current frontier.
