Possessive

A possessive is a word or grammatical construction used to indicate a relationship of possession or close association between entities — for example, ownership ("John's car"), kinship ("Mary's brother"), or part-whole relations ("the car's engine").

English possessives

In English, possession is marked in several ways: the possessive clitic or case ending -'s (the genitive) added to a noun phrase; possessive determiners such as my, your, his, her, its, our, and their; and possessive pronouns such as mine, yours, his, hers, ours, and theirs, which stand alone. Historically -'s descends from the Old English genitive case ending -es. Possessive apostrophes follow conventions: singular nouns generally take 's (the dog's bone), plural nouns ending in s take a bare apostrophe (the dogs' bones), and pronouns like its take no apostrophe — a common source of confusion with the contraction it's.

In other languages

Languages express possession through genitive case inflections, possessive affixes on the possessed noun, adpositions, or possessive classifiers (common in Oceanic languages, distinguishing e.g. food, drink, and general possessions). Some languages distinguish alienable from inalienable possession grammatically.
