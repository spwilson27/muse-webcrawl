Penn State wrestling, the dominant NCAA programme of the 2010s and 2020s

The Penn State Nittany Lions wrestling team represents Pennsylvania State University in NCAA Division I collegiate wrestling. Under head coach Cael Sanderson, hired in 2009, the programme became the dominant force in American college wrestling, winning a long string of NCAA team championships beginning in 2011.

History

Penn State's wrestling programme dates to the early 20th century and won its first NCAA team title in 1953. For decades it was a strong regional programme but was overshadowed nationally by Iowa and Oklahoma State. The hiring of Sanderson — himself an undefeated four-time NCAA champion at Iowa State and an Olympic gold medallist — transformed the programme.

Sanderson era

From 2011 onward, Penn State won the overwhelming majority of NCAA team championships, often by wide margins, and set scoring records at the national tournament. The programme developed a distinctive style emphasising aggressive scoring, riding, and bonus points, and produced numerous individual national champions and Hodge Trophy winners, including David Taylor, Zain Retherford, Bo Nickal, Jason Nolf, and Carter Starocci. Home duals at the Bryce Jordan Center have drawn crowds exceeding 15,000, among the largest in college wrestling history.
