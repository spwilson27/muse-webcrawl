Grand Rapids, Michigan

Grand Rapids is a city in and the county seat of Kent County, Michigan, United States. It is the second-largest city in Michigan, with a population of about 198,000 at the 2020 census, and the centre of a metropolitan area of over one million people. The city lies on the Grand River about 30 miles (48 km) east of Lake Michigan.

History

The area was inhabited by the Ottawa people before European settlement; a fur trading post was established in 1826 and the settlement was incorporated as a city in 1850. Grand Rapids became a national centre of furniture manufacturing — earning it the nickname "Furniture City" — and hosted the first hydroelectric generator in the United States in 1880. President Gerald R. Ford was raised in Grand Rapids, and his presidential museum and grave are located in the city.

Economy and culture

The modern economy is anchored by health care, education, manufacturing, and logistics, with major employers including Corewell Health, Meijer (headquartered nearby in Walker), Steelcase, and Herman Miller's successor MillerKnoll. The city is known for its craft beer scene — it has repeatedly been voted "Beer City USA" — and for ArtPrize, a large open art competition held since 2009. Frederik Meijer Gardens & Sculpture Park is a major regional attraction.
