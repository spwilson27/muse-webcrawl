Funkadelic was an American band most prominent during the 1970s. The band and its sister act Parliament, both led by George Clinton, began the funk music collective Parliament-Funkadelic (P-Funk). Funkadelic's music was more guitar-driven and rooted in psychedelic rock than Parliament's horn- and R&B-based sound.

The group originated as the backing band for Clinton's doo-wop group The Parliaments in Plainfield, New Jersey. After a hit with "(I Wanna) Testify" in 1967 and the temporary loss of the Parliament name in a contract dispute, Clinton reorganised the musicians as Funkadelic, signing with Westbound Records.

Albums and legacy

Funkadelic's early albums — Funkadelic (1970), Free Your Mind... and Your Ass Will Follow (1970) and especially Maggot Brain (1971), with Eddie Hazel's celebrated ten-minute title-track guitar solo — blended funk, rock and psychedelia. Later in the decade the band scored major hits with One Nation Under a Groove (1978), whose title track reached number one on the R&B chart, and "(Not Just) Knee Deep" (1979). Legal disputes over the Funkadelic name ended the band's original run in the early 1980s, but its records were heavily sampled by hip-hop artists, and Parliament-Funkadelic was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997.
