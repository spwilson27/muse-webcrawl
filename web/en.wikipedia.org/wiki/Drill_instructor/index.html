A drill instructor is a non-commissioned officer in the armed forces responsible for the initial training of recruits — their drill, discipline, physical conditioning, customs and basic military skills. The role is iconic in several militaries, symbolised in the United States by the campaign cover worn by Marine Corps drill instructors and Army drill sergeants, and the figure is a staple of popular culture, notably R. Lee Ermey's portrayal in the film Full Metal Jacket (1987).

Role and methods

Drill instructors are deliberately the recruits' first and most intimidating point of contact with military life: through close-order drill, inspections, physical training and relentless correction they break down civilian habits and build unit cohesion, instant obedience to orders and attention to detail. Modern training doctrine emphasises controlled stress and mentorship over the abusive "shark attack" methods of earlier eras, which have been restricted after investigations into recruit deaths and abuse scandals.

By country and service

In the US Marine Corps, drill instructors are selected volunteers or screened assignees who complete Drill Instructor School at Parris Island or San Diego and serve demanding multi-year tours, working in teams of three or four per recruit platoon. The US Army equivalent is the drill sergeant (identified by the campaign hat and "This We'll Defend" badge), the Navy uses Recruit Division Commanders, the Air Force Military Training Instructors, and the Coast Guard company commanders. Other militaries have analogous figures, such as the drill sergeants and regimental sergeant majors of British recruit training and the drill staff of Commonwealth forces.

Selection and prestige

Drill instructor duty is considered career-enhancing and among the most demanding billets for enlisted personnel, involving very long hours and constant exemplary bearing, since instructors are expected to embody the standards they enforce.
