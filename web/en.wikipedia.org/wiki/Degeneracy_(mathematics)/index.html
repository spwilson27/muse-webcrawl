Degeneracy (mathematics)

In mathematics, a degenerate case is a limiting case of a class of objects that appears qualitatively different from the rest, often simpler, such as a point regarded as a circle of radius zero.

Examples

A degenerate triangle has collinear vertices and zero area; a degenerate conic may split into lines; degenerate eigenvalues have multiple eigenvectors in linear algebra contexts.

Usage

The term is usually relative to a definition and may be pejorative or simply descriptive depending on context.

Related concepts

Degeneracy also appears in physics for states sharing the same energy, and in optimisation for solutions with special structure.
