Bearing (navigation)

Measured: In degrees clockwise from a reference direction
Types: True, magnetic, compass, relative

In navigation, the bearing is the horizontal angle between the direction of an object and another reference direction, most commonly (absolute) true north. Usually a bearing is measured clockwise through 360 degrees in units of degrees. Bearings are used to describe the direction of an object from an observer, the direction of travel, and to fix a position by taking bearings on two or more charted objects (triangulation or a "fix").

Types

  • True bearing — measured from true (geographic) north.
  • Magnetic bearing — measured from magnetic north; differs from true bearing by the local magnetic variation (declination).
  • Compass bearing — as read on a particular compass, differing by the vessel's or aircraft's deviation.
  • Relative bearing — measured from the heading of the observer's vessel or aircraft: an object dead ahead is at relative bearing 000°, abeam to starboard 090°, astern 180°, and abeam to port 270°.

Usage

Mariners take bearings with a hand-bearing compass, pelorus, or radar; aviators use radio bearings such as VOR radials and NDB bearings. A series of bearings on a single object can yield a "running fix", and the rate of change of bearing is used in collision avoidance: if the bearing of an approaching vessel remains constant while the range decreases, a risk of collision exists ("constant bearing, decreasing range"). Bearings are often expressed in three figures, e.g. 045°, to avoid error.
