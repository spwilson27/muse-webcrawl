Duke of Marlborough is a title in the Peerage of England, created in 1702 for John Churchill, the great general of the War of the Spanish Succession. The dukes' seat, Blenheim Palace in Oxfordshire, was built as a gift from the nation to the 1st Duke and is the only non-royal, non-episcopal country house in England styled a palace.

History

John Churchill won celebrated victories at Blenheim (1704), Ramillies (1706), Oudenarde (1708) and Malplaquet (1709). Because the 1st Duke's sons died young, a special Act of Parliament allowed the dukedom to pass through his daughters. The title descended to the Spencer family, who later took the name Spencer-Churchill. Notable holders and relatives include the 7th Duke, grandfather of Winston Churchill, who was born at Blenheim in 1874, and Consuelo Vanderbilt, American wife of the 9th Duke.

The current holder is James Spencer-Churchill, 12th Duke of Marlborough. The heir apparent is George Spencer-Churchill, Marquess of Blandford.
