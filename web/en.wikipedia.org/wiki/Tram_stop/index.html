Tram stop

A tram stop, tram station, or streetcar stop is a designated place where a tram (streetcar) stops so passengers can board or alight. Tram stops vary from a simple signpost beside the track to sheltered platforms, and in size and fittings according to the volume of passengers.

Design

Older systems often require passengers to board from the road surface or a low kerb, while modern low-floor trams are matched with raised platforms of about 30 cm for level boarding, satisfying accessibility requirements. Side platforms are most common; island platforms serve both directions from one structure. Safety considerations include the risk of passengers crossing the track in front of other trams and the "dooring" hazard where stops adjoin traffic lanes.

Request stops

Some stops are request stops, where the tram halts only if a passenger signals or presses the stop button. In many cities stop spacing on tram lines is shorter than on heavy rail, typically 300–500 metres in city centres, reflecting the tram's role as a local distributor.

Relationship to bus stops

Tram stops are similar in function to bus stops, but are fixed by the track layout and often involve more substantial infrastructure; in shared streetscapes, combined bus–tram stops with raised boarding islands are increasingly common.
