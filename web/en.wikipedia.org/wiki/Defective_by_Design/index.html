Defective by Design

Defective by Design (often abbreviated DbD) is an anti-digital rights management (DRM) initiative launched by the Free Software Foundation (FSF) in May 2006. Its goal is to raise awareness of the restrictions that DRM technologies place on users of computers, media players and other electronic devices, and to discourage manufacturers from designing products that deliberately limit what users can do with content they have lawfully obtained.

History

The campaign was launched in 2006 with a protest at the WinHEC conference in Seattle, where FSF staff and supporters wearing yellow hazmat suits handed out leaflets describing DRM as "defective by design". The phrase itself plays on the concept of being "digital by design", arguing that restrictions are not accidental flaws but deliberate design decisions that work against the interests of users.

Activities

Defective by Design has organised protests and boycotts directed at companies and products that employ restrictive DRM, including music download stores, e-book readers and video streaming hardware. The campaign declared 3 October 2006 and later dates as "Day Against DRM", an annual international day of action. It has also opposed measures such as the trusted computing initiative and software patents affecting media playback.
