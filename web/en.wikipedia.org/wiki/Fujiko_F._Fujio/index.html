Fujiko F. Fujio (Japanese: 藤子・F・不二雄), born Hiroshi Fujimoto (藤本 弘, 1 December 1933 – 23 September 1996), was a Japanese manga artist. He and his childhood friend Motoo Abiko formed the duo Fujiko Fujio, under whose name they worked together from 1951 until their amicable split in 1987. Fujimoto thereafter worked as Fujiko F. Fujio, while Abiko became Fujiko Fujio A.

Career

Fujimoto and Abiko met as schoolboys in Toyama Prefecture and began drawing manga together, debuting professionally in 1951. They moved to Tokyo in 1954 and lived at the famous Tokiwa-sō apartment building alongside Osamu Tezuka and other young artists. Working as Fujiko Fujio, the pair created series such as Obake no Q-tarō. Fujimoto's most famous creation, Doraemon — about a robot cat from the 22nd century who travels back in time to help the hapless schoolboy Nobita — began in 1969 and became one of the best-selling manga series of all time, spawning a long-running anime franchise and dozens of films.

Fujimoto favoured gentle science-fiction and children's stories, in contrast to Abiko's darker works. His other solo series include Kiteretsu Daihyakka, Esper Mami, Chimpui, and the science-fiction short stories collected as Fujiko F. Fujio SF Tanpenshū, which are admired for their darker, adult themes. He died of liver failure in 1996 while still drawing Doraemon. The Fujiko F. Fujio Museum opened in Kawasaki in 2011.
