Izaak Walton

Izaak Walton (baptised 21 September 1593 – 15 December 1683) was an English writer, best remembered as the author of The Compleat Angler (1653), one of the most reprinted books in English literature, and of a series of short biographies known as Walton's Lives.

Life

Walton was born in Stafford and spent most of his working life in London as an ironmonger and linen draper in Fleet Street, where he befriended the poet and clergyman John Donne. A devout Anglican and royalist sympathiser, he withdrew from London during the Civil War, later dividing his time between Staffordshire and Winchester, where his son-in-law was a prebendary of the cathedral; he is buried in Winchester Cathedral.

Works

The Compleat Angler, or the Contemplative Man's Recreation is a dialogue celebrating fishing and the contemplative life, interwoven with pastoral song, natural lore, and recipes; Walton revised and expanded it through five editions in his lifetime, the last (1676) incorporating Charles Cotton's section on fly fishing. His Lives of Donne (1640), Sir Henry Wotton, Richard Hooker, George Herbert, and Bishop Robert Sanderson pioneered modern English biography, written from personal acquaintance and research into documents. Walton's gentle, devotional tone made him a favourite of later essayists such as Charles Lamb.
