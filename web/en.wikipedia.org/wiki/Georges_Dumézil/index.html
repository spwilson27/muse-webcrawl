Georges Dumézil

Georges Dumézil (4 March 1898 – 11 October 1986) was a French philologist, linguist and religious studies scholar best known for his trifunctional hypothesis of Proto-Indo-European society.

Georges Dumézil
Born: 4 March 1898, Paris, France
Died: 11 October 1986, Paris
Occupation: Philologist, linguist
Known for: Trifunctional hypothesis

Life and career

Dumézil studied at the École normale supérieure, served in the First World War, and taught in Istanbul and Uppsala before holding a chair at the Collège de France from 1949 to 1968. He was elected to the Académie française in 1978.

Trifunctional hypothesis

Dumézil argued that Indo-European societies and myths were structured around three functions: sovereignty (sacred and juridical), military force, and fertility and production. He developed the idea through comparative study of Vedic, Roman, Norse and Ossetian traditions, linking figures such as mythic sovereigns across cultures.

Works

Major works include Mythe et épopée (1968–1973), Archaic Roman Religion, and studies of the Ossetian Nart sagas. His influence extends across anthropology, history of religions and Indo-European studies, though critics have debated political readings of his work and the rigidity of the tripartite scheme.
