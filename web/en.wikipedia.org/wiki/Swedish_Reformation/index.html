Swedish Reformation

The Swedish Reformation was the process by which Sweden adopted Lutheran Christianity in the 16th century, beginning under King Gustav I (Gustav Vasa) after his election in 1523 and formally consolidated over the following decades. It severed Sweden's ties with the papacy and transferred church wealth to the crown.

Background

Gustav Vasa, needing funds to pay war debts and consolidate the new Swedish state, moved against the wealthy Catholic Church. At the Diet of Västerås in 1527 the estates authorised the crown to confiscate church property and allowed Lutheran preaching.

Reformers

The brothers Olaus and Laurentius Petri, educated at Wittenberg, led the theological reform. Laurentius became Archbishop of Uppsala in 1531, and a Swedish translation of the New Testament appeared in 1526, with the full Gustav Vasa Bible in 1541.

Consolidation

Despite Catholic revival attempts under John III and Sigismund, the Uppsala Synod of 1593 adopted the Augsburg Confession, confirming Sweden as a Lutheran state under the Church of Sweden.
