Montgolfier brothers

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: An ornate 18th-century Montgolfier balloon ascending with a decorative envelope, period-style illustration]
A Montgolfier balloon, 1783 style.

The Montgolfier brothers — Joseph-Michel (1740–1810) and Jacques-Étienne (1745–1799) — were paper manufacturers from Annonay, France, who invented the hot-air balloon (the montgolfière). In 1783 they staged the first public balloon demonstration, the first flight carrying living creatures (a sheep, a duck and a rooster), and the first human free flight, by Pilâtre de Rozier and the Marquis d\'Arlandes over Paris on 21 November 1783 — the birth of human flight.

Invention

Joseph noticed laundry billowing over a fire and, with Étienne, experimented with paper and fabric bags filled with hot air (they believed \'levity\' came from a special gas in smoke). Their public demonstration at Annonay on 4 June 1783 sent an unmanned balloon about 2 km.

Versailles and Paris flights

Before Louis XVI at Versailles in September 1783, a balloon carried the famous animal trio safely — proving living beings could survive ascent. Rival Jacques Charles flew a hydrogen balloon in August 1783 (the first \'Charlière\'), setting up a friendly rivalry of balloon types that persists in French terminology.

Legacy

Étienne became the first human to leave the ground (tethered ascent, October 1783) before the free flight a month later. The brothers\' balloons sparked \'balloonmania\' across Europe; the montgolfière design — paper-lined linen, ornate decoration — remains the visual archetype of the balloon age, celebrated annually at Annonay.
