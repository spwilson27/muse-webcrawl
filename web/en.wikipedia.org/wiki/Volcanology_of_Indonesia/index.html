Volcanology of Indonesia


Mount Merapi erupting

Indonesia has more volcanoes than any other country in the world, with around 130 active volcanoes along the Sunda Arc and the arcs of the Banda Sea and Halmahera. This volcanism results from the subduction of the Indo-Australian Plate beneath the Sunda Plate and the complex plate interactions of the region.

Major eruptions

Indonesia has hosted some of the largest eruptions in recorded history: the 1815 eruption of Mount Tambora, the largest of the nineteenth century, caused the "Year Without a Summer"; the 1883 eruption of Krakatoa generated tsunamis that killed more than 36,000 people; and the 74,000-year-old Toba eruption was among the largest known anywhere on Earth.

Active volcanoes

Frequently active centres include Merapi, Semeru, Sinabung, Dukono and Ibu. Dense populations living on fertile volcanic soils near these volcanoes make Indonesia's eruption risk among the highest in the world.

Monitoring

The Center for Volcanology and Geological Hazard Mitigation (PVMBG) operates observatories and an alert-level system for Indonesia's volcanoes, issuing warnings and evacuation recommendations.
