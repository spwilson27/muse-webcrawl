Uyghur Khaganate


The khaganate at its greatest extent, c. 800

The Uyghur Khaganate (744–840) was a Turkic empire of Central Asia and Mongolia, succeeding the Second Turkic Khaganate and ruling a vast steppe territory from the Altai to Manchuria. Its capital was Ordu-Baliq (Khar Balgas) in the Orkhon Valley. The confederation was led by the Yaghlakar clan of the Nine Oghuz (Toquz Oghuz) Uyghurs.

History

Founded by Qutlugh Bilge Köl Khagan in 744 in alliance with the Karluks and Basmyls, the khaganate became Tang China's principal steppe partner: Uyghur cavalry helped the Tang recapture Chang'an and Luoyang from the An Lushan rebels (757), in return for a lucrative silk-for-horses trade and imperial marriages. Under Bögü Khagan the court adopted Manichaeism (762–763) — the only case of a steppe empire taking it as a state religion — bringing Sogdian merchants and scribes to the capital. In 840, after years of famine, internal strife and a Kyrgyz invasion that sacked Ordu-Baliq, the khaganate collapsed.

Legacy

Fleeing Uyghur groups founded successor states: the Qocho (Gaochang) kingdom in the Turfan depression, which became a flourishing Buddhist, urban civilisation, and the Ganzhou Uyghur kingdom in Gansu. The Uyghurs' adoption of a Sogdian-derived script — later adapted for Mongolian — and their role as administrators in the Mongol Empire made them central to Inner Asian culture.
