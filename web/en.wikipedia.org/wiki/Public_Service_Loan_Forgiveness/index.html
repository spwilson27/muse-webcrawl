Public Service Loan Forgiveness

Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF) is a United States federal program, created by the College Cost Reduction and Access Act of 2007, that forgives the remaining balance on Direct Loans after a borrower has made 120 qualifying monthly payments — equivalent to ten years — while working full-time for a qualifying employer, such as a federal, state, or local government agency or a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization. Amounts forgiven under PSLF are not treated as taxable income by the federal government.

Eligibility

To qualify, borrowers must have Direct Loans (other federal loans can be made eligible through consolidation), be enrolled in an income-driven repayment plan or the standard ten-year plan, and work full-time for a qualifying employer while making the 120 payments. Borrowers submit Employment Certification Forms, and since 2022 use the PSLF Help Tool, to track qualifying employment and payments.

Implementation and reforms

The first borrowers became eligible for forgiveness in October 2017, but initial approval rates were extremely low — around 1 percent — owing to program complexity, servicer errors, and confusion about qualifying loans, plans, and payments. Congress created the Temporary Expanded PSLF (TEPSLF) in 2018 to help borrowers denied because of their repayment plan. In October 2021 the Department of Education announced a limited-time waiver that credited many previously ineligible payments; more than a million borrowers ultimately received forgiveness under the waiver and subsequent account adjustments, and processing of PSLF was transferred to the federal StudentAid platform from contracted servicers in 2024.
