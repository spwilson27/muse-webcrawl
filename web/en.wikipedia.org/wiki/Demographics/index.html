Demographics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia. For the academic discipline, see Demography.

Demographics are statistical data about a population and the characteristics of the groups within it. The term is commonly used for the attributes of a specific population — such as the population of a country, city, market segment, or electorate — including its size, age structure, sex distribution, income, education, occupation, ethnicity, religion, and household composition. The academic study of populations is called demography.

Content of demographic data

Governments collect demographic data through censuses, population registers, and sample surveys. Common demographic variables include:

  • Age and sex: the basic structure of a population, often displayed in a population pyramid.
  • Vital statistics: births, deaths, fertility rates, mortality rates, and life expectancy.
  • Migration: immigration, emigration, and internal migration flows.
  • Social characteristics: marital status, household size, education, and language.
  • Economic characteristics: employment, occupation, and income.

Uses

Demographics inform public policy — planning schools, hospitals, pensions, and infrastructure — and electoral analysis. In marketing, demographic segmentation divides consumers by age, income, family life cycle, and other characteristics to target products and advertising. Businesses, media companies, and researchers rely on demographic profiles to understand audiences and anticipate trends such as population ageing and urbanisation.

Terminology

While "demographics" strictly denotes the data themselves, the word is widely used loosely in business and media to mean the demographic characteristics of a target group ("the show appeals to key demographics"). Demographers sometimes prefer "demographic characteristics" for precision, reserving "demography" for the discipline.
