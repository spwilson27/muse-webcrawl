Pahlavuni

The Pahlavuni (Armenian: Պահլավունի) were an Armenian noble family, a branch of the Kamsarakan house, prominent in the Bagratid kingdom of Armenia from the 10th to the 12th centuries. They claimed descent — as their name suggests — from the Parthian (Pahlav) Arsacid tradition, and the Kamsarakans in turn traced their lineage to the Arsacid kings of Armenia.

History

The Pahlavuni rose to prominence when Gregory Pahlavuni became Catholicos of the Armenian Church (as Gregory II the Martyr-lover, r. 1066–1105) — the office remained largely in the family for generations. Vahram Pahlavuni led Armenian resistance to Byzantine expansion and was killed at the Battle of Manzikert's prelude conflicts in 1045 defending Ani. His son Gregory Magistros (c. 990–1058) was a leading scholar, translator, and founder of the Kecharis Monastery, which became a great centre of learning.

Descendants

Branches of the family later ruled in Cilicia; the Hethumids' rivals and allies included Pahlavuni descendants, and the family name is tied to the monasteries of Kecharis and to the ecclesiastical politics of medieval Armenia.
