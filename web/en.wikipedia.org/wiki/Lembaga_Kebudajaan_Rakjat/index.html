Lembaga Kebudajaan Rakjat (in modernised spelling, Lembaga Kebudayaan Rakyat, "Institute of People's Culture"), universally known by its acronym Lekra, was the cultural organisation of the Indonesian left, closely aligned with the Communist Party of Indonesia (PKI). Founded on 17 August 1950 — the fifth anniversary of the Indonesian proclamation of independence — it grew into the largest cultural movement in the country's history, organising writers, painters, dancers, musicians and filmmakers around a programme of socialist realism.

Programme

Lekra held that art should serve the people and the revolution, summed up in its guiding principles of "politics in command" and art for the masses. Members were encouraged to undertake turun ke bawah ("going down") — extended stays in villages and factories — and to create works about the lives of workers and peasants. The organisation ran publishing ventures, exhibitions and performances across the archipelago.

Suppression

Following the events of 1965, Lekra was banned by the incoming Suharto regime. Its members suffered heavily in the anti-communist purges: many were killed, and others spent years in prison camps such as Buru Island or in exile. Its literature and artwork were systematically removed from libraries and public memory.
