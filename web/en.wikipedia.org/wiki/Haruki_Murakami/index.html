Haruki Murakami

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Haruki Murakami running a marathon in sunglasses, a cat waiting at home among shelves of jazz records]

Haruki Murakami (born 12 January 1949) is a Japanese writer. His novels, essays and short stories have been best-sellers in Japan and internationally, with his work translated into 50 languages and having sold millions of copies outside Japan. He has received numerous awards for his work, including the World Fantasy Award, the Franz Kafka Prize and the Jerusalem Prize.

Running a jazz bar in Tokyo, Murakami began writing at 29 after an epiphany at a baseball game. His breakthrough, Norwegian Wood (1987), made him a reluctant celebrity in Japan. Works such as The Wind-Up Bird Chronicle (1994), Kafka on the Shore (2002) and 1Q84 (2009) blend the mundane with dreamlike parallel worlds, wells, cats and disappearing women; he is also a marathon runner and wrote the memoir What I Talk About When I Talk About Running.
