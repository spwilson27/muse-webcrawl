Honoré de Balzac

Honoré de Balzac
Born: 20 May 1799, Tours, France
Died: 18 August 1850 (aged 51), Paris, France
Occupation: Novelist, playwright
Notable works: La Comédie humaine, Le Père Goriot, Eugénie Grandet, Illusions perdues
Spouse: Ewelina Hańska (m. 1850)

Honoré de Balzac (born Honoré Balzac; 20 May 1799 – 18 August 1850) was a French novelist and playwright. The novel sequence La Comédie humaine, which presents a panorama of post-Napoleonic French life, is generally viewed as his magnum opus.

Owing to his keen observation of detail and unfiltered representation of society, Balzac is regarded as one of the founders of realism in European literature. He is renowned for his multi-faceted characters — even minor figures are complex, morally ambiguous, and fully human.

Life

Born in Tours, Balzac was sent to school in Vendôme and was briefly a law clerk in Paris before defying his family's wishes to write. His early potboiler novels under pseudonyms left him in debt, a burden that drove his prodigious output — he often wrote 12–16 hours a day, fuelled by coffee. After decades of correspondence, he married Polish noblewoman Ewelina Hańska in March 1850, months before his death in Paris.

La Comédie humaine

Balzac organised his works — some 91 finished novels and stories — into scenes of private, provincial, Parisian, political, military, and country life, with recurring characters across volumes. Major works include Le Père Goriot (1835), Eugénie Grandet (1833), Illusions perdues, and Le Cousin Pons.

Influence

Balzac influenced writers including Gustave Flaubert, Émile Zola, Marcel Proust, Fyodor Dostoevsky, and Henry James, and was admired by Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels for his anatomising of class and money.
