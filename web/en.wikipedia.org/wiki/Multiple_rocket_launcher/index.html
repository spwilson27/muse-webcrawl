Multiple rocket launcher

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A multiple rocket launcher firing a salvo; saturation is its principal advantage over tube artillery.

A multiple rocket launcher (MRL), also called a multiple launch rocket system (MLRS), is a type of rocket artillery system consisting of multiple launch tubes mounted on a single platform, capable of firing many rockets in rapid succession. Unlike conventional tube artillery, an MRL delivers a large volume of fire onto an area target in seconds, producing a saturating effect, at the cost of lower accuracy per round and a lengthy reload.

History

Early multiple rocket launchers include the Korean hwacha (15th century), Congreve rockets, and the Hale spin-stabilized rockets of the 19th century. The modern type emerged in the Second World War with the Soviet Katyusha (BM-13) — truck-mounted rails firing 132 mm rockets, feared for their screaming sound and massed salvos — the German Nebelwerfer, and the American T34 Calliope tank-mounted launcher. During the Cold War the USSR fielded the widely exported BM-21 Grad (122 mm, 40 tubes), and the United States answered with the tracked M270 MLRS (1983), which also fires the ATACMS tactical missile. Rocket artillery proved prominent again in 21st-century conflicts, notably the truck-mounted HIMARS firing GPS-guided GMLRS rockets.

Characteristics

MRLs are usually mounted on trucks or tracked vehicles, fire unguided or (increasingly) guided spin- or fin-stabilized rockets in calibres from about 70 mm to over 300 mm, and carry warheads ranging from high explosive and submunitions to thermobaric and mine-scattering types. Modern systems emphasize dispersion, rapid shoot-and-scoot tactics to avoid counter-battery fire, and precision-guided rockets that blur the line between artillery and tactical missiles. Reloading traditionally took many minutes with loose rockets; sealed, swappable launch pods have greatly reduced reload times.
