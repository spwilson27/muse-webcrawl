Battle of Lugdunum

Date19 February 197 AD
LocationLugdunum, Gallia Lugdunensis (modern Lyon, France)
ResultVictory for Septimius Severus
BelligerentsForces of Septimius Severus vs. forces of Clodius Albinus
StrengthPossibly ~75,000 on each side — among the largest battles ever fought between Roman armies

The Battle of Lugdunum, also called the Battle of Lyon, was fought on 19 February 197 AD between the armies of the Roman emperors Septimius Severus and Clodius Albinus. It was the climax of the civil wars of the Year of the Five Emperors and one of the largest battles in Roman history, with perhaps 150,000 soldiers engaged in total.

Background

After the murder of Pertinax in 193, Severus seized Rome while Albinus, governor of Britain, controlled Britain, Gaul and Spain. Severus initially bought peace by naming Albinus as Caesar, but in 196 Albinus proclaimed himself Augustus, crossed into Gaul and made Lugdunum his capital. Severus marched west through the Alpine passes to meet him.

The battle

The ancient sources (Cassius Dio and Herodian) describe a hard-fought, evenly matched battle lasting two days. Albinus's troops initially drove back Severus's left wing and pursued it toward his camp, but the pursuit disordered them; a concealed Severan force and a cavalry charge led, in Dio's account, with the help of the general Laetus, struck the pursuers in flank and converted the action into a rout. Severus himself was reportedly unhorsed and threw off his imperial cloak to avoid recognition.

Aftermath

Albinus fled into Lugdunum and killed himself as the city was taken; his head was sent to Rome. Severus proscribed his rival's supporters, executed many senators, confiscated estates, and sacked Lugdunum, which never fully regained its former status. The victory left Severus sole master of the Roman world and founder of the Severan dynasty.
