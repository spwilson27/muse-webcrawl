The Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA; English: International Automobile Federation) is an international organisation that brings together national motoring organisations and serves as the governing body for many forms of motor racing, most notably Formula One and the World Rally Championship. Founded in Paris in 1904 as the Association Internationale des Automobile Clubs Reconnus, it adopted its present name in 1946.

History

The FIA was established on 20 June 1904 to represent the interests of motorists and to bring order to the young sport of motor racing. After the Second World War it became the sanctioning authority for the Formula One World Championship, inaugurated in 1950, and progressively assumed control of world championships in rallying, endurance racing, touring cars and karting.

Roles

Beyond sport, the FIA campaigns for road safety, sustainable mobility and consumer protection, working through member clubs in more than 140 countries. Its sporting arm drafts the technical and sporting regulations for its championships, licenses drivers and circuits, and oversees safety standards in competition vehicles and venues.
