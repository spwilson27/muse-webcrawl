Eastern Thrace

Eastern Thrace (Turkish: Doğu Trakya) is the part of the region of Thrace that lies within modern Turkey — the European portion of the country, west of the Bosphorus, the Sea of Marmara, and the Dardanelles. It comprises the Turkish provinces of Edirne, Kırklareli, and Tekirdağ, plus the European parts of Istanbul and Çanakkale, and accounts for about 3% of Turkey's land area but more than 10% of its population because of Istanbul.

History

The region was inhabited by Thracian peoples in antiquity and later ruled by Persia, Macedon, Rome, and Byzantium. Adrianople (Edirne) became the Ottoman capital before the conquest of Constantinople in 1453. Eastern Thrace was contested in the Balkan Wars and the First World War, including the Gallipoli campaign on its western edge, and its modern borders were fixed by the Treaty of Lausanne (1923). The 1923 population exchange between Greece and Turkey reshaped its demographics, and most of its Greek population left in the 20th century.
