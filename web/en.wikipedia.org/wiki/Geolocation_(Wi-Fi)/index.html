Geolocation (Wi-Fi)

Wi-Fi positioning, also called Wi-Fi geolocation, is a method of determining the geographic location of a device using the characteristics of nearby wireless access points. It is widely used as a complement or alternative to satellite positioning systems such as GPS, especially indoors and in dense urban areas where satellite signals may be weak or blocked.

How it works

A Wi-Fi enabled device scans for nearby access points and records their unique identifiers (BSSID/MAC addresses) and signal strengths. This information is compared against a reference database that maps access points to known geographic locations. By triangulating or fingerprinting the set of visible access points and their relative signal strengths, the device or a cloud service estimates its position, often to within tens of metres.

Databases

Reference databases are built by wardriving surveys, by crowdsourcing observations from smartphones, and by recording the location of access points reported by connected devices. Major providers include Google, Apple, Microsoft and Mozilla, each maintaining their own location services.

Privacy

Wi-Fi positioning has raised privacy concerns, both for device users whose movements can be tracked and for access point owners whose hardware identifiers are collected into databases. Many platforms now randomize MAC addresses during scanning and offer opt-out mechanisms for access point owners.
