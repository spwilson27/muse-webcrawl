USS Gerald R. Ford

USS Gerald R. Ford (CVN-78) is the lead ship of the Gerald R. Ford-class of United States Navy aircraft carriers and the largest warship ever built. Named for the 38th President of the United States, Gerald Ford, who served aboard the light carrier USS Monterey in World War II, she was constructed by Newport News Shipbuilding in Virginia, christened on 9 November 2013, and commissioned on 22 July 2017.

Design

The Ford class introduces major advances over the preceding Nimitz class: the Electromagnetic Aircraft Launch System (EMALS) replacing steam catapults, Advanced Arresting Gear, dual-band radar, a redesigned island set further aft, and two new A1B nuclear reactors producing substantially more electrical power. Automation allows the ship to operate with several hundred fewer crew than a Nimitz-class carrier. Displacing about 100,000 long tons at full load and 1,106 feet long, she can carry more than 75 aircraft.

Development difficulties

The ship's cost grew to roughly $13 billion, making her the most expensive warship in history, and new technologies — particularly the eleven advanced weapons elevators — delayed completion of post-delivery work; the last elevator was certified in late 2021. Her first full deployment began in 2023, including a deployment to the Mediterranean Sea following the October 2023 Hamas attack on Israel.

Service

Homeported at Naval Station Norfolk, Gerald R. Ford completed shock trials in 2021, enduring a series of underwater explosions to validate the design. She embarked her first carrier strike group deployment in October 2022 and was extended in the eastern Mediterranean in 2023 as a deterrent presence during the Israel–Hamas war.
