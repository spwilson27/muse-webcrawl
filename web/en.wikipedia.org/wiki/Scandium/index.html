Scandium

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Scandium
Symbol: Sc; atomic number 21
Discovered: 1879, by Lars Fredrik Nilson
Uses: Aerospace aluminium alloys; stadium lighting

Scandium is a chemical element with the symbol Sc and atomic number 21, a silvery-white transition metal — the first of the d-block — named for Scandinavia. It was discovered in 1879 by Lars Fredrik Nilson in Uppsala, in the minerals euxenite and gadolinite, and recognised as Mendeleev's predicted "eka-boron", the second of his prophecies fulfilled (see gallium and germanium).

Scandium is rare and dispersed — it is extracted as a by-product of uranium, tungsten and aluminium refining, mostly in China, Russia, Kazakhstan and the Philippines — and expensive. Its aluminium alloys are exceptionally strong and weldable, used in fighter aircraft, missile parts, bicycle frames and baseball bats; scandium iodide lamps produce a near-daylight light used in stadiums and film sets. A niche element, but one whose predicted existence once proved a system of chemistry.
