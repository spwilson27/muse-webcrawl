Appalachian Spring

ComposerAaron Copland
ChoreographerMartha Graham
Premiere30 October 1944, Library of Congress, Washington, D.C.
GenreBallet

Appalachian Spring is an American ballet created by choreographer Martha Graham with music by Aaron Copland. Commissioned by Elizabeth Sprague Coolidge, it was premiered on 30 October 1944 at the Library of Congress in Washington, D.C., with sets by Isamu Noguchi. Copland was awarded the 1945 Pulitzer Prize for Music for the score.

Story and music

The ballet depicts a 19th-century pioneer celebration in Pennsylvania: a young married couple building a new home, a revivalist preacher and his flock, and the community around them. The score famously incorporates the Shaker tune "Simple Gifts," which Copland sets in a celebrated series of variations. The title came from a line in Hart Crane's poem "The Dance" and was chosen after the music was composed; it has no direct connection to the story beyond evoking springtime renewal.

Versions

The original ballet was scored for thirteen instruments. Copland subsequently arranged the music into an orchestral suite (1945) and later versions for full orchestra, which are widely performed in the concert hall. The ballet remains a cornerstone of American modern dance and of Copland's "populist" style.
