National People's Army

Nationale Volksarmee (NVA)
Founded: 1 March 1956
Disbanded: 3 October 1990
Country: East Germany (GDR)
Branches: Land Forces, Volksmarine, Air Forces

The National People's Army (German: Nationale Volksarmee, NVA) was the armed forces of the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) from 1956 until German reunification in 1990. It comprised land forces (Landstreitkräfte), a navy (Volksmarine), and an air force (Luftstreitkräfte), with the Border Troops (Grenztruppen) under separate administration for much of its history.

History

The NVA was founded on 1 March 1956 from the paramilitary Kasernierte Volkspolizei (Barracked People's Police). Conscription was introduced in 1962. The NVA was a member of the Warsaw Pact and was considered one of its most capable and loyal armies, equipped largely with Soviet weaponry. Its only deployments abroad were largely advisory and training missions, for example in Africa; contrary to earlier speculation, the NVA did not take part in the 1968 invasion of Czechoslovakia in a combat role, though it supported the operation logistically.

Dissolution

Following the Peaceful Revolution of 1989 and reunification, the NVA was dissolved on 3 October 1990. Some personnel and equipment were temporarily taken over by the Bundeswehr under Bundeswehrkommando Ost, but most conscripts and many officers were discharged, and most NVA equipment was scrapped, sold, or given away.

Organization and ethos

The NVA was tightly integrated with the ruling Socialist Unity Party (SED), with political officers throughout the chain of command. Its motto was Für den Schutz der Arbeiter- und Bauernmacht ("For the protection of the workers' and farmers' power").
