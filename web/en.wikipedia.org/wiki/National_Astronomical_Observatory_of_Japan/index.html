National Astronomical Observatory of Japan

NAOJ
Established: 1988
Headquarters: Mitaka, Tokyo, Japan
Type: Inter-university research institute

The National Astronomical Observatory of Japan (NAOJ; 国立天文台, Kokuritsu Tenmondai) is an astronomical research institution in Japan, operating as one of the Inter-University Research Institute Corporations under the National Institutes of Natural Sciences. It was formed in 1988 by merging the Tokyo Astronomical Observatory of the University of Tokyo with other Japanese astronomical facilities.

Facilities

NAOJ operates the 8.2-metre Subaru Telescope at Mauna Kea Observatory in Hawaii, one of the world's largest optical-infrared telescopes. In Japan it runs the Nobeyama Radio Observatory, including the 45-metre radio telescope, and the Okayama Astrophysical Observatory. Its headquarters campus is in Mitaka, Tokyo, and it has centers in Mizusawa (Iwate) and elsewhere.

Projects

NAOJ is Japan's partner in the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) in Chile. It led the Hinode solar satellite collaboration (with JAXA, NASA, and UK partners), the Kaguya lunar mission's laser altimeter work, and hosts the Astronomy Data Center and the Advanced Technology Center for instrument development. NAOJ is also Japan's representative in the Thirty Meter Telescope project.
