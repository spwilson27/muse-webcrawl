ClassificationProtestant

The Moravian Church, formally the Unitas Fratrum (Latin for "Unity of the Brethren"), is one of the oldest Protestant denominations in the world, tracing its origins to the Bohemian Reformation of Jan Hus in the 15th century. It is known for its emphasis on community life, simplicity, music, education, and an exceptionally early and extensive missionary movement that began in 1732.

History

Bohemian origins

The Unity of the Brethren was founded in 1457 in Kunvald, Bohemia, by followers of Jan Hus, a generation after Hus's execution in 1415. It organised with its own ministry and bishops decades before Luther, making it a pre-Reformation Protestant church. Persecution after the Battle of White Mountain (1620) nearly destroyed it in its homeland; the educator John Amos Comenius, its last great bishop in exile, prayed for a "hidden seed" to revive it.

Renewal at Herrnhut

In 1722 Moravian refugees settled on the Saxon estate of Count Nikolaus Ludwig von Zinzendorf, founding the village of Herrnhut ("the Lord's watch"). A spiritual revival in 1727 reorganised the community, and in 1732 the renewed church sent its first missionaries to the enslaved Africans of St. Thomas in the Danish West Indies — the start of a worldwide mission movement remarkable for its era, reaching Greenland, North America, South Africa, and the Caribbean.

Settlements and influence

Moravian congregational settlements such as Bethlehem and Winston-Salem in America, Bethlehem (1737) in Pennsylvania, and Gracehill in Northern Ireland became known for communal economies, schools, and music. The Moravians influenced John Wesley, who encountered their calm faith during an Atlantic storm and drew on their model for Methodism. Their motto is: "In essentials, unity; in non-essentials, liberty; in all things, love." Moravian settlement churches in Christiansfeld (Denmark), Herrnhut (Germany), Gracehill, and Bethlehem (USA) are inscribed together as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Beliefs and practice

The church emphasises the essentials of faith in Christ, a simple and heartfelt piety, the priesthood of all believers, daily watchwords (Losungen, published annually since 1731), lovefeasts, and strong traditions of congregational singing and brass music.
