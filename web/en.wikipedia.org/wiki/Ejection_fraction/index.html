Ejection fraction (EF) is the volumetric fraction of blood ejected from a cardiac chamber with each contraction. Most commonly it refers to the left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF): the stroke volume divided by end-diastolic volume. It is a key measure of systolic heart function.

Measurement

EF is most often measured by transthoracic echocardiography (e.g., Simpson's biplane method). Cardiac MRI is considered the reference standard; nuclear MUGA scans and ventriculography are also used.

Interpretation

A normal LVEF is roughly 50–70%. Values of 41–49% are mildly reduced; 40% or below indicates reduced EF, a hallmark of heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF). Heart failure with preserved EF (HFpEF) occurs despite a normal EF, reflecting diastolic dysfunction.

Clinical significance

EF guides therapy: many drugs and devices (such as implantable defibrillators, typically at EF ≤35%) are indicated based on EF thresholds. EF can improve with treatment or worsen with myocardial infarction or cardiomyopathy.
