Dimmer

A dimmer is a device connected to a light fixture that changes the voltage waveform applied to the lamp, thereby reducing its brightness. Dimmers are used in homes, theatres, and architectural lighting to control light levels, save energy, and set the mood of a space.

Early dimmers used variable rheostats or autotransformers to drop the voltage, wasting the unused power as heat. Modern solid-state dimmers instead switch the current on and off rapidly within each half-cycle of the AC mains: in phase-cut (forward-phase) dimming, a TRIAC delays conduction until partway through each half-cycle, so the lamp receives only a portion of the waveform. Reverse-phase dimmers, which switch off partway through the cycle, suit electronic low-voltage and many LED loads better.

Compatibility is a recurring issue: compact fluorescent and LED lamps may flicker, buzz, or fail to dim smoothly on dimmers designed for incandescent bulbs, and dimmers specify minimum loads for reliable operation. In theatrical lighting, dimmers are rack-mounted, remotely controlled (historically by analogue 0–10 V signals, today commonly by DMX512), and grouped into dimmer racks serving dozens of circuits. Household dimmers typically appear as rotary knobs or sliders replacing an ordinary wall switch.
