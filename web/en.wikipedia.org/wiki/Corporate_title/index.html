Corporate title

Corporate titles or business titles are given to corporate officers to show what duties and responsibilities they have in the organization. Such titles are used by publicly and privately held for-profit corporations, cooperatives, non-profit organizations, educational institutions, and other entities. The titles typically follow a hierarchy headed by the chief executive officer (CEO) and other "chief" (C-level) officers, followed by presidents, vice presidents, directors, and managers.

Hierarchy

C-level executives

The highest-ranking executives are often called C-level or C-suite officers: chief executive officer (CEO), chief operating officer (COO), chief financial officer (CFO), chief technology officer (CTO), chief information officer (CIO), chief marketing officer (CMO), chief human resources officer (CHRO), and chief legal officer (CLO, often styled general counsel). The CEO typically reports to the board of directors and may also hold the title of president.

Vice presidents

Below C-level executives are vice presidents, often graded executive vice president (EVP), senior vice president (SVP), vice president (VP), assistant vice president (AVP), and associate vice president. In the banking and financial sectors, "vice president" is common even for mid-level staff, and "managing director" is widely used in British and investment-banking contexts.

Directors and managers

Directors head departments or functions, and managers supervise teams. Usage varies by country: in the United Kingdom, "director" commonly denotes a board member or senior manager, while in the United States board members are directors and the title is also used below vice-president level in some industries, such as film and broadcasting.

Variation

Corporate titles vary considerably across industries, countries, and company sizes. Startups may bestow inflated titles; some firms use non-hierarchical or idiosyncratic titles. Titles also carry legal significance in some contexts: certain officers are authorized to bind the corporation, and doctrines such as apparent authority may attach to a title.
