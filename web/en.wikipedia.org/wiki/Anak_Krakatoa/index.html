Anak Krakatoa (Indonesian: Anak Krakatau, "Child of Krakatoa") is a volcanic island in the Sunda Strait between Java and Sumatra, Indonesia. It emerged from the caldera left by the catastrophic 1883 eruption of Krakatoa, which destroyed the original island. Submarine eruptions began in 1927, and the new cone broke the surface by 1930, growing steadily for most of the twentieth century and providing a famous natural laboratory for the study of island colonisation by plants and animals.

2018 eruption and tsunami

On 22 December 2018, following months of eruptive activity, a large part of the cone's southwestern flank collapsed into the sea. The landslide displaced a huge volume of water and generated a tsunami that struck the coasts of Banten and Lampung without warning, killing more than 400 people and injuring thousands. The collapse reduced the island's height from about 338 metres to roughly 110 metres and removed a large fraction of its volume. Eruptive activity resumed and the cone has since been rebuilding.
