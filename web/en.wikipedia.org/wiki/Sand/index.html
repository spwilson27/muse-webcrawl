Sand

Composition: Mostly silica (quartz); varies by locality

Grain size: 0.0625 – 2 mm

Classification: Between silt (finer) and gravel (coarser)

Sand is a granular material composed of finely divided rock and mineral particles, defined by size as finer than gravel and coarser than silt — grains between about 0.0625 mm and 2 mm in diameter. Sand forms by the weathering and erosion of rocks and is transported by wind and water, accumulating as beaches, dunes, riverbeds and desert sand seas.

Composition

The most common constituent of sand in inland and non-tropical coastal settings is silica in the form of quartz, valued for its hardness and chemical stability. In tropical coastal areas much sand is biogenic, made of fragments of coral, shells and foraminifera — hence the white carbonate sands of many islands. Other sands are rich in magnetite, gypsum, or volcanic minerals such as olivine.

Formation and transport

Sand grains are produced by mechanical and chemical weathering, then carried by rivers, glaciers, currents and wind. Wind-blown (aeolian) sand builds dunes and is generally well sorted and rounded; desert sand seas cover large parts of regions such as the Sahara and Arabia.

Uses

Sand is essential to construction as an ingredient of concrete, asphalt and mortar, and is used to make glass, in foundry moulding, water filtration, abrasives and land reclamation. Demand is enormous — sand and aggregates are among the most extracted solid materials on Earth — and scarcity of suitable construction sand has driven sand mining with significant environmental effects. Special rounded silica "frac sand" is used in hydraulic fracturing.
