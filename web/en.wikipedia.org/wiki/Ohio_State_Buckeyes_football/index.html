The Ohio State Buckeyes football team represents Ohio State University in college football. The Buckeyes are a member of the NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision and the Big Ten Conference, and play their home games at Ohio Stadium in Columbus, Ohio. The programme is among the most successful in college football history, with nine claimed national championships and a record number of Heisman Trophy winners.

History

Ohio State fielded its first team in 1890 and joined the Big Ten in 1912. Under Paul Brown the Buckeyes won their first national title in 1942. Woody Hayes (1951–1978) won five national championships and thirteen Big Ten titles; his tenure ended after the 1978 Gator Bowl. Later eras were led by Earle Bruce, John Cooper, Jim Tressel — who won the 2002 national championship — and Urban Meyer, whose team won the inaugural College Football Playoff in the 2014 season. Ryan Day became head coach in 2019 and led Ohio State to the 2024 season national championship.

Traditions and rivalry

The rivalry with the Michigan Wolverines, simply called "The Game", is regarded as one of the greatest in American sports. Traditions include the dotting of the "i" by a sousaphone player in the Ohio State University Marching Band's Script Ohio formation, the singing of "Carmen Ohio", and the awarding of gold pants charms for victories over Michigan. Running back Archie Griffin remains the only two-time Heisman Trophy winner (1974, 1975).
