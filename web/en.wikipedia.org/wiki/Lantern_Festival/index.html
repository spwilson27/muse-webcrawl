The Lantern Festival (traditional Chinese: 元宵節) is a traditional Chinese festival celebrated on the fifteenth day of the first month in the Chinese calendar, marking the final day of the Chinese New Year celebrations. Dating to the Western Han dynasty, it is one of China's oldest festivals and is also observed in Taiwan, Vietnam (as Tết Nguyên Tiêu), and Chinese communities worldwide.

Customs

Children and adults carry or display brightly lit lanterns, often in elaborate shapes — animals, flowers, historical scenes, and in modern times cartoon characters and LED constructions. Lantern riddles (dēngmí) are pasted on lanterns for visitors to guess, a custom dating to the Song dynasty. Families eat tangyuan (called yuanxiao in the north), glutinous rice balls with sweet or savoury fillings whose round shape symbolises family reunion and completeness. Lion dances, dragon dances, stilt-walking, and fireworks traditionally accompany the festivities.

Origins

Various legends explain the festival: worship of the deity Taiyi in Han-dynasty courts, Buddhist customs of lighting lanterns on the fifteenth day, and folk tales such as the story of a celestial crane killed by villagers, prompting the Jade Emperor to threaten fire — averted by filling the town with lanterns to simulate flames. Historically, the festival was also one of the few occasions when young women could go out at night, giving it a romantic character celebrated in classical poetry.
