Year of the Three Emperors

The Year of the Three Emperors (German: Dreikaiserjahr) is the year 1888 in the history of the German Empire, in which three emperors held the throne in quick succession: Wilhelm I, his son Frederick III, and his grandson Wilhelm II.

Events

Wilhelm I, the first German emperor, who had reigned since the proclamation of the Empire in 1871 (and as King of Prussia since 1861), died in Berlin on 9 March 1888 at the age of 90. He was succeeded by his 56-year-old son Frederick III, who was already terminally ill with cancer of the larynx. Frederick, married to Victoria, Princess Royal of Britain, was associated with liberal hopes for constitutional reform, but his illness — he could barely speak and communicated by writing pad — limited his reign to a few symbolic acts. He died at Potsdam on 15 June 1888 after a reign of only 99 days. His son, the 29-year-old Wilhelm II, ascended the throne the same day and reigned until his abdication in November 1918 at the end of the First World War.

Significance

The rapid succession has often been treated as a turning point in modern German history: historians debate whether a longer-lived Frederick III might have steered Germany toward a more liberal, parliamentary order, and whether Wilhelm II's assertive personal rule worsened the tensions that led to 1914. The phrase "Year of the Three Emperors" echoes the Roman "Year of the Four Emperors" (69 CE) and was coined in its own time. A popular mnemonic notes that three generations of Hohenzollern monarchs were commemorated in the many "Kaiser" memorials erected in Wilhelm II's reign.
