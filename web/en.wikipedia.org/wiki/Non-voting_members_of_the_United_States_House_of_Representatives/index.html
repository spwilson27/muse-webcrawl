Non-voting members of the United States House of Representatives


Labelled placeholder: House chamber

Non-voting members of the United States House of Representatives (called either delegates or resident commissioner, in the case of Puerto Rico) are representatives of their territory in the House of Representatives, who do not have a right to vote on the floor of the House but retain most other privileges of a member of the House. Non-voting members sit on and vote in congressional committees, and may introduce legislation. They also receive offices, staff, benefits, and allowances comparable to voting members.

There are currently six non-voting members: a resident commissioner representing Puerto Rico, and delegates representing the District of Columbia, Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa, and the Northern Mariana Islands. A seventh delegate, representing the Cherokee Nation, has been formally proposed since treaty rights were established in 1835; a delegate from the Choctaw Nation was proposed in 2023.

Privileges and limitations

Non-voting members may serve on committees and vote there; they may debate on the floor and introduce bills, but they cannot vote on final passage of legislation on the House floor. In the Committee of the Whole, they have at times been permitted to vote when their votes would not change the outcome — a provision first granted in 1993, rescinded in 1995, restored in 2007, rescinded again in 2011, restored in 2019, and removed again in 2023 under successive changes in House rules and majority control.

History

The practice of territorial delegates dates to the Northwest Ordinance of 1787 and the Continental Congress. Delegates from organized territories preceded statehood throughout the expansion of the United States. The resident commissioner of Puerto Rico, created in 1901, serves a four-year term unlike the two-year terms of delegates.

Current members

Delegates represent the District of Columbia, Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands; the resident commissioner represents Puerto Rico. All are elected by the voters of their jurisdictions.
