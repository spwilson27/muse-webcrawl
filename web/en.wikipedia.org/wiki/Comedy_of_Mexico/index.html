Comedy of Mexico

The comedy of Mexico encompasses comic theatre, film, television and stand-up produced in Mexico, from the satirical carpa tent shows of the early 20th century through the Golden Age of Mexican cinema to modern sitcoms and stand-up. Mexican comedy is characterized by wordplay, albur (double-entendre punning), social satire and beloved stock characters drawn from urban working-class life.

Early theatre and film

The teatro de carpa and teatro de revista traditions mixed sketches, music and political satire. From these stages emerged Mario Moreno "Cantinflas", whose rapid, convoluted speech — cantinflear — made him the most famous comic of the Spanish-speaking world; his films such as Ahí está el detalle (1940) anchored the Golden Age of Mexican cinema. Germán Valdés "Tin-Tan" brought pachuco culture to the screen, while the duo Viruta y Capulina and performers such as Resortes and Clavillazo filled theatres for decades.

Television era

Television made Roberto Gómez Bolaños, "Chespirito", the dominant figure of late 20th-century Mexican comedy; his programmes El Chavo del Ocho and El Chapulín Colorado became phenomenally popular across Latin America and Spain. Other long-running institutions include the work of Eugenio Derbez, the sketch show La carabina de Ambrosio, and political satirists on news-parody programmes.

Contemporary comedy

Since the 2010s, stand-up comedy has grown rapidly in Mexico City and beyond, with comedians building audiences through streaming specials and podcasts, alongside a successful commercial film comedy industry drawing large domestic box-office returns.
