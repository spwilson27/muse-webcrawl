Regions of Turkey

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The regions of Turkey (Turkish: Türkiye'nin coğrafi bölgeleri) are seven geographical regions defined at the First Geography Congress held in Ankara in 1941. Unlike the provinces of Turkey, the regions are not administrative divisions and have no governmental function; they were created for statistical, climatic and geographical purposes and are widely used in education and reference works.

Definition

The First Geography Congress of 1941 divided Turkey into regions and sub-regions on the basis of climate, vegetation, relief and human geography. The boundaries generally follow mountain ranges and climatic transitions rather than provincial borders, although provinces are conventionally assigned wholly to one region. The Eastern Anatolia Region is the largest by area, while the Marmara Region, though the second smallest, contains the country's largest city, Istanbul, and a large share of its industry and population.
