Comic book convention

Attendees, many in costume, at a major comic convention

A comic book convention or comic con is an event with a primary focus on comic books and comic book culture, in which fans gather to meet creators, experts, and each other. Common features include panel discussions, screenings, autograph sessions, exhibitions of artwork, dealer rooms selling comics and memorabilia, and cosplay. Larger conventions have expanded into general popular-culture events covering film, television, anime, gaming, and science fiction.

History

The first recorded comic book convention was held in New York City in 1964, organised by fans including Bernie Bubnis. The San Diego Comic-Con, founded in 1970, grew from a few hundred attendees to the largest convention of its kind in the United States, drawing over 130,000 people annually. New York Comic Con, founded in 2006, has rivalled or exceeded its attendance.

Activities

Conventions host panels by publishers and studios, often used to announce new titles and screen exclusive trailers; portfolio reviews for aspiring artists; award ceremonies such as the Eisner Awards (presented at San Diego) and Harvey Awards; and costume contests. Exhibitors range from major publishers such as Marvel and DC to independent artists in "artists' alley".

Notable conventions

  • San Diego Comic-Con (United States)
  • New York Comic Con (United States)
  • Comiket (Tokyo, Japan) — the world's largest fan convention, focused on dōjinshi
  • Angoulême International Comics Festival (France)
  • Lucca Comics & Games (Italy)
