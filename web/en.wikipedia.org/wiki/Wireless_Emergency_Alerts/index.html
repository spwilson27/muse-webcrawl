Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA) is a public safety system in the United States that allows authorized government agencies to send emergency messages to mobile devices in a geographically targeted area. WEA messages are delivered using cell broadcast technology, which sends the alert to all compatible phones connected to cell towers in the affected area rather than to individual phone numbers.

A WEA message displayed on a mobile phone

History

WEA was established under the Warning, Alert and Response Network (WARN) Act of 2006 and launched in April 2012, replacing the earlier Commercial Mobile Alert System (CMAS) naming. It is administered jointly by the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), which operates the Integrated Public Alert and Warning System (IPAWS) gateway, and the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), which sets technical rules for participating wireless carriers.

Alert types

WEA supports several alert classes. Presidential alerts, issued by the President of the United States, cannot be opted out of. Imminent threat alerts cover severe weather and other dangers to life and property; AMBER alerts concern abducted children; public safety alerts cover lesser emergencies; and test alerts allow agencies to verify the system. Users may disable all classes except presidential alerts. Since WEA 3.0 enhancements, messages may be up to 360 characters, include URLs and phone numbers, and be issued in Spanish.

Notable uses and incidents

The system drew wide attention in January 2018 when a false ballistic missile alert was transmitted to Hawaii due to operator error during a drill, causing public panic before a correction was issued 38 minutes later. The first national test of the presidential alert class took place on 3 October 2018.
