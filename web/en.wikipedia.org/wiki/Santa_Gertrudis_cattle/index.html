Santa Gertrudis cattle

Santa Gertrudis cattle are a breed of beef cattle developed in the United States, in southern Texas. The breed was created on the King Ranch beginning in the early 20th century by crossing Brahman cattle with Shorthorn cattle, producing an animal approximately three-eighths Brahman and five-eighths Shorthorn.

History

The breeding program was led by the Kleberg family of the King Ranch, whose land had once formed part of the Spanish Santa Gertrudis land grant, from which the breed takes its name. The foundation sire was a bull called Monkey, born in 1920. In 1940 the United States Department of Agriculture recognized Santa Gertrudis as the first beef breed developed in the United States.

Characteristics

Santa Gertrudis cattle are typically a deep cherry-red color, with a short, sleek coat. They combine the heat and insect resistance and hardiness of the Brahman with the beef qualities and milk production of the Shorthorn. Bulls generally weigh 1,500–2,000 pounds. The breed is now raised in many countries, including Australia, South Africa, and Brazil, and a breed association, Santa Gertrudis Breeders International, maintains the herd book.
