Breguet equation

The Breguet range equation relates an aircraft's range to its speed, lift-to-drag ratio and fuel fraction.

The Breguet equation (or Breguet range equation) describes the range of an aircraft as a function of its aerodynamic and propulsive efficiency and the fraction of its take-off weight devoted to fuel. It is named after the French aviation pioneer Louis Charles Breguet.

The equation

For a jet aircraft, range R is given by R = (V / cT) × (L/D) × ln(Winitial / Wfinal), where V is airspeed, cT is thrust-specific fuel consumption, L/D is the lift-to-drag ratio, and the logarithm term involves the aircraft's weight before and after fuel burn. For propeller aircraft the equivalent form uses power-specific fuel consumption and propeller efficiency.

Significance

The equation shows why range depends logarithmically on fuel weight: doubling fuel does not double range. It underlies the design of long-range airliners and military aircraft and explains the drive toward higher lift-to-drag ratios and lighter structures in aviation.
