History of the Jews in Russia

The history of the Jews in Russia spans from early medieval Jewish communities in the Caucasus and Crimea to the large Jewish population the Russian Empire acquired through the partitions of Poland in the late 18th century.

The Pale of Settlement

Catherine II established the Pale of Settlement (1791), confining most Jews to the western borderlands. Jews faced conscription of cantonists under Nicholas I, discriminatory laws, and waves of pogroms in 1881–84 and 1903–06, which drove mass emigration, especially to the United States.

Revolution and Soviet era

The Provisional Government abolished Jewish disabilities in 1917. Soviet policy suppressed religion but initially promoted Yiddish culture; the Jewish Autonomous Oblast was created in 1934. Roughly half of Soviet Jews perished in the Holocaust on occupied Soviet territory.

Late Soviet and post-Soviet period

Postwar antisemitic campaigns, including the Doctors' plot (1953), gave way to the refusenik movement; over a million Soviet Jews emigrated to Israel and the West after 1989. See also Pale of Settlement.
