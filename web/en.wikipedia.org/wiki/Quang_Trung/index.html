Quang Trung


Statue of Emperor Quang Trung

Quang Trung (1753 – 16 September 1792), born Hồ Thơm and later renamed Nguyễn Huệ, was the second emperor of the Tây Sơn dynasty of Vietnam, reigning from 1788 to 1792. One of the most successful military commanders in Vietnamese history, he rose from the Tây Sơn rebellion to unify the country after more than two centuries of division between the Trịnh and Nguyễn lords.

Rise

With his brothers Nguyễn Nhạc and Nguyễn Lữ, Nguyễn Huệ led the Tây Sơn uprising from 1771, overthrowing the Nguyễn lords in the south and the Trịnh in the north, and defeating a Siamese intervention force at the Battle of Rạch Gầm–Xoài Mút in 1785.

Victory over the Qing

In late 1788, a Qing army invaded to restore the last Lê emperor. Nguyễn Huệ proclaimed himself Emperor Quang Trung and marched north with speed, destroying the Qing force in the surprise Tet offensive at the Battle of Ngọc Hồi–Đống Đa in early 1789 — still celebrated as one of Vietnam's greatest victories. He then negotiated a tributary settlement with the Qianlong Emperor.

Reforms and death

Quang Trung promoted the chữ Nôm script for administration, reformed taxation and education, and planned further campaigns, but died suddenly in 1792, aged about 40. His young son Quang Toản succeeded him, and the Tây Sơn dynasty fell to Nguyễn Ánh — founder of the Nguyễn dynasty — in 1802.
