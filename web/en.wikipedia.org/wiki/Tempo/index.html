Tempo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In musical terminology, tempo (Italian for 'time'; plural tempos or tempi) is the speed or pace of a given musical composition. It is usually measured in beats per minute (BPM): for example, a tempo of 60 BPM means one beat per second. Tempo is typically indicated by a metronome marking (e.g., ♩ = 120) or by a conventional Italian term.

Tempo markings

Common Italian tempo terms, from slowest to fastest, include Grave, Largo, Lento, Adagio, Andante ("walking pace"), Moderato, Allegretto, Allegro, Vivace, and Presto. Terms are modified by words such as molto (very), non troppo (not too much), and con moto (with motion). Changes of tempo are directed by ritardando and rallentando (slowing down), accelerando and stringendo (speeding up), and a tempo (returning to the original speed).

History

Before the invention of the metronome by Johann Maelzel in 1815, tempo was communicated only through words and the character of the music; Beethoven was among the first major composers to publish metronome marks. In popular and electronic music, BPM values are standard and often define genres.
