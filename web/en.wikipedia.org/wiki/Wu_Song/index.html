Wu Song (武松) is a fictional hero of the classic Chinese novel Water Margin, one of the Four Great Classical Novels. Nicknamed the "Pilgrim", he is one of the most beloved of the 108 outlaws of Liangshan Marsh, and his adventures also feature in the novel Jin Ping Mei.

Story

Wu Song's most famous episode recounts how, after drinking heavily at an inn despite warnings, he crossed Jingyang Ridge and killed a man-eating tiger with his bare hands, becoming a local hero and a constable in Yanggu County. There he reunited with his elder brother, Wu Dalang, a humble pancake seller married to the beautiful Pan Jinlian. When Pan Jinlian and her lover Ximen Qing poisoned Wu Dalang, Wu Song investigated, killed both murderers, and surrendered to the authorities. Exiled to Mengzhou, he was befriended by the jailer's son Shi En; Wu Song retook the restaurant that a strongman had seized from Shi En (the "drunken beating of Jiang the Door God's" episode), escaped an assassination plot at Flying Cloud Pool, and massacred the household of his persecutors at Mandarin Duck Tower before fleeing disguised as a wandering pilgrim — the origin of his nickname. He eventually joined the Liangshan band and became one of its infantry leaders. In the novel's later campaigns he loses an arm fighting Fang La's rebels, refuses office, and ends his days as a monk at the Liuhe Temple in Hangzhou, dying at the age of eighty.

Legacy

Wu Song is celebrated in Chinese opera, storytelling and film as a paragon of strength, loyalty and rough justice.
