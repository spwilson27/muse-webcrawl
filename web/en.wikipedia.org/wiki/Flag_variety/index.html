Flag variety

In mathematics, a flag is an increasing sequence of subspaces of a finite-dimensional vector space. A complete flag is a maximal such chain, in which each successive subspace increases the dimension by one. The set of all complete flags in an n-dimensional vector space forms an algebraic variety called the flag variety (or flag manifold), usually denoted Fl(V) or Fln.

More generally, for a fixed sequence of integers 0 < d1 < d2 < … < dk < n, the partial flag variety parameterises all chains of subspaces V1 ⊂ V2 ⊂ … ⊂ Vk with dim Vi = di. The best-known partial cases are the Grassmannian, which parameterises subspaces of a single fixed dimension, and projective space itself.

Homogeneous space description

The flag variety can be realised as a homogeneous space: the general linear group GL(V) acts transitively on flags of a given type, and the stabiliser of a complete flag is the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices (with respect to a compatible basis). Thus the complete flag variety is GL(n)/B, and partial flag varieties are quotients GL(n)/P by parabolic subgroups. Over the complex numbers, the flag variety is a compact Kähler manifold and a central object in representation theory, where it features in the Borel–Weil theorem.

Schubert calculus

Flag varieties carry a canonical cellular decomposition into Schubert cells, indexed by elements of the Weyl group, which allows the intersection theory of their algebraic cycles — Schubert calculus — to be computed combinatorially. This links flag varieties to the geometry of the Bruhat decomposition.
