Árpád dynasty

The Árpád dynasty (Hungarian: Árpád-ház) was the ruling dynasty of the Principality and then Kingdom of Hungary from around 895 to 1301. Founded by the Grand Prince Árpád, who led the Magyar tribes into the Carpathian Basin during the Hungarian conquest, the dynasty produced Hungary's first Christian king, Stephen I (Saint Stephen), and a line of monarchs — several of them canonised — who shaped the medieval Hungarian state. The male line died out with Andrew III in 1301.

Origins and grand princes

According to Hungarian chronicle tradition, the dynasty descended from the chieftain Álmos, whose son Árpád led the seven Magyar tribes across the Carpathians around 895. Árpád's descendants ruled as grand princes for a century, consolidating the tribal federation. Géza (r. c. 972–997) began orienting Hungary toward Christian Europe and arranged the marriage of his son Vajk — baptised Stephen — to Gisela of Bavaria.

The Christian kingdom

Stephen I was crowned king in 1000 or 1001 and organised Hungary as a Christian kingdom with counties, bishoprics, and written laws; he was canonised in 1083, as was his son Emeric. The dynasty weathered succession crises, pagan revolts, and conflicts with the Holy Roman Empire. Ladislaus I (Saint Ladislaus) extended Hungarian power into Croatia. In the 12th century Béla III made Hungary a major regional power. Béla IV rebuilt the kingdom after the Mongol invasion of 1241–1242 and is called its "second founder". Andrew II's Golden Bull of 1222 limited royal power in favour of the nobility.

Extinction and legacy

The dynasty's male line ended with the death of Andrew III in 1301, after which the crown passed, following an interregnum, to the Angevins. The Árpáds left Hungary with saint-kings central to national identity; the striped arms associated with the dynasty remain part of Hungary's coat of arms, and modern genetic studies of royal remains have been used to trace the family's origins.
