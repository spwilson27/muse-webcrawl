General Sir Peter John Cosgrove, AK, CVO, MC (born 28 July 1947) is an Australian retired senior Army officer who served as the 26th Governor-General of Australia from 2014 to 2019. He previously served as Chief of the Defence Force from 2002 to 2005, and gained wide public prominence as commander of the International Force East Timor (INTERFET) in 1999.

Military career

Cosgrove graduated from the Royal Military College, Duntroon in 1968 and served in the Vietnam War, where he was awarded the Military Cross. He rose through a series of command and staff appointments over four decades. In 1999 he was appointed to lead INTERFET, the Australian-led international force that restored order in East Timor following the violence that accompanied its independence referendum; the mission's success made him a prominent national figure. He was named Australian of the Year in 2001 and became Chief of the Defence Force in 2002.

Governor-General

Cosgrove was appointed Governor-General by Queen Elizabeth II on the advice of Prime Minister Tony Abbott and was sworn in on 28 March 2014. During his term he performed the ceremonial and constitutional duties of the office, represented Australia abroad, and was active in veterans' and community causes. He was succeeded by David Hurley on 1 July 2019.

Later roles

After leaving office, Cosgrove led the national bushfire recovery effort following the 2019–20 Australian bushfire season as head of the National Bushfire Recovery Agency.
