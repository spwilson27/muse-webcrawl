Household Words was an English weekly magazine edited by Charles Dickens and published from 1850 to 1859. Named after the line "Familiar in their mouths as household words" from Shakespeare's Henry V, it sold for twopence and mixed fiction, poetry, essays and social journalism aimed at a broad middle- and working-class readership.

Contents and contributors

Dickens, billed as "Conducted by Charles Dickens", exercised close editorial control, contributing articles and serializing his own novel Hard Times in its pages in 1854. Other notable serials included Elizabeth Gaskell's North and South and Cranford, and Wilkie Collins's The Dead Secret. Regular contributors included Gaskell, Collins, Adelaide Anne Procter, Harriet Martineau and the staff journalist William Henry Wills, Dickens's sub-editor. Articles were published anonymously, a practice that frustrated some contributors.

End and succession

The magazine ended in 1859 after Dickens quarrelled with his publishers Bradbury and Evans over their handling of publicity for his separation from his wife Catherine. Dickens bought out the venture, wound it up, and immediately launched a successor journal, All the Year Round, which he owned outright and which serialized A Tale of Two Cities and Great Expectations.
