Florida A&M University

Florida Agricultural and Mechanical University (Florida A&M University or FAMU) is a public, historically black land-grant university in Tallahassee, Florida. Founded in 1887 as the State Normal College for Colored Students, it is the largest historically black university (HBCU) in the United States by enrollment and a member of the State University System of Florida.

History

The school opened in 1887 with 15 students in Tallahassee, with Thomas DeSaille Tucker as its first president. It became Florida's land-grant institution for African Americans under the second Morrill Act and was renamed Florida Agricultural and Mechanical College in 1909, gaining university status in 1953. Its campus includes the Carnegie Library (1908), among the oldest buildings in Florida's black higher education, and the campus was designated a National Historic District.

Academics and campus life

FAMU offers bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees through colleges including agriculture and food sciences, engineering (shared with Florida State University as the FAMU-FSU College of Engineering), law, pharmacy, and allied health. Its marching band, the Marching "101", is internationally famous and performed at the Super Bowl and the inauguration parade of President Barack Obama. Athletic teams compete as the Rattlers in the Southwestern Athletic Conference (SWAC).
