Impedance matching

Impedance matching
FieldElectrical engineering, acoustics, optics
GoalMaximum power transfer; elimination of signal reflections

In electronics, impedance matching is the practice of designing or adjusting the input impedance of an electrical load, or the output impedance of its corresponding signal source, to maximise power transfer or to minimise reflections from the load. The maximum power transfer theorem states that maximum power is delivered when the load impedance is the complex conjugate of the source impedance.

Applications

Matching is essential in radio-frequency and microwave engineering, where mismatches on transmission lines produce reflected waves and standing waves — measured by the voltage standing wave ratio (VSWR) — reducing power transfer and sometimes damaging transmitters. Antenna systems, audio equipment, telephone lines, and high-speed digital interconnects all employ matching techniques.

Methods

Networks of inductors and capacitors (L-sections, pi and T networks), quarter-wave transformers, stubs, and broadband transformers can transform one impedance to another. The Smith chart is the classical graphical tool for designing matching networks. Matching is frequency-dependent, so broadband matches involve trade-offs (the Bode–Fano criterion).

Beyond electronics

Analogous concepts exist in acoustics (the middle ear matches airborne sound to the fluid of the cochlea; horn loudspeakers match a driver to the air) and in optics (anti-reflection coatings act as impedance-matching layers).
