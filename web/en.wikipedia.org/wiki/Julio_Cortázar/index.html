Julio Cortázar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Julio Cortázar]

Julio Cortázar (26 August 1914 – 12 February 1984) was an Argentine novelist, short story writer, essayist, and translator, widely regarded as one of the most innovative authors of the Latin American Boom. Born in Brussels to Argentine parents, he grew up in Buenos Aires and lived much of his adult life in Paris.

Works

His masterpiece is the novel Hopscotch (Rayuela, 1963), which can be read in multiple sequences according to the author's "table of instructions," a landmark of experimental fiction. His short story collections, including Bestiario (1951) and End of the Game (1956), contain classics such as "Axolotl" and "The Night Face Up." His story "Blow-Up" inspired Michelangelo Antonioni's 1966 film of the same name.

Later life

Cortázar became increasingly politically engaged, supporting leftist causes in Latin America and opposing the Argentine military dictatorship. He became a French citizen in 1981 and died in Paris in 1984.
