Noma is a restaurant in Copenhagen, Denmark, founded in 2003 by chef René Redzepi. Its name is a syllabic abbreviation of the Danish words nordisk mad ("Nordic food"). Noma became the leading restaurant of the New Nordic Cuisine movement, built on local, seasonal, and foraged Scandinavian ingredients, and was named the world's best restaurant by Restaurant magazine in 2010, 2011, 2012, 2014, and 2021.

History

Noma originally occupied an 18th-century warehouse on the Christianshavn waterfront. Redzepi, who had worked at El Bulli and The French Laundry, developed an approach based on ingredients sourced from the Nordic region — such as musk ox, skyr, sea buckthorn, wild herbs, and edible insects — and on fermentation techniques explored in the restaurant's dedicated fermentation lab. In 2016 the original restaurant closed; after a pop-up period including residencies in Sydney, Tulum, and Kyoto, "Noma 2.0" reopened in 2018 in a purpose-built complex of greenhouses and pavilions on Refshaleøen, organised around three seasons: seafood, vegetable, and game and forest.

Recognition

The restaurant held two Michelin stars for many years and received a third star in 2021. Its influence on global fine dining — foraging, fermentation, hyper-local sourcing, and test-kitchen research — has been widely credited with transforming how chefs approach regional ingredients.

Closure announcement

In January 2023, Redzepi announced that Noma would end regular restaurant service at the end of 2024, transforming into "Noma 3.0", a food laboratory and pop-up project. The announcement prompted wide debate about the sustainability of the fine-dining model, including its reliance on unpaid intern labour, a practice Noma had begun paying for shortly before the announcement.
