Capillary electrophoresis

AcronymCE
ClassificationElectrophoresis; analytical chemistry
AnalytesIons, small molecules, proteins, DNA

Capillary electrophoresis (CE) is a family of electrokinetic separation methods performed in submillimeter diameter capillaries and in micro- and nanofluidic channels. Very often, CE refers to capillary zone electrophoresis (CZE), but other electrophoretic techniques including capillary gel electrophoresis (CGE), capillary isoelectric focusing (CIEF), capillary isotachophoresis and micellar electrokinetic chromatography (MEKC) belong also to this class of methods.

Principle

In CE separations, an electric field is applied across a capillary filled with an electrolyte buffer. Analytes migrate under the combined influence of electrophoresis and electroosmotic flow (EOF). The inner wall of a fused silica capillary carries ionised silanol groups, which attract cations from the buffer to form an electrical double layer; when voltage is applied, this layer moves toward the cathode, carrying the bulk solution with it. Charged analytes separate according to their electrophoretic mobilities superimposed on this flow.

Instrumentation

A basic CE instrument consists of a high-voltage power supply, two buffer reservoirs with electrodes, the capillary, a sample introduction system (hydrodynamic or electrokinetic injection), and an on-capillary detector, most commonly UV–visible absorbance or laser-induced fluorescence. Capillaries are typically 25–100 micrometres in internal diameter and 20–100 cm long.

Applications

Capillary electrophoresis played a central role in the Human Genome Project: capillary array electrophoresis with fluorescently labelled DNA fragments enabled high-throughput Sanger sequencing. Other applications include analysis of pharmaceuticals and chiral separations, protein and peptide characterisation, forensic analysis, and the measurement of inorganic ions in environmental samples.
