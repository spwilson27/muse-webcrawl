Silver Springs (attraction)

Silver Springs is a group of artesian springs in Marion County, Florida, that forms the headwaters of the Silver River. Developed as one of Florida's earliest tourist attractions in the 1870s, it became famous for its glass-bottom boat tours over crystal-clear water. The attraction is now the centerpiece of Silver Springs State Park.

History

Commercial glass-bottom boat tours began in the late 1870s after Phillip Morrell fixed a pane of glass in the hull of a dugout canoe. In the 20th century Silver Springs hosted film and television productions, including the Creature from the Black Lagoon and episodes of Sea Hunt. Ross Allen's Reptile Institute added a herpetology attraction from 1929. Privately operated through the 20th century — neighbors to the former Six Gun Territory theme park — the attraction struggled after Walt Disney World drew tourism south, and the State of Florida took it over in 2013, merging it with the adjacent state park.

Hydrology and ecology

The springs group comprises numerous vents in the Floridan aquifer, historically among the largest first-magnitude springs in the world. Declining flow and nitrate-driven algae growth have raised conservation concerns. The springs and Silver River support manatees, alligators, and a self-sustaining population of rhesus macaques descended from animals released in the 1930s.
