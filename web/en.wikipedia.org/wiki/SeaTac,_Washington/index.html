SeaTac, Washington

SeaTac is a city in southern King County, Washington, United States, an inner-ring suburb of Seattle. Its name is a portmanteau of Seattle and Tacoma, taken from Seattle–Tacoma International Airport (SEA), which lies within the city and is operated by the Port of Seattle. SeaTac was incorporated in 1990, and its population was about 31,000 at the 2020 census.

Geography and economy

The city wraps around the airport on three sides and is crossed by Interstate 5 and State Route 99. Its economy is dominated by the airport and related hotels, parking, rental-car and logistics businesses, making it a major employment centre despite its modest residential population. Angle Lake, with its surrounding park, is the city's principal natural feature.

Government and transport

SeaTac operates under a council–manager government. Link light rail serves the city at SeaTac/Airport and Angle Lake stations, connecting it to downtown Seattle and the wider Puget Sound region. The city attracted national attention for an early local minimum-wage initiative, Proposition 1 of 2013, which raised pay for airport and hospitality workers.
