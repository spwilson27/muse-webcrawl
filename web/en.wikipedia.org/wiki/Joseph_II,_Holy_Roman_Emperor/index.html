Joseph II, Holy Roman Emperor

Joseph II (13 March 1741 – 20 February 1790) was Holy Roman Emperor from 1765 to 1790 and ruler of the Habsburg lands from 1780 to 1790. The eldest son of Empress Maria Theresa and Emperor Francis I, he was a proponent of enlightened absolutism, issuing thousands of edicts reforming law, religion, serfdom, and administration — reforms whose speed and scope provoked resistance that forced him to revoke many of them on his deathbed.

Co-regency and sole rule

Joseph became Holy Roman Emperor on his father's death in 1765 and co-regent of the Habsburg hereditary lands with his mother, Maria Theresa, whose caution restrained his radicalism until her death in 1780. As sole ruler he unleashed a torrent of reform — contemporaries estimated some 6,000 edicts and 11,000 new laws during his decade of sole rule.

Reforms ("Josephinism")

The Patent of Toleration (1781) granted limited religious freedom to Lutherans, Calvinists, and Orthodox Christians, and later edicts improved the status of Jews. Joseph suppressed hundreds of contemplative monasteries and redirected their wealth to parishes and hospitals under state control (Josephinism). The Serfdom Patent of 1781 abolished personal servitude in the Bohemian and hereditary lands, and in 1789 he decreed that peasants pay taxes in cash rather than labour. He reformed the criminal code, abolished torture and (briefly) the death penalty, centralized administration, imposed German as the administrative language, founded general hospitals and schools, and pursued centralization in Hungary and the Austrian Netherlands.

Resistance and death

His reforms provoked the Brabant Revolution in the Austrian Netherlands (1789) and near-revolt in Hungary, while a war against the Ottoman Empire (1788–91) went badly and damaged his health. Dying in Vienna in February 1790, Joseph revoked most of his own reforms except the Toleration Patent, serfdom abolition, and religious reforms. His self-composed epitaph is said to have read: "Here lies Joseph II, who failed in all he undertook." His brother Leopold II succeeded him.
