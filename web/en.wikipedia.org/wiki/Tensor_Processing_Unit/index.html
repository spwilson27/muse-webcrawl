Tensor Processing Unit

A Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) is an AI accelerator application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) developed by Google specifically for neural network machine learning, particularly using Google's own TensorFlow software. Announced in May 2016, TPUs have been deployed in Google's data centres since 2015 and power services including Search, Translate, Photos and the AlphaGo system that defeated Lee Sedol.

A Tensor Processing Unit circuit board

Design

The TPU is built around a large systolic array of multiply-accumulate units optimised for the dense matrix operations at the heart of neural network computation, performed at reduced (8-bit integer or bfloat16 floating-point) precision to maximise throughput per watt. The first-generation TPU performed inference only and delivered roughly 15–30 times the performance of contemporary CPUs and GPUs on Google's workloads, with far better energy efficiency.

Generations

Second-generation TPUs (2017) added floating-point training capability and were grouped into "pods" of up to 256 chips linked by high-speed interconnects. Third-generation (2018) and fourth-generation (2021) devices increased performance and adopted optical circuit switching in pods of up to 4,096 chips. Later generations, including the v5 series and the sixth-generation "Trillium" chips announced in 2024, continued the scaling. A smaller Edge TPU (marketed as the Coral Edge TPU) provides inference for embedded devices, and Google's Pixel phones incorporate related Tensor processors.

Access

TPUs are offered to outside users through Google Cloud Platform as Cloud TPUs, and are used to train and run large models including Google's own Gemini family of language models.
