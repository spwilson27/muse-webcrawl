In Buddhism, the bodhipakkhiyādhammā (Pali; Sanskrit bodhipakṣa dharma) are "qualities conducive to awakening" — a set of 37 practices and mental qualities, grouped in seven sets, that lead to enlightenment (bodhi).

The seven sets

The 37 qualities comprise: the four establishments of mindfulness (satipaṭṭhāna); the four right efforts (sammappadhāna); the four bases of spiritual power (iddhipāda); the five spiritual faculties (indriya); the five powers (bala); the seven factors of awakening (bojjhaṅga); and the Noble Eightfold Path (ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo).

Role in doctrine

The grouping, set out in texts such as the Mahāparinibbāna Sutta, serves as a comprehensive map of the path, and is especially prominent in Theravāda exegesis. Overlapping qualities — mindfulness, energy and concentration recur across the sets — show how the same factors deepen through stages of practice.
