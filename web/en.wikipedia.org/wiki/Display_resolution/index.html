Display resolution

Display resolution
Measured inPixels (width × height)
Common standardsHD 1280×720; Full HD 1920×1080; QHD 2560×1440; 4K UHD 3840×2160; 8K 7680×4320
Related measurePixel density (PPI)

The display resolution or display modes of a digital television, computer monitor, or other display device is the number of distinct pixels in each dimension that can be displayed. It is usually quoted as width × height, with the units in pixels: for example, 1920 × 1080 means the width is 1,920 pixels and the height is 1,080 pixels.

Terminology

Marketing labels have evolved with the technology: standard-definition television (480i/576i), HD (720p), Full HD (1080p), Quad HD (1440p), and Ultra HD "4K" (3840 × 2160) and "8K" (7680 × 4320). Resolution should not be confused with pixel density, measured in pixels per inch, which depends on the physical size of the screen, nor with the resolution of the signal source, which may be upscaled or downscaled by the display.

History

Early computer displays used resolutions such as 640×480 (VGA, 1987) and 1024×768 (XGA). LCD monitors standardised on progressively higher native resolutions through the 2000s, while television moved from analogue broadcasts to HD and then UHD. Smartphones pushed pixel densities beyond the resolving power of the human eye at normal viewing distances, marketed under terms such as "Retina display".
