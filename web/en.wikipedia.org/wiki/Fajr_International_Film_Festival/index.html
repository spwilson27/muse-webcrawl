The Fajr International Film Festival (Persian: جشنواره بین‌المللی فیلم فجر) is an annual film festival held in Tehran, Iran. Founded in 1983, it is organized under the supervision of the Ministry of Culture and Islamic Guidance and the Farabi Cinema Foundation. The festival takes place each February during the Fajr decade, the ten-day period commemorating the anniversary of the 1979 Iranian Revolution.

History

The festival began in 1983 as part of the Fajr celebrations. Over the decades it grew into Iran's most important film event, showcasing the annual output of Iranian cinema alongside international productions. In 2015, the festival was split into two separate events: a national festival held in February and an international festival held in April or May.

Awards

The festival's principal award is the Crystal Simorgh (Simorgh being a mythical bird of Persian legend), presented in many categories including best film, best director, best screenplay, best actor and best actress. The international competition has its own section with awards for Asian and world cinema.

Significance

Fajr is the premier showcase for new Iranian films, and its selections and prizes strongly influence the domestic box office and the international circulation of Iranian cinema in the following year.
