Intensive care unit

An intensive care unit (ICU), also known as an intensive therapy unit or critical care unit, is a specialised department of a hospital that provides intensive care medicine to critically ill patients. ICUs are staffed continuously by specially trained doctors, nurses and allied health professionals, and are equipped for continuous monitoring of vital signs, mechanical ventilation, and support or replacement of failing organs.

Equipment and staffing

A typical ICU bed space includes a multi-parameter monitor displaying electrocardiogram, blood pressure, oxygen saturation and respiratory rate; a mechanical ventilator; infusion pumps for precise delivery of drugs and fluids; and access to renal replacement therapy equipment, point-of-care blood testing and portable imaging. Nurse-to-patient ratios are much higher than on general wards — often 1:1 for the most severely ill — and care is coordinated by consultant intensivists in daily multidisciplinary rounds. Care bundles, such as those for ventilator-associated pneumonia prevention and sepsis management, are used to standardise evidence-based practice.

Types of unit

Hospitals may run general (mixed medical-surgical) ICUs or specialised units: neonatal intensive care units (NICU) for newborn infants, paediatric intensive care units (PICU), coronary care units for cardiac patients, neurological and neurosurgical units, burns units, and cardiothoracic units for post-heart-surgery patients. High-dependency units (HDUs) provide an intermediate level of care ("step-down") for patients who no longer need full ICU support but require more monitoring than a general ward can offer.

Admission and outcomes

Admission is generally reserved for patients with potentially reversible life-threatening illness who require organ support, or who need close observation after major surgery or deterioration. Outcomes depend heavily on the underlying condition; ICU mortality is commonly in the range of 10–20% for general units. Survivors may experience post-intensive care syndrome — physical weakness, cognitive impairment and psychological problems including anxiety, depression and post-traumatic stress — which has led to follow-up clinics and rehabilitation services. During the COVID-19 pandemic, ICU capacity became a central constraint on health systems, driving rapid expansion of beds, staff redeployment and public reporting of occupancy.
