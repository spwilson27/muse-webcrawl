Islamophobia

Islamophobia is the fear of, hatred of, or prejudice against Islam or Muslims, particularly when seen as a geopolitical force or a source of terrorism. The term is used by scholars, governments and international organisations to describe hostility towards Islam and Muslims in discourse, policy and acts of discrimination and violence.

History of the term

The term was used as early as the early 20th century — in French, islamophobie, in 1910 — but entered wide use after the 1997 report Islamophobia: A Challenge for Us All by the Runnymede Trust in the United Kingdom, which defined Islamophobia as unfounded hostility towards Islam and its practical consequences of unfair discrimination against Muslim individuals and communities.

Manifestations

Islamophobia is studied in forms including hate crimes against Muslims, vandalism of mosques, discriminatory policies such as travel bans and headscarf bans, negative media portrayals, and anti-Muslim rhetoric in political campaigns. After the September 11 attacks in 2001 and subsequent terrorist attacks in Europe, reported anti-Muslim incidents rose sharply in many countries.

Debate

The term is contested. Some critics argue it conflates criticism of Islam as a religion with prejudice against Muslims as people, and could be used to stifle debate. Others, including the United Nations and the European Union's Fundamental Rights Agency, treat it as a recognised form of racism and intolerance. The United Nations General Assembly declared 15 March the International Day to Combat Islamophobia in 2022.
