The Seneca Falls Convention was the first women's rights convention in the United States, held in Seneca Falls, New York, on 19–20 July 1848. It launched the organized women's suffrage movement.

Convention

Organized principally by Elizabeth Cady Stanton and Lucretia Mott — who had met at the 1840 World Anti-Slavery Convention in London, where women delegates were excluded — with Martha Wright, Mary Ann M'Clintock, and Jane Hunt, the meeting drew about 300 people. Stanton read the Declaration of Sentiments, modelled on the Declaration of Independence, listing grievances and demanding rights including, controversially, women's suffrage. Frederick Douglass spoke in support, helping carry the suffrage resolution; 100 attendees (68 women, 32 men) signed the Declaration.

Legacy

Press coverage ranged from ridicule to support. The convention began a decades-long campaign culminating in the Nineteenth Amendment (1920). The site is preserved as Women's Rights National Historical Park. Compare the Selma to Montgomery marches, a later rights milestone.
