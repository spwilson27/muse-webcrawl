Hebron

Hebron (Arabic: الخليل, al-Khalīl; Hebrew: חֶבְרוֹן, Ḥevron) is a Palestinian city in the southern West Bank, about 30 kilometres (19 mi) south of Jerusalem. It is the largest city in the West Bank, with a population of roughly 200,000 Palestinians alongside several hundred Israeli settlers living in and around the old city under military protection. Hebron is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world and is considered holy in Judaism, Islam and Christianity.

The Cave of the Patriarchs / Ibrahimi Mosque dominates Hebron's Old Town.

Religious significance

Hebron is traditionally associated with the patriarch Abraham, who is said to have purchased the Cave of Machpelah as a family burial place; the tombs of Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and their wives Sarah, Rebecca and Leah are venerated there. King David was anointed king in Hebron and reigned there seven years before moving to Jerusalem. The massive Herodian enclosure over the cave now houses the Ibrahimi Mosque and a synagogue, with access divided between Muslims and Jews.

History

Archaeological evidence indicates settlement from the Early Bronze Age. Hebron passed through Roman, Byzantine, Muslim, Crusader, Mamluk and Ottoman rule. In 1929, riots in the city killed 67 Jews and ended the ancient Jewish community there. After the 1967 Six-Day War, Israel occupied the West Bank; Jewish settlement in and near Hebron resumed, notably Kiryat Arba (founded 1968), and settlers returned to the old Jewish quarter from 1979.

In 1994, Israeli settler Baruch Goldstein murdered 29 Palestinian worshippers at the Ibrahimi Mosque, an atrocity that led to riots and to international monitoring. The 1997 Hebron Protocol divided the city into H1 (about 80%, under Palestinian Authority control) and H2 (about 20%, including the Old City and the shrine, under Israeli military control). The Old Town of Hebron/Al-Khalil was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2017.

Economy

Hebron is a major commercial and industrial centre of the West Bank, noted for ceramics, glass-blowing, leather and shoe manufacture, stone and marble quarrying, and its historic souks.
