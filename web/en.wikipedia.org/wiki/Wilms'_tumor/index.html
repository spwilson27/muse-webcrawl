Wilms' tumor

Wilms' tumor is also written as Wilms tumor. For the surgeon, see Max Wilms.

Wilms' tumor, also known as nephroblastoma, is a cancer of the kidneys that typically occurs in children, rarely in adults. It is named after the German surgeon Max Wilms (1867–1918), who first described the tumor in detail. Wilms' tumor is the most common type of kidney cancer in children, accounting for the great majority of childhood renal tumors, and is the fourth most common childhood cancer overall. Approximately 650 cases are diagnosed in the United States each year.

Signs and symptoms

Most children present with an asymptomatic abdominal mass discovered by a parent or during a routine examination. Other signs may include abdominal pain, hematuria (blood in the urine), fever, loss of appetite, nausea, and hypertension, which occurs in about a quarter of cases due to increased renin production. The tumor usually affects one kidney, though about 5–10% of cases are bilateral at diagnosis.

Causes and genetics

Wilms' tumor arises from persistent embryonal renal tissue (nephrogenic rests). Several genetic loci are implicated, including the WT1 gene on chromosome 11p13, which is mutated in the WAGR syndrome and Denys–Drash syndrome, and the WT2 locus on 11p15, associated with Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome. Most cases are sporadic, though a small percentage are familial.

Pathology

Classic Wilms' tumor is triphasic, containing blastemal, epithelial, and stromal elements resembling developing kidney tissue. Tumors with diffuse anaplasia have a poorer prognosis. Staging follows systems developed by the Children's Oncology Group (COG) in North America and SIOP in Europe.

Treatment and prognosis

Treatment combines surgery (nephrectomy) with chemotherapy; radiotherapy is reserved for higher stages. The two main strategies differ in timing: the COG approach begins with primary surgery, while SIOP uses preoperative chemotherapy. Outcomes have improved dramatically: overall survival for localized disease now exceeds 90%, making Wilms' tumor one of the success stories of pediatric oncology.
