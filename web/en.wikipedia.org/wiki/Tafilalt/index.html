Tafilalt

Date palm groves of the Tafilalt, the largest oasis region in Morocco

Tafilalt or Tafilalet (Berber: Tafilalt; Arabic: تافيلالت) is a region and oasis complex in south-eastern Morocco, in the Drâa-Tafilalet region, along the valley of the Ziz River. It is the largest oasis in Morocco and is famous for its date palms and its fortified villages (ksars), including the towns of Erfoud and Rissani.

History

The Tafilalt was the site of the medieval trading city of Sijilmasa, founded in 757 CE on the northern edge of the Sahara. Sijilmasa became a great entrepôt of the trans-Saharan trade in gold, salt, and slaves, and one of the principal gateways between sub-Saharan Africa and the Mediterranean world, before its decline in the later Middle Ages.

The region holds special importance in Moroccan history as the cradle of the Alaouite dynasty, which has ruled Morocco since the 17th century. The Alaouite sharifs rose to power from their base in the Tafilalt, and the region's town of Rissani remains associated with the dynasty's origins and its mausoleums.

Geography and economy

The oasis stretches along the lower Ziz valley before the river dissipates into the desert. Its economy is based on date production — the Tafilalt is renowned for large, sweet Medjool-type dates — irrigated agriculture supported by traditional underground channels (khettaras) and modern wells, and increasingly tourism connected with the nearby sand dunes of Erg Chebbi at Merzouga.
