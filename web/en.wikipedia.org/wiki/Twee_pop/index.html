Twee pop

Twee pop is a subgenre of indie pop that originates from the 1986 NME compilation cassette C86. It is known for its simple, catchy melodies, romantic and innocent lyrics, jangling guitars, and a do-it-yourself aesthetic that celebrates sweetness and vulnerability, often in deliberate contrast to the aggression of much rock music. The word "twee" — British slang for daintily or quaintly sweet — was initially sometimes used dismissively, but was embraced by fans and musicians.

Characteristics

Twee pop songs are typically short, melodic and lo-fi in production, drawing on 1960s girl groups, the Velvet Underground, and punk's three-chord simplicity. Lyrics dwell on crushes, hand-holding, and everyday small pleasures; many bands feature boy–girl harmonies and female vocalists. The aesthetic extended to fashion (anoraks, cardigans, hairslides) and to hand-made fanzines and seven-inch singles released on tiny independent labels.

History

The movement coalesced in the United Kingdom around labels such as Sarah Records of Bristol, home to the Field Mice, Heavenly and Talulah Gosh, and around bands associated with the C86 cassette — Primal Scream, the Pastels, and the Shop Assistants among them. The riot grrrl and indie scenes of the early 1990s in the United States developed a parallel twee current, centred on K Records of Olympia, Washington, and bands such as Beat Happening, Tiger Trap and Tullycraft. The Scottish band Belle and Sebastian, formed in 1996, brought a refined, literate version of twee to international audiences. A revival in the 2000s and 2010s included bands such as Camera Obscura, The Pains of Being Pure at Heart and Alvvays.
