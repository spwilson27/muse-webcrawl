Lagoon Nebula

The Lagoon Nebula (catalogued as Messier 8 or M8, and as NGC 6523) is a giant interstellar cloud and H II region in the constellation Sagittarius. It is estimated to lie 4,100 light-years (1,250 parsecs) from Earth and spans roughly 110 by 50 light-years. One of only two star-forming nebulae faintly visible to the unaided eye from mid-northern latitudes (the other being the Orion Nebula), it is a vast stellar nursery containing the young open cluster NGC 6530 and numerous dark Bok globules.

Characteristics

The nebula's name refers to the dark, lagoon-shaped dust lane that crosses its bright centre. Embedded within it is the open cluster NGC 6530, formed from the nebula's material only a few million years ago; the massive O-type stars of the cluster — notably Herschel 36 and the blue supergiant 9 Sagittarii — ionise the surrounding gas and drive its glow. A particularly active star-forming structure near the centre, the Hourglass Nebula (not to be confused with the planetary nebula of the same name), was imaged in detail by the Hubble Space Telescope, revealing funnel-shaped clouds sculpted by stellar winds and ultraviolet radiation.

Observation history

The nebula was recorded by Giovanni Battista Hodierna before 1654, rediscovered by John Flamsteed in 1680 and by Le Gentil in 1747; Charles Messier catalogued it in 1764, noting both the nebulosity and the cluster within. It lies in the direction of the galactic centre, among the rich star fields of the Sagittarius Milky Way, and is a popular target for amateur astronomers in summer.
