Aging

Aging is the process of becoming older. In humans it refers to the accumulation of physical, psychological, and social changes over time, and more broadly to the time-dependent functional decline that affects most living organisms. Aging is characterised by a progressive loss of physiological integrity, leading to impaired function and increased vulnerability to death. It is the primary risk factor for major human diseases including cancer, diabetes, cardiovascular disorders, and neurodegenerative diseases.

Biology of aging

Research in biogerontology identifies "hallmarks of aging" that include genomic instability, telomere attrition, epigenetic alterations, loss of proteostasis, disabled macroautophagy, deregulated nutrient sensing, mitochondrial dysfunction, cellular senescence, stem cell exhaustion, altered intercellular communication, chronic inflammation, and dysbiosis. Telomeres—protective DNA sequences at chromosome ends—shorten with each cell division, eventually triggering cellular senescence or death, a limit known as the Hayflick limit. Cells that stop dividing but do not die (senescent cells) accumulate with age and secrete inflammatory factors.

Theories of aging

Evolutionary theories explain aging as a result of declining natural-selection pressure with age: the mutation accumulation theory (Peter Medawar), the antagonistic pleiotropy theory (George C. Williams)—genes beneficial early in life may be harmful later—and the disposable soma theory (Thomas Kirkwood), which frames aging as a trade-off between reproduction and bodily maintenance. Damage-based theories emphasise accumulated molecular damage, including from free radicals.

Aging in humans

Human aging brings changes such as grey hair, wrinkles, presbyopia, hearing loss, sarcopenia (loss of muscle mass), reduced bone density, and slower cognition, alongside increased incidence of chronic disease. Life expectancy has risen dramatically since the nineteenth century due to sanitation, nutrition, and medicine; the maximum verified human lifespan is that of Jeanne Calment, who died aged 122 in 1997. Societies worldwide face population aging, with growing proportions of older people—raising questions for pensions, healthcare, and labour markets.

Interventions

Caloric restriction extends lifespan in many model organisms. Genetic and pharmacological interventions—including rapamycin, metformin, and senolytic drugs that clear senescent cells—extend healthspan or lifespan in animals and are under study in humans, but no intervention is yet proven to slow human aging. Lifestyle factors such as exercise, not smoking, and adequate sleep are associated with healthier aging.
