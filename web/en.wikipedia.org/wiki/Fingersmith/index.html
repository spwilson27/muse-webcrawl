Fingersmith

Fingersmith Author: Sarah Waters
Language: English
Genre: Historical fiction, crime novel
Publisher: Virago Press
Publication date: 2002
Adaptations: BBC miniseries (2005); film The Handmaiden (2016)

Fingersmith is a 2002 historical crime novel by the Welsh author Sarah Waters. Set in Victorian London and the English countryside, it tells the story of Sue Trinder, an orphan raised among thieves ("fingersmiths"), who is drawn into an elaborate confidence scheme targeting a young heiress, Maud Lilly. The novel is structured in three parts and is noted for its plot twists and its exploration of class, gender, and sexuality.

Plot

Sue Trinder is recruited by a swindler known as Gentleman to help defraud Maud Lilly, an heiress living with her uncle at Briar, a country house. Sue poses as Maud's maid while Gentleman courts her; the plan is to have Maud declared insane after the marriage and seize her fortune. The narrative then shifts perspective, revealing betrayals and hidden identities that invert the reader's understanding of the scheme.

Reception and adaptations

The novel was shortlisted for the Booker Prize and the Orange Prize in 2002 and won the Crime Writers' Association's Ellis Peters Historical Dagger. It was adapted by the BBC into a two-part television serial in 2005 starring Sally Hawkins and Imelda Staunton, and loosely adapted by director Park Chan-wook as the 2016 South Korean film The Handmaiden, which relocated the story to Japanese-occupied Korea.
