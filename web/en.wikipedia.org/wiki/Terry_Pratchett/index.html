Sir Terence David John Pratchett (28 April 1948 – 12 March 2015) was an English author, humorist, and satirist, best known for the Discworld series of 41 comic fantasy novels set on a flat world carried through space on the backs of four elephants standing on a giant turtle. His books have sold more than 100 million copies worldwide in 43 languages.

Career

Pratchett began as a journalist and worked as a press officer for the Central Electricity Generating Board before the success of the early Discworld novels, beginning with The Colour of Magic (1983), allowed him to write full-time. Later books such as Small Gods, Night Watch, and the Tiffany Aching sequence combined comedy with sharp social and political satire. He co-wrote Good Omens (1990) with Neil Gaiman.

Illness and advocacy

In 2007 Pratchett announced he had been diagnosed with posterior cortical atrophy, a rare form of early-onset Alzheimer's disease, which he called "an embuggerance." He became a prominent advocate for dementia research funding and for assisted dying, and continued writing with the aid of speech-recognition software and his assistant Rob Wilkins.

Honours

Pratchett was appointed OBE in 1998 and knighted in 2009 for services to literature. He won the Carnegie Medal for The Amazing Maurice and His Educated Rodents (2001) and received numerous other awards.
