The Peabody Museum of Natural History at Yale University — renamed the Yale Peabody Museum — is among the oldest, largest, and most prolific university natural history museums in the world. It was founded in 1866 by philanthropist George Peabody at the urging of his nephew, paleontologist Othniel Charles Marsh, whose fossil collections (including dinosaurs collected during the "Bone Wars" era) formed its core.

Collections

The museum's divisions span anthropology, botany, entomology, geology and paleontology, scientific computing, vertebrate and invertebrate zoology, and the history of science. Holdings exceed 13 million specimens, including the world's first scientifically described dinosaurs from the American West and an important collection of material collected by Hiram Bingham at Machu Picchu.

Exhibits

The Great Hall of Dinosaurs displays mounted skeletons including Brontosaurus (now Apatosaurus), Triceratops, and Stegosaurus, beneath Rudolph Zallinger's 110-foot mural The Age of Reptiles (1947), which won a Pulitzer Prize for painting. Other permanent exhibits cover human origins, Egyptian antiquities, Native American cultures, minerals, and Connecticut wildlife.

Renovation

The museum closed in 2020 for a comprehensive renovation — its first major expansion since 1925 — funded in part by a $160 million gift from alumnus Edward P. Bass, and reopened to the public on March 26, 2024, with free admission, expanded galleries, and new teaching laboratories.
