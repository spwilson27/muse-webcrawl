Hypopituitarism

Hypopituitarism is the decreased (hypo) secretion of one or more of the eight hormones normally produced by the pituitary gland at the base of the brain. If there is decreased secretion of most or all pituitary hormones, the term panhypopituitarism is used. Because pituitary hormones control other endocrine glands — the thyroid, adrenals, and gonads among them — their deficiency produces wide-ranging effects.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms depend on which hormones are deficient and develop gradually. Deficiency of growth hormone causes short stature in children and reduced energy and altered body composition in adults. Gonadotropin deficiency leads to loss of libido, infertility, and amenorrhoea; thyroid-stimulating hormone deficiency produces secondary hypothyroidism; and adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) deficiency causes secondary adrenal insufficiency, with fatigue, weight loss, low blood pressure, and a potentially life-threatening inability to respond to stress or illness. Sudden onset, as in pituitary apoplexy, can present with severe headache and visual loss.

Causes

Causes include pituitary tumours (most often benign adenomas) and their treatment by surgery or radiotherapy, traumatic brain injury, subarachnoid haemorrhage, inflammation and infection, infiltrative diseases such as sarcoidosis and haemochromatosis, and Sheehan's syndrome — pituitary infarction following severe postpartum haemorrhage.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis combines measurement of pituitary and target-gland hormones, dynamic stimulation tests, and MRI imaging of the pituitary. Treatment involves addressing the cause and replacing the missing hormones — typically hydrocortisone (given before thyroid hormone, to avoid precipitating an adrenal crisis), levothyroxine, sex steroids, growth hormone, and desmopressin if posterior pituitary function is also affected.
