The Intouchables (French: Intouchables) is a 2011 French buddy comedy-drama film written and directed by Olivier Nakache and Éric Toledano. It stars François Cluzet and Omar Sy in the story of an unlikely friendship between a wealthy quadriplegic aristocrat and his live-in carer, a young man from the Paris suburbs. The film was inspired by the true story of Philippe Pozzo di Borgo and his carer Abdel Sellou.

Plot

Philippe, a rich aristocrat paralysed from the neck down in a paragliding accident, interviews candidates for the position of live-in carer. Driss, a Senegalese immigrant from the banlieues, shows up only to obtain a signature proving his job application was rejected so he can continue receiving welfare benefits. Impressed by his irreverence, Philippe hires him on a trial basis. Despite their vastly different backgrounds, the two form a deep bond: Driss brings spontaneity and humour to Philippe's regimented life, while Philippe introduces Driss to art, music, and stability. When Driss must leave to deal with a family crisis, Philippe realises how much the friendship has changed him, and the two reunite.

Reception

The film became a cultural phenomenon in France, selling about 19.4 million tickets and becoming one of the most successful French films of all time. Worldwide it grossed over $426 million, making it one of the highest-grossing non-English-language films ever released. Omar Sy won the César Award for Best Actor for his performance, becoming the first Black actor to receive the honour. The film was France's submission for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film at the 85th Academy Awards.

Remakes

The film's success prompted several remakes, including the Argentinian Inseparables (2016), the Indian film Oopiri (2016), and the American The Upside (2017), starring Bryan Cranston and Kevin Hart, which was released theatrically in 2019.
