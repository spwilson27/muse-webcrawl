René Redzepi

René Redzepi (born 16 December 1977) is a Danish chef and co-owner of the three-Michelin-star restaurant Noma in Copenhagen, widely credited with inventing and leading the New Nordic Cuisine movement. Noma was ranked first on The World's 50 Best Restaurants list in 2010, 2011, 2012, 2014 and 2021, and Redzepi has been described by Time magazine, which included him in the Time 100 in 2012, as one of the most influential chefs in the world.

Early life and training

Redzepi was born in Copenhagen to a Danish mother and an ethnic Albanian father from Tetovo, North Macedonia. He trained as a chef in Copenhagen and worked at restaurants including Le Jardin des Sens in Montpellier, elBulli in Spain under Ferran Adrià, and The French Laundry in California, before becoming head chef at Kong Hans Kælder in Copenhagen.

Noma and New Nordic cuisine

Redzepi co-founded Noma (from nordisk mad, "Nordic food") in 2003 in the Christianshavn district of Copenhagen. The restaurant became famous for foraging, fermentation and a strict focus on Nordic ingredients — serving dishes built around sea buckthorn, spruce, ants, live shrimps and reindeer moss — and for its fermentation laboratory. Noma earned three Michelin stars in 2021. Redzepi announced in 2023 that Noma would close for regular restaurant service at the end of 2024 and transform into "Noma 3.0", a food laboratory, while continuing pop-ups such as those held in Kyoto.

Other work

Redzepi founded the MAD symposium and the non-profit MAD Academy to discuss food and sustainability. His books include Noma: Time and Place in Nordic Cuisine (2010), A Work in Progress (2013), and The Noma Guide to Fermentation (2018, with David Zilber). His restaurants and kitchen culture have also attracted scrutiny and debate over the use of unpaid interns and workplace conditions in fine dining.
