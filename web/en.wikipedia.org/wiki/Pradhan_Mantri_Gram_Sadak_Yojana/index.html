The Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY) is a nationwide programme of the Government of India, launched on 25 December 2000 under Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee, to provide single all-weather road connectivity to unconnected rural habitations. It is implemented by the Ministry of Rural Development through the National Rural Infrastructure Development Agency and state-level agencies, and is widely regarded as one of India's most successful infrastructure programmes.

Design

Under the original programme, habitations with a population of 500 or more in the plains (250 or more in hill states, tribal, and desert areas) were eligible for a paved, all-weather road. Funding initially came from a cess on diesel, supplemented by loans from institutions such as the World Bank and the Asian Development Bank, with costs shared between the central and state governments. PMGSY introduced important technical innovations for rural roads: a standardised design manual, a three-tier quality monitoring system, the use of local materials and plastic-waste-modified bitumen, and an online monitoring system (OMMAS) with GIS-based tracking and citizen feedback through the "Meri Sadak" app.

Phases and impact

Subsequent phases expanded the scheme: PMGSY-II (from 2013) upgraded existing rural road networks, and PMGSY-III (from 2019) targeted the upgrading of about 125,000 km of routes linking villages to markets, schools, and hospitals. By the early 2020s the programme had sanctioned over 800,000 km of roads and connected the overwhelming majority of eligible habitations. Studies credit the roads with improved school attendance, maternal health access, agricultural marketing, and rural employment, and a related scheme, the Pradhan Mantri Gramin Awaas Yojana, applies the same naming pattern to rural housing.
