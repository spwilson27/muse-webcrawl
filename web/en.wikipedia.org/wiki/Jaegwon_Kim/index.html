Jaegwon Kim (Korean: 김재권; 12 September 1934 – 27 November 2019) was a Korean-American philosopher. At the time of his death he was William Herbert Perry Faunce Professor Emeritus at Brown University. Kim is best known for his work on mental causation, the mind–body problem and the metaphysics of supervenience and events.

Life and career

Kim was born in Daegu and studied French literature at Seoul National University before moving to the United States and taking his doctorate in philosophy at Princeton University in 1962. After teaching at Swarthmore College he joined the University of Michigan, where he taught for over two decades, and then Brown University in 1987. He served as president of the American Philosophical Association's Central Division.

Philosophical work

Kim developed an influential event ontology in which events are property-exemplifications by objects at times. He did much to articulate and popularise the concept of supervenience as the standard way of stating non-reductive physicalism, before arguing, in his celebrated exclusion argument (or causal exclusion problem), that non-reductive physicalism cannot make room for genuine mental causation: if a mental property supervenes on a physical base that is causally sufficient for an effect, the mental property appears causally excluded unless it is reducible to the physical.

His books include Supervenience and Mind (1993), Mind in a Physical World (1998), Physicalism, or Something Near Enough (2005) — in which he defended a partly reductive "functional reduction" of most mental states while holding qualia to be irreducible — and Essays in the Metaphysics of Mind (2010). Kim's rigorous defence of reduction reshaped debate in the philosophy of mind, even among the non-reductionists who defined their positions against his arguments.
