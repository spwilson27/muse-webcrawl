Mu'in al-Din Hasan Chishti (1143–1236), popularly known as Muinuddin Chishti and honoured as Gharib Nawaz ("Benefactor of the Poor"), was a Persian Sufi saint and philosopher who introduced and established the Chishti Order of Sufism in the Indian subcontinent. He is one of the most revered figures in South Asian Islam, and his shrine at Ajmer draws millions of pilgrims of all faiths each year.

Life

Muinuddin was born in 1143 in Sistan, in present-day Iran/Afghanistan borderlands. Orphaned young, he distributed his inheritance, embraced a wandering ascetic life, and studied in Bukhara and Samarkand before becoming a disciple of the Chishti master Usman Harooni. After years of travel — including time in Baghdad, Mecca and Medina — he journeyed to India, arriving around 1191–1192, shortly before the establishment of the Delhi Sultanate. Rejecting the centres of political power, he settled in Ajmer in Rajasthan, then a frontier town, where he preached a message of love, charity and devotion open to people of every religion and caste.

Teachings and legacy

Muinuddin taught that the highest form of devotion was service to the poor, the distressed and the hungry, and his hospice at Ajmer fed all comers. His sayings, recorded in later Chishti literature, emphasise generosity "like the river", affection "like the sun" and humility "like the earth". Through his disciples — above all Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki of Delhi and, in turn, Baba Farid of Pakpattan and Nizamuddin Auliya — the Chishtiyya became the most influential Sufi order in medieval India.

Muinuddin died at Ajmer in 1236. His tomb, the Ajmer Sharif Dargah, was patronised by later rulers including the Mughal emperor Akbar, who made pilgrimages there on foot, and remains one of the subcontinent's most visited shrines; its annual Urs festival commemorates his death anniversary.
