Heinrich Luitpold Himmler (7 October 1900 – 23 May 1945) was a German Nazi politician, Reichsführer of the Schutzstaffel (SS), and one of the principal architects of the Holocaust.

Early life

Born in Munich to a middle-class Catholic family, Himmler served briefly at the end of World War I, studied agronomy, and joined the Nazi Party in 1923, participating in the Beer Hall Putsch milieu.

Rise of the SS

Appointed Reichsführer-SS in 1929, he expanded the SS from a small guard into a vast organisation controlling the police, concentration camps, the Waffen-SS, and intelligence. He oversaw the Gestapo after 1934 and, with Reinhard Heydrich, directed the machinery of persecution.

The Holocaust

Himmler supervised the Einsatzgruppen mass shootings, the extermination camp system, and the deportation and murder of six million Jews and millions of others. His Posen speeches of 1943 explicitly described the extermination programme to SS leaders.

Fall and death

In late World War II he briefly commanded Army Group Vistula and attempted unauthorised peace contacts with the Western Allies, for which Hitler expelled him. Captured by British forces in May 1945, he died by suicide using a cyanide capsule on 23 May 1945.
