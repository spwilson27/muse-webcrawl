Coffee in Canada

[Image placeholder: A cup of coffee beside a Tim Hortons cup]

Coffee in Canada is a significant part of Canadian culture and commerce. Canada is consistently ranked among the world's top coffee-consuming countries per capita, and coffee is the most popular hot beverage among Canadian adults, ahead of tea and water in some surveys.

Market

The dominant chain is Tim Hortons, founded in Hamilton, Ontario, in 1964, whose "double-double" (two creams, two sugars) entered the Canadian Oxford Dictionary. Other major chains include Starbucks, Second Cup, McDonald's McCafé and Country Style. Canada imports green coffee beans — chiefly arabica from Brazil, Colombia and Central America — as the climate does not permit domestic cultivation.

Culture

The coffee shop functions as a community meeting place across Canada, and the Tim Hortons "Roll Up the Rim to Win" promotion became a national ritual. A strong specialty and third-wave coffee scene has grown in cities such as Vancouver, Toronto and Montreal.
