The Mariinsky Ballet is the ballet company of the Mariinsky Theatre in Saint Petersburg, Russia. One of the world's leading classical ballet companies, it was founded in the 1740s as the Imperial Ballet and is closely associated with the development of Russian ballet. During the Soviet era it was known outside Russia as the Kirov Ballet, after the Kirov Theatre, the name the Mariinsky carried from 1935 to 1992.

History

Ballet in Russia traces its origins to the imperial court of the 18th century, when a school for dancers was established in Saint Petersburg in 1738. The company grew under a succession of French and Italian ballet masters, most famously Marius Petipa, who served as premier maître de ballet from 1869 and choreographed many of the works that define the classical repertory today.

Petipa's collaborations with composers Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky and Alexander Glazunov produced The Sleeping Beauty (1890), The Nutcracker (1892, with Lev Ivanov), and the definitive 1895 revival of Swan Lake. These works remain cornerstones of the company's repertory.

After the Russian Revolution, the company survived a period of uncertainty and emigration, losing stars such as George Balanchine, who left in 1924. Under Agrippina Vaganova, who directed the company's school in the 1930s, a codified teaching method emerged that still bears her name. The Soviet-era company produced dancers including Galina Ulanova (before her move to Moscow), Natalia Makarova, Rudolf Nureyev, and Mikhail Baryshnikov, the latter three of whom defected to the West.

The company today

The company performs at the historic Mariinsky Theatre (opened 1860) and at the Mariinsky II, a modern second stage opened in 2013. Its dancers are trained primarily at the Vaganova Academy of Russian Ballet. The repertory combines the 19th-century classics with works by Balanchine, Alexei Ratmansky, and contemporary choreographers.
