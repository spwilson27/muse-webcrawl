Once Upon a Time in China (Chinese: 黃飛鴻, Wong Fei-hung) is a 1991 Hong Kong martial arts film written and directed by Tsui Hark and starring Jet Li as the Chinese folk hero Wong Fei-hung. It is the first instalment of the Once Upon a Time in China series and is regarded as one of the landmark films of modern Hong Kong cinema, revitalising the period kung fu genre.

Plot

In late 19th-century Foshan, physician and martial arts master Wong Fei-hung runs the Po Chi Lam clinic and trains the local militia. When a rival martial arts school, corrupt officials, and foreign traders trafficking Chinese labourers to America threaten the community, Wong must confront both the Shaho gang and an American slaver's hired gunmen, culminating in celebrated duels — including a famous ladder fight — against the iron-skinned master "Iron Vest" Yim.

Production

Tsui Hark's Film Workshop produced the film, with action choreography by Yuen Woo-ping's team. The film mixes elaborate wire-assisted fight sequences with a nationalist historical drama about China's humiliation by foreign powers in the Qing dynasty. Its theme music adapts the traditional Chinese melody "General's Mandate" (將軍令), which became inseparably associated with the Wong Fei-hung character.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major box-office success in Hong Kong and won Best Director at the 11th Hong Kong Film Awards. Critics praised its kinetic action, production design, and thematic ambition. It launched a series of sequels — five more films through 1997, with Jet Li returning for most of them — and helped propel both Li and Tsui to international fame. It is frequently cited among the greatest martial arts films ever made.
