The Top 14 (French: Top 14) is the top division of the French rugby union league system, administered by the Ligue Nationale de Rugby (LNR). It is contested by 14 professional clubs from across France and is widely regarded as one of the richest and most competitive domestic rugby union competitions in the world.

The league was rebranded as the Top 14 for the 2005–06 season, having previously been known as the Top 16. The champion is awarded the Bouclier de Brennus (Brennus Shield), one of the oldest trophies in French rugby, first contested in 1892.

History

The French rugby championship dates back to 1892, when Racing Club de France defeated Stade Français in the first final, refereed by Pierre de Coubertin. In the professional era the competition went through several formats before stabilizing at 14 clubs in 2005. The league became famous for its wealthy club owners, star signings from the Southern Hemisphere, and fierce local derbies.

Competition format

The 14 teams play each other home and away during the regular season (26 rounds). The top two teams qualify directly for the semi-finals; teams finishing third to sixth contest barrage (play-off) matches for the remaining semi-final places. The final is held at the Stade de France in Saint-Denis (except when the stadium is unavailable, as in 2024 when it was held in Marseille). The bottom two teams are relegated to the Pro D2, with the 13th-placed team entering a play-off against the Pro D2 runner-up in most seasons.

Top 14 clubs also compete in European competitions organised by EPCR: the European Rugby Champions Cup and the EPCR Challenge Cup.

Notable clubs

Stade Toulousain is the most successful club in the competition's history and also the most successful in European rugby. Other major clubs include ASM Clermont Auvergne, Racing 92, RC Toulon, Stade Rochelais, Montpellier Hérault Rugby, Union Bordeaux Bègles, Stade Français Paris and Castres Olympique.
