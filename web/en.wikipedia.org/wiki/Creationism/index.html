Creationism

Creationism is the religious belief that nature, and aspects such as the universe, Earth, life and humans, originated with supernatural acts of divine creation. In its broadest sense it covers a spectrum of views, from young Earth creationism — which holds that the Earth and life were created directly by God roughly 6,000–10,000 years ago, as a literal reading of the Book of Genesis suggests — to theistic evolution, which accepts the scientific account of evolution and an ancient universe as compatible with faith.

Types

Young Earth creationists interpret Genesis literally, accept a global flood, and reject common descent. Old Earth creationists accept the geological age of the Earth while still positing special creation, sometimes via "day-age" or "gap" readings of Genesis. The intelligent design movement, prominent in the United States from the 1990s, argues that certain features of living things are best explained by an intelligent cause; the scientific community regards it as a form of creationism rather than science.

Science education controversies

Creationism has been at the centre of long-running legal disputes over public-school science teaching in the United States, from the Scopes Trial of 1925 through the Supreme Court's ruling in Edwards v. Aguillard (1987), which struck down "creation science" laws, to Kitzmiller v. Dover (2005), which held that intelligent design is not science and may not be taught as such in public-school science classes.
