Terrace (geology)

In geology, a terrace is a step-like landform: a flat or gently sloping surface (the tread) bounded by a steeper slope (the riser), found above a current floodplain, shoreline or valley floor. Terraces record former levels of a river, lake or sea.

River terraces

Fluvial terraces are remnants of earlier floodplains left perched above the modern river when it incises downward — in response to tectonic uplift, falling base level (e.g. sea-level change), or climate-driven changes in discharge and sediment load. Paired terraces occur at the same height on both valley sides; unpaired terraces form during continuous lateral migration. Strath terraces are cut into bedrock with a thin gravel cap; fill terraces are built of thick alluvium later incised.

Marine terraces and significance

Wave-cut platforms raised above the sea by uplift (or exposed by sea-level fall) form marine terraces, famous along the coasts of California and New Zealand. Because terraces can be dated (by luminescence, cosmogenic nuclides or contained artefacts), they are key archives of landscape evolution, climate change and archaeological settlement.
