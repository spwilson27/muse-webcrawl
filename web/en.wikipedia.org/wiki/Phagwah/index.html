Phagwah

Phagwah is the Bhojpuri and Caribbean name for the Hindu spring festival of Holi, celebrated with particular enthusiasm in the Indo-Caribbean communities of Guyana, Suriname, Trinidad and Tobago, and by their diaspora in the Netherlands, North America and elsewhere. The name derives from the Hindu month of Phalguna, in which the festival falls (usually March).

Celebration

Phagwah marks the arrival of spring and commemorates legends including the story of Holika and Prahlad, celebrating the triumph of good over evil. On the night before, bonfires (Holika Dahan) are lit; on the day itself, participants throw coloured powder and water, called abir, on one another in public festivities. Chowtal folk songs, sung to dholak drums and accompanied by kartal cymbals, are a distinctive musical tradition of the festival in Guyana and Suriname.

Significance

Brought to the Caribbean by indentured labourers from India in the nineteenth century, Phagwah became a major national celebration: in Guyana it is a public holiday attended by people of all backgrounds, and the festivities are widely seen as a symbol of national unity and the renewal of community bonds. In Suriname it is likewise a national holiday, known as Holi-Phagwa.
