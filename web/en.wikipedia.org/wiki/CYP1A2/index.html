CYP1A2

Cytochrome P450 1A2 (CYP1A2) is a member of the cytochrome P450 superfamily of monooxygenase enzymes, encoded in humans by the CYP1A2 gene on chromosome 15. It is expressed mainly in the liver and accounts for roughly 13% of hepatic P450 content.

Function

CYP1A2 metabolizes a variety of xenobiotics and endogenous compounds. It is the principal enzyme responsible for the demethylation of caffeine, which is why caffeine clearance is used as a probe of CYP1A2 activity. Other substrates include theophylline, clozapine, olanzapine, tizanidine, melatonin, and the analgesic phenacetin. It also activates certain procarcinogens, including heterocyclic amines formed in cooked meat and aflatoxin B1.

Regulation and variability

CYP1A2 is inducible through the aryl hydrocarbon receptor: tobacco smoke, cruciferous vegetables, and char-grilled food increase its activity, while drugs such as fluvoxamine and ciprofloxacin inhibit it. Activity varies widely between individuals because of genetic polymorphisms, diet, smoking status, and hormonal factors, which has practical consequences for dosing of narrow-therapeutic-index substrates such as clozapine and theophylline.
