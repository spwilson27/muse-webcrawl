Chloroprene

IUPAC name: 2-chlorobuta-1,3-diene

Formula: C4H5Cl

Appearance: Colourless liquid

Use: Monomer for neoprene (polychloroprene)

Chloroprene (IUPAC name 2-chlorobuta-1,3-diene) is an organic compound with the formula CH2=CCl–CH=CH2. It is a colourless, volatile liquid produced almost exclusively as the monomer for polychloroprene, the synthetic rubber sold under the name neoprene.

Production

Chloroprene is manufactured industrially from butadiene: butadiene is chlorinated to a mixture of dichlorobutenes, which is then isomerised and dehydrochlorinated with base to yield chloroprene. An older process based on acetylene (the Nieuwland synthesis) was developed at DuPont in the early 1930s, where chloroprene's polymerisation was investigated by Wallace Carothers' group and commercialised as one of the first successful synthetic rubbers.

Properties and hazards

Chloroprene polymerises readily — which is the basis of neoprene manufacture by emulsion polymerisation — so commercial material is stabilised with inhibitors. The monomer is flammable and a respiratory irritant; it is classified as reasonably anticipated to be a human carcinogen, and occupational exposure is regulated. Environmental releases near production plants have attracted regulatory and public-health attention.
