Topkapı Palace

Topkapı Palace
Location: Istanbul, Turkey
Built: from 1459, under Mehmed II
Function: Ottoman sultans' residence until mid-19th c.
Now: Museum; UNESCO World Heritage Site

The Topkapı Palace (Turkish: Topkapı Sarayı, 'Cannon Gate Palace') in Istanbul is a large museum and former residence of the Ottoman sultans for nearly 400 years, from the 1460s to the completion of Dolmabahçe Palace in 1856.

Layout

Commissioned by Mehmed the Conqueror after the 1453 conquest of Constantinople, the palace is a sprawling complex of four courtyards behind the Imperial Gate, overlooking the Golden Horn and Bosphorus. It housed up to 4,000 people: the Imperial Council (Divan), treasury, kitchens, stables, the palace school (Enderun), and the Harem.

Collections

Highlights include the Spoonmaker's Diamond, the Topkapı Dagger, Ottoman miniatures and porcelain, and the Chamber of Sacred Relics holding items attributed to the Prophet Muhammad. The palace became a museum in 1924 by order of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk. See Ottoman Empire and Hagia Sophia.
