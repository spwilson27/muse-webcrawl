The Bullock Texas State History Museum (often referred to as The Story of Texas) is a history museum in downtown Austin, Texas, dedicated to interpreting the continually unfolding "Story of Texas" for visitors. It is located a few blocks north of the Texas State Capitol at 1800 Congress Avenue.

The museum is named in honor of Bob Bullock, the 38th Lieutenant Governor of Texas, who championed its creation before his death in 1999. It opened to the public on April 21, 2001 — San Jacinto Day, the anniversary of the decisive battle of the Texas Revolution.

History

The Texas State History Museum was authorized by the Texas Legislature in the 1990s as a project of the State Preservation Board. Lieutenant Governor Bob Bullock was the driving political force behind the museum and regarded it as a capstone of his long career in Texas public life. The building was designed with a large rotunda and is fronted by a 35-foot-tall bronze Lone Star sculpture, which has become one of the most photographed landmarks in Austin.

Exhibitions

The museum's three floors of permanent exhibits trace Texas history from the earliest Native American inhabitants through Spanish and French exploration, Mexican Texas, the Republic of Texas, statehood, the Civil War, the cattle and oil eras, and into the modern period. Signature artifacts have included the hull of La Belle, the ship of French explorer René-Robert Cavelier, Sieur de La Salle, which sank in Matagorda Bay in 1686 and was excavated by the Texas Historical Commission in the 1990s.

The museum also houses an IMAX theatre — for many years the largest in Texas — and the Texas Spirit Theater, a special-effects multimedia venue. Rotating temporary exhibitions supplement the permanent galleries.
