EUMETSAT

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Meteosat Third Generation satellite, operated by EUMETSAT.

EUMETSAT (the European Organisation for the Exploitation of Meteorological Satellites) is an intergovernmental organisation that operates Europe's weather and climate-monitoring satellites. Founded in 1986 and headquartered in Darmstadt, Germany, it has 30 European member states. Its purpose is to supply weather and climate-related satellite data, images and products around the clock to the national meteorological services of its members and to other users worldwide.

Satellites

EUMETSAT inherited the Meteosat programme of geostationary weather satellites begun by the European Space Agency (ESA) in 1977. The Meteosat Second Generation satellites (MSG, from 2002) provide imagery of Europe, Africa and the Atlantic every 15 minutes; the first Meteosat Third Generation (MTG) satellite, with faster imaging and a lightning imager, was launched in December 2022.

In polar (low Earth) orbit, EUMETSAT operates the Metop series — Metop-B (2012) and Metop-C (2018) still in service — which provide global temperature and humidity soundings, wind and ocean data as the European contribution to the joint polar system with the US agency NOAA. EUMETSAT also operates the Jason-3 and Sentinel-6 Michael Freilich ocean altimetry missions in cooperation with ESA, the European Commission, CNES and NASA/NOAA, and distributes data from the EU Copernicus Sentinel satellites relevant to the atmosphere and oceans.

Activities

From Darmstadt, EUMETSAT controls its fleets and disseminates data in near-real time via the EUMETCast satellite broadcast system and online services. Its data underpin numerical weather prediction at centres such as the ECMWF and the national weather services, nowcasting of severe storms, and long-term climate data records. The organisation is funded by member-state contributions proportional to gross national income.
