Abraham Isaac Kook

Born7 September 1865, Grīva, Russian Empire (now Latvia)
Died1 September 1935, Jerusalem, Mandatory Palestine
TitleChief Rabbi of Jerusalem; first Ashkenazi Chief Rabbi of Mandatory Palestine (1921–1935)
Known forReligious Zionist philosophy; founder of Mercaz HaRav yeshiva

Abraham Isaac Kook (7 September 1865 – 1 September 1935), often known as Rav Kook, was an Orthodox rabbi, Jewish theologian, and kabbalist who became the first Ashkenazi Chief Rabbi of Mandatory Palestine. He is regarded as the founding thinker of religious Zionism.

Early life and career

Born in Grīva, Latvia, Kook was a child prodigy who studied at the Volozhin Yeshiva and served as rabbi of Žeimelis and Bauska. In 1904 he emigrated to Ottoman Palestine as rabbi of Jaffa, where he engaged with the largely secular pioneers of the Second Aliyah with unusual warmth and openness.

Chief Rabbi and philosophy

After the First World War, Kook became Chief Rabbi of Jerusalem and, in 1921, the first Ashkenazi Chief Rabbi under the British Mandate. In 1924 he founded the Mercaz HaRav yeshiva in Jerusalem. His philosophy held that even secular Jewish settlement of the Land of Israel carried redemptive, messianic significance, and that the sacred and the profane, religious and secular Jews, would ultimately be reconciled in a higher unity — a dialectic developed in works such as Orot ("Lights").

Legacy

Rav Kook's writings, much of them published posthumously by his son Zvi Yehuda Kook, became the ideological foundation of religious Zionism and of the Gush Emunim settlement movement. The moshav Kfar Haroeh and numerous schools and streets in Israel bear his name.
