Overview

Shenandoah National Park is a US national park in the Blue Ridge Mountains of Virginia, a long, narrow park straddling the crest of the ridge. Its spine is the 105-mile Skyline Drive, built by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s, famed for autumn colour. The park was authorised in 1926 and established in 1935, on land assembled by the Commonwealth of Virginia — a creation that displaced hundreds of mountain families, a history the park now interprets directly.

Features

Old Rag Mountain is the park's most famous (and strenuous) summit scramble; Dark Hollow Falls and Big Meadows are other landmarks. The Appalachian Trail runs the length of the park, roughly paralleling Skyline Drive. Black bears, white-tailed deer and over 190 bird species inhabit its recovering eastern hardwood forest.
