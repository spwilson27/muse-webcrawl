Osman Yusuf Kenadid

Osman Yusuf Kenadid
Somali: Cusmaan Yuusuf Keenadiid
Born: 1889, Sultanate of Hobyo
Died: 14 August 1972, Mogadishu, Somalia
Known for: Inventing the Osmanya script

Osman Yusuf Kenadid (Somali: Cusmaan Yuusuf Keenadiid; 1889 – 14 August 1972) was a Somali poet, writer, teacher and ruler. He is principally remembered as the inventor of the Osmanya script, one of the principal writing systems developed for transcribing the Somali language.

Biography

Kenadid was born into the royal family of the Sultanate of Hobyo in northeastern Somalia. He was the son of Sultan Yusuf Ali Kenadid, the founder of the sultanate, and the brother of Ali Yusuf Kenadid, its second ruler. He received a broad education and became known as a scholar of Somali language, history and poetry.

The Osmanya script

Frustrated by the lack of a standard orthography for Somali — which was variously written in Arabic script or not at all — Kenadid devised a new phonetic alphabet after hearing of a similar endeavour by the Ethiopian emperor Menelik II. He began work on the Osmanya script around 1920–1922. The script is written left-to-right and contains distinct characters for the consonants and long and short vowels of Somali, designed to represent the language's phonology precisely.

The Osmanya script (also known as Far Soomaali, "Somali writing") gained considerable popularity in the sultanates of Hobyo and Majeerteen, where it was used in official correspondence and taught in schools. It competed in the following decades with other proposed scripts, including the Borama/Gadabuursi script and the Kaddare script, as well as the Arabic and Latin alphabets.

Later life and legacy

Although the Osmanya script was widely used for decades, in 1972 the government of Siad Barre adopted a modified Latin alphabet, devised by the linguist Shire Jama Ahmed, as the official orthography of Somali. Kenadid died in Mogadishu on 14 August 1972, the same year the Latin script was made official.

Kenadid also authored lexicographical and historical works, including a dictionary project for Somali. The Osmanya script was added to the Unicode Standard in 2003 (version 4.0), ensuring its preservation in digital form, and it remains a symbol of Somali cultural identity.
