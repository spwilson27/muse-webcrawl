The Battle of Ramillies was fought on 23 May 1706 during the War of the Spanish Succession. A Grand Alliance army under John Churchill, 1st Duke of Marlborough, decisively defeated a Franco-Bavarian army commanded by the Duke of Villeroi near the village of Ramillies in the Spanish Netherlands. The victory collapsed French control of the Spanish Netherlands: within weeks, Brussels, Antwerp, Ghent, Bruges and other major towns fell to the Allies.

Background

After the victory at Blenheim in 1704 and a frustrated campaign in 1705, Marlborough sought a decisive battle in the Low Countries. Louis XIV, eager to avenge earlier defeats, ordered Villeroi to take the offensive; the two armies met on the plateau between the Mehaigne and the Little Gete rivers.

The battle

Marlborough feinted against the French left, drawing Villeroi's reserves northward, then shifted troops by concealed march to mass against the French right around Ramillies and Taviers. A huge cavalry fight on the open plain south of Ramillies ended in Allied success; Allied infantry stormed Ramillies village, and the French line collapsed into a rout. The pursuit continued into the night.

Aftermath

Ramillies ranks among Marlborough's masterpieces and is sometimes compared with Blenheim. The French lost the Spanish Netherlands in a single campaign season, shifting the strategic balance of the war.
