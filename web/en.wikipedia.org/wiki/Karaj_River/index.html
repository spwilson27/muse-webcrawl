Karaj River

The Karaj River (Persian: رود کرج, Rud-e Karaj) is a river in northern Iran that flows from the Alborz Mountains through Alborz Province, passing the city of Karaj, before continuing southward towards the desert basin south of Tehran. It is an important source of drinking water and hydroelectric power for the Tehran–Karaj metropolitan area.

Course

The river rises on the southern slopes of the Alborz range, in the high valleys north-west of Tehran. It flows south-west through a deep mountain valley along the route of the Chalus Road, one of the principal highways linking Tehran with the Caspian coast. The Amir Kabir Dam (Karaj Dam), completed in 1961, impounds the river in this valley, creating a reservoir used for water supply, flood control and electricity generation.

Below the dam the river passes through the city of Karaj, where it is used for irrigation of the surrounding orchards and farmland. Further downstream, the river continues across the plain and eventually dissipates in the salt desert region south of Tehran, its waters largely consumed by irrigation and groundwater recharge.

Importance

Together with the Jajrud, Lar and Latyan reservoirs, the Karaj River system supplies a large share of the drinking water of greater Tehran, one of the largest metropolitan areas in Western Asia. The river valley is also a popular recreational area, with villages and restaurants along the Chalus Road attracting weekend visitors from the capital.
