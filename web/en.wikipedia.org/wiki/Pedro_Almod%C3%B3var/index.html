Pedro Almodóvar Caballero (born 1949) is a Spanish film director, screenwriter and producer, and one of the most internationally celebrated filmmakers to emerge from Spain since Luis Buñuel. Emerging from Madrid's countercultural movida madrileña after the death of Franco, he became known for vibrant melodramas mixing comedy, desire, strong female characters, and sympathetic portrayals of LGBTQ lives.

Career

After working for the telephone company Telefónica and shooting Super 8 shorts, Almodóvar made his feature debut with Pepi, Luci, Bom (1980). With his brother Agustín he founded the production company El Deseo in 1985. His international breakthrough came with Women on the Verge of a Nervous Breakdown (1988). All About My Mother (1999) won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film, and Talk to Her (2002) won the Oscar for Best Original Screenplay. Later works include Volver (2006), The Skin I Live In (2011) and the autobiographical Pain and Glory (2019), starring Antonio Banderas and Penélope Cruz, two of his recurring collaborators.

Style

Almodóvar's films are marked by bold color design, interwoven narratives, melodrama in the tradition of Douglas Sirk, and themes of family, identity, memory and desire. He frequently writes complex roles for women, and his work has been credited with redefining Spanish cinema for the democratic era.
