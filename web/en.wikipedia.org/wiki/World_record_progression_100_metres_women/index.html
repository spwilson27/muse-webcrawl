The first world record in the 100 metres for women was recognised by the Fédération Sportive Féminine Internationale (FSFI) in 1922. The current record of 10.49 seconds was set by Florence Griffith Joyner of the United States on 16 July 1988 at the US Olympic trials in Indianapolis, and it remains unbeaten more than three decades later.

Early history

Early records were hand-timed. The first recognised mark was 12.8 seconds by Marie Mejzlíková in 1922. Times fell steadily through the hand-timed era, reaching 11.0 seconds by Wyomia Tyus and others in the 1960s. Fully automatic timing became standard in the 1970s, with Annegret Richter (11.01, 1976) and Marlies Göhr (10.88 in 1982, then 10.81 in 1983) among the record holders.

Griffith Joyner's records

Evelyn Ashford set records of 10.79 (1983) and 10.76 (1984). Griffith Joyner's 10.49 in 1988 improved the mark dramatically; her 10.61 and 10.62 runs later that season are the next fastest wind-legal times of that year. Subsequent close performances include Shelly-Ann Fraser-Pryce's and Elaine Thompson-Herah's 10.54 in 2021.
