Bumin Qaghan

Bumin Qaghan (Old Turkic: 𐰉𐰆𐰢𐰣 𐰴𐰍𐰣‎; Chinese: 土門可汗, Tumen Khan; died 552) was the founder of the Turkic Khaganate, the first great empire of the Turks. A chieftain of the Ashina clan of the Turks, who were then vassals and iron-workers of the Rouran Khaganate, Bumin rose to unite the steppe peoples of the Altai region.

Rise

Chinese sources describe the Turks (Tujue) as blacksmith servants of the Rouran. In 546 Bumin suppressed a revolt of Tiele tribesmen against the Rouran and, expecting reward, asked for the hand of a Rouran princess. The khagan Anagui insultingly refused, calling him a "blacksmith slave". Bumin instead married Princess Changle of Western Wei in 551, gaining Chinese backing, and in 552 he defeated Anagui at Huaihuang, north of the Gobi; Anagui committed suicide.

Khaganate

After his victory Bumin took the title Illig Qaghan ("ruler of the people") and established his court in the Ötüken forest region. He died within the year, in 552. His realm was divided between his son Mugan Qaghan, who ruled the east, and his brother Istämi, who received the west as yabgu and expanded Turkic power to the Oxus and into relations with Sasanian Persia and Byzantium. The empire his descendants ruled stretched, at its height, from Manchuria to the Black Sea steppe.
