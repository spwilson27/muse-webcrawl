Taqsim

Taqsim (Arabic: تقسيم; Turkish: taksim) is a melodic musical improvisation in Arabic, Turkish, Persian and related Middle Eastern traditions. Usually performed solo, a taqsim unfolds freely without a fixed metre, exploring a maqam (melodic mode) and often modulating through several related maqamat before returning to the starting mode.

Function and form

A taqsim may open a performance or a composed suite (such as the Arabic wasla or Turkish fasıl), serving to establish the maqam in the listener's ear, or stand alone as a display of the performer's artistry. Typical instruments include the oud, qanun, ney, violin, clarinet and tanbur. The improvisation conventionally begins in the lower register of the mode, ascends through its characteristic phrases and melodic "seeds", reaches a climax in the upper register, and descends to a cadential close (qafla).

Related forms

The vocal counterpart of the taqsim is the mawwal or layali, and in Persian music the improvised avaz occupies an analogous role within the dastgah system. The Turkish spelling taksim also gave its name, unrelated directly to the musical form, to Taksim Square in Istanbul.
