Healthcare in India

Healthcare in India is delivered through a mixed system of public and private providers. The Constitution of India makes health primarily a state responsibility, and the country operates a network of primary health centres, community health centres, district hospitals and tertiary teaching hospitals, alongside a large and growing private sector that provides the majority of outpatient care.

Structure

The public system is organised in tiers: sub-centres and primary health centres at village level, community health centres at block level, and district hospitals above them. Centrally run institutions include the All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS) network. National programmes address tuberculosis, malaria, HIV/AIDS, immunisation and maternal and child health.

Financing

India spends roughly 3–4% of GDP on health, with a historically high share of out-of-pocket expenditure by households. The Ayushman Bharat scheme, launched in 2018, introduced health-protection coverage of ₹5 lakh per family per year for hospitalisation for economically vulnerable households, and a network of Health and Wellness Centres for primary care.

Challenges

Key challenges include shortages of doctors and nurses in rural areas, uneven quality, the dual burden of communicable and non-communicable diseases, and high costs in private hospitals. India is also a major destination for medical tourism and the world's largest producer of generic medicines.
