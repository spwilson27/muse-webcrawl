Canadians (French: Canadiens) are people identified with the country of Canada. This connection may be residential, legal, historical or cultural. For most Canadians, many (or all) of these connections exist and are collectively the source of their being Canadian.

Canada is a multilingual and multicultural society home to people of groups of many different ethnic, religious, and national origins, with the majority of the population made up of Old World immigrants and their descendants. Following the initial period of French and then British colonization, different waves (or peaks) of immigration and settlement of non-indigenous peoples took place over the course of nearly two centuries and continue today. Elements of Indigenous, French, British, and more recent immigrant customs, languages, and religions have combined to form the culture of Canada, and thus a Canadian identity.

Terminology

The word Canadian originally applied to the French settlers of New France — the Canadiens — and was gradually extended to British settlers and then to all inhabitants and citizens of the country after Confederation in 1867. Citizenship is governed by the Citizenship Act, under which a Canadian citizen is a national of Canada.

Population

As of 2024, Canada's population is estimated at roughly 41.5 million, swelled by historically high levels of immigration in the early 2020s. About three million Canadians live abroad, most notably in the United States. The largest cities are Toronto, Montreal, Vancouver, Calgary and Ottawa–Gatineau.

Ethnic origins

According to census responses, the most commonly reported ethnic or cultural origins include "Canadian" itself (the single most common response), followed by English, Scottish, French, Irish, German, Chinese and Indian origins, among many others. Indigenous peoples — First Nations, Métis and Inuit — made up about 5 percent of the population in the 2021 census.

Language and identity

English and French are the official languages of Canada, and bilingualism is promoted under the Official Languages Act. Canadian identity is often discussed in terms of multiculturalism (a federal policy adopted in 1971), the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, and distinctions from the neighbouring United States.
