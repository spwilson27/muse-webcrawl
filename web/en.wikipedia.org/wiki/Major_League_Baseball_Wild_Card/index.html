Major League Baseball Wild Card

In Major League Baseball (MLB), the wild card is a playoff berth awarded to teams that qualify for the postseason without winning their division. The wild card was introduced in 1994 when MLB realigned into three divisions per league, so that the best non-division-winner in each league would join the three division champions in the playoffs. Since the 2022 season, three wild card teams per league qualify, and the two lower-seeded wild card teams and the lowest-seeded division champion contest a best-of-three Wild Card Series.


The bracket of the MLB postseason showing the Wild Card Series.

History

The wild card was approved as part of the 1993 expansion-era realignment for the 1994 season, but the players' strike wiped out that year's postseason; the first wild card teams therefore played in 1995. The single wild card in each league opened the Division Series round alongside the three division winners. The format was expanded in 2012, when a second wild card per league was added and the two wild cards met in a single-game Wild Card Game playoff; the one-game format produced celebrated moments such as the 2014 American League game between Kansas City and Oakland, but was also criticised for deciding a season on one game.

Under the collective bargaining agreement implemented for 2022, the field grew to twelve teams: in each league, the three division champions and three wild cards. The top two division champions receive byes to the Division Series, while the remaining teams play best-of-three Wild Card Series, all hosted by the higher seed.

Wild card champions

Wild card teams have repeatedly won the World Series. The first was the 1997 Florida Marlins; others include the 2002 Anaheim Angels, the 2003 Florida Marlins, the 2004 Boston Red Sox (whose comeback in the 2004 ALCS ended the "Curse of the Bambino"), the 2011 St. Louis Cardinals, the 2014 San Francisco Giants, the 2019 Washington Nationals, and the 2023 Texas Rangers. The 2014 World Series between San Francisco and Kansas City was the first contested by two wild card teams.

Strategy and debate

The wild card keeps more teams — and their fans — in contention late in the season, and supports September pennant races between teams that are not division leaders. Critics argue that expanding the field dilutes the value of the 162-game regular season and of winning a division, a debate renewed with each expansion of the format.
