Bharhut

[Image placeholder: Bharhut stupa railing sculpture]

Bharhut is a village in the Satna district of Madhya Pradesh, India, famed for the remains of a Buddhist stupa dating mainly to the 2nd century BCE, during the Shunga period. The stupa's carved stone railing and gateway are among the earliest surviving examples of Indian Buddhist art.

The site was excavated by Alexander Cunningham in 1873. The red sandstone railings are covered with medallions depicting Jataka tales — stories of the Buddha's previous lives — many helpfully labelled with inscriptions in Brahmi script. Notably, the Buddha is represented aniconically, by symbols such as the empty throne, footprints and the Bodhi tree.

Collections

Most of the railing and one gateway are now housed in the Indian Museum, Kolkata, while other pieces are in the Allahabad Museum and collections abroad. The inscriptions record donations by lay devotees from across central India.
