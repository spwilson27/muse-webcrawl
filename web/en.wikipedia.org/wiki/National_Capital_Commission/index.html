National Capital Commission

National Capital Commission (NCC)
French: Commission de la capitale nationale (CCN)
Formed: 1959 (successor to the Federal District Commission, 1927)
Type: Crown corporation
Headquarters: Ottawa, Ontario, Canada
Responsibility: Planning and stewardship of federal lands in the National Capital Region

The National Capital Commission (NCC; French: Commission de la capitale nationale, CCN) is a Canadian Crown corporation responsible for planning, development, conservation, and improvement of Canada's National Capital Region—the federal lands in and around Ottawa, Ontario, and Gatineau, Quebec—on behalf of the Government of Canada.

History

The NCC was created by the National Capital Act of 1958 (in force 1959), succeeding the Federal District Commission (1927–1959) and, before it, the Ottawa Improvement Commission (1899). It implemented much of the 1950 Gréber Plan prepared by French planner Jacques Gréber, which reshaped the capital by removing downtown railway tracks, creating scenic parkways, and assembling the Greenbelt.

Responsibilities

The NCC manages roughly 10 percent of the land in the National Capital Region, including Gatineau Park (about 361 km²), the Greenbelt surrounding Ottawa, scenic parkways, the Rideau Canal corridor lands, pathways, and major parks. It maintains six official residences, including Rideau Hall and 24 Sussex Drive, and organizes signature national events such as Winterlude and the Canada Day celebrations on Parliament Hill, as well as the Rideau Canal Skateway, the world's largest natural ice rink. As the federal planning authority for the region, it approves land use and design for federal projects.

Governance and criticism

The NCC is governed by a board of directors appointed by the federal government and reports to Parliament through the responsible minister. Its appointed (rather than elected) control over significant land in two cities has occasionally attracted criticism from municipal governments, although reforms have increased public consultation and board openness.
