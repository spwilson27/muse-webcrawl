Olympisch Stadion (Amsterdam)

LocationAmsterdam, Netherlands
ArchitectJan Wils
Opened17 May 1928
Capacity22,288 (current)

The Olympic Stadium (Dutch: Olympisch Stadion) is the main stadium for the 1928 Summer Olympics in Amsterdam, Netherlands. It was designed by the architect Jan Wils in the style of the Amsterdam School, and is famous for its Marathon Tower, from which the Olympic flame burned during the Games — the first time the flame was lit in a stadium at the modern Olympics.

History

When the stadium was finished in 1928, it had a capacity of 31,600. After the Second World War, a second ring was added, increasing capacity to about 64,000. For decades it was the home ground of AFC Ajax, who played their bigger matches there until the Amsterdam Arena (now the Johan Cruyff Arena) opened in 1996.

Between 1996 and 2000 the stadium was renovated: the second ring added after the war was removed, restoring much of Jan Wils's original design, and capacity was reduced to 22,288. The stadium is a national monument (rijksmonument) and today hosts athletics events, including the annual Amsterdam Marathon, cultural events, and offices and apartments in its surrounding development.
