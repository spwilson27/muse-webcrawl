Rudolf E. Kálmán

Rudolf Emil Kálmán
Born: 19 May 1930, Budapest, Hungary
Died: 2 July 2016, Gainesville, Florida, USA
Fields: Electrical engineering, mathematics, control theory
Known for: Kalman filter; controllability and observability

Rudolf Emil Kálmán (1930–2016) was a Hungarian-American electrical engineer and mathematician, best known as the co-inventor of the Kalman filter, one of the most widely used algorithms in control systems, navigation and signal processing.

Life and career

Born in Budapest, Kálmán emigrated with his family to the United States in 1943. He studied electrical engineering at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (BS 1953, MS 1954) and earned his doctorate at Columbia University in 1957. He worked at the Research Institute for Advanced Studies in Baltimore and later held professorships at Stanford University and the University of Florida, as well as a chair at ETH Zurich.

Contributions

In 1960 Kálmán published his famous paper describing a recursive solution to the discrete-data linear filtering problem — the Kalman filter — which estimates the state of a dynamic system from noisy measurements. The filter became essential to the Apollo space programme's navigation computers and is now ubiquitous in applications from GPS receivers and autopilots to econometrics and computer vision. With co-workers he also developed the modern state-space theory of control, including the foundational concepts of controllability and observability, and the Kalman decomposition.

Honours

Kálmán received the IEEE Medal of Honor (1974), the Kyoto Prize (1985), the Charles Stark Draper Prize (2008) and the US National Medal of Science (2009).
