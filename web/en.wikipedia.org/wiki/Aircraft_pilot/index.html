Aircraft pilot

An aircraft pilot or aviator is a person who controls the flight of an aircraft by operating its directional flight controls. Some other aircrew members, such as navigators or flight engineers, are also considered aviators because they are involved in operating the aircraft's navigation and engine systems.

Licensing

Pilots are licensed by national aviation authorities under standards harmonized by the International Civil Aviation Organization. The main categories progress from student and private pilot licences, through the commercial pilot licence, to the airline transport pilot licence, with additional ratings for instruments, multi-engine aircraft, night flying, and specific aircraft types. Pilots must also pass periodic medical examinations appropriate to their licence level.

Roles

In airline operations the flight crew consists of a captain (pilot in command) and a first officer, sharing flying and monitoring duties. Pilots also work in cargo, charter, agricultural, aerial survey, flight instruction, military, and test-flying roles. The profession is notable for its standardized phraseology, checklists, and safety culture.
