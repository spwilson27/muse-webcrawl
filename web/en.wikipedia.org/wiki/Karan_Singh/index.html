Karan Singh

Karan Singh (born 9 March 1931) is an Indian politician, philosopher and author. The son of Maharaja Hari Singh, the last ruling Maharaja of the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir, he served as regent of the state from 1949 to 1952, its elected head of state (Sadr-i-Riyasat) from 1952 to 1965, and its Governor from 1965 to 1967. He was the first and only person to hold these positions in the post-independence state.

Early life and role in Jammu and Kashmir

Karan Singh was born in Cannes, France, and educated at the Doon School and in Kashmir, later studying at Delhi University, where he earned a doctorate with a thesis on the political thought of Sri Aurobindo. Appointed regent by his father in June 1949 as the Maharaja withdrew from active rule amid the upheavals of Partition and the first Kashmir war, he signed the proclamation convening the state's Constituent Assembly. In 1952 he was elected Sadr-i-Riyasat by the state legislature — the youngest head of a state in India — and held the office until the post was replaced by that of Governor in 1965.

National political career

In 1967 he entered the Union Cabinet of Indira Gandhi as Minister for Tourism and Civil Aviation, then among the youngest cabinet ministers in India, and later held the Health and Family Planning and Education portfolios. He was elected repeatedly to the Lok Sabha from Udhampur and later served in the Rajya Sabha. From 1989 to 1990 he was India's Ambassador to the United States. A senior Congress figure for decades, he resigned from the party in 2022.

Scholarship and public life

An author of numerous books on religion, philosophy and politics — including Heir Apparent, his autobiography, and works on Hinduism and the Shiva Sutras — he has chaired cultural and educational bodies, served as Chancellor of Banaras Hindu University and Jammu University, and been closely involved with the India International Centre and the Dharmarth Trust. In 2005 he was awarded the Padma Vibhushan, India's second-highest civilian honour.
