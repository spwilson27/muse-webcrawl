Charles Hartshorne (5 June 1897 – 9 October 2000) was an American philosopher who concentrated primarily on the philosophy of religion and metaphysics. He developed Alfred North Whitehead's process philosophy into what he called neoclassical metaphysics, and is regarded, together with Whitehead, as a founder of process theology. He also made contributions to ornithology, notably in the study of birdsong.

Life

Hartshorne studied at Harvard, earning his doctorate in 1923, and spent two years in Europe studying with Edmund Husserl and Martin Heidegger. He worked as an assistant editor on the collected papers of Charles Sanders Peirce, taught at the University of Chicago (1928–1955) and Emory University, and finished his career at the University of Texas at Austin. He published into his hundredth year and died aged 103.

Philosophy

Hartshorne defended panentheism — the view that the world is in God, though God is more than the world — and a "dipolar" conception of God: God is both eternal and unchanging in abstract character, and temporal and changing in concrete experience, so that God genuinely knows and is affected by the world (divine passibility). He developed a modal version of the ontological argument for God's existence, building on Anselm, and argued that God's existence is either necessary or impossible, not merely contingent.

His psychological relativity and doctrine of creative experience influenced generations of process theologians, including John B. Cobb Jr. and David Ray Griffin.
