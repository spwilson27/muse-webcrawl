Dioscorides

Pedanius Dioscorides (c. 40 – 90 AD) was a Greek physician, pharmacologist, and botanist in the service of the Roman army, and the author of De materia medica, a five-volume encyclopedia of herbal medicine and related substances that remained the supreme authority on the subject for some 1,500 years.

Born in Anazarbus in Cilicia, Dioscorides travelled widely with the legions, observing plants and drugs in their native habitats across the Mediterranean world. De materia medica, written in Greek around 50–70 AD, describes roughly 600 plants, as well as animal products and minerals, with their preparations, effects, and tests for adulteration. It was translated into Latin, Syriac, and Arabic, copied and re-copied through the Middle Ages — the lavish illustrated Vienna Dioscurides manuscript of about 512 AD is a famous survival — and first printed in 1478.

Dioscorides is regarded as the father of pharmacognosy. Unlike earlier writers he organised his remedies by their properties rather than alphabetically, and he emphasised first-hand observation. The botanical genus Dioscorea (yams) is named after him by Linnaeus in his honour.
