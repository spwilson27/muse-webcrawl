Overview

The Universal Transverse Mercator (UTM) system is a global grid of flat, metric coordinates built for land mapping and military use. It divides Earth between 80°S and 84°N into 60 zones, each 6° of longitude wide, and maps each zone with its own transverse Mercator projection — the cylinder turned sideways so distortion stays tiny within the zone.

Positions are given as a zone number plus an easting and northing in metres from defined false origins, which makes distances and areas on the ground readable straight from the numbers — the reason soldiers, surveyors and rescue services quote UTM. Near the poles, where the zones converge uselessly, the companion Universal Polar Stereographic system takes over.
