Nazi architecture

Nazi architecture is the architecture promoted by Adolf Hitler and the Nazi regime from 1933 until its fall in 1945. Characterised by monumental scale, stripped classicism and the deliberate evocation of imperial Rome, it served as an instrument of propaganda intended to express the power and permanence of the Third Reich.

Style and ideology

The favoured idiom drew on neoclassicism as filtered through architects such as Paul Ludwig Troost and, after 1937, Albert Speer, Hitler's chief architect and planner for the rebuilding of Berlin as the world capital "Germania". Features included vast axial boulevards, stone-clad facades, colonnades and enormous assembly grounds such as the Nazi party rally grounds in Nuremberg.

Legacy

Most of the grandest schemes were never built or were destroyed in the war. Surviving structures, including the Olympiastadion in Berlin and parts of the Nuremberg rally grounds, remain subjects of debate over preservation and interpretation.
