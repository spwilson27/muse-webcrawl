Advanced metering infrastructure

Image placeholder: a smart meter, the endpoint of an AMI system

Advanced metering infrastructure (AMI) is an integrated system of smart meters, communication networks and data management systems that enables two-way communication between utilities and customers. Unlike earlier automatic meter reading (AMR), which collected consumption data in one direction, AMI meters record usage at frequent intervals — often every 15 to 60 minutes — and transmit it to the utility, while utilities can send pricing signals, outage information and remote connect/disconnect commands back to the meter.

Components

An AMI deployment typically comprises smart meters at customer premises; a neighbourhood or wide-area communications network using radio mesh, power-line carrier or cellular links; data concentrators and a head-end system; and a meter data management system that validates, stores and passes interval data to billing, outage management and customer information systems.

Benefits

Utilities cite elimination of manual meter reading, faster outage detection and restoration verification, support for time-of-use and dynamic tariffs, improved load forecasting, detection of electricity theft, and better integration of rooftop solar and electric vehicles. Customers may gain access to detailed consumption data through web portals and in-home displays.

Criticism

AMI rollouts have been criticised over cost recovery from ratepayers, privacy risks from fine-grained consumption data (which can reveal household behaviour), cybersecurity of the meter networks, and disputed claims about radio-frequency health effects. Several national programmes, including those in Great Britain, the United States, Australia and the European Union, proceeded despite these debates.
