Mathieu function

Mathieu functions ce0, ce1, se1 and se2 for a typical parameter value.

In mathematics, Mathieu functions, sometimes called angular Mathieu functions, are solutions of Mathieu's differential equation,

d²y/dx² + (a − 2q cos 2x) y = 0,

where a is the characteristic value and q is a parameter. They were first introduced by Émile Léonard Mathieu (1835–1890), who encountered them in his 1868 work on vibrating elliptical drumheads: the vibrations of an elliptical membrane separate in elliptic coordinates into an angular equation of this form and a companion radial equation whose solutions are the (radial) Mathieu functions.

Periodic solutions and characteristic values

Only for special values of a (the characteristic values an(q) and bn(q)) does the equation have periodic solutions of period π or 2π. The even solutions are denoted cen(x, q) (cosine-elliptic) and the odd ones sen(x, q) (sine-elliptic); when q = 0 they reduce to cos(nx) and sin(nx). Because the equation is of Sturm–Liouville type, the periodic solutions form orthogonal families. Floquet theory governs the stability of solutions for general a and q, with bands of stable and unstable parameter regions.

Applications

Mathieu functions arise in problems with elliptical or periodic structure: the vibration of elliptical membranes, the motion of a particle in a periodic potential and the Paul ion trap (whose equations of motion are Mathieu equations), parametric resonance and the inverted pendulum stabilized by vibration, wave propagation in elliptical waveguides, and the quantum pendulum. Computing the functions and characteristic values to high accuracy is nontrivial and is handled by continued-fraction and matrix methods in modern software libraries.
