Il Cannone Guarnerius

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Il Cannone Guarnerius of 1743, also called the Cannone or the Canon, is a violin made by the Italian luthier Giuseppe Antonio Guarneri del Gesù of Cremona (1687–1744). It is the most famous of Guarneri's instruments, owing principally to its association with the virtuoso Niccolò Paganini, who called it "my cannon" (il mio cannone) for its powerful, resonant tone.

History

Paganini acquired the violin around 1802, legend holding that he won it (or was given it) in Livorno after a wager involving a sight-reading challenge. He played it for the rest of his career and bequeathed it to his native city of Genoa, Italy, where it is kept in the Palazzo Doria Tursi as a civic treasure. Paganini's will stipulated that the instrument should never be played again, though the city has since allowed it to be played occasionally by distinguished violinists and examined and copied by luthiers.

Characteristics

Built in Guarneri's bold late style, the Cannone has a one-piece maple back, a wide grain spruce top and an unusually full, dark sound. It retains original features unusual among old master violins that have been modernised, making it a frequent model for contemporary copies. Winners of the Premio Paganini competition in Genoa are traditionally given the opportunity to perform on it, and it has been recorded by soloists including Salvatore Accardo — the first to record Paganini's works on the instrument itself.
