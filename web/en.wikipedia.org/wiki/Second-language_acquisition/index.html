Second-language acquisition
AbbreviationSLA
FieldApplied linguistics
Key figuresStephen Krashen, Rod Ellis, Larry Selinker

Second-language acquisition (SLA) is the scientific discipline devoted to studying the process by which people learn a language other than their first language. The term also refers to the process itself, in classrooms or naturalistic settings.

Key concepts

Researchers distinguish acquisition (subconscious picking-up) from conscious learning in Stephen Krashen's influential Monitor Model, with its input hypothesis (comprehensible input slightly beyond current level, "i+1"), affective filter, and natural order hypotheses. Larry Selinker proposed interlanguage — the learner's evolving system — and fossilization, where errors persist.

Stages and factors

Learners typically pass through predictable sequences in morphology and syntax regardless of instruction order. Age, aptitude, motivation (integrative and instrumental), first-language transfer, and social context affect outcomes; the critical period hypothesis holds that native-like attainment is easier before puberty, a claim still debated.

Approaches

Major perspectives include cognitive/interactionist theories (Michael Long's interaction hypothesis), sociocultural theory after Vygotsky, usage-based approaches, and instructed SLA research informing teaching methods from grammar-translation to communicative language teaching.
