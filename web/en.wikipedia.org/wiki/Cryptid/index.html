Cryptid

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bigfoot (Sasquatch) is among the best-known cryptids.

A cryptid is an animal whose present-day existence is disputed or unsubstantiated by science. The term was coined in 1983 by John E. Wall and popularized within cryptozoology, a pseudoscientific field that searches for animals known mainly from folklore, legend, and anecdotal sightings. Well-known cryptids include Bigfoot (Sasquatch), the Loch Ness Monster, the Yeti, the chupacabra, Mothman, and the Jersey Devil.

Classification

Cryptozoologists commonly divide cryptids into categories: animals known from folklore in regions where no similar species is recognized by science; supposedly extinct species reported as surviving, such as the thylacine; and animals reported far outside their known range, such as big cats in Britain. Some animals once dismissed as legend were later confirmed by science — the okapi, the coelacanth (a Lazarus taxon rediscovered alive), and the giant squid are often cited by enthusiasts as precedents — though scientists note these were documented through physical evidence rather than anecdote alone.

Reception by science

Mainstream zoologists regard cryptozoology as a pseudoscience because its claims rest on eyewitness testimony, ambiguous photographs, and folklore rather than verifiable specimens or DNA evidence. Reported sightings are generally explained as misidentifications of known animals, hoaxes, or cultural phenomena. Folklorists, meanwhile, study cryptid legends as part of local tradition and identity, whatever the creature's biological reality.

Despite the scientific consensus, cryptids occupy a large place in popular culture: they anchor festivals and tourism in places such as Loch Ness and Point Pleasant, West Virginia (Mothman), and feature in television series, films, games, and internet culture, where figures such as Bigfoot have become commercial icons.

Notable examples

  • Bigfoot — a large ape-like creature said to inhabit North American forests.
  • Loch Ness Monster — a creature said to live in Loch Ness, Scotland.
  • Yeti — an ape-like being of Himalayan folklore.
  • Chupacabra — a creature reported in the Americas, said to drink the blood of livestock.
  • Mothman — a winged figure reported around Point Pleasant, West Virginia, in 1966–67.
