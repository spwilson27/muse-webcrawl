Japanese porcelain

Japanese porcelain is porcelain ware made in Japan. Production began in the early 17th century around Arita in Kyushu, after deposits of kaolin clay were found there and Korean potters brought to Japan during the invasions of Korea in the 1590s introduced the technology.

History

Porcelain from Arita was shipped from the port of Imari, so early exports to Europe were known as Imari ware. When Chinese exports were disrupted by the Ming–Qing transition, Dutch East India Company orders turned to Japan, and Arita wares — including the enamelled Kakiemon style — flooded European markets and influenced European porcelain factories. The Nabeshima clan kilns produced refined ware for domestic elite use.

Styles

Major styles include blue-and-white sometsuke, Imari with its underglaze blue and overglaze red and gold, Kakiemon enamel ware, and Kutani ware from Ishikawa. After the Meiji Restoration, Japanese porcelain was promoted at world expositions and became a major export industry.

Modern production

Arita, Hasami, Kutani and other centres continue to produce tableware and studio porcelain today.
