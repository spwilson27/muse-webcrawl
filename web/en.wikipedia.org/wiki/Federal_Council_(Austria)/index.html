The Federal Council (German: Bundesrat, pronounced [ˈbʊndəsˌʁaːt]) is the upper house of the Austrian Parliament, representing the nine states (Länder) of Austria at the federal level. Together with the National Council (Nationalrat), it forms the legislative branch of the Republic of Austria. The Federal Council has its seat in the Austrian Parliament Building in Vienna.

Unlike the National Council, the Federal Council is not directly elected. Its members are delegated by the state parliaments (Landtage) in proportion to each party's strength in those assemblies, following each state election. The number of seats per state depends on population: the most populous state sends twelve members, and each other state sends a proportional number, with a minimum of three. The total membership therefore varies slightly with census results, standing at 60 in recent years.

Powers

The Federal Council is generally regarded as the weaker chamber. Most legislation originates in and is decided by the National Council; the Federal Council may object to a bill (an Einspruch), but the National Council can override the objection simply by re-passing the bill with at least half its members present (a Beharrungsbeschluss). The Federal Council's consent is genuinely required only for a limited class of laws, notably constitutional laws that restrict the competences of the states, changes to its own rules, and certain treaties affecting state competences.

The Federal Council also participates in the Federal Assembly (Bundesversammlung), a joint sitting of both chambers that, among other functions, can declare war and swear in the Federal President. Its presidency rotates among the states every six months in alphabetical order of the state's name; the member at the head of that state's delegation serves as President of the Federal Council.

Composition and criticism

Because delegates are bound by Landtag party strengths rather than a free mandate tied to state governments — unlike Germany's Bundesrat, whose members are state ministers — the Austrian Federal Council is frequently criticised as a chamber of party-political rather than genuinely federal representation. Reform proposals, ranging from direct election to abolition, recur in Austrian constitutional debate.
