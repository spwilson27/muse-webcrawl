Giovanni Bellini (c. 1430 – 29 November 1516) was an Italian Renaissance painter, probably the best known of the Bellini family of Venetian painters. He was raised and taught with his brother, the painter Gentile Bellini, in the workshop of their father Jacopo Bellini. Over a career spanning some 65 years, he transformed Venetian painting, moving it toward the sensuous, colouristic style of the High Renaissance.

Career

Early works such as the Agony in the Garden reflect the influence of his brother-in-law Andrea Mantegna. From the 1470s, Bellini pioneered the use of oil paint in Venice — reportedly learning from Antonello da Messina — and developed luminous, atmospheric colour. His many Madonnas, altarpieces such as the San Giobbe and San Zaccaria altarpieces, and portraits made him the dominant Venetian painter of his generation; Albrecht Dürer, visiting in 1506, called him "the best painter of them all".

Late work and influence

Bellini remained innovative into old age, painting the mythological Feast of the Gods (1514, later reworked by Titian). His pupils and followers included Giorgione and Titian, through whom his influence shaped the course of Venetian art.
