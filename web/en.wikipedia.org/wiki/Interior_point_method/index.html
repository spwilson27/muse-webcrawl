Interior point method

Interior point methods (also referred to as barrier methods) are a class of algorithms used to solve linear and nonlinear convex optimisation problems. Unlike the simplex method, which travels along the boundary of the feasible region, interior point methods approach the optimum through the interior of the feasible set.

History

John von Neumann suggested an interior point technique in the 1940s. The modern era began with Narendra Karmarkar's 1984 algorithm for linear programming, which was polynomial in complexity and practical in performance, provoking an explosion of research. Primal-dual methods, developed in the late 1980s and 1990s, are the basis of most modern solvers.

Barrier functions

A barrier method replaces inequality constraints with a barrier function — typically a logarithmic barrier — added to the objective. As a parameter is reduced, the barrier's effect weakens and the iterates converge to the constrained optimum, following the so-called central path.

Applications

Interior point methods are the algorithm of choice for large-scale linear, quadratic, semidefinite and general convex problems, and are implemented in commercial and open-source solvers used in engineering, finance, machine learning and operations research.
