Bloomsbury Publishing plc is a British worldwide publishing house of fiction and non-fiction, based in the Bloomsbury district of London. Founded in 1986 by Nigel Newton, it is best known as the original publisher of J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter series, whose success transformed the company from a small independent into a major international publisher. It is a constituent of the FTSE SmallCap Index.

History

Bloomsbury was floated on the London Stock Exchange in 1994. In 1997 it published Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone after the manuscript had been rejected by numerous other publishers; the series' global success funded rapid expansion. The company grew through acquisitions including A & C Black (2000), the Whitaker's Almanack reference titles, Berg Publishers (2008), Continuum (2011), and I.B. Tauris (2018), building a substantial academic and professional division alongside trade fiction and children's books.

Divisions and imprints

Bloomsbury's publishing is organized into consumer (adult and children's) and non-consumer (academic and professional, and special interest) divisions. Imprints and acquired lists include Bloomsbury Academic, Arden Shakespeare, Methuen Drama, T & T Clark, Osprey Publishing, and Bloomsbury Children's Books. In the 2020s the romantic-fantasy novels of Sarah J. Maas became a further major revenue driver, alongside backlist sales of the Harry Potter books.
