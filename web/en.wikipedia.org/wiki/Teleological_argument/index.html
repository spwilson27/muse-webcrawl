Teleological argument

The teleological argument (from Greek τέλος, telos, "end, purpose"), also known as the physico-theological argument or the argument from design, is an argument for the existence of God or, more generally, that complex functionality in the natural world—particularly the apparent order and purpose in living organisms—indicates a designer. The earliest recorded versions appear in ancient Greek philosophy, in the works of Socrates as reported by Xenophon and Plato, and the argument was developed by Aristotle, the Stoics, and later by medieval thinkers such as Thomas Aquinas, whose "fifth way" argues from the governance of things.

The argument's most famous formulation is William Paley's watchmaker analogy in his Natural Theology (1802): just as a watch found on a heath, with its parts intricately fitted for the purpose of telling time, implies a watchmaker, so the complexity of living things implies an intelligent designer.

Criticisms

David Hume, in his Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion (1779), raised influential objections: the analogy between organisms and artifacts is weak, the argument does not establish a single, perfect, or benevolent designer, and order might arise from nature itself. Charles Darwin's theory of evolution by natural selection (1859) provided a naturalistic explanation for the apparent design of organisms, widely regarded as undermining the biological form of the argument. Critics such as Richard Dawkins have argued that natural selection is a "blind watchmaker" capable of producing complex adaptations without foresight.

Modern versions

Contemporary forms include the fine-tuning argument, which points to the sensitivity of the constants of physics to the possibility of life, and the intelligent design movement, which argues that certain features of organisms exhibit "irreducible complexity" (Michael Behe) or "specified complexity" (William Dembski). Intelligent design has been rejected by the scientific community as not being science, a position affirmed in the 2005 United States federal court case Kitzmiller v. Dover Area School District.
