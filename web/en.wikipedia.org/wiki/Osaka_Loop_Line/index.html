The Osaka Loop Line (大阪環状線, Ōsaka Kanjō-sen) is a railway loop line in Japan operated by the West Japan Railway Company (JR West). It encircles central Osaka and is the second-longest loop line in Japan after the Yamanote Line in Tokyo. Together with the Yamanote it is one of the two great urban loop lines of Japan, carrying passengers to major districts including Umeda, Kyōbashi, Tennōji, Shin-Imamiya and the Universal Studios Japan area via the connecting Yumesaki Line.

Overview

The loop is 21.7 km long with 19 stations. Trains run as "inner loop" (counterclockwise) and "outer loop" (clockwise) services, and the line also carries through services onto the JR Yumesaki Line, Hanwa Line and Yamatoji Line. The line is electrified at 1,500 V DC overhead and is double-tracked throughout. It is one of the most heavily used lines in the Kansai region, with roughly a million passengers per day before the COVID-19 pandemic.

History

The first section, between Osaka and Imamiya, opened in 1898 as part of the Kansai Railway. Further sections were built by the Nishinari Railway and Osaka Railway, and the various segments were nationalised in the early 20th century. The final link closing the loop, between Nishikujō and Sakuranomiya, opened on 25 April 1961. In 1987, with the privatisation of Japanese National Railways, the line passed to JR West.

Rolling stock

The line was for many years dominated by 103 series and 201 series trains in the line's signature orange livery. From December 2016, new 323 series eight-car trains were introduced specifically for the line, and they progressively replaced the older stock; the 323 series features a distinctive orange design and three doors per car side to speed boarding.

Stations

Stations on the loop (in outer-loop order from Osaka) include: Osaka, Fukushima, Noda, Nishikujō, Bentenchō, Taishō, Ashiharabashi, Imamiya, Shin-Imamiya, Tennōji, Teradachō, Momodani, Tsuruhashi, Tamatsukuri, Morinomiya, Ōsakajō-kōen, Kyōbashi, Sakuranomiya, and Temma.
