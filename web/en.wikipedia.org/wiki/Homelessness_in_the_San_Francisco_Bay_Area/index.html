Homelessness in the San Francisco Bay Area

The San Francisco Bay Area has one of the largest and most visible populations of people experiencing homelessness in the United States. Point-in-time counts in the early 2020s estimated roughly 38,000 people homeless across the nine-county region, including about 8,000 in San Francisco itself.

Causes

Analysts generally attribute the crisis primarily to the region's severe shortage of affordable housing, driven by high housing costs, decades of underbuilding relative to job growth, and displacement of lower-income residents amid the technology industry's expansion. Other contributing factors include mental illness, substance-use disorders, and gaps in social services.

Responses

Local governments have pursued strategies including permanent supportive housing, shelters, navigation centres, safe parking and sanctioned encampment sites, and prevention programmes. The issue remains a central topic in regional politics and the subject of ongoing litigation over encampment clearances.
