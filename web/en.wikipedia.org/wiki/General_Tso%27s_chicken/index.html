General Tso's chicken

General Tso's chicken
Cuisine: Chinese-American
Course: Main
Main ingredients: Chicken, soy sauce, dried chili peppers, sugar

General Tso's chicken is a sweet and spicy deep-fried chicken dish that is served in North American Chinese restaurants. The dish is named after Zuo Zongtang (also romanized as Tso Tsung-t'ang), a Qing dynasty statesman and military leader, although there is no recorded connection between the dish and him.

History

The dish is most often credited to chef Peng Chang-kuei, who created a version in Taiwan in the 1950s and later brought it to New York City, where it was adapted at Shun Lee Palace in the 1970s with a sweeter sauce and crispier coating. Another New York chef, Tsung Ting Wang, also claimed a role in popularizing the dish.

Preparation

Chunks of dark-meat chicken are coated in a batter or cornstarch, deep-fried until crisp, and tossed in a glossy sauce of soy sauce, rice vinegar, sugar, hoisin or tomato paste, garlic, ginger, and dried red chili peppers, often served with broccoli and steamed rice.

Names and variants

The dish appears under many names, including General Tao's chicken in Canada and Governor Tso's chicken. Similar dishes include sesame chicken and orange chicken, which use related sweet sauces.
