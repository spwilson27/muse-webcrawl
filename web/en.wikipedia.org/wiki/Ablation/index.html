Ablation

Ablation (Latin: ablatio, "carrying away") is the removal or destruction of material from the surface of an object by vaporisation, chipping, erosion or other processes. The term is used in medicine, glaciology, physics, aerospace engineering and artificial intelligence.

Medicine

In medicine, ablation is the removal or destruction of tissue. Examples include catheter ablation of abnormal electrical pathways in the heart using radiofrequency or cryotherapy, laser ablation of the skin, endometrial ablation, and tumour ablation by radiofrequency, microwave or cryoablation.

Glaciology

Ablation in glaciology is the loss of ice or snow from a glacier or snowfield by melting, sublimation, evaporation and calving. The ablation zone is the lower region of a glacier where annual loss exceeds accumulation.

Aerospace and physics

Ablative materials protect spacecraft heat shields during atmospheric entry: the surface chars, melts and vaporises, carrying heat away. Laser ablation uses pulsed lasers to remove material for surgery, micromachining and mass spectrometry. In meteorology and planetary science, ablation describes erosion of meteors and cometary surfaces.

Artificial intelligence

An ablation study in machine learning systematically removes components of a model to measure their contribution to performance.
