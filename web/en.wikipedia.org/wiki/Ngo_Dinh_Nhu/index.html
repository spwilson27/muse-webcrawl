Ngo Dinh Nhu

Ngo Dinh Nhu (1910–1963) was a Vietnamese archivist and politician who served as the chief political adviser to his elder brother, Ngo Dinh Diem, president of South Vietnam. Widely regarded as the power behind the throne, Nhu directed the regime's secret police, its ruling political movement and the Strategic Hamlet Program.

Role in the regime

Trained as an archivist in Paris, Nhu built the clandestine Cần Lao (Personalist Labour) Party that underpinned his brother's rule, and controlled the secret police and special forces through loyal officers. He was the architect of the Strategic Hamlet Program (1962), meant to isolate rural villagers from the Viet Cong, and promoted the ideology of Personalism drawn from Catholic social thought. His wife, Trần Lệ Xuân — known as Madame Nhu — acted as the regime's de facto first lady and became infamous abroad for callous remarks about the Buddhist crisis.

Death

Nhu's harsh repression, culminating in the August 1963 raids on Buddhist pagodas by his special forces, convinced the Kennedy administration to signal support for a coup. On 2 November 1963, Nhu and Diem were captured and murdered by ARVN officers after surrendering at a church in Cholon.
