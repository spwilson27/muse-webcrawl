An enchilada (Spanish: [entʃiˈlaða]) is a Mexican dish consisting of a corn tortilla rolled around a filling and covered with a savoury sauce. The name derives from the Spanish verb enchilar, "to add chile pepper to". Fillings can include meats, cheese, beans, potatoes, vegetables or combinations, and the dish is typically garnished with cheese, onions, shredded lettuce, sour cream or olives.

History

Rolling tortillas around food dates to pre-Hispanic Maya and Aztec practice, when tortillas were wrapped around small fish or dipped in chile sauces. The dish appears in the first Mexican cookbook, El cocinero mexicano (1831), and was defined in the Diccionario de Cocina (1845). Street vendors popularised enchiladas across Mexico in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Varieties

Regional varieties are distinguished by their sauces: enchiladas rojas use a red sauce of dried chiles such as ancho or guajillo; enchiladas verdes use a tomatillo-based green sauce; enchiladas suizas are topped with a creamy sauce and melted cheese; enchiladas de mole are covered in mole poblano; enfrijoladas use bean sauce and entomatadas a tomato sauce. Tex-Mex versions in the United States typically use flour or corn tortillas with a chili gravy and yellow cheese.
