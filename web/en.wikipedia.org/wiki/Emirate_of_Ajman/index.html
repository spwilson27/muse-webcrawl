The Emirate of Ajman is one of the seven emirates of the United Arab Emirates. Its capital is the city of Ajman. It is the smallest emirate by land area, covering about 259 square kilometres, and consists of the coastal city plus two small inland exclaves, Manama and Masfout. It is ruled by the Al Nuaimi family.

History

Ajman emerged around 1816, when the Al Nuaimi tribe under Rashid bin Humaid Al Nuaimi established control of the small coastal settlement. The emirate concluded treaties with Britain during the 19th century, becoming part of the Trucial States, and in 1971 joined the newly formed United Arab Emirates. Its rulers include Humaid bin Rashid Al Nuaimi, who has reigned since 1981.

Economy

Historically dependent on fishing, pearling and boat building — Ajman remains known for its dhow-building yard — the emirate has developed a free zone (established 1988) attracting light industry and trading companies, alongside a growing real-estate and tourism sector centred on its Gulf beaches. Ajman Port and the emirate's proximity to Sharjah and Dubai support its role as a lower-cost residential and industrial location.
