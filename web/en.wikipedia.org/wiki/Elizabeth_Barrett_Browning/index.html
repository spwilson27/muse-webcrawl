Elizabeth Barrett Browning (née Moulton-Barrett; 1806–1861) was an English poet of the Victorian era, among the most prominent and influential poets of her time, popular in Britain and the United States.

Life

A precocious scholar raised at Hope End, Herefordshire, she published poetry from her teens but lived semi-invalid in London after spinal injury and chronic illness. Her Poems (1844) brought fame. Robert Browning's admiration began a secret courtship by letter; they married in 1846 against her father's prohibition of his children's marriages, and eloped to Italy, where her health improved in Florence.

Works

Sonnets from the Portuguese (1850) — including "How do I love thee? Let me count the ways" — chronicles their love. Casa Guidi Windows (1851) supported Italian nationalism; Aurora Leigh (1856), a novel in blank verse about a woman poet, was a bestseller and her most ambitious work. She campaigned against child labour in mines and factories ("The Cry of the Children") and was considered for Poet Laureate in 1850.
