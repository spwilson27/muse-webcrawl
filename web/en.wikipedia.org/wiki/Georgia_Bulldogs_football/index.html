The Georgia Bulldogs football program represents the University of Georgia in American football. The Bulldogs compete in the Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS) of the NCAA and the Southeastern Conference (SEC). They play their home games at Sanford Stadium in Athens, Georgia — "between the hedges" — one of the largest stadiums in college football.

History

Georgia fielded its first team in 1892 under Dr. Charles Herty, a chemistry professor regarded as the father of Georgia football. The program rose to national prominence under Wally Butts (1939–1960), winning the 1942 national championship behind Heisman Trophy winner Frank Sinkwich and Charley Trippi, including a victory in the 1943 Rose Bowl. Vince Dooley (1964–1988) built the Bulldogs into a perennial power, winning six SEC titles and the 1980 national championship with freshman running back Herschel Walker, who won the Heisman in 1982.

After leaner years under Ray Goff and Jim Donnan, Mark Richt (2001–2015) restored Georgia to consistent top-ten finishes with two SEC championships. His successor, alumnus Kirby Smart, took the Bulldogs to the national championship game in his second season (2017) and then ended a 41-year title drought by winning the College Football Playoff national championship for the 2021 season, defeating Alabama 33–18. Georgia repeated as champions for 2022, routing TCU 65–7 — the largest margin in a national title game — to complete back-to-back championships.

Traditions and rivalries

The Bulldogs' live mascot is Uga, a white English bulldog kept by the Seiler family since 1956; deceased Ugas are interred in a mausoleum at Sanford Stadium. Traditions include the ringing of the chapel bell after victories, the "Dawg Walk", and Larry Munson's legendary radio calls — Munson coined "hobnail boot" and was the voice of the Bulldogs for over four decades.

Georgia's principal rivals are Florida (the annual game in Jacksonville, long known as the "World's Largest Outdoor Cocktail Party"), Auburn (the Deep South's Oldest Rivalry, first played in 1892), Georgia Tech (Clean, Old-Fashioned Hate), Tennessee, and Alabama.

Honours

The Bulldogs claim four national championships (1942, 1980, 2021, 2022) and have won more than a dozen SEC championships. Georgia players have won two Heisman Trophies, and the program has produced numerous NFL players, including Fran Tarkenton, Champ Bailey, Hines Ward, Matthew Stafford, and Nick Chubb.
