Vyākaraṇa

Vyākaraṇa (Sanskrit: व्याकरण, "analysis, grammatical analysis") is one of the six Vedangas, the auxiliary disciplines associated with the study of the Vedas, and refers to the study of Sanskrit grammar and linguistic analysis. Its primary aim in the Vedic tradition was the correct preservation, pronunciation, and understanding of the sacred texts.

Pāṇini and the Aṣṭādhyāyī

The most celebrated work of the vyākaraṇa tradition is the Aṣṭādhyāyī ("Eight Chapters") of Pāṇini (roughly 5th–4th century BCE), a remarkably concise and systematic grammar of Sanskrit comprising about 4,000 sūtras (rules). Pāṇini's grammar describes the structure of Sanskrit with an algebraic precision that has drawn comparisons to modern formal systems and influenced the development of linguistics worldwide.

Later tradition

Kātyāyana wrote critical notes (vārttikas) on Pāṇini's rules, and Patañjali's Mahābhāṣya ("Great Commentary") expanded the discussion into philosophy of language. Together Pāṇini, Kātyāyana, and Patañjali are known as the munitraya, the "three sages" of grammar. Later schools, including those of Jain and Buddhist grammarians and works such as the Kāśikā commentary and Bhaṭṭoji Dīkṣita's Siddhānta Kaumudī, continued the tradition for over two millennia.
