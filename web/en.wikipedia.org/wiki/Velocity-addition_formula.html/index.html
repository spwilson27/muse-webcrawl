Velocity-addition formula

In relativistic physics, a velocity-addition formula is an equation that specifies how the velocities of moving objects combine. It has different forms depending on the frame of reference and the theory (Galilean vs. special relativity).

Galilean relativity

In classical (Galilean) relativity, velocities simply add: if a train moves at speed u and a passenger walks forward at speed v relative to the train, the passenger's speed relative to the ground is u + v.

Special relativity

In special relativity, for collinear motion the combined velocity is w = (u + v) / (1 + uv/c²), where c is the speed of light. This formula guarantees that the result never exceeds c, and reduces to the Galilean formula when u, v << c. For non-collinear velocities, the composition involves a Thomas–Wigner rotation, and velocity addition is neither commutative nor associative in the naive vector sense.
