Kalles kaviar

Kalles kaviar is a Swedish brand of smörgåskaviar ("sandwich caviar"), a creamy spread made from cod roe, sold in distinctive aluminium tubes. Launched in 1954 by the Abba Seafood company, it has become one of Sweden's most recognisable food brands, traditionally eaten on crispbread or on boiled eggs.

Product

Despite the name, Kalles is not true caviar. The spread is made principally of salted and sugar-cured cod roe blended with tomato paste, potato flakes, sugar, salt, and oil, giving it a salty-sweet, smoky flavour and a pale orange colour. It is typically squeezed from the tube onto buttered rye crispbread (knäckebröd), often topped with sliced egg or cheese.

History and branding

The recipe is said to date to the 19th century, based on a formula bought from a fisherman. The brand takes its name and image from Kalle, the young son of Abba's managing director at the time of the launch; the portrait of the blond, blue-eyed boy on the tube has changed little since 1954 and is one of Sweden's most durable pieces of packaging design. The brand is now owned by Orkla Foods.

Variants and availability

The range has expanded to include organic, low-salt, egg-and-caviar, and other flavoured varieties. Kalles is stocked in IKEA food departments worldwide, where it serves many Swedes abroad and curious foreigners as a taste of home; its strong flavour makes it a frequent subject of "love it or hate it" comparisons with products such as Vegemite.
