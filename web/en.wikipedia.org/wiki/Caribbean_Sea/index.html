Caribbean Sea

[Image: A white-sand Caribbean beach with turquoise water]
A white-sand Caribbean beach with turquoise water

The Caribbean Sea is a sea of the North Atlantic Ocean in the tropics of the Western Hemisphere. It is bounded by Mexico and Central America to the west, the Greater Antilles to the north, the Lesser Antilles to the east, and the northern coast of South America to the south. It covers about 2.75 million square kilometres and contains the Cayman Trough, whose floor is the deepest point of the sea at roughly 7,700 metres below sea level.

The Caribbean is known for its warm waters, coral reefs and island nations. Its name derives from the Carib people, an Indigenous group present in the region when Europeans arrived. The Panama Canal connects it to the Pacific Ocean.

Economy

Tourism, shipping, fishing and offshore finance dominate the economies around the Caribbean. Hurricanes form over its warm waters each summer and autumn, sometimes causing devastating damage to island and coastal communities.
