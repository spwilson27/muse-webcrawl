Human Cell Atlas

The Human Cell Atlas (HCA) is an international scientific consortium that aims to create comprehensive reference maps of all human cells — the fundamental units of life — as a basis for understanding human health and for diagnosing, monitoring and treating disease. It was launched in 2016, with founding leadership including Aviv Regev and Sarah Teichmann, and is often compared in ambition to the Human Genome Project.

Approach

The atlas is built chiefly with single-cell genomics: tissues are dissociated and the RNA (and increasingly chromatin state, proteins and spatial position) of individual cells is measured, allowing researchers to classify cell types and states across organs. Draft atlases have been published for many tissues, including the lung, gut, skin, immune system and the developing human body.

Organisation and data

The consortium involves thousands of scientists in dozens of countries, organised into biological networks for each major organ system, together with groups for technology, analysis and ethics. All data are released openly through the HCA Data Store and data portal, with standardised processing pipelines. Major funders have included the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative, Wellcome, the Helmsley Charitable Trust and the European Union. First drafts of a whole-body atlas — tens of millions of cells — were reported in a collection of papers in 2024, marking a step toward the long-term goal of mapping the estimated 37 trillion cells of the human body.
