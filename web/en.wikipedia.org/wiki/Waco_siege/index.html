Waco siege

The Waco siege, also known as the Branch Davidian siege, was a law enforcement siege of the religious compound belonging to the religious sect Branch Davidians, carried out by the U.S. federal government, Texas state law enforcement, and the Texas Military Forces between February 28 and April 19, 1993. The Branch Davidians were led by David Koresh and were headquartered at Mount Carmel Center ranch in unincorporated McLennan County, Texas, 13 miles (21 kilometers) northeast of Waco. Suspecting the group of stockpiling illegal weapons, the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, and Firearms (ATF) obtained a search warrant for the compound and an arrest warrant for Koresh.

Background

The Branch Davidians were a religious movement that originated in 1955 from a schism in the Shepherd's Rod/Davidians. Under Vernon Wayne Howell, who took the name David Koresh in 1990, the group established itself at the Mount Carmel Center. Koresh taught that he was a prophet and prepared his followers for an imminent apocalyptic battle. Federal authorities alleged that the group was converting semi-automatic rifles into fully automatic weapons and stockpiling other arms.

The initial raid

On February 28, 1993, the ATF attempted to execute its search warrant in a raid codenamed "Operation Showtime." The Davidians had been tipped off, and a gun battle erupted. Four ATF agents and six Branch Davidians were killed in the initial exchange, and the raid failed. The Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) then took command and began a 51-day siege, during which negotiators secured the release of some children while tactical teams pressed for surrender.

The fire and final assault

On April 19, 1993, the FBI launched a tear gas attack using combat engineering vehicles to inject CS gas into the compound. Several hours into the operation, a fire broke out and rapidly engulfed the buildings. Seventy-six Branch Davidians, including Koresh and more than 20 children, died in the fire, from smoke inhalation, or from gunshot wounds. Nine members survived. The origin of the fire remains disputed: government investigations concluded the Davidians set it deliberately, while critics argued the government's tactics contributed to the disaster.

Aftermath and legacy

The siege became one of the most controversial law-enforcement operations in modern American history, prompting congressional hearings and reforms in federal rules of engagement. The handling of the incident, together with the earlier Ruby Ridge standoff, became a rallying point for the militia movement; Timothy McVeigh cited Waco as a motivation for the Oklahoma City bombing, which he carried out on the siege's second anniversary, April 19, 1995.
