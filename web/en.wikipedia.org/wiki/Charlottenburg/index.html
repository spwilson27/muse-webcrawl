Charlottenburg

Charlottenburg is a locality of Berlin within the borough of Charlottenburg-Wilmersdorf. Established as a town in 1705 by King Frederick I of Prussia in honour of his wife, Queen Sophia Charlotte, it grew around Charlottenburg Palace and was, until its incorporation into Greater Berlin in 1920, one of the wealthiest independent cities in Prussia.

History

The palace, built from 1695 as a summer residence named Lietzenburg, was renamed Charlottenburg after Queen Sophia Charlotte's death in 1705, when the surrounding settlement received its town charter. The town grew steadily, and by the late 19th century it was a prosperous city with its own technical university (now TU Berlin). In 1920 the Greater Berlin Act merged Charlottenburg into Berlin.

The "New West"

During the Weimar era the area around the Kurfürstendamm, the Zoologischer Garten station, and Tauentzienstraße developed into Berlin's glittering "New West," rivalling the historic centre with theatres, cafés, department stores such as KaDeWe, and the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church. After 1945, lying in the British sector of West Berlin, Charlottenburg again became the commercial heart of the western half of the divided city. Since reunification it remains one of Berlin's most affluent and elegant districts.
