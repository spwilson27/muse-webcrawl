The Cerrejón mine, among the largest open-pit coal mines in the world.

Cerrejón is a large open-pit coal mine in the Guajira Peninsula of northern Colombia, in the municipalities of Albania, Barrancas and Hatonuevo, department of La Guajira. It is one of the largest open-pit coal mines in the world, with a concession area of roughly 69,000 hectares, and a major source of thermal coal exported mainly to Europe and the Americas through its own port, Puerto Bolívar, connected to the mine by a 150-kilometre railway.

History

Production began in the 1980s under a partnership between the Colombian state company Carbocol and Exxon subsidiary Intercor. Ownership later passed in equal thirds to BHP, Anglo American and Glencore; in 2022 Glencore acquired the other partners' stakes and became sole owner. The mine has produced on the order of 20–30 million tonnes of coal per year.

Controversies

The mine has been the subject of long-running disputes with the Indigenous Wayuu people and Afro-Colombian communities over water use, the diversion of the Bruno stream, dust, health effects and forced displacements of communities such as Tabaco. Colombian courts and international bodies have repeatedly ruled on the protection of local communities' rights, and the company faces pressure from coal phase-out policies in its export markets.

Palaeontology

The mine exposes the fossil-rich Paleocene Cerrejón Formation, famous for the giant snake Titanoboa.
