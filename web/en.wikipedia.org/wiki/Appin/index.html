Appin (Scottish Gaelic: An Apainn) is a remote coastal district of the Scottish West Highlands, bounded to the west by Loch Linnhe, to the south by Loch Creran, to the east by the districts of Benderloch and Lorne, and to the north by Loch Leven. It lies north-east of Oban, in the historic county of Argyll and the modern council area of Argyll and Bute.

The district is made up of the parishes of Appin and Lismore, and it includes the villages and hamlets of Port Appin, Portnacroish, Duror, Kentallen and Ballachulish. The area is famed for its rugged scenery, its views across Loch Linnhe to Morvern and Ardgour, and for the island of Lismore offshore.

History

Appin was historically the territory of the Stewarts of Appin, a branch of Clan Stewart descended from Sir John Stewart of Bonkyll. The Stewarts of Appin supported the Jacobite cause in the rising of 1745, fielding a regiment that fought at Culloden. The Appin Regiment's colours are preserved in the National Museum of Scotland.

The district is also remembered for the Appin Murder of 1752, in which Colin Campbell of Glenure, a government factor on the forfeited Stewart estates, was shot near Ballachulish. James Stewart of the Glens ("James of the Glen") was convicted of the crime and hanged at Cnap a' Chaolais; the case later inspired Robert Louis Stevenson's novel Kidnapped, which opens with the murder as a central episode.

Landmarks

Castle Stalker, a picturesque four-storey tower house on a tidal islet in Loch Laich, stands near Port Appin and is one of the best-known landmarks of the area. The ruins of other castles and the parish church at Portnacroish also attract visitors.

Transport

The A828 road runs through the district, connecting Connel near Oban with Ballachulish and the A82. A passenger ferry links Port Appin with the island of Lismore.
