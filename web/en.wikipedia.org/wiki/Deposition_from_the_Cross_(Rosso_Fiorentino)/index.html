The Deposition from the Cross is an altarpiece painted in 1521 by the Italian Renaissance artist Rosso Fiorentino. It is housed in the Pinacoteca Comunale (town art gallery) of Volterra, Tuscany, and is widely considered Rosso's masterpiece and one of the key works of early Mannerism.

History

The panel was commissioned for the chapel of the Villani family in the church of San Francesco in Volterra, though it was later moved within the town's churches before entering the municipal gallery. Rosso, a pupil of Andrea del Sarto, painted it at the age of about twenty-seven, shortly before leaving Tuscany for Rome.

Description

The painting depicts the moment when the body of Christ is taken down from the Cross. Unlike calm, harmonious Renaissance treatments, Rosso's scene is crowded, angular and emotionally agitated: figures swarm across ladders leaning against the crossbeam, their elongated bodies twisting in precarious poses, while below, the mourners — including the swooning Virgin — form a knot of grief. The palette of acid reds, greens and oranges against a deep blue sky, and the harsh, sculptural light, are characteristic of Rosso's expressive style. A monkey crouches at the foot of the cross, an unusual detail variously interpreted as a symbol of sin or of art as the "ape of nature".

Interpretation

Art historians have contrasted the Volterra Deposition with the more composed treatment of the same subject by Rosso's contemporary Pontormo, and with Fra Bartolomeo's earlier version. Its dramatic instability and deliberate distortions exemplify the break with High Renaissance balance that defines early Florentine Mannerism.
