Lanai City is a census-designated place (CDP) on the island of Lanai, in Maui County, Hawaii, United States. It is the largest settlement on the island and home to the great majority of Lanai's residents. The town was founded in the 1920s as a company town for the Hawaiian Pineapple Company plantation established by James Dole.

Lanai City is laid out around Dole Park, a broad grassy square lined with shops, the island's hotel, a theatre and community facilities, and shaded by Cook pines. At an elevation of about 500 metres the town is noticeably cooler than the island's coasts.

History

In 1922 James Dole purchased most of Lanai for pineapple cultivation, and Lanai City was built to house plantation workers, many of them immigrants from Japan, the Philippines, Korea, Portugal and elsewhere. For decades Lanai was the world's largest pineapple plantation, styling itself the "Pineapple Isle." Pineapple production was phased out in the early 1990s, and the island's economy shifted to tourism centred on two large resorts near the coast, while Lanai City remained its residential and service centre.

Geography and climate

The town sits on the high Palawai Basin in the island's centre. Its upland climate is mild, with frequent cloud and lighter rainfall than windward Hawaiian districts.

Demographics

The community is ethnically diverse, reflecting its plantation origins, with a large Filipino-American population alongside Native Hawaiian, Japanese-American and other residents.

Transport

Lanai Airport lies a short distance southwest of the town, with ferry connections to Maui from Manele Harbor on the south coast.
