Lévy distribution

Lévy distribution
Type: Continuous probability distribution
Parameters: scale c > 0, location μ
Support: x ≥ μ
Named for: Paul Lévy

The Lévy distribution, named after the French mathematician Paul Lévy, is a continuous probability distribution for a non-negative random variable. It is a special case of the inverse-gamma distribution and one of the few distributions that are stable and have probability density functions expressible in closed form.

Definition

For scale parameter c > 0 and location μ, the probability density function is f(x; μ, c) = sqrt(c / (2π)) · exp(−c / (2(x−μ))) / (x−μ)3/2 for x ≥ μ. The distribution has a heavy tail: its mean and variance are infinite.

Properties

The Lévy distribution is a stable distribution with stability parameter α = 1/2 and skewness β = 1. Sums of Lévy-distributed variables, suitably scaled, remain Lévy-distributed, which connects it to Brownian motion: the time at which a Brownian particle first reaches a given distance from its starting point follows a Lévy distribution.

Applications

It appears in modelling of first-passage times, stock market data in stochastic process theory, and the analysis of diffusion in disordered media.
