Bokeh

Bokeh (from Japanese boke, "blur" or "haze") is the aesthetic quality of the blur in out-of-focus areas of a photograph, produced by a lens with shallow depth of field.

Characteristics

Good bokeh is smooth and pleasing, with soft circular highlights; poor bokeh may show harsh edges, onion rings or swirly patterns. It is influenced by aperture shape, lens aberrations, and aperture blade count.

Technique

Photographers achieve strong bokeh with fast lenses (wide apertures like f/1.4), longer focal lengths, and distance between subject and background. Portrait photography especially values subject isolation via bokeh. Computational bokeh is simulated in smartphone portrait modes.

Origin of term

The spelling "bokeh" was popularised in English in the 1990s by Photo Techniques magazine to suggest correct pronunciation.
