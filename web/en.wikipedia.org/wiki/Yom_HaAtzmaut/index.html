Yom HaAtzmaut

Yom HaAtzmaut (Hebrew: יום העצמאות, lit. "Day of Independence") is the national day of Israel, commemorating the Israeli Declaration of Independence, proclaimed by David Ben-Gurion on 14 May 1948 (5 Iyar 5708 in the Hebrew calendar). It is celebrated annually on or near the 5th of Iyar, subject to adjustments so that neither it nor the preceding memorial day, Yom Hazikaron, falls on the Jewish Sabbath.

History

On 14 May 1948, hours before the British Mandate for Palestine expired, David Ben-Gurion proclaimed the establishment of the State of Israel at the Tel Aviv Museum. The new state was recognised de facto by the United States and de jure by the Soviet Union within days. The following day Israel was invaded by neighbouring Arab states, beginning the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. The Knesset legislated Yom HaAtzmaut as a public holiday in 1949.

Observance

The holiday begins at sundown with an official ceremony on Mount Herzl in Jerusalem, at which twelve torches are lit, symbolising the Twelve Tribes of Israel, and the Speaker of the Knesset delivers an address. The transition from the solemnity of Yom Hazikaron—Israel's memorial day for fallen soldiers and victims of terrorism, held the previous day—to celebration is a defining feature of the occasion.

Across Israel, the day is marked by public concerts, fireworks, street parties, barbecues in national parks, and an Israel Air Force flyover. The International Bible Contest for Jewish youth and the awarding of the Israel Prize take place in Jerusalem. Many synagogues recite Hallel prayers, and communities in the Jewish diaspora hold festivals and parades.

Palestinian perspective

For Palestinians, 1948 is remembered as the Nakba ("catastrophe"), when more than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled from their homes. Nakba Day is commemorated on 15 May, the day after Israeli independence is celebrated on the Gregorian calendar.
