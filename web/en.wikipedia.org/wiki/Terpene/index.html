Terpene

Terpenes are a large and diverse class of natural products, more than 30,000 compounds, produced mainly by plants — particularly conifers — and by some insects. They are hydrocarbons built from isoprene units (C5H8) and are the major constituents of essential oils and resins.

Classification

Terpenes are classified by the number of isoprene units: hemiterpenes (1), monoterpenes (2, e.g. pinene and limonene), sesquiterpenes (3), diterpenes (4), triterpenes (6, the basis of steroids), and tetraterpenes (8, the carotenoids). Oxygen-containing derivatives are called terpenoids.

Roles and uses

In plants terpenes serve as attractants for pollinators, defences against herbivores, and signalling compounds. Industrially they are used as fragrances, flavours, solvents (turpentine), and pharmaceuticals — the antimalarial artemisinin and the anticancer drug paclitaxel are terpenoids.
