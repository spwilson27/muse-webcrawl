Energy in Brazil is characterized by one of the most renewable energy matrices among large economies. Brazil relies heavily on hydroelectricity, sugarcane-derived ethanol, and increasingly on wind and solar power. Total primary energy consumption exceeds 300 million tonnes of oil equivalent, serving a population of more than 200 million.

History

Brazil developed large hydroelectric projects throughout the 20th century, culminating in the Itaipu Dam (shared with Paraguay, 14 GW) and Belo Monte. The 1970s oil crises spurred the Proálcool programme promoting ethanol fuel. Petrobras, founded in 1953, led oil exploration, with major pre-salt deepwater discoveries announced from 2006 onwards off the southeast coast.

Electricity

Hydroelectricity historically supplied 60-70% of electricity, though droughts in 2001 and 2021 exposed vulnerabilities. Wind power, concentrated in the Northeast (Bahia, Rio Grande do Norte, Ceará), and utility solar have grown rapidly since the 2010s. The national interconnected system (SIN) links most of the country.

Oil and natural gas

Brazil is among the world's top ten oil producers, with output around 3 million barrels per day, dominated by pre-salt fields such as Tupi (Lula) and Búzios operated by Petrobras.

Biofuels

Brazil is the world's second-largest ethanol producer and a pioneer of flex-fuel vehicles, which can run on any blend of gasoline and hydrous ethanol.
