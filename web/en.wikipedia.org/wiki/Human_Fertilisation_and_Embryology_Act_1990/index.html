Human Fertilisation and Embryology Act 1990

The Human Fertilisation and Embryology Act 1990 (c. 37) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that created the Human Fertilisation and Embryology Authority (HFEA) and established the world's first statutory system for regulating fertility treatment and embryo research.

Background

The Act followed the 1984 Warnock Report, chaired by philosopher Mary Warnock, which examined the ethical and social questions raised by in vitro fertilisation (IVF) and embryology in the wake of the birth of Louise Brown in 1978.

Provisions

The Act requires clinics providing IVF, donor insemination and embryo storage or research to be licensed by the HFEA. It permits research on human embryos only under licence and only up to the appearance of the primitive streak, taken to be 14 days after fertilisation — the so-called "14-day rule". It also amended abortion law, setting a general time limit of 24 weeks for most abortions. Provisions on legal parenthood in donor conception were substantially revised by the Human Fertilisation and Embryology Act 2008.
