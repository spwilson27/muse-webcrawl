Art glass

Art glass encompasses decorative and sculptural works in glass made by individual artists and studios.

Art glass is a term for fine glassware and glass sculpture produced primarily for its artistic and decorative qualities rather than for utilitarian use. The term is applied both to the richly coloured decorative glass of the late 19th and early 20th centuries and to the studio glass movement that began in the 1960s.

Historical art glass

During the Victorian era and the Art Nouveau and Art Deco periods, firms such as Tiffany Studios, Steuben, Lalique, Gallé and Daum produced celebrated decorative glass — iridescent Favrile glass, cameo glass with acid-etched layered designs, and opalescent glass used in lamps and windows. Makers competed in developing new colours and effects, and art glass became a collectible luxury.

Studio glass movement

In 1962 Harvey Littleton held workshops at the Toledo Museum of Art, aided by Dominick Labino's development of a small furnace and suitable glass batch, which freed glassblowing from the factory. Artists could now work in small studios, and glass became a medium of contemporary art and university study. Dale Chihuly, who co-founded the Pilchuck Glass School in 1971, became the movement's best-known figure with monumental installations. Techniques include blowing, casting (kiln-forming), lampworking, fusing, slumping, pâte de verre and cold working.

Collecting

Both historic art glass and contemporary studio glass are widely collected and displayed in museums; Corning Museum of Glass in New York and the Chrysler Museum of Art hold major collections.
