Relative density

Relative density
SynonymSpecific gravity
DefinitionDensity of a substance ÷ density of a reference substance
UnitDimensionless

Relative density, also called specific gravity, is a dimensionless quantity defined as the ratio of the density (mass of a unit volume) of a substance to the density of a given reference material. Specific gravity for liquids is nearly always measured with respect to water at its densest (at 4 °C or 39.2 °F); for gases, the reference is air at room temperature (20 °C or 68 °F).

If a substance's relative density is less than 1 then it is less dense than the reference; if greater than 1 then it is denser. If the relative density is exactly 1 then the densities are equal. Two substances with the same volume have the same mass only if their relative densities are equal.

Measurement

Relative density can be measured with a hydrometer, a pycnometer, or digital oscillating U-tube meters. Brewers, vintners and the petroleum industry use specific gravity scales such as the Plato, Baumé and API gravity scales.

Uses

Geologists and mineralogists use specific gravity to identify minerals; gemologists use it to identify gemstones. In medicine, urine specific gravity is a routine measure of concentration. Relative density also determines buoyancy: a substance will float in a reference liquid if its relative density is less than 1.
