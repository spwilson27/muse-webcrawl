Hostel

A hostel is a form of low-cost, sociable lodging in which guests rent a bed — usually in a dormitory with shared bathroom, lounge and kitchen facilities — rather than a private room. Hostels are especially popular with backpackers, students and budget travellers.

History

The youth hostel movement began in Germany in 1909–1912 when schoolteacher Richard Schirrmann organized overnight stays for school groups and founded the first permanent youth hostel at Altena Castle; the German Youth Hostel Association followed in 1919. The idea spread internationally, and the International Youth Hostel Federation (now Hostelling International) was founded in 1932. The YHA in England and Wales dates from 1930.

Features

Modern hostels range from traditional dormitory-only establishments to "poshtels" with private en-suite rooms. Common features include bunk beds, individual lockers, communal kitchens, common rooms fostering social interaction, organized activities, and central locations. Prices depend on the sharing of space: a dorm bed costs considerably less than a private room, and less than most hotel rooms.

Other meanings

The term also denotes residential accommodation for specific groups — student hostels, workers' hostels, and supported housing for homeless people — in some countries.
