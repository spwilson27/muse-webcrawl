Kavir National Park (Persian: پارک ملی کویر) is a protected area in north-central Iran, lying on the western edge of the Dasht-e Kavir, the great salt desert of the Iranian plateau. Covering roughly 4,000 square kilometres about 120 km south of Tehran, it preserves a landscape of salt flats, gravel plains, sand dunes and isolated rocky hills known locally as an example of the "kavir" desert ecosystem.

Landscape and wildlife

The park has an arid climate with minimal rainfall and extreme summer heat. Vegetation is sparse — halophytic shrubs, saxaul and tamarisk around seasonal watercourses. Despite the harshness, the park shelters Persian gazelles (jebeer), wild sheep, wild goats (ibex), striped hyenas, sand cats, caracals, wolves, and the rare Asiatic cheetah and Persian onager, both the subjects of conservation programmes. The seasonal salt lake Namak lies partly within the region.

Within the park stands the Siah-Kuh ("Black Mountain") range and the historic Qasr-e Bahram caravanserai, a Safavid-era staging post on the old road to Isfahan, near which ancient qanat irrigation lines can still be traced.

Protection

The area has been protected since 1964 and was designated a national park in 1982; it is also recognised by UNESCO as a biosphere reserve. Threats include poaching, competition from domestic livestock, and drought, which have reduced populations of the cheetah and onager across central Iran.
