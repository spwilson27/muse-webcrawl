Landmarks in Paris

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Paris, the capital of France, contains an exceptional concentration of famous landmarks — monuments, churches, museums, bridges and districts recognised worldwide. The historic centre along the Seine was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1991. This article surveys the most notable of them.

Major monuments

  • Eiffel Tower (1889) — Gustave Eiffel's 330-metre iron lattice tower, built for the World's Fair of 1889; the most visited paid monument in the world.
  • Arc de Triomphe (1836) — Napoleon I's triumphal arch at the Place Charles de Gaulle, honouring those who fought for France, with the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier beneath.
  • Notre-Dame Cathedral (begun 1163) — masterpiece of French Gothic architecture on the Île de la Cité; severely damaged by fire in April 2019 and reopened in December 2024 after restoration.
  • Sacré-Cœur Basilica (1914) — white travertine basilica crowning Montmartre, the city's highest point.
  • Panthéon (1790) — neoclassical mausoleum containing the remains of Voltaire, Rousseau, Victor Hugo, Marie Curie and others.
  • Les Invalides (1670s) — complex founded by Louis XIV for wounded soldiers, containing the tomb of Napoleon under its golden dome.
  • Luxor Obelisk — a 3,000-year-old Egyptian obelisk, erected in the Place de la Concorde in 1836.

Museums

  • Louvre — the world's most-visited museum, housed in a former royal palace and entered through I. M. Pei's glass pyramid (1989); home of the Mona Lisa and the Venus de Milo.
  • Musée d'Orsay — Impressionist and Post-Impressionist masterpieces in a converted Beaux-Arts railway station.
  • Centre Pompidou (1977) — high-tech landmark housing the national museum of modern art.

Districts, gardens and bridges

Celebrated districts include Montmartre, the Latin Quarter, Le Marais and Saint-Germain-des-Prés. The Champs-Élysées runs from the Place de la Concorde to the Arc de Triomphe. Gardens include the Tuileries and the Luxembourg Gardens. Of Paris's 37 bridges over the Seine, the Pont Neuf (1607) is the oldest, and the Pont Alexandre III (1900) the most ornate. In the modern business district of La Défense stands the Grande Arche (1989), aligned on the city's historic axis.
