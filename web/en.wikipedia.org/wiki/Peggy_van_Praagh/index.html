Dame Peggy van Praagh (1910–1990) was a British ballet dancer, teacher and director who became the founding artistic director of the Australian Ballet.

Career in Britain

Born in London, van Praagh trained with Margaret Morris and others and danced with companies including Antony Tudor's London Ballet and the Sadler's Wells Ballet. Injured during the Second World War years, she turned increasingly to teaching, producing and administration, becoming a noted exponent and stager of Tudor's ballets.

Australia

In 1960 van Praagh was invited to become artistic director of the newly formed Australian Ballet, which gave its first performance in 1962. She built the company's repertoire, combining classics with works by Australian choreographers, and established its international touring reputation. She was made a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 1970. Her leadership laid the foundations for ballet in Australia, and the Peggy van Praagh Auditorium and various awards commemorate her.
