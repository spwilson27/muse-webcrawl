Sheet pile

A sheet pile is a section of sheet material with interlocking edges that is driven into the ground to provide earth retention and excavation support. Sheet piles, also called piling, are most commonly made of steel, but may also be formed of timber or reinforced concrete.

Sheet piles are designed to resist vertical, horizontal and lateral loads by creating a continuous vertical wall. They are widely used as retaining walls, cofferdams, seawalls, and in the construction of basements, underground car parks and bridge abutments.

Construction

Individual sheet piles are driven into the soil one at a time using vibratory hammers, impact hammers, or hydraulic presses. The interlocking edges (clutches) of adjacent piles engage as each new pile is installed, forming a continuous barrier. Cold-formed and hot-rolled steel sections are the most common profiles; the "Z" and "U" shapes are frequently used because their geometry places material near the outer edges of the wall, maximising the section modulus.

Design

Design of sheet pile walls takes account of active and passive earth pressures, water pressures, surcharge loads, and the bending strength of the section. Walls may be cantilevered for modest retained heights, or supported by one or more levels of anchors or struts for deeper excavations.

Applications

Typical applications include temporary cofferdams for bridge pier construction, permanent quay walls and bulkheads, flood defence walls, cutoff walls beneath dams, and shoring for deep excavations in urban areas where space is limited.
