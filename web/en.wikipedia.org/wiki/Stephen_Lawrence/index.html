Stephen Adrian Lawrence (13 September 1974 – 22 April 1993) was an 18-year-old Black British student from Plumstead, south-east London, who was murdered in an unprovoked, racially motivated attack while waiting at a bus stop in Eltham on the evening of 22 April 1993, with his friend Duwayne Brooks. The failure of the Metropolitan Police investigation became one of the most influential cases in modern British history, reshaping the criminal law and the country's understanding of institutional racism.

The murder and investigations

Lawrence was attacked by a gang of five or six white youths who shouted racial slurs, and was stabbed twice, severing arteries; he managed to run over 100 yards before collapsing. Despite the names of suspects being given to police within days, arrests came slowly and evidence was mishandled. A private prosecution brought by Lawrence's parents, Neville and Doreen Lawrence, collapsed in 1996, as did an earlier prosecution attempt, and an inquest in 1997 returned a verdict of unlawful killing "in a completely unprovoked racist attack by five white youths".

The Macpherson Inquiry

In 1997 the Home Secretary, Jack Straw, ordered a public inquiry led by Sir William Macpherson. Its 1999 report concluded that the original investigation was "marred by a combination of professional incompetence, institutional racism and a failure of leadership", and made 70 recommendations covering police practice, the recording of racist incidents, and public institutions generally. The report is widely credited with changing British public life, and the Race Relations (Amendment) Act 2000 extended anti-discrimination duties to the police and other public bodies.

Convictions and legacy

The case also led to the reform of the double jeopardy rule in the Criminal Justice Act 2003, allowing retrial after acquittal for serious offences on new and compelling evidence. Following a cold-case forensic review that found Lawrence's DNA evidence on their clothing, Gary Dobson and David Norris were convicted of the murder on 3 January 2012 and sentenced to life imprisonment. The other suspects have never been convicted. Doreen Lawrence founded the Stephen Lawrence Charitable Trust (now Blueprint for All), and 22 April is marked as Stephen Lawrence Day in the United Kingdom.
