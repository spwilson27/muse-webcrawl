Luristan bronzes (also spelled Lorestan bronzes) are small cast bronze objects from the Early Iron Age found in the mountainous Lorestan region of western Iran, in and around the Zagros Mountains. Most were produced roughly between 1000 and 650 BCE and came to scholarly attention after large numbers appeared on the art market from the late 1920s onward, many looted from unrecorded burials.

A Luristan "master of animals" finial with confronted ibexes

Styles and subjects

The bronzes display a distinctive, highly stylised art: confronted animals such as ibexes, stags and felines, fantastic hybrid creatures, and "master of animals" figures flanked by beasts. Common types include standards and finials in the form of openwork tubes topped by figures, cheekpieces and bits for horses, axes, daggers, quivers, vessels, jewellery and dress pins with elaborate heads. Because most pieces lack archaeological context, their exact function and the identity of their makers — possibly nomadic or semi-nomadic peoples of the Zagros, sometimes linked to the Cimmerians or early Medes — remain debated.

Collections

Important collections are held by the Louvre, the British Museum, the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York and the National Museum of Iran in Tehran. The flood of market pieces also produced many forgeries and pastiches, and authentication of unprovenanced Luristan bronzes is a persistent problem.
