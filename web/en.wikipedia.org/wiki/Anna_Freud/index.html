Anna Freud

Anna Freud
Born: 3 December 1895, Vienna, Austria-Hungary
Died: 9 October 1982 (aged 86), London, England
Known for: Child psychoanalysis, defence mechanisms
Parent: Sigmund Freud

Anna Freud (3 December 1895 – 9 October 1982) was a British psychoanalyst of Austrian Jewish descent. She was the youngest daughter of Sigmund Freud, the founder of psychoanalysis, and became a leading figure in the field in her own right, pioneering the psychoanalytic treatment of children and systematising the theory of the ego's defence mechanisms.

Early life

Born in Vienna, Anna Freud trained as a teacher before turning to psychoanalysis. She was analysed by her father, became a member of the Vienna Psychoanalytic Society in 1922, and began working with children, developing techniques adapted from adult analysis, including attention to the child's relationships and developmental stage.

The Ego and the Mechanisms of Defence

Her 1936 book The Ego and the Mechanisms of Defence catalogued defences including repression, regression, reaction formation, isolation, undoing, projection, introjection, turning against the self, reversal, and sublimation. The work helped establish ego psychology and made her a central figure in debates with the school of Melanie Klein over child analysis.

Later work

After fleeing Nazi Austria in 1938, she settled in London, where she founded the Hampstead Child Therapy Course and Clinic (now the Anna Freud National Centre for Children and Families). Her studies of children separated from their parents during the Second World War influenced developmental psychology and child welfare law. She remained director of the clinic until her death in 1982.
