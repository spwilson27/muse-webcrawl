FIFA World Cup Trophy

The FIFA World Cup Trophy is a gold trophy awarded to the winners of the FIFA World Cup association football tournament. Since the advent of the World Cup in 1930, two trophies have been used: the Jules Rimet Trophy from 1930 to 1970, and the FIFA World Cup Trophy from 1974 to the present day.

The current FIFA World Cup Trophy, introduced in 1974.

Jules Rimet Trophy

The first trophy, originally named Victory and later renamed in honour of FIFA president Jules Rimet, was made of gold-plated sterling silver on a lapis lazuli base. Brazil won it outright in 1970 after their third title. The original was stolen in Brazil in 1983 and has never been recovered.

Current trophy

The current trophy, designed by Italian artist Silvio Gazzaniga and introduced at the 1974 World Cup, is made of 18-carat gold with bands of malachite on its base. It stands 36.8 centimetres high and weighs 6.175 kilograms. It depicts two human figures holding up the Earth. The names of winning countries are engraved on its base. Unlike its predecessor, it remains the property of FIFA, and winning teams receive a gold-plated bronze replica.
