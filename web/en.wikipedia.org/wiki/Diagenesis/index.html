Diagenesis

Diagenesis is the process that describes physical and chemical changes in sediments first caused by water-rock interactions, microbial activity, and compaction after their initial deposition. Increased pressure and temperature only start to play a role as sediments become buried much deeper in the Earth's crust.

In the early stages, the transformation of poorly consolidated sediments into sedimentary rock (lithification) is simply accompanied by a reduction in porosity and water expulsion, while their main mineral assemblages remain unaltered. As the rock is carried deeper by further deposition above, its organic content is progressively transformed into kerogens and bitumens.

Processes

Key diagenetic processes include compaction, cementation (precipitation of minerals such as calcite, quartz or clays in pore spaces), dissolution, recrystallisation, and the activity of bacteria, which is especially important in the shallowest zone. The study of diagenesis in rocks is used to understand the geologic history they have experienced and the nature and type of fluids that have circulated through them.

Role in petroleum geology

Diagenesis controls the porosity and permeability that make a rock a good hydrocarbon reservoir, and it governs the maturation of organic matter into oil and gas in source rocks. From a petroleum perspective, it is integral to the assessment of suitable reservoirs and productive shale-gas formations.

Anthropology and archaeology

The term is also used for the post-depositional alteration of bone and other organic materials in archaeological deposits, affecting their preservation and analysis.
