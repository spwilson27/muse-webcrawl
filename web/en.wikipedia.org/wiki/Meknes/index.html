Meknes

Meknes (Arabic: مكناس, romanised: maknās) is one of the four imperial cities of Morocco, located in northern central Morocco and the sixth largest city by population in the kingdom. Founded in the 11th century by the Almoravids as a military settlement, Meknes became the capital of Morocco under Sultan Moulay Ismail ibn Sharif (1672–1727), son of the founder of the Alaouite dynasty. The city has a population of roughly 630,000 (2022 estimates) and is the capital of the Fès-Meknes region.

History

The name Meknes derives from the Berber tribe of the Miknasa. The Almoravid fortress town grew under the Almohads and Marinids. Under Moulay Ismail, Meknes was transformed with vast palaces, granaries (the Heri es-Souani), stables said to hold 12,000 horses, enormous reservoirs, gardens, and some 25 km of walls, built partly by armies of labourers and captives; the monumental gate Bab Mansur, completed in 1732, is regarded as one of the finest in Morocco. After Ismail's death and earthquake damage in 1755, the capital moved back to Fes and Meknes declined, though it later revived as an agricultural market centre under colonial and modern administrations.

Geography and economy

Meknes lies on a plateau at about 550 m elevation between the Middle Atlas and the Rif foothills, in one of Morocco's most fertile agricultural regions, noted for olives, cereals, and the wine-growing areas around the nearby Saïss plain. The ancient Roman city of Volubilis and the holy town of Moulay Idriss lie nearby. The historic medina and the imperial city of Moulay Ismail were designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996.
