UNESCO Convention on the Protection of the Underwater Cultural Heritage

Underwater Cultural Heritage Convention
Signed2 November 2001, Paris
Effective2 January 2009
DepositaryUNESCO
LanguagesArabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, Spanish

The Convention on the Protection of the Underwater Cultural Heritage is a treaty adopted on 2 November 2001 by the General Conference of UNESCO. The convention is intended to protect "all traces of human existence having a cultural, historical or archaeological character" which have been under water for at least 100 years. This includes the protection of shipwrecks, sunken cities, prehistoric art, and other submerged heritage.

Content

The convention establishes basic principles: states parties must preserve underwater cultural heritage, with preservation in situ (in its original location) as the preferred option; the commercial exploitation of such heritage is fundamentally incompatible with its protection; and recovered heritage must be properly conserved, documented, and made accessible. Its Annex contains 36 Rules concerning activities directed at underwater cultural heritage, setting professional archaeological standards for project design, funding, documentation, conservation, and reporting.

The convention complements the provisions on underwater heritage in the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (articles 149 and 303) and applies to internal waters, territorial seas, contiguous zones, exclusive economic zones, continental shelves, and the high seas (the "Area").

Entry into force and ratification

The convention entered into force on 2 January 2009, three months after the deposit of the twentieth instrument of ratification. Dozens of states have since become parties, although some major maritime nations—including the United States and the United Kingdom—have not ratified it, in part over concerns about sovereign immunity of sunken warships and the law of salvage, though several apply its Annex Rules as best practice.
