Jehoram of Israel

Reign: c. 852 – 841 BCE
Predecessor: Ahaziah
Successor: Jehu
Dynasty: House of Omri
Father: Ahab
Mother: Jezebel

Jehoram (Hebrew: יְהוֹרָם, Yehoram, "Yahweh is exalted"; also called Joram) was the ninth king of the northern Kingdom of Israel, reigning for twelve years in the mid-9th century BCE. He was a son of Ahab and Jezebel and succeeded his elder brother Ahaziah.

Reign

According to the Second Book of Kings, Jehoram removed the sacred pillar of Baal his father had made, though he continued in other practices the biblical text criticizes. His reign was marked by wars against Moab and Aram (Syria). Together with Jehoshaphat of Judah and the king of Edom, he campaigned against Mesha, king of Moab; the campaign is also known from the Moabite Mesha Stele, which presents the conflict from the Moabite perspective.

During the Aramean siege of Samaria, the city suffered a severe famine until, in the biblical account, the besiegers fled. Jehoram also benefited from the ministry of the prophet Elisha, whose miracles included the healing of the Aramean commander Naaman.

Death

Wounded in battle against Hazael of Aram at Ramoth-Gilead, Jehoram withdrew to Jezreel to recover. There his army commander Jehu, anointed king by a disciple of Elisha, revolted and killed Jehoram, throwing his body into the field of Naboth — an act presented as vengeance for Ahab's seizure of Naboth's vineyard. Jehu's coup ended the Omride dynasty.

Chronology

William F. Albright dated Jehoram's reign to 849–842 BCE, while Edwin R. Thiele proposed 852–841 BCE.
