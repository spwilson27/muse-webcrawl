Blithe Spirit (play)

Blithe Spirit
Written byNoël Coward
Premiere2 July 1941, Piccadilly Theatre, London
GenreComic play
SettingThe drawing room of Charles Condomine's house in Kent

Blithe Spirit is a comic play by Noël Coward, first performed in London in 1941. The title comes from Percy Bysshe Shelley's poem "To a Skylark" ("Hail to thee, blithe Spirit!"). The play was a major wartime success and became one of Coward's best-known works.

Plot

Novelist Charles Condomine, researching a book about a fraudulent medium, invites the eccentric clairvoyant Madame Arcati to hold a séance at his house. The séance unexpectedly summons the ghost of Charles's first wife, Elvira, who is visible only to him and who sets about sabotaging his second marriage to Ruth. Comic chaos follows; when an attempt to exorcise Elvira instead kills Ruth, Charles finds himself haunted by both wives, who wreck the house as he escapes them.

Production history

Coward wrote the play in less than a week in 1941. The original London production, with Margaret Rutherford as Madame Arcati, ran for 1,997 performances, then a record for a non-musical play in the West End. It opened on Broadway the same year. The play has been revived frequently, including notable productions with Angela Lansbury as Madame Arcati in London and on tour.

Adaptations

David Lean directed a 1945 film version starring Rex Harrison and Margaret Rutherford. The play was also adapted as the Broadway musical High Spirits (1964) and remade as a film in 2020 with Judi Dench as Madame Arcati.
