Butterfly doors

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Type: Automobile door design
First use: Alfa Romeo 33 Stradale, 1967

Butterfly doors are a type of car door sometimes seen on high-performance cars. They are similar to scissor doors: while scissor doors move straight up via hinge points at the bottom of the A-pillar, butterfly doors also move outwards as they open, which makes for easier entry and exit and saves space beside the car.

The hinges are mounted along the A-pillar and roof edge, so the door rotates up and outward in a motion resembling a butterfly's wings. The design was first used on the 1967 Alfa Romeo 33 Stradale. It later appeared on the McLaren F1 and became a signature of McLaren's production supercars, as well as appearing on cars such as the Ferrari Enzo, the BMW i8 and the Toyota Sera.

Advantages and drawbacks

Butterfly doors can improve access to cars with wide sills and low roofs, and require less lateral clearance than conventional doors when partially open. However, they are heavier and more complex than conventional doors, can complicate roof strength and sealing, and may trap occupants if the car rolls over, since the doors cannot open against the ground.
