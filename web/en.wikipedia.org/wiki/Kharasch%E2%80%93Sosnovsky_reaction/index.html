Kharasch–Sosnovsky reaction

The Kharasch–Sosnovsky reaction is a radical oxidation that converts an alkene bearing an allylic C–H bond into an allylic alcohol derivative, typically an allylic ester, using a copper catalyst and a perester oxidant such as tert-butyl peroxybenzoate.

Mechanism

The reaction proceeds through a radical mechanism involving a copper(I)/copper(II) catalytic cycle. Hydrogen atom abstraction at the allylic position gives an allyl radical, which is trapped by a copper(II) benzoate species to form the allylic benzoate product.

History

The reaction was reported by Morris S. Kharasch and G. Sosnovsky in 1958. Later variants have employed chiral copper complexes to achieve enantioselective allylic oxidation.
