Ulaanbaatar Railway

Founded: 1949
Headquarters: Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia
Type: Joint-stock company (Mongolia–Russia)
Network: Trans-Mongolian Railway and branches

Ulaanbaatar Railway (Mongolian: Улаанбаатар төмөр зам, Ulaanbaatar tömör zam), abbreviated UBTZ, is the national railway operator of Mongolia. It is a joint-stock company owned equally by the governments of Mongolia and Russia and operates the Trans-Mongolian Railway, the country's principal rail artery.

History

The company was established in 1949 by an agreement between Mongolia and the Soviet Union. The main line, completed in stages through the 1950s, links Ulan-Ude in Russia with Ulaanbaatar and continues south to Zamyn-Üüd on the Chinese border, forming part of the shortest rail route between Moscow and Beijing. Construction was carried out with Soviet assistance, and the line uses the 1,520 mm broad gauge.

Network and operations

UBTZ operates roughly 1,800 kilometres of track, including branch lines to Erdenet, Baganuur and other industrial centres. Rail transport carries the overwhelming majority of Mongolia's freight, including coal, copper concentrate and transit traffic between Russia and China, as well as international passenger trains such as the Moscow–Beijing service.
