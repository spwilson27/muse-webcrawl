The 1st Armored Division, nicknamed "Old Ironsides", is a combined arms division of the United States Army. One of the Army's oldest armored formations, it first saw combat in North Africa during the Second World War and has since served in the Cold War, the Gulf War, and the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan. Since 2011 the division has been headquartered at Fort Bliss, Texas.

Second World War

Activated on 15 July 1940 at Fort Knox, Kentucky, the division landed in North Africa as part of Operation Torch in November 1942 and fought in the Tunisia campaign, suffering heavy losses at the Battle of Kasserine Pass before recovering to participate in the final Axis defeat in Africa. It then fought in Italy, landing at Anzio and advancing through the Gothic Line, ending the war in northern Italy in 1945.

Cold War and Gulf War

Reactivated in 1951, the division served as a training formation and later deployed to Germany, where it spent much of the Cold War. In 1990–91 it deployed to Saudi Arabia and fought in the Gulf War, taking part in the Battle of Medina Ridge, one of the largest tank battles of the conflict, during the ground offensive that liberated Kuwait.

Iraq and Afghanistan

The division deployed to Iraq in 2003 as Task Force 1st Armored Division, responsible for Baghdad and later extended for operations in the south, and returned for further tours in 2007–08 and 2009–10. Elements also deployed to Afghanistan. In May 2011 the division colors moved from Wiesbaden, Germany, to Fort Bliss, Texas, its current home station.
