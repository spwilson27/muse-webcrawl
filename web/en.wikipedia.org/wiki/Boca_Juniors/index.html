Club Atlético Boca Juniors is an Argentine sports club headquartered in the La Boca neighborhood of Buenos Aires, best known for its professional football team, one of the most successful in South America. Founded in 1906 by Italian — particularly Genoese — immigrants, Boca wear blue and gold, colors adopted in 1907 after the flag of the first ship to dock in port, a Swedish vessel. Their home ground, La Bombonera ("the chocolate box"), is famous for its steep stands and intense atmosphere, and their rivalry with River Plate, the Superclásico, is among the fiercest in world football.

History

Boca joined the top flight in 1913 and won their first league championship in 1919. Amassing dozens of domestic titles, they became a continental power especially from the late 1970s and again under coach Carlos Bianchi (1998–2001, 2003–2004), winning the Copa Libertadores in 2000, 2001, and 2003 and beating Real Madrid and AC Milan in Intercontinental Cup finals. In total Boca have won six Copa Libertadores titles (1977, 1978, 2000, 2001, 2003, 2007), second only to Independiente among Argentine clubs, and three Intercontinental Cups.

Players

Diego Maradona played for Boca in 1981 and returned to finish his career there (1995–1997). Other celebrated players include Juan Román Riquelme, Martín Palermo — the club's all-time top scorer — Carlos Tevez, and Fernando Gago. Juan Román Riquelme later became club president in 2023.

Supporters and other sports

Boca's fans, La Doce ("the twelfth man"), are central to the club's identity as the team of the working class and immigrants. The multi-sport club also fields successful basketball and volleyball teams and has historically supported many other disciplines.
