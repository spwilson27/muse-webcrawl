Mural

A mural is any piece of graphic artwork painted or applied directly on a wall, ceiling or other permanent substrate. Mural techniques include fresco, mosaic, graffiti and marouflage. The word mural derives from the Latin murus, meaning "wall". Murals are distinguished from other forms of painting by their direct integration with the architecture of a space.

History

Wall painting is among the oldest art forms: Upper Palaeolithic cave paintings at sites such as Chauvet and Lascaux date back some 30,000–40,000 years. Ancient Egyptian tomb paintings, Minoan frescoes at Knossos, Roman wall paintings preserved at Pompeii, and the murals of Ajanta and Bonampak continued the tradition. In the Italian Renaissance, fresco technique reached its peak with works such as Michelangelo's Sistine Chapel ceiling (1508–1512) and Raphael's Vatican stanze.

In the 20th century, Mexican muralism — led by Diego Rivera, José Clemente Orozco and David Alfaro Siqueiros, "Los Tres Grandes" — used public walls to depict history and social struggle, influencing public art programmes worldwide, including the Works Progress Administration murals of the New Deal in the United States. From the 1960s onward, community mural movements in cities such as Los Angeles, Belfast, Derry and Philadelphia combined art with local identity and political expression, while graffiti and street art brought murals into contemporary urban culture.

Techniques

In buon fresco, pigment is applied to wet lime plaster so the paint becomes part of the wall as it dries. Fresco secco is painted on dry plaster. Modern murals are often painted in acrylic or aerosol paint, sometimes after projection or gridding of a design, and may be protected with anti-graffiti coatings.
