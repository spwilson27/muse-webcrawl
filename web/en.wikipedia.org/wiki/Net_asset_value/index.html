Net asset value

Net asset value (NAV) is the value of an entity's assets minus the value of its liabilities, most often used in the context of a mutual fund or exchange-traded fund (ETF). For a fund, the per-share NAV is calculated by dividing the total net assets by the number of shares outstanding, typically once each business day after the close of markets.

Use in funds

Open-end mutual funds buy and sell shares at prices based on the next computed NAV ("forward pricing"). ETFs trade on exchanges at market prices that may sit at a premium or discount to NAV, with authorized participants arbitraging the difference through creation and redemption of shares. Real estate investment trusts and closed-end funds also report NAV, and their market prices can deviate persistently from it.

Accounting context

The term is closely related to book value or shareholders' equity in corporate accounting, though fund NAV uses market prices for securities held rather than historical cost.
