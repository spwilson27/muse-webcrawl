Chocolate truffle

Chocolate truffles coated in cocoa

A chocolate truffle is a type of chocolate confectionery traditionally made with a chocolate ganache centre coated in chocolate, cocoa powder, coconut, or chopped toasted nuts (typically hazelnuts or almonds), usually in a spherical, conical, or curved shape. The name derives from their resemblance in appearance to the truffle, an edible fungus.

History

Chocolate truffles are said to have been created in the French town of Chambéry by patissier Louis Dufour in December 1895, when, having run out of other ideas for Christmas confections, he shaped flavored ganache into balls and dusted them with cocoa. They later gained popularity through the Prestat shop in London, established in 1902, which popularized the "Napoleon III" truffle.

Varieties

Major regional types include the Swiss truffle, made by emulsifying fresh cream and butter into melted chocolate and dusted with cocoa; the French truffle, made with fresh cream and chocolate and rolled in cocoa or nut powder; the American truffle, a half-egg-shaped mixture of dark or milk chocolate and butterfat; and the Belgian truffle or praline, with a hard chocolate shell filled with ganache, buttercream, or nut paste. Modern variants flavor the ganache with liqueurs, coffee, fruit, spices, or sea salt.
