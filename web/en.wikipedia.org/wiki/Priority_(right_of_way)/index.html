Priority (right of way)

Placeholder image: a "priority road" sign (Vienna Convention)

Priority, or right of way, in traffic law is the legal right of a pedestrian, vehicle, or vessel to proceed with precedence at a particular place or in a specific situation, requiring others to yield until it has passed. Priority rules govern behaviour at intersections, crossings, and in navigation.

Road traffic

At uncontrolled intersections many jurisdictions apply priority to the right: a driver must give way to vehicles approaching from the right. Elsewhere, major/minor road relationships, signs (such as "give way" and the yellow-diamond "priority road" sign), markings, and signals allocate priority. Pedestrians typically have priority on marked crossings, and emergency vehicles are granted special precedence.

Variations

Priority rules historically derived from custom and were codified with motor traffic in the early 20th century. The United Kingdom generally does not use priority to the right, relying instead on markings and signs; most of continental Europe does. In the United States, right of way at four-way stops is typically taken in order of arrival, with ties resolved by yielding to the right.

Other modes

At sea, the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGs) define stand-on and give-way vessels, including the rule that a vessel approaching from starboard has priority. Similar priority conventions exist for aircraft and for railways at level crossings.
