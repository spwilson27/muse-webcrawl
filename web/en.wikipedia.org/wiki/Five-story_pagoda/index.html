A five-story pagoda (Japanese: 五重塔, gojū-no-tō) is a traditional Japanese Buddhist tower with five roofs, derived from the Indian stupa through the Chinese pagoda. Built almost entirely of wood, Japanese five-story pagodas are renowned for surviving centuries of earthquakes, a resilience attributed to their flexible joinery and a loose central pillar.

History

Buddhism reached Japan in the 6th century, and pagodas were built at temple complexes to enshrine relics of the Buddha. The pagoda of Hōryū-ji near Nara, completed around the early 8th century (its timbers felled by 594, according to dendrochronology of the central pillar), is among the oldest surviving wooden buildings in the world. Other famous examples include the pagodas of Yakushi-ji, Daigo-ji in Kyoto, Sensō-ji (Asakusa Kannon) and Itsukushima Shrine.

Structure

Each story is framed in timber with bracket complexes (tokyō) supporting broad, gently upturned eaves roofed with tiles or cypress bark. Crucially, the stories are not rigidly fixed: stacked frames can slide and sway relative to one another. From the ground (or from a stone base) rises the shinbashira, a massive central pillar — in later designs suspended from the top rather than resting on the ground — around which the tower is built.

Earthquake resistance

No wooden five-story pagoda in Japan is known to have collapsed in an earthquake. Engineers attribute this to the layered structure dissipating energy through friction between sliding members, combined with the central pillar acting as a pendulum-like damper or counterweight that restrains excessive sway. These principles influenced the design of modern structures; the Tokyo Skytree (2012) explicitly incorporates a shinbashira-inspired central column damping system.

Famous examples

  • Hōryū-ji (Nara) — 32.5 m; oldest and among the most revered.
  • Yakushi-ji East Pagoda (Nara) — famous for its "frozen music" elegance; appears to have six stories owing to intermediate skirt roofs.
  • Daigo-ji (Kyoto) — built 936; the oldest building in Kyoto.
  • Itsukushima Shrine pagoda (Hiroshima) — vermilion five-story pagoda by the sea, 1407.

Symbolism

The five stories correspond to the five elements (godai) of Japanese Buddhist cosmology, from the base upward: earth, water, fire, wind, and void (sky). The finial (kurin, nine rings) atop the spire also represents Buddhist realms and sacred numbers.
