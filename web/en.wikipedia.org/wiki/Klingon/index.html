Klingons (Klingon: tlhIngan) are a fictional alien species in the American science fiction franchise Star Trek. Developed by screenwriter Gene L. Coon in 1967 for the original series (TOS), they are humanoid aliens characterised by a proud, tradition-bound warrior culture with a strong code of honour. In the original series they were the principal antagonists of the United Federation of Planets; later series developed them into complex, sometimes allied, figures.

Appearance

In TOS, Klingons appeared as dark-skinned humanoids with heavy makeup and facial hair, a look limited by budget. For Star Trek: The Motion Picture (1979) they were redesigned with prominent ridged foreheads, and this became their standard appearance. The discrepancy was joked about in later series and eventually explained in Star Trek: Enterprise as the result of a viral enhancement experiment — the "Augment virus" — that temporarily smoothed the foreheads of a generation of Klingons. Star Trek: Discovery (2017) introduced another, more elaborate redesign.

Culture

Klingon society is organised around honour, combat, and loyalty to one's house and the Empire. Rituals such as the Rite of Ascension mark a warrior's passage; the afterlife includes Sto-vo-kor for the honoured dead and Gre'thor for the dishonoured. The legendary Kahless the Unforgettable, founder of the Empire, is a messianic figure. Klingon opera, blood wine, and weapons such as the bat'leth (a crescent-shaped sword) are recurring cultural elements. Notable Klingon characters include Kang, Kor, Koloth, Gowron, Kahless, Martok, and Worf, the first Klingon main character in Starfleet.

History within the franchise

Klingons debuted in the TOS episode "Errand of Mercy" (1967) as a stand-in for Cold War adversaries. By Star Trek VI: The Undiscovered Country (1991), whose plot echoes the end of the Cold War, the Empire moves toward peace with the Federation (the Khitomer Accords). In The Next Generation and Deep Space Nine, Klingon politics, civil war, and alliance with the Federation against the Dominion receive extensive development.

Klingon language

The Klingon language, tlhIngan Hol, was developed by linguist Marc Okrand for Star Trek III: The Search for Spock (1984) into a fully functional constructed language with its own grammar and vocabulary, described in The Klingon Dictionary (1985). It has a small but dedicated community of speakers, translations of works such as Hamlet and the Epic of Gilgamesh, and is supported by the Klingon Language Institute.
