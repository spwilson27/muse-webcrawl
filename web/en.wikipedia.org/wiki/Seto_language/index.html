Seto language

Native to: Estonia, Russia
Region: Setomaa
Speakers: roughly 13,000 (with Võro)
Family: Uralic → Finnic → South Estonian
Writing system: Latin

Seto (Seto: seto kiilʼ, Estonian: setu keel) is a Finnic language or dialect of South Estonian spoken by the Seto people in Setomaa, the historical region straddling the border between south-eastern Estonia and Russia's Pskov Oblast. It is closely related to Võro, and the two are often grouped together as South Estonian varieties distinct from standard (northern) Estonian.

Classification and status

Whether Seto is a separate language or a dialect of Estonian is politically and culturally contested. Seto activists regard it as a distinct language, an emblem of Seto identity alongside Orthodox Christianity and the leelo polyphonic singing tradition, which UNESCO inscribed on its list of intangible cultural heritage in 2009. The Estonian state generally treats South Estonian varieties as regional languages or dialects; Seto has no official status, though it is used in some local media, education, and cultural life.

Features

Like other South Estonian varieties, Seto preserves features lost in standard Estonian, including vowel harmony remnants and a glottal stop (written with an apostrophe-like character) in certain forms. Its vocabulary and phonology have also been influenced by centuries of contact with Russian, reflecting the Setos' position on the linguistic and religious frontier between Estonia and Russia.

Use and revitalisation

Most speakers live in the Setomaa region, with communities in Tartu and Tallinn. The 2011 Estonian census recorded several thousand speakers of Seto (counted together with Võro by many tabulations). A written standard has been developed in the Latin script, a Seto-language newspaper (Setomaa) is published, and the Seto Congress and cultural associations promote teaching the language to children, though transmission to younger generations is weakened by urbanisation and the dominance of standard Estonian and Russian.
