Frank Rosenthal

Frank Lawrence "Lefty" Rosenthal (June 12, 1929 – October 13, 2008) was an American professional sports gambler, casino executive and organised crime associate. He ran several Las Vegas casinos for the Chicago Outfit in the 1970s and was the basis for Sam "Ace" Rothstein in Martin Scorsese's film Casino (1995).

Early life and gambling career

Born in Chicago, Rosenthal learned the sports-betting trade at Wrigley Field and became one of the country's leading oddsmakers and handicappers. By the 1950s he was running a large bookmaking operation and was repeatedly investigated for bribery and gambling offences; in 1961 he testified before a Senate subcommittee on organised crime, invoking the Fifth Amendment numerous times.

Las Vegas

Rosenthal moved to Las Vegas in 1968. Installed by the Chicago Outfit, he effectively ran the Stardust, Fremont, Marina and Hacienda casinos on behalf of the Argent Corporation, although he was repeatedly denied a gaming licence and held nominal titles such as food and beverage director and entertainment director. He pioneered innovations including female dealers and live sports betting inside a casino sportsbook, and hosted a television talk show from the Stardust.

In 1982 Rosenthal survived a car bombing outside a restaurant in Las Vegas, saved, it was said, by a metal plate in the driver's door. The bombing was never solved.

Later life

Rosenthal was placed in Nevada's "Black Book" of excluded persons in 1988, barring him from every casino in the state. He moved to Florida, where he ran a sports-betting website and worked as a consultant. He died of a heart attack in Miami Beach in 2008.

In popular culture

Journalist Nicholas Pileggi chronicled Rosenthal's career in the book Casino: Love and Honor in Las Vegas (1995), the basis for Scorsese's film, in which Robert De Niro's character was modelled on Rosenthal.
