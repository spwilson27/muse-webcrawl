The Sierpiński carpet is a plane fractal first described by Wacław Sierpiński in 1916. It is one of the generalizations of the Cantor set to two dimensions; the other is the Sierpiński triangle.

Construction

The construction begins with a square, which is divided into nine congruent subsquares in a 3-by-3 grid; the central subsquare is removed. The procedure is then applied recursively to the remaining eight subsquares, ad infinitum. The resulting set has zero area but infinite perimeter, and Hausdorff dimension log 8 / log 3 ≈ 1.8928.

Properties

The Sierpiński carpet is a universal curve: every one-dimensional planar continuum can be embedded in it. Menger's theorem and the topological characterization by Whyburn (1958) show that any compact metric space of topological dimension 1 embeds in the carpet. The three-dimensional analogue of the construction yields the Menger sponge.

Applications

Sierpiński carpet fractals are used in the design of fractal antennas, which can operate at many resonant frequencies, and in models of diffusion and porous media.
