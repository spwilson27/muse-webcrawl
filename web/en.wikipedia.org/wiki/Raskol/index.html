Raskol (Russian: раскол, "schism") is the split in the Russian Orthodox Church in the mid-17th century, when the reforms of Patriarch Nikon — intended to align Russian liturgical practice with Greek practice — were rejected by traditionalists who became known as Old Believers (or Old Ritualists).

Nikon's reforms

From 1653, Patriarch Nikon corrected service books and rituals: the sign of the cross was to be made with three fingers instead of two, "alleluia" sung three times instead of twice, processions moved counter-clockwise, and the spelling of Jesus' name and details of the creed were altered.

Resistance and schism

Archpriest Avvakum and other traditionalists held the changes to be heretical, regarding the two-fingered cross and other old usages as sacred. The councils of 1666–1667 confirmed the reforms and anathematised the old rite; the Solovetsky Monastery resisted in an armed uprising (1668–1676), and many Old Believers fled to the frontiers of Siberia and the north, some resorting to ritual self-immolation. Avvakum was burned at the stake in 1682.

Aftermath

Old Believers formed numerous priestly and priestless denominations and remained subject to disabilities until the edict of toleration of 1905. The Moscow Patriarchate lifted the anathemas on the old rite in 1971, and the Russian Orthodox Old-Rite Church coexists with the mainstream church.
