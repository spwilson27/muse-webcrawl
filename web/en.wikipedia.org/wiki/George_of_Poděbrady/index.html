George of Poděbrady

George of Kunštát and Poděbrady (23 April 1420 – 22 March 1471) was King of Bohemia from 1458 to 1471.

Born: Poděbrady, Bohemia
Died: Prague, Bohemia
Reign: 1458–1471
Religion: Utraquist (Hussite)

George of Poděbrady (Czech: Jiří z Poděbrad) was King of Bohemia from 1458 until his death, the only Hussite king of Bohemia and the last Bohemian king of native Czech stock. He is remembered for his pioneering proposal for a pan-European peace organisation, sometimes seen as a forerunner of the European Union.

Rise to power

George was born at Poděbrady Castle into the noble House of Kunštát. Raised in the Utraquist (moderate Hussite) faith, he fought as a young man in the later Hussite Wars and became leader of the Utraquist party. After 1448 he was the effective ruler of Bohemia as regent for the young Ladislaus the Posthumous, and on Ladislaus's death in 1457 the Bohemian estates elected George king in March 1458.

Reign

George sought to rule a kingdom divided between Utraquists and Catholics under the Compactata of Basel, allowing communion "in both kinds". His attempts to win papal recognition failed: Pope Pius II and then Paul II denounced the Compactata, excommunicated George in 1466, and proclaimed a crusade against him. The Hungarian king Matthias Corvinus invaded and had himself crowned rival King of Bohemia at Olomouc in 1469, seizing Moravia, Silesia, and Lusatia.

The peace project

Between 1462 and 1464 George, advised by Antoine Marini, advanced a remarkable scheme for a league of Christian princes — a congress and common court to settle disputes without war, directed partly against the Ottoman threat. Envoys, including his brother-in-law Leo of Rozmital, toured the courts of Europe, but the plan found no lasting support.

Death and succession

George died in Prague in 1471. To secure peace he had arranged for the succession of the Polish prince Vladislaus Jagiellon rather than his own sons. His heart was buried in the Church of Our Lady before Týn in Prague, beside the tomb of Jan Rokycana.
