Web novel

A web novel is a novel published online. Web novels exist in both free-to-read and pay-to-read forms and are typically released serially, chapter by chapter, on dedicated platforms. The format is especially developed in China, South Korea, Japan, and increasingly in English-language markets, and it differs from the traditional novel in its rapid update cycles, reader feedback loops, and platform-based business models.

By region

In China, platforms such as Qidian (founded 2002, part of China Literature) built large commercial markets around micropayments per chapter, with popular works running to millions of words in genres like xianxia, wuxia, and xuanhuan; successful titles are adapted into television dramas, animation, and games. South Korea's market, led by platforms such as KakaoPage and Naver Series, pairs web novels with webtoon adaptations. In Japan, user-generated sites such as Shōsetsuka ni Narō (founded 2004) became a major pipeline into light novel publishing, with titles like Re:Zero and Sword Art Online (the latter originally a web novel) achieving international franchises. English-language platforms include Royal Road, Scribble Hub, and Amazon's Kindle Vella.

Characteristics

Web novels favor fast pacing, cliffhanger chapter endings, and genre conventions shaped by reader rankings and comments. Authors may publish daily or several times a week, and revenue models include per-chapter unlocks, subscriptions, tipping, and licensing for adaptation. The format has blurred lines with light novels and web fiction more generally, and its most popular works form a significant part of contemporary East Asian popular culture.
