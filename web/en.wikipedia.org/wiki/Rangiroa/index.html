Rangiroa

LocationTuamotu Archipelago, French Polynesia
AreaAbout 79 km² of land; lagoon about 1,446 km²
PopulationAbout 2,500
Main villagesAvatoru, Tiputa

Rangiroa is the largest atoll of the Tuamotu Archipelago in French Polynesia and one of the largest atolls in the world. It consists of a ring of about 415 islets (motu) surrounding an immense lagoon, roughly 80 kilometres long and up to 32 kilometres wide, connected to the ocean chiefly by the Avatoru and Tiputa passes.

Tourism and diving

Rangiroa is renowned for scuba diving, especially drift dives through Tiputa Pass, where grey reef sharks, dolphins, manta rays and other pelagic species are commonly seen. Its lagoon contains the noted "Blue Lagoon", a smaller lagoon formed within the atoll's rim.

Economy

Besides tourism, the local economy depends on black pearl farming, copra production and fishing. Rangiroa also produces a rare coral wine — a vineyard planted on the motu grows grapes in coral soil near the lagoon.
