Death sentence with reprieve

Death sentence with reprieve (Chinese: 死刑缓期执行, sǐxíng huǎnqī zhíxíng, often abbreviated as 死缓, "death reprieve") is a form of capital punishment used in the People's Republic of China and, in a related form, in Vietnam. Under the system, a court imposes a death sentence but suspends its execution for a period of two years. If the condemned person commits no further intentional crime during the reprieve period, the sentence is almost always commuted to life imprisonment; if the person demonstrates meritorious conduct it may be further reduced to a fixed term of imprisonment.

Operation

The suspended death sentence was developed in the People's Republic of China in the early 1950s, during the Campaign to Suppress Counterrevolutionaries, and was described by Mao Zedong as a policy of "death sentence with a two-year reprieve for the purpose of reform through labour". It was subsequently codified in the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

During the two-year reprieve, the prisoner is held in prison and performs labour. At the end of the period:

  • If the prisoner has not committed an intentional crime, the sentence is commuted to life imprisonment.
  • If the prisoner has rendered meritorious service, the sentence may be commuted to a fixed term of not less than 15 and not more than 20 years (later amended to 25 years in serious cases).
  • If the prisoner commits an intentional crime verified by investigation, the death sentence may be carried out with the approval of the Supreme People's Court.

In practice, executions following a reprieve are rare, and the sentence functions effectively as a severe form of life imprisonment. It is frequently applied in cases involving corruption, drug trafficking, and homicide where the court finds circumstances that do not require immediate execution.

Statistics and discussion

Because China does not publish comprehensive execution figures, the exact number of suspended death sentences is not known, but legal scholars and human rights organizations estimate that reprieved sentences may outnumber immediate executions. The institution is sometimes cited by Chinese officials as evidence of a policy of "killing fewer and cautiously killing", while critics argue that the death penalty framework in which it sits remains opaque.

Vietnam

Vietnamese law has included a comparable mechanism under which a death sentence may be commuted if the condemned person cooperates with authorities, remedies the consequences of the crime, or meets other statutory conditions, including, following amendments in 2025, certain corruption cases where most of the misappropriated assets are returned.
