The Yellowstone River is a tributary of the Missouri River, approximately 692 miles (1,114 km) long, in the western United States. Considered the principal tributary of the upper Missouri, it drains a large area of the Rocky Mountains and northern Great Plains in Wyoming, Montana, and North Dakota. It is the longest undammed river in the contiguous United States.

Course

The river rises in northwestern Wyoming near Younts Peak in the Absaroka Range, flows through Yellowstone National Park, drops over the Upper and Lower Falls in the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone, crosses Montana through Paradise Valley and the cities of Livingston, Billings, and Miles City, and joins the Missouri River just east of the Montana–North Dakota border near Fort Union.

History

Indigenous peoples used the river corridor for millennia. Members of the Lewis and Clark Expedition travelled portions of it in 1806, and the river became a route for fur traders and later steamboats supplying Montana mining camps. In 2011 a pipeline rupture near Laurel, Montana, spilled oil into the river, and a larger spill occurred near Glendive in 2015.

Ecology

The Yellowstone supports native cutthroat trout, pallid sturgeon in its lower reaches, and important riparian habitat. It is regarded as one of the finest trout fisheries in the United States and a keystone free-flowing river ecosystem.
