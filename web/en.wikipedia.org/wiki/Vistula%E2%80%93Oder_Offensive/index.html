The Vistula–Oder Offensive was a Red Army operation on the Eastern Front in the European theatre of World War II. Launched on 12 January 1945, it was carried out by the 1st Belorussian Front under Georgy Zhukov and the 1st Ukrainian Front under Ivan Konev against the German Army Group A.

Course of the offensive

Soviet forces burst from their bridgeheads over the Vistula River, shattering the German lines within days. Warsaw was liberated on 17 January, and armoured spearheads advanced up to 500 kilometres in three weeks, reaching the Oder River and establishing bridgeheads less than 70 kilometres from Berlin.

Outcome

The offensive destroyed or mauled dozens of German divisions, liberated much of Poland, including the Auschwitz concentration camp, and placed the Red Army in position for the final Battle of Berlin later in the spring. Konev's forces also overran the industrial region of Upper Silesia largely intact, at Zhukov's request redirected to encircle rather than bombard it.
