Fell

A fell (from Old Norse fall and fjall, "mountain") is a high and barren landscape feature, such as a mountain or moor-covered hill. The term is most often applied in Fennoscandia, Iceland, the Isle of Man and areas of northern England such as the Lake District, the North Pennines and Yorkshire Dales, which were once settled by the Norsemen.

Usage

In Scandinavia, fjell or fjäll denotes mountains generally, or any upland rising above the treeline — including the extensive low-arctic tundra plateaus that are the grazing lands of Sámi reindeer herders. In Cumbria and the northern Pennines, fells are the open, uncultivated hills above the enclosed farmland of the valleys, traditionally grazed by hardy breeds of sheep; the boundary between field and fell is often marked by a drystone "fell wall" and "fell gates". Famous English fells include Scafell Pike, England's highest mountain, Helvellyn and Cross Fell, the highest point of the Pennines.

Related terms and activities

The uplands gave their names to fell running, the sport of running and racing over them, and to the Fell pony, a hardy native pony breed of northern England. In forestry the verb "to fell" — to cut down a tree — is a separate derivation.
