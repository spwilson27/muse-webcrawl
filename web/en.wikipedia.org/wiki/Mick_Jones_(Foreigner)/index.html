Michael Leslie Jones (born 27 December 1944) is an English musician, songwriter and record producer, best known as the founder and lead guitarist of the British-American rock band Foreigner. He is the band's principal songwriter and its only continuous original member, and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Foreigner in 2024. He is not to be confused with Mick Jones of the Clash and Big Audio Dynamite.

Early career

Born in Portsmouth and raised in London, Jones began his career in the early 1960s with the band Nero and the Gladiators, who scored minor UK hits. He moved to France, working as a session guitarist and songwriter, before joining a reformed Spooky Tooth in the early 1970s and later the Leslie West Band in New York.

Foreigner

In 1976, in New York City, Jones formed Foreigner with former King Crimson saxophonist Ian McDonald and vocalist Lou Gramm. The band's self-titled debut album (1977) sold over four million copies in the United States and produced the hits "Feels Like the First Time" and "Cold as Ice". Jones wrote or co-wrote most of the band's catalogue, including "Hot Blooded", "Double Vision", "Urgent", "Waiting for a Girl Like You" and the worldwide number-one ballad "I Want to Know What Love Is" (1984), which he wrote alone.

As Foreigner's producer and bandleader, Jones steered the group through major commercial success in the late 1970s and 1980s and through numerous line-up changes in later decades. By the 2020s he remained the sole original member, though health issues increasingly limited his live appearances.

Other work

Jones has produced albums for other artists, including Van Halen's 5150 (1986) and Billy Joel's Storm Front (1989). He released a solo album, Mick Jones, in 1989, and co-wrote "Bad Love" for Eric Clapton's Journeyman.
