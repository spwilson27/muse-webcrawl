Local government in Hong Kong

Local government in Hong Kong has historically been limited compared with other jurisdictions, because Hong Kong is a special administrative region of China with a highly centralised administration. The principal bodies at the district level are the 18 District Councils, which are advisory and consultative bodies, while executive functions rest with government departments.

History

Under British rule, the Urban Council (from 1883 as the Sanitary Board) and, from 1980, the Regional Council provided municipal services and had elected members. District Boards were created in 1982 to improve local consultation. After the 1997 handover, the two municipal councils were abolished in 2000 and their functions absorbed by government departments, leaving District Councils as the main local representative bodies.

District Councils

The 18 District Councils advise the government on district affairs, fund community projects, and promote recreation and culture. Their composition and electoral arrangements have changed over time, most significantly in reforms before the 2023 elections, which reduced the proportion of directly elected seats and introduced appointment and indirect election by committees.

Administration

Each district has a District Officer from the Home Affairs Department, who coordinates government services locally. Unlike municipalities elsewhere, Hong Kong's local bodies have no independent taxation or legislative powers.
