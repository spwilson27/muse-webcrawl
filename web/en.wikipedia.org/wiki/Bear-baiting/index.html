Bear-baiting
Type: Blood sport
Popular: Medieval and early modern Europe
Status: Banned in most countries

Bear-baiting is a blood sport in which a chained or confined bear is attacked by dogs for public entertainment. It was popular in England from medieval times until it was banned in the 19th century.

History

Baiting arenas such as the Beargarden on London's Bankside — near the Globe Theatre — drew large crowds; bears with names like Harry Hunks became famous. Puritan and later animal-welfare campaigns opposed the practice.

Prohibition

The Cruelty to Animals Act 1835 banned baiting in England and Wales, and similar prohibitions spread. The practice persisted illegally in parts of South Asia into modern times, where it has been targeted by animal welfare groups.

Cultural references

References to bear-baiting appear in Shakespeare and other early modern literature, reflecting its place in popular culture of the period.
