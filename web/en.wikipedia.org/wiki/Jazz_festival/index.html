Jazz festival

[Image: Outdoor jazz festival performance]

A jazz festival is an annual celebration of jazz music, typically held over several days and featuring performances by numerous artists on one or more stages. Jazz festivals vary widely in size and character, from club-based urban events to large open-air festivals that draw hundreds of thousands of visitors and include artists from related genres such as blues, soul, funk, and world music.

History

The Newport Jazz Festival, founded in 1954 by George Wein in Newport, Rhode Island, is generally regarded as the first major jazz festival and set the template for the format. Wein went on to found festivals in New Orleans (the New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Festival, from 1970) and elsewhere. In Europe, the Montreux Jazz Festival (founded 1967) and the North Sea Jazz Festival (founded 1976) became leading events, while the Monterey Jazz Festival (founded 1958) became California's signature festival.

Notable festivals

  • Newport Jazz Festival — Newport, Rhode Island, USA
  • Montreux Jazz Festival — Montreux, Switzerland
  • New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Festival — New Orleans, USA
  • North Sea Jazz Festival — Rotterdam, Netherlands
  • Monterey Jazz Festival — Monterey, California, USA
  • Cape Town International Jazz Festival — Cape Town, South Africa

Format

Many festivals combine headline evening concerts with free daytime stages, workshops, masterclasses, and jam sessions. Large festivals are significant tourist draws for their host cities, and festival recordings — notably from Newport and Montreux — form an important part of the jazz discography.
