Quantitative analysis (chemistry)

In analytical chemistry, quantitative analysis is the determination of the absolute or relative abundance (often expressed as a concentration) of one, several, or all particular substances present in a sample. It contrasts with qualitative analysis, which identifies which substances are present without measuring how much of each there is.

Methods

Once the presence of components is known, quantitative methods measure their amounts. Classical ("wet chemical") methods include gravimetric analysis, in which an analyte is converted to an insoluble product, separated, and weighed, and volumetric analysis (titration), in which the volume of a reagent of known concentration needed to react completely with the analyte is measured.

Modern instrumental methods include spectroscopy (relating absorbance or emission intensity to concentration), chromatography with calibrated detectors, electrochemical methods such as potentiometry and coulometry, and mass spectrometry.

Calibration and standards

Quantitative measurements are made against calibration curves prepared from standards of known concentration. Internal standards, standard additions, and reference materials are used to control for matrix effects, drift, and losses during sample preparation.

Applications

Quantitative analysis underpins quality control in pharmaceuticals, environmental monitoring, clinical diagnostics, food safety, and materials science, where precise composition determines safety, legality, and performance.
