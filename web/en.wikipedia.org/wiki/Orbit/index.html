Orbit

Orbit
DefinitionCurved trajectory of an object under gravity
LawsKepler's three laws (1609–1619)
[Image: An elliptical orbit diagram with a planet sweeping equal areas]

An orbit is the gravitationally curved trajectory of an object around a point in space — a planet around a star, a moon around a planet, a satellite around Earth. Isaac Newton showed orbits follow from his law of gravitation; Kepler had described their shapes empirically.

Types

Orbits are usually elliptical; circular and parabolic/hyperbolic (escape) paths are limiting cases. Artificial satellites use specialised orbits — geostationary above the equator, polar, sun-synchronous — and interplanetary craft borrow gravity assists, as Psyche and Europa Clipper do.
