Topology optimization

Topology optimization
FieldStructural optimization, engineering design
Core methodsSIMP (density method), level-set, BESO
OriginBendsøe & Kikuchi (1988)

Topology optimization is a mathematical method that optimizes the layout of material within a given design space, for a set of loads, boundary conditions, and constraints, with the goal of maximizing (or minimizing) some measure of system performance. Unlike traditional size and shape optimization, topology optimization can create entirely new holes and members, so it typically produces organic, lightweight structures that a designer would not have drawn by hand. It is widely used in aerospace, automotive, and civil engineering, and is a natural partner for additive manufacturing, which can fabricate the complex geometries it generates.

Methodology

The design domain is usually discretized into finite elements, and the algorithm decides where material should be solid and where it should be void. The most common approach is the density-based SIMP method (Solid Isotropic Material with Penalization), in which each element has a continuous density variable between 0 and 1 and its stiffness is scaled by the density raised to a power greater than one, penalizing intermediate densities so the solution converges toward a black-and-white design. Alternatives include level-set methods, which track an explicit boundary, and evolutionary methods such as BESO (bidirectional evolutionary structural optimization).

Formulation and challenges

The classical problem minimizes structural compliance (equivalently, maximizes stiffness) subject to a constraint on the volume of material used, though stress, frequency, thermal, and multi-physics objectives are also common. Practical implementations must contend with numerical artefacts such as checkerboard patterns and mesh dependence, addressed by density or sensitivity filtering, and with manufacturability, addressed by adding overhang, extrusion, or minimum-member-size constraints. Results are often smoothed and reinterpreted in CAD before production.
