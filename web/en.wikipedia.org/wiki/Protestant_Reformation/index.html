The Reformation, also called the Protestant Reformation, was a major theological movement in Western Christianity in 16th-century Europe that posed religious and political challenges to the Roman Catholic Church and papal authority in particular. It is conventionally dated from 1517, when Martin Luther published his Ninety-five Theses against indulgences in Wittenberg.

Reformers including Luther, John Calvin, Huldrych Zwingli and (in England) the architects of the Henrician and Elizabethan settlements emphasised justification by faith alone and scripture's supremacy, rejecting papal primacy; printing spread their ideas rapidly. The Catholic Church responded with its own Counter-Reformation (Council of Trent). The schism permanently divided Western Christianity and contributed to a century of religious wars, ended in part by the Peace of Westphalia (1648).
