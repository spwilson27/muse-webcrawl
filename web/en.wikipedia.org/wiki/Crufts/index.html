Crufts

Crufts is an international dog show held annually in the United Kingdom, organised and hosted by the Kennel Club. It is the largest show of its kind in the world and is held over four days in early March at the National Exhibition Centre (NEC) in Birmingham, England.

Crufts
First held: 1891
Venue: NEC Birmingham
Organiser: The Kennel Club
Attendance: over 150,000 visitors

History

The show is named after its founder, Charles Cruft, a travelling salesman of dog food who organised the first show in 1891 at the Royal Agricultural Hall in Islington, London. After Cruft's death in 1938, the Kennel Club took over the event in 1948. Crufts moved to the NEC in 1991 for its centenary year and has remained there since.

Competition

More than 20,000 dogs compete at Crufts, having qualified at championship shows during the preceding year. Dogs compete within breed and group categories (Toy, Gundog, Working, Pastoral, Terrier, Hound and Utility), with group winners advancing to the Best in Show finale. In addition to conformation showing, the event features agility, flyball, heelwork to music, obedience championships and the Friends for Life award honouring heroic dogs.

Controversy

The show has periodically faced criticism over pedigree breeding practices and breed health, notably after the 2008 BBC documentary Pedigree Dogs Exposed, which led the BBC to drop its coverage. The Kennel Club subsequently introduced revised breed standards and veterinary checks for Best of Breed winners at the show.
