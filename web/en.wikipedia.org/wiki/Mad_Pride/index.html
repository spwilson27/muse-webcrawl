Mad Pride

Mad Pride is a mass movement of the users of mental health services, former users, and their allies, which seeks to reclaim terms and language associated with mental illness, such as "mad," and to celebrate psychiatric consumer, survivor, and ex-patient culture. It emerged in the 1990s alongside psychiatric survivor activism and the wider disability rights movement.

Origins

The first known event organized under the name Mad Pride took place on 18 September 1993 in Toronto, Canada, when it was called "Psychiatric Survivors Day." The event was founded by ex-psychiatric patients including Reva Gerash, together with activists associated with organizations such as People First and the Ontario Psychiatric Survivors' Alliance. An inspiration was the book On Our Own by Judi Chamberlin, a foundational text of the psychiatric survivor movement.

Aims and events

Mad Pride events — including parades, marches, concerts, and "bed pushes" (in which participants push a gurney or psychiatric bed through the streets) — aim to combat stigma, protest forced treatment, and affirm the identities and creativity of people who experience mental distress. Events have since been held in cities around the world, including London, Dublin, Cape Town, and Accra.
