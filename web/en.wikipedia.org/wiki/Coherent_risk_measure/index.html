Coherent risk measure

In the fields of actuarial science and financial economics, a coherent risk measure is a function that satisfies the properties of subadditivity, homogeneity, translation invariance and monotonicity. The concept was introduced by Artzner, Delbaen, Eber and Heath in 1999.

[ Image placeholder: illustration of diversification reducing portfolio risk under a coherent measure ]

Definition

A risk measure ρ is coherent if, for random outcomes X and Y, a deterministic amount m and a positive scalar λ:

  • Subadditivity: ρ(X + Y) ≤ ρ(X) + ρ(Y)
  • Positive homogeneity: ρ(λX) = λρ(X)
  • Translation invariance: ρ(X + m) = ρ(X) − m
  • Monotonicity: if X ≤ Y then ρ(X) ≥ ρ(Y)

Examples

Expected shortfall (conditional value at risk) is a coherent risk measure, whereas value at risk (VaR) is not, because VaR can fail subadditivity and thus penalise diversification.

Representation theorem

Every coherent risk measure can be represented as a supremum of expected losses over a set of probability scenarios, which links the axiomatic definition to robust worst-case evaluation.
