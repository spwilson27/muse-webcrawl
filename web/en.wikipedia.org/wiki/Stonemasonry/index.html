Stonemasonry

Stonemasonry or stone masonry is the craft of shaping and assembling stones into structures, buildings, sculptures and other artefacts. It is one of the oldest activities and professions in human history: many of the long-lasting monuments of the pre-modern world — the Egyptian pyramids, Greek temples, Roman roads and aqueducts, and the great medieval cathedrals — are works of stonemasonry.

A mason working a stone with mallet and chisel.

Materials

Masons work with many kinds of natural stone, chosen for durability, appearance and workability: granite, marble, limestone, sandstone, slate and travertine are among the most common. Stones may be laid dry (without mortar) or bedded in mortar, and dressed to forms ranging from rough rubble to precisely squared ashlar.

Techniques

The craft begins at the quarry, where blocks are split along natural bedding planes using wedges, plugs and feathers, or sawn free. At the banker (the mason's workbench) stones are dressed with mallets, chisels, pitching tools and points; diagonal-toothed "claw" tools and flat chisels finish the surface. Fixer masons set the finished stones in place on site, and carver masons produce ornament, mouldings, lettering and sculpture. Modern practice adds diamond saws, CNC milling and pneumatic tools, but the fundamental hand skills are little changed.

History

Stone construction dates to prehistoric monuments such as megaliths and dry-stone walls. In the medieval period, master masons directed the building of cathedrals and castles, travelling between sites and guarding their geometry in lodge traditions — an organisation that later influenced the fraternal society of Freemasonry, whose name and much of whose symbolism derive from the operative stonemasons' craft.

Training

Traditionally masons served a long apprenticeship — often seven years — progressing from apprentice to journeyman to master. Today training combines college coursework with site experience in banker masonry, fixing, carving, conservation and letter cutting; heritage conservation is a major employer of skilled masons.
