Power-flow study

In power engineering, a power-flow study, or load-flow study, is a numerical analysis of the flow of electric power in an interconnected system. A power-flow study usually uses simplified notations such as a one-line diagram and the per-unit system, and focuses on various aspects of AC power parameters, such as voltages, voltage angles, real power, and reactive power.

Problem formulation

The network is modelled as a set of buses (nodes) connected by transmission lines and transformers. Each bus is classified as a slack (reference) bus, a generator (PV) bus with fixed voltage magnitude and real power, or a load (PQ) bus with fixed real and reactive demand. The power balance equations at each bus are nonlinear because power depends on products of voltages, so they are solved iteratively.

Methods

Classic solution methods include the Gauss–Seidel method and the Newton–Raphson method, the latter dominant for large systems because of its fast (quadratic) convergence. The fast-decoupled load flow exploits the weak coupling between real power/angle and reactive power/voltage in transmission networks for still greater speed. Commercial software packages are used by utilities for planning and operations.

Applications

Load-flow results underpin contingency analysis, generation and transmission planning, voltage stability assessment, optimal power flow (which adds an economic objective), and the setting of transfer limits between areas of a grid.
