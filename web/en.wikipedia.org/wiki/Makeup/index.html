Makeup

Makeup, also called make-up or cosmetics in the decorative sense, refers to products applied to the face and body to enhance, alter or protect appearance. Common items include foundation, concealer, powder, blush, eyeshadow, eyeliner, mascara and lipstick. Makeup is used in daily grooming, fashion, theatre, film and special-effects work, and forms part of a global beauty industry worth hundreds of billions of dollars.

History

Cosmetics have been used for thousands of years: ancient Egyptians of both sexes lined their eyes with kohl made from galena and malachite, and red ochre coloured lips and cheeks in many early cultures. In ancient Greece and Rome, pale skin was fashionable and sometimes achieved with hazardous white lead. Attitudes varied across centuries — makeup was at times associated with theatre and moral suspicion — but the 20th century brought mass production, cinema glamour and entrepreneurs such as Max Factor, Elizabeth Arden and Helena Rubinstein, followed by the countercultural and punk styles of the 1970s and the tutorial-driven beauty culture of social media.

Products and application

Basic makeup typically proceeds from primer and foundation to concealer, powder and colour products for cheeks, eyes and lips. Specialised fields include theatrical and prosthetic makeup (ageing, wounds, creatures), airbrush application, and permanent makeup (cosmetic tattooing). Regulation of ingredients varies by jurisdiction; the EU bans animal testing for cosmetics, and concerns over ingredients such as lead, asbestos-contaminated talc and "forever chemicals" (PFAS) have driven reformulation and clean-beauty marketing.
