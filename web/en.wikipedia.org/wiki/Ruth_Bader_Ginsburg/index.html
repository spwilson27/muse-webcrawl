Ruth Bader Ginsburg (15 March 1933 – 18 September 2020) was an American lawyer and jurist who served as an associate justice of the Supreme Court of the United States from 1993 until her death in 2020. She was the second woman, and the first Jewish woman, to serve on the Court.

Early life and career

Born in Brooklyn to a Jewish immigrant family, Ginsburg graduated from Cornell University in 1954 and went on to Harvard Law School and Columbia Law School, tying for first in her Columbia class despite the era's discrimination against women in the legal profession. She became a professor at Rutgers Law School and later Columbia, where she was the school's first tenured female professor. As director of the Women's Rights Project of the American Civil Liberties Union in the 1970s, she argued six gender-discrimination cases before the Supreme Court, winning five, and laid the groundwork for the constitutional law of sex equality, beginning with Reed v. Reed (1971).

Supreme Court

Appointed to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit by President Jimmy Carter in 1980, Ginsburg was nominated to the Supreme Court by President Bill Clinton in 1993 and confirmed by the Senate 96–3. On the Court she was a leader of its liberal wing, writing majority opinions such as United States v. Virginia (1996), which struck down the male-only admissions policy of the Virginia Military Institute, and forceful dissents, including in Ledbetter v. Goodyear (2007), which prompted Congress to pass the Lilly Ledbetter Fair Pay Act.

Cultural status and death

In her eighties Ginsburg became an unlikely popular-culture icon, known as "Notorious R.B.G." after the rapper The Notorious B.I.G., celebrated for her dissenting opinions and her collection of decorative collars. She died of complications from pancreatic cancer on 18 September 2020, weeks before a presidential election, and became the first woman and first Jewish person to lie in state at the U.S. Capitol. She was succeeded by Amy Coney Barrett.
