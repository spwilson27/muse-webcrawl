Real tennis

Real tennis — one of several games sometimes called "the sport of kings" — is the original racquet sport from which the modern game of tennis (lawn tennis) is derived. It is also known as court tennis in the United States, royal tennis in England and Australia, and courte-paume in France.

History

The game developed in medieval France, evolving from handball played in monastery cloisters into a racquet sport enjoyed by royalty; Henry VIII of England was an enthusiastic player, and a court survives at Hampton Court Palace. For centuries it was the dominant racquet game before lawn tennis, codified in the 1870s, overtook it in popularity.

The court and play

Real tennis is played indoors on an asymmetric enclosed court with sloping roofs (penthouses), openings (galleries), a protruding wall buttress (the tambour), and a main wall with special targets such as the grille and dedans. Balls are solid, handmade cork-cored spheres, and racquets are wooden with tightly strung small heads. Its scoring system shares the 15–30–40–game structure of lawn tennis, but adds the distinctive "chase" mechanism: a shot that bounces twice does not immediately lose the point but sets a chase that the opponent must later beat.

Today

Only a few dozen courts exist worldwide, in the United Kingdom, United States, France, and Australia, but the sport maintains an active competitive circuit and a world championship dating to about 1740 — the longest-running world championship in sport.
