Covenant (religion)

A covenant in religion is a formal, solemn agreement or alliance. In the Abrahamic traditions the term (Hebrew: berith) chiefly denotes the agreements between God and humanity described in the Bible — in particular the covenants with Noah, Abraham and Moses — which structure the relationship between God and the people of Israel and, in Christian theology, between God and the Church.

Biblical covenants

The Noahic covenant (Genesis 9) is made with all living creatures after the Flood, sealed with the rainbow. The Abrahamic covenant (Genesis 12–17) promises Abraham land, descendants and blessing, with circumcision as its sign. The Mosaic covenant, given at Mount Sinai, binds Israel to the Torah in return for God's protection and the Promised Land. The Davidic covenant (2 Samuel 7) promises David an enduring dynasty. Jeremiah's prophecy of a New Covenant (Jeremiah 31) is interpreted in Christianity as fulfilled in Jesus Christ, commemorated in the Eucharist ("the new covenant in my blood").

Theology

Covenant theology is a major framework in Reformed Christianity, organising the whole Bible around the covenants of works, grace and redemption. In Judaism, the covenant remains the foundation of Jewish law, identity and practice.
