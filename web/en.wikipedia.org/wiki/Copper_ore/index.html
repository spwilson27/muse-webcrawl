Copper ore

Copper ores are naturally occurring mineral assemblages from which the metal copper can be extracted at a profit. Copper occurs in a variety of ore minerals, both as native copper and as compounds, chiefly sulfides, oxides and carbonates. Copper mining has been practiced since prehistoric times, and copper was one of the first metals used by humans.

Principal ore minerals

The most important copper ore minerals include:

  • Chalcopyrite (CuFeS2) — the most abundant and economically significant copper ore mineral.
  • Bornite (Cu5FeS4) — sometimes called "peacock ore" for its iridescent tarnish.
  • Chalcocite (Cu2S) and covellite (CuS) — rich sulfide ores, often in supergene enrichment zones.
  • Malachite (Cu2CO3(OH)2) and azurite — green and blue carbonate ores of the oxidized zone.
  • Chrysocolla and cuprite (Cu2O) — oxide-zone minerals.
  • Native copper — metallic copper found in basaltic lavas, famously in Michigan's Keweenaw Peninsula.

Ore deposits

Major deposit types include porphyry copper deposits (the largest source, e.g. Chuquicamata in Chile and Bingham Canyon in Utah), sediment-hosted stratiform deposits (such as the Central African Copperbelt), volcanogenic massive sulfide deposits, skarn deposits and iron oxide copper gold (IOCG) deposits such as Olympic Dam in Australia.

Extraction

Sulfide ores are typically concentrated by froth flotation and smelted, while oxide ores are often treated by heap leaching followed by solvent extraction and electrowinning (SX-EW).
