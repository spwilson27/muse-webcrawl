Port of Liverpool

The Port of Liverpool is a major seaport on the River Mersey in northwest England, centred on the city of Liverpool and extending along both banks of the Mersey and into Liverpool Bay. Once one of the busiest ports in the world, it remains one of Britain's largest, handling tens of millions of tonnes of cargo a year.

History

Liverpool's first enclosed dock, the Old Dock, opened in 1715 and is regarded as the world's first commercial wet dock. During the 18th and 19th centuries the port grew rich on trade with the Americas — including, notoriously, the transatlantic slave trade — and on cotton imports for the mills of Lancashire. At its peak in the early 20th century, a chain of docks stretched for miles along the Mersey waterfront, overlooked by the "Three Graces" of the Pier Head, including the Royal Liver Building. The port declined after the Second World War with containerisation and the shift of trade to the east coast, but revived with the opening of the Royal Seaforth Dock and later the Liverpool2 deep-water container terminal in 2016.

Operations

The port is owned and operated by Peel Ports. It handles containers, bulk liquids and solids, forest products, metals, and roll-on/roll-off ferries to Ireland and the Isle of Man, and is a turnaround port for cruise ships.
