Narikala (Georgian: ნარიყალა) is an ancient fortress overlooking Tbilisi, the capital of Georgia, and the Mtkvari (Kura) River. The fortress consists of two walled sections on a steep hill between the sulphur baths of Abanotubani and the Tbilisi Botanical Garden. It is one of the city's most recognisable landmarks.

History

The fortress was founded in the 4th century, probably during the reign of King Varaz-Bakur, and was known at first as Shuris-tsikhe ("Envied Fortress"). It was greatly expanded by the Umayyad rulers in the 7th–8th centuries, from whom much of the present structure dates, and again under the Safavids in the 16th–17th centuries. The name "Narikala" is believed to derive from Persian nari-qala, "unassailable fortress". David the Builder retook the fortress in 1122, after which it served Georgian kings.

In 1827 an earthquake — or, by another account, an explosion of the fortress's gunpowder stores — destroyed large parts of the walls and the church within. The Church of St. Nicholas inside the lower court was rebuilt in the 1990s on its medieval foundations, with frescoes depicting scenes from the Bible and Georgian history.

Access

The fortress can be reached on foot from the Old Town or by the Tbilisi cable car across the river from Rike Park. At night its walls are floodlit, and the colossal aluminium statue Kartlis Deda ("Mother of Georgia"), erected in 1958, stands nearby on Sololaki Hill.
