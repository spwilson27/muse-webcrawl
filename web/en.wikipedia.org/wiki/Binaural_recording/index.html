Binaural recording
MethodTwo microphones placed at a dummy head's ears
PlaybackHeadphones (optimal)
EffectThree-dimensional stereo sound

Binaural recording is a method of recording sound that uses two microphones arranged to capture the cues the human auditory system uses to localize sound, creating a three-dimensional listening experience when played back through headphones.

Technique

The microphones are typically mounted in the ear canals of a dummy head (such as the Neumann KU 100) or worn in a person's own ears. Because the head, torso, and outer ears filter sound differently depending on its direction, the recording preserves interaural time and level differences as well as spectral cues described by the head-related transfer function (HRTF).

History and uses

Experiments date to the early 1880s, when Clément Ader's Théâtrophone transmitted opera performances to listeners via paired telephone lines. Modern binaural recording is used in music production, field recording, virtual reality audio, hearing research, and ASMR content. Unlike surround sound formats, binaural recordings are intended specifically for headphone playback and generally do not translate well to loudspeakers without crosstalk cancellation.
