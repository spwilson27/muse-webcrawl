Fleet Air Arm

The Fleet Air Arm (FAA) is the naval aviation component of the United Kingdom's Royal Navy. The Fleet Air Arm operates fixed-wing and rotary aircraft from Royal Navy ships and shore stations, providing aviation support to the fleet at sea.

History

British naval aviation began with the Royal Naval Air Service in 1914, which merged into the Royal Air Force in 1918. The Fleet Air Arm of the RAF was formed in 1924, and the Admiralty regained full control of naval aviation in 1939, shortly before the Second World War, during which it flew carrier aircraft such as the Fairey Swordfish in actions including the attack on Taranto and the hunt for the Bismarck.

Modern role

Today the Fleet Air Arm operates the F-35B Lightning II jointly with the RAF from the Queen Elizabeth-class aircraft carriers, alongside Merlin and Wildcat helicopters for anti-submarine, airborne surveillance and utility roles. Its main air stations include RNAS Yeovilton and RNAS Culdrose.
