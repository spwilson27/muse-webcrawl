Formalin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Formalin is an aqueous solution of formaldehyde, typically containing about 37–40% formaldehyde by weight, often with a small amount of methanol added as a stabiliser to prevent polymerisation. It is widely used as a disinfectant, preservative, and tissue fixative.

Uses

In histology and pathology, a 10% solution of formalin in water (equivalent to about 4% formaldehyde) — often buffered to neutral pH as "neutral buffered formalin" — is the standard fixative for preserving tissue specimens. Formalin cross-links proteins, halting autolysis and decay while retaining cellular structure for microscopic examination. It is also used to preserve biological specimens in collections, as an embalming agent, and in various industrial processes.

Safety

Formaldehyde is toxic, allergenic, and classified as a known human carcinogen; exposure to formalin should be minimised, and it is handled with ventilation and protective equipment.
