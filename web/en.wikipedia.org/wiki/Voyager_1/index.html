Voyager 1

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The Voyager spacecraft with its huge white dish antenna and long boom against black space, artist's impression]
Voyager in deep space (artist's concept).

Voyager 1 is a space probe launched by NASA on 5 September 1977 to study the outer planets as part of the Voyager programme. It flew by Jupiter (1979) and Saturn (1980) — its Titan encounter bending its path out of the ecliptic — and in 2012 became the first human-made object to enter interstellar space. It is now the most distant human-made object, well over 15 billion miles from Earth, still returning data on a few watts of power and carrying the Golden Record.

Grand Tour

The Voyagers exploited a rare planetary alignment occurring every 176 years. Voyager 1 imaged Jupiter\'s Great Red Spot and active volcanoes on Io (the first seen beyond Earth), then Saturn\'s rings and its moon Titan\'s thick nitrogen atmosphere, which ended its planet tour to preserve the Titan data.

Interstellar mission

In 1990 Voyager 1 looked back for the famous \'Pale Blue Dot\' image of Earth and the Solar System family portrait. It crossed the termination shock (2004) and the heliopause into interstellar space in August 2012, measuring plasma and cosmic rays there. A 2023–24 computer fault interrupted science data for months before engineers restored it.

The Golden Record

Each Voyager carries a gold-plated copper record with sounds and images of Earth — greetings in 55 languages, music from Bach to Chuck Berry, and 115 images — intended as a message to any interstellar traveller that might find it in a billion years.
