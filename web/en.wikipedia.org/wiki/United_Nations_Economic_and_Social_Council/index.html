United Nations Economic and Social Council

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The United Nations Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC), established by the UN Charter as one of the six principal organs of the United Nations, is the central platform for fostering debate and innovative thinking, forging consensus and coordinating efforts to achieve internationally agreed goals in the economic, social and environmental fields. Its 54 members are elected by the General Assembly for overlapping three-year terms, with seats distributed by region.

Functions

ECOSOC coordinates the work of the UN's specialised agencies (which report to it, at least formally), its own functional commissions (statistics, population, status of women, narcotic drugs and others), regional commissions, and expert bodies; it accredits and consults with thousands of non-governmental organisations — the gateway through which civil society formally enters the UN system. Its annual cycle (high-level segment, the High-Level Political Forum on Sustainable Development reporting under its auspices) makes it the hub of development governance. Critics have long called ECOSOC the weakest principal organ — its coordination authority more constitutional than real, specialised agencies answering to their own boards and funders — and reform proposals to strengthen or bypass it recur with each UN reform round; its defenders note the forum functions (norm-setting, standardisation, NGO access) are irreplaceable and undervalued.
