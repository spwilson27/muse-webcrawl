Johann Weyer

Johann Weyer or Johannes Wier (Latin: Ioannes Wierus or Piscinarius; 1515 – 24 February 1588) was a Dutch physician who was among the first to write critically against the persecution of witches and the witch trials of early modern Europe. He is best known for his influential work De praestigiis daemonum (1563), in which he argued that accusations of witchcraft were often the result of mental illness, melancholy, and delusion rather than actual pacts with the Devil.

Life

Weyer was born in Grave, in the Duchy of Brabant. He studied medicine in Paris under Jean Fernel and in Orléans, and also came under the influence of Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa, whose work shaped his early interest in magic and the occult. He served as town physician of Arnhem before becoming court physician to William V, Duke of Jülich-Cleves-Berg, a post he held for many years.

De praestigiis daemonum

Published in Basel in 1563, De praestigiis daemonum et incantationibus ac venificiis ("On the Tricks of Demons") attacked the Malleus Maleficarum and the methods of witch-hunters. Weyer did not deny the existence of demons, but he argued that most so-called witches were innocent women suffering from melancholy or mental disorders, and that confessions extracted under torture were worthless. The book went through many editions and was repeatedly placed on the Index of Prohibited Books.

Legacy

Weyer is often regarded as a forerunner of modern psychiatry; Sigmund Freud and others praised his humane treatment of the mentally ill. An appendix to later editions of his work, the Pseudomonarchia Daemonum, catalogued a hierarchy of demons and remains a source for demonological literature.
