Flatford Mill is a Grade I listed watermill on the River Stour at Flatford in East Bergholt, Suffolk, England. It was built in 1733 and is notable as part of the landscape — "Constable Country" — painted by John Constable, whose family owned the mill.

History

A mill at Flatford was recorded as early as the 15th century. The present red-brick mill was built in 1733. Abram Constable, and later his more famous son the corn merchant Golding Constable, owned Flatford Mill as part of the family's milling and navigation business on the Stour; the painter John Constable was born nearby at East Bergholt and made the mill and its surroundings — including Willy Lott's Cottage, Flatford Bridge and the lock — the subjects of many of his paintings, among them scenes connected with The Hay Wain (1821).

Present use

The mill is owned by the National Trust and leased to the Field Studies Council, which operates it as a field studies centre offering courses in geography, biology and art. The nearby Bridge Cottage contains an exhibition about John Constable.
