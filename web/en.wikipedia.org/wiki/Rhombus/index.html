Rhombus

In plane Euclidean geometry, a rhombus (plural: rhombi or rhombuses) is a quadrilateral whose four sides all have the same length. It is a special case of a parallelogram, in which opposite sides are parallel, and of a kite. A rhombus with four right angles is a square.

Properties

Every rhombus has two diagonals connecting pairs of opposite vertices, and two axes of symmetry through those pairs. The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular bisectors of each other and bisect the vertex angles. Opposite angles of a rhombus are equal.

Area

The area of a rhombus can be computed as the product of its diagonals divided by two, or as the product of its base and the corresponding height, or as the square of a side times the sine of any vertex angle.

Characterizations

A quadrilateral is a rhombus if and only if it is a parallelogram with perpendicular diagonals, or a parallelogram in which a diagonal bisects a vertex angle, or a quadrilateral with four equal sides. Every rhombus is tangential in the sense that it has an inscribed circle tangent to all four sides.
