Fabian Society

The Fabian Society is a British socialist organisation founded in London in 1884, advocating gradual, democratic reform rather than revolution — "Fabian" after the Roman general Fabius Maximus, famed for delaying tactics. It was a founding organisation of the Labour Party in 1900 and remains affiliated to it as a think tank.

Fabian Society
Founded: 4 January 1884, London
Type: Think tank / socialist society
Affiliation: Labour Party (UK)

History

Early members included Sidney and Beatrice Webb, George Bernard Shaw, H.G. Wells, Annie Besant and Ramsay MacDonald. The Fabian Essays (1889) set out its programme; the Webbs helped found the London School of Economics (1895). Fabian ideas influenced the post-1945 Labour government and welfare state, and many decolonisation leaders encountered Fabian thought in London.
