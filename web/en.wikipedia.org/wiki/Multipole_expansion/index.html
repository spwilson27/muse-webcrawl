Multipole expansion

A multipole expansion is a mathematical series representing a function that depends on angles—usually of the form of a potential or field produced by a localized source—expressed in terms of simpler angular functions. The terms of the series are named by the number of poles: monopole (0th order), dipole (1st), quadrupole (2nd), octupole (3rd), hexadecapole (4th), and so on, each falling off faster with distance than the previous.

Electrostatic potential

For a distribution of electric charge, the potential at a distant point can be expanded in powers of 1/r. The monopole term depends on the total charge, the dipole term on the dipole moment, and the quadrupole term on the quadrupole moment tensor. If the total charge is zero, the dipole term dominates at large distances; if the dipole also vanishes, the quadrupole dominates. The analogous expansion applies to gravitational potentials of mass distributions and to magnetostatics.

Spherical harmonics

In spherical coordinates the angular dependence of each term is described by spherical harmonics Ylm, and the expansion of 1/|r − r′| in Legendre polynomials underlies the technique. A related expansion in plane waves or cylindrical harmonics is used for scattering problems.

Applications

Multipole expansions are used throughout physics and engineering: characterizing the radiation patterns of antennas and the fields of molecules, describing Earth's gravitational and magnetic fields (the cosmic microwave background is likewise decomposed into multipoles, whose low orders were famously measured by the COBE, WMAP, and Planck satellites), and accelerating n-body simulations via the fast multipole method, which groups distant particles' fields into multipole terms and dramatically reduces computation in gravitation and molecular dynamics.
