General Social Survey

The General Social Survey (GSS) is a sociological survey conducted by the research organization NORC at the University of Chicago. Since 1972 it has regularly collected data on the attitudes, behaviors and demographic characteristics of American society, making it one of the most frequently analyzed sources of information in the social sciences.

Methodology

The GSS interviews a nationally representative sample of adults in the United States, historically in person and more recently through mixed modes. A core set of questions is repeated each round so that trends can be tracked over decades, while rotating modules address special topics. The survey was conducted annually for much of its history and biennially from the 1990s onward.

Topics

Questions cover confidence in institutions, religion, political views, race relations, gender roles, civil liberties, crime, work and family life. Well-known findings derived from GSS data include long-term declines in religious affiliation, changing attitudes toward same-sex marriage, and the generational replacement of social attitudes.

Access

GSS data are freely available to researchers and the public through the GSS Data Explorer, and the cumulative dataset is used in teaching statistics and sociology worldwide. The project has been led by figures including James A. Davis and is funded primarily by the National Science Foundation.
