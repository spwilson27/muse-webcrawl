Astronomical catalogue

An astronomical catalogue is a list or tabulation of astronomical objects, typically grouped together because they share a common type, morphology, origin, means of detection, or method of discovery. Catalogues are usually the result of an astronomical survey of some kind, and their designations (such as HD, HIP, NGC, or 2MASS numbers) provide the standard identifiers by which objects are known.

Historical catalogues

Star catalogues date back to antiquity: the catalogue in Ptolemy's Almagest listed about 1,022 stars. Ulugh Beg and Tycho Brahe produced influential catalogues in the 15th and 16th centuries, and John Flamsteed's Historia Coelestis Britannica (1725) introduced the Flamsteed numbers still in use. Charles Messier's catalogue of nebulae and clusters (final version 1781) and the New General Catalogue (NGC, 1888) of John Louis Emil Dreyer remain standard references for deep-sky objects.

Modern catalogues

The Henry Draper Catalogue (1918–1924) provided spectral classifications for some 225,000 stars. Space-based astrometry missions produced the Hipparcos Catalogue (118,000 stars with high-precision positions) and the far larger Gaia catalogues, whose third data release (2022) covers nearly 1.8 billion sources. Sky surveys such as 2MASS and the Sloan Digital Sky Survey have generated catalogues of hundreds of millions of objects across multiple wavelengths. Most catalogues are now accessed electronically through databases such as SIMBAD and VizieR.
