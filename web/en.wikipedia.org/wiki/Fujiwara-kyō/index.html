Fujiwara-kyō

Fujiwara-kyō (藤原京) was the capital of Japan from 694 to 710, located in Yamato Province in what is now Kashihara, Nara Prefecture. It was Japan's first capital city built on the Chinese grid-plan model, following the example of Chang'an.


Site of the Fujiwara Palace

History

Construction of the capital was begun under Emperor Tenmu and completed under Empress Jitō, who moved the court there in 694. The capital served three sovereigns — Jitō, Monmu and Genmei — before the court relocated to Heijō-kyō (Nara) in 710, only sixteen years after its founding. A fire in 711 destroyed much of what remained of the palace complex.

Layout

The city was laid out in a grid of wards (jōbō), with the Imperial Palace (Fujiwara Palace) in the northern-centre, rather than the north, of the city. The Suzaku Avenue ran south from the palace. Excavations, conducted intensively since the 1930s, have revealed foundation stones of the palace halls, roof tiles and wooden tablets (mokkan) that illuminate the administration of the ritsuryō state. The palace grounds covered about 1 km², and the whole capital is estimated at roughly 5 km by 5 km.
