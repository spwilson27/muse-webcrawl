Hail Mary pass

A Hail Mary pass is a very long forward pass in American football, typically made in desperation at the end of a half or game, with a very small chance of success. The term derives from the Hail Mary prayer, invoked as a last hope. More broadly, "Hail Mary" has entered general usage for any desperate long-shot attempt.

Origin of the term

The phrase was used by Notre Dame players in the 1920s and 1930s for long desperation passes. It was popularized in its modern sense after the 1975 NFC Divisional Playoff game, when Dallas Cowboys quarterback Roger Staubach threw a game-winning 50-yard touchdown pass to Drew Pearson against the Minnesota Vikings and told reporters afterward, "I closed my eyes and said a Hail Mary."

Strategy

On a Hail Mary, the offense sends several receivers deep into the end zone while the quarterback buys time and throws the ball high into a crowd, hoping a receiver catches it or it is tipped to a teammate. Defenders are coached to knock the ball down rather than attempt an interception. Because so many players leap for the ball at once, outcomes are chaotic and success is rare.

Famous examples

  • 1975: Staubach to Pearson, Cowboys over Vikings.
  • 1980 "Miracle Bowl": BYU's Jim McMahon comeback against SMU.
  • 1984: Doug Flutie's 63-yard pass to Gerard Phelan, Boston College over Miami — the "Hail Flutie."
  • 1994: Kordell Stewart's "Miracle at Michigan" for Colorado.
  • 2015: Aaron Rodgers's "Miracle in Motown" against Detroit, and his Hail Mary against Arizona in the playoffs.
