Akeman Street

Akeman Street is a major Roman road in England that linked the Roman towns of Verulamium (modern St Albans) and Corinium Dobunnorum (modern Cirencester). It runs generally west-northwest from St Albans through the Chilterns and the upper Thames valley, passing near Bicester, and its course is still followed for much of its length by modern roads, including parts of the A41 and the A4095. The road is approximately 75 miles (120 km) long.

The modern road preserves the straight alignment of Akeman Street.

Route

From Verulamium the road ran northwest through the Ver valley and across the Chiltern escarpment near Tring and Aston Clinton, then through Fleet Marston and toward a crossing of the River Cherwell near its junction with the Ray. It passed the Roman settlement of Alchester, near modern Bicester, where it crossed Ermine Street's western branch traffic, and continued through Kirtlington and across the line of the Cherwell valley, passing the Roman town at Dorchester-on-Thames's region and the small town at Asthall, before descending into Cirencester — one of the largest towns of Roman Britain and junction of the Fosse Way and Ermine Street.

Name

The origin of the name "Akeman" is uncertain. It is often connected with the city of Bath, known in Old English as Acemannesceastre ("the city of the sick men", from its healing waters) — Akeman Street may have been understood as the road towards Bath, although the road itself ends at Cirencester, from which the Fosse Way continued to Bath. The name is attested in medieval documents and survives in place names such as Akeman Street in Tring and Cambridge (where a different Roman road bears the same name).

Survival

Long stretches of Akeman Street survive as public roads, green lanes, and parish boundaries, and its straight surveyed alignment across the Oxfordshire limestone is a classic example of Roman road engineering. Archaeological sections show a cambered gravel metalling on a raised agger, flanked by drainage ditches.
