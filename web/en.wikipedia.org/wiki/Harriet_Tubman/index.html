Harriet Tubman

Harriet Tubman (born Araminta Ross)
Born: c. March 1822, Maryland
Died: 10 March 1913, Auburn, New York
Known for: Underground Railroad conductor, scout and spy for the Union Army

Harriet Tubman (c. 1822–1913) was an American abolitionist who escaped slavery and then made some 13 missions to rescue about 70 enslaved people via the Underground Railroad.

Early life and escape

Born enslaved in Dorchester County, Maryland, she suffered a severe head injury from an overseer. In 1849 she escaped to Philadelphia, then repeatedly returned south to guide family and others to freedom, earning the nickname 'Moses'.

Civil War and later life

During the Civil War she worked for the Union Army as cook, nurse, scout and spy, and helped lead the Combahee Ferry Raid (1863) that freed over 700 enslaved people. She later campaigned for women's suffrage, settled in Auburn, New York, and struggled for a military pension.

Legacy

Tubman is an American icon; schools, museums and a national historical park bear her name, and a long-planned redesign of the US $20 bill to feature her was announced in 2016 and remains discussed.
