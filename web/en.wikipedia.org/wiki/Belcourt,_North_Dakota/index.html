Belcourt is a census-designated place (CDP) in Rolette County, North Dakota, United States. It is the seat of government and largest community of the Turtle Mountain Band of Chippewa Indians, lying within the Turtle Mountain Indian Reservation near the Canadian border. The population was about 2,050 at the 2020 census.

History

The community was named for Georges-Antoine Belcourt (1803–1874), a French-Canadian Catholic missionary who worked among the Ojibwe (Chippewa) and Métis peoples of the Red River and upper Missouri country in the mid-19th century. The townsite developed around the tribal agency after the Turtle Mountain Reservation was established by executive order in 1882 and reduced to its present two-township size in 1884.

Geography and community

Belcourt sits in the rolling, lake-dotted Turtle Mountains of north-central North Dakota, just south of the Manitoba border and the International Peace Garden. It hosts the Turtle Mountain Community College, the tribal government offices, the Sky Dancer Casino and Hotel, and headquarters functions for the reservation. The surrounding area is known for its Michif-speaking Métis heritage, fiddling traditions and annual cultural celebrations.

Demographics

The population is overwhelmingly Native American, primarily enrolled members and descendants of the Turtle Mountain Band of Chippewa (Ojibwe) with significant Métis ancestry. Belcourt serves as the commercial centre for the reservation, despite the county seat being Rolla, a few miles to the west.
