Bakhshi

A bakhshi (also spelled bagşy, baxşı, or bakshi) is a traditional folk singer, storyteller, and instrumentalist in the cultures of Central Asia, particularly among the Turkmen, Uzbek, Kazakh, and Karakalpak peoples, and in the Khorasan region of Iran. The bakhshi performs epic poems known as dastans, accompanying himself on a long-necked lute — the dutar in Turkmenistan and Khorasan, or the dombra in Kazakhstan — and traditionally serves as a bearer of oral history, genealogy, and moral teaching.

Role and training

The art of the bakhshi is transmitted through long apprenticeship to a master (halypa), and performers traditionally explain their calling through a dream in which they receive their instrument or their gift of song from a saint or ancestor. A skilled bakhshi commands a repertoire of dozens of dastans — heroic, romantic, and historical epics such as the tales of Görogly (Koroghlu) — that can be performed over many nights. Performances combine singing in a distinctive, strained vocal style with narration and instrumental interludes.

Regional traditions

In Turkmenistan, the bagşy is a central figure of national culture, and the dutar-accompanied epic tradition was inscribed by UNESCO on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. In Khorasan, in northeastern Iran, the bakhshi sings in Kurdish, Turkmen, Persian, or Turkish and uses a two-stringed dotara; the music of the bakhshis of North Khorasan was likewise recognised by UNESCO in 2010. In Azerbaijan the closely related figure is the ashiq. Among Kazakhs and Kyrgyz the corresponding epic singers are the akyn and the manaschi, the latter reciting the vast Epic of Manas.

History

The word derives from a term used across the Turkic and Mongol worlds for teachers, scribes, and healers, reflecting the bakhshi's earlier shamanic associations. Over the Soviet period the tradition was reshaped by state folklore ensembles and competitions, but solo epic performance survived and has been revived since independence in the Central Asian republics.
