Costume design

Costume design is the creation of clothing and appearance for characters in theatre, film, television, opera, and other performing arts. The costume designer works with the director and other designers to define how each character's clothing expresses personality, status, period, and mood, and how the costumes function with lighting, set, and movement.

Process

The designer begins by analyzing the script and conducting research into period, place, class, and occupation. Ideas are communicated through sketches and renderings, after which fabrics are chosen or “swatched”. Garments may be built new in a costume shop, pulled from stock, rented, or purchased, and are fitted to performers; in theatre, changes, durability, and the needs of dance or quick changes impose practical constraints. The department is supported by cutters, drapers, milliners, dyers, and wardrobe supervisors who maintain costumes during a run.

History

In classical and medieval theatre performers supplied their own garments, but the 19th and 20th centuries saw costume emerge as a distinct design discipline with historically researched productions. In film, studios created large costume departments; designers such as Adrian and Edith Head became public figures. Head won eight Academy Awards, more than any other woman, and the Oscar for Best Costume Design has existed since 1949. Milena Canonero, Colleen Atwood, and Sandy Powell are among the most honored contemporary designers.

Theory

Costume is a powerful storytelling tool: color palettes can distinguish factions or emotional states; silhouette and fit signal status and character change; and wear, distressing, and accessories add biography. Designers speak of building a character's “closet” to reflect an inner life, balancing historical accuracy against audience legibility and directorial concept.

Related fields and training

Costume designers are closely related to fashion designers but answer to narrative rather than retail demands. Professional training is offered by drama schools and universities, and designers are represented by unions such as the Costume Designers Guild in the United States.
