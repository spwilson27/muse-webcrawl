Rent stabilization

Rent stabilization is a form of rent regulation that limits how much landlords may increase rents each year, rather than freezing them at a fixed level. The term is most closely associated with New York City, where the Rent Stabilization Law of 1969 and the Emergency Tenant Protection Act of 1974 created a system covering roughly a million apartments, administered with annual increase guidelines set by the Rent Guidelines Board.

New York system

Stabilized apartments are generally those in buildings of six or more units built before 1974, plus newer units that received tax incentives. Tenants have the right to renew leases and to required services. Historically, units could leave stabilization through vacancy and high-rent deregulation thresholds; the Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act of 2019 largely ended high-rent deregulation and tightened the system.

Distinction from rent control

New York's separate, older "rent control" programme applies to a small and shrinking number of apartments occupied continuously since before 1971 and caps rents at a maximum base rent. Rent stabilization, by contrast, permits regulated annual increases and is the far larger programme. Other cities, including San Francisco and Washington, D.C., operate analogous stabilisation-style regimes under their own names.
