It is a 1986 horror novel by American author Stephen King. His 22nd book and 17th novel written under his own name, it was the best-selling hardcover fiction book in the United States in 1986. The story follows the experiences of seven children in the fictional town of Derry, Maine, as they are terrorized by an evil entity that exploits the fears of its victims to disguise itself while hunting its prey. "It" primarily appears in the form of Pennywise the Dancing Clown to attract young children.

Plot summary

The narrative alternates between two periods. In 1957–58, seven outcast children — the self-styled "Losers' Club" led by Bill Denbrough, whose younger brother Georgie was murdered by It — confront the creature in the sewers beneath Derry and wound it, vowing to return if it ever resurfaces. Twenty-seven years later, in 1984–85, a new series of murders signals It's return; librarian Mike Hanley, the only Loser who stayed in Derry, summons the others back. As adults they descend again into the sewers, performing the Ritual of Chüd to destroy It, while a great storm devastates the town. The novel explores themes of childhood trauma, memory, friendship, and the power of belief; King has said he set out to ask "what children can do that adults can't".

Adaptations

The novel was adapted as a two-part television miniseries in 1990, with Tim Curry acclaimed as Pennywise. A two-part film adaptation followed: It (2017), starring Bill Skarsgård, became the highest-grossing horror film of its time, and It Chapter Two (2019) completed the story.
