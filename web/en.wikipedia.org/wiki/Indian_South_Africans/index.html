Indian South Africans

Indian South Africans are South African citizens and residents of Indian or broader South Asian ancestry. Numbering roughly 1.5 million — about 2.5 per cent of the population — they form one of the largest Indian diaspora communities in Africa, concentrated principally in KwaZulu-Natal, especially Durban, which is sometimes described as the largest "Indian" city outside India.

History

The community descends mainly from indentured labourers brought to the Colony of Natal from 1860 to work on sugar plantations, and from "passenger Indians" — merchants and traders, largely Gujarati, who arrived under their own means from the 1870s onward. Mohandas K. Gandhi's two decades in South Africa (1893–1914) were formative both for his philosophy of satyagraha and for Indian political organisation in the country, including the founding of the Natal Indian Congress in 1894.

Apartheid and after

Under apartheid, Indians were classified as a separate racial group, subjected to the Group Areas Act, disenfranchised and targeted by discriminatory legislation, yet also positioned between the white minority and the Black majority in the official hierarchy. Indian South Africans played significant roles in the liberation struggle, and since 1994 the community has voted across party lines while remaining prominent in commerce, the professions and public life. Languages historically spoken include Tamil, Hindi, Gujarati, Telugu and Urdu, though English is now dominant.
