Silovik

Silovik (Russian: силовик, IPA: [sʲɪlɐˈvʲik]; plural: siloviki, Russian: силовики) is a term used in Russian politics to describe officials who come from the security services, military, or law-enforcement agencies — the so-called "power ministries" (Russian: силовые министерства). The word derives from the Russian sila (сила), meaning "force" or "power."

Background

The influence of the siloviki rose markedly in the late Soviet and post-Soviet periods. After Boris Yeltsin, and especially under President Vladimir Putin — himself a former KGB officer and director of the FSB — veterans of the intelligence services, armed forces, and police came to occupy a large share of senior posts in the presidential administration, government ministries, state corporations, and regional governments.

Role in politics

Analysts commonly contrast the siloviki with the "liberals" or technocrats (often called sistemnye liberaly, "systemic liberals") within the Russian elite. Siloviki are generally associated with statism, a strong centralised state, suspicion of the West, and the prominence of state-controlled corporations in strategic sectors such as energy and defence. Scholars debate the extent to which the siloviki form a coherent faction as opposed to a loose network of personal loyalties and institutional interests.
