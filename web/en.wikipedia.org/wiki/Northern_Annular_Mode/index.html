Northern Annular Mode

The Northern Annular Mode (NAM), also known as the Arctic Oscillation (AO), is a dominant pattern of atmospheric variability in the Northern Hemisphere. It describes a seesaw in atmospheric mass and sea-level pressure between the Arctic and the mid-latitudes, with a roughly annular (ring-like) structure centred over the North Pole.

Description

The NAM is defined as the leading empirical orthogonal function (EOF) of sea-level pressure anomalies poleward of about 20°N. When the NAM is in its positive phase, pressure over the Arctic is lower than normal and pressure in the mid-latitudes is higher than normal; the pattern reverses in the negative phase.

The NAM is closely related to the North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO), which is its regional expression over the Atlantic sector. Some researchers regard the NAO as part of the hemispheric annular pattern, while others treat the two as distinct but correlated phenomena.

Impacts

The phase of the NAM strongly influences winter weather across North America, Europe and Asia. A positive NAM is generally associated with a strong polar vortex, milder winter temperatures over much of the northern continents, and storm tracks displaced poleward. A negative NAM weakens the polar vortex, allowing cold Arctic air to spill southward, and is associated with cold outbreaks and increased chances of snow in the mid-latitudes.

Variations and trends

The NAM operates on timescales from days to decades and exists throughout the troposphere and stratosphere; its stratospheric counterpart is tied to the strength of the polar vortex and sudden stratospheric warming events. Its Southern Hemisphere counterpart is the Southern Annular Mode (SAM), also called the Antarctic Oscillation.
