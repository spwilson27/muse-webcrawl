Chapter (religion)

A chapter (Latin: capitulum, "little head") is, in religious usage, a formal meeting or assembly of clergy, monks, nuns or members of a religious order, and by extension the body of persons who constitute such an assembly. The term derives from the custom of reading a chapter of the monastic rule at the daily meeting of a community.

Cathedral chapters

In cathedrals of the Roman Catholic, Anglican and some Lutheran churches, the cathedral chapter is the body of clergy — historically the canons — who advise the bishop and, in earlier centuries, governed the diocese during a vacancy of the see. In the Church of England the chapter, chaired by the dean, is the governing body of the cathedral, responsible for its worship, fabric and administration under the Cathedrals Measure. The meeting place of a cathedral or monastic chapter is the chapter house, often a distinguished piece of architecture, as in the octagonal chapter houses of York Minster and Wells Cathedral.

Monastic chapters

In monastic life the chapter is the daily assembly of the professed members of the community, held in the chapter house after the office of Prime or Terce. Business includes the reading of the martyrology and a chapter of the Rule of St Benedict, confession of faults (the "chapter of faults"), and discussion of the community's affairs. A general chapter is a meeting of representatives from all the houses of a religious order, empowered to legislate for the whole order; a provincial chapter serves the same function for a province of an order.

Orders of knighthood

The governing assemblies of orders of knighthood and of collegiate bodies such as the College of Arms are also called chapters; the Chapter of the Order of the Garter, for example, meets at Windsor Castle around the annual Garter service.
