Parachuting, also called skydiving in its sporting form, is the method of descending through the atmosphere from a high point — typically an aircraft — using a parachute to slow the descent to a safe landing. It is practised as a recreational sport, a competitive discipline, a military insertion technique and a last-resort means of escape from damaged aircraft.

History

Parachute concepts appear in Renaissance sketches by Leonardo da Vinci, and the modern device is credited to Louis-Sébastien Lenormand, who demonstrated a jump from a tower in 1783 and coined the word parachute (from French parer, to avert, and chute, fall). André-Jacques Garnerin made the first successful parachute descent from a balloon in 1797. Parachutes became standard aircrew safety equipment in the First World War era, and mass military jumps in the Second World War — Crete, Normandy, Arnhem — made paratroops a major arm. After 1945 surplus equipment and the development of steerable ram-air canopies turned the activity into a popular sport.

The sport

Sport jumpers typically exit at 3,000–4,000 m, spend up to a minute in freefall at about 190 km/h, and deploy a ram-air parachute at around 900 m for a steerable, flared landing. Training routes include tandem jumps, static-line progression and Accelerated Freefall (AFF). Competitive disciplines include formation skydiving, freeflying, canopy relative work, accuracy landing, canopy piloting ("swooping") and wingsuit flying. Modern rigs include a main and reserve canopy and an automatic activation device (AAD) that deploys the reserve if the jumper is still falling fast at low altitude. Sport parachuting is regulated through bodies such as the United States Parachute Association (USPA) and the British Parachute Association, under the FAI's International Skydiving Commission.

Military and other uses

Airborne forces use low-altitude static-line jumps for mass assault and high-altitude HALO/HAHO techniques for covert insertion; special forces also use tandem and equipment-carrying jumps. Parachutes recover spacecraft (Apollo, Soyuz), deliver cargo and humanitarian aid by airdrop, and lower smokejumpers to wildfires. Jumping from fixed objects rather than aircraft is the separate, higher-risk activity of BASE jumping.

Safety

Modern sport parachuting records roughly a fraction of a fatality per 100,000 jumps in well-regulated countries, with injuries most often occurring on landing under high-performance canopies. Malfunctions are handled by cutting away the main canopy and deploying the reserve.
