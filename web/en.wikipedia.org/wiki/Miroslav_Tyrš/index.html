Miroslav Tyrš

Miroslav Tyrš (17 September 1832 – 8 August 1884), born Friedrich Tirsch, was a Czech art historian, critic and founder, with Jindřich Fügner, of the Sokol gymnastics movement, a central institution of the Czech national revival.

Life

Born in Děčín to a German-speaking family, Tyrš was educated in Prague, studied philosophy, aesthetics and mathematics at Charles University, and became a lecturer in art history. Influenced by classical ideals of harmony of body and mind, he devised a Czech system of physical education and terminology.

Sokol

The Sokol ("Falcon") society, founded in 1862, combined gymnastics with patriotic and democratic ideals; its mass public displays, the slets, became symbols of Czech national identity. Tyrš wrote its theoretical foundations and designed its organisational principles.

Tyrš also helped organise the National Theatre art exhibitions and was a professor at the Czech Technical University and Charles University. He died in 1884, drowning in the Ötz river in Tyrol.
