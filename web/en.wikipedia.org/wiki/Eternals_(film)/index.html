Eternals is a 2021 American superhero film based on the Marvel Comics race of the same name. Produced by Marvel Studios and directed by Chloé Zhao, it is the 26th film in the Marvel Cinematic Universe (MCU). The film features an ensemble cast led by Gemma Chan as Sersi and Richard Madden as Ikaris, and follows the Eternals, an immortal alien race sent to Earth by the Celestials thousands of years ago to protect humanity from the Deviants, who reunite in the present day when their monstrous counterparts return.

Plot

In 5000 BC the Celestial Arishem sends ten Eternals to Earth aboard the Domo to exterminate the Deviants. Over millennia the team lives among humans, and after the Deviants are apparently destroyed the group disperses. In the present, the sudden return of evolved Deviants — and the death of their leader Ajak — brings the Eternals back together. They discover the truth of their mission: Earth is incubating a new Celestial, Tiamut, whose "Emergence" will destroy the planet. Most of the Eternals rebel against Arishem's design; Ikaris, loyal to the mission, betrays the group. United with the Uni-Mind, Sersi transmutes Tiamut into marble, stopping the Emergence. Arishem abducts the remaining Earth-based Eternals to judge whether humanity is worth sparing, while Thena, Druig and Makkari leave aboard the Domo to warn Eternals on other worlds.

Production

Marvel Studios began developing an Eternals film in 2018, with Ryan and Kaz Firpo writing the screenplay. Chloé Zhao was hired to direct and co-wrote the final script with Patrick Burleigh; her pitch emphasised a sweeping, millennia-spanning story shot largely on location with natural light. Principal photography ran from July 2019 to February 2020, in England and the Canary Islands. The film features the MCU's first deaf superhero (Makkari, played by Lauren Ridloff) and an openly gay superhero (Phastos, played by Brian Tyree Henry).

Reception

Eternals grossed about $402 million worldwide against a budget of roughly $236 million. It received mixed reviews: critics praised its ambition, visuals and direction but criticised the pacing, exposition and character development. It holds the lowest Rotten Tomatoes score of any MCU film (47%) from its release period, though audience scores were considerably higher.
