WSPR (amateur radio software)

WSPR (pronounced "whisper") stands for Weak Signal Propagation Reporter. It is a computer program used for weak-signal radio communication between amateur radio operators. The program was initially written by Joe Taylor, K1JT, and is now open source and developed by a small team. Many error-correcting digital radio protocols, including WSPR, were designed by Taylor, a Nobel Prize–winning astrophysicist.

Protocol

WSPR implements a mode designed for probing potential propagation paths on the MF and HF bands, and also works on LF, VHF, and UHF bands. Transmissions carry a station's callsign, Maidenhead grid locator, and power output in dBm, compressed into 50 bits and protected by a convolutional code, then sent as a four-symbol-per-second phase-coherent FSK signal occupying only about 6 Hz of bandwidth. A transmission lasts slightly under two minutes and can be decoded at signal-to-noise ratios as low as about −28 dB in a 2500 Hz reference bandwidth.

WSPRnet

Stations receiving WSPR transmissions can automatically upload decoded spots to the WSPRnet database on the Internet, creating a near-real-time record of radio propagation conditions worldwide. Amateur stations and dedicated low-cost beacon transmitters use WSPR to study ionospheric propagation, compare antennas, and monitor band openings.
