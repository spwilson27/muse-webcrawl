The Folketing (Danish: Folketinget, pronounced [ˈfʌlkəˌtsʰe̝ŋˀð̩]; lit. "The people's thing" or "People's assembly") is the unicameral national parliament (legislature) of the Kingdom of Denmark — Denmark proper together with Greenland and the Faroe Islands. Founded in 1849, until 1953 the Folketing was the lower house of a bicameral parliament, the Rigsdag; the upper house was the Landsting.

The Folketing meets in the Christiansborg Palace, on the islet of Slotsholmen in central Copenhagen. It passes all laws, approves the cabinet, and supervises the work of the government. It is also responsible for adopting the state's budgets and approving the state's accounts. Under the Constitution of Denmark, the Folketing shares power with the monarch, although in practice the monarch's involvement is limited to signing laws and the formal appointment of the prime minister.

Composition

The Folketing consists of 179 members, of whom 175 are elected in Denmark proper, two in Greenland, and two in the Faroe Islands. Members are elected for four-year terms by proportional representation. Elections may be called early by the prime minister, but must be held no later than four years after the previous election.

History

The Folketing was established with the adoption of the first Constitution of Denmark in 1849. From 1849 to 1953 it was the lower house of the Rigsdag, alongside the Landsting. The constitutional amendment of 1953 abolished the Landsting and made the Folketing the sole chamber of the legislature, also introducing the possibility of referendums and extending the franchise.
