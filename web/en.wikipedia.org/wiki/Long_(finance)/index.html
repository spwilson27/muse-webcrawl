Long (finance)

Long position
MeaningOwnership of a security in expectation of a price rise
Profit conditionPrice of the asset increases
OppositeShort position

In finance, a long position in a financial instrument is the conventional buying of a security such as a stock, commodity or currency, with the expectation that the asset will rise in value. An investor who holds a long position owns the asset and profits if its price increases, and loses if it decreases; the maximum loss is limited to the amount invested, while the potential gain is theoretically unlimited.

Long positions in derivatives

In options, a long position arises from buying a call option (profiting from a rise in the underlying) or selling a put option. In futures markets, a long position is an agreement to buy the underlying asset at the contract's maturity. A "long" stance in a market is also used informally to mean a bullish outlook: an investor who is "long the market" expects prices generally to rise.

Contrast with short positions

The opposite of a long position is a short position, in which the investor borrows and sells an asset, expecting to buy it back later at a lower price. Long/short equity is an investment strategy that combines long positions in undervalued stocks with short positions in overvalued ones.
