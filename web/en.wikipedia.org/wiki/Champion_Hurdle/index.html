The Champion Hurdle is a Grade 1 National Hunt hurdle race in Great Britain which is open to horses aged four years or older. It is run on the Old Course at Cheltenham over a distance of about 2 miles and ½ furlong (3,319 metres), with eight hurdles to be jumped. The race is the first leg of the Triple Crown of Hurdling and the feature event of the opening day of the Cheltenham Festival in March.

History

The Champion Hurdle was established in 1927, and its first winner was Bende Or. It has grown into the most prestigious hurdling event in the British and Irish racing calendar. Five horses have won the race three times: Hatton's Grace, Sir Ken, Persian War, See You Then and Istabraq.

Notable winners

Other celebrated winners include Night Nurse, Monksfield and Sea Pigeon, whose duels in the late 1970s are regarded as a golden age of hurdling; Dawn Run, the only horse to win both the Champion Hurdle (1984) and the Cheltenham Gold Cup (1986); Hurricane Fly, winner in 2011 and 2013; Buveur d'Air, back-to-back winner in 2017 and 2018; and Constitution Hill, an emphatic winner in 2023. The leading trainer in the race's history is Nicky Henderson.

Records

  • Most successful horse: 3 wins — Hatton's Grace, Sir Ken, Persian War, See You Then, Istabraq
  • Leading jockey: Tim Molony and Ruby Walsh, 4 wins each
  • Leading trainer: Nicky Henderson, 9 wins
