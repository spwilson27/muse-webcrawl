Shanshan

Shanshan (Chinese: 鄯善; pinyin: Shànshàn), earlier known as Loulan (樓蘭), was an ancient kingdom located at the eastern edge of the Tarim Basin, near the dried lake Lop Nur in what is now Xinjiang, China. Its capital was near the oasis of Loulan, a key staging post on the northern route of the Silk Road.

History

The kingdom first appears in Chinese records in the 2nd century BC, when the Han dynasty envoy Zhang Qian reported on the states of the Western Regions. Loulan's position made it a prize contested between Han China and the Xiongnu confederation; its kings frequently shifted allegiance. In AD 77 Chinese sources record the renaming of the kingdom to Shanshan after a Han intervention restored a friendly ruler. The general Ban Chao later consolidated Han influence in the region.

Shanshan controlled traffic between Dunhuang and the oasis states of the Tarim Basin, and its inhabitants — speakers of a Tocharian-related language and later of Prakrit-influenced tongues — administered a flourishing trade in documents written in the Kharosthi script. The kingdom gradually declined as its water supply failed and trade routes shifted south; the area was abandoned by the 5th century AD.

Archaeology

The Swedish explorer Sven Hedin rediscovered the ruins of Loulan in 1900, and Aurel Stein's expeditions recovered letters, wooden documents and the famous mummies of the region, including the "Beauty of Loulan". The modern county of Shanshan in Xinjiang, centred on Piqan, takes its name from the ancient kingdom, though it lies further west.
