Sir Run Run Shaw GBM CBE (born Shao Renleng; 23 November 1907 – 7 January 2014), also known as Shao Yifu, was a Hong Kong entertainment mogul and philanthropist. He was a towering figure in Asian cinema and television, founder of the Shaw Brothers Studio — one of the largest film production companies in the world — and co-founder of Television Broadcasts Limited (TVB), Hong Kong's dominant broadcaster.

Career

The youngest of six sons of a Shanghai textile merchant, Shaw joined his brothers' film business in the 1920s and helped expand their theatre chain and studios into Singapore and Malaya. In 1957 he moved to Hong Kong and established Shaw Brothers, opening the vast Movietown studio complex in Clearwater Bay in 1961. The studio produced around 1,000 films — lavish historical epics, musicals, and the martial-arts pictures that conquered world markets in the 1970s — and launched stars such as Jimmy Wang Yu and directors including King Hu and Chang Cheh. Shaw notably declined to sign the young Bruce Lee on Lee's terms, a famous missed opportunity, but his studio's kung fu output shaped global popular culture, and in later life he co-produced the cult science-fiction film Blade Runner (1982).

In 1967 Shaw co-founded TVB and served as its executive chairman, building it into the principal producer of Chinese-language television drama.

Philanthropy and honours

Shaw donated billions of Hong Kong dollars to education and medicine, funding thousands of school and university buildings across China — so many that thousands of buildings bear his name (逸夫樓, "Yifu Building"). In 2002 he founded the Shaw Prize, an international award in astronomy, life science and medicine, and mathematical sciences, sometimes called the "Nobel of the East", with a monetary award of US$1 million (later raised). He was knighted by Queen Elizabeth II in 1977 and received Hong Kong's Grand Bauhinia Medal in 1998. Shaw died in Hong Kong in January 2014 at the age of 106.
