Tafsir

Tafsir (Arabic: تفسير, tafsīr, "interpretation") refers to the exegesis of the Qur'an, the body of commentary written to explain the meanings of its verses. The Arabic word derives from the root f-s-r, meaning to explain or expound. A scholar of tafsir is called a mufassir (مفسر).

Principles and methodology

Classical tafsir draws on several sources in a generally accepted hierarchy: the Qur'an itself (explaining one verse by another), the Sunnah and hadith of Muhammad, reports from the Companions (sahaba) and the following generation, the Arabic language and its pre-Islamic poetry, and reasoned interpretation (tafsir bi'l-ra'y). Two broad approaches are distinguished: tafsir bi'l-ma'thur, based on transmitted reports, and tafsir bi'l-ra'y, based on considered opinion and reasoning.

History

Interpretation of the Qur'an began in the lifetime of Muhammad and was continued by Companions such as Ibn Abbas, regarded as a foundational figure of the discipline. The first comprehensive written commentaries appeared in the 9th and 10th centuries CE. The Jami' al-bayan of al-Tabari (839–923) became the most influential early work, combining transmitted reports with grammatical analysis. Later landmarks include the Kashshaf of al-Zamakhshari, the Mafatih al-ghayb of Fakhr al-Din al-Razi, the concise and widely read Tafsir al-Jalalayn, and the traditionalist Tafsir of Ibn Kathir.

Types

Tafsir works vary by school and emphasis: Sunni, Shia, and Sufi commentaries reflect distinct theological and spiritual concerns, with Sufi works such as those attributed to Ibn Arabi's tradition emphasising esoteric (ishari) meanings. Modern commentaries, such as Sayyid Qutb's Fi Zilal al-Qur'an and the Turkish Risale-i Nur of Said Nursî, address contemporary readers. Tafsir is related to, but distinct from, ta'wil, often understood as allegorical or deeper interpretation.
