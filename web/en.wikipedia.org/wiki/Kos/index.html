Kos

Kos Town harbour and the Castle of Nerantzia

Kos (Greek: Κως) is a Greek island in the southeastern Aegean Sea, part of the Dodecanese chain, lying just off the coast of Anatolia near Bodrum, Turkey. It is the third largest of the Dodecanese after Rhodes and Karpathos, about 40 km (25 mi) long, with a population of roughly 37,000. Kos is famous as the birthplace of Hippocrates, the "father of medicine", and its economy today rests largely on tourism.

History

Inhabited since prehistoric times, Kos was colonised by Dorian Greeks and joined the Dorian Hexapolis. Under the Hellenistic kingdoms it prospered; the Asklepieion of Kos — a great sanctuary and healing school of Asklepios on a terraced hillside — drew patients from across the Mediterranean and was linked to the medical tradition of Hippocrates, born on the island around 460 BC. The island passed through Roman, Byzantine, Knights Hospitaller, and Ottoman rule; the Knights built the imposing Castle of Nerantzia guarding the harbour. Italy occupied Kos from 1912 until after the Second World War, when the Dodecanese were united with Greece in 1948. A strong earthquake damaged Kos Town in 2017.

Geography and tourism

The mostly low-lying island rises to Mount Dikeos in the south, and its long sandy northern beaches, cycling-friendly flat terrain, hot springs at Therma, and villages such as Zia, Antimachia (with its windmill and traditional house), and Kefalos attract large numbers of visitors in summer. Kos International Airport connects the island with charter flights from across Europe. The Plane Tree of Hippocrates in Kos Town — a venerable descendant, by legend, of the tree under which Hippocrates taught — and the archaeological remains of the agora and Roman Odeon anchor the town's heritage tourism.
