The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask is an action-adventure game developed and published by Nintendo for the Nintendo 64 in 2000. The sixth main instalment of The Legend of Zelda series and the direct sequel to Ocarina of Time, it is distinguished by its three-day time cycle, transformation masks, and an unusually dark, melancholic tone.

Gameplay and story

Link, searching for his lost fairy Navi, is ambushed by the Skull Kid wearing the cursed Majora's Mask and stranded in Termina, a parallel land whose moon will crash into Clock Town in three days. Using the Ocarina of Time, the player repeatedly rewinds the 72-hour cycle — with townsfolk following fixed, learnable schedules — while Link must free four giants to stop the moon. Transformation masks turn Link into a Deku Scrub, a Goron and a Zora, and two dozen further masks reward intricate side quests, including the celebrated intertwined fates of Anju and Kafei.

Development and reception

Produced under a demanding one-year schedule reusing Ocarina of Time's engine and assets, the game required the N64 Expansion Pak for its enhanced graphics and larger areas. It sold over three million copies and is now frequently ranked among the greatest video games ever made; its themes of grief, death and acceptance have inspired extensive critical literature. An enhanced remake, Majora's Mask 3D, was released for Nintendo 3DS in 2015.
