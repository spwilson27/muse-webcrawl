Karkonosze

The Karkonosze (Czech: Krkonoše; German: Riesengebirge, "Giant Mountains") are a mountain range on the border between Poland and the Czech Republic, forming the highest part of the Sudetes. Their highest summit, Śnieżka (1,603 m), is the highest point in the Czech Republic and a landmark visible across the region.

Geography and nature

The range runs roughly northwest–southeast for about 40 km. Its granite ridge is marked by glacial cirques, rock formations, alpine tundra and raised bogs; rare relict and endemic plants survive from the Ice Age. Two national parks — Karkonosze National Park in Poland (est. 1959) and Krkonoše National Park in the Czech Republic (est. 1963) — protect the range, which is also a UNESCO biosphere reserve.

Culture and tourism

The mountains are associated in folklore with the mountain spirit Liczyrzepa (Rübezahl). They are among Central Europe's most popular hiking and skiing areas, with resorts such as Szklarska Poręba, Karpacz and Špindlerův Mlýn, and attractions including the Wang Church in Karpacz and the observatory atop Śnieżka.
