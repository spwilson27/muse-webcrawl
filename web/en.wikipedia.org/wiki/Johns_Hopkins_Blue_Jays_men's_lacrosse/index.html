The Johns Hopkins Blue Jays men's lacrosse team represents Johns Hopkins University in National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) Division I men's college lacrosse. Founded in 1883, it is one of the most storied programmes in the sport's history, with a total of 44 national championships when pre-NCAA era titles are included.

History

Playing at Homewood Field in Baltimore, the Blue Jays dominated college lacrosse for long stretches, winning NCAA championships in 1974, 1978, 1979, 1980, 1984, 1985, 1987, 2005 and 2007 under coaches including Bob Scott, Henry Ciccarone, Don Zimmerman and Dave Pietramala. The programme's fiercest rivalry is with the Maryland Terrapins, a series dating to 1895 and regarded as lacrosse's greatest rivalry; the teams compete for the Rivalry Trophy ("The Big Crabcake"). Other historic rivals include Virginia, Syracuse, Navy and Princeton.

Long an independent in lacrosse, Johns Hopkins joined the Big Ten Conference as an affiliate member when the league began sponsoring the sport in 2015. The programme has produced numerous members of the National Lacrosse Hall of Fame and more All-Americans than any other school, and it helped supply players to the United States national teams that won early world championships.
