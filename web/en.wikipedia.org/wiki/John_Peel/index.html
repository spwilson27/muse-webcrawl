John Robert Parker Ravenscroft (30 August 1939 – 25 October 2004), better known as John Peel, was an English disc jockey and radio presenter. He was the longest-serving of the original BBC Radio 1 DJs, broadcasting regularly from 1967 until his death in 2004.

Early career

After national service, Peel worked in the United States in the early 1960s, presenting on stations in Dallas and Oklahoma City and in California, where he capitalised on American interest in British music during the "British Invasion". Returning to England in 1967, he broadcast on the pirate station Radio London under the name John Peel before joining the new BBC Radio 1.

Radio 1

Peel became famous for championing new and underground music across genres: progressive rock, reggae, punk, post-punk, indie rock, hip hop, electronic music and extreme metal all received early national exposure on his late-night shows. The "Peel sessions" — live studio recordings by visiting bands — became a rite of passage; thousands were recorded, including sessions by Led Zeppelin, Pink Floyd, The Smiths, Joy Division, Nirvana and his favourite band, The Fall. He famously played the Undertones' "Teenage Kicks" twice in a row and called it his favourite record of all time.

From 1998 he also presented the Radio 4 magazine programme Home Truths. Peel died of a heart attack while on a working holiday in Cusco, Peru, in 2004; thousands of fans and musicians paid tribute, and the Glastonbury Festival named a stage after him.
