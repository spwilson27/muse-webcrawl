Buys Ballot's law

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In meteorology, Buys Ballot's law states that if a person stands with their back to the wind in the Northern Hemisphere, the atmospheric pressure is lower on their left than on their right. The rule is reversed in the Southern Hemisphere. It was formulated in 1857 by the Dutch meteorologist C. H. D. Buys Ballot, building on the theoretical work of William Ferrel.

Explanation

The law follows from the geostrophic balance: air flowing around pressure systems is deflected by the Coriolis effect, circling anticlockwise around low pressure in the Northern Hemisphere and clockwise in the Southern Hemisphere. A sailor facing downwind could thus locate the centre of a storm — the traditional mnemonic warns mariners that facing the wind puts the storm centre to their right in northern latitudes.

Applications

Before the era of weather charts and radio forecasts, Buys Ballot's law was a practical tool for sailors and farmers to infer the position of pressure systems and anticipate weather changes from wind direction alone.
