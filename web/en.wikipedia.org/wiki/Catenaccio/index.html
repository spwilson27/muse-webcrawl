Catenaccio

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Catenaccio (Italian for "door-bolt", pronounced [kateˈnattʃo]) is a tactical system in association football with a strong emphasis on defence. Originating in the 1930s and perfected in Italy in the 1960s, the system is characterised by an organised back line protected by a sweeper (libero) positioned behind the defenders, man-marking of opposing forwards, and rapid counter-attacks once possession is won.

Origins

The system derives from the verrou ("bolt") of Austrian coach Karl Rappan at Servette and the Swiss national team in the 1930s, which introduced a defender held back behind the defensive line. In Italy, Nereo Rocco applied a defensive, counter-attacking approach at Triestina and later at AC Milan, winning the European Cup in 1963 and 1969.

Grande Inter

Catenaccio reached its most famous form under Helenio Herrera at Inter Milan in the 1960s. Herrera's "Grande Inter" combined strict man-marking, Giacinto Facchetti's attacking full-back play and lethal counter-attacks to win three Serie A titles and consecutive European Cups in 1964 and 1965, plus two Intercontinental Cups.

Legacy

Although criticised as negative, catenaccio established durable principles — defensive compactness, the sweeper role, and transitional counter-attacking — that influenced later Italian coaching and the development of zonal marking systems. The term is now often used loosely, and sometimes pejoratively, for any strongly defensive Italian style of play.
