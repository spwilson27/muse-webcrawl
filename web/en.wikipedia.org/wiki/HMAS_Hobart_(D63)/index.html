HMAS Hobart (D 63) was a Perth-class guided missile destroyer of the Royal Australian Navy (RAN). Built in the United States to a modified Charles F. Adams-class design, she was commissioned in 1965 and was one of three Perth-class ships that formed the backbone of the RAN's destroyer force for over three decades.

Service history

Hobart served on multiple deployments to the Vietnam War, conducting naval gunfire support off the Vietnamese coast. During her 1968 deployment she was attacked by United States Air Force aircraft in a friendly-fire incident in June 1968, in which two crewmen were killed and the ship damaged; the attack was attributed to misidentification amid the same series of incidents that struck other Allied vessels that night.

In December 1974, following Cyclone Tracy, Hobart took part in Operation Navy Help Darwin, the relief effort for the devastated city of Darwin. She also deployed to the Persian Gulf in 1990–91 as part of Australia's contribution to the Gulf War.

Fate

Hobart was decommissioned on 12 May 2000. After preparation as an artificial reef and dive site, she was scuttled on 5 November 2002 in Yankalilla Bay, South Australia, off the Fleurieu Peninsula, where she remains a popular wreck dive. Her name was later carried by the lead ship of the Hobart-class air warfare destroyers, commissioned in 2017.
