Ryogo Kubo

Born1920, Tokyo, Japan
Died1995
Known forKubo formula; KMS condition
FieldsStatistical mechanics, physics

Ryogo Kubo (1920–1995) was a Japanese mathematical physicist best known for his foundational work in statistical mechanics and nonequilibrium thermodynamics. His name is attached to the Kubo formula, which expresses the response of a physical system to an external perturbation in terms of equilibrium correlation functions, and to the Kubo–Martin–Schwinger (KMS) condition, a characterisation of thermal equilibrium states in quantum statistical mechanics.

Career

Kubo studied at the University of Tokyo, where he spent most of his career. His 1957 paper on the statistical-mechanical theory of irreversible processes became one of the most cited works in theoretical physics, laying the groundwork for linear response theory.

Contributions

Working with collaborators including Kazuhisa Tomita, Kubo developed methods for treating magnetic resonance absorption and relaxation. The Kubo formula underlies modern calculations of electrical conductivity, viscosity and other transport coefficients from first principles.

Legacy

Kubo received numerous honours, including the Boltzmann Medal. His textbook-style reviews and lectures shaped generations of researchers in condensed matter and statistical physics.
