The blue crane (Grus paradisea, also placed in the genus Anthropoides) is a bird in the crane family, Gruidae. It is the national bird of South Africa, where the great majority of the population is found.

Description

Blue cranes stand just over a metre tall and are pale blue-grey overall, with a white crown, a pinkish bill and long, drooping black tail feathers that sweep the ground and are sometimes mistaken for a long tail. They are relatively small and lightly built for cranes.

Habitat and conservation

The species favours dry grasslands, especially in the Karoo and the eastern grasslands of South Africa, and increasingly feeds in agricultural fields. Numbers fell sharply in parts of its range during the late 20th century owing to habitat conversion, poisoning and collisions with power lines; the species is listed as Vulnerable, and it remains culturally prominent, appearing on South Africa's five-cent coin.
