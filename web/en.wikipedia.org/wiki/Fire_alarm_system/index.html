Fire alarm system

A fire alarm system is a building system designed to detect and alert occupants and emergency forces of the presence of smoke, fire, carbon monoxide, or other fire-related emergencies. Fire alarm systems are required in most commercial buildings. They may include smoke detectors, heat detectors, and manual fire alarm activation devices, all of which are connected to a fire alarm control panel (FACP), normally found in an electrical room or panel room. Fire alarm systems generally use visual and audio signalization to warn the occupants of the building. Some fire alarm systems may also disable elevators, which under most circumstances, are unsafe to use during a fire.

Design

Fire protection system design begins with an assessment of the needs of the building and its occupancy. In the United States, fire alarm systems in most structures are governed by NFPA 72, the National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code, which sets requirements for installation, performance, testing, inspection and maintenance.

Components

  • Fire alarm control panel (FACP): the central hub of the system, which monitors inputs and controls outputs.
  • Initiating devices: smoke detectors, heat detectors, duct detectors, manually operated pull stations, and waterflow switches.
  • Notification appliances: strobe lights, horns, bells, chimes, and speakers that alert occupants.
  • Primary and secondary power supplies, including backup batteries.
  • Remote annunciators that indicate the location of an alarm.

Occupant notification

Notification appliances use audible, visible, tactile, or textual signals to alert occupants. In the United States, audible signals typically use a standardized temporal-three (T-3) pattern. Voice evacuation systems use recorded or live voice messages to direct occupants to safety, and are required in certain high-rise and assembly occupancies.
