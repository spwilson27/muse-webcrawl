Toast (honor)

A toast is a ritual in which a drink is taken as an expression of honour or goodwill.

A toast is a ritual in which a drink is taken as an expression of honour or goodwill. The term may refer to the person or thing so honoured, the drink taken, or the verbal expression accompanying the drink. Thus, a person could be "the toast of the evening", for whom someone "proposes a toast" to congratulate them and in agreement with which everyone lifts their glass and takes a drink.

History

The custom of drinking to another's health descends from ancient religious practices such as libations, in which offerings of wine were poured out to the gods. Toasting became common in ancient Rome and Greece. The English word derives from the practice of putting a piece of spiced toast into a communal wine cup — mentioned by Shakespeare in The Merry Wives of Windsor — so that the flavour would improve. By the 18th century the person honoured was figuratively said to flavour the drink like the toast itself, and "toast" came to denote the honouree.

Etiquette

Toasts are typically offered at times of celebration, such as weddings, birthdays, holidays and retirements. The host usually offers the first toast. Guests stand, raise their glasses, take a sip after the toast is concluded, and may gently clink glasses — a custom traditionally said to drive away evil spirits or to ensure the drink was not poisoned by splashing between cups. In many cultures eye contact is maintained while clinking.

International expressions

Common toast phrases include "Cheers" in English, "Santé" in French, "Prost" in German, "Salud" in Spanish, "Kanpai" in Japanese, "Na zdorovie" in Russian contexts, and "L'chaim" ("to life") in Hebrew.
