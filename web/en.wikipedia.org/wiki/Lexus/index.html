Lexus (Japanese: レクサス) is the luxury vehicle division of the Japanese automaker Toyota. The Lexus brand is marketed in more than 90 countries and territories worldwide and is Japan's largest-selling make of premium cars. It has ranked among the ten largest Japanese global brands in market value.

History

Lexus was created at the same time as Japanese rivals Honda and Nissan created their Acura and Infiniti luxury divisions. The first Lexus vehicles, the LS 400 flagship sedan and the ES 250 entry-level sedan, debuted in 1989 in the United States. The LS 400 was the product of a secret flagship project, codenamed F1 ("Flagship One"), which began in 1983 and involved thousands of engineers, designers and support staff.

The brand's slogan in its early years was "The Relentless Pursuit of Perfection." Lexus was initially sold only in the United States before expanding to other markets; it was introduced in its home market of Japan in 2005, making it the first Japanese luxury car marque to be launched in Japan.

Operations

Since the 2000s, Lexus design has been characterized by distinctive styling including the "spindle grille" introduced in 2012. Lexus vehicles are produced largely in Japan, with manufacturing centred in the Chūbu and Kyūshū regions, and in Ontario, Canada. Lexus models include sedans, coupes, convertibles, SUVs and performance "F" marque variants. The marque has also been a pioneer of luxury hybrid vehicles, beginning with the RX 400h in 2005.

Motorsport

Lexus entered motorsport with the SC 430 in Japan's Super GT series and has competed in endurance racing, including the Rolex 24 at Daytona, with the RC F GT3.
