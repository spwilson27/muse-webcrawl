Maple

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Maples are trees and shrubs of the genus Acer, with about 130 species, mostly in temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. They are recognised by their opposite leaves — often palmately lobed, as on the Canadian flag — and their paired winged fruits (samaras) that spin as they fall. Maples are planted worldwide for shade and autumn colour.

Maple syrup and uses

The sugar maple of North America is tapped in late winter: its sap is boiled down — roughly 40 litres of sap make 1 litre of syrup — a tradition learned from Indigenous peoples and now a major industry in Quebec and Vermont. Maple timber ('hard maple') is used for flooring, furniture and musical instruments, and soft maples and Japanese maples are garden staples.

Ecology

Maple seeds, buds and leaves feed many animals, and maples are important forest trees in eastern North America and East Asia. Autumn colour peaks when cool nights and sunny days trigger red anthocyanin pigments. Some species, such as Norway maple, have become invasive in North American forests.
