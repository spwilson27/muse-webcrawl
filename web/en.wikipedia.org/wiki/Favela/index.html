Favela

A favela is a type of low-income informal settlement in Brazil that has experienced historical governmental neglect. The term originates from the Canudos War era, named after the faveleira plant and Morro da Favela in Rio de Janeiro.

History

The first favelas appeared in the late 19th century, built by soldiers returning from the War of Canudos who camped on hills in Rio de Janeiro. Over the 20th century, rural migration and rapid urbanization expanded informal settlements in Brazilian cities, particularly Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo.

Characteristics

Favelas are characterized by self-built housing, dense occupation, insecure land tenure, and historically limited access to public services such as sanitation, piped water and formal electricity. Many have developed strong community organizations, cultural scenes, and local economies.

Demographics

According to the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE), millions of Brazilians live in favelas, formally termed "aglomerados subnormais". Rocinha in Rio de Janeiro is among the largest.
