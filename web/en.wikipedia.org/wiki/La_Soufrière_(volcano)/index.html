La Soufrière (also known as Soufrière Guadeloupe) is an active stratovolcano on the island of Basse-Terre in Guadeloupe, an overseas department of France in the Caribbean. At 1,467 metres it is the highest mountain peak in the Lesser Antilles. The volcano is a young edifice within the caldera of the older Sans Toucher volcano; its present summit consists of a lava dome emplaced around five centuries ago.

Eruptive history

La Soufrière's most significant historical eruption occurred in 1976–77. A phreatic crisis beginning in July 1976 produced explosions, ash falls, and fears of a major magmatic eruption, leading the authorities to evacuate about 73,000 people from Basse-Terre and surrounding communes for more than three months. The episode became famous for a public dispute among volcanologists—notably Haroun Tazieff and Claude Allègre—over the danger posed, and was documented in Werner Herzog's film La Soufrière. In the end no magmatic eruption occurred and there were no direct deaths, but the evacuation imposed heavy economic costs.

Monitoring and hazards

The volcano is monitored by the Observatoire Volcanologique et Sismologique de Guadeloupe. Its crater area is characterised by fumaroles, acid lakes, and fractures emitting sulfurous gases; access to the summit is regulated because of toxic gas emissions and unstable ground. The 1976 crisis remains a reference case in volcanic risk management and the science-policy debate over precautionary evacuation.

La Soufrière should not be confused with La Soufrière on the island of Saint Vincent, which erupted explosively in 2021, or with the Soufrière Hills volcano on Montserrat.
