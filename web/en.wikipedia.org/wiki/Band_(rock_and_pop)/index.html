Band (rock and pop)

A typical rock band line-up performing live

In rock and pop music, a band is a small musical ensemble of typically three to five musicians who perform with electric and acoustic instruments, as distinct from a solo artist or an orchestra. The classic rock band line-up, established in the 1960s, consists of a lead vocalist (who may also play an instrument), one or two electric guitarists, a bass guitarist and a drummer; keyboards, synthesisers and other instruments are common additions.

Forms and organisation

Common configurations include the power trio (guitar, bass, drums) and the four-piece band with separate lead singer. Bands usually perform under a group name rather than the members' individual names, and the group's identity persists — sometimes controversially — through changes of personnel. Bands write and arrange songs collectively or through a principal songwriter, record albums, and tour as self-contained live acts, traditionally supported by a record label, manager and road crew. The model grew out of skiffle, rhythm-and-blues and dance bands and was popularised worldwide by groups such as the Beatles, whose success made the self-written, self-performed band the dominant unit of popular music from the 1960s onward. Unlike orchestral players, band members are usually expected to be creative contributors and public personalities, and the band's name, image and back catalogue function as a durable commercial brand.
