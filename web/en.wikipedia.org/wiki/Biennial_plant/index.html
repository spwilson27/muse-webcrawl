Biennial plant

A biennial plant is a flowering plant that normally takes two years to complete its biological life cycle. In the first year the plant grows leaves, stems and roots (vegetative structures); it then enters a period of dormancy over the colder months. In the second year the stem elongates rapidly ("bolts"), the plant flowers, produces fruits and seeds, and then dies.

Many biennials form a low rosette in their first year.

Life cycle

Ordinarily the plant survives only one flowering season, but under favourable conditions some biennials may persist for additional years, and some annuals behave as biennials when their growth is checked by cold or drought. The transition to flowering in the second year usually requires vernalisation — exposure to winter cold — which triggers the hormonal changes, including gibberellin production, that cause bolting.

Examples and horticulture

Familiar biennials include parsley, carrot, parsnip, foxglove, hollyhock, sweet William, Canterbury bells, honesty and many varieties of onion and cabbage grown for seed. Vegetable growers who want roots harvest biennial crops in the first year; seed producers keep them into the second. Some weeds, such as poison hemlock, are biennial, which affects control strategies.
