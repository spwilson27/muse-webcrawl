Larmor precession

A magnetic moment precessing about an applied magnetic field B.
Named afterJoseph Larmor
FieldElectromagnetism, quantum mechanics

In physics, Larmor precession (named after Joseph Larmor) is the precession of the magnetic moment of an object about an external magnetic field. Objects with a magnetic moment experience a torque when placed in a magnetic field, causing the moment to rotate about the field direction at a characteristic angular frequency rather than simply aligning with it.

ω = −γB

where ω is the Larmor (angular) frequency, γ is the gyromagnetic ratio, and B is the applied magnetic field. For an electron the Larmor frequency in a field of 1 tesla is approximately 28 GHz per tesla when expressed in ordinary frequency units through the electron gyromagnetic ratio.

Applications

Larmor precession is the physical basis of nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI): radio-frequency pulses resonant with the Larmor frequency of nuclei such as hydrogen-1 tip the nuclear magnetisation, and the subsequent precession signal is detected. It also plays a central role in electron spin resonance, atomic physics (including the normal Zeeman effect) and the operation of atomic clocks.

Thomas precession

A related relativistic effect, Thomas precession, arises for spinning objects moving along curved trajectories in special relativity and contributes, for example, to the fine structure of atomic spectra through spin–orbit coupling.
