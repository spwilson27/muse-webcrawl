Martinus Beijerinck

Martinus Willem Beijerinck (16 March 1851 – 1 January 1931) was a Dutch microbiologist and botanist, one of the founders of virology and environmental microbiology. He is credited with being one of the founders of the Delft school of microbiology.

Life and career

Born in Amsterdam, Beijerinck studied at the Technical School in Delft and the University of Leiden, where he earned his doctorate in 1877. He worked at the Netherlands Yeast and Spirit Factory in Delft, founding its microbiological laboratory, and in 1895 became professor at the Polytechnical School in Delft, where he remained until his retirement in 1921.

Scientific contributions

In 1898, studying tobacco mosaic disease, Beijerinck showed that the infectious agent passed through a filter that trapped bacteria and, crucially, that it multiplied only in living tissue — he called it a contagium vivum fluidum, reviving the term "virus". Dmitri Ivanovsky had reported similar filtration results in 1892, but Beijerinck grasped the agent's nature more fully.

Beijerinck discovered nitrogen fixation in legume root nodules, isolating the root-nodule bacterium (later named Rhizobium leguminosarum), and he isolated the first free-living nitrogen fixer, Azotobacter. He developed the enrichment culture technique — selecting microbes by designing their environment — which became fundamental to microbiology. He also discovered sulfate-reducing bacteria (Spirillum desulfuricans) and bacterial denitrification. His aphorism "everything is everywhere, but the environment selects" summarises his ecological view of microbial distribution.
