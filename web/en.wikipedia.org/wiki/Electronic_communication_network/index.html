Electronic communication network

An electronic communication network (ECN) is a type of computerized forum or network that facilitates the trading of financial products outside traditional stock exchanges. An ECN is registered with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission as a broker-dealer and reflects orders of investors and buyers by matching buy and sell orders among its subscribers.

How it works

ECNs display the best available bid and ask quotes from multiple market participants and automatically match and execute orders. They allow trading outside traditional exchange hours (extended-hours trading) and give smaller orders anonymity and often lower commissions. Major historical ECNs include Instinet (founded 1969), Island, Archipelago, and BRUT; many were eventually acquired by or converted into formal exchanges.

History and regulation

The SEC's Regulation ATS (1998) formalized the status of alternative trading systems including ECNs, and Regulation NMS (2005) integrated them into the national market system, requiring order protection across venues. ECNs contributed to the rise of high-frequency and algorithmic trading and to the decline of floor trading. In foreign exchange markets, "ECN" brokers similarly aggregate liquidity from banks and other participants.
