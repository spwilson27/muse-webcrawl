The Niagara Whirlpool is a natural whirlpool located along the Niagara River about 4.5 kilometres (2.8 mi) downstream from Niagara Falls, straddling the international border between Ontario, Canada, and New York, United States. It is one of the most powerful whirlpools in the world.

Formation

The whirlpool formed about 7,500 years ago when the retreating falls intersected a pre-glacial river channel, the buried St. David's Gorge. The river suddenly changed direction and began flowing through loose glacial debris at a right angle, creating the churning basin. The water enters the whirlpool at high speed and exits beneath the incoming flow, producing a continuous counterclockwise rotation, which can reverse when water levels change.

Tourism

The whirlpool is a popular tourist attraction. The Whirlpool Aero Car, a Spanish-designed cable car in operation since 1916, carries passengers across the gorge above the whirlpool. On the American side, the site lies within Whirlpool State Park, while the Canadian side offers viewing platforms and trails.
