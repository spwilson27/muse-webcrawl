Pythagorean triple

A Pythagorean triple consists of three positive integers a, b and c such that a2 + b2 = c2. Such a triple is commonly written (a, b, c); a famous example is (3, 4, 5). The numbers correspond to the side lengths of a right triangle with integer sides.

Primitive triples

A triple is called primitive if a, b and c are coprime. Every primitive triple can be generated by Euclid's formula: a = m2 − n2, b = 2mn, c = m2 + n2, for coprime positive integers m > n of opposite parity. Non-primitive triples are multiples of primitive ones.

History

Lists of Pythagorean triples appear on the Babylonian clay tablet Plimpton 322, dating to around 1800 BCE, long before Pythagoras. Triples were also known in ancient Indian and Chinese mathematics, and their study connects to Fermat's Last Theorem, which states that no analogous triples exist for exponents higher than 2.
