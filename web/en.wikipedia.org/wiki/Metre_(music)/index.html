Metre (music)

In music, metre (American spelling: meter) is the regular pattern of recurring beats and accents that structures a piece of music. Metre is specified at the beginning of a score by a time signature and organises durations into uniform groups called bars or measures.

Types of metre

Metres are classified by the number of beats per bar and by how each beat subdivides. Metres with two, three, or four beats are duple, triple, and quadruple respectively. When each beat divides into two, the metre is simple (e.g. 2/4, 3/4, 4/4); when each beat divides into three, it is compound (e.g. 6/8, 9/8, 12/8). Irregular or asymmetric metres, such as 5/4 or 7/8, combine unequal beat groups and are prominent in folk traditions of the Balkans and in progressive rock. Additive rhythms build patterns from unequal small units, and some music, such as plainsong or free improvisation, is effectively ametrical.

Perception

Listeners entrain to metre, perceiving a hierarchy of stronger and weaker beats. Metrical dissonances — hemiola, syncopation, and polyrhythm — arise when patterns conflict with the prevailing metre, a resource exploited in much Western, African, and Indian music.
