MATE (pronounced "mah-teh") is a free and open-source desktop environment that runs on Linux and other Unix-like operating systems. It was forked from the codebase of GNOME 2 in response to the redesign of GNOME 3, which many users found unsuitable. The project's name derives from yerba mate, a South American plant used to brew a traditional tea.

History

MATE was announced in 2011 by Argentine developer Perberos on the Arch Linux forums, after GNOME 3 replaced the traditional desktop metaphor with the GNOME Shell. The fork retained the classic two-panel interface and traditional menus of GNOME 2 while development continued on its components.

Core applications were renamed to avoid conflicts with GNOME components: the Nautilus file manager became Caja, the Eye of GNOME image viewer became Eye of MATE, and the gedit text editor became Pluma. Over time, the codebase was ported to GTK 3 and modernised.

Applications and adoption

MATE includes its own window manager, Marco, and file manager, Caja. It is the default desktop environment of Ubuntu MATE and is available on most major Linux distributions, where it is appreciated for its low resource usage and familiar layout.
