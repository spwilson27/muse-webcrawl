Leo the Lion (MGM)

Leo the Lion is the mascot of the American film studio Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer (MGM) and its predecessors, appearing in the studio's production logo since 1924. The lion is framed by a ring of film inscribed with the studio's motto, Ars Gratia Artis ("Art for Art's Sake"), and famously roars at the start of MGM films.

The lions

Several different lions have played the role over the decades. The first, Slats (1917–1928), was used by Goldwyn Pictures from 1917 and by the newly merged MGM in the silent era; he merely looked around rather than roaring. His successor Jackie was the first to roar on screen, his growl introduced via phonograph record for MGM's first sound-era logo (1928). The 1930s–1950s saw the brief tenures of Telly and Coffee, then Tanner, whose roar accompanied Technicolor productions for two decades. The lion most familiar to modern audiences is Leo himself, in use from 1957 — the longest-serving of the mascots — whose logo was rendered in CGI in 2021, modeled on Leo's filmed performance while Leo remains the reference image.

Origin

The logo was designed for Goldwyn Pictures by publicist Howard Dietz, a Columbia University alumnus who took the lion from his alma mater's athletic mascot. The trained animals were supplied by pioneering Hollywood animal trainers such as Volney Phifer. The roaring lion — parodied countless times, including by the Marx Brothers and in MGM's own cartoons — became one of the most recognisable trademarks in cinema history.
