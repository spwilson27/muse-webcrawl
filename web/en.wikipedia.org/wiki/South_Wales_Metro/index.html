South Wales Metro

South Wales Metro
Location: Cardiff and the South Wales Valleys, Wales
Operator: Transport for Wales
Type: Integrated heavy rail, light rail and bus network
Key works: Core Valley Lines electrification

The South Wales Metro is an integrated public transport network under development in south-east Wales, centred on Cardiff and the former coal-mining Valleys. Led by Transport for Wales, the project aims to transform travel in the Cardiff Capital Region with faster, more frequent services and integrated ticketing.

Core Valley Lines

The centrepiece is the upgrade of the Core Valley Lines (to Merthyr Tydfil, Aberdare, Treherbert, Rhymney, and Coryton), transferred from Network Rail to Welsh Government ownership in 2020. The lines are being electrified with 25 kV AC overhead equipment (with battery capability on some vehicles), and new fleets — Class 231 diesel FLIRTs, Class 756 tri-mode FLIRTs, and Class 398 tram-trains — are being introduced to raise frequencies to up to four trains per hour on valley routes.

Wider network

Plans include new stations, bus integration, and possible extensions such as tram-train services to Cardiff Bay and beyond, forming the first phase of a broader "Metro" concept for the region.
