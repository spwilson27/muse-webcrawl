Driving test

A driving test (also called a road test or practical test) is a procedure designed to test a person's ability to drive a motor vehicle. It exists in various forms worldwide and is usually a prerequisite for obtaining a driver's licence. A typical test combines a theory component, examining knowledge of road rules and signs, with a practical on-road assessment by an examiner.

PurposeAssess fitness to hold a driver's licence
ComponentsTheory test; practical road test; eyesight check
Administered byGovernment licensing agencies

Theory test

Most jurisdictions require a written or computer-based theory test covering traffic laws, road signs and hazard perception. In the United Kingdom, the theory test introduced in 1996 includes a multiple-choice section and a video-based hazard perception section. In the United States, a written knowledge test is usually taken to obtain a learner's permit.

Practical test

The practical test assesses vehicle control, manoeuvres such as parking and reversing, observation, junctions, and independent driving following directions or a satellite navigation route. Faults are classified by seriousness; accumulating too many minor faults, or a single serious or dangerous fault, results in failure. Examiners may also include an emergency stop and questions about basic vehicle safety checks.

History

Compulsory driving tests were introduced in France in the 1890s and in the United Kingdom by the Road Traffic Act 1934, taking effect in 1935. Standards and formats have evolved with graduated licensing schemes in many countries restricting new drivers by age, night driving and passengers.
