Fréjus Rail Tunnel

The Fréjus Rail Tunnel, also called the Mont Cenis Tunnel, is a rail tunnel of 13.7 km (8.5 mi) length in the European Alps, carrying the Turin–Modane railway through Mont Cenis to an end-on connection with the Culoz–Modane railway and therefore linking Modane, France and Bardonecchia, Italy.

History

Construction began in 1857 under the Kingdom of Sardinia, directed by the engineer Germain Sommeiller, whose team pioneered the use of pneumatic drilling machines powered by compressed air. The tunnel was officially opened on 17 September 1871, becoming the first great Alpine tunnel and an engineering landmark of the nineteenth century.

Geography

Passing beneath the Fréjus Pass, the tunnel's highest point is about 1,338 metres above sea level. The original single bore was later complemented by a second tube, and the route remains a key freight and passenger link between France and Italy, although traffic has declined with the opening of competing routes.
