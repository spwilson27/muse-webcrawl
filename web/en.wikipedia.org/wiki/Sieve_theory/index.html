Sieve theory

Sieve theory is a set of general techniques in number theory, designed to count, or more realistically to estimate the size of, sifted sets of integers. The prototypical example of a sifted set is the set of prime numbers up to some prescribed limit X, obtained by removing from the integers all numbers divisible by small primes, as in the sieve of Eratosthenes and the Legendre sieve.

Development

Modern sieve theory grew from Viggo Brun's work in the early 20th century, which produced Brun's theorem on twin primes. Later developments include the large sieve, the Selberg sieve, the combinatorial sieve, and the work of Chen Jingrun on Goldbach's conjecture, as well as the GPY sieve and the Maynard–Tao results on bounded gaps between primes.

Limitations

A notorious difficulty is the parity problem, which limits the ability of classical sieve methods to distinguish numbers with an odd number of prime factors from those with an even number.
