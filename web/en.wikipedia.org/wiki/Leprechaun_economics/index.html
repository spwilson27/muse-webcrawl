Leprechaun economics

Leprechaun economics is a term coined by Nobel Prize-winning economist Paul Krugman in July 2016 to describe the extraordinary distortion of Ireland's national accounts by the tax and transfer-pricing practices of multinational corporations. The phrase went viral after Ireland's Central Statistics Office reported that Irish GDP had grown by 26.3% in 2015.

Background

The reported 2015 growth figure — later revised to 34.4% — reflected not a surge in domestic economic activity but bookkeeping movements: corporate inversions and relocations of intellectual property and aircraft-leasing assets into Ireland, largely by U.S. multinationals taking advantage of Ireland's low corporate tax regime and structures such as the "Double Irish." Because Ireland's economy is small relative to the balance sheets of the companies booking assets there, these flows overwhelmed the headline statistics, rendering GDP and GNP nearly useless as measures of Irish living standards.

Response

The episode drew international ridicule and serious criticism from the IMF, the European Commission, and Irish economists. In response, the Central Statistics Office convened a review group and in 2017 introduced an alternative measure, GNI* (modified Gross National Income), designed to strip out the effects of foreign-owned intellectual property, aircraft leasing, and redomiciled firms. The affair became a standard case study in the economics of globalisation and tax avoidance.
