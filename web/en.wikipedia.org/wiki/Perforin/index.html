Perforin

Perforin (perforin-1, gene symbol PRF1) is a pore-forming cytotoxic protein stored in the granules of cytotoxic T lymphocytes and natural killer (NK) cells. Upon degranulation at the immunological synapse, perforin is released onto a target cell, polymerises in its plasma membrane in the presence of calcium, and forms pores that allow the entry of pro-apoptotic granzymes, triggering the target cell's death by apoptosis.

Mechanism

Perforin monomers bind the target membrane and assemble into ring-shaped pores of up to roughly twenty subunits. Through these pores, granzyme proteases enter the cytosol and activate caspases and other death pathways. The killer cell itself is protected from its own perforin by the low pH and membrane composition of its granules and by surface protective proteins.

Clinical significance

Inherited perforin deficiency in humans causes familial haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis type 2, a life-threatening hyperinflammatory syndrome of infancy caused by failure to kill infected antigen-presenting cells and switch off the immune response. Perforin-deficient mice likewise show impaired control of viral infections and increased susceptibility to some tumours. Perforin is structurally related to the membrane attack complex protein complement C9, and both belong to the MACPF protein family.
