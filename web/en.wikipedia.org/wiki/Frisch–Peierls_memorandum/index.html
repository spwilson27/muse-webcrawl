Frisch–Peierls memorandum

The Frisch–Peierls memorandum was a March 1940 document by émigré physicists Otto Frisch and Rudolf Peierls at the University of Birmingham that first set out a practical design for an atomic bomb: it showed the critical mass of uranium-235 needed was far smaller — on the order of kilograms — than previously assumed, making an air-delivered weapon feasible.


Date: March 1940
Authors: Otto Frisch, Rudolf Peierls
Effect: Led to MAUD Committee and Tube Alloys

Contents

The memorandum estimated the critical mass, the destructive effects of the explosion and fallout, and proposed isotope separation methods. It warned that Germany might pursue such a weapon.

Impact

Passed via Mark Oliphant to British authorities, it prompted the MAUD Committee, the Tube Alloys programme, and British input to the Manhattan Project. Compare the physics in Fusion power, and Frisch's aunt Lise Meitner's role in discovering fission.
