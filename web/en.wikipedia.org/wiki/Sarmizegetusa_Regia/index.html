Sarmizegetusa Regia

The sanctuary complex at Sarmizegetusa Regia
LocationOrăștie Mountains, Hunedoara County, Romania
Altitude~1,200 m
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (1999)

Sarmizegetusa Regia was the capital and the most important military, religious, and political centre of the Dacian kingdom before and during the wars with the Roman Empire in the early second century AD. Situated at about 1,200 metres altitude in the Orăștie Mountains of present-day Romania, it was the largest of the Dacian fortresses.

Description

The site consists of a fortress, a sacred zone, and a civilian settlement spread over terraces cut into the mountainside. The sacred area contains several temples and sanctuaries, including a famous circular sanctuary and the "andesite sun", a round stone altar whose arrangement is thought to have had calendrical significance. A paved ceremonial road linked the fortress to the sanctuaries.

History

Sarmizegetusa Regia reached its height under Decebalus, the last Dacian king. After the Roman conquest of Dacia following the campaigns of the emperor Trajan (101–102 and 105–106 AD), the fortress was destroyed and its population removed; the Romans built a new provincial capital, Ulpia Traiana Sarmizegetusa, some 40 km away. The site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1999 as part of the Dacian Fortresses of the Orăștie Mountains.
