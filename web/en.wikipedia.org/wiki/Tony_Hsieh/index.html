Tony Hsieh

Tony Hsieh (December 12, 1973 – November 27, 2020) was an American internet entrepreneur and venture capitalist, best known as the longtime chief executive of the online shoe retailer Zappos, which he built around a culture of customer service and employee happiness and sold to Amazon in 2009 for about $880 million.

Early career

Born in Illinois to Taiwanese immigrant parents and raised in California, Hsieh graduated from Harvard University in 1995 with a degree in computer science. After a brief stint at Oracle he co-founded the advertising network LinkExchange, sold to Microsoft in 1998 for $265 million. Through his investment firm Venture Frogs he became an early investor in the fledgling shoe site ShoeSite.com, soon renamed Zappos, joining as CEO in 2000.

Zappos and "Delivering Happiness"

Under Hsieh, Zappos grew on free shipping, generous returns and a call-centre ethos in which staff were encouraged to delight customers; the company moved to Las Vegas in 2004. His 2010 book Delivering Happiness became a bestseller. From 2013 he adopted the self-management system Holacracy at Zappos and committed roughly $350 million to the Downtown Project, an effort to revitalise downtown Las Vegas.

Later life and death

Hsieh retired as Zappos CEO in August 2020. He died on November 27, 2020, from complications of smoke inhalation sustained in a house fire in New London, Connecticut, aged 46; subsequent reporting examined his final months, marked by heavy drug and alcohol use and erratic spending in Park City, Utah.
