Sesshin

Sesshin
TraditionZen Buddhism (esp. Rinzai and Sōtō)
Literal meaning"Touching/gathering the heart-mind" (接心)
Typical length1–7 days (traditionally up to 49 or 90 days in intensive training periods)

A sesshin (接心) is a period of intensive meditation (zazen) in a Japanese Zen monastery or lay retreat. The literal translation is "touching the heart-mind"; the term is sometimes translated as "gathering the mind" or "heart-to-heart contact".

Structure

A typical sesshin lasts from one to seven days (some run much longer; the week-long Rohatsu sesshin in early December commemorates the Buddha's enlightenment). Days begin before dawn and alternate 25–40 minute periods of seated zazen with walking meditation (kinhin), punctuated by meals taken in formal ōryōki style, short work periods (samu), chanting, dharma talks (teishō) and private interviews with the teacher (dokusan or sanzen), in which Rinzai students present their work on kōans. Silence is normally maintained throughout.

Purpose and experience

Sesshin is intended to deepen practice beyond home sitting: the strict schedule, minimal sleep and sustained concentration create conditions for insight and often for intense physical and psychological challenges, which teachers frame as part of the training. Lay sesshin have become central to Zen practice in Europe and North America since the mid-20th century.
