Four stages of competence

The four stages of competence, also known as the four stages of learning, is a learning model in psychology relating to the stages a person passes through when acquiring a new skill. The model is generally attributed to Noel Burch of Gordon Training International in the 1970s.

The stages

  1. Unconscious incompetence — the individual does not understand or know how to do something and does not necessarily recognize the deficit; they may deny the usefulness of the skill.
  2. Conscious incompetence — the individual recognizes the deficit and the value of the skill, and begins to learn; making mistakes is integral to this stage.
  3. Conscious competence — the individual can perform the skill reliably but must concentrate and think through the steps.
  4. Unconscious competence — the skill has become "second nature"; it can be performed easily, while doing other things, and can be taught to others.

A proposed fifth stage, "conscious competence of unconscious competence" or reflective competence, is sometimes added for mastery and the ability to teach instinctively.
