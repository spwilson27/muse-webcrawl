Neural development

Neural development, or neurodevelopment, is the biological process by which the nervous system forms, matures, and is reshaped during the life of an organism. In animals it begins in early embryogenesis with the specification of neural tissue from the ectoderm and continues through neurogenesis, neuronal migration, axon guidance, synaptogenesis, and activity-dependent refinement, into postnatal life.

Neurulation

In vertebrates, signals from the notochord induce the overlying ectoderm to form the neural plate, whose edges rise and fuse to create the neural tube — the precursor of the brain and spinal cord — while neural crest cells delaminate and migrate widely to form much of the peripheral nervous system. Failure of tube closure produces neural tube defects such as spina bifida and anencephaly.

Regionalisation and neurogenesis

Gradients of morphogens, including sonic hedgehog, Wnt, BMP, and retinoic acid, pattern the tube along its axes and specify distinct progenitor domains. Neural progenitor cells then divide — first symmetrically to expand the pool, then asymmetrically — generating neurons in an orderly inside-out sequence in the cerebral cortex, followed by glial cells.

Migration and axon guidance

Newborn neurons migrate, often along radial glia, to their final positions. Their axons navigate to distant targets using growth cones that read attractive and repulsive cues — netrins, slits, semaphorins, and ephrins — so that orderly topographic maps, such as the retinotectal projection, are established.

Synapse formation and pruning

Synapses form in excess and are then refined: spontaneous and sensory-evoked activity stabilises some connections and eliminates others, a process accompanied by programmed cell death of surplus neurons and by myelination, which continues into early adulthood in humans, particularly in the prefrontal cortex.

Disorders

Errors in neurodevelopment underlie conditions including lissencephaly, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorders, and schizophrenia, in which genetic and environmental influences act at different developmental windows.
