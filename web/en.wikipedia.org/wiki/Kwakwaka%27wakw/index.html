Kwakwaka'wakw

RegionNorthern Vancouver Island and mainland inlets, British Columbia
LanguageKwak'wala (Wakashan)

The Kwakwaka'wakw ("Kwak'wala-speaking peoples") are an Indigenous people of the Pacific Northwest Coast in British Columbia, Canada, living around the Queen Charlotte Strait and Johnstone Strait. Their language, Kwak'wala, belongs to the Wakashan family.

History and culture

Traditional Kwakwaka'wakw society was based on fishing — especially salmon and eulachon — and on ranked kin groups holding hereditary rights displayed at potlatches. Contact with Europeans began in the late 18th century; epidemic disease and colonial policies, including the criminalisation of the potlatch from 1885 to 1951, caused severe disruption. The U'mista Cultural Centre in Alert Bay houses repatriated potlatch regalia confiscated in 1922.
