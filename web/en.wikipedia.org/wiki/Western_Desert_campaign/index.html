Western Desert campaign

The Western Desert campaign (also known as the Desert War) took place in the deserts of Egypt and Libya from June 1940 to February 1943, during the Second World War. It formed the principal land theatre of the wider North African campaign, fought between the Axis forces of Italy and Germany and the Allied forces, dominated by the British Commonwealth.

Background

Italy declared war on Britain and France on 10 June 1940. Italian forces in Libya under Marshal Rodolfo Graziani faced the much smaller British Western Desert Force in Egypt. After a tentative Italian advance to Sidi Barrani in September 1940, the British counter-attacked in December.

Operation Compass

Operation Compass (December 1940 – February 1941), commanded by Major-General Richard O'Connor, destroyed the Italian Tenth Army, taking some 130,000 prisoners and advancing 800 kilometres to El Agheila. The success prompted Germany to send the Afrika Korps under Erwin Rommel to rescue its ally.

The seesaw war, 1941–42

Rommel's arrival in February 1941 began a period of advance and retreat across Cyrenaica. Axis forces besieged Tobruk for much of 1941; the port was relieved by Operation Crusader in November–December 1941. Rommel counter-attacked in January 1942, and in June won a major victory at the Battle of Gazala, capturing Tobruk and advancing into Egypt, where he was halted at the First Battle of El Alamein in July 1942.

El Alamein and the end

Under General Bernard Montgomery, the British Eighth Army won the decisive Second Battle of El Alamein (23 October – 11 November 1942), forcing the Axis into a long retreat westward. Following the Allied landings of Operation Torch in November 1942, Axis forces were squeezed into Tunisia, where the Western Desert campaign merged into the Tunisian campaign, ending with the Axis surrender in May 1943.

Character of the war

The campaign was fought largely by mobile, mechanised forces across open desert, with supply lines stretched along a single coastal road. Control of ports such as Tobruk and Benghazi was vital, and the war was characterised by rapid advances, encirclements and chronic logistical strain on both sides.
