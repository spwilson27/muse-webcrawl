Carlos Manuel de Céspedes

Carlos Manuel de Céspedes
Born18 April 1819, Bayamo, Cuba
Died27 February 1874, Sierra Maestra, Cuba

Carlos Manuel de Céspedes del Castillo (18 April 1819 – 27 February 1874) was a Cuban revolutionary hero and independence leader, regarded in Cuba as the Father of the Homeland (Padre de la Patria). A wealthy planter and lawyer from Bayamo, he launched Cuba's first war of independence against Spain, the Ten Years' War (1868–1878).

Grito de Yara

On 10 October 1868, at his sugar estate La Demajagua, Céspedes freed his slaves and called on them to join the struggle for Cuban independence — an act known as the Grito de Yara. The date is still celebrated in Cuba as the beginning of the independence wars. His manifesto declared Cuba free and called for the gradual abolition of slavery.

Later life

Céspedes served as the first president of the Republic of Cuba in Arms, elected by the Guáimaro Assembly in 1869, but he was deposed in 1873 amid disputes with the revolutionary legislature. He was killed on 27 February 1874 when Spanish troops surprised his refuge at San Lorenzo in the Sierra Maestra, after the government had withdrawn his escort.

His son, Carlos Manuel de Céspedes y Quesada, later briefly served as President of Cuba in 1933.
