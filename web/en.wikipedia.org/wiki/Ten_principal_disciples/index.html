Ten principal disciples

The ten principal disciples (Japanese: 十大弟子, Jūdai Deshi) were the ten foremost male disciples of Gautama Buddha in Mahayana Buddhist tradition. Each is traditionally regarded as foremost in a particular quality or power.

The ten

  • Śāriputra — foremost in wisdom
  • Maudgalyāyana (Mahāmoggallāna) — foremost in supernatural powers (iddhi)
  • Mahākāśyapa — foremost in ascetic practices; convener of the First Council
  • Subhūti — foremost in understanding emptiness
  • Pūrṇa Maitrāyaṇīputra — foremost in preaching the Dharma
  • Kātyāyana (Mahākaccāna) — foremost in expounding the teachings in detail
  • Aniruddha (Anuruddha) — foremost in the divine eye (clairvoyance)
  • Upāli — foremost in observing the precepts (Vinaya)
  • Rāhula — the Buddha's son; foremost in inconspicuous practice and desire for training
  • Ānanda — the Buddha's cousin and attendant; foremost in hearing (memory of the teachings)

Significance

The group is frequently depicted in East Asian Buddhist art flanking the Buddha. Lists vary slightly between Pali and Sanskrit sources, and separate traditions recognise foremost bhikkhunīs (nuns) such as Kṣemā and Utpalavarṇā.
