Scottish National Portrait Gallery

Location: 1 Queen Street, Edinburgh
Opened: 1889
Architect: Sir Robert Rowand Anderson
Collection: Portraits of notable Scots, Scottish photography
Part of: National Galleries of Scotland

The Scottish National Portrait Gallery is an art museum in Edinburgh, Scotland, holding the national collection of portraits of historically important and famous Scots, together with the Scottish National Photography Collection. It is housed in a distinctive red sandstone neo-Gothic building on Queen Street in the New Town, and is administered by the National Galleries of Scotland. Admission is free.

Building and history

The gallery was funded principally by John Ritchie Findlay, proprietor of The Scotsman newspaper, who donated £50,000 for a purpose-built home. Designed by Sir Robert Rowand Anderson and built between 1885 and 1890, the building is clad in red Dumfriesshire sandstone, with statues of Scottish heroes along its facades and a great hall decorated with a processional frieze by William Hole depicting figures from Scottish history. It opened in 1889 as one of the first purpose-built portrait galleries in the world. The building was originally shared with the National Museum of Antiquities of Scotland, whose collections moved to the National Museum of Scotland in 2009. After a major refurbishment — the "Portrait of the Nation" project — the gallery reopened in December 2011 with greatly expanded display space.

Collection

The collection spans from the Renaissance to the present day and includes portraits of Mary, Queen of Scots; Robert Burns; David Hume; Adam Smith; Sir Walter Scott; and Flora MacDonald, as well as contemporary photography and commissions. Portraits need not depict Scots exclusively by birth — the criterion is significance to Scottish history and culture. The photography collection includes major holdings of Hill & Adamson calotypes and work by later Scottish photographers.
