The Actors' Equity Association (AEA), commonly called Equity, is an American labor union representing professional actors and stage managers working in live theatre. Founded in 1913, it negotiates wages and working conditions and administers contracts covering Broadway, touring productions and hundreds of regional and smaller theatres across the United States.

History

Equity was founded on 26 May 1913 by 112 actors in New York, with Broadway producer-supported exploitation as its spur: actors at the time rehearsed without pay for unlimited periods and could be stranded when tours collapsed. After affiliating with the American Federation of Labor in 1919, the union called its first strike that August, shutting down Broadway productions and winning recognition with the first standard Equity contract — a landmark victory featuring figures such as Ethel Barrymore and union president Francis Wilson.

Role and contracts

Equity negotiates collective agreements — including the Production Contract (Broadway and tours), LORT (League of Resident Theatres), and various regional and developmental codes — setting minimum salaries, rehearsal and performance hours, breaks, safe working conditions and health and pension contributions. Performers working under Equity contracts are generally union members, whose Equity Membership Candidate (EMC) programme provides a path to membership through work at participating theatres. The union also campaigns on issues such as workplace safety, bullying and harassment, and diversity in casting.

Relations with other unions

Equity is distinct from SAG-AFTRA, which represents film and television performers; the boundary between the two has historically been contested as productions cross media. British performers are represented by the separate UK union also called Equity.
