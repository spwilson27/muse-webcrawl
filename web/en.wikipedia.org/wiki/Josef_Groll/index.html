Josef Groll

Josef Groll (21 August 1813 – 22 November 1887) was a Bavarian brewmaster best known as the inventor of the Pilsner beer style. In 1842, at the new Citizens' Brewery (Bürgerbrauerei) in Plzeň (Pilsen), Bohemia, he brewed the first batch of pale lager using soft local water, pale malt, Saaz hops, and Bavarian bottom-fermenting technique — the beer that became Pilsner Urquell and gave its name to the world's most widely imitated beer style.

Background

Groll came from a brewing family in Vilshofen, Lower Bavaria; his father ran a brewery and Groll himself became a master brewer. Bavarian brewers of the era were pioneers of cold-stored, bottom-fermented lager beers.

The first Pilsner

In 1838 the citizens of Plzeň, dissatisfied with the quality of their top-fermented beer — legend holds that 36 barrels were publicly poured away in protest — obtained a brewing charter and built a modern lager brewery. They hired Groll as their first brewmaster. On 5 October 1842 he brewed the first batch of the new beer, combining Bavarian lagering with very pale malts dried in a new English-style kiln, soft Plzeň water, and aromatic Saaz hops. The golden, clear beer was first served on 11 November 1842 and caused a sensation; the style spread across Europe and the world as "Pilsner".

Later life

Groll's contract in Plzeň ended in 1845, and he returned to Vilshofen, where he later inherited his father's brewery. He died there in 1887, reportedly in the brewery taproom. The Plzeň brewery he launched still brews Pilsner Urquell ("Original Source") and commemorates him as the father of the pilsner style.
