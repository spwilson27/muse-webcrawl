Accusation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An accusation is a statement or claim that a person has done something wrong, illegal or blameworthy. In law, an accusation is the formal charge that initiates or accompanies criminal proceedings against an accused person, who in many legal traditions is protected by the presumption of innocence until proven guilty. Accusations may also be informal — made in everyday life, in the media or in politics — in which case they carry social and reputational consequences without legal process, and a false accusation may itself be actionable as defamation or malicious prosecution.

In criminal law

Formal accusation typically takes the form of an indictment (a charge presented by a grand jury in common-law jurisdictions such as the United States, where the Fifth Amendment requires indictment for serious federal crimes), an information filed by a prosecutor, or a complaint. The accused — the defendant — is entitled in constitutional democracies to be informed of the nature and cause of the accusation: the Sixth Amendment to the United States Constitution guarantees this right, together with a speedy and public trial and the right to confront witnesses. The burden of proving an accusation beyond a reasonable doubt rests on the prosecution.

False accusations

A false accusation is one made without basis, whether mistakenly or deliberately. Legal systems variously penalise false reporting to police, perjury (false accusation under oath) and malicious prosecution. Wrongful convictions arising from false testimony or mistaken identification have been documented by innocence projects in many countries. Accusations made in bad faith for political purposes — as in witch trials, show trials and McCarthy-era hearings in the United States — are a recurring historical theme.

In rhetoric and everyday life

Outside the law, an accusation is a speech act attributing fault. Recriminations ("counter-accusations") are a common response, and debaters describe the diversionary tactic of answering an accusation with one of their own as whataboutism. In psychology and conflict resolution, framing complaints as "I-statements" rather than accusations is recommended to reduce defensiveness.
