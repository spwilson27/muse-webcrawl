The Communist Party of Nepal (Unified Marxist–Leninist), commonly abbreviated CPN–UML, is a major communist political party in Nepal. It was formed in 1991 through the merger of the Communist Party of Nepal (Marxist) and the Communist Party of Nepal (Marxist–Leninist).

History

The party emerged from the unification of two communist factions ahead of Nepal's first multiparty elections after the 1990 popular movement restored parliamentary democracy. Under leaders such as Man Mohan Adhikari and Madan Bhandari, the CPN–UML became one of the two dominant parties in Nepali politics.

In 1994, Man Mohan Adhikari became Prime Minister, leading the first communist government in Nepal's history. Madan Bhandari's doctrine of "People's Multiparty Democracy" (Janata ko Bahudaliya Janabad) remains the party's guiding ideology.

Electoral performance

The CPN–UML has repeatedly been among the largest parties in the Constituent Assembly and later the federal parliament. In 2018 it briefly merged with the Maoist party to form the Nepal Communist Party before the merger was annulled by the Supreme Court in 2021, reviving the CPN–UML.

Leadership

Prominent leaders have included Madan Bhandari, Man Mohan Adhikari, Madhav Kumar Nepal, Jhala Nath Khanal, and K. P. Sharma Oli, who has served multiple terms as Prime Minister of Nepal.
