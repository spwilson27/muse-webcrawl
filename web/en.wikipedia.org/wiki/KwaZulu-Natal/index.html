KwaZulu-Natal (KZN) is a province of South Africa created in 1994 by the merger of the former province of Natal with the Zulu bantustan of KwaZulu. It lies in the southeast of the country, bordering the Indian Ocean to the east, Mozambique and Eswatini to the north, and the provinces of Mpumalanga, Free State and Eastern Cape. Its capital is Pietermaritzburg; its largest city and principal port is Durban.

Geography

The province rises from a warm, humid coastal plain through rolling midlands to the dramatic escarpment of the Drakensberg, whose peaks exceed 3,000 metres and form part of the uKhahlamba-Drakensberg Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site noted for San rock art. Major rivers include the Tugela (Thukela), which drops over the Tugela Falls, among the tallest waterfalls in the world. Game reserves such as Hluhluwe–iMfolozi Park, the oldest proclaimed nature reserve in Africa, protect rhino populations and anchor a major tourism industry.

History

The region was unified in the early 19th century under Shaka, founder of the Zulu kingdom. Zulu forces defeated a British column at Isandlwana in 1879 before the kingdom was subdued in the Anglo-Zulu War. Natal became a British colony and then a province of the Union of South Africa in 1910. Under apartheid, Zulu-speaking areas were consolidated into the KwaZulu homeland, led for decades by Mangosuthu Buthelezi and the Inkatha Freedom Party. The transition to democracy in the early 1990s was accompanied by severe political violence between Inkatha and ANC supporters in the province. Since 1994 KwaZulu-Natal has generally been governed by the ANC, with coalition politics prominent after the 2024 elections.

Economy

Durban is South Africa's busiest general-cargo port, and Richards Bay is a major bulk-export harbour. Manufacturing, sugar-cane agriculture, forestry and tourism are leading sectors; the province accounts for a substantial share of national GDP, second only to Gauteng.
