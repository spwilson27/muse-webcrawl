Firearms regulation in the United States

Firearms regulation in the United States is governed by a combination of federal, state, and local laws. The right to keep and bear arms is protected by the Second Amendment to the United States Constitution, which the Supreme Court interpreted in District of Columbia v. Heller (2008) as protecting an individual right to possess a firearm for self-defense in the home, and in New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen (2022) as protecting a right to carry a handgun in public.

Federal law

Major federal statutes include the National Firearms Act of 1934, which taxes and registers machine guns, short-barreled rifles and shotguns, suppressors, and destructive devices; the Gun Control Act of 1968, which regulates interstate commerce in firearms, licenses dealers, and prohibits sales to certain categories of persons; and the Brady Handgun Violence Prevention Act of 1993, which established the National Instant Criminal Background Check System (NICS). The Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF) administers federal firearms law.

State law

States vary widely: some require permits to purchase or carry firearms, universal background checks, and restrictions on certain semiautomatic rifles and magazines, while others have adopted permitless ("constitutional") carry. Many states preempt local firearms regulation. Federal law sets a floor, and state rules generally operate in addition to it except where preempted.

Statistics and debate

The United States has the highest rate of civilian firearm ownership in the world, with more guns than people. Regulation remains one of the most contested issues in American politics, with debate centred on gun violence, mass shootings, self-defense, and constitutional rights.
