Pierre de Fermat (1607–1665) was a French mathematician and lawyer whose work founded much of modern number theory and who, with Blaise Pascal — of Pascal's wager fame — created the theory of probability. A magistrate by profession, he published little and circulated results in letters to other mathematicians.

Legacy

Fermat anticipated differential calculus with his method of finding maxima and tangents, stated Fermat's principle of least time in optics, and posed the problem known as Fermat's Last Theorem, whose marginal claim to a proof tantalised mathematicians for more than 350 years.
