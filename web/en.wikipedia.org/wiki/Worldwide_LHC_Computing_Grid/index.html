Worldwide LHC Computing Grid

Launched2008 (full operation)
OperatorCERN, with partner institutions worldwide
PurposeStorage and analysis of Large Hadron Collider data
ScaleOver 170 computing centers in more than 40 countries

The Worldwide LHC Computing Grid (WLCG), formerly the LHC Computing Grid (LCG), is a global collaboration of computing centers designed to store, distribute, and analyze the enormous volume of data produced by the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) at CERN. It is one of the largest distributed computing infrastructures in the world, and a direct descendant of the research networking culture that produced the World Wide Web at CERN.

Why a grid

The LHC's detectors record hundreds of collisions per second, generating tens of petabytes of data per year — far more than CERN alone could store or process. The grid distributes data and computation across a tiered hierarchy of centers so that thousands of physicists worldwide can analyze the data as if it were local.

Tiered structure

Tier 0 is CERN itself, which records the raw data, performs first-pass reconstruction, and distributes copies to Tier 1 centers — a dozen or so large national facilities (including Fermilab in the US, GridPP in the UK, and centers in France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Canada, the Nordic countries, and Asia) that hold permanent archival copies and perform large-scale reprocessing. Roughly 160 Tier 2 centers, mostly at universities, provide simulation and end-user analysis capacity, while Tier 3 facilities serve local groups.

Scale and operation

The WLCG links its centers with dedicated high-bandwidth networks and grid middleware for job scheduling, data transfer, and authentication. By the 2010s it routinely handled more than a million computing jobs per day. The infrastructure was essential to processing the data that led to the discovery of the Higgs boson in 2012.
