Population growth is the increase in the number of people in a population, driven by births and immigration minus deaths and emigration (natural increase plus net migration). World population passed 1 billion around 1804, 2 billion in 1927, and 8 billion in November 2022 (UN estimate) — an acceleration driven by falling mortality (sanitation, medicine, food supply) preceding falling fertility: the 'demographic transition'.

Growth is now slowing: global fertility is around 2.3 children per woman (approximate) and the UN projects a peak near 10.4 billion in the 2080s, with growth concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa while many countries in Europe and East Asia face population decline and ageing. Malthus's 1798 warnings about geometric population outrunning arithmetic food supply framed two centuries of debate, offset so far by agricultural and industrial productivity — and sharpened by environmental limits arguments.
