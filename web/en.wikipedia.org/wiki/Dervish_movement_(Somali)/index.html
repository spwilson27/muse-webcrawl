The Dervish movement (Somali: Dhaqdhaqaaqa Darawiish) was a popular Somali resistance movement led by the Muslim leader Mohammed Abdullah Hassan that operated in the Horn of Africa from 1899 to 1920. The movement resisted the colonial partition of Somali lands by the British, Italian and Ethiopian empires and sought to establish an independent Somali state organised around Islam and the tariqa tradition.

Origins

Mohammed Abdullah Hassan, a mullah of the Ogaden clan who had studied in Mecca and joined the Salihiyya Sufi order, returned to Somalia in the 1890s preaching against the encroachment of Christian colonial powers and against Somali clans that cooperated with them. In 1899 he declared a jihad and began assembling an army of followers known as Darawiish ("Dervishes"), drawn largely from the Dhulbahante and other clans of the northern interior.

The campaigns

Between 1900 and 1904 the Dervishes fought a series of engagements against British-led expeditions in the Somaliland Protectorate, at times scoring notable victories, including the defeat of a British column at Dul Madoba in 1913, where the commanding officer Richard Corfield was killed. The movement repeatedly shifted its base between the Somali interior, and at points received sympathy and material support from the Ottoman Empire and, during the First World War, Germany.

Under the 1905 Illig Agreement the movement was granted a measure of autonomy in Italian Somaliland, but fighting resumed. The Dervishes built a chain of stone forts across the interior, the most famous being the capital at Taleh.

Defeat

In early 1920 the British launched a combined air, sea and land offensive — one of the earliest uses of air power against an insurgency — bombing Taleh and the other Dervish forts. The movement collapsed; Hassan fled to the Ogaden, where he died in December 1920, reportedly of influenza. The twenty-year resistance remains a central episode in Somali national memory, and Hassan is celebrated in Somali oral poetry, much of it his own composition.

Legacy

The Dervish movement is regarded as an early expression of Somali nationalism. Its leader's poetry became foundational to modern Somali literature, and the movement's memory was later invoked by independent Somalia, where Hassan is honoured as a national hero, commonly remembered by the epithet given him by the British: the "Mad Mullah".
