Bonn is a city on the banks of the Rhine in the German state of North Rhine-Westphalia, about 25 km south of Cologne. Founded by the Romans in the 1st century BC, it is one of Germany's oldest cities. From 1949 to 1990 Bonn was the capital of the Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany), and it remained the seat of government until 1999.


Bonn and the Rhine

History

The Roman fort of Castra Bonnensia guarded the Rhine frontier. In the Middle Ages Bonn became a residence of the prince-electors of Cologne, whose Baroque palaces still shape the city. Ludwig van Beethoven was born in Bonn in 1770; his birthplace is now a museum, and the city celebrates him with the annual Beethovenfest. The University of Bonn, founded in 1818, became one of Germany's leading universities.

Capital of West Germany

In 1949 the Parliamentary Council, meeting in Bonn, drafted the Basic Law, and the city was chosen — narrowly, over Frankfurt — as the provisional capital of the new Federal Republic. For four decades the modest Rhineland city hosted the Bundestag, the federal ministries, and the embassies of the world. After reunification, the Bundestag voted in 1991 to move the seat of government to Berlin; the move was completed in 1999, but under the Berlin/Bonn Act several federal ministries retain their headquarters in Bonn, and the city styles itself the "Federal City" (Bundesstadt).

Modern Bonn

Today Bonn is a centre for telecommunications and logistics — Deutsche Telekom and DHL Group are headquartered there — and hosts numerous United Nations institutions, especially those concerned with climate and the environment, including the secretariat of the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change. The former government quarter now forms a museum and conference district along the "Museum Mile".
