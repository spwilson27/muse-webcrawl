Soju

Soju (Korean: 소주; 燒酒) is a clear, distilled alcoholic beverage native to Korea. It is usually consumed neat and is by far the most popular alcoholic drink in South Korea. Most modern brands are produced in South Korea, and the best-selling brand, Jinro, has repeatedly ranked as the world's top-selling spirit by volume.

History

Distilled liquor arrived on the Korean Peninsula in the 13th century during the Mongol invasions, when Yuan dynasty forces introduced distillation techniques derived from Middle Eastern arak traditions; the name soju means "burned liquor", referring to distillation. Traditional soju is made by fermenting and distilling rice wine. Goryeo- and Joseon-era centres such as Kaesong and Andong became famous for their styles; Andong soju remains a protected traditional product at around 45% ABV.

During a rice shortage in 1965, the South Korean government banned the use of rice for soju. Producers switched to diluted ethanol made from sweet potatoes, tapioca, and other starches, flavoured and sweetened — the style that came to dominate the market in the familiar green bottle. Typical modern soju is about 16–20% ABV, milder and sweeter than earlier versions. Fruit-flavoured sojus have expanded the market since the mid-2010s.

Production and brands

Traditional soju is single-distilled from fermented grain mash. Mass-market "diluted" soju blends neutral spirit with water, sweeteners such as stevia, and flavourings. Major producers include HiteJinro (Chamisul and Jinro brands), Lotte Chilsung (Chum Churum), and regional makers, each Korean province historically having its own brand. Craft and premium distilled sojus have grown in popularity both domestically and abroad.

Culture

Soju is central to Korean drinking culture and etiquette: it is poured for others (traditionally with two hands for elders), received with two hands, and drunk in shots, often alongside food or mixed with beer as somaek. The standard 360 ml green bottle is a fixture of Korean restaurants and convenience stores, and soju is one of Korea's signature exports.
