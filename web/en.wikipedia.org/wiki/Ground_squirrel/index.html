Ground squirrel

Ground squirrels are rodents of the squirrel family (Sciuridae) that generally live on or in the ground, rather than in trees. The term is most often applied to the medium-sized ground squirrels of the tribe Marmotini, which includes marmots, chipmunks, prairie dogs and the many species of true ground squirrels in genera such as Spermophilus, Urocitellus and Ictidomys.

Description

Ground squirrels vary considerably in size and habits. They typically have stout bodies, short legs, and fur that ranges from grizzled grey-brown to patterned with stripes or spots. Most dig extensive burrow systems with multiple entrances, used for shelter, raising young and hibernation.

Behaviour and ecology

Many species are highly social, living in colonies with alarm-call systems to warn of predators. They feed mainly on seeds, grasses, roots and insects. Numerous temperate species hibernate for much of the year, and some aestivate during hot, dry summers. Ground squirrels are important prey animals and ecosystem engineers in grasslands across North America, Europe and Asia.
