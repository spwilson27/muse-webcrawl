Chronostratigraphy

Chronostratigraphy is the branch of stratigraphy that studies the ages of rock strata in relation to time. The ultimate aim of chronostratigraphy is to arrange the sequence of deposition and the time of deposition of all rocks within a geological region, and eventually, the entire geologic record of the Earth.

The standard nomenclature used by geologists is based on a system of chronostratigraphic divisions developed since the 19th century and maintained by the International Commission on Stratigraphy, which publishes the International Chronostratigraphic Chart.

Units

Chronostratigraphic units are bodies of rock formed during a specific interval of time. They are distinguished from geochronologic units, which are the units of time itself:

  • Eonothem — corresponds to the eon
  • Erathem — corresponds to the era
  • System — corresponds to the period
  • Series — corresponds to the epoch
  • Stage — corresponds to the age

For example, the Jurassic System is the body of rock deposited during the Jurassic Period. Most boundaries of chronostratigraphic units are defined by Global Boundary Stratotype Sections and Points (GSSPs, colloquially "golden spikes") — physical reference points in rock outcrops agreed internationally.

Methods

Chronostratigraphy combines biostratigraphy (correlation by fossils), radiometric dating, magnetostratigraphy, and chemostratigraphy to place rock units into the global time framework. Unlike absolute dating alone, chronostratigraphy seeks a globally consistent ordering of strata.
