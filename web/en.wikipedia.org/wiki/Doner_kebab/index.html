Doner kebab
Origin: Ottoman Empire / Turkey; sandwich form popularised in Berlin
Main ingredients: Lamb, beef, or chicken; flatbread; salad; sauces
Variations: Dürüm, döner plate, iskender kebab

Doner kebab is a Turkish dish of seasoned meat cooked on a vertical rotisserie and shaved into thin slices. The word döner means 'rotating' in Turkish.

History

Vertical grilling developed in the 19th-century Ottoman Empire, associated with Bursa and the related İskender kebab. Turkish guest workers in West Berlin in the 1970s popularised doner served in flatbread as a fast-food sandwich, which became one of Germany's most popular street foods.

Preparation and serving

Marinated meat is stacked on a spit, roasted, and sliced with a long knife or electric cutter. It is served in pita or flatbread (dürüm wrap), or on a plate with rice, salad, pickled peppers, and sauces. Related dishes include shawarma in the Levant and gyros in Greece.
