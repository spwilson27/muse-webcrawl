Tyrol

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tyrol (German: Tirol) is a historical region in the Alps of western Austria and northern Italy, and a federal state of Austria. The region is centred on the upper Inn valley and the historic County of Tyrol, ruled for centuries by the Habsburgs from Innsbruck.

Geography

The Austrian state of Tyrol is divided into North Tyrol and East Tyrol, separated by a strip of Salzburg state. Its capital is Innsbruck, a two-time Winter Olympic host city (1964 and 1976). The landscape is dominated by high Alpine ranges including the Ötztal Alps and the Zillertal Alps, and by the Brenner Pass, a principal route across the Alps.

History

After the First World War the Treaty of Saint-Germain (1919) divided the old crown land: the southern part, including Bolzano and Trento, went to Italy, becoming today's autonomous provinces of South Tyrol and Trentino, while the north remained Austrian. South Tyrol's large German-speaking population obtained extensive autonomy within Italy in 1972.

Economy and culture

Tourism — skiing in winter and hiking in summer — is the mainstay of the economy, alongside hydropower and manufacturing. Tyrolean culture is known for its folk music, yodelling, traditional costume, and figures such as the freedom fighter Andreas Hofer.
