Howard Elliott Ashman (May 17, 1950 – March 14, 1991) was an American playwright and lyricist. He was the artistic director of the WPA Theatre and collaborated with composer Alan Menken on several films. He is best known for his work for Walt Disney Animation Studios, for which he wrote the lyrics for songs in The Little Mermaid (1989), Beauty and the Beast (1991), and Aladdin (1992). His work on these films is widely credited with helping to launch the Disney Renaissance.

Career

Ashman first worked with Menken on the 1979 musical adaptation of Kurt Vonnegut's God Bless You, Mr. Rosewater, and on the 1982 Off-Broadway hit Little Shop of Horrors, which Ashman directed and for which he wrote the book and lyrics; the pair also wrote the songs for its 1986 film adaptation, including the Oscar-nominated "Mean Green Mother from Outer Space." Ashman's Broadway musical Smile (1986), with music by Marvin Hamlisch, was a commercial disappointment, but his success at Disney soon followed.

At Disney, Ashman served as executive producer on The Little Mermaid and championed the Broadway-musical structure — including the "I want" song — that reshaped Disney animation. He and Menken won Academy Awards for "Under the Sea" and for "Beauty and the Beast." Ashman died of complications from AIDS in March 1991, before Beauty and the Beast was released; the film is dedicated to him with the inscription "To our friend Howard, who gave a mermaid her voice and a beast his soul." He was posthumously named a Disney Legend in 2001. The 2018 documentary Howard, directed by Don Hahn, chronicles his life.
