Cosmic horror

Cosmic horror (also known as Lovecraftian horror) is a subgenre of horror fiction and weird fiction that emphasises the horror of the unknowable and incomprehensible more than gore or other elements of shock. Its progenitor is the American author H. P. Lovecraft, who popularised themes of cosmic indifference, human insignificance, and ancient, godlike entities whose existence challenges human sanity.

Themes

Cosmic horror is grounded in cosmicism, Lovecraft's philosophical outlook that humanity is an insignificant speck in a vast, indifferent universe. Recurring motifs include forbidden knowledge that drives investigators mad, non-human entities such as Cthulhu and the other Great Old Ones, ancient ruins and cults, and the fragility of human reason and identity. As Lovecraft wrote in the opening of "The Call of Cthulhu" (1928): "The most merciful thing in the world, I think, is the inability of the human mind to correlate all its contents."

History and influence

Lovecraft developed the mode in the pulp magazine Weird Tales in the 1920s–30s, drawing on earlier writers such as Edgar Allan Poe, Lord Dunsany, Algernon Blackwood and Arthur Machen. His circle — including Robert E. Howard, Clark Ashton Smith and August Derleth — expanded what became the Cthulhu Mythos. Cosmic horror has profoundly influenced modern horror literature, film (such as Annihilation and The Lighthouse), and video games (such as Bloodborne).
