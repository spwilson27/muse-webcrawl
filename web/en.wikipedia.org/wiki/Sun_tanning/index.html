Sun tanning

Sun tanning
MechanismMelanogenesis triggered by ultraviolet radiation
Health effectsVitamin D synthesis; increased skin cancer and photoaging risk

Sun tanning or tanning is the process whereby skin colour is darkened. It is most often a result of exposure to ultraviolet (UV) radiation from sunlight or from artificial sources, such as a tanning lamp found in indoor tanning beds. People who deliberately tan their skin by exposure to the sun engage in a passive recreational activity of sun bathing.

Process

Tanning occurs when UV radiation causes melanocytes in the skin to produce more melanin pigment and to distribute it to surrounding keratinocytes. UVA radiation oxidises existing melanin and triggers rapid darkening within hours, while UVB stimulates production of new melanin over several days and also drives synthesis of vitamin D. The resulting tan provides a modest degree of protection — roughly equivalent to a sun protection factor of 2 to 4 — but this comes at the cost of DNA damage to skin cells.

Health effects

Excessive exposure to UV radiation is the principal environmental cause of skin cancers, including melanoma and non-melanoma skin cancers, and contributes to premature skin ageing (photoaging), sunburn and eye damage. Public health authorities including the World Health Organization recommend limiting deliberate tanning and using protective measures such as shade, clothing and sunscreen. At the same time, moderate sun exposure is a major source of vitamin D for much of the world's population, and recommendations balance these competing considerations.

Cultural history

In Western culture, pale skin was prized for centuries as a marker of leisure-class status, but in the 1920s — often credited to fashion designer Coco Chanel's accidental tan acquired on the French Riviera in 1923 — tanned skin became fashionable as a sign of health, wealth and outdoor leisure. The commercial sunscreen and tanning industries grew around the trend, followed later by indoor tanning salons and sunless tanning products such as dihydroxyacetone (DHA) lotions and spray tans.
