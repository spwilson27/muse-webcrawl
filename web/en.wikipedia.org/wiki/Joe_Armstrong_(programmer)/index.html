Joseph Leslie Armstrong (December 27, 1950 – April 20, 2019) was a British computer scientist who, working at the Ericsson Computer Science Laboratory, was one of the co-creators of the Erlang programming language, together with Robert Virding and Mike Williams. Erlang, designed in the 1980s for programming large, fault-tolerant telecommunications systems, became influential in distributed and concurrent computing, and Armstrong was its most prominent public advocate.

Career

Armstrong studied physics before turning to computing, and worked at Ericsson on systems for fault-tolerant software. With his colleagues he designed Erlang around lightweight processes, message passing, and the philosophy of "let it crash" — letting supervisors restart failed components rather than attempting defensive recovery. He wrote the book Programming Erlang: Software for a Concurrent World (2007) and was a widely sought conference speaker. He received a PhD from the Royal Institute of Technology (KTH) in Stockholm in 2003 and later taught there as an adjunct professor. His death in 2019 was widely mourned in the programming community.
