German South West Africa

Status: Colony of the German Empire
Established: 7 August 1884
Capital: Windhoek (from 1891)
Ended: 1915 (South African occupation); formally ceded 1919

German South West Africa (German: Deutsch-Südwestafrika) was a German colony in present-day Namibia from 1884 until 1915, when it was occupied by South African forces during World War I. It was administered by the Union of South Africa under a League of Nations mandate after 1919 and remained under South African control until Namibian independence in 1990.

Establishment

In 1883 the Bremen merchant Adolf Lüderitz purchased land around Angra Pequena (later Lüderitz Bay), and in April 1884 Chancellor Otto von Bismarck placed the territory under German protection. The colony's borders were settled with Britain and Portugal at the Berlin Conference era negotiations, and the Caprivi Strip was added in 1890 to give access to the Zambezi River.

Herero and Nama genocide

German rule provoked armed resistance, culminating in the Herero Wars of 1904–1908. After the Battle of Waterberg, General Lothar von Trotha issued an extermination order against the Herero; tens of thousands of Herero and Nama died from violence, starvation in the Omaheke desert, and in concentration camps such as Shark Island. The events are widely recognised as the first genocide of the 20th century, and Germany formally acknowledged and apologised for it in 2021.

Economy and settlement

German settlement remained small (about 15,000 by 1914). The discovery of diamonds near Lüderitz in 1908 triggered a rush, and railways linked the coast to Windhoek and the interior. Land expropriation for settlers was a central driver of conflict with indigenous peoples.
