Tomáš Baťa

Tomáš Baťa (3 April 1876 – 12 July 1932) was a Czech entrepreneur who founded the Bata shoe company in Zlín in 1894 and built it into one of the world's largest footwear manufacturers.

Career

Baťa started the business with his siblings Antonín and Anna using their inheritance. After setbacks, the firm prospered supplying boots to the Austro-Hungarian army in the First World War. Baťa adopted American mass-production methods, cut prices dramatically during the postwar recession by halving wages while lowering prices in his company stores, and pioneered the "Baťa system" of semi-autonomous workshops each acting as a profit centre. The company built company towns — most famously Zlín, rebuilt in functionalist style with housing, schools, and a hospital for workers — and replicated the model abroad, from Batanagar in India to Batawa in Canada.

Death and legacy

Baťa died in 1932 when his private aircraft crashed shortly after takeoff from Otrokovice in poor visibility. Control passed to his half-brother Jan Antonín Baťa. His son Thomas J. Bata later rebuilt the business from Canada after the Czechoslovak company was nationalized following the Second World War. Tomáš Baťa University in Zlín bears his name.
