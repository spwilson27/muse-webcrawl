Proton pump

A proton pump is an integral membrane protein pump that builds up a proton gradient across a biological membrane by transporting protons (hydrogen ions) against their concentration gradient, usually using ATP hydrolysis or another energy source.

Examples

Proton pumps occur throughout life. In mitochondria and chloroplasts, complexes of the respiratory and photosynthetic electron transport chains pump protons, and ATP synthase uses the resulting gradient to make ATP. The gastric H+/K+-ATPase acidifies the stomach and is the target of proton pump inhibitor drugs such as omeprazole. Bacteriorhodopsin is a light-driven proton pump in archaea.
