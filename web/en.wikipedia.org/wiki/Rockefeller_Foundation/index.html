Rockefeller Foundation

The Rockefeller Foundation is an American private foundation and medical research philanthropic organization based in New York City. Established by the six-generation Rockefeller family, it was founded on May 14, 1913, by Standard Oil magnate John D. Rockefeller, his son John D. Rockefeller Jr., and their senior advisor Frederick Taylor Gates.

Mission and history

The foundation's stated mission is "promoting the well-being of humanity throughout the world." Its early work included campaigns against hookworm in the American South, the founding of schools of public health including the Johns Hopkins School of Hygiene and Public Health, and support for medical education in China through the Peking Union Medical College.

Green Revolution

From the 1940s, the foundation funded agricultural research in Mexico and later Asia, supporting scientists such as Norman Borlaug, whose high-yield wheat varieties were central to the Green Revolution.

Recent work

Recent initiatives have focused on health systems, food security, climate resilience, and economic opportunity. The foundation's assets total several billion dollars, and it operates from its headquarters at 420 Fifth Avenue in New York.
