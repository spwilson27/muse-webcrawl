Theban Legion

Theban Legion

Allegiance: Roman Empire (legendary)

Size: 6,666 men (per tradition)

Commander: Saint Maurice

Place of martyrdom: Agaunum (Saint-Maurice, Switzerland)

Principal source: Eucherius of Lyon, Passio Acaunensium Martyrum (c. 443–450)

The Theban Legion (Latin: Legio Thebaica) is a legendary Roman legion said to have been recruited entirely from Christian soldiers in Thebes, Egypt, and to have been martyred en masse at Agaunum (modern Saint-Maurice-en-Valais, Switzerland) around 286 AD, during the reign of the emperor Maximian, for refusing to sacrifice to the pagan gods and to participate in the persecution of Christians. Its commander is remembered as Saint Maurice.

The legend

According to the earliest account, the Passio Acaunensium Martyrum by Eucherius, bishop of Lyon (written about 443–450), the legion of 6,666 men was twice decimated and finally executed in its entirety rather than obey impious orders. Maurice and his officers Exuperius and Candidus were venerated as martyrs, and the abbey of Saint-Maurice d'Agaune, founded in 515 by the Burgundian king Sigismund, became one of the great pilgrimage sites of the early medieval West.

Historicity

Historians debate whether the story rests on a historical kernel. No contemporary Roman source records the event, and the numbers and unanimity of the martyrdom are typical of hagiography. Some scholars connect the legend with the historical presence of Egyptian troops in the Alps, or with the martyrdom of Maurice transferred from elsewhere. Whatever its origins, the cult became immensely influential: Maurice became patron saint of the Holy Roman Empire, of soldiers and of the Swiss Guard tradition, and hundreds of churches bear the names of Theban martyrs, including those of the supposed detachments at Cologne (Saint Gereon) and Xanten (Saint Viktor).
