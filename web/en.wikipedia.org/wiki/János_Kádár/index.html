János Kádár (born János Czermanik, 26 May 1912 – 6 July 1989) was a Hungarian communist leader who ruled Hungary as general secretary of the Hungarian Socialist Workers' Party from 1956, following the suppression of the Hungarian Revolution of 1956, until 1988 — the longest-serving leader of a Warsaw Pact state besides the Soviet Union's own.

Rise

An illegal communist under the Horthy regime and imprisoned both then and later under Mátyás Rákosi's Stalinist purges, Kádár was installed by Moscow after Soviet troops crushed the revolution led by Imre Nagy, whom Kádár's government executed in 1958. Initial repression killed thousands and drove some 200,000 Hungarians into exile.

Kádárism

From the 1960s Kádár pursued a pragmatic reconciliation — "whoever is not against us is with us" — amnesties, relative cultural tolerance, and the New Economic Mechanism (1968), which introduced market elements. The resulting "Goulash Communism" gave Hungary the highest living standards in the Eastern Bloc and a reputation as "the happiest barrack in the camp," though funded increasingly by Western loans.

Decline

Stagnation and debt in the 1980s eroded his authority; reform communists removed him as general secretary in May 1988. He died on 6 July 1989, the day Hungary's Supreme Court rehabilitated Imre Nagy — weeks before the communist system collapsed.
