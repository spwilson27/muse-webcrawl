RTHK

Radio Television Hong Kong (RTHK) is the public broadcasting service in Hong Kong. Established in 1928 as the territory's first radio station (with the call sign GOW), it is unique among Hong Kong broadcasters in being operated as a government department — under the Commerce and Economic Development Bureau — while historically maintaining editorial independence under a charter. Its motto and mission emphasize serving the public with informative, educational, and entertainment programming in Cantonese, English, and Putonghua.

Services

RTHK operates seven radio channels, including Radio 1 (Cantonese news and information), Radio 2 (Cantonese youth and entertainment), Radio 3 (English), Radio 4 (classical music), Radio 5 (elderly and community programming), a Putonghua channel, and digital audio broadcasting successors. Its television division, long a producer of programmes aired on commercial channels, launched its own TV channels (RTHK TV 31, 32, and 33) in the 2010s. Well-known productions include the long-running current-affairs series Hong Kong Connection and the drama anthology Below the Lion Rock.

Editorial independence and controversies

RTHK's journalism has frequently won awards and public trust, but its status inside government has generated recurring debates over editorial independence, funding, and governance, intensifying after 2019–2021 with a government review, changes to its management, and requirements that it uphold national security education among its programming duties.
