Razavi Khorasan Province (Persian: استان خراسان رضوی) is one of the 31 provinces of Iran, located in the northeast of the country. Its capital and largest city is Mashhad, the second most populous city in Iran and one of the holiest cities in Shia Islam, home to the shrine of Imam Reza. The province borders Turkmenistan to the north and Afghanistan to the east.

The Imam Reza shrine in Mashhad draws millions of pilgrims each year.

History

The province forms the central part of the historic region of Khorasan, a cultural and political heartland of the Persian world since antiquity. On 29 September 2004, the former Khorasan Province — then Iran's largest — was divided into three provinces: Razavi Khorasan, North Khorasan, and South Khorasan. The name "Razavi" refers to Imam Reza (Ali al-Rida), the eighth Shia Imam, whose tomb in Mashhad made the city a major pilgrimage destination.

Geography

Razavi Khorasan covers about 118,884 square kilometres. Its terrain includes the Binalud and Hezar Masjed mountain ranges and arid plains extending toward the deserts of central Iran. The province has a generally dry, continental climate with cold winters and hot summers.

Economy and culture

Religious tourism centred on Mashhad dominates the economy; the city receives tens of millions of pilgrims annually. The province is also Iran's main centre for saffron production — Iran produces most of the world's saffron, much of it grown around Torbat-e Heydarieh — as well as agriculture, food processing, and carpet weaving. Other major cities include Nishapur, Sabzevar, Quchan, and Torbat-e Jam. Nishapur was a famed medieval centre of learning and the home of the poet Omar Khayyam.
