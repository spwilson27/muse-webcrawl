Govardhan Hill

Govardhan Hill (Sanskrit: गोवर्धन पर्वत), also called Mount Govardhana or Giriraj, is a sacred hill of sandstone and boulder near the town of Govardhan in Mathura district, Uttar Pradesh, India. It is a major site of pilgrimage in the Braj region, sacred to Vaishnavism.

According to Hindu tradition recorded in the Bhagavata Purana, the young Krishna lifted Govardhan Hill on his little finger for seven days to shelter the villagers of Vrindavan and their cattle from the torrential rains sent by the god Indra, humbling Indra's pride. The festival of Govardhan Puja commemorates this event.

Devotees perform parikrama, a ritual circumambulation of the hill of about 21 kilometres, visiting temples and sacred ponds such as Radha Kund and Kusum Sarovar along the route. The hill, which is slowly sinking according to legend, is protected as a religious site, and quarrying on it is banned.
