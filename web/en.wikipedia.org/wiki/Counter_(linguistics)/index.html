Counter (linguistics)

[Image placeholder: counter words example]

In linguistics, a counter is a type of numeral classifier: a word or morpheme used together with a numeral to indicate the kind of object being counted. Many languages, including Japanese, Korean, Chinese, and numerous Southeast Asian and Mesoamerican languages, require a counter between a number and a noun, and the choice of counter depends on properties of the noun such as shape, animacy, size or social status.

In Japanese, for example, flat objects are counted with mai (枚), long cylindrical objects with hon (本), small animals with hiki (匹), people with nin (人), and machines and vehicles with dai (台). Japanese has several hundred counters, of which a few dozen are in everyday use; the numeral-plus-counter combination often undergoes sound changes, as in ippon for "one long object". Korean and Chinese have analogous systems, such as Chinese 個/个 (gè) as a general-purpose measure word.

Counters are grammatically obligatory in these languages in most counting contexts, and learning them is a standard part of language study. Similar, though less grammaticalised, constructions exist in English in phrases such as "three head of cattle" or "fifty sheets of paper", where the intervening noun is sometimes called a measure word rather than a true counter.
