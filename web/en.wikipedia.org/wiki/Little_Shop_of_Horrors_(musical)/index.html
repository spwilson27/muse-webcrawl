Little Shop of Horrors is a horror comedy rock musical with music by Alan Menken and lyrics and a book by Howard Ashman. The story follows a hapless florist shop worker who raises a plant that feeds on human blood and flesh. The musical is loosely based on the low-budget 1960 black comedy film The Little Shop of Horrors directed by Roger Corman. The music, composed by Menken in the style of early 1960s rock and roll, doo-wop and early Motown, includes several well-known tunes, including the title song, "Skid Row (Downtown)", "Somewhere That's Green", and "Suddenly, Seymour".

Plot

Seymour Krelborn, a meek and dejected assistant at a flower shop on Skid Row owned by Mr. Mushnik, discovers an unusual plant during a total eclipse of the sun. He names it Audrey II after his co-worker Audrey, with whom he is secretly in love. The plant, a flytrap-like organism, refuses ordinary food and begins to wilt until Seymour discovers it craves human blood. As Audrey II grows larger and more demanding, it promises Seymour fame and fortune in exchange for feeding it. Business at the shop booms, but the plant's appetite escalates from drops of Seymour's blood to whole bodies, beginning with Audrey's abusive boyfriend, the sadistic dentist Orin Scrivello. In the musical's darker ending, Audrey II consumes both Audrey and Seymour and achieves world domination; the original film-inspired finale was revised for the 1986 film adaptation to give the leads a happier fate.

Productions

The musical premiered Off-Off-Broadway at the WPA Theatre in 1982 before moving to the Orpheum Theatre Off-Broadway, where it ran for five years and more than 2,200 performances. It has since been produced around the world and received a Broadway production in 2003. A long-running Off-Broadway revival opened at the Westside Theatre in 2019. The 1986 film adaptation, directed by Frank Oz, starred Rick Moranis, Ellen Greene and Steve Martin, and featured a giant animatronic plant designed by Lyle Conway.
