History of Kashmir

The history of Kashmir spans several millennia, from its early Buddhist and Hindu kingdoms through Sultanate and Mughal rule to the Dogra kingdom, partition in 1947, and the continuing dispute between India, Pakistan and China over the Himalayan region.

Early history

According to tradition recorded in Kalhana's 12th-century chronicle Rajatarangini, Kashmir was originally a lake drained by the sage Kashyapa, from whom the region takes its name. The valley became an important centre of Buddhism under the Mauryan emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE, and later of Hindu learning. King Lalitaditya Muktapida of the Karkota dynasty (8th century CE) made Kashmir a regional power, building the Martand Sun Temple.

Muslim rule

Islam spread to Kashmir in the 14th century. Shah Mir founded a dynasty in 1339, and under Sultan Zain-ul-Abidin (r. 1420–1470), remembered as "Bud Shah", Kashmir enjoyed a period of tolerance and cultural flowering. The Mughal emperor Akbar annexed Kashmir in 1586; it later passed to Afghan Durrani rule (1752) and then to the Sikh Empire of Ranjit Singh (1819).

Dogra rule and 1947

By the Treaty of Amritsar (1846), the British sold Kashmir to Gulab Singh, founder of the Dogra dynasty, creating the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir. At the partition of India in 1947, Maharaja Hari Singh initially sought independence, but after a tribal invasion from Pakistan he signed the Instrument of Accession to India, triggering the First Indo-Pakistani War and the division of the region along what became the Line of Control.

Modern period

The Kashmir conflict has driven wars in 1965 and 1999 (Kargil) and a long insurgency from 1989. In August 2019 India revoked Article 370, ending the special status of Jammu and Kashmir, and reorganised it into two union territories. Aksai Chin is administered by China but claimed by India.
