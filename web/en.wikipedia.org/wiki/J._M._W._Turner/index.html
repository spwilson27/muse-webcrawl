J. M. W. Turner

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Fighting Temeraire (1839): the ghostly yellow warship towed to its last berth by a small dark steam tug at sunset]

Joseph Mallord William Turner RA (23 April 1775 – 19 December 1851), known in his time as William Turner, was an English Romantic painter, printmaker and watercolourist. Considered a controversial figure in his day, he is now regarded as the artist who elevated landscape painting to an eminence rivalling history painting, and as a precursor of Impressionism and abstract art through his expressive colourisation and turbulent, luminous compositions.

Famous works include The Fighting Temeraire (1839, voted Britain's favourite painting in a 2005 poll), Rain, Steam and Speed (1844), The Slave Ship (1840) and the late, near-abstract seascapes. Turner left more than 550 oil paintings, 2,000 watercolours and 30,000 works on paper to the British nation, now largely at Tate Britain.
