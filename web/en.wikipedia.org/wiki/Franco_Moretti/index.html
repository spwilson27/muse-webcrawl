Franco Moretti

Franco Moretti (born 1950 in Sondrio, Italy) is an Italian literary historian and theorist. He graduated in Modern Literature from the University of Rome in 1972. He has taught at the universities of Salerno and Verona, at Columbia University, and at Stanford University, where he founded the Center for the Study of the Novel and, with Matthew Jockers, the Stanford Literary Lab. He is the author of numerous influential works on the history and theory of the novel.

Work

Moretti is best known for advocating a quantitative, sociological approach to literary history. In essays such as "Conjectures on World Literature" (2000), he proposed the method of distant reading — analyzing literature at scale through graphs, maps, and trees rather than close reading of individual canonical texts — as a way to study the world literary system. His books include The Way of the World (1987), Atlas of the European Novel, 1800–1900 (1998), Graphs, Maps, Trees (2005), The Bourgeois: Between History and Literature (2013), and A Literature of One's Own (2022).

The Stanford Literary Lab's pamphlets, produced collaboratively, extended his program into computational literary studies and the digital humanities more broadly.
