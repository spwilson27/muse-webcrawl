Spoon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A spoon is a utensil consisting of a shallow bowl — oval or round — at the end of a handle. It is used primarily for eating liquid or semi-liquid foods such as soup, stew, and ice cream, and for preparing and serving food more generally. Spoons are among the oldest eating tools known; preserved examples from ancient Egypt, made of wood, ivory, flint, and slate, date back thousands of years, and ornate spoons were used in religious ceremonies in Egypt and China. The modern English word derives from the Old English spon, meaning a chip of wood.

History

In the ancient world, Greeks and Romans used spoons of bronze and silver; the Roman cochlea had a pointed handle used to extract snails and shellfish. In medieval Europe, spoons of horn, wood, pewter, and brass were personal possessions carried by their owners, and silver apostle spoons became traditional christening gifts. By the seventeenth century the bowl grew flatter and the handle broader, producing the familiar modern form. In China and Japan, the deep ceramic soup spoon developed independently, and spoons for the Japanese and Chinese tea traditions took specialised forms.

Types

  • Teaspoon and tablespoon — standard items of cutlery; both names are also units of volume in cooking (approximately 5 ml and 15 ml respectively).
  • Soup spoon — with a large, round bowl.
  • Dessert spoon — intermediate in size between teaspoon and tablespoon.
  • Serving spoon and slotted spoon — for serving food from shared dishes.
  • Iced tea spoon — long-handled, for tall glasses.
  • Demitasse spoon — small spoon for espresso.
  • Chopstick rests and Asian soup spoons — the short, deep ceramic spoon used across East and Southeast Asia.
  • Measuring spoons — sold in graduated sets for cooking.

Materials

Spoons are manufactured from stainless steel, silver (sterling or plate), plastic, wood, porcelain, and other materials. Wooden spoons are favoured in cooking because they do not conduct heat readily, do not react with acidic foods, and do not scratch non-stick surfaces. Plastic spoons are common as disposable items, and elaborate souvenir and commemorative spoons are collected as memorabilia.

Cultural references

The spoon figures in numerous idioms and customs: to be "born with a silver spoon in one's mouth" denotes inherited wealth; "spooning" describes a cuddling position imitating two nested spoons; and the spoons serve as folk percussion instruments in several musical traditions. In chronic-illness communities, "spoon theory" is a widely used metaphor for limited daily energy.
