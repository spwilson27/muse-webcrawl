Edwin Howard Armstrong (December 18, 1890 – January 31, 1954) was an American electrical engineer and inventor best known for developing FM (frequency modulation) radio. He held 42 patents and received numerous awards, including the first Medal of Honor awarded by the Institute of Radio Engineers (now IEEE), the French Legion of Honor, the Franklin Medal and the Edison Medal. Armstrong was one of the most prolific and influential inventors in radio history.

Inventions

Regenerative circuit

While still an undergraduate at Columbia University, Armstrong invented the regenerative (feedback) circuit in 1912, which greatly amplified weak radio signals by feeding the output of a vacuum tube back through its input. The discovery became the subject of a protracted priority dispute with Lee de Forest that the courts ultimately decided against Armstrong.

Superheterodyne receiver

During the First World War, serving in the U.S. Army Signal Corps in France, Armstrong invented the superheterodyne receiver (1918), which converts incoming radio signals to a fixed intermediate frequency for amplification. It remains the basis of nearly all radio and television receivers.

Frequency modulation

Armstrong's greatest and most contested work was wideband frequency modulation, patented in 1933 and publicly demonstrated in 1935–1936. FM provided static-free, high-fidelity reception far superior to AM radio. His experimental station at Alpine, New Jersey, and the Yankee Network demonstrated its advantages, but the established AM industry, led by RCA, resisted FM, and in 1945 the FCC moved the FM band — a decision that rendered existing FM receivers obsolete and devastated Armstrong's venture.

Death and legacy

Exhausted by years of patent litigation over FM with RCA and others, and facing financial ruin, Armstrong died by suicide on the night of January 31, 1954. His widow Marion continued the litigation and ultimately prevailed in many of the suits, vindicating his patents. FM eventually became the dominant broadcast medium for music radio.
