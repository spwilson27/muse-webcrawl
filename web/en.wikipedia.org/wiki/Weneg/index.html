Weneg (also read as Weneg-Nebty) was the throne name (nebty name) of an ancient Egyptian king (pharaoh) who ruled during the 2nd Dynasty, around 2740 BC. His historic identity remains uncertain, and Egyptologists debate which of the better-documented Horus names of the period — such as Raneb or Nynetjer — corresponds to the king who used the nebty name Weneg.

Attestation

The name Weneg is known from black-ink inscriptions and clay seal impressions found in the underground galleries beneath the Step Pyramid of Djoser at Saqqara, and from a group of vessel fragments. He is the earliest king known to use the so-called "Weneg flower" — a stylised plant emblem associated with his nebty title — as part of his titulary, and his name appears in later king lists in positions consistent with an early 2nd-Dynasty ruler.

Identification

Scholars have variously identified Weneg with Horus Raneb, with Nynetjer, or proposed that he was a separate, short-reigning king between them. The debate matters because later sources, including the Turin King List and Manetho, give differing sequences of 2nd-Dynasty rulers, and the equation of Horus names with nebty names is one of the main tools for reconstructing the chronology of the period.
