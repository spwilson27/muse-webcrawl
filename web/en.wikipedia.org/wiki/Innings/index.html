Innings

An innings is one of the divisions of a cricket or baseball match during which one team or one player has their turn to bat. The word has distinct meanings in the two sports, and in cricket the same word serves as both singular and plural.

In cricket

In cricket, an innings is the turn of a team to bat, ending when ten wickets fall (the team is "all out"), when the captain declares the innings closed, or when the allocated overs or time expire. The term also denotes an individual batsman's turn at the crease and the runs he scores: a batsman who remains not out at the end is said to have played an unbeaten innings. Match formats determine the number of innings per side: two each in first-class and Test cricket, one each in limited-overs cricket. A side batting second that trails heavily may be asked to follow on and bat its next innings immediately.

In baseball

In baseball, an inning (singular spelled with one s) is the basic unit of play, consisting of two halves — the top, in which the visiting team bats, and the bottom, in which the home team bats. Each half-inning ends after three outs. A standard game lasts nine innings, extended into extra innings if tied. The term "innings pitched" is also a fundamental statistic for pitchers.
