Bolt (fastener)

A bolt is a form of threaded fastener with an external male thread, designed to be used with a nut. Bolts are very closely related to screws; the practical distinction is that a bolt is passed through unthreaded holes in the parts being joined and secured with a nut, whereas a screw is typically threaded into one of the parts.

Types

Common types include hex bolts, carriage bolts, anchor bolts, eye bolts, U-bolts and stud bolts. Bolt heads may be hexagonal, square, round or countersunk, and drives include hex socket (Allen), Torx and slotted forms.

Materials and grades

Bolts are most often made of steel, graded by strength — for example property classes 8.8 and 10.9 in the ISO metric system, or Grades 2, 5 and 8 in the SAE system. Stainless steel, brass, titanium and plastic bolts are used where corrosion resistance, weight or electrical insulation matter.

Jointing

A bolted joint relies on clamping force (preload) generated as the nut is tightened. Correct preload stretches the bolt slightly and holds the joint together by friction or by bearing of the bolt shank against the holes.
