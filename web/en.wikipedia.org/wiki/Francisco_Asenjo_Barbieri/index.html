Francisco Asenjo Barbieri (3 August 1823 – 19 February 1894) was a Spanish composer and musicologist who is best known for his contributions to the zarzuela, the Spanish lyric-dramatic genre combining spoken dialogue with song. He is regarded as one of the founders of the modern zarzuela and a central figure in the revival of Spanish national opera in the nineteenth century.

Life

Barbieri was born in Madrid. He studied at the Madrid Conservatory, learning clarinet, piano, and composition. Early in his career he worked in many capacities in the theatre — as a clarinettist, chorus master, prompter, and copyist — which gave him an intimate knowledge of stagecraft. He also pursued parallel careers as a music critic and historian, assembling an important library of Spanish music manuscripts.

In 1850 Barbieri joined with other composers, including Joaquín Gaztambide, Rafael Hernando, and Cristóbal Oudrid, to found the Sociedad Artística, which promoted the zarzuela at Madrid's Teatro de la Zarzuela, inaugurated in 1856. His works quickly became popular for their lively melodies drawn from Spanish folk and popular dance rhythms.

Works

Barbieri composed more than sixty zarzuelas. His most famous include Jugar con fuego (1851), Los diamantes de la corona (1854), Pan y toros (1864), and especially El barberillo de Lavapiés (1874), set in eighteenth-century Madrid and still considered a masterpiece of the genre. He also wrote religious music, songs, and the musicological volume Cancionero musical de los siglos XV y XVI, an edition of the famous palace songbook of the Spanish Renaissance.

Legacy

Beyond his compositions, Barbieri was instrumental in founding Spanish musicology, championing the recovery of Spain's early music heritage. He was a member of the Real Academia de Bellas Artes de San Fernando and of the Real Academia Española. His extensive music library is preserved at the Biblioteca Nacional de España in Madrid.
