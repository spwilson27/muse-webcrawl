Battle of Eniwetok

The Battle of Eniwetok was fought from 17 to 23 February 1944 at Eniwetok Atoll in the Marshall Islands, during the Pacific campaign of the Second World War. United States Army and Marine Corps forces captured the atoll from its Japanese garrison, securing an anchorage and airfield site that supported further advances toward the Mariana Islands.

Background

Following the capture of Kwajalein in early February 1944, Admiral Raymond Spruance advanced the timetable for seizing Eniwetok, some 300 miles further west. The operation, codenamed Catchpole, used the reserve that had not been needed at Kwajalein: the 22nd Marine Regiment and the Army's 106th Infantry Regiment. The atoll's three main islets — Engebi, Enewetak and Parry — were garrisoned by about 2,700 Japanese troops of the 1st Amphibious Brigade under Major General Nishida, who had dug in among the palms with concealed spider holes and bunkers.

The battle

After minesweepers cleared the lagoon and warships bombarded the islands, Marines landed on Engebi on 18 February and secured it within two days, capturing its airfield. On 19 February, soldiers of the 106th Infantry landed on Enewetak Island, where resistance proved heavier than expected and progress slow; the 3rd Battalion, 22nd Marines was committed to help finish the fight on 20 February. Parry Island was subjected to an especially heavy naval bombardment before the Marines assaulted it on 22 February, declaring it secure the next day.

Aftermath

American casualties were roughly 260–310 killed and over 900 wounded across the operation; nearly the entire Japanese garrison died, with only a few dozen prisoners taken. Eniwetok's lagoon became a fleet anchorage and its airfield supported operations against the Marianas and, later, provided a base used in postwar nuclear testing at the atoll.
