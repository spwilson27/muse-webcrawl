The Hebrew Union College – Jewish Institute of Religion (also known as HUC, HUC-JIR, and The College-Institute) is a Jewish seminary with a number of locations in the United States and one location in Jerusalem. It is the oldest extant Jewish seminary in the Americas and the main seminary for training rabbis, cantors, educators, and communal workers in Reform Judaism.

HUC-JIR has campuses in Cincinnati, Ohio; New York City; Los Angeles, California; and Jerusalem. Its Cincinnati campus serves as the main site of the school's library and archives, housing the American Jewish Archives and one of the largest collections of Judaica in the world.

History

HUC was founded in 1875 by Rabbi Isaac Mayer Wise in Cincinnati, Ohio. The first class of four students graduated in 1883, and the first ordination class was a source of controversy: the so-called "trefa banquet" served at the graduation celebration included non-kosher dishes such as shellfish, which led more traditional rabbis to break away and eventually found the Jewish Theological Seminary as the center of Conservative Judaism.

The Jewish Institute of Religion was founded in New York in 1922 by Rabbi Stephen S. Wise, as an alternative seminary open to rabbis of all movements. It merged with HUC in 1950. A Los Angeles campus was established in 1954, and the Jerusalem campus, founded in 1963, trains Israeli Reform rabbis and hosts the first-year program for North American rabbinical students.

Hebrew Union College has ordained women as rabbis since 1972, when Sally Priesand became the first woman ordained as a rabbi in the United States. The college has also been a center for scholarship in biblical archaeology, Jewish history, and liturgy.

Academics

The College-Institute offers graduate and professional programs including:

  • The Rabbinical School, a five-year program leading to ordination, including a year of study in Jerusalem.
  • The Debbie Friedman School of Sacred Music, training cantors.
  • The Rhea Hirsch School of Education in Los Angeles.
  • The School of Jewish Nonprofit Management.
  • Graduate programs including the Ph.D. in Hebraic and Cognate Studies at Cincinnati.

Libraries and museums

The Klau Library in Cincinnati is one of the largest Jewish libraries in the world, with some 700,000 volumes. The American Jewish Archives, founded by historian Jacob Rader Marcus, preserves the documentary record of Jewish life in the Western Hemisphere. The Cincinnati campus also includes the Skirball Museum of biblical archaeology and Jewish ceremonial art.

Governance

HUC-JIR is the seminary of the Union for Reform Judaism, the congregational arm of the Reform movement in North America. Its board of governors includes rabbis, lay leaders, and academics. In 2022, the college announced the consolidation of its undergraduate and some graduate operations, citing declining enrollment in its Cincinnati rabbinical program while retaining the campus's library, archives, and graduate programs.
