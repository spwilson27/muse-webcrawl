Lutsk (Ukrainian: Луцьк) is a city on the Styr River in north-western Ukraine, the administrative centre of Volyn Oblast. One of the oldest cities in Ukraine, it is historically a centre of Volhynia. Its landmark is Lubart's Castle, begun in the 14th century by Liubartas, the Lithuanian prince of Volhynia.

History

Lutsk was first mentioned in the Hypatian Codex in 1085. It belonged successively to Kievan Rus', the Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia, and from the late 14th century to the Grand Duchy of Lithuania; in 1429 it hosted a congress of European monarchs. After the Union of Lublin (1569) it passed to the Polish Crown. It received Magdeburg rights and became a multicultural city of Ukrainians, Poles, Jews, Germans, Armenians, and Karaites — the Lutsk Karaites were one of the oldest Karaite communities in Europe, and the poet Lesya Ukrainka spent part of her childhood in the city.

Partitioned to the Russian Empire in 1795, Lutsk was contested in both World Wars, lay in interwar Poland, and was annexed to the Ukrainian SSR in 1939. In 1941 the NKVD murdered thousands of prisoners in Lutsk castle, and under Nazi occupation the city's large Jewish population was annihilated in 1942. Since 1991 it has been part of independent Ukraine.

Culture

Lutsk hosts the Bandershtat festival of Ukrainian music and an annual "Night at Lubart's Castle" historical festival. Notable sites include Lubart's Castle, St. Peter and Paul Cathedral, the old town, and one of the longest residential buildings in the world — a Soviet-era block stretching over 3 km.
