Agni (missile)

The Agni missile (Sanskrit: अग्नि, "fire") is a family of medium- to intercontinental-range ballistic missiles developed by India under the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme and its successors. Named after the Hindu god of fire, the Agni series forms the land-based leg of India's nuclear deterrent and is operated by the Strategic Forces Command.

Variants

  • Agni-I — single-stage, solid-fuelled medium-range missile with a range of roughly 700–1,200 km, first tested in 1989 as a technology demonstrator and developed into a deployable system in the 2000s.
  • Agni-II — two-stage missile with a range of about 2,000–3,500 km.
  • Agni-III — two-stage intermediate-range missile, range roughly 3,000–5,000 km.
  • Agni-IV — two-stage missile of about 4,000 km range using advanced composite rocket motors and ring-laser gyroscope guidance.
  • Agni-V — three-stage, canister-launched intercontinental ballistic missile with a range exceeding 5,000 km, first tested in 2012; a 2024 test demonstrated multiple independently targetable reentry vehicle (MIRV) capability under Mission Divyastra.
  • Agni-P (Prime) — a newer canisterised medium-range missile intended to replace Agni-I and Agni-II.
  • Agni-VI — a longer-range ICBM reported to be under development.

Development

The programme was led for many years by A. P. J. Abdul Kalam at the DRDO. The missiles use solid propellant in all stages, are road- and rail-mobile, and are designed to carry conventional or nuclear warheads consistent with India's declared no-first-use policy and doctrine of credible minimum deterrence.
