The Government of Wallonia (French: Gouvernement wallon) is the executive branch of the Walloon Region, the predominantly French-speaking region of southern Belgium. It is accountable to the Parliament of Wallonia and is headed by the Minister-President of Wallonia.

Competences

Within Belgium's federal system, the Walloon Government is responsible for regional matters including the economy and employment, agriculture, water policy, housing, public works and transport, energy, local government supervision, land-use planning, environment, and rural renewal. Cultural, educational, and person-related matters in the French-speaking area fall mainly to the Government of the French Community, while the small German-speaking Community has its own government.

Seat and composition

The government sits at the Élysette in Namur, the capital of Wallonia, on the bank of the Meuse. Its members — a maximum of nine ministers including the Minister-President — are appointed by the Parliament of Wallonia after regional elections. The Walloon administration is known as the Public Service of Wallonia (Service public de Wallonie).

History

The region's institutions developed through Belgium's successive state reforms, beginning with the cultural communities of 1970 and the creation of the regions in 1980, with the first Walloon regional executive taking office in 1981–1982. Later reforms in 1988, 1993, 2001, and 2014 progressively expanded regional competences, including agriculture and parts of employment and family policy.
