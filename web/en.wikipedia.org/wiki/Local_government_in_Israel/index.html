Local government in Israel

Local government in Israel is exercised by local authorities responsible for municipal services within their jurisdictions, under the supervision of the Ministry of the Interior. The system descends from Ottoman and British Mandate municipal law, adapted by the State of Israel after 1948.

Types of authority

There are three main kinds of local authority. City councils (iriyot) govern municipalities that have been granted city status, generally the larger towns. Local councils (mo'atzot mekomiot) administer smaller towns and large villages. Regional councils (mo'atzot azoriot) provide services to groups of rural communities, such as kibbutzim and moshavim, spread over a wide area. In addition, a small number of industrial local councils administer industrial zones.

Responsibilities

Local authorities provide services including local planning and building permits, sanitation and waste collection, water and sewage (largely through municipal water corporations), local roads, parks, cultural and sports facilities, welfare services, and much of the operation of primary and secondary education, within frameworks set by central government ministries. Mayors and council members are elected directly by residents in municipal elections held every five years.

Finance and oversight

Authorities are financed by local property taxes (arnona), fees, and central-government grants, including equalisation ("balance") grants for weaker authorities. The Minister of the Interior has extensive powers of supervision, including approving budgets and, in cases of mismanagement, dissolving an elected council and appointing a committee in its place.
