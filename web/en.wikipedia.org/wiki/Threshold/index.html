Threshold

A threshold is the sill of a door or entryway: the piece of wood, stone, or metal that lies at the bottom of a doorway, crossed when entering a building or room. The word derives from Old English þerscold or þrescwald, related to "threshing", perhaps originally a plank that kept threshed grain inside a barn.

Architecture

In construction, thresholds provide a weather seal at exterior doors, bridge differences in floor level, and protect the edge of flooring materials. Modern thresholds may incorporate ramps for accessibility, draft excluders, and saddle shapes to shed water.

Figurative use

By extension, "threshold" denotes a point of beginning or transition: the level at which a stimulus becomes perceptible (the absolute threshold in psychophysics), an income level triggering a tax, or a value that must be exceeded before an effect occurs, as in the pain threshold or threshold voltage in electronics.
