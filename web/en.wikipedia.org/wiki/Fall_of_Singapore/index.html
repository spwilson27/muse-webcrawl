Fall of Singapore

The Fall of Singapore, also known as the Battle of Singapore, took place from 8 to 15 February 1942 during the Second World War. It culminated in the surrender of the British-led garrison of Singapore to Japanese forces under General Tomoyuki Yamashita, following the rapid Japanese conquest of Malaya. About 80,000 British, Indian, Australian and local troops became prisoners of war — the largest surrender of British-led military personnel in history. Winston Churchill called it the "worst disaster" in British military history.

Background

Singapore, Britain's principal naval base in Southeast Asia (the "Gibraltar of the East"), was defended chiefly against attack from the sea; its heavy guns faced seaward. Japanese forces landed in southern Thailand and northern Malaya on 8 December 1941, sank the British capital ships Prince of Wales and Repulse on 10 December, and drove Allied forces down the peninsula in about 70 days, crossing to Singapore Island over the Johore Strait on 8–9 February 1942.

The battle and surrender

Japanese landings in the northwest of the island overwhelmed depleted Australian units; despite fierce fighting at Bukit Timah and Pasir Panjang, the defenders were pushed into a shrinking perimeter around the city, with water supplies failing after the reservoirs fell. On 15 February 1942, Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival surrendered unconditionally at the Ford Motor factory at Bukit Timah. Singapore was renamed Syonan-to and remained under Japanese occupation until September 1945. Many prisoners were later sent to work on the Burma Railway and elsewhere, where thousands died.
