Grizzly bear

The grizzly bear (Ursus arctos horribilis), also known as the North American brown bear or simply grizzly, is a population or subspecies of the brown bear inhabiting North America. Grizzlies are distinguished by long, straight claws, a pronounced shoulder hump of muscle for digging, and fur that is often tipped with white or silver — "grizzled" — giving them their name.

Description

Adult grizzlies typically weigh 130–360 kg (290–790 lb), with coastal bears that feed on salmon growing much larger than interior bears. Females are substantially smaller than males. Despite their size, grizzlies can run at speeds of up to 55 km/h (34 mph) over short distances. They are omnivorous; diet varies by season and region and includes berries, roots, grasses, fish, rodents, carrion, and ungulates such as moose and elk calves.

Distribution and habitat

Historically, grizzly bears ranged throughout western North America from Alaska to Mexico and as far east as Hudson Bay. They were eliminated from most of the contiguous United States during the 19th and early 20th centuries. Today, significant populations remain in Alaska and western Canada, with smaller isolated populations in the Northern Rockies, centered on the Greater Yellowstone Ecosystem and the Northern Continental Divide Ecosystem in Montana.

Behavior

Grizzlies are generally solitary except for females with cubs and where food is concentrated, such as salmon streams. They den during winter and enter a state of hibernation lasting five to seven months, during which they do not eat, drink, urinate, or defecate. Females usually give birth to one to four cubs in the den in January or February.

Conservation

Grizzly bears in the contiguous United States were listed as threatened under the Endangered Species Act in 1975. Recovery efforts have increased populations in Yellowstone and the Northern Rockies, and the legal status of these populations has been the subject of repeated litigation and policy changes. Conflicts with humans — over livestock, food storage, and recreation — remain the principal management challenge.
