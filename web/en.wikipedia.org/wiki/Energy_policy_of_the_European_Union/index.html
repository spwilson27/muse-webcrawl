Energy policy of the European Union

The energy policy of the European Union concerns the production, distribution and consumption of energy within the European Union and its member states. Although each member state retains the right to determine its own energy mix, EU policy sets common rules for the internal energy market, binding targets for renewable energy and energy efficiency, and collective climate goals. Energy has been central to European integration since the European Coal and Steel Community of 1951 and the Euratom Treaty of 1957.

Energy Union

In 2015 the European Commission launched the Energy Union strategy, built on five dimensions: energy security and solidarity; a fully integrated internal energy market; energy efficiency; decarbonisation of the economy; and research, innovation and competitiveness. The strategy aimed to reduce the EU's dependence on imported fossil fuels and to give consumers secure, sustainable and affordable energy.

Climate and targets

Under the European Green Deal and the European Climate Law, the EU committed to climate neutrality by 2050 and to reducing net greenhouse gas emissions by at least 55% by 2030 compared with 1990 levels. The "Fit for 55" legislative package and revised directives raised targets for renewable energy (at least 42.5% of final energy consumption by 2030) and energy efficiency. The EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) caps emissions from power generation, industry and aviation.

Security of supply

The 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine exposed the EU's dependence on Russian pipeline gas and prompted the REPowerEU plan to diversify supplies, accelerate renewables and reduce demand. Member states agreed to emergency storage obligations and demand-reduction measures, and imports of Russian fossil fuels fell sharply.
