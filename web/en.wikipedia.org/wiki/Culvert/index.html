Culvert

A culvert is a structure that channels water past an obstacle or through an embankment, typically allowing a stream or drainage to pass under a road, railroad, or trail. Culverts are made from pipe, reinforced concrete, or other materials and come in shapes including round pipe, pipe-arch, elliptical, and box culverts (single or multi-cell reinforced concrete boxes). The term may derive from an obsolete sense of the French couvert, "covered".

Design and function

A culvert consists of an inlet, a barrel, and an outlet, often with headwalls, wingwalls, and aprons to control erosion. Hydraulically, culverts operate under inlet control or outlet control depending on the headwater depth, barrel roughness, length, and slope; design floods are chosen by roadway class, since undersized or blocked culverts can overtop and wash out the road above. Unlike a bridge, a culvert is usually buried in fill and derives structural support from the surrounding soil.

Environmental considerations

Poorly designed culverts are a major barrier to fish passage: excessive water velocity, perched outlets, and shallow flow can block migratory fish such as salmon and trout from spawning habitat. Modern practice increasingly favors stream-simulation designs, embedded and oversized culverts, and bottomless arch structures that preserve a natural streambed. Culvert failures due to corrosion, plugging by debris, or scour can cause sinkholes and road collapses, making inspection and maintenance significant public-works concerns.
