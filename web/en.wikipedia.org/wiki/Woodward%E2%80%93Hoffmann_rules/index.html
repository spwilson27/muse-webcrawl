Woodward–Hoffmann rules

Woodward–Hoffmann rules
Proposed: 1965
Authors: Robert Burns Woodward; Roald Hoffmann
Field: Organic chemistry; pericyclic reactions

The Woodward–Hoffmann rules, proposed by Robert Burns Woodward and Roald Hoffmann in 1965, predict the stereochemistry and feasibility of pericyclic reactions based on the conservation of orbital symmetry.

Content

The rules classify reactions as thermally or photochemically "allowed" or "forbidden" according to the number of (4q+2)-electron and 4r-electron components reacting suprafacially or antarafacially. They explain, for example, why electrocyclic ring closures proceed in a conrotatory fashion under heat and a disrotatory fashion under light, and they govern cycloadditions such as the Diels–Alder reaction and sigmatropic rearrangements.

Recognition

Hoffmann shared the 1981 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for this work (Woodward, who died in 1979, was ineligible). Kenichi Fukui independently developed the related frontier molecular orbital approach. See also pericyclic reaction.
