20th Century Limited

The 20th Century Limited was an express passenger train operated by the New York Central Railroad between Grand Central Terminal in New York City and LaSalle Street Station in Chicago from 1902 to 1967. Famous for its luxury, speed and the red carpet rolled out for boarding passengers, it was long regarded as the most famous train in America and a symbol of the golden age of rail travel.

History

The train was introduced by George H. Daniels to compete for wealthy travellers on the New York–Chicago route, cutting journey times to 20 hours (later 15½). Its name promised travel in twentieth-century style. Passengers at Grand Central boarded along a crimson carpet — the origin of the phrase "red carpet treatment" — and enjoyed all-Pullman sleeping cars, a barber, valet service, stenographers and fine dining. In 1938 the train was re-equipped with celebrated streamlined equipment designed by Henry Dreyfuss, hauled by streamlined J-3a Hudson steam locomotives, becoming an icon of Art Deco industrial design.

Decline and end

After the Second World War, competition from airliners and automobiles steadily eroded patronage. Dieselisation and cost-cutting followed; coaches were added to the once all-sleeping-car consist. The New York Central discontinued the 20th Century Limited on 3 December 1967, weeks before its merger into Penn Central. In later years Amtrak's Lake Shore Limited has served the same route corridor.

In culture

The train has featured in films including North by Northwest and The Sting, on Broadway in the musical On the Twentieth Century, and remains a byword for luxury travel; a Dreyfuss Hudson locomotive is preserved in model form worldwide, though no complete example of the steam locomotives survives.
