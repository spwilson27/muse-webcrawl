Overview

The Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties between States and International Organizations or between International Organizations is an international treaty adopted in Vienna in 1986 that extends the rules of the 1969 Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties to treaties to which international organisations (such as the United Nations or the European Union) are parties.

Content and status

The 1986 Convention tracks the 1969 text closely — conclusion, reservations, interpretation (Articles 31–33), invalidity, termination — while addressing organisations' "capacity" to conclude treaties and the act of formal confirmation (their analogue of ratification), and it wrestles with whether jus cogens and dispute clauses can bind organisations as they bind states. It has not entered into force: it requires ratification by 35 states and remains a few short, though many of its provisions are applied in practice as evidence of customary international law by courts and the UN's treaty practice.
