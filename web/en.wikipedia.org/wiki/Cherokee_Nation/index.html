The Cherokee Nation is the largest of the three federally recognized Cherokee tribes in the United States and one of the largest Native American tribes in the country, with more than 450,000 enrolled citizens. Its capital is Tahlequah, Oklahoma. The Cherokee people (ᎠᏂᏴᏫᏯ, Aniyvwiya) speak an Iroquoian language written in the syllabary devised by Sequoyah in the 1820s.

History

The Cherokee homeland originally covered large parts of the southeastern United States. Following the Indian Removal Act, most Cherokee were forcibly removed to Indian Territory in 1838–1839 on the journey known as the Trail of Tears, in which thousands died. In Oklahoma the tribe rebuilt its government, schools, and a bilingual newspaper. The tribal government was dismantled under allotment-era policies in the early 20th century but was reorganized in the 1970s under a new constitution.

Government and territory

The Cherokee Nation operates under a constitution with executive, legislative (Tribal Council), and judicial branches. In 2020 the U.S. Supreme Court's McGirt decision framework and subsequent state rulings affirmed that the Cherokee reservation was never disestablished; the Cherokee Nation reservation covers much of northeastern Oklahoma.

Culture and language

The Nation runs language revitalization programs, including immersion schools, to sustain the Cherokee language. Cherokee Nation Businesses operates casinos, healthcare facilities, and other enterprises that fund tribal services. The Cherokee National Holiday is celebrated each Labor Day weekend in Tahlequah.
