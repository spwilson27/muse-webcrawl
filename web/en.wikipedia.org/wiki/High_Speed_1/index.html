High Speed 1

High Speed 1 (HS1), legally the Channel Tunnel Rail Link (CTRL), is a 109.9-kilometre high-speed railway in England linking London St Pancras International to the Channel Tunnel, where it connects to LGV Nord in France. It carries Eurostar international trains and Southeastern high-speed domestic services using Class 395 Javelin trains.

High Speed 1
Opened: 2003 (Section 1), 2007 (full)
Length: 109.9 km
Max speed: 300 km/h (Eurostar), 225 km/h domestic
Termini: London St Pancras – Channel Tunnel
Stations: St Pancras, Stratford Intl, Ebbsfleet Intl, Ashford Intl

History

After the Channel Tunnel opened in 1994, Eurostar trains ran on slow classic lines in Kent. Railtrack's financial problems led to restructuring; Section 1 (Tunnel to Fawkham Junction) opened in September 2003, and Section 2 into the rebuilt St Pancras opened 14 November 2007, cutting London–Paris times to 2h15.

Route and engineering

The line includes the Medway Viaduct, North Downs Tunnel, Thames Tunnel at Swanscombe, and long tunnels under London to St Pancras. It uses French TVM-430 cab signalling with KVB/TPWS for domestic trains, and is electrified at 25 kV AC overhead.

Services

Eurostar runs to Paris, Brussels, Amsterdam and seasonal destinations. Southeastern Highspeed services, launched fully in December 2009, link Ashford, Canterbury, Margate, Dover and Medway towns to London in significantly reduced times, and Javelin shuttles served the 2012 Olympics.

Ownership

HS1 Ltd holds a 30-year concession (from 2010 sale) to operate the line, owned by infrastructure investors; Network Rail (High Speed) operates and maintains it.
