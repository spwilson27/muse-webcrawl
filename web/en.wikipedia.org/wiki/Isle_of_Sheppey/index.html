The Isle of Sheppey is an island off the northern coast of Kent, England, in the Thames Estuary, some 74 km (46 mi) east of central London. It has an area of 36 square miles (93 km²). The island forms part of the local government district of Swale.

The name Sheppey derives from the Old English Sceapige, "Sheep Island." It is separated from the mainland by the Swale channel and is connected by the Kingsferry Bridge and the Sheppey Crossing, a high-level road bridge opened in 2006.

Geography

The island is low-lying marshland in the south and east, rising to cliffs of London Clay at Minster and Warden Point in the north. The London Clay cliffs are famous for Eocene fossils. The Elmley Marshes and Swale estuary are important bird habitats protected as nature reserves and SSSIs.

History

Sheerness, at the island's north-western tip, was developed as a naval dockyard from the 17th century and was raided by the Dutch fleet in 1667 during the Raid on the Medway. The Royal Navy dockyard closed in 1960; Sheerness remains a commercial port. The island was also a cradle of British aviation: the Aero Club of Great Britain (later the Royal Aero Club) and Short Brothers' early aircraft factory were at Leysdown and Eastchurch, where some of the first British flights took place in 1909.

The island hosts three prisons (Elmley, Swaleside, and Standford Hill) near Eastchurch. Holiday resorts at Leysdown-on-Sea and Minster serve the island's leisure industry.
