Thelema is a Western esoteric and occult social philosophy and religious movement founded in 1904 by the English writer and ceremonial magician Aleister Crowley. Its central sacred text, The Book of the Law, was, according to Crowley, dictated to him in Cairo by a supernatural entity named Aiwass.

Beliefs

Thelema's core teaching is the primacy of the True Will — each individual's unique purpose — expressed in the phrases "Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law" and "Love is the law, love under will". Its cosmology draws on ancient Egyptian deities, notably Nuit, Hadit and Ra-Hoor-Khuit, alongside Hermetic, Kabbalistic and yogic elements. Followers typically practise Crowley's system of ceremonial magic ("magick"), and many belong to orders such as the A∴A∴ and Ordo Templi Orientis.

Influence

Thelema influenced modern occultism, Wicca and the counterculture, and Crowley's Abbey of Thelema in Cefalù, Sicily (1920–1923) remains notorious. The greeting "93" derives from the Greek numerical value of thelema and agape.
