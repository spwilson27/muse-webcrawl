Nine Years' War

The Nine Years' War (1688–1697), also known as the War of the League of Augsburg or the War of the Grand Alliance, was a major conflict fought between France under Louis XIV and a European-wide coalition, the Grand Alliance, led by the Dutch Republic, England and the Habsburg monarchy. It was fought mainly in Europe but also in Ireland, Scotland and colonial North America, where it is called King William's War.

Nine Years' War

Date: 1688–1697

Location: Europe, Ireland, North America

Belligerents: France vs. Grand Alliance (Dutch Republic, England, Habsburgs, Spain, Savoy)

Result: Treaty of Ryswick (1697)

Causes and course

The war began with the French invasion of the Palatinate in 1688 and Louis XIV's attempts to expand France's frontiers. The Glorious Revolution of 1688 brought William III of Orange to the English throne, aligning England with the Dutch against France. Major land battles included Fleurus (1690), Steenkerque (1692), Landen (1693) and Namur (1695), while at sea the French won at Beachy Head (1690) but were checked at Barfleur and La Hogue (1692). In Ireland, William's victory at the Boyne (1690) and Aughrim (1691) secured the Protestant ascendancy.

Outcome

Exhaustion and famine pushed all parties to negotiate. The Treaty of Ryswick (1697) restored most conquests made since 1679, Louis recognized William III as king of England, and France retained Strasbourg and Haiti (Saint-Domingue). The settlement proved temporary, as the question of the Spanish succession soon led to the War of the Spanish Succession.
