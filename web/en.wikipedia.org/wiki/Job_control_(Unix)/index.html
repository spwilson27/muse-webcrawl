Job control (Unix)

In Unix computing, job control refers to the control of multiple processes ("jobs") by a single terminal session, implemented in shells such as csh, bash and zsh. It allows the user to suspend, resume, background and foreground processes interactively.

Operation

Pressing Ctrl-Z suspends the foreground job (sending SIGTSTP). The jobs builtin lists jobs; bg resumes a job in the background, fg brings it to the foreground, and appending & starts a command in the background directly. Jobs are identified by job IDs such as %1, distinct from process IDs.

Implementation

Job control relies on process groups and the terminal's controlling process group, with signals SIGTSTP, SIGTTIN and SIGTTOU stopping processes that attempt terminal I/O from the background. It was introduced in BSD Unix with the C shell around 1980 and later standardised in POSIX.
