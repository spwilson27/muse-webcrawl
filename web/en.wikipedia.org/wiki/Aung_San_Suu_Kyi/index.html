Aung San Suu Kyi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Aung San Suu Kyi (born 19 June 1945) is a Burmese politician and diplomat who led Myanmar's pro-democracy movement for three decades. The daughter of Aung San, the independence hero assassinated in 1947, she returned to Burma in 1988 during the 8888 Uprising and co-founded the National League for Democracy (NLD), which won the 1990 election by a landslide that the military annulled; she spent most of the following two decades under house arrest, receiving the Nobel Peace Prize in 1991 as the world's emblem of non-violent resistance to dictatorship.

Released in 2010, she led the NLD to victory in the 2015 general election and, barred by the military-drafted constitution from the presidency, governed as State Counsellor (2016–21) in uneasy cohabitation with the armed forces. Her international standing collapsed over the Rohingya crisis of 2016–17: she did not condemn the military's campaign that drove some 700,000 Rohingya into Bangladesh and was widely described by UN investigators as ethnic cleansing or genocide, and in 2019 she personally defended Myanmar against the genocide charge at the International Court of Justice — though the army, not her government, controlled the security forces, a structural exculpation her critics found insufficient. On 1 February 2021 the military seized power in a coup, detained her, and subsequently convicted her in closed trials on a series of charges (importation of walkie-talkies, corruption and others) widely regarded internationally as politically motivated, sentencing her to a cumulative term initially of 33 years, later reduced by partial pardons to 27 years. Her present circumstances in detention are not publicly verifiable in detail; the NLD has been dissolved by the junta. This article records the settled history of her career and does not attempt to report her current condition beyond those widely reported facts.
