Zhangjiajie National Forest Park (Chinese: 湖南张家界国家森林公园) is a national forest park located in Zhangjiajie, Hunan Province, China. It is one of several national parks within the Wulingyuan Scenic and Historic Interest Area, which was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1992. The park is famed for its thousands of quartzite sandstone pillars and peaks, many over 200 metres (660 ft) high, formed by long-term physical erosion.

History

In 1982 the park was recognized as China's first national forest park, covering an area of 4,810 hectares. In 1992, Wulingyuan was officially designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and in 2004 Zhangjiajie was listed as a UNESCO Global Geopark. The park's distinctive pillar formations provided visual inspiration for the floating "Hallelujah Mountains" in James Cameron's 2009 film Avatar; one pillar, formerly called the Southern Sky Column, was officially renamed "Avatar Hallelujah Mountain" in 2010.

Features

The park contains more than 3,000 quartzite sandstone pillars and peaks, along with ravines, gorges, natural bridges, streams, pools and waterfalls. Notable attractions include:

  • Yuanjiajie — a scenic area containing the Avatar Hallelujah Mountain and the "First Bridge Under Heaven," a natural rock bridge.
  • Tianzi Mountain — known for its sea of clouds and tall stone peaks.
  • Golden Whip Stream — a stream running through a valley of steep cliffs.
  • Bailong Elevator — a 326-metre (1,070 ft) glass elevator built onto the side of a cliff, once recognized as the world's tallest outdoor lift.
  • Ten-Mile Gallery — a valley with rock formations resembling human and animal figures.

Climate and ecology

The park has a humid subtropical climate with abundant rainfall. Its dense forests support a rich variety of flora and fauna, including rare species such as the Chinese giant salamander in the surrounding region and several species of monkeys, notably macaques that roam the scenic areas.
