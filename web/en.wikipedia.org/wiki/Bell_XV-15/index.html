The Bell XV-15 is an American experimental tiltrotor aircraft developed by Bell Helicopter for NASA and the US military. It was the first tiltrotor to demonstrate successful transition between helicopter and fixed-wing flight, and it paved the way for the V-22 Osprey.

Development

The programme began in the early 1970s with a NASA research contract. The XV-15 combined two turboshaft engines mounted in rotating nacelles at the wingtips, each driving a three-bladed proprotor. It first hovered in May 1977 and achieved a full conversion to forward flight in 1979.

Testing and legacy

The two prototypes were used for extensive test flying, demonstrating the tiltrotor concept's speed and range advantages — the XV-15 reached about 300 knots (560 km/h) in level flight — and investigating handling, noise and shipboard operations. One aircraft was damaged in a 1992 accident; the surviving example was later used in the Eagle Eye UAV and BA609 (now Leonardo AW609) programmes and is preserved at the National Air and Space Museum's Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center.
