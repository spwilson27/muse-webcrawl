Operating leverage

Operating leverage is a measure of how revenue growth translates into growth in operating income. It arises because a firm has fixed operating costs that do not change with output, alongside variable costs that do. A company with high fixed costs and low variable costs has high operating leverage: a small percentage change in sales produces a larger percentage change in operating profit, in either direction.

The degree of operating leverage (DOL) can be calculated as the percentage change in earnings before interest and taxes (EBIT) divided by the percentage change in sales, or equivalently as contribution margin divided by operating income.

High operating leverage increases business risk: profits rise quickly when sales grow but fall quickly when sales decline, making earnings more volatile. It is commonly compared with financial leverage, which arises from fixed financing costs such as interest.
