Russell–Einstein Manifesto

The Russell–Einstein Manifesto was issued in London on 9 July 1955 by Bertrand Russell in the midst of the Cold War. Bearing the signatures of eleven eminent scientists, including Albert Einstein — for whom it was one of the last public acts before his death in April 1955 — it highlighted the dangers posed to humanity by nuclear weapons and called upon world leaders to seek peaceful resolutions to international conflict.

Contents

The manifesto asked signatories and governments to consider a stark question: "Shall we put an end to the human race; or shall mankind renounce war?" It urged its audience to "remember your humanity, and forget the rest", and called for a conference of scientists to appraise the perils created by weapons of mass destruction.

Legacy

The manifesto led directly to the first Pugwash Conference on Science and World Affairs, held in July 1957 in Pugwash, Nova Scotia, and to the continuing Pugwash movement, which was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1995. The Russell–Einstein Manifesto is regarded as a foundational document of the nuclear disarmament movement.
