Freddie King

Freddie King (September 3, 1934 – December 28, 1976) was an American blues guitarist, singer, and songwriter. He is considered one of the "Three Kings of the Blues Guitar", along with Albert King and B.B. King, and his string-bending Texas blues style influenced generations of guitarists, including Eric Clapton and Stevie Ray Vaughan.

Career

Born in Gilmer, Texas, King moved to Chicago as a teenager and immersed himself in the city's electric blues scene. Signing with Federal Records in 1960, he scored hits with "Have You Ever Loved a Woman" and the instrumental "Hide Away" (1961), which reached the pop charts and became a blues standard. Later recordings such as "Going Down" kept him in demand, and in the 1970s he toured relentlessly with a large band, earning the nickname "The Texas Cannonball".

Legacy

King died of heart failure in 1976 at age 42. He was inducted into the Blues Hall of Fame in 1982 and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2012. His recordings remain staples of the blues repertoire, and his powerful attack and phrasing are central to the Texas blues tradition.
