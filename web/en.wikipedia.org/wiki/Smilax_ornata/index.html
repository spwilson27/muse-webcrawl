Smilax ornata

Smilax ornata is a perennial trailing vine with prickly stems that is native to Mexico and Central America. It is the primary source of the flavouring ingredient sarsaparilla, and is also known as Honduran sarsaparilla or Jamaican sarsaparilla (the latter name arising because the roots were shipped to Europe via Jamaica).

Description

The plant is a climbing vine with slender, prickly stems and heart-shaped evergreen leaves. Its long roots and rhizomes, which can extend several metres, are harvested for use in flavourings and traditional medicine. The roots are rich in saponins, including sarsaponin, along with starch, resins and trace compounds.

Uses

From the 17th century onwards, sarsaparilla root was imported into Europe and North America, where it was promoted (incorrectly) as a cure for syphilis and used as a blood purifier and tonic. Its major lasting use is as a flavouring: extracts give the characteristic taste of sarsaparilla soft drinks and traditional root beer, and the root is still an ingredient in drinks in several Central American and Asian countries. Related species, including Smilax aristolochiifolia and Smilax regelii, are also traded as sarsaparilla.
