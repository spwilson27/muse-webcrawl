Mie goreng

Mie goreng (Indonesian and Malay for "fried noodles"), also known as mi goreng, mee goreng and bakmi goreng, is an Indonesian stir-fried noodle dish. It is made with yellow wheat noodles stir-fried in cooking oil with garlic, onion or shallots, and a protein such as chicken, beef, shrimp or meatballs, together with cabbage, tomatoes, egg and other vegetables. Its signature flavour comes from kecap manis, a thick sweet Indonesian soy sauce that gives the dish its savoury-sweet taste and brown colour.

Origins

The dish descends from Chinese chow mein brought to Southeast Asia by Hokkien immigrants, adapted over time to local tastes and ingredients. Sweet soy sauce distinguishes the Indonesian version from its Chinese ancestor, and halal adaptations replaced pork with chicken, beef or seafood.

Variations

Numerous regional and vendor variations exist. Mie goreng Jawa is the Javanese style; mie goreng Aceh from Aceh uses thick noodles in a rich, spicy curry-like sauce; mie goreng mamak is the Tamil Muslim version popular in Malaysia and Singapore, characteristically spicy and reddish. A common home and street-food preparation uses instant noodles, most famously the Indomie Mi Goreng brand, which has become a global pantry staple.

Serving

Mie goreng is served hot, commonly garnished with fried shallots, prawn crackers (kerupuk), pickled shallots and cucumber (acar), a wedge of lime and a fried egg. It is sold everywhere in Indonesia, from street-side warteg and night-market carts to restaurants, and is closely related to nasi goreng, the fried-rice counterpart seasoned in the same way.
