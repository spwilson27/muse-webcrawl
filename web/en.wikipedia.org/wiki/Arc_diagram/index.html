Arc diagram

An arc diagram is a style of graph drawing in which the vertices of a graph are placed along a single line and the edges are drawn as semicircles (arcs) above or below the line. Arc diagrams are a form of one-dimensional network visualisation, useful when the ordering of vertices carries meaning — for example, the sequence of words in a text or of characters in a story.

Properties and uses

By choosing the vertex order carefully, repeated patterns and clusters in the connections become visible: dense bundles of arcs reveal groups of strongly related vertices, and long arcs expose long-range links. Arc diagrams have been used to visualise repeated substrings in texts, character co-occurrence in novels (such as the well-known visualisations of Les Misérables), musical structure, and relationships in genomic data. A graph drawn with arcs entirely on one side of the line is planar exactly when it is outerplanar; allowing arcs on both sides generalises the layout.

History

The layout predates computing — similar drawings appear in sociograms and in diagrams of musical form — but it was popularised in information visualisation by Martin Wattenberg's "arc diagrams" of music and text in 2002. Arc diagrams are closely related to the linear arrangements studied in graph theory and to the "circular" variant in which the vertex line is bent into a circle, producing a chord-like layout.
