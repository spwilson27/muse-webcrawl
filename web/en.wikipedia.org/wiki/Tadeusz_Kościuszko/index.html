Andrzej Tadeusz Bonawentura Kościuszko (1746–1817) was a Polish military engineer, statesman and revolutionary hero in both Poland and the United States. Trained in Warsaw and Paris, he volunteered for the American Revolutionary War, where his fortifications contributed to the American victory at Saratoga and to the defence of West Point. Returning to Poland, he led the 1794 Kościuszko Uprising against the partitioning powers, Russia and Prussia, becoming a national symbol of the struggle for independence and for social reform.

American service

Kościuszko arrived in Philadelphia in 1776 and was commissioned a colonel of engineers in the Continental Army. He designed the fortifications at Bemis Heights for the Battle of Saratoga (1777), widely credited with helping force Burgoyne's surrender, and later supervised the defences of West Point. He also served in the southern campaign under Nathanael Greene. In 1783 Congress promoted him to brigadier general; he became a member of the Society of the Cincinnati and an original member of the American Philosophical Society.

In Poland

Back in Poland he commanded forces in the Polish–Russian War of 1792. After the Second Partition, he proclaimed an uprising at Kraków on 24 March 1794, vowing to restore Poland's territory and reform its society. His mixed army of regulars and peasant scythemen won at Racławice (4 April 1794), and his Połaniec Manifesto promised personal liberty and reduced corvée labour for peasants. The uprising was crushed by Russian and Prussian forces; Kościuszko was wounded and captured at Maciejowice (10 October 1794) and imprisoned in St. Petersburg until released by Tsar Paul I in 1796.

Later life and legacy

Kościuszko spent his final years in the United States and then Switzerland, where he died in Solothurn in 1817. His American will attempted to direct his estate to buying freedom and education for enslaved people, naming Thomas Jefferson as executor—a bequest that was never executed as written, though a portion later funded a school for Black children in New Jersey. His remains were interred in Wawel Cathedral, Kraków, among Poland's kings and national heroes. Monuments to Kościuszko stand in many countries; Australia's highest peak, Mount Kosciuszko, is named for him, as are places and institutions across the United States and Poland.
