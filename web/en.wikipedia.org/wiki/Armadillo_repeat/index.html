Armadillo repeat

An armadillo repeat is an approximately 42-amino-acid-long repeating motif characteristic of proteins in the armadillo (ARM) superfamily. Each repeat folds into a pair of alpha helices that pack against neighbouring repeats to form a curved, elongated superhelix known as an armadillo domain.

Structure

Successive repeats stack to create a right-handed superhelical solenoid with a shallow groove along its surface. This groove serves as a binding site for extended peptide segments of partner proteins, allowing ARM-repeat proteins to act as versatile scaffolds in protein–protein interactions.

Occurrence and function

The motif takes its name from the Drosophila segment polarity protein Armadillo, the homologue of vertebrate β-catenin. Armadillo-repeat proteins include β-catenin, plakoglobin, the tumour suppressor APC, and nuclear transport factors such as importin-α, and are involved in cell adhesion, Wnt signalling and nucleocytoplasmic transport.
