Kwalliso

Kwalliso (Korean: 관리소, "control centre" or "management office") is the term for the system of political prison camps in North Korea, formally rendered in English as "political penal-labor colonies". The camps, operated by the Ministry of State Security, hold people convicted of political offences — and frequently their extended families — under the principle of songbun-based collective punishment known as yeonjwaje, which can extend guilt across three generations.

Organization

Satellite imagery analyses and testimony from former prisoners and guards have identified camps numbered 14 (Kaechon), 15 (Yodok), 16 (Hwasong/Myonggan), 18 (Pukchang), 22 (Hoeryong, reported closed around 2012) and 25 (Chongjin), among others, although North Korea denies that political prison camps exist. The camps are large complexes, some tens of kilometres across, usually located in remote mountain valleys. Prisoners perform forced labour in mining, logging, agriculture and manufacturing, generally for life sentences with little prospect of release. Former detainees describe chronic food shortages, disease, public executions and deaths from overwork and punishment.

Distinction from kyohwaso

Kwalliso are distinguished from kyohwaso ("re-education camps"), which are run by the Ministry of Social Security for criminal offenders serving fixed sentences, and from short-term labour training camps (rodong danryeondae). The kwalliso population has been estimated by the UN Commission of Inquiry on Human Rights in the DPRK (2014) and by human rights organizations at roughly 80,000 to 120,000 people, though figures are uncertain because outside access is denied.

International findings

The 2014 UN Commission of Inquiry concluded that crimes against humanity, including extermination, enslavement, torture, imprisonment and persecution, had been committed in the kwalliso system, and recommended referral of the situation to the International Criminal Court. North Korea rejects the findings as fabricated.
