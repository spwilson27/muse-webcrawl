The Blue Angel (German: Der blaue Engel) is a 1930 German tragicomic film directed by Josef von Sternberg, adapted from Heinrich Mann's 1905 novel Professor Unrat. It stars Emil Jannings as a respected school professor destroyed by his infatuation with a cabaret singer, Lola Lola, played by Marlene Dietrich in the role that made her an international star. It is considered one of the first major German sound films.

Plot

Professor Immanuel Rath, a strict and ageing gymnasium teacher, visits the Blue Angel cabaret to catch his pupils passing around postcards of its singer, Lola Lola. Instead he falls for her himself; he marries her, loses his post, and sinks into humiliation, reduced to a clown in her troupe and driven finally to madness and death back in his old classroom.

Music and legacy

Dietrich's songs in the film, with music by Friedrich Hollaender, include "Falling in Love Again (Can't Help It)" (Ich bin von Kopf bis Fuß auf Liebe eingestellt), which became her signature song. The film was shot simultaneously in German and English versions. Its success brought both von Sternberg and Dietrich to Hollywood and Paramount, where they made a celebrated series of films together. The Blue Angel remains a landmark of Weimar cinema.
