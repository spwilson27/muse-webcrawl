Neretva

The Neretva (Serbian Cyrillic: Неретва), also known as the Narenta, is one of the largest rivers of the eastern part of the Adriatic basin. Four hydroelectric power plants with large dams (higher than 15 metres) provide flood protection, electricity generation, and water storage. The river is also recognized for its natural beauty and diverse landscape.

Geography

The Neretva rises in the Dinaric Alps beneath the Zelengora and Lebršnik mountains at an elevation of about 1,095 metres, and flows 225 kilometres northwest and then southwest, of which 203 kilometres are in Bosnia and Herzegovina and the final 22 kilometres in Dubrovnik-Neretva County, Croatia, before emptying into the Adriatic Sea near Ploče. Its principal towns include Konjic, Jablanica, Mostar, Čapljina, and Metković.

Upper Neretva

The upper course, noted for its cold, emerald-green, drinking-quality water, cuts a dramatic canyon between high mountains. It is an important habitat for endemic fish species and a popular destination for rafting and fly fishing.

Dams and hydroelectric power

The river is dammed at Jablanica, Grabovica, Salakovac, and Mostar, forming large reservoirs that generate a substantial share of Bosnia and Herzegovina's electricity. The Jablanica dam, completed in the 1950s, created the artificial Jablanica lake.

Delta

The lower Neretva forms a wide delta in Croatia, extensively reclaimed in the 20th century for agriculture; it is famous for mandarins and other citrus fruit. The delta is an important wetland for migratory birds.

History

In antiquity the river was known as the Narenta and was associated with the Illyrian tribe of the Ardiaei and later with medieval pirates of the Neretva channel. During the Second World War the valley was the scene of the Battle of the Neretva (Operation Weiss) in 1943.
