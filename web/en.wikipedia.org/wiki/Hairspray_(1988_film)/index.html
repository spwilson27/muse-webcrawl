Hairspray is a 1988 American comedy film written and directed by John Waters. Set in Baltimore in 1962, it stars Ricki Lake as Tracy Turnblad, a plump, dance-loving teenager who wins a spot on a local television dance programme, The Corny Collins Show, and uses her new fame to campaign for the show's racial integration. The film features Divine — in the last film released during his lifetime — as Tracy's mother Edna, with Debbie Harry, Sonny Bono, and Jerry Stiller in supporting roles.

Plot

Tracy Turnblad auditions for The Corny Collins Show and, despite her size, becomes a regular dancer and a rival to the show's spoiled star Amber Von Tussle, whose scheming parents (Debbie Harry and Sonny Bono) run the station's segregationist policies. After being sent to detention, Tracy befriends Black students who teach her dance moves, and she joins a protest when the show cancels its monthly "Negro Day". The film climaxes at the Miss Teenage Hairspray pageant, where Tracy — having escaped from a reform school van — arrives with an integrated dance troupe, wins the crown, and integrates the show.

Production and reception

Waters, the Baltimore "Pope of Trash" known for transgressive films such as Pink Flamingos, deliberately made Hairspray his most mainstream picture; it was his only film rated PG. Filmed in Baltimore for about $2 million, it was a modest commercial success and a major critical one — reviewers praised its joy, its period soundtrack of early-1960s R&B, and its civil-rights storyline, which drew on the real desegregation of Baltimore's Buddy Deane Show. Divine died three weeks after the film's release.

Legacy and adaptations

Hairspray became a cult favourite on home video and was adapted into a Tony Award–winning Broadway musical (2002, music by Marc Shaiman), which was in turn filmed in 2007 with John Travolta as Edna. In 2022 the original film was selected for the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically, and aesthetically significant.
