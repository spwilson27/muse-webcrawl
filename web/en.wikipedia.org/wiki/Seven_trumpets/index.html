Seven trumpets

In the Book of Revelation, the seven trumpets are sounded, one at a time, by seven angels, after the opening of the seventh seal (Revelation 8–11). Each trumpet blast announces a judgement or catastrophic event visited upon the earth during the eschatological end times. The trumpets form the second of three sevenfold judgement series in Revelation, following the seven seals and preceding the seven bowls.

Interpretation

Interpreters differ widely over the trumpets. Historicist readings map them onto successive events of church and imperial history; futurist readings take them as literal judgements of a coming tribulation; preterist readings connect them with the fall of Jerusalem in 70 CE or the decline of Rome; and idealist readings treat them as recurring symbols of divine judgement through history. The final three trumpets are singled out as "woes" by an eagle (or angel) flying in mid-heaven (Revelation 8:13). Between the sixth and seventh trumpets the narrative is interrupted by the vision of the mighty angel with the little book and the measuring of the temple with the two witnesses.

In culture

The imagery of the trumpets has influenced art, hymnody, and popular apocalyptic writing, and the sounding of the "seventh trumpet" is a recurring motif in Christian eschatological discourse.
