Terence Joseph Nation (8 August 1930 – 9 March 1997) was a Welsh screenwriter and novelist. He is best known for creating the villainous Daleks for the long-running BBC science fiction television series Doctor Who, for whom he also created their home planet Skaro and their creator Davros. Nation also created two major BBC series of his own, the post-apocalyptic drama Survivors (1975) and the space opera Blake's 7 (1978).

Early career

Nation began as a stand-up comedian and comedy writer, working for performers including Tony Hancock, before turning to drama. He established himself with scripts for series such as The Avengers, The Saint, The Champions, and The Persuaders!

Doctor Who and the Daleks

In 1963 Nation was commissioned to write the second Doctor Who serial, The Daleks (also known as The Mutants). The Daleks — mutants encased in armoured travel machines, designed by Raymond Cusick — became an immediate sensation and are credited with securing the programme's survival and popularity. Nation wrote many of the Daleks' subsequent stories, including "The Dalek Invasion of Earth" and "Genesis of the Daleks" (1975), which introduced Davros. Shrewdly, he retained ownership rights in the Daleks, licensing them back to the BBC and building a personal fortune; the creatures also appeared in two 1960s feature films starring Peter Cushing.

Later work

Survivors (1975–77) depicted Britain after a global plague; Blake's 7 (1978–81) followed a band of rebels against a corrupt Terran Federation and became a cult classic. Nation moved to Los Angeles in the 1980s, working on American series including MacGyver. He died in 1997.
