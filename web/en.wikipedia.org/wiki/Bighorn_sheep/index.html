Bighorn sheep

The bighorn sheep (Ovis canadensis) is a species of sheep native to North America, named for the large curved horns of the rams. It inhabits rugged mountain terrain from Canada through the western United States to northern Mexico.

Scientific nameOvis canadensis
FamilyBovidae
Mass53–143 kg (rams larger)
RangeWestern North America
Conservation statusLeast Concern (IUCN)

Description and behaviour

Rams bear massive horns that can weigh up to 14 kg and curl in a spiral beside the head; ewes have smaller, straighter horns. During the autumn rut, rams charge and clash horns at speeds of over 30 km/h in contests for dominance, their skulls adapted to absorb the impacts. Bighorns are sure-footed climbers that escape predators on steep cliffs.

Conservation

Hunting and disease from domestic sheep reduced bighorn numbers from perhaps two million in the 19th century to a few tens of thousands by 1930. Reintroduction and management programmes have rebuilt many populations, though isolated subspecies such as the Sierra Nevada bighorn remain endangered. The bighorn is the provincial mammal of Alberta and the state animal of Colorado.
