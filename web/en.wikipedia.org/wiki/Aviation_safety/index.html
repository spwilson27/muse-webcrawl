Aviation safety

Aviation safety is the study and practice of managing risks in aviation. It includes preventing aviation accidents and incidents through research, education, and regulation, as well as improving survivability when accidents do occur. Commercial air transport has become one of the safest modes of travel: worldwide accident rates for large passenger jets have fallen to a small fraction of their 1960s levels, with fatal accidents occurring on the order of once per several million flights in recent years.

History

In the early decades of powered flight, accidents were frequent and investigation rudimentary. The growth of airline travel after the Second World War brought systematic regulation, independent accident investigation, and design standards. Mid-century disasters such as the 1956 Grand Canyon mid-air collision prompted the modernisation of air traffic control. From the 1970s, crew resource management, ground proximity warning systems, and collision-avoidance systems (TCAS) addressed the leading causes of accidents.

Risks and investigation

The most common categories of fatal accident include controlled flight into terrain, loss of control in flight, runway excursions, and mid-air collisions. Human factors account for a majority of accidents in most analyses. Annex 13 of the Chicago Convention provides the international framework for accident investigation, conducted in most states by independent bodies such as the NTSB (United States), the AAIB (United Kingdom) and the BEA (France), with the goal of prevention rather than apportioning blame. Modern safety management systems emphasise reporting culture, flight-data monitoring, and proactive risk assessment.
