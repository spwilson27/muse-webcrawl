French Community of Belgium

The flag of the French Community features the Walloon rooster.

The French Community of Belgium (French: Communauté française de Belgique), also known since 2011 by the name Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles (Wallonia-Brussels Federation), is one of the three linguistic communities of Belgium. It exercises jurisdiction over French-speaking Belgians in Wallonia and in the Brussels-Capital Region in matters such as education, culture, and certain social affairs.

Institutions

The Community has its own parliament (the Parliament of the French Community, with 94 members drawn from the Walloon Parliament and the Brussels Parliament) and a government headed by a Minister-President. Its competences, defined by Belgium's federal structure, include schooling and universities, the public broadcaster RTBF, cultural policy, and parts of health and welfare policy. It represents about 4.5 million people, roughly 40 per cent of Belgium's population.

Context

The community system exists alongside Belgium's three regions (Wallonia, Flanders, and Brussels). The French Community should not be confused with the Walloon Region: the Community's authority is defined by language and persons, the Region's by territory. Since 2011 the name "Wallonia-Brussels Federation" has been used in official communication to emphasise its two components, though the constitutional name remains the French Community.
