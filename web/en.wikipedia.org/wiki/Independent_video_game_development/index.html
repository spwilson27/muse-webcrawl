Independent video game development

Independent video game development is the process of creating video games by individuals or small teams without the financial or technical support of a large game publisher. Games produced this way are commonly called indie games. Independent developers rely on digital distribution platforms, crowdfunding, and word of mouth rather than the retail channels and marketing budgets of major publishers.

History

The earliest computer games of the 1950s–1970s were effectively independent creations. As the home computer market grew in the 1980s, hobbyist "bedroom coders" produced games for systems like the ZX Spectrum and Commodore 64, and shareware distribution in the 1990s let small teams such as id Software and Epic MegaGames reach wide audiences. Rising development costs in the late 1990s and early 2000s pushed most developers toward publishers, but the arrival of digital storefronts — notably Steam (2003), Xbox Live Arcade (2005), and later itch.io and mobile app stores — dramatically lowered the cost of distribution and triggered an indie renaissance.

Notable successes

Landmark indie successes include Cave Story (2004), Braid (2008), Super Meat Boy (2010), Minecraft (2009–2011, developed by Mojang before its acquisition by Microsoft), Undertale (2015), Stardew Valley (2016), Hollow Knight (2017), Hades (2020), and Balatro (2024). Many of these were made by teams of one to a handful of people.

Tools and funding

Affordable game engines such as Unity, Unreal Engine, and Godot, along with tools like GameMaker and RPG Maker, have made professional-grade development accessible to small teams. Funding sources include personal savings, grants, crowdfunding platforms such as Kickstarter, early access sales, and, increasingly, indie publishers and platform deals.

Community

The Independent Games Festival, game jams such as Ludum Dare and Global Game Jam, and online communities play a central role in the indie ecosystem, giving new developers visibility and feedback.
