The Quasi-War (French: Quasi-guerre) was an undeclared naval war fought from 1798 to 1800 between the United States and the French First Republic, primarily in the Caribbean and off the American coast. It arose from the abrogation of the treaties of 1778, French seizures of American merchant shipping during the French Revolutionary Wars, and the diplomatic fallout of the XYZ Affair.

Background

France, angered by the Jay Treaty between the United States and Britain, authorised privateers to seize American merchantmen; by 1797 hundreds had been taken. When French agents (the infamous X, Y, and Z) demanded bribes before negotiations in Paris, American opinion hardened. President John Adams obtained congressional authorisation to arm merchant vessels, created the Department of the Navy, and completed the frigates of the new United States Navy.

Naval operations

American warships and revenue cutters, operating with tacit Royal Navy cooperation, captured dozens of French privateers and recaptured merchantmen. Famous single-ship actions included USS Constellation's capture of the frigate L'Insurgente (1799) and her hard-fought battle with La Vengeance (1800). Losses inflicted on French commerce helped persuade the Consulate to negotiate.

Resolution

The Convention of 1800 (Treaty of Mortefontaine) ended the conflict and formally dissolved the 1778 alliance — the only alliance the United States entered until the twentieth century — in exchange for the release of American claims for French spoliations.
