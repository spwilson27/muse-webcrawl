Big Red Machine

The Big Red Machine is the nickname given to the Cincinnati Reds baseball team that dominated the National League during the 1970s. Managed chiefly by Sparky Anderson, the Reds won six division titles, four National League pennants, and back-to-back World Series championships in 1975 and 1976.

The team

The Machine's core, known as the "Great Eight", included catcher Johnny Bench, first baseman Tony Pérez, second baseman Joe Morgan, shortstop Dave Concepción, third baseman Pete Rose, and outfielders George Foster, Ken Griffey Sr., and César Gerónimo. Bench, Morgan, and Pérez are members of the Baseball Hall of Fame, as is manager Anderson.

Achievements

The 1975 team defeated the Boston Red Sox in a celebrated seven-game World Series, and the 1976 team swept both the playoffs and the World Series against the New York Yankees — still the only team to go undefeated through the postseason in the divisional era. The nickname "Big Red Machine" had earlier been applied to the 1970 pennant-winning club and stuck throughout the decade.

Legacy

The Big Red Machine is widely considered one of the greatest teams in baseball history, and its 1975–76 seasons remain the benchmark for National League dynasties of the era.
