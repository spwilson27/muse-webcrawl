1935 Labor Day hurricane

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 1935 Labor Day hurricane was an extremely powerful and compact Category 5 Atlantic hurricane that struck the Florida Keys over the Labor Day weekend in September 1935. With a central pressure of 892 millibars at landfall near Long Key, it was the most intense tropical cyclone ever recorded to make landfall in the United States, and one of the strongest Atlantic hurricanes on record.

Impact

The storm's core, only about 15–25 km across, crossed the Middle Keys with a storm surge of roughly 5.5 metres, obliterating Islamorada and other settlements and destroying the Florida East Coast Railway's Key West Extension — the "railroad that went to sea" — which was never rebuilt. A rescue train sent from Miami was derailed by the surge near Islamorada, surviving only in its locomotive.

At least 423 people were killed, including several hundred World War I veterans employed in federal work camps building the Overseas Highway; the failure to evacuate them in time became a national scandal and the subject of a congressional investigation. Ernest Hemingway, who lived in Key West, angrily criticised the government in his essay "Who Murdered the Vets?"

Aftermath

The railway damage led to its purchase by the state, and the rail bridges were converted into the Overseas Highway, completed in 1938. The Veterans' memorial in Islamorada — the Florida Keys Memorial — commemorates the victims.
