Fellowship

A fellowship is a period of advanced training or research undertaken by a person who has been awarded a competitive grant or appointment, commonly called a fellow. Fellowships are most often associated with academia, medicine and research institutions, where they support scholars, physicians and professionals in developing specialised expertise.

In academia

In universities, a fellowship may denote a funded position for graduate study or postdoctoral research, such as those offered by foundations, learned societies and research councils. Fellows of colleges at the universities of Oxford and Cambridge are members of the governing body of their college and typically hold teaching or research posts.

In medicine

In medical training, a fellowship follows completion of a residency and provides subspecialty training — for example in cardiology, oncology or surgery — usually lasting one to three years. Physicians in such programmes are referred to as fellows.

Learned societies

Election as a Fellow of a learned society, such as the Royal Society, is an honour recognising distinguished contributions to a field, and fellows may use post-nominal letters such as FRS.
