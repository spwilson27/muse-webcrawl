Augusto Boal

Augusto Boal (16 March 1931 – 2 May 2009) was a Brazilian theatre practitioner, founder of the Theatre of the Oppressed, a set of theatrical techniques intended to turn spectators into active participants ("spect-actors") and to use theatre as a tool for social and political change. His book Theatre of the Oppressed (1974) has been translated into many languages and his methods are practised worldwide.

Life

Boal studied chemical engineering at the Federal University of Rio de Janeiro and theatre at Columbia University in New York. Returning to Brazil, he directed the Arena Theatre of São Paulo, developing productions with strong social themes. After the 1964 military coup his work attracted the regime's attention; in 1971 he was arrested, tortured and exiled, living in Argentina, Portugal and France, where he founded the first Centre for the Theatre of the Oppressed in Paris in 1979. He returned to Brazil in 1986 after the restoration of democracy.

Theatre of the Oppressed

Influenced by the pedagogy of his compatriot Paulo Freire, Boal argued that conventional theatre separates passive spectators from actors, mirroring political oppression. His techniques — including Image Theatre, Forum Theatre (in which audience members stop and redirect a scene of oppression), Invisible Theatre and Rainbow of Desire — aim to rehearse real-life action collectively. As a Rio de Janeiro city councillor in the 1990s he developed "Legislative Theatre", using forum techniques with constituents; a number of the resulting proposals became municipal law.

Legacy

Boal was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize and served as a UNESCO World Theatre Ambassador. Centres for the Theatre of the Oppressed now operate in many countries, applying his methods in education, community work, prisons and therapy.
