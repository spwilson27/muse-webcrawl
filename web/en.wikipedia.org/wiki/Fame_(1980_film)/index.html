Fame is a 1980 American teen musical drama film directed by Alan Parker, written by Christopher Gore, and produced by David De Silva and Alan Marshall. Set at the New York City High School for the Performing Arts, the film follows a group of students through their four years at the school, from auditions to graduation, as they pursue ambitions in acting, dance and music. It is remembered for its energetic musical sequences and for its unusually frank treatment of teenage issues for a mainstream musical of the period.

Plot

The film is structured in chapters by school year. Among the students are singer Coco Hernandez (Irene Cara), dancer Leroy Johnson (Gene Anthony Ray), composer Bruno Martelli (Lee Curreri), actress Doris Finsecker (Maureen Teefy), actor Raúl García, who renames himself Ralph Garci, and the shy Montgomery MacNeil. Their stories trace the exhilaration and costs of chasing fame — exploitation, failure, identity and perseverance.

Music

The score by Michael Gore produced two songs nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Song: the title track "Fame" and "Out Here on My Own", both sung by Irene Cara. "Fame" won the Oscar and became an international pop hit. The finale, "I Sing the Body Electric", sets words from Walt Whitman's poem in Leaves of Grass.

Reception and legacy

The film was a commercial success and received generally positive reviews; it won two Academy Awards (Best Original Score and Best Original Song for "Fame") from six nominations. It launched a franchise: a television series (1982–1987), a stage musical, and a 2009 remake. The real school depicted was later renamed Fiorello H. LaGuardia High School.
