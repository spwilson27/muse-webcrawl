Javelin

A javelin is a light spear designed in ancient times to be thrown as a ranged weapon. Unlike a heavier spear used for thrusting, the javelin was typically carried in multiples by skirmishers and used to disrupt enemy formations before close combat.

History

Javelins were used throughout the ancient world: Greek peltasts, Roman legionaries with the pilum (a heavy javelin designed to bend on impact and disable shields), and Celtic and Germanic warriors all employed them. Cavalry in many cultures also used light javelins, a tradition surviving in the Iberian jinete style of riding.

Sport

The modern javelin throw is an Olympic athletics event in which competitors throw a regulation javelin — 2.6–2.7 m long and 800 g for men, 2.2–2.3 m and 600 g for women — for distance. See Javelin throw.
