The ASEAN–China Free Trade Area (ACFTA) is a free trade area among the ten member states of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) and the People's Republic of China. The framework agreement was signed on 4 November 2002 in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, and the free trade area came fully into effect on 1 January 2010, when the largest part of trade in goods between China and the six original ASEAN signatories became tariff-free.

History

China first proposed the idea of a free trade area in November 2000. Following the Framework Agreement on Comprehensive Economic Cooperation signed in 2002, agreements on trade in goods (2004), trade in services (2007), and investment (2009) were concluded in stages. From 1 January 2010, the average tariff on Chinese goods sold in ASEAN countries decreased from 12.8 to 0.6 percent, while the average tariff on ASEAN goods sold in China decreased from 9.8 to 0.1 percent.

Significance

By population and combined economic output, the ACFTA was described at its creation as the largest free trade area in terms of population and the third largest in terms of nominal GDP. An upgrade protocol signed in 2015, and subsequent negotiations toward a further "3.0" upgrade, aimed to modernise the agreement, including provisions on the digital economy and green economy.
