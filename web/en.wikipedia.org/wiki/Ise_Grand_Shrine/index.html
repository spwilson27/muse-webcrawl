Ise Grand Shrine

LocationIse, Mie Prefecture, Japan
ReligionShinto
DeityAmaterasu Ōmikami; Toyouke Ōmikami
EstablishedTraditionally 4 BCE; shrine complex from late 7th century

The Ise Grand Shrine (Ise Jingū) is a Shinto shrine complex in Ise, Mie Prefecture, Japan, dedicated principally to the sun goddess Amaterasu. It is the most sacred and important shrine in Shinto, comprising 125 shrines centred on the Inner Shrine (Naikū, dedicated to Amaterasu) and the Outer Shrine (Gekū, dedicated to the food goddess Toyouke).

History

Tradition dates Amaterasu's enshrinement here, guided by Princess Yamatohime, to around 4 BCE; historically the complex took shape under Emperors Tenmu and Jitō in the late 7th century. The shrine houses the Sacred Mirror (Yata no Kagami), one of the Imperial Regalia, and its chief priestess has traditionally been an imperial princess.

Rebuilding — Shikinen Sengū

Since 690 the main buildings have been ritually rebuilt every twenty years in the Shikinen Sengū ceremony — most recently in 2013, the 62nd rebuilding — on an adjacent plot, in the ancient shinmei-zukuri style of raised cypress wood with thatched roofs, without nails. The old building is then dismantled. The practice embodies impermanence and renewal and preserves traditional crafts.

Pilgrimage

In the Edo period mass pilgrimages (okage-mairi) brought millions to Ise. Today the shrine draws millions of visitors yearly, crossing the Uji Bridge over the Isuzu River; only priests enter the innermost precinct, glimpsed over high fences.
