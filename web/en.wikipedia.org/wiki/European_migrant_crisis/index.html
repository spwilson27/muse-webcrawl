2015 European migrant crisis

The 2015 European migrant crisis was a period characterised by high numbers of people arriving in the European Union (EU) from across the Mediterranean Sea or overland through Southeast Europe, applying for asylum. In 2015 the EU recorded about 1.3 million asylum applications, the largest annual figure since the Second World War. The largest groups came from Syria, Afghanistan, and Iraq, fleeing war and persecution.

Background and course

Rising arrivals during 2015 placed heavy pressure on frontline states such as Greece and Italy and on the Western Balkan route into Hungary and Austria. In August 2015 German Chancellor Angela Merkel suspended the Dublin Regulation procedure for Syrian refugees, and Germany ultimately received close to 890,000 asylum seekers that year. Images of drowned toddler Alan Kurdi on a Turkish beach in September 2015 galvanised public attention worldwide.

The November 2015 Paris attacks and other security incidents intensified debate about border controls. In March 2016 the EU and Turkey concluded a statement under which irregular migrants crossing to the Greek islands would be returned to Turkey, sharply reducing Aegean crossings; the closure of the Balkan route the same month ended the main overland flow. Arrivals fell to roughly 360,000 in 2016 and continued to decline, though the Central Mediterranean route to Italy remained dangerous, and thousands died at sea during the decade.

Political consequences

The crisis strained solidarity among member states: a scheme to relocate 160,000 asylum seekers from Greece and Italy was only partly implemented amid opposition from several Central European governments, and it contributed to the rise of anti-immigration parties and to debates preceding the 2016 Brexit referendum.
