Tea (meal)

Tea
Type: Meal
Origin: United Kingdom
Variants: Afternoon tea, high tea, cream tea

Tea is an umbrella term for several different meals consisting of food accompanied by the drink tea, principally in the United Kingdom and some Commonwealth countries. The usages of the term vary considerably by region and class, and in parts of northern England, Scotland, Wales, Australia and New Zealand "tea" denotes the main evening meal.

Afternoon tea

Afternoon tea is a light meal traditionally served in the mid-afternoon. It is credited to Anna Russell, Duchess of Bedford, who in the 1840s began requesting a tray of tea, bread and cake to stave off hunger before the late evening dinner. The custom spread through fashionable society and developed into a formal occasion featuring tiered stands of sandwiches with the crusts cut off, scones with clotted cream and jam, and cakes and pastries, accompanied by loose-leaf tea.

High tea

High tea, despite the name, is a more substantial early-evening meal historically associated with the working classes, who returned home hungry after work. Served at a high dining table (hence "high"), it includes a hot dish such as meat pie or fish, bread and butter, and cakes, with tea to drink. The term is frequently misapplied, particularly outside Britain, to the more elegant afternoon tea.

Cream tea

A cream tea, associated especially with Devon and Cornwall, consists of scones served with jam and clotted cream alongside a pot of tea; the two counties famously disagree over whether the jam or the cream should be spread first.

Tea as the evening meal

In many households across the north of England and the Commonwealth, the evening meal is simply called "tea", while "dinner" refers to the midday meal — a distinction that remains a well-known marker of regional and social dialect in Britain.
