Afterburner

An afterburner (also called reheat in British English) is an additional combustion component in some jet engines, mostly those on military supersonic aircraft. It injects fuel directly into the hot exhaust stream behind the turbine and burns it using the remaining oxygen, greatly increasing thrust — typically by 50% or more — at the cost of very high fuel consumption.

Operation

Because gas temperatures entering the afterburner are already high, a flame holder stabilises combustion in the fast-moving exhaust. A variable-area nozzle opens when reheat is lit to pass the increased gas volume. Afterburning produces the characteristic long flames and Mach (shock) diamonds visible in the exhaust, and a dramatic rise in noise. Engines without afterburners are said to run "dry"; thrust with reheat is "wet" thrust.

Use

Afterburners are used for take-off, rapid acceleration, transonic climb and combat manoeuvring, and are essential for most supersonic flight; only a few aircraft, such as the F-22 Raptor, can sustain supersonic cruise without them ("supercruise"). Sustained use is limited by the enormous fuel flow — often several times dry consumption. Civil use was essentially confined to Concorde, which used reheat for take-off and transonic acceleration.
