The Amateur Cracksman

The Amateur Cracksman is an 1899 collection of short stories by E. W. Hornung, introducing A. J. Raffles, the gentleman thief, and his accomplice Harry "Bunny" Manders. The stories first appeared in The Strand Magazine and Cassell's Magazine.

Contents

The collection includes "The Ides of March", in which Bunny, ruined by gambling debts, turns to his old school friend Raffles and is drawn into burglary; "A Costume Piece", involving a diamond theft; "Gentlemen and Players"; "Le Premier Pas"; "Wilful Murder"; "The Gift of the Emperor"; and "The Spy and the Capture". A common thread is Raffles's double life as a celebrated amateur cricketer and daring burglar.

Legacy

Raffles became one of the most popular characters of Victorian crime fiction, a deliberate inversion of Sherlock Holmes (Hornung was Arthur Conan Doyle's brother-in-law). The stories inspired plays, films and later pastiches.
