The Battle of Samugarh was fought on 29 May 1658 at Samugarh, about 13 km east of Agra, during the Mughal war of succession that followed the illness of Emperor Shah Jahan. The combined armies of the princes Aurangzeb and Murad Baksh defeated the imperial army commanded by the heir apparent, Dara Shikoh. The victory decided the succession struggle: Aurangzeb went on to occupy Agra, imprison his father, and crown himself emperor.

Background

When Shah Jahan fell seriously ill in September 1657, his four sons — Dara Shikoh, Shah Shuja, Aurangzeb and Murad Baksh — contested the throne. After defeating Shuja's forces at Bahadurpur and an imperial army at Dharmat (April 1658), Aurangzeb and Murad advanced on Agra, where Dara assembled a large but hastily raised army.

The battle

Fighting in extreme summer heat, Dara's army initially pressed hard, and his Rajput vanguard fought fiercely, but the imperial host lacked cohesion. A critical moment came when Dara descended from his elephant to mount a horse; his troops, seeing the empty howdah, believed him dead and began to flee. Aurangzeb's disciplined artillery and reserves completed the rout.

Aftermath

Dara fled toward Delhi and eventually to Sindh and Gujarat, where he was betrayed, captured, and executed in 1659. Aurangzeb deposed Shah Jahan, who spent his remaining years imprisoned in Agra Fort, and later eliminated Murad as well, securing the throne as Emperor Alamgir.
