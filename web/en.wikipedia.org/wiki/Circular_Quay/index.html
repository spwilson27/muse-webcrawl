Circular Quay

Circular Quay is a harbour, former working port and now a residential, recreational and tourist precinct in Sydney, New South Wales, Australia. It is located on the northern edge of the Sydney central business district, on Sydney Cove, between Bennelong Point and The Rocks.

History

The area was originally known to the local Aboriginal people as Warrane. The quay was constructed in 1837–1844 by convict labour, during the governorship of Sir George Gipps, on the site of the original landing place of the First Fleet in 1788. It was originally known as the Semi-Circular Quay, this being the actual shape of the quay. The name was shortened for convenience.

Transport

Circular Quay is a major Sydney transport hub, with large ferry, rail and bus interchanges. The Cahill Expressway runs above the railway station. It is also served by trams on the L2 and L3 light rail lines.

Events

The precinct is a popular focal point for community celebrations, due to its central Sydney CBD location, its prominence as a major vantage point for watching New Year fireworks, and views of the Sydney Opera House and Sydney Harbour Bridge.
