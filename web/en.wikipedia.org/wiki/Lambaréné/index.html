Lambaréné

Lambaréné is a town in west-central Gabon, the capital of Moyen-Ogooué Province. It lies on the Ogooué River near the equator, about 75 kilometres southeast of the Atlantic coast, and has a population of roughly 25,000. The town is built on both banks of the river and on an island in between.

Albert Schweitzer Hospital

Lambaréné is best known for the hospital founded in 1913 by the Alsatian theologian, musician, and physician Albert Schweitzer, whose work there — guided by his philosophy of "reverence for life" — earned him the Nobel Peace Prize in 1952. The original hospital is now a museum, while the modern Hôpital Albert Schweitzer continues to serve the region and hosts research on malaria and other tropical diseases.

Geography and economy

The town sits amid equatorial rainforest and a maze of lakes connected to the Ogooué. Fishing, timber, and palm-oil production are important local activities, and Lambaréné is connected by road to Libreville and the interior.
