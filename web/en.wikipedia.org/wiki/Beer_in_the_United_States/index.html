Beer in the United States

Beer in the United States is produced by more than 9,000 breweries, ranging from industry giants to brewpubs and microbreweries. The United States is one of the world's largest beer markets. Light pale lager dominates national sales, but since the 1980s a craft beer movement has made the US a world leader in stylistic diversity, reviving styles such as India pale ale and inventing new ones.

History

Indigenous peoples brewed corn-based beers before European colonisation. British-style ales dominated colonial brewing; German immigrants in the mid-19th century introduced lager brewing and founded firms such as Anheuser-Busch (1852), Miller (1855), Coors (1873) and Pabst. Refrigeration and rail distribution created national brands.

Prohibition (1920–1933) closed most breweries; many survivors made near beer, malt syrup or soda. After repeal, the industry consolidated relentlessly: by the late 1970s fewer than 100 brewing companies remained, and mass-market pale lager — made lighter with adjuncts such as rice and corn — was nearly universal.

The craft beer movement

The revival began with Fritz Maytag's purchase of San Francisco's Anchor Brewing in 1965 and Jack McAuliffe's New Albion Brewing (1976). Legalisation of homebrewing in 1978 and state brewpub laws stimulated growth; Sierra Nevada (founded 1979) and the Boston Beer Company (1984) became national craft brands. Growth accelerated in the 2010s, when the number of US breweries passed the pre-Prohibition record. Craft brewers popularised assertively hopped American IPAs, barrel-aged stouts and sour beers. The largest producers overall remain AB InBev (Bud Light, Budweiser, Michelob), Molson Coors (Coors Light, Miller Lite) and Constellation Brands' Mexican imports (Modelo, Corona).

Regulation and distribution

Alcohol regulation is primarily a state matter. Most states enforce a "three-tier system" separating producers, distributors and retailers, a legacy of repeal. The federal Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau (TTB) taxes and regulates production. The legal drinking age is 21 in all states.

Economics

The beer industry contributes hundreds of billions of dollars to US economic activity and supports brewing clusters in states such as California, Colorado, Pennsylvania, New York and North Carolina. Overall beer volumes have been flat or declining in recent years as spirits, hard seltzer and ready-to-drink cocktails gained share, and craft growth has slowed or reversed since the early 2020s.
