Mirkwood

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mirkwood is a name used for two distinct fictional forests in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, and the name of a great dark forest in Germanic and Norse mythology (Old Norse Myrkviðr), from which Tolkien took the term.

In Tolkien's works

The best-known Mirkwood is the vast forest of Rhovanion in Middle-earth, east of the Misty Mountains and the river Anduin. Originally called Greenwood the Great (Eryn Galen), it was the realm of the Silvan Elves under Thranduil in its north. During the Third Age a shadow fell on it after Sauron established himself at Dol Guldur in the south, and it became a place of giant spiders, black squirrels and enchanted streams, renamed Mirkwood (Eryn Lasgalen only after its cleansing). In The Hobbit, Bilbo Baggins and the thirteen dwarves travel through Mirkwood, are captured first by spiders and then by Thranduil's Elves, and escape by barrel down the Forest River. After Sauron's fall, the forest was divided between Thranduil and Celeborn and renamed Eryn Lasgalen, the Wood of Greenleaves.

Tolkien also used Mirkwood for a forest on the borders of Eriador in an earlier conception, and the name appears in his 1930s poem and unfinished works.

Mythological source

Myrkviðr ("murky wood") appears in Norse texts such as the Poetic Edda as a dark boundary forest, and a "Mirkwood" of Germanic legend was described by William Morris, whose writings influenced Tolkien. Tolkien regarded the name as "very ancient" and evocative of the primeval forests of northern Europe.
