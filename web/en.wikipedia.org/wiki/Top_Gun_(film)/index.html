Top Gun is a 1986 American action drama film directed by Tony Scott and produced by Don Simpson and Jerry Bruckheimer. Inspired by Ehud Yonay's 1983 California magazine article "Top Guns", the film stars Tom Cruise as Lieutenant Pete "Maverick" Mitchell, a young naval aviator aboard the aircraft carrier USS Enterprise.

Plot

Maverick and his radar intercept officer "Goose" (Anthony Edwards) are sent to the Navy's elite Fighter Weapons School at Naval Air Station Miramar — "Top Gun" — to compete against the best naval aviators. Maverick clashes with the cool, methodical "Iceman" (Val Kilmer) and romances civilian instructor Charlotte "Charlie" Blackwood (Kelly McGillis). During a training flight, Maverick's F-14 Tomcat enters a flat spin; the crew ejects, and Goose is killed. Blamed by a board of inquiry but absolved, a shaken Maverick nearly quits before returning for graduation and a real combat engagement over the Indian Ocean, where he overcomes his fear and defeats enemy MiGs, finally at peace with his past.

Legacy

Made with extensive U.S. Navy cooperation, the film's aerial photography and soundtrack — including Kenny Loggins's "Danger Zone" and Berlin's Oscar-winning "Take My Breath Away" — made it the highest-grossing film of 1986 and a cultural phenomenon that measurably boosted Navy recruitment. It was selected for preservation in the National Film Registry in 2015. A sequel, Top Gun: Maverick, followed in 2022.
