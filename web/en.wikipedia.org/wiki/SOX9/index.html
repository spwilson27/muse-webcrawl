SOX9 (SRY-box transcription factor 9) is a protein-coding gene that encodes a transcription factor essential for embryonic development. It is a member of the SOX family, characterised by a high-mobility group (HMG) DNA-binding domain related to that of the sex-determining protein SRY. SOX9 has two headline roles: it is the key downstream effector of SRY in testis development, and it is the master regulator of chondrogenesis, the formation of cartilage.

Role in sex determination

In XY embryos, SRY protein (together with SF1) binds the testis-specific enhancer TESCO upstream of SOX9 and upregulates it in the bipotential gonad. SOX9 then drives differentiation of Sertoli cells, which orchestrate testis formation, in part by activating anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) and the FGF9/PGD2 signalling loops that lock in the male pathway, while repressing the ovarian pathway genes such as WNT4 and RSPO1. Duplications or regulatory mutations that raise SOX9 expression in XX individuals can cause female-to-male sex reversal, while loss-of-function mutations in XY individuals cause male-to-female sex reversal, demonstrating that SOX9 dosage is decisive.

Role in cartilage and other tissues

SOX9 is expressed in chondrocyte progenitors and directly activates cartilage matrix genes including COL2A1 (type II collagen) and aggrecan, working with its partners SOX5 and SOX6. Heterozygous loss-of-function mutations cause campomelic dysplasia, a severe skeletal disorder characterised by bowed long bones ("campomelia"), underdeveloped shoulder blades and often sex reversal; it is usually lethal in the neonatal period because of respiratory insufficiency. SOX9 also functions in the development of the pancreas, gut, hair follicles, neural crest and in adult stem-cell niches, and its overexpression is implicated in several cancers, including colorectal and prostate cancer.
