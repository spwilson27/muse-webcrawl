LocationRoman Forum, Rome
BegunAD 308, by Maxentius
CompletedAD 312, by Constantine I
Also known asBasilica Nova; Basilica of Maxentius and Constantine

The Basilica of Maxentius, also known as the Basilica Nova, was the largest building in the Roman Forum. Begun by the emperor Maxentius in AD 308 and completed after his defeat by Constantine I in 312, it stood on the north side of the Via Sacra on the Velia hill.

Architecture

The basilica's central nave, about 65 by 25 metres, was covered by three enormous groin vaults rising some 35 metres — a concrete construction modelled on the great imperial bath halls (frigidaria). Only the north aisle, with its three towering barrel vaults, survives today, yet the ruin remains one of the most imposing in Rome. A colossal statue of Constantine, over 12 metres high, stood in the apse; its marble head, hand and foot are now in the Capitoline Museums.

History

Constantine modified the building after his victory at the Milvian Bridge, adding a new apse and entrance. The basilica's vast vaulted interior later inspired Renaissance architects, and its engineering influenced the design of early railway stations and other great halls.
