Liliʻuokalani

Reign: January 29, 1891 – January 17, 1893
Born: Lydia Liliʻu Loloku Walania Kamakaʻeha, September 2, 1838, Honolulu, Kingdom of Hawaii
Died: November 11, 1917, Honolulu, Territory of Hawaii

Liliʻuokalani (Hawaiian pronunciation: [liˌliʔuokəˈlɐni]; born Lydia Liliʻu Loloku Walania Kamakaʻeha; September 2, 1838 – November 11, 1917) was the only queen regnant and the last sovereign monarch of the Hawaiian Kingdom, ruling from January 29, 1891, until the overthrow of the Hawaiian Kingdom on January 17, 1893.

Early life and accession

Liliʻuokalani was born in Honolulu to high chiefs Kapaʻakea and Keohokālole and was adopted in the Hawaiian tradition of hānai by Abner Pākī and Laura Kōnia. She was educated at the Chiefs' Children's School and married John Owen Dominis in 1862. Her brother, King Kalākaua, named her heir apparent in 1877 after the death of their younger brother Leleiohoku; she served as regent during Kalākaua's world tour in 1881 and ascended the throne on his death in 1891.

Overthrow and later life

Her attempt in January 1893 to promulgate a new constitution restoring royal authority and voting rights to Native Hawaiians precipitated a coup by a committee of American and European businessmen, supported by the United States minister and troops from USS Boston. She yielded under protest to avoid bloodshed. After an attempted counter-rebellion in 1895 she was arrested, tried, and imprisoned in ʻIolani Palace for nearly a year, during which she formally abdicated. She spent her remaining years contesting the annexation of Hawaii by the United States (accomplished in 1898) and caring for orphaned and destitute children through her trust.

Legacy

An accomplished composer, Liliʻuokalani wrote many songs, most famously "Aloha ʻOe" ("Farewell to Thee"). Her memoir Hawaii's Story by Hawaii's Queen (1898) presented her account of the overthrow. In 1993 the United States Congress passed the Apology Resolution, acknowledging the illegality of the overthrow.
