Flooring

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Flooring is the general term for a permanent covering of a floor, or for the work of installing such a floor covering. Floor covering is a term to generically describe any finish material applied over a floor structure to provide a walking surface. Materials almost always classified as flooring include carpet, laminate, tile, and vinyl.

Materials

Wood flooring is made from timber, either solid or engineered with a veneer over plywood, and includes parquet and strip flooring. Laminate flooring imitates wood or stone using a photographic layer under a clear protective coating. Vinyl flooring, including sheet vinyl, vinyl tiles and luxury vinyl planks, is a resilient synthetic option valued for water resistance. Ceramic and porcelain tile and natural stone such as marble, granite, slate and travertine are common in wet areas and high-traffic spaces. Other options include linoleum, cork, bamboo, terrazzo, polished concrete and carpet, which may be fitted wall-to-wall or supplied as carpet tiles.

Subfloor and installation

Flooring is installed over a subfloor — typically plywood, oriented strand board, or a concrete slab — which provides structural support. Underlayment may be added for moisture control, sound insulation or cushioning. Installation methods include nailing or stapling, gluing down, and "floating" floors whose planks lock together without attachment to the subfloor. Choice of material depends on durability, moisture exposure, maintenance, comfort, acoustics and cost.
