Bikini Atoll

Bikini Atoll (Marshallese: Pikinni) is a coral atoll in the Marshall Islands in the Pacific Ocean, consisting of 23 islands surrounding a central lagoon. It is best known as the site of 23 United States nuclear weapons tests between 1946 and 1958, which forced the evacuation of its inhabitants and left lasting contamination. Bikini Atoll was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2010 as a symbol of the dawn of the nuclear age.

Nuclear testing

In 1946 the United States persuaded the 167 Bikinians, led by King Juda, to move temporarily so the atoll could be used for testing "for the good of mankind". Operation Crossroads detonated two bombs that July against a fleet of target ships. Testing resumed in the 1950s under Operation Castle; the Castle Bravo shot of 1 March 1954, at 15 megatons, was the largest ever US test and spread heavy fallout over inhabited atolls including Rongelap. In all, 23 devices were detonated at Bikini by 1958.

Displacement and aftermath

The Bikinians were moved first to Rongerik, where they nearly starved, then to Kwajalein and finally to Kili Island, which lacks a lagoon and cannot support their seafaring culture. A brief resettlement of Bikini in the 1970s was reversed when dangerous levels of caesium-137 were found in local food. The United States established trust funds and a Nuclear Claims Tribunal for compensation. Today the atoll is visited by technical divers drawn to its sunken "ghost fleet", and limited tourism operates under radiological monitoring.
