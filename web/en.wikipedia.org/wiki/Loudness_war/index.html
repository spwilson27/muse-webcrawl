Loudness war

The loudness war (or loudness race) refers to the trend in recorded music, accelerating with the compact disc era, of mastering recordings at ever-higher average levels by means of dynamic range compression and limiting. Because louder records were thought to sound better on radio, in jukeboxes and in A/B comparisons, artists and labels competed for loudness at the expense of dynamics, transients and listening fatigue.

History

Competition for loudness existed in the vinyl era (Motown singles were cut hot), but digital audio removed physical constraints: once CDs made 0 dBFS a hard ceiling, engineers could push the average level ever closer to it. Frequently cited examples include Oasis's (What's the Story) Morning Glory? (1995), Iggy Pop's brickwalled 1997 remix of Raw Power, and Metallica's Death Magnetic (2008), whose distorted CD master provoked fan petitions after the more dynamic Guitar Hero version circulated. Audiophile engineers such as Bob Katz and organisations including the Dynamic Range Database campaigned against the trend, and remasters sometimes restore dynamics (e.g., the Beatles' remasters debate).

Resolution

Loudness normalisation by streaming platforms (Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube) — which turn loud masters down to a target around −14 LUFS — largely removed the incentive to hyper-compress, and engineers began mastering for dynamics again in the 2010s. Radio broadcast standards such as EBU R 128 had earlier achieved a similar effect in television.
