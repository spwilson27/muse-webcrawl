Romansh language


Native to: Canton of Graubünden, Switzerland
Speakers: about 40,000–60,000
Family: Indo-European → Romance → Rhaeto-Romance
Status: Fourth national language of Switzerland

Romansh (also spelled Romansch, Rhaeto-Romance, or Rumantsch) is a Romance language spoken predominantly in the southeastern Swiss canton of Graubünden (Grisons). It descends from the Vulgar Latin spoken by Roman soldiers and settlers who colonised the region of Raetia in the first century BC, and it forms part of the Rhaeto-Romance group together with Ladin and Friulian in northern Italy. Romansh was recognised as Switzerland's fourth national language in a 1938 referendum, partly to assert Swiss distinctness from Nazi Germany's claims on German-speaking regions.

Dialects and standardisation

Romansh exists in five regional written dialects — Sursilvan, Sutsilvan, Surmiran, Putèr, and Vallader — each with its own literary tradition beginning with the Reformation-era texts of the sixteenth century. In 1982 the linguist Heinrich Schmid created a unified written standard, Rumantsch Grischun, for use in federal and cantonal administration and pan-Romansh media. Its adoption was controversial and several municipalities later returned to regional idioms in schools, while federal texts continue to use the standard.

Status and use

Romansh speakers number roughly 0.5% of the Swiss population and are nearly all bilingual in German, which has steadily displaced Romansh since the nineteenth century through tourism, military service, and media. The language is supported by the liaison organisation Lia Rumantscha (founded 1919), Romansh radio and television (Radiotelevisiun Svizra Rumantscha), and the daily newspaper La Quotidiana. Many Romansh villages still conduct primary schooling in Romansh, and the canton of Graubünden is officially trilingual (German, Romansh, Italian).
