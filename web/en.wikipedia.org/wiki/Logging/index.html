Logging

Logging is the process of cutting, processing, and moving trees to a location for transport. It may include felling trees, skidding and hauling logs, on-site processing such as delimbing and bucking (cutting logs to length), and loading onto trucks or trains. Logging supplies the raw material for timber, pulp and paper, and many other wood products, and is a central activity of the forestry industry.

Methods

Harvesting systems range from chainsaw felling by hand to fully mechanised operations using feller-bunchers, harvesters that fell, delimb, and cut trees to length in one pass, and forwarders that carry logs to the roadside. Common silvicultural approaches include clearcutting, in which most trees in an area are removed at once; selection and shelterwood cutting, which remove trees in stages; and thinning of younger stands. Cable logging and helicopter logging are used on steep ground.

Environmental issues

Logging — particularly illegal and unsustainable logging in tropical forests — is a major driver of deforestation, habitat loss, and carbon emissions, and can cause soil erosion and damage to watercourses. Certification schemes such as those of the Forest Stewardship Council aim to assure consumers that wood comes from responsibly managed forests, and sustainable forestry practices seek to balance timber production with regeneration, biodiversity, and ecosystem services.

History and safety

Historically logs were moved by river drives, oxen and horses, and later by railways and trucks. Logging remains one of the most hazardous occupations in many countries due to falling trees, heavy machinery, and remote terrain.
