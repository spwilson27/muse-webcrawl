The Nez Perce (self-name Nimíipuu, "the people") are an Indigenous people of the Pacific Northwest of the United States, historically centred on the Wallowa Valley and the Clearwater and Snake river country of present-day Idaho, Oregon and Washington. The French name nez percé ("pierced nose") was a misnomer applied by early traders.

History

The Nez Perce were renowned horse breeders who developed the Appaloosa. They aided the Lewis and Clark expedition in 1805–06. After a disputed 1863 treaty drastically reduced their reservation, several non-treaty bands under leaders including Chief Joseph resisted removal in the Nez Perce War of 1877, conducting a fighting retreat of about 1,170 miles before surrendering in Montana, just short of Canada.

Today

The Nez Perce Tribe is a federally recognised tribe headquartered at Lapwai, Idaho, with a reservation of about 750,000 acres; the Confederated Tribes of the Colville Reservation in Washington also include Nez Perce descendants.
