Executive Order 9066 was a United States presidential executive order signed and issued during World War II by President Franklin D. Roosevelt on February 19, 1942. It authorised the Secretary of War to prescribe certain areas as military zones, clearing the way for the forced removal and incarceration of nearly all persons of Japanese ancestry living on the West Coast of the United States, as well as the deportation of German and Italian nationals in later phases.

Implementation

The order itself did not name any ethnic group, but it was applied overwhelmingly against Japanese Americans: approximately 120,000 people, two-thirds of them US citizens (Nisei), were removed from their homes and confined in ten camps administered by the War Relocation Authority, at sites including Manzanar and Tule Lake in California, Poston in Arizona, and Heart Mountain in Wyoming. General John L. DeWitt, head of the Western Defense Command, implemented the exclusion through a series of civilian exclusion orders. The Supreme Court upheld the programme's curfew and exclusion measures in Hirabayashi v. United States (1943) and Korematsu v. United States (1944), though in Ex parte Endo (1944) it ruled that loyal citizens could not continue to be detained.

Redress

President Gerald Ford formally terminated the order in 1976, calling the evacuation a national mistake. The Commission on Wartime Relocation and Internment of Civilians concluded in 1983 that the incarceration had been caused by "race prejudice, war hysteria and a failure of political leadership". The Civil Liberties Act of 1988 granted a formal apology and $20,000 in reparations to each surviving internee. In 2018, the Supreme Court, in Trump v. Hawaii, stated in dicta that Korematsu had been gravely wrong and was repudiated.
