Carnassial

Carnassials are a pair of large teeth found in many carnivorous mammals — most characteristically in members of the order Carnivora — modified to shear flesh and tendon like the blades of scissors.

Structure

The carnassial pair consists of the upper fourth premolar (P4) and the lower first molar (m1), which slide past each other as the jaw closes, producing an efficient slicing action while the jaw joint restricts sideways movement. Behind the carnassials, many carnivorans retain crushing molars (bunodont teeth); in highly carnivorous "hypercarnivores" such as cats, the crushing molars are reduced or lost.

Evolution

Shearing cheek teeth evolved convergently in several extinct lineages — creodonts, mesonychians, and the marsupial thylacoleonids among them — with different teeth recruited as the carnassial pair, showing the feature is an ecomorphological adaptation to meat-shearing rather than a single inherited trait.

Veterinary significance

The upper carnassial of dogs is a common site of slab fractures and tooth-root abscesses because of the force it bears when chewing hard objects.
