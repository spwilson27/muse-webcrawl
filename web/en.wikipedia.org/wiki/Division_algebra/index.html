Division algebra

In abstract algebra, a division algebra is an algebra over a field in which division, except by zero, is always possible. More precisely, an algebra D over a field F is a division algebra if for every nonzero element a of D and every element b of D, there exists exactly one element x of D with a·x = b, and exactly one element y with y·a = b.

The Fano plane encodes the multiplication rules of the octonions.

Examples

The best-known examples of division algebras are the real numbers, the complex numbers, the quaternions, and the octonions.

Frobenius theorem

The Frobenius theorem (1877) states that the only finite-dimensional associative division algebras over the real numbers are the real numbers, the complex numbers, and the quaternions. A theorem of Hopf and others extends this: the only finite-dimensional real division algebras (not assumed associative) have dimension 1, 2, 4, or 8.

Related concepts

Every finite-dimensional division algebra over an algebraically closed field is the field itself. Over general fields, finite-dimensional central division algebras are classified by the Brauer group of the field.
