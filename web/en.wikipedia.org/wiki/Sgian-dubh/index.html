Sgian-dubh

The sgian-dubh (Scottish Gaelic for "black knife/dagger", pronounced roughly "skee-an doo") is a small, single-edged knife worn as part of traditional Scottish Highland dress. It is carried in the top of the kilt hose (sock) on the dominant-hand side, with only the hilt visible.

Name and origins

The name is often explained as referring to a concealed ("black") weapon: a small utility knife, skean, was traditionally hidden under the arm or in clothing, and etiquette required a guest entering a friend's house to reveal it — hence its visible placement in the stocking. "Dubh" may alternatively describe the black bog-oak or ebony traditionally used for the handle.

Description

A typical sgian-dubh has a blade of about 7.5–10 cm (3–4 inches), often with file work or a serrated section on the spine, a leather scabbard, and a handle of carved wood, horn or synthetic material, frequently decorated with Celtic knotwork and a mounted stone. Military versions are issued with some Scottish regiments' dress uniforms.

Legality

Because it is part of national dress, the wearing of a sgian-dubh with Highland dress is specifically permitted under UK knife law (in Scotland, under the Criminal Law (Consolidation) (Scotland) Act 1995), although venues and airlines may still restrict it, and blunt or replica versions are commonly worn in such settings.
