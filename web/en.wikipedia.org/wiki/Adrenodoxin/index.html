Adrenodoxin (also known as ferredoxin 1, encoded in humans by the FDX1 gene) is a small soluble iron–sulfur protein found in the matrix of mitochondria. It functions as an electron shuttle, transferring single electrons from NADPH-dependent adrenodoxin reductase to mitochondrial cytochrome P450 enzymes involved in the synthesis of steroid hormones, bile acids, and vitamin D metabolites.

Structure

Adrenodoxin is a protein of about 14 kDa in its mature form. It binds a single [2Fe–2S] cluster coordinated by four cysteine residues, which cycles between the oxidized (Fe3+–Fe3+) and reduced (Fe3+–Fe2+) states as it accepts and donates one electron at a time. Its acidic surface residues mediate transient, electrostatic interactions with both its reductase and its P450 partners.

Function

Electrons flow from NADPH through the FAD-containing enzyme adrenodoxin reductase to adrenodoxin, which then reduces mitochondrial P450s including cholesterol side-chain cleavage enzyme (CYP11A1/P450scc), steroid 11β-hydroxylase (CYP11B1), aldosterone synthase (CYP11B2), and sterol 27-hydroxylase (CYP27A1). The first of these catalyses the rate-limiting step of all steroid hormone production in the adrenal cortex, gonads, and placenta.

Clinical relevance

Because adrenodoxin is indispensable for mitochondrial P450 activity, defects in the electron-transfer chain impair steroidogenesis. Adrenodoxin has also attracted interest in cancer biology, as FDX1 expression modulates sensitivity to copper-dependent cell death (cuproptosis).
