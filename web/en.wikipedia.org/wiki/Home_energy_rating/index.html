Home energy rating

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A home energy rating is an assessment of the energy efficiency of a residential building, usually expressed as a score, index or letter grade that allows the energy performance of different homes to be compared on a standard basis. Ratings are used by homebuyers, lenders, builders and policymakers to estimate energy costs and to identify cost-effective improvements.

Methods

A rating is typically produced by a trained energy assessor or rater who inspects the home and models its performance using simulation software. The assessment takes account of insulation levels in walls, floors and roofs, the efficiency of heating, cooling and water-heating systems, air leakage measured with a blower door, window performance, lighting and appliances. Some ratings are "asset ratings" based on the building itself under standard operating conditions; others are "operational ratings" based on measured energy use.

Ratings around the world

In the United States, the Home Energy Rating System (HERS) Index, administered by RESNET, is widely used: a reference home built to code scores 100, and a net-zero-energy home scores 0. In the European Union, Energy Performance Certificates grade buildings from A (most efficient) to G (least efficient) and are required when a property is sold or let. The United Kingdom uses Standard Assessment Procedure (SAP) ratings on Energy Performance Certificates. Australia uses the Nationwide House Energy Rating Scheme (NatHERS), which awards star ratings out of ten.

Uses

Ratings support energy-efficient mortgages, building-code compliance, green building certification schemes such as LEED, and retrofit programmes by identifying the measures — for example added insulation or a more efficient boiler — that offer the best value for money.
