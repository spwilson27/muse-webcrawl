Jack Foley (sound effects artist)

BornJack Donovan Foley, April 12, 1891, Yorkville, New York, U.S.
DiedNovember 9, 1967 (aged 76)
OccupationSound effects artist
Known forDeveloping the technique of Foley sound effects

Jack Donovan Foley (April 12, 1891 – November 9, 1967) was an American sound effects artist and the developer of the technique known as Foley, the reproduction of everyday sound effects that are added in post-production to enhance the audio quality of films, television and other media. The artists who practise the craft today are still called "Foley artists" in his honour.

Early life

Foley was born in Yorkville, New York, and grew up in the Seagate neighborhood of Coney Island. He worked in various jobs including as a harbour worker and a clerk before moving to California, where he worked in hardware and later as a reporter and editor for a local newspaper in Bishop, California.

Career

Foley became involved with the film business through location work and stunt doubling in silent films, and joined Universal Pictures. When the industry converted to sound in the late 1920s, he moved into sound work. For the 1929 film version of the musical Show Boat, Foley and a small crew synchronised live sound effects — footsteps, rustling clothes, doors, props — to the picture in a single take, because the technology of the day allowed only one audio track to be recorded at a time.

The Foley technique

Working from a specially equipped stage, Foley created sounds in real time while watching the projected film: walking in various shoes on different surfaces (gravel pits, wood, concrete), shaking and handling props, and swinging objects through the air. He is credited with many classic improvisations of the craft, such as using coconut shells for horses' hooves (a technique popularised by his work) and cornstarch in a leather pouch for the sound of walking on snow. He worked at Universal for decades, on films including Spartacus (1960) and The Great Impostor, and mentored a generation of effects artists.

Legacy

The Foley stage at Universal Studios and the profession's very name preserve his legacy. Foley remains one of the standard post-production crafts of filmmaking worldwide, largely unchanged in principle: effects are still performed live to picture on a Foley stage.
