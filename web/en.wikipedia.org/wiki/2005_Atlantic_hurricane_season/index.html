The 2005 Atlantic hurricane season was the most active Atlantic hurricane season in recorded history, producing 28 tropical or subtropical storms, 15 hurricanes, and seven major hurricanes. It was the first season to require the auxiliary Greek-letter naming list, and it caused damage estimated at more than $170 billion, making it the costliest season on record at the time.

Major storms

The season is remembered above all for Hurricane Katrina, which devastated New Orleans and the Mississippi Gulf Coast in August 2005, killing more than 1,800 people. Other destructive storms included Emily, Rita, Wilma — the most intense Atlantic hurricane ever recorded by pressure — and Stan, whose associated floods killed around 1,700 people in Central America. Four names (Dennis, Katrina, Rita, Stan, and Wilma) were retired after the season, a record.

Records

The 2005 season broke numerous records: most named storms, most hurricanes, most Category 5 hurricanes in a single season (four), and the earliest formation of several lettered storms. Activity continued into January 2006 with Tropical Storm Zeta.
