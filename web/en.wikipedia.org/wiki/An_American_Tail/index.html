An American Tail is a 1986 American animated musical family film directed by Don Bluth and produced by Steven Spielberg's Amblin Entertainment. It tells the story of Fievel Mousekewitz, a young Russian-Jewish mouse who emigrates with his family to the United States in 1885 and becomes separated from them in New York City.

Plot and themes

Fleeing cats in Russia, the Mousekewitz family sails to America, believing there are "no cats in America". Fievel is swept overboard, reaches New York in a bottle, and searches for his family with the help of friends including the pigeon Henri and the cat Tiger, while opposing the villainous cat Warren T. Rat. The film draws on the immigrant experience of Spielberg's own grandfather, after whom Fievel is named.

Reception and legacy

The film was a box-office success, grossing about $84 million worldwide, and its song "Somewhere Out There" won a Grammy and was nominated for an Academy Award. It spawned a franchise including An American Tail: Fievel Goes West (1991) and the television series Fievel's American Tails.
