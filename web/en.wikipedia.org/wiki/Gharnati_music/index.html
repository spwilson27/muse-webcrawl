Gharnati music

Gharnati (Arabic: الغرناطي) is a style of Andalusi classical music originating in Granada and preserved principally in western Algeria, especially in Tlemcen, and also in Oran and Sidi Bel Abbès. Together with the related schools of Algiers (sanâa) and Constantine (malouf), it forms the Algerian tradition of the Andalusi nuba.

Origins

The tradition descends from the music of Muslim Spain (al-Andalus). After the fall of Granada in 1492 and the subsequent expulsions of Muslims and Jews, refugees carried their repertoire across the Mediterranean to North African cities. Tlemcen, which had historic ties to Granada, became the principal home of the Gharnati school; its name itself means "from Granada" (gharnāṭī).

Form and performance

Like the other Andalusi schools, Gharnati is organised into nubas: suites of vocal and instrumental pieces in a single melodic mode (tab'), unfolding through movements of increasing tempo, traditionally beginning with an instrumental overture (touchia) and proceeding through m'saddar, b'tayhi, darj, insiraf, and khlas. Texts are classical Arabic poems, including muwashshah and zajal forms praising love, nature, and the divine. Ensembles feature instruments such as the oud, kwitra, rabab, mandoline, violin, derbouka, and tar. Distinguished 20th-century masters included Larbi Bensari and his son Redouane Bensari, and the tradition is maintained today by associations and festivals in Tlemcen and by the Algerian diaspora.
