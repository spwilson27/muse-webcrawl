Richard P. Stanley

Born: 23 June 1944, New York City
Alma mater: California Institute of Technology; Harvard University
Known for: Algebraic combinatorics
Awards: Leroy P. Steele Prize (2001)

Richard Peter Stanley (born 23 June 1944) is an American mathematician, widely regarded as the leading figure in modern algebraic combinatorics. He is the Norman Levinson Professor Emeritus of Applied Mathematics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology.

Education and career

Stanley received his B.S. from the California Institute of Technology in 1966 and his Ph.D. from Harvard University in 1971, under the supervision of Gian-Carlo Rota. He joined the MIT faculty in 1973. He has supervised more than fifty doctoral students, including Ira Gessel and Miklós Bóna.

Contributions

Stanley's two-volume work Enumerative Combinatorics is the standard reference for counting methods in combinatorics. He made fundamental contributions to the theory of symmetric functions, posets, and Ehrhart theory, including his proof that the Dehn–Sommerville equations imply the g-theorem ingredients for simplicial polytopes and his work on order polytopes and hook-length formulas for trees.

Awards

He received the Leroy P. Steele Prize for Mathematical Exposition in 2001 and was elected to the National Academy of Sciences in 1995.
