CHIPS and Science Act

The CHIPS and Science Act is a U.S. federal statute enacted by the 117th United States Congress and signed into law by President Joe Biden on August 9, 2022. The act authorizes roughly $280 billion in new funding to boost domestic research and manufacturing of semiconductors in the United States, for which it appropriates $52.7 billion. The act includes $39 billion in subsidies for chip manufacturing on U.S. soil along with 25% investment tax credits for costs of manufacturing equipment, $13 billion for semiconductor research and workforce training, and $500 million for international information and communication technology security.

Background

The act was passed in the context of a global semiconductor shortage beginning in 2020 and growing concern about the concentration of advanced chip manufacturing in East Asia, particularly Taiwan. The United States' share of global semiconductor manufacturing capacity had fallen from about 37 percent in 1990 to around 12 percent by 2020, and the country manufactured essentially none of the most advanced logic chips domestically.

Provisions

Division A, the CHIPS Act of 2022, created the CHIPS for America Fund administered by the Department of Commerce, funding incentives for fabrication facilities ("fabs"), assembly, testing and packaging, and the National Semiconductor Technology Center. "Guardrail" provisions prohibit recipients of subsidies from expanding advanced semiconductor manufacturing in China and other countries of concern for ten years.

Division B, the Research and Innovation Act, authorized major funding increases for the National Science Foundation (including a new Directorate for Technology, Innovation and Partnerships), the Department of Energy's Office of Science, and NIST, and authorized the designation of regional technology hubs.

Awards and impact

Major grant awards under the act went to companies including Intel, TSMC Arizona, Samsung, Micron, GlobalFoundries, and others, anchoring new fabs in Arizona, Ohio, New York, Texas, and elsewhere. The Commerce Department announced its final major award decisions through late 2024. Supporters credit the act with catalyzing hundreds of billions of dollars in announced private semiconductor investment; critics have raised concerns about implementation pace, cost, and industrial policy.
