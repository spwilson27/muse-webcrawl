Liaquat Ali Khan

Liaquat Ali Khan

Born: 1 October 1895, Karnal, British India

Died: 16 October 1951, Rawalpindi, Pakistan

Office: 1st Prime Minister of Pakistan (1947–1951)

Political party: Muslim League

Liaquat Ali Khan (1 October 1895 – 16 October 1951) was a Pakistani lawyer, politician and statesman who served as the first prime minister of Pakistan from 1947 until his assassination in 1951. He was one of the leading figures of the Pakistan Movement and a close associate of Muhammad Ali Jinnah, the founder of Pakistan. He is revered in Pakistan as Quaid-e-Millat ("Leader of the Nation") and Shaheed-e-Millat ("Martyr of the Nation").

Early life and education

Liaquat Ali Khan was born in Karnal, in present-day Haryana, into a wealthy Muslim family of landowners. He was educated at Aligarh Muslim University and later at Exeter College, Oxford, and was called to the bar at the Inner Temple in London. After returning to India he entered politics, joining the All-India Muslim League in 1923 and winning election to the provincial legislature of the United Provinces and later to the Central Legislative Assembly.

Role in the Pakistan Movement

As general secretary of the Muslim League and Jinnah's principal lieutenant, Liaquat played a central role in organising the party and in the negotiations that led to the Partition of India. In 1946 he became finance member in the interim government of India, where his 1947 budget was noted for its proposals affecting the business community. Upon independence on 14 August 1947, he became Pakistan's first prime minister.

Prime Ministership

As prime minister, Liaquat confronted the immense problems of the new state: the settlement of millions of refugees, the Kashmir dispute and war with India, and the framing of a constitution. His government passed the Objectives Resolution in 1949, which remains a foundational document of Pakistan's constitutional order. In foreign policy he steered Pakistan toward the Western camp, making a notable state visit to the United States in 1950.

Assassination

On 16 October 1951, Liaquat Ali Khan was shot and killed while addressing a public meeting of the Muslim League at Company Bagh in Rawalpindi — the park was later renamed Liaquat Bagh in his memory. His assassin, Said Akbar, was shot dead at the scene, and the motives behind the killing remain a subject of speculation. Liaquat is buried at Mazar-e-Quaid in Karachi, beside Jinnah.
