Lazy Susan

A Lazy Susan is a turntable (rotating tray) placed on a table or countertop to aid in distributing food. Lazy Susans may be made from a variety of materials but are usually glass, wood, or plastic. They are circular and placed in the center of a table to share dishes easily among diners.

History

Rotating serving trays existed in 18th-century England in the form of the "dumbwaiter", a revolving or tiered tray used so that servants were not needed at table. The term "lazy Susan" appears in American sources in the early 20th century; rotating trays became widely popular in Chinese restaurants in the United States in the 1960s, where they remain a fixture of round banquet tables used for sharing dishes.

Uses

Beyond dining, smaller turntables are used in kitchen cabinets and refrigerators to bring items at the back within reach, and in crafts and workshops for painting and assembling objects from all sides.
