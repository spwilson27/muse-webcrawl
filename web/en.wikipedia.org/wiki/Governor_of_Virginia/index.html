Governor of Virginia

The Governor of the Commonwealth of Virginia is the head of government of the U.S. state of Virginia and the commander-in-chief of the state's military forces. The governor has a duty to enforce state laws and the power to either approve or veto bills passed by the Virginia General Assembly, to convene the legislature, and to grant pardons, except in cases of impeachment.

Virginia's governors are elected to four-year terms and are unique in the United States in being barred from serving consecutive terms — a governor may run again after a term out of office. The line of succession and the office date to the colonial era; Patrick Henry was the first governor of the independent Commonwealth in 1776, and eight U.S. presidents were born in Virginia, several having served as governor.

The governor's official residence is the Executive Mansion in Richmond, the oldest continuously occupied governor's residence in the United States. The current holder of the office is listed in the state constitution as chief executive alongside separately elected lieutenant governor and attorney general.
