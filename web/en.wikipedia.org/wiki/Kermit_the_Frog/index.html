Kermit the Frog

Kermit the Frog is a Muppet character created by puppeteer Jim Henson, who performed him from his debut in 1955 until his death in 1990. An anthropomorphic frog, Kermit is the central character and de facto leader of the Muppets, serving as the mild-mannered showrunner of The Muppet Show amid the chaos of the other characters, and is one of the best-loved puppet characters in the world.

History

Kermit first appeared on Henson's local television show Sam and Friends in Washington, D.C., in 1955; the original puppet was made from Henson's mother's old coat and ping-pong balls for eyes, and was not initially a frog at all. He went on to appear on Sesame Street from 1969, as a news reporter and in sketches such as the song "Bein' Green". As host of The Muppet Show (1976–1981) he opened with the cry "It's the Muppet Show!", managed backstage mayhem, fended off Miss Piggy's romantic advances, and starred in The Muppet Movie (1979), in which he sang "Rainbow Connection", later inducted into the National Recording Registry.

Later performers and legacy

After Henson's death, Steve Whitmire performed Kermit from 1990 until 2016; Matt Vogel took over in 2017. Kermit has made countless guest appearances, addressed the Oxford Union, received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame (as part of the Muppets), and in 2005 his original puppet form was donated to the Smithsonian Institution. His image and catchphrases remain prominent in popular culture and internet memes.
