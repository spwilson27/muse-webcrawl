Ritchie Valens (born Richard Steven Valenzuela; 13 May 1941 – 3 February 1959) was an American guitarist, singer and songwriter. A pioneering figure of rock and roll and the Chicano rock movement, he had a career lasting only eight months before dying at 17 in the plane crash that also killed Buddy Holly and The Big Bopper — the event later immortalised as "The Day the Music Died". His hits include "La Bamba", "Come On, Let's Go" and "Donna".

Early life

Valens was born in Pacoima, in Los Angeles's San Fernando Valley, to Mexican immigrant parents, and grew up surrounded by mariachi, flamenco and R&B music. He taught himself guitar — famously building his first instrument and playing left-handed despite being left-handed in a way that made early playing awkward — and joined a local band, the Silhouettes, while at San Fernando High School. A mid-air collision of two airliners over Pacoima in 1957, which he witnessed the aftermath of, left him with a lasting fear of flying.

Career

Discovered by Del-Fi Records owner Bob Keane, Valens signed in May 1958 and shortened his name at Keane's suggestion. His first single, "Come On, Let's Go", was a hit; the follow-up paired the ballad "Donna", written for his high-school girlfriend Donna Ludwig, with "La Bamba", a rock arrangement of a traditional Mexican son jarocho wedding song from Veracruz. Though Valens' Spanish was limited and he initially resisted recording it, "La Bamba" became his signature song and the first Spanish-language rock hit. He toured nationally, appeared in the film Go, Johnny, Go!, and recorded tracks such as "We Belong Together" and "Bonny Moronie".

Death and legacy

In February 1959 Valens was touring the Midwest on the Winter Dance Party tour with Holly and J.P. "The Big Bopper" Richardson. After the show at Clear Lake, Iowa, on 2 February, the three chartered a small plane to Fargo, North Dakota; it crashed shortly after takeoff in bad weather, killing all aboard. Valens was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2001. His life was portrayed by Lou Diamond Phillips in the 1987 biopic La Bamba, which carried his music to a new generation; Los Lobos' cover of "La Bamba" from the film reached number one. His star stands on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, and he is regarded as the first Mexican-American rock star.
