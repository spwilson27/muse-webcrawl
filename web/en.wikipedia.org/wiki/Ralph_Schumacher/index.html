Ralf Schumacher (born 30 June 1975) is a German former racing driver and broadcaster, best known for his career in Formula One from 1997 to 2007. He is the younger brother of seven-time Formula One World Champion Michael Schumacher. Ralf won six Grands Prix and finished on the podium 27 times.

Racing career

Early career

Schumacher began karting as a child at his parents' track in Kerpen. He won the German Junior Kart Championship and finished runner-up in the German Formula 3 Championship in 1995. In 1996 he won the Formula Nippon championship in Japan and the Macau Grand Prix, attracting the attention of Formula One teams.

Formula One

He made his debut with Jordan in 1997, scoring a podium at the Argentine Grand Prix in only his third race. After two seasons with Jordan, he moved to Williams in 1999 and remained there until 2004. His most successful years were 2001, when he took his first victories at Imola, Montreal and Hockenheim, and 2002–2003, when he added wins at Sepang, the Nürburgring and Magny-Cours. A heavy crash during testing at Indianapolis in 2004 fractured his spine vertebrae and kept him out for several races. From 2005 to 2007 he raced for Toyota, achieving podium finishes but no wins before leaving Formula One.

DTM and later work

From 2008 to 2012 Schumacher competed in the Deutsche Tourenwagen Masters (DTM) for Mercedes-Benz. After retiring from driving, he became a television pundit for Sky Sport Germany, a kart-team and junior-racing team owner (KSM Schumacher Racing Team), and a mentor to young drivers including his son David.

Personal life

Schumacher was formerly married to Cora-Caroline Brinkmann, with whom he has a son, David. In July 2024 he publicly came out as gay, announcing his relationship with his partner Étienne Bousquet-Cassagne, a moment widely covered in international media.
