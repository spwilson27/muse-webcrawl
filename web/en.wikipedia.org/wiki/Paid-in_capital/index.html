Paid-in capital

Paid-in capital (also called contributed capital) is the amount of capital "paid in" by investors during common or preferred stock issuances, including the par value of the shares themselves and amounts received in excess of par. It is a component of shareholders' equity on a company's balance sheet.

Components

Paid-in capital is traditionally divided into two accounts:

  • Common stock (or capital stock) — the par or stated value of shares issued.
  • Additional paid-in capital (APIC) — amounts paid by investors above par value, also called capital in excess of par or share premium.

When shares are issued, cash (or other assets) is debited and the common stock account is credited for the par value, with the remainder credited to APIC. Because many modern shares have minimal or no par value, most of the contributed amount often sits in APIC.

Distinction from earned capital

Paid-in capital represents funds contributed by shareholders, as opposed to earned capital (retained earnings), which accumulates from profitable operations. Together with retained earnings and accumulated other comprehensive income, less treasury stock, it makes up total shareholders' equity. Paid-in capital does not change with secondary-market trading of the shares; only transactions between the company and investors affect it.
