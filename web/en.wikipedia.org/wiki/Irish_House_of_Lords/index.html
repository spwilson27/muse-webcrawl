Irish House of Lords

The Irish House of Lords was the upper house of the Parliament of Ireland, which existed from medieval times until 1800. It was modelled on the House of Lords of England and later of Great Britain, and its membership consisted of members of the Peerage of Ireland together with a small number of bishops of the Church of Ireland.

History

The Lords met in Parliament House on College Green in Dublin, in the first purpose-built parliament house chamber in Europe, built in the 1730s. Like its English counterpart, the Irish Lords held appellate judicial functions and reviewed legislation passed by the Irish House of Commons.

The house's powers were curtailed by Poynings' Law and the Declaratory Act 1719. It was abolished by the Acts of Union 1800, which united the Kingdom of Ireland with Great Britain; thereafter Irish peers elected 32 representative peers and bishops to sit in the United Kingdom House of Lords.

Composition

Temporal peers included dukes, marquesses, earls, viscounts and barons of the Peerage of Ireland. The Lords Spiritual were the Church of Ireland archbishops and bishops sitting in rotation.
