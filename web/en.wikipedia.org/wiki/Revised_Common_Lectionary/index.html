The Revised Common Lectionary (RCL) is a three-year cycle of Bible readings appointed for use in Christian worship on Sundays and festivals, with daily readings also provided. It was developed by the Consultation on Common Texts, an ecumenical group of liturgical scholars largely from North America, and published in 1992. It builds on the Roman Catholic Ordo Lectionum Missae of 1969 and the earlier Common Lectionary.

Structure

Each Sunday ordinarily has four readings: a passage from the Old Testament (or Acts during Eastertide), a Psalm, a reading from an Epistle or Revelation, and a Gospel reading. The three years of the cycle are designated Year A, Year B and Year C, focusing mainly on the Gospels of Matthew, Mark and Luke respectively, with readings from John interspersed in all three years, especially in Lent and Easter.

Use

The RCL is used, in adapted forms, by many mainline Protestant denominations — including the Episcopal Church, the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, the United Methodist Church, the Presbyterian Church (USA), the United Church of Christ, the Church of England (as the principal service lectionary alongside the Book of Common Prayer cycle), the Anglican Church of Canada and the Uniting Church in Australia — as well as some Baptist and other congregations. During Ordinary Time it offers two "tracks" of Old Testament readings: a semi-continuous track and a complementary track pairing thematically with the Gospel.
