Hemel Hempstead is a town in the Borough of Dacorum in Hertfordshire, England, located about 24 miles (39 km) northwest of central London. It was developed as a New Town after the Second World War and today combines a historic old town with a planned modern centre.

History

The settlement dates to Saxon times and is recorded in the Domesday Book. The old town grew around St Mary's Church, built in the 12th century with a distinctive spire, and along a characterful High Street. In 1539 Henry VIII granted the town a charter. The arrival of the Grand Union Canal and later the railway on the West Coast Main Line encouraged modest growth, and industries such as paper making (at nearby Apsley, where John Dickinson developed his mills) became important.

New Town

Hemel Hempstead was designated a New Town in 1947 under the New Towns Act to relieve overcrowding in London. The new neighbourhoods — including Adeyfield, Bennetts End, Chaulden, Gadebridge, Leverstock Green, and Warners End — were built around a new town centre at Marlowes with water gardens along the River Gade. The town's "Magic Roundabout" (the Plough Roundabout), where traffic circulates around six mini-roundabouts, is a notable feature of the road layout.

2005 Buncefield fire

On 11 December 2005, a major explosion and fire at the Buncefield oil depot on the outskirts of the town caused one of the largest peacetime fires in Britain, damaging homes and businesses and injuring dozens of people, though remarkably no one was killed.

Transport and economy

The town is served by Hemel Hempstead railway station on the West Coast Main Line and lies close to the M1 motorway. It is a significant employment centre for logistics, information technology, and services.
