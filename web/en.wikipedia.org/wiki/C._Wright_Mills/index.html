C. Wright Mills (1916–1962).

Charles Wright Mills (28 August 1916 – 20 March 1962) was an American sociologist and professor at Columbia University, known for his analyses of power in the United States and for The Sociological Imagination (1959), one of the most influential methodological manifestos in sociology. His work challenged both orthodox Marxism and the pluralist consensus of post-war American political science.

Major works

Mills's trilogy on American stratification comprised The New Men of Power (1948), on labour leaders; White Collar (1951), a classic study of the new middle classes; and The Power Elite (1956), which argued that the United States is dominated by an interlocking directorate of corporate executives, military leaders and political officials whose shared backgrounds and institutional interests align their decisions. The book popularised analysis of the "military-industrial complex," a phrase President Eisenhower used in his 1961 farewell address. In The Sociological Imagination Mills urged scholars to connect "the personal troubles of milieu" with "the public issues of social structure," criticising both grand theory and abstracted empiricism.

Political engagement

An intellectual of the New Left avant la lettre, Mills wrote the widely circulated "Letter to the New Left" (1960), supported the Cuban Revolution in Listen, Yankee (1960), and became a hero to 1960s student movements shortly after his death from a heart attack in 1962. His ideas stand in a line of elite theory descending in part from Vilfredo Pareto, though Mills recast elite dominance as a product of institutional position rather than psychological traits.

Legacy

The Sociological Imagination was ranked among the most influential sociology books of the twentieth century in an International Sociological Association survey, and the terms "power elite" and "sociological imagination" have entered general usage. The C. Wright Mills Award of the Society for the Study of Social Problems honours books in his tradition of engaged scholarship.
