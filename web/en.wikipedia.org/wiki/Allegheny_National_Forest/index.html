The Allegheny National Forest is a United States National Forest in northwestern Pennsylvania, about 100 miles (160 km) northeast of Pittsburgh. It covers 513,175 acres of Elk, Forest, McKean and Warren counties and is the only national forest in Pennsylvania.

History

The forest was established in 1923 on land that had been almost completely cleared by logging, tanning and oil extraction in the previous century — the so-called "Pennsylvania Desert" of stump fields. Under Forest Service management the cut-over lands were replanted and allowed to regenerate, producing today's mature second-growth forest of black cherry, Allegheny hardwoods and hemlock. Black cherry from the Allegheny plateau is prized worldwide for furniture veneer.

Geography and recreation

The forest surrounds much of the Allegheny Reservoir, created by Kinzua Dam on the Allegheny River (completed 1965), which flooded lands of the Seneca Nation and remains controversial. Two wilderness areas, Hickory Creek and Allegheny Islands, are protected within the forest, and a tract of old-growth white pine and hemlock survives at Hearts Content Scenic Area. Recreation includes camping, fishing, boating, hunting, snowmobiling and over 200 miles of hiking trails, while the forest also supports timber production, oil and gas wells, and wildlife habitat.
