Charles University (Czech: Univerzita Karlova, Latin: Universitas Carolina; German: Karls-Universität) is a public research university in Prague, Czech Republic. Founded in 1348 by King and later Emperor Charles IV, it was the first university established in Central Europe east of Italy and north of the Alps, and is the oldest and largest university in the Czech Republic today.

History

Modelled on the universities of Bologna and Paris, Charles University opened with four faculties: theology, law, medicine and the arts. Its elector status was transformed by the Decree of Kutná Hora (1409), issued in the context of the reform movement of Jan Hus, who served as rector; the resulting exodus of German masters led to the founding of Leipzig University. During the Hussite wars and the Thirty Years' War the university declined; its merger with the Jesuit Clementinum college in 1654 created the Charles-Ferdinand University. In 1882 the university split into Czech and German institutions, reunified in 1920 under the name Charles University. Closed by the Nazi occupation in 1939 (following the student protests commemorated on International Students' Day, 17 November), it reopened in 1945.

Organisation

The university comprises 17 faculties, including three faculties of medicine in Prague, medical faculties in Plzeň and Hradec Králové, faculties of law, arts, science, mathematics and physics, education, social sciences, humanities, physical education and sport, pharmacy, and theology faculties (Catholic, Protestant and Hussite). Its historic seat is the Carolinum in Prague's Old Town.

Notable people

Alumni and faculty include Jan Hus, Franz Kafka, Nikola Tesla (briefly), Max Planck and Albert Einstein (professors at the German university), chemist Jaroslav Heyrovský (Nobel laureate), writer Karel Čapek, and former president Václav Havel (honorary).
