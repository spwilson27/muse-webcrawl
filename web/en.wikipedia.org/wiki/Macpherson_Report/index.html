Macpherson Report

The Macpherson Report is the published outcome of the public inquiry, chaired by Sir William Macpherson of Cluny, into the circumstances of the death of Stephen Lawrence, a Black British teenager murdered in a racially motivated attack in Eltham, south-east London, on 22 April 1993. The report was published in February 1999 and is widely regarded as one of the most significant documents in the modern history of British policing and race relations.

Background

The original Metropolitan Police investigation failed to secure convictions, and a private prosecution brought by Lawrence's family collapsed in 1996. Home Secretary Jack Straw ordered a judicial inquiry in July 1997, which held hearings through 1998 and heard evidence about the conduct of the first investigation, the treatment of the Lawrence family, and the wider relationship between the police and minority ethnic communities.

Findings

The report concluded that the investigation had been "marred by a combination of professional incompetence, institutional racism and a failure of leadership by senior officers". It defined institutional racism as "the collective failure of an organisation to provide an appropriate and professional service to people because of their colour, culture, or ethnic origin", detectable in "processes, attitudes and behaviour which amount to discrimination through unwitting prejudice, ignorance, thoughtlessness and racist stereotyping". It made 70 recommendations covering racist crime recording, stop and search, police training, accountability and the application of race relations legislation to the police.

Impact

The report prompted reforms including the Race Relations (Amendment) Act 2000, new standards for recording and investigating racist incidents, and changes to the law of double jeopardy, which ultimately allowed the retrial and conviction of two of the suspects, Gary Dobson and David Norris, in 2012. Its definition of institutional racism has remained influential — and periodically contested — in British public debate.
