Viral infection

A viral infection is the proliferation of a harmful virus inside the body. Unlike bacterial infections, viral infections cannot be treated with antibiotics. Symptoms occur due to cell damage, tissue destruction, and the immune response to the virus. Viruses infect all types of life forms, from animals and plants to microorganisms, including bacteria and archaea.

Mechanism

A virus enters the body through routes such as inhalation, ingestion, insect bites or bodily fluids, binds to receptors on host cells, and enters the cell. Inside, it hijacks the cell's machinery to replicate its genetic material and produce viral proteins, assembling new virions that are released — often destroying the host cell — to infect neighbouring cells. The immune system responds with inflammation, fever, antibodies and cytotoxic T cells that destroy infected cells.

Examples

Common viral infections include influenza, the common cold (usually rhinoviruses), measles, mumps, rubella, chickenpox, viral gastroenteritis, hepatitis, herpes simplex, HIV, and COVID-19. Some viruses establish latency (as herpesviruses do), while others persist chronically.

Treatment and prevention

Most viral infections are managed with supportive care while the immune system clears the virus. Specific antiviral drugs exist for influenza, HIV, herpes, hepatitis B and C, and SARS-CoV-2. Vaccination is the most effective preventive measure for many viral diseases and has eradicated smallpox entirely. Hygiene measures, vector control and safe practices reduce transmission.
