Dyckman House

Dyckman House Museum
Location: 4881 Broadway, Inwood, Manhattan, New York City
Built: c. 1785
Status: New York City Landmark; National Historic Landmark

The Dyckman House, now the Dyckman Farmhouse Museum, is the oldest remaining farmhouse on Manhattan island, a Dutch Colonial-style house built around 1785 on a site at present-day Broadway and 204th Street in Inwood. It is one of the few surviving reminders of Manhattan's rural past.

History

The Dyckman family owned extensive farmland in northern Manhattan from the 1660s. The original farmhouse was burned during the American Revolutionary War, when the area was occupied by British and Hessian troops; William Dyckman built the current house shortly after the war ended. The gambrel-roofed house, with its fieldstone foundation, brick and wood construction, and spring-fed kitchen in the basement, served generations of the family until the early 20th century.

Museum

In 1915–16, two Dyckman daughters purchased and restored the deteriorating house and donated it to the City of New York as a museum of Dutch and early American life. Furnished with period antiques, portraits, and a relic garden including a Hessian hut from Revolutionary encampments, it opened to the public in 1916 and remains operated as a historic house museum.
