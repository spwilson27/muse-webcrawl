The Caucasus is a region spanning Eastern Europe and Western Asia, located between the Black Sea and the Caspian Sea and mainly occupied by Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, and parts of Southern Russia. It is dominated by the Caucasus Mountains, whose main ridge — the Greater Caucasus — is often taken as the continental boundary between Europe and Asia, and includes Mount Elbrus, the highest peak in Europe.

Geography

The Greater Caucasus range runs some 1,100 km from the Taman Peninsula on the Black Sea to the Absheron Peninsula on the Caspian, with numerous peaks above 5,000 m. South of it lie the Armenian Highlands and the Lesser Caucasus. The region is divided into the North Caucasus, within Russia, and the South Caucasus, comprising Georgia, Armenia, and Azerbaijan. Great rivers, including the Kuban, Terek, Kura, and Aras, drain from the mountains to the two seas.

Peoples and languages

The Caucasus is one of the most linguistically diverse regions on Earth, sometimes called a "mountain of tongues". It is home to three indigenous language families found nowhere else — Kartvelian (including Georgian), Northeast Caucasian (including Chechen and Avar), and Northwest Caucasian (including Circassian and Abkhaz) — alongside Indo-European languages such as Armenian, Ossetian, and Russian, and Turkic languages such as Azerbaijani.

History

Ancient kingdoms including Colchis, Iberia, and Armenia flourished here; Armenia and Georgia were among the earliest states to adopt Christianity, in the early 4th century. Contested for centuries by Roman, Persian, Arab, Mongol, Ottoman, and Russian empires, the region was incorporated into the Russian Empire in the 19th century after the long Caucasian War, became part of the Soviet Union in the 20th, and saw the South Caucasus states regain independence in 1991 — accompanied by conflicts in Nagorno-Karabakh, Abkhazia, South Ossetia, and Chechnya.
