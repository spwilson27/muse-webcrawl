History of the Los Angeles Angels

The history of the Los Angeles Angels begins in 1961, when the franchise was founded as one of the American League's first expansion teams. The club was owned by entertainer Gene Autry, who had originally sought broadcasting rights and instead purchased the team.

Early years (1961–1969)

The original Los Angeles Angels played at Wrigley Field in Los Angeles in 1961, then shared Dodger Stadium (called Chavez Ravine for Angels games) from 1962 to 1965. In 1966 the team moved to Anaheim in Orange County and became the California Angels, playing at the new Anaheim Stadium.

California Angels (1970–1996)

Led by stars such as Nolan Ryan, who threw four of his record seven no-hitters with the club, the Angels won their first division title in 1979 under manager Jim Fregosi. They reached the playoffs again in 1982 and 1986, when they came within one strike of the World Series before losing the American League Championship Series to the Boston Red Sox.

Anaheim Angels and the 2002 championship

The Walt Disney Company acquired a controlling interest in 1996 and renamed the team the Anaheim Angels. Under manager Mike Scioscia, the 2002 Angels won 99 games and, as a wild card, defeated the New York Yankees and Minnesota Twins before beating the San Francisco Giants in a seven-game World Series — the franchise's first and, to date, only championship. The Rally Monkey became a popular symbol during that run.

Los Angeles Angels of Anaheim and later years

Businessman Arte Moreno bought the team in 2003, the first Mexican-American owner of a major U.S. sports franchise. In 2005 the club renamed itself the Los Angeles Angels of Anaheim, a name contested by the city of Anaheim; the "of Anaheim" suffix was dropped in 2016. The 2010s featured two-way star Shohei Ohtani (2018–2023) and perennial MVP candidate Mike Trout, though playoff appearances remained rare.
