Geothermal heat pump

A geothermal heat pump (or ground-source heat pump, GSHP) is a heating and cooling system that transfers heat to or from the ground, exploiting the stable underground temperature a few metres below the surface.

Geothermal heat pump
Type: HVAC system
Uses: Space heating, cooling, hot water
Typical COP: 3–6

How it works

A fluid circulates through buried pipes (a ground loop — horizontal trenches or vertical boreholes) exchanging heat with the earth. A vapour-compression refrigeration cycle, like that in a refrigerator, concentrates the heat indoors in winter and reverses in summer. Because it moves heat rather than generating it, a GSHP delivers 3–6 units of heat per unit of electricity (coefficient of performance).

Advantages and costs

Systems have high installation costs due to drilling or excavation but low running costs and emissions, especially with renewable electricity. Ground conditions, available land and local geology affect design.

History

Lord Kelvin described heat pumps in 1852; the first ground-source systems appeared in the 1940s, with wider adoption after the 1970s energy crises.
