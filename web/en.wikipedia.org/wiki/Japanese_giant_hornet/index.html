Japanese giant hornet

Vespa mandarinia japonica
Family: Vespidae
Range: Japan
Body length: up to ~40 mm

The Japanese giant hornet (Vespa mandarinia japonica) is a subspecies of the Asian giant hornet, the world's largest hornet, native to Japan where it is known as the ōsuzumebachi ("giant sparrow bee").

Description

Queens can exceed 40 mm in body length with a wingspan of about 7 cm. Workers are smaller. The hornet has a distinctive orange head and thorax with a dark striped abdomen and a stinger about 6 mm long.

Behaviour

Japanese giant hornets build underground nests and prey heavily on other insects, including honey bees. A small group of hornets can destroy an entire hive of European honey bees; Japanese honey bees defend themselves by forming a "heat ball" around an intruding scout, raising the temperature enough to kill it. The hornets are also valued in Japan as food and in traditional drinks in some rural areas.

Venom

The venom is potent and the sting painful; dozens of people in Japan die each year from anaphylactic reactions to stings.
