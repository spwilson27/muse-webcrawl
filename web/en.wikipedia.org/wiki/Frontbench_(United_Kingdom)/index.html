Frontbench (United Kingdom)

Frontbench
LocationPalace of Westminster
Applies toHouse of Commons; House of Lords

In the United Kingdom, a frontbench is the collective term for the senior members of a political party in Parliament: government ministers sit on the government front bench in the House of Commons, while their counterparts in the largest opposition party — the Official Opposition frontbench, or shadow cabinet — sit opposite them. Members who occupy these seats are called frontbenchers; all other members of their parties are backbenchers.

Government frontbench

The government frontbench is composed of the Prime Minister, members of the Cabinet, and other ministers. In the Commons chamber, the front bench is traditionally separated from the opposition front bench by a distance of two sword-lengths, a feature often cited as contributing to the chamber's adversarial style of debate.

Opposition frontbenches

The Official Opposition frontbench (the shadow cabinet) is formed by the Leader of the Opposition and shadow ministers who mirror government portfolios. Smaller parties in the Commons also maintain frontbench teams of spokespersons, sometimes called frontbench teams rather than shadow cabinets.
