Littlewood–Richardson rule

In mathematics, the Littlewood–Richardson rule is a combinatorial description of the coefficients that arise when decomposing a product of two Schur functions into a sum of Schur functions. These coefficients, the Littlewood–Richardson coefficients cνλμ, are non-negative integers that also describe the decomposition of tensor products of irreducible representations of general linear groups, and appear in the cohomology of Grassmannians and the theory of symmetric functions.

The rule was stated by Dudley Littlewood and Archibald Richardson in 1934, though their original proof was incomplete and contained a gap; the first complete proofs were given in the 1970s by Gordon James and by Robert Steinberg's circle of work, with the standard modern proof due to Marcel-Paul Schützenberger and others using the combinatorics of Young tableaux.

Statement

Let λ and μ be partitions. The Schur functions multiply according to

sλ · sμ = Σν cνλμ sν

The Littlewood–Richardson coefficient cνλμ equals the number of skew semistandard Young tableaux of shape ν/λ and content μ whose reading word is a lattice word (also called a ballot sequence): when the entries of the tableau are read in the prescribed order, at every point the number of 1s read is at least the number of 2s, the number of 2s is at least the number of 3s, and so on. Such tableaux are called Littlewood–Richardson tableaux.

Example

For λ = (2,1) and μ = (2,1), the coefficient of s(3,2,1) in the product s(2,1)·s(2,1) is 2: exactly two skew tableaux of shape (3,2,1)/(2,1) with content (2,1) have lattice reading words. In representation-theoretic language, this says the representation (3,2,1) occurs with multiplicity 2 in the tensor product of two copies of the representation (2,1).

Applications

  • Decomposition of tensor products of polynomial representations of GLn.
  • Intersection theory on Grassmannians: the coefficients give the structure constants of the cohomology ring in the basis of Schubert classes.
  • Horn's problem on eigenvalues of sums of Hermitian matrices, whose solution (the saturation theorem of Knutson and Tao) is phrased in terms of these coefficients.
  • Connections with the Robinson–Schensted–Knuth correspondence and the plactic monoid.
