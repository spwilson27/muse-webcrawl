The Democratic Alliance of Hungarians in Romania (Hungarian: Romániai Magyar Demokrata Szövetség, RMDSZ; Romanian: Uniunea Democrată Maghiară din România, UDMR) is a political party in Romania representing the Hungarian minority, the country's largest ethnic minority, concentrated mainly in Transylvania and especially in the Székely Land counties of Harghita, Covasna and Mureș. Founded on 25 December 1989, days after the fall of Nicolae Ceaușescu, it describes itself as an alliance of organisations rather than a conventional party and has been represented in the Romanian parliament continuously since 1990.

History and role in government

The UDMR has typically won around 5–7% of the national vote, closely tracking the Hungarian share of Romania's population (about 6%). Its pivotal position has made it a frequent coalition partner: it participated in governments led by the Democratic Convention (1996–2000), supported or joined PSD- and PNL-led cabinets in the 2000s and 2010s, and entered the PSD–PNL grand coalition in 2021, holding ministerial portfolios, before leaving government in 2023. It re-entered government following the 2024 elections. The party's long-serving president Béla Markó (1993–2011) was succeeded by Hunor Kelemen in 2011.

Policies

The UDMR's principal aims are the protection and expansion of Hungarian minority rights in Romania: mother-tongue education at all levels (including a long-running campaign to restore an independent Hungarian-language Babeș-Bolyai University division and later support for Sapientia University), the use of Hungarian in local administration where the minority exceeds 20% of the population, cultural autonomy, and territorial autonomy for the Székely Land — a demand that has remained politically contentious and unrealised. Economically and socially the party is generally centre-right and pro-European, and is a member of the European People's Party. Internally it contains organised platforms spanning liberal, Christian-democratic and social-democratic tendencies.

Relations and criticism

The party maintains close ties with Hungary's governing Fidesz party and the Hungarian government, which funds Hungarian cultural and educational institutions in Transylvania. Romanian nationalist critics have periodically accused the UDMR of separatism, while rival Hungarian organisations in Romania have criticised it as too willing to compromise with Bucharest governments. It is generally credited with channelling ethnic Hungarian politics into parliamentary and coalition processes, contributing to the absence of large-scale ethnic conflict in Transylvania since the March 1990 Târgu Mureș clashes.
