VisiCalc

VisiCalc ("visible calculator") is the first computer spreadsheet program for personal computers. Released in 1979 for the Apple II by VisiCorp, it is widely credited as the first "killer application" of the personal computer era — software so compelling that people bought the hardware just to run it. It was conceived by Dan Bricklin, refined by Bob Frankston, developed by Software Arts, and published by Personal Software Inc., later named VisiCorp.

Origins

Bricklin, a Harvard Business School student, imagined a "word processor for numbers": business students' case studies required laborious recalculation whenever an assumption changed, and he envisioned an electronic ledger sheet that would update automatically. He built a prototype on a Harvard minicomputer, then implemented the product with Frankston at Software Arts, founded in January 1979. Written in assembly language, the first version fit in about 27 KB and displayed a grid of cells referenced by row-and-column coordinates (A1 notation, later conventions aside), with automatic recalculation, formulas, and replication of formulas with relative references.

Impact

VisiCalc sold for about $100 and went on to sell over 700,000 copies in six years, more than a million in total. It is often credited with transforming the microcomputer from a hobbyist toy into a business tool; Steve Jobs said VisiCalc "propelled the Apple II to the success it achieved more than any other single event". It effectively created the spreadsheet category, a paradigm of interactive computing that spread to accounting and planning everywhere.

Decline

Ports to other machines followed, including the IBM PC. But VisiCorp and Software Arts became embroiled in litigation, and competitors — first SuperCalc, then Mitch Kapor's Lotus 1-2-3 (1983), which was faster and added charts — eclipsed VisiCalc on the IBM PC. Software Arts was sold to Lotus in 1985 and VisiCalc was discontinued. Bricklin has made a version for the IBM PC downloadable from his website as a historical reference.
