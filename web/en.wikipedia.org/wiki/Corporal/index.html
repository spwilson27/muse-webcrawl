Corporal

Service branches: Armies, air forces, police
Rank group: Non-commissioned officer
NATO code: OR-4 (typical)
Next higher: Sergeant
Next lower: Lance corporal / Private

Corporal is a military rank in use in many nations, as well as some police forces. It is the second non-commissioned officer rank in many services, above lance corporal and below sergeant. A corporal typically commands a section or squad of soldiers, or serves as second-in-command of a larger unit.

Etymology

The word derives from the Italian caporale, from capo ("head"), ultimately from the Latin caput. The rank entered English usage in the sixteenth century, originally denoting the leader of a small body of men.

Usage by country

In the British Army, a corporal wears two chevrons and commonly commands a section of eight to ten soldiers, serving as a rifle section commander or a specialist team leader. In the United States Army, corporal (E-4) is the most junior non-commissioned officer rank, typically held by specialists promoted to leadership positions. In the United States Marine Corps, corporal is the lowest NCO rank and a squad or team leader grade. Many Commonwealth and European forces, and civil police services such as those in parts of Canada and Australia, retain equivalent grades.
