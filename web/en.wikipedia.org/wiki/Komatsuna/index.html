Komatsuna

Komatsuna (Japanese: 小松菜) is a leafy vegetable in the family Brassicaceae, a variety of Brassica rapa grown in Japan and other parts of East Asia. Its English names include Japanese mustard spinach and tenderstem mustard. The plant is harvested young, producing tender, spinach-like leaves on long stems with a mild, slightly mustardy flavour — milder than many related greens such as mizuna or tatsoi.

Name and history

The name is traditionally said to derive from the Komatsugawa district of Edo (present-day Edogawa, Tokyo), where the vegetable was cultivated in the Edo period. According to legend, the shogun Tokugawa Yoshimune, stopping at Katori shrine on a hunting trip, was served the greens in a meal and gave them their name after the village. Komatsuna remains one of the traditional vegetables of Tokyo (Edo yasai).

Cultivation and use

Komatsuna is fast-growing, cold-hardy, and can be harvested at almost any size, from baby leaves to full plants; it is grown year-round in mild climates. In Japanese cooking it is simmered in dashi-based dishes (ohitashi), stir-fried, added to soups and hot pots, pickled, or used in smoothies.

Nutritionally, komatsuna is notable for an unusually high calcium content for a leafy green, along with vitamin A, vitamin C, folate, and iron, which has made it popular in school lunches and health food in Japan.
