Always Look on the Bright Side of Life

Song by: Monty Python
Written by: Eric Idle
Released: 1979
From the film: Monty Python's Life of Brian

"Always Look on the Bright Side of Life" is a comedy song written by Eric Idle and performed by Monty Python in the closing scene of the 1979 film Monty Python's Life of Brian. Sung by a character crucified alongside Brian, it urges cheerful optimism in the face of disaster, and its whistled refrain has made it one of the most recognisable songs in British popular culture.

Origins

Idle wrote the song for the film's crucifixion finale, deliberately contrasting an upbeat music-hall tune with the grim scene; the crucified characters join in, kicking their legs in rhythm. Idle later recalled writing it in a deliberately old-fashioned style, and the song was re-recorded for the 1991 compilation Monty Python Sings, in which form it became a hit single, reaching the UK Top 3 during the Gulf War era after adoption by British forces and football crowds.

Cultural impact

The song is a staple at football grounds, where fans sing it when their team is losing, and it has been voted among the most popular songs for funerals in Britain. Idle performed it at the closing ceremony of the 2012 London Olympics. It also features prominently in the Monty Python musical Spamalot. Its refrain — "If life seems jolly rotten, there's something you've forgotten, and that's to laugh and smile and dance and sing" — has entered common usage as an emblem of gallows humour and stoic cheerfulness.
