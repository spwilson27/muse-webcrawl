Battle of Coronea (394 BC)

The Battle of Coronea was fought in 394 BC between Sparta and an alliance of Thebes, Athens, Corinth and Argos, during the Corinthian War. The Spartan king Agesilaus II, returning overland from Asia Minor, defeated the allied army on the plain of Coronea in Boeotia, in a battle notable for a rare clash in which victorious wings wheeled and met head-on.

Hoplite battle at Coronea, 394 BC.

Background

Recalled from his campaign against Persia by the outbreak of the Corinthian War, Agesilaus marched his army back through Thrace and Thessaly into Boeotia, defeating Thessalian cavalry on the way. The allied army positioned itself at Coronea to bar his route to the Peloponnese. The battle was fought at about the same time as the Spartan naval disaster at Cnidus, news of which had not yet reached the armies.

The battle

As described by Xenophon, who was present with Agesilaus's army, the Thebans on the allied right defeated the Orchomenians opposite them, while Agesilaus's Spartans on the other wing crushed the Argives. Rather than open his ranks to let the returning Thebans pass and then strike them from behind — as some advised — Agesilaus chose to meet them head-on. In a brutal frontal collision of phalanxes, the Spartans broke through, although many Thebans cut their way to safety on Mount Helicon. Xenophon called it a battle "unlike any other in our time."

Aftermath

Agesilaus, himself wounded, held the field and granted a truce for the allies to recover their dead — the conventional acknowledgement of defeat — before marching on to the Peloponnese. The victory could not, however, offset the strategic consequences of Cnidus, and the Corinthian War continued until the King's Peace of 387/386 BC.
