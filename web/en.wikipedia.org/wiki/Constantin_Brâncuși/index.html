Constantin Brâncuși

Constantin Brâncuși
Born: 19 February 1876, Hobița, Romania
Died: 16 March 1957, Paris, France
Known for: Sculpture
Movement: Modernism, abstract sculpture

Constantin Brâncuși (1876–1957) was a Romanian sculptor, painter, and photographer who made his career in France and is regarded as a pioneer of modernism and one of the most influential sculptors of the twentieth century. Working mainly in Paris from 1904, he developed a radically simplified, abstract style, carving directly in stone and wood or casting in polished bronze, and seeking the essence of his subjects rather than their surface detail.

Early life and training

Brâncuși was born in the peasant village of Hobița in Oltenia, Romania, and trained at the School of Arts and Crafts in Craiova and the National School of Fine Arts in Bucharest. In 1904 he travelled — partly on foot, by legend — to Paris, where he studied at the École des Beaux-Arts and briefly worked as an assistant in Auguste Rodin's studio. He soon left, remarking that "nothing can grow under big trees," and began to develop his own reductive approach.

Major works

His mature oeuvre revisits a small set of themes in long series: The Kiss (1907–08), Sleeping Muse (1910), Mademoiselle Pogany, the ovoid Beginning of the World, and above all the Bird in Space series, whose sleek polished forms reduce flight to a soaring curve. A 1926 Bird in Space was famously classified by United States customs as a metal object rather than art; the ensuing case, Brâncuși v. United States (1928), ruled in his favour and became a landmark in the legal definition of art. In 1937–38 he realised his masterpiece in the open air at Târgu Jiu, Romania: the sculptural ensemble of the Table of Silence, the Gate of the Kiss, and the 29.3-metre Endless Column, commemorating Romanian soldiers of the First World War.

Legacy

Brâncuși's emphasis on direct carving, pure form, and carefully finished materials influenced generations of sculptors, including Isamu Noguchi, who worked as his assistant. He bequeathed his Paris studio and its contents to the French state; the studio has been reconstructed outside the Centre Pompidou in Paris.
