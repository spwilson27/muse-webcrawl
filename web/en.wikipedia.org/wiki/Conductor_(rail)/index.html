Conductor (rail)

A conductor is a member of a train crew who holds overall responsibility for the operation of a train and the welfare of its passengers and freight, other than the mechanical operation of the locomotive, which is the duty of the engineer (driver). The conductor is traditionally regarded as the person "in charge" of the train: the engineer may not move the train until the conductor signals that it is safe to do so.

Duties

On passenger trains, conductors collect and inspect tickets, make announcements, supervise boarding and alighting, operate doors where assigned, coordinate with station staff, and handle passenger emergencies and disputes. On freight trains, the conductor manages the train's documentation (the consist and waybills), directs switching and coupling operations by radio or hand signals, monitors the train en route, walks the train to inspect for defects when stopped, and communicates with dispatchers. In North America the conductor works with brakemen (where still employed) and the engineer; in the era of the caboose, the conductor's office was traditionally in the caboose at the rear of the freight train.

Terminology around the world

The same role carries different titles in different countries. In the United Kingdom and much of the Commonwealth the senior on-train staff member is the guard. In Germany the role corresponds to the Zugführer. On many modern commuter and metro systems, ticket inspection has been separated from operational duties, and staff may be titled train managers, customer service agents, or revenue protection officers; driver-only operation has removed the conductor from some services entirely, a change that has been the subject of industrial disputes in several countries.
