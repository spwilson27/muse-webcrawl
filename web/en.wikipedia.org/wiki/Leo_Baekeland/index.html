Leo Baekeland

Leo Hendrik Baekeland (14 November 1863 – 23 February 1944) was a Belgian-American chemist best known for inventing Bakelite in 1907, the first fully synthetic plastic, an achievement that helped launch the modern plastics industry.

Born14 November 1863, Ghent, Belgium
Died23 February 1944, Beacon, New York, US
Known forBakelite, Velox photographic paper
FieldChemistry

Early life and education

Baekeland studied chemistry at the University of Ghent, earning his doctorate at a young age, and emigrated to the United States in 1889. In the 1890s he developed Velox, a photographic paper that could be developed under artificial light, and sold the rights to George Eastman of Kodak for a substantial sum, giving him financial independence.

Bakelite

Working in his laboratory in Yonkers, New York, Baekeland experimented with reactions between phenol and formaldehyde. In 1907 he discovered that, under controlled heat and pressure, the mixture formed a hard, mouldable, electrically insulating material he named Bakelite. He founded the General Bakelite Company in 1910.

Legacy

Bakelite's success made Baekeland wealthy and earned him recognition as the "father of the plastics industry." He served as president of the American Chemical Society in 1924.
