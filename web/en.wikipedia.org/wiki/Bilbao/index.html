Bilbao

Bilbao (Basque: Bilbo) is the largest city in Spain's Basque Country, capital of the province of Biscay, on the Nervión river near the Bay of Biscay. A great industrial and shipbuilding port of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, it reinvented itself after industrial decline and the catastrophic floods of 1983 around the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao (opened 1997) — Frank Gehry's titanium-clad landmark whose success at urban regeneration gave the world the phrase "the Bilbao effect". The city proper has about 345,000 inhabitants, its metropolitan area about a million.

History

Chartered in 1300, Bilbao exported Castilian wool and Biscayan iron through its estuary port. Basque iron, coal imports and shipbuilding made it an industrial powerhouse in the late nineteenth century, drawing migrant labour from across Spain and creating both a banking oligarchy and a strong labour movement. In the Spanish Civil War the Basque government of José Antonio Aguirre made Bilbao its capital; the city's "iron ring" defences were betrayed and it fell to Franco's forces in June 1937. Post-war industrial zenith gave way to crisis in the 1970s–80s; the regeneration strategy — metro by Norman Foster, the Guggenheim, the Abandoibarra waterfront — made Bilbao the textbook case of culture-led urban renewal. Basque (Euskara) is co-official with Spanish, and Athletic Club, signing only Basque-connected players, is the city's sporting emblem.
