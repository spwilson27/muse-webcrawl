Isaiah 53

Isaiah 53 is the fifty-third chapter of the Book of Isaiah in the Hebrew Bible or the Old Testament of the Christian Bible. This book contains the prophecies attributed to the prophet Isaiah and is one of the Nevi'im (Prophets). The chapter contains the fourth of the "Servant Songs" (or "Songs of the Suffering Servant"), passages in which a figure called the "Servant" suffers on behalf of others. It is one of the most debated and influential passages in biblical interpretation.

Text

The original text was written in Hebrew. In most Christian Bibles the "Suffering Servant" passage begins at Isaiah 52:13 and runs to 53:12, while in the Hebrew Bible the chapter division differs slightly, with the passage beginning at 52:13 within the previous chapter's numbering scheme. The passage is preserved in the Dead Sea Scrolls, including the Great Isaiah Scroll, which attests a very early form of the text.

Content

The song describes a servant of the Lord who is "despised and rejected by men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief", who is "wounded for our transgressions" and "bruised for our iniquities", and upon whom "the chastisement that brought us peace" falls. The passage speaks of the servant being "like a lamb that is led to the slaughter", being "cut off out of the land of the living", making his grave "with the wicked and with a rich man in his death", and afterwards seeing his offspring and prolonging his days, with the promise that he "shall divide the spoil with the strong" because he "poured out his soul to death" and "bore the sin of many".

Interpretation

In Judaism

In Jewish interpretation, the servant is generally understood to be the nation of Israel collectively, which suffers among the nations yet is vindicated; this reading is supported by other passages in Isaiah where Israel is explicitly called God's servant (e.g. Isaiah 41:8–9; 44:1–2; 49:3). Some Jewish interpreters have also applied the passage to Moses, Jeremiah, or a righteous remnant of Israel.

In Christianity

Christian tradition reads the passage as a prophecy of Jesus Christ: the New Testament quotes or alludes to it repeatedly, including Acts 8, where the Ethiopian eunuch is reading Isaiah 53 when Philip explains it to him, and 1 Peter 2, which applies its language of bearing sins to Jesus. The chapter is read liturgically in many churches on Good Friday.

Use in music

Several verses of the chapter were set in Handel's Messiah (1741), including "He was despised", "Surely He hath borne our griefs", "And with His stripes we are healed", and "All we like sheep have gone astray".
