Abu Qir

Abu Qir (Arabic: أبو قير), also spelled Abukir or Aboukir, is a town on the Mediterranean coast of Egypt, about 23 kilometres northeast of Alexandria, situated on the Abu Qir Bay at the mouth of the western branch region of the Nile Delta. It stands near the site of the ancient city of Canopus and is famous in military history as the scene of three battles of the French Revolutionary Wars era.

History

The area was inhabited in antiquity; the nearby city of Canopus was a major port and religious centre, and the now-submerged city of Heracleion lay offshore — its remains, along with part of Canopus, were rediscovered underwater by Franck Goddio's team beginning in 2000. The town's modern name is traditionally said to derive from an early Christian martyr, Saint Cyrus (Abu Qir, "Father Cyrus").

The battles

Abu Qir Bay was the site of the Battle of the Nile (1–3 August 1798), in which a British fleet under Horatio Nelson destroyed the French fleet anchored there, stranding Napoleon's army in Egypt. On land, the Battle of Abukir (25 July 1799) saw Napoleon defeat an Ottoman amphibious force, and a second land battle on 8 March 1801 opened the British campaign that ended the French occupation of Egypt. Nelson's victory gave its name to numerous places and ships, and "Aboukir" appears on battle honours of British regiments.

Modern town

Today Abu Qir is a suburb and port of greater Alexandria, home to a naval base, a ferry and fishing harbour, beaches popular with Alexandrians, and the Arab Academy for Science, Technology and Maritime Transport campus. Underwater archaeology in the bay continues to recover material from the sunken cities and from the French fleet sunk in 1798, including the flagship L'Orient.
