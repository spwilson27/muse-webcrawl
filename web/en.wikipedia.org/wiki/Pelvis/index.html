The pelvis (plural pelves or pelvises) is the lower part of the trunk of the human body, between the abdomen and the thighs. It consists of the bony pelvis (the pelvic skeleton), the pelvic cavity enclosed by it, the pelvic floor below and the perineum beneath. The bony pelvis is formed by the two hip bones (each comprising the ilium, ischium and pubis fused in adulthood), together with the sacrum and coccyx of the vertebral column.

Structure

The pelvic skeleton forms a strong basin-shaped ring that transfers the weight of the upper body from the spine to the lower limbs. Its upper opening is the pelvic inlet (pelvic brim) and its lower opening the pelvic outlet, closed by the muscular pelvic floor. The cavity contains the urinary bladder, the rectum, and in females the uterus and ovaries, in males the prostate. The greater pelvis (false pelvis) above the brim supports the lower abdominal viscera, while the lesser pelvis (true pelvis) below it forms the birth canal in females.

Sexual dimorphism

The adult female pelvis is broader, shallower and has a wider subpubic angle and a larger, rounder pelvic inlet than the male pelvis, adaptations for childbearing. The male pelvis is taller, narrower and more heart-shaped at the inlet. These differences allow forensic anthropologists to estimate the sex of skeletal remains from the pelvis with high accuracy.

Functions

The pelvis protects the pelvic and lower abdominal organs, anchors powerful muscles of locomotion and posture, transmits body weight in standing, sitting and walking, and in females supports the fetus and forms the bony birth canal. Its joints — the sacroiliac joints and the pubic symphysis — allow slight movement, increasing somewhat in pregnancy under hormonal influence.

Clinical significance

Pelvic fractures, often from high-energy trauma, can cause serious internal bleeding and damage to enclosed organs. Disorders of the pelvic floor can lead to organ prolapse and incontinence. In obstetrics, the dimensions of the maternal pelvis are assessed for their adequacy for vaginal delivery (cephalopelvic disproportion being a reason for caesarean section).
