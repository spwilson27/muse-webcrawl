Sea eagle

A sea eagle or fish eagle is any of the birds of prey in the genus Haliaeetus in the bird family Accipitridae. These large eagles live near coasts, lakes and rivers and feed mainly on fish, which they snatch from the water with their talons, supplemented by waterbirds, carrion and pirated prey.

Species

The genus includes the white-tailed eagle of Eurasia, the bald eagle of North America — the national bird of the United States — the huge Steller's sea eagle of the Russian Far East and Japan, the African fish eagle, the white-bellied sea eagle of Asia and Australia, Pallas's fish eagle, the lesser and grey-headed fish eagles, Sanford's sea eagle of the Solomon Islands, and the Madagascar fish eagle, one of the world's rarest raptors.

Conservation

Several species declined sharply in the 20th century through persecution, habitat loss and pesticide poisoning, notably DDT-induced eggshell thinning. Bans on DDT, legal protection and reintroduction programmes — such as the white-tailed eagle's return to Scotland — have allowed strong recoveries in many regions.
