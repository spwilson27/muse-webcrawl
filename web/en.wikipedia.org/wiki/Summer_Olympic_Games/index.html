The Summer Olympic Games, also known as the Games of the Olympiad, are a major international multi-sport event normally held once every four years. The inaugural Games took place in 1896 in Athens, Greece, and the most recent were held in 2024 in Paris, France. The International Olympic Committee (IOC), founded in 1894 by Pierre de Coubertin, organises the Games and oversees the Olympic movement. The Summer Games are the larger and more prestigious counterpart to the Winter Olympic Games, first held in 1924.

The Games have grown from a 42-event competition with fewer than 250 male competitors from 14 nations in 1896 to 329 events in 32 sports in Paris 2024, with more than 10,700 competitors from 206 national Olympic committees. They are widely considered the world's foremost sports competition, watched by billions of television viewers. The Olympic Games as a whole take inspiration from the Ancient Olympic Games held at Olympia, Greece, from the 8th century BC to the 4th century AD.

History

The first modern Games opened on 6 April 1896 in Athens' Panathenaiko Stadium. The 1900 Paris and 1904 St. Louis Games were overshadowed by the World's Fairs with which they were combined, but the 1908 London and 1912 Stockholm editions established many enduring features, including the marathon distance and fully electronic timing experiments. The Games scheduled for 1916, 1940 and 1944 were cancelled because of the two world wars; the 1948 London Games were accordingly known as the "Austerity Games".

Television transformed the event from the 1960s onward, bringing vast broadcast revenue and global stars. The boycotts of 1980 (Moscow) and 1984 (Los Angeles) marked the Cold War low point of Olympic universality, while the commercially successful Los Angeles Games also pioneered the modern sponsorship model. Professional athletes were gradually admitted from the late 1980s, most famously producing the United States men's basketball "Dream Team" at Barcelona 1992.

The Tokyo 2020 Games were postponed by a year because of the COVID-19 pandemic — the first postponement in Olympic history — and were staged largely without spectators in 2021 while retaining the "2020" name. Paris 2024 staged events at landmark venues across the city and along the Seine. Los Angeles will host for a third time in 2028, followed by Brisbane in 2032.

Sports

The Paris 2024 programme comprised 32 sports, including athletics, swimming, gymnastics, football, basketball, tennis, cycling and boxing. Newer additions include skateboarding, sport climbing and surfing (from Tokyo 2020) and breaking, which debuted in Paris. Los Angeles 2028 is set to add cricket, lacrosse, squash, flag football and baseball/softball. Athletics — centred on the Olympic Stadium — traditionally closes with the men's marathon.

Medals and records

The United States leads the all-time Summer Games medal table by a wide margin, followed by the Soviet Union, Germany, Great Britain and China. American swimmer Michael Phelps is the most decorated Olympian of all time with 28 medals, 23 of them gold. Other defining Summer Olympians include sprinter Usain Bolt of Jamaica, gymnast Simone Biles, and Finnish long-distance runner Paavo Nurmi. Medals are awarded in a ceremony at which the winner's national anthem is played and flags are raised.
