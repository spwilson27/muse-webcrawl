The Cologne Carnival (German: Kölner Karneval) is one of the largest and most famous carnival festivals in Germany, often called the "fifth season" of the year in Cologne. The carnival season officially begins on 11 November at 11:11 a.m., but the main street festivities run from Weiberfastnacht (the Thursday before Lent) through Ash Wednesday, culminating in the Rose Monday parade, which draws around a million spectators.

Traditions

Central figures are the Dreigestirn, the triumvirate of Prince, Peasant and Virgin (traditionally played by a man), who preside over the festivities. Revellers in costume fill the streets and pubs, greeting each other with "Kölle Alaaf!" On Weiberfastnacht, women symbolically storm the city hall and cut off men's ties. The Rose Monday parade features elaborately decorated floats — often satirising current politics — from which sweets (Kamelle) and flowers are thrown to the crowds.

History

Carnival customs in Cologne date back centuries, rooted in pre-Lenten feasting and older winter rites. The modern organised carnival was established in 1823 with the founding of the Festkomitee Kölner Karneval, which introduced the first Rose Monday parade. The festival was revived after the Second World War and has grown into a major economic and cultural event for the city, alongside the rival carnival traditions of nearby Düsseldorf and Mainz.
