Sitka (Tlingit: Sheetʼká) is a unified city-borough in the southeast of the U.S. state of Alaska. It lies on the west side of Baranof Island in the Alexander Archipelago of the Pacific Ocean and is one of the largest incorporated cities by area in the United States, encompassing most of Baranof Island and parts of neighbouring islands.

History

The area was long inhabited by Tlingit clans, who called it Sheetʼká, "settlement by the sea". Russian settlers under Alexander Baranov established a post in 1799; after Tlingit destruction of the settlement in 1802 and a Russian victory in the Battle of Sitka in 1804, the town of New Archangel became the capital of Russian America. St. Michael's Orthodox Cathedral, built in the 1840s, remains a landmark. On 18 October 1867 the formal transfer of Alaska from Russia to the United States took place at Sitka's Castle Hill, an event commemorated annually as Alaska Day.

Geography and economy

Sitka faces the Gulf of Alaska, with the dormant volcano Mount Edgecumbe visible across the water on Kruzof Island. The economy rests on fishing and seafood processing, tourism, healthcare and government. Sitka is accessible only by air and sea; it is served by the Alaska Marine Highway ferry system and Sitka Rocky Gutierrez Airport.

Demographics

The population was 8,458 at the 2020 census. Sitka has a significant Alaska Native population, principally Tlingit, and the Sitka Tribe of Alaska is headquartered there.
