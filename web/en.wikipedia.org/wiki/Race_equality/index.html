Race equality

Race equality (or racial equality) is the concept that all people are equal regardless of their race and that characteristics attributed to individual racial groups are not used to determine rights, opportunities, or treatment. It is a central goal of civil rights movements and anti-discrimination law, holding that institutions should give equal consideration to everyone irrespective of racial or ethnic background.

History

The pursuit of race equality developed through abolitionism, the civil rights movement in the United States, anti-colonial movements, and the worldwide campaign against apartheid in South Africa. Landmark legal changes included the abolition of slavery, desegregation rulings such as Brown v. Board of Education (1954), and civil rights legislation of the 1960s.

Legal frameworks

Many jurisdictions have enacted equality legislation, such as the United Kingdom's Race Relations Acts and the Equality Act 2010, and the United States' Civil Rights Act of 1964. International instruments include the International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination (1965).

Debates

Scholars debate the relationship between formal legal equality and substantive equality of outcomes, including the role of affirmative action, institutional racism, and intersectionality with class, gender and other identities.
