Khoja

The Khojas are a predominantly Nizari Isma'ili Shia Muslim community originating in western India, chiefly Gujarat, Sindh and Kutch. Traditionally a mercantile caste of converts from Hindu Lohana and related trading communities, the Khojas were formed from the 14th century onward by the preaching of Isma'ili pirs (missionaries), most famously Pir Sadruddin. In the 19th and 20th centuries many Khojas migrated to East Africa, and from there to Britain, North America and elsewhere, forming a prosperous global diaspora under the leadership of the Aga Khan.

History

The word "Khoja" derives from the Persian khwaja, "master" or "lord", a title of honour given to the converted merchants. Under the pirs, the community developed a distinctive devotional literature of hymns called ginans, written in the Khojki script, which blended Ismaili teaching with Hindu imagery. In the 19th century, the arrival of Aga Khan I in India and the 1866 Bombay High Court judgment (the "Aga Khan Case") confirmed the Khojas as Shia Imami Ismailis under the Aga Khan's authority; dissenting groups formed Khoja Ithna Ashari and Sunni Khoja communities.

Migration

Khoja merchants followed Indian Ocean trade routes to Zanzibar, Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda and Mozambique, where they became prominent traders. The 1972 expulsion of Asians from Uganda under Idi Amin drove many East African Khojas to Canada and the United Kingdom, where the community rebuilt its institutions, jamatkhanas and businesses.

Culture

Khoja identity combines Gujarati language and cuisine with Ismaili religious practice. The community is known for strong communal institutions, voluntary service, and philanthropy associated with the Aga Khan Development Network.

Note on the term

"Khoja" (Khwaja) is also used in Central Asia for Sufi lineages claiming descent from early Islamic teachers — for example the khojas who ruled in Kashgar and were involved in the history of Khiva's neighbouring khanates — but that usage denotes an unrelated title, not the Indian community described here.
