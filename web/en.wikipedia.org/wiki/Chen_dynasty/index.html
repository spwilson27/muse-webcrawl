Chen dynasty

The Chen dynasty (Chinese: 陳朝; pinyin: Chén Cháo), also known as the Southern Chen, was the fourth and last of the Southern dynasties of China, ruling from 557 to 589. It was founded by Chen Baxian (Emperor Wu of Chen), who seized the throne of the Liang dynasty. Its capital was Jiankang (modern Nanjing).

History

Chen Baxian rose to power during the chaos following the Hou Jing rebellion, which had devastated the Liang state. The Chen dynasty began in a weakened position, ruling a depopulated south and facing the powerful northern states of Northern Qi and later Northern Zhou. Nevertheless, Emperor Wu and his successor Emperor Wen consolidated the regime, and Emperor Xuan briefly recovered territory north of the Yangtze.

In 589 the Sui dynasty, which had reunified the north, launched a massive invasion across the Yangtze. The last Chen emperor, Chen Shubao, famous for his devotion to poetry and palace pleasures, was captured when Nanjing fell — legend says he was found hiding in a well with two consorts — ending nearly three centuries of division and reuniting China under the Sui.

Sovereigns

  • Emperor Wu (Chen Baxian), r. 557–559
  • Emperor Wen (Chen Qian), r. 559–566
  • Emperor Fei (Chen Bozong), r. 566–568
  • Emperor Xuan (Chen Xu), r. 569–582
  • Chen Shubao (the "Last Emperor"), r. 582–589
