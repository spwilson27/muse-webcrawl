Energy poverty

Cooking over an open fire — a common marker of energy poverty

Energy poverty is the lack of access to modern energy services — affordable, reliable electricity and clean cooking facilities — or, in developed countries, the inability of a household to afford adequate heating, cooling and power (closely related to the concept of fuel poverty). Globally, roughly 700–750 million people lack access to electricity, and more than 2 billion rely on polluting fuels such as wood, dung, charcoal and kerosene for cooking.

Dimensions

Energy poverty is multidimensional. In low-income countries it is primarily a problem of access: unconnected rural households, unreliable grids, and dependence on biomass whose smoke causes household air pollution linked to millions of premature deaths annually, disproportionately among women and children who gather fuel and cook. In high-income countries it is primarily a problem of affordability and housing quality: low-income households in poorly insulated homes spending a large share of income on energy, sometimes defined by the "10 percent rule" originating in UK fuel-poverty statistics.

Measurement

Indicators range from binary electrification rates to multi-tier frameworks that score capacity, reliability, quality, affordability and safety of supply. The International Energy Agency publishes annual estimates of populations without electricity and clean cooking access, and energy access is the subject of UN Sustainable Development Goal 7 ("affordable and clean energy").

Responses

Responses include grid extension, off-grid solar home systems and mini-grids, pay-as-you-go solar business models, liquefied petroleum gas and improved cookstove programmes, energy-efficiency retrofits, and targeted subsidies or social tariffs. Energy-price shocks, such as those following the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine, pushed energy poverty up the political agenda in Europe.
