Plov

Uzbek plov served on a communal platter

Plov (also called pilaf, pilau or osh in its home region) is a rice dish of Central Asia in which long-grain rice is cooked in a seasoned broth with oil or fat, onions, carrots and usually meat, most often lamb or mutton, sometimes beef or chicken. It is the signature dish of Uzbek and Tajik cuisine and is widespread, in many regional variants, across the Caucasus, the Middle East and South Asia — a member of the wider pilaf family.

Preparation

Traditional plov is cooked in a heavy cast-iron cauldron, the qozon or kazan. Meat is browned, then onions and julienned carrots are fried in oil or sheep's tail fat to form a base called zirvak, flavoured with cumin, barberries, chickpeas and garlic; rinsed rice is layered on top, water added, and the dish steamed until the rice is tender, then rested and mixed before serving heaped on a large shared platter (lagan). Quince, raisins, dried apricots and whole heads of garlic are common additions.

Cultural significance

Plov is served at weddings, funerals, holidays and to honour guests, often cooked by specialist male cooks, the oshpaz, in enormous cauldrons for hundreds of people. In 2016 the palov culture and tradition of Uzbekistan and the osh tradition of Tajikistan were inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
