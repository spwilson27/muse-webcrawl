Scots property law

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Scots property law governs the transfer of property and the rights in property in Scotland. As part of Scots private law, it is a "mixed" system influenced by both civil law (Roman) and common law traditions, and it differs in significant respects from the property law of England and Wales — notably in its feudal history, its system of land registration, and its treatment of real burdens and servitudes.

Foundations

Scots law classifies property as heritable (land and rights in land) or moveable (everything else). Ownership of land historically derived from the Crown under a feudal hierarchy of superior and vassal; the Abolition of Feudal Tenure etc. (Scotland) Act 2000 abolished the feudal system, with the appointed day of 28 November 2004 converting feudal estates into outright ownership. Ownership of corporeal moveables generally passes by delivery, while ownership of land passes only on registration of the disposition in the land register.

Land registration

Scotland's historic register was the Register of Sasines, established by the Registration Act 1617, which recorded deeds rather than titles. The Land Registration (Scotland) Act 1979 introduced a map-based register of title, the Land Register of Scotland, county by county, guaranteeing titles with state indemnity. The Land Registration etc. (Scotland) Act 2012 reformed the system, aiming to complete the register, and Registers of Scotland — the non-ministerial government department headed by the Keeper of the Registers — administers it and set a goal of completing registration of all land.

Real rights in land

Beyond ownership, Scots law recognises subordinate real rights (rights in the property of another): servitudes (such as rights of way), real burdens (conditions on land use introduced comprehensively by the Title Conditions (Scotland) Act 2003), leases, securities (such as the standard security, the sole means of creating a fixed heritable security since 1970), and liferents. The Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003 created statutory rights of responsible access to most land and inland water — the "right to roam" — and community rights to buy land, further reformed in later legislation.
