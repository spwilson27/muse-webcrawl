Kähler manifold

In mathematics and especially differential geometry, a Kähler manifold is a manifold with three mutually compatible structures: a complex structure, a Riemannian structure, and a symplectic structure. It is named after the mathematician Erich Kähler, who introduced the concept in the 1930s.

Equivalently, a Kähler manifold is a complex manifold equipped with a Hermitian metric whose associated two-form (the Kähler form) is closed. Important examples include complex projective space with the Fubini–Study metric, complex tori, and smooth complex algebraic varieties with the induced metric.

Kähler geometry sits at a crossroads of complex, Riemannian and symplectic geometry. Calabi–Yau manifolds — Kähler manifolds with vanishing first Chern class — play a central role in string theory, and Hodge theory on compact Kähler manifolds gives strong restrictions on their topology.
