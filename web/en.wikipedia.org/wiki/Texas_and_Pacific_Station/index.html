Texas and Pacific Station

Location: 1600 Throckmorton Street, Fort Worth, Texas
Opened: 1931
Architect: Wyatt C. Hedrick
Style: Art Deco (Zigzag Moderne)
Services: Amtrak, Trinity Railway Express, TEXRail

The Texas and Pacific Station, commonly known as the T&P Station, is a passenger railway station in Fort Worth, Texas, United States. It was built in 1931 as the main terminal of the Texas and Pacific Railway, which had its headquarters in the city, and is a prominent example of Art Deco architecture.

Architecture

Designed by architect Wyatt C. Hedrick, the station is part of a complex that includes a ten-storey office tower and a large warehouse, all clad in Texas granite and limestone with elaborate Zigzag Moderne ornament. The main waiting room is noted for its high ceiling decorated with gold-leafed medallions, chevron-pattern detailing, and ornate chandeliers, making it one of the finest surviving Art Deco interiors in Texas.

History

The station replaced an earlier Texas and Pacific terminal across the street. It served famous trains such as the Texas Eagle before intercity passenger service declined; the last Texas and Pacific passenger trains ran in the late 1960s. The building was restored in the 1990s, with the upper floors converted to residential lofts (the T&P Lofts).

Passenger service returned in 2000 when the station became the western terminus of the Trinity Railway Express commuter line to Dallas. It is also served by Amtrak's Texas Eagle (the intermodal station function is shared with the nearby Fort Worth Central Station) and by TEXRail trains to DFW International Airport that use the station. The grand waiting room is now rented as an event venue, the Grand Ballroom.
