Bioelectrochemistry

Bioelectrochemistry
FieldElectrochemistry, biophysics, biochemistry
TopicsRedox proteins, membrane potentials, biosensors

Bioelectrochemistry is a branch of electrochemistry and biophysical chemistry concerned with electrically related phenomena in biological systems — including electron transfer in proteins, charge transport across cell membranes, and the behavior of biomolecules at electrode surfaces.

Scope

The field studies redox-active biomolecules such as cytochromes, ferredoxins, and enzymes; the electrochemical basis of cellular respiration and photosynthesis; and membrane potentials essential to nerve conduction and energy transduction. Historical roots reach back to Luigi Galvani's experiments on "animal electricity" in the eighteenth century.

Applications

Bioelectrochemical methods underpin biosensors such as the enzyme-electrode glucose meter, microbial fuel cells, bioelectrosynthesis, and neural interfaces. Techniques such as cyclic voltammetry of immobilized proteins allow researchers to measure electron-transfer rates and reaction mechanisms of biological catalysts directly.
