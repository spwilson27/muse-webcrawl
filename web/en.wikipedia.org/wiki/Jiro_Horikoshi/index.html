Jiro Horikoshi

Born: 22 June 1903, Fujioka, Gunma, Japan

Died: 11 January 1982 (aged 78), Tokyo, Japan

Occupation: Aeronautical engineer

Known for: Mitsubishi A6M Zero

Jiro Horikoshi (Japanese: 堀越 二郎, Horikoshi Jirō; 22 June 1903 – 11 January 1982) was a Japanese aeronautical engineer and the chief designer of many Japanese fighter designs of World War II, most famously the Mitsubishi A6M Zero.

Early life and career

Horikoshi was born near the city of Fujioka, Gunma Prefecture. He graduated from the Aviation Laboratory of Tokyo Imperial University in 1927 and joined Mitsubishi Internal Combustion Engine Company Limited the same year. On a study tour of Europe in 1929 he visited the Junkers works in Germany and other factories, an experience that shaped his later design philosophy.

Designs

His first major success was the Mitsubishi A5M, the world's first monoplane carrier-based fighter when it entered service. He then led the design of the A6M Zero, which first flew in 1939 and became Japan's principal naval fighter of the Pacific War, renowned for its range and manoeuvrability. Later projects included the Mitsubishi J2M Raiden interceptor and the A7M Reppu, which did not reach mass production before the war ended.

Later life

After the war Horikoshi helped design the YS-11 passenger aircraft with Nihon Aircraft Manufacturing Corporation and taught at several universities. He died of pneumonia in Tokyo in 1982. His wartime diary and memoirs inspired Hayao Miyazaki's 2013 animated film The Wind Rises.
