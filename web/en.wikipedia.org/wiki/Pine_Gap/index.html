Pine Gap

The radomes of the Joint Defence Facility Pine Gap

Pine Gap, formally the Joint Defence Facility Pine Gap, is a satellite surveillance station and signals intelligence facility located about 18 kilometres (11 mi) south-west of Alice Springs in Australia's Northern Territory. It is jointly operated by Australian and United States intelligence agencies and is one of the most important Western intelligence collection sites in the world.

Functions

The base controls and receives data from a constellation of geosynchronous signals-intelligence satellites positioned over the Indian Ocean, the Pacific and Eurasia. Its radomes — more than three dozen are visible at the site — cover antennas that intercept communications and missile telemetry and provide early warning of ballistic missile launches. Documents disclosed by Edward Snowden in 2013 indicated Pine Gap plays a key role in global surveillance, geolocation of communications devices, and support to military operations including drone strikes.

History

Australia and the United States agreed to establish the station in 1966, and it began operations around 1970, publicly described at the time as a space research facility. Its existence and role became a major issue in Australian politics during the Whitlam government in the 1970s. The facility employs roughly 800 staff, Australians and Americans, and its operations are coordinated with the Australian Signals Directorate and U.S. agencies including the NSA and NRO.

Protests

Pine Gap has been a focus of peace and anti-base protests for decades. In 2016–2017 several Christian pacifist protesters who entered the facility were prosecuted under defence legislation, drawing renewed attention to the base's role in U.S. military operations.
