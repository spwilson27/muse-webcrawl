Colonial Revival architecture

The Colonial Revival was a nationalistic architectural style, garden design and interior design movement in the United States that sought to revive elements of American colonial architecture, principally the Georgian and Federal styles, in reaction to the rapid industrialisation and immigration of the late 19th century. The movement flourished from about 1880 to 1955 and remains a popular idiom for American houses.

History

Interest in colonial design was stimulated by the 1876 Centennial Exposition in Philadelphia and by restorations and publications documenting early American buildings, including the work of architects such as McKim, Mead & White. The restoration of Colonial Williamsburg, begun in the 1920s, greatly popularised the style. Dutch Colonial Revival, Spanish Colonial Revival and cape-cod forms are often counted within the broader movement, though Spanish Colonial Revival is usually treated as its own style.

Characteristics

Typical features of Colonial Revival houses include:

  • Symmetrical façades with a centred front door, often with a decorative crown or portico
  • Multi-pane, double-hung sash windows with shutters
  • Brick or clapboard siding, gambrel roofs in Dutch Colonial variants
  • Dentil mouldings, pilasters and other classical details
  • Gabled or hipped roofs, sometimes with dormers

Influence

The style was applied to houses, churches, schools, courthouses and commercial buildings, and strongly shaped the look of American suburbs in the first half of the 20th century. Simplified versions continue to be built in New Urbanist and traditional neighbourhood developments.
