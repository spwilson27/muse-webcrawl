Makgeolli (Korean: 막걸리), also known as makkoli or makgeoli, is a Korean alcoholic drink. It is a milky, off-white, and lightly sparkling rice wine that has a slight viscosity and tastes slightly sweet, tangy, bitter, and astringent. Chalky sediment gives it a cloudy appearance.

Production

Makgeolli is made from rice using nuruk, a traditional Korean fermentation starter containing wild yeasts, bacteria and moulds (notably Aspergillus oryzae), which saccharify the starch and ferment the sugars in parallel. After fermentation for about a week it is strained and diluted, and it is traditionally sold unpasteurised, so it continues to ferment in the bottle and must be refrigerated and consumed quickly. Modern commercial versions may be pasteurised for shelf life and sometimes sweetened.

History and culture

Makgeolli is Korea's oldest alcoholic beverage, long associated with farming communities — it was nicknamed nongju ("farmer's liquor"). Traditionally served from a kettle into bowls, it is commonly paired with savoury pancakes (jeon) on rainy days. Once dismissed as a cheap, old-fashioned drink, makgeolli underwent a major revival in South Korea in the late 2000s and became fashionable among younger drinkers, with regional and craft varieties proliferating.

Related drinks

The cloudy, unsettled form is makgeolli; the clearer fraction of the same brew yields cheongju (yakju), while the distilled spirit is soju. A less refined, single-fermentation variant is dongdongju.
