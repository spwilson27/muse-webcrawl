Elgin Marbles

Elgin Marbles / Parthenon Marbles
Created447–432 BC, Athens (Phidias workshop)
Present locationBritish Museum, London; Acropolis Museum, Athens
Removed byAgents of Lord Elgin, 1801–1812

The Elgin Marbles, also called the Parthenon Marbles, are a collection of Classical Greek marble sculptures that originally adorned the Parthenon and other buildings on the Acropolis of Athens. They include much of the frieze, metopes and pedimental figures.

Removal and acquisition

Between 1801 and 1812 agents of Thomas Bruce, 7th Earl of Elgin, British ambassador to the Ottoman Empire, removed about half the surviving sculptures, claiming permission under an Ottoman firman whose scope has been disputed ever since. Parliament bought the collection in 1816 for the British Museum.

Restitution debate

Greece has long requested their return, arguing they are integral to a single monument; the opening of the Acropolis Museum (2009), where the remaining sculptures are displayed alongside plaster casts of those in London, strengthened the Greek case. The British Museum Act restricts disposal, and the museum maintains the sculptures are held for a world audience, though loan discussions recur. The dispute is a landmark case in cultural-property debates.
