Battle of Fort Sumter

The Battle of Fort Sumter, fought from 12 to 13 April 1861, was the bombardment of Fort Sumter in Charleston Harbor, South Carolina, by Confederate forces under Brigadier General P. G. T. Beauregard. The attack on the United States Army garrison commanded by Major Robert Anderson opened the American Civil War.

Background

After South Carolina seceded in December 1860, Major Anderson moved his small garrison from Fort Moultrie to the stronger, unfinished Fort Sumter on an island in Charleston Harbor. The Confederacy demanded the fort's surrender, while the Lincoln administration resolved to resupply it with food. Faced with the arrival of a relief expedition, Confederate President Jefferson Davis authorised Beauregard to reduce the fort if Anderson refused terms.

The bombardment

At 4:30 a.m. on 12 April 1861, a mortar shell from Fort Johnson burst over Sumter, signalling some 43 Confederate guns and mortars in the ring of batteries around the harbor to open fire. Anderson's garrison replied with the few guns it could work, hampered by shortages of men, powder and fuzes. Fires ignited in the wooden barracks threatened the powder magazine, and after about 34 hours of bombardment, with his provisions nearly gone, Anderson agreed on 13 April to evacuate the fort. Remarkably, no one on either side was killed by enemy fire during the action; a Union private was killed by an accidental explosion during the surrender salute on 14 April.

Aftermath

The fall of Fort Sumter rallied the North, and President Lincoln called for 75,000 volunteers, prompting four more states to secede. The fort remained in Confederate hands until February 1865. Its flag, lowered in 1861, was raised again by Anderson — by then a retired major general — on 14 April 1865.
