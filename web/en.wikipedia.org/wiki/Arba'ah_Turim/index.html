Arba'ah Turim

The Arba'ah Turim (Hebrew: אַרְבָּעָה טוּרִים), often called simply the Tur, is an important Halakhic code composed by Jacob ben Asher (c. 1269–1343, called by extension the Ba'al ha-Turim, "Master of the Turim"). The four-part structure of the Tur and its division into chapters (simanim) were adopted by the later code, the Shulchan Aruch.

The title means "Four Rows," in allusion to the four rows of jewels on the breastplate of the High Priest. The work was written in Spain in the early 14th century and printed in 1475, making it one of the earliest dated Hebrew printed books.

Structure

The Arba'ah Turim is divided into four sections:

  • Orach Chayim — laws of prayer, synagogue, blessings, Shabbat, and festivals.
  • Yoreh De'ah — laws of kashrut, slaughter, forbidden mixtures, charity, and mourning.
  • Even Ha'ezer — laws of marriage, divorce, and family relations.
  • Choshen Mishpat — civil, monetary, and judicial law.

Approach and influence

Unlike Maimonides' Mishneh Torah, the Tur surveys the views of earlier authorities — especially the author's father, Asher ben Jehiel (the Rosh) — and presents the reasoning and disagreements before arriving at a conclusion, drawing together Ashkenazi and Sephardi traditions. It aimed to cover only the laws applicable in the post-Temple Diaspora.

Joseph Karo's commentary, the Beit Yosef, was written on the framework of the Tur and became the basis of Karo's Shulchan Aruch, which preserved the Tur's fourfold division and chapter order. In traditional editions the Tur is printed surrounded by the Beit Yosef and other commentaries, and it remains a standard work of halakhic study.
