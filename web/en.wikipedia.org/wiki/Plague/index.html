Plague

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Plague is an infectious disease caused by the bacterium Yersinia pestis, transmitted mainly by fleas that live on rodents. It is infamous for three pandemics: the Plague of Justinian (6th century), the Black Death (14th century), which killed a large share of Europe's population, and a third pandemic that began in 19th-century China. Plague still occurs in small numbers in parts of Africa, Asia and the Americas.

Forms

Bubonic plague, the most common form, causes fever and swollen, painful lymph nodes (buboes) after a flea bite. Septicaemic plague infects the bloodstream, and pneumonic plague infects the lungs and can spread between people in droplets — the only form that does so. Untreated bubonic plague kills roughly half or more of its victims; pneumonic plague is almost always fatal untreated.

Control

Prompt antibiotics (such as streptomycin, gentamicin or doxycycline) cure most cases. Control focuses on rodent and flea management, isolation of pneumonic cases, and surveillance of animal die-offs. Y. pestis was identified in 1894 by Alexandre Yersin during the Hong Kong outbreak, and the flea–rat cycle was worked out soon after.
