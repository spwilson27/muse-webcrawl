Soldier settlement (Australia)


Soldier settlement schemes settled returned servicemen on farms after both world wars.

Soldier settlement was a programme in Australia that settled returned servicemen on farming land after the First and Second World Wars. State governments acquired and subdivided land, often Crown land or large estates, and leased or sold small blocks to ex-soldiers, who were expected to develop them into productive farms.

After the First World War

Following 1918, more than 37,000 returned soldiers were settled on the land. Many blocks were too small or on marginal country, settlers lacked capital and farming experience, and falling prices during the 1920s and the Great Depression forced large numbers to walk off their farms. Royal commissions in several states examined the schemes' failings.

After the Second World War

The War Service Land Settlement scheme after 1945 applied lessons from the earlier failure: blocks were larger and better selected, settlers were trained, and prices were more favourable. These later schemes, including those of the Snowy Mountains region and the irrigation areas, were generally more successful.

Legacy

Soldier settlement reshaped rural Australia, creating districts and towns, and it also dispossessed Aboriginal people from some of the land taken up. Its history is a significant theme in Australian social memory of the wars.
