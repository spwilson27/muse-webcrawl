Basilisk

In European bestiaries and legends, a basilisk is a legendary reptile reputed to be the "king of serpents" — the name derives from the Greek basiliskos, "little king" — whose gaze, breath or venom causes instant death. It is often depicted with the head of a cockerel, the body of a serpent and a crown-like crest.

Description in ancient and medieval sources

Pliny the Elder described the basilisk of Cyrenaica as a small snake whose venom and scorching breath withered plants and killed any creature that met its eyes. Medieval lore added that the creature was hatched from a cockerel's egg incubated by a toad or serpent, that it could be killed by a crowing rooster or by seeing its own reflection, and that the weasel was its natural enemy. The related cockatrice shares most of these traits.

In heraldry and modern culture

The basilisk appears in heraldry, most famously supporting the arms of the city of Basel, Switzerland. It has become a staple of modern fantasy literature and games, usually as a giant serpent or lizard with a petrifying or lethal gaze — notably the monster in the Chamber of Secrets in Harry Potter.
