Neum

Neum is a town and municipality in Bosnia and Herzegovina, located on the Adriatic coast. It is the country's only coastal town, giving Bosnia and Herzegovina about 24 km of coastline — one of the shortest in the world — and separating the southern part of Croatia's Dubrovnik region from the rest of Croatia.

Geography and history

The Neum corridor dates to the 1699 Treaty of Karlowitz, when the Republic of Ragusa ceded the strip to the Ottoman Empire to create a buffer against Venetian territory. The corridor was confirmed at the Congress of Berlin in 1878 and inherited by Yugoslavia and then Bosnia and Herzegovina.

Tourism

Neum is a popular seaside resort, especially with domestic visitors and tourists from the region, offering lower prices than nearby Croatian resorts. The Pelješac Bridge, opened by Croatia in 2022, connects the two parts of Croatia while bypassing the corridor.
