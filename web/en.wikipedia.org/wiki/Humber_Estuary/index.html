Humber Estuary

The Humber Estuary is a large tidal estuary on the east coast of Northern England, formed by the confluence of the tidal rivers Ouse and Trent at Trent Falls and flowing east into the North Sea at Spurn Head. It drains a catchment covering about a fifth of England's land area.

Geography and economy

The estuary separates the East Riding of Yorkshire on its north bank from Lincolnshire on the south. Since 1981 the Humber Bridge — for a time the world's longest single-span suspension bridge — has linked the two banks near Kingston upon Hull. The ports of Hull, Grimsby, Immingham and Goole together form one of the United Kingdom's busiest port complexes, handling bulk cargoes, chemicals and North Sea energy traffic.

Environment

The estuary's mudflats and saltmarshes support internationally important populations of wading birds and are protected as a Special Area of Conservation and Ramsar site. Its strong tides and shifting channels, including the sand spit of Spurn Point, make navigation challenging.
