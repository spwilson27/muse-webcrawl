Eugen Herrigel

Eugen Herrigel (20 March 1884 – 18 April 1955) was a German philosopher who taught philosophy at Tohoku Imperial University in Sendai, Japan, from 1924 to 1929. He is best known for his short book Zen in the Art of Archery (German: Zen in der Kunst des Bogenschießens, 1948), which introduced many Western readers to Zen Buddhism through his account of learning Japanese archery, kyūdō.

Life

Herrigel studied theology and philosophy and was influenced by neo-Kantian thought. Seeking a direct experience of mysticism unavailable in European philosophy, he accepted a post in Japan, hoping to study Zen. Because direct instruction in Zen meditation was not readily offered to foreigners, he took up archery under the master Awa Kenzō, who taught an idiosyncratic, spiritually oriented form of the art. Over some six years Herrigel struggled with the master's paradoxical teaching — in particular the demand to release the bowstring without intention, so that "It" shoots rather than the archer.

Zen in the Art of Archery

Published posthumously in English in 1953, the book describes Herrigel's apprenticeship: learning to draw the great bow, to breathe, to wait motionless at full draw, and finally to loose the shot "like snow falling from a bamboo leaf". It became one of the most influential popular presentations of Zen in the West and inspired similar "Zen in the art of…" books, including Robert Pirsig's Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance. Later scholars have questioned how accurately Herrigel understood his teacher — Awa's doctrine owed as much to his own personal philosophy as to Zen — and have noted that translation gaps between master and pupil shaped the celebrated dialogues. Herrigel's book nevertheless remains a classic of spiritual literature.
