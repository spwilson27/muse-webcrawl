Police commissioner

A police commissioner is the head of a police force in many jurisdictions around the world. The exact duties vary: in some places the commissioner is a senior operational police officer, while in others the title belongs to a civilian official appointed or elected to oversee a police department.

By jurisdiction

In the United States, police commissioners are typically civilian administrators appointed by a mayor or city council; the New York City Police Commissioner is the civilian head of the NYPD. In India, commissioners of police head the police forces of large metropolitan cities under the commissionerate system, and are senior officers of the Indian Police Service. In Australia and New Zealand, the commissioner is the senior operational officer of a state or national police force. In the United Kingdom, the Commissioner of Police of the Metropolis heads London's Metropolitan Police, while most other forces are overseen by an elected police and crime commissioner working with a chief constable.
