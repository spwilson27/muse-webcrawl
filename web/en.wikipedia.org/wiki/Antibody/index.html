An antibody (immunoglobulin) is a large Y-shaped protein used by the immune system to identify and neutralise foreign objects such as pathogenic bacteria and viruses. Each antibody's tips contain a paratope that binds a specific epitope on its target antigen, like a lock and key.

Antibodies are produced by plasma cells (differentiated B cells), first as IgM, then class-switching to IgG (the main blood antibody), IgA (mucosal), IgE (allergy/parasites) or IgD. They neutralise toxins and viruses, opsonise pathogens for phagocytes, and activate complement. Monoclonal antibodies — identical antibodies cloned in the laboratory (Köhler & Milstein, 1975) — are major medicines (e.g. trastuzumab, adalimumab) and research/diagnostic tools.
