The Robotics Institute (RI) is a division of the School of Computer Science at Carnegie Mellon University in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. Founded in 1979 by Raj Reddy and colleagues, it is generally regarded as one of the world's leading centres for robotics research and education, and it established the first PhD programme in robotics (1989).

Research

The institute's work spans field robotics, computer vision, manipulation, medical robotics, human–robot interaction and autonomous vehicles. Its Navlab vehicles pioneered autonomous highway driving in the 1980s and 1990s; its field robotics centre built robots for mining, agriculture, space exploration and nuclear-site clean-up, including the robots sent into the Chernobyl and Fukushima disaster zones. The affiliated National Robotics Engineering Center (NREC), founded in 1996, develops applied robotic systems for government and industry, and CMU teams won the 2007 DARPA Urban Challenge with the self-driving vehicle Boss.

Education

The institute runs graduate programmes — a research master's and the Robotics PhD — and undergraduate minors and majors in robotics, drawing students from computer science, engineering and design.
