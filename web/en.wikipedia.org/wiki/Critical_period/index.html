Critical period

During a critical period, experience has an outsized effect on development

In developmental psychology and developmental biology, a critical period is a maturational stage in the lifespan of an organism during which the nervous system is especially sensitive to certain environmental stimuli. If the organism does not receive the appropriate stimulus during this window, the corresponding skill or trait may fail to develop fully, and later learning may be difficult or impossible. Periods with a strong, sharply bounded effect are called critical; more gradual windows of heightened plasticity are often termed sensitive periods.

Vision

The classic experiments of David Hubel and Torsten Wiesel on kittens showed that depriving one eye of input during a brief early window permanently shifts cortical responses to that eye, while the same deprivation in adults has little effect. In humans, untreated congenital cataracts or severe amblyopia in early childhood can cause lasting visual deficits, which is why early screening and treatment are emphasised.

Language

The critical period hypothesis, popularised by Eric Lenneberg (1967), holds that first-language acquisition must occur before roughly puberty to achieve full native competence; evidence includes the severely limited language of isolated children such as "Genie". In second-language learning, younger learners generally attain more native-like pronunciation and grammar, though researchers debate whether the decline reflects a true critical period ending abruptly or a sensitive period with gradual decline, and how much is explained by input, motivation, and interference from the first language.

Other domains

Critical or sensitive periods have been described for imprinting in birds (Konrad Lorenz's work on goslings following the first moving object they see), auditory development and cochlear implant outcomes, binocular vision, attachment, and the effects of early deprivation or stress. In development more broadly, teratogens such as certain drugs and infections cause the greatest harm during specific embryonic windows when particular organs are forming.
