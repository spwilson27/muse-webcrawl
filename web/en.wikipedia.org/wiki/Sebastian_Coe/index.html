Sebastian Newbold Coe, Baron Coe (born 29 September 1956), often known as Seb Coe, is a British sports administrator, former politician and retired track and field athlete. As a middle-distance runner, Coe won four Olympic medals — gold in the 1500 metres at the 1980 and 1984 Olympic Games and silver in the 800 metres at both — and set numerous world records. He later chaired the London Organising Committee for the 2012 Olympic Games and became president of World Athletics in 2015.

Athletics career

Coe was coached by his father, Peter Coe, and emerged as a leading British middle-distance runner in the late 1970s. In the summer of 1979 he set world records in the 800 metres, 1500 metres and mile within 41 days, the first athlete to hold all three simultaneously. His rivalry with compatriots Steve Ovett and Steve Cram defined a golden era of British middle-distance running.

At the 1980 Moscow Olympics, Coe lost the 800 metres to Ovett but rebounded to win the 1500 metres. He repeated the pattern at the 1984 Los Angeles Games, again taking silver in the 800 metres — this time behind Joaquim Cruz — and gold in the 1500 metres, becoming the first man to win consecutive Olympic 1500 m titles. Injuries hampered his later career; he won gold at the 1986 European Championships in the 800 metres and retired from competitive athletics in 1990.

Political career

Coe was elected Conservative Member of Parliament for Falmouth and Camborne in 1992, serving until 1997. He became a life peer in 2000, sitting as Baron Coe of Ranmore, and worked in senior Conservative Party roles, including as chief of staff to party leader William Hague.

Sports administration

Coe led London's successful bid to host the 2012 Olympic and Paralympic Games, defeating Paris in the 2005 IOC vote, and chaired the organising committee (LOCOG) that delivered the Games. In 2015 he was elected president of the IAAF (renamed World Athletics in 2019), succeeding Lamine Diack amid a major doping and corruption scandal, and he has overseen reforms including the creation of the Athletics Integrity Unit and the federation's suspension of Russia. Coe was re-elected in 2019 and 2023.
