The Yuktibhasa ("Exposition of Rationales"), written in Malayalam around 1530 and attributed to the astronomer Jyesthadeva, is a major treatise of the Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics. Unusually for Indian mathematical texts, it presents full proofs and derivations rather than bare results.

Contents

The work explains the results of Madhava of Sangamagrama and his successors, including infinite series for π and for the sine, cosine, and arctangent functions, rational approximations, and methods of iterative correction — developments in analysis that predate their European counterparts by more than a century. It also treats astronomy, including planetary motion and eclipses.

Significance

Because it sets out the reasoning behind the Kerala school's formulas, the Yuktibhasa is a key source for historians of mathematics and a notable example of a major scientific work composed in a regional vernacular rather than Sanskrit. A critical edition with English translation was published in 2008.
