Lucas Cranach the Elder (German: Lucas Cranach der Ältere; c. 1472 – 16 October 1553) was a German Renaissance painter and printmaker in woodcut and engraving. He was court painter to the Electors of Saxony for most of his career, and is known for his portraits—of Saxon princes and of the leaders of the Protestant Reformation, whose cause he embraced with enthusiasm—as well as for his religious and mythological paintings. He was a close friend of Martin Luther.

Life

Cranach was born at Kronach in Franconia, from which he took his name, and trained with his father Hans Maler. After early years in Vienna, where he came into contact with humanist circles, he was appointed court painter at Wittenberg in 1505 by Frederick the Wise, Elector of Saxony. He remained in Saxon service for nearly fifty years, also prospering as a businessman with a workshop, an apothecary licence, a printing business and property; he served repeatedly as burgomaster of Wittenberg. He followed his patron John Frederick into exile and died in Weimar in 1553.

Works

Cranach's workshop produced an enormous output—over a thousand paintings survive—including altarpieces, portraits of Luther and Melanchthon, and a distinctive series of mythological and biblical nudes such as Venus, Judith, Lucretia and The Judgment of Paris, notable for their slender, stylised figures. His paintings were signed with a winged serpent device granted to him in 1508. His sons Hans Cranach and Lucas Cranach the Younger continued the workshop.

Reformation art

As a leading artist of the Reformation, Cranach designed woodcut illustrations for Luther's publications, including the first edition of Luther's German New Testament (1522), and devised a visual language for Protestant altarpieces such as the Wittenberg altarpiece. He stood as witness at Luther's wedding and was godfather to one of his children.
