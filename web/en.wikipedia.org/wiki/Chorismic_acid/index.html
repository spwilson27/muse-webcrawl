Chorismic acid

Chorismic acid
Formula: C10H10O6
Role: intermediate in the shikimate pathway
Anion name: chorismate

Chorismic acid, more commonly known as its anionic form chorismate, is an important biochemical intermediate in plants and microorganisms in the production of aromatic amino acids. It is the final product of the shikimate pathway and a branch point for many biosynthetic routes.

Biosynthesis

Chorismate is formed from shikimate through shikimate kinase and EPSP synthase, the latter being the target of the herbicide glyphosate. Because the shikimate pathway is absent in animals, its enzymes are attractive targets for herbicides and antimicrobial agents.

Reactions

Chorismate mutase catalyzes the Claisen rearrangement of chorismate to prephenate, the committed step toward phenylalanine and tyrosine. Anthranilate synthase converts chorismate toward tryptophan, and other enzymes direct it to folate (via p-aminobenzoate), menaquinone, ubiquinone, and salicylic acid.
