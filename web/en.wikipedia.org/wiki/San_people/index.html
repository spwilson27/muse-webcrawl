The San peoples (also Saan), historically called Bushmen, are Indigenous hunter-gatherer groups of Southern Africa whose territories span Botswana, Namibia, Angola, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Lesotho, and South Africa. They are among the oldest continuous populations in the world: genetic studies indicate the San diverged from other human populations roughly 100,000 years or more ago. Traditionally the San lived by hunting game with poisoned arrows and gathering wild plants in and around the Kalahari Desert.

Languages and culture

San languages are famous for their use of click consonants and belong to three unrelated families — Khoe, Tuu, and Kx'a — once grouped under the now-disputed label "Khoisan". San culture is noted for egalitarian social organization, trance healing dances, and rock art traditions that survive across Southern Africa, some of it thousands of years old. Hunting is governed by strong sharing norms; anthropologists have called groups such as the Ju/'hoansi among the most egalitarian societies documented.

History and modern challenges

San communities were displaced over centuries by migrating Bantu-speaking farmers and later by European colonization, which brought violence, servitude, and loss of land. In modern Botswana, the relocation of San (Basarwa) communities from the Central Kalahari Game Reserve in the early 2000s became an international legal case; Botswana's High Court ruled in 2006 that the removals were unlawful. Today many San face poverty and marginalization, while advocacy groups press for land rights, mother-tongue education, and a share of benefits from traditional knowledge — notably a landmark benefit-sharing agreement over the appetite-suppressant plant hoodia.
