Sacramento is the capital city of the U.S. state of California and the seat of Sacramento County. Located at the confluence of the Sacramento and American Rivers in the Sacramento Valley, it is the sixth-most populous city in California and the ninth-largest state capital in the United States, with a population of 524,669 at the 2020 census. The Sacramento metropolitan area, with about 2.4 million residents, is the fourth-largest in California.

History

The area was inhabited by the Nisenan and Miwok peoples for thousands of years. In 1839, Swiss immigrant John Augustus Sutter established New Helvetia, including Sutter's Fort, near the confluence of the rivers. The city of Sacramento was laid out in 1848 by John A. Sutter Jr. and grew rapidly after the discovery of gold at Sutter's Mill in Coloma in 1848, becoming a major distribution point and commercial and agricultural centre during the California Gold Rush. Sacramento was chosen as the state capital in 1854 and incorporated in 1850, making it one of California's earliest incorporated cities. It was the western terminus of the Pony Express and the First transcontinental railroad.

Geography and climate

Sacramento sits in the flat Sacramento Valley, ringed at a distance by the Sierra Nevada to the east and the Coast Ranges to the west. It has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and cool, wet winters. The city is known for its extensive urban tree canopy and styles itself the "City of Trees" and the "Farm-to-Fork Capital" in recognition of its agricultural hinterland.

Government and economy

As the state capital, Sacramento hosts the California State Capitol and the seat of the state government, which is the region's largest employer. Other major sectors include health care, education and technology. Landmarks include Old Sacramento State Historic Park, the Crocker Art Museum, the California State Railroad Museum, and the Tower Bridge. Professional sports teams include the Sacramento Kings of the NBA.
