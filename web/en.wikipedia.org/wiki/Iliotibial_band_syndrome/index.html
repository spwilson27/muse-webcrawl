Iliotibial band syndrome

Iliotibial band syndrome
Specialty: Sports medicine, orthopaedics
Symptoms: Pain on the outer side of the knee
Usual onset: Gradual, with running or cycling

Iliotibial band syndrome (ITBS) is a common overuse injury causing pain on the lateral (outer) side of the knee, particularly in runners and cyclists. It is one of the leading causes of lateral knee pain in distance runners and is sometimes called "runner's knee", though that term is also used for patellofemoral pain syndrome.

Mechanism

The iliotibial band is a thick band of fascia running from the pelvis down the outside of the thigh to just below the knee. Pain was traditionally attributed to friction as the band rubs over the lateral femoral epicondyle; more recent work emphasises compression of the fat and connective tissue beneath the band.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually clinical, based on the history and tenderness over the lateral epicondyle. Treatment centres on activity modification, ice, anti-inflammatory measures, stretching and strengthening of the hip abductors, and gradual return to training; surgery is rarely needed.
