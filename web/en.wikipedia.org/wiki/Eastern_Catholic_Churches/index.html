The Eastern Catholic Churches or Oriental Catholic Churches are 23 Eastern Christian autonomous (sui iuris) particular churches of the Catholic Church that are in full communion with the Pope in Rome but follow the liturgical, theological and devotional traditions of Eastern Christianity. Together with the Latin Church — the 24th sui iuris church — they make up the worldwide Catholic Church. Their combined membership is about 18 million, a small minority of the world's Catholics, the largest of them being the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church.

History

Some Eastern Catholic churches trace their origins to the earliest centuries of Christianity and entered or restored communion with Rome at various points; others arose from unions concluded after the East–West Schism of 1054, notably the Union of Brest (1596), which brought the Ruthenian Church of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth into communion, and the Union of Uzhhorod (1646). Many suffered severe persecution in the twentieth century: the Ukrainian and Romanian Greek Catholic churches were forcibly suppressed under Soviet and communist rule and their faithful driven underground, re-emerging legally only after 1989.

Organisation and law

Each Eastern Catholic church is governed by its own hierarchy — patriarchs (in the largest churches), major archbishops, metropolitans or bishops — and by the Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches (1990). Married men may be ordained to the priesthood in most Eastern Catholic traditions, and the liturgy is celebrated in languages such as Greek, Church Slavonic, Ukrainian, Arabic, Syriac, Malayalam and Ge'ez, according to the rite.

Principal churches

  • Byzantine (Greek) rite: Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church (the largest, about 4–5 million), Melkite Greek Catholic Church, Romanian Greek Catholic Church, Ruthenian Greek Catholic Church, Slovak and Hungarian Greek Catholic churches, and others.
  • Alexandrian rite: Coptic Catholic Church, Ethiopian Catholic Church, Eritrean Catholic Church.
  • West Syriac (Antiochene) rite: Maronite Church, Syriac Catholic Church, Syro-Malankara Catholic Church.
  • East Syriac (Chaldean) rite: Chaldean Catholic Church, Syro-Malabar Catholic Church (the second largest, in India).
  • Armenian rite: Armenian Catholic Church.

Terminology

Eastern Catholics were formerly sometimes called "Uniates" — a term now regarded as pejorative and avoided in official Catholic usage. The Second Vatican Council's decree Orientalium Ecclesiarum (1964) affirmed the equal dignity of the Eastern churches and their right to preserve their own traditions; the 1993 Balamand declaration rejected "uniatism" as a method for seeking unity while recognising the Eastern Catholic churches' right to exist.
