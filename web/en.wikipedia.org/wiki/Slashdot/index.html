Slashdot (sometimes abbreviated as /.) is a social news website that originally billed itself as "News for Nerds. Stuff that Matters". It features news stories on science, technology and politics that are submitted and evaluated by its users and editors. Each story has a comments section attached to it where users can add online comments.

History

Slashdot was founded in 1997 by Hope College students Rob Malda (CmdrTaco) and Jeff Bates (Hemos). The site was an early pioneer of user-driven web content: readers submitted stories, moderated comments through a reputation system, and awarded and spent "karma". In 1999 the site was acquired by Andover.net, which later became part of VA Linux; it passed through SourceForge and Geeknet before being acquired by Dice Holdings in 2012 and later by BIZX.

Culture and influence

Slashdot popularised several features now common on the web, including user moderation and meta-moderation systems. The "Slashdot effect" described the phenomenon of a popular website linking to a smaller site and overwhelming it with traffic. Classic site traditions included the race to post the "first post", the recurring "In Soviet Russia..." joke format, and April Fools' stories. The underlying software, Slash, was released as free software and powered a number of other community sites.

Moderation

Comments are moderated by randomly selected users given moderation points, who rate comments as insightful, funny, troll, flamebait and so on; a second layer of meta-moderation judges the fairness of those ratings. Logged-in users can filter comment visibility by score threshold.
