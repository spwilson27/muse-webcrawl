Karl Ove Knausgård (born 6 December 1968) is a Norwegian author. He became internationally known for the six volumes of his autobiographical novel cycle Min kamp (My Struggle), published between 2009 and 2011, which sold hundreds of thousands of copies in Norway and was translated into dozens of languages.

Life and career

Knausgård was born in Oslo and grew up on Tromøya in Arendal and in Kristiansand. He studied art and literature at the University of Bergen. His debut novel, Ute av verden (Out of the World, 1998), won the Norwegian Critics Prize for Literature; his second novel, En tid for alt (A Time for Everything, 2004), was also acclaimed.

My Struggle

The six Min kamp volumes describe Knausgård's life in exhaustive, unflinching detail, from his fraught relationship with his father — whose death from alcoholism is the centrepiece of the first volume — to his marriages and life as a father. The books' use of real names and intimate depictions of family members prompted threats of legal action and public debate in Norway about the ethics of autofiction. The final volume includes a long essay on Hitler and the title's resonance with Mein Kampf.

Later work

Knausgård later published a seasonal cycle of short books (Autumn, Winter, Spring, Summer), the novel Morgenstjernen (The Morning Star, 2020) and its sequels, and essay collections. He has lived in Sweden and London, and is regarded as one of the most influential Scandinavian writers of his generation.

Bibliography (selected)

Out of the World (1998); A Time for Everything (2004); My Struggle, Books 1–6 (2009–2011); The Morning Star (2020).
