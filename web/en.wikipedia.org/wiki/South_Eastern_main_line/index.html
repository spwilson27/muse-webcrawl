South Eastern main line

The South Eastern main line is a major railway line in south-east England, running from London to Dover via Sevenoaks and Ashford. It was built by the South Eastern Railway and opened in stages between 1842 and 1844, providing the first rail route from the capital to the Channel ports.

History

The South Eastern Railway obtained authorisation in 1836 and initially shared the London and Croydon Railway's route into London Bridge. The line through Redhill opened in 1841, reaching Tonbridge in May 1842, Ashford in December 1842, Folkestone in 1843 and Dover in February 1844. The circuitous route via Redhill reflected the company's need to share the Brighton main line's expensive works through the North Downs.

A more direct line via Orpington and Sevenoaks, authorised in 1862, opened in 1868 and shortened the route considerably. The line became famous for boat trains connecting London with cross-Channel ferries at Dover and Folkestone, and later for Eurostar services to the Channel Tunnel at Ashford International until those were rerouted to High Speed 1 in 2007.

Route

Trains depart London Charing Cross or Cannon Street, cross the Thames, and run through London Bridge, Hither Green, Grove Park, Orpington, Sevenoaks, Tonbridge, Paddock Wood, Ashford International, Folkestone and Dover Priory. The line is electrified at 750 V DC third rail and is mostly double track, with quadruple-track sections near London.

Services

Southeastern operates suburban and long-distance services, including services to Ramsgate and Hastings that branch from the main line at Tonbridge and Ashford respectively.
