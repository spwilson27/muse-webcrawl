Women in government

Women in government concerns the representation of women in legislatures, executives and judiciaries worldwide. Women's political participation expanded after suffrage victories in the late 19th and 20th centuries, but women remain underrepresented in most countries.

Representation

Globally, women hold roughly a quarter of national parliamentary seats, with the Nordic countries and Rwanda among the highest. Gender quotas — reserved seats or candidate quotas — have been adopted in many states to accelerate representation.

Women heads of state and government

Notable firsts include Sirimavo Bandaranaike of Ceylon, the world's first female prime minister (1960), Indira Gandhi, Margaret Thatcher, and many later leaders. Research examines barriers such as campaign finance, media coverage, and party recruitment, as well as the policy effects of greater representation.
