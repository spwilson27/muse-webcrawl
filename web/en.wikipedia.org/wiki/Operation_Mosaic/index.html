Operation Mosaic

Operation Mosaic was a series of two British nuclear tests conducted in the Montebello Islands in Western Australia in May and June 1956. The tests, codenamed G1 and G2, were in support of the development of thermonuclear weapons and followed the earlier Operation Hurricane test at the same site in 1952.

The tests

The first test, G1, was detonated from a tower on Trimouille Island on 16 May 1956 and produced a yield of about 15 kilotons. The second, G2, was detonated on Alpha Island on 19 June 1956. G2 was intended to test a device using lithium-6 deuteride; its yield, later measured at about 98 kilotons, was higher than predicted — announcements at the time gave a figure of 60 kilotons — making it the largest British device detonated in Australia.

Context

Mosaic formed part of the British nuclear testing programme in Australia, which also included Operation Totem at Emu Field and the major Operation Buffalo and Operation Antler series at Maralinga. The tests were later examined by the McClelland Royal Commission (1984–85), which criticised the safety and consultation arrangements of the Australian tests.
