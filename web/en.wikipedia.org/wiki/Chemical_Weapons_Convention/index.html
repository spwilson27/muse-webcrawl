Chemical Weapons Convention

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC) is an arms-control treaty administered by the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW) that prohibits the development, production, stockpiling and use of chemical weapons and requires the destruction of existing stockpiles. Opened for signature in Paris in January 1993 and in force from 29 April 1997, it has 193 parties; only Egypt, North Korea and South Sudan remain outside (Israel signed but has not ratified).

The CWC was the first multilateral treaty to ban an entire class of weapons of mass destruction under international verification (routine industry inspections and challenge inspections), completing the norm begun by the 1925 Geneva Protocol. Declared stockpiles (about 72,000 tonnes of agents) were verified destroyed by July 2023, when the United States completed destruction of its declared arsenal, the last possessor state to do so. Violations — Syria's use of sarin and chlorine after its 2013 accession, the Novichok poisonings in Salisbury (2018) and of Alexei Navalny (2020) — tested enforcement: the OPCW's attribution mechanism (created 2018) has formally blamed Syria's air force for several attacks, and treaty bodies suspended some of Syria's rights in 2021.
