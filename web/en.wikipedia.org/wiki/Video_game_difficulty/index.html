Difficulty level

In video games, the difficulty level is a setting that determines how challenging gameplay is for the player. Difficulty settings typically adjust enemy strength, numbers, and artificial intelligence; the resources available to the player such as health and ammunition; and the severity of penalties for failure. Common labels include Easy, Normal (or Medium), Hard, and sometimes humorous or thematic extremes such as "Nightmare" in the Doom series.

Design approaches

Static settings

Many games ask the player to choose a difficulty at the start, or allow it to be changed in a settings menu. Higher settings may grant enemies more health and damage, reduce checkpoints, restrict saving, or unlock special endings and achievements. Some titles rename difficulty for flavour: the Halo series uses Easy, Normal, Heroic, and Legendary, and its designers have described Heroic as the intended experience.

Dynamic adjustment

Dynamic difficulty adjustment (DDA) alters challenge in response to player performance. Valve's Left 4 Dead (2008) popularized an "AI Director" that varies enemy spawns and item placement to pace tension. Capcom's Resident Evil 4 quietly scales enemy aggression and drops based on how well the player is doing. Such systems aim to keep players in a state of flow — neither bored nor overwhelmed — though they are sometimes criticized when players notice the manipulation.

Ramping difficulty

Arcade games traditionally increased difficulty continuously to end sessions and encourage further coin insertion; Space Invaders famously speeds up as fewer aliens remain — originally a hardware artefact of the processor rendering fewer sprites. Modern roguelikes and score-attack games retain continuous ramping as a core design.

Debates

Difficulty is a recurring subject of discussion in game design and accessibility. Some designers argue that selectable difficulty preserves an intended, uniform challenge — FromSoftware's Dark Souls series is a prominent example with no difficulty settings — while others argue that options broaden access for players with disabilities or limited time. Journalist and academic discourse often distinguishes between difficulty as artistic intent and difficulty as a barrier to accessibility features such as remappable controls or assist modes, as in Celeste's Assist Mode (2018).
