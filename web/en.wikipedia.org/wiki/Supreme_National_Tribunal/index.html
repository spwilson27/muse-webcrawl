Supreme National Tribunal

The Tribunal tried senior Nazi officials responsible for crimes committed in occupied Poland.

The Supreme National Tribunal (Polish: Najwyższy Trybunał Narodowy, NTN) was a war crimes tribunal established by the Polish government in 1946 to try senior Nazi German officials and collaborators responsible for crimes committed in occupied Poland during the Second World War. It operated until 1948 and conducted seven major trials, parallel in purpose to the international proceedings at Nuremberg but focused on crimes in Poland.

Trials

  • Arthur Greiser (1946) — Reichsstatthalter of the Warthegau; the Tribunal's first trial, ending in a death sentence, carried out in Poznań.
  • Amon Göth (1946) — commandant of the Płaszów concentration camp; convicted and hanged in Kraków.
  • Albert Forster (1948) — Gauleiter of Danzig-West Prussia; sentenced to death.
  • Rudolf Höss (1947) — commandant of Auschwitz; tried in Warsaw and hanged at Auschwitz, near the camp he had commanded.
  • The Auschwitz trial (1947) — 40 members of the Auschwitz camp staff, including Arthur Liebehenschel and Maria Mandl, tried in Kraków; 23 death sentences.
  • Josef Bühler (1948) — deputy to Hans Frank in the General Government; sentenced to death.
  • The Trial of the Institute — proceedings against officials of the Institut für Deutsche Ostarbeit.

Legacy

The Tribunal's proceedings documented Nazi crimes in Poland shortly after the war and contributed evidence and legal precedents — notably on crimes against humanity and the criminal character of the occupation administration — to later Polish and international jurisprudence. Its records remain an important source for historians of the Holocaust and the occupation of Poland.
