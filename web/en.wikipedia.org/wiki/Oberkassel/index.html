Oberkassel

Oberkassel is a name borne by several places in Germany, most notably a district of Bonn in North Rhine-Westphalia, situated on the right (eastern) bank of the Rhine at the foot of the Siebengebirge hills. It is part of the city district of Beuel and was an independent municipality until its incorporation into Bonn in 1969.

Archaeology

Oberkassel is famous in archaeology for the Oberkassel double burial, discovered in 1914 by quarry workers in a basalt quarry at the Stingenberg. The grave, dating to about 14,000 years ago in the late Upper Palaeolithic (Magdalenian period), contained the skeletons of a man of about 50 and a woman of about 20–25, accompanied by carved bone and antler artefacts and the remains of a dog — among the earliest evidence for the domestic dog in Europe. Genetic studies of the Oberkassel individuals have contributed to knowledge of the population history of post-glacial Europe.

Geography

The Bonn district is primarily residential and is connected to the opposite bank of the Rhine by ferry and to central Bonn by tram and the B42 road. The surrounding basalt hills were quarried for centuries for building stone.
