Giovanni de' Bardi

Born5 February 1534, Florence, Italy
DiedSeptember 1612, Rome, Italy
OccupationComposer, writer, patron of music

Giovanni de' Bardi, Count of Vernio (1534–1612), was an Italian literary critic, writer, composer, and soldier. He is best known as the host and patron of the Florentine Camerata, the circle of musicians and scholars whose experiments with monody laid the groundwork for opera.

The Florentine Camerata

From the 1570s, Bardi's palace in Florence hosted gatherings of figures including Vincenzo Galilei, Giulio Caccini, and Jacopo Peri. Their discussions of ancient Greek drama and music led to the development of the recitative style and the earliest operas, including Peri's Dafne (1598) and Euridice (1600).

Other work

Bardi composed madrigals and contributed to the famous Florentine intermedi of 1589. He also wrote a treatise on the Florentine game of calcio, an early form of football, and served the Medici court in military and diplomatic roles before moving to Rome in the service of Pope Clement VIII.
