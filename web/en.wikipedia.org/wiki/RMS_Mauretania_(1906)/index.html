RMS Mauretania was a British ocean liner designed by Leonard Peskett and built by Swan Hunter & Wigham Richardson on the River Tyne for the Cunard Line. Launched in 1906, she was the sister ship of the Lusitania and, with her, one of the largest and fastest liners of her era. She was named after the ancient Roman province of Mauretania in North Africa.

The British government subsidised her construction on condition that she be built to Admiralty specifications for conversion to an armed merchant cruiser in wartime. Her steam turbine machinery made her the fastest liner in the world: she captured the Blue Riband for the fastest transatlantic crossing in 1909 and held the record for twenty years, a feat unmatched by any other ship.

Career

Mauretania made her maiden voyage from Liverpool to New York in November 1907. She quickly established herself as a favourite of transatlantic passengers, noted for luxurious interiors in period styles and for public rooms panelled in fine woods.

During the First World War she served as a troopship (HMT Mauretania) and briefly as a hospital ship, painted white with red crosses, before carrying Canadian and American troops. After the war she returned to passenger service, surviving a fire in 1921 and being converted to oil fuel. In the late 1920s she faced competition from newer German and Italian liners, and during the Depression she was increasingly used for cruising. Cunard withdrew her in 1934 following the Cunard–White Star merger, and she was scrapped at Rosyth in 1935.

Legacy

Mauretania was among the most famous ocean liners ever built, remembered for her speed record and elegant Edwardian interiors. A later Cunard liner, launched in 1938, also bore the name Mauretania.
