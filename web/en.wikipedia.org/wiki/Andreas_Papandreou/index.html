Andreas Papandreou

Andreas Georgiou Papandreou (5 February 1919 – 23 June 1996) was a Greek economist and politician who served as Prime Minister of Greece from 1981 to 1989 and again from 1993 to 1996. He founded the Panhellenic Socialist Movement (PASOK) and was one of the most influential figures in modern Greek politics.

Born5 February 1919, Chios, Greece
Died23 June 1996, Athens, Greece

Early life and career

Papandreou was the son of Georgios Papandreou, himself a former prime minister. He studied economics at the University of Athens and later at Harvard University, where he earned a doctorate, and taught economics in the United States, including at the University of California, Berkeley. He returned to Greece in the 1960s and entered politics in his father's Centre Union party.

Prime minister

After the fall of the military junta, Papandreou founded PASOK in 1974 and led it to victory in the 1981 elections. His governments pursued socialist policies, expanded the welfare state, recognised the wartime resistance, and took a distinctive, often Eurosceptic and anti-American line in foreign policy. He returned to power in 1993 but resigned due to ill health in January 1996, dying later that year.
