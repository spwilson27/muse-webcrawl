Media of Israel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The media of Israel consists of newspapers, television, radio, and online outlets operating primarily in Hebrew, with significant media also in Arabic, Russian, and English. Israel has a long tradition of a vigorous and pluralistic press, though media content relating to security matters is subject to military censorship.

Newspapers

Major Hebrew-language dailies include Yedioth Ahronoth, long the country's best-selling paper; Haaretz, a broadsheet known for its liberal editorial line; Maariv; and the free daily Israel Hayom, founded in 2007, which became the most widely distributed newspaper. The English-language Jerusalem Post serves an international readership, while Globes covers business news.

Television and radio

Public broadcasting was provided for decades by the Israel Broadcasting Authority (IBA), replaced in 2017 by the Israeli Public Broadcasting Corporation, known as Kan, which runs the Kan 11 television channel and several radio stations. Commercial television is led by Keshet 12 and Reshet 13. Galei Tzahal (Army Radio) is a nationwide station operated by the Israel Defense Forces and is among the most listened-to radio outlets.

Freedom of the press

Israel is generally described by press-freedom monitors as having a free and lively media environment within its borders, while noting constraints from military censorship of security reporting and periodic restrictions affecting Palestinian journalists in the occupied territories. Reporters Without Borders has ranked Israel in the middle range of its World Press Freedom Index.

Online media

Leading news websites include Ynet (affiliated with Yedioth Ahronoth), Walla!, Haaretz's Hebrew and English sites, and the Times of Israel in English.
