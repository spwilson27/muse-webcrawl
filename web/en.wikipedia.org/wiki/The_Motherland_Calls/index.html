The Motherland Calls

Location: Mamayev Kurgan, Volgograd, Russia
Sculptor: Yevgeny Vuchetich
Completed: 1967
Height: 85 m (279 ft)

The Motherland Calls (Russian: Родина-мать зовёт!, Rodina-mat' zovyot!) is a monumental statue on Mamayev Kurgan in Volgograd, Russia, commemorating the Battle of Stalingrad (1942–43). Designed by sculptor Yevgeny Vuchetich and engineer Nikolai Nikitin, it was unveiled in 1967 as the centrepiece of the memorial complex "To the Heroes of the Battle of Stalingrad."

Description

The statue depicts an allegorical female figure — the Motherland — striding forward with a raised sword, calling her sons to battle. At 85 metres from the tip of the sword to the base of the plinth, it was the tallest statue in the world when completed and remains the tallest statue of a woman and the tallest in Europe. The figure itself is 52 metres tall; the sword adds 33 metres.

Construction

The statue was built in prestressed concrete, hollow, with internal cells held in tension by steel cables. Its dynamic pose — one foot forward, drapery billowing — made it an engineering landmark of Soviet monumental art. The Mamayev Kurgan complex also contains mass and individual graves of defenders of Stalingrad, including Marshal Vasily Chuikov.
