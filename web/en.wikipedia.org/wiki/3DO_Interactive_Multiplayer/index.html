The 3DO Interactive Multiplayer is a home video game console platform developed by The 3DO Company, founded by Trip Hawkins. Rather than manufacturing the hardware itself, 3DO licensed the design to companies including Panasonic, Sanyo and GoldStar. The first model, Panasonic's FZ-1, launched in North America in October 1993.

Business model

The 3DO was conceived as an industry standard for interactive media, like VHS for video. Manufacturers paid for the hardware business while 3DO earned low per-game royalties, encouraging cheap software but forcing hardware prices high: the FZ-1 launched at $699, far more than competing consoles sold at a loss. This pricing severely limited adoption despite advanced CD-ROM-based hardware and titles such as Crash 'n Burn, Gex, The Need for Speed (which debuted on 3DO), Road Rash and Star Control II.

Decline

Price cuts and the GoldStar model brought the cost down, but the arrival of the cheaper Sony PlayStation and Sega Saturn in 1995 ended the 3DO's prospects. Production ceased in 1996 after roughly two million units were sold, and the planned M2 successor was sold to Matsushita and never released as a console.
