List of conspiracy theories

A conspiracy theory is an explanation for an event or situation that invokes a conspiracy by sinister and powerful groups, often political in motivation, when other explanations are more probable. This article lists notable conspiracy theories that have attracted substantial public attention. Inclusion records that the theory is widely known, not that it is true; most of the claims below are rejected by the relevant scientific, journalistic, or official consensus.

Assassinations and disappearances

  • John F. Kennedy assassination theories — claims of a second gunman or involvement by the CIA, the Mafia, Cuba, or others, beyond Lee Harvey Oswald acting alone.
  • Princess Diana — theories that her 1997 death in Paris was an arranged killing rather than a car crash.
  • Malaysia Airlines Flight 370 — numerous theories about the aircraft's 2014 disappearance.
  • Avril Lavigne / Paul McCartney ("Paul is dead") — celebrity replacement and death-hoax theories.

Government and politics

  • 9/11 conspiracy theories — claims that the US government knew of, or carried out, the September 11 attacks as a "false flag".
  • New World Order — the belief that a secretive elite is conspiring to rule the world through an authoritarian one-world government.
  • Illuminati — claims that the short-lived 18th-century Bavarian society secretly survives and controls world events.
  • Deep state, QAnon, and Pizzagate — 21st-century political conspiracy movements.
  • Birtherism — the false claim that Barack Obama was not born in the United States.

Science, technology, and medicine

  • Moon landing conspiracy theories — claims the Apollo landings were staged by NASA.
  • Flat Earth — the belief that the Earth is a flat disc, and that evidence of a globe is fabricated.
  • Chemtrails — the claim that aircraft condensation trails are chemical or biological agents sprayed for secret purposes.
  • Vaccines and autism — the discredited claim, originating in a retracted 1998 paper, linking the MMR vaccine to autism.
  • Climate change denial — claims that global warming is a hoax or fraud.
  • 5G and microchip theories — claims linking mobile networks or vaccines to surveillance microchips.
  • HAARP — claims the Alaskan ionospheric research facility is a weather-control weapon.

Extraterrestrials and the unexplained

  • Roswell incident — claims of a crashed alien craft and government cover-up in 1947 New Mexico.
  • Area 51 — theories about alien technology stored at the Nevada base.
  • Ancient astronauts — the claim that extraterrestrials visited Earth in antiquity and shaped human civilisation.
  • Reptilian humanoids — the claim, popularised by David Icke, that shape-shifting reptilian aliens control Earth.
