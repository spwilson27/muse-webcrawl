Von Neumann universe

In set theory and related branches of mathematics, the von Neumann universe, or von Neumann hierarchy of sets, denoted V, is the class of hereditary well-founded sets. It is constructed by a transfinite recursion over the ordinals: V0 = ∅; Vα+1 = the power set of Vα; and for a limit ordinal λ, Vλ is the union of all previous stages. The universe V is the union of all stages.

Overview

The hierarchy was introduced by John von Neumann in 1929. The axiom of foundation (regularity) is equivalent to the statement that every set belongs to V, so within Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory (ZFC) the von Neumann universe comprises all sets. The first levels are small: V0 has 0 elements, V1 has 1, V2 has 2, V3 has 4, V4 has 16, and V5 has 65,536; growth beyond that is tetrational.

Rank and applications

The stage at which a set first appears defines its rank. Sets of finite rank, the union Vω, are the hereditarily finite sets and model arithmetic. Stages such as Vω+1 and higher model progressively stronger fragments of set theory, and the hierarchy is used in relative consistency proofs, reflecting the idea of building all sets "from the bottom up" out of the empty set.
