Mille Miglia

The Mille Miglia ("Thousand Miles") was an open-road endurance motor race held in Italy from 1927 to 1957. Cars raced roughly 1,000 miles (about 1,600 km) from Brescia to Rome and back over public roads, making it one of the most famous and dangerous races of its era.

History

Founded by young Brescia aristocrats Aymo Maggi and Franco Mazzotti with Renzo Castagnetto, the race made stars of drivers such as Tazio Nuvolari and Stirling Moss, whose record 1955 run with navigator Denis Jenkinson averaged nearly 98 mph. After fatal accidents in 1957, including the crash that killed Alfonso de Portago and spectators, the race was banned.

Legacy

Since 1977 the Mille Miglia has been revived as a regularity rally — the Mille Miglia Storica — for cars of the types that contested the original event.
