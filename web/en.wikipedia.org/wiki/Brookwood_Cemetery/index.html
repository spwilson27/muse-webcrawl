Brookwood Cemetery

Brookwood Cemetery, opened in 1854 near Woking in Surrey, England, is the largest cemetery in the United Kingdom, covering about 500 acres (202 ha). It was established by the London Necropolis Company to relieve London's overcrowded churchyards, and was famously served by a dedicated funeral railway from a terminus near Waterloo station — the London Necropolis Railway.

History

By the early 1850s London's population growth and cholera epidemics had made its burial grounds a public health crisis. The London Necropolis and National Mausoleum Company bought land at Brookwood on the cheap heathland of Woking Common, and the cemetery was consecrated in November 1854. Coffins and mourners travelled from the Necropolis station in London by special train to two stations within the cemetery, one for Anglicans and one for Nonconformists; the service ran until 1941, when bomb damage ended it.

Sections

Brookwood is divided into plots for many faiths and communities, including what was Britain's first Muslim burial ground (established 1917 by Lord Headley), a Parsi (Zoroastrian) section founded in 1862, and plots for Chelsea Pensioners and various religious orders. Adjacent Brookwood Military Cemetery, administered by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission, is the largest CWGC cemetery in the UK, with over 5,000 war burials.

Notable burials

Burials include the painter John Singer Sargent, writer Rebecca West, former Iranian prime minister's circle members, and Edward the Martyr, whose relics were enshrined here for a period in the twentieth century.
