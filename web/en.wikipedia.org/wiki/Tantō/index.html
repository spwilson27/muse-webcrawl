Tantō

Tantō (短刀)
Type: Short blade / dagger
Origin: Japan
Blade length: 15–30 cm (one shaku or less)

A tantō is a single- or double-edged Japanese short blade with a length between 15 and 30 cm. It was carried by the samurai class of feudal Japan, primarily as a weapon but also in ceremonial contexts.

History

The tantō arose in the Heian period and flourished in the Kamakura period, when renowned smiths forged fine examples for close combat and honourable suicide (seppuku). In the Edo period it was worn with the katana and wakizashi in the daishō pairing by samurai; women also carried a smaller variant, the kaiken, in their obi for self-defence.

Types and construction

Forms include the yoroi-dōshi armour-piercer, hamidashi, aikuchi (without a guard), and kaiken. Like other Japanese blades, tantō are made of tamahagane steel, differentially hardened to produce a visible hamon temper line, and mounted in a shirasaya storage scabbard or formal koshirae fittings.
