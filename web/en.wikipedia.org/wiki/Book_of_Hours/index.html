Book of Hours

A book of hours is a Christian devotional book popular in the Middle Ages. It is the most common type of surviving medieval illuminated manuscript. Each book is unique in content but all contain a collection of texts, prayers, and psalms, along with appropriate illustrations, to form a convenient reference for those following the rules of life in the Christian Church.

Structure

The core of a book of hours is the Little Office of the Blessed Virgin Mary, a cycle of prayers to be recited at the canonical hours of the day. Typical contents also include a calendar of feast days, extracts from the Gospels, the Hours of the Cross and of the Holy Spirit, the Penitential Psalms, the Litany of the Saints, and the Office of the Dead. Deluxe examples, such as the Très Riches Heures du Duc de Berry, contain celebrated cycles of miniatures.

History

Books of hours developed from the psalter and breviary in the 13th century, as laypeople sought books for private devotion. They were produced in enormous numbers in France and Flanders in the 14th and 15th centuries and ranged from modest volumes to luxury commissions for royalty. Their popularity declined with the spread of printed prayer books and the upheavals of the Reformation.
