Peter Maurin

Born: Aristide Pierre Maurin, 9 May 1877, Oultet, Lozère, France

Died: 15 May 1949 (aged 72), Newburgh, New York, U.S.

Known for: Co-founding the Catholic Worker movement

Aristide Pierre "Peter" Maurin (9 May 1877 – 15 May 1949) was a French Catholic social activist, theologian and De La Salle Brother who founded the Catholic Worker Movement in 1933 together with Dorothy Day.

Life

Born into a poor farming family in southern France — one of 23 children — Maurin joined the Institute of the Brothers of the Christian Schools and taught before emigrating, first to Canada and then to the United States around 1909. He worked as a French tutor, labourer and teacher, living in voluntary poverty, and was influenced by the papal social encyclicals, personalism, and thinkers such as Emmanuel Mounier.

The Catholic Worker

In December 1932 Maurin met Dorothy Day, a journalist and recent Catholic convert, and pressed on her his programme: a newspaper to propagate Catholic social teaching, houses of hospitality for the poor, and farming communes. The first issue of The Catholic Worker newspaper appeared on 1 May 1933, sold for a penny — a price it retains. Maurin expressed his ideas in short didactic poems he called Easy Essays, advocating "a new society within the shell of the old", built on the works of mercy, voluntary poverty, pacifism and a return to the land through what he called the Green Revolution.

Maurin died in 1949 at the Catholic Worker farm at Newburgh, New York. Dorothy Day called him "a saint of our time", and a cause for his canonisation has been promoted by admirers.
