Aviation law

Aviation law is the branch of law that concerns flight, air travel, and associated legal and business concerns. Some of its area of concern overlaps with admiralty law, and because of the diverse and international nature of air travel, much of aviation law is international in character, governed by treaties and by the work of the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO).

International framework

The cornerstone of international aviation law is the Chicago Convention of 1944, which established the ICAO and affirmed that every state has complete and exclusive sovereignty over the airspace above its territory. The Convention and its annexes set standards for airworthiness, licensing, rules of the air, and accident investigation. Scheduled international services additionally depend on bilateral air service agreements granting traffic rights, the so-called "freedoms of the air".

Liability and regulation

Passenger liability for injury or death in international carriage is governed by the Warsaw Convention (1929) as modernised by the Montreal Convention (1999), which imposes a two-tier liability regime on carriers. At the national level, authorities such as the Federal Aviation Administration (United States) and the European Union Aviation Safety Agency regulate certification, operations, airspace and licensing. Aviation law also covers aircraft finance and leasing (notably under the Cape Town Convention of 2001), airport law, environmental controls on noise and emissions, competition, and criminal offences involving aircraft, including hijacking under the Tokyo, Hague and Montreal conventions.
