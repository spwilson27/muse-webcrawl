Kimberley is the capital and largest city of the Northern Cape province of South Africa. It is famous as the centre of the 19th-century diamond rush that transformed South Africa, and as the site of the Big Hole, one of the largest hand-dug excavations in the world.

History

Diamonds were discovered on farms in the area in the late 1860s, most famously on the De Beers farm in 1871, where the "New Rush" diggings drew thousands of fortune-seekers. The settlement, first called New Rush, was renamed Kimberley in 1873 after John Wodehouse, 1st Earl of Kimberley, the British Colonial Secretary. The rival diamond companies were progressively consolidated, culminating in Cecil Rhodes's De Beers Consolidated Mines in 1888, which controlled the world's diamond supply.

Kimberley was among the first South African towns to receive electric street lighting (1882) and had an early stock exchange. During the Second Boer War the city endured the Siege of Kimberley (October 1899 – February 1900), during which Rhodes himself was besieged in the town before its relief by British forces.

The Big Hole

The Big Hole is an open-pit and underground diamond mine considered one of the deepest cavities excavated by hand. Between 1871 and 1914 some 22.5 million tonnes of earth were removed, yielding about 2,722 kilograms of diamonds. The pit is roughly 215 metres deep with a surface area of about 17 hectares. A museum complex now surrounds the site.

Geography and climate

Kimberley lies on the Highveld at an elevation of about 1,184 metres, near the confluence of the Vaal and Orange river systems. It has a semi-arid climate with hot summers, frequent thunderstorms, and cool, dry winters. The Kamfers Dam just north of the city supports an important breeding population of lesser flamingos.

Economy and transport

Diamond mining declined in importance after the closure of the open pit in 1914, though underground mining and the reprocessing of tailings continued. The city is a regional service and administrative centre, served by Kimberley Airport and the N1, N8 and N12 highways, and by rail lines linking the Cape with Gauteng.
