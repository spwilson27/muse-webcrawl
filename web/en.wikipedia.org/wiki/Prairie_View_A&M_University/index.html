Prairie View A&M University (PVAMU or PV) is a public historically Black land-grant university in Prairie View, Texas. Founded in 1876, it is one of the oldest public institutions of higher education in the state and the second-oldest public university in Texas, behind Texas A&M University. It is a member institution of the Texas A&M University System and is designated a historically Black college and university (HBCU).

History

The university was established under the Texas Constitution of 1876 as a land-grant institution for the education of Black Texans, in compliance with the Morrill Act. It opened on the site of a former plantation in Waller County. Under the leadership of principals such as L. C. Anderson and his brother E. H. Anderson, the school grew from a small normal school into a full university offering graduate and professional degrees. In 1945 the Texas Legislature changed its name to Prairie View A&M College, and in 1973 it became Prairie View A&M University.

Academics

PVAMU offers bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees through colleges including the Roy G. Perry College of Engineering, the College of Agriculture and Human Sciences, the College of Nursing, and the Marvin D. and June Samuel Brailsford College of Arts and Sciences. The university is particularly noted for producing engineers, nurses, and educators, and its graduates include a large share of the Black engineers trained in Texas.

Athletics

The Prairie View A&M Panthers compete in NCAA Division I as members of the Southwestern Athletic Conference (SWAC). The university's marching band, the Marching Storm, is nationally recognized. See also Texas A&M University and historically Black colleges and universities.
