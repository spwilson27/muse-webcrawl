Peninsular War

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Peninsular War (1807/1808–1814) was the military conflict fought in the Iberian Peninsula by Spain, Portugal and the United Kingdom against the invading and occupying forces of the French Empire during the Napoleonic Wars. It began when France and Spain invaded Portugal (1807) and Napoleon then seized the Spanish throne for his brother Joseph (1808), provoking a popular uprising — the Madrid rising of 2 May 1808, immortalised by Goya's paintings The Second of May and The Third of May 1808.

Course of the war

The war fused conventional campaigns with savage guerrilla warfare — the word guerrilla ("little war") entered world languages from this conflict — by Spanish irregulars who tied down hundreds of thousands of French troops. British and Portuguese forces under Arthur Wellesley (the future Duke of Wellington) held Portugal behind the Lines of Torres Vedras, then ground forward: Salamanca (1812), the storming of Ciudad Rodrigo and Badajoz, Vitoria (1813), and finally across the Pyrenees into France itself (1814). Napoleon called Spain his "ulcer" — a running sore of men and money that, with the Russian catastrophe of 1812, broke his empire. The war devastated Iberia, destroyed Spain's American empire indirectly (independence movements flourished while Spain was occupied), and redrew Spanish politics for a century.
