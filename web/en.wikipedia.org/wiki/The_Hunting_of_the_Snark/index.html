The Hunting of the Snark, subtitled An Agony in 8 Fits, is a nonsense poem by the English writer Lewis Carroll (Charles Lutwidge Dodgson), published in 1876 with illustrations by Henry Holiday. It is considered a classic of literary nonsense alongside Carroll's Jabberwocky.

Synopsis

The poem recounts a sea voyage by an improbable crew — a Bellman, a Boots, a Bonnet-maker, a Barrister, a Broker, a Billiard-marker, a Banker, a Beaver, a Butcher, and a Baker — hunting the mysterious Snark with the aid of a perfectly blank map of the ocean. The crew hunts "with thimbles, with care, with forks and hope," as the refrain advises. In the final fit the Baker at last encounters the Snark, which proves to be a Boojum, and he vanishes — "For the Snark was a Boojum, you see."

Background and legacy

Carroll said the poem began with its final line, which came to him while walking. Its portmanteau words (including "frumious" and "manxome") and unresolved mystery have inspired numerous interpretations, adaptations, and a 1980s stage musical by Mike Batt.
