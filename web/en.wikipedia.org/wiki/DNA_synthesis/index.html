DNA synthesis

DNA synthesis is the natural or artificial creation of deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) molecules. DNA is a macromolecule made up of nucleotide subunits — adenine, guanine, cytosine, and thymine. In nature, DNA synthesis occurs during DNA replication, in which a double-stranded template is copied by DNA polymerases before cell division. In the laboratory, artificial gene synthesis builds DNA molecules of designed sequence from chemical precursors.

DNA replication

In cells, DNA synthesis is carried out by a replisome of enzymes. Helicase unwinds the double helix, primase lays down short RNA primers, and DNA polymerase extends the new strand in the 5′ to 3′ direction, continuously on the leading strand and in Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand. Proofreading exonuclease activity and mismatch repair keep error rates near one mistake per billion base pairs.

Reverse transcription

Retroviruses such as HIV synthesize DNA from an RNA template using the enzyme reverse transcriptase — an exception to the simplest form of the central dogma. Telomerase likewise extends chromosome ends by reverse transcription of its internal RNA template.

Artificial gene synthesis

Chemical DNA synthesis, typically by the phosphoramidite method, assembles oligonucleotides base by base on a solid support. Short synthetic oligos are then stitched together — by overlap extension PCR or Gibson assembly — to form genes and even whole genomes. Costs have fallen by orders of magnitude since the 1980s, making mail-order synthetic genes a routine commodity; in 2010 researchers at the J. Craig Venter Institute booted up a cell controlled by a chemically synthesized genome of over one million base pairs.

Applications

Synthetic DNA underpins molecular cloning, site-directed mutagenesis, DNA sequencing primers and probes, synthetic biology, gene therapy research, and DNA data storage. Because synthetic sequences can be used to recreate pathogens, synthesis providers screen orders against lists of regulated organisms under international biosecurity guidance.
