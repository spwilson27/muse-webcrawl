Orderic Vitalis

Orderic Vitalis (Latin: Ordericus Vitalis; 16 February 1075 – c. 1142) was an English chronicler and Benedictine monk who wrote one of the great contemporary chronicles of 11th- and 12th-century Normandy and Anglo-Norman England. His Historia Ecclesiastica, in thirteen books, is an indispensable source for the social and political history of the period.

Life

Orderic was born in Atcham, Shropshire, the eldest son of Odelerius of Orléans, a priest in the household of Roger de Montgomery, 1st Earl of Shrewsbury, and a Welsh or English mother. At the age of five he was sent to school at the church of Saints Peter and Paul in Shrewsbury. When he was ten, his father gave him as a child oblate to the Norman abbey of Saint-Évroul in the forest of Ouche, with a gift of thirty marks. Orderic arrived in Normandy in September 1085 knowing no French, and spent the rest of his life at Saint-Évroul, where he was ordained subdeacon, then deacon in 1093, and priest in 1107. He took the monastic name Vitalis. Though he lived as a Norman monk, he wrote with feeling of himself as English — "Vitalis the Englishman" (Vitalis Angligena).

The Historia Ecclesiastica

Orderic began the Historia Ecclesiastica at the command of his abbot around 1114, initially as a history of his abbey, but it grew into a vast general history of his times, completed in thirteen books by about 1141. Books 3–6 preserve a copy of William of Poitiers' Gesta Guillelmi; the later books cover the Norman Conquest's aftermath, the reigns of William Rufus and Henry I, the First Crusade, and the affairs of Normandy, England, France, and southern Italy. The chronicle is rich in anecdote, character sketches, and monastic detail, though its arrangement is often unchronological. Orderic wrote in a monastery well placed on routes used by knights and pilgrims, gathering testimony from travellers, and preserving material found nowhere else, including his famous account of the White Ship disaster of 1120.
