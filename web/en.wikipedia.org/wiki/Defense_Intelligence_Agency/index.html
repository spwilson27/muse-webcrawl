Defense Intelligence Agency
Founded: 1 October 1961
Headquarters: DIA Headquarters, Joint Base Anacostia–Bolling, Washington, D.C.
Parent: U.S. Department of Defense

The Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) is an intelligence agency and combat support agency of the United States Department of Defense. Established in 1961 under Secretary of Defense Robert McNamara, the DIA is the nation's primary manager and producer of foreign military intelligence, providing assessments to policymakers, warfighters and defense planners.

Role and organisation

The DIA coordinates the intelligence work of the military services' intelligence components and operates the Defense Attaché System, the Defense Clandestine Service, and the Joint Worldwide Intelligence Communications System (JWICS). It is led by a three-star general or admiral serving as Director. The agency is headquartered at Joint Base Anacostia–Bolling in Washington, D.C., with major facilities including the Defense Intelligence Analysis Center.

History

Created to eliminate duplication among the service intelligence branches, the DIA grew during the Cold War and expanded significantly after the September 11 attacks. It is a member of the United States Intelligence Community.
