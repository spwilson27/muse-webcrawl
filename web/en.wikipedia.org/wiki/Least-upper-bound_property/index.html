Least-upper-bound property

The least-upper-bound property (also called the completeness axiom or supremum property) is a fundamental property of the real numbers: every nonempty subset of the real numbers that is bounded above has a least upper bound (supremum) that is itself a real number.

Significance

The property distinguishes the real numbers ℝ from the rational numbers ℚ. For example, the set of rational numbers whose square is less than 2 is bounded above in ℚ but has no least upper bound in ℚ, because its supremum in ℝ is √2, which is irrational. In ℚ the set has "a hole" where the supremum should be.

The least-upper-bound property underpins many central theorems of real analysis, including the intermediate value theorem, the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem, the extreme value theorem and the equivalence of the Cauchy and monotone convergence criteria. In the axiomatic construction of the reals, an ordered field satisfying the property is unique up to isomorphism; equivalent formulations of completeness include Dedekind completeness, used in the construction of ℝ via Dedekind cuts. There is a dual greatest-lower-bound property concerning infima, which follows from the least-upper-bound property applied to negated sets.
