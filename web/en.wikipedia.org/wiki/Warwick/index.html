Warwick (/ˈwɒrɪk/ WORR-ik) is a market town, civil parish and the county town of Warwickshire in the Warwick District of England, adjacent to the River Avon, 11 miles (18 km) south of Coventry and 19 miles (31 km) south-east of Birmingham. At the 2021 Census, the population was 32,719. The town lies immediately west of Leamington Spa, with which it forms part of a contiguous urban area.

History

There has been human activity at Warwick as early as the Neolithic era. In 914 AD, the Anglo-Saxon burh of Warwick was established on the site by Æthelflæd, Lady of the Mercians, to defend against Danish invaders. After the Norman Conquest, William the Conqueror founded Warwick Castle in 1068; the castle, developed by the earls of Warwick, dominated the town through the Middle Ages. The Earl of Warwick — notably Richard Neville, the "Kingmaker" — played a central role in the Wars of the Roses.

The Great Fire of Warwick in 1694 destroyed much of the medieval town, and the centre was rebuilt in the fine stone Georgian architecture that survives today. Notably the town's churches of St Mary and St Nicholas were rebuilt, and Lord Leycester Hospital, a group of 14th-century timber-framed buildings, survived the fire.

Landmarks and economy

Warwick Castle is one of England's most visited historic attractions. Other landmarks include the Collegiate Church of St Mary, with the Beauchamp Chapel and one of the tallest church towers in England, and the Lord Leycester Hospital. Warwick Racecourse hosts flat and National Hunt racing. Although the University of Warwick carries the town's name, its campus is actually in Coventry. The town's economy is based on services, tourism, engineering and technology companies in nearby business parks.
