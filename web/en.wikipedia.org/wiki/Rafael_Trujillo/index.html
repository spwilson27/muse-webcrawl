Rafael Trujillo

Rafael Trujillo, ruler of the Dominican Republic from 1930 to 1961.

Rafael Leónidas Trujillo Molina (24 October 1891 – 30 May 1961) was a Dominican military officer and dictator who ruled the Dominican Republic from 1930 until his assassination in 1961. His regime, known as the Era of Trujillo, was one of the longest-lasting and most repressive dictatorships in Latin American history.

Rise to power

Trujillo joined the National Army created during the United States occupation of the Dominican Republic and rose rapidly through its ranks. After leading the army in a revolt against President Horacio Vásquez, he was elected president in 1930 in elections marred by intimidation, and thereafter ruled directly or through puppet presidents for three decades.

Rule

Trujillo established a totalitarian state built around a personality cult — the capital was renamed Ciudad Trujillo — and a secret police, the SIM, while his family amassed a fortune controlling much of the economy. His regime ordered the 1937 Parsley massacre, in which thousands of Haitians were killed along the border, and the 1960 murder of the Mirabal sisters, which galvanised opposition. Although long supported by the United States as an anti-communist ally, his excesses eventually alienated Washington.

Death and legacy

Trujillo was assassinated on 30 May 1961 by Dominican conspirators. His regime is remembered for its brutality, corruption, and the suppression of civil liberties, though also for infrastructure development and national solvency. See also Dominican Republic and Mirabal sisters.
