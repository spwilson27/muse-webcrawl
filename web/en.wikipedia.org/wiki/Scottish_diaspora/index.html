Scottish diaspora

The Scottish diaspora consists of Scots and their descendants who emigrated from Scotland. Tens of millions of people worldwide claim Scottish ancestry, with the largest populations in the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand and England. Scottish emigration occurred in successive waves from the 17th century onwards, driven by economic change, religious conflict, the Highland Clearances, and the search for opportunity in the British Empire and beyond.

Early emigration

Scots served as soldiers, merchants and settlers across Europe from medieval times, notably in France, Poland, Scandinavia and the Netherlands. In the 17th century, large numbers settled in Ulster during the Plantation, forming the Ulster Scots community from which many Scots-Irish Americans descend. Others were transported or emigrated to the Caribbean and American colonies.

18th and 19th centuries

After the Jacobite rising of 1745 and during the Highland Clearances, whole communities left for Canada — especially Nova Scotia ("New Scotland"), Prince Edward Island and Glengarry County, Ontario — and for North Carolina. In the 19th century Scots became prominent in the settlement of Australia and New Zealand, in Canadian business and politics, and in the administration, engineering and missions of the British Empire.

Cultural legacy

The diaspora carried Presbyterian churches, pipe bands, Highland games, Burns suppers and St Andrew's societies around the world. Scottish names, place names and institutions remain prominent in former settler colonies, and interest in Scottish ancestry and tartan heritage is a major part of Scotland's modern cultural tourism.
