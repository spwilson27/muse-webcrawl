2023 SAG-AFTRA strike

The 2023 SAG-AFTRA strike was a labour strike by the Screen Actors Guild – American Federation of Television and Radio Artists (SAG-AFTRA) against the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers (AMPTP), lasting from 14 July to 9 November 2023 — 118 days, the longest actors' strike against Hollywood film and television studios in the union's history. Together with the concurrent Writers Guild strike, it effectively shut down scripted production in the United States.

Background and issues

Negotiations over the union's TV/Theatrical contracts broke down over economics transformed by streaming: actors sought a share of streaming revenue and higher residuals, general wage increases to offset inflation, regulation of self-taped auditions, and — most prominently — consent and compensation rules for the use of artificial intelligence to replicate performers' likenesses, especially for background actors. SAG-AFTRA's board approved the strike on 13 July after the contract expired; union president Fran Drescher's fiery press conference became a defining public moment of the dispute.

Course and settlement

Striking actors were barred from filming, premieres, press junkets and awards campaigning, delaying film releases and the Emmy Awards. After the writers settled in late September, talks with the actors resumed; a tentative agreement was reached on 8 November 2023 and the strike ended at 12:01 a.m. on 9 November. The deal, valued by the union at over $1 billion, included wage increases, a streaming participation bonus, and AI consent/compensation guardrails. Members ratified it in December with about 78% voting yes.
