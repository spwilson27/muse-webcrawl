Blohm & Voss BV 141

Role: Reconnaissance aircraft
Manufacturer: Blohm & Voss
Designer: Richard Vogt
First flight: 25 February 1938
Status: Not accepted for service

The Blohm & Voss BV 141 was a German World War II tactical reconnaissance aircraft notable for its striking structural asymmetry. Designed by Richard Vogt, it placed the crew in a glazed gondola on the starboard side of the fuselage, offsetting the single engine and tail boom.

Design

The asymmetric layout gave the crew an unobstructed field of view for observation while torque and P-factor effects were countered by the configuration. Prototypes flew well, and the design progressed to the enlarged BV 141B with a more powerful BMW 801 engine.

Outcome

Despite satisfactory handling, the Luftwaffe preferred the twin-engine Focke-Wulf Fw 189, and the BV 141 never entered operational service. Around 38 aircraft were built in total.
