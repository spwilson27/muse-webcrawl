Molly Weasley (née Prewett) is a fictional character in J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter novel series. She is the matriarch of the Weasley family, the wife of Arthur Weasley, and the mother of seven children: Bill, Charlie, Percy, Fred, George, Ron and Ginny. Molly is portrayed by Julie Walters in the film adaptations.

Character development and role

Molly first appears in Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone (1997), helping Harry Potter through the barrier at Platform Nine and Three-Quarters at King's Cross station. She becomes a surrogate mother figure to the orphaned Harry throughout the series, welcoming him into her home, the Burrow, and famously knitting him jumpers at Christmas along with her own children.

A plump, kind and fiercely protective woman, Molly is nevertheless a formidable witch. A member of the Order of the Phoenix, she lost her brothers Fabian and Gideon Prewett, who were murdered by Death Eaters during the First Wizarding War. In the final novel, Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows (2007), she duels and kills the Death Eater Bellatrix Lestrange during the Battle of Hogwarts, after Bellatrix threatens her daughter Ginny — a scene in which she delivers the celebrated line, "Not my daughter, you bitch!"

Family and background

Born Molly Prewett into a pure-blood wizarding family, she attended Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry, where she was sorted into Gryffindor, and eloped with Arthur Weasley shortly after leaving school. The couple settled at the Burrow in Devon. Financially modest, the family is known for its warmth and its hand-me-down possessions, and Molly is famed for her cooking and for domestic enchantments, including a magical clock whose hands point to the location or state of each family member.

Reception

Molly Weasley is frequently cited as one of the series' most beloved maternal figures, embodying the theme that love — and particularly a mother's protective love — is the most powerful magic in Rowling's world. Julie Walters' portrayal across all eight films was widely praised.
