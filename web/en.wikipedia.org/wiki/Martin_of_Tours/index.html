Martin of Tours (Latin: Martinus Turonensis; c. 316 – 8 November 397) was the third bishop of Tours in Gaul. He is one of the most familiar and recognisable Christian saints in France, heralded as the patron saint of the Third Republic, and patron of soldiers, tailors and beggars. Born in Pannonia (present-day Hungary), he served as a cavalry soldier in the Roman army before becoming a monk and, reluctantly, bishop of Tours in 371.

Life

Martin's biography, the Life of St. Martin by his contemporary Sulpicius Severus, is one of the earliest and most influential works of hagiography. Martin, the son of a Roman tribune, was enrolled in the army as a youth. While stationed at Amiens, he famously cut his military cloak in two with his sword to share it with a shivering beggar; that night, tradition relates, he dreamt of Christ wearing the half-cloak. He was baptised and, after obtaining his discharge, became a disciple of Hilary of Poitiers.

Martin founded a hermitage at Ligugé — often considered the first monastery in Gaul — and later the great abbey of Marmoutier near Tours. In 371 he was acclaimed bishop of Tours, an office he accepted unwillingly and exercised while continuing an austere monastic life. He was a zealous evangeliser of the countryside, destroying pagan temples and founding parishes, and a vigorous opponent of Priscillianism — though he protested against the execution of Priscillian by the secular authorities, an early stand against capital punishment for heresy.

Veneration

Martin died at Candes in 397 and was buried at Tours on 11 November, the date of his feast (Martinmas). His tomb became one of the great pilgrimage shrines of Western Christendom. His cloak (cappa) was preserved as a relic by the Frankish kings and carried into battle; the priests who guarded it (cappellani) gave rise to the words "chapel" and "chaplain". Martinmas was traditionally a quarter day for settling accounts, hiring servants and slaughtering livestock ("St. Martin's summer" denotes a spell of mild autumn weather). Numerous churches across Europe are dedicated to him, and he is patron saint of France and of Buenos Aires.
