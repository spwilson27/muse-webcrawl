Encyclopædia Britannica

The Encyclopædia Britannica is a general-knowledge English-language encyclopaedia, founded in Edinburgh in 1768 and for centuries the most prestigious reference work in the English-speaking world. Its first edition was issued in weekly parts between 1768 and 1771 by Andrew Bell and Colin Macfarquhar, edited by William Smellie. The fifteenth and final print edition (2010) ran to 32 volumes and some 32,000 pages.

History

Successive editions grew in size and ambition; the celebrated eleventh edition (1910–1911), produced with Cambridge University, is often regarded as a monument of scholarship of its era, with contributors including prominent scientists, historians, and writers. Ownership passed to American hands in the 20th century, and the work was long marketed by door-to-door sales. Notable contributors across its history included Walter Scott, Thomas Malthus, Marie Curie, and Albert Einstein.

Digital era

The Britannica appeared on CD-ROM in the 1990s and online from 1994. In 2012 the company announced that the 2010 edition would be the last printed one, ending 244 years of print publication; the encyclopaedia continues as a digital product, competing with free resources such as Wikipedia.
