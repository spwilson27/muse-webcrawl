Bokmål

[Image placeholder: Bokmål]

Bokmål (literally "book language") is one of two official written standards of the Norwegian language, alongside Nynorsk. It is used by an estimated 85–90% of the population of Norway and is the standard most commonly taught to foreign learners. Bokmål evolved from the Danish written language used during Denmark–Norway's union, gradually Norwegianised through reforms in 1907, 1917, 1938 and later.

History

After independence in 1814, Norway retained Danish as its written language. Knud Knudsen advocated a gradual Norwegianisation ("fornorsking") of the written standard, in contrast to Ivar Aasen's Landsmål (later Nynorsk), which he constructed from rural dialects. Bokmål's official name replaced the earlier Riksmål in 1929.

Usage

Each Norwegian municipality may declare itself Bokmål, Nynorsk or language-neutral; most choose Bokmål or neutrality. Schoolchildren learn a main form (hovedmål) and a secondary form (sidemål). Conservative variants of Bokmål, often called Riksmål, remain in use privately though not officially sanctioned.
