Modelling clay

Coloured oil-based modelling clay

Modelling clay is any group of malleable materials used in building and sculpture that can be shaped by hand and tools. The category includes water-based ceramic clays that harden by firing, air-drying clays, polymer clays that cure with heat, and oil-based clays that never harden and can be reused indefinitely.

Types

Oil-based clay, of which Plasticine is the best-known example (invented by William Harbutt in 1897), is made from calcium salts, petroleum jelly and fatty acids; it stays workable, does not dry out, and is used by sculptors, animators in claymation, and automotive stylists for full-scale car models. Polymer clay (such as Fimo and Sculpey) hardens in a domestic oven and is popular for crafts and jewellery. Paper clay and air-dry clays harden by evaporation of water. Industrial design studios use wax-based styling clays that soften when warmed.

Uses

Modelling clays are used in fine-art sculpture as the stage before casting, in stop-motion animation, in education and play (for example Play-Doh, a related wheat-flour dough), and in prototyping. Because oil-based clay cannot be fired, works in it are normally moulded and cast in another material if permanence is required.
