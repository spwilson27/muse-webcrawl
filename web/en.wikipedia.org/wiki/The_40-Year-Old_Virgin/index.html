The 40-Year-Old Virgin

The 40-Year-Old Virgin
Directed by Judd Apatow
Written by Judd Apatow, Steve Carell
Starring Steve Carell, Catherine Keener, Paul Rudd, Seth Rogen
Released: August 19, 2005
Box office: $177 million

The 40-Year-Old Virgin is a 2005 American romantic comedy film directed by Judd Apatow in his directorial debut, and written by Apatow and Steve Carell. Carell stars as Andy Stitzer, a mild-mannered 40-year-old stock supervisor at a big-box electronics store who has never had sex and whose life revolves around action figures, video games and a pristine apartment. When his co-workers David (Paul Rudd), Jay (Romany Malco) and Cal (Seth Rogen) discover his secret during a poker game, they make it their mission to change his situation. Andy meanwhile meets and falls for Trish (Catherine Keener), the owner of a shop that sells customers' items on eBay.

Production

The concept grew out of a sketch Carell performed at The Second City. Apatow encouraged an improvisational style, shooting over a million feet of film. A chest-waxing scene was performed for real — Carell's chest hair was genuinely ripped off by an unprepared esthetician while five cameras rolled; co-star Seth Rogen has noted that Carell's screamed insult ("Kelly Clarkson!") became one of the film's best-known moments. The cast also includes Jane Lynch as Andy's formidable boss Paula and Elizabeth Banks as a bookstore clerk.

Reception

Made on a budget of about $26 million, the film grossed more than $177 million worldwide and was the breakthrough that launched Apatow's run of commercially and critically successful comedies, including Knocked Up and Superbad. Critics praised its combination of raunchy set pieces with an unusually sweet romantic core, and it was named one of the American Film Institute's top ten films of 2005. The film holds a high approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes and is frequently cited among the best comedies of the 2000s.
