Endometrial ablation

Endometrial ablation is a surgical procedure that is used to remove (ablate) or destroy the endometrial lining of the uterus. The procedure is most commonly used to treat heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia) in people who have not responded to other treatments and who do not wish to become pregnant in the future, as pregnancy following endometrial ablation can be dangerous for both the mother and the fetus.

Procedure

A number of techniques are used to destroy the endometrium, including radiofrequency electrical energy, heated fluid (thermal balloon ablation), cryotherapy (freezing), and microwave energy. The procedure is usually performed on an outpatient basis under local or general anesthesia and takes only a few minutes with second-generation devices. The endometrial lining is destroyed so that it scars, reducing or stopping menstrual flow.

Effectiveness

Most patients experience substantially reduced menstrual bleeding, and a significant proportion develop amenorrhea (complete absence of periods). Endometrial ablation is less invasive than hysterectomy and has a shorter recovery time, but it may need to be repeated, and some patients eventually require further surgery.

Risks

Complications are uncommon but can include uterine perforation, burns to surrounding organs, infection, and post-ablation tubal sterilization syndrome in patients who have previously had a tubal ligation. The procedure is not recommended for people who wish to have children in the future.
