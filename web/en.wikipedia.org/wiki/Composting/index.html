Composting

Composting is a process for recycling organic matter, such as leaves and food scraps, into a valuable soil amendment called compost, or humus. Under aerobic conditions, microorganisms and larger decomposers break down the material, generating heat, carbon dioxide and water.

Process

Effective composting balances carbon-rich "browns" (leaves, straw, paper) with nitrogen-rich "greens" (food scraps, manure, grass clippings), keeps the pile moist and aerated, and may pass through a hot thermophilic phase (around 40–65 °C) that kills pathogens and weed seeds. Finished compost is dark, crumbly and earthy-smelling.

Methods

Methods range from backyard bins and tumblers to vermicomposting with worms, bokashi fermentation, and industrial windrow or in-vessel systems processing municipal green waste. Compost improves soil structure, water retention and fertility, and diverts organic waste from landfill, reducing methane emissions.
