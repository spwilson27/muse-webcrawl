The Love Bug is a 1968 American sports adventure comedy film directed by Robert Stevenson from a screenplay by Bill Walsh and Don DaGradi, produced by Walt Disney Productions. It stars Dean Jones, Michele Lee, David Tomlinson, and Buddy Hackett. It was the first film featuring Herbie, a sentient 1963 Volkswagen Beetle with racing stripes and the number 53.

Plot

Down-on-his-luck racing driver Jim Douglas (Dean Jones) buys a seemingly ordinary Volkswagen Beetle from the showroom of pompous dealer Peter Thorndyke (David Tomlinson). The car—which his mechanic friend Tennessee Steinmetz (Buddy Hackett) names "Herbie"—proves to have a mind of its own and extraordinary speed. With Herbie's help, Jim's racing career revives, but Thorndyke, humiliated at losing to the little car, schemes to destroy it, culminating in the climactic El Dorado road race.

Production

The Volkswagen Beetle was selected for the role after a casting process in which crew members reacted warmly to a 1961 model. Over 100 Beetles were used in filming, modified for effects such as wheelies, oil-squirting, and driving on two wheels. The car was given racing stripes, the number 53—chosen by producer Bill Walsh in honor of baseball player Don Drysdale—and the name "Herbie" after a character in a Buddy Hackett comedy routine.

Legacy

The Love Bug was one of the highest-grossing films of 1969 and spawned a franchise: the sequels Herbie Rides Again (1974), Herbie Goes to Monte Carlo (1977), Herbie Goes Bananas (1980), a 1997 television remake, and Herbie: Fully Loaded (2005). It was Dean Jones's most famous Disney role and made Herbie one of the most recognizable cars in film history.
