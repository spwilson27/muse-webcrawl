Christ Church Cathedral is the cathedral of the diocese of Oxford and uniquely also the college chapel of Christ Church, one of the constituent colleges of the University of Oxford. It is thus the only church in the world to be both a cathedral and a college chapel, and its dean is the head of the college.

History

The church originated as the priory church of St Frideswide's Priory, an Augustinian house on the site of the shrine of Saint Frideswide, the patron saint of Oxford. The present building is predominantly late Norman, constructed in the later twelfth century, with a distinctive spire that is among the earliest in England. When Cardinal Wolsey suppressed the priory in 1525 to found his college, the church was retained; under Henry VIII it became, in 1546, the cathedral of the new diocese of Oxford and the chapel of Christ Church simultaneously.

Features

The cathedral contains the shrine of St Frideswide, notable stained glass including windows by Edward Burne-Jones, and the tomb of Lady Montacute. Its choir is internationally known, and the cathedral's separate choral foundation echoes the college's musical tradition. The novelist Lewis Carroll (Charles Dodgson), a Christ Church mathematician, wrote Alice's Adventures in Wonderland for Alice Liddell, daughter of the dean; the cathedral appears in the story's orbit and Alice is depicted in one of its windows.
