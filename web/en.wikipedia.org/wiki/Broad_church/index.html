Broad church

MovementAnglicanism
Emerged17th–19th centuries
Key figuresF. D. Maurice, Charles Kingsley, Benjamin Jowett

Broad church is a term referring to latitudinarian Church of England clergy and laity, and a movement within the Anglican Communion that emphasises tolerance, inclusiveness, and a liberal approach to theology and liturgy. It is typically contrasted with the "high church" (Anglo-Catholic) and "low church" (evangelical) parties.

Origins

The term grew out of the seventeenth-century "latitude-men", Cambridge Platonists and others who sought a comprehensive national church able to include differing opinions. In the nineteenth century the broad church party became associated with figures such as Thomas Arnold of Rugby, F. D. Maurice, Charles Kingsley, and Benjamin Jowett, many of whom contributed to the controversial essay collection Essays and Reviews (1860).

Characteristics

Broad church Anglicans typically stress reason and personal conscience alongside Scripture and tradition, are open to biblical criticism and modern scholarship, favour a moderate, non-dogmatic liturgy, and support an inclusive national church encompassing a wide spectrum of belief. The modern liberal wing of Anglicanism is often seen as its heir.
