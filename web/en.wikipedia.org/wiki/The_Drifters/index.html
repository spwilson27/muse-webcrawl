The Drifters are an American doo-wop and R&B/soul vocal group. Formed in 1953 by Clyde McPhatter of Billy Ward and his Dominoes, they went through numerous line-up changes over a long career; the group's name was famously controlled by manager George Treadwell, who owned the trademark and frequently replaced members.

Classic hits

With McPhatter, the group scored with "Money Honey" (1953). A second incarnation led by Ben E. King produced the classic Atlantic Records recordings of 1959–1960, including "There Goes My Baby", "Save the Last Dance for Me", and "This Magic Moment". With Rudy Lewis and then Johnny Moore as lead singers, hits continued with "Up on the Roof", "On Broadway", and "Under the Boardwalk" (1964). A later line-up enjoyed renewed success in the United Kingdom in the 1970s with songs such as "Kissin' in the Back Row of the Movies".

Legacy

The Drifters were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1988, and their recordings are staples of oldies and soul radio.
