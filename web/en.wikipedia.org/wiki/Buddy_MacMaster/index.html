Buddy MacMaster

Buddy MacMaster, master of the Cape Breton fiddle style.

Hugh Allan "Buddy" MacMaster (October 18, 1924 – May 20, 2014) was a Canadian folk musician from Cape Breton, Nova Scotia, widely regarded as the master of the Cape Breton fiddle style. Born in Timmins, Ontario to Scottish immigrant parents, he grew up in Judique, Cape Breton, where he absorbed the Scottish Gaelic fiddle tradition that immigrants had brought from the Highlands.

Career

MacMaster began playing at local dances as a teenager and worked for Canadian National Railways for most of his life, performing at dances, concerts and on radio and television. From the 1970s onward he toured internationally, taught at fiddle camps in Scotland and North America, and recorded albums including Glencoe Hall and The Judique Flyer. He was the uncle of fiddler Natalie MacMaster and a relative of Ashley MacIsaac, both of whom he influenced.

Honours

MacMaster was appointed to the Order of Canada and the Order of Nova Scotia, received honorary doctorates, and won both Juno and East Coast Music Awards. His playing was noted for its crisp ornamentation, powerful bow work and faithful preservation of the old Scottish strathspey and reel repertoire.
