Final Fantasy is a Japanese science fantasy media franchise created by Hironobu Sakaguchi and developed and owned by Square Enix (formerly Square). The franchise centres on a series of role-playing video games, but has expanded into films, anime, manga, novels and other media. The first game was released in 1987 for the Famicom; according to lore its title reflected that it might have been the struggling company's final game — and Sakaguchi's final attempt at the industry.

Main series

Each numbered main entry is a standalone story with new worlds and characters, united by recurring elements: airships, crystals, chocobos, moogles, summons, a character named Cid, and music by composers including Nobuo Uematsu. Landmark entries include Final Fantasy IV (1991), VI (1994), VII (1997) — whose PlayStation release was a landmark for cinematic storytelling and helped popularise Japanese RPGs worldwide — X (2001), the MMORPGs XI and XIV, and XVI (2023).

Gameplay

The series is known for turn-based and Active Time Battle (ATB) combat, job/class systems, and increasingly action-oriented battle systems in later entries. Final Fantasy XIV, relaunched as A Realm Reborn in 2013 after its troubled original release, became one of the most successful MMORPGs in the world. The Final Fantasy VII Remake trilogy (2020–) reimagines the 1997 game for modern hardware.

Reception

The franchise has sold over 185 million copies worldwide and is credited with popularising console RPGs and setting standards for narrative ambition in games.
