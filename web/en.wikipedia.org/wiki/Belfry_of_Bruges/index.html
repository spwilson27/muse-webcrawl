Belfry of Bruges

Location: Markt, Bruges, Belgium

Height: 83 metres (272 ft)

Built: 1240–1480s

Bells: carillon of 47 bells

The Belfry of Bruges (Dutch: Belfort van Brugge) is a medieval bell tower in the centre of Bruges, Belgium. One of the city's most prominent symbols, the belfry formerly housed a treasury and the municipal archives, and served as an observation post for spotting fires and other dangers. It stands 83 metres (272 feet) tall and leans slightly to the east. Together with other belfries of Belgium and France, it is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

History

The first tower on the site was built around 1240, when Bruges was a flourishing centre of the Flemish cloth trade. It was largely destroyed by fire in 1280, and the archives of the city were lost. A new tower was built, and the octagonal upper stage was added between 1483 and 1487. A wooden spire crowned the tower but was destroyed by fire in 1493 and again, after rebuilding, in 1741; it was never replaced, giving the tower its present flat-topped silhouette.

The carillon

The belfry houses a carillon of 47 bells, cast over several centuries, with a total weight of about 27.5 tonnes. The largest bells were cast by the foundry of Melchior de Haze in the 17th century. A steep staircase of 366 steps leads to the top, from where there are panoramic views over Bruges.

In culture

The belfry features prominently in the 2008 film In Bruges, and was celebrated in a poem by Henry Wadsworth Longfellow. The tower's bells ring on the quarter hour, and the carillon is played for concerts on market days.
