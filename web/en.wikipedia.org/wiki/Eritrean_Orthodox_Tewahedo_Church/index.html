Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church

The Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church is an Oriental Orthodox church with its headquarters in Asmara, Eritrea. It became autocephalous in 1993 after Eritrean independence, separating from the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church with which it shares the Alexandrine rite, Ge'ez liturgical heritage, and Miaphysite Christology. It is the largest Christian denomination in Eritrea, claiming several million adherents.

History

Christianity reached the region in the 4th century through Frumentius and the Kingdom of Aksum. For centuries Eritrea's church was governed from Ethiopia, ultimately under the Coptic Patriarch of Alexandria until Ethiopian autocephaly in 1959. After the 1991 liberation and 1993 independence referendum, the church was granted its own patriarch; Abune Phillipos was enthroned as first Patriarch in 1998.

Beliefs and practice

"Tewahedo" (Ge'ez for "being made one") expresses the united nature of Christ. The church observes a rich calendar of fasts, Saturday Sabbath observance alongside Sunday, ark-centred worship modelled on the tabot, and a canon including broader Old Testament books such as Enoch. Government interference in patriarchal succession since the 2000s has produced disputes and a diaspora synod in exile.
