Chevrolet Camaro

The Chevrolet Camaro is a mid-size American automobile manufactured by Chevrolet, classified as a pony car and, in some versions, a muscle car. It was introduced on 29 September 1966 for the 1967 model year as a direct rival to the Ford Mustang, and was produced in six generations, from 1967–2002 and, after an eight-year hiatus, from 2010–2024.

History

The first-generation Camaro (1967–1969) was built on the F-body platform shared with the Pontiac Firebird, offering straight-six and V8 engines and famous performance packages such as the Z/28, RS and SS. Later generations included the long-lived second generation (1970–1981), the sleeker third generation (1982–1992) that introduced the IROC-Z, and the fourth generation (1993–2002).

After production ended in 2002, a retro-styled concept unveiled in 2006 led to the fifth generation (2010–2015). The sixth generation (2016–2024), built on GM's Alpha platform at Lansing, Michigan, culminated in the 650-horsepower ZL1. Chevrolet ended Camaro production in January 2024, with no immediate successor announced.

In popular culture

The Camaro is widely recognised as Bumblebee in the Transformers film series, and the car has a long record in Trans-Am and drag racing, including the COPO Camaro factory drag car.
