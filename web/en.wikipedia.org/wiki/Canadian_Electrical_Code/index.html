Canadian Electrical Code

The Canadian Electrical Code, formally CSA C22.1, is a standard published by CSA Group that governs the installation and maintenance of electrical equipment in Canada. First published in 1927, it is one of the most widely adopted model codes in the country and is referenced, with provincial amendments, by every province and territory.

History

The code was first issued in 1927 by the Canadian Engineering Standards Association, the predecessor of CSA Group. Early editions were brief documents concerned mainly with wiring methods and fire safety. Over the following decades the code expanded to cover grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, hazardous locations, and renewable-energy systems such as photovoltaic installations.

Modern editions are revised on a three-year cycle, paralleling the cycle of the United States National Electrical Code (NFPA 70), although the two documents differ substantially in structure and technical detail. The 2021 edition was the 25th edition of the code.

Structure

Part I of the Canadian Electrical Code contains the safety standard for electrical installations. It is organized into sections covering general requirements, wiring methods, protection and control, grounding, services and service equipment, and special occupancies. Part II consists of a series of individual standards for electrical products, and Part III covers requirements for electrical power distribution systems.

Objective-based requirements were introduced to allow designers to demonstrate compliance through alternative engineering solutions rather than prescriptive rules alone.

Adoption

The code is a model document: it has legal force only when adopted by a province or territory, usually through regulation under an electrical safety act. Adoption is frequently accompanied by amendments reflecting local conditions, inspection practices, and administrative requirements. Electrical work performed under the code is inspected by the local authority having jurisdiction, such as Ontario's Electrical Safety Authority.
