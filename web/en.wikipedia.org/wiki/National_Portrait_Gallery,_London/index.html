National Portrait Gallery, London

The National Portrait Gallery (NPG) is an art gallery in London housing a collection of portraits of historically important and famous British people. It was the first portrait gallery in the world when it opened in 1856. The gallery moved in 1896 to its current site at St Martin's Place, just off Trafalgar Square, adjacent to the National Gallery.

History

The gallery was founded through the efforts of Philip Henry Stanhope, 4th Earl Stanhope, with support from Thomas Babington Macaulay and Thomas Carlyle, who argued for the importance of the sitter over the artist. Its first acquisition was the Chandos portrait of William Shakespeare, believed to be the most famous portrait of the playwright, donated by the Earl of Ellesmere. Early displays were held at Great George Street, Westminster, before the purpose-built home in St Martin's Place opened.

Collection

The collection includes more than 200,000 portraits from the 16th century to the present day, in media ranging from oil paintings and sculpture to photographs — the gallery holds an extensive photographic archive. Portraits are selected for the significance of the sitter in British history and culture rather than the fame of the artist, though works by artists such as Hans Holbein the Younger and Lucian Freud feature prominently.

Redevelopment

The gallery closed in 2020 for the "Inspiring People" redevelopment, its largest building project since 1896, and reopened on 22 June 2023 with a new entrance, redisplayed collection, and restored spaces.
