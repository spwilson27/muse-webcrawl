Coventry Cathedral

Coventry Cathedral, formally the Cathedral Church of St Michael, is the seat of the Bishop of Coventry and the Church of England Diocese of Coventry, in the West Midlands, England. The present cathedral stands beside the ruins of its predecessor, destroyed in the Coventry Blitz of 14 November 1940.

The old cathedral

St Michael's was a large Gothic parish church before Coventry became a diocese in 1918. On the night of the November 1940 raid it was struck by incendiary bombs and burned out; only the tower, spire (at 91 metres the third highest in England), outer walls and the effigy of its first bishop survived. Behind the altar of the ruins stand the words "Father Forgive", and a Cross of Nails fashioned from medieval roof nails has become an international symbol of reconciliation.

The new cathedral

A competition for the replacement was won by Basil Spence, whose design retained the ruins as a garden of remembrance and narthex. The foundation stone was laid in 1956 and the cathedral was consecrated on 25 May 1962. It is renowned for its modern artworks: Jacob Epstein's statue of St Michael defeating the Devil on the exterior wall, Graham Sutherland's vast tapestry Christ in Glory, John Hutton's engraved glass saints, the baptistery window by John Piper, and Elisabeth Frink's bronze eagle lectern. Benjamin Britten's War Requiem was premiered at the consecration festival.

Ministry of reconciliation

The cathedral's Community of the Cross of Nails now links hundreds of partners worldwide, including centres in Dresden and other cities destroyed in war. Daily prayer for reconciliation continues in the ruins.
