Contrast agent

A contrast agent (or contrast medium) is a substance used to increase the contrast of structures or fluids within the body in medical imaging. Contrast agents improve the visibility of internal structures by altering how the imaging modality interacts with tissues — for example by absorbing X-rays more strongly, changing magnetic relaxation times, or reflecting ultrasound.

Types

Radiocontrast agents for X-ray and computed tomography are usually iodinated compounds given intravenously or orally; barium sulfate suspensions are used for imaging the gastrointestinal tract. MRI contrast agents are most often gadolinium chelates that shorten T1 relaxation time, making vessels and certain tissues appear bright. Ultrasound contrast agents consist of gas-filled microbubbles that strongly reflect sound waves and are used mainly in echocardiography.

Safety

Contrast agents are generally safe, but adverse reactions occur: iodinated agents can cause allergic-like reactions and, in susceptible patients, kidney injury; gadolinium agents carry a risk of nephrogenic systemic fibrosis in severe renal impairment and have been the subject of debate about gadolinium retention in the body. Patients are screened for risk factors before administration.
