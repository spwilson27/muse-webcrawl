Expulsion (education)

Expulsion, also known as exclusion or permanent exclusion, is the removal of a student from a school or university as a disciplinary sanction, normally the most serious punishment available to an educational institution. It differs from suspension, which is temporary.

Grounds for expulsion commonly include serious violence, weapons or drug offences, persistent defiance of rules, and (in higher education) academic dishonesty such as cheating or plagiarism. Procedures and rights of appeal vary by jurisdiction; in many systems expelled pupils must still be provided alternative education.

Expulsion rates are a subject of debate, with research and policy discussion focusing on disparities affecting disadvantaged groups and on alternatives such as restorative practices. In the United Kingdom, the term "permanent exclusion" is used in England, while Scotland largely avoids permanent exclusion.
