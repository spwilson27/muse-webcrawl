Ismail ibn Umar ibn Kathir (Arabic: إسماعيل بن عمر بن كثير; c. 1300 – 1373), known as Ibn Kathir, was a highly influential Syrian Islamic scholar, historian, and Qur'an commentator of the Mamluk era. A student of Ibn Taymiyyah and al-Dhahabi, he is best known for his Qur'anic exegesis, Tafsir Ibn Kathir, one of the most widely read commentaries in the Sunni world.

A manuscript opening of Ibn Kathir's celebrated Qur'an commentary.

Life

Ibn Kathir was born in Busra, in southern Syria, and moved to Damascus after his father's death, studying hadith, law, and history under leading scholars including al-Mizzi and al-Dhahabi, and becoming a devoted disciple of Ibn Taymiyyah. He taught at Damascus's mosques and madrasas and was appointed to preach and lecture at the Umayyad Mosque.

Works

His Tafsir al-Qur'an al-Azim interprets the Qur'an through the Qur'an itself, the hadith, and the statements of the earliest generations, and includes famous narratives such as the expanded stories of the prophets — drawing partly on Isra'iliyyat material. His universal history Al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya ("The Beginning and the End") runs from creation to his own century and remains a standard chronicle of Islamic history. He also wrote biographical works and treatises on hadith methodology.

Legacy

Ibn Kathir's works are staples of classical Sunni scholarship; abridgements and translations of his tafsir are printed throughout the Muslim world, and he is frequently cited in contemporary Qur'anic study.
