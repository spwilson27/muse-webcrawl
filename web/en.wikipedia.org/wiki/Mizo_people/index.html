Mizo people

The Mizo people are an ethnic group native to the Mizo Hills of northeastern India, western Myanmar and southeastern Bangladesh, principally the Indian state of Mizoram. They belong to the wider Zo (Chin-Kuki-Mizo) peoples and speak the Mizo language, a Tibeto-Burman language written in the Latin alphabet.

History

Mizo society was traditionally organised in villages ruled by chiefs (lal), practising shifting cultivation (jhum). British annexation of the Lushai Hills in the 1890s and the arrival of Welsh and American missionaries transformed Mizo society; conversion to Christianity was nearly universal and Mizoram today has one of India's highest literacy rates. After Indian independence the Mizo Hills were part of Assam until Mizoram became a Union Territory in 1972 and a full state in 1987, following the Mizo National Front insurgency (1966–1986) and the Mizoram Peace Accord.

Culture

Important festivals include Chapchar Kut, Mim Kut and Pawl Kut. The ethical code of tlawmngaihna — selfless service to others — is a celebrated feature of Mizo social life.
