Cockermouth

Cockermouth is a market town and civil parish in Cumbria, England, situated at the confluence of the River Cocker, from which it takes its name, and the River Derwent. It lies at the north-western edge of the Lake District National Park. Historically in Cumberland, the town recorded a population of about 8,800 in the 2021 census.

History

The Romans built the fort of Derventio at nearby Papcastle to guard the river crossing. Cockermouth grew around its castle, founded by the Normans in the early twelfth century, and received a market charter in 1221. The town's broad Main Street of Georgian houses reflects later prosperity from the textile and agricultural trades.

The poet William Wordsworth was born in 1770 at Wordsworth House on Main Street, now preserved by the National Trust; his contemporary John Dalton, the atomic theorist, was born in the nearby village of Eaglesfield and educated in the town.

Flooding

Cockermouth was severely flooded in November 2009, when record rainfall across Cumbria caused the Cocker and Derwent to inundate the town centre, and again during Storm Desmond in December 2015. Flood-defence works, including raised walls and a self-closing barrier, were subsequently installed.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the ruins of Cockermouth Castle, All Saints' Church, the Kirkgate Centre, and the statue of the Earl of Mayo. The Jennings Brewery, founded in 1828, was long a fixture of the town. Cockermouth is a gateway for visitors to the north-western fells and the lakes of Bassenthwaite, Crummock Water, and Buttermere.
