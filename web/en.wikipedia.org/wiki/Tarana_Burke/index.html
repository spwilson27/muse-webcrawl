Tarana Burke

Tarana Burke (born 12 September 1973) is an American civil rights activist from the Bronx, New York. She is the founder of the Me Too movement, having begun using the phrase "Me Too" in 2006 to build solidarity and empathy among survivors of sexual violence, particularly young women of colour from low-income communities.

Activism

Burke's activism grew out of community work in Alabama during the 1990s, including co-founding the Just Be Inc. organisation for young girls. She developed "Me Too" as a grassroots programme of what she calls "empowerment through empathy" more than a decade before the hashtag went viral in October 2017 during the Harvey Weinstein scandal, after which Burke was widely recognised as the movement's originator. She was named among the "Silence Breakers" honoured as Time magazine's Person of the Year in 2017.

Later work

Burke has served as a senior director at Girls for Gender Equity in Brooklyn, launched the 'me too.' organisation to support survivors, and co-edited the 2021 anthology You Are Your Best Thing with Brené Brown. Her memoir Unbound: My Story of Liberation and the Birth of the Me Too Movement was published in 2021.
