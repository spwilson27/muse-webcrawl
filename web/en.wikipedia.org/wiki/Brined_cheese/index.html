Brined cheese

Brined cheese, also called pickled cheese, is cheese that is matured and stored in brine. Brining gives the cheese a distinctive salty flavour and moist, crumbly texture, inhibits harmful bacteria, and keeps the cheese fresh for long periods without refrigeration — an important property in the warm climates of the Balkans, the Middle East and the Mediterranean where such cheeses originated.

Varieties

The best-known brined cheese is Greek feta, protected by an EU designation of origin. Other examples include Bulgarian sirene, Romanian telemea, Serbian and Bosnian white cheeses, Turkish beyaz peynir, Egyptian domiati, Cypriot halloumi (often stored in brine with mint), and mozzarella in its fresh form, which is kept in brine or whey. Brined cheeses are usually white, rindless, and made from sheep's, goat's or cow's milk.

Production

After curdling, cutting and pressing, the cheese blocks are immersed in a salt solution of roughly 7–16% salinity for weeks to months. Salt migrates into the cheese while whey is drawn out, firming the body and suppressing spoilage organisms. The cheese is commonly sold in tins or vacuum packs together with some of its brine.
