Cunningham number


Factorisations of Cunningham numbers, the subject of the Cunningham Project

In mathematics, a Cunningham number is a positive integer of the form bn ± 1 for integers b and n, where b is not itself a power of another integer. They are named for the English mathematician Allan Joseph Champneys Cunningham (1842–1928), who published tables of their factors in 1925.

Definitions

A Cunningham number of the first kind, C+(b, n), has the form bn + 1; one of the second kind, C−(b, n), has the form bn − 1. Familiar special cases include Fermat numbers (22k + 1) among the plus family and Mersenne numbers (2n − 1) among the minus family, for base b = 2. For a Cunningham number to be prime, n must in the minus case be prime (if b = 2) and in the plus case a power of two, by elementary divisibility arguments.

The Cunningham Project

The Cunningham Project is a long-running collaborative effort, begun in 1925 and computerised from the 1980s, to factor Cunningham numbers for bases b = 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 10, 11 and 12 up to high powers. Its tables are a standard testbed for factorisation algorithms such as the elliptic curve method and the number field sieve, and results are maintained in regularly updated published tables.
