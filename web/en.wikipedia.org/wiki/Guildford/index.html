Guildford

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Guildford is a town in west Surrey, England, around 27 miles (43 km) southwest of central London. As of the 2021 census, it had a population of about 77,000. Situated on the River Wey where it cuts through the North Downs, it is the county town of Surrey and the seat of the University of Surrey and the Anglican Diocese of Guildford.

History

Guildford grew up at a ford of the River Wey below the North Downs gap; its name is usually interpreted as "golden ford", perhaps for golden sand or flowers. The town appears in Domesday Book (1086) as Gildeford. The ruins of Guildford Castle, built shortly after the Norman Conquest, overlook the town gardens. It received its first charter in 1257, and the Guildhall on the steep cobbled High Street, with its projecting clock of 1683, dates from the late 16th century.

Modern town

The Wey Navigation, opened in 1653, made Guildford an early canal town. Lewis Carroll, who had a family home at The Chestnuts, is buried in Mount Cemetery and his connections are marked around the town. The University of Surrey, chartered in 1966, adjoins the modern brick cathedral of Stag Hill, consecrated in 1961. In 1974 the town was the scene of the Guildford pub bombings attributed to the IRA. Guildford is a major retail and commuter centre, with fast rail links to London Waterloo, and a notable cluster of video-game studios.
