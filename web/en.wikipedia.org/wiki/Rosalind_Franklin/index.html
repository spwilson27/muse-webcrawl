Rosalind Elsie Franklin (25 July 1920 – 16 April 1958) was a British chemist and X-ray crystallographer whose work was central to understanding the molecular structures of DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid), RNA, viruses, coal and graphite. Her X-ray diffraction image of DNA — 'Photo 51', taken with her student Raymond Gosling at King's College London in 1952 — was key evidence for the double-helix structure published by Watson and Crick in 1953.

Franklin's data reached Watson and Crick without her direct consent through Maurice Wilkins and an MRC report — a history that, after her early death from ovarian cancer at 37, made her the emblem of overlooked women in science: she was ineligible for the 1962 Nobel Prize awarded to Watson, Crick and Wilkins, as Nobels are not given posthumously. Her later work at Birkbeck on the tobacco mosaic and polio viruses was first-rate and recognised in her lifetime; Aaron Klug, her collaborator, continued it to his own Nobel Prize.
