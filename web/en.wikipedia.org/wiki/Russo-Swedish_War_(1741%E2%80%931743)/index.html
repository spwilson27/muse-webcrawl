Date1741–1743
LocationFinland

The Russo-Swedish War of 1741–1743, known in Sweden as the Hats' Russian War, was instigated by the Swedish Hats party, which sought to recover territories lost to Russia in the Great Northern War. Sweden declared war in the summer of 1741, encouraged by French diplomacy and by the hope that internal turmoil in Russia following the death of Empress Anna would aid the campaign.

Course of the war

The war went badly for Sweden. Russian forces under Field Marshal Peter Lacy defeated the Swedes at Villmanstrand (Lappeenranta) in 1741 and advanced through Finland during 1742, in what Finns remember as the Lesser Wrath (pikkuviha). A coup in St Petersburg brought Elizabeth of Russia to the throne; she initially offered generous peace terms in hope of creating a buffer state, but negotiations stalled and the occupation of Finland continued until hostilities formally ended.

Treaty of Åbo

By the Treaty of Åbo (Turku) of 1743, Sweden ceded a strip of south-eastern Finland east of the Kymi River, including the fortresses of Villmanstrand and Fredrikshamn (Hamina), moving the border further from St Petersburg. Sweden also accepted Adolf Frederick of Holstein-Gottorp as heir to the Swedish throne.
