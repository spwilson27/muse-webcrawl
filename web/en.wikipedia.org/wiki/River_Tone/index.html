The River Tone is a river in Somerset, England, about 33 km (21 mi) long. It rises in the Brendon Hills, is dammed to form Clatworthy Reservoir, and flows east-north-east through the Vale of Taunton Deane and the county town of Taunton to join the River Parrett at Burrowbridge.

Course

Below Clatworthy Reservoir the young Tone runs through Waterrow and past the villages of the western vale to Bishops Lydeard and Bradford-on-Tone, then enters Taunton, where it flows through the town centre beside the cricket ground of Somerset County Cricket Club and the Museum of Somerset. From Taunton a canalised section, improved in the 19th century, carries it past Creech St Michael across the Somerset Levels floodplain to its confluence with the Parrett at Burrowbridge, near the conical Burrow Mump and the island of Athelney, where Alfred the Great sheltered from the Danes in 878.

Flooding

The lower Tone crosses the low moors of the Levels, where its embanked channel at Curry Moor and Hay Moor can be pumped into to store floodwater. During the winter flooding of 2013–14 the Levels around the Parrett–Tone confluence were inundated for weeks, prompting a long-term Somerset rivers plan including capital dredging of the Tone and Parrett and the raising of banks and roads.

Navigation and history

The Bridgwater and Taunton Canal, opened in 1827, runs beside the river between the two towns and largely replaced river navigation; boats had earlier reached Taunton from the Parrett. The Grand Western Canal once linked the Tone valley to the Exe. Taunton's name itself means "farmstead on the Tone".
