Personal rapid transit

Personal rapid transit (PRT), also called podcars or guided/railed taxis, is a public transport mode featuring small automated vehicles ("pods") that carry a few passengers on demand over a network of dedicated guideways. Unlike conventional transit, trips are non-stop from origin to destination station, with stations located off the main line so other vehicles can pass.

Concept

PRT was developed in concept from the 1950s onward (notably by Donn Fichter in the US) and promises the privacy and directness of a taxi with the efficiency of automated electric transport. Vehicles typically seat two to six passengers travelling together, are summoned at stations, and merge at speed into guideway traffic under computer control.

Systems in operation

The first and longest-running system is the Morgantown Personal Rapid Transit system at West Virginia University, opened in 1975, though its larger vehicles make it closer to a group rapid transit system. Modern true-PRT installations include the ULTra system at London Heathrow Airport (opened 2011), connecting Terminal 5 with a business car park, and the Vectus system in Suncheon, South Korea. Many grander citywide schemes, such as the proposed system for Masdar City in Abu Dhabi (built only in reduced form), foundered on cost and complexity, and PRT remains a niche technology.
