Weanling

A weanling is an animal that has just been weaned. The term is usually used to refer to a type of young horse, a foal that has been weaned, usually between six months and a year. Once it attains its first year, it is known as a yearling.

Usage in horse breeding

Weaning is the process of gradually introducing a young mammal to what will be its adult diet and withdrawing the supply of its mother's milk. Young horses are generally weaned at between four and six months of age. The period immediately after weaning can be stressful for foals, and breeders employ techniques such as gradual separation, fenceline contact, or companion animals to reduce stress.

Other animals

The word is also used in livestock production for young animals recently separated from their mothers, including cattle calves ("weanling calves"), pigs ("weanling pigs" or "weaners"), and sheep. Weanling weight is an important production measurement in beef cattle breeding, recorded at about 205 days of age.
