Functional calculus

In mathematics, a functional calculus is a theory allowing one to apply mathematical functions to mathematical operators. It is now a branch (more accurately, several related areas) of the field of functional analysis, connected with spectral theory.


Applying a function to an operator

Idea

If f is an ordinary function, for example a polynomial, and T is a linear operator on a vector space, then f(T) can be defined by simply substituting T into the polynomial. A functional calculus extends this substitution to wider classes of functions — continuous, measurable or holomorphic functions — and to operators such as matrices, bounded operators on Hilbert space, and unbounded self-adjoint operators.

Types

Well-known versions include the holomorphic functional calculus for Banach algebra elements, defined by a contour integral generalising the Cauchy integral formula; the continuous functional calculus for normal elements of C*-algebras; and the Borel functional calculus for self-adjoint operators, which follows from the spectral theorem and permits definitions such as the exponential of an operator and the square root of a positive operator. The Riesz–Dunford calculus is the holomorphic calculus for bounded operators on Banach spaces.

Applications

Functional calculi are essential in quantum mechanics, where observables are self-adjoint operators and functions of them (such as the unitary evolution operator e−itH) are defined spectrally, and in the theory of differential equations, where solution operators for linear PDEs are constructed from functions of generators of semigroups.
