Transitional justice

Transitional justice is a process which responds to human rights violations through judicial and non-judicial mechanisms, including criminal prosecutions, truth commissions, reparations, and institutional reforms, in a society attempting to come to terms with a legacy of large-scale past abuses.

History

The field emerged after the Nuremberg trials and expanded after transitions from authoritarian rule in Latin America in the 1980s, Eastern Europe after 1989, and South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission (1995–2002) after apartheid.

Mechanisms

  • Criminal trials, domestic and international
  • Truth commissions
  • Reparations to victims
  • Memorialisation and lustration / vetting

It is linked to civil rights and racial equality.

Criticism

Debates concern amnesty versus prosecution, local ownership, and whether justice can be achieved without addressing economic inequality.
