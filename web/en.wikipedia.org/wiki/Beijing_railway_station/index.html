Beijing railway station

Beijing railway station (Chinese: 北京站), or simply Beijing station, is a passenger railway station in Dongcheng District, Beijing, just southeast of Tiananmen Square and south of Jianguomen. Opened in 1959, it was the largest railway station in China at the time and remains one of the capital's principal rail terminals, as well as a landmark of socialist-era architecture. It is sometimes called "Beijing East station" in older English usage, though it is distinct from the present Beijing East station.

History

The station was built as one of the "Ten Great Buildings" erected to mark the tenth anniversary of the People's Republic of China in 1959. Designed with Soviet assistance, it combines socialist monumentalism with traditional Chinese roof elements and is distinguished by its two clock towers. For decades it was Beijing's main station and the symbolic gateway to the capital, welcoming state guests and serving international trains, including the Trans-Mongolian service to Moscow and trains to Pyongyang.

Services

Today Beijing station handles conventional and intercity trains, including services on the Beijing–Shanghai railway, the Beijing–Harbin railway and the Beijing–Baotou railway, as well as international trains to Moscow, Ulaanbaatar and Pyongyang. Much high-speed traffic has moved to Beijing South and Beijing West stations. The station is served by Line 2 of the Beijing Subway.
