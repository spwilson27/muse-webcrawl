Kåta

A kåta is a traditional dwelling used by the Sámi people of northern Scandinavia and Russia. It is a tent or hut structure with a conical or peaked frame of wooden poles covered with materials such as animal hides, cloth, peat or wood, depending on the type and period.

The most widely known form resembles a tipi or lavvu, with poles leaning together at the top around a central fire whose smoke escapes through an opening at the apex. More permanent versions, sometimes called gamme or peat huts, were insulated with turf and birch bark and used as semi-permanent dwellings.

Kåtas served both as homes and, in later times, as shelters for herders. Today they remain a symbol of Sámi culture and are still raised for gatherings, tourism and traditional reindeer-herding life.
