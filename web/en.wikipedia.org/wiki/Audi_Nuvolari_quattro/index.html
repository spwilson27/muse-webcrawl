Audi Nuvolari quattro

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Audi Nuvolari quattro was a concept car created by Audi, first presented at the 2003 Geneva Motor Show. It was a grand tourer coupé named in honour of the Italian racing driver Tazio Nuvolari, who had raced for Auto Union in the 1930s. The concept previewed the design and technology of the first-generation Audi A5 coupé, launched in 2007.

Design and specifications

Built on an aluminium Audi Space Frame structure, the Nuvolari quattro combined a long bonnet, muscular wheel arches and a tapered cabin. Its 5.0-litre biturbo V10 with FSI direct injection produced 600 PS and 750 N·m of torque, giving a claimed 0–100 km/h time of 4.1 seconds and an electronically limited top speed of 250 km/h. Features included adaptive air suspension, electronic parking brake, fingerprint-recognition driver settings and early LED lighting elements, foreshadowing later Audi production technology.

Legacy

Although the Nuvolari itself never entered production, its proportions and styling directly shaped the Audi A5 (B8) designed under Walter de Silva, who regarded the A5 as one of his most beautiful designs. The car remains displayed in Audi's museum mobile in Ingolstadt.
