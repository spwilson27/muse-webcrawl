The Mensalão scandal (from mensalão, "big monthly payment") was a Brazilian political corruption scandal that broke in 2005, in which officials of the governing Workers' Party (PT) were accused of paying allied congressmen monthly sums in exchange for their votes supporting government legislation under President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva.

Exposure and fallout

The scandal erupted when deputy Roberto Jefferson alleged the payments in a 2005 newspaper interview. It forced the resignation of presidential chief of staff José Dirceu and badly damaged the PT, though Lula himself was re-elected in 2006 and was never charged in the case.

Trial

In 2012 the Supreme Federal Court tried 38 defendants in the case known as Criminal Action 470. Twenty-five were convicted of offences including corruption, money laundering and embezzlement, in a nationally televised trial presided over by Justice Joaquim Barbosa, and several senior party figures received prison sentences.
