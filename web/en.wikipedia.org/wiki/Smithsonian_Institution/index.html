Smithsonian Institution

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Smithsonian Castle on the National Mall in Washington, D.C., its red sandstone Norman towers against the Capitol dome's axis]

The Smithsonian Institution is a group of museums, education and research centres, and the National Zoo, administered by the United States federal government and described as the world's largest museum, education and research complex. It comprises 21 museums, the National Zoo and nine research facilities, with some 155 million items in its collections, most displayed in Washington, D.C., along and around the National Mall.

It was founded in 1846 from the bequest of the English scientist James Smithson, who — though he never visited America — left his estate to the United States to found at Washington an institution 'for the increase and diffusion of knowledge', its red sandstone 'Castle' (completed 1855) remaining its emblem.

The museums

The Mall's free museums are America's attic and classroom: the National Air and Space Museum (the Wright Flyer, the Apollo 11 capsule, the Spirit of St. Louis), the National Museum of American History (the original Star-Spangled Banner), Natural History (the Hope Diamond), the National Gallery's Smithsonian cousins, and the newest, the National Museum of African American History and Culture (opened 2016, David Adjaye's bronze crown on the Mall). Two congressionally authorised additions — for American women's history and the Latino museum — are in development.

Reach

Beyond Washington, the institution runs museums in New York (the Cooper Hewitt design museum, the American Indian museum's Heye Center), a vast magazine and television operation, travelling exhibitions, and research from the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory to the tropics station in Panama. Its digitisation programme has put millions of objects into open access — the attic, catalogued for everyone.
