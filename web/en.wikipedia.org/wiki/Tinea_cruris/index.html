Tinea cruris

Tinea cruris, colloquially known as jock itch, is a dermatophyte fungal infection of the groin region. It is most common in adult men, particularly athletes, and flourishes in the warm, moist environment of the inguinal folds.

Signs and symptoms

The infection causes an itchy, red, often ring-shaped rash in the groin, inner thighs and buttocks, typically sparing the scrotum — a feature distinguishing it from candidiasis. The most common cause is Trichophyton rubrum, often spread from a concurrent athlete's foot infection. Risk factors include tight clothing, sweating, obesity and diabetes.

Treatment

Most cases clear with topical antifungal creams such as terbinafine or clotrimazole, keeping the area dry, and treating any foot infection to prevent recurrence. Oral antifungals are used for extensive or resistant cases.
