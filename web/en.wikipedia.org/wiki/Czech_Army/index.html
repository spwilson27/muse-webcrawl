Army of the Czech Republic

The Army of the Czech Republic (Czech: Armáda České republiky, AČR) is the military force of the Czech Republic. Together with the Air Force of the Czech Republic it forms the main branches of the Armed Forces of the Czech Republic, which are responsible for the defence of the country. The Czech Army dates its modern lineage to the division of the Czechoslovak Army on 1 January 1993, following the dissolution of Czechoslovakia.

History

The Armed Forces of Czechoslovakia were formed after the country gained independence in 1918, drawing heavily on the Czechoslovak Legions of the First World War. After the Velvet Revolution of 1989, the army was depoliticized and reduced in size. Following the split of Czechoslovakia, the Czech Republic inherited roughly two-thirds of the former federal army's personnel and equipment. The Czech Republic joined NATO on 12 March 1999. Conscription was abolished at the end of 2004, making the army a fully professional force.

Structure

The land forces comprise mechanized brigades — notably the 4th Rapid Deployment Brigade and the 7th Mechanized Brigade — together with artillery, engineer, logistics, signals and special forces units, including the 601st Special Forces Group. The General Staff is headquartered in Prague, and the President of the Czech Republic is the commander-in-chief of the armed forces.

Deployments

Czech soldiers have served in operations in the Balkans (IFOR, SFOR, KFOR), Iraq and Afghanistan (ISAF and Resolute Support), as well as in Mali and in NATO enhanced Forward Presence battlegroups in the Baltic states and Slovakia.

Equipment

The army operates T-72M4 CZ tanks (being supplemented by Leopard 2A4s, with Leopard 2A8s on order), BVP-2 infantry fighting vehicles (to be replaced by CV90s), Pandur II wheeled armoured vehicles, DANA self-propelled howitzers and CAESAR guns on order, and a range of Soviet-legacy and modern Western systems.
