Gollum

Gollum is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium. He was originally a Hobbit-like creature named Sméagol, whose life was consumed and prolonged for nearly 500 years by possession of the One Ring. He appears in The Hobbit (1937) as the riddling creature beneath the Misty Mountains who loses the Ring to Bilbo Baggins, and plays a pivotal role in The Lord of the Rings (1954–55), in which his craving for the Ring — his "Precious" — ultimately destroys both it and him. The name Gollum comes from the gurgling, choking sound of his speech.

Story

Around T.A. 2463 Sméagol's cousin Déagol found the Ring in the river Anduin; Sméagol murdered him for it, calling it his "birthday present", and was banished by his people. Retreating into caves under the Misty Mountains, he lived on raw fish and grew into the emaciated, large-eyed Gollum, hating and loving the Ring. In 2941 Bilbo Baggins found the Ring and won a riddle-game against Gollum, escaping with it. Captured and questioned by Sauron in Mordor, Gollum later attached himself to Frodo and Sam's quest, serving as guide to Mordor while plotting to recover the Ring; at the Cracks of Doom he bit the Ring from Frodo's hand and fell, rejoicing, into the fire — fulfilling the pity that Bilbo, Frodo and Sam had shown him.

Character and portrayal

Tolkien presents Gollum as a study in addiction and corruption, yet pitiable: his speech alternates between the whining, sibilant Gollum personality and the more childlike Sméagol. In Peter Jackson's films Andy Serkis's motion-captured performance made Gollum a landmark of digital character work, and the character has become one of the most recognisable in modern fantasy.
