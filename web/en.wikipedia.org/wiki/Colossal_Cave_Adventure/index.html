Colossal Cave Adventure (also known as Adventure or ADVENT) is a text-based adventure game, released in 1976 by developer Will Crowther for the PDP-10 mainframe computer. It was expanded upon in 1977 by Don Woods. In the game, the player explores a cave system rumored to be filled with treasure and gold, taking text commands to interact with objects and solve puzzles. The game is widely regarded as the first work of interactive fiction and a foundational influence on the adventure game genre.

Gameplay

The player types simple verb–noun commands ("take lamp", "go north") and the program responds with descriptions. Beginning at a small building in the woods, the player descends into a vast cave modelled on Kentucky's Mammoth Cave system (Crowther was a caver and based the geography on the Bedquilt region of Mammoth Cave). The player collects treasures, avoids or placates hazards such as a snake and angry dwarves, and returns the treasures to the building for points. Famous phrases originating in the game include "You are in a maze of twisty little passages, all alike" and the magic word "xyzzy".

Development

Crowther, a programmer at Bolt, Beranek and Newman working on ARPANET software, wrote the game in Fortran in 1975–76 for his daughters after his divorce. Don Woods, a Stanford graduate student, found the game on the ARPANET, obtained Crowther's permission, and expanded it with more rooms, treasures, puzzles and fantasy elements such as the dragon and the pirate, releasing his version in 1977. The game spread rapidly across the ARPANET and university systems.

Legacy

Adventure inspired the founding of Infocom and its flagship Zork series, Sierra On-Line's graphical adventures, and indirectly the MUD and roguelike traditions. It was inducted into discussions of canonical computing culture; "xyzzy" became a piece of hacker folklore, and the game has been ported to virtually every platform, including a 2023 graphical remake by Ken and Roberta Williams' studio Cygnus Entertainment.
