Black September (Arabic: أيلول الأسود) was a conflict fought in the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan between the Jordanian Armed Forces, under the leadership of King Hussein, and the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO), under the leadership of Yasser Arafat, primarily between 16 and 27 September 1970, with certain aspects of the conflict continuing until July 1971.

Background

After Jordan lost the West Bank to Israel in the Six-Day War of 1967, Palestinian fedayeen groups established bases in Jordan and increasingly operated as a state within a state, challenging King Hussein's authority. Tensions escalated through a series of clashes and assassination attempts against the king.

Course of the conflict

In early September 1970, the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine hijacked several international airliners, blowing up three of them at Dawson's Field in Jordan after removing the passengers. On 16 September, Hussein declared martial law, and the Jordanian army attacked fedayeen strongholds in Amman and other cities. Syria briefly intervened in support of the Palestinians but withdrew. Casualty figures remain disputed, ranging from several thousand to much higher Palestinian estimates. Under an agreement brokered in Cairo, fighting wound down, but by July 1971 Jordanian forces had expelled the remaining guerrillas, and the PLO relocated its base to Lebanon.

Aftermath

A clandestine Palestinian militant faction took the name Black September Organization in memory of the conflict; it later carried out the 1972 Munich Olympics massacre. The PLO's move to Lebanon contributed to the destabilisation preceding the Lebanese Civil War.
