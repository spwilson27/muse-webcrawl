Adreno

Adreno is a series of graphics processing unit (GPU) semiconductor intellectual property cores developed by Qualcomm and used in many of their SoCs. The core was initially developed under the Imageon brand name by ATI Technologies, which was acquired by AMD in 2006. After AMD sold the division in January 2009 to Qualcomm, Qualcomm rebranded the technologies as Adreno — an anagram of "Radeon", AMD's graphics brand.

Architecture

Adreno GPUs are integrated into Qualcomm's Snapdragon system-on-chip designs and provide hardware acceleration of rendering to 2- or 3-dimensional graphics using APIs such as OpenGL ES, Vulkan, DirectX, and OpenCL for general-purpose computing. The architecture is a unified shader design with support for tile-based deferred rendering in earlier generations and, in the Adreno 600 and later series, flex rendering that can switch between tiled and direct modes. Higher series support hardware ray tracing (from the Adreno 700 series in Snapdragon 8 Gen 1 onwards) and variable rate shading.

Series

The line runs from the Adreno 1xx series used in early Snapdragon and MSM chipsets, through the 2xx, 3xx and 4xx families, to the 5xx, 6xx, 7xx and the rebranded "Adreno" naming used in the Snapdragon 8 Gen 2/Gen 3 era, and Adreno GPUs also appear in the Snapdragon X series laptop processors. Models are identified by three-digit numbers (e.g. Adreno 660, Adreno 730, Adreno 750), with performance tiers rising with the number, and variant parts for the "XR" extended-reality chips and automotive platforms.

Drivers

On Android devices Adreno GPUs are driven by Qualcomm's proprietary user-space libraries, while Linux systems can use the open-source Freedreno driver in Mesa, which supports much of the Adreno 200–700 hardware.
