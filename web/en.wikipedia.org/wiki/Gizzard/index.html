Gizzard

A chicken gizzard, opened to show the internal grinding surfaces

The gizzard, also known as the ventriculus, gastric mill, or gigerium, is a muscular organ found in the digestive tract of some animals, including archosaurs (birds and other dinosaurs, and crocodilians), earthworms, some gastropods, some fish, and some crustaceans. It is specialised for grinding up food, often with the aid of particles of stone or grit swallowed by the animal (gastroliths), compensating for the absence of teeth in birds.

In birds

In birds, food passes first into the crop for storage, then into the glandular stomach (proventriculus) where enzymes are added, and finally into the gizzard. The gizzard's thick, powerful muscles contract rhythmically, kneading food against a tough inner lining (the koilin layer) and any grit present, mechanically breaking it down before it passes to the intestine. Seed-eating and grain-eating birds have especially muscular gizzards; birds of prey, which swallow softer food, have weaker ones, and regurgitate indigestible material as pellets.

As food

Chicken and other poultry gizzards are eaten in many cuisines — stewed, fried, grilled on skewers (as in Japanese sunagimo yakitori), or chopped into giblet gravy and traditional dishes such as dirty rice. Duck gizzards preserved in fat (gésiers confits) are a speciality of south-western France.
