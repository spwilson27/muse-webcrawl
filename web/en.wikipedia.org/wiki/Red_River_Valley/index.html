Red River Valley

The Red River Valley is a region in central North America that is drained by the north-flowing Red River of the North. The valley forms the border between the US states of Minnesota and North Dakota, extending north into Manitoba, Canada, where the river flows into Lake Winnipeg. It is the former floor of glacial Lake Agassiz, and consequently is exceptionally flat and fertile.

Geography

The valley is about 30–60 km (20–40 mi) wide on either side of the river and extends for hundreds of kilometres. Its flatness results from fine lake sediments laid down by Lake Agassiz, which drained at the end of the last ice age. The soil is rich but the terrain floods readily: because the river flows north into still-frozen reaches each spring, meltwater backs up and spreads broadly over the plain. Major floods, including those of 1997 and 2009, devastated Grand Forks and Fargo and prompted large flood-diversion projects.

Agriculture

The valley is one of North America's most productive agricultural regions, producing wheat, sugar beets, potatoes, soybeans, corn, and sunflowers. Fargo, North Dakota, and Grand Forks are its principal US urban centres; Winnipeg, Manitoba, lies at the northern end where the Red meets the Assiniboine River.

In culture

"Red River Valley" is also the name of a famous folk song and cowboy ballad, "From this valley they say you are going", whose origins have been debated and which has been recorded by many artists.
