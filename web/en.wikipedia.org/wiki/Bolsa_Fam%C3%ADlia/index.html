Bolsa Família

Bolsa Família
Country: Brazil
Launched: 2003 (law 2004), under President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva
Type: conditional cash transfer
Reach: about 14 million families (roughly a quarter of the population)
Administered by: Ministry of Social Development; payments via Caixa Econômica Federal

Bolsa Família (Portuguese for "Family Allowance") is a Brazilian conditional cash transfer programme that provides monthly payments to poor families, conditional on children attending school and receiving vaccinations and health check-ups. Created in 2003 by the government of President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva by unifying several earlier transfer schemes, it became one of the largest and most studied social welfare programmes in the world.

Design

Eligibility is based on per-capita household income recorded in the Cadastro Único registry. Benefits are paid preferentially to women, usually the mother, through a magnetic card issued by the state bank Caixa Econômica Federal. Conditions include minimum school attendance for children aged 6–15, vaccination schedules, and prenatal care for pregnant women.

Impact

Studies credit Bolsa Família with significant reductions in extreme poverty and income inequality in Brazil, improvements in school enrolment and child health, and a contribution to Brazil's removal from the UN hunger map in 2014. The programme costs roughly 0.5% of GDP, comparatively cheap for its reach. In 2021 it was rebranded as Auxílio Brasil by the Bolsonaro government, then restored to the Bolsa Família name in 2023 when Lula returned to office.
