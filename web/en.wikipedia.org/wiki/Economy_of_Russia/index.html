The economy of Russia is an upper-middle income, mixed market economy. It is the fifth-largest economy in Europe and among the largest in the world by purchasing power parity. Russia is highly dependent on revenues from oil and natural gas exports, which account for a large share of federal budget revenues and export earnings.

History

Following the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, Russia underwent a difficult transition from a centrally planned economy to a market economy, marked by rapid privatisation, a severe contraction in the 1990s, and the 1998 financial crisis. Growth in the 2000s was driven by rising energy prices.

Sectors

Russia is one of the world's largest producers of oil, natural gas, wheat, and metals such as nickel and palladium. Services account for the largest share of GDP, followed by industry and agriculture.

International sanctions

Since 2014, and especially following the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, Western countries imposed extensive sanctions on Russia, restricting its access to financial markets, technology, and energy trade, prompting a reorientation of exports toward Asia.
