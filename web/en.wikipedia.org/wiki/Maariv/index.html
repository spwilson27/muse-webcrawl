Maariv

Maariv (Maʿariv, Hebrew: מַעֲרִיב, "Evening") is a Hebrew-language daily newspaper published in Israel. Founded in 1948, it was for decades one of the country's two leading newspapers, and for many years the most widely read. Its name is taken from the Jewish evening prayer service, also called Maariv (or Arvit).

History

Maariv was founded on 15 February 1948 by Azriel Carlebach, who had been editor of Yedioth Ahronoth, together with a group of journalists who left Yedioth in a dispute over its management. Initially published as an afternoon paper — the first edition appeared the day after the group broke away — it rapidly overtook its competitors, and for much of the 1950s through the 1970s it was Israel's best-selling newspaper.

The paper was owned for many years by the Nimrodi family. In 2012, amid financial difficulties, it was sold to publisher Shlomo Ben-Zvi and merged with the newspaper Makor Rishon; its news website, NRG, had been launched in 2004. In 2014 the paper was sold again, to Eli Azur's Jerusalem Post group, and it continues to appear, mainly as a morning daily alongside its website.

Character

Maariv developed a reputation for accessible popular journalism and a distinctive layout. Its long-serving contributors included political commentators, cartoonists and writers central to Israeli public life.
