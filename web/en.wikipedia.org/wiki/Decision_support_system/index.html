Decision support system

A decision support system (DSS) is an information system that supports business or organizational decision-making activities. DSSs serve the management, operations, and planning levels of an organization (usually mid- and higher-level management) and help people make decisions about problems that may be rapidly changing and not easily specified in advance — that is, unstructured and semi-structured decision problems. Decision support systems can be either fully computerized or human-powered, or a combination of both.

History

The concept grew out of theoretical studies of organizational decision making in the late 1950s and 1960s, including the work of Herbert A. Simon on bounded rationality, and became an established field of research and practice in the 1970s as interactive computer-based systems began to be used by managers. The field expanded through the 1980s with executive information systems, group decision support systems, and knowledge-based systems, and later merged in practice with business intelligence, data warehousing, and analytics.

Components

A typical DSS is described as having three fundamental components:

  • Database management system — stores and provides access to internal and external data relevant to decisions.
  • Model base management system — provides access to statistical, financial, optimization, simulation, and other quantitative models.
  • Dialogue (user interface) management system — enables the decision maker to interact with the system, enter parameters, and explore results.

A knowledge base is sometimes included as a fourth component in intelligent DSSs.

Taxonomies

DSSs are commonly classified by their dominant component as model-driven, data-driven, knowledge-driven, document-driven, or communication-driven (group) systems. They are also distinguished by mode of assistance, from passive systems that merely aid the process, through traditional systems that make suggestions, to normative systems that recommend courses of action.

Applications

Decision support systems are used in many domains: clinical decision support in medicine, financial planning and credit scoring, supply chain and inventory management, agricultural planning, military command and control, and environmental management such as water resources and climate adaptation planning.
