Cervical weakness

In cervical weakness the cervix shortens and dilates painlessly in mid-pregnancy.

Cervical weakness, also known as cervical insufficiency or cervical incompetence, is a medical condition of pregnancy in which the cervix begins to dilate and efface before the pregnancy has reached term, without contractions or labour pain. It is a recognised cause of second-trimester miscarriage and preterm birth, typically defined as the inability of the cervix to retain a pregnancy in the absence of clinical contractions.

Causes and risk factors

Risk factors include previous cervical surgery or trauma (such as cone biopsy, loop electrosurgical excision, or dilation and curettage), congenital uterine or cervical anomalies, in-utero exposure to diethylstilbestrol (DES), and connective tissue disorders such as Ehlers–Danlos syndrome. In many cases no clear cause is identified.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is based on history — classically painless cervical dilation leading to mid-trimester loss — and on transvaginal ultrasound measurement of cervical length (a cervix shorter than 25 mm before 24 weeks is considered short). Management includes cervical cerclage, a stitch placed around the cervix (such as the McDonald or Shirodkar procedures), vaginal progesterone supplementation, and in selected cases a cervical pessary.
