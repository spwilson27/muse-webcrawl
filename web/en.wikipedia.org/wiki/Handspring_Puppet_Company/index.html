The Handspring Puppet Company is a South African puppetry and theatre company based in Cape Town. Founded in 1981 by Adrian Kohler and Basil Jones, together with Jill Joubert and Jon Weinberg, it grew from children's theatre into one of the most acclaimed puppet companies in the world, known for its hand-crafted rod puppets operated in full view of the audience.

Work

Early adult productions such as Episodes of an Easter Rising (1985) and collaborations with artist William Kentridge — including Woyzeck on the Highveld (1992), Faustus in Africa! (1995) and Ubu and the Truth Commission (1997) — brought international attention. The company also creates large processional puppets and works exploring South African history and politics.

War Horse

Handspring's most famous creation is the life-size horse puppets — Joey and Topthorn — for the National Theatre of Great Britain's stage adaptation of Michael Morpurgo's novel War Horse (2007). The cane-and-fabric horses, each operated by three puppeteers, were central to the production's success in London's West End, on Broadway and worldwide, and the company received a Special Tony Award in 2011, among many other honours including the Olivier Award for Best Set Design.
