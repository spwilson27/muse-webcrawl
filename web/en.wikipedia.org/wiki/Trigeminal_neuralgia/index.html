Trigeminal neuralgia (TN), also called tic douloureux, is a long-term pain disorder that affects the trigeminal nerve, the nerve responsible for sensation in the face. It causes episodes of intense, stabbing, electric-shock-like pain in the areas of the face where the branches of the nerve are distributed: the jaw, teeth, gums, lips, and less often the eye and forehead.

Signs and symptoms

Attacks last from a few seconds to several minutes and can be triggered by trivial stimuli such as touching the face, chewing, speaking or brushing the teeth. The pain is usually unilateral and may occur in clusters over weeks, with periods of remission.

Cause

Most cases are attributed to compression of the trigeminal nerve root by a looping blood vessel, leading to demyelination. A minority of cases are secondary to multiple sclerosis, tumours or other structural lesions.

Treatment

The anticonvulsant carbamazepine is the first-line medical treatment. For refractory cases, surgical options include microvascular decompression, stereotactic radiosurgery and percutaneous rhizotomy procedures.
