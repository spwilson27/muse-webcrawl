Tenet v. Doe

Tenet v. Doe, 544 U.S. 1 (2005), was a United States Supreme Court decision in which the Court unanimously held that spies (contract intelligence agents) cannot sue the Central Intelligence Agency or the United States to enforce alleged espionage agreements.

Background

The plaintiffs, a former Soviet diplomat and his wife who had spied for the United States during the Cold War, sued claiming the CIA had promised resettlement assistance and had failed to support them adequately. The named defendant, George J. Tenet, was Director of Central Intelligence.

Decision

Chief Justice William Rehnquist's opinion relied on Totten v. United States (1876), which barred suits founded on secret espionage contracts because litigation would inherently risk disclosing state secrets. The Court held that the suit could not proceed at all, affirming the longstanding Totten bar.
