Alfred Hitchcock filmography

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Alfred Hitchcock (1899–1980) directed more than fifty feature films over a career spanning six decades, from silent British productions of the 1920s to Hollywood classics of the 1950s and 1960s, becoming the most recognisable film director of his era. He also directed episodes of his television anthology series Alfred Hitchcock Presents. Famous for his cameo appearances, he appeared in nearly all of his own films.

Silent films (1920s)

Hitchcock began at Famous Players–Lasky in London designing title cards, then directed his first completed feature, The Pleasure Garden (1925). Early works include The Lodger: A Story of the London Fog (1927), his first thriller and a critical breakthrough; The Ring (1927); Downhill (1927); Easy Virtue (1928); The Farmer's Wife (1928); Champagne (1928); The Manxman (1929); and Blackmail (1929), released in both silent and pioneering sound versions.

Hollywood period (1940s)

Signed by David O. Selznick, Hitchcock debuted in America with Rebecca (1940), which won the Academy Award for Best Picture. The decade continued with Foreign Correspondent (1940), Mr. & Mrs. Smith (1941), Suspicion (1941), Saboteur (1942), Shadow of a Doubt (1943) — his personal favourite of his films — Lifeboat (1944), Spellbound (1945), Notorious (1946), The Paradine Case (1947), Rope (1948), his first in Technicolor and designed to appear as a single take, and Under Capricorn (1949).

Final films

Hitchcock returned to Britain for Frenzy (1972), a return to form, and closed his career with the comedy-thriller Family Plot (1976). Vertigo, initially mixed in reception, was voted the greatest film ever made in the 2012 Sight & Sound critics' poll, displacing Citizen Kane.
