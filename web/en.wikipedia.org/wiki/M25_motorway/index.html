The M25 is a motorway that encircles most of Greater London, England. At 117 miles (188 km), it is one of the longest ring roads in Europe and among the busiest motorways in Britain. It was built in sections between 1975 and 1986, and was opened in full by Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher in October 1986.

Route

The motorway forms an almost complete ring around London, passing through or near counties including Kent, Surrey, Berkshire, Buckinghamshire, Hertfordshire and Essex. The circle is completed in the east by the A282 Dartford Crossing over the Thames. Key junctions connect it with radial motorways: the M20, M26, M23, M3, M4, M40, M1, A1(M) and M11, as well as Heathrow and (via the M23) Gatwick airports.

Traffic and reputation

The M25 carries up to around 200,000 vehicles per day on its busiest sections and is notorious for congestion, variable speed limits and "smart motorway" operation. It has acquired a place in British popular culture as a symbol of traffic misery, while also serving as the informal boundary of the London metropolitan area. Large stretches have been widened to four lanes in each direction, and the Dartford Crossing remains a major bottleneck.
