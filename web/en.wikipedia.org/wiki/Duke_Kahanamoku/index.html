Duke Kahanamoku

Duke Paoa Kahinu Mokoe Hulikohola Kahanamoku (August 24, 1890 – January 22, 1968) was a Hawaiian competition swimmer who popularised the sport of surfing. A Native Hawaiian, he was born in Honolulu to a minor noble family less than three years before the overthrow of the Hawaiian Kingdom. He won five Olympic medals in swimming, including golds in the 100 metres freestyle at the 1912 Stockholm and 1920 Antwerp Games, and is widely regarded as the father of modern surfing.

Swimming career

Kahanamoku burst onto the scene in 1911 in Honolulu Harbor, where his sprint times — unofficially timed and at first doubted by mainland officials — smashed existing records. At the 1912 Olympics he won the 100 m freestyle, and at Antwerp in 1920 he won the 100 m freestyle and the 4×200 m freestyle relay; he took silver at Paris in 1924 behind Johnny Weissmuller, and was a member of the United States water polo team at the 1932 Los Angeles Games. He set world records and, in exhibition tours, introduced swimming techniques and surfboard riding to audiences in the United States and Australia — famously demonstrating board riding at Freshwater Beach, Sydney, in 1915.

Surfing and later life

Kahanamoku grew up surfing at Waikiki on heavy wooden boards in the revived Hawaiian tradition, and his exhibitions, surf clubs and lifelong ambassadorship spread modern surfing around the world. Between and after his Olympic years he lived in Los Angeles, appeared in Hollywood films, worked as a lifeguard and gas station owner, and in 1925 rescued eight men from a capsized yacht off Newport Beach using his surfboard. Returning to Hawaii, he served as sheriff of the City and County of Honolulu for 26 years (1932–1961) and was the territory's and then state's official greeter, welcoming visiting dignitaries and celebrities.

Legacy

Kahanamoku died of a heart attack in Honolulu in 1968 and was given a state funeral, with a procession to Waikiki Beach. A statue of him stands at Waikiki, he is honoured in both the International Swimming Hall of Fame and the United States Olympic Hall of Fame, and the Duke Kahanamoku Invitational Surfing Championship was named for him. In 2015 Google marked his 125th birthday with a Doodle.
