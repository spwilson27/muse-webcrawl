Northern Cyprus, officially the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus (TRNC), is a state that comprises the northeastern portion of the island of Cyprus. It declared independence unilaterally in 1983 and is recognised only by Turkey; all other states and the United Nations recognise the sovereignty of the Republic of Cyprus over the whole island.

History

Following intercommunal violence between Greek and Turkish Cypriots after 1963, Turkish Cypriots withdrew into enclaves. In 1974, after a coup in Cyprus backed by the Greek military junta aiming at union with Greece, Turkey intervened militarily, and the island was partitioned along a UN-patrolled buffer zone, the Green Line. Large-scale population movements followed. In 1983 the Turkish Cypriot leadership declared independence, which the UN Security Council declared legally invalid.

Politics and economy

The TRNC has its own elected president, assembly and courts, but depends heavily on Turkey economically and militarily. In 2004 Turkish Cypriots voted in favour of the UN's Annan Plan for reunification, which Greek Cypriots rejected; the Republic of Cyprus then joined the European Union, with EU law suspended in the north. Negotiations on a bizonal, bicommunal federation have continued intermittently, while some Turkish Cypriot leaders and Turkey have since advocated a two-state solution. The economy relies on tourism, higher education and services, using the Turkish lira.

Geography

Northern Cyprus includes the Kyrenia mountain range along the north coast, the long Karpas Peninsula in the north-east, and part of the Mesaoria plain, including the northern half of Nicosia, the last divided capital in Europe.
