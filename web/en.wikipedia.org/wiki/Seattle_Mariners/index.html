The Seattle Mariners are an American professional baseball team based in Seattle, Washington. They compete in Major League Baseball (MLB) as a member club of the American League West Division. The team joined the American League as an expansion franchise in 1977, playing their home games at the Kingdome until moving to the retractable-roof ballpark now known as T-Mobile Park in 1999. The Mariners are named for the maritime culture of the Pacific Northwest.

History

Seattle had lost its first MLB team, the Pilots, after a single season in 1969 when the club was moved to Milwaukee. The Mariners were created in settlement of a lawsuit over that departure. The early Mariners were consistently poor, not posting a winning season until 1991. The 1990s brought the club's first stars — Ken Griffey Jr., Randy Johnson, Edgar Martínez, and later Alex Rodriguez and Ichiro Suzuki — and its first playoff appearances, including the celebrated 1995 season in which Martínez's double in the Division Series ("The Double") beat the New York Yankees and is credited with saving baseball in Seattle by building momentum for a new publicly financed stadium.

In 2001 the Mariners won 116 regular-season games, tying the MLB record set by the 1906 Chicago Cubs, with rookie Ichiro winning both the Rookie of the Year and Most Valuable Player awards; they fell to the Yankees in the American League Championship Series. Thereafter the club endured the longest postseason drought in North American major professional sports — 21 seasons — before clinching a wild-card berth in 2022, highlighted by Cal Raleigh's walk-off, drought-ending home run on 30 September 2022.

Notable players and honours

Hall of Famers who played principally for Seattle include Ken Griffey Jr. and Edgar Martínez, both of whose numbers (24 and 11) are retired, as is Jackie Robinson's 42 across MLB. Ichiro Suzuki, acquired from Japan's Orix BlueWave in 2001, set the single-season hits record with 262 in 2004 and was elected to the Hall of Fame in 2025. Félix Hernández, "King Félix", won the 2010 Cy Young Award and pitched a perfect game in 2012.

The Mariners remain the only current MLB franchise never to have played in a World Series.
