Socket wrench

A socket wrench is a type of wrench that turns fasteners, typically nuts and bolts, using interchangeable socket heads that fit over the fastener. The most common form is the ratcheting socket wrench, whose handle can be swung back and forth without lifting the socket from the fastener.

Design

Sockets are usually six-point or twelve-point, in metric and imperial sizes, and attach to the handle by a square drive (commonly 1/4", 3/8", or 1/2"). Accessories include extensions, universal joints, and adapters. Other forms include torque wrenches, breaker bars, and speed wrenches.

History

Socket designs date to the 19th century; the ratcheting wrench was popularised in the early 20th century, with manufacturers such as Snap-on (founded 1920) building interchangeable socket systems.
