Bodhicaryāvatāra

Bodhicaryāvatāra
Author: Śāntideva (c. 8th century CE)
Language: Sanskrit
Tradition: Mahāyāna Buddhism (Madhyamaka)
Structure: 10 chapters, about 913 verses
English title: "A Guide to the Bodhisattva's Way of Life"

The Bodhicaryāvatāra ("Entering the Path of Awakening"), often translated as A Guide to the Bodhisattva's Way of Life, is a Sanskrit Mahāyāna Buddhist text composed by the Indian monk Śāntideva around the 8th century CE at Nālandā. It is one of the most beloved and widely studied works of Buddhist literature, especially in the Tibetan tradition, where the 14th Dalai Lama has taught it extensively.

Contents

The poem's ten chapters guide the reader through the development of bodhicitta — the resolve to attain awakening for the benefit of all beings — and the cultivation of the six perfections (pāramitās): generosity, ethics, patience, diligence, meditation, and wisdom. Famous passages include the confession of faults in chapter 2, the meditations on patience in chapter 6, often called the longest sustained argument for patience in Indian literature, and the dedication of merit in chapter 10. Chapter 9 presents Madhyamaka arguments for emptiness (śūnyatā) against rival schools.

Influence

More than a hundred commentaries were written on the text in India and Tibet. It remains a core text of Tibetan Buddhist education and a popular source for contemporary teachings on compassion and altruism.
