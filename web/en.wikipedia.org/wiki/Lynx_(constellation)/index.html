Lynx (constellation)

Lynx
Abbreviation: Lyn
Genitive: Lyncis
Introduced by: Johannes Hevelius, 1687
Brightest star: Alpha Lyncis
Area: 545 sq. deg.

Lynx is a constellation in the northern celestial hemisphere, introduced in the late seventeenth century by the Polish astronomer Johannes Hevelius. It is a faint constellation, containing no bright stars; Hevelius remarked that one would need the eyes of a lynx to see it, which gave the constellation its name.

Stars

The brightest star is Alpha Lyncis, an orange giant of magnitude about 3.1. The constellation contains several notable double stars, such as 38 Lyncis, and the red dwarf Gliese 277. Many of its stars bear only Flamsteed numbers, reflecting how obscure the region was to earlier cataloguers.

Deep-sky objects

Lynx contains the globular cluster NGC 2419, nicknamed the "Intergalactic Wanderer" because of its great distance from the Milky Way's centre, and the spiral galaxies NGC 2683 and the Bear Paw Galaxy (NGC 2537). The Lynx Arc, a distant gravitationally lensed star-forming region, was discovered in this constellation.

History

Because the region was unclaimed by the classical constellations, Hevelius filled it in his 1687 star atlas; the figure is traditionally drawn as a small wild cat crouching between Ursa Major and Auriga.
