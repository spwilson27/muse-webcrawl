Math Olympiad

A mathematics competition or math olympiad is a competition in which students solve mathematical problems under time constraints, usually emphasising creative problem solving and proof rather than routine calculation. Competitions range from school-level contests to the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO), the world's premier competition for pre-university students.

History

The earliest modern competitions were held in Hungary in the late 19th century (the Eötvös competition, from 1894). National olympiads spread through Eastern Europe in the 20th century, and the first IMO was held in Romania in 1959 with seven participating countries; it now attracts over 100 countries, each sending a team of up to six students.

Structure

Most countries select their international teams through a series of rounds, from multiple-choice school contests to proof-based national olympiads and training camps. In the United States the sequence runs from the AMC examinations through the AIME and the USA Mathematical Olympiad (USAMO) to the Mathematical Olympiad Program. IMO problems cover algebra, combinatorics, geometry, and number theory, but require no calculus. Other major events include the International Mathematical Olympiad, the European Girls' Mathematical Olympiad (founded 2012), and the Putnam Competition for university students in North America.
