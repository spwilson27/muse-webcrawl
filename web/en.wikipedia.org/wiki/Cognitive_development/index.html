Cognitive development

Cognitive development is a field of study in neuroscience and psychology focusing on a child's development in terms of information processing, conceptual resources, perceptual skill, language learning, and other aspects of the developed adult brain and cognitive psychology. Qualitative differences between how a child processes their waking experience and how an adult processes their waking experience are acknowledged, such as object permanence, the understanding of logical relations, and cause-and-effect reasoning in school-age children.

Piaget's theory

The Swiss psychologist Jean Piaget proposed the most influential stage theory of cognitive development, describing four stages: the sensorimotor stage (birth to about 2 years), in which infants learn through action and develop object permanence; the preoperational stage (2–7 years), marked by symbolic play, language growth, and egocentrism; the concrete operational stage (7–11 years), in which children master conservation and logical operations on concrete objects; and the formal operational stage (from about 11–12 years), characterised by abstract and hypothetical reasoning.

Sociocultural and information-processing approaches

Lev Vygotsky emphasised the social origins of cognition, proposing the zone of proximal development — the gap between what a child can do alone and with guidance — and the role of language and cultural tools in shaping thought. Information-processing theories instead model development as gradual changes in attention, memory capacity, processing speed, and executive function, studying tasks such as working memory and inhibitory control in fine detail.

Later research

Modern research, using methods such as preferential looking and violation-of-expectation, has shown that infants possess more sophisticated knowledge of objects, number, and other minds than Piaget supposed, fuelling "core knowledge" theories of innate domain-specific systems. Developmental cognitive neuroscience links cognitive change to brain maturation, including synaptic pruning and the protracted development of the prefrontal cortex into early adulthood. Development continues across adolescence and adulthood, with gains in expertise and knowledge alongside declines in fluid abilities in old age.
