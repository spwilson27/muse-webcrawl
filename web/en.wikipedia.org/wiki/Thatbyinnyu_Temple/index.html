Thatbyinnyu Temple

The Thatbyinnyu Temple (Burmese: သဗ္ဗညုဘုရား) is a famous Buddhist temple located in Bagan (Pagan), Myanmar. Completed in 1144 during the reign of King Alaungsithu of the Pagan dynasty, it is the tallest temple in Bagan, rising about 61 metres (201 feet) above the plain. Its name derives from the Pali sabbannu, "the Omniscient", an epithet of the Buddha.

Architecture

Thatbyinnyu marks a transitional stage between the earlier Mon-influenced temple designs of Bagan and the later Burmese style. Unlike the dark, single-storey interiors of earlier temples such as the Ananda Temple, Thatbyinnyu is a five-storey structure with a bright, hollow interior: the principal Buddha image is placed not on the ground floor but on the second storey, and the upper levels are lit by windows — a notable innovation in Bagan architecture. The plan is asymmetrical, with a projecting portico on the north side rather than the eastern entrance typical of earlier temples.

The temple sits on a broad terrace within the walls of Old Bagan, close to the Ananda Temple and the old city gate that still bears its name. Its whitewashed bulk makes it one of the most visible landmarks on the plain.

Damage and restoration

Earthquakes have repeatedly damaged the temple. The great 1975 Bagan earthquake destroyed the central stupa finial and part of the upper shrine; restoration work was carried out with UNESCO assistance. The 2016 Chauk earthquake again damaged the upper structure, and subsequent conservation has been undertaken as part of the inscription of Bagan on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2019.
