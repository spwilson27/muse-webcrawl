American Foxhound

The American Foxhound is a breed of dog that is a cousin of the English Foxhound. They are scent hounds, bred to hunt foxes by scent, and were developed in the American colonies from imported English hounds crossed with French hounds to produce a lighter, faster dog suited to American terrain.

History

English foxhounds were imported to Maryland and Virginia in the 17th and 18th centuries; George Washington, an avid foxhunter, kept and bred hounds at Mount Vernon and crossed them with French hounds sent by the Marquis de Lafayette. Lines developed by hunting families — including the Walker, July, Trigg, and Goodman strains — shaped the modern breed. The American Foxhound was recognised by the American Kennel Club in 1886 and is the state dog of Virginia.

Appearance and temperament

The breed stands about 53–64 cm (21–25 in) at the shoulder and weighs roughly 20–29 kg (45–65 lb), with a short, hard, close-lying coat in any hound colour. American Foxhounds have a famously musical bell-like voice when on the trail. They are gentle, intelligent, and independent, but, as pack hunting dogs, they are energetic, vocal, and require substantial exercise, making them challenging apartment pets.

Health

The breed is generally healthy with a lifespan around 10–13 years. Problems occasionally reported include hip dysplasia, ear infections, and thrombocytopathy (a platelet disorder) in some lines.
