Valentinian III
Reign: 23 October 425 – 16 March 455
Born: 2 July 419, Ravenna
Died: 16 March 455, Rome
Dynasty: Valentinianic / Theodosian
Mother: Galla Placidia (regent)

Valentinian III (Latin: Placidus Valentinianus; 2 July 419 – 16 March 455) was Western Roman emperor from 425 until his assassination in 455, one of the longest reigns of any Roman emperor. Crowned as a small child, he reigned under the regency of his mother Galla Placidia and the dominance of the general Aëtius, during decades in which the Western Empire lost North Africa, Britain and much of Gaul and Hispania, and Rome itself was threatened by Attila the Hun.

Accession

Valentinian was the son of Constantius III and Galla Placidia, daughter of Theodosius I. After the death of the Western emperor Honorius in 423, the usurper Joannes seized power at Ravenna; the Eastern emperor Theodosius II installed his young cousin Valentinian as Caesar, then as Augustus on 23 October 425, restoring him with an Eastern army. The six-year-old emperor's betrothal to Theodosius's daughter Licinia Eudoxia (whom he married in 437) bound the two courts together.

Reign

Real power lay successively with Galla Placidia, the generalissimo Aëtius, and court factions at Ravenna. The great events of the reign were disastrous for the West: the Vandal conquest of North Africa (429–442) stripped the empire of its richest provinces and grain supply; the Hunnic invasions of 451–452, checked at the Catalaunian Plains and turned back from Italy after Pope Leo I's embassy to Attila; and the growing independence of Visigothic and Frankish kingdoms.

Death and succession

In September 454 Valentinian personally murdered Aëtius, an act contemporaries described as cutting off his right hand with his left. Six months later, on 16 March 455, two of Aëtius's former retainers assassinated the emperor as he practised archery on the Campus Martius. He left no son; his successor Petronius Maximus reigned only weeks before the Vandal sack of Rome in June 455. Valentinian's reign marks the effective end of hereditary, long-lived rule in the West: none of the nine emperors who followed him reigned for more than a few years.
