Ulster Rugby is one of the four professional provincial rugby union teams from the island of Ireland. It represents the IRFU Ulster Branch, covering the province of Ulster — the six counties of Northern Ireland plus Cavan, Donegal, and Monaghan in the Republic of Ireland — and plays its home games at the Kingspan Stadium (Ravenhill) in Belfast.

Ulster in their white home kit at the Kingspan Stadium.

History

The Ulster Branch of the IRFU was founded in 1879, and the provincial side played representative fixtures for over a century, including celebrated victories over touring sides. With the advent of professionalism in 1995, Ulster became one of Ireland's four professional provincial teams.

The club's finest achievement came in January 1999, when Ulster defeated French side Colomiers 21–6 at Lansdowne Road in Dublin to win the European Cup (Heineken Cup), becoming the first Irish team to lift the trophy. Ulster won the Celtic League in 2005–06 and reached a second European final in 2012, losing to Leinster at Twickenham.

Stadium

Ravenhill, Ulster's home since 1923, was redeveloped between 2012 and 2014 with sponsorship naming as the Kingspan Stadium, raising capacity to just over 18,000.

Supporters and identity

Ulster Rugby draws support from across the community in Northern Ireland and the border counties; its anthem among supporters is "Stand Up for the Ulstermen". Notable players to have represented Ulster include Rory Best, Tommy Bowe, Stephen Ferris, Andrew Trimble, and Iain Henderson.
