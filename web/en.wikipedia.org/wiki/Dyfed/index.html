Dyfed (Welsh pronunciation: [ˈdəvɛd]) is a preserved county in southwestern Wales. It was originally formed as a county council area on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, combining the historic counties of Cardiganshire, Carmarthenshire and Pembrokeshire. It takes its name from the early medieval Welsh kingdom of Dyfed, itself named after the Demetae tribe of the Roman period.

History

The early medieval kingdom of Dyfed occupied approximately the territory of modern Pembrokeshire and western Carmarthenshire. Its kings appear in the genealogies of the Welsh princes, and the kingdom was eventually absorbed into the realm of Deheubarth.

As a local government county, Dyfed existed from 1974 until 31 March 1996, when it was split into the three unitary authorities of Ceredigion, Carmarthenshire and Pembrokeshire. The name survives as a preserved county for lieutenancy purposes, and in bodies such as Dyfed-Powys Police and the Dyfed Pension Fund.

Geography

Dyfed is largely rural, containing the Pembrokeshire Coast National Park, the southern slopes of the Cambrian Mountains and the Preseli Hills, source of the bluestones of Stonehenge. Its principal towns include Carmarthen, Aberystwyth, Haverfordwest and Llanelli.
