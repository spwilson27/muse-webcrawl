Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic

Flag of the Latvian SSR (1953–1990)

The Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic (Latvian: Latvijas Padomju Sociālistiskā Republika), also known as Soviet Latvia, was a constituent republic of the Soviet Union from 1940 to 1991. It was proclaimed on 21 July 1940, following the Soviet occupation of the independent Republic of Latvia, and was formally admitted to the USSR on 5 August 1940. Its capital was Riga.

History

Under the secret protocols of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, Latvia fell within the Soviet sphere of influence. After an ultimatum and military occupation in June 1940, a staged election produced a pro-Soviet assembly that requested incorporation into the USSR. The first Soviet occupation (1940–41) brought nationalisation, arrests, and the June 1941 deportation of more than 15,000 people to Siberia. Nazi Germany occupied Latvia from 1941 to 1944–45, during which most of Latvia's Jewish population was murdered in the Holocaust. Soviet forces retook the country in 1944–45.

Postwar decades

The postwar years saw renewed deportations — in March 1949 some 42,000 people were deported during forced collectivisation — as well as guerrilla resistance by the "Forest Brothers". Latvia was heavily industrialised and subject to large-scale immigration from other Soviet republics, which reduced the ethnic Latvian share of the population from about 77 percent before the war to about 52 percent by 1989.

End of the republic

During the Singing Revolution, the Latvian independence movement grew rapidly. On 4 May 1990 the republic's Supreme Council adopted a declaration restoring the independence of the Republic of Latvia, renaming the state and beginning a transition period. Full independence was recognised by the USSR in September 1991, after the failed August coup in Moscow.
