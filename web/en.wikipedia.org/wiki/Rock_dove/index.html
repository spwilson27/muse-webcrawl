Rock dove

The rock dove, rock pigeon, or common pigeon (Columba livia) is a member of the bird family Columbidae (doves and pigeons). In common usage, it is often simply referred to as the "pigeon". The domestic pigeon (Columba livia domestica), which includes about 1,000 different breeds, descended from this species.

Description

The wild rock dove is pale grey with two black bars on each wing, a white rump, and iridescent green and purple feathers on the neck. It measures 29 to 37 cm (11 to 15 in) in length with a wingspan of 62 to 72 cm (24 to 28 in).

Distribution and habitat

Wild rock doves nest on sea cliffs and inland rock faces across Europe, North Africa, and western and southern Asia. Escaped domestic birds have established feral populations in cities worldwide, where buildings substitute for cliff faces.

Domestication

Rock doves have been domesticated for several thousand years, giving rise to fancy breeds, racing homers, and utility pigeons. Charles Darwin used pigeon breeding as a key analogy for natural selection in On the Origin of Species.
