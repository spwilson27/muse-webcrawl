Ford Explorer

ManufacturerFord Motor Company
Production1990–present
ClassMid-size SUV / crossover

The Ford Explorer is a range of sport utility vehicles manufactured by the Ford Motor Company since the 1991 model year. The first mid-size SUV sold by Ford, it played a central role in popularizing the SUV segment in the United States during the 1990s.

History

Launched in 1990 as a replacement for the smaller Bronco II, the first-generation Explorer was offered in three- and five-door forms and became one of America's best-selling vehicles. In the late 1990s the model was at the center of the Firestone tire controversy, when tire tread separations on Explorers were linked to rollover accidents and a massive recall.

Later generations

Subsequent generations grew in size and refinement. The fifth generation (2011) switched from a body-on-frame truck platform to a unibody crossover design based on the Ford Taurus platform, while the sixth generation (2020) returned to rear-wheel drive with a longitudinal engine layout, built alongside the Lincoln Aviator in Chicago. A police version, the Police Interceptor Utility, became the best-selling law-enforcement vehicle in the United States.
