Space industry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The space industry comprises the companies, government agencies and other organisations engaged in spaceflight, satellite manufacture and operation, launch services, and related ground equipment and services. It forms the commercial core of the wider 'space economy', which also includes consumer services dependent on space infrastructure.

Sectors

Major sectors include satellite communications, Earth observation, global navigation satellite services, launch vehicle production and operation, crewed and cargo spacecraft, space stations, and emerging activities such as on-orbit servicing, space tourism and lunar exploration support.

Scale and development

Long dominated by government programmes and established aerospace contractors, the industry was transformed from the 2000s by 'NewSpace' entrants such as SpaceX, whose reusable Falcon rockets sharply cut launch costs and made large satellite constellations such as Starlink practical. Estimates of the global space economy in the 2020s run to several hundred billion US dollars annually, with commercial revenue exceeding government budgets.
