Japanese war crimes

Japanese war crimes were committed by the Empire of Japan during its period of expansion, principally in the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945) and the Pacific War. They included massacres of civilians, mistreatment of prisoners of war, forced labour, sexual slavery and biological warfare experiments.

Major incidents

The Nanjing Massacre (1937–1938) saw the mass murder and rape of Chinese civilians in the captured capital. Unit 731 conducted lethal human experimentation and biological warfare in Manchuria. The army's system of forced prostitution ("comfort women") enslaved women across occupied Asia, and the Bataan Death March and the Burma–Thailand "Death Railway" killed many Allied prisoners and Asian labourers.

Trials and aftermath

After the war, the International Military Tribunal for the Far East (the Tokyo Trial, 1946–1948) convicted senior leaders, sentencing seven, including Prime Minister Hideki Tojo, to death; many other trials were held across Asia. The extent of Imperial responsibility, textbook treatment, and apologies and compensation remain politically contested in East Asia.
