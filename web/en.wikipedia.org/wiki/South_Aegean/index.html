South Aegean

The South Aegean (Greek: Περιφέρεια Νοτίου Αιγαίου) is one of the thirteen administrative regions of Greece. It consists of the Cyclades and Dodecanese island groups in the south-eastern Aegean Sea. The region's capital is Ermoupoli on the island of Syros, while its largest island is Rhodes.

Geography

The region comprises the Cyclades — including Mykonos, Santorini (Thira), Naxos, Paros, Syros, Milos and Andros — and the Dodecanese — including Rhodes, Kos, Karpathos, Kalymnos, Leros and Patmos. The islands are among Greece's most important tourist destinations, famous for whitewashed villages, beaches, and archaeological sites such as Delos and the medieval Old Town of Rhodes.

Administration

Like all Greek regions, the South Aegean was established in its modern form by the 1987 administrative reform; under the 2011 Kallikratis Plan it became a self-governing region with an elected regional council and governor. It is subdivided into 13 regional units corresponding largely to island groups.

Economy

Tourism dominates the economy, supplemented by shipping, agriculture (wine, olives, tomatoes), and marble quarrying on Paros and Naxos. The region contains the active South Aegean volcanic arc, including the volcanoes of Santorini and Nisyros.
