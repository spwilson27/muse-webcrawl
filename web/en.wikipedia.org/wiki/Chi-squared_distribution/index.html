Overview

In probability theory and statistics, the chi-squared distribution (also chi-square or chi2 distribution) with k degrees of freedom is the distribution of a sum of the squares of k independent standard normal random variables. It is a special case of the gamma distribution and one of the most widely used probability distributions in inferential statistics.

Definition

If Z1, ..., Zk are independent standard normal variables, then the sum of their squares follows a chi-squared distribution with k degrees of freedom. Its probability density function involves the gamma function, and the distribution is defined only for non-negative values. For large degrees of freedom it approaches a normal distribution with mean k and variance 2k.

Applications

The chi-squared distribution underlies the chi-squared test for independence and goodness of fit, confidence intervals for the variance of a normal population, and hypothesis testing in contingency tables. It is closely related to Student's t distribution and the F distribution used in regression and analysis of variance.
