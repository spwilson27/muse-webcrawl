Calgary Stampede

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Venue: Stampede Park, Calgary, Alberta, Canada
First held: 1912
Attendance: ~1 million over 10 days

The Calgary Stampede is an annual rodeo, exhibition and festival held every July in Calgary, Alberta, Canada. The ten-day event, which bills itself as "The Greatest Outdoor Show on Earth," attracts over one million visitors per year and features one of the world's largest rodeos, a parade, midway, stage shows, concerts, agricultural competitions, chuckwagon racing and First Nations exhibitions.

History

American promoter Guy Weadick organized the first Stampede in 1912 with financing from Calgary's "Big Four" ranchers and businessmen. After a successful Frontier Days in 1919, the event merged with the Calgary Industrial Exhibition in 1923 and became an annual fixture. The Stampede survived the 2013 Alberta floods, which inundated the grounds weeks before the event.

Events

The Stampede Rodeo offers one of the richest purses in professional rodeo, with events including bull riding, barrel racing, steer wrestling, tie-down roping, saddle bronc and bareback riding. The evening chuckwagon races—the "Rangeland Derby"—are a signature attraction dating to 1923. The Stampede Parade, led by notable marshals, opens the festival through downtown Calgary.

Cultural significance

The Stampede is central to Calgary's identity as "Stampede City" and to Canada's image of western heritage, though its animal events have drawn sustained criticism from animal-welfare groups, leading to rule and format changes over the years.
