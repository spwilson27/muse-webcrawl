Goldfish

Scientific nameCarassius auratus
FamilyCyprinidae
OriginEast Asia (domesticated in China)
LifespanCommonly 10–15 years; recorded over 40

The goldfish (Carassius auratus) is a freshwater fish in the carp family Cyprinidae, and one of the most commonly kept aquarium fish in the world. It was domesticated in China over a thousand years ago from the Prussian carp, and numerous distinct breeds are now recognised.

History

During the Tang and Song dynasties, Chinese breeders raised carp with naturally occurring red, orange, and yellow colour mutations in ponds. By the Song dynasty (960–1279), selective breeding of yellow — the imperial colour — was established. Goldfish were introduced to Japan in the 1500s and to Europe in the late 17th century, where they became fashionable pets.

Biology and varieties

Goldfish are omnivorous, cold-temperate fish with strong learning abilities; contrary to popular myth, their memory spans at least several months. Selective breeding has produced varieties including the common goldfish, comet, shubunkin, fantail, veiltail, oranda, ranchu, telescope eye, and bubble eye, differing in body shape, finnage, and eyes. Given adequate space and care, common goldfish can grow beyond 30 cm (12 in) and live for decades.

As an invasive species

Released pet goldfish have established feral populations in many countries, where they can grow large, stir up sediment, and compete with native fish; they are regarded as an invasive pest in parts of North America and Australia.
