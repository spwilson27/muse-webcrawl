Charley Patton (April 1891 – April 28, 1934), also known as Charlie Patton, was an American Delta blues musician and songwriter. Considered by many to be the "Father of the Delta Blues", he created an enduring body of American music and inspired most Delta blues musicians. The musicologist Robert Palmer considered him one of the most important American musicians of the twentieth century.

Biography

Patton was born in Hinds County, Mississippi, and spent most of his life in the Mississippi Delta, much of it at Dockery Plantation near Ruleville, where he learned guitar from Henry Sloan and became mentor to younger players including Howlin' Wolf, Robert Johnson and John Lee Hooker. A consummate showman, he played guitar behind his head and between his legs, and his gravelly, powerful voice could reportedly be heard 500 yards away without amplification.

Recordings

Patton recorded some 57 sides for Paramount Records in 1929–1930 and a final session for Vocalion in New York in 1934, weeks before his death from a heart condition. His best-known songs include "Pony Blues", "High Water Everywhere" (about the Great Mississippi Flood of 1927), "A Spoonful Blues", "Down the Dirt Road Blues" and "Mississippi Boweavil Blues". He also recorded gospel songs under the pseudonym Elder J.J. Hadley.

Legacy

Patton was inducted into the Blues Hall of Fame in 1980, and his complete recordings have been reissued in acclaimed box sets. Bob Dylan dedicated the song "'Til I Fell in Love with You" to him. His influence through the Delta tradition runs into rock and roll, and he is frequently placed at the head of any history of the blues.
