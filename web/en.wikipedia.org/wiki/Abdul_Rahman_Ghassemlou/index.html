Abdul Rahman Ghassemlou (Kurdish: عەبدولڕەحمان قاسملوو; 22 December 1930 – 13 July 1989) was an Iranian Kurdish politician and academic who led the Democratic Party of Iranian Kurdistan (PDKI) from 1973 until his assassination in Vienna in 1989, in a killing widely attributed to Iranian government agents — the same negotiators with whom he was meeting.

Early life and education

Ghassemlou was born in Urmia to a wealthy landowning family. He co-founded a Kurdish youth organisation in his teens, studied in Tehran, and in 1948 went to Europe, studying economics in Paris and then Prague, where he taught himself several languages and became an assistant professor of economics. He briefly joined and then left Iran's Tudeh Party. He returned to Iran in 1952, worked with the PDKI, and later taught at Prague again before returning to Iranian Kurdistan.

Leadership of the PDKI

Elected secretary-general in 1973, Ghassemlou rebuilt the PDKI as a social-democratic movement demanding "democracy for Iran, autonomy for Kurdistan". After the 1979 Iranian Revolution he led the Kurdish resistance to the Islamic Republic's military offensives from bases in the mountains along the Iraqi border, while continuing to seek a negotiated settlement.

Assassination

On 13 July 1989, in a Vienna apartment, Ghassemlou and two colleagues, Abdullah Ghaderi-Azar and Fadil Rasoul, were shot dead during a secret meeting with men presenting themselves as envoys of President Rafsanjani. Austrian investigators identified Iranian-linked suspects, but they were allowed to leave Austria amid political pressure, and no one was convicted. Ghassemlou's successor, Sadegh Sharafkandi, was likewise assassinated in Berlin in 1992 (the Mykonos affair).
