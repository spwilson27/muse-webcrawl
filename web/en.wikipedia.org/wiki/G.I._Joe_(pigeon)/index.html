G.I. Joe (pigeon)

G.I. Joe (March 24, 1943 – June 3, 1961) was a pigeon famous for his service in the United States Army Pigeon Service during World War II. He is credited with saving the lives of a thousand troops during the Italian campaign.

Service

On 18 October 1943, the village of Calvi Vecchia, Italy, had been captured by Allied forces, but a scheduled American bombing raid on the German positions there risked striking the British troops who had taken the town. A request for the raid to be called off, sent by radio, failed, so G.I. Joe was dispatched with the message. The pigeon flew 20 miles in 20 minutes, arriving just as the bombers were preparing to take off, and the attack was halted.

Honours and legacy

In November 1946 G.I. Joe was presented with the Dickin Medal — often described as the animals' Victoria Cross — at a ceremony at the Tower of London, the only American animal to receive it during the war era presentations for pigeons. After the war he was housed at the U.S. Army's pigeon facility at Fort Monmouth, New Jersey, and later at the Detroit Zoological Gardens, where he died in 1961. His body was mounted and displayed at Fort Monmouth and later at the U.S. Army Communications-Electronics Museum.
