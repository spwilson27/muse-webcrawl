Householder transformation

In linear algebra, the Householder transformation (also known as a Householder reflection or elementary reflector) is a linear transformation that describes a reflection about a plane or hyperplane containing the origin. It was introduced by Alston Scott Householder in 1958.

Definition

Given a nonzero vector v, the Householder matrix is

H = I − 2 vvT / (vTv)

where I is the identity matrix. H is symmetric and orthogonal (HT = H−1 = H), has determinant −1, and maps v to −v while fixing every vector orthogonal to v.

Applications

Householder reflectors are the standard tool for introducing zeros into vectors and matrices in numerical linear algebra. Successive reflectors reduce a matrix to Hessenberg or tridiagonal form, and they form the basis of the most stable method for computing the QR decomposition: choosing v so that Hx is a multiple of a unit vector annihilates all but the first component of x. They are likewise used in least-squares solvers and eigenvalue algorithms, where their perfect orthogonality confers backward stability.
