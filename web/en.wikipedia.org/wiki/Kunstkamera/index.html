Kunstkamera

LocationSaint Petersburg, Russia
Established1714
TypeAnthropology and ethnography museum

The Kunstkamera is the first museum in Russia, established by Peter the Great and completed in 1727. Located on the Neva River embankment in Saint Petersburg, it is now the museum of anthropology and ethnography of the Russian Academy of Sciences, officially named the Peter the Great Museum of Anthropology and Ethnography.

History

Peter the Great founded the museum in 1714, initially housing it in the Kikin Palace. Its first collection grew from anatomical preparations, curious natural specimens, and objects the tsar had gathered on his travels, including anatomical models purchased from the Dutch anatomist Frederik Ruysch. The museum's distinctive baroque tower building on Vasilievsky Island was constructed between 1718 and 1734.

Collections

The museum holds nearly two million items, including ethnographic collections from peoples around the world, anatomical specimens, and artifacts collected by Russian explorers such as Nikolai Miklukho-Maklai. Its tower once housed astronomical instruments and a large Gottorf globe.
