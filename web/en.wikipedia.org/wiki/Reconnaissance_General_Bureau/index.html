The Reconnaissance General Bureau (RGB) is the primary foreign intelligence service of North Korea.

The Reconnaissance General Bureau (Korean: 정찰총국; Chongch'al Ch'ongguk), often abbreviated RGB, is North Korea's primary foreign intelligence agency. It is responsible for clandestine operations abroad, including espionage, special operations, and cyber warfare. The RGB operates under the General Staff Department of the Korean People's Army (KPA), although it is widely assessed to report in practice to the senior leadership of the Workers' Party of Korea and the country's supreme leader.

History

The RGB was created in 2009 by consolidating several existing intelligence and special-operations organisations. The merger brought together the Reconnaissance Bureau of the KPA General Staff, Office 35 of the Workers' Party (responsible for intelligence collection and operations in South Korea), and elements of the party's Operations Department. The reorganisation placed both human intelligence and military special-operations assets under a single command, making the RGB responsible for the dispatch of agents, infiltration teams, and saboteurs abroad.

General Kim Yong-chol, a senior KPA officer long associated with inter-Korean affairs, was widely reported to have headed the bureau for much of the period after its creation. Like other North Korean intelligence organs, the bureau's internal workings are opaque, and details of its leadership and structure are reconstructed by foreign governments and defectors rather than officially published.

Organisation

Analysts generally describe the RGB as being organised into several bureaus with functional responsibilities, including operations in South Korea, overseas intelligence collection, technical intelligence, and rear-services support for agents in the field. Its responsibilities have been reported to include:

  • Clandestine collection against South Korea, Japan, the United States, and other targets;
  • Training and dispatch of special-operations forces for infiltration missions;
  • Cyber operations, conducted through subordinate units such as Bureau 121;
  • Support to North Korean intelligence officers operating under diplomatic and commercial cover abroad.

Cyber operations

The RGB is best known internationally for its cyber units, which foreign governments have linked to a series of high-profile operations attributed to North Korea. These include the 2014 destructive attack on Sony Pictures Entertainment, attributed by the United States to North Korea in retaliation for the film The Interview; the 2016 theft of US$81 million from Bangladesh Bank via the SWIFT network; and the 2017 WannaCry ransomware attack. Cyber units under the RGB umbrella are also assessed to conduct financially motivated operations, including cryptocurrency theft, intended to generate revenue for the state in defiance of international sanctions.

Sanctions

The RGB has been designated under sanctions by the United States, the European Union, and the United Nations Security Council, which list it as an entity involved in North Korea's weapons programmes and illicit revenue generation.
