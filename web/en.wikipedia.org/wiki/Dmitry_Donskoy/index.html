Dmitry Ivanovich (12 October 1350 – 19 May 1389), known as Dmitry Donskoy ("of the Don"), was Grand Prince of Moscow from 1359 and Grand Prince of Vladimir from 1363 until his death. He was the first prince of Moscow to openly challenge Mongol (Golden Horde) authority in Russia, and his victory over the forces of Mamai at the Battle of Kulikovo in 1380 earned him his epithet and a lasting place in Russian national memory.

Reign

Succeeding his father Ivan II as a child, Dmitry was guided by Metropolitan Alexy and secured the grand-princely patent of Vladimir over rival Tver with Mongol approval. He rebuilt the Moscow Kremlin in white stone (1367–68), defeated rival princes, and repelled two sieges by Algirdas of Lithuania. In 1378 his forces defeated a Mongol raiding army at the Vozha River; two years later, blessed — according to tradition — by St Sergius of Radonezh, he led a coalition of Russian princes across the Don to defeat Mamai at Kulikovo Field on 8 September 1380.

Legacy

The triumph was short-lived: in 1382 Khan Tokhtamysh sacked Moscow and reimposed tribute, which Dmitry's successors continued to pay for another century. Nevertheless, Kulikovo came to symbolise Russian unity and resistance, and Dmitry was canonised by the Russian Orthodox Church in 1988. His name was later borne by warships, including the imperial cruiser Dmitrii Donskoi and a modern Russian nuclear submarine.
