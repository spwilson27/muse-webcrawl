Hayfield Fight

The Hayfield Fight was an engagement during Red Cloud's War on 1 August 1867, near Fort C. F. Smith in present-day Montana, United States. A small detachment of U.S. Army soldiers and civilian hay cutters, defending a log corral, held off a much larger force of Lakota Sioux and Cheyenne warriors for most of a day.

Background

Fort C. F. Smith guarded the Bozeman Trail through territory claimed by the Lakota and their allies. Parties from the fort cut hay for winter forage in a meadow a few miles away, protected by an infantry escort and a corral of log boxes. The day before the fight, a similar haying party near Fort Philip Kearny had fought the Wagon Box Fight.

The fight

On the morning of 1 August, several hundred warriors attacked the working party. The soldiers and civilians — about thirty men under Lieutenant Sigismund Sternberg — took refuge in the corral. Crucially, they were armed in part with new Springfield Model 1866 breech-loading rifles, whose rapid fire, unlike the muzzle-loaders used in the Fetterman disaster of the previous December, discouraged massed charges. Sternberg was killed early in the fight and command passed to Sergeant James Gibson. The defenders held out until a relief column with a howitzer arrived from the fort. Reported casualties were light on the American side — three killed and a few wounded — while Native losses are uncertain.

Significance

Together with the Wagon Box Fight the following day, the Hayfield Fight demonstrated the effect of the Army's new breech-loading weapons in open defensive positions, though the war itself ended in 1868 with the Treaty of Fort Laramie and the abandonment of the Bozeman Trail forts.
