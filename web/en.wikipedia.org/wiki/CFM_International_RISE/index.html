CFM International RISE

The CFM International RISE ("Revolutionary Innovation for Sustainable Engines") is a technology development and demonstration programme launched by CFM International, the joint venture between GE Aerospace and Safran Aircraft Engines, on 14 June 2021. The programme aims to develop a new generation of aircraft engines delivering more than 20 percent lower fuel consumption and CO2 emissions than the CFM LEAP engine, with entry into service targeted for the mid-2030s.

Development

The RISE programme encompasses more than 300 separate component, module and full-engine demonstration tests. Its centerpiece technology is the open fan (open rotor) architecture: a single rotating fan stage with variable-pitch composite blades, followed by a set of stationary outlet guide vanes, without the surrounding nacelle duct of a conventional turbofan. Removing the duct eliminates nacelle drag and weight and allows a much larger fan diameter, raising propulsive efficiency.

The programme also develops a compact high-pressure core, advanced heat-resistant materials including ceramic matrix composites, hybrid-electric technologies, and compatibility with 100 percent sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) and hydrogen. A ground and flight test demonstrator based on an Airbus A380 testbed was announced with Airbus, with flight demonstrations planned later in the decade.

Background

Open rotor concepts were flight-tested in the 1980s with the GE36 unducted fan, but low oil prices and cabin noise challenges kept them from production. Modern blade aerodynamics, computational design, and acoustic treatment have renewed interest, and CFM positions RISE as the technology base for the successor to the narrowbody aircraft powered by the LEAP, including a future replacement for the Boeing 737 and Airbus A320 families.
