Gun laws of Australia

Gun laws in Australia are administered by the states and territories under a framework harmonised by the National Firearms Agreement (NFA) of 1996. The agreement was adopted following the Port Arthur massacre of April 1996, in which 35 people were killed, and introduced strict licensing and registration, a ban on most semi-automatic rifles and semi-automatic and pump-action shotguns, and a government-funded buyback that destroyed more than 650,000 firearms.

The framework

Under the NFA and state legislation, a person must hold a firearms licence, demonstrate a genuine reason for owning a gun (personal protection is not accepted), complete safety training, store firearms securely, and register each firearm. Firearms are divided into categories A to H of increasing restriction, from air rifles and rimfire rifles to handguns and prohibited weapons.

Effects and debate

Australia has had no mass shootings on the scale of Port Arthur in the decades since, and firearm homicide and suicide rates continued their decline. Researchers debate how much of the reduction is attributable to the laws versus the buyback and pre-existing trends, but the Australian model is frequently cited in international gun-policy debates.
