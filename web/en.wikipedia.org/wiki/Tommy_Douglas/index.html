Thomas Clement Douglas (20 October 1904 – 24 February 1986) was a Scottish-born Canadian politician who served as the seventh premier of Saskatchewan from 1944 to 1961 and as the first federal leader of the New Democratic Party (NDP) from 1961 to 1971. He is best known as the "Father of Medicare" for introducing North America's first universal, publicly funded health care system. In 2004 a CBC television series named him "The Greatest Canadian" of all time.

Early life and Saskatchewan

Douglas's family emigrated from Falkirk, Scotland, to Winnipeg, Manitoba, in 1910. A childhood leg injury, cured for free by a Winnipeg orthopaedic surgeon after his family could scarcely afford treatment, shaped his conviction that health care should not depend on wealth. Ordained a Baptist minister, he became pastor in Weyburn, Saskatchewan, during the Great Depression, and entered politics with the Co-operative Commonwealth Federation (CCF), winning a federal seat in 1935.

Premier and Medicare

As CCF leader, Douglas led his party to victory in Saskatchewan in 1944 — the first avowedly socialist government in North America. His governments balanced budgets, built Crown corporations in electricity, telephones and insurance, and in 1947 introduced universal hospital insurance. The culmination was the Saskatchewan Medical Care Insurance Act of 1961, implemented in 1962 by his successor Woodrow Lloyd through a bitter doctors' strike. The model was adopted nationally by Lester B. Pearson's federal Medical Care Act of 1966.

Federal career and legacy

Douglas resigned as premier in 1961 to lead the newly formed NDP, serving in the House of Commons through most of the decade and as party leader until 1971. His folksy storytelling — most famously the fable "Mouseland" — made him a beloved national figure. He is the grandfather of actor Kiefer Sutherland.
