Oesophagus

The oesophagus (American English: esophagus) is an organ in vertebrates through which food passes, aided by peristaltic contractions, from the pharynx to the stomach. The oesophagus is a fibromuscular tube, about 25 cm (10 in) long in adult humans, that travels behind the trachea and heart, passes through the diaphragm, and empties into the uppermost and anterior part of the stomach.

Structure and function

During swallowing, food passes from the mouth through the pharynx, into the oesophagus, and onwards to the stomach. The word oesophagus is from Ancient Greek οἰσοφάγος (oisophágos), from οἴσω (oísō, "I shall carry") + ἔφαγον (éphagon, "I ate"). Conditions affecting it include reflux (heartburn), Barrett's oesophagus, and oesophageal cancer.
