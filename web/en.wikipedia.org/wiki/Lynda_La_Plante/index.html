Lynda La Plante CBE (born 15 March 1943) is an English author, screenwriter and former actress, best known for creating the television crime series Prime Suspect and Widows. She trained at the Royal Academy of Dramatic Art and acted under the name Lynda Marchal before turning to writing.

Career

La Plante's breakthrough came with the 1983 series Widows, about four women who take over their dead husbands' armed robbery plan. In 1991 she created Prime Suspect, starring Helen Mirren as Detective Chief Inspector Jane Tennison; the series won numerous awards, including BAFTAs and an Emmy, and is regarded as a landmark of British television drama. Through her production company, La Plante Productions, she retained unusual creative control over her work.

Her many other television credits include Bella Mafia, Civvies, The Commander and Above Suspicion, the latter adapted from her bestselling novels featuring detective Anna Travis. She has also written numerous standalone crime novels, including the prequel series Tennison and the Jack Warr books. She was appointed CBE in 2008 for services to literature, drama and charity.
