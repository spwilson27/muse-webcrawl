Mayapan (Spanish: Mayapán) is a pre-Columbian Maya site a couple of kilometres south of the town of Telchaquillo in the Mexican state of Yucatán, about 40 km southeast of Mérida. It was the political and cultural capital of the Maya in Yucatán from approximately the late 1220s until the 1440s, succeeding Chichen Itza as the dominant power in the northern lowlands.

History

According to Maya chronicles, Mayapan was founded by the culture hero Kukulkan and ruled jointly by a confederation of noble lineages — the multepal or joint rule — headed by the Cocom family, with lords from the Xiu, Chel, Canul and other houses resident (and effectively hostage) in the city. The city's principal temple is a smaller replica of El Castillo at Chichen Itza. Around 1441–1461 a revolt led by the Xiu destroyed the city: the Cocom rulers were massacred, Mayapan was abandoned, and Yucatán fragmented into competing provinces that the Spanish encountered a century later.

The site

The walled city covers about 4.2 km² within a defensive wall some 9 km long and contained more than 4,000 structures, including an estimated 2,000 houses densely packed around a ceremonial centre of pyramids, colonnaded halls, oratories and shrines. Its population is estimated at 15,000–17,000. Long-term archaeological mapping and excavation, notably by the Carnegie Institution in the 1950s and by PEMY (Proyecto Económico de Mayapán) in the 2000s, revealed evidence of dense urbanism, household production and long-distance trade in obsidian, copper and ceramics, as well as mass burials and burned buildings associated with the city's violent fall.
