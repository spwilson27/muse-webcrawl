Stok Palace

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stok Palace is a palace in Stok, Ladakh, in northern India, about 15 kilometres south of Leh. It was built in 1820 by King Tsepal Namgyal and became the summer home of the royal family of Ladakh after the royal residence at Leh Palace was abandoned. The palace remains the residence of the descendants of the Namgyal dynasty, and one wing houses a museum.

History and architecture

The four-storey palace is built in traditional Ladakhi style, with mud-brick and timber construction, carved wooden balconies, and painted interiors. Its architecture reflects Tibetan influences typical of the region. King Tsepal Namgyal moved the court here from Leh, and the palace was the seat of the last kings of Ladakh before the kingdom's absorption into the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir in the 19th century.

Museum

The palace museum displays royal artefacts, including thangkas, jewellery, crowns, coins, ceremonial objects, and a celebrated 12th-century thangka collection. A notable exhibit is a knotted sword, said to have been tied in a knot by an oracle. The palace also hosts the Stok Guru Tsechu festival, featuring masked cham dances by monks of the Spituk monastery.
