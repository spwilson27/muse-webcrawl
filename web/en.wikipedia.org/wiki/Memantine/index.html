Memantine is a medication used to treat moderate-to-severe Alzheimer's disease. It acts as a low-to-moderate affinity, uncompetitive antagonist of the N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptor, reducing abnormal glutamatergic activity thought to contribute to neuronal dysfunction in dementia. It is sold under brand names including Namenda and Ebixa.

Medical uses

Memantine is approved for moderate-to-severe Alzheimer's disease, alone or in combination with a cholinesterase inhibitor such as donepezil (a fixed-dose combination is marketed as Namzaric). Evidence for mild Alzheimer's disease is insufficient. It has been studied off-label for other conditions, including vascular dementia and obsessive–compulsive disorder, with mixed results.

Pharmacology

Memantine blocks NMDA receptors in a use-dependent manner, preferentially inhibiting excessive, tonic glutamate activation while leaving normal synaptic transmission relatively intact. It also has antagonist activity at 5-HT3 and nicotinic acetylcholine receptors and agonist activity at dopamine D2 receptors at higher concentrations. The drug is excreted largely unchanged by the kidneys, with a long elimination half-life of roughly 60–80 hours.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include dizziness, headache, constipation, and confusion; the drug is generally well tolerated compared with cholinesterase inhibitors. Dose reduction is recommended in severe renal impairment.

History

Memantine was first synthesised at Eli Lilly in 1963 and developed by Merz Pharma in Germany, where it was marketed for dementia from the 1980s. It was approved in the European Union in 2002 and by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2003.
