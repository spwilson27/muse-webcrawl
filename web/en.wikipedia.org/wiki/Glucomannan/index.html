Glucomannan

Glucomannan is a water-soluble dietary fibre and polysaccharide composed of β-(1→4)-linked D-mannose and D-glucose units, typically in a ratio of about 1.6:1, with occasional acetyl groups along the chain. It is a component of the cell walls of some plants and is obtained commercially mainly from the tubers (corms) of the konjac plant, Amorphophallus konjac, whose flour may consist of 40–60% glucomannan.

Properties

Glucomannan is remarkable for its water-absorbing capacity: it can absorb many times its own weight in water, forming one of the most viscous solutions of any dietary fibre. When the acetyl groups are removed by mild alkali treatment, the polysaccharide forms a thermally stable gel — the basis of the traditional Japanese food konnyaku and of shirataki noodles.

Uses

In the food industry glucomannan (konjac gum) is used as a thickener, emulsifier, gelling agent and fat replacer, particularly in noodles, jellies and vegan seafood analogues. Bacteria in the colon ferment it to short-chain fatty acids.

Health claims and safety

The European Food Safety Authority has authorised a claim that konjac glucomannan contributes to weight loss in the context of an energy-restricted diet, and to the maintenance of normal blood cholesterol levels, when consumed in specified amounts with adequate water. Because the fibre swells greatly, dry tablets pose a choking and oesophageal-obstruction risk, and regulators have required warnings and ample fluid intake; konjac mini-cup jellies have been restricted in several jurisdictions for the same reason.
