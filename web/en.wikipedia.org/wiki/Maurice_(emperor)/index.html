Maurice (emperor)

Maurice (Latin: Mauricius; Greek: Μαυρίκιος; 539 – 27 November 602) was Byzantine emperor from 582 to 602. A successful general, he concluded a favorable peace with Sasanian Persia in 591 that greatly expanded the empire's eastern frontier, and he campaigned extensively in the Balkans against the Avars and Slavs.

Early career and accession

Born in Arabissus in Cappadocia, Maurice rose through the military, becoming commander of the eastern armies under Emperor Tiberius II Constantine, who adopted him, married him to his daughter Constantina, and named him successor shortly before his death in 582.

Reign

Maurice reorganized the empire's defenses, creating the exarchates of Ravenna and Africa. In the east, his intervention in a Persian civil war restored Khosrow II to the Sasanian throne and secured a peace in 591 that ceded much of Armenia to Byzantium. In the Balkans, his armies pushed back the Avars and restored the Danube frontier. He is traditionally credited with the Strategikon, an influential military manual. His fiscal austerity, however, made him unpopular with the army.

Overthrow and death

In 602, after Maurice ordered the Balkan army to winter north of the Danube, the troops mutinied and proclaimed the centurion Phocas emperor. Maurice fled Constantinople but was captured and executed at Chalcedon on 27 November 602, after being forced to watch the execution of his five sons. His overthrow ended the Justinian dynasty and triggered the last Byzantine–Sasanian war.
