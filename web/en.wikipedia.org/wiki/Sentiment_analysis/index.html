Sentiment analysis

Sentiment analysis, also called opinion mining, is the use of natural language processing, text analysis, and computational linguistics to identify, extract, and quantify the affective states and subjective information expressed in text — for example, whether a review, social-media post, or survey response is positive, negative, or neutral toward a subject.

Approaches

Early approaches were lexicon-based, scoring documents using dictionaries of words labelled for polarity, sometimes with rules for negation and intensifiers. Supervised machine-learning methods train classifiers such as Naive Bayes, logistic regression, and support vector machines on labelled corpora. More recent work uses deep learning, including recurrent networks and transformer models, and large language models prompted or fine-tuned for sentiment tasks. Aspect-based sentiment analysis goes further, identifying the sentiment expressed toward particular features — for instance, the battery life versus the screen of a phone.

Applications

Applications include monitoring brand reputation and customer feedback, analysing product reviews, gauging public reaction to news and political events, and research in finance and the social sciences. Challenges include sarcasm and irony, domain-specific vocabulary, negation, mixed sentiments, and the differing conventions of informal text.
