Skinhead

A skinhead is a member of a subculture that originated among working-class youth in London, England, in the 1960s. Skinheads are named for their close-cropped or shaved hairstyles. The subculture developed out of an intersection between British mod culture and the Jamaican rude boy scene, with early skinheads listening to ska, reggae and soul music.

Origins

By around 1968, elements of the mod scene "hardened" into a plainer, more working-class style — the so-called "hard mods" — featuring cropped hair, working boots such as Dr. Martens, braces (suspenders), straight-leg jeans, button-down Ben Sherman shirts, Fred Perry polo shirts and Harrington jackets. These first skinheads were heavily influenced by West Indian immigrant culture, particularly Jamaican ska and rocksteady, and the early scene was notably multiracial in character.

Decline and revival

The original culture faded in the early 1970s as many skinheads moved into the suedehead and other styles. A revival in the late 1970s, intertwined with punk rock and the Oi! and 2 Tone ska movements, produced a second wave. During this period, parts of the scene were targeted for recruitment by far-right organisations such as the National Front and the British Movement, and violent, racist "bonehead" factions received heavy media attention. This created a lasting public association between skinheads and racism, even though many skinheads — including the traditional ("Trojan") skinhead scene and the explicitly anti-racist SHARP (Skinheads Against Racial Prejudice), founded in New York in 1987 — reject that association.

Subgroups and identity

Contemporary skinheads span a spectrum of beliefs: apolitical traditional skinheads focused on music and style; left-wing and anti-fascist "redskins" and anarchist skins; SHARP members; and a minority of white-power skinheads. Because of the conflicting politics, identity and allegiance are often signalled through details of dress, such as bootlace and braces colours, though such codes vary by region and are frequently overstated.

Style and music

Core musical genres include 1960s ska, reggae, rocksteady and soul for traditionalists; Oi!, punk, hardcore and 2 Tone for revival generations. The uniform of boots, jeans, braces and cropped hair remains the most recognisable feature of the subculture worldwide.
