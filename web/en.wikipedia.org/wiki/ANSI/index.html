ANSI

The American National Standards Institute (ANSI) is a private nonprofit organization that oversees the development of voluntary consensus standards for products, services, processes, systems, and personnel in the United States. Founded in 1918 and headquartered in Washington, D.C., ANSI is the US member body of the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC).

Role

ANSI does not itself write standards; it accredits standards-developing organizations (such as ASME, IEEE, and ASTM) and approves their outputs as American National Standards when due-process requirements are met. It also accredits certification bodies for products and personnel. Well-known "ANSI standards" in computing include ANSI C, ANSI escape codes for terminals, and the SQL standard maintained jointly with ISO.

History

The institute began in 1918 as the American Engineering Standards Committee, reorganizing several times before adopting the ANSI name in 1969. Its membership includes companies, government agencies, standards organizations, and academic institutions.
