Nai (pan flute)

The nai (archaic Romanian for "reed flute") is a Romanian pan flute traditionally made of bamboo or reed. Unlike most pan flutes, whose tubes are arranged in a straight line, the nai's tubes — usually twenty or more — are set in a distinctively curved row, allowing rapid glissandi and wide melodic leaps.

Construction and technique

Each tube is stopped at one end and tuned by adding or removing material such as beeswax or small stoppers. The player blows across the open rims, shifting the instrument along the lips to reach different notes. The curved layout brings the longest and shortest tubes within comfortable reach and is well suited to the fast ornaments and sliding tones of Romanian folk music.

History and performers

The nai was long a village instrument of the lăutari (professional folk musicians) before being taken up in the twentieth century by virtuosos such as Fănică Luca, who taught a generation of players in Bucharest, and his pupil Gheorghe Zamfir, whose recordings from the 1970s onwards brought the nai to a worldwide audience. Damian Drăghici later introduced the instrument to jazz and popular audiences. The nai is also used in the music of neighbouring Moldova.
