Belisarius

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Belisarius
Born: c. 500, Germane (Thrace)
Died: 565
Known for: Reconquering North Africa and Italy for Justinian I

Belisarius (c. 500–565) was a general of the Byzantine Empire under Justinian I and the leading commander of the emperor's wars of reconquest in the West. He is often ranked among the greatest generals of history, repeatedly defeating larger armies with small, professional forces built around heavy cavalry (bucellarii).

Campaigns

After distinguishing himself against Persia — victory at Dara (530), defeat at Callinicum (531) — and helping crush the Nika riots in 532, Belisarius conquered the Vandal kingdom of North Africa in a single campaigning season (533–534). Sent to Italy in 535, he took Sicily, Naples and Rome, withstood a year-long Gothic siege of Rome (537–538), and captured Ravenna in 540, briefly restoring imperial rule to most of Italy.

Later life

Recalled amid court suspicion — his popularity aroused Justinian's jealousy, and his wife Antonina's friendship with the empress Theodora cut both ways — Belisarius returned to Italy in 544 with inadequate troops and could not complete the reconquest; he was recalled in 549. In 559 he came out of retirement to repel a Bulgar raid threatening Constantinople itself. Accused in a conspiracy in 562, he was disgraced and briefly imprisoned before being pardoned; he died in 565, the same year as Justinian. The later legend that he ended as a blind beggar is a fabrication of much later centuries.
