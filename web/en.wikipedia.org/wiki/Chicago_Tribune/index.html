The Chicago Tribune is a daily newspaper based in Chicago, Illinois, founded in 1847. Long owned by the Tribune Company (later Tribune Publishing, and from 2021 Alden Global Capital's group), it was the flagship of a media empire that included the Los Angeles Times and the WGN radio and television stations, whose call letters derive from the paper's slogan, 'World's Greatest Newspaper'.

Founding and the McCormick era

Founded in 1847, the paper rose under Joseph Medill, an abolitionist who helped found the Republican Party. His grandson Robert R. McCormick ran it for decades as a conservative, isolationist powerhouse and built Tribune Tower (1925).

Influence

The Tribune's foreign service, architecture criticism and investigations won many Pulitzers; its 1948 'Dewey Defeats Truman' headline became the most famous error in American newspaper history.

Ownership turmoil

The Tribune Company was taken private in Sam Zell's troubled 2007 deal and entered bankruptcy in 2008; its newspaper arm spun off as Tribune Publishing (later Tronc), and in 2021 Alden Global Capital acquired it, prompting newsroom cuts and buyouts.

The modern paper

The Tribune remains Chicago's principal newspaper, publishing digitally and in print from offices it moved to after selling Tribune Tower. Its alumni include the columnist Mike Royko (later at the Sun-Times) and the architecture critic Blair Kamin.
