Duchy of Amalfi

The Duchy of Amalfi (Italian: Ducato di Amalfi) was a de facto independent state centred on the city of Amalfi on the south coast of the Sorrentine Peninsula in Italy, from the ninth to the twelfth century. One of the Italian maritime republics, Amalfi became the first and, for a time, the most powerful of them through its trade throughout the Mediterranean, before being eclipsed by Pisa, Genoa and Venice.

History

Amalfi was nominally part of the Byzantine duchy of Naples, but in 839 its citizens elected their own prefect and killed the Byzantine-appointed ruler, asserting practical independence. The duchy's first hereditary duke was Mastalus I (from 914); his successor Manso I (966–1004) brought the duchy to its greatest power, briefly ruling Salerno as well. Amalfitan merchants maintained trading quarters and fondachi in Constantinople, Antioch, Egypt and Muslim Sicily and Spain, exporting timber, grain, wine and chestnuts and importing silks, spices and precious goods from the East. Amalfi minted its own gold coinage (the tarì modelled on Islamic issues), and its maritime law, the Tabula Amalphitana (Tavole Amalfitane), was recognised as authoritative in Mediterranean commerce for centuries. Amalfitans also founded the Hospital in Jerusalem from which the Knights Hospitaller grew.

Decline

Positioned on a narrow strip of mountainous coast with little agricultural hinterland, Amalfi was subordinated by the Lombard princes of Salerno (falling to Guaimar IV in 1039) and, on regaining autonomy, was conquered by the Norman Robert Guiscard in 1073. The city retained commercial privileges and local autonomy under the Normans, but a Pisan attack in 1135, which sacked Amalfi and its neighbouring towns, destroyed its fleet and effectively ended its great-power status. The duchy was absorbed into the Norman Kingdom of Sicily.
