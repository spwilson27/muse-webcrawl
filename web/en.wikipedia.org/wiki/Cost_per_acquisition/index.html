Cost per acquisition

CPA pricing pays the publisher only when a defined action occurs.

Cost per acquisition (CPA), also called cost per action or pay per acquisition (PPA), is an online advertising pricing model and metric in which the advertiser pays only when a specified acquisition occurs — for example a sale, a completed lead form, an app install or a registration. It is also the corresponding efficiency metric: total advertising cost divided by the number of acquisitions attributed to a campaign.

As a pricing model

Under CPA pricing, the risk of advertising spend is shifted toward the publisher or affiliate, who must deliver conversions rather than mere impressions (CPM) or clicks (CPC). CPA is common in affiliate marketing, where networks and affiliates are paid a commission for each sale or lead they generate. Offers typically specify payout rules, attribution windows and cookie durations.

As a metric

Marketers compute CPA = total campaign cost / number of conversions to judge whether acquisition is profitable relative to a customer's value. A related quantity is effective CPA (eCPA), used when campaigns bought on another pricing model (such as CPC or CPM) are evaluated on the implied cost per conversion. Target CPA ("tCPA") is an automated bidding strategy offered by advertising platforms such as Google Ads, in which the system sets bids to achieve a chosen average cost per conversion.

Limitations

CPA depends heavily on attribution: conversions may be influenced by several channels, and view-through or cross-device actions may be under- or over-counted. Privacy changes, such as restrictions on third-party cookies and mobile tracking, have made CPA measurement harder and pushed advertisers toward modelled and aggregated conversion reporting.
