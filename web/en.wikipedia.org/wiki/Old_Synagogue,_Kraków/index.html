The Old Synagogue (Polish: Stara Synagoga) in Kraków, Poland, is the oldest synagogue building still standing in the country and one of the most precious landmarks of Jewish architecture in Europe. It stands in Kazimierz, the historic Jewish quarter of Kraków, at the eastern end of Szeroka Street. Until 1939 it served as the central house of prayer and the seat of the Jewish community's authorities in the district.

History

The original synagogue was built in the 15th century, traditionally dated to 1407 or 1492, in the Gothic style as a two-nave hall with ribbed vaulting supported on two pillars — a design related to German and Bohemian synagogues such as the Old New Synagogue in Prague. After a fire in 1557 it was rebuilt by the Florentine architect Matteo Gucci, who gave it a Renaissance character while retaining the late-Gothic walls; its high attic parapet, unusual in synagogue architecture, may have served a defensive purpose. A women's prayer room and annexes were added over the following century.

During the Second World War the German occupiers devastated the interior, using the building as a warehouse; its Torah ark, bimah and ceiling ornament were destroyed, and the synagogue's treasures were looted. Restored between 1956 and 1959 under architect Stanisław Michalczyk, the building reopened as a museum of Jewish history and culture, a function it keeps today as a branch of the Historical Museum of the City of Kraków.

Interior and museum

The restored prayer hall displays the stone Torah ark frame, the reconstructed bimah, and exhibitions on Jewish holidays, rituals, the history of Kraków's Jews, and the Holocaust in the city. The adjacent square and the nearby Remah Cemetery form, with Szeroka Street, the heart of Jewish heritage tourism in Kraków.
