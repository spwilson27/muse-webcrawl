David is a masterpiece of Italian Renaissance sculpture, created in marble between 1501 and 1504 by Michelangelo. David is a 5.17-metre (17 ft) marble statue of the biblical figure David, a favoured subject in the art of Florence.

History

The statue was commissioned for one of the buttresses of Florence Cathedral. The block of Carrara marble had been worked on — and abandoned — by Agostino di Duccio and Antonio Rossellino decades earlier; Michelangelo, then twenty-six, took up the challenge in 1501 and completed the figure in 1504. A committee including Leonardo da Vinci and Botticelli decided instead to place it in front of the Palazzo Vecchio in the Piazza della Signoria, where it became a symbol of the Florentine Republic. In 1873 the original was moved indoors to the Galleria dell'Accademia to protect it from the weather; a replica stands in the square.

Description

Unlike earlier depictions by Donatello and Verrocchio, which show David after his victory over Goliath, Michelangelo depicts him before the battle, tense and alert, sling over his shoulder. The figure stands in classical contrapposto, with disproportionately large head and hands that emphasize his concentration and divine favour.

Legacy

David is widely regarded as one of the greatest sculptures ever made and an emblem of Renaissance humanism. Full-size replicas stand in the Piazza della Signoria and the Victoria and Albert Museum, among many other places.
