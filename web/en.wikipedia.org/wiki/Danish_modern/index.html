Danish modern

Danish modern is a style of minimalist furniture and housewares from Denmark associated with the Danish design movement. In the 1920s, Kaare Klint embraced the principles of Bauhaus modernism in furniture design, creating a line of very clean, pure lines based on an understanding of classical furniture craftsmanship coupled with careful research into materials, proportions, and the requirements of the human body.

Characteristics

Danish modern pieces are characterised by clean lines, warm natural materials — especially teak, rosewood, and beech — superb joinery, and forms sculpted to the human body. Unlike the colder industrial aesthetic of some international modernism, the Danish school kept one foot in the cabinetmaker's workshop: many landmark pieces were produced in small workshops before being adapted for mass manufacture.

Designers

Notable designers include Arne Jacobsen (the Egg, Swan, and Ant chairs), Hans Wegner (the Wishbone Chair and more than 500 other chair designs), Finn Juhl, Børge Mogensen, Poul Kjærholm, and Poul Henningsen, whose PH lamps became icons of Danish lighting. Verner Panton pushed the style toward pop modernism in the 1960s.

Influence

The style peaked in international popularity in the 1950s and 1960s as part of the broader mid-century modern movement and Scandinavian design, furnishing American and European homes and offices. After a period of eclipse, original Danish modern furniture became highly collectible from the 1990s onward, and the aesthetic strongly influenced the 21st-century revival of warm minimalism in interior design.
