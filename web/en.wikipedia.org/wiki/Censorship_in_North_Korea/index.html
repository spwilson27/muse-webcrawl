Censorship in North Korea is among the most extensive in the world. The government of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea controls all domestic media, prohibits independent journalism, and tightly restricts access to foreign information. North Korea routinely ranks at or near last place in the Reporters Without Borders World Press Freedom Index, and a 2014 United Nations Commission of Inquiry described near-total state control of information as part of a wider system of human rights violations.

Media system

All newspapers, broadcasting, and publishing are organs of the ruling Workers' Party of Korea or the state. The Korean Central News Agency (KCNA) is the sole source of news; the party newspaper Rodong Sinmun sets the editorial line, and Korean Central Television broadcasts limited hours of programming dominated by news, propaganda, and approved entertainment. Journalists function as party cadres, and media content is checked before publication by censorship organs within the Propaganda and Agitation Department.

Control of foreign information

Radios and televisions are sold fixed to domestic frequencies and are subject to inspection; tampering with them is a serious offence. Foreign broadcasts, South Korean media, and foreign films are banned, yet smuggled USB drives, SD cards, and Chinese mobile phones near the border circulate foreign content widely. Possession or distribution of South Korean media can be punished severely, and the 2020 Reactionary Ideology and Culture Rejection Act intensified penalties, reportedly including the death penalty for large-scale distribution. Defectors consistently describe foreign media consumption as widespread despite the risks.

Internet

Ordinary citizens have no access to the global internet. A closed national intranet, Kwangmyong, provides email and approved websites to institutions and some households. A small number of officials and foreigners in Pyongyang have monitored internet access.

Cult of personality

Censorship sustains the state cult of the Kim dynasty: media treat the leaders Kim Il Sung, Kim Jong Il, and Kim Jong Un as infallible, historical facts are revised to fit official narratives, and criticism of the leadership is unthinkable in public life.
