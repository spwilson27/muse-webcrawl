Moravian Slovakia

CountryCzech Republic
RegionSouth Moravia / Zlín, south-east Moravia
Main townsUherské Hradiště, Hodonín, Strážnice, Kyjov, Veselí nad Moravou
Czech nameMoravské Slovácko (Slovácko)

Moravian Slovakia (Czech: Moravské Slovácko, usually shortened to Slovácko) is a cultural region in the south-east of Moravia, Czech Republic, along the lower Morava River and the border with Slovakia and Austria. Inhabited by the Moravian Slovaks, the region is renowned within the Czech Republic for the vitality of its folk traditions — embroidered costumes, cimbalom music, folk singing and dance, wine-making, and distinctive painted architecture.

Culture

Slovácko's villages preserve an unusually rich calendar of feasts, including Easter Monday customs, St. Martin's wine celebrations, parish fairs (hody) with costumed youth, and the Ride of the Kings (Jízda králů), a Pentecost procession of young men on decorated horses, inscribed by UNESCO in 2011 on its Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage. The Strážnice International Folklore Festival, held since 1946, is one of the oldest and largest folklore festivals in the country. Local folk costumes (kroje) vary from village to village and are among the most ornate in Bohemia and Moravia.

Sub-regions and language

The region comprises sub-areas including Horňácko, Dolňácko, Podluží, and the Moravian Kopanice. Its inhabitants speak East Moravian dialects of Czech transitional toward Slovak, historically closer to the speech across the border than to standard Czech. Viticulture is central to the landscape and economy, with white wine varieties predominating around Bzenec, Mutěnice, and Polešovice.
