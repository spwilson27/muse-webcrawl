The International Paralympic Committee (IPC; German: Internationales Paralympisches Komitee) is an international non-profit organisation and the global governing body for the Paralympic Movement. The IPC organises the Paralympic Games and functions as the international federation for several Para sports.

The Agitos, the Paralympic symbol, consists of three swooshes in red, blue and green.

History

Para sport grew from rehabilitation programmes after World War II, notably Ludwig Guttmann's Stoke Mandeville Games of 1948. The first Paralympic Games were held in Rome in 1960. Before the IPC, international disability sport was coordinated by the International Co-ordinating Committee (ICC) from 1982. The IPC was founded on 22 September 1989 in Düsseldorf, with Robert Steadward as its first president; headquarters moved to Bonn in 1999. Since a 2001 agreement with the IOC, the same city hosts both the Olympic and Paralympic Games.

Role and structure

The IPC's members include National Paralympic Committees, international federations, regional organisations, and international organisations of sport for the disabled. Its General Assembly is the supreme authority. The IPC directly governs sports including Para athletics, Para swimming, Para powerlifting and wheelchair dance sport, while other Para sports are governed by their own federations. It oversees athlete classification systems, the Paralympic Games, anti-doping and the IPC Athletes' Council.
