Gaya is a city in the Indian state of Bihar, situated on the banks of the Falgu River about 100 kilometres south of Patna. It is one of the most sacred cities of Hinduism and an important pilgrimage centre, and it lies near Bodh Gaya, where the Buddha is said to have attained enlightenment under the Bodhi tree.

Religious significance

Gaya is renowned for the Vishnupad Temple, built in 1787 by Queen Ahilyabai Holkar of Indore, which enshrines a footprint of the god Vishnu. Hindus believe that performing the pind daan ritual here — offerings of rice balls for one's ancestors — grants salvation to departed souls. During the Pitru Paksha period, hundreds of thousands of pilgrims visit to perform the rites at sacred sites along the Falgu and at the Akshayavat, the immortal banyan tree.

According to tradition, the city takes its name from the demon Gayasura, whose body became the region's sacred geography after Vishnu subdued him. Gaya also features in the Ramayana: Rama is said to have come here with Sita to offer pind daan for his father Dasharatha.

History

Gaya formed part of the ancient kingdom of Magadha. It was a significant centre in the time of the Buddha and Mahavira, both of whom preached in the region. Under British rule it became a district headquarters (1865) and an important trading town. The nearby Barabar Caves, among the oldest rock-cut caves in India, bear inscriptions of the Mauryan emperor Ashoka.

Transport and economy

Gaya Junction is a major railway station on the Howrah–Delhi line, and Gaya Airport connects the city to Delhi, Kolkata, and international destinations, serving Buddhist pilgrims visiting Bodh Gaya. The economy is based on agriculture, trade, tourism, and pilgrimage services. Gaya is part of the Government of India's HRIDAY and Smart Cities programmes.
