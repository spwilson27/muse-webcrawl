400 metres hurdles

The 400 metres hurdles is a track and field hurdling event. The standard event has been a fixture of the Olympic athletics programme for men since 1900 and for women since 1984. Athletes run one full lap of a standard 400 m track, clearing ten hurdles spaced 35 metres apart.

Specifications

For men the hurdles are 0.914 m (36 in) high; for women, 0.762 m (30 in). The first hurdle is placed 45 m from the start. Because of the bend and the fixed spacing, hurdlers develop distinctive stride patterns, typically taking 13 to 15 strides between hurdles early in the race.

Records and champions

The men's world record is 45.94 seconds, set by Karsten Warholm of Norway in the Tokyo 2020 Olympic final — the first sub-46 performance. Kevin Young's 46.78 from Barcelona 1992 had stood for 29 years. The women's world record is 50.37 seconds by Sydney McLaughlin-Levrone of the United States, set in 2024; she has broken the record repeatedly since 2021. Edwin Moses dominated the men's event from 1976 to 1987, winning 122 consecutive races.
