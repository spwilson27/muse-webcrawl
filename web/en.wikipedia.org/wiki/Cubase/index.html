Cubase is a digital audio workstation (DAW) developed by the German company Steinberg for music and MIDI recording, arranging and editing. First released in 1989 for the Atari ST, it was one of the earliest graphical MIDI sequencers and became a standard tool in professional and home studios.

History

The original Cubase (1989) offered drag-and-drop MIDI arranging on the Atari ST. Cubase Audio (1991) added digital audio recording. In 1996 Cubase VST introduced the Virtual Studio Technology plug-in standard, allowing software instruments and effects to run inside the program in real time — a landmark that reshaped the music software industry. Steinberg, acquired by Yamaha in 2004, has continued the line with successive versions (Cubase SX, and the modern Pro, Artist and Elements editions), adding features such as VariAudio pitch editing, chord tracks and advanced mixing.

Editions

Cubase is sold in tiered editions: Pro (full feature set), Artist (mid-level) and Elements (entry-level), alongside the free bundled LE and AI versions supplied with audio hardware.
