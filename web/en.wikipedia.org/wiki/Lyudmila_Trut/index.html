Lyudmila Trut

Lyudmila Nikolayevna Trut (born 1933) is a Russian biologist best known for her long-running experiment on the domestication of the silver fox at the Institute of Cytology and Genetics in Novosibirsk, Siberia. She joined the project under geneticist Dmitry Belyayev in 1959 and led it after Belyayev's death in 1985.

The farm-fox experiment

Starting with silver foxes (Vulpes vulpes) from fur farms, Belyayev and Trut selected each generation solely for tameness — willingness to approach and be handled by humans. Within a few dozen generations the selected foxes sought human contact, wagged their tails, and whined like dogs; they also developed changes such as floppy ears, piebald coats, curled tails, altered reproductive timing, and changes in stress and serotonin physiology — a suite of traits resembling the "domestication syndrome" seen in other domestic animals. A contrasting line selected for aggression was also maintained.

Significance

With science writer Lee Alan Dugatkin, Trut authored How to Tame a Fox (and Build a Dog) (2017), popularising the experiment as evidence that selection on behaviour alone can rapidly produce the correlated traits associated with domestication, illuminating how wolves may have become dogs.
