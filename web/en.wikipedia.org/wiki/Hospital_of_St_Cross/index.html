Hospital of St Cross

The Hospital of St Cross and Almshouse of Noble Poverty is a medieval almshouse in Winchester, Hampshire, England. Founded between 1132 and 1136 by Henry of Blois, Bishop of Winchester, it is reputed to be the oldest almshouse in England still in operation.

History

The hospital was founded to support thirteen poor men, known as the "Brothers of St Cross", who wear black gowns with a silver cross. A second foundation, the Almshouse of Noble Poverty, was added in the fifteenth century under Cardinal Henry Beaufort, whose brothers wear claret-red gowns with a silver cardinal's hat badge.

The brothers are not monks, though the hospital maintains a strong choral tradition. The buildings, including the Norman church of St Cross, the Master's Garden and the medieval gatehouse, form one of the finest groups of medieval almshouse buildings in the country.

Wayfarer's Dole

The hospital is famous for the Wayfarer's Dole, a tradition under which any traveller who asks at the Porter's Lodge may receive a small piece of bread and a drink of beer. The custom is believed to date back to the hospital's foundation and is still observed today.
