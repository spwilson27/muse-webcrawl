Turkmeneli

Turkmeneli is a historical and political term used to describe the region of northern Iraq inhabited by the Iraqi Turkmens, a Turkic ethnic group. The region stretches in an arc from Tal Afar in the northwest through Mosul, Erbil, Kirkuk, Tuz Khurmatu and Altun Kupri to Mandali near the Iranian border.

Geography

Turkmeneli lies between the Arab-majority areas of Iraq to the south and west and the Kurdish-majority areas to the north and east. Its principal city is Kirkuk, a major centre of Iraq's oil industry and the cultural heart of the Iraqi Turkmen community.

History

Turkmen settlement in the region dates to successive waves of Oghuz Turkic migration beginning in the early medieval period and continuing under the Seljuk, Ottoman and other Turkic dynasties. Under the Ottoman Empire the region's Turkmen population formed an important part of the urban elites of cities such as Kirkuk and Erbil.

After the First World War the region became part of the British Mandate of Mesopotamia and then the Kingdom of Iraq. Iraqi Turkmens suffered episodes of violence, including the 1959 Kirkuk massacre, and policies of Arabisation under the Ba'athist regime. Since 2003, the status of Kirkuk and the wider region has been disputed between the federal government in Baghdad and the Kurdistan Regional Government.

Culture

The Iraqi Turkmens speak a form of Oghuz Turkic closely related to Azerbaijani and Turkish, and maintain a distinct culture expressed in literature, music (including the Kirkuk hoyrat tradition), and press published in both Arabic and Latin scripts.
