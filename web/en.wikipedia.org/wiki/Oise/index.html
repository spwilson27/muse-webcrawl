Oise is a department in the Hauts-de-France region of northern France, named after the river Oise. Its prefecture is Beauvais, and other important towns include Compiègne, Creil, and Senlis. The department lies immediately north of Paris and forms part of the wider Paris metropolitan influence.

Geography

The Oise river flows southwest through the department before joining the Seine near Conflans-Sainte-Honorine. The landscape is one of plateaus, forests such as the Forêt de Compiègne, and fertile agricultural plains devoted largely to cereals and sugar beet.

History

The department was created during the French Revolution in 1790. The Armistice of 11 November 1918 ending the First World War was signed in a railway carriage in the Forest of Compiègne near Rethondes; the same carriage was used for the French surrender to Germany in 1940.
