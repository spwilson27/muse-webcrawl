The Seventh-day Adventist Church is an Adventist Protestant Christian denomination which is distinguished by its observance of Saturday, the seventh day of the week in the Christian (Gregorian) and Hebrew calendars, as the Sabbath, and its emphasis on the imminent second coming (advent) of Jesus Christ.

History

The denomination grew out of the Millerite movement in the United States in the mid-19th century. After the "Great Disappointment" of 22 October 1844 — when William Miller's expectation of Christ's return was not realised — a small group concluded that Christ had instead entered the Most Holy Place of the heavenly sanctuary, beginning an "investigative judgment". Guided by pioneers including Joseph Bates (who championed the Sabbath), James White and his wife Ellen G. White, whose prophetic ministry shaped the church, the movement organised formally in Battle Creek, Michigan, in 1863. Missionary expansion, beginning with J. N. Andrews's departure for Switzerland in 1874, made it one of the fastest-growing worldwide denominations.

Beliefs and practices

The church's 28 Fundamental Beliefs include the Trinity, the authority of Scripture, salvation by faith in Christ, adult baptism by immersion, observance of the Saturday Sabbath, conditional immortality ("soul sleep") and annihilation of the wicked, the sanctuary and investigative judgment doctrine, and the imminent premillennial return of Christ. Adventists emphasise healthful living — many are vegetarian, and the church operates one of the world's largest Protestant hospital and school systems — and religious liberty advocacy. Ellen White's writings are held to be an authoritative source of guidance, subordinate to the Bible.

Organisation and mission

The church is governed in a representative system through local conferences, unions, thirteen world divisions and the General Conference, which meets in session every five years. It reports baptised membership of over 22 million and runs extensive publishing, broadcasting (Hope Channel), humanitarian (ADRA) and education ministries on every continent.
