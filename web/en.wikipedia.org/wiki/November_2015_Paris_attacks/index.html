November 2015 Paris attacks

The November 2015 Paris attacks were a series of coordinated Islamist terrorist attacks that took place on the evening of Friday, 13 November 2015, in Paris and the northern suburb of Saint-Denis, France. The attacks killed 130 people and injured more than 400 others, making them the deadliest attack on French soil since the Second World War. The Islamic State (IS) claimed responsibility.

The attacks

Beginning at 21:16 CET, three teams of attackers struck in sequence. Suicide bombers detonated outside the Stade de France during a France–Germany football match attended by President François Hollande. Other gunmen fired on café and restaurant terraces in the 10th and 11th arrondissements, including Le Carillon, Le Petit Cambodge, La Belle Équipe, and Café Bonne Bière. The deadliest assault came at the Bataclan theatre, where three gunmen opened fire during a concert by the American rock band Eagles of Death Metal and took hostages; 90 people were killed there before police stormed the venue in the early hours of 14 November. Seven attackers died that night.

Aftermath

France declared a state of emergency, closed its borders, and observed three days of national mourning. The suspected organiser, Abdelhamid Abaaoud, was killed in a police raid in Saint-Denis on 18 November. Salah Abdeslam, the only surviving member of the attacking teams known to have taken part directly, was captured in Brussels in March 2016 and later convicted, including in the landmark Paris trial of 2021–22 that sentenced him to a whole-life term. The attacks intensified French military operations against IS in Syria and Iraq and reshaped French counterterrorism law and politics.
