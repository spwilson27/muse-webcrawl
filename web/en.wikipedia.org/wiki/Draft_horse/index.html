Draft horse

A draft horse (UK: draught horse) is a large horse bred to work, primarily for heavy tasks such as ploughing and farm labour. Draft breeds are tall, heavily muscled, and generally calm in temperament; many show "feathering" — long hair on the lower legs. Well-known breeds include the Belgian, the Clydesdale, the Percheron, the Shire, and the Suffolk Punch. The Shire is among the largest, with stallions standing over 17 hands and weighing around a tonne.

Uses

Before mechanisation, draft horses were essential for agriculture, haulage, canal boats, and military logistics. Today they are used for showing, forestry work where machinery is unsuitable, carriage driving, and promotional purposes — the Clydesdales of Budweiser being a famous example. Crosses between draft breeds and lighter horses produce sport and riding horses of greater size and substance.
