Fermium

Fermium is a synthetic chemical element with the symbol Fm and atomic number 100. It is a member of the actinide series and is named after the physicist Enrico Fermi.

Discovery

Fermium was first identified in the debris of the Ivy Mike hydrogen bomb test of 1 November 1952, where intense neutron flux had built up heavy isotopes; the isotope fermium-255 was found in fallout analyzed by Albert Ghiorso and colleagues. The discovery was kept secret until 1955 for security reasons, and a Swedish group that independently synthesized element 100 in 1954 agreed to the name fermium. The longest-lived isotope, fermium-257, has a half-life of about 100.5 days.

Properties and uses

Only minute quantities have ever been produced, by neutron bombardment of lighter actinides in nuclear reactors. Fermium has no uses outside basic scientific research.
