Saturday Night and Sunday Morning (film)

Directed by: Karel Reisz
Screenplay by: Alan Sillitoe
Based on: Saturday Night and Sunday Morning by Alan Sillitoe
Starring: Albert Finney, Shirley Anne Field, Rachel Roberts
Release date: 1960
Country: United Kingdom

Albert Finney as Arthur Seaton.

Saturday Night and Sunday Morning is a 1960 British drama film directed by Karel Reisz and adapted by Alan Sillitoe from his 1958 novel of the same name. It stars Albert Finney, in his first leading film role, as Arthur Seaton, a young Nottingham factory worker who spends his wages on weekend drinking and affairs while railing against the routines of working-class life. The film is a landmark of the British New Wave and of "kitchen sink realism".

Plot

Arthur Seaton works at a lathe in a Nottingham bicycle factory, living by the creed "Don't let the bastards grind you down". He is having an affair with Brenda, the wife of a workmate, while also courting the younger Doreen. When Brenda becomes pregnant and an attempted abortion fails, and Arthur is beaten by soldiers connected to Brenda's husband, his defiance begins to give way to an uneasy acceptance of marriage and a new housing estate, though his rebellious spirit remains unbroken at the end.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major critical and commercial success, winning BAFTA Awards including Best British Film, and Best British Actress for Rachel Roberts; Finney won the BAFTA for Most Promising Newcomer. It was one of the defining portrayals of the "angry young man" of post-war British culture and remains frequently listed among the greatest British films.
