Isis (Egyptian Aset, "throne") is a goddess of ancient Egyptian mythology, wife-sister of Osiris and mother of Horus, revered as the archetypal wife and mother, the greatest magician among the gods, and a protector of the dead. Her name is written with the throne sign, linking her to kingship itself: the pharaoh is, theologically, her child.

Myths

Isis's defining story is her search for Osiris after his murder by Set (god): she recovered and reassembled his scattered body, fanned breath into him with her wings, and conceived Horus, whom she hid in the papyrus marshes of the Delta from Set's violence. In another tale she won power by learning Ra's secret name, having fashioned a serpent to bite the ageing sun god and bargaining the cure for his true name — becoming "more powerful than a million gods". Her laments for Osiris, sung in temple ritual, were the model of funerary mourning.

Spread of her cult

Isis worship grew through the New Kingdom and Late Period until she became effectively a universal goddess; under the Ptolemies and Rome her mystery cult spread across the Mediterranean — temples stood from Philae (her last active temple, closed in the 6th century CE) to Pompeii and London — promising initiation, protection and hope after death. Apuleius's Golden Ass (2nd century CE) gives a famous account of conversion to her service. Her iconography as nursing mother (Isis lactans) is often noted as a precursor in form, though not in doctrine, to images of the Virgin and Child.
