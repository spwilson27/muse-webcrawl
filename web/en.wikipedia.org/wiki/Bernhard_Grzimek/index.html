Bernhard Grzimek

Born24 April 1909, Neisse, German Empire
Died13 March 1987, Frankfurt am Main, West Germany
OccupationZoologist, zoo director, filmmaker, author
Known forFrankfurt Zoo; Serengeti Shall Not Die

Bernhard Klemens Maria Grzimek (24 April 1909 – 13 March 1987) was a German zoologist, long-serving director of the Frankfurt Zoo, and one of the most influential wildlife conservationists of the 20th century. Through bestselling books and television programmes he brought the cause of African wildlife conservation to a mass audience.

Life and work

Grzimek studied veterinary medicine in Leipzig and Berlin. After the Second World War he became director of the Frankfurt Zoo in 1945, rebuilding it from wartime ruins into one of Europe's leading zoological gardens, a post he held until 1974.

From the 1950s he campaigned for the preservation of the Serengeti in Tanzania. With his son Michael he made the film Serengeti Shall Not Die (1959), which won the Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature; Michael was killed in a plane crash during its production. Grzimek's aerial census of migratory herds helped establish the case for protecting the Serengeti ecosystem as a whole.

His television series Ein Platz für Tiere ("A Place for Animals") ran for decades on German television, and the proceeds from his books and appeals funded conservation projects across Africa. He was a founder figure of the Frankfurt Zoological Society's modern conservation work.
