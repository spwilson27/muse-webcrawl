Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer

Rudolph, the ninth reindeer

Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer is a fictional reindeer created by Robert L. May in 1939 for the Montgomery Ward department store chain. Distinguished by his luminous red nose, Rudolph is usually depicted as the ninth and youngest of Santa Claus's reindeer, who uses his nose to lead the sleigh team on Christmas Eve.

Creation

Copywriter Robert L. May invented Rudolph for a promotional colouring-book story given away in Montgomery Ward stores; nearly 2.4 million copies were distributed in 1939. In the story, the teased and excluded young reindeer's glowing nose proves invaluable on a foggy Christmas Eve.

The song and television special

May's brother-in-law Johnny Marks turned the story into the song "Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer", recorded by Gene Autry in 1949; it became one of the best-selling singles of all time. The 1964 Rankin/Bass stop-motion television special, narrated by Burl Ives, has been broadcast annually ever since and fixed Rudolph's image in popular culture, adding characters such as Hermey the elf and Yukon Cornelius.
