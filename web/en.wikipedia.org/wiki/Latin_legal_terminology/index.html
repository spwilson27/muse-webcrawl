Latin legal terminology

Latin maxims remain common in legal writing.
OriginRoman law
Used inCommon law and civil law systems

Many legal terms used in modern courts, especially in common-law jurisdictions, are Latin phrases inherited from Roman law and medieval legal practice. They survive as terms of art in constitutional law, criminal law, property, and civil procedure.

PhraseMeaning
Habeas corpus"You shall have the body" — writ requiring a prisoner be brought before a court
Stare decisis"To stand by things decided" — the doctrine of precedent
Mens rea / actus reus"Guilty mind" / "guilty act" — elements of a crime
Bona fide"In good faith"
Pro bonoWork undertaken without charge for the public good
Prima facie"At first sight" — sufficient on its face
Res judicataA matter already judged and no longer appealable
Ultra viresBeyond one's legal power or authority

Use today

Plain-language movements have reduced the use of Latin in everyday practice, but core terms remain embedded in case law and statutes. Latin legal terminology is also widespread in civil-law countries, where Roman law forms the historical foundation of private law.
