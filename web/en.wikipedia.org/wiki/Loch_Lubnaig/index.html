Loch Lubnaig (Gaelic: Loch Lùbnaig) is a small freshwater loch in the council area of Stirling, Scotland, lying within the Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park. Its name derives from the Gaelic word lùb, meaning "bend", a reference to the loch's crooked, dog-leg shape.

Geography

The loch stretches for roughly 6.5 kilometres (4 miles) between the village of Strathyre at its northern end and the Pass of Leny near Callander to the south. It is fed from the north by the River Balvag, which drains Loch Voil and Loch Doine past Balquhidder, and its outflow at the southern end is the Garbh Uisge, which flows through the Pass of Leny and joins the Eas Gobhain to form the River Teith at Callander. The A84 road runs along the eastern shore, following the route of the former Callander and Oban Railway, whose trackbed is now a cycle path forming part of National Cycle Route 7 and the Rob Roy Way long-distance footpath.

The surrounding hills include Ben Ledi (879 m) to the north-east, a prominent Corbett overlooking the loch, and the forested slopes of Strathyre Forest on the western shore. The loch is popular for canoeing, kayaking, wild swimming, and fishing for brown trout, and there are car parks, picnic areas, and a cabin café on its eastern shore. On calm days the hills are famously reflected in the still water, making Loch Lubnaig one of the most photographed small lochs in the southern Highlands.
