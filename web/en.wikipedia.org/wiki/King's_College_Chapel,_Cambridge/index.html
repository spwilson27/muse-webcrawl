King's College Chapel, Cambridge

King's College Chapel is the chapel of King's College in the University of Cambridge, England. Regarded as one of the finest surviving examples of late Perpendicular Gothic English architecture, it is famous for the largest fan vault in the world and for its medieval stained glass. The chapel is an iconic landmark of Cambridge and an active place of worship, home to the celebrated Choir of King's College.

Construction

The first stone was laid by King Henry VI on 25 July 1446, the feast of St James; Henry himself specified the chapel's dimensions and austere decoration. Building proceeded intermittently through the Wars of the Roses and was completed under Henry VIII in 1515, when the stained glass was installed. The chapel is 289 feet (88 m) long and 40 feet (12 m) wide, and the fan vault rises 80 feet (24 m) above the floor.

Interior

The interior contains twenty-six large stained-glass windows, mostly dating from 1515–1531, depicting biblical scenes — one of the most complete sets of Tudor glass to survive. The dark oak rood screen of 1532–36, a gift of Henry VIII, divides the chapel and supports the organ. Above the altar hangs The Adoration of the Magi by Peter Paul Rubens (1634), installed in the 1960s.

Music

The Choir of King's College, founded by Henry VI, sings daily services during term and is known worldwide for the Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols, broadcast from the chapel every Christmas Eve since 1928. The chapel is also familiar from the BBC television broadcast of Carols from King's.
