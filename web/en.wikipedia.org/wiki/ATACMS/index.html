The MGM-140 Army Tactical Missile System (ATACMS) is an American short-range ballistic missile manufactured by Lockheed Martin. It is fired from the M270 MLRS and M142 HIMARS launchers, fits in a standard rocket pod, and uses inertial navigation augmented by GPS to strike targets at ranges up to about 300 kilometres.

Development

ATACMS grew out of the Joint Tactical Missile System program of the 1980s and entered service in time for the 1991 Gulf War, where it was used against air-defense sites and logistics targets. The original Block I carried 950 M74 anti-personnel/anti-materiel submunitions. Later variants reduced submunition counts for greater range, added Brilliant Anti-armor (BAT) submunitions experiments, and eventually adopted a unitary high-explosive warhead with GPS guidance (Block IA Unitary and the M57 series), reflecting the U.S. policy of moving away from cluster munitions.

Operational use

The missile was used extensively in the 2003 invasion of Iraq. From 2023 the United States supplied ATACMS to Ukraine, which employed it against Russian airfields, air defenses, and logistics in occupied territory, including strikes that destroyed helicopters at Berdiansk and later targets in Crimea. Export users include South Korea, Poland, Romania, Turkey, Greece, Bahrain, Qatar, the UAE, Morocco, and others.

Replacement

The Precision Strike Missile (PrSM) is being developed as its longer-range successor, with ATACMS expected to remain in service for years alongside it.
