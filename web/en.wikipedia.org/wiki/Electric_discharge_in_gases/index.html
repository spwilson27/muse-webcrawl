Electric discharge in gases

Electric discharge in gases occurs when an electric current flows through a gaseous medium due to ionisation of the gas. The phenomenon spans phenomena from the faint corona discharge around high-voltage conductors to the intense channel of a lightning bolt, and underlies technologies such as neon signs, fluorescent lamps and plasma displays.

Mechanism

A neutral gas is normally an insulator. When the applied electric field is strong enough, free electrons are accelerated until they ionise gas molecules by collision, producing an avalanche of charged particles described by Townsend's theory. The breakdown voltage for a given gas depends on pressure and electrode separation, a relationship expressed by Paschen's law.

Types of discharge

Discharge regimes include the dark (Townsend) discharge, the glow discharge — used in neon lamps and sputtering — corona discharge, the arc discharge with its high current and bright emission, and spark discharge such as lightning. Glow discharges show characteristic structures: cathode glow, negative glow, the Faraday dark space and the positive column.

Applications

Gas discharges are used in lighting (fluorescent, neon, sodium and metal-halide lamps), gas lasers, plasma etching and deposition in semiconductor manufacture, ozone generation, and electrostatic precipitation.
