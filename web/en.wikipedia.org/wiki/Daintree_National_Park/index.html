Daintree National Park

Daintree National Park is a national park in Far North Queensland, Australia, about 100 km northwest of Cairns. Covering roughly 1,200 km², it forms part of the Wet Tropics of Queensland World Heritage Site and contains some of the oldest continuously surviving tropical rainforest on Earth.

Description

The park is divided into two main sections: Mossman Gorge, a popular visitor area on the Mossman River, and Cape Tribulation, where the rainforest meets the reef and which is accessible via the Daintree ferry across the Daintree River. The region is named after geologist and photographer Richard Daintree.

Flora and fauna

The Daintree preserves an exceptionally complete record of the evolution of Australian plants, including many primitive flowering plant families, and is home to a large share of Australia's animal species: hundreds of bird species, a third of the country's frog species, and mammals such as tree-kangaroos, as well as the endangered southern cassowary. The traditional owners of the land are the Eastern Kuku Yalanji people.

History

The park was established in 1981 and incorporated into the Wet Tropics World Heritage Area in 1988. Protests in 1983–84 against a road through the rainforest (the "Daintree blockade") were a landmark in Australian conservation history.
