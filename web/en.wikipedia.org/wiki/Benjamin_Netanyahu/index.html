Benjamin Netanyahu (born 21 October 1949) is an Israeli politician who has served as prime minister of Israel since 2022, having previously held the office from 1996 to 1999 and from 2009 to 2021. He is Israel's longest-serving prime minister in total.

Early life

Born in Tel Aviv and partly raised in the United States, Netanyahu served in the elite Sayeret Matkal unit of the Israel Defense Forces, participating in the rescue at Sabena Flight 571. His brother Yonatan was killed leading Operation Entebbe in 1976. He studied at MIT and worked in consulting and diplomacy, becoming Israel's ambassador to the United Nations in 1984.

Political career

Leader of Likud, Netanyahu first became prime minister in 1996. His long second tenure saw economic liberalisation, confrontation with Iran over its nuclear programme, the Abraham Accords normalising relations with several Arab states (under US auspices in 2020), and repeated conflicts in Gaza. His current government, formed in December 2022, pursued controversial judicial reforms and has led Israel during the war that began after the Hamas attack of 7 October 2023. He has faced corruption charges, which he denies.
