Singin' in the Rain

Directed byGene Kelly, Stanley Donen
StarringGene Kelly, Donald O'Connor, Debbie Reynolds
Released1952
StudioMetro-Goldwyn-Mayer

Singin' in the Rain is a 1952 American musical romantic comedy film directed and choreographed by Gene Kelly and Stanley Donen, starring Kelly, Donald O'Connor and Debbie Reynolds. Set in Hollywood during the transition from silent films to talkies in the late 1920s, it is widely regarded as one of the greatest musical films ever made.

Plot

Silent-film star Don Lockwood is paired with the shrill-voiced Lina Lamont. When their studio converts to sound, Lina's voice threatens disaster, so Don and his friend Cosmo Brown arrange for aspiring actress Kathy Selden to dub Lina's lines and songs. The title number, in which Kelly dances through rain-soaked streets, is among the most famous scenes in cinema.

Production

The screenplay by Betty Comden and Adolph Green was built around a catalogue of songs by Nacio Herb Brown and Arthur Freed from early MGM musicals. Kelly's rain dance was filmed with a mixture of water and milk for visibility, over several days while Kelly was ill with a fever.

Legacy

The film was a modest success on release but grew in reputation; the American Film Institute ranked it the greatest movie musical of all time, and it is preserved in the US National Film Registry.
