The Estádio Nacional (National Stadium), also known as the Estádio do Jamor, is a football stadium in Oeiras, near Lisbon, Portugal. Inaugurated in 1944, it is best known as the traditional home of the Portuguese Cup final and as the venue of the 1967 European Cup final, in which Celtic defeated Inter Milan to become the first Northern European club to win the trophy.

History

The stadium was built under the Estado Novo regime of António de Oliveira Salazar as part of the Jamor sports complex. Set in a wooded valley, its open, single-tier design and stone architecture are distinctive among European stadiums. It hosted the Portuguese Cup final annually for decades and occasionally the Portugal national team. In May 1967, Jock Stein's Celtic—the "Lisbon Lions"—beat Inter 2–1 in the European Cup final played here.

Present use

Although modern club stadiums in Lisbon and Porto now host most major fixtures, the Estádio Nacional continues to host the Portuguese Cup final and athletics events. Plans to renovate and modernize the ageing stadium and its surroundings have been discussed for many years.
