National Electrical Code

The National Electrical Code (NEC), also known as NFPA 70, is a regionally adoptable standard for the safe installation of electrical wiring and equipment in the United States. It is part of the National Fire Code series published by the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA), a private trade association. Despite the use of the term "national", it is not a federal law; it is commonly adopted by state or local governments and enforced by local electrical inspectors.

History

The NEC was first published in 1897, arising from the merger of several earlier codes developed by insurance, electrical, and architectural interests. It has been revised on a three-year cycle ever since, with editions reflecting new technology and recurring fire-safety lessons. The code is developed by committees of subject-matter experts through the NFPA consensus process.

Structure

The code is organized into an introduction and nine chapters. Chapters 1 through 4 apply generally; chapters 5 through 7 cover special occupancies, special equipment, and special conditions; chapter 8 covers communications systems; and chapter 9 consists of tables. Key articles address grounding and bonding, wiring methods, overcurrent protection, and specific equipment such as motors, generators, and transformers. Article 250, covering grounding and bonding, is among the most frequently cited.

Adoption

Most U.S. states and many municipalities adopt the NEC with local amendments. Because adoption occurs on different cycles, the legally enforceable edition varies by jurisdiction. Electricians, engineers, and inspectors use the code as the benchmark for safe electrical installations, and it is frequently referenced in product standards used by testing organizations such as UL.
