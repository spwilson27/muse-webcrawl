Halawa Valley

Halawa Valley (Hawaiian: Hālawa, lit. "curve") is a valley on the eastern end of the island of Molokai in Hawaii, United States. About half a mile wide and three to four miles deep, it is one of the oldest inhabited areas in the Hawaiian Islands, with archaeological evidence of settlement by Polynesian voyagers dating to around the 7th century CE.

History and geography

Halawa was historically a major centre of taro cultivation, irrigated by the Halawa Stream, and once supported a population of many hundreds in numerous small settlements with temples (heiau) and fish ponds. The valley's population declined sharply in the 19th century through disease and emigration. A tsunami in 1946 and flooding in 1957 damaged the remaining community, and most residents subsequently left, though a small number of families remain. The valley opens onto a double-crescent bay with two beaches and is overlooked by the 250-foot Moaula Falls and the taller Hipuapua Falls inland.

Access

Halawa is reached by a narrow, winding stretch of Hawaii Route 450 along Molokai's east coast, a drive of roughly 28 miles from Kaunakakai. Cultural tours led by local residents are a popular way to visit the valley and its falls.
