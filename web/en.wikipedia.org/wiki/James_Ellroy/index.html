James Ellroy

Lee Earle "James" Ellroy (born March 4, 1948) is an American crime fiction writer and essayist, known for his distinctive staccato, telegraphic prose style in novels including The Black Dahlia (1987), The Big Nowhere (1988), L.A. Confidential (1990) and White Jazz (1992), collectively the L.A. Quartet, and American Tabloid (1995).

James Ellroy
Born: March 4, 1948, Los Angeles, California, U.S.
Occupation: Novelist, essayist
Genre: Crime fiction, noir, historical fiction
Notable works: L.A. Quartet, Underworld USA trilogy

Early life

Ellroy was born in Los Angeles. When he was ten, his mother Geneva was murdered — a crime that remains unsolved and that, together with his reading of Jack Webb's The Badge and the Black Dahlia case, obsessed him. After expulsion from school and a period of petty crime, addiction and homelessness, he began writing while working as a golf caddy.

Career

His early novels Brown's Requiem (1981) and Clandestine (1982) were followed by the Lloyd Hopkins trilogy. The L.A. Quartet, set in 1940s–1950s Los Angeles, explored police corruption, racism and organised crime, drawing on the LAPD under Chief William Parker. L.A. Confidential was adapted into the acclaimed 1997 film. The Underworld USA trilogy — American Tabloid, The Cold Six Thousand (2001) and Blood's a Rover (2009) — rewrote American history from 1958 to 1972, including the assassination of John F. Kennedy.

Style and memoir

Ellroy's later style omits connecting words, using short declarative sentences. His memoir My Dark Places (1996) recounts his investigation of his mother's murder. He calls himself "the Demon Dog of American crime fiction." Later works include Perfidia (2014) and This Storm (2019), a second L.A. Quartet.
