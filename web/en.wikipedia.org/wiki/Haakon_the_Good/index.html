Haakon the Good

Haakon the Good (Old Norse: Hákon góði) is the byname of Haakon I Haraldsson (c. 920 – 961), king of Norway from 934 to 961 and the youngest son of Harald Fairhair. Fostered in England at the court of King Æthelstan, he returned to Norway with the king's support and ousted his half-brother Eric Bloodaxe.

Haakon earned his epithet from his popularity and just administration. He reorganised the law assemblies of western and northern Norway and established the leiðangr coastal defence levy. Although Christian himself, his efforts to spread the faith were resisted by pagan magnates, and tradition holds that he participated in pagan sacrifices under pressure. He died of wounds received at the Battle of Fitjar in about 961 and was commemorated in the skaldic poem Hákonarmál.
