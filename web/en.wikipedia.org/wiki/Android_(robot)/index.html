Android (robot)

An android is a humanoid robot or synthetic organism designed to resemble a human in appearance and behaviour, typically with a body, two arms, two legs and a head with a face. The word derives from the Greek andr- ("man, male") and the suffix -eides ("likeness"). A robot resembling a woman is sometimes called a gynoid. Androids are a staple of science fiction and an active goal of real-world robotics research.

History of the concept

The word appeared as early as Albertus Magnus and was popularised by French writer Auguste Villiers de l'Isle-Adam in his 1886 novel L'Ève future. Mechanical human figures predate the term: eighteenth-century automata such as Jacques de Vaucanson's mechanisms and the Jaquet-Droz writer imitated human actions. In fiction, androids range from the artificial workers of Karel Čapek's R.U.R. (1920) to the near-human replicants of Blade Runner and the android Data of Star Trek: The Next Generation.

Real androids

Modern research androids include Honda's ASIMO (a humanoid robot not designed for lifelike appearance), the Japanese Actroid and Geminoid series developed under Hiroshi Ishiguro, which copy the faces of real people, and Hanson Robotics' Sophia, which received extensive media attention. Engineers pursue realistic skin (silicone), facial expression via pneumatic or motor actuation, speech and social interaction. The "uncanny valley" hypothesis of Masahiro Mori suggests that imperfect human likeness provokes unease, a key design problem for the field.

Applications and issues

Proposed uses include reception and customer service, healthcare assistance, education, entertainment and research into human social cognition. Androids raise ethical and legal questions about anthropomorphism, deception, employment and robot rights.
