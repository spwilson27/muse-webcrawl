Phocis

Country: Greece
Region: Central Greece
Capital: Amfissa
Area: About 2,120 km²

Delphi, in ancient Phocis, was home to the most famous oracle of the Greek world.

Phocis (Greek: Φωκίδα, Fokida) is one of the regional units of Greece, in the region of Central Greece, named after the ancient territory of the same name. It stretches from the western slopes of Mount Parnassus to the Ozolian Locris mountains and the northern shore of the Gulf of Corinth. Its capital is Amfissa.

Ancient Phocis

In antiquity, Phocis was a federation of small cities whose territory included Delphi, site of the oracle of Apollo and one of the most sacred places in the Greek world. The Phocians shared control of the sanctuary with the Amphictyonic League, and struggles over Delphi repeatedly drew Phocis into war: in the Third Sacred War (356–346 BC) the Phocians seized Delphi and spent its treasures on mercenaries, provoking the intervention of Philip II of Macedon, who crushed them at the Battle of Crocus Field and expelled them from the Amphictyony. Ancient Phocian cities included Elatea, Crissa, Daulis, and Antikyra, and the keel-shaped region played a key role in routes between northern and southern Greece.

Modern regional unit

Today's Phocis is a largely mountainous, sparsely populated area known for Delphi and the town of Arachova, the olive groves around Amfissa, the port of Itea, and the Mornos reservoir, which supplies Athens with water.
