St Ives Bay

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

St Ives Bay, looking towards Godrevy Point

St Ives Bay (Cornish: Baya Pluw Wyn) is a bay on the Atlantic coast of north-west Cornwall, England, United Kingdom. It lies between St Ives in the west and the estuary of the River Hayle in the east, opening onto the Celtic Sea. The bay is noted for its long sandy beaches, surfing conditions, and the Godrevy Lighthouse on Godrevy Island at its north-eastern end.

Geography

The shoreline of the bay runs for about four miles. At the western end is St Ives Head and the town of St Ives, a historic fishing port and artists' colony. The central shore is formed by the Towans — a system of sand dunes stretching from Hayle to Gwithian — fronted by a continuous beach popular for surfing, especially at Gwithian and Godrevy. The Hayle estuary, an important habitat for wading birds and a designated Site of Special Scientific Interest, separates the Towans from the town of Hayle.

Names and administration

"St Ives Bay" is also the name of a former parliamentary constituency (1885–1918 was the St Ives division; a St Ives Bay division existed under different boundaries). A passenger rail service, the St Ives Bay Line, runs along the coast from St Erth to St Ives and is regarded as one of Britain's most scenic branch lines.

Tourism and wildlife

The bay's beaches and consistent surf support a major tourism economy. Grey seals haul out seasonally at Godrevy, and the offshore waters are visited by basking sharks, dolphins, and seabirds. The South West Coast Path follows the bay's perimeter.
