Helium-3

Helium-3
Symbol: ³He
Protons: 2, Neutrons: 1
Natural abundance: ~0.000137% of helium
Half-life: stable

Helium-3 (³He, also helion for its nucleus) is a light, stable isotope of helium with two protons and one neutron. It is rare on Earth — about 1.37 parts per million of helium in the atmosphere — but is thought to be more abundant on the Moon, where solar wind has deposited it in regolith over billions of years.

Sources

Terrestrially, helium-3 is produced mainly as the decay product of tritium in nuclear weapons stockpiles and reactors. It is also released from the Earth's mantle through volcanic activity.

Uses

Helium-3 is used in neutron detectors for radiation portal monitors, in cryogenics (dilution refrigerators reaching millikelvin temperatures), in medical lung imaging, and has been widely discussed as a potential fuel for aneutronic nuclear fusion (D–³He), which would produce fewer neutrons than deuterium-tritium fusion.

Lunar mining proposals

The Moon's regolith is estimated to contain large quantities of helium-3 implanted by the solar wind, motivating proposals — not yet realised — to mine it as a fusion fuel.
