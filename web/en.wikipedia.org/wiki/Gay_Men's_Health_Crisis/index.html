Gay Men's Health Crisis (GMHC) is a New York City–based non-profit, volunteer-supported and community-based AIDS service organisation. It was the first community-based AIDS organisation in the United States, founded in the earliest days of the epidemic, and has since served tens of thousands of people living with and affected by HIV and AIDS of all backgrounds.

Founding

GMHC grew out of meetings held in the summer of 1981 in the Chelsea apartment of playwright and activist Larry Kramer, after reports emerged of a rare cancer, Kaposi's sarcoma, striking gay men. The founders included Kramer, Nathan Fain, Larry Mass, Paul Popham, Paul Rapoport and Edmund White. In January 1982 the organisation was formally established; it ran the world's first AIDS hotline from a single telephone in volunteer Rodger McFarlane's apartment and produced some of the earliest safer-sex education materials.

Services and advocacy

GMHC pioneered the "buddy" programme pairing trained volunteers with people with AIDS, and developed services including HIV testing and counselling, legal assistance, housing support, meals, mental-health care and harm reduction. Its annual AIDS Walk New York, begun in 1986, became one of the largest AIDS fundraising events in the world.

The organisation has also been a leading policy advocate on HIV/AIDS funding, HIV criminalisation, housing for people living with HIV, and access to prevention tools such as PrEP. In 2010 GMHC moved to a new headquarters and service centre in Chelsea, later named the Peter Krueger Center.
