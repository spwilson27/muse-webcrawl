Kondiaronk (c. 1649 – 2 August 1701), known to the French as Le Rat ("The Rat"), was a chief of the Huron-Wendat of Michilimackinac and one of the most celebrated Indigenous diplomats of the Great Lakes region. He is best known as the principal architect of the Great Peace of Montreal, signed in 1701 between New France and some forty Indigenous nations.

Life and diplomacy

Kondiaronk was renowned among French officials—who at times admired and feared him—as a brilliant orator and strategist. In 1688, seeking to sabotage a Franco-Iroquois peace that threatened Huron interests, he ambushed an Iroquois embassy travelling to Montreal under a French safe-conduct, an episode that reignited war between the French and the Five Nations. A decade later, recognising the toll of the Beaver Wars, he reversed course and worked to broker a general peace.

He travelled to Montreal in the summer of 1701 gravely ill and addressed the assembled delegates in speeches praised even by French observers. He died on 2 August 1701, two days before the treaty was signed; the French gave him a grand funeral with military honours, and his diplomacy was completed by the signing of the Great Peace on 4 August 1701.

Legacy

Kondiaronk's reported debates with the Baron de Lahontan, published as dialogues criticising European society, made him a famous figure in Enlightenment Europe, though historians debate how faithfully Lahontan recorded his words. A lookout on Mount Royal in Montreal, the Belvédère Kondiaronk, is named for him.
