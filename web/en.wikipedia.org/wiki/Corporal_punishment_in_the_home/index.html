Corporal punishment in the home

Corporal punishment in the home, also called domestic corporal punishment or parental corporal punishment, is the use of physical force by a parent or guardian against a child for purposes of discipline and control, most commonly spanking. Its forms range from slapping and spanking to striking with implements such as belts or paddles.

Legal status

Legality varies widely by jurisdiction. Sweden became the first country to ban all corporal punishment of children in 1979, and more than sixty states worldwide have since prohibited it in all settings, including the home. In other jurisdictions, including all U.S. states, parental corporal punishment remains lawful within limits of "reasonable" force, though excessive punishment may be prosecuted as assault or child abuse. Scotland (2020) and Wales (2022) banned the practice, while England and Northern Ireland retained a "reasonable punishment" defence.

Research and debate

A large body of research, summarised in meta-analyses such as Gershoff and Grogan-Kaylor (2016), associates customary spanking with increased aggression, antisocial behaviour, mental-health problems and impaired parent–child relationships in children, and finds no evidence of improved long-term compliance. Professional bodies including the American Academy of Pediatrics recommend against spanking. Supporters argue that mild, controlled spanking is a legitimate parental prerogative within cultural and religious traditions; critics frame all corporal punishment as a violation of children's rights under the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child.

Prevalence

Surveys indicate corporal punishment in the home remains common worldwide but has declined in most countries over recent decades, with the steepest declines in countries that have enacted legal bans accompanied by public education campaigns.
