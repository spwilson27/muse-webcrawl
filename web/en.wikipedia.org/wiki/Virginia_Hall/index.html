Virginia Hall Goillot DSC, MBE (April 6, 1906 – July 8, 1982) was an American spy who worked for the British Special Operations Executive (SOE) and the American Office of Strategic Services (OSS) during the Second World War, and later for the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA). Working under cover in occupied France, she organised resistance networks, arranged arms drops and helped downed Allied airmen escape. The Gestapo regarded her as one of the most dangerous Allied agents in France, calling her "the limping lady" because of her wooden prosthetic leg, which she nicknamed "Cuthbert".

Early life

Hall was born in Baltimore, Maryland, into a wealthy family. She studied at Radcliffe College and Barnard College and pursued further studies in Europe, hoping to join the United States Foreign Service. After losing her lower left leg in a hunting accident in Turkey in 1932, she was refused a diplomatic career because of the amputation, and she resigned from the State Department in 1939.

Second World War

When war broke out, Hall was in France; she joined the French ambulance service and, after the fall of France, made her way to London, where she volunteered for the SOE. In August 1941 she was sent into Vichy France — the first female SOE agent to take up residence in France — posing as an American journalist in Lyon. She built networks of contacts, organised escape lines, and reported on German activities.

In November 1942, when German forces occupied the previously unoccupied zone, Hall escaped over the Pyrenees into Spain — a gruelling crossing made on her prosthetic leg. She returned to France in March 1944 as an OSS agent, disguised as an elderly milkmaid, and organised and armed maquis groups in support of the Allied invasion, disrupting German communications and rail lines.

Later life and honours

Hall was awarded the Distinguished Service Cross — the only civilian woman to receive it during the Second World War — as well as an MBE from Britain and the Croix de Guerre from France. She joined the CIA after the war and worked there until her mandatory retirement in 1966. She died in 1982 in Rockville, Maryland.
