Zhonghua minzu

Zhonghua minzu (simplified Chinese: 中华民族; traditional Chinese: 中華民族; pinyin: Zhōnghuá mínzú), translated as the "Chinese nation" or "Chinese ethnic peoples", is a political concept in modern Chinese nationalism denoting the state-dominant national identity of China as a unified multiethnic nation-state.

History

The term was coined by the scholar and reformer Liang Qichao in 1902. It was initially associated with the Han Chinese, but was broadened during the Republican era — notably in Sun Yat-sen's doctrine of the "Five Races Under One Union" — to encompass all peoples within China's borders. In the People's Republic of China, the concept covers the 56 officially recognized ethnic groups (minzu), with the Han majority alongside 55 minority nationalities.

Usage

Zhonghua minzu serves as a supra-ethnic national identity intended to unite the country's diverse ethnicities under a single Chinese nationhood, and is a central concept in official patriotic education and in the discourse of national rejuvenation. Scholars debate its relationship to Han identity and to the civic and ethnic dimensions of Chinese nationalism.
