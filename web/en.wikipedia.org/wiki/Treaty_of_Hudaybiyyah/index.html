Treaty of Hudaybiyyah

Signedc. March 628 CE (Dhu al-Qa'dah, 6 AH)
LocationAl-Hudaybiyyah, near Mecca
PartiesMuslims of Medina; Quraysh of Mecca
SignatoriesMuhammad; Suhayl ibn Amr

Al-Hudaybiyyah, where the truce was concluded in 628 CE.

The Treaty of Hudaybiyyah (Arabic: صلح الحديبية) was an event that took place during the time of the Islamic prophet Muhammad. It was a pivotal treaty between Muhammad, representing the state of Medina, and the tribe of the Quraysh in Mecca in March 628 (corresponding to Dhu al-Qa'dah, AH 6).

Background

In 628, Muhammad set out from Medina with around 1,400 followers intending to perform the Umrah pilgrimage in Mecca. The Quraysh, who controlled Mecca, blocked their advance at al-Hudaybiyyah, a plain on the outskirts of the city. After tense negotiations, during which the Quraysh sent several envoys — most notably Suhayl ibn Amr — the two sides concluded a truce.

Terms

The treaty stipulated a ten-year period of peace during which there would be no fighting between the parties. The Muslims would return to Medina that year without performing the pilgrimage, but would be permitted to enter Mecca the following year for three days. One controversial clause required that any Meccan who came to Muhammad without the permission of his guardian be returned to Mecca, while Muslims who defected to Mecca would not be returned. Arab tribes were free to ally with either side.

Aftermath

Many of Muhammad's companions were initially dismayed by the treaty's seemingly unfavorable terms; Umar ibn al-Khattab famously questioned it. However, the truce allowed Islam to spread peacefully, and within two years the number of converts grew dramatically. The Quraysh violated the treaty in 630 by backing an attack by the Banu Bakr tribe on the Banu Khuza'ah, allies of Muhammad, leading to the Muslim conquest of Mecca. The episode is referenced in the Quran, particularly in Surah Al-Fath, which describes it as a "clear victory."
