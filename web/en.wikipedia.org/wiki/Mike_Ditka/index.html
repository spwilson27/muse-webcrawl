Michael Keller Ditka (born 18 October 1939) is an American former professional football player, coach and television commentator. A tight end for the Chicago Bears, Philadelphia Eagles and Dallas Cowboys, he was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1988, the first tight end so honoured.

Playing career

Ditka was the NFL Rookie of the Year in 1961, won the 1963 NFL championship with Chicago and Super Bowl VI with Dallas, and was a five-time Pro Bowl selection. He helped redefine the tight end as a pass-catching threat.

Coaching career

After serving as an assistant under Tom Landry in Dallas, Ditka became head coach of the Chicago Bears in 1982. His 1985 Bears, built on Buddy Ryan's "46" defence, went 15–1 and won Super Bowl XX, and "Da Coach" became a Chicago folk hero. He later coached the New Orleans Saints (1997–1999), remembered for trading an entire draft class for running back Ricky Williams. Ditka subsequently became a prominent ESPN analyst.
