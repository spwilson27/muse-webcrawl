Samkhya (Sāṃkhya) is one of the six orthodox schools of Indian philosophy and among the oldest Indian philosophical systems, traditionally founded by the sage Kapila. It is a rigorous dualism: reality consists of puruṣa, pure consciousness — passive, inactive, and plural, one for each being — and prakṛti, primordial nature, which evolves the entire material and mental world.

Doctrine

Prakṛti evolves through the interplay of three guṇas — sattva (lucidity, lightness), rajas (activity, passion) and tamas (inertia, darkness) — producing intellect, ego, mind, the senses and the elements in a standard list of 25 principles (tattvas). Suffering arises when puruṣa mistakenly identifies with prakṛti's evolutes; liberation (kaivalya, "isolation") is the discriminating knowledge that consciousness is utterly distinct from nature. Classical Samkhya is notably non-theistic: no creator God is needed to explain the world.

Influence

Samkhya categories pervade Indian thought: Yoga philosophy adopted its metaphysics wholesale, the Bhagavad Gita uses its vocabulary (reinterpreting it theistically), and Āyurvedic medicine employs its guṇa theory. Though eclipsed as a living school by Vedanta, its framework remained a shared intellectual currency across Hindu, Buddhist and Jain texts.
