Hugo (cocktail)

TypeSpritz / aperitif cocktail
Base spiritProsecco
ServedOver ice, in a wine glass
OriginSouth Tyrol, Italy (2005)

A Hugo served with mint and lime.

The Hugo is a mildly alcoholic aperitif cocktail originating in South Tyrol, Italy, made with prosecco, elderflower (or lemon balm) syrup or elderflower liqueur, soda water or sparkling water, mint leaves and ice, often garnished with a slice of lime or lemon.

History

The drink was created in 2005 by Roland Gruber, a bartender in Naturno (Naturns) in the Vinschgau valley of South Tyrol, as an alternative to the ubiquitous Aperol Spritz. Gruber originally used lemon balm syrup; the better-known elderflower version spread as the drink travelled through Austria and Germany, where in the 2010s the Hugo rivalled or surpassed the spritz in popularity as a summer aperitif. Typical proportions are roughly 150 ml prosecco, 20 ml elderflower syrup, a splash of soda, and several mint leaves lightly pressed to release their aroma, served over ice in a large wine glass.
