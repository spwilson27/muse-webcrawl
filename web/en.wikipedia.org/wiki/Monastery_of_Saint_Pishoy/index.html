The Monastery of Saint Pishoy (also spelled Bishoy) is a Coptic Orthodox monastery in Wadi El Natrun, in the Beheira Governorate of Egypt, about 100 km northwest of Cairo. It is the most famous of the four surviving monasteries of Wadi El Natrun and is named after Saint Pishoy, a 4th-century desert father and disciple of Saint Macarius the Great.

The Monastery of Saint Pishoy, the largest of the Wadi El Natrun monasteries.

History

Monastic life at the site traces back to Saint Pishoy (c. 320–417), who settled in the Natrun valley and gathered disciples around him. The monastery was repeatedly damaged during raids by desert tribes and Berbers in the 5th through 9th centuries, when many monks were martyred, and was rebuilt several times. Its fortified keep (qasr), with a drawbridge, dates from the medieval period, built for protection against such attacks.

Under Pope Shenouda III (1971–2012) the monastery became the principal papal residence and a centre of the Coptic Church's modern monastic revival; the community expanded greatly, new churches and facilities were constructed, and the monastery began reclaiming desert land for agriculture. Pope Shenouda III is buried in the monastery, making it a major pilgrimage site, and his successor Pope Tawadros II also resided there for periods.

Relics and churches

The monastery's main church, dedicated to Saint Pishoy, is said to preserve the incorrupt bodies of Saint Pishoy and Saint Paul of Tammah in a wooden shrine. The complex contains five churches in total, including churches dedicated to the Virgin Mary, Saint Iskhirun, Saint George and the Archangel Michael (the latter in the keep). The monastery also preserves historic wall paintings, manuscripts and a large library.

The monastery today

The community numbers well over a hundred monks, supported by extensive agricultural and publishing activities. The monastery receives large numbers of Coptic pilgrims, particularly during the feast of Saint Pishoy in July, and is known for its retreats and conferences. It is one of the four ancient monasteries still inhabited in Wadi El Natrun, together with the monasteries of Saint Macarius, Saint Anthony's neighbour community of Baramos (Paromeos), and the Monastery of the Syrians (Deir al-Surian).
