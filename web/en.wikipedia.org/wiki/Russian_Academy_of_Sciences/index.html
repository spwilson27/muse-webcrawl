The Russian Academy of Sciences (RAS; Russian: Росси́йская акаде́мия нау́к) is the national academy of Russia, consisting of a network of scientific research institutes across the Russian Federation and a body of elected members — full members (academicians) and corresponding members. It is the successor to the Academy of Sciences of the USSR and, before that, to the imperial St Petersburg Academy of Sciences.

History

The Academy was founded in Saint Petersburg by a decree of Peter the Great in 1724, opening in 1725, shortly after the Tsar's death. Its early members were recruited largely from abroad — including the mathematicians Leonhard Euler and Daniel Bernoulli — and it quickly became a centre of European science. The polymath Mikhail Lomonosov, who joined in the 1740s, was its most famous early Russian member. The Academy operated schools and a university, ran expeditions across the empire (including the Kamchatka expeditions associated with Vitus Bering), and published Russia's early scientific literature.

In the Soviet period the institution became the Academy of Sciences of the USSR (from 1925), moved its headquarters to Moscow in 1934, and grew into a vast research empire employing tens of thousands of scientists. After the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991 it was re-established as the Russian Academy of Sciences. A controversial reform in 2013 transferred management of its institutes' property and much of their funding to a federal agency (FANO), diminishing the Academy's autonomy, though the institutes themselves remained affiliated with it.

Organisation

The Academy is organised into thematic divisions (mathematics; physics; chemistry and materials; biological sciences; earth sciences; social sciences; historical and philological sciences; and others) and regional branches for Siberia, the Urals and the Far East. Its institutes conduct fundamental research across the sciences and humanities. The Academy's Presidium and headquarters are in Moscow, in a building nicknamed the "Golden Brains" for the gilded glass structure on its roof.

Membership

Membership is by election and is among the highest honours in Russian science. Full members bear the title of Academician. Past members include Ivan Pavlov, Pyotr Kapitsa, Lev Landau, Andrei Sakharov and Zhores Alferov, several of them Nobel laureates.
