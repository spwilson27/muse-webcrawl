Repo Man

Repo Man is a 1984 American science fiction black comedy film written and directed by Alex Cox. It stars Emilio Estevez as Otto Maddox, a young Los Angeles punk who is recruited by a car repossession agency, and Harry Dean Stanton as his mentor Bud. The film blends punk subculture, Cold War paranoia and UFO conspiracy themes.

Plot

Laid off from his supermarket job, Otto is lured into repossessing cars for the Helping Hand Acceptance Corporation. He learns the "repo code" from Bud while competing with rival repo men. Meanwhile, a 1964 Chevrolet Malibu with a $20,000 bounty circulates through Los Angeles; its trunk contains something — implied to be the corpses of aliens from Roswell — that vaporises anyone who opens it. Government agents, UFO researchers and Otto's punk friends all converge on the car, which ultimately becomes radioactive, glows green and flies away with Otto and his girlfriend aboard.

Production and soundtrack

The film was produced by Michael Nesmith, who also executive-produced through his company. The soundtrack is a landmark of American hardcore punk, featuring Black Flag, Suicidal Tendencies, the Circle Jerks, Fear and the Plugz, whose members performed the title song written by Iggy Pop.

Reception and legacy

Initially given a limited release, Repo Man became a cult classic, praised for its satire of consumer culture — including its generic white-labelled "food" and "beer" products — and its deadpan dialogue, such as Bud's line that "the life of a repo man is always intense". It was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 2024. Cox later made the unrelated "spiritual sequel" Repo Chick (2009); the 2010 film Repo Men is unrelated.
