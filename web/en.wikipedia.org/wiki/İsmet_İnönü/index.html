İsmet İnönü, second president of the Turkish Republic.

Mustafa İsmet İnönü (24 September 1884 – 25 December 1973) was a Turkish army officer, diplomat and statesman who served as the second President of Turkey from 1938 to 1950 and as prime minister three times. He was the closest collaborator of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk and succeeded him both as president and as leader of the Republican People's Party.

As commander of the western front in the Turkish War of Independence, he won the First and Second Battles of İnönü in 1921 against the Greek army — victories from which he later took his surname under the 1934 Surname Law. He then headed the Turkish delegation at the Lausanne Conference, negotiating the Treaty of Lausanne (1923) that internationally recognised the new republic.

Presidency and later career

As president after Atatürk's death, İnönü — styled Millî Şef ("National Chief") — kept Turkey neutral through almost all of the Second World War. In 1950 his party accepted defeat in free elections, a landmark peaceful transfer of power to the Democrat Party. He returned as prime minister in the 1960s, heading coalition governments, and remained CHP leader until 1972. He died in Ankara in 1973 and is buried in Anıtkabir, near Atatürk's mausoleum.
