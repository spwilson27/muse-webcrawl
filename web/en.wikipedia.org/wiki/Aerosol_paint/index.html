Aerosol paint

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Aerosol paint (also called spray paint) is paint supplied in a sealed, pressurised container together with a propellant gas, and released in an aerosol spray when a valve button is pressed. It allows smooth, even application of paint to many surfaces without brushes and is used for everything from touch-up and model-making to industrial coating and graffiti and street art. Cans typically contain a small mixing ball (the "pea") that rattles when the can is shaken to resuspend the pigment.

History

The aerosol spray can was developed in the 1920s–40s (early patents by Eric Rotheim and, in the United States, work leading to the insecticide "bug bombs" of the Second World War). In 1949 Edward Seymour of Sycamore, Illinois, at the suggestion of his wife Bonnie, added paint to the spray can and founded Seymour of Sycamore to sell it; aluminium spray paint was his first product. Brands such as Krylon (founded 1947) and Rust-Oleum became household names, and from the 1970s cheap, varied spray paint became the defining medium of New York subway graffiti and then of street art worldwide.

Composition and mechanism

A can holds paint (pigment, resin binder and solvents) and a liquefied or compressed propellant such as propane/butane or dimethyl ether; pressing the actuator opens a valve and the propellant drives paint up a dip tube and out through the nozzle as a fine mist. Formulations include enamels, lacquers, acrylics and primers, with finishes from matte to metallic. Interchangeable caps ("fat" and "skinny") change the spray width and are prized by artists. Original chlorofluorocarbon propellants were phased out under the Montreal Protocol because of ozone depletion.

Uses, hazards and regulation

Beyond art, aerosol paints are used for marking, corrosion protection, appliance and vehicle repair, and crafts. Hazards include flammability, solvent fumes (requiring ventilation and respirators for heavy use), and volatile organic compound emissions, which have prompted low-VOC formulations and water-based ranges. Because of graffiti and inhalant abuse, many jurisdictions restrict sales to minors and require cans to be kept locked or behind counters.
