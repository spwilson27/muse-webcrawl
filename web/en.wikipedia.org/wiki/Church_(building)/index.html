Church (building)

A church, church house or church building is a building used for Christian religious activities, particularly for Christian worship services. The term is also used for the community of Christians who meet there, reflecting the biblical Greek word ekklēsia, meaning "assembly" or "congregation", which in the New Testament refers to people rather than a structure.

History

The earliest Christians met in private houses — "house churches" such as the one excavated at Dura-Europos in Syria (c. 233 CE). After the legalisation of Christianity in the Roman Empire in the 4th century, purpose-built churches proliferated, often modelled on the Roman basilica, a public hall with a central nave flanked by aisles and an apse.

Architecture

Typical elements of a traditional church include the nave (where the congregation sits), the chancel and sanctuary containing the altar, the narthex or entrance vestibule, side aisles, transepts forming a cruciform plan, a tower or steeple often housing bells, and stained glass windows. In many Eastern churches an iconostasis separates the sanctuary from the nave.

Styles have varied enormously across periods — Byzantine domes, Romanesque round arches, Gothic pointed arches and flying buttresses, Renaissance, Baroque, Neoclassical, Gothic Revival, and modernist designs. Some traditions, notably Quakers and certain Protestant groups, prefer plain meeting houses, while many contemporary congregations meet in converted commercial or industrial spaces.

Terminology

Larger or especially significant churches may be called cathedrals (the seat of a bishop), basilicas (a title of honour), parish churches, chapels, abbey churches, or minsters, depending on tradition and function.
