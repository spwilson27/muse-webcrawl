The Tempest (Giorgione)

ArtistGiorgione
Yearc. 1508
MediumOil on canvas
LocationGallerie dell'Accademia, Venice

The Tempest (La Tempesta) is a Renaissance painting by the Italian master Giorgione, dated to about 1508. It is famous as one of the most enigmatic pictures in Western art: despite centuries of study, no interpretation of its subject commands agreement.

Description

In a lush landscape, a stream crosses the foreground. On the right bank a nude woman sits nursing a baby, gazing out at the viewer; on the left a young man in contemporary dress stands with a staff, glancing toward her. Behind them are broken classical columns, a bridge and a walled town, beneath a dark blue-green sky lit by a flash of lightning. X-rays reveal that Giorgione originally painted a second nude woman in the stream where the man now stands.

Interpretations

Scholars have proposed the figures as Adam and Eve after the expulsion, a scene from classical mythology, an allegory of Fortune or of the contrast between city and country, and many other readings. The earliest surviving description, by Marcantonio Michiel in 1530, simply calls it "the little landscape with the tempest, with the gypsy and the soldier" — suggesting that even contemporaries did not expect a specific narrative. The picture is often praised as the first true landscape in Italian painting and as an early example of a painting made for the mood it evokes rather than the story it tells.
