Catawba (grape)

Catawba is a red American grape variety, a hybrid of the native Vitis labrusca with Vitis vinifera ancestry, named after the Catawba River in North Carolina. In the 19th century it was the most celebrated wine grape in the United States, the basis of famous sparkling wines from Ohio.

SpeciesVitis labrusca hybrid
OriginNorth Carolina / Maryland, USA, c. 1801
ColourRed / pink
Notable winesOhio sparkling Catawba

History

The vine was propagated by Major John Adlum in Georgetown, Washington, D.C., in the 1820s. Nicholas Longworth of Cincinnati built a major wine industry on Catawba from the 1830s, producing still and sparkling wines praised internationally and celebrated in verse by Longfellow. By the 1850s Ohio was the leading wine state in America, largely on Catawba.

Decline and modern use

Disease pressure (notably black rot and mildews), Prohibition and the Civil War era decline ended Catawba's dominance, though it survives in the Finger Lakes, Lake Erie and other eastern regions, used for pink and white "labrusca"-style wines and juices. Catawba is also a parent of the Concord grape seedling generation crosses and of the Diana grape.
