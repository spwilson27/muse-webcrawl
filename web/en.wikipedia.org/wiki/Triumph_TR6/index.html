Triumph TR6

The Triumph TR6 is a sports car that was built by the Triumph Motor Company of England from 1968 until 1976. It was the best-seller of the TR range when production ended, surpassed only later by the TR7. Of the 91,850 TR6s produced, 83,480 were exported, mainly to the United States, while only 8,370 were sold in the United Kingdom.

Design

The bodywork was generally similar to the TR4 and TR5, but the front and rear ends were squared off, a result of a commission from the German coachbuilder Karmann, giving the car a more contemporary look. Underneath it retained the separate chassis frame, rack-and-pinion steering and independent rear suspension with semi-trailing arms of its predecessors.

Engine

All TR6s were powered by Triumph's 2.5-litre straight-six engine. Cars for the United Kingdom and most export markets used Lucas mechanical fuel injection, producing 150 bhp (110 kW) in early form and 125 bhp after 1973 detuning. The US-market cars used twin Zenith-Stromberg carburettors and produced about 104 bhp. A four-speed manual gearbox with optional overdrive was standard.

Legacy

The TR6 remains one of the most popular classic British roadsters, supported by a strong owners' club scene and good parts availability. Its combination of six-cylinder performance, simple mechanics and muscular Karmann styling has made it a staple of the classic car market.
