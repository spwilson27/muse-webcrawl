Sheikh Safi al-Din Khanegah and Shrine Ensemble

The Sheikh Safi al-Din Khanegah and Shrine Ensemble is a religious complex in Ardabil, north-western Iran, built around the tomb of Sheikh Safi al-Din (1252–1334), the founder of the Safaviyya Sufi order from which the Safavid dynasty — and thus modern Iran — took its name. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2010.

Description

Constructed between the early 16th and late 18th centuries on the site of the Sheikh's Sufi lodge (khanegah), the ensemble applies traditional Iranian architectural forms within a compact, inward-looking plan: a sequence of courtyards leads from the entrance through a library and mosque to the octagonal Allah-Allah dome over Safi al-Din's tomb, its exterior decorated with the name of God repeated in turquoise tilework. The complex also contains the tomb of Shah Ismail I, founder of the Safavid Empire, a porcelain house (Chini Khaneh) built to display Chinese ceramics gifted to the shrine, cemeteries, a kitchen and retreat cells for Sufi devotees.

History and collections

Under the Safavid shahs, who revered their ancestor, the shrine became one of the most richly endowed religious sites in Persia. Its library and treasures were partly looted during the Russo-Persian wars of the 1820s, when the celebrated Ardabil Carpet — now in the Victoria and Albert Museum in London and regarded as the world's oldest dated Islamic carpet (1539–40) — was among the works later sold abroad. The ensemble remains an active place of pilgrimage and a museum.
