Metabolic acidosis

Metabolic acidosis is the accumulation of acid in the body due to increased acid production or ingestion, decreased acid excretion, or loss of bicarbonate. It is characterised by a low arterial blood pH with a primary fall in serum bicarbonate; the respiratory system compensates by hyperventilation (as in Kussmaul breathing).

Causes

Causes are commonly classified using the anion gap. High anion gap metabolic acidosis results from lactic acidosis, ketoacidosis (diabetic, alcoholic or starvation), kidney failure, and toxins such as methanol, ethylene glycol and salicylates — remembered with mnemonics such as GOLDMARK or MUDPILES. Normal anion gap (hyperchloraemic) acidosis arises from gastrointestinal bicarbonate loss, e.g. diarrhoea, or renal tubular acidosis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on arterial blood gas analysis and serum electrolytes, with calculation of the anion gap and assessment of respiratory compensation using Winter's formula. Treatment targets the underlying cause — insulin and fluids for diabetic ketoacidosis, dialysis for renal failure or certain poisonings — while intravenous sodium bicarbonate is reserved for severe acidaemia in selected circumstances.
