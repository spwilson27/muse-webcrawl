Fort Douaumont (French: Fort de Douaumont) was the largest and highest fort on the ring of 19 large defensive forts that had protected the city of Verdun, France, since the 1890s. It was built as the keystone of the Séré de Rivières fortifications after the Franco-Prussian War, with reinforced concrete and steel turrets sunk into a sand cushion.

Battle of Verdun

By the Battle of Verdun in 1916, French commanders had partly stripped the fort of its guns and garrison, judging forts vulnerable after the rapid fall of Belgian fortresses to German super-heavy artillery. On 25 February 1916, four days into the German offensive, a small party of German pioneers entered the fort almost unopposed through an unguarded moat gallery, and the tiny garrison surrendered — a shock to France. The maxim "Ils ne passeront pas" became associated with the defence of Verdun.

Inside the German-held fort, an ammunition explosion and fire on 8 May 1916 killed hundreds of soldiers, whose remains were walled up in a gallery that remains a sealed war grave. French forces recaptured the fort on 24 October 1916 with massive artillery preparation. Today the fort is preserved as a memorial; the nearby Douaumont Ossuary contains the remains of over one hundred thousand unidentified soldiers.
