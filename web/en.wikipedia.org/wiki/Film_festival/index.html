Film festival

A film festival is an organised, extended presentation of films in one or more cinemas or screening venues, usually in a single city or region, showcasing new and independent work and awarding prizes.

Major festivals

The most prestigious — the 'Big Five' — are Venice (founded 1932, the oldest), Cannes, Berlin (Berlinale), Toronto (TIFF) and Sundance. Top prizes include Cannes' Palme d'Or, Venice's Golden Lion and Berlin's Golden Bear.

Function

Festivals launch careers, drive distribution deals and criticism, and include markets, juries and retrospectives. Thousands of smaller genre and regional festivals run worldwide. See Cinema of France.
