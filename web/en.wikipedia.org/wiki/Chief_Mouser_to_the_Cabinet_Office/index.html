Chief Mouser to the Cabinet Office is the title given to the official resident cat at 10 Downing Street, the residence and executive office of the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom. The cat's traditional role is to control the rodent population of the building, though the position is largely ceremonial and the cats are beloved public figures. The title was officially bestowed upon Humphrey, the incumbent in the 1990s, and has been held since 2011 by Larry, a tabby cat recruited from Battersea Dogs and Cats Home.

History

Cats have been employed by the British government as mousers since at least the 16th century; records mention a cat kept by Cardinal Wolsey during the reign of Henry VIII. Treasury records from the 19th and 20th centuries document a small official allowance for the upkeep of office cats. Famous Downing Street cats have included Peter (in office from the 1920s to the 1940s), Munich Mouser during the Second World War, Wilberforce (1973–1987), who served under four prime ministers, and Humphrey (1989–1997), named after the character in the sitcom Yes, Minister.

Larry arrived in February 2011 under David Cameron and has remained through the premierships of Theresa May, Boris Johnson, Liz Truss, Rishi Sunak, and Keir Starmer, making him one of the longest-serving and most publicly visible holders of the post. He has occasionally been joined by — and feuded with — cats from neighbouring buildings, including Palmerston of the Foreign Office and Freya, a former Chancellor's cat.

Duties

According to the official Number 10 description, the Chief Mouser's duties include "greeting guests to the house, inspecting security defences and testing antique furniture for napping quality." The Cabinet Office states that the cats are not the personal property of the prime minister and remain at Downing Street when administrations change.
