SU-8 photoresist

SU-8 is a commonly used epoxy-based negative photoresist. Negative refers to the fact that a photoresist is cross-linked by light, making the exposed material insoluble to the photoresist developer. As with most other negative photoresists, the chemical amplification is key to SU-8's sensitivity, in this case greater than 10 times.

SU-8 was originally developed by IBM and patented in 1989. It is now mainly produced and sold by the company Microchem and Gersteltec. The main components of SU-8 are Bisphenol A Novolac epoxy and a photoacid generator such as a triarylsulfonium salt.

Properties and processing

SU-8 can be made in a very viscous form, allowing it to be spun or spread over a thickness ranging from below 1 micrometre to over 300 micrometres, or applied as thick sheets of film for lamination. Up to 500 µm thick layers can be obtained from the standard formulation. Its cured films have very high chemical and thermal stability, making SU-8 poorly suited to stripping once exposed, and well suited to permanent applications such as microfluidic channels and moulds for soft lithography.

Processing involves spin-coating, a soft bake, near-UV exposure, a post-exposure bake that drives the acid-catalysed cross-linking, development in a solvent such as PGMEA, and an optional hard bake.

Applications

SU-8 is a standard material technology for the fabrication of microelectromechanical systems (MEMS), microfluidics devices, and masters for PDMS soft lithography. It is also used in inkjet nozzle plates and other high-aspect-ratio structures.
