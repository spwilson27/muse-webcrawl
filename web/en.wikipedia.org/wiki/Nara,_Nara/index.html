Nara (Japanese: 奈良市, Nara-shi) is the capital city of Nara Prefecture in the Kansai region of Japan. It was Japan's first permanent capital, established in 710 as Heijō-kyō, and flourished as the centre of Japanese political and Buddhist culture during the Nara period (710–784).

History

Modelled on the Chinese Tang capital Chang'an, Heijō-kyō was laid out on a grid and adorned with great state temples, including Tōdai-ji, whose Daibutsuden houses the Great Buddha (Daibutsu), a colossal bronze statue dedicated in 752, and Kōfuku-ji, the clan temple of the Fujiwara. After the capital moved away in 784, Nara (as Nanto, the "southern capital") remained a major religious centre.

Sights

Eight sites in the city — Tōdai-ji, Kōfuku-ji, Kasuga Grand Shrine, Gangō-ji, Yakushi-ji, Tōshōdai-ji, the Heijō Palace site and the Kasugayama Primeval Forest — are inscribed as the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara". The city is also famous for the roughly 1,200 free-roaming sika deer of Nara Park, regarded in Shinto tradition as messengers of the gods of Kasuga Shrine and protected as a national treasure.
