The British Rail Class 395, marketed by its operator as the Javelin, is a dual-voltage electric multiple unit built by Hitachi for high-speed commuter services in south-east England. Capable of 140 mph (225 km/h), it is the fastest domestic train in Britain and one of the fastest EMUs in everyday service in the country.

Services

The 29 six-car trains were built in Japan and entered service in 2009, running Southeastern Highspeed services from London St Pancras over High Speed 1 to Ashford, then continuing on conventional third-rail lines to destinations including Canterbury, Margate, Dover and Medway towns. The trains cut journey times to east Kent dramatically — Ashford is reached in about 38 minutes, compared with over 80 minutes on conventional services.

Technical details

The Class 395 is based on Hitachi's A-train family with aluminium carbodies. It can run on 25 kV AC overhead line equipment on High Speed 1 and 750 V DC third rail elsewhere, switching between systems while stationary or (between power systems) automatically. During the London 2012 Olympic Games, the trains ran a special "Javelin" shuttle between St Pancras and Stratford International, from which the Javelin brand derives; the name was later adopted for the class generally.
