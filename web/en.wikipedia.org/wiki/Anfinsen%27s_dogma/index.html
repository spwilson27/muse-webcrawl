Anfinsen's dogma

Anfinsen's dogma, also known as the thermodynamic hypothesis, states that the native three-dimensional structure of a protein is determined entirely by its amino acid sequence: at physiological conditions the native conformation is the unique, stable structure of lowest Gibbs free energy, and no information beyond the sequence itself is required for folding. It was formulated by Christian B. Anfinsen from his experiments on the enzyme ribonuclease A.

The ribonuclease experiments

Anfinsen showed that ribonuclease A, fully denatured in urea with its disulfide bonds reduced, spontaneously regained its active native structure when the denaturant was removed — even though the four disulfide bonds could in principle pair in 105 ways, only the native set formed. This demonstrated that the sequence encodes the fold.

Scope and exceptions

The dogma underlies protein structure prediction, crowned by deep-learning systems such as AlphaFold. Known qualifications include proteins requiring chaperones to avoid kinetic traps, intrinsically disordered proteins, prions whose alternative conformations are also stable, and structures influenced by environment or post-translational modification.
