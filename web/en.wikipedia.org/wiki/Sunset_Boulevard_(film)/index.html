Sunset Boulevard (styled Sunset Blvd.) is a 1950 American film noir directed by Billy Wilder and co-written by Wilder, Charles Brackett and D. M. Marshman Jr. It stars William Holden as Joe Gillis, a down-on-his-luck screenwriter, and Gloria Swanson as Norma Desmond, a forgotten silent-film star who draws him into her decaying mansion and her delusional plan for a comeback. Erich von Stroheim plays Max von Mayerling, her devoted butler and former director and husband.

Plot

The film opens with Gillis's body floating face-down in a swimming pool; in flashback, narrated by the dead man, he recounts fleeing debt collectors into Desmond's Sunset Boulevard estate. Norma hires him to edit her unwieldy script of Salome, intended as her return to the screen, and gradually makes him her kept lover. When director Cecil B. DeMille gently deflects Norma's comeback hopes, Max fabricates fan mail to sustain her illusions. Joe, secretly collaborating on a script with young reader Betty Schaefer, tries to leave; Norma shoots him, and when police and newsreel cameras arrive she descends her staircase imagining she is before DeMille's cameras, delivering the famous closing line.

Production and reception

Wilder cast real silent-era figures — Swanson, von Stroheim, and "waxworks" bridge partners Buster Keaton, H. B. Warner and Anna Q. Nilsson — to sharpen the film's portrait of Hollywood's discarded past. Released by Paramount, it was a critical success and earned eleven Academy Award nominations, winning three (Best Screenplay, Best Art Direction – Black and White, and Best Score for Franz Waxman). Hollywood insiders were divided: Louis B. Mayer reportedly denounced Wilder, while others recognised a masterpiece.

Legacy

Sunset Boulevard is consistently ranked among the greatest films ever made; it was selected for the U.S. National Film Registry in 1989 and placed in the top twenty of the American Film Institute's 1998 and 2007 lists. Lines such as "I am big! It's the pictures that got small" entered the language. A stage musical by Andrew Lloyd Webber premiered in London in 1993.
