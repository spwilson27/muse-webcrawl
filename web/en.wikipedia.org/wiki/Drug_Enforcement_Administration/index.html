Drug Enforcement Administration

The Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) is a United States federal law enforcement agency under the Department of Justice, tasked with combating drug trafficking and distribution within the United States. It is the lead agency for domestic enforcement of the Controlled Substances Act, sharing concurrent jurisdiction with the FBI. The DEA also has sole responsibility for coordinating and pursuing U.S. drug investigations both domestically and abroad.

History

The DEA was established on July 1, 1973, by Reorganization Plan No. 2, signed by President Richard Nixon, which merged the Bureau of Narcotics and Dangerous Drugs, the Office of Drug Abuse Law Enforcement, and other offices into a single agency. It was created as part of the "war on drugs" declared by Nixon in 1971.

Responsibilities

The DEA enforces the controlled substances laws and regulations of the United States, investigates drug trafficking organizations, regulates the legal manufacture and distribution of controlled substances, and maintains the scheduling system that classifies drugs into five schedules based on accepted medical use and abuse potential. It operates laboratories, intelligence programs, and diversion control efforts aimed at pharmaceutical drugs.

Criticism

The agency has been criticized over drug scheduling decisions (such as its long-standing classification of cannabis in Schedule I), asset forfeiture practices, and the effectiveness of supply-side drug enforcement.
