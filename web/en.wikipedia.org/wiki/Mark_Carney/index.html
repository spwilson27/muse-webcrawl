Mark Joseph Carney (born March 16, 1965) is a Canadian economist and politician serving as the 24th prime minister of Canada since 2025 and as leader of the Liberal Party since 2025. He previously served as governor of the Bank of Canada from 2008 to 2013 and as governor of the Bank of England from 2013 to 2020, becoming the first non-Briton to hold the latter post since the Bank's founding in 1694.

Early life and education

Carney was born in Fort Smith, Northwest Territories, and raised in Edmonton, Alberta. He earned a bachelor's degree in economics from Harvard University in 1988 and went on to study at the University of Oxford, receiving a master's degree in 1993 and a doctorate in economics in 1995. He was a goaltender for the Harvard ice hockey team.

Career in finance

Carney spent 13 years with Goldman Sachs, working in London, Tokyo, New York and Toronto, rising to managing director. In 2003 he joined the Bank of Canada as a deputy governor, moved to the federal Department of Finance in 2004, and returned as governor of the Bank of Canada on 1 February 2008. He steered Canadian monetary policy through the 2008 financial crisis, earning praise for Canada's relative resilience, and chaired the Financial Stability Board from 2011 to 2018.

In July 2013 Carney became governor of the Bank of England, overseeing policy through the Scottish independence referendum, the Brexit referendum and the early COVID-19 pandemic before stepping down in March 2020. He subsequently served as the United Nations Special Envoy for Climate Action and Finance, vice-chair of Brookfield Asset Management, and chair of Bloomberg L.P.'s board, and authored the book Value(s) (2021).

Prime Minister of Canada

Following Justin Trudeau's resignation, Carney won the Liberal Party leadership on 9 March 2025 and was sworn in as prime minister on 14 March 2025. He called a snap federal election and led the Liberals to victory on 28 April 2025, winning a seat in Parliament (Nepean) for the first time. His government focused on responding to United States tariffs under President Donald Trump, interprovincial trade, housing, and major national projects.
