The Shirvanshahs (Persian: شروانشاه) were the rulers of Shirvan, a historical region in the eastern Caucasus in present-day Azerbaijan. The dynasty ruled, with brief interruptions, from 861 to 1538 — one of the longest-reigning dynasties in the Islamic world — successively as vassals of the Abbasid Caliphate, and later contending with Seljuks, Mongols, Timurids, Qara Qoyunlu, Aq Qoyunlu, and finally the Safavids, who annexed Shirvan.

History

The title Shirvanshah predates Islam in the region. The Islamic-era dynasty (the Yazidids, or Mazyadids) was founded by Yazid ibn Mazyad al-Shaybani, an Arab governor under the Abbasids, and became effectively independent when his descendant Haytham ibn Khalid assumed the Shirvanshah title in 861. Capitals were at Shamakhi and later Baku. The dynasty became Persianized over time.

Under rulers such as Manuchihr III and Akhsitan I (12th century), Shirvan flourished culturally; the poets Khaqani and Nizami were associated with its court. The Shirvanshahs allied periodically with Georgia against common enemies. After periods of Mongol and Timurid domination, the Derbend branch under Khalilullah I and Farrukh Yasar restored prosperity in the 15th century, building the Palace of the Shirvanshahs in Baku. In 1500 Farrukh Yasar was killed by Shah Ismail I at the Battle of Jabani; Shirvan was finally annexed by Tahmasp I in 1538.

Culture

The Shirvanshah court patronized Persian literature, architecture, and miniature painting. Surviving monuments include the Palace of the Shirvanshahs complex and the Maiden Tower in Baku's Old City.
