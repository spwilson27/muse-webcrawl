The Gallic invasion of the Balkans was a series of military campaigns by Gaulish (Celtic) tribes in 280–278 BC that defeated Macedonian, Thracian and Greek forces and culminated in an attack on Delphi. Driven by population pressure and the lure of the wealth of the Hellenistic world, large tribal groups — descendants of the Celtic migrations of the 4th century BC — moved south from the Danube basin into the Balkans.

The invasion

In 280 BC Gauls under Cerethrius, Brennus and Bolgios advanced in three divisions. Bolgios defeated a Macedonian army and killed the king Ptolemy Ceraunus in 279 BC. Brennus then led a great host against Delphi in 279/8 BC, intending to plunder the sanctuary's treasures, but was repelled — Greek sources credited the god Apollo, aided by storms, rockfalls and freezing weather — and Brennus died of wounds or suicide during the retreat.

Aftermath

Surviving groups settled in several places: the kingdom of Tylis in Thrace under Comontorius; tribes that became the Scordisci around the Danube confluence; and, famously, the three tribes invited into Asia Minor by Nicomedes I of Bithynia in 278 BC, whose descendants, the Galatians, gave their name to the region of Galatia. Lysimachia and Byzantium paid the Gauls tribute for years, and the shock of the invasion reshaped Macedonian politics, consolidating Antigonus II Gonatas's throne.
