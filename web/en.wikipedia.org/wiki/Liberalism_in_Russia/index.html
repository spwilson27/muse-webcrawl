Liberalism in Russia

Liberalism in Russia has a history stretching from the Enlightenment-influenced reformers of the 18th and 19th centuries to the pro-democracy movements of the late Soviet and post-Soviet periods. Russian liberals have generally advocated constitutional government, civil liberties, the rule of law, private property and a market economy, in tension with both the autocratic Tsarist state and Soviet communism.

Imperial era

Liberal ideas circulated among the Decembrists of 1825 and the Westernisers of the 1840s. The zemstvo local government reform of 1864 created a base of liberal professionals; in 1905 liberals founded the Constitutional Democratic Party (Kadets), led by Pavel Milyukov, which dominated the early State Dumas and formed the core of the Provisional Government after the February Revolution of 1917, before being swept aside by the Bolsheviks.

Soviet and post-Soviet periods

Liberal thought survived in dissident and samizdat circles. Under Mikhail Gorbachev's glasnost, liberal democrats such as Andrei Sakharov gained prominence; after 1991, parties including Democratic Russia and Yabloko (founded 1993) and later the Union of Right Forces championed market reforms and democratic institutions. From the 2000s liberal parties lost parliamentary representation, and organised liberalism operated largely as an extra-parliamentary opposition.
