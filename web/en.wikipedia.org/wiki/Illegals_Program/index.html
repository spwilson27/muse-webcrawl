Illegals Program

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Illegals Program (so named by the FBI) was a network of Russian sleeper agents planted in the United States by the Russian Foreign Intelligence Service (SVR) in the post–Cold War era. The agents lived under false identities, some assumed from deceased persons, and posed as ordinary American couples and professionals, tasked with developing contacts and collecting information rather than stealing classified documents directly.

Exposure and arrests

On 27 June 2010, after an FBI investigation lasting roughly a decade, ten agents were arrested in the United States, including the journalist Vicky Peláez and the persona "Anna Chapman", whose social media presence made her a tabloid sensation; an eleventh suspect was detained in Cyprus and disappeared after being bailed. The agents pleaded guilty to conspiring to act as unregistered agents of a foreign government. None was charged with espionage proper, since little evidence showed classified material had been passed.

Spy swap and aftermath

On 9 July 2010 the ten were exchanged in Vienna for four prisoners held in Russia, among them Sergei Skripal — the largest US–Russia spy swap since the Cold War. The operation is considered a modern echo of the Soviet "illegals" tradition exemplified by Rudolf Abel, and inspired the television drama series The Americans.
