Joanna (6 November 1479 – 12 April 1555), historically known as Joanna the Mad (Spanish: Juana la Loca), was Queen of Castile from 1504 and of Aragon from 1516, though she exercised little actual power. The third child of Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile, she inherited the Spanish crowns through the deaths of her elder siblings, but her father and then her son Charles ruled in her name while she was confined at Tordesillas for nearly five decades.

Marriage and succession

In 1496 Joanna married Philip of Habsburg ("the Handsome"), an alliance binding Spain to the Habsburg Netherlands and Austria. After the deaths of her brother John (1497), her sister Isabella (1498), and nephew Miguel da Paz (1500), Joanna became heir presumptive. On Isabella's death in 1504 she was proclaimed Queen of Castile; Philip was recognized as king alongside her, but he died suddenly in 1506.

Confinement

Joanna's grief and erratic behaviour — contemporaries described her refusal to part with Philip's embalmed corpse — were used to justify her removal from power. Ferdinand had her confined in the palace at Tordesillas from 1509, and ruled Castile as regent until his death in 1516. Her son, the future Charles V, arrived in Spain in 1517 and was crowned co-monarch, but Joanna remained imprisoned at Tordesillas until her death in 1555, technically queen for over fifty years.

Assessment

Historians debate the reality and extent of Joanna's mental illness — diagnoses proposed retrospectively include depression, psychosis, and schizophrenia — and the degree to which claims of madness were a political instrument used by her father, husband, and son to exclude a legitimate queen from power.
