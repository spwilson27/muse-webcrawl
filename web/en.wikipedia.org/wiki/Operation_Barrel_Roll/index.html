Operation Barrel Roll was a covert United States Air Force and Central Intelligence Agency campaign conducted in the Kingdom of Laos between 1964 and 1973, during the Vietnam War and the concurrent Laotian Civil War. It formed part of the "Secret War" in Laos, conducted despite the country's formal neutrality under the 1962 Geneva agreements.

Background

After the Second Battle of the Plain of Jars in 1964, US Ambassador William H. Sullivan requested air support for the Royal Lao Army and for the Hmong irregular forces led by General Vang Pao, who were defending northeastern Laos against the communist Pathet Lao and their North Vietnamese allies. The first Barrel Roll strikes were flown on 14 December 1964.

Operations

Barrel Roll initially provided close air support in northeastern Laos (Military Region II), centred on the Plain of Jars. A companion campaign, Operation Steel Tiger, covered the Laotian panhandle and the Ho Chi Minh Trail. Missions were tightly controlled by the US Embassy in Vientiane, with targets approved by Ambassador Sullivan — a constraint that generated lasting friction with Air Force commanders. Forward air controllers, flying as "Ravens" and guiding strikes for Vang Pao's forces, became a hallmark of the campaign.

Scale and legacy

Over the course of the wider bombing of Laos, the United States dropped more than two million tons of ordnance — more than was dropped on Germany and Japan combined in the Second World War — making Laos, per capita, the most heavily bombed country in history. Unexploded ordnance, especially cluster submunitions, has killed and injured tens of thousands of Laotians since 1973 and remains a major humanitarian problem. Barrel Roll ended with the ceasefire of February 1973 and the completion of the US withdrawal in March.
