Anhidrosis

SpecialtyDermatology, neurology
SymptomsAbsent or reduced sweating, heat intolerance, flushing, dizziness
CausesSweat gland damage, neuropathy, genetic disorders, medications
RelatedHypohidrosis (reduced sweating)

Anhidrosis is the inability to sweat normally. Because sweating is the body's principal means of releasing heat, widespread anhidrosis can lead to dangerous overheating, heat exhaustion, or heat stroke.

Causes

Anhidrosis may result from damage to the sweat glands themselves, as in burns, skin diseases, or congenital ectodermal dysplasia; from nerve damage affecting the autonomic nervous system, as in diabetic neuropathy, Ross syndrome, or Fabry disease; or from medications, particularly anticholinergic drugs. Reduced sweating in a limited area is more common and often less serious than generalised anhidrosis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis may involve sweat tests using indicators such as starch-iodine, skin biopsy, and investigation of underlying causes. Treatment focuses on the underlying condition, cooling measures, and avoiding overheating; there is no direct cure for damaged sweat glands.
