Sidney K. Meier (born February 24, 1954) is a Canadian-American video game designer and programmer, best known for creating the Civilization series of turn-based strategy games. He has been called "the father of computer gaming" and was the second person inducted into the Academy of Interactive Arts and Sciences' Hall of Fame.

Career

Meier co-founded MicroProse in 1982 with Bill Stealey, producing simulation and strategy titles such as Sid Meier's Pirates! (1987), Railroad Tycoon (1990), and Civilization (1991). In 1996 he co-founded Firaxis Games, where he created Sid Meier's Alpha Centauri, Sid Meier's Gettysburg!, and further Civilization instalments. His name is frequently included in game titles as a branding mark.

Design philosophy

Meier famously described a game as "a series of interesting decisions", and has written and spoken extensively about player psychology and game design, summarized in his 2020 memoir.
