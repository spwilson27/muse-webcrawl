Archibald Campbell Tait

Archibald Campbell Tait (1811–1882), Archbishop of Canterbury.

Archibald Campbell Tait (21 December 1811 – 3 December 1882) was a Scottish Anglican cleric who served as Archbishop of Canterbury from 1868 until his death. He was the first Scottish-born archbishop of Canterbury and was noted for his moderate, conciliatory approach amid the intense party conflicts of the Victorian Church of England.

Early life and career

Tait was born in Edinburgh and educated at the University of Glasgow and at Balliol College, Oxford, where he became a fellow. He was one of the four tutors who protested against Newman’s Tract 90 in 1841. After a brief period as headmaster of Rugby School, ill health forced his resignation; he became Dean of Carlisle in 1849 and Bishop of London in 1856, where his energetic administration during cholera epidemics and his support for mission work earned him a wide reputation.

Archbishop of Canterbury

Appointed by Benjamin Disraeli in 1868, Tait sought to hold the Church together through the ritualist controversies that followed the Oxford Movement. He reluctantly supported the Public Worship Regulation Act 1874, intended to suppress ritualist practices, though its prosecutions proved divisive and largely unsuccessful. Tait also played a central role in the revision of the Lectionary, the debates over the Athanasian Creed, and the organisation of the first Lambeth Conference in 1867 (convened by his predecessor, Longley). His diaries and letters, edited by his son-in-law Randall Davidson, remain a major source for Victorian church history.
