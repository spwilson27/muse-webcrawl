Renfrewshire

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Paisley Abbey

Renfrewshire (Scots: Renfrewshire; Scottish Gaelic: Siorrachd Rinn Friù) is one of the 32 council areas of Scotland, and also a historic county and registration county in the west central Lowlands. The modern council area borders Glasgow to the east, which it surrounds in part, and takes its name from the town of Renfrew, the historic county town, although the largest centre of population is Paisley, where the council is based.

The historic county of Renfrewshire was larger than the modern council area, including territory now in East Renfrewshire and Inverclyde; the modern area was created in 1996 following the abolition of the Strathclyde region.

History

Renfrewshire's origins lie in the lands held by the FitzAlan family, ancestors of the Stewart dynasty, from which the High Stewards and then the royal House of Stewart emerged. Paisley became famous for its weaving industry and the Paisley pattern, named after the town, while the River Clyde ports of the area were involved in shipbuilding and trade. Paisley Abbey, founded in the 12th century, is the burial place of many of the Stewarts.

Settlements

Principal settlements include Paisley, Renfrew, Johnstone, Erskine, Linwood, Bishopton, Bridge of Weir, Elderslie, Houston, Howwood, Kilbarchan, Lochwinnoch and Inchinnan. Glasgow Airport is located within Renfrewshire, beside Paisley.

Governance

Renfrewshire Council is the local authority, headquartered at Renfrewshire House in Paisley. The area is represented in the Scottish Parliament and the UK Parliament by several constituencies, including Paisley and Renfrewshire North and Paisley and Renfrewshire South.
