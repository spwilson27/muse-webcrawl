TypeTribunal of the Roman Curia
Established12th century
HeadMajor Penitentiary
SeatPalazzo della Cancelleria, Rome

The Apostolic Penitentiary (Latin: Paenitentiaria Apostolica), formerly called the Supreme Tribunal of the Apostolic Penitentiary, is one of the three tribunals of the Roman Curia. It is chiefly a tribunal of mercy, responsible for matters concerning the forgiveness of sins and the internal forum — the realm of conscience that is not publicly provable — in the Catholic Church.

Competence

The Penitentiary deals with matters reserved to the Holy See in the sacrament of Penance: absolution of certain grave sins whose censure is reserved to the Apostolic See, dispensations from irregularities, and the granting of indulgences. Unlike the Roman Rota and the Apostolic Signatura, it does not hear contentious cases; its work is largely confidential, since it concerns the sacramental and non-sacramental internal forum. Its head, the Major Penitentiary, is one of the few Vatican officials who retains his post during a sede vacante, because the tribunal's work of absolving sins cannot be interrupted.

History

The office grew out of the medieval practice by which a cardinal was charged with hearing confessions and granting absolutions in the pope's name. By the 12th and 13th centuries a structured office of penitentiaries existed at the papal court and at the major Roman basilicas. Successive popes reorganised it, notably Pius V in the 16th century, Benedict XV in 1917, and Paul VI and John Paul II in the 20th century. The apostolic constitution Pastor Bonus (1988) and its successor Praedicate evangelium (2022) define its modern competence.

Major Penitentiaries

The Major Penitentiary is appointed by the pope and is customarily a cardinal. He is assisted by a regent and a staff of officials, and by the minor penitentiaries — confessors serving in the four major basilicas of Rome. See also Roman Curia, Indulgence, and Sacrament of Penance.
