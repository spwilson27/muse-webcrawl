Soapy Smith

Jefferson Randolph "Soapy" Smith II (1860 – July 8, 1898) was an American con artist and gangster in the American frontier West, most famously in Denver and Creede, Colorado, and in Skagway, Alaska, during the Klondike Gold Rush. He earned his nickname from the "soap with a prize" swindle, in which he sold bars of soap — some supposedly containing money wrapped inside — to crowds, with confederates "winning" the prizes to draw in buyers.

Early life and career

Smith was born in Coweta County, Georgia, into a wealthy family ruined by the Civil War. Drawn west as a young man, he worked the boomtowns of Texas and Colorado, perfecting short cons such as the shell game and three-card monte, and building a gang of rogues known as the Soap Gang.

Denver and Creede

By the late 1880s Smith effectively controlled Denver's underworld of gambling and graft, from his base at the Tivoli Saloon. When silver was discovered at Creede in 1892, he moved in and briefly ran the camp, opening the Orleans Club saloon and gambling hall, and even displaying a petrified-man hoax he called "McGinty".

Skagway and death

With the Klondike Gold Rush, Smith moved to Skagway, Alaska, in 1897, building a network that preyed on prospectors through fake telegraph offices, information bureaus and gambling dens. After his men robbed a returning miner of his gold, a vigilante committee confronted him; on July 8, 1898, Smith was killed in a shootout on the Juneau wharf with Frank Reid, who died of his own wounds days later. Smith is buried in Skagway's Gold Rush Cemetery.

Legacy

Smith is remembered as one of the most notorious confidence men of the frontier era; an annual wake in Skagway commemorates him, and his career has been retold in numerous books and the biography by his descendant Jeff Smith.
