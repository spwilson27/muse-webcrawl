History of Dominica

The history of Dominica concerns the Caribbean island of Dominica in the Lesser Antilles, inhabited successively by Arawak and Kalinago (Island Carib) peoples, sighted by Columbus in 1493, contested between France and Britain during the colonial era, and independent from the United Kingdom since 1978.

Indigenous peoples

The island was first settled by Arawakan-speaking peoples, who were displaced or absorbed by the Kalinago arriving from South America around the 14th century. The Kalinago called the island Waitikubuli, meaning "tall is her body". Their fierce resistance discouraged European settlement for more than a century after Columbus sighted the island on Sunday, 3 November 1493 — hence the name Dominica, from the Latin for Sunday.

French and British colonial rivalry

France formally claimed Dominica in 1635 but established lasting settlements only in the 18th century. The island was declared neutral by treaty in 1748, but British forces captured it in 1761 during the Seven Years' War, and it was ceded to Britain by the Treaty of Paris in 1763. France retook it in 1778 during the American Revolutionary War, but it was returned to Britain in 1783.

Slavery and emancipation

Under British rule, sugar and coffee plantations worked by enslaved Africans expanded. Maroon communities of escaped slaves resisted in the mountainous interior. Slavery was abolished in 1834, and Dominica became notable in 1838 when an elected assembly with a Black majority was formed — unique in the British Caribbean at the time.

Toward independence

Dominica was administered as part of the Leeward Islands and later the Windward Islands colonies, and joined the short-lived West Indies Federation (1958–1962). It became an associated state of the United Kingdom in 1967 with internal self-government. Full independence followed on 3 November 1978, with Patrick John as the first prime minister. Dame Mary Eugenia Charles, prime minister from 1980 to 1995, was the Caribbean's first female head of government. The island retains a Kalinago Territory, established in 1903, home to the last remaining community of indigenous Kalinago people.
