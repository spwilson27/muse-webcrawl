Turnus

Turnus is a legendary figure in Roman mythology, best known as the king of the Rutuli and the principal antagonist of Virgil's epic poem the Aeneid. In the poem, Turnus is the favored suitor of Lavinia, daughter of King Latinus of Latium, and he leads the native Italian peoples in war against the Trojan refugees led by Aeneas, who is destined to marry Lavinia and found the line that will lead to Rome.

In the Aeneid

Turnus is betrothed to Lavinia before the Trojans arrive, but prophecies and omens lead Latinus to promise her instead to Aeneas. Urged on by the fury Allecto, sent by the goddess Juno, Turnus raises the peoples of Italy against the Trojans. Virgil portrays him as a brave and formidable warrior, comparable to Achilles in the Iliad, but also as proud and wrathful. He kills the young Trojan ally Pallas and takes his sword belt, an act that seals his fate.

In the poem's final book, the war is settled by single combat between Turnus and Aeneas. Aeneas defeats and wounds him; seeing Turnus wearing the belt of Pallas, Aeneas kills him in anger rather than sparing him, ending the epic with Turnus's soul fleeing "with a groan, indignant, to the shades below." The abrupt ending has been debated since antiquity.

Other traditions

Outside Virgil, Turnus appears in Roman histories such as Livy's Ab Urbe Condita, where he is a Rutulian king defeated by Aeneas and Latinus. His sister Juturna, a nymph, plays a role in the Aeneid as his divine protector. Turnus is traditionally said to be descended from Daunus and the nymph Venilia.
