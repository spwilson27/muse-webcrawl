Historia Brittonum

The Historia Brittonum (History of the Britons) is a purported history of the indigenous British people written in Latin around 828–830, which survives in numerous recensions and manuscripts. Traditionally attributed to the Welsh monk Nennius, it assembles older traditions, genealogies and chronicles into a narrative running from the legendary settlement of Britain by Brutus of Troy to the seventh century.

Contents

The work opens with a chronology of the world and the legendary origins of the Britons, then describes Roman Britain, the coming of the Saxons under Hengist and Horsa, and the career of Vortigern — including the famous episode of the boy prophet (Ambrosius, later identified with Merlin) and the two fighting dragons beneath Vortigern's tower. It contains one of the earliest references to Arthur as a military leader (dux bellorum) and gives a list of the twelve battles he is said to have fought, culminating in the Battle of Badon. Later sections include northern British genealogies and a list of the wonders (mirabilia) of Britain.

Authorship and dating

Only some recensions name Nennius as author in a preface, and modern scholars debate whether the attribution is authentic or a later addition. The core text is usually dated to 829/830, in the reign of Merfyn Frych of Gwynedd, and it draws on sources including Gildas, Bede, Irish annals and oral tradition.

Significance

The Historia Brittonum is a foundational source for the Arthurian legend and for early medieval Welsh and northern British history, though its mixture of fact, legend and political propaganda requires careful handling. Geoffrey of Monmouth drew heavily on it for his Historia Regum Britanniae. Its account of Arthur's twelve battles remains one of the most debated passages in early British historiography.
