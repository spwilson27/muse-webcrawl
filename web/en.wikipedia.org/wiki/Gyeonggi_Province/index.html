Gyeonggi Province

Gyeonggi Province (Korean: 경기도) is the most populous province in South Korea, encircling the capital city of Seoul — which is administratively separate — together with the metropolitan city of Incheon. Its name means "the area surrounding the capital." The provincial capital and largest city is Suwon.

Geography

Gyeonggi occupies the west-central part of the Korean Peninsula, bordering North Korea along the Han River and the Demilitarized Zone to the north, Gangwon Province to the east, and the Chungcheong provinces to the south; its west coast faces the Yellow Sea. The Han River flows through the province, and the landscape is a mixture of river plains and low hills. Together with Seoul and Incheon, it forms the Seoul Capital Area (Sudogwon), home to roughly half of South Korea's population.

Economy and administration

The province is a national centre of manufacturing and high technology, hosting major industrial complexes and headquarters associated with electronics, semiconductors and automobiles in cities such as Suwon, Pyeongtaek, Hwaseong and Yongin. It is administered by an elected governor and provincial council, with a northern branch office in Uijeongbu serving the areas north of the Han River. Notable attractions include the Hwaseong Fortress (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), the DMZ tourist sites near Paju, and the Korean Folk Village in Yongin.
