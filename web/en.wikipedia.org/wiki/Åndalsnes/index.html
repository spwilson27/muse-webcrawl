Åndalsnes (Norwegian pronunciation: [ˈɔ̂ndɑɫsneːs]) is a town in Rauma Municipality in Møre og Romsdal county, Norway. It is the administrative centre of the municipality and lies at the mouth of the Rauma River, where it enters the Romsdalsfjorden, surrounded by steep mountains including Romsdalshornet and the Trolltindene peaks.

Geography and tourism

Åndalsnes is a gateway to some of Norway's most famous mountain scenery. The Trollstigen (Troll's Path) mountain road, with its eleven hairpin bends, begins nearby, and the Rauma Line railway — one of Norway's most scenic train journeys — terminates in the town after descending the Romsdalen valley past the Trollveggen (Troll Wall), the tallest vertical rock face in Europe. The town is a popular cruise-ship port and a centre for mountaineering, hiking, and base jumping (the latter officially prohibited at Trollveggen).

History

Åndalsnes was heavily bombed during the Second World War: in April 1940 it was a landing point for British forces during the Norwegian Campaign, and the wooden town centre was largely destroyed by German air raids before being rebuilt after the war.

Transport

The town is served by the Rauma Line to Dombås, by the E136 highway, and by port facilities on the fjord. The nearest airport is Molde Airport, Årø.
