Athanasian Creed

Also calledQuicunque vult
Date5th–6th century (Latin West)

The Athanasian Creed — named after Athanasius of Alexandria but not written by him — is a Christian statement of belief focused on Trinitarian doctrine and the Incarnation. Known by its Latin opening Quicunque vult (“Whoever wishes”), it is one of the three ecumenical creeds in Western Christianity alongside the Apostles' and Nicene Creeds.

Content

The creed affirms one God in three persons, equal in glory, and Jesus Christ as fully God and fully man. It contains anathemas against those denying its teaching, a feature that has made its liturgical use controversial in modern times.

History and use

Scholars date it to southern Gaul in the late 5th or early 6th century. It was used in the Roman Catholic Liturgy of the Hours, in Anglican Morning Prayer on certain feasts in the Book of Common Prayer, and by some Lutherans. It is not used in Eastern Orthodoxy.
