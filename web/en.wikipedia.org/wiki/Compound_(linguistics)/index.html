Compound (linguistics)

In linguistics, a compound is a lexeme formed from two or more independent words (free morphemes) that functions as a single word with its own meaning. Examples in English include blackbird, keyboard, sunflower, and airport. Compounding is one of the most productive word-formation processes in many languages.

Types

  • Endocentric compounds have a head that determines the word's category and core meaning: a blackbird is a kind of bird.
  • Exocentric compounds have no such head: a redhead is not a kind of head but a person who has red hair.
  • Copulative (dvandva) compounds join equal elements, as in singer-songwriter.
  • Appositional compounds combine two descriptions of the same entity, as in maidservant.

Orthography and stress

English compounds may be written closed (sunflower), hyphenated (mother-in-law), or open (post office), with conventions varying by word and dialect. Typically the first element carries the main stress, distinguishing the compound ˈblackbird from the phrase black ˈbird. German is famous for very long closed compounds such as Donaudampfschifffahrtsgesellschaft.
