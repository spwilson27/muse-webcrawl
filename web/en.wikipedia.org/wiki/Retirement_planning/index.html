Retirement planning

Retirement planning, in a financial context, refers to the allocation of savings or revenue for retirement. The goal of retirement planning is to achieve financial independence so that individuals are not required to be employed through a job for survival, but this is not the only goal: planning also addresses lifestyle goals, longevity risk, health care costs, and the desired standard of living after leaving the workforce.

Steps

The planning process typically involves determining retirement income goals and estimating future expenses; identifying sources of income such as social security or public pensions, employer-sponsored plans (e.g. 401(k) and pension plans), and individual accounts (e.g. IRAs); implementing a savings and investment program; and managing assets and risk over the accumulation and decumulation phases.

Key considerations

Important variables include the savings rate, investment returns, inflation, the planned retirement age, life expectancy, tax treatment of accounts, and withdrawal strategies. A widely cited guideline is the "4% rule", derived from the Trinity study, which suggests that retirees can withdraw about 4% of their portfolio in the first year of retirement, adjusted for inflation thereafter, with a high historical probability of not outliving their savings over 30 years.

Behavioural aspects

Beyond the financial calculations, retirement planning involves preparing psychologically and socially for the transition from work, including decisions about part-time work, housing, and activities in retirement.
