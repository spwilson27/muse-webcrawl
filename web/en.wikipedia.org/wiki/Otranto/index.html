Otranto

Otranto (Salentino: Uṭṛàntu; Latin: Hydruntum) is a town and comune in the province of Lecce in Apulia, Italy, on the east coast of the Salento peninsula. It is the easternmost town in Italy, lying on the Strait of Otranto, which connects the Adriatic Sea with the Ionian Sea and separates Italy from Albania; on clear days the Albanian mountains are visible across the 70 km strait. Otranto is celebrated for its cathedral, its castle, and its role in the Ottoman invasion of 1480.

History

Otranto occupies the site of the ancient Hydruntum, a town of Greek origin that became an important Roman port, the usual point of embarkation for the East. Under Byzantine rule it grew in importance as Brindisi declined, and its archbishopric was a bridge between the Latin and Greek churches. Norman, Angevin and Aragonese rulers successively fortified the town.

The Ottoman invasion of 1480

On 28 July 1480 an Ottoman fleet under Gedik Ahmed Pasha landed near Otranto and besieged the town. After a two-week siege the city fell on 11 August; much of the population was massacred or enslaved. According to tradition, some 800 inhabitants who refused to convert to Islam were executed on the hill of Minerva on 14 August; they were canonised by Pope Francis in 2013 as the Martyrs of Otranto, with Saint Antonio Primaldo as their leader. The relics of the martyrs are preserved in Otranto Cathedral. The Ottomans held the town for about a year before withdrawing in September 1481, after the death of Sultan Mehmed II and under pressure from Neapolitan and papal forces.

Main sights

Otranto Cathedral, consecrated in 1088, contains one of the masterpieces of medieval art: a vast mosaic pavement executed in 1163–1165 by the monk Pantaleone, depicting a Tree of Life and a rich bestiary of biblical, historical and mythological figures, including Alexander the Great and King Arthur. The cathedral also preserves the bones of the martyrs in tall glass-fronted cases. Other sights include the Aragonese Castle (Castello Aragonese), rebuilt after 1480 and familiar to readers of Horace Walpole's The Castle of Otranto (1764), the first Gothic novel, which is nominally set here; the Byzantine church of San Pietro; and the old town within its walls, a maze of whitewashed lanes above a small harbour and sandy beaches.
