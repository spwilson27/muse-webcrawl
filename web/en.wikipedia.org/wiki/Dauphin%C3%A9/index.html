Dauphiné

The Dauphiné is a former province in southeastern France, roughly corresponding to the modern departments of Isère, Drôme and Hautes-Alpes. Its capital was Grenoble. The region takes its name from the Dauphins of Viennois, whose emblem was a dolphin (dauphin in French).

History

In 1349 the last Dauphin, Humbert II, sold the province to the future Charles V of France, on condition that the heir to the French throne bear the title Dauphin and that the region retain its privileges and its own estates. The Dauphiné was later integrated into the French crown lands, though it kept distinctive institutions until the Revolution. The province was one of the cradles of the French Revolution: the Assembly of Vizille in 1788, sometimes called the birthplace of the Revolution, was held in the region.

Geography

The Dauphiné spans Alpine terrain in the east, including the Écrins massif, and lower Rhône valley country in the west. It is known for walnuts from Grenoble, Chartreuse liqueur, and winter sports resorts; Grenoble hosted the 1968 Winter Olympics. The Critérium du Dauphiné cycling race carries the region's name.
