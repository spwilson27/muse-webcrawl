Walter Plecker

Walter Ashby Plecker (April 2, 1861 – August 2, 1947) was an American physician and public health official who served as Virginia's first state registrar of vital statistics from 1912 to 1946. He is known for his role in enforcing the state's Racial Integrity Act of 1924 and for systematically reclassifying Virginia Indians and other people as "colored" in public records.

Career

Plecker trained as a physician and worked in public health before becoming Virginia's registrar in 1912. A committed eugenicist influenced by the pseudoscience of the era, he promoted the "one-drop rule" and lobbied for legislation defining racial categories in law. He played a key part in securing the passage of the Racial Integrity Act of 1924, which prohibited interracial marriage in Virginia — a law that stood until overturned by the U.S. Supreme Court in Loving v. Virginia (1967).

Records campaign

Plecker directed clerks, midwives, and registrars across Virginia to classify people of any discernible African ancestry as "colored", and he denied that any Virginia Indians remained who were not mixed with African ancestry. Under his direction, birth, death, and marriage records were altered, families were split in the records, and members of Virginia's recognised tribes — including the Pamunkey, Mattaponi, Chickahominy, and Rappahannock — were recorded as Black. In 1943 he circulated to local registrars lists of surnames of families he deemed "mixed".

Legacy

Plecker's decades-long campaign led to what tribal leaders and historians have called a "paper genocide" of Virginia Indians: the destruction of the documentary evidence of tribal identity, which later hindered federal recognition of Virginia tribes for decades. Virginia formally recognised several tribes in the 1980s, and six tribes gained federal recognition in 2018. Plecker's records and correspondence are preserved in the Library of Virginia.
