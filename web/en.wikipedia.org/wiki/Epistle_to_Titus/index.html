Epistle to Titus

Book: Epistle to Titus
Category: Pastoral epistles
Chapters: 3
Addressee: Titus, in Crete

The Epistle to Titus is one of the three pastoral epistles in the New Testament, traditionally attributed to Paul the Apostle and addressed to Titus, a Gentile companion of Paul left on the island of Crete to organize the Christian communities there. It is grouped with the two epistles to Timothy as pastoral letters concerned with church order and conduct.

Contents

The letter is brief — three chapters. It opens with qualifications for elders and overseers, warns against false teachers (quoting the Cretan prophet Epimenides: "Cretans are always liars, evil brutes, lazy gluttons"), and gives household instructions for older and younger men and women and for slaves. The third chapter exhorts believers to be subject to rulers, gentle and ready for good works, remembering that they were saved "not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to his mercy" through the "washing of regeneration" (Titus 3:5).

Authorship

The majority of critical scholars regard the pastoral epistles as pseudepigraphal, written in Paul's name after his death, citing vocabulary and church structure that seem later than Paul's lifetime. A minority of scholars and most traditional interpretations defend Pauline authorship, proposing composition near the end of Paul's life after the events of Acts, during a release from imprisonment not recorded in Acts.
