Geography of Ethiopia

Ethiopia
Area: 1,104,300 km² (426,370 sq mi)
Highest point: Ras Dashen, 4,550 m
Lowest point: Danakil Depression, −125 m
Largest lake: Lake Tana

Ethiopia is located in the Horn of Africa. It is bordered by Eritrea to the north, Djibouti and Somalia to the east, Sudan and South Sudan to the west, and Kenya to the south. With an area of about 1,104,300 square kilometres (426,370 sq mi), Ethiopia is the world's 27th-largest country and the largest landlocked country in the world by population. Ethiopia is the only African country that was never formally colonised, apart from a five-year occupation by Italy during the Second World War.

Terrain

The country is dominated by the Ethiopian Highlands, a rugged mass of mountains and plateaus split diagonally by the Great Rift Valley. The highest peak, Ras Dashen, rises to 4,550 m (14,928 ft). To the northeast, the land falls to the Danakil Depression, one of the lowest and hottest places on Earth at about 125 m below sea level. Western and southern lowlands descend towards Sudan and Kenya.

Climate

Ethiopia's climate varies greatly with elevation. The highlands enjoy a temperate climate, while the lowlands are hot and arid. The main rainy season (kiremt) runs from June to September, with a shorter rainy season (belg) in some areas from February to April. Traditional climate zones are described by altitude: kolla (hot lowlands), woyna dega (temperate midlands), and dega (cool highlands).

Rivers and lakes

The Blue Nile (Abay) rises in Lake Tana, Ethiopia's largest lake, and supplies the greater part of the Nile's water. Other major rivers include the Awash, the Omo, the Shebelle, and the Tekezé. The Great Rift Valley is dotted with lakes, including Lake Turkana on the Kenyan border, shared with Ethiopia's neighbours.
