Baltic Offensive

Baltic Offensive (1944)
Date14 September – 24 November 1944
LocationBaltic states (Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania)
ResultSoviet victory; Army Group North cut off in Courland
BelligerentsSoviet Union vs. Germany
Soviet commandersHovhannes Bagramyan; Andrei Yeryomenko; Leonid Govorov

The Baltic Offensive, also known as the Baltic Strategic Offensive, was a campaign fought between the Soviet Red Army and German forces on the Eastern Front of the Second World War from 14 September to 24 November 1944. It drove German forces from most of the Baltic states and trapped Army Group North in the Courland Pocket.

[ Image: Soviet troops entering Riga, 1944 ]
Soviet forces liberate Riga, October 1944

Background

Following Operation Bagration in the summer of 1944, Soviet forces reached the Baltic coast near the Gulf of Riga, briefly severing Army Group North's land connection to the rest of the German front before a German counterattack reopened it. The autumn offensive was designed to clear Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania once and for all.

The offensive

The operation comprised several sub-offensives: the Riga Offensive, the Tallinn Offensive (which took the Estonian capital on 22 September), the Moonsund landing operation against the West Estonian archipelago, and the Memel Offensive, in which Bagramyan's 1st Baltic Front reached the Baltic Sea near Memel (Klaipėda) on 10 October, finally cutting off Army Group North in Latvia.

Outcome

Riga fell on 13 October. The German forces in the Courland Peninsula — some 30 divisions — held out under blockade until the general surrender in May 1945. The offensive restored Soviet control of the Baltic states, which remained Soviet republics until 1991, a period the Baltic states regard as occupation.
