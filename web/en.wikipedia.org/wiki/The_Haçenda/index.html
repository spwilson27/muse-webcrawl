The Haçenda was a nightclub in Manchester, England, which became famous during the "Madchester" years of the late 1980s and early 1990s. Officially named Fac 51 Haçenda in the Factory Records catalogue system, it was principally funded by the label and by the band New Order, and designed by Ben Kelly with an industrial, warehouse-like interior.

History

The club opened in May 1982 in a converted yacht showroom on Whitworth Street West. Despite early financial difficulty, it became the epicentre of the acid house explosion: nights such as Nude, with DJs Mike Pickering and Graeme Park, and later Laurent Garnier, helped introduce house music to Britain. The Haçenda was central to the rise of the Happy Mondays, The Stone Roses' era of baggy, and rave culture generally. Plagued by gang-related violence, licensing battles and chronic losses subsidised by New Order's record sales, it closed in June 1997. The building was demolished and replaced by the Hacienda Apartments, which retain the name.

Legacy

The club's name derives from a Situationist text by Ivan Chtcheglov, who wrote "The hacienda must be built." Its story is dramatised in the film 24 Hour Party People (2002). The Haçenda remains a byword for the fusion of guitar music and dance culture that reshaped British popular music.
