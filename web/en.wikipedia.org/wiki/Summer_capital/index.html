Summer capital

A summer capital is a city or town that serves, or historically served, as the seat of government during the summer months, when the official capital becomes uncomfortable because of heat, humidity, or disease. The custom is most associated with colonial and imperial administrations in Asia, but examples occur worldwide.

Rationale

Before air conditioning, lowland capitals in the tropics could become extremely hot and prone to epidemics such as cholera and malaria during the summer. Moving the court or administration to a cooler upland or coastal town — often a hill station — protected officials and symbolised the seasonal rhythm of rule. Whole government secretariats, with their files and staff, migrated twice a year.

Examples

British India

Shimla (Simla), in the Himalayan foothills at over 2,200 metres, was the summer capital of British India from 1864 until independence in 1947; the government of India moved there from Calcutta (later Delhi) for roughly half the year. Provincial governments had their own summer seats, such as Ootacamund (Udhagamandalam) for the Madras Presidency and Darjeeling for Bengal. In British Burma, Maymyo (Pyin Oo Lwin) served a similar role.

Philippines

Baguio, in the Cordillera mountains of Luzon, was developed under American colonial rule and is officially designated the "Summer Capital of the Philippines"; the president, Supreme Court, and legislature maintain official residences and hold sessions there.

China

The Qing dynasty emperors retreated to the Chengde Mountain Resort in Hebei, which functioned as a summer capital and diplomatic venue. In the Republic and People's Republic eras, the coastal resort of Beidaihe hosted leadership summer gatherings.

Elsewhere

Srinagar alternates with Jammu as capital of the Indian union territory of Jammu and Kashmir under the darbar move tradition. Peterhof served the Russian tsars as a summer residence outside Saint Petersburg. In the United States, presidents used informal "summer White Houses", such as Calvin Coolidge's in the Black Hills and John Adams's Peacefield, though never formal capitals.
