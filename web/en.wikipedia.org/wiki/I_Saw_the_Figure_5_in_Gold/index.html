I Saw the Figure 5 in Gold, also known as The Figure 5 in Gold, is a 1928 painting by American artist Charles Demuth. It is a Precisionist work inspired by the 1920 poem "The Great Figure" by Demuth's friend, the poet William Carlos Williams. The poem describes a red fire engine with the number 5 in gold rushing through a dark, rainy city street at night.

The painting does not depict a fire truck literally. Instead, a large gold numeral "5" repeats in diminishing, overlapping forms against a field of red, black, and grey geometric shapes suggesting the truck, city lights, and buildings. The initials "W.C.W." appear in the composition as a tribute to Williams, and elements such as the names "Bill" and "Art Co." nod to the poet and Demuth's circle.

Interpretation

The work is regarded as a landmark of American modernism and of Precisionism, translating the speed, sound, and energy of Williams's poem into abstract form — an artistic equivalent of a literary portrait. It is sometimes called a "poster portrait," one of a series of symbolic portraits Demuth painted of his creative contemporaries.

Provenance

The painting was bequeathed to the Metropolitan Museum of Art as part of the Alfred Stieglitz Collection in 1949, where it remains one of the museum's best-known American paintings.
