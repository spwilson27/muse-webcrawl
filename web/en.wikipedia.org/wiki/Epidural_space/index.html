Epidural space

The epidural space is the area between the dura mater (the outermost meningeal layer) and the vertebral wall of the spinal canal. It contains loose areolar tissue, fat, lymphatics, spinal nerve roots, and the internal vertebral venous plexuses (Batson's plexus). It extends from the foramen magnum to the sacral hiatus.

Clinical significance

The epidural space is the target of epidural anaesthesia and analgesia, in which local anaesthetic and opioids are injected through a needle or catheter to block spinal nerve roots, widely used for labour pain and surgery. Blood collecting in the space forms an epidural haematoma, which can compress the spinal cord and requires emergency treatment; infection can produce an epidural abscess. In the cranium the epidural space is only a potential space, but arterial bleeding (classically from the middle meningeal artery after skull fracture) can create a life-threatening epidural haematoma there as well.
