Siding Spring Survey

The Siding Spring Survey (SSS) was a near-Earth object search program conducted at Siding Spring Observatory in New South Wales, Australia. It was the Southern Hemisphere counterpart of the Catalina Sky Survey and operated from 2004 until 2013, when funding ended.

Operations

The survey used the 0.5-metre Uppsala Southern Schmidt Telescope, upgraded with a large CCD detector. Its role was to discover and track asteroids and comets — particularly potentially hazardous near-Earth objects — in southern skies not covered by the northern Catalina telescopes. The program was funded by NASA as part of its mandate to catalogue near-Earth objects and run by the University of Arizona's Lunar and Planetary Laboratory with the Australian National University.

Discoveries

Siding Spring Survey discovered numerous asteroids and comets. Its most famous find was Comet C/2013 A1, discovered by Robert H. McNaught in January 2013 and nicknamed "Siding Spring" — the comet made an exceptionally close pass by Mars on 19 October 2014. McNaught alone discovered dozens of comets under the survey.
