Warfare

Warfare is the organised business of war: the conduct of military operations by armed forces, encompassing strategy, operational art, tactics, logistics, and the technologies and doctrines used to fight. It is commonly categorised by domain — land warfare, naval warfare, and air warfare, joined in the modern era by space and cyber warfare — and by type, such as conventional, guerrilla, siege, trench, armoured, and nuclear warfare.

Combined-arms warfare integrates infantry, armour, artillery, and air power

Levels of war

Military theory distinguishes the strategic level, which sets national objectives and allocates resources; the operational level, which plans campaigns linking battles to strategy; and the tactical level, the conduct of individual engagements. Classic theorists such as Sun Tzu (The Art of War) and Carl von Clausewitz (On War) remain central to the study of warfare, emphasising deception, morale, friction, and the political nature of war.

History

Warfare evolved from raiding and tribal conflict to the massed infantry of Greek phalanxes and Roman legions, medieval cavalry and castles, and the gunpowder armies of early modern states. Industrialisation produced conscript mass armies, railways, rifled weapons, and the static trench systems of the First World War; the Second World War combined armour, aircraft, submarines, and amphibious operations on a global scale and ended with nuclear weapons. Since 1945, warfare has included limited wars, insurgency and counterinsurgency, proxy wars, and increasingly unmanned and information-based systems.

Law and ethics

The conduct of warfare is regulated by international humanitarian law — the Hague and Geneva Conventions — covering the treatment of prisoners, civilians, and the wounded, and by doctrines of just war theory addressing when war may be waged (jus ad bellum) and how (jus in bello).
