Tasimelteon

Tasimelteon, sold under the brand name Hetlioz, is a melatonin receptor agonist used to treat non-24-hour sleep–wake disorder (Non-24) and nighttime sleep disturbances in Smith–Magenis syndrome. It was developed by Vanda Pharmaceuticals and approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in January 2014 — the first drug approved for Non-24, a circadian rhythm disorder affecting many totally blind people.

Mechanism

Tasimelteon binds with high affinity to the MT1 and MT2 melatonin receptors in the suprachiasmatic nucleus, the brain's master clock, mimicking the action of endogenous melatonin. Taken at the same time each evening, it entrains the free-running circadian rhythm to the 24-hour day.

Uses

Non-24-hour sleep–wake disorder occurs in up to about 70% of totally blind people, whose circadian rhythms drift because the clock cannot be reset by light. In clinical trials tasimelteon significantly increased entrainment compared with placebo. It was later approved for nighttime sleep disturbances in Smith–Magenis syndrome in adults (capsules) and children (liquid formulation).

Side effects

The most common adverse effects are headache, abnormal dreams or nightmares, upper respiratory and urinary tract infections, and elevated liver enzymes (ALT). It is metabolised mainly by CYP1A2, so smoking and interacting drugs can alter its levels.
