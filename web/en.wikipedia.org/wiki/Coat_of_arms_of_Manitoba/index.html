Coat of arms of Manitoba

The coat of arms of Manitoba

Armiger: His Majesty the King in Right of Manitoba

Adopted: Shield 1905; full achievement 1992

Shield: Vert on a chief Argent the Cross of St. George, in base a buffalo statant proper

Motto: Gloriosus et Liber ("Glorious and Free")

The coat of arms of Manitoba is the heraldic symbol representing the Canadian province of Manitoba. The original arms — the shield alone — were granted by royal warrant of King Edward VII on 10 May 1905. The full achievement, including crest, supporters, motto and compartment, was augmented and granted by the Canadian Heraldic Authority on 23 October 1992.

Shield

The shield's green field represents Manitoba's fertile land. On a silver (white) chief appears the red Cross of St. George, recalling the Hudson's Bay Company, whose arms bore the same cross and which governed the territory before its transfer to Canada. In the lower portion stands a North American bison (buffalo) in its natural colours, an animal that once roamed the prairies in immense herds and was central to the lives of Indigenous peoples and the Red River settlers. The buffalo also features on the provincial flag.

Augmentation of 1992

The crest consists of a beaver — Canada's national animal — holding a prairie crocus, Manitoba's floral emblem, and backed by the Royal Crown. The supporters are a unicorn on the dexter side, recalling Scotland, collared with a green mural coronet bearing maple leaves and a medallion of the Order of Manitoba, and a white horse on the sinister side, collared with a woven Indigenous sash pattern and a medallion showing the cycles of life. The compartment depicts a landscape of field, forest, lake and river representing the province's regions, and includes the prairie crocus and a Red River cart wheel motif.

The motto, Gloriosus et Liber, "Glorious and Free", is drawn from the English-language lyrics of the national anthem, "O Canada".

Use

The arms are used by the provincial government, appear on official documents and the Great Seal of Manitoba, and form the basis of the shield used by provincial agencies and the Manitoba courts.
