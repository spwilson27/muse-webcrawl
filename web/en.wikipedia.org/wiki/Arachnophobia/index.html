Arachnophobia is a specific phobia characterised by an intense, irrational fear of spiders and other arachnids such as scorpions. It is among the most common of all specific phobias. Reactions of people with arachnophobia often seem irrational to others, and sufferers may go to great lengths to avoid places where spiders might be present and to remove spiders encountered indoors.

The word derives from the Greek arachne (ἀράχνη, "spider") and phobos (φόβος, "fear").

Signs and symptoms

People with arachnophobia may experience anxiety, rapid heartbeat, sweating, trembling, nausea, or a panic attack when they see a spider, a picture of a spider, or even when they anticipate encountering one. They typically avoid basements, attics, gardens, and wooded areas, and may insist that rooms be checked for spiders before entering.

Causes

Several explanations have been proposed. Evolutionary psychologists argue that fear of spiders may have been selected for because some spiders are venomous, though very few species are dangerous to humans. Disgust sensitivity, rather than fear alone, appears to play a large role. Cultural transmission and childhood experiences — including watching fearful reactions in parents — also contribute, and heritability studies suggest a genetic component shared with other specific phobias.

Treatment

The standard treatment is exposure therapy, in which the person is gradually and repeatedly exposed to spider stimuli — pictures, models, virtual-reality spiders, and eventually live spiders — while learning to manage the fear response. Studies using virtual reality exposure have reported substantial reductions in fear after a single session. Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) addressing catastrophic beliefs about spiders is also effective.

In popular culture

Arachnophobia gives its name to the 1990 American horror-comedy film Arachnophobia, directed by Frank Marshall. Fear of spiders is a recurring motif in literature and film, from the giant spider Shelob in Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings to the Acromantulas of the Harry Potter series.
