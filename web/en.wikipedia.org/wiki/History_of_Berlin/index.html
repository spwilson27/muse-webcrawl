History of Berlin

The history of Berlin begins with the twin trading towns of Berlin and Cölln on the River Spree, founded in the early 13th century and first documented in 1237 and 1244. Berlin grew as a member of the Hanseatic League and became the residence of the margraves and electors of Brandenburg.

Prussian capital and imperial city

Devastated by the Thirty Years' War, Berlin was rebuilt under the Great Elector Frederick William, who invited Huguenot refugees from 1685. In 1701 Elector Frederick III crowned himself King Frederick I in Prussia, and Berlin became a royal capital; in 1709 the cities were formally united. Under Frederick the Great it became a centre of the Enlightenment. Industrialisation in the 19th century made it one of Europe's great metropolises, and in 1871 it became the capital of the German Empire.

Twentieth century

The Weimar era made Berlin a cultural capital amid political turmoil. Under the Nazi regime (1933–1945) it was the seat of government of the Third Reich; the city was devastated by bombing and the Battle of Berlin in 1945. Divided between the Western Allies and the Soviet Union, Berlin became the emblem of the Cold War, physically split by the Berlin Wall from 1961 to 1989, with West Berlin an exclave of West Germany and East Berlin the capital of East Germany. After the Wall fell on 9 November 1989, Berlin was reunified and became the capital of the reunified Germany in 1990, with the government moving from Bonn in 1999.
