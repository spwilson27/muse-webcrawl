Marshal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Marshal is a term used in several official titles across various branches of society and government. Historically derived from the Old High German marahscalc ("horse servant" or "stable master"), the office of marshal evolved at medieval courts from supervision of the royal stables into one of the highest military and ceremonial dignities. As a military rank, field marshal or its equivalent is the highest rank in the armies of many countries; as a civil office, marshals serve in law enforcement, courts and ceremonial roles.

Military rank

In numerous armies the marshal ranks above general officers. Examples include the Maréchal de France, a distinction granted to exceptional commanders; the British field marshal, the highest rank in the British Army; the German Generalfeldmarschall; and the Marshal of the Soviet Union. The baton is the traditional symbol of a marshal's authority. In the United States, the five-star equivalent is General of the Army, and the title "marshal" is not used as an army rank.

Law enforcement and courts

In the United States, marshals include the officers of the United States Marshals Service, the oldest federal law-enforcement agency, responsible for judicial security, fugitive apprehension, prisoner transport and witness protection; U.S. marshals are appointed for each federal judicial district. Many states and cities also have marshals performing court, enforcement or ceremonial duties. In the American Old West, town and deputy marshals were iconic frontier lawmen.

Other uses

  • Earl Marshal — a senior officer of state in England, responsible for heraldry and state ceremonies, held hereditarily by the Dukes of Norfolk.
  • Marshal of the Sejm and Marshal of the Senate — the speakers of the two chambers of the Polish parliament.
  • Air marshal and related ranks in several air forces.
  • Grand marshal and race marshals — ceremonial leaders of parades and safety officials at motorsport events.
  • Fire marshal — an official responsible for fire safety enforcement.
  • Court marshal / provost marshal — military police and ceremonial roles.

Etymology

The word entered English through Old French mareschal, from a Frankish compound meaning "horse-servant." Cognate offices appeared across medieval Europe, including the German Marschall and the Polish marszałek, whose functions ranged from court administration to supreme military command; in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth the Grand Marshal of the Crown was among the highest offices of state.
