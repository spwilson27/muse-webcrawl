A moshav shitufi (Hebrew: מושב שיתופי, "collective moshav") is a type of cooperative Jewish village in Israel. It combines the collective production of the kibbutz with the private household life of the moshav: land, machinery, production, and marketing are held and managed communally and members work in the collective enterprises, but each family maintains its own home, cooks and eats privately, and raises its children at home.

Organisation

In a classic moshav shitufi, profits from agriculture and industry are shared by the community and decisions are made by a general assembly of members, as on a kibbutz. Unlike a kibbutz, there are no communal dining halls or children's houses; consumption is private, and members traditionally received budgets or wages that varied with family needs rather than being strictly equal. The first moshav shitufi, Kfar Hittim near Tiberias, was founded in 1936 by German Jewish immigrants, and the model appealed to settlers who wanted economic cooperation without the kibbutz's full communal living.

Development

Several dozen moshavim shitufiim were established, concentrated in the years around and after the founding of the state in 1948. Since the 1980s, in common with the kibbutz movement, many have undergone privatisation: introducing differential salaries, converting collective assets into shares, and admitting non-member residents in expansion neighbourhoods.
