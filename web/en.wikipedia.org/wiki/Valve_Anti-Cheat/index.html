Valve Anti-Cheat

Valve Anti-Cheat (VAC) is an anti-cheat tool developed by Valve, first released with Counter-Strike in 2002. It is a component of the Steam platform.

Operation

VAC scans the memory and processes of a running game for known cheat signatures. When a cheat is detected, the account is marked and, after a delay of days or weeks, a permanent "VAC ban" is applied, preventing the account from playing on VAC-secured servers for the affected game. The delay is intended to prevent cheat developers from immediately knowing which hacks were detected.

Scope and criticism

VAC is used in Valve games such as Counter-Strike 2, Team Fortress 2, Dota 2 and the Half-Life and Left 4 Dead series, as well as many third-party titles on Steam. Bans are permanent and non-negotiable, though false positives have occasionally occurred and been reversed in waves.
