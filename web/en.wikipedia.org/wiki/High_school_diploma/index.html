High school diploma

A high school diploma is a school-leaving qualification awarded upon successful completion of a course of study at a high school. In the United States and Canada it is granted to students who complete the required credits in grades 9 through 12 (typically ages 14–18), and it is the standard prerequisite for admission to colleges and universities and for many jobs.

Requirements in the United States

There is no single national standard: each state and school district sets its own graduation requirements, generally including credits in English, mathematics, science, social studies, and electives, and some states require passing exit examinations. Diplomas may carry endorsements or types — such as honours, college-preparatory, career/technical, or International Baccalaureate tracks. Students who leave school without a diploma may later earn an equivalent credential by passing a high school equivalency test such as the GED or HiSET.

Significance

The diploma marks the completion of secondary education in the U.S. system and a dividing line in labour-market outcomes: high school graduates earn substantially more on average than those without a diploma and experience lower unemployment. Graduation rates are a widely tracked policy statistic; the U.S. average public high school graduation rate reached roughly 86–87% in recent years.

Equivalents elsewhere

Analogous school-leaving qualifications include the GCSE/A-level system in England and Wales, the Abitur in Germany, the baccalauréat in France, and the Higher School Certificate in parts of Australia — though structures, ages, and the mix of examinations differ considerably.
