Fairmont Hotels & Resorts is a Canadian-based operator of luxury hotels and resorts. Founded in California with the opening of the Fairmont San Francisco in 1907, the brand merged with Canadian Pacific Hotels in 1999 to form Fairmont Hotels and Resorts, retaining the Fairmont name. Since 2016 it has been part of the French hospitality group Accor, following Accor's acquisition of FRHI Hotels & Resorts.

History

The first Fairmont hotel opened in San Francisco in 1907; the building was damaged by the 1906 earthquake and fire but was restored and opened a year later. The American chain grew to include landmark properties such as the Plaza Hotel in New York (managed by Fairmont from 1999) and hotels in Dallas, New Orleans, and Boston (the Fairmont Copley Plaza).

In Canada, Canadian Pacific Hotels — itself descended from the railway hotels of Canadian Pacific Railway — acquired Fairmont in 1999 and adopted Fairmont as its corporate and brand name in 2001. Its Canadian properties include grand railway hotels such as the Fairmont Banff Springs (opened 1888), the Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise, the Fairmont Royal York in Toronto, the Fairmont Hotel Vancouver, and the Fairmont Le Château Frontenac in Quebec City (opened 1893), an iconic National Historic Site.

International expansion and Accor era

Under FRHI (formed in 2006 when Fairmont combined with Raffles Hotels & Resorts), the brand expanded into the Middle East, Asia, and Europe, with properties including the Fairmont Monte Carlo, Fairmont Dubai, the Fairmont Peace Hotel in Shanghai, and the Fairmont Jaipur. The 2016 acquisition by Accor — for a reported US$2.9 billion involving Qatari and Saudi investors retaining stakes — placed Fairmont within Accor's luxury division alongside Raffles and Swissôtel. The brand's loyalty program, Fairmont President's Club, was folded into Accor's Le Club AccorHotels (now ALL – Accor Live Limitless) in 2018.

Many Fairmont hotels are designated heritage properties, reflecting the brand's origins in the grand railway hotel era.
