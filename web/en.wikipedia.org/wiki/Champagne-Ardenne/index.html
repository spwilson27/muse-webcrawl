Champagne-Ardenne

Champagne-Ardenne (French pronunciation: [ʃɑ̃paɲ aʁdɛn]) is a former administrative region of northeastern France. Created in the 1950s–1970s, it comprised the four departments of Ardennes, Aube, Haute-Marne, and Marne, with its prefecture at Châlons-en-Champagne, while its largest city was Reims. On 1 January 2016 it merged with Alsace and Lorraine to form the new region of Grand Est.

Geography

The region combined the open chalk plains of Champagne in the west — some of the finest vineyard land in the world — with the wooded, hilly Ardennes plateau in the north along the Belgian border, cut by the meandering Meuse valley. The Marne, Aube, Seine, and Aisne rivers cross the region. Reims, with its Gothic cathedral where the kings of France were crowned, and Épernay, centre of the Champagne trade, are its best-known cities; Troyes, Charleville-Mézières, and Chaumont are other principal towns.

History

The historic province of Champagne was famous in the Middle Ages for the Champagne fairs at Troyes, Provins, and other towns, which linked the trade of northern Europe with Italy. The region's sparkling wine, developed from the 17th–18th centuries and industrialised in the 19th, gave it worldwide fame; the Champagne production zone and its hillsides, houses, and cellars were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2015.

Champagne-Ardenne was a battlefield in both world wars — the Battles of the Marne (1914 and 1918) were fought largely within its territory, and the Ardennes saw major fighting in 1940 and in the Battle of the Bulge's western sector in 1944.

Economy

Agriculture and viticulture dominate: besides Champagne wine, the chalk plains produce cereals, sugar beet, and alfalfa on very large farms. Industry, once based on textiles (Troyes hosiery), metallurgy (the Ardennes and Haute-Marne), and glass, declined over the late 20th century. The region was consistently among France's least densely populated, with long-standing population decline in its rural south.

2016 merger

Under the territorial reform law of 2014, Champagne-Ardenne was combined with Alsace and Lorraine to create the Grand Est region on 1 January 2016, with its capital at Strasbourg. The former region's name survives in common usage and in the Champagne wine appellation.
