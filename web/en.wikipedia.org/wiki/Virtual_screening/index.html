Virtual screening


Workflow of a typical virtual screening campaign

Virtual screening (VS) is a computational technique used in drug discovery to search libraries of small molecules in order to identify those structures which are most likely to bind to a drug target, typically a protein receptor or enzyme. Virtual screening has been defined as the "automatically evaluating very large libraries of compounds" using computer programs.

Approaches

There are two broad categories of screening techniques: ligand-based and structure-based. Ligand-based methods use information from known active molecules, such as pharmacophore models or quantitative structure–activity relationships (QSAR). Structure-based methods rely on the three-dimensional structure of the target, usually obtained by X-ray crystallography or NMR spectroscopy, and use molecular docking to predict binding poses and scores.

Workflow

A typical campaign prepares a compound library by standardising structures and generating conformations, filters out undesirable compounds, docks the remainder into the binding site, and rescales or rescues top-ranked hits with more expensive methods such as molecular dynamics or free-energy calculations before experimental testing.

Applications

Virtual screening is widely used to prioritise compounds for synthesis and assay, to repurpose approved drugs, and to explore ultra-large make-on-demand chemical spaces containing billions of molecules.
