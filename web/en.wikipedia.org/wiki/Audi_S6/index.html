The Audi S6 is the high-performance variant of the Audi A6 range of executive cars, produced by the German manufacturer Audi. It was introduced in 1994 as the performance derivative of the C4-generation A6, succeeding the Audi S4 based on the Audi 100. The S6 is available as a sedan (saloon) and as an Avant estate car, and sits below the RS 6 in Audi's range.

C4 (1994–1997)

The original S6 was offered with a 2.2-litre turbocharged five-cylinder engine producing 230 PS, or a 4.2-litre V8 producing 290 PS (later 326 PS in the S6 Plus of 1996–1997, the first vehicle developed by quattro GmbH).

C5 (1999–2005)

The second-generation S6 used a 4.2-litre V8 producing 340 PS, paired with quattro all-wheel drive and either a six-speed manual or five-speed Tiptronic automatic gearbox.

C6 (2006–2011)

For the C6 generation, the S6 received a 5.2-litre naturally aspirated V10 engine, derived from the unit used by Lamborghini, producing 435 PS (320 kW). This made it one of the few production sedans of its era with a ten-cylinder engine.

C7 (2012–2018)

The C7 S6 replaced the V10 with a 4.0-litre twin-turbocharged V8 TFSI producing 420 PS, later raised to 450 PS. Cylinder deactivation and a seven-speed S tronic dual-clutch gearbox improved efficiency, and Audi quoted 0–100 km/h in 4.5 seconds.

C8 (2019–present)

The current S6 uses different powertrains by market: in Europe, a 3.0-litre V6 TDI diesel with electric compressor and 48-volt mild hybrid producing 349 PS and 700 N·m; in North America and other markets, a 2.9-litre twin-turbo V6 TFSI producing 450 PS. Both are paired with an eight-speed Tiptronic automatic and quattro all-wheel drive.
