Aimé Césaire

Aimé Fernand David Césaire (26 June 1913 – 17 April 2008) was a Martinican poet, author, and politician, and one of the founders of the négritude movement in Francophone literature. His long poem Cahier d'un retour au pays natal (1939) is a landmark of 20th-century poetry, and his Discourse on Colonialism (1950) a foundational anticolonial text.

Life and work

Césaire won a scholarship to Paris, where he studied at the École Normale Supérieure and, with Léopold Sédar Senghor and Léon-Gontran Damas, developed négritude — an affirmation of Black identity and culture against colonial assimilation — launching the journal L'Étudiant noir in 1935. Returning to Martinique, he taught (Frantz Fanon was among his students) and wrote plays including Une tempête, a postcolonial reworking of Shakespeare.

Political career

Elected mayor of Fort-de-France and deputy to the French National Assembly in 1945, Césaire co-authored the 1946 law departmentalising Martinique. He resigned from the French Communist Party in 1956, protesting its response to Soviet actions, and founded the Martinican Progressive Party. He remained mayor until 2001.
