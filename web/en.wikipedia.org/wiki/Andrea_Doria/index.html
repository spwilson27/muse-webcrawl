Andrea Doria (30 November 1466 – 25 November 1560) was a Genoese admiral, naval commander and statesman, the dominant maritime figure of the western Mediterranean in the first half of the 16th century. Born into the great Doria family of Genoa, orphaned young, he served as a condottiere on land before taking to the sea in his forties, building a private fleet of galleys that made him indispensable to the great powers of the Italian Wars.

Career

Doria first served Francis I of France, clearing Corsica and Ligurian waters of Barbary corsairs and Imperial forces. Dissatisfied with French treatment of Genoa, he made his celebrated switch of allegiance in 1528 to the Emperor Charles V, securing in return Genoa's independence under his own oligarchic constitution of 1528, which he designed. Thereafter he commanded Imperial fleets against the Ottomans and their ally Hayreddin Barbarossa: he took Coron and Patras in 1532, helped capture Tunis in 1535, and survived disasters such as Preveza (1538) and the Algiers expedition (1541). In 1547 he survived the Fieschi conspiracy, which killed his nephew Giannettino. Dying at 93, Doria left Genoa prosperous, independent and tied to Spain — a system that endured for centuries. He is famously portrayed by Bronzino as Neptune.

Legacy

Numerous warships have borne his name, including the Italian ocean liner Andrea Doria, whose sinking in 1956 after collision with the Stockholm became one of the most famous maritime disasters of the 20th century.
