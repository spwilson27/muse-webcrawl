The 17th Lancers (Duke of Cambridge's Own) was a cavalry regiment in the British Army, raised in 1759 and famous for its participation in the Charge of the Light Brigade during the Crimean War. In 1922 it was amalgamated with the 21st Lancers to form the 17th/21st Lancers, an ancestor of today's Royal Lancers.

Formation and early service

The regiment was formed in Ireland on 7 November 1759 by Colonel John Hale of the 47th Foot as the 18th Regiment of (Light) Dragoons, in response to the expansion of light cavalry during the Seven Years' War. Hale, who had carried the news of Quebec to London, chose as the badge a death's head with the motto "Death or Glory" in memory of General Wolfe. Disbanded and re-raised, the regiment was renumbered 17th in 1761 and served in Germany and Ireland before deploying to North America in 1775.

American War and Napoleonic era

In America the regiment fought at Bunker Hill's aftermath operations, White Plains, Brandywine, and Monmouth, and its detachment was the only British cavalry present in the southern campaigns. In 1795 a troop under Captain Stapleton Cotton was present in the West Indies. The regiment returned to South America in 1806–07 for the invasions of the River Plate, and later furnished detachments for the Peninsular War era garrisons.

Crimea and the Charge of the Light Brigade

At the Battle of Balaclava on 25 October 1854 the regiment — about 350 strong under Lieutenant-Colonel Douglas — charged with the Light Brigade down the North Valley against Russian guns. Captain William Morris led the regiment's squadrons in breaking through the Russian cavalry behind the guns. Losses were severe, but the 17th rallied and returned. It also fought at Alma and Inkerman and served through the siege of Sevastopol.

Later campaigns

The regiment served in the Indian Mutiny (Central India), the Zulu War (charging at Ulundi in 1879), and the Second Boer War. It spent the First World War largely in France and Flanders, including mounted action at Monchy-le-Preux in 1917. In 1922, as part of post-war reductions, it amalgamated with the 21st Lancers to form the 17th/21st Lancers.
