The 17th/21st Lancers was a cavalry regiment of the British Army, formed in India in 1922 by the amalgamation of the 17th Lancers (Duke of Cambridge's Own) and the 21st Lancers (Empress of India's). It inherited the battle honours and traditions of both, including the 17th's part in the Charge of the Light Brigade at Balaclava in 1854 and the 21st's charge at Omdurman in 1898, in which Winston Churchill took part.

History

The regiment served as horsed cavalry in India and Egypt before mechanising in the late 1930s. During the Second World War it fought as an armoured regiment in North Africa — including the Tunisian campaign and the crossing of the Fondouk Pass in 1943 — and in Italy, serving with the 6th Armoured Division. Post-war, it served in Greece, Palestine, Hong Kong, Germany and Northern Ireland, and deployed a squadron to the Falklands War in 1982 and the regiment to the Gulf War of 1991.

Amalgamation

Under the Options for Change defence review, the 17th/21st Lancers amalgamated with the 16th/5th The Queen's Royal Lancers in 1993 to form the Queen's Royal Lancers. Its regimental museum forms part of the Royal Lancers and Nottinghamshire Yeomanry Museum at Thoresby Park.
