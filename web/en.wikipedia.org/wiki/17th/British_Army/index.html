British Army

Soldiers of the British Army on ceremonial duty in London.

The British Army is the principal land warfare force of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland, a part of the British Armed Forces along with the Royal Navy and the Royal Air Force. As of the 2020s it comprises roughly 72,000–80,000 regular full-time personnel, supplemented by the Army Reserve, for a trained strength of around 100,000.

History

The English Army was founded in 1660 by Charles II, and the British Army proper dates from the Acts of Union of 1707, which united the English and Scottish armies. Unlike the other services it is not styled "Royal," because it historically owed its existence to Parliament rather than the Crown — a legacy of the English Civil War and the Bill of Rights 1689, which requires Parliament's consent for a standing army in peacetime. The Army fought in the major wars of the British Empire, the Napoleonic Wars, the Crimean War, the Boer War, and both World Wars, in which it expanded massively through conscription and volunteer enlistment.

Organisation

The professional head of the Army is the Chief of the General Staff. Field formations are organised into divisions and brigades, while the regiment — particularly the infantry and cavalry regiment — remains the focus of soldiers' loyalty and tradition rather than a deployable tactical unit. Major headquarters are at Army Headquarters in Andover, Hampshire. Recruits train at establishments such as the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst (officers) and the Army Foundation College, Harrogate.

Modern role

Since the end of the Cold War and the last conscription era (National Service ended in 1960), the all-volunteer Army has deployed on operations in Northern Ireland, the Gulf War, the Balkans, Sierra Leone, Iraq, and Afghanistan, and maintains garrisons and training areas in places including Cyprus, Gibraltar, the Falkland Islands, Brunei, and Canada. Restructuring programmes such as Army 2020 and the Future Soldier programme have reshaped its size and structure for expeditionary and armoured warfare.
