Allies of World War II

The Allies, formally the United Nations from 1942, were the coalition of nations that opposed the Axis powers during the Second World War. The principal members — the "Big Three" — were the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union and the United States, joined by China, France, Poland, Canada, Australia and many others.

Formation

Poland, Britain and France were at war with Germany from September 1939. The coalition widened after Germany invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941 (Operation Barbarossa) and Japan attacked Pearl Harbor in December 1941, bringing in the United States. On 1 January 1942, twenty-six nations signed the Declaration by United Nations, pledging to defeat the Axis and not to make a separate peace.

Cooperation and legacy

The Allies coordinated strategy through conferences at Tehran, Yalta and Potsdam, combined their resources through Lend-Lease, and fought across Europe, North Africa, the Atlantic and the Pacific. Victory came in Europe in May 1945 and in the Pacific in September 1945. The wartime alliance laid the foundation for the United Nations, founded in 1945, though it soon fractured into the Cold War.
