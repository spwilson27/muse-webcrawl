Queen's Royal Lancers

The Queen's Royal Lancers was a cavalry regiment of the British Army, formed on 25 June 1993 by the amalgamation of the 16th/5th The Queen's Royal Lancers and the 17th/21st Lancers. It served as an armoured reconnaissance regiment of the Royal Armoured Corps until 2 May 2015, when it amalgamated with the 9th/12th Royal Lancers to form the Royal Lancers.

History

The regiment inherited the traditions of four regiments of horse and lancers, among them the 17th Lancers of Balaclava fame, whose skull-and-crossbones "Death or Glory" badge the Queen's Royal Lancers wore. Based for much of its existence at Catterick Garrison, it operated the CVR(T) family of light armoured vehicles — Scimitar and Sabre — in the formation reconnaissance role, after earlier service with Challenger 2 squadrons attached to armoured regiments.

Operations

The regiment deployed squadrons and full tours to Bosnia and Kosovo in the 1990s, to Iraq on Operation Telic, and repeatedly to Afghanistan on Operation Herrick, providing reconnaissance and mentoring Afghan forces in Helmand. Its final years included training teams in Iraq and a last operational tour before the 2015 merger under the Army 2020 reforms.

Legacy

On amalgamation its cap badge, motto and lineage passed to the Royal Lancers (Queen Elizabeths' Own); the regimental museum collections are held at Thoresby Park, Nottinghamshire.
