Sir Winston Leonard Spencer Churchill (1874–1965) was a British statesman, soldier, and writer who twice served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, most famously leading Britain through the Second World War from 1940 to 1945.

Early life and career

Churchill began as a cavalry officer and war correspondent, serving in India, Sudan, and the Boer War, where his escape from captivity made him famous. Elected to Parliament in 1900, he held major offices including First Lord of the Admiralty, Minister of Munitions, and Chancellor of the Exchequer before spending the 1930s out of government, warning against Nazi rearmament.

Second World War and later years

Becoming prime minister in May 1940, Churchill's speeches and refusal to negotiate sustained British resistance. He worked closely with Roosevelt and Stalin in the Grand Alliance. Defeated in 1945, he returned to office from 1951 to 1955, received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1953, and died in 1965, receiving a state funeral.
