Cold War

The Cold War was a period of geopolitical rivalry between the United States and the Soviet Union and their respective allies, the Western and Eastern blocs, from the end of the Second World War in 1945–47 until the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991. It was "cold" because the superpowers never fought each other directly in a major war, though they supported opposing sides in proxy wars in Korea, Vietnam and Afghanistan.

Course

The wartime alliance of the Allies broke down over the settlement of Europe. The Truman Doctrine, the Marshall Plan, the Berlin Blockade and the formation of NATO (1949) and the Warsaw Pact (1955) fixed the division of the continent. Crises — the Korean War, the Cuban Missile Crisis of 1962, and the Able Archer scare of 1983 — brought the world close to nuclear war, while an arms race produced vast arsenals of nuclear weapons and delivery systems. The space race, espionage and ideological competition extended the struggle worldwide.

End

Mikhail Gorbachev's reforms of glasnost and perestroika, the fall of the Berlin Wall in November 1989, and the reunification of Germany preceded the dissolution of the USSR in December 1991, ending the Cold War and leaving the United States as the sole superpower.
