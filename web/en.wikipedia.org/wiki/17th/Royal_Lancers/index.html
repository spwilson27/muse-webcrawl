Royal Lancers

The Royal Lancers serve as an armoured cavalry (formation reconnaissance) regiment.

The Royal Lancers (Queen Elizabeths' Own) is a cavalry regiment of the British Army, formed on 2 May 2015 by the amalgamation of the 9th/12th Royal Lancers (Prince of Wales's) and the Queen's Royal Lancers. It is an armoured cavalry regiment within the Royal Armoured Corps, based at Catterick Garrison in North Yorkshire.

Formation and role

The amalgamation was carried out under the Army 2020 restructuring programme. The new regiment inherited the traditions, battle honours, and seniority of its antecedent regiments, whose lineages stretch back to regiments raised in 1715. Its title honours Queen Elizabeth II and Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother, both of whom were colonels-in-chief of antecedent regiments. The regiment's nickname, inherited from the Queen's Royal Lancers, is the "Death or Glory Boys," and its cap badge is the famous skull and crossed lances "motto" of the 17th Lancers: Death or Glory.

Organisation

As an armoured cavalry regiment, the Royal Lancers operate vehicles of the Ajax family (principally the Ares and Ajax reconnaissance variants, replacing the CVR(T) Scimitar), providing formation reconnaissance, surveillance, and screening for armoured brigade combat teams. The regiment consists of a regimental headquarters and three sabre squadrons, with personnel also serving on deployments and exercises worldwide.

Antecedents

Antecedent regiments include the 9th Queen's Royal Lancers, the 12th (Prince of Wales's) Royal Lancers, the 16th The Queen's Lancers, the 17th Lancers (famous for the Charge of the Light Brigade at Balaclava in 1854), and the 21st Lancers (famous for the charge at Omdurman in 1898), whose amalgamations across the twentieth century produced the two regiments merged in 2015.
