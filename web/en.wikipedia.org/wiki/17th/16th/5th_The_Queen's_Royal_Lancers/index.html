16th/5th The Queen's Royal Lancers

The 16th/5th The Queen's Royal Lancers was a cavalry regiment of the British Army formed in 1922 by the amalgamation of the 16th The Queen's Lancers and the 5th Royal Irish Lancers. It served as an armoured reconnaissance regiment for most of its existence before being amalgamated again in 1993 with the 17th/21st Lancers to form the Queen's Royal Lancers.

History

The regiment's predecessors carried long histories: the 16th Lancers were raised in 1759 and fought at Waterloo, while the 5th Royal Irish Lancers traced their lineage to 1689 and served in the Boer War before disbandment in 1921 and re-raising for the amalgamation. In the Second World War the 16th/5th fought in North Africa and Italy as an armoured car and tank regiment. During the Cold War it served repeatedly in West Germany as part of the British Army of the Rhine, and detachments served in Aden, Cyprus, Northern Ireland and the 1991 Gulf War.

Legacy

The regiment inherited the scarlet tunic traditions of the 16th and the motto Quis separabit of the 5th Royal Irish Lancers. Its traditions are carried on by the Royal Lancers, formed in 2015.
