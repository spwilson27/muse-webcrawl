Axis powers

The Axis powers were the nations that fought the Allies in the Second World War. The core members were Nazi Germany, Fascist Italy and Imperial Japan, bound by the Tripartite Pact of 27 September 1940, and joined at various times by Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, Slovakia and Croatia, with Finland and Thailand as co-belligerents or partners.

Origins

The term derives from the Rome–Berlin "Axis" proclaimed by Mussolini in 1936. Germany and Japan signed the Anti-Comintern Pact in 1936; Germany and Italy concluded the Pact of Steel in 1939. The Tripartite Pact formalised the alliance, envisaging a new order in Europe and Asia and mutual assistance if attacked by a power not then in the war.

Course of the war

The Axis reached its greatest territorial extent in 1942, controlling most of Europe and large parts of East and Southeast Asia and North Africa. Defeats at Stalingrad, El Alamein and Midway turned the tide. Italy left the alliance after the Allied invasion of Sicily and the fall of Mussolini in 1943; the remaining members were defeated in 1945.
