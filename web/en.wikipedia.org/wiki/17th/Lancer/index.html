Lancer

A lancer was a type of cavalryman armed principally with a lance. Lancer regiments, originating with Polish uhlans, spread through European armies during the Napoleonic Wars and remained in service into the early twentieth century.

History

Light cavalry armed with lances had long been used in Poland and Hungary. After the partitions of Poland, lancer units were raised in Prussian, Austrian, Russian, and French service. Napoleon formed Polish lancer regiments, and the lance proved effective against infantry and in pursuit. Britain converted several light dragoon regiments to lancers after 1816, and lancers famously charged at Balaclava (1854) and Omdurman (1898).

Equipment and decline

Lancers typically carried a 2.5–3 m lance with a steel head and pennon, plus a sabre and carbine or pistol. The lance's battlefield utility faded with modern firepower in the First World War, and most lancer regiments were mechanised in the interwar period, though many retained the title in armoured roles.
