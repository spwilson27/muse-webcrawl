Charge of the Light Brigade

The Charge of the Light Brigade was a failed British cavalry charge against Russian forces during the Battle of Balaclava on 25 October 1854, in the Crimean War. Led by Lord Cardigan, some 600 horsemen of the Light Brigade rode down a valley swept by artillery fire from three sides, owing to a misinterpreted order from the commander-in-chief, Lord Raglan.

The charge

Raglan intended the cavalry to prevent the Russians from removing captured British guns from the Causeway Heights. His written order was ambiguous, and from the valley floor Lord Lucan could see only the Russian battery at the valley's end. The brigade advanced over a mile under fire, reached and overwhelmed the battery, fought Russian cavalry behind it, and withdrew. Of roughly 670 men who charged, about 110 were killed and 160 wounded.

Legacy

Alfred, Lord Tennyson's poem "The Charge of the Light Brigade" (1854) fixed the event in public memory — "Theirs not to reason why, / Theirs but to do and die" — celebrating the soldiers' obedience while questioning the command failure. Participating regiments included the 17th Lancers, whose successor traditions connect with the Queen's Royal Lancers entry in this index.
