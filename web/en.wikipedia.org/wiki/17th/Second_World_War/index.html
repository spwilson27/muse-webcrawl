The Second World War, often abbreviated as WWII or WW2, was a global conflict that lasted from 1939 to 1945. It involved the vast majority of the world's countries — including all of the great powers — forming two opposing military alliances: the Allies and the Axis powers. In a state of total war, the major participants threw their entire economic, industrial, and scientific capabilities behind the war effort, blurring the distinction between civilian and military resources.

The war began in Europe on 1 September 1939 with the German invasion of Poland, after which the United Kingdom and France declared war on Germany. In Asia, the Second Sino-Japanese War had already been under way since 1937. The war in Europe ended with the German surrender in May 1945; the war in the Pacific continued until Japan surrendered on 2 September 1945, following the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the Soviet declaration of war against Japan.

Causes

The rise of fascism in Europe, the unresolved grievances of the Treaty of Versailles, the Great Depression, and the expansionist ambitions of Nazi Germany under Adolf Hitler, Fascist Italy under Benito Mussolini, and Imperial Japan are widely regarded as the principal causes. The policy of appeasement pursued by Britain and France in the 1930s failed to deter aggression.

Course of the war

Germany's early Blitzkrieg campaigns overran Poland, Denmark, Norway, the Low Countries, and France in 1939–40. The Battle of Britain prevented a German invasion of the British Isles. In June 1941 Germany invaded the Soviet Union in Operation Barbarossa, opening the vast Eastern Front. The Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor in December 1941 brought the United States into the war. Turning points included the battles of Stalingrad, El Alamein, and Midway in 1942–43. Allied forces invaded Normandy on D-Day, 6 June 1944, and advanced into Germany from both east and west; Berlin fell to the Red Army in May 1945.

Aftermath

The war reshaped the political alignment and social structure of the globe. The United Nations was established to prevent future conflicts, and the United States and the Soviet Union emerged as rival superpowers, setting the stage for the Cold War. The war's devastation and the revelations of the Holocaust — the genocide of six million Jews by Nazi Germany — left an enduring mark on world consciousness. Decolonisation accelerated across Asia and Africa in the decades that followed.
