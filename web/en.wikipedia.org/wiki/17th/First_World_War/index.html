World War I

World War I (the First World War, or the Great War) was a global conflict between the Allied Powers and the Central Powers, fought from 28 July 1914 to 11 November 1918. It mobilised more than 70 million military personnel and caused an estimated 15–22 million deaths, making it one of the deadliest conflicts in history.

Outbreak and course

The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 triggered a cascade of alliance commitments and ultimatums. Germany invaded Belgium and France, while Russia attacked in the east. On the Western Front, the war settled into trench stalemate punctuated by vast battles — Verdun and the Somme in 1916, Passchendaele in 1917 — while the war at sea featured the Battle of Jutland and unrestricted submarine warfare, which helped bring the United States into the war in 1917. Russia left the war after the 1917 revolutions. The Allied Hundred Days Offensive in 1918 broke the German army, and an armistice took effect on 11 November 1918.

Consequences

The Treaty of Versailles (1919) imposed reparations and territorial losses on Germany. Four empires — German, Austro-Hungarian, Ottoman and Russian — collapsed, the map of Europe and the Middle East was redrawn, and the League of Nations was created. The war's unresolved grievances contributed to the rise of fascism and the Second World War.
