17th Lancers

The 17th Lancers (Duke of Cambridge's Own) was a cavalry regiment of the British Army, raised in 1759. It is forever associated with the Charge of the Light Brigade at Balaclava in 1854, in which it rode as part of the doomed brigade immortalised by Tennyson. In 1922 the regiment was amalgamated with the 21st Lancers to form the 17th/21st Lancers.

History

Raised by Colonel John Hale as the 18th Regiment of (Light) Dragoons and renumbered the 17th in 1763, the regiment served in the American War of Independence, including the southern campaigns. Hale had brought home the news of General Wolfe's death at Quebec, and the regiment adopted a skull-and-crossbones badge with the motto "Death or Glory" (Or Glory), after the manner of Wolfe's memory.

Converted to lancers in 1822–23, the regiment fought in the Crimean War. At Balaclava on 25 October 1854, under Lieutenant-Colonel William Morris, it charged the Russian guns as the second line of the Light Brigade, suffering severe casualties. It later served in India during the Mutiny's aftermath, in the Zulu War — where its lancers were decisive in the final charge at Ulundi (1879) — and in the Boer War. In the First World War it served on the Western Front, largely dismounted, and in 1919 took part in operations in Ireland and India before amalgamation.

Legacy

The skull-and-crossbones badge and "Death or Glory" motto passed through the 17th/21st Lancers to today's Royal Lancers, formed in 2015.
