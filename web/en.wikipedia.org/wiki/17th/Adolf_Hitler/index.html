Adolf Hitler

Adolf Hitler (20 April 1889 – 30 April 1945) was an Austrian-born German politician who became dictator of Germany from 1933 until his death. As leader of the Nazi Party, he was appointed Chancellor in January 1933, combined the offices of president and chancellor as Führer in 1934, and ruled a totalitarian regime responsible for the Second World War and the Holocaust.

Rise to power

Born in Braunau am Inn, Hitler moved to Vienna as a young man and served as a dispatch runner in the German Army during the First World War. He joined the German Workers' Party, precursor of the Nazi Party, in 1919, and assumed its leadership in 1921. After the failed Beer Hall Putsch of 1923 he was imprisoned, during which he dictated Mein Kampf. Amid the Great Depression, the Nazis became the largest party in the Reichstag, and President Hindenburg appointed Hitler Chancellor on 30 January 1933. Following the Reichstag Fire, civil liberties were suspended, and the Enabling Act gave his cabinet legislative power.

War and the Holocaust

Hitler's expansionist foreign policy led to the invasion of Poland on 1 September 1939, beginning the Second World War in Europe (see also this index's war entries). Nazi Germany and its Axis partners occupied most of Europe. Under Hitler's direction the regime murdered six million Jews in the Holocaust, along with millions of other victims including Roma, Poles, Soviet prisoners of war and people with disabilities. With the Red Army closing on Berlin, Hitler killed himself in the Führerbunker on 30 April 1945; Germany surrendered days later.
