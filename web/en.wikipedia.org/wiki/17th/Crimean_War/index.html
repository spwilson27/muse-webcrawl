Crimean War

The Crimean War was fought from October 1853 to February 1856 between the Russian Empire and an alliance of the Ottoman Empire, France, the United Kingdom and Sardinia. It arose from disputes over the rights of Christian minorities in the Holy Land and, more fundamentally, from the great powers' competition over the declining Ottoman Empire — the "Eastern Question".

Course

After Russia occupied the Danubian principalities and destroyed the Ottoman fleet at Sinop, Britain and France declared war in March 1854. The allies landed in Crimea in September 1854, won the battles of Alma, Balaclava and Inkerman, and settled into the eleven-month Siege of Sevastopol. Disease killed far more soldiers than combat: of roughly 650,000–900,000 total deaths, the majority were from cholera, dysentery and exposure.

Legacy

The Treaty of Paris (1856) neutralised the Black Sea and checked Russian expansion for a generation. The war is remembered for the Charge of the Light Brigade, Florence Nightingale's nursing reforms, the first war reporting by telegraph and photography, and military lessons in logistics, rifled firearms and trench warfare that foreshadowed later industrial wars.
