Battle of Balaclava

The Battle of Balaclava, fought on 25 October 1854 during the Crimean War, was an engagement between the allied British, French and Ottoman forces besieging Sevastopol and the Imperial Russian Army. It is famous for three actions: the stand of the 93rd Highlanders — the "Thin Red Line" — the successful Charge of the Heavy Brigade, and the disastrous Charge of the Light Brigade.

Course of the battle

Russian forces under General Pavel Liprandi advanced on the port of Balaclava, the British supply base, and captured redoubts along the Causeway Heights. Sir Colin Campbell's Highlanders repelled Russian cavalry in a two-deep line, while Brigadier-General Scarlett's Heavy Brigade routed a much larger Russian cavalry force uphill. Ordered by Lord Raglan to prevent the Russians carrying away captured guns — an order misunderstood by Lord Lucan and Lord Cardigan — the Light Brigade charged the wrong valley, against a prepared artillery battery, suffering heavy casualties in about twenty minutes.

Legacy

The battle was tactically inconclusive, but the Light Brigade's charge became a byword for both gallantry and military blunder, immortalised by Alfred, Lord Tennyson's 1854 poem and Florence Nightingale's subsequent nursing reforms after reports of the army's suffering.
