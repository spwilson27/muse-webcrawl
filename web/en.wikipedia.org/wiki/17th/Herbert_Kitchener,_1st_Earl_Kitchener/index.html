Herbert Kitchener, 1st Earl Kitchener (1850–1916) was a British field marshal and colonial administrator who won fame for his campaigns in Sudan and South Africa and served as Secretary of State for War at the start of the First World War.

Imperial career

As Sirdar of the Egyptian Army, Kitchener reconquered Sudan, defeating the Mahdist forces at the Battle of Omdurman in 1898. He then served as chief of staff and commander-in-chief in the Second Boer War, where his scorched-earth policy and concentration camps drew lasting controversy. He later reorganised the British Indian Army as its commander-in-chief and served as consul-general in Egypt.

First World War

Appointed Secretary of State for War in August 1914, Kitchener foresaw a long war and raised the mass volunteer "New Armies" — the famous "Your country needs you" recruiting image made him their emblem. He died on 5 June 1916 when HMS Hampshire struck a mine off Orkney while he was travelling to Russia.
