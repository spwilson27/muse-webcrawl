The Mahdist War (1881–1899) was fought between the Mahdist Sudanese state and Egyptian, then Anglo-Egyptian, forces. It began with the revolt of Muhammad Ahmad, who proclaimed himself the Mahdi, against Turco-Egyptian rule in Sudan.

Course of the war

Mahdist forces annihilated several Egyptian expeditions, captured Khartoum in January 1885 — in which General Charles Gordon was killed — and established a theocratic state ruled after the Mahdi's death by Khalifa Abdallahi. Britain, initially reluctant, authorised reconquest in 1896. Herbert Kitchener's Anglo-Egyptian army advanced along the Nile, defeating the Mahdists at Atbara and decisively at Omdurman on 2 September 1898.

Consequences

The war ended with the establishment of the Anglo-Egyptian condominium over Sudan in 1899. The conflict caused enormous loss of life in Sudan through battle, famine, and disease, and featured in British imperial culture through accounts such as Winston Churchill's The River War.
