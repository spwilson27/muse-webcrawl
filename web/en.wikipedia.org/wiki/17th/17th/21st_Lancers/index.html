21st Lancers

The 21st Lancers charging at the Battle of Omdurman, 2 September 1898.

The 21st Lancers (Empress of India's) was a cavalry regiment of the British Army, raised in 1858 and amalgamated with the 17th Lancers in 1922 to form the 17th/21st Lancers. The regiment is best remembered for its charge at the Battle of Omdurman in 1898, in which the young Winston Churchill took part.

History

The regiment was originally raised in Bengal in 1858 by Colonel John Jacob as the 3rd Regiment of Scinde Horse for service with the East India Company; after the Indian Mutiny it passed to the British Army as the 21st Regiment of Hussars. For much of its existence it was the only British cavalry regiment without overseas battle experience, a circumstance that led to the barrack-room joke that its motto was "Thou shalt not kill."

Battle of Omdurman

Serving in Kitchener's army during the reconquest of the Sudan, the regiment charged a concealed force of Dervish warriors at Omdurman on 2 September 1898, believing it faced a small body of infantry; instead some 2,500 men rose from a depression in the ground. The lancers cut their way through at a cost of over 70 men and more than 100 horses killed and wounded in a matter of minutes. Three Victoria Crosses were awarded for the action. In 1899 the regiment was converted to lancers and granted the title "Empress of India's" by Queen Victoria.

First World War and amalgamation

During the First World War the regiment served on the North-West Frontier of India and did not deploy to the Western Front as a formed unit, though detachments and drafts served elsewhere. In 1922, as part of post-war reductions, it was amalgamated with the 17th Lancers (Duke of Cambridge's Own) to form the 17th/21st Lancers.
