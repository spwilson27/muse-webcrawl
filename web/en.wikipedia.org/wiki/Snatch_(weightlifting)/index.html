Snatch (weightlifting)

Snatch
EventOlympic weightlifting
TypeCompound barbell lift
StatusOne of two lifts contested in the sport (with the clean and jerk)

The snatch is one of the two lifts contested in the sport of Olympic weightlifting, the other being the clean and jerk. In the snatch, the athlete lifts the barbell from the platform to arms' length overhead in one continuous, explosive movement, using a wide grip, and receives the bar in a squat position before standing upright with the weight under control.

Technique

The lift is usually broken into phases for coaching purposes:

  • First pull — the bar is lifted from the floor to about knee height, keeping the back flat and the bar close to the shins.
  • Transition (scoop) — the knees shift forward under the bar as the torso becomes more upright.
  • Second pull — the most powerful phase: the hips, knees and ankles extend violently (the "triple extension"), the shoulders shrug and the bar is driven upward.
  • Turnover and catch — the lifter pulls under the bar, rotating the elbows and punching the arms straight, receiving the bar overhead in a deep squat.
  • Recovery — the lifter stands up from the squat with the bar stable overhead and waits for the referee's down signal.

Competition

In competition, each lifter has three attempts in the snatch and three in the clean and jerk; the best successful lift in each is added to form the total. A lift is judged good by at least two of three referees. The arms must not bend once the bar is overhead, and the lifter must remain motionless with the bar under control before receiving the signal to lower it. The snatch is generally considered the more technical of the two lifts, and snatch results are typically about 80–85% of clean and jerk results for the same lifter.

Variations

Training variants include the power snatch (caught above parallel), the hang snatch (starting from above the knees), snatch pulls and snatch balances. Historically, a split style of receiving the bar was also used, and a one-handed snatch was contested in early Olympic competition before the modern two-handed lifts were standardised.
