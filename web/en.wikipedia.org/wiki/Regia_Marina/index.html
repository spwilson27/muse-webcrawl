Regia Marina

The Regia Marina (Italian for "Royal Navy") was the navy of the Kingdom of Italy (Regno d'Italia), existing from 17 March 1861, when the kingdom was proclaimed, until 18 June 1946, when the Italian Republic was proclaimed following the referendum that abolished the monarchy. After 1946 the fleet was reconstituted as the Marina Militare.

History

The Regia Marina was formed from the navies of the pre-unification states, principally those of Sardinia-Piedmont and Naples. It fought in the Third Italian War of Independence, suffering a notable defeat at the Battle of Lissa in 1866 against the Austrian fleet. In the late nineteenth century Italy built powerful battleships such as the Duilio and Italia classes, pioneering large-calibre guns and all-steel construction.

During the First World War the Regia Marina operated in the Adriatic Sea against the Austro-Hungarian Navy, relying heavily on light craft and MAS motor torpedo boats, one of which sank the battleship SMS Szent István in 1918. In the interwar period the navy expanded under Fascism and fought the British Royal Navy in the Battle of the Mediterranean during the Second World War, including the battles of Calabria, Cape Matapan and the operations against Malta convoys. Crippled by fuel shortages and the loss of much of its fleet after the Armistice of Cassibile in September 1943, the remaining ships largely operated alongside the Allies as part of the Italian Co-belligerent forces.
