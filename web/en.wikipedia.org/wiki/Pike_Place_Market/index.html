Pike Place Market

Pike Place Market is a public market in Seattle, Washington, United States, overlooking Elliott Bay. Opened on 17 August 1907, it is one of the oldest continuously operated public farmers' markets in the United States and one of Seattle's most popular tourist destinations, drawing around 10 million visitors a year.

History

The market was founded so that farmers could sell directly to consumers, cutting out middlemen amid anger over rising produce prices. Threatened with demolition for urban renewal in the 1960s, it was saved by a citizen initiative in 1971 that created the Pike Place Market Historical District and the Pike Place Market Preservation and Development Authority.

Features

The market spans several levels of arcades, shops, restaurants, and stalls for farmers, craftspeople, and fishmongers — famous for the fish-throwing staff of the Pike Place Fish Market. The original Starbucks store, opened in 1971, is located at 1912 Pike Place. Other landmarks include Rachel the Piggy Bank, the bronze mascot pig, and the Gum Wall in Post Alley.
