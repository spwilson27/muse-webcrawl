The 1976 FA Cup Final was played on 1 May 1976 at Wembley Stadium between Manchester United and Second Division Southampton. In one of the greatest shocks in FA Cup history, Southampton won 1–0 through a late goal by Bobby Stokes, securing the only major trophy in the club's history.

Route to the final

Tommy Docherty's young Manchester United side, newly promoted back to the First Division, reached Wembley with an exciting, attacking team featuring Steve Coppell, Gordon Hill and Stuart Pearson. Lawrie McMenemy's Southampton, a division below, had beaten Aston Villa, Blackpool, West Bromwich Albion, Bradford City and Crystal Palace — the latter in a semi-final at Stamford Bridge — to reach their first final since the competition's early years.

Match summary

United dominated long stretches of the match but found Southampton goalkeeper Ian Turner in inspired form, and centre-halves Mel Blyth and Jim Steele immovable. Alex Forsyth struck the woodwork for United, and Lou Macari had a goal disallowed. With seven minutes remaining, Jim McCalliog slipped a pass through to Bobby Stokes, who finished low past Alex Stepney. United and many observers suspected offside, but replays suggested Stokes was played onside by a defender; the goal stood, and Southampton held on for a famous victory.

Aftermath

The winning Southampton team — including Peter Osgood, Mick Channon and captain Peter Rodrigues — became club legends, and McMenemy's side was promoted to the First Division two years later. Docherty's United returned to Wembley the following year and beat Liverpool 2–1 in the 1977 final.
