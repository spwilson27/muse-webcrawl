West Yorkshire is a ceremonial and metropolitan county in the Yorkshire and the Humber region of England. It comprises five metropolitan boroughs — the cities of Leeds, Bradford and Wakefield, and the boroughs of Kirklees (Huddersfield) and Calderdale (Halifax) — and corresponds broadly to the urbanised core of the historic West Riding of Yorkshire. With about 2.4 million people it is one of England's most populous counties, dominated by the Leeds city region.

History

The area industrialised rapidly in the 18th and 19th centuries on the strength of the woollen textile industry, coal mining and engineering: Leeds became a centre of ready-made clothing and finance, Bradford the world capital of wool trading ("Wool City"), Halifax known for carpets and confectionery, and Huddersfield for fine worsteds. The modern county was created in 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972; its county council was abolished in 1986, and since 2014 the districts have cooperated through the West Yorkshire Combined Authority, which gained an elected mayor in 2021.

Geography and culture

The west of the county rises into the Pennine moorlands around Hebden Bridge and Haworth — Brontë country — while the east flattens toward the Vale of York. Cultural landmarks include the Royal Armouries and Leeds Playhouse, the National Science and Media Museum in Bradford (also a UNESCO City of Film), the Hepworth Wakefield, Yorkshire Sculpture Park, and Saltaire, a UNESCO-listed mill village. Universities include Leeds, Leeds Beckett, Bradford, Huddersfield and the University of Leeds's rivals in sport, Leeds Rhinos (rugby league) and Leeds United (football) have large followings.
