Arsenic contamination of groundwater

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A tube well in Bangladesh marked red, indicating arsenic contamination

Arsenic contamination of groundwater is a form of groundwater pollution which is often due to naturally occurring high concentrations of arsenic in deeper levels of groundwater. It is a high-profile problem due to the use of deep tube wells for water supply in the Ganges Delta, causing serious arsenic poisoning to large numbers of people.

A 2007 study found that over 137 million people in more than 70 countries are probably affected by arsenic poisoning from drinking water. The problem became most serious in Bangladesh, where it has been described as the largest mass poisoning of a population in history.

Chemistry

Arsenic contamination of groundwater is widespread, and in areas where groundwater is alkaline and poorly flushed, arsenic may be mobilized by reductive dissolution of arsenic-bearing iron oxides in aquifer sediments. The arsenic is transported primarily in its trivalent form, arsenite, which is more toxic and more mobile than arsenate.

Health effects

Chronic exposure to arsenic in drinking water causes skin lesions, including hyperpigmentation and keratosis, as well as cancers of the skin, bladder, and lung, and cardiovascular and neurological effects. The World Health Organization guideline value for arsenic in drinking water is 10 micrograms per litre.

Affected regions

Regions with significant natural arsenic contamination include Bangladesh and West Bengal (India), Vietnam, Argentina, Chile, Mexico, China, and parts of the United States, notably the Southwest and New England.
