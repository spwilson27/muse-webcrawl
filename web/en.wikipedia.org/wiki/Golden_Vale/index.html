Golden Vale

The Golden Vale (Irish: Machaire na Mumhan) is a fertile agricultural region in the province of Munster in southwestern Ireland, stretching across parts of Counties Limerick, Tipperary, and Cork. It is famous for its deep, rich limestone soils and lush grassland, which support some of Ireland's most productive dairy and beef farming.

Geography

The vale lies between the Galtee Mountains to the south and the hills of north Tipperary and east Limerick, watered by tributaries of the River Suir and the River Maigue. Towns within or on its edge include Tipperary, Cashel, Cahir, Mitchelstown, and Charleville.

Agriculture and industry

The Golden Vale has long been described as the finest dairy land in Ireland. It gave its name to Golden Vale, a major Irish dairy co-operative group founded in the twentieth century, whose Charleville operations became one of the largest cheese and butter producers in the country; the business was later absorbed into the Kerry Group. Horse breeding also flourishes in the region, and the area around Cashel is noted for the Rock of Cashel, one of Ireland's most famous historic sites.
