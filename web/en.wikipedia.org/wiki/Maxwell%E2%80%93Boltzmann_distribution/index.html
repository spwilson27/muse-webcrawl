Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution
TypeProbability distribution
FieldStatistical mechanics

In physics (particularly statistical mechanics), the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution is a particular probability distribution named after James Clerk Maxwell and Ludwig Boltzmann. It describes the distribution of speeds of particles in an ideal gas at thermal equilibrium.

Definition

The distribution gives the fraction of molecules with speeds between v and v + dv as a function of temperature T and particle mass m. It vanishes at zero speed, rises to a peak at the most probable speed √(2kT/m), and falls off with a long tail; the mean speed is √(8kT/πm) and the root-mean-square speed √(3kT/m).

Derivation and assumptions

Maxwell derived the distribution in 1860 by assuming molecular velocity components are independent and isotropic; Boltzmann later placed it within a general kinetic theory and equilibrium statistical mechanics. It applies to classical particles where quantum degeneracy is negligible; Fermi–Dirac and Bose–Einstein statistics supersede it for dense or very cold systems.

Applications

The distribution underlies the kinetic theory of gases, explaining diffusion, effusion, thermal conductivity, and the rates of chemical reactions through the fraction of collisions exceeding an activation energy.
