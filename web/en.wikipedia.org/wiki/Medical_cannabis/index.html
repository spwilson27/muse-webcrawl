Medical cannabis

Medical cannabis, also called medical marijuana, refers to the use of the cannabis plant and its cannabinoids to treat disease or alleviate symptoms. It is distinct from recreational cannabis use, though the same plant material is involved.

Active compounds

The two best-studied cannabinoids are tetrahydrocannabinol (THC), the main psychoactive component, and cannabidiol (CBD), which is non-intoxicating. Both interact with the body's endocannabinoid system, a network of receptors involved in pain, appetite, nausea, mood, and immune function.

Medical uses

The strongest evidence supports cannabis-based medicines for certain conditions:

  • Chronic pain: modest benefit in neuropathic and chronic pain, though evidence quality varies.
  • Multiple sclerosis spasticity: nabiximols (Sativex), an oromucosal spray, is approved in many countries.
  • Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting: synthetic cannabinoids such as dronabinol and nabilone are approved for this use.
  • Epilepsy: cannabidiol (Epidiolex) is approved for Dravet syndrome, Lennox–Gastaut syndrome, and tuberous sclerosis complex.

Evidence is weaker or inconclusive for many other claimed uses, including anxiety, sleep disorders, and inflammatory conditions, and research continues.

Administration

Medical cannabis may be taken orally (capsules, oils, edibles), inhaled (smoked or vaporized), or applied as tinctures or sprays. Dosing is difficult to standardize because cannabinoid concentrations vary between products, and inhaled doses are hard to measure precisely.

Adverse effects

Common short-term effects include dizziness, drowsiness, dry mouth, impaired memory and coordination, and increased heart rate. Concerns with long-term use include dependence, worsening of psychosis in vulnerable individuals, and respiratory effects from smoking.

Legal status

Legal status varies widely by country and, in federal systems, by state or province. Some jurisdictions permit medical use with a prescription or recommendation, others permit only specific pharmaceutical products, and others prohibit all cannabis use. This patchwork continues to evolve.
