French presidential elections under the Fifth Republic

Presidential elections in France under the Fifth Republic determine the President of the French Republic, the head of state. Since a constitutional amendment approved by referendum in 1962, the president has been elected by direct universal suffrage using a two-round system: if no candidate wins an absolute majority in the first round, the two leading candidates contest a run-off two weeks later.

Rules

Candidates must be French citizens aged at least 18 and obtain the sponsorship (parrainage) of 500 elected officials from at least 30 departments. The presidential term was originally seven years (the septennat); a referendum in 2000 reduced it to five years (the quinquennat) from the 2002 election onward. A president may serve a maximum of two consecutive terms since the constitutional revision of 2008. Campaign finance is regulated, official campaign broadcasts are allocated by the broadcasting regulator, and results are proclaimed by the Constitutional Council.

The 2002 election was notable for the shock elimination of the Socialist candidate Lionel Jospin in the first round, which sent the far-right candidate Jean-Marie Le Pen into the run-off against Jacques Chirac, who was re-elected with over 82 percent of the vote. The 2017 election saw Emmanuel Macron, a first-time candidate leading a new centrist movement, defeat Marine Le Pen; he repeated the victory in 2022.
