Almaz-Antey (Russian: Концерн ВКО «Алмаз-Антей») is a Russian state-owned defence conglomerate specialising in air and missile defence systems. Formed in 2002 by the merger of the Almaz and Antey concerns, it unites dozens of enterprises that design and manufacture radar systems, command-and-control equipment, and surface-to-air missile systems, including the S-300, S-400 Triumf, and S-500 Prometey families.

The company consistently ranks among the world's largest defence contractors by revenue and is the principal supplier of long-range air defence to the Russian armed forces, as well as an exporter to customers including China, India, and Turkey.

History

Almaz-Antey was created by a Russian presidential decree in April 2002, consolidating some 46 enterprises, design bureaus, and research institutes in the air-defence sector. The merger joined NPO Almaz, known for the S-300P series defending Moscow and other fixed sites, with NPO Antey, developer of the S-300V family for ground forces. In 2015 the concern's formal name was expanded to reflect its aerospace-defence (VKO) mission.

Subsidiaries include the MKB Fakel and NPO Novator design bureaus, developers respectively of the 9M96 and 9M82/9M83 missile families, and manufacturing plants in Moscow, St. Petersburg, Yekaterinburg, and Nizhny Novgorod.

Products

  • S-300 family of long-range surface-to-air missile systems (PMU/VM variants).
  • S-400 Triumf — long-range system entering Russian service from 2007, exported to China, Turkey, and India.
  • S-500 Prometey — next-generation system intended for ballistic and hypersonic missile defence.
  • Buk and Tor medium- and short-range systems produced by member plants.
  • Nebo and other radar families, plus civilian air-traffic-control and meteorological radar products.

Sanctions

Almaz-Antey was placed under sanctions by the United States, the European Union, and other jurisdictions following Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014, with measures expanded after the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. In 2015 a Dutch-led investigation report on the downing of Malaysia Airlines Flight 17 identified the Buk system — manufactured by an Almaz-Antey subsidiary — as the weapon involved; the company publicly disputed the investigation's conclusions about the launch location and missile variant, presenting its own experiments in rebuttal.
