International trade law

International trade law is the body of rules governing cross-border trade in goods and services, including multilateral treaties, regional agreements, and the law of the World Trade Organization (WTO).

Framework

The modern system stems from the GATT (1947), absorbed into the WTO in 1995 alongside GATS (services) and TRIPS (intellectual property). Core principles include most-favoured-nation treatment, national treatment, tariff bindings and prohibitions on quantitative restrictions, with exceptions for health, security and regional integration.

Dispute settlement and issues

WTO panels and the (currently impaired) Appellate Body adjudicate disputes. Contemporary debates cover subsidies, digital trade, industrial policy, supply-chain security and trade-and-climate measures such as carbon border adjustments.
