Experience and Education

Experience and Education is a short book by the American philosopher and educational reformer John Dewey, published in 1938. Drawing on the Kappa Delta Pi lecture series, Dewey set out to clarify his philosophy of education in response to both traditional schooling and what he saw as the excesses and confusions of some self-described progressive schools.

Argument

Dewey argued that education must be based on a sound theory of experience. Not all experiences are genuinely educative: an experience is educative when it satisfies the criteria of continuity — it connects with past and future experiences so as to promote growth — and interaction — it arises from the transaction between the learner's internal conditions and objective, environmental conditions. Experiences that are disconnected, routine, or that arrest further growth are "mis-educative."

Rather than opposing traditional education (organised subject matter, discipline) with a romantic child-centredness, Dewey held that the mature knowledge of the teacher and the organised curriculum remain essential, but must be brought into connection with the learner's present experience. The book criticised progressive classrooms that treated freedom as an end in itself and lacked a planful organisation of subject matter.

Influence

The book is regarded as Dewey's most concise and mature statement of his educational philosophy, and remains a standard text in teacher education and in the theory of experiential learning.
