Quadriplegia
Also calledTetraplegia
DefinitionParalysis affecting all four limbs and the torso
Common causesCervical spinal cord injury, disease, congenital conditions

Quadriplegia, also known as tetraplegia, is the paralysis of all four limbs and the torso, usually resulting from injury or disease affecting the cervical (neck) region of the spinal cord. Depending on the level and completeness of the injury, it may also affect breathing, bladder and bowel function, and autonomic regulation.

Causes

The most common cause is traumatic spinal cord injury from road accidents, falls, sports injuries or violence. Other causes include spinal tumours, infections, multiple sclerosis, cerebral palsy and stroke.

Management

There is no cure for the paralysis itself; care focuses on rehabilitation, prevention of complications such as pressure injuries and infections, assistive technology (including powered wheelchairs and environmental controls), and support for independence. The degree of function retained depends strongly on the neurological level of injury.
