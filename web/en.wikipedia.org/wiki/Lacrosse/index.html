Lacrosse is a contact team sport played with a lacrosse stick ("crosse") and a hard rubber ball. Players use the head of the stick to carry, pass, catch, and shoot the ball into the opposing team's goal. The sport originates with Indigenous peoples of North America — the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) called it the "Creator's game" and played it for ritual, healing, and inter-village contests involving hundreds of players over days.

History

French Jesuit missionaries documented the game in the 1630s; the name derives from the French la crosse ("the crozier"), for the stick's shape. In 1867 Montreal dentist William George Beers codified the first rules and founded the Canadian Lacrosse Association; lacrosse became Canada's national summer sport. The modern game spread through the eastern United States, especially in the Mid-Atlantic and New England college ranks.

Varieties

  • Field lacrosse — ten-a-side outdoor game (men's) and a related women's game with limited contact and shallower stick pockets.
  • Box lacrosse — six-a-side indoor version played in hockey rinks, dominant in Canada, with a 30-second shot clock.
  • Sixes — a fast six-a-side outdoor variant developed by World Lacrosse, chosen for the sport's return to the Olympic Games at Los Angeles 2028.

The Haudenosaunee Nationals

The Iroquois (Haudenosaunee) Nationals compete in world championships as representatives of the Haudenosaunee Confederacy, travelling on Haudenosaunee passports — the only Indigenous national team in any sport. Their eligibility struggles before recent World Games drew international attention, and they are perennial medal contenders alongside Canada and the United States.
