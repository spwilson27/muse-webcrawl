Filofax

Founded1921 (as Norman & Hill)
HeadquartersUnited Kingdom
ProductsRing-bound personal organisers, diary refills

Filofax is a British company known for its range of personal organisers: ring-bound notebooks, typically in leather, that hold loose-leaf diary pages, address sheets, maps, and other inserts. The name is a contraction of "file of facts".

History

The system originated with the London firm Norman & Hill, which from 1921 sold an American loose-leaf organiser system in Britain; the Filofax name was registered in 1930. The organiser — based on six rings holding standard-size leaves that users replace each year — spread slowly through professional users such as the military and clergy before becoming a mass phenomenon.

Cultural symbol

In the 1980s the Filofax became an emblem of the young urban professional, particularly in London's financial district, where a bulging leather organiser served as both tool and status symbol. Sales declined with the rise of PDAs and smartphones in the 2000s, though the company continues to sell paper organisers, refills, and stationery, and the format has enjoyed a revival among planner hobbyists.
