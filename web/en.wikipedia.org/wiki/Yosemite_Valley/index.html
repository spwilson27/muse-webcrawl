Yosemite Valley

Location: Yosemite National Park, California, United States
Length: ~7.5 miles (12 km)
Floor elevation: ~4,000 ft (1,220 m)
Named for: Miwok word for the Ahwahnechee people

Yosemite Valley is a glacial valley in Yosemite National Park in the western Sierra Nevada of California. Carved by the Merced River and deepened by glaciers, the valley is about 7.5 miles (12 km) long and up to 3,500 feet (1,070 m) deep, ringed by granite summits including El Capitan, Half Dome, Sentinel Rock, and the Cathedral Rocks, and by waterfalls such as Yosemite Falls and Bridalveil Fall. It is the centrepiece of the national park and receives the great majority of its roughly four million annual visitors.

Geology

The valley began as a river canyon in the Sierra Nevada batholith, a mass of Cretaceous granite. Successive Ice Age glaciers, fed from the Tuolumne and Merced watersheds, quarried and widened the canyon into its present U-shape, leaving hanging valleys whose streams plunge as waterfalls. When the last glacier retreated about 15,000 years ago, its terminal moraine dammed Lake Yosemite, whose sediments produced the flat meadow floor of today.

History

The Ahwahnechee band of Miwok people inhabited the valley for generations before the Mariposa Battalion entered in 1851 during the Mariposa War and forcibly removed them. Reports and photographs by early visitors inspired the Yosemite Grant of 1864, signed by Abraham Lincoln, which ceded the valley to California for public use — a landmark in the conservation movement and a precursor of the national park system. John Muir's advocacy led to the surrounding national park in 1890; the valley itself rejoined federal control in 1906.

Landmarks and recreation

The classic "Tunnel View" panorama frames El Capitan, Bridalveil Fall, and Half Dome. El Capitan's 3,000-foot granite face is a mecca for big-wall rock climbing, including the first free ascent of the Dawn Wall in 2015 and Alex Honnold's free solo of Freerider in 2017. Seasonal phenomena include the February "firefall" effect at Horsetail Fall, when the setting sun briefly turns the waterfall orange.
