Chen Youliang (1320 – 3 October 1363) was a Chinese rebel leader who founded the short-lived Chen Han dynasty and was the principal rival of Zhu Yuanzhang, founder of the Ming dynasty, in the struggle to succeed the Yuan dynasty.

Rise

Of a fishing family from Hubei, Chen rose as an officer under Ni Wenjun in the Red Turban rebellion, then seized power himself, murdering his superiors. In 1360 he killed the rebel emperor Xu Shouhui and proclaimed himself emperor of Han, controlling the rich middle Yangtze region from his capital at Jiangzhou (Jiujiang) — for a time the strongest of the rebel regimes.

War with Zhu Yuanzhang

Chen's huge fleets repeatedly attacked Zhu's base at Nanjing but were repelled, notably in 1360–61. The climax came at the Battle of Lake Poyang in 1363, among the largest naval battles in history: Zhu's smaller fleet used fire ships to trap and burn Chen's towering war junks in the lake. Chen Youliang was killed, reportedly by an arrow, while trying to break out. His son Chen Li surrendered the following year, clearing Zhu Yuanzhang's path to found the Ming dynasty in 1368.
