Doncaster

Doncaster is a city in South Yorkshire, England. Named after the River Don, which flows through it, it is the administrative centre of the City of Doncaster metropolitan borough. Historically part of the West Riding of Yorkshire, Doncaster is known for its horse racing, its railway works, and its market. It was granted city status in 2022 as part of the Platinum Jubilee Civic Honours.

History

Doncaster began as the Roman fort of Danum, guarding a crossing of the Don on the road between York and Lincoln. It later appears in the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle and the Domesday Book. In the Middle Ages it grew as a market town on the Great North Road. The town charter dates from the 12th century, and Doncaster's markets and fairs became famous throughout the north of England.

Railways and industry

In 1853 the Great Northern Railway established its locomotive works — the Plant — in Doncaster, which built celebrated locomotives including Flying Scotsman and Mallard. Coal mining in the surrounding borough was another major industry in the 20th century. The town remains a significant railway hub on the East Coast Main Line.

Horse racing

Doncaster Racecourse is one of the oldest and largest horse racing venues in Britain, home to the St Leger Stakes, first run in 1776 and the oldest of the five British Classic races, and the Racing Post Trophy. The racecourse hosts the four-day St Leger Festival each September.

Transport and education

Doncaster is served by Doncaster railway station, the A1(M) motorway, and Doncaster Sheffield Airport (opened 2005 on the former RAF Finningley site, closed in 2022). Local institutions include Doncaster College and the Doncaster Museum and Art Gallery.
