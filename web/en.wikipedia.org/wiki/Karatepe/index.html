Karatepe

Karatepe (Turkish for "black hill", ancient Azatiwataya) is a late Hittite fortress and open-air museum in Osmaniye Province, Turkey, overlooking the Ceyhan River in the foothills of the Taurus Mountains. It was founded in the 8th century BCE by Azatiwada, a ruler in the Cilician kingdom of Quwe, and destroyed by fire, probably by the Assyrians, later in that century.

Inscriptions

Karatepe is famous for the Karatepe bilingual, a long inscription of King Azatiwada carved in both Phoenician and Hieroglyphic Luwian on the orthostats of the fortress's two gates. Discovered in 1946 and excavated under Halet Çambel, the bilingual text played a key role in the decipherment of Hieroglyphic Luwian, functioning for that script much as the Rosetta Stone did for Egyptian hieroglyphs.

Site

The citadel's walls, towers and two monumental gates are decorated with basalt reliefs depicting lions, sphinxes, ships, warriors, hunting and banquet scenes. The site was preserved as one of Turkey's first open-air museums, with protective shelters designed in the 1950s, and forms part of the Karatepe-Aslantaş National Park. In 2021 it was added to UNESCO's tentative World Heritage list as part of the "Karatepe-Aslantaş Archaeological Site".
