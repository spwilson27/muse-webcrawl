Nishapur

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Mausoleum of Omar Khayyam, Nishapur

Nishapur or Neyshabur (Persian: نیشابور) is a city in Razavi Khorasan Province, northeastern Iran, situated on a fertile plain at the foot of the Binalud Mountains near Mashhad. In the medieval period it was one of the great cities of the Islamic world — a centre of trade, scholarship, ceramics, and turquoise mining.

History

Tradition attributes the city's founding to the Sasanian king Shapur I in the 3rd century CE. After the Muslim conquest of Persia, Nishapur grew into the capital of the Tahirid dynasty and a major stop on the Silk Road. It flourished under the Samanids and Seljuks as a centre of learning; the Nizamiyya school here was among the most prestigious in the Islamic world.

The city suffered catastrophe repeatedly: earthquakes flattened it in the 12th and 13th centuries, and the Mongol invasion of 1221 brought a massacre of its inhabitants under Tolui, son of Genghis Khan, after which it never fully recovered its former standing. The modern city was rebuilt on a nearby site.

Culture

Nishapur is the birthplace and burial place of the poet, astronomer, and mathematician Omar Khayyam, author of the Rubaiyat, and of the Sufi poet Farid ud-Din Attar, author of The Conference of the Birds; the tombs of both men are landmarks of the city. Its distinctive turquoise-glazed pottery and the turquoise mined nearby have been prized for a thousand years — the city's name is popularly linked to the Persian word for its blue stone.

Economy

Today Nishapur is an agricultural and industrial centre, known for turquoise mining and cutting, steel production, and food processing, and is connected by road and rail to Mashhad and Tehran.
