Persian onager

Persian onager
Scientific name: Equus hemionus onager
Conservation status: Endangered
Range: Iran (central plateau and northeast)
Family: Equidae

The Persian onager (Equus hemionus onager), called gur in Persian, is a subspecies of the Asiatic wild ass (onager) native to Iran. It lives in the mountain steppes, semi-deserts, and desert plains of the Iranian plateau and is one of the country's most threatened large mammals.

Description

The Persian onager is sandy brown above and white below, with a dark dorsal stripe running from the mane to the tail. Adults stand about 1.2–1.5 m at the shoulder and weigh roughly 200–260 kg. It is adapted to arid conditions, can endure long periods without water, and digs in dry riverbeds to reach moisture. Herds graze on grasses, shrubs, and halophytic plants.

Distribution and status

Historically widespread across the Iranian plateau, the onager declined through hunting, habitat conversion, and competition with domestic livestock. The main remaining populations are in Touran Biosphere Reserve and Bahram-e Gur Protected Area, with additional animals in other reserves and reintroduction sites. The total wild population is estimated in the hundreds to low thousands and is protected under Iranian law; a captive population is maintained in zoos internationally as part of breeding programmes.

Cultural significance

The onager appears in classical Persian poetry — the poet Bahram Gur is named for his legendary hunting of the gur — and features in Ferdowsi's Shahnameh.
