Cromarty Firth

The Cromarty Firth (Scottish Gaelic: Caolas Chrombaidh) is an arm of the Moray Firth in the Highland region of northern Scotland. The deep, sheltered inlet runs inland from the Sutors of Cromarty to Dingwall, flanked by the Black Isle to the south.

History and industry

The firth has long served as a safe anchorage: it was a Royal Navy base in both world wars, centred on Invergordon, site of the 1931 Invergordon Mutiny. From the 1970s, North Sea oil development brought rig construction and repair yards to Nigg and Invergordon, and semi-submersible rigs are frequently stacked in the firth. The Port of Cromarty Firth is now a hub for offshore wind energy work.

Wildlife

The firth's mudflats and saltmarshes are protected for their birdlife, and bottlenose dolphins from the Moray Firth population are regularly seen at its mouth near Chanonry Point.
