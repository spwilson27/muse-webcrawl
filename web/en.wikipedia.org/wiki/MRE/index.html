MRE

The Meal, Ready-to-Eat (MRE) is a self-contained individual field ration used by the United States Armed Forces and other militaries. It is intended to sustain a service member in field conditions where organised food facilities are unavailable. Each meal provides an average of about 1,250 kilocalories (5,230 kJ) and has a minimum shelf life of three years at 27 °C (81 °F), depending on storage conditions.

History

The MRE replaced the earlier C-ration and Meal, Combat, Individual (MCI) rations. Development began in the 1970s at the US Army Natick Soldier Research, Development and Engineering Center, and the first production MREs were delivered in 1981 and issued in 1983. Early menus were criticised for quality, and successive revisions broadened the menu list — from 12 menus to 24 — and added items such as the flameless ration heater.

Contents

A typical MRE contains a main entrée (such as beef stew, chicken fajita or vegetarian pasta), a side dish, crackers or bread, a spread (peanut butter, cheese or jam), a dessert, powdered beverage mixes, an accessory pack with salt, pepper, sugar, coffee, creamer, chewing gum, moist towelette and toilet paper, and a flameless ration heater (FRH) that uses a magnesium–iron alloy reacting with water to heat the entrée without fire.

Requirements and use

MREs must be able to withstand parachute drops and short-term storage at temperatures from −51 °C to 49 °C. They are designed so that three MREs per day provide the full daily nutritional requirements for a soldier in moderate activity. MREs are also used in humanitarian relief and disaster response, and surplus or imitation MREs are sold commercially to campers and emergency-preparedness consumers.
