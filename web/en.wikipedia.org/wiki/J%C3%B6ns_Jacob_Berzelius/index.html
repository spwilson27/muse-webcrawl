Jöns Jacob Berzelius (20 August 1779 – 7 August 1848) was a Swedish chemist regarded, together with Antoine Lavoisier, John Dalton and Robert Boyle, as one of the founders of modern chemistry. Based at the Karolinska Institute and the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, he dominated European chemistry in the first half of the nineteenth century.

Work

Berzelius introduced the modern system of chemical notation, using one- and two-letter symbols for the elements, and determined the atomic weights of nearly all elements then known with remarkable precision. He discovered the elements cerium (1803, with Wilhelm Hisinger), selenium (1817), silicon (1824) and thorium (1829).

He developed the electrochemical dualistic theory of chemical combination, coined terms including catalysis, polymer, isomer, allotrope and protein, and for decades published an authoritative annual review of chemical progress. His textbook and his rigorous analytical methods trained a generation of chemists.
