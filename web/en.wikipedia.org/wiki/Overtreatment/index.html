Overtreatment

Unnecessary interventions expose patients to harm without benefit

Overtreatment is the provision of medical treatment that is unnecessary, inappropriate, or unlikely to benefit the patient, and which may expose the patient to harm. It is closely related to overdiagnosis, in which conditions are detected and labelled that would never have caused symptoms or death, leading to treatment that can only harm. Overtreatment is a form of low-value care and a major source of waste and iatrogenic injury in health systems.

Causes

Drivers of overtreatment include fee-for-service payment incentives, defensive medicine driven by fear of malpractice claims, patient expectations and demand, clinical guidelines based on disease definitions expanded by screening, financial conflicts of interest, and the "technological imperative" — the tendency to use available interventions. Common examples include antibiotics for viral infections, imaging for uncomplicated low back pain, arthroscopic surgery for degenerative knee conditions, and aggressive treatment of indolent prostate and thyroid cancers.

Consequences and responses

Harms include adverse drug effects, surgical complications, psychological effects of labelling, and the opportunity cost of resources spent on ineffective care; studies have estimated that a substantial share of health care spending in the United States goes to services of low or no value. Responses include the Choosing Wisely campaign (launched by the ABIM Foundation in 2012), in which specialty societies list tests and treatments that should be questioned, and initiatives such as Too Much Medicine and Right Care aimed at identifying and reducing overdiagnosis and overtreatment.
