Joy Division

Joy Division were an English rock band formed in Salford in 1976. The group consisted of vocalist Ian Curtis, guitarist/keyboardist Bernard Sumner, bassist Peter Hook and drummer Stephen Morris. They pioneered the post-punk movement with a sparse, atmospheric sound built on Hook's melodic bass lines, Morris's precise drumming and Curtis's baritone voice and troubled lyrics.

History

Sumner and Hook formed the band after attending a Sex Pistols concert in Manchester in June 1976. Curtis joined as singer, and Morris completed the line-up in 1977; after early gigs as Warsaw, they renamed themselves Joy Division in 1978. Signed to Tony Wilson's Factory Records, they recorded their debut album Unknown Pleasures (1979) with producer Martin Hannett, whose echoing, cavernous production and Peter Saville's stark sleeve design - a diagram of radio pulses from the first discovered pulsar - became iconic.

Closer and Curtis's death

The single "Love Will Tear Us Apart" and second album Closer followed in 1980. Curtis suffered from epilepsy and depression, and his health and marriage deteriorated under the strain of touring. On 18 May 1980, the eve of the band's first American tour, Curtis died by suicide at his home in Macclesfield, aged 23. Closer was released posthumously to great acclaim, and "Love Will Tear Us Apart" became the band's biggest hit.

Legacy

The surviving members continued as New Order, achieving major commercial success in the 1980s. Joy Division's two studio albums are routinely ranked among the greatest records ever made, and their influence extends across alternative rock, gothic rock and indie music. Curtis's life was portrayed in the biopic Control (2007), directed by Anton Corbijn.
