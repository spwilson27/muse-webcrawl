Nivelle Offensive

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


French troops on the Chemin des Dames

The Nivelle Offensive was a Franco-British offensive on the Western Front in the spring of 1917 during the First World War. Named for its planner, French commander-in-chief Robert Nivelle, it centred on a massive French assault along the river Aisne and the Chemin des Dames ridge, intended to achieve a decisive breakthrough within 48 hours. The offensive failed at heavy cost and triggered widespread mutinies in the French Army.

Planning

Nivelle, who had gained fame at Verdun with concentrated artillery tactics, replaced General Joffre in December 1916 and promised a war-winning rupture of the German lines using a creeping barrage of unprecedented weight. British forces would mount a preliminary attack at Arras, and a diversion at Vimy Ridge, to draw off German reserves. Secrecy was poor: German intelligence obtained copies of the plan, and the German withdrawal to the Hindenburg Line in March 1917 further disrupted the scheme.

The battle

The main French attack began on 16 April 1917. Although some ground was gained — including parts of the Chemin des Dames — the breakthrough did not materialise; German defences in depth, machine-gun fire, and counter-attacks inflicted around 187,000 French casualties in the offensive as a whole. French tanks (Schneider CA1s) were used in numbers for the first time with disappointing results.

Aftermath

Disillusionment at the broken promise of a quick, cheap victory sparked the French Army mutinies of 1917, in which dozens of divisions were affected by acts of collective indiscipline. Nivelle was removed in May and replaced by Philippe Pétain, who restored morale through improved conditions, rest, and a defensive strategy while awaiting American reinforcements. The failure of the offensive marked a turning point in France's war effort.
