The Birkenhead Docks are a complex of docks on the west bank of the River Mersey at Birkenhead, in Wirral, England, forming part of the Port of Liverpool. They were built in the tidal inlet of Wallasey Pool and include Morpeth Dock, Egerton Dock, Alfred Dock, and the Great Float — among the largest expanses of dock water in the United Kingdom.

History

Development began in the 1840s under plans associated with engineer James Rendel, with Morpeth Dock opening in 1847. The docks passed to the Mersey Docks and Harbour Board in 1858, integrating them with the Liverpool dock system across the river. Alfred Dock and the West and East Floats followed in the 1860s, handling trade in grain, timber, and general cargo, and serving nearby shipbuilding at Cammell Laird.

Decline and regeneration

Containerization and the decline of transatlantic passenger trade reduced traffic in the later twentieth century, and parts of the dock estate were filled in or redeveloped. The Twelve Quays terminal, opened in 2002, operates roll-on/roll-off ferry services to Belfast and Dublin, while surrounding areas have been earmarked for the Wirral Waters regeneration scheme.
