Afonso I (c. 1109 – 6 December 1185), known as Afonso Henriques, was the first King of Portugal. He proclaimed Portuguese independence from the Kingdom of León and founded the Kingdom of Portugal, earning the epithets "the Conqueror" and "the Founder".

Rise to power

Afonso was the son of Henry, Count of Portugal, and Teresa of León. After his father's death he was raised to succeed in the County of Portugal; in 1128, at the Battle of São Mamede, he defeated the forces of his mother and her Galician allies and took control of the county. On 25 July 1139, after defeating Muslim forces at the Battle of Ourique, he was acclaimed king by his troops. León recognized Portuguese independence in the Treaty of Zamora (1143), and the papal bull Manifestis Probatum (1179) formally recognized Afonso as king.

Reconquista

Afonso steadily expanded his kingdom southward at Muslim expense, capturing Santarém and Lisbon in 1147 — the latter with the help of northern European crusaders en route to the Holy Land — and later Évora and Beja. He founded monasteries, notably Alcobaça, and made Coimbra his capital, where he is buried at the Monastery of Santa Cruz.
