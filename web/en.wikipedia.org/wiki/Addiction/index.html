Addiction

Addiction
Field: Psychiatry, addiction medicine
Key features: Compulsive use despite harm, craving, withdrawal

Addiction is a neuropsychological disorder characterized by a persistent and intense urge to use a drug or engage in a behaviour that produces a natural reward, despite substantial harm and other negative consequences. Repetitive drug use can boost brain functioning in ways that perpetuate craving and weak impulse control.

Addictions can include alcohol, opioids, cocaine, nicotine, cannabis, gambling, and other substances or behaviours. The term is often reserved for severe substance use disorders as classified in the DSM-5.

Mechanisms

Addiction involves the mesolimbic reward pathway and dopamine signalling in the nucleus accumbens, changes in the prefrontal cortex affecting self-control, and learned cues triggering relapse. Tolerance and withdrawal are physiological adaptations but are not by themselves addiction.

Risk factors

Genetics accounts for a substantial share of risk, alongside early life adversity, mental health disorders, availability, and social environment. Adolescence is a period of heightened vulnerability.

Treatment

Treatments include medications such as methadone, buprenorphine, naltrexone and varenicline; behavioural therapies including cognitive behavioural therapy and contingency management; mutual support groups; and harm reduction measures.
