Multi-club ownership

Multi-club ownership is the ownership of more than one professional football (soccer) club by a single entity. The model allows an owner to share players, scouting, coaching methods, commercial operations and branding across a network of clubs in different countries and leagues.

Prominent examples include City Football Group, whose network is headed by Manchester City and includes clubs such as New York City FC, Melbourne City, Girona and Mumbai City; Red Bull's stable of clubs (including RB Leipzig, Red Bull Salzburg and the New York Red Bulls); and the networks of groups such as Eagle Football Holdings and 777 Partners, as well as individual owners such as Evangelos Marinakis (Olympiacos and Nottingham Forest).

The practice is controversial. Supporters argue it creates pathways for player development, efficient recruitment and financial resilience; critics argue it can turn smaller clubs into "feeder" teams, threaten competitive integrity and conflict with supporters' identities. UEFA rules historically barred two clubs under common ownership from the same European competition, but the rules have evolved toward permitting participation with safeguards — for example, the use of "blind trusts" — a change debated in cases such as Manchester United and OGC Nice under INEOS co-ownership, and Manchester City and Girona.
