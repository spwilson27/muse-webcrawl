Drum

The drum is a member of the percussion group of musical instruments. In the Hornbostel–Sachs classification system, it is a membranophone: drums consist of at least one membrane, called a drumhead or drum skin, stretched over a shell and struck, directly with the player's hands, or with a percussion mallet, to produce sound.

History

Drums are among the oldest and most ubiquitous instruments in the world. Ancient examples include alligator-skin drums from Neolithic China and bronze Dong Son drums of Southeast Asia. Drums have served in music, ritual, communication — as with African talking drums — and military signalling.

Construction and playing

There is normally a resonator (the shell) underneath the head; the shape and material of the shell and the tension of the head determine pitch and timbre. Drums may be played individually or in sets; the modern drum kit combines bass drum, snare drum, toms, and cymbals played by one drummer.
