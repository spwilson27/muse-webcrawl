History of Iraq

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Mesopotamia, the "cradle of civilisation", lies largely within modern Iraq.

The history of Iraq begins in ancient Mesopotamia, home to Sumer, Akkad, Babylon and Assyria, and the invention of writing, the wheel and codified law. Conquered by Persians, Alexander the Great, and then Arab Muslim armies in the 7th century CE, the region became the heart of the Abbasid Caliphate, with Baghdad founded in 762 as its capital. Ottoman rule followed from the 16th century until the First World War; the modern Iraqi state emerged under a British mandate, became a kingdom, then a republic in 1958, and endured the Ba'athist era of Saddam Hussein, the Iran–Iraq War, the Gulf War, and the 2003 US-led invasion.

Ancient Mesopotamia

The Tigris–Euphrates valley hosted the world's earliest cities, including Uruk and Ur. Hammurabi of Babylon (r. 1792–1750 BCE) issued a famous law code; the Neo-Assyrian and Neo-Babylonian empires later dominated the Near East. The region passed to the Achaemenid, Seleucid, Parthian and Sasanian empires.

Islamic golden age and Ottoman rule

Under the Abbasids, Baghdad became one of the world's largest cities and a centre of translation, mathematics, medicine and philosophy — the era of Bayt al-Hikma (House of Wisdom). The Mongol sack of 1258 devastated the city and its irrigation systems. From 1534 Iraq was an Ottoman frontier province contested with Safavid Persia, governed from Baghdad, Mosul and Basra.

Mandate, kingdom and republic

British forces occupied Iraq in the First World War; the League of Nations mandate combined the three Ottoman provinces. Faisal I became king in 1921; formal independence came in 1932, though British influence remained. The monarchy fell in the 14 July Revolution of 1958. After coups, the Ba'ath Party seized power in 1968, and Saddam Hussein became president in 1979.

Wars and after 2003

Saddam launched the Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988) and invaded Kuwait in 1990, prompting the Gulf War of 1991 and a decade of sanctions. The 2003 invasion toppled the Ba'ath regime; insurgency and sectarian civil war followed, culminating in the rise of the Islamic State (ISIL), which seized Mosul in 2014 and was militarily defeated in Iraq by 2017.
