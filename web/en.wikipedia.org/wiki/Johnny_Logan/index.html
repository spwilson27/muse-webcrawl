Johnny Logan (born Seán Patrick Michael Sherrard; 13 May 1954) is an Australian-born Irish singer and songwriter. He is best known as the only performer to have won the Eurovision Song Contest twice as a singer, in 1980 and 1987, and he also composed the winning song in 1992, earning him the nickname "Mr Eurovision".

Early life

Logan was born in Frankston, near Melbourne, Australia, to the Irish tenor Patrick O'Hagan, who performed under that stage name, and his wife Ellen. The family returned to Ireland when Logan was three years old. He learned guitar and began writing songs in his early teens, and after leaving school he worked as an electrician while performing in pubs and clubs. His stage name was taken from the title character of the film Johnny Guitar.

Eurovision success

Logan first attempted to represent Ireland in 1979 before winning the Eurovision Song Contest 1980 in The Hague with "What's Another Year", written by Shay Healy. The song reached number one in the United Kingdom and several other countries. In 1987, in Brussels, he won again with the self-penned "Hold Me Now", which became a major European hit and is regularly voted among the most popular Eurovision songs of all time. In 1992 he composed "Why Me?", performed by Linda Martin, which won the contest in Malmö and gave Logan a third victory and a unique treble in Eurovision history.

Later career

Logan has continued to record and tour extensively, particularly in Ireland, Germany and Scandinavia. His albums include Hold Me Now, Reach for Me and The Irish Connection, the latter a collection of traditional and folk material. He remains a frequent guest and mentor on Eurovision-related programmes.

Discography (selection)

Studio albums include In London (1979), What's Another Year (1980), Straight from the Heart (1985), Hold Me Now (1987), Reach for Me (2000) and It Is What It Is (2017).
