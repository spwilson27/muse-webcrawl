President of Germany

The president of Germany, officially the Federal President of the Federal Republic of Germany (German: Bundespräsident), is the head of state of Germany. The office is a largely ceremonial one, with the president ranking first in the order of precedence but exercising limited executive power; real political authority rests with the chancellor and the Bundestag. The incumbent is Frank-Walter Steinmeier, in office since 2017 and re-elected in 2022.

Selection

The president is elected for a five-year term, renewable once, by the Federal Convention, a body consisting of the members of the Bundestag and an equal number of state delegates. Any German citizen aged 40 or over is eligible. The president must suspend party political activity while in office.

Duties

Duties include signing federal laws into force (with a rarely-used power to refuse on constitutional grounds), nominating the chancellor for election by the Bundestag, appointing and dismissing federal ministers on the chancellor's proposal, appointing judges and officials, granting pardons in individual cases, and representing Germany internationally. The official residence is Bellevue Palace in Berlin, with Villa Hammerschmidt in Bonn as a secondary residence.

History

The Basic Law of 1949 deliberately made the federal president weaker than the powerful Reich President of the Weimar Republic, whose emergency powers had contributed to the Republic's collapse. Theodor Heuss was the first president of the Federal Republic.
