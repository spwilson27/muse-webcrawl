Fuzzball

Fuzzball may refer to several topics:

Fuzzball (string theory)

In string theory, the fuzzball proposal, advanced by Samir Mathur and collaborators beginning in the late 1990s, holds that the classical picture of a black hole — a singularity surrounded by an event horizon and empty interior — is an artefact of coarse-graining. Individual microstates of a black hole are instead conjectured to be smooth, horizonless configurations ("fuzzballs") of strings and branes that end just at the would-be horizon radius. Because radiation can escape from the surface of such a structure, the proposal aims to resolve the black hole information paradox without abandoning unitarity, and it is connected with the firewall paradox debate in quantum gravity.

Table football

Fuzzball is also the name of a variant of table football played with a standard table football (foosball) table but using a soft felt- or fabric-covered ball, which is quieter and grips the playing surface more than a conventional hard ball. The slower, noisier-free game is popular in schools, youth clubs and offices, and the ball's grip allows different techniques than standard foosball.

Other uses

  • A "fuzzball" is an informal term for a small fluffy object or animal, such as a kitten or a seed head.
  • In computing slang, a fuzzball router was an early Internet router built on PDP-11 hardware by David Mills.
