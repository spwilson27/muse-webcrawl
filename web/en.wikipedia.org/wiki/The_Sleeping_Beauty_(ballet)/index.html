The Sleeping Beauty (ballet)

The Sleeping Beauty
ChoreographerMarius Petipa
MusicPyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky (Op. 66)
LibrettoIvan Vsevolozhsky, after Charles Perrault
Based onLa Belle au bois dormant by Charles Perrault
Premiere15 January 1890, Mariinsky Theatre, St. Petersburg

The Sleeping Beauty (Russian: Спящая красавица, Spyashchaya krasavitsa) is a ballet in a prologue and three acts, first performed in 1890 at the Mariinsky Theatre in St. Petersburg. The music was composed by Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky (his opus 66), the choreography by Marius Petipa, and the libretto by Ivan Vsevolozhsky, based on Charles Perrault's fairy tale La Belle au bois dormant.

It is the second of Tchaikovsky's three ballets and is regarded by many as his finest ballet score. The work has become one of the most famous ballets of the classical repertoire.

Synopsis

At the christening of Princess Aurora, the wicked fairy Carabosse—furious at not being invited—curses the baby to die by pricking her finger on a spindle. The Lilac Fairy, unable to undo the curse entirely, softens it: Aurora will not die but sleep for a hundred years, to be awakened by a prince's kiss. On Aurora's sixteenth birthday the curse comes to pass and the whole court falls asleep. A century later Prince Désiré, guided by the Lilac Fairy, finds the palace, kisses Aurora, and breaks the spell. The ballet ends with their wedding, attended by fairy-tale characters including Puss in Boots, Little Red Riding Hood, and Cinderella.

Music

Tchaikovsky worked closely with Petipa, who supplied extremely detailed instructions on tempo, character, and length for each number. Famous passages include the Rose Adagio, the grand waltz, and the Panorama. The composer considered the score among his best works.

Revivals

The 1890 production was lavishly staged and became a cornerstone of the Imperial Ballet. Major later productions include the 1921 Diaghilev staging in London (as The Sleeping Princess), the Royal Ballet's 1946 production that reopened the Royal Opera House after the war, and Rudolf Nureyev's versions for several companies.
