A Hard Day's Night (film)

A Hard Day's Night is a 1964 British musical comedy film starring the English rock band the Beatles — John Lennon, Paul McCartney, George Harrison and Ringo Starr — during the height of Beatlemania. It was directed by Richard Lester, produced by Walter Shenson, and written by Alun Owen. The film portrays a "typical" day in the life of the band as they travel to London for a television performance, pursued by fans and hampered by McCartney's fictional grandfather, played by Wilfrid Brambell.

Plot

The Beatles board a train to London, evading crowds of screaming fans. During the journey they play cards, tease their managers, and slip away to a club. At the television studio, rehearsals are interrupted by a press reception full of inane questions, a costume fitting, and Ringo's brief escape to a pub and canal-side wandering after being rattled by a journalist. In the evening the band performs before a studio audience of screaming teenagers, then escapes by helicopter.

Music

The soundtrack album of the same name, released in July 1964, was the first Beatles album to consist entirely of original Lennon–McCartney songs, including "A Hard Day's Night", "I Should Have Known Better", "If I Fell", "And I Love Her", "Can't Buy Me Love" and "Tell Me Why". The title phrase was coined accidentally by Starr, who, after a long session, remarked that it had been "a hard day's..." before adding "...night".

Reception and legacy

Made on a low budget of about £180,000 and shot in black and white in roughly seven weeks, the film was a critical and commercial success. Reviewers praised its witty script, energetic editing and semi-documentary style. It was nominated for two Academy Awards, including Best Original Screenplay for Owen. The film is frequently ranked among the greatest British films and most influential rock-and-roll movies ever made; its quick-cut performance sequences are regarded as forerunners of the modern music video.
