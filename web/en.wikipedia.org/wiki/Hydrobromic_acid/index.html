Hydrobromic acid

Hydrobromic acid
Formula: HBr (aq)
Appearance: colourless liquid
Nature: strong acid

Hydrobromic acid is a strong acid formed by dissolving the diatomic molecule hydrogen bromide (HBr) in water. It is one of the strongest mineral acids, with a pKa of about −9. Constant-boiling hydrobromic acid is an aqueous solution distilling at 124.3 °C containing about 47.6% HBr by mass, widely sold as a reagent.

Preparation

Hydrobromic acid can be prepared by reacting bromine with sulfur dioxide and water, by the reaction of bromine with phosphorus and water, or by treating a bromide salt (such as potassium bromide) with a non-oxidising acid. Industrially it is commonly produced as a by-product or by direct catalytic reaction of hydrogen and bromine at elevated temperatures.

Uses

Hydrobromic acid is used to prepare inorganic and organic bromides, as a catalyst and reducing agent, for the extraction of ores, and in the cleavage of ethers (for example with anisole). Historically, bromide salts prepared from it were used as sedatives and anticonvulsants. Concentrated acid is also used for etching and in certain pharmaceutical processes.
