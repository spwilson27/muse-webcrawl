Neckar

The Neckar is a river in southwestern Germany, a major right-bank tributary of the Rhine. It rises in the Black Forest foothills near Villingen-Schwenningen and flows about 362 kilometres (225 mi) northeast and then west through Tübingen, Stuttgart, Heilbronn, and Heidelberg before joining the Rhine at Mannheim. The river's name derives from Celtic roots meaning "wild water".

Course

From its source on the Baar plateau, the young Neckar cuts through a narrow valley past Rottweil, Oberndorf, and Horb. It flows beneath the Hohenzollern Castle region and through the university town of Tübingen, then into the densely populated Stuttgart basin, passing Esslingen and Bad Cannstatt. North of Ludwigsburg the valley becomes deeply incised and winding, with steep vineyard slopes. At Heilbronn the river enters a series of engineered reaches with locks; it then passes Bad Wimpfen and the castle-lined stretch through Eberbach to Heidelberg, where it emerges from the Odenwald hills into the Upper Rhine Plain, flowing the last kilometres to its confluence with the Rhine at Mannheim.

Navigation and industry

The lower Neckar was canalised between the 1920s and 1960s; 27 locks make the 203 km from Plochingen to Mannheim navigable, linking the Stuttgart region's industry — including the ports of Stuttgart, Heilbronn, and Mannheim — to the Rhine waterway network. Dams along the canalised river also generate hydroelectric power. Major tributaries include the Fils, Rems, Murr, Enz, Kocher, and Jagst.

Culture

The Neckar valley is celebrated in German literature and song; Friedrich Hölderlin, born at Lauffen am Neckar and a student at Tübingen, dedicated an ode to the river. The steep terraced vineyards of the middle Neckar produce Trollinger and Riesling wines, and the "Neckar Käpt'n" excursion boats and the Neckar Valley cycle path make the river a popular leisure corridor.
