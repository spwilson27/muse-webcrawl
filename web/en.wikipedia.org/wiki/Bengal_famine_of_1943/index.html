Bengal famine of 1943
Date: 1943-44
Location: Bengal, British India

The Bengal famine of 1943 was a major famine in British India that killed an estimated 2 to 3 million people in Bengal province from starvation, malnutrition and disease during the Second World War.

Causes

Causes remain debated and include wartime inflation, loss of rice imports from Japanese-occupied Burma, cyclone damage in 1942, crop disease, denial policies, and failures of imperial and provincial government to organise relief, as analysed by Amartya Sen in terms of entitlement failure.

Consequences

The famine devastated rural Bengal, drove migration to Calcutta, and became a defining event in Indian nationalism and post-war debates on food security.
