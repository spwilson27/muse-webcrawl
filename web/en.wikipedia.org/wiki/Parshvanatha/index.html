Parshvanatha (Sanskrit: Pārśvanātha), also known as Parshva and Paras, was the 23rd of 24 tirthankaras (supreme preachers) of Jainism. He is the only tirthankara who is generally accepted as a historical figure. His biography is dated to the 9th–8th century BCE, roughly two and a half centuries before Mahavira, the 24th tirthankara.

Life

According to Jain tradition, Parshvanatha was born in Varanasi (Benares) to King Ashvasena and Queen Vama of the Ikshvaku dynasty. At the age of thirty he renounced the world and became an ascetic. After 84 days of deep meditation and spiritual practice, he attained kevala jnana (omniscience) under a dhaataki tree near Varanasi. He preached for seventy years and attained moksha (liberation) at Shikharji on Parasnath Hill, in present-day Jharkhand, a site that remains one of the holiest places of Jain pilgrimage.

Teachings

Parshvanatha taught the chaturyama dharma, the fourfold restraint: ahimsa (non-violence), satya (truth), asteya (non-stealing) and aparigraha (non-possession). Mahavira later added brahmacharya (celibacy) as a fifth vow. Parshvanatha allowed his monks to wear clothing, whereas Mahavira's followers practised nudity — one of the points distinguishing the traditions that later crystallised into the Shvetambara and Digambara sects.

Iconography

Parshvanatha is identifiable by the hood of serpents — usually seven or eleven heads of the serpent king Dharanendra — sheltering his head, recalling the legend in which Dharanendra and his wife Padmavati protected the meditating ascetic from a storm sent by the demon Kamath. His emblem is the snake, and his attendant spirits are Dharanendra (yaksha) and Padmavati (yakshi).
