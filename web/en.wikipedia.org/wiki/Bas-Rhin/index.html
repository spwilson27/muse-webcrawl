Bas-Rhin (French pronunciation: [bɑ ʁɛ̃]; Alsatian: Unterelsàss; German: Niederrhein) is a department in northeastern France, in the historic region of Alsace. Named "Lower Rhine" for its position downstream along the Rhine relative to neighbouring Haut-Rhin, it is bounded by the Rhine and Germany to the east, the Vosges mountains to the west, and has its prefecture at Strasbourg, seat of the European Parliament. Since 2021 it has been administered, together with Haut-Rhin, by the European Collectivity of Alsace, though the two departments remain for national purposes.

History

Bas-Rhin was one of the original 83 departments created in 1790 during the French Revolution. The territory was annexed by the German Empire in 1871 as part of Alsace-Lorraine, returned to France in 1918, annexed again by Nazi Germany in 1940, and restored to France in 1945. Strasbourg's position on the Franco-German frontier made it a symbol of postwar reconciliation and the seat of European institutions including the Council of Europe and the European Court of Human Rights.

Geography and culture

The department stretches along the fertile Rhine plain, famed for wine villages on the Alsace wine route, half-timbered towns such as Obernai and Wissembourg, and the Northern Vosges Regional Nature Park, a UNESCO biosphere reserve shared with the Moselle department. Mont Sainte-Odile and the castle of Haut-Kœnigsbourg (in Haut-Rhin, near the border) are major landmarks. Alsatian, a Germanic dialect, is still spoken by part of the population, and the region retains distinctive legal and cultural particularities, including the Concordat and its Christmas markets.
