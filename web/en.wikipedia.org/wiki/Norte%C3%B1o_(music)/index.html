Norteño (music)

Stylistic originsMexican folk music, polka, waltz, corrido
Cultural originsLate 19th century, northern Mexico / Texas borderlands
Typical instrumentsAccordion, bajo sexto, bass, drums
RelatedConjunto, tejano, banda, corridos

Norteño (Spanish for "northern") is a genre of Mexican folk and popular music, named for its association with northern Mexico. Its defining sound is the pairing of the diatonic button accordion with the bajo sexto, a twelve-string bass guitar, performing polkas, waltzes, redovas, and narrative ballads called corridos.

History

The genre took shape in the late 19th and early 20th centuries along the Mexico–Texas border, where German, Polish, and Czech immigrants introduced the accordion and European dance forms that blended with Mexican musical traditions. Recording spread the style on both sides of the border; on the Texas side it developed into conjunto music.

Development

Pioneers such as Narciso Martínez established the accordion–bajo sexto format, while bands like Los Alegres de Terán popularized the duet style. Later groups including Los Tigres del Norte and Ramón Ayala y sus Bravos del Norte modernized the instrumentation with electric bass and drums and brought corridos about migration, labor, and border life to mass audiences. Norteño remains one of the most popular forms of regional Mexican music, with offshoots including norteño-banda.
