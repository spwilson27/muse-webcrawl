Operation Buffalo

Operation Buffalo is the name given to several military operations. The best known is the Allied breakout from the Anzio beachhead in Italy during the Second World War, launched on 23 May 1944 as part of the offensive that led to the liberation of Rome. After months of stalemate following the January 1944 landings, VI Corps attacked toward Cisterna and Velletri; Rome fell on 4 June 1944.

The name was also used for a German operation on the Eastern Front in March 1943, in which the 9th Army evacuated the Rzhev salient in Operation Büffel ("Buffalo"), shortening the front line and freeing divisions for later operations such as the Battle of Kursk.

Other uses include Australian operations in Korea and various police and conservation operations named after the animal. This article focuses on the 1944 Anzio operation.
