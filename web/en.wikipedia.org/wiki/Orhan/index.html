Orhan Ghazi (Ottoman Turkish: اورخان غازی; c. 1281 – 1362) was the second ruler of the Ottoman Empire, succeeding his father Osman I around 1323/4 and reigning until his death. Under Orhan the small frontier principality in northwestern Anatolia was transformed into a state: he captured the major Byzantine cities of Bithynia, organised a standing army, minted the first Ottoman silver coinage, and oversaw the first permanent Ottoman foothold in Europe.

Conquests in Anatolia

Orhan's reign opened with the fall of Bursa in 1326, which became his capital. Ottoman forces defeated the Byzantine emperor Andronikos III at the Battle of Pelekanon in 1329, after which Nicaea (İznik) fell in 1331 and Nicomedia (İzmit) in 1337, bringing all of Byzantine Bithynia under Ottoman control. Orhan also annexed the neighbouring Turkish principality of Karesi around 1345, gaining its coastline and experienced sailors.

Expansion into Europe

Orhan allied with the future Byzantine emperor John VI Kantakouzenos, marrying his daughter Theodora in 1346 and supplying Ottoman troops for Byzantine civil wars. In return, and increasingly on their own initiative, Ottoman forces crossed into Thrace; the fortress of Tzympe was taken in 1352 and Gallipoli in 1354, after an earthquake destroyed its walls. His son Süleyman Pasha directed the settlement of Turks in Europe before his death in 1357.

Administration and legacy

Orhan is credited with early Ottoman state organisation: the first akçe silver coins in his name, a vizierate (held by his brother Alaeddin), a regular military force that preceded the Janissaries, and religious and charitable endowments (waqfs), including the first Ottoman madrasa at İznik. He died in 1362 and was buried in Bursa beside his father. He was succeeded by his son Murad I.
