2600: The Hacker Quarterly

2600: The Hacker Quarterly is an American quarterly magazine specializing in publishing technical information on a variety of subjects, including telephone switching systems, Internet protocols, computer security, and the politics surrounding hacking. It was founded in 1984 by Eric Corley, who publishes under the pen name Emmanuel Goldstein — a reference to the character in George Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four.

Name

The magazine takes its name from the 2600 hertz tone once used in phreaking: producing a tone at that frequency on a telephone line could trick the phone system into treating the caller as an operator, giving access to long-distance calling functions. Whistles producing the tone were famously given away in boxes of Cap'n Crunch cereal.

Related projects

2600 hosts monthly hacker meetings in cities around the world on the first Friday of each month. It also founded the Hackers on Planet Earth (HOPE) conference series, held in New York City since 1994, and produces the long-running radio programs Off the Hook (on WBAI) and Off the Wall. The magazine was a party to litigation over the DeCSS DVD decryption code, decided in Universal City Studios, Inc. v. Corley.
