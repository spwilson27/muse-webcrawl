Mohammed Zahir Shah

Mohammed Zahir Shah (15 October 1914 – 23 July 2007) was the last King of Afghanistan, reigning from 8 November 1933 until he was deposed on 17 July 1973. Often remembered as the father of the nation, he presided over four decades of relative stability in Afghanistan, and his reign spanned the Second World War and much of the Cold War.

Early life and accession

Zahir Shah was born in Kabul, the son of King Mohammed Nadir Shah. Educated at Esteqlal High School in Kabul and in France, he was serving as deputy minister of war when his father was assassinated in 1933. He acceded to the throne at the age of 19; during the first two decades of his reign, effective power was exercised by his uncles, notably Sardar Shah Mahmud Khan.

Reign

Zahir Shah maintained Afghanistan's neutrality in the Second World War and pursued cautious modernisation. From 1963 he ruled in his own right, inaugurating the "New Democracy" period with the liberal constitution of 1964, which introduced a constitutional monarchy, free elections, and expanded civil rights, though the king remained head of state. His reign saw the growth of Kabul University, road-building with Soviet and American aid, and the beginnings of tourism.

Deposition and exile

While Zahir Shah was in Italy for medical treatment in July 1973, his cousin and former prime minister Mohammed Daoud Khan seized power and declared a republic. The king formally abdicated and lived in exile in Rome for 29 years. After the fall of the Taliban, he returned to Afghanistan in April 2002 to open the Loya Jirga, and was honoured with the title "Father of the Nation". He died in Kabul on 23 July 2007.
