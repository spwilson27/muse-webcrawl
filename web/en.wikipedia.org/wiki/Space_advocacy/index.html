Space advocacy is the support, promotion, and lobbying for human spaceflight and space exploration. Advocates argue for expanding humanity's presence beyond Earth for scientific, economic, and survival reasons, and work through non-profit societies, professional organisations, and commercial ventures.

History

Early advocacy grew out of the rocket societies of the 1930s and the popular writing of figures such as Wernher von Braun and Willy Ley, who promoted spaceflight through magazines and television in the 1950s. Gerard K. O'Neill's 1976 book The High Frontier inspired the space colonisation movement and the L5 Society. The Planetary Society was founded in 1980 by Carl Sagan, Bruce Murray, and Louis Friedman.

Organisations

The National Space Society (formed by a 1987 merger) lobbies for a spacefaring civilisation; the Mars Society, founded by Robert Zubrin in 1998, promotes human missions to Mars; and the Space Frontier Foundation supports commercial space development. Advocacy increasingly includes private companies such as SpaceX and Blue Origin, whose founders promote settlement and lowering launch costs.

Arguments

Common arguments include the scientific value of exploration, economic benefits of space resources, inspiration and education, and the "single-planet" risk: that humanity's survival requires becoming a multiplanetary species. Critics argue that robotic exploration is cheaper, that Earth's problems should take priority, and that advocacy overstates near-term feasibility.
