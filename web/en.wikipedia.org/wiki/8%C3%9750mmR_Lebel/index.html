The 8×50mmR Lebel (8 mm Lebel) was the standard French military rifle cartridge from 1886 into the mid-20th century. Developed for the Lebel Model 1886 rifle, it was revolutionary: the first military cartridge loaded with smokeless powder (Poudre B) and one of the first to use a jacketed spitzer bullet, adopted with the Balle D in 1898.

Design

The cartridge was necked down from the black-powder 11 mm Gras case, giving it a pronounced rim and a sharply tapered, rimmed bottleneck shape. This geometry, designed for the Lebel's tube magazine, required care with pointed bullets stacked nose-to-primer, addressed by a primer groove and cover design.

Service

The round chambered the Lebel and Berthier rifles, the Chauchat light machine gun, and the Hotchkiss machine guns of both world wars. Although obsolete in concept by the 1920s and replaced in front-line roles by the 7.5×54mm French, it remained in service through the Second World War.
