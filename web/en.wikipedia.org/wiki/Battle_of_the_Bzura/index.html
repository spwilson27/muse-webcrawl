Battle of the Bzura

The Battle of the Bzura (Polish: Bitwa nad Bzurą) was fought from 9 to 19 September 1939 during the German invasion of Poland. It was the largest Polish counteroffensive of the September campaign, in which the Poznań and Pomorze armies attacked the flank of the German 8th Army along the Bzura River west of Warsaw. After initial success, the offensive was crushed by German reinforcements and air power.

Background

The Polish Poznań Army under General Tadeusz Kutrzeba had not been heavily engaged in the opening week and stood north of the German advance toward Warsaw. Kutrzeba proposed, and headquarters approved, a counterstroke southward across the Bzura against the exposed flank of the German 8th Army, later joined by the retreating Pomorze Army.

The battle

Beginning on the night of 9 September, Polish forces attacked toward Łęczyca and Łowicz, driving back the German 30th Infantry Division and advancing up to twenty kilometres; for several days the offensive forced the Germans to divert substantial forces, including panzer divisions, from the assault on Warsaw. From 16 September, however, German reinforcements closed around the Polish armies in the bend of the Bzura, and the Luftwaffe bombed the crowded river crossings almost unopposed. Attempts to break out toward Warsaw largely failed, though some units — including part of the Poznań Army and General Kutrzeba himself — reached the capital.

Aftermath

Polish losses were severe: some 20,000 killed and around 100,000 taken prisoner, with most of the two armies destroyed. The battle nonetheless delayed the German timetable and remains the most significant Polish offensive operation of 1939, remembered in Poland as an example of determined resistance in a hopeless strategic situation.
