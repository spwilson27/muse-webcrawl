Schutzstaffel

The Schutzstaffel (SS; also written ᛋᛋ with the Armanen runes) was a major paramilitary organisation under Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party in Nazi Germany, and later throughout German-occupied Europe during World War II. It began with a small guard unit known as the Saal-Schutz made up of NSDAP volunteers to provide security for party meetings in Munich. In 1925, Heinrich Himmler joined the unit, which had by then been reformed and given its final name. Under his direction (1929–1945) it grew from a small paramilitary formation during the Weimar Republic to one of the most powerful organisations in Nazi Germany.

Origins

The SS was founded in April 1925 as a bodyguard detachment for Hitler. Initially subordinate to the much larger Sturmabteilung (SA), it remained a small formation until Heinrich Himmler became Reichsführer-SS in January 1929. Himmler remodelled the SS as an elite racial order, imposing strict racial and physical selection criteria, and expanded it rapidly after the Nazi seizure of power in 1933.

Structure and functions

The SS comprised several main branches: the Allgemeine SS (General SS), the general political and administrative corps; the SS-Totenkopfverbände, which ran the concentration camps; the Sicherheitsdienst (SD), the party's intelligence service; and from 1939 the Waffen-SS, armed military units that fought alongside the Wehrmacht. Through the Reich Main Security Office (RSHA), formed in 1939, the SS controlled the Gestapo and the criminal police, fusing party and state security functions.

Role in the Holocaust

The SS was the organisation most responsible for the genocidal murder of an estimated 5.5 to 6 million Jews and millions of other victims during the Holocaust. SS units, including the Einsatzgruppen mobile killing squads, carried out mass shootings in occupied Eastern Europe, while the camp system administered by the SS ran the extermination camps of Auschwitz, Treblinka, Belzec, Sobibor, Chełmno and Majdanek.

Postwar

After Germany's defeat, the SS was declared a criminal organisation by the International Military Tribunal at Nuremberg, and its members were subject to denazification. Himmler committed suicide in British custody in May 1945. Many senior SS officers were tried and executed, although others escaped abroad.
