Crucifixion of Jesus

The crucifixion of Jesus was the execution by crucifixion of Jesus of Nazareth in 1st-century Judea, most likely in AD 30 or AD 33. It is described in the four canonical gospels and is central to Christian theology.

Crucifixion of Jesus

Date: AD 30 or 33
Location: Golgotha, Jerusalem
Type: Execution by crucifixion

Narrative

According to the gospels, Jesus was arrested in Gethsemane, tried before the Sanhedrin and Pontius Pilate, and sentenced to death. He carried his cross to Golgotha, where he was crucified between two criminals, with a sign reading 'Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews'. He died after several hours; his body was placed in a tomb by Joseph of Arimathea.

Historical aspects

Most scholars regard the crucifixion under Pilate as historically secure, supported by Tacitus and Josephus references to Jesus's execution. The exact date, linked to Passover, remains debated.

Theological significance

Christians believe Jesus's death atones for sin and is followed by the Resurrection. In Islam, the Quran states Jesus was not crucified but was raised by God, a view interpreted in various ways.
