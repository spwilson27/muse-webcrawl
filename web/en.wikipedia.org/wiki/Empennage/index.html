Empennage

Empennage

Also called: Tail assembly, tail unit

Components: Horizontal stabilizer, vertical stabilizer (fin), elevators, rudder

Function: Stability and control in pitch and yaw

The empennage (from the French empenner, "to feather an arrow"), also known as the tail or tail assembly, is a structure at the rear of an aircraft that provides stability during flight, in a way similar to the feathers on an arrow. The term has become common in the aviation industry but is also used in general for the tail section of other vehicles, such as boats.

Structure

Most aircraft empennages consist of two main surfaces:

  • The horizontal stabilizer, a fixed (or adjustable) horizontal surface carrying hinged elevators that control pitch.
  • The vertical stabilizer or fin, a fixed vertical surface carrying a hinged rudder that controls yaw.

Trim tabs are often fitted to the control surfaces to relieve control forces, and many modern airliners use a fully adjustable (trimmable) horizontal stabilizer.

Configurations

The most common arrangement is the conventional tail, with the horizontal stabilizer mounted on the fuselage at the base of the fin. Other configurations include the cruciform tail, the T-tail (horizontal stabilizer mounted atop the fin, as on the Boeing 727), twin tails, V-tails (combining pitch and yaw control in two slanted surfaces, as on the Beechcraft Bonanza), and inverted V and Y arrangements. Some aircraft, such as canard designs and flying wings, lack a conventional empennage altogether.

Function

The stabilizers act like small wings positioned far behind the aircraft's centre of gravity, so the aerodynamic forces on them generate strong restoring moments: the horizontal stabilizer keeps the nose from pitching up or down uncontrollably, and the fin provides directional ("weathervane") stability. The movable elevators and rudder allow the pilot to control these axes deliberately.
