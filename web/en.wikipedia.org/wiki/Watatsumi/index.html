Watatsumi (綿津見), also read Ōwatatsumi ("great deity of the sea"), is a legendary kami, Japanese water deity and dragon god of the sea in Shinto mythology. The name derives from wata ("sea") and tsumi, often interpreted as a spirit or master; the three Watatsumi deities born to Izanagi are sometimes distinguished from the singular sea god who appears in the mythic narratives.

Mythology

According to the Kojiki, the Watatsumi kami were born when Izanagi purified himself after returning from Yomi, the land of the dead: deities of the sea's depths, middle waters and surface were born as he washed. In the myth of the brothers Hoori and Hoderi, the younger brother Hoori descended to Watatsumi's palace, Ryūgū-jō, at the bottom of the sea, where he married the god's daughter Toyotama-hime and received the tide jewels — the jewel that makes the tide flow and the jewel that makes it ebb — with which he subdued his elder brother. Toyotama-hime's sister Tamayori-hime later raised the child Ugayafukiaezu, ancestor of Japan's first emperor, Jimmu; Watatsumi is thus an ancestral deity of the imperial line.

Worship

Watatsumi is enshrined at numerous shrines associated with the sea, seafarers and fishing, including Watatsumi Shrine in Tarumi, Kobe, and Shikaumi Shrine on Shika Island in Fukuoka, regarded as a head shrine of Watatsumi worship. The sea god also appears under the form of the dragon king Ryūjin in later folklore, where the undersea palace, its messenger the flounder and soles, and the tide jewels are recurring motifs.
