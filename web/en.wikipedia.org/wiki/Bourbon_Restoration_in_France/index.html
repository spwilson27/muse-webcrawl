Bourbon Restoration in France

Bourbon Restoration
Period: 1814/15 – 1830
Kings: Louis XVIII, Charles X
Ended by: July Revolution 1830

The Bourbon Restoration was the period in French history following the fall of Napoleon in 1814, when the Bourbon monarchy was restored under Louis XVIII and then Charles X, until the July Revolution of 1830 replaced it with the July Monarchy of Louis-Philippe.

History

After Napoleon's abdication, the allies restored Louis XVIII under the Charter of 1814, a constitutional monarchy retaining many Revolutionary and Napoleonic reforms (legal equality, Code Napoléon) while restoring royal and Catholic authority. The Hundred Days (1815) briefly returned Napoleon; a second restoration followed Waterloo, with the White Terror against revolutionaries.

Louis XVIII ruled pragmatically; the ultra-royalist Charles X (from 1824) provoked opposition with indemnities to émigrés, sacrilege laws and the July Ordinances dissolving the newly elected chamber — triggering the Trois Glorieuses and his abdication. See Napoleon, Louis XVIII, July Revolution.
