National Archives and Records Administration
Agency overview
Formed: April 1, 1985 (as an independent agency)
Headquarters: National Archives Building, Washington, D.C.
Motto: "What is past is prologue"
Agency executive: Archivist of the United States

The National Archives and Records Administration (NARA) is an independent agency of the United States federal government charged with the preservation and documentation of government and historical records. It is also tasked with increasing public access to those documents. NARA is officially responsible for maintaining and publishing the legally authentic and authoritative copies of acts of Congress, presidential directives, and federal regulations.

History

The National Archives was established in 1934 under President Franklin D. Roosevelt. For much of its early history it was part of the General Services Administration, but in 1985 it became an independent agency. The head of the agency is the Archivist of the United States.

Holdings and facilities

NARA preserves the Declaration of Independence, the Constitution of the United States, and the Bill of Rights, which are displayed in the rotunda of the National Archives Building in Washington, D.C. The agency operates regional archives and the system of presidential libraries across the United States, and publishes the Federal Register.
