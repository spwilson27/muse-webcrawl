David Ogilvy (businessman)

This article is about the 13th Earl of Airlie. For the advertising executive, see David Ogilvy.

David George Patrick Coke Ogilvy, 13th and 8th Earl of Airlie (17 May 1926 – 26 June 2023) was a Scottish landowner, soldier, banker, and courtier. He served as Lord Chamberlain of the Household from 1984 to 1997, the senior official of the Royal Household under Queen Elizabeth II, and was Chancellor of the Order of the Thistle. He should not be confused with his distant namesake, the advertising executive David Ogilvy.

Life

Ogilvy was educated at Eton and served with the Scots Guards during the Second World War. He succeeded his father as Earl of Airlie in 1968. In business, he joined the merchant bank Schroders, rising to become its chairman — a career in the City that, together with his management of the family estates in Angus, gave him the practical background reflected in this article's "businessman" disambiguation. He was a director of companies including General Accident and the Royal Bank of Scotland.

Lord Chamberlain

Appointed Lord Chamberlain in 1984, Ogilvy oversaw the Royal Household during a period of modernisation and crisis, including the aftermath of the 1992 Windsor Castle fire and the royal family's annus horribilis; he helped steer reforms such as the Queen's agreement to pay income tax. He was appointed a Knight of the Order of the Thistle in 1985 and served as its Chancellor from 2010. He died in June 2023, aged 97.
