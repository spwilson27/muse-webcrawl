Svaneti (Georgian: სვანეთი) is a historic region in the northwestern part of Georgia, inhabited by the Svans, an ethnic subgroup of the Georgians. Surrounded by the high peaks of the Greater Caucasus, including Mount Shkhara, the highest mountain in Georgia, the region is known for its picturesque landscapes, medieval defensive tower houses, and distinctive culture and language.

Geography

Svaneti is traditionally divided into Upper Svaneti (Zemo Svaneti), along the upper Enguri River with its administrative centre at Mestia, and Lower Svaneti (Kvemo Svaneti), in the valley of the Tskhenistsqali River, centred on Lentekhi. The village community of Ushguli in Upper Svaneti, lying at about 2,100 m above sea level, is one of the highest continuously inhabited settlements in Europe and is part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

In antiquity the Svans were part of the kingdoms of Colchis and later of Iberia. Their remote, mountainous homeland allowed Svaneti to preserve a large degree of autonomy under local princely families such as the Dadiani and Gelovani, even as lowland Georgia was conquered by successive empires. The region was absorbed into the Russian Empire in the nineteenth century and became part of Soviet and then independent Georgia. Svaneti is famous for its medieval churches with frescoes, its icons and illuminated manuscripts, and the treasure preserved in the museum in Mestia.

Culture

The Svans speak the Svan language, the most divergent of the Kartvelian languages, alongside Georgian. The region is also known for its polyphonic singing, its distinctive round knitted hats, and the fortified towers (murkvani) built between the 9th and 12th centuries to defend families against invaders and blood feuds.
