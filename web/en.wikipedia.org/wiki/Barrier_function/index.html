Barrier function

In constrained optimization, a barrier function is a continuous function whose value increases to infinity as its argument approaches the boundary of the feasible region. Barrier functions are used to convert constrained problems into sequences of unconstrained ones, most famously in interior point methods.

FieldMathematical optimization
Used inInterior point methods
Classic exampleLogarithmic barrier

Motivation

Given a problem of minimising f(x) subject to inequality constraints g(x) ≤ 0, a barrier method adds a term that is small in the interior of the feasible set but explodes near its boundary, keeping iterates strictly feasible. As a parameter shrinks, the modified solutions approach the true optimum.

Logarithmic barrier

The most widely used barrier for an inequality g(x) ≤ 0 is −log(−g(x)). Logarithmic barrier methods underlie the polynomial-time interior point algorithms developed for linear programming in the 1980s and now standard in convex optimization software.

History

Barrier ideas appeared in the SUMT method of Fiacco and McCormick in the 1960s, fell from favour, and were revived after Karmarkar's 1984 algorithm showed the power of interior point approaches.
