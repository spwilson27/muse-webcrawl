Saxo Grammaticus (c. 1150 – c. 1220), also known as Saxo cognomine Longus, was a Danish historian, theologian, and author. He is the author of the Gesta Danorum, the first full history of Denmark, written in elegant Latin under the patronage of Archbishop Absalon of Lund.

Life

Little is known about Saxo's life. He was probably a clerk in the service of Absalon, and came from a family that had served Danish kings in war. The epithet "Grammaticus" (the Learned) reflects the sophistication of his Latin, which draws on classical authors such as Valerius Maximus and Plato.

Gesta Danorum

The Gesta Danorum ("Deeds of the Danes") comprises sixteen books, moving from Denmark's mythical founders through legendary heroes to Saxo's own time in the late 12th century. Its early books are the principal surviving source for many Scandinavian myths and legends, including the story of Amleth, which became the source for Shakespeare's Hamlet, and the tale of the archer Toko, related to William Tell.
