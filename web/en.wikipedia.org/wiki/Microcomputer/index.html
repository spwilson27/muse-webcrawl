Microcomputer

A microcomputer is a small, relatively inexpensive computer with a microprocessor as its central processing unit. It includes memory, input/output circuitry and storage mounted on a motherboard.

History

The term became popular in the 1970s following the introduction of single-chip microprocessors such as the Intel 4004 and 8080. Early machines included the Altair 8800 (1975), followed by mass-market home and personal computers including the Apple II, Commodore PET, TRS-80, ZX Spectrum and Commodore 64.

Modern usage

As processing power grew, the distinction between microcomputers and larger systems blurred. Today desktops, laptops and single-board computers such as the Raspberry Pi are all descendants of the microcomputer concept.
