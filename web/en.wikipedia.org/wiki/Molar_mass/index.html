Molar mass
Field: Chemistry

The molar mass of a substance is the mass of one mole of it, in grams per mole (g/mol). It is numerically equal to the relative formula mass: water (relative formula mass 18.0) has a molar mass of about 18.0 g/mol, and sodium chloride about 58.4 g/mol.

Molar mass converts between the weighing scale of the laboratory and the counting scale of atoms: moles = mass ÷ molar mass. It underlies nearly every quantitative calculation in chemistry, from preparing solutions to gas molar volumes.

[Image: A balance weighing out one mole samples of several substances]

Computing it

Add the relative atomic masses of all atoms in the formula, counting subscripts: glucose C₆H₁₂O₆ = (6×12.0) + (12×1.0) + (6×16.0) = 180 g/mol, approximately. Hydrated salts include their waters of crystallisation.

Measurement

Mass spectrometry measures molecular masses directly with high precision, giving molar masses of large biomolecules to within a fraction of a dalton.
