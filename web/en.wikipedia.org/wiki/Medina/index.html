Medina, officially Al-Madinah al-Munawwarah ('The Luminous City'), is a city in the Hejaz region of western Saudi Arabia and the second-holiest city in Islam, after Mecca. It was to Medina (then Yathrib) that the Prophet Muhammad emigrated in the Hijra of 622 CE, built the Prophet's Mosque (Al-Masjid an-Nabawi, where he is buried), and governed the first Muslim community under the Constitution of Medina.

Medina remained the capital of the early caliphate until 661. Today the vast Prophet's Mosque — with its famous Green Dome and ten minarets — draws millions of pilgrims who combine it with Hajj or Umrah in Mecca, some 450 km south. Like Mecca, the city centre is closed to non-Muslims. Medina is also known for its dates and for the trench of the Battle of the Trench (627), dug on Salman the Persian's advice.
