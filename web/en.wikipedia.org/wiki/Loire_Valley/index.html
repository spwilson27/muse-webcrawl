The Loire Valley (French: Val de Loire) is the area in the middle stretch of the Loire River in central France, renowned for its historic towns, architecture and wines. It is famous for its many châteaux — more than 300, from medieval fortresses to Renaissance palaces — built when the French court resided in the region in the 15th and 16th centuries, and is often called the "Garden of France" and the "cradle of the French language".

Châteaux

Notable châteaux include Chambord, the largest, built for Francis I with a double-helix staircase attributed to the circle of Leonardo da Vinci (who died at Clos Lucé, Amboise, in 1519); Chenonceau, spanning the River Cher and shaped by Catherine de' Medici; the royal fortresses of Amboise, Blois, Chinon and Loches; Villandry, famous for its reconstructed Renaissance gardens; and Azay-le-Rideau and Cheverny. Many are open to the public, and the region is served by the Loire à Vélo long-distance cycle route.

Wine

The Loire Valley wine region follows the river from the Atlantic coast to Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé in the east. It is celebrated for white wines — Muscadet, Vouvray, Sancerre — made from Melon de Bourgogne, Chenin Blanc and Sauvignon Blanc, as well as Cabernet Franc reds from Chinon and Bourgueil and sparkling Crémant de Loire. See Loire Valley (wine).

World Heritage

In 2000 UNESCO inscribed the central Loire Valley between Sully-sur-Loire and Chalonnes-sur-Loire as a World Heritage Site, an outstanding cultural landscape of great beauty shaped by two thousand years of interaction between people and the river.
