Eugène Joseph Delporte

Eugène Joseph Delporte (10 January 1882 – 19 October 1955) was a Belgian astronomer known for discovering numerous asteroids and for drawing up the modern boundaries of the 88 constellations.

Born10 January 1882, Genappe, Belgium
Died19 October 1955, Uccle, Belgium
NationalityBelgian
FieldsAstronomy
InstitutionsRoyal Observatory of Belgium

Career

Delporte worked at the Royal Observatory of Belgium at Uccle, where he discovered 66 asteroids, including the Amor asteroid 1221 Amor and the Apollo asteroid 2101 Adonis — two objects that gave their names to entire classes of near-Earth asteroids. He also discovered or co-discovered comets, including the periodic comet 57P/du Toit–Neujmin–Delporte.

Constellation boundaries

In 1928 the International Astronomical Union adopted Delporte's precise delineation of the boundaries of the 88 recognized constellations, published in 1930 as Délimitation scientifique des constellations. His boundaries, drawn along lines of right ascension and declination for epoch 1875, remain the standard in use today. The lunar crater Delporte is named in his honour.
