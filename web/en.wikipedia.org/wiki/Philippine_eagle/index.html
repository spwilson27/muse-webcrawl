Philippine eagle

Scientific name: Pithecophaga jefferyi
Conservation status: Critically Endangered
Habitat: Dipterocarp forests of the Philippines
Wingspan: Up to about 2.2 m

The Philippine eagle is one of the world's largest forest eagles.

The Philippine eagle (Pithecophaga jefferyi), also known as the monkey-eating eagle or great Philippine eagle, is a critically endangered species of eagle of the family Accipitridae which is endemic to forests in the Philippines. It has brown and white plumage, a shaggy crest, and stands about a metre tall; it is considered one of the largest and most powerful forest raptors in the world and is the national bird of the Philippines.

Biology

The eagle is characterised by a large, deep, high-arched bill, piercing blue-grey eyes, and long crown feathers forming a crest. Its diet varies by island and includes flying lemurs (colugos), civets, monkeys, large snakes, monitor lizards, and other birds; despite the popular name, monkeys form only part of its diet. Pairs mate for life, nest in tall dipterocarp trees, and raise a single eaglet in a breeding cycle lasting about two years — one of the slowest reproductive rates of any bird.

Conservation

Deforestation of its lowland rainforest habitat and hunting have driven the species to critically low numbers, with the wild population estimated at only a few hundred breeding pairs. Killing a Philippine eagle is punishable by up to 12 years imprisonment under Philippine law. The Philippine Eagle Foundation in Davao City runs a captive breeding and release programme, and the first chick bred in captivity, Pag-asa ("hope"), hatched in 1992.
