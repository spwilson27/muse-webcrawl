Khuushuur

Khuushuur (Mongolian: хуушуур) is a fried meat pastry in Mongolian cuisine. It consists of a wheat-flour pocket filled with minced or ground meat — usually mutton or beef, seasoned with onion, garlic, salt, pepper and sometimes caraway — folded into a flat half-moon or pocket shape and deep-fried in mutton fat or oil.

Preparation and serving

The dough is rolled thin, filled, sealed by pinching the edges, and fried until golden and crisp, with the meat cooking in its own juice inside. Khuushuur is traditionally eaten by hand, held at the edges, and is especially popular during the Naadam festival. A cheese-filled variation is also made.

Related dishes

Khuushuur is the fried counterpart of the steamed dumpling buuz and is related to fried meat pastries across Central Asia.
