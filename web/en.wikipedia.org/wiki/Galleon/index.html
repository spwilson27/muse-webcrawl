Galleon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A Spanish galleon with ornate sterncastle under sail]
A Spanish treasure galleon.

A galleon was a large, multi-decked sailing warship and merchantman used by Spain, Portugal, England, France and the Netherlands from the 16th to the 18th centuries. Combining a carrack\'s high sterncastle with a lower, sleeker hull and a beakhead prow, galleons carried the Spanish treasure fleets from the Americas and the famous Manila galleons across the Pacific for 250 years.

Design and use

Galleons carried two or three masts (square-rigged fore and main, lateen mizzen), cannon on gun decks, and ornate sterns marking their status. Spain\'s flota system sailed treasure ships from Cartagena and Veracruz to Havana and home to Seville/Cádiz, guarded against pirates and rival powers.

Famous actions

English \'race-built\' galleons, lower and handier, fought the Spanish Armada in 1588. Dutch and English East Indiamen evolved from galleon forms. The Manila galleon route (1565–1815) exchanged American silver for Chinese silk and porcelain via Acapulco and Manila.

Legacy

Wrecks such as the Atocha (1622) and their recovered treasure keep galleons in the popular imagination; the type gradually gave way to the ship of the line and the frigate in the 1700s.
