Communist Party of Greece

The Communist Party of Greece (KKE) was founded in 1918.

The Communist Party of Greece (Greek: Κομμουνιστικό Κόμμα Ελλάδας, Kommounistikó Kómma Elládas, KKE) is a Marxist–Leninist political party in Greece. It was founded in 1918 as the Socialist Labour Party of Greece and adopted its current name in 1924. It is the oldest political party in modern Greek politics.

History

The KKE played a leading role in the Greek resistance during the Axis occupation of World War II through the National Liberation Front (EAM) and its army, ELAS. After the war, the party was defeated in the Greek Civil War (1946–1949) and banned until 1974, with many members exiled to Eastern Europe and the Soviet Union. It split in 1968 into the KKE and the KKE Interior over the Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia.

Legalised after the fall of the military junta in 1974, the party has since maintained a consistent parliamentary presence. Unlike many European communist parties, the KKE did not abandon orthodox Marxism–Leninism after 1991; it characterises the European Union as an imperialist alliance and advocates withdrawal from it and from NATO.

Organisation and support

The party's youth organisation is the Communist Youth of Greece (KNE), and it is influential in the All-Workers Militant Front (PAME) trade union confederation. Its newspaper is Rizospastis. The KKE typically wins between about 4 and 10 percent of the national vote and holds seats in the Hellenic Parliament and the European Parliament.
