The Boeing B-47 Stratojet (Boeing company designation Model 450) is a retired American long-range, six-engined, turbojet-powered strategic bomber designed to fly at high subsonic speed and at high altitude to avoid enemy interceptor aircraft. The primary mission of the B-47 was as a nuclear bomber capable of striking targets within the Soviet Union.

Development

The B-47 pioneered the combination of a thin, swept wing (35°) with podded jet engines hung in pylons beneath it — a configuration that became standard for nearly all subsequent jet airliners and bombers. Its six General Electric J47 engines were mounted in four pods, and the aircraft used bicycle-style landing gear with outriggers because the thin wing could not house gear retracting into it. Rocket-assisted takeoff (RATO) bottles aided heavily loaded departures.

Operational history

The B-47 became the backbone of Strategic Air Command in the 1950s; at its peak in 1956, more than 1,300 were in service. Although it never dropped bombs in combat, reconnaissance variants (RB-47) flew sensitive peripheral missions around the Soviet bloc, and some were fired upon; an RB-47H was shot down over the Barents Sea in 1960. The bomber versions were retired by 1966 as the B-52 took over, while electronic reconnaissance variants served until 1977.

Legacy

The Stratojet's design established Boeing's swept-wing jet formula that led directly to the B-52 Stratofortress and the 707 airliner. Its high-speed, high-altitude performance briefly outclassed contemporary fighters.

Specifications (B-47E)

Crew: 3. Length: 107 ft 1 in (32.6 m). Wingspan: 116 ft (35.4 m). Maximum speed: 607 mph (977 km/h). Combat radius: about 2,013 mi (3,240 km). Bomb load: up to 25,000 lb (11,340 kg).
