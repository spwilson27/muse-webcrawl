The Met Gala, formally the Costume Institute Benefit, is an annual fundraising gala held for the benefit of the Metropolitan Museum of Art's Costume Institute in Manhattan, New York City. Widely regarded as among the most prestigious and glamorous fashion events in the world — often called "fashion's biggest night" — it is held on the first Monday in May and marks the opening of the Costume Institute's annual fashion exhibition, whose theme sets the evening's dress code.

History

The Gala was founded in 1948 by fashion publicist Eleanor Lambert as a midnight supper to raise money for the newly formed Costume Institute. From 1972, under consultant Diana Vreeland, it took on its modern form, tying each ball to a blockbuster exhibition. Since 1995 it has been chaired by Vogue editor-in-chief Anna Wintour, who approves the guest list of roughly 400–700 celebrities, designers and public figures. Individual tickets in recent years have cost tens of thousands of dollars (about $75,000 in 2024), and the event routinely raises well over ten million dollars in an evening.

Themes and famous editions

Memorable exhibition themes include China: Through the Looking Glass (2015), Heavenly Bodies: Fashion and the Catholic Imagination (2018) — among the most visited exhibitions in the Met's history — Camp: Notes on Fashion (2019), Karl Lagerfeld: A Line of Beauty (2023) and Sleeping Beauties: Reawakening Fashion (2024). Guests' interpretations of the dress code dominate global media coverage each year.
