Yi Sam-pyeong (died 1655) was a Korean potter who was brought to Japan during the Japanese invasions of Korea (1592–1598) and became the founder of porcelain production in Arita, Saga Prefecture — the origin of Imari ware. He is revered in Arita as the "father of Japanese porcelain".

Life

Like many Korean craftsmen taken to Japan during the invasions, Yi was settled in Kyushu under the Nabeshima lords of Saga. Around 1616 he is credited with discovering porcelain stone (kaolin-rich rock) at Izumiyama in Arita and firing Japan's first porcelain there.

Legacy

Arita became the centre of Japanese porcelain, exported to Europe through the Dutch East India Company from the port of Imari. Yi is enshrined at Sueyama Shrine in Arita, where a monument commemorates him; his story is a notable, if sometimes debated, chapter in Korean–Japanese cultural history.
