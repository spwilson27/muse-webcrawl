History of the National Football League

The history of the National Football League begins in 1920, when representatives of several professional teams met in Canton, Ohio, to form the American Professional Football Association, renamed the National Football League in 1922. Jim Thorpe served as its first president.

Growth and the championship era

The league survived competition, the Depression, and wartime disruption. The 1933 introduction of a championship game, and stars such as Red Grange and later Johnny Unitas, raised its profile. The 1958 NFL Championship Game, won by the Baltimore Colts in sudden-death overtime, is often called "the greatest game ever played."

AFL rivalry and the Super Bowl

The rival American Football League (1960–69) forced a merger, producing the Super Bowl, first played in 1967. Under Commissioner Pete Rozelle, television revenue sharing made the NFL the richest American sports league.

Modern era

Free agency, salary caps, expansion to 32 teams, and enormous broadcast contracts followed, alongside controversies over player safety and concussions. See also National Football League.
