Arsène Lupin is a fictional gentleman thief and master of disguise created in 1905 by French writer Maurice Leblanc. Lupin was first introduced in a series of short stories published in the magazine Je sais tout, the first being "The Arrest of Arsène Lupin" (15 July 1905). He went on to appear in 17 novels and 39 novellas by Leblanc, as well as sequels by other authors, and numerous film, television, stage and comic adaptations.

Character

Lupin is a master burglar of wit, charm and flamboyant audacity: he often announces his thefts in advance, leaves calling cards, and steals from the rich, the corrupt and the criminal rather than the innocent. He is a brilliant detective as well as a thief — in several stories he solves crimes the police cannot — and a patriotic Frenchman who serves his country in wartime. His recurring adversaries include Inspector Ganimard of the Sûreté and, in a famous copyright-evading crossover, the English detective "Herlock Sholmès", a transparent parody of Sherlock Holmes (Conan Doyle objected to the direct use of Holmes's name in the earliest stories).

  • The Countess of Cagliostro (1924)

Legacy and adaptations

Lupin became an archetype of the "gentleman thief" and a fixture of French popular culture; the town of Étretat, Leblanc's home and Lupin's fictional base, hosts a Lupin museum. Internationally he inspired the Japanese manga/anime franchise Lupin III (whose hero is Lupin's grandson) and the Netflix series Lupin (2021), in which a modern thief played by Omar Sy models himself on the character.
