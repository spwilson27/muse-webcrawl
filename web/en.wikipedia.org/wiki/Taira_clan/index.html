The Taira clan (平氏, Heishi) was one of the four most important clans that dominated Japanese politics during the Heian period, alongside the Minamoto, the Fujiwara and the Tachibana. The clan was founded when members of the imperial family were granted the surname Taira and removed from the line of succession, becoming subjects.

Origins

Several Taira lines existed, the most prominent being the Kanmu Heishi, descended from princes of the Emperor Kanmu given the Taira name in the 9th century. Taira no Masakado, of this line, led a famous rebellion in the Kantō in the 930s. The Ise Heishi branch, descended from Taira no Korehira, rose to national power in the 12th century through the military service of Taira no Tadamori and his son Taira no Kiyomori.

Rise and fall

Victories in the Hōgen (1156) and Heiji (1160) rebellions made the Taira under Kiyomori the dominant force at court. Kiyomori became the first samurai to hold the office of daijō-daijin (chancellor), married his daughter into the imperial family, and placed his grandson, the Emperor Antoku, on the throne. Taira dominance provoked the rival Minamoto, whose uprising ignited the Genpei War (1180–1185). After defeats at Kurikara, Ichi-no-Tani and Yashima, the Taira were annihilated at the naval Battle of Dan-no-ura in 1185, where the young Emperor Antoku drowned. Their dramatic fall is chronicled in the epic The Tale of the Heike.
