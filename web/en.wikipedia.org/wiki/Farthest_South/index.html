Farthest South

Farthest South was the most southerly latitude reached by explorers before the conquest of the South Pole in 1911. Progressively advanced during the age of Antarctic exploration, the record marked successive milestones in the exploration of Antarctica.

History

Captain James Cook crossed the Antarctic Circle in 1773 and reached 71°10′S before being turned back by ice. In 1842, James Clark Ross, having discovered the Ross Sea and the Ross Ice Shelf, reached 78°10′S. The record stood for nearly sixty years until the Heroic Age of Antarctic Exploration: Carsten Borchgrevink's party reached 78°50′S in 1900, and Robert Falcon Scott's Discovery expedition reached 82°17′S in 1902. Ernest Shackleton's Nimrod expedition pushed the record to 88°23′S on 9 January 1909, only 97 nautical miles from the Pole. The term "Farthest South" became obsolete when Roald Amundsen reached the South Pole on 14 December 1911.
