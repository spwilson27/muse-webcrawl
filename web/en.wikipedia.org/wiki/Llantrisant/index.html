Llantrisant (Welsh for "church of the three saints") is a town and community in the county borough of Rhondda Cynon Taf, Wales, situated on a hilltop about 12 miles (19 km) northwest of Cardiff. The town's name derives from its parish church, dedicated to three Welsh saints: Illtud, Gwynno, and Tyfodwg. Llantrisant is home to the Royal Mint, which moved from London to a site at Llantrisant beginning in 1968.

History

Settlement at Llantrisant dates back at least to the sixth century; the hilltop site commanded routes between the upland valleys and the coastal plain. Llantrisant Castle was built around 1246 by Richard de Clare, lord of Glamorgan, and was later damaged in the revolts of Llywelyn Bren (1316) and Owain Glyndŵr. The town's freemen held ancient rights, and its charter and the town's bullring survive in the old town. In the nineteenth century the eccentric Dr William Price of Llantrisant — physician, Chartist, and self-styled druid — gained fame for the attempted cremation of his infant son, whose 1884 trial established the legality of cremation in Britain.

The Royal Mint

The Royal Mint relocated production from Tower Hill, London, to a 38-acre site at Llantrisant in stages from 1968, officially opening its new home in 1968–1971; all UK coin production moved to Wales by 1975. The Mint, which also produces coins for many other countries and operates the Royal Mint Experience visitor attraction (opened 2016), is the town's largest employer.

Governance and transport

Llantrisant Town Council serves the community; the town lies near the M4 motorway (junction 34) and has grown substantially as a residential area for Cardiff commuters, with the nearby retail development at Talbot Green.
