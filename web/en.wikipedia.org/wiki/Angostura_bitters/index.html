Angostura bitters

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Angostura bitters is a concentrated bitters (herbal alcoholic preparation) based on gentian, herbs, and spices, produced by House of Angostura in Trinidad and Tobago. It is typically used for flavouring beverages or, less often, as a flavouring in food. The bitters were first produced in the town of Angostura (now Ciudad Bolívar, Venezuela), hence the name, but the company later moved to Port of Spain, Trinidad, where production continues today.

History

The recipe was developed by Johann Gottlieb Benjamin Siegert, a German doctor who served as Surgeon General under Simón Bolívar in the Venezuelan army. Around 1824, Siegert created the bitters in Angostura as a tonic intended to help soldiers suffering from digestive ailments and the effects of the tropical climate. The product was first sold commercially in 1830, and Siegert's sons later moved the operation to Trinidad in 1875, where the company also became a major producer of rum.

Angostura bitters were exported widely during the nineteenth century and became a standard ingredient in many classic cocktails, including the Manhattan, the Old fashioned, and the Martini. The company received royal warrants, including appointment as a supplier to the Prussian court and, later, to the British royal household.

Characteristics

The bitters are made from a secret blend of herbs, spices, and botanicals macerated in alcohol; the company states that only a handful of people know the complete recipe. The liquid is deep brown and intensely aromatic and bitter, so it is normally used only in dashes. The bottle is recognisable by its oversized paper label, which extends well above the neck — a result, according to company lore, of a sizing mix-up that was kept as a branding signature.

Uses

Angostura bitters are a defining ingredient of the pink gin, and a few dashes are added to drinks such as the Manhattan, the Old fashioned, and the champagne cocktail. Beyond cocktails, they are used in low-alcohol drinks such as lemon, lime and bitters — a popular beverage in Australia and New Zealand — and occasionally as a seasoning in soups, sauces, and desserts. The company also produces an orange bitters variant and the Amaro di Angostura liqueur.
