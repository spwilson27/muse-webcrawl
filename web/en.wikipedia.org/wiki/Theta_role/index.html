Theta role
Field: Linguistics (syntax and semantics)
Also called: Thematic role

In linguistics, a theta role (also called a thematic role) is the semantic relationship that an argument bears to the predicate (usually a verb) in a sentence. Examples include the agent (the doer of an action), the patient or theme (the entity affected or moved), the experiencer, the goal, and the instrument.

Theta criterion

In generative grammar, theta roles are assigned by predicates to their arguments in accordance with the theta criterion, proposed in Noam Chomsky's government and binding theory: each argument bears one and only one theta role, and each theta role is assigned to one and only one argument.

Inventory of roles

There is no universally agreed list of theta roles. Commonly proposed roles include agent, experiencer, theme, patient, instrument, goal, source, location, and beneficiary. Different frameworks — such as Fillmore's case grammar, Jackendoff's conceptual semantics, and Dowty's proto-roles — analyse the same phenomena in different ways.

Relation to grammatical structure

Theta roles link lexical semantics to syntax: the choice of which role is realised as subject or object varies across languages and verb classes, as in the contrast between active and passive sentences, where the patient becomes subject while retaining its theta role.
