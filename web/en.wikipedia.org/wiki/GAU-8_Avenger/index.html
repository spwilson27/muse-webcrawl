GAU-8 Avenger

The GAU-8/A Avenger is a 30 mm hydraulically driven seven-barrel Gatling-style autocannon built by General Electric (now General Dynamics Ordnance and Tactical Systems). It is carried exclusively by the United States Air Force's A-10 Thunderbolt II attack aircraft — famously, the aeroplane was designed around the gun. The Avenger is one of the most powerful aircraft cannons ever flown, intended primarily to destroy tanks and other armoured vehicles.

Design

The complete GAU-8 system — gun, feed system, and ammunition drum — is about 6 metres long and accounts for some 16 percent of the A-10's empty weight. The gun fires at selectable rates (originally 2,100 or 4,200 rpm, later standardised at 3,900 rpm) and produces recoil forces averaging about 45 kN (10,000 lbf), comparable to the thrust of one of the A-10's two engines; the aircraft noticeably decelerates in long bursts. The firing barrel is offset so that it aligns with the aircraft's centreline, and the nose landing gear is displaced to one side to accommodate the drum. Spent cases are retained in the drum to preserve the aircraft's centre of gravity.

Ammunition

The standard combat load is a mix of high-explosive incendiary (HEI) rounds and armour-piercing incendiary (API) rounds with depleted uranium penetrators, typically loaded in a 4:1 ratio. The 30×173 mm projectile leaves the muzzle at about 1,010 m/s and can defeat the top and side armour of main battle tanks of its era at practical ranges, with effective firing ranges around 1,220 metres.

History

The GAU-8 was developed in the early 1970s under the Air Force's A-X programme, winning a competitive shoot-off against the Philco-Ford GAU-9. The A-10 entered service in 1977, and the Avenger saw extensive combat in the Gulf War of 1991 — where A-10s were credited with destroying hundreds of Iraqi tanks and vehicles — and in Kosovo, Iraq, and Afghanistan. A variant, the four-barrel GAU-22/A, arms the F-35A. The Dutch Goalkeeper naval close-in weapon system also uses the GAU-8.
