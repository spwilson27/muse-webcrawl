Modulus of continuity

In mathematical analysis, a modulus of continuity is a function ω : [0, ∞] → [0, ∞] used to measure the uniform continuity of functions. Intuitively, a modulus of continuity bounds how much a function can change in terms of how much its argument changes: a function f admits ω as a modulus of continuity if and only if for all x and y in the domain,

|f(x) − f(y)| ≤ ω(|x − y|).

Thus a function is uniformly continuous if and only if it admits some modulus of continuity ω with ω(δ) → 0 as δ → 0.

Definition

Formally, a modulus of continuity is a non-decreasing, subadditive function ω defined on [0, ∞) with ω(0) = 0 that is continuous at 0. Every uniformly continuous function between metric spaces admits a (minimal) modulus of continuity, defined by taking the supremum of |f(x) − f(y)| over all pairs with distance at most δ.

Special cases

Important special moduli include ω(δ) = Cδ, corresponding to Lipschitz continuity; ω(δ) = Cδα for 0 < α ≤ 1, corresponding to Hölder continuity with exponent α; and moduli such as ω(δ) = 1/|log δ|, which describe functions that are uniformly continuous but not Hölder continuous for any positive exponent.

Applications

Moduli of continuity are used in approximation theory to quantify rates of approximation (for example in Jackson's inequality), in the theory of stochastic processes to establish path continuity via the Kolmogorov continuity theorem, and in partial differential equations to state Schauder estimates for solutions.
