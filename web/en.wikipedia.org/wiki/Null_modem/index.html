Null modem

A null modem is a serial communications arrangement that connects two pieces of data terminal equipment (DTE), such as two computers, directly together without the modems or data circuit-terminating equipment normally required by the RS-232 standard. It is most commonly implemented as a null modem cable in which the transmit and receive lines are cross-linked.

Wiring

In a standard RS-232 connection, a terminal transmits on the TXD pin and receives on RXD, expecting a modem wired oppositely. A null modem cable reverses this: each device's transmitted data pin is connected to the other's received data pin, and the handshaking lines (such as RTS/CTS and DTR/DSR/DCD) are similarly crossed or looped back, depending on the variant, so that each computer believes it is talking to a modem. Both simple cables with only the data lines crossed and full-handshaking cables exist.

Uses

Null modem connections were widely used for transferring files between computers (with programs such as LapLink), for direct-cable networking in early operating systems, for two-player serial link games, and for connecting to the console ports of network equipment, embedded systems and industrial devices, a use that survives in modern serial console cables.
