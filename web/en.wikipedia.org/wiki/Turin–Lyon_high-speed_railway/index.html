Turin–Lyon high-speed railway

The Turin–Lyon high-speed railway is a planned 270 km-long, 220 km/h railway line under construction through the Alps between Turin in Italy and Lyon in France. Its centrepiece is the 57.5 km Mont d'Ambin Base Tunnel, which upon completion will be among the longest rail tunnels in the world.

Project

The line forms part of the Mediterranean Corridor of the Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T) and is intended to shift freight from road to rail across the Alps, cutting journey times between Milan and Paris and serving an estimated 4.5 million passengers per year. The cross-border section is being built by the binational promoter TELT (Tunnel Euralpin Lyon Turin).

Controversy

The project has been the focus of long-running opposition in Italy's Susa Valley, where the No TAV movement has protested since the 1990s over cost, environmental impact and doubts about traffic forecasts. Supporters counter that the existing Fréjus line is obsolete and that the new line is essential for European integration and emissions reduction.

Construction

Exploratory tunnels at Saint-Martin-de-la-Porte, La Praz and Chiomonte preceded main works. Boring of the base tunnel's twin tubes began in earnest in the 2020s, with opening projected for the early 2030s.
