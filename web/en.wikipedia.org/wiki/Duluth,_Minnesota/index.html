Duluth, Minnesota

Duluth is a port city in the U.S. state of Minnesota and the county seat of St. Louis County. Situated on the north shore of Lake Superior at the westernmost point of the Great Lakes, Duluth forms a metropolitan area with Superior, Wisconsin, called the Twin Ports. The Port of Duluth–Superior is the largest inland port in the world, shipping iron ore, coal, grain and other bulk cargo.

Geography and climate

The city climbs a steep hillside rising some 200 metres above the lake, giving many neighbourhoods sweeping views of the water. Lake Superior moderates Duluth's climate—cooler summers and slightly milder winters than inland Minnesota—but the city is nevertheless known for cold, snowy winters and its famous lake-effect weather. The historic Aerial Lift Bridge (1905) connects Canal Park with the long sand spit of Minnesota Point.

History

The area was long inhabited by Ojibwe peoples before French explorers, including Daniel Greysolon, Sieur du Lhut—for whom the city is named—arrived in the 17th century. Duluth boomed in the late 19th century with lumber, railroads and Mesabi Range iron ore. It is the birthplace of Bob Dylan (born Robert Zimmerman in Duluth in 1941) and home to Grandma's Marathon, run each June along the lake shore.

Economy

Shipping, health care, education (the University of Minnesota Duluth), aviation (Cirrus Aircraft) and tourism anchor the modern economy. The North Shore scenic drive to Canada begins here.
