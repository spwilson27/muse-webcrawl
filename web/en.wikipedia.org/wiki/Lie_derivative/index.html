Lie derivative

In differential geometry, the Lie derivative, named after Sophus Lie by Władysław Ślebodziński, evaluates the change of a tensor field (including scalar functions, vector fields, and differential forms) along the flow defined by another vector field. This change is coordinate invariant, so the Lie derivative can be defined on any differentiable manifold.

Definition

For a vector field X with flow φt, the Lie derivative of a tensor field T is the rate of change of T when its values are pulled back by the flow:

(𝓛XT)p = limt→0 [ (φ−t)*Tφt(p) − Tp ] / t

For a function f, the Lie derivative reduces to the directional derivative, 𝓛Xf = X(f). For a vector field Y, it is the Lie bracket: 𝓛XY = [X, Y].

Properties

The Lie derivative is a derivation on the algebra of tensor fields: it satisfies the Leibniz rule with respect to tensor products and commutes with contraction. It is related to the exterior derivative and interior product by Cartan's formula, 𝓛X = d ιX + ιX d, where d is the exterior derivative and ιX denotes contraction with X. A tensor field is invariant under the flow of X if and only if its Lie derivative along X vanishes; in general relativity such symmetries give rise to Killing vector fields.

Applications

Lie derivatives are used throughout geometry and physics: in the study of symmetries and conservation laws, in fluid dynamics (where they describe quantities advected by a flow), in control theory, and in general relativity, where the Lie derivative expresses changes of fields under diffeomorphisms independently of any connection or coordinates.
