Tk'emlúps te Secwépemc


Tk'emlúps te Secwépemc territory near Kamloops

Tk'emlúps te Secwépemc, abbreviated as TTS and formerly known as the Kamloops Indian Band, is a First Nation in the Canadian province of British Columbia. It is the largest member band of the Secwépemc (Shuswap) Nation, with its main community located near the city of Kamloops at the confluence of the North and South Thompson Rivers. The name Tk'emlúps means "where the rivers meet" in the Secwépemc language.

History and community

The Secwépemc have occupied the region for millennia, and the Kamloops area was a major gathering and trading place. The band administers several reserves covering roughly 33,000 hectares, and operates the Secwépemc Museum and Heritage Park on the site of the former Kamloops Indian Residential School. The community is a significant economic presence in the region, with ventures in tourism, agriculture, and commercial development.

Residential school

In May 2021 the band announced that ground-penetrating radar had indicated approximately 200 potential burial sites at the grounds of the former Kamloops Indian Residential School, which operated from 1893 to 1969. The announcement drew international attention and renewed calls for investigation of residential school burial sites across Canada. As of subsequent reports, the band has emphasized that the findings are preliminary and that investigative work continues under its direction.
