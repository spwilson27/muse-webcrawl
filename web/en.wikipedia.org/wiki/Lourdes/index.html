Lourdes (Occitan: Lorda) is a market town in southwestern France, in the foothills of the Pyrenees, in the department of Hautes-Pyrénées. It is overlooked by a fortified castle, the Château fort de Lourdes, occupied since Roman times. Since the mid-19th century the town has been one of the world's most visited centres of Catholic pilgrimage, receiving several million visitors each year.

The apparitions

In 1858, a 14-year-old girl, Bernadette Soubirous, reported eighteen apparitions of a "lady" in the grotto of Massabielle, on the bank of the Gave de Pau. During the apparitions Bernadette uncovered a spring whose water is associated with reports of miraculous healings. In the sixteenth apparition, on 25 March 1858, the figure identified herself in the local dialect as "I am the Immaculate Conception", a dogma proclaimed four years earlier. After investigation, the Bishop of Tarbes recognised the apparitions in 1862. Bernadette was canonised in 1933.

Sanctuary

The Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes developed around the grotto, comprising the Upper Basilica (consecrated 1876), the Rosary Basilica, the underground Basilica of St Pius X (1958), and the Crowned Statue esplanade across the river. Pilgrims bathe in or collect water from the spring; an international medical bureau has examined claims of cures since 1883, and the Church has recognised around seventy healings as miraculous.

Economy

Pilgrimage dominates the town's economy: Lourdes has the second-largest hotel capacity in France after Paris, despite its small resident population. Organised pilgrimages, including those bringing sick and disabled pilgrims, run mainly from Easter to October.
