Clyfford Still Museum

The Clyfford Still Museum in Denver's Golden Triangle Creative District.

The Clyfford Still Museum is a museum in Denver, Colorado, dedicated to the work of the American abstract expressionist painter Clyfford Still (1904–1980). Opened in November 2011, the museum holds the great majority of Still's artistic output, making it one of the most comprehensive single-artist collections in the world.

History

Still stipulated in his will that his estate be given to an American city willing to establish a permanent museum devoted solely to his work. After his death, the Clyfford Still Estate — comprising some 3,125 works, roughly 94 percent of his total output — remained sealed for more than two decades. In 2004, Patricia Still, the artist's widow, agreed to donate the collection to the city of Denver, partly because the artist's archives had gone to the city as well. Patricia Still also bequeathed her own estate, including additional artworks and the artist's papers, to the museum.

Building

The museum building was designed by Brad Cloepfil of Allied Works Architecture. The two-story, 28,500-square-foot structure is built of textured cast-in-place concrete, with perforated concrete screens and wooden slats that filter natural light into the galleries. Nine interconnected galleries on the upper level display Still's work chronologically, from his early representational paintings of the 1920s through his mature jagged abstractions. The museum sits adjacent to the Denver Art Museum.

Collection

The collection includes approximately 830 paintings and more than 2,300 works on paper, spanning Still's career from the 1920s to 1980. Because Still sold relatively little of his work during his lifetime, the museum preserves an unusually complete record of an artist central to the development of Abstract Expressionism and the color field style. Rotating exhibitions draw on the holdings to trace Still's influence on contemporaries such as Jackson Pollock and Mark Rothko.
