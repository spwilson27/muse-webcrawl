2017 World Series

The 2017 World Series was the championship series of Major League Baseball's 2017 season, the 113th edition of the World Series. The American League champion Houston Astros defeated the National League champion Los Angeles Dodgers in seven games, winning the first championship in Astros franchise history.

Series details

Runner-upLos Angeles Dodgers (3)

VenuesDodger Stadium; Minute Maid Park

Background

Both clubs had won over 100 games in the regular season — Houston 101 and Los Angeles 104 — making it a highly anticipated matchup dubbed a clash of titans. The Astros, managed by A. J. Hinch, represented a city recovering from Hurricane Harvey and wore "Houston Strong" patches. The Dodgers, under Dave Roberts, were seeking their first title since 1988.

Summary

The series swung back and forth. Houston won a wild Game 2 in extra innings after multiple late home runs, and took Game 5 in Houston 13–12 in ten innings in one of the most dramatic games in World Series history. Los Angeles forced a Game 7 by winning Game 6. In the decider at Dodger Stadium on 1 November, the Astros scored five early runs off Yu Darvish and won 5–1, with Charlie Morton recording the final out. George Springer, who hit five home runs, was named MVP.

Sign-stealing scandal

In November 2019, reporting revealed that the 2017 Astros had used a camera-based electronic sign-stealing system during the season. MLB's investigation confirmed the scheme; manager Hinch and general manager Jeff Luhnow were suspended and then dismissed, and the club was fined and stripped of draft picks, though the championship was not vacated. The revelations permanently clouded the legacy of the 2017 title.
