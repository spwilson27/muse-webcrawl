Rus' people

The Rus' were a people in early medieval Eastern Europe to whom the name gave rise to the lands of Rus' and later Russia and Belarus. Historians generally agree that the Rus' were originally Norse — Scandinavian seafarers and traders, commonly identified with the Varangians — who came from what is now Sweden and settled among the East Slavic and Finnic peoples of the river routes between the Baltic and Black Seas.

Origins and name

The name is widely thought to derive from an Old Norse root related to "rowing" or "men who row", and it survives in Finnish and Estonian words for Sweden (Ruotsi, Rootsi). The Primary Chronicle describes the East Slavs inviting the Rus' prince Rurik to rule them in 862, an account debated in its details but long central to the "Normanist" view of Rus' origins — a view contested by anti-Normanist scholars who emphasize Slavic or other origins.

Assimilation

During the 10th and 11th centuries the Rus' elite intermarried with and was absorbed into the Slavic population, adopting the Slavic language and culture while giving their name to the polity of Kievan Rus'. Rus' traders and warriors travelled the Volga and Dnieper routes, served as mercenaries in Constantinople, and appear in Byzantine and Arabic sources such as the writings of Ibn Fadlan.
