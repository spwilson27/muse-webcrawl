Ornithopoda is a clade of ornithischian dinosaurs that began as small, cursorial bipedal herbivores and grew in size and numbers until they became one of the most successful groups of herbivores in the Cretaceous world. Their major evolutionary advantage was the progressive development of a chewing apparatus that became the most sophisticated ever developed by a non-mammalian vertebrate, rivalling that of modern mammals such as cows.

Description

Ornithopods ranged from the small bipedal Hypsilophodon to the large hadrosaurids (duck-billed dinosaurs) such as Edmontosaurus and Shantungosaurus, the latter exceeding 14 metres in length and weighing many tonnes. Many ornithopods could shift between bipedal and quadrupedal stances. They possessed beaked mouths for cropping vegetation, cheek pouches, and batteries of grinding teeth.

Classification

The group includes the iguanodontians and the hadrosaurids among its best-known members. Iguanodon was one of the first dinosaurs ever described, by Gideon Mantell in 1825. Ornithopods were the dominant large herbivores of Late Cretaceous North America and Asia before their extinction in the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event 66 million years ago.
