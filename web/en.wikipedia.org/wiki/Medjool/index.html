Medjool date

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Glossy Medjool dates piled in a market crate
Glossy Medjool dates piled in a market crate

The Medjool is a large, sweet cultivated variety of date (Phoenix dactylifera), originally from Morocco and now grown mainly in Israel, Palestine, Jordan and California. Called the 'king of dates', it is eaten soft and fresh rather than fully dried, with caramel-like flesh. Its modern history includes a rescue story: from a handful of offshoots smuggled out of disease-hit Morocco in the 1920s descends the entire Californian industry.

History

Medjools grew in Morocco's Tafilalt oases; when bayoud disease (a Fusarium wilt) devastated Moroccan groves in the early twentieth century, eleven offshoots were taken to the US in 1927 and nursed in Nevada and California quarantine — the ancestors of the Coachella Valley's date gardens. Cuttings reached Israel in the 1950s–60s, where the Jordan Valley and Arava became leading producers; Morocco has since replanted from its own survivors and imports.

Cultivation and trade

Labour-intensive (hand pollination, thinning, bagging, selective harvest passes), Medjools command premium prices and are a Ramadan staple and health-food staple alike (energy balls' natural sweetener). The West Bank's Palestinian Medjool sector is a flagship export and a friction point over water, land and labelling of settlement-grown produce in the Jordan Valley.
