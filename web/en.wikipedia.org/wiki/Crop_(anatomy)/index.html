Crop (anatomy)

A crop (also called a croup or craw) is a thin-walled expanded portion of the alimentary tract used for the storage of food before digestion. This anatomical structure is found in a wide variety of animals, including birds, gastropods, earthworms, leeches and insects.

In birds

In birds, the crop is an enlargement of the oesophagus located just before the entrance to the thorax. Food is swallowed and stored there, moistened and softened, before passing in small amounts to the proventriculus and gizzard for digestion. This allows a bird to eat a large meal quickly — reducing exposure to predators — and digest it later in safety. Parent birds of many species regurgitate food from the crop to feed their young.

In pigeons and doves, both parents produce "crop milk", a nutritious secretion of the crop lining rich in fat and protein, which is regurgitated to the nestlings during their first days of life. The hoatzin is unusual in having a greatly enlarged crop that houses bacterial fermentation, giving the bird a manure-like odour.

In other animals

Earthworms have a crop posterior to the oesophagus where ingested soil and plant material are stored before passing to the muscular gizzard. In insects such as bees and cockroaches, a diverticulum of the foregut serves as a crop for storing liquid food; in honey bees, nectar is carried back to the hive in the crop (honey stomach) and regurgitated. Leeches possess an extensive crop with pouches that can hold many times their body weight in blood.
