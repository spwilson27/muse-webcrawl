Duccio

Duccio di Buoninsegna
Born: c. 1255, Siena
Died: c. 1319, Siena
Known for: Painting
Movement: Sienese school, Gothic

Duccio di Buoninsegna (c. 1255 – c. 1319) was an Italian painter active in Siena in the late 13th and early 14th centuries. He is considered one of the founders of the Sienese school of painting and, alongside Giotto, one of the pivotal figures in the transition from medieval Byzantine conventions toward the greater naturalism of the Renaissance.

Life and works

Little is documented of Duccio's early life. His earliest surviving work is the Rucellai Madonna (c. 1285), commissioned for the church of Santa Maria Novella in Florence and long attributed to Cimabue. Duccio's workshop produced altarpieces, polyptychs and small devotional panels marked by graceful line, luminous colour and a new emotional tenderness in the rendering of the Virgin and Child.

The Maestà

His masterpiece is the Maestà (1308–1311), a vast double-sided altarpiece commissioned for Siena Cathedral. The front depicts the Madonna and Child enthroned in majesty, surrounded by saints and angels; the reverse carries 26 scenes of the Passion of Christ. On its completion in June 1311 the panel was carried in solemn procession from Duccio's workshop to the cathedral. Most of the altarpiece remains in the Museo dell'Opera del Duomo in Siena, though panels are dispersed among museums worldwide.

Legacy

Duccio's pupils and followers, including Segna di Buonaventura and the Lorenzetti brothers' generation, carried the Sienese style through the 14th century. His fusion of Byzantine iconography with Gothic grace and incipient humanism made Siena a rival to Florence in early Renaissance art.
