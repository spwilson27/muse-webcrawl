Big Four (banking)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The term Big Four is used in many countries to describe the four largest banking groups in that country. The identity of the group varies by jurisdiction, and the term is frequently used in discussions of market concentration and "too big to fail" institutions.

By country

China

China's "Big Four" are the four large state-owned commercial banks: the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (ICBC), the China Construction Bank, the Agricultural Bank of China and the Bank of China. These are among the largest banks in the world by total assets, with ICBC generally ranked the largest bank globally.

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom the term historically referred to the four great London clearing banks; today the group usually comprises Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds Banking Group and NatWest Group, which dominate British retail and commercial banking.

Australia

Australia's "four pillars" are the Commonwealth Bank, Westpac, National Australia Bank and ANZ. A longstanding "four pillars policy" prevents mergers among these four banks in order to preserve competition.

United States

In the United States the four largest banks by assets are JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Citigroup and Wells Fargo, which together hold a large share of the nation's deposits.
