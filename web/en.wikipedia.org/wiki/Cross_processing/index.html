Cross processing

Cross processing (sometimes abbreviated to Xpro) is the deliberate processing of photographic film in a chemical solution intended for a different type of film. The effect was discovered independently by many photographers, often by mistake, in the days of hand processing. The most common practice is to process colour slide (reversal) film designed for the E-6 process in C-41 colour negative chemistry, or vice versa.

Effects

Processing E-6 slide film in C-41 chemicals produces negatives with strong colour shifts, high contrast and saturation, often with green or yellow casts in the shadows and magenta highlights; processing C-41 film in E-6 yields low-contrast transparencies with unusual pastel tones. Results are unpredictable and vary with the film stock, exposure and laboratory, which is part of the technique's appeal in fine-art and fashion photography.

History and digital simulation

Cross processing became fashionable in the 1990s, appearing in advertising, music videos and films such as Three Kings (1999), which made extensive use of cross-processed and bleach-bypassed footage. With the decline of commercial film processing, the look is now more often simulated digitally: photo-editing software and apps, including early versions of Instagram, offer "cross process" filters that mimic the characteristic colour curves.
