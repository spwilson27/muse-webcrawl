Homogentisic acid

Homogentisic acid (2,5-dihydroxyphenylacetic acid) is a phenolic acid found in the metabolic breakdown of the amino acids phenylalanine and tyrosine. It is best known for its accumulation in the inborn error of metabolism known as alkaptonuria.

Metabolism

In the normal catabolic pathway, homogentisate is cleaved by the enzyme homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase to yield maleylacetoacetate, which proceeds onward to fumarate and acetoacetate. The enzyme is encoded by the HGD gene on chromosome 3.

Alkaptonuria

When homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase is deficient, homogentisic acid accumulates and is excreted in urine, which darkens on standing as the acid oxidises and polymerises. Over decades the dark pigment, often called ochronotic pigment, deposits in cartilage and connective tissue, causing ochronosis and a severe form of early arthritis. Alkaptonuria was one of the first conditions Archibald Garrod described as an "inborn error of metabolism" in 1902.

Occurrence

Homogentisic acid also occurs in nature, for example in the leaves and flowers of the strawberry tree (Arbutus unedo), where it contributes to the properties of arbutus honey.
