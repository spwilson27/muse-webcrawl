Angular momentum coupling

In quantum mechanics, angular momentum coupling is the procedure of constructing eigenstates of a total angular momentum operator from eigenstates of the individual angular momenta that make up a system. When two angular momenta j1 and j2 are combined, the total angular momentum quantum number j can take values from |j1 − j2| to j1 + j2 in integer steps.

Coupled and uncoupled bases

The expansion of a coupled state in terms of uncoupled product states is given by the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients. Coupling schemes arise whenever a system has several sources of angular momentum: for example, an electron's orbital angular momentum and spin combine (in LS coupling or jj coupling) to give the total angular momentum of an atom, which determines its spectroscopic term symbols.

Applications

Angular momentum coupling underlies atomic and nuclear shell-model calculations, the classification of multiparticle states, and the computation of transition probabilities. Related mathematical tools include the Wigner 3-j, 6-j and 9-j symbols, which express recoupling coefficients for two, three and four angular momenta respectively.
