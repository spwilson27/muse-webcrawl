Erbil (Kurdish: هەولێر, Hawlêr; Arabic: أربيل, Arbīl), also rendered Arbil, is the capital and largest city of the Kurdistan Region of Iraq, and the administrative centre of Erbil Governorate. It lies roughly 350 kilometres north of Baghdad. The city is among the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in the world, with settlement on the citadel mound reaching back at least to the 5th millennium BC.

History

Known in ancient sources as Arbela and Urbilum, the city was a centre of the Assyrian Empire and later of the worship of Ishtar; the famous Battle of Gaugamela (331 BC), in which Alexander the Great defeated Darius III, was fought nearby and is often misnamed for Arbela. Erbil passed through Persian, Arab, Mongol and Ottoman hands — it formed part of the Ottoman vilayet of Mosul — before becoming part of the Kingdom and then Republic of Iraq. Since 1970, and effectively since 1991–92, it has been the capital of the autonomous Kurdistan Region, seat of the Kurdistan Regional Government and parliament.

Landmarks and economy

The Erbil Citadel, a tell rising some 25–32 metres above the plain, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2014. Below it lies the Qaysari bazaar. Since 2003 the city has seen extensive construction, oil-driven investment, the Erbil International Airport, and large communities of displaced people who arrived during the war against ISIL, whose forces were halted near the city in 2014.
