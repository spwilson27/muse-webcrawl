Tatra National Park (Polish: Tatrzański Park Narodowy) is a national park located in the Tatra Mountains in Tatra County, in the Lesser Poland Voivodeship, Małopolska region of central-southern Poland. The park is headquartered in the town of Zakopane. Together with the adjoining Slovak Tatra National Park it forms a UNESCO biosphere reserve designated in 1993.

Geography

The Polish Tatras comprise the High Tatras, with sharp granite ridges and glacial lakes such as Morskie Oko, and the Western Tatras, built mainly of sedimentary rocks with extensive cave systems. The highest summit in Poland, Rysy (2,499 m), lies on the border with Slovakia within the park. About 270 km of marked hiking trails cross the park.

Flora and fauna

Vegetation follows alpine zones from mixed forest through dwarf pine to alpine meadows and rocky peaks. Characteristic animals include the Tatra chamois, alpine marmot, brown bear, lynx, wolf and golden eagle. The park also protects endemic and relict plant species surviving from glacial periods.

Tourism

The park receives roughly three to four million visitors a year, making it one of Poland's most visited national parks. Attractions include the cable car to Kasprowy Wierch, mountain refuges, and climbs on Orla Perć, an exposed ridge path in the High Tatras.
