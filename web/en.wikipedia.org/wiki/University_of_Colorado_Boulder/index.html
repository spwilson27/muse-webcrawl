The University of Colorado Boulder (CU Boulder, CU, or Colorado) is a public research university in Boulder, Colorado, United States. Founded in 1876, five months before Colorado became a state, it is the flagship campus of the University of Colorado system and one of the founding members of the Association of American Universities.

History

The university opened in 1877 with 44 students in Old Main, the first building on campus. It expanded through the 20th century, developing strengths in the physical sciences thanks in part to the arrival of federal laboratories in Boulder, including the National Bureau of Standards (now NIST) and the National Center for Atmospheric Research. The university's architect Charles Klauder established its distinctive Tuscan Vernacular Revival style of rough sandstone and red tile roofs in the 1920s.

Academics and research

CU Boulder comprises nine colleges and schools, including the College of Arts and Sciences, College of Engineering and Applied Science, Leeds School of Business, and Colorado Law. It is classified among "R1: Very high research activity" universities, with annual research expenditures around $600 million. Affiliated institutes include the Laboratory for Atmospheric and Space Physics (LASP), JILA (a joint institute with NIST in quantum and atomic physics), and the Cooperative Institute for Research in Environmental Sciences (CIRES). Five Nobel laureates have been associated with the university, and its aerospace engineering program benefits from Colorado having the nation's largest concentration of aerospace-related federal labs.

Athletics

The Colorado Buffaloes compete in NCAA Division I, returning to the Big 12 Conference in 2024 after a period in the Pac-12. The football team plays at Folsom Field and won a share of the 1990 national championship; the live buffalo mascot Ralphie, led onto the field by student handlers, is one of college football's best-known traditions. The ski team has won more than 20 national championships.
