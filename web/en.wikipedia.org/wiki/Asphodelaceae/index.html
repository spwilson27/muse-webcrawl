Asphodelaceae

Scientific name: Asphodelaceae (Juss.)

Rank: Family

Order: Asparagales

Genera: approx. 40

Asphodelaceae is a family of flowering plants in the order Asparagales. The family has a wide but scattered distribution throughout the tropics and temperate zones; many species are cultivated as ornamentals. Recognition of the family varies between classification systems: in APG IV it is treated broadly, with three subfamilies — Asphodeloideae, Hemerocallidoideae and Xanthorrhoeoideae.

Genera and distribution

The family includes well-known plants such as Aloe (including Aloe vera), Haworthia, Asphodelus, Bulbine, Kniphofia (red hot pokers), Hemerocallis (daylilies), Phormium (New Zealand flax) and the Australian grasstrees, Xanthorrhoea. The greatest diversity is in southern Africa, with centres also in Australia and the Mediterranean.

Description

Members are herbs, shrubs or (in the grasstrees) tree-like perennials, frequently succulent-leaved xerophytes of arid regions. Flowers are six-tepalled, usually in racemes, and the fruit is a capsule or berry. Many contain anthraquinones such as aloin, used in traditional medicine and cosmetics.
