Sarasvati River

The Sarasvati River (Sanskrit: Sarasvatī) is a river celebrated in the hymns of the Rigveda, where it is praised as a great and sacred stream flowing from the mountains to the sea. In later Hindu tradition it became identified with the goddess Sarasvati and was said to flow underground, joining the Ganges and Yamuna at the Triveni Sangam in Prayagraj.

Identification

Many scholars identify the Vedic Sarasvati with the seasonal Ghaggar-Hakra river system of Haryana, Punjab, and Rajasthan, whose dry paleochannels are lined with numerous Indus Valley Civilization sites. Satellite imagery has revealed ancient channel systems in the Thar Desert region consistent with a formerly larger river, fed in part by Himalayan tributaries that later shifted to the Indus and Ganges systems.

Decline

Geological and climatic changes — including the weakening of the monsoon after about 2000 BCE and the capture of tributaries such as the Sutlej and Yamuna by neighbouring river systems — are thought to have caused the river to diminish and eventually dry up in its lower course, a process some link to the decline of Harappan settlements along its banks. The question remains debated among archaeologists and geologists.
