Ernest Augustus (German: Ernst August; 5 June 1771 – 18 November 1851) was King of Hanover from 20 June 1837 until his death in 1851. As the fifth son of King George III of the United Kingdom and Hanover, he initially seemed far from any throne, but the death of his elder brother William IV with no surviving legitimate issue brought him the Hanoverian crown, since Hanover's Salic law barred the accession of Queen Victoria. His accession ended the personal union of Britain and Hanover that had lasted since 1714.

Early life and British career

Created Duke of Cumberland and Teviotdale in 1799, Ernest Augustus served as a cavalry officer in the French Revolutionary Wars and was wounded at Tournai. In Britain he was a prominent and controversial ultra-Tory figure, opposing Catholic emancipation and parliamentary reform, and rumours linked him to several scandals. He married Frederica of Mecklenburg-Strelitz in 1815.

King of Hanover

On his accession Ernest Augustus revoked the liberal Hanoverian constitution of 1833, provoking the protest of the Göttingen Seven, including the Brothers Grimm, whom he dismissed and exiled. His conservative rule was unpopular in Germany but he restored Hanoverian finances and in 1840 promulgated a more authoritarian constitution. He was succeeded by his blind son, George V.
