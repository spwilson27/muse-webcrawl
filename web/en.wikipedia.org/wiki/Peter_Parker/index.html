Peter Benjamin Parker, also known by his superhero alias Spider-Man, is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer-editor Stan Lee and artist Steve Ditko, he first appeared in Amazing Fantasy #15 (cover-dated August 1962), and is one of the most popular and commercially successful superheroes in the world.

Character biography

Peter Parker is a shy, science-minded high school student from Queens, New York, raised by his Aunt May and Uncle Ben after his parents' deaths. Bitten by a radioactive spider at a science exhibition, he gains spider-like abilities: proportionate strength and agility, the ability to cling to walls, and a precognitive "spider-sense." Initially using his powers for personal gain, Peter fails to stop a burglar who later murders Uncle Ben, teaching him the defining lesson of the series: "With great power comes great responsibility."

As Spider-Man, Peter battles villains including the Green Goblin, Doctor Octopus, Venom, and the Kingpin, while struggling with the ordinary problems of work, school, money, and relationships — a grounded, relatable approach that was revolutionary for superhero comics of the era. Supporting characters include Mary Jane Watson, Gwen Stacy, J. Jonah Jameson of the Daily Bugle, and Harry Osborn.

In other media

Spider-Man has appeared in numerous animated series, video games, and blockbuster film franchises, portrayed in live action by Tobey Maguire, Andrew Garfield, and Tom Holland, and in animation — notably Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse (2018) and its sequels, which centre on Miles Morales with Peter Parker as mentor. The character is Marvel's flagship hero and one of its most valuable media properties.
