Green Revolution

The Green Revolution was a period of agricultural transformation from the 1940s to the late 1960s (and beyond) in which high-yielding crop varieties, synthetic fertilisers, pesticides and irrigation dramatically increased food production, especially in Mexico, India and the Philippines.

Period1940s–1970s

History

Norman Borlaug, working in Mexico with Rockefeller Foundation support, bred short-stemmed, disease-resistant wheat that did not lodge under fertiliser. India adopted the varieties in the 1960s under M. S. Swaminathan, becoming self-sufficient in grain. Borlaug received the 1970 Nobel Peace Prize; the revolution is credited with saving over a billion people from starvation.

Criticism

Critics note environmental costs — water depletion, soil and chemical pollution, loss of crop diversity — and social inequalities favouring large farmers. A proposed 'Evergreen Revolution' seeks sustainable intensification. See Norman Borlaug and Great Acceleration.
