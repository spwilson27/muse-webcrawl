Xerxes (opera)

Xerxes (Italian: Serse, HWV 40) is an opera seria in three acts by George Frideric Handel. It was first performed in London on 15 April 1738 at the King's Theatre, Haymarket. The libretto, adapted by an unknown hand from earlier libretti by Nicolò Minato and Silvio Stampiglia, is loosely based on the life of Xerxes I of Persia.

Performance history

The opera was not a success at its premiere, receiving only five performances. It was revived in the 20th century and is now among Handel's most frequently performed operas, prized for its blend of comedy and serious drama, unusual for opera seria of the period.

Famous arias

The opera opens with one of Handel's best-known arias, "Ombra mai fu", in which Xerxes sings of his beloved plane tree. Often arranged as an instrumental piece known as Handel's "Largo", the aria has become one of the most famous melodies in classical music. Other notable arias include "Va godendo" and Arsace's music throughout the drama.

Synopsis

King Xerxes desires Romilda, who loves his brother Arsace; Arsace's former lover Atalanta also schemes for his affection, while Xerxes' fiancée Amastre, disguised as a soldier, plots against his infidelity. After a series of intrigues, forged letters, and near-tragedies, the couples are reconciled and Xerxes returns to Amastre.

Roles

Principal roles include Xerxes (originally a castrato role, now sung by a countertenor or mezzo-soprano), Arsace, Amastre, Romilda, Atalanta, and the comic servant Elviro.
