Hofburg

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Hofburg is the former principal imperial palace of the Habsburg dynasty in Vienna, Austria. Built in the 13th century and expanded over the following centuries, it served as the winter residence of the Austrian emperors while Schönbrunn Palace was their summer home. Since 1946 it has been the official residence and workplace of the President of Austria.

History and layout

The palace complex grew organically with each ruler: the medieval Schweizerhof contains the Imperial Chapel and Treasury (Schatzkammer), which holds the Imperial Crown of the Holy Roman Empire and the Austrian Imperial Crown. The Renaissance and Baroque Stallburg houses part of the Spanish Riding School and its Lipizzaner horses. The Amalienburg, Leopoldine Wing, Imperial Chancellery Wing, and the Michaelertrakt face the Michaelerplatz, while the monumental curved Neue Burg (1881–1913) fronts the Heldenplatz.

Today

The Hofburg houses several museums, including the Sisi Museum and Imperial Apartments, the Imperial Silver Collection, and the Weltmuseum Wien in the Neue Burg. The Austrian National Library's State Hall is in the complex, and the palace hosts the Vienna Ball season's events and numerous conferences. On Heldenplatz in 1938 Adolf Hitler proclaimed Austria's annexation (Anschluss) to a large crowd — a moment still commemorated in Austrian public memory.
