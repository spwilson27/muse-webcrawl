Medicine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A doctor with a stethoscope examining a patient in a clinic]

Medicine is the science and practice of caring for patients, managing the diagnosis, prognosis, prevention, treatment and palliation of their injury or disease. It encompasses a wide range of health-care practices evolved to maintain and restore health.

Medical practice combines science — biology, chemistry, physics and the social sciences — with the art of applying knowledge to individual patients. Contemporary medicine uses biomedical sciences, genetics and medical technology alongside clinical judgement.

History

Prehistoric medicine drew on plants, animal materials and ritual. Systematic traditions developed in ancient Egypt, India (Ayurveda), China (traditional Chinese medicine) and the Greek world, where Hippocrates and later Galen shaped Western practice for centuries. The scientific transformation of medicine came in the 19th and 20th centuries with germ theory, anaesthesia, vaccination and antibiotics.

Branches

Clinical practice is organised into specialties such as internal medicine, surgery, paediatrics, obstetrics and gynaecology, psychiatry, radiology and pathology, among many others. Medical education typically involves a degree followed by supervised residency training.
