Newspapers in Greece

This article lists newspapers published in Greece. The Greek press has a long tradition of partisan alignment, with major dailies historically associated with political parties and prominent publishing families. Most national newspapers are published in Athens and circulate throughout the country.

Major national dailies

Among the best-known Greek newspapers are Kathimerini, a conservative broadsheet founded in 1919; Ta Nea, founded in 1931 and long one of the country's best-selling papers; To Vima, a Sunday-oriented title founded in 1922; Eleftherotypia, a centre-left paper founded in 1975 that closed in 2011 amid the financial crisis; Efimerida ton Syntakton, a cooperative left-wing daily launched in 2012; and Rizospastis, the newspaper of the Communist Party of Greece, founded in 1916. Financial papers such as Naftemporiki (founded 1924) cover business news, while sports dailies form a large segment of the market.

History and challenges

The first Greek newspapers appeared in the early nineteenth century, during and after the Greek War of Independence. The press endured censorship under the 1967–1974 military junta. Since the 2010s, the Greek debt crisis, collapsing advertising revenue, and the shift to online media have sharply reduced print circulation and led to the closure or restructuring of several historic titles, while digital editions and news websites have grown in importance.
