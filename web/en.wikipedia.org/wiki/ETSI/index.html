ETSI

Full name: European Telecommunications Standards Institute
Founded: 1988
Headquarters: Sophia Antipolis, France
Members: ~900 organisations from over 60 countries

The European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) is an independent, non-profit standardisation organization in the field of information and communications. ETSI supports the development and testing of global technical standards for ICT-enabled systems, applications and services. It is one of three bodies officially recognised by the European Union as a European Standards Organisation (alongside CEN and CENELEC), and its standards underpin the EU's harmonised standards system for the single market.

History

ETSI was created in 1988 by the European Conference of Postal and Telecommunications Administrations (CEPT), in response to proposals from the European Commission, and is headquartered in Sophia Antipolis, France. It has some 900 member organisations worldwide, drawn from manufacturers, network operators, service providers, administrations, research bodies, and users.

Standards

ETSI is best known for producing the standards behind major global technologies, including GSM (developed initially within ETSI's Groupe Spécial Mobile), DECT cordless telephony, TETRA professional mobile radio, DVB digital broadcasting (with partners), and the SIM/USIM card specifications used in mobile phones. Working with partner standards bodies through the 3GPP organizational partnership (which it co-founded in 1998), ETSI publishes the 3GPP mobile standards (UMTS, LTE, 5G NR) as ETSI technical specifications. Other work areas include network functions virtualisation (NFV), multi-access edge computing (MEC), cybersecurity, electronic signatures, intelligent transport systems, and quantum-safe cryptography. All ETSI standards are freely downloadable.
