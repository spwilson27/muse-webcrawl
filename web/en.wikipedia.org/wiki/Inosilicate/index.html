Inosilicate

Inosilicates, or chain silicates, are silicate minerals whose structures are built from silica tetrahedra linked in chains. They form one of the main structural classes in the classification of silicate minerals, which make up most of the Earth's crust.

Structure and groups

In single-chain inosilicates, each tetrahedron shares two of its oxygen atoms with neighbours, giving a repeating SiO3 unit; the pyroxene group, including augite and enstatite, has this structure. In double-chain inosilicates, two chains are cross-linked so that tetrahedra alternately share two or three oxygens, giving a Si4O11 unit; the amphibole group, including hornblende and the asbestos minerals, belongs here. The chain structure explains the prismatic crystal habit and the prominent cleavage of these minerals — in amphiboles at about 56° and 124°, in pyroxenes near 90°.

Occurrence

Pyroxenes and amphiboles are major rock-forming minerals in igneous and metamorphic rocks, from basalt and gabbro to amphibolite.
