The Mesoamerican Barrier Reef System (MBRS), also known as the Mesoamerican Reef or the Great Mayan Reef, is a marine region that stretches over about 1,000 kilometres (620 mi) along the coasts of four countries in the western Caribbean Sea: Mexico (Quintana Roo), Belize, Guatemala and Honduras, from Isla Contoy at the tip of the Yucatán Peninsula south to the Bay Islands of Honduras. It is the largest barrier reef system in the Western Hemisphere and the second largest in the world, after Australia's Great Barrier Reef.

Ecology

The system includes barrier reefs, fringing reefs, offshore atolls (three of the Caribbean's four atolls lie off Belize), mangrove forests, coastal lagoons and seagrass beds. It is home to more than 65 species of stony coral, over 500 species of fish, populations of West Indian manatees, sea turtles, queen conch, spiny lobster, and one of the world's largest aggregations of whale sharks near Isla Contoy and Gladden Spit. The Belize Barrier Reef, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the Great Blue Hole, a giant marine sinkhole off Belize, are its best-known features.

Conservation

The reef supports the tourism and fishing economies of the region but faces threats from coastal development, pollution, overfishing, coral bleaching and the invasive lionfish. In 1997 the four countries signed the Tulum Declaration committing to its conservation, and the Healthy Reefs for Healthy People Initiative publishes regular report cards on reef health. Belize removed its portion of the reef from UNESCO's endangered list in 2018 after banning offshore oil exploration.
