River Soar
LocationLeicestershire and Nottinghamshire, England
SourceNear Monks Kirby, Warwickshire
MouthRiver Trent at Trent Lock, near Sawley

The River Soar is a major tributary of the River Trent in the East Midlands of England. It is 59 miles (95 km) long and flows from the south of Leicestershire through the city of Leicester to its confluence with the Trent near Sawley. Its name is of pre-English, probably Celtic, origin. For much of its length the Soar is paralleled by the Grand Union Canal, with which it shares its channel through Leicester.

Course

The Soar rises near Monks Kirby in Warwickshire and flows north-east past Sharnford and Narborough, then through Leicester, where it passes the King Power Stadium, the site of the Roman town of Ratae Corieltauvorum, and the National Space Centre, just east of the city centre. In Leicester the river and canal merge for several miles (the "Mile Straight"). North of the city it passes Watermead Country Park, created from flooded gravel pits beside the river, then Mountsorrel and Barrow upon Soar (a village named for the river), Loughborough, and Kegworth, before entering the Trent at Trent Lock, close to the Nottinghamshire border.

Navigation and history

The Soar was made navigable to Leicester in the late 18th century as part of the Leicestershire and Loughborough navigations, later absorbed into the Grand Union Canal system; commercial carrying continued into the 20th century. King Richard III's body was, according to tradition, thrown into the Soar from Bow Bridge in Leicester after the Battle of Bosworth in 1485; later accounts hold that his remains were in fact buried in Greyfriars church, where his skeleton was rediscovered in 2012 beneath a car park. The wide Soar valley floods readily in winter, and settlements along it have extensive flood meadows; severe flooding in the city in 1947 and elsewhere remains part of local record.

Tributaries

Tributaries include the River Wreake near Syston, the River Sence, the Rothley Brook, the Kingston Brook, and Black Brook near Loughborough.
