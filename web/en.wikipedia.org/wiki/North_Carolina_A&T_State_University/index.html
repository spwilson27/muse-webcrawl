North Carolina A&T State University

North Carolina Agricultural and Technical State University (North Carolina A&T State University, or simply A&T) is a public, historically Black land-grant research university in Greensboro, North Carolina. Founded in 1891 as the Agricultural and Mechanical College for the Colored Race, it is a constituent institution of the University of North Carolina system and has grown into the largest historically Black university (HBCU) in the United States by enrolment.

History

The college was established under the Second Morrill Act of 1890 and opened in Raleigh before moving to Greensboro in 1893. It became a university in 1967 and joined the University of North Carolina system in 1972.

Civil rights

A&T students played a central role in the civil rights movement: on 1 February 1960, four freshmen — the "Greensboro Four" — began the sit-in at the Woolworth lunch counter in Greensboro that helped ignite the nationwide sit-in movement. The February One monument on campus commemorates them.

Academics

The university is particularly noted for its College of Engineering, one of the leading producers of African-American engineers, and for its agricultural research. Its athletic teams, the Aggies, compete in NCAA Division I.
