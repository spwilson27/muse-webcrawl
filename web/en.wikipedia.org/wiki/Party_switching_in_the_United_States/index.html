Party switching in the United States

Party switching in the United States is the practice of an elected official or candidate changing political party affiliation, whether between elections or during a term of office. Switches can alter the balance of power in legislatures and often reflect ideological realignment, constituency change or personal ambition.

Notable examples

Major historical switches include Strom Thurmond, who left the Democrats for the Republicans in 1964 amid the civil-rights realignment of the South, and the long movement of conservative Southern Democrats ("boll weevils") toward the Republican Party. At the congressional level, Senator Jim Jeffords' 2001 departure from the Republicans to sit as an independent flipped control of the US Senate. Arlen Specter switched from Republican to Democrat in 2009, and Charlie Crist changed affiliation several times in Florida politics. State legislatures see frequent switches, sometimes dozens in a decade, occasionally shifting chamber control.

Causes and effects

Analysts attribute switching to ideological drift of the parties, primary challenges, redistricting, and voters' changing loyalties. Switchers face accusations of opportunism but may argue their party changed rather than they did. A related phenomenon is voters changing party registration, tracked in states with party registration.
