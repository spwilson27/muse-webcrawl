Valacyclovir, sold under the brand name Valtrex among others, is an antiviral medication used to treat infections caused by herpes viruses, including herpes simplex (cold sores and genital herpes), herpes zoster (shingles), and cytomegalovirus infection, particularly in people with weakened immune systems or after organ transplantation. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Pharmacology

Valacyclovir is the L-valyl ester prodrug of aciclovir. After oral administration it is rapidly converted by first-pass intestinal and hepatic metabolism to aciclovir and the amino acid valine, achieving aciclovir bioavailability of about 54 percent — several times higher than oral aciclovir itself — which permits less frequent dosing. Aciclovir is then phosphorylated by viral thymidine kinase and cellular enzymes to its active triphosphate form, which inhibits viral DNA synthesis.

Uses and side effects

Typical uses include treatment and suppression of genital herpes, treatment of cold sores, treatment of shingles, and prevention of cytomegalovirus disease after kidney transplant. Common side effects are headache and nausea; high doses, especially in dehydrated or elderly patients, can cause kidney injury, and rare neurotoxic effects are reported. Doses must be reduced in renal impairment. The drug was patented in the 1980s by Burroughs Wellcome (Glaxo Wellcome) and came into medical use in 1995.
