Tamas (philosophy)

TraditionSāṃkhya, Hindu philosophy, Ayurveda
MeaningDarkness, inertia, heaviness
One ofThe three guṇas (with sattva and rajas)

Tamas (Sanskrit: तमस्, "darkness") is one of the three guṇas, or fundamental qualities of nature (prakṛti), in Sāṃkhya philosophy and later Hindu thought. It is associated with inertia, heaviness, resistance, obscurity, and ignorance, in contrast to sattva (clarity, purity, harmony) and rajas (activity, passion, motion).

In the Bhagavad Gita

The Bhagavad Gita (chapter 14) describes tamas as born of ignorance, binding the embodied soul through negligence, laziness, and sleep. Foods, sacrifices, knowledge, and actions are each classified elsewhere in the text according to the guṇa that predominates in them; tamasic food, for example, is stale, tasteless, and impure.

In Ayurveda and daily life

In Ayurvedic psychology, an excess of tamas manifests as lethargy, confusion, depression, and attachment to unhealthy habits. Practices such as exercise, fresh food, light, and disciplined routine are recommended to reduce tamas and cultivate sattva.
