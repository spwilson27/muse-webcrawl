Urethra

The urethra (from Greek ourḗthrā) is a tube that connects the urinary bladder to the urinary meatus, through which urine is removed from the body in both sexes. In males, the urethra additionally serves as the passageway for semen during ejaculation. It is part of the urinary system.

Structure

Female urethra

In human females, the urethra is about 4 cm (1.6 in) long and exits the body between the clitoris and the vagina, extending from the internal to the external urethral orifice. Because of its short length, the female urethra is more susceptible to bacterial colonization, which is one reason urinary tract infections are more common in women.

Male urethra

In human males, the urethra is approximately 20 cm (8 in) long and is traditionally divided into four parts: the preprostatic (intramural) part, the prostatic urethra, the membranous urethra, and the spongy (penile) urethra, which runs the length of the penis and opens at the external urethral meatus at the tip of the glans.

Function

The urethra provides an exit for urine from the bladder. In males it has the additional reproductive function of conveying semen. Sphincter muscles — the internal urethral sphincter at the bladder neck and the external urethral sphincter — control the flow of urine.

Clinical significance

Inflammation of the urethra is called urethritis. Other conditions include urethral stricture, hypospadias, and urethral cancer. Catheterization of the bladder is performed through the urethra.
