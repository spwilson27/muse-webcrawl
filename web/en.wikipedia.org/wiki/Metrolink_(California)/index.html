Metrolink is a commuter rail system in Southern California, operated by the Southern California Regional Rail Authority (SCRRA), a joint powers authority of the transportation agencies of Los Angeles, Orange, Riverside, San Bernardino, and Ventura counties. Service began on 26 October 1992. The network radiates from Los Angeles Union Station and covers roughly 545 route miles, making it one of the largest commuter rail systems in the United States.

Lines

Metrolink operates eight lines: the Antelope Valley, Arrow, Burbank–Bob Hope Airport (part of the Ventura County Line), Inland Empire–Orange County, Orange County, Riverside, San Bernardino, and Ventura County lines. Trains run primarily on tracks owned by the SCRRA or shared with the BNSF Railway and Union Pacific freight railroads, and with Amtrak's Pacific Surfliner.

History and safety

The system grew out of Los Angeles County's purchase of Southern Pacific trackage in the early 1990s. Metrolink was the operator of the trains involved in two major accidents — the 2005 Glendale derailment caused by a vehicle on the tracks, and the 2008 Chatsworth collision, in which a Metrolink train collided head-on with a freight train, killing 25 people. The Chatsworth crash spurred the federal mandate for positive train control, which Metrolink was the first US commuter railroad to implement system-wide.

In 2024 Metrolink restructured its schedules under the SCORE programme, moving toward more frequent, all-day service rather than a purely peak-hour commuter model.
