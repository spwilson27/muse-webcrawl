Hurrem Sultan (Turkish: Hürrem Sultan; c. 1504 – 15 April 1558), known in Europe as Roxelana, was the chief consort and legal wife of the Ottoman sultan Suleiman the Magnificent. She rose from slavery in the imperial harem to become one of the most powerful women in Ottoman history, inaugurating the period known as the Sultanate of Women.

Origins

Hurrem was probably born in Ruthenia, then part of the Kingdom of Poland, possibly in the town of Rohatyn. She is believed to have been the daughter of an Orthodox Christian priest and to have been captured in a Crimean Tatar slave raid as a teenager, passing through the slave market of Kaffa before entering the sultan's harem in Constantinople. Her birth name is uncertain; European sources call her Roxelana, "the Ruthenian maiden," while Ottoman tradition associates her with the name Aleksandra Lisowska.

Rise to power

Hurrem attracted Suleiman's attention and became his favorite (haseki), displacing the earlier favorite Mahidevran. In an extraordinary breach of Ottoman custom, Suleiman freed her and married her legally, probably around 1533–1534, making her the first former slave to become the lawful wife of a sultan. She remained at court in the capital rather than accompanying her sons to provincial governorships, another precedent-breaking step that increased her influence.

Political influence

Hurrem corresponded with foreign rulers, including Sigismund II Augustus of Poland, and acted as an adviser to Suleiman. Her rivalry with the grand vizier Ibrahim Pasha, executed in 1536, and her role in the events surrounding the execution of Şehzade Mustafa, Suleiman's eldest son, in 1553, have been debated by historians. Her son-in-law Rüstem Pasha twice served as grand vizier. Of her sons, Selim eventually succeeded Suleiman as Selim II after defeating his brother Bayezid in a civil war.

Charitable works

Hurrem founded numerous charitable endowments, including the Haseki Sultan Complex in Istanbul — a mosque, madrasa, primary school, hospital (darüşşifa), and public kitchen designed by the architect Sinan — and foundations in Jerusalem, where she built the Imaret, a large soup kitchen for the poor. She also commissioned baths in Istanbul, including the Haseki Hürrem Sultan Hamamı near Hagia Sophia.

Death and legacy

Hurrem died in 1558 and was buried in a mausoleum at the Süleymaniye Mosque. She remains a famous figure in Turkish and Ukrainian culture, the subject of novels, plays, operas, and the television series Muhteşem Yüzyıl ("Magnificent Century").
