Pompano dolphinfish

The pompano dolphinfish (Coryphaena equiselis) is a species of surface-dwelling ray-finned fish found in tropical and subtropical waters worldwide. It is one of only two members of the family Coryphaenidae, the other being the larger and better-known mahi-mahi (common dolphinfish, Coryphaena hippurus).

Identification

The pompano dolphinfish resembles a juvenile mahi-mahi and is frequently mistaken for one. It has a deeper, more compressed body relative to its length, the greatest body depth occurring at or in front of the pectoral fins, and a distinctive broad, square-ended tongue patch of teeth. Adults usually reach about 50 cm, occasionally up to 75 cm, far smaller than the mahi-mahi.

Distribution and behaviour

It is an oceanic, pelagic species that often travels in schools and associates with floating debris and sargassum weed lines. Like its relative it is a fast-growing, short-lived fish feeding on small fish, squid and crustaceans.

Fisheries

The species is taken incidentally in tuna and mahi-mahi fisheries and is occasionally sold fresh, where it is valued as food, although it is usually marketed without distinction from the common dolphinfish.
