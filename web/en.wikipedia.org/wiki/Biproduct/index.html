Biproduct

In category theory and related fields of mathematics, a biproduct is a construction that is simultaneously a product and a coproduct of a family of objects in a category. In a category with a zero object, a biproduct of objects A and B comes equipped with projection morphisms p1: A ⊕ B → A and p2: A ⊕ B → B and injection morphisms i1: A → A ⊕ B and i2: B → A ⊕ B satisfying the natural identity relations, so that the object behaves both as a product and as a coproduct.

Examples

The classic example is the category of vector spaces (or modules over a ring, or abelian groups), where the biproduct of two objects is their direct sum, which is at once the cartesian product and the disjoint-style coproduct of the underlying structures. In the category of groups, by contrast, the product and coproduct (direct product and free product) differ, so finite biproducts do not generally exist there.

Properties

A category in which every finite family of objects has a biproduct is called a semiadditive category; its hom-sets carry a commutative monoid structure, and morphisms can be "added". When biproducts exist, morphisms between biproducts can be represented as matrices, and composition corresponds to matrix multiplication. Additive and abelian categories are fundamental examples, and the biproduct is the categorical underpinning of block-matrix algebra.
