Apple IIc

The Apple IIc (1984), Apple's first attempt at a portable computer.

The Apple IIc is a personal computer introduced by Apple Computer in April 1984. It is a compact, portable version of the Apple IIe, and the "c" in the name stands for "compact". Although it was marketed as a portable computer — it has a built-in carrying handle and weighs about 7.5 pounds (3.4 kg) — it lacks a built-in battery and display, and so is perhaps better described as a "luggable" machine. It was the first computer sold by Apple under its "Snow White" design language, created by the Frog Design firm.

Specifications

The IIc is based on the 65C02 processor running at 1 MHz, with 128 KB of RAM, a built-in 5.25-inch floppy disk drive, and built-in support for a mouse, a modem, and 80-column text — features that were optional add-ons for the Apple IIe. It has no internal expansion slots; instead, all peripherals connect through ports on the rear of the machine. The computer runs ProDOS and AppleSoft BASIC in ROM.

Reception and revisions

The IIc was a commercial success at launch, with Apple preparing to sell 100,000 units by the end of 1984. Several revisions followed, including models with a memory expansion slot, an updated UniDisk 3.5 drive controller, and the Apple IIc Plus of 1988, which replaced the 5.25-inch drive with a 3.5-inch drive and used a faster 4 MHz processor. Production of the IIc line ended in 1990.
