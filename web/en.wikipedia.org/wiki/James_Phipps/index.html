James Phipps

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

James Phipps
Born: 1788
Died: 1853
Known for: First person vaccinated against smallpox (1796)

James Phipps (1788–1853) was an English boy who, at the age of eight, became the first person known to be vaccinated against smallpox, in Edward Jenner's experiment of 14 May 1796.

Phipps was the son of Jenner's gardener in Berkeley, Gloucestershire. Jenner inoculated him with pus from cowpox blisters on the hand of milkmaid Sarah Nelmes. Phipps developed a slight fever and discomfort and recovered within days. On 1 July Jenner variolated him with smallpox material — no disease followed, and later challenges also failed to infect him. Jenner concluded that cowpox inoculation protected against smallpox, and the result became the foundation of his 1798 Inquiry and of vaccination generally (see vaccine).

Phipps lived a quiet life as a gardener in Berkeley; Jenner later gave him a cottage. Modern commentators note that the experiment would raise serious ethical questions today, though by the standards of the variolation era the procedure Jenner tested was far safer than the existing practice (see variolation).
