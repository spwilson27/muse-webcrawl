Robert Angus Smith

Born15 February 1817, Glasgow, Scotland
Died12 May 1884, Colwyn Bay, Wales
Known forDiscovering and naming acid rain
FieldsChemistry, environmental science

Robert Angus Smith FRS (15 February 1817 – 12 May 1884) was a Scottish chemist who pioneered the study of air pollution and coined the term "acid rain". After studying in Glasgow and Giessen, where he worked in Justus von Liebig's circle, he settled in Manchester as a consulting chemist and became the first Chief Inspector under the Alkali Act of 1863, the world's first national air-pollution inspectorate.

Acid rain

In his great report Air and Rain: The Beginnings of a Chemical Climatology (1872), Smith analysed rainwater across Britain and showed that precipitation near industrial Manchester was markedly acidic from sulphur compounds released by coal burning, damaging plants, buildings and fabrics. His work founded atmospheric chemistry and anticipated modern environmental science by a century.

Other work

Smith also advised on water supply and sanitation, studied disinfectants — a carbolic-acid preparation he developed was marketed as McDougall's powder — and worked on the ventilation of sewers and public buildings.
