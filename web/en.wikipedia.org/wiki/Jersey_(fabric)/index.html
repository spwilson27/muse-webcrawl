Jersey (fabric)

Jersey is a knit fabric used predominantly for clothing manufacture. It was originally made of wool, but is now made of wool, cotton, and synthetic fibres. The fabric takes its name from the island of Jersey in the Channel Islands, where it was traditionally used for fishermen's sweaters and underwear.

Structure and properties

Jersey is a weft-knitted fabric produced by a single set of needles, giving it a smooth face of vertical wales on the right side and horizontal courses on the reverse. It is lightweight, elastic, and drapes well, curling at the edges when cut because of the imbalance of the single-knit structure. Single jersey is the basic form; double jersey (or interlock) is knitted with two sets of needles, producing a thicker, reversible fabric that does not curl. Stretch jerseys incorporate elastane (spandex) for additional recovery.

History and uses

Knitted woollen jersey cloth was produced on Jersey for centuries, notably for stockings and fishermen's garments. In the early 20th century the designer Coco Chanel popularized jersey for women's fashion, using the humble fabric for suits and dresses in a then-daring departure from its utilitarian roots. Today jersey is the standard material for T-shirts, dresses, underwear, sportswear, and bed sheets (in the form of cotton jersey "T-shirt sheets").
