Wholesale

Wholesale is the sale of goods or merchandise in large quantities to retailers, industrial or commercial users, or other wholesalers, rather than to end consumers. Wholesalers sit between producers and retailers in the distribution chain, buying in bulk at a discount and reselling in smaller lots at a margin, at a wholesale price below the eventual retail price.

Functions

Wholesalers break bulk, hold inventory, extend credit, bear storage and spoilage risk, transport goods, and provide market information to both suppliers and retailers. By aggregating the output of many producers and the demand of many shops, they reduce the number of transactions needed to move goods through an economy.

Types

  • Merchant wholesalers take title to goods; they include full-service wholesalers and limited-service operators such as cash-and-carry warehouses, drop shippers, and rack jobbers.
  • Agents and brokers negotiate sales without taking title, earning commissions.
  • Manufacturers' sales branches and offices sell a producer's output directly to retailers or businesses.
  • Warehouse clubs such as Costco blur the wholesale/retail boundary, selling bulk goods to member businesses and consumers.

Wholesale markets

The term also applies to markets in commodities, electricity, telecommunications capacity, and finance, where goods are traded in bulk between businesses — for example, the wholesale electricity market in which generators sell power to utilities. Wholesale trade is a major sector of developed economies, typically accounting for a significant share of GDP, and it is usually taxed differently from retail, with sales or value-added taxes collected at the final sale to consumers.
