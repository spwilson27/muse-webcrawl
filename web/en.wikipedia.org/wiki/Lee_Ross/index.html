Lee Ross

Lee David Ross (25 August 1942 – 14 May 2021) was a Canadian-American social psychologist at Stanford University, known for foundational work on attribution theory and the psychology of judgment, conflict, and belief. With Richard Nisbett he co-authored the influential book The Person and the Situation (1991), a central statement of the situationist tradition in social psychology.

Fundamental attribution error

Ross coined the term fundamental attribution error for the tendency to overestimate the role of dispositional factors and underestimate situational factors when explaining others' behaviour. In a classic 1977 study with Teresa Amabile and others — the "quizmaster" experiment — observers rated quizmasters as more knowledgeable than contestants even though the roles, and the advantage in writing the questions, had been assigned arbitrarily. Ross's broader programme showed systematic biases in how people interpret their social worlds, including naïve realism, the false consensus effect, belief perseverance, and the hostile media effect.

Conflict and peace psychology

Later in his career Ross applied social psychology to intractable conflicts. With Andrew Ward he studied "reactive devaluation" in negotiation, and he co-founded Stanford's Center on International Conflict and Negotiation. He was closely involved in experimental exercises bringing together Israelis and Palestinians, and with colleagues developed interventions based on mindset theory to reduce hostility between groups.

Career

Born in Toronto, Ross earned his doctorate from Columbia University and joined Stanford's faculty in 1969, remaining there for his entire career. He was elected to the American Academy of Arts and Sciences and the National Academy of Sciences. He died in Palo Alto, California, in 2021, aged 78.
