Ensemble Studios was an American video game developer founded in 1994 in Dallas, Texas, best known for the Age of Empires series of real-time strategy games. It was acquired by Microsoft in 2001 and closed in 2009 after completing Halo Wars.

History

The studio's first game, Age of Empires (1997), was a breakthrough success for the real-time strategy genre, combining historical settings with city-building elements. Age of Empires II: The Age of Kings (1999) sold over two million copies and remains highly regarded. Age of Mythology (2002) applied the formula to mythological settings, and Age of Empires III (2005) moved the series to the colonial Americas. The studio also developed the Genie game engine used by its early titles and by LucasArts' Star Wars: Galactic Battlegrounds.

Closure and legacy

Microsoft announced the studio's closure in September 2008, effective on the completion of the console strategy game Halo Wars, released in 2009. Former staff founded several successor studios, including Robot Entertainment, which maintained the Age of Empires online service and developed the Orcs Must Die! series. Interest in the franchise later revived with the Definitive Edition remasters and Age of Empires IV (2021).
