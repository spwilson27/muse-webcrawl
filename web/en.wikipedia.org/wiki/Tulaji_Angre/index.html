Tulaji Angre was a Maratha naval commander of the eighteenth century, the younger son of the celebrated admiral Kanhoji Angre. After the death of his elder brother Sambhaji Angre in 1748, Tulaji inherited command of the southern division of the Maratha navy, based at the great sea fortress of Vijaydurg (Gheriah) on the Konkan coast. The English, who regarded the Angres as pirates, called him "Tullaji Angria"; he is the Angre most often meant in British accounts of "Angria the Pirate".

Conflict with the Peshwa and the British

Tulaji refused to acknowledge the authority of the Peshwa (the Maratha prime minister) to the extent demanded, remitting only a share of his revenues and conducting independent operations against shipping on the western coast of India. His seizures of European vessels, including English East India Company ships, made him a marked enemy of the Bombay government. Peshwa Balaji Baji Rao (Nanasaheb), determined to subordinate him, allied with the British against his own admiral.

Fall of Vijaydurg

In 1755 a combined campaign began against Tulaji's forts. His northern stronghold of Suvarnadurg fell to a combined Maratha-British force under Commodore William James in April 1755. In February 1756 Admiral Charles Watson and Lieutenant-Colonel Robert Clive led the English squadron and troops against Vijaydurg itself, while the Peshwa's army invested it by land. After a bombardment in which Tulaji's fleet of grabs and gallivats caught fire in the creek, the fortress surrendered. Tulaji, who had gone to negotiate with the Peshwa's camp, was detained and spent the rest of his life as a prisoner of the Marathas; Vijaydurg was eventually handed over to the Peshwa in exchange for territory near Bombay.

The destruction of Tulaji Angre ended the Angre family's half-century of sea power and removed the most serious Indian naval challenge to the East India Company on the west coast of India.
