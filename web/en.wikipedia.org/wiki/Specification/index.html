Specification

A specification often refers to a set of documented requirements to be satisfied by a material, design, product, or service. It is a common early part of the engineering design and product development processes in many fields. Specifications may be written by government agencies, standards organizations, trade associations, corporations, and other parties, and are used to ensure that delivered items conform to agreed characteristics.

Use

In engineering and manufacturing, a specification defines technical details — dimensions, materials, performance criteria, and tolerances — against which products are tested and accepted. In construction, specifications accompany drawings to describe the materials and workmanship required. In computing, specifications describe protocols, file formats, and programming interfaces, allowing independent implementations to interoperate; well-known examples include the specifications maintained by the W3C, IETF requests for comments, and language standards such as those for C and JavaScript.

Types

Specifications may be prescriptive (design specifications), prescribing exactly how something must be made; performance specifications, defining required outcomes while leaving methods open; or functional specifications, describing what a system must do without detailing how. Requirements specifications in software engineering document the behavior expected of a system for its users and stakeholders.

Standards and compliance

A specification differs from a technical standard in that a standard is often produced by consensus bodies and intended for wide use, while a specification may be project- or contract-specific. Compliance may be verified by inspection, testing, or certification, and disputes in procurement frequently turn on whether delivered goods conform to the specification referenced in the contract.
