Cognition

[Image: Cognition spans perception, memory, attention, language and reasoning]

Overview

Cognition is the mental action or process of acquiring knowledge and understanding through thought, experience and the senses. It encompasses perception, attention, memory, learning, language, problem-solving, reasoning and decision-making. Cognitive science studies these processes across psychology, neuroscience, linguistics, philosophy, computer science and anthropology.

Approaches

Major frameworks include the information-processing approach (the mind as manipulating representations), embodied and enactive cognition (thought grounded in the body and environment), and dual-process accounts distinguishing fast intuitive "System 1" from slow deliberate "System 2" (Kahneman). Cognitive development in children was mapped by Piaget's stages; Vygotsky emphasised social scaffolding. Disorders of cognition include dementia, delirium and the deficits of schizophrenia and depression; cognitive biases (documented by Tversky and Kahneman) systematically shape judgement.
