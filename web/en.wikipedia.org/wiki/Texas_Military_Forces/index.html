The Texas Military Forces (TXMF) are the combined military forces of the U.S. state of Texas. They consist of three branches: the Texas Army National Guard, the Texas Air National Guard, and the Texas State Guard. Together they form the organised militia of the state under the command of the Governor of Texas, exercised through the Adjutant General, and are administered by the Texas Military Department at Camp Mabry in Austin.

History

Texas military forces trace their lineage to the militia of the Republic of Texas (1836–1845) and to the famed Texas Rangers tradition. Texas units served in the Mexican–American War, the Civil War, both World Wars, and every major conflict since. During the Second World War the 36th Infantry Division ("T-Patchers") fought in North Africa and Italy, suffering heavy losses at the Rapido River crossing in 1944.

Organisation

The two National Guard branches are dual-status: they serve the governor for state emergencies (hurricanes, floods, border operations, and civil support) but can be federalised by the President. Major Army Guard formations include the 36th Infantry Division headquarters and several brigade combat teams; the Air Guard operates fighter, airlift, and intelligence missions at bases including Ellington Field and Lackland. The Texas State Guard is a state defence force — entirely under state control, unpaid volunteers for the most part — that supports disaster relief, medical, and engineering missions and cannot be federalised.

Role

Texas forces are among the most frequently activated in the nation, responding to hurricanes such as Harvey (2017) with tens of thousands of troops for search and rescue, and sustaining long-running state border security missions such as Operation Lone Star (from 2021).
