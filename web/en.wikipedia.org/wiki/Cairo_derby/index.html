The Cairo derby between Al Ahly and Zamalek is one of the most intense football rivalries in Africa and the Middle East.

The Cairo derby (Arabic: ديربي القاهرة) is the association football rivalry between Egypt's two most successful clubs, Al Ahly SC and Zamalek SC, both based in Cairo. First played in 1917, it is regarded as one of the fiercest derbies in world football, regularly drawing enormous television audiences across the Arab world. Matches are frequently officiated by foreign referees because of the sensitivity surrounding the fixture.

Background

Al Ahly was founded in 1907 and Zamalek in 1911. Al Ahly, the "Red Devils", traditionally drew support associated with Egyptian nationalism, while Zamalek, the "White Knights", historically had ties to the monarchy-era establishment — a contrast that deepened the social dimension of the rivalry. Al Ahly is Africa's most decorated club, with a record number of CAF Champions League titles, while Zamalek is its closest domestic rival in league and cup honours.

Competitions

The clubs meet in the Egyptian Premier League, the Egypt Cup, the Egyptian Super Cup, and occasionally in African competition — most memorably the 2020 CAF Champions League final, the first final contested by two clubs from the same country and city, which Al Ahly won 2–1 with a late goal by Mohamed Magdy "Afsha".

Fan culture and incidents

Both clubs have large ultras groups — Ultras Ahlawy and the Ultras White Knights — known for elaborate tifo displays. The fixture has at times been marred by crowd trouble, and for several years league matches were played behind closed doors or with limited attendance for security reasons.
