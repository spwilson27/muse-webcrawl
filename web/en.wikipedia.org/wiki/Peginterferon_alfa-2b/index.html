Peginterferon alfa-2b

Peginterferon alfa-2b, sold under the brand name PegIntron among others, is a medication used to treat hepatitis C and melanoma. It is a pegylated form of recombinant interferon alfa-2b: a polyethylene glycol (PEG) chain is attached to the interferon molecule, prolonging its half-life and allowing once-weekly subcutaneous injection.

Medical uses

For chronic hepatitis C, peginterferon alfa-2b was used in combination with ribavirin and later with direct-acting antivirals, though interferon-based regimens have largely been superseded by all-oral therapies. Under the name Sylatron it was approved as adjuvant therapy for melanoma with nodal involvement. It has also been used for hepatitis B in some jurisdictions.

Side effects

Common adverse effects include flu-like symptoms, fatigue, injection-site reactions, depression and other neuropsychiatric effects, cytopenias, and autoimmune phenomena. Because interferon can exacerbate psychiatric illness, patients are monitored closely during treatment.

Pharmacology

Interferon alfa binds cell-surface receptors (IFNAR), activating JAK-STAT signaling and the expression of antiviral and immunomodulatory genes. Pegylation with a 12 kDa linear PEG reduces renal clearance compared with unmodified interferon alfa-2b, whose half-life is only a few hours.
