The Baedeker Blitz or Baedeker raids were a series of attacks by the German Luftwaffe on English cities during the Second World War. The name derives from the Baedeker guidebooks: the targets were supposedly chosen for their cultural and historical significance rather than military value, in retaliation for the RAF's destruction of Lübeck and Rostock.

Raids

Beginning on the night of 23–24 April 1942, the Luftwaffe attacked Exeter, Bath, Norwich, York, and Canterbury. Bath suffered some of the heaviest damage, with around 400 people killed. The raids were carried out by small numbers of bombers but achieved considerable destruction of historic buildings because the cities were lightly defended.

Impact

More than 1,600 civilians were killed and tens of thousands of homes destroyed during the campaign. The raids prompted improvements in the air defences of provincial cities, and the Luftwaffe lost a number of its most experienced crews over the following months, weakening its bomber force over Britain.
