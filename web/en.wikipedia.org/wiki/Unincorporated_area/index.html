An unincorporated area is a region of land that is not governed by its own local municipal corporation but is instead administered as part of a larger division, such as a county, township, or state. Residents of unincorporated areas typically receive services such as policing, fire protection, and road maintenance from the county government rather than from a city.

In the United States, an unincorporated community may have a name and identity, and may be recognised by the Census Bureau as a census-designated place, without having an elected local government of its own. Some large urbanised areas, such as Paradise, Nevada (home to much of the Las Vegas Strip), are unincorporated.

By country

In Canada, unincorporated communities are governed by regional districts or provincial ministries. In Australia, vast unincorporated areas exist in states such as New South Wales and South Australia, where population density is too low to sustain local councils. Rules and terminology vary considerably between jurisdictions.
