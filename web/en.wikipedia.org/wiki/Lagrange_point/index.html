Lagrange point

Lagrange points
Number: Five (L1–L5)
Described by: Euler (three collinear) and Lagrange (1772)
Notable users: JWST and Gaia at Sun–Earth L2; SOHO at L1

A Lagrange point is a position in an orbital configuration of two large bodies where a small object affected only by gravity can maintain a stable or near-stable position relative to the two bodies. There are five such points, labelled L1 to L5.

Mechanics

At these points the gravitational forces of the two large bodies and the centrifugal force balance. L1, L2 and L3 lie on the line through the two bodies and are metastable, requiring station-keeping; L4 and L5, forming equilateral triangles, are genuinely stable and collect Trojan asteroids — notably Jupiter's vast Trojan camps.

Applications

Sun–Earth L1 hosts solar observatories such as SOHO and DSCOVR; L2, 1.5 million km anti-sunward, hosts the James Webb Space Telescope, Gaia and Euclid in halo orbits, shaded from Earth and Sun. Proposed uses for L4/L5 include space colonies. See Three-body problem and James Webb Space Telescope.
