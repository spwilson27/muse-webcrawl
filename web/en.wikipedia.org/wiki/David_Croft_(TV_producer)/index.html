David Croft (TV producer)

David Croft OBE (born David John Sharland; 7 September 1922 – 27 September 2011) was an English television comedy producer, director and writer. Working chiefly for the BBC, he created — mostly in partnership with Jimmy Perry and later Jeremy Lloyd — some of Britain's most enduring situation comedies.

Croft co-created Dad's Army, It Ain't Half Hot Mum, Hi-de-Hi! and 'Allo 'Allo!.

Career

Croft began as an actor and singer before moving into producing at the BBC in the 1960s. With Jimmy Perry he created Dad's Army (1968–1977), about a Home Guard platoon in the Second World War, drawing on Croft's own wartime service and Perry's time in the Home Guard. The partnership went on to produce It Ain't Half Hot Mum (1974–1981) and Hi-de-Hi! (1980–1988), set in a holiday camp.

With Jeremy Lloyd, Croft co-wrote Are You Being Served? (1972–1985) and the wartime farce 'Allo 'Allo! (1982–1992), set in occupied France, which became one of the BBC's most successful comedy exports. Unusually, Croft typically both produced and directed his series, maintaining close control over casting and scripts. He was appointed OBE in 1978.

Legacy

Croft's series remain staples of British television repeats and are frequently cited among the greatest British sitcoms. His son-in-law co-founded the Croft and Perry appreciation societies, and his autobiography, You Have Been Watching…, was published in 2004.
