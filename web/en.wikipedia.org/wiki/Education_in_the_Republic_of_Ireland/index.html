Education in the Republic of Ireland

Education in the Republic of Ireland is organised in three levels — primary, secondary and tertiary — overseen by the Department of Education. Education is compulsory for children aged six to sixteen, or until they have completed three years of second-level education. Unusually for Europe, most primary and many secondary schools are publicly funded but privately owned and managed, historically by religious bodies (mostly the Catholic Church), although multi-denominational provision through bodies such as Educate Together has grown. The curriculum, examinations and teacher registration are state-regulated; the main state examinations are the Junior Cycle and the Leaving Certificate, administered by the State Examinations Commission.

Primary education

Most children start primary school at age four or five, spending eight years through infant classes and first to sixth class. The vast majority of primary schools are under church patronage ("national schools"), though the state pays teachers' salaries and most running costs. Irish (Gaeilge) is a compulsory subject, except in limited exemption circumstances; a growing number of schools — Gaelscoileanna — teach entirely through Irish.

Secondary education

Second-level schooling normally lasts five or six years. The Junior Cycle (three years) ends with the reformed Junior Cycle Profile of Achievement. An optional Transition Year follows, emphasising personal development and work experience, before the two-year Leaving Certificate programme. The Leaving Certificate (Established) is the main school-leaving qualification and the basis for entry to higher education through the Central Applications Office (CAO) points system; Leaving Certificate Vocational and Applied programmes offer alternative routes.

Third-level education

Ireland has universities (including Trinity College Dublin, University College Dublin and the National University of Ireland colleges), technological universities formed from merged institutes of technology, and colleges of education. Undergraduate tuition for EU students is largely covered by the state's Free Fees Initiative, with students paying an annual student contribution. Ireland has one of the highest rates of tertiary educational attainment in the European Union, and education has been closely linked to the country's economic development since free secondary education was introduced in 1967.
