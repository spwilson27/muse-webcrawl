Walther von der Vogelweide

Walther von der Vogelweide (c. 1170 – c. 1230) was a German-language poet and composer (Minnesänger) of the Middle Ages — widely regarded as the greatest German lyric poet before Goethe. His oeuvre comprises courtly love songs (Minnesang), religious and political didactic poems (Sprüche), and the long narrative poem form called the Leich. His famous songs include "Under der linden" and the "Palästinalied".

Life

Almost everything known of Walther's life comes from his own poems. He learned his craft at the Babenberg court in Vienna under Reinmar von Hagenau, with whom he later quarrelled. After Duke Frederick I's death in 1198 he became an itinerant singer, serving patrons including Philip of Swabia, Emperor Otto IV and Emperor Frederick II, from whom he finally received a small fief ("Ich hân mîn lêhen" — "I have my fief!") around 1220. Tradition places his grave at Würzburg.

Works

Walther transformed the conventions of courtly love poetry, grounding it in natural imagery and genuine feeling — "Under der linden" is sung from a woman's perspective. His political Sprüche comment sharply on the imperial–papal struggles of the day, and his crusading songs, including the "Palästinalied" (Palestine Song), remain among the most performed medieval German works. His portrait in the Codex Manesse, seated pensively on a rock, is iconic.
