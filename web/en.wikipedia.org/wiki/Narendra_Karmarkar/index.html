Narendra Krishna Karmarkar (born 1957) is an Indian mathematician and computer scientist best known for Karmarkar's algorithm, a polynomial-time interior-point method for linear programming announced in 1984 while he was a researcher at Bell Labs. The result transformed the theory and practice of mathematical optimisation.

Education and the 1984 result

Karmarkar earned a bachelor's degree in electrical engineering from IIT Bombay, a master's degree from the California Institute of Technology and a PhD in computer science from the University of California, Berkeley. Joining Bell Labs in New Jersey, he published in 1984 a new algorithm for linear programming that travelled through the interior of the feasible region rather than along its edges as the simplex method did. He proved it ran in polynomial time and reported dramatic speedups on large problems, a claim that drew enormous press attention, including a front-page story in the New York Times, and intense debate within the optimisation community. The paper is regarded as the founding document of modern interior-point methods, a field consolidated soon afterwards by primal-dual methods.

Controversy and later work

AT&T sought to commercialise the algorithm through its KORBX system, and a patent granted on the software became a landmark in the controversy over patenting mathematical algorithms and software. Karmarkar later returned to India, working at the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research and holding a chair at the Indian Institute of Science, and has since pursued research on new computing architectures for optimisation.
