Adbusters

Adbusters is a Canadian anti-consumerist magazine published by the Adbusters Media Foundation, a not-for-profit organisation founded in 1989 by Kalle Lasn and Bill Schmalz in Vancouver, British Columbia. The magazine describes itself as "a global network of artists, activists, writers, pranksters, students, educators and entrepreneurs who want to advance the new social activist movement of the information age."

Initiatives

Adbusters is known for popularising the concept of "culture jamming" — subverting advertising imagery, including through its own "subvertisements" — and for launching campaigns that entered wider activist culture: Buy Nothing Day (held on the day after American Thanksgiving), TV Turnoff Week, and, most prominently, the July 2011 call for a protest occupation of Wall Street that catalysed the Occupy movement, including the #OccupyWallStreet hashtag and the poster of a ballerina atop the Charging Bull statue.

Reception

The magazine has been praised for its design and its influence on anti-globalisation and environmental activism, and criticised for the commercial sale of its own products (such as the Blackspot shoe) and, at times, for controversial editorial content. It accepts no paid advertising and is supported by subscriptions and sales.
