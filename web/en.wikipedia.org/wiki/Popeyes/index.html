Popeyes is an American multinational chain of fried chicken restaurants founded in 1972 in Arabi, Louisiana, and headquartered in Miami, Florida. It is known for its Louisiana-style fried chicken, marinated for 12 hours and hand-battered, and for dishes such as its chicken sandwich, introduced in 2019 to enormous demand and sparking the so-called "chicken sandwich wars".

History

Al Copeland opened the first restaurant, originally called Chicken on the Run, in the New Orleans suburb of Arabi in 1972. After a slow start he reformulated the menu around spicy, New Orleans-style fried chicken and renamed the chain Popeyes — after Popeye Doyle, the detective in the film The French Connection, rather than the cartoon sailor. Franchising began in 1976, and the chain grew rapidly through the southern United States.

In 1989 Popeyes' parent company acquired the rival Church's Chicken chain; Church's was later sold to a private equity firm in 2004. Popeyes itself was bought in 2017 by Restaurant Brands International, the parent of Burger King and Tim Hortons, for $1.8 billion.

Products

The menu centres on bone-in fried chicken (mild and spicy), chicken tenders, fried shrimp and seafood, and side dishes including Cajun fries, mashed potatoes with Cajun gravy, red beans and rice, coleslaw and buttermilk biscuits. The 2019 chicken sandwich became a viral phenomenon, selling out nationwide within weeks of its launch.

Operations

Popeyes operates more than 4,000 restaurants in the United States and over 30 other countries and territories, the great majority franchised.
