Peckham Library is a public library in Peckham, in the London Borough of Southwark, London. Opened on March 8, 2000, it was designed by architect Will Alsop of Alsop & Störmer as part of the regeneration of the Peckham town centre. In 2000 it won the Stirling Prize, the United Kingdom's most prestigious architecture award — the first time the prize went to a library building.

Design

The building is a striking inverted "L" shape: a five-storey block clad in pre-patinated green copper, propped at an angle on slender, randomly placed steel columns, creating a sheltered public arcade beneath its overhang. A bold orange "tongue" extends from the entrance. Inside, the main reading room is a large open space on the upper floors, lit from a central slot, with elevated, timber-lined "pods" on stilts used for the children's library and meeting rooms, and a row of study carrels overlooking the square.

Context

The library formed the centrepiece of the Peckham Partnership regeneration programme, together with a new town square and the Peckham Arch. Its colourful, unconventional appearance was intended to raise the area's profile and to present the public library as an inviting civic landmark. The building houses lending and reference collections, a one-stop council services point, and community spaces.
