Mortgage fraud

Mortgage fraud is a crime in which the intent is to materially misrepresent or omit information on a mortgage loan application in order to obtain a loan or to obtain a larger loan than could legitimately be obtained, or to defraud a lender or borrower out of money. The United States Federal Bureau of Investigation defines mortgage fraud as "some type of material misstatement, misrepresentation, or omission in relation to a mortgage loan, which is then relied upon by a lender."

Types

Fraud for housing

Fraud for housing is typically committed by borrowers who misstate income, assets, or employment on an application in order to qualify for a loan they might not otherwise receive. Although the borrower usually intends to live in the property and make payments, the misrepresentation itself is a federal and state offence in the United States and many other jurisdictions.

Fraud for profit

Fraud for profit is usually committed by industry insiders — brokers, appraisers, loan officers, attorneys, or straw buyers — and involves extracting equity or fees from transactions. Classic schemes include:

  • Property flipping: purchasing a property, fraudulently inflating its appraised value, and quickly reselling it at the artificial price.
  • Straw buyers: using a person with good credit to obtain a loan on behalf of the scheme's operator, who never intends to repay.
  • Air loans: obtaining a loan for a non-existent property or borrower.
  • Equity skimming: taking control of a distressed property, collecting rents or fees, and letting the loan default.

Scale and enforcement

Mortgage fraud rose sharply during the United States housing bubble of the mid-2000s. The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network reported that Suspicious Activity Reports characterising possible mortgage fraud grew dramatically between 2004 and 2008, and the FBI at one point described it as an "epidemic." In response, the Fraud Enforcement and Recovery Act of 2009 expanded federal fraud statutes, and prosecutions following the 2008 financial crisis targeted both individual schemes and institutional lending practices.
