Neurologic music therapy

Neurologic music therapy (NMT) is a research-based system of standardised clinical techniques for the training of sensorimotor, speech and language, and cognitive functions in people with neurologic injury or disease. It was developed from research in the neuroscience of music perception and production, particularly through the work of Michael Thaut and colleagues at Colorado State University and the University of Toronto. NMT is used in the rehabilitation of stroke, traumatic brain injury, Parkinson's disease, cerebral palsy, and other conditions affecting movement, communication, and cognition.

Rationale

Music engages widely distributed brain networks, and rhythm in particular provides a precise temporal scaffold that can entrain motor activity. Because musical processing overlaps with systems controlling movement, speech, attention, and memory, structured musical exercises can be used to retrain those functions and to drive neuroplastic change.

Techniques

Twenty standardised techniques are grouped into three domains. Sensorimotor rehabilitation includes rhythmic auditory stimulation (RAS), in which patients walk in time with a metronome or strongly rhythmic music to improve gait speed, cadence, and stride length — a technique with strong evidence in Parkinson's disease and stroke; patterned sensory enhancement (PSE); and therapeutic instrumental music performance (TIMP). Speech and language rehabilitation includes melodic intonation therapy (MIT), adapted from work with non-fluent aphasia, in which phrases are sung or intoned to recruit right-hemisphere language capacity, as well as oral motor and respiratory exercises. Cognitive rehabilitation includes musical attention control training and musical mnemonics, in which information is encoded in song to aid recall.

Professional practice

Neurologic music therapy is practised by credentialed music therapists who complete additional specialised training and certification through the Academy of Neurologic Music Therapy. It sits within the broader field of music therapy but is distinguished by its standardised protocols and grounding in neuroscience research. See also neuropsychology.
