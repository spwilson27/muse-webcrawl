Wenceslaus IV (Czech: Václav IV.; 1361–1419) was King of Bohemia from 1378 until his death and King of the Romans from 1376 until his deposition in 1400. The eldest son of Emperor Charles IV, he inherited a powerful position but proved unable to manage the growing religious and political crises of his reign.

Reign

Crowned King of the Romans during his father's lifetime, Wenceslaus struggled with the German princes and was deposed as King of the Romans in 1400, though he retained Bohemia. His reign saw rising tensions between the Catholic Church and reformers around Jan Hus at Charles University in Prague; his Decree of Kutná Hora (1409) altered the university's constitution in favour of the Czech "nation".

Death and aftermath

Wenceslaus died in August 1419, weeks after the First Defenestration of Prague, and was succeeded by his brother Sigismund — an inheritance the Bohemian estates resisted, igniting the Hussite Wars.
