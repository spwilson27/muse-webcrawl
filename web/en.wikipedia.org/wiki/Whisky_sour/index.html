Whisky sour

Type: Cocktail
Base spirit: Whiskey (usually bourbon)
Ingredients: Whiskey, lemon juice, sugar
IBA official cocktail

The whiskey sour (also whisky sour) is a mixed drink containing whiskey, lemon juice and a sweetener, shaken with ice and strained into a chilled glass, traditionally garnished with a half slice of lemon and a maraschino cherry. The International Bartenders Association recipe uses bourbon, fresh lemon juice and simple syrup; a popular variant includes a small amount of egg white, which gives the drink a silky texture and foamy head — the version with egg white is sometimes called a Boston sour.

History

Sours descend from the practice of mixing citrus into spirits to prevent scurvy on long sea voyages. The whiskey sour itself is first mentioned in print in an 1870 Wisconsin newspaper, though similar drinks appear in Jerry Thomas's 1862 How to Mix Drinks. It remains one of the classic cocktails, and forms the template for a large family of sour drinks including the amaretto sour and the New York sour, which adds a float of red wine.
