Massey Ferguson Limited is an agricultural machinery manufacturer and one of the best-known tractor brands in the world. The company was formed in 1953 by the merger of the Canadian firm Massey-Harris with the British Ferguson Company founded by Harry Ferguson, inventor of the three-point linkage system for tractors. Since 1994 Massey Ferguson has been a brand of the American agricultural equipment corporation AGCO.

History

The Massey side of the business began in 1847, when Daniel Massey established a farm implement workshop in Newcastle, Ontario. His firm, which became Massey-Harris in 1891 through a merger with the Harris company, grew into one of the world's largest makers of threshing machines, reapers, and combine harvesters, and it pioneered the self-propelled combine harvester in the late 1930s.

Harry Ferguson's company in Britain produced the Ferguson TE20 — the famous "Little Grey Fergie" — from 1946, which popularised Ferguson's three-point hitch with hydraulic draft control, a system that became the industry standard for attaching implements to tractors. After a decade of rivalry and a patent dispute over the Ford-Ferguson tractor venture in the United States, Massey-Harris and Ferguson merged in 1953, first as Massey-Harris-Ferguson and from 1958 simply as Massey Ferguson.

The merged company became a global manufacturer with major factories at Coventry in England, Beauvais in France, and plants in North America, Australia, and elsewhere. Notable models include the MF 35, MF 65, and the long-running MF 100 and 200 series, sold in huge numbers worldwide. Financial difficulties in the 1980s led to restructuring, and in 1994 the brand was acquired by AGCO of Duluth, Georgia.

Products

Massey Ferguson's current range includes compact, utility, and high-horsepower tractors (the MF 1700, 4700, 5700, 6700, 7700, 8700, and 8S/9S series), combine harvesters, square and round balers, mowers, tedders, and a wide range of implements, together with precision-farming technology under the Fuse and MF Guide brands. Tractors for most markets are built at Beauvais, France, one of the largest tractor plants in Europe.
