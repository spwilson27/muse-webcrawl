W1A (TV series)

W1A is a British comedy television series that satirises the management of the BBC. It was written and directed by John Morton. The series stars Hugh Bonneville as Ian Fletcher, former Head of the Olympic Deliverance Commission, who becomes Head of Values at the BBC. The series is a sequel to the BBC series Twenty Twelve, which followed Fletcher's team in the run-up to the 2012 London Olympics.

The series' title derives from the postal district of Broadcasting House, the BBC's headquarters in central London. The series is narrated by David Tennant, as in Twenty Twelve.

Overview

Three series were broadcast between 2014 and 2017, followed by a lockdown-themed special in 2020. The comedy draws on deadpan meetings, management jargon, branding initiatives, and crises of corporate "values", with characters including the relentlessly positive Head of Better, Siobhan Sharpe (Jessica Hynes), entertainment format producer David Wilkes, and the hapless intern Will Humphries. Recurring jokes involve fold-up bicycles, hot-desking, and the BBC's fictional initiatives such as the "Way Ahead" taskforce.

The show was well received by critics for its sharp, insider satire of corporate culture; several of its fictional scenarios later appeared to be mirrored by real BBC announcements.
