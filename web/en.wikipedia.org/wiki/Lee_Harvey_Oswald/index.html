Lee Harvey Oswald (October 18, 1939 – November 24, 1963) was a former U.S. Marine who assassinated John F. Kennedy, the 35th president of the United States, on November 22, 1963, in Dallas, Texas. Oswald was arrested about 80 minutes later for the murder of Dallas police officer J. D. Tippit. Two days after the assassination, Oswald was fatally shot by Dallas nightclub operator Jack Ruby while being transferred from police headquarters, live on television.

Life

Oswald was born in New Orleans and raised largely in poverty by his mother Marguerite. He joined the Marine Corps in 1956 and served as a radar operator, including at Atsugi Naval Air Facility in Japan. In 1959 he defected to the Soviet Union, living in Minsk and working as a lathe operator; disillusioned, he returned to the United States in 1962 with his Soviet wife, Marina Prusakova, and their daughter. In April 1963 he attempted to shoot former general Edwin Walker. In the autumn of 1963 he travelled to Mexico City seeking visas for Cuba and the USSR, then took a job at the Texas School Book Depository in Dallas.

Assassination and aftermath

Investigations concluded that Oswald fired three shots from the sixth floor of the Depository at President Kennedy's motorcade, killing Kennedy and wounding Texas governor John Connally. The Warren Commission (1964) concluded Oswald acted alone; the House Select Committee on Assassinations (1979) concluded Kennedy was "probably assassinated as a result of a conspiracy" based principally on disputed acoustic evidence, while later reviews rejected that finding. Oswald's murder by Ruby and the questions around his biography have fuelled decades of conspiracy theories.
