Steam injection (oil industry)

Steam injection is an increasingly common method of extracting heavy crude oil. Used in both "cylical" and continuous applications to extract heavy oils, it is considered an enhanced oil recovery (EOR) method, as it relies on some external components, in this case steam, to recover hydrocarbons.

Methods

Cyclic steam stimulation

Cyclic steam stimulation, also known as the "huff and puff" method, consists of three phases: injection, soaking, and production. Steam is injected into a well at a temperature of 300 to 340 degrees Celsius for a period of weeks to months. The well is then allowed to sit for days to weeks to allow heat to soak into the formation, and later, the hot oil is pumped out of the well for a period of weeks or months. Once the production rate falls off, the well is put through another cycle of steam injection, soak and production. This process is repeated until the cost of injecting steam becomes higher than the money made from producing oil.

Steam flooding

In a steam flood, sometimes known as a steam drive, some wells are used as steam injection wells and other wells are used for oil production. Two mechanisms are at work to improve the amount of oil recovered. The first is heating the oil to higher temperatures and decreasing its viscosity so that it more easily flows through the formation toward the producing wells. The second mechanism is the physical displacement occurring in a manner similar to water flooding, in which oil is meant to be pushed to the production wells.

Steam-assisted gravity drainage

Steam-assisted gravity drainage (SAGD) is an enhanced oil recovery technology for producing heavy crude oil and bitumen. It involves drilling a pair of horizontal wells, one above the other; steam is injected into the upper well, heating the bitumen so that it drains by gravity into the lower producing well. SAGD is widely used in the Alberta oil sands in Canada.

Use

Steam injection is widely used in the heavy-oil fields of California (such as the Kern River and Midway-Sunset fields), in Venezuela's Orinoco Belt, in Alberta, Canada, and in parts of Indonesia, Oman and China. The energy required to generate steam makes the method expensive and carbon-intensive relative to conventional production, which has driven interest in solvent-assisted and electromagnetic alternatives.
