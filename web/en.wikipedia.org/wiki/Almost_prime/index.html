Almost prime

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In number theory, an almost prime is a natural number containing at most a fixed number of prime factors, counted with multiplicity. A number with exactly k prime factors (with multiplicity) is called a k-almost prime; the quantity is measured by the arithmetic function Ω(n), the total number of prime factors.

Examples

The 1-almost primes are simply the prime numbers themselves. The 2-almost primes are the semiprimes — products of two primes, such as 4, 6, 9, 10, 14, 15, 21, 22. The 3-almost primes begin 8, 12, 18, 20, 27, 28, 30, and include both products of three distinct primes (sphenic numbers) and cubes of primes.

Significance

Almost primes are natural approximations to the primes in sieve theory and analytic number theory. Chen's theorem (1973), the strongest result towards the Goldbach conjecture, states that every sufficiently large even number is the sum of a prime and a 2-almost prime. Results on the distribution of k-almost primes refine the prime number theorem: for fixed k, the count of k-almost primes up to x is asymptotic to x(log log x)k−1 / ((k−1)! log x), a theorem of Landau.
