Lhotse

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Lhotse's steep south face, a wall of snow and ice next to Everest]
The south face of Lhotse.

Lhotse, at 8,516 metres (27,940 ft), is the fourth-highest mountain in the world. It is connected to Mount Everest via the South Col, on the border between Nepal and Tibet, China. Its name means 'South Peak' in Tibetan. Lhotse's main summit was first climbed in 1956 by a Swiss team; its huge south face is one of the great challenges in mountaineering.

Contents1. Geography and climbing

Geography and climbing

Lhotse shares much of Everest's standard climbing route from Nepal as far as the South Col camps. In addition to the main peak, the massif includes Lhotse Middle (8,410 m), unclimbed until 2001, and Lhotse Shar (8,383 m). The Lhotse Face — a steep wall of blue ice above Camp II in the Western Cwm — is climbed with fixed ropes by Everest and Lhotse expeditions alike.
