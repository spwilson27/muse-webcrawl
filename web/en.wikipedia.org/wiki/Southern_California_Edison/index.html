Southern California Edison (SCE) is one of the largest electric utilities in the United States, the primary electricity provider for much of Southern California outside the city of Los Angeles and a few other municipal territories. A subsidiary of Edison International, it serves about 15 million people across a service territory of roughly 50,000 square miles in central, coastal, and southern California.

History

The company traces its origins to 1886 and grew through the consolidation of many small power companies during the early electrification of the Los Angeles basin. It built major hydroelectric projects such as the Big Creek system in the Sierra Nevada, and later held a majority stake in the San Onofre Nuclear Generating Station, which closed in 2013 after steam-generator failures.

Operations and issues

SCE owns and operates extensive transmission and distribution networks, buys and generates power from natural gas, renewables, and storage, and has invested heavily in grid modernisation and wildfire mitigation. Its equipment has been implicated in igniting major wildfires, including the 2017 Thomas Fire, leading to large liabilities, public-safety power shutoffs during high winds, and billions spent on hardening the grid in fire-prone areas.
