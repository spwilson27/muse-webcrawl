Oscar (fish)

Scientific nameAstronotus ocellatus
FamilyCichlidae
OriginAmazon Basin, South America
SizeUp to ~45 cm

The oscar (Astronotus ocellatus) is a species of fish from the cichlid family, native to slow-moving waters of the Amazon Basin and other river systems of tropical South America. It is one of the most popular large freshwater aquarium fish, prized for its size, bold coloration and unusually interactive behaviour — oscars are often said to recognise their keepers.

Description

Wild oscars are dark-bodied with orange-ringed spots (ocelli) on the caudal peduncle and dorsal fin, markings thought to confuse predators and deter fin-nipping piranhas. Adults commonly reach 25–30 cm and can approach 45 cm and 1.6 kg. Selective breeding has produced colour forms including tiger, red, albino and lutino varieties.

In the aquarium

Oscars require large tanks (typically 200 litres or more for a single adult), strong filtration and warm water around 23–27 °C. They are intelligent, long-lived (10–20 years in captivity) and notoriously rearrangers of tank décor. They are opportunistic feeders, eating smaller fish, insects, crustaceans and prepared foods.

Distribution and invasiveness

Beyond its native range in Peru, Colombia, Brazil and French Guiana, the oscar has become established in places such as Florida through aquarium releases, where it is considered an introduced species.
