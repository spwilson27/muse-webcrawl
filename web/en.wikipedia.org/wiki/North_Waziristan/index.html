North Waziristan District is a district in the Bannu Division of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, Pakistan. Its administrative headquarters is the town of Miranshah. Until 2018 it was an agency of the Federally Administered Tribal Areas; it became a district when FATA was merged into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

Geography and people

The district covers rugged mountain country along the border with Afghanistan, including the Tochi Valley. Its inhabitants are predominantly Pashtuns, chiefly of the Wazir and Dawar tribes, from whom the region takes its name. The Wazirs are noted historically for their fierce independence and resistance to outside authority, including during the British colonial period, when the region saw repeated military expeditions and the resistance movement of the Faqir of Ipi.

Recent history

After 2001, North Waziristan became a stronghold of militant groups, including the Pakistani Taliban and the Haqqani network, and was the target of numerous United States drone strikes. In June 2014 the Pakistan Army launched Operation Zarb-e-Azb, a major offensive that displaced much of the population before residents gradually returned. A 2006 peace agreement, the Waziristan Accord, had earlier attempted to end fighting between the army and local militants.
