Hiero II of Syracuse

Reignc. 270 – 215 BC
TitleKing of Syracuse
Bornc. 308 BC
Died215 BC, Syracuse

Hiero II (c. 308 – 215 BC) was king of Syracuse in Sicily from about 270 BC until his death. A former general of Pyrrhus of Epirus, Hiero rose to power after the Syracusan army proclaimed him commander; following victories over the Mamertines at the Longanus River, the citizens confirmed him as king. He briefly allied with Carthage against Rome in the First Punic War, but after early defeats he made peace with Rome in 263 BC and thereafter remained a loyal and valuable Roman ally for the rest of his long reign.

Reign

Hiero's reign was a period of prosperity for Syracuse. He reformed taxation through the lex Hieronica, later adopted by Rome for governing Sicily, strengthened fortifications, and built a great navy and public works, including the huge sacrificial altar that bears his name. He was a patron of the mathematician and inventor Archimedes, his relative and adviser, and commissioned the enormous ship Syracusia. During the Second Punic War Hiero supplied Rome with grain and troops, and Syracuse remained loyal until his death in 215 BC; his young successor Hieronymus reversed the policy, leading to the Roman siege and fall of Syracuse.

Legacy

Ancient historians remembered Hiero as a wise and moderate ruler; Polybius and Livy praised his statesmanship, and Cicero contrasted his rule favourably with later Syracusan tyrants.
