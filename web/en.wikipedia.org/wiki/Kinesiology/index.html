Kinesiology is the scientific study of human body movement. It addresses physiological, anatomical, biomechanical, pathological, and neuropsychological principles and mechanisms of movement. Applications include biomechanics and orthopedics, strength and conditioning, sport psychology, motor control, skill acquisition, rehabilitation, and occupational and exercise physiology.

History

The systematic study of movement dates to Aristotle's treatises on the movement of animals and was advanced by Galen, Leonardo da Vinci, and Giovanni Alfonso Borelli, who applied mechanics to the body in the 17th century. In the 19th century, the Weber brothers, Eadweard Muybridge, and Étienne-Jules Marey pioneered gait analysis and chronophotography. As a university discipline, kinesiology grew out of physical education programmes during the 20th century; the term itself derives from the Greek kínēsis (movement) and -logía (study).

Scope and subdisciplines

Major areas of kinesiology include:

  • Biomechanics – the mechanics of biological systems, including forces on muscles, joints, and tissues.
  • Exercise physiology – acute and chronic responses of the cardiovascular, muscular, and metabolic systems to physical activity.
  • Motor control and learning – how the nervous system coordinates movement and acquires skills.
  • Sport and exercise psychology – mental processes affecting performance and participation.
  • Physical activity and public health – epidemiology of activity, sedentary behaviour, and health outcomes.

Practice and careers

Graduates work in rehabilitation clinics, sports organisations, ergonomics, coaching, fitness, and research. In parts of Canada kinesiology is a regulated health profession, and registered kinesiologists prescribe exercise for injury and chronic-disease management. "Applied kinesiology", a chiropractic-derived muscle-testing technique marketed for diagnosing illness, is distinct from academic kinesiology and is regarded as pseudoscientific.
