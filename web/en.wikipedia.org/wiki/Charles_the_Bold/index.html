Charles Martin (10 November 1433 – 5 January 1477), called Charles the Bold (French: Charles le Téméraire), was the last Duke of Burgundy from the House of Valois-Burgundy, reigning from 1467 until his death in battle. Ruling the wealthy Burgundian State — Burgundy, Franche-Comté, and the Burgundian Netherlands — he sought to weld his scattered territories into a kingdom and to gain a royal crown, ambitions that brought him into conflict with Louis XI of France and the Swiss Confederates.

Reign

Charles inherited from his father Philip the Good one of Europe's richest and most splendid courts. He centralised government, reformed his army into ordinance companies combining pikemen, archers, and artillery, and acquired Alsace (1469) and Guelders (1473). His marriage to Margaret of York (1468) allied him with England against France. In 1473 at Trier he came close to an agreement with Emperor Frederick III to be crowned king, but the emperor departed abruptly and the project collapsed.

Burgundian Wars and death

Charles's expansion alarmed the Swiss Confederates and their allies. In the Burgundian Wars his armies suffered catastrophic defeats at Grandson and Morat (Murten) in 1476, losing his artillery, treasury, and the flower of his army to Swiss pikemen. Besieging Nancy in midwinter, he was killed at the Battle of Nancy on 5 January 1477; his body was found frozen days later.

Aftermath

Charles left only a daughter, Mary of Burgundy, whose marriage to Maximilian of Habsburg carried the Burgundian Netherlands into the Habsburg world, while France annexed the duchy of Burgundy itself — a partition shaping European politics for centuries.
