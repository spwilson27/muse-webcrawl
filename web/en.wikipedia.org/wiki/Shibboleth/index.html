Shibboleth

A shibboleth (/ˈʃɪbəlɛθ/) is any custom or tradition, usually a choice of phrasing or even a single word, that distinguishes one group of people from another. Shibboleths have been used throughout history in many societies to distinguish true members of a group from impostors, and as a form of linguistic password. The term originates from the Hebrew word šibbolet (שִׁבֹּלֶת), which means the part of a plant containing grain, such as the head of a stalk of wheat or rye; or, in different accounts, "flood" or "stream".

Biblical origin

The term derives from the Book of Judges, chapter 12. After the Gileadites defeated the Ephraimites in battle, the Gileadites captured the fords of the Jordan River to prevent the fleeing Ephraimites from escaping. When a fugitive asked to cross, the Gileadites asked him "Art thou an Ephraimite?" If he said no, they demanded that he say the word shibboleth. Ephraimites, whose dialect lacked the /ʃ/ sound, pronounced it sibboleth — and were identified and killed. The text states that 42,000 Ephraimites fell at that time.

Historical uses

Pronunciation tests have repeatedly been used as deadly shibboleths. During the Sicilian Vespers of 1282, French occupiers were allegedly identified by their inability to pronounce the Sicilian word ciciri (chickpeas). In the Frisian–Hollandic conflicts, the phrase bûter, brea, en griene tsiis ("butter, bread, and green cheese") was used to detect Hollandic speakers. In 1937, during the Parsley Massacre, Dominican soldiers identified Haitians by asking them to pronounce the Spanish word perejil (parsley); those who pronounced it in the Haitian Creole manner were killed. During the Second World War, Dutch resistance members used the place name Scheveningen to unmask German infiltrators, and American servicemen in the Pacific used the word lollapalooza, whose sounds are difficult for native Japanese speakers. Finnish forces used the tongue-twister Höyryjyrä ("steamroller") in the Winter War.

Modern usage

In contemporary usage, "shibboleth" has acquired broader figurative meanings: a belief, practice, jargon, or piece of insider knowledge that identifies members of a group, profession, or ideology — often with the connotation of an outdated or unexamined orthodoxy, as in the phrase "old shibboleths". In information technology, the term is also the name of Shibboleth, a widely used single sign-on federated identity system for web applications.
