Churn rate

Customer churn erodes the subscriber base over time

The churn rate, also known as the rate of attrition or customer churn, is the rate at which customers stop doing business with a company or cancel a subscription over a given period. It is widely used in subscription businesses — such as telecommunications, pay television, software as a service, banking, and insurance — as a key indicator of customer satisfaction, competitiveness, and the health of the business.

Definition and calculation

The basic churn rate for a period is calculated as the number of customers lost during the period divided by the number of customers at the start of the period, expressed as a percentage. Related measures include revenue churn, which weights losses by the revenue the departing customers generated, and net revenue churn, which offsets losses against expansion revenue from remaining customers; a negative net churn means growth from existing customers exceeds losses. Definitions vary between companies, complicating comparisons.

Causes and management

Customers churn because of price, poor service quality, better competing offers, changes in their circumstances, or involuntary factors such as failed payments or relocation. Because acquiring a new customer is generally more expensive than retaining an existing one, firms invest in churn prediction — analysing usage and service data to identify customers at risk — and in retention programmes such as loyalty schemes, save desks, and targeted offers. In employment contexts, the analogous concept measures the rate at which staff leave an organisation.

Criticism

Churn figures can be manipulated by the choice of definition, cohort, and period, and reported rates are not always comparable between firms. Analysts therefore often examine churn alongside customer acquisition cost and customer lifetime value.
