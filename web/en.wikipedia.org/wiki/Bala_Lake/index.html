Bala Lake (Llyn Tegid), the largest natural lake in Wales

Bala Lake (Welsh: Llyn Tegid, [ɬɨ̞n ˈtɛɡɨd]) is a large freshwater glacial lake in Gwynedd, Wales. At nearly four miles (6 km) long it is the largest natural body of water in Wales. The lake lies at the head of the River Dee valley, just south of the town of Bala, within Snowdonia National Park. It has long been a site of legend, most famously as the home of the monster Teggie, and in Welsh tradition the drowned palace of Tegid Foel lies beneath its waters.

Geography and geology

The lake occupies a glacial ribbon trough along the Bala Fault. It is fed by the River Dee and smaller streams, and its level, once prone to sudden flooding of the town, has been controlled since the 1950s by sluices built as part of the River Dee regulation scheme, which also helps regulate flow to Llyn Celyn and the lower Dee. The narrow-gauge Bala Lake Railway runs along its south-eastern shore.

Wildlife

Bala Lake is noted for the gwyniad (Coregonus lavaretus), a rare freshwater fish found only here and a relict of the last Ice Age, and for the glutinous snail Myxas glutinosa. The lake and its shores are protected as a Site of Special Scientific Interest and a Ramsar wetland. Stocks of gwyniad eggs have been translocated to another Welsh lake to safeguard the species against the invasive ruffe, introduced in the 1980s.

Legend

According to Welsh folklore, the lake bed conceals the palace of Tegid Foel, husband of the enchantress Ceridwen in the tale associated with Taliesin. A modern legend, the lake monster "Teggie," has been reported in the Welsh press since the 1920s, drawing comparisons with the Loch Ness Monster.

Recreation

The lake is popular for sailing, windsurfing, kayaking, fishing (for perch, roach, pike and the protected gwyniad under licence restrictions), and wild swimming, while the town of Bala serves as a centre for white-water rafting on the nearby River Tryweryn.
