Antioxidant

Antioxidant
ExamplesVitamin C, vitamin E, glutathione, carotenoids, flavonoids
OpposesFree radicals, oxidative stress
UsesFood preservation, dietary supplements, industry

An antioxidant is a molecule that inhibits the oxidation of other molecules. Oxidation is a chemical reaction that can produce free radicals, in turn leading to chain reactions that may damage cells. Antioxidants such as thiols, ascorbic acid (vitamin C), and polyphenols terminate these chain reactions by being oxidised themselves, thereby removing free radical intermediates.

Biology and health

Organisms maintain complex systems of antioxidant enzymes — including superoxide dismutase, catalase, and glutathione peroxidase — together with dietary antioxidants such as vitamins C and E, to prevent damage from reactive oxygen species generated by normal metabolism. An imbalance between oxidants and antioxidants is termed oxidative stress and has been linked to many diseases. While diets rich in fruit and vegetables are associated with good health, large clinical trials of antioxidant supplements have generally failed to show benefits for preventing chronic disease, and some trials (notably of beta-carotene in smokers) found increased harm, leading medical authorities to caution against high-dose supplementation.

Industrial and food uses

Antioxidants are widely used as preservatives in foods to prevent rancidity of fats; common additives include butylated hydroxyanisole (BHA), butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT), and propyl gallate. They are also used in fuels, lubricants, rubber, and plastics to slow degradation by oxygen.
