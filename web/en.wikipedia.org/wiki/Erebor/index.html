Erebor, also known as the Lonely Mountain, is a fictional mountain and Dwarven kingdom in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium. It is the setting of much of The Hobbit (1937) and is mentioned in The Lord of the Rings. Erebor stands alone in the plains east of Mirkwood, north of the Long Lake and the town of Esgaroth, and is the source of the River Running (Celduin).

History

The kingdom was founded by Thráin I in the year 1999 of the Third Age, after the Dwarves fled the Balrog in Khazad-dûm. Under Thrór the kingdom reached its greatest wealth, trading with the Men of Dale and delving deep for gold, mithril and the great jewel called the Arkenstone, the Heart of the Mountain.

Smaug and the Quest

In T.A. 2770 the dragon Smaug, drawn by tales of Erebor's treasure, descended on the mountain, destroyed Dale and drove the Dwarves into exile. In T.A. 2941, Thorin Oakenshield led a company of thirteen Dwarves, the hobbit Bilbo Baggins and the wizard Gandalf on a quest to reclaim Erebor. Smaug was killed by Bard the Bowman of Lake-town, and after the Battle of Five Armies the kingdom was restored under Dáin II Ironfoot, Thorin having fallen in the battle.

Later history

Erebor endured through the War of the Ring, during which it was besieged by Easterlings; Dáin was killed defending the gates, but the kingdom survived and flourished into the Fourth Age under Thorin III Stonehelm.
