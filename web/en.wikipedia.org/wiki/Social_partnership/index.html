Social partnership

Social partnership is a system of industrial relations in which employers, trade unions and government ("social partners") negotiate and cooperate on economic and social policy, typically through tripartite agreements on wages, working conditions and welfare. It is characteristic of corporatist political economies in continental Europe.

Examples

Austria's Sozialpartnerschaft is the classic case, institutionalising cooperation between chambers of labour, unions, business and agriculture since the 1950s, and is credited with low strike rates and consensus policymaking. Ireland operated national social partnership agreements from 1987 to 2009, exchanging wage moderation for tax cuts and policy influence. Germany's collective bargaining autonomy and works councils, and the Nordic models, embody related principles.

Characteristics

Social partnership contrasts with adversarial industrial relations: it involves encompassing organisations, regular consultation, peak-level bargaining and an exchange in which unions accept restraint in return for employment security, training and social protection. Critics note it can entrench insider interests and exclude atypical workers, the unemployed and smaller firms.
