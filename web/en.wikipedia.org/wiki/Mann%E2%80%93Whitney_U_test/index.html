Mann–Whitney U test

Mann–Whitney U test
TypeNon-parametric statistical test
Proposed byHenry Mann and Donald Whitney (1947)
Equivalent toWilcoxon rank-sum test

The Mann–Whitney U test (also called the Mann–Whitney–Wilcoxon test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test) is a non-parametric test of the null hypothesis that, for randomly selected values X and Y from two populations, the probability of X being greater than Y equals the probability of Y being greater than X. It is commonly used as a non-parametric alternative to the two-sample t-test.

Method

All observations from the two samples are ranked together. The statistic U is computed from the sum of ranks in one sample: U = R − n(n+1)/2, where R is that sample's rank sum and n its size. For equal samples, the smaller of the two possible U values is compared against tabulated critical values, or a normal approximation is used for large samples.

Interpretation

Unlike the t-test, the Mann–Whitney test does not assume normally distributed data, requiring only that observations be independent and at least ordinal. It is widely used in medicine, psychology and the social sciences. Care is needed in interpretation: strictly, the test detects differences in distribution, and it equates to a test of medians only under additional assumptions.
