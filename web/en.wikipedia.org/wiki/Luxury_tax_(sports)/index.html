Luxury tax (sports)

A luxury tax in professional sports is a surcharge imposed on teams whose player payroll exceeds a predetermined amount set by the league. Unlike a hard salary cap, a luxury tax allows teams to spend above the threshold but charges them a tax — often escalating for repeat offenders and for amounts further above the line — with the aim of promoting competitive balance between wealthy and smaller-market clubs. The proceeds are typically redistributed to other teams or to league funds.

Teams above the threshold pay a tax on their excess payroll

Major League Baseball

MLB's system, called the Competitive Balance Tax, was introduced in 1997 and is the best-known luxury tax in sports. Teams exceeding the threshold pay rates that increase with consecutive years over the line (20%, 30% and 50% for first, second and third-plus years), plus surcharges at higher tiers, and may face draft-pick penalties. The New York Yankees and Los Angeles Dodgers have historically been the most frequent payers.

NBA

The NBA combines a soft salary cap with a luxury tax: teams above the tax line pay progressive rates for each increment of $5 million over the threshold, with steep "repeater" rates for teams paying the tax in three of the previous four seasons. A 2023 collective bargaining agreement added a restrictive "second apron" above the tax line. Tax revenues are redistributed to non-taxpaying teams, creating a strong financial deterrent that effectively functions as a quasi-hard cap for most franchises.
