Deborah Ann Harry (born Angela Clare Trimble, July 1, 1945) is an American singer, songwriter and actress, best known as the lead vocalist of the band Blondie. Four of her songs with the band reached No. 1 on the US charts between 1979 and 1981.

Born in Miami, Florida, Harry was adopted as an infant and raised in Hawthorne, New Jersey. After college she worked various jobs, including as a Playboy Bunny and a Max's Kansas City waitress, before forming bands. In 1974 she co-founded Blondie with guitarist Chris Stein. The band was a pioneer of the American new wave scene, first achieving commercial success in Britain and Australia before topping US charts with "Heart of Glass" (1979). Blondie's other hits include "Call Me", "Atomic", "The Tide Is High" and "Rapture", the latter widely noted as the first rap song to reach No. 1 in the United States.

Solo career and acting

Harry released her debut solo album, KooKoo, in 1981, followed by Rockbird (1986) and Debravation (1993). As an actress she appeared in films such as Videodrome (1983) and John Waters's Hairspray (1988). Blondie reformed in 1997 and released the hit single "Maria" (1999). Her memoir, Face It, was published in 2019.

Legacy

Harry's two-tone bleached-blonde hair, cool stage persona and fusion of punk attitude with pop melody made her one of the most recognisable figures of the new wave era, and she has been widely cited as an influence by later performers including Madonna and Lady Gaga.
