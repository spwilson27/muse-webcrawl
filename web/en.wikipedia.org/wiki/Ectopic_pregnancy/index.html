Ectopic pregnancy

SpecialtyObstetrics and gynaecology
SymptomsAbdominal pain, vaginal bleeding
FrequencyAbout 1–2% of pregnancies

An ectopic pregnancy is a complication of pregnancy in which the embryo attaches outside the uterus. Most commonly this occurs in a fallopian tube, known as a tubal pregnancy, but implantation can also occur in the cervix, ovaries or abdominal cavity. Because the growing embryo cannot be supported in these locations, an ectopic pregnancy cannot proceed to a live birth and, if untreated, can cause life-threatening internal bleeding.

Signs and symptoms

Early symptoms may be absent or may resemble a normal pregnancy. Classically, ectopic pregnancy presents with abdominal or pelvic pain and vaginal bleeding in the first trimester. Rupture of the fallopian tube can cause severe pain, shoulder-tip pain from blood irritating the diaphragm, dizziness and shock.

Risk factors

Risk factors include previous ectopic pregnancy, pelvic inflammatory disease, tubal surgery or ligation, use of assisted reproductive technology, smoking, and pregnancy occurring with an intrauterine device in place. In many cases, however, no risk factor is identified.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis typically combines blood tests for human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) with transvaginal ultrasound. An empty uterus on ultrasound together with rising hCG levels strongly suggests an ectopic pregnancy.

Treatment

Early, unruptured ectopic pregnancies may be treated with the drug methotrexate, which stops the growth of the embryonic tissue. Surgery — usually laparoscopic removal of the pregnancy, with or without the affected tube (salpingectomy or salpingostomy) — is required if the tube has ruptured, if methotrexate is unsuitable, or if treatment fails. Emergency treatment of rupture includes fluid resuscitation and blood transfusion.

Epidemiology

Ectopic pregnancy occurs in roughly 1–2 per cent of pregnancies in developed countries. Despite improvements in diagnosis and treatment, it remains a leading cause of maternal death in the first trimester.
