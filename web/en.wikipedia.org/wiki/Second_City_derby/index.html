Second City derby

A Second City derby at Villa Park.

The Second City derby is the local football derby between the two major clubs of Birmingham, England: Aston Villa and Birmingham City. It takes its name from Birmingham's status as England's "second city". The fixture, first played in 1879, is one of the oldest and fiercest rivalries in English football.

Location: Birmingham, England

Teams: Aston Villa, Birmingham City

First meeting: 27 September 1879 (Small Heath Alliance 1–0 Aston Villa, friendly)

Stadiums: Villa Park (Aston Villa), St Andrew's (Birmingham City)

History

The clubs first met on 27 September 1879, when Small Heath Alliance — the club that became Birmingham City — beat Aston Villa 1–0 on a muddy, uneven pitch at Muntz Street. The rivalry intensified as the Football League era began: Villa became one of England's most successful early clubs, winning league titles and the European Cup in 1982, while City have historically spent more seasons outside the top flight but have twice won the League Cup (1963 and 2011).

Notable matches

Memorable derbies include Birmingham's 3–0 win in September 2002, their first top-flight meeting in 16 years, marred for Villa by goalkeeper Peter Enckelman's error from a throw-in. The clubs have met well over 120 times in all competitions; league meetings became less frequent after Birmingham's relegations from the Premier League, though cup ties have kept the fixture alive. Meetings are heavily policed, and the fixture has a long record of crowd disorder dating back to the 19th century.
