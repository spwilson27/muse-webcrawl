Sarah (Hebrew: שָׂרָה, Sārā; originally named Sarai) is a biblical matriarch and a major figure of the Abrahamic religions. In the Book of Genesis she is the wife — and half-sister — of Abraham and the mother of Isaac, through whom the covenant promises to Abraham were fulfilled.

Narrative

Sarah accompanies Abraham on his journeys from Ur and Haran into Canaan and Egypt. Long barren, she gives her Egyptian maidservant Hagar to Abraham, who bears Ishmael. When Sarah is ninety, God announces that she will bear a son; she laughs at the prospect, and the child is named Isaac, from the Hebrew root meaning "laughter". Sarah dies at the age of 127 in Kiriath-arba (Hebron), and Abraham purchases the cave of Machpelah as her burial place.

Religious significance

In Judaism, Sarah is one of the four matriarchs. In Christianity, she is cited in the New Testament as an example of faith. In Islam, Sarah (Sara) is honoured as the wife of the prophet Ibrahim and mother of Ishaq, though she is not named in the Quran.

The given name

Sarah, meaning "princess" or "noblewoman" in Hebrew, has been one of the most enduringly popular female given names in the Western world.
