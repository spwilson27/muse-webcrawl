Lattice tower

A lattice tower or truss tower is a freestanding vertical framework tower made of a lattice of steel members, typically in a triangular or square cross-section. Lattice towers are widely used as transmission towers for overhead power lines, as telecommunications towers carrying antennas, and as supports for wind turbines and observation structures.

A lattice tower carrying high-voltage power lines.

Design

The lattice construction distributes loads efficiently through tension and compression members, giving a high strength-to-weight ratio. Members are usually angle steel or tubular steel, bolted or welded together, and hot-dip galvanized for corrosion protection. The tapering profile — wide at the base and narrow at the top — resists overturning moments from wind.

Uses

The most common lattice towers are electricity pylons. Others include guyed and self-supporting radio masts, cellular telephone towers, and famous structures such as the Eiffel Tower in Paris, whose puddled-iron lattice design was revolutionary when completed in 1889.
