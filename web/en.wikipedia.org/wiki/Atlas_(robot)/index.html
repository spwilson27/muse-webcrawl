Atlas (robot)

ManufacturerBoston Dynamics
Year of creation2013
TypeHumanoid robot
PurposeSearch and rescue research; dynamic mobility research

Atlas is a bipedal humanoid robot developed by the American robotics company Boston Dynamics, originally with funding from the U.S. Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA). Unveiled in July 2013, Atlas was designed for search-and-rescue tasks in environments dangerous or inaccessible to humans, such as damaged buildings and disaster sites.

Development

Atlas was built on Boston Dynamics' earlier PETMAN humanoid platform. The first version stood about 6 feet 2 inches (1.88 m) tall and weighed roughly 330 pounds (150 kg), was tethered to an external power supply, and used hydraulic actuation. Several Atlas units were supplied to teams competing in the DARPA Robotics Challenge (2013–2015), where robots performed tasks such as driving a vehicle, opening doors, turning valves, and climbing stairs; IHMC Robotics' Atlas ("Running Man") placed second in the 2015 finals.

Later versions

In 2016 Boston Dynamics revealed a redesigned, battery-powered "next generation" Atlas, shorter and lighter, able to walk over rough and snowy terrain, open doors, lift boxes, and recover its balance after being pushed. Subsequent demonstrations showed the hydraulic Atlas running, jumping between platforms, performing parkour, backflips, and gymnastics routines, made possible by advanced control software and custom hydraulics.

In April 2024 Boston Dynamics retired the hydraulic Atlas and unveiled a fully electric version of Atlas, built around electric actuators, intended as a commercial product for industrial applications such as material handling, initially with Hyundai — Boston Dynamics' parent company since 2021 — as a pilot customer.

Capabilities

Atlas perceives its environment with stereo cameras and a laser range finder (lidar in early versions), and its control system coordinates the whole body for dynamic balancing, manipulation, and whole-body maneuvers. The robot has become widely known through demonstration videos showing increasingly athletic behavior.
