Grand Duchess Anastasia Nikolaevna of Russia (18 June 1901 – 17 July 1918) was the youngest daughter of Tsar Nicholas II, the last sovereign of Imperial Russia, and his wife, Tsarina Alexandra Feodorovna. She was murdered with her parents, her three sisters and her brother Alexei by Bolshevik revolutionaries in Yekaterinburg on 17 July 1918.

Life

Anastasia grew up at the Alexander Palace at Tsarskoe Selo with her sisters Olga, Tatiana and Maria — collectively known by the acronym OTMA — and her younger brother, the hemophiliac Tsarevich Alexei. She was known as a lively, mischievous child, nicknamed "Shvibzik" (roughly "imp" or "little mischief"). During the First World War she visited wounded soldiers in the palace hospitals with her sisters. After the February Revolution of 1917 the family was placed under house arrest, exiled to Tobolsk in Siberia, and finally moved to Yekaterinburg, where they were executed in the basement of the Ipatiev House.

Survival rumours and identification

For most of the twentieth century, persistent rumours claimed Anastasia had survived. The most famous claimant was Anna Anderson, who surfaced in Berlin in 1920; her claim divided the surviving Romanov family and was litigated for decades. The mass grave of most of the family was found near Yekaterinburg in 1991, and a second grave containing two remaining children was discovered in 2007. DNA testing published in 2009 confirmed that all five Romanov children died in 1918, definitively excluding Anderson, whose DNA matched the Polish factory worker Franziska Schanzkowska. Anastasia was canonised by the Russian Orthodox Church as a passion bearer, and her remains were interred with her family's in the Peter and Paul Cathedral, St Petersburg.
