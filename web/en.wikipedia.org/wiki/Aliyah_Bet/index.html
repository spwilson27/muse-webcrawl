Aliyah Bet

Aliyah Bet (Hebrew: עלייה ב׳, "Aliyah 'B'") was the code name for the clandestine immigration of Jews to Mandatory Palestine between 1920 and 1948, in defiance of British restrictions on Jewish immigration imposed by the White Papers. It was organised chiefly by the Haganah's Mossad LeAliyah Bet and, during the war years, aided by the Bricha network that moved Holocaust survivors from Europe to Mediterranean ports.

Background

British policy progressively limited Jewish immigration to Palestine, culminating in the White Paper of 1939, which capped immigration at 75,000 over five years just as European Jews faced extermination. Illegal immigration voyages — beginning in the 1930s with ships such as the Velos — became a central project of the Yishuv.

After the Second World War

Between 1945 and 1948, more than 70,000 displaced persons attempted the journey on some 60 ships. Most vessels were intercepted by the Royal Navy and their passengers interned in camps in Cyprus. The 1947 voyage of the Exodus, whose passengers were forcibly returned to Germany, became an international scandal that helped turn world opinion toward the creation of a Jewish state. With the establishment of Israel in May 1948, immigration became legal and unrestricted, ending Aliyah Bet.
