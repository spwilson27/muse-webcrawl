General Motors Company (GM) is an American multinational automotive manufacturing company headquartered in Detroit, Michigan. Founded in 1908 by William C. Durant as a holding company for Buick, GM rapidly acquired brands including Oldsmobile, Cadillac, and Chevrolet, and for much of the 20th century was the world's largest automaker, a position it held for 77 consecutive years until 2008.

History

Under president Alfred P. Sloan in the 1920s, GM pioneered the multi-brand strategy summarized as "a car for every purse and purpose," annual model changes, and consumer credit through GMAC. The company became a symbol of American industrial power; by the mid-1950s it was the largest corporation in the world, and a U.S. Secretary of Defense remarked on the alignment of its interests with the nation's.

GM lost market share from the 1980s onward amid import competition, quality problems, and legacy pension and healthcare costs. During the 2008 financial crisis GM filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on June 1, 2009, emerging 40 days later as a new company majority-owned by the U.S. Treasury after a government-backed restructuring that shed brands including Pontiac, Saturn, Hummer, and Saab.

Recent developments

Under CEO Mary Barra (from 2014), GM has invested heavily in electric vehicles built on its Ultium battery platform and in the Cruise autonomous-vehicle subsidiary, while remaining one of the largest sellers of internal-combustion trucks and SUVs. GM regained the U.S. sales lead in 2022 after Toyota briefly overtook it in 2021.
