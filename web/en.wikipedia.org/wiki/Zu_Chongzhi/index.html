Zu Chongzhi

Zu Chongzhi (Chinese: 祖冲之; 429–500 AD), courtesy name Wenyuan, was a Chinese mathematician, astronomer, writer, and politician during the Liu Song and Southern Qi dynasties. He is best known for calculating the value of pi to unprecedented accuracy.

Pi approximation

Zu Chongzhi calculated pi to seven decimal places, giving two approximations: the famous fraction 355/113 (called the milü, "precise ratio") and 22/7 (the yuelü, "approximate ratio"). His value, between 3.1415926 and 3.1415927, remained the most accurate in the world for nearly a thousand years, until improved by Madhava of Sangamagrama in the 14th century and Jamshid al-Kashi in the 15th century.

Astronomy and engineering

He compiled the Daming Calendar (大明曆), introduced in 465, which used a more accurate determination of the tropical year and the precession of the equinoxes. Zu also worked on the formula for the volume of a sphere, wrote the mathematical text Zhui Shu (Method of Interpolation), and is credited with devices such as a south-pointing chariot and improvements to water-powered mills. His son Zu Gengzhi continued his mathematical work.

Legacy

The lunar crater Tsu Chung-Chi and the asteroid 1964 VO1 (1964 VO1, "Zuchongzhi") are named in his honour.
