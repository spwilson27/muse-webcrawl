Mahjong

Mahjong
OriginChina, 19th century
Players4 (variants for 3)
Equipment144 tiles (typical set)
Chinese麻將 (májiàng)

Mahjong is a tile-based game developed in 19th-century China and played throughout the world, especially in East and Southeast Asia and in diaspora communities. Played by four players in its standard form, it combines skill, strategy and chance: players draw and discard tiles to assemble a winning hand of sets, typically four sets of three tiles and a pair.

Tiles and play

A standard set contains 144 tiles: suited tiles in bamboo, characters and dots (numbered 1–9), honour tiles (four winds and three dragons), and bonus flower and season tiles. Players build a wall of tiles, take turns drawing and discarding, and may claim discards to complete a pung (triplet), chow (sequence, only from the player to the left) or kong (set of four). The first player to complete a legal hand, or to declare mahjong on another's discard, wins the hand.

History and variants

The game crystallised in the mid-to-late Qing dynasty from earlier card and tile games, then spread worldwide in the 1920s, when it became a craze in the United States and Europe. Regional variants differ substantially: Chinese classical and competition rules, Japanese riichi mahjong — which adds the riichi declaration and dora bonus tiles — Hong Kong rules, and the distinct American mahjong promoted by the National Mah Jongg League, which uses jokers and annual scoring cards.

Culture

Mahjong is a fixture of family and social life in much of East Asia, associated with festivals and gatherings; it is also played competitively in tournaments and extensively online and in video-game adaptations. Gambling forms of the game are regulated or restricted in some jurisdictions.
