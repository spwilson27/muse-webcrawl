Darth Vader is a fictional character in the Star Wars franchise created by George Lucas. He is the primary antagonist of the original film trilogy and, as his younger self Anakin Skywalker, a protagonist of the prequel trilogy. Vader has become one of the most iconic villains in popular culture and is widely recognised by his black armour, cape, respirator breathing and red-bladed lightsaber.

Character overview

Anakin Skywalker is introduced as a nine-year-old slave on Tatooine with an exceptionally strong connection to the Force. Freed and trained by the Jedi, he becomes a heroic general in the Clone Wars but is progressively manipulated by Chancellor Palpatine — secretly the Sith lord Darth Sidious — through fear of losing his wife, Padmé Amidala. Anakin falls to the dark side, takes the name Darth Vader, and helps destroy the Jedi Order. After a duel with his former master Obi-Wan Kenobi on Mustafar leaves him grievously burned, he is rebuilt in a black life-support suit.

As the Emperor's enforcer, Vader hunts the remnants of the Jedi and the Rebel Alliance. In The Empire Strikes Back (1980) he reveals to Luke Skywalker that he is his father — one of the most famous plot twists in cinema. In Return of the Jedi (1983), Luke's compassion helps redeem him; Vader kills the Emperor to save his son, dying as Anakin Skywalker once more.

Cultural impact

Vader ranked third on the American Film Institute's list of the greatest movie villains. His design drew inspiration from samurai armour, and his mechanical breathing, created by sound designer Ben Burtt, is among the most recognisable sounds in film. James Earl Jones's voice performance — recorded without screen credit in the first film at his own request — became integral to the character.
