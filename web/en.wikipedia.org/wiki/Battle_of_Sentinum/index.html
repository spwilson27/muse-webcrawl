Battle of Sentinum

The Battle of Sentinum was fought in 295 BC near Sentinum (modern Sassoferrato, in Marche, Italy). It was the decisive battle of the Third Samnite War, in which a Roman army commanded by the consuls Quintus Fabius Maximus Rullianus and Publius Decius Mus defeated a coalition of Samnites, Etruscans, Umbrians and Senone Gauls.

Background

By 295 BC the Samnites had assembled a grand coalition against Rome, hoping to overwhelm the republic by attacking it from several directions at once. The Romans responded by concentrating an unusually large army — four legions with allied contingents and cavalry — under the two consuls in Umbria, while smaller forces pinned the Etruscans in the north.

The battle

Fabius commanded the right wing against the Samnites, while Decius faced the Gauls on the left. The Gallic chariots and cavalry drove back the Roman horse, and the Roman left began to give way. Following the example of his father, who had performed the same rite at the Battle of Vesuvius in 340 BC, Decius devoted himself (devotio) — solemnly dedicating himself and the enemy to the gods of the underworld — and rode alone into the Gallic ranks, where he was killed. His sacrifice rallied the Roman line, and with the Samnites also being worn down by Fabius, the coalition army collapsed.

Aftermath

Ancient sources give enormous casualty figures, with Livy reporting some 25,000 enemy dead and 8,700 captured. Whatever the exact numbers, Sentinum broke the coalition: the Samnites fought on until 290 BC but never again threatened Rome's dominance of central Italy, and the battle is often regarded as a turning point that cleared Rome's path to mastery of the Italian peninsula.
