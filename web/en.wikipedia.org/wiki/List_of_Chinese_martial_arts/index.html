List of Chinese martial arts

Chinese martial arts, collectively known as kung fu or wushu, comprise hundreds of fighting styles developed over centuries in China. Styles are often classified as northern or southern, and as internal (neijia) or external (waijia), and many are associated with particular regions, temples, families, or animal movements.

Internal styles

  • Tai chi (taijiquan) — slow, flowing movements; major family styles include Chen, Yang, Wu, Wu (Hao), and Sun.
  • Baguazhang — circle walking and continuous palm changes, associated with Dong Haichuan.
  • Xingyiquan — direct, linear five-element fist techniques.
  • Liuhebafa — "six harmonies, eight methods".
  • Yiquan — standing-post practice founded by Wang Xiangzhai.

Northern styles

  • Shaolin kung fu — the large family of styles associated with the Shaolin Monastery.
  • Changquan (Long Fist), Cha quan, Hua quan, Pao Chui.
  • Tai chi praying mantis, Seven Star Praying Mantis and other Northern Praying Mantis styles.
  • Eagle Claw (Ying Jow Pai).
  • Bajiquan, Piguaquan, Tongbeiquan, Fanziquan.
  • Modern wushu taolu — the standardised performance routines of contemporary sport wushu.

Southern styles

  • Wing Chun — close-range style popularised by Yip Man and Bruce Lee.
  • Hung Gar — strong stances and the Tiger Crane form.
  • Choy Li Fut — a combination of northern and southern techniques.
  • Southern Praying Mantis, White Crane (Fujian), Five Ancestors (Ngo Cho Kun), Dragon style, Bak Mei, Mok Gar, Lau Gar.

Weapons and other practices

Most styles include training with weapons such as the staff (gun), broadsword (dao), straight sword (jian), spear (qiang), and flexible weapons like the three-section staff and rope dart. Sport sanda provides a full-contact competitive format.
