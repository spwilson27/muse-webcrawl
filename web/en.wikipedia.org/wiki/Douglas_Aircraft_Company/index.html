The Douglas Aircraft Company was a major American aerospace manufacturer founded in 1921 by Donald Wills Douglas Sr. in Santa Monica, California. It produced some of the most successful commercial and military aircraft of the twentieth century before merging with McDonnell Aircraft in 1967 to form McDonnell Douglas.


The Douglas DC-3 revolutionised air transport in the 1930s.

History

Early successes included the World Cruiser biplanes that made the first aerial circumnavigation in 1924. The DC-2 and especially the DC-3 (1935) — and its military C-47 Skytrain variant — made Douglas dominant in air transport. During the Second World War the company built nearly 30,000 aircraft, including SBD Dauntless dive-bombers and A-26 Invaders.

Jet age

Postwar products included the DC-6 and DC-7 piston airliners, the DC-8 and DC-9 jetliners, the A-4 Skyhawk carrier attack jet, and Thor and Delta rockets and missiles. Financial strain from the DC-8 and DC-9 programmes led to the 1967 merger with McDonnell; the Long Beach plant later produced the DC-10 and MD-11 before Boeing absorbed McDonnell Douglas in 1997.
