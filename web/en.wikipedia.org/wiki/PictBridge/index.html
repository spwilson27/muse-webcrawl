PictBridge

TypeDirect-print standard
DeveloperCamera & Imaging Products Association (CIPA)
Published2003 (CIPA DC-001)
ConnectionUSB, later wireless (DPS over IP)

PictBridge is a standard developed by the Camera & Imaging Products Association (CIPA) that allows images to be printed directly from a digital camera to a printer, without a computer in between. Published in 2003 as CIPA standard DC-001, it was supported by essentially all major camera and printer manufacturers.

Operation

The camera and printer are connected, originally by a USB cable. PictBridge layers a print protocol (based on PTP, the Picture Transfer Protocol) over the connection: the user browses images on the camera's screen, selects prints, paper size, layout and quantity, and the printer reports progress and errors back to the camera. Features include index prints, multiple copies, date imprinting and printing portions cropped on the camera. A later extension, DPS over IP, carried the same printing service over network connections.

Legacy

PictBridge displaced incompatible proprietary direct-print systems and was for over a decade a standard feature of digital cameras and photo printers. Its importance declined as smartphone photography, Wi-Fi printing and cloud services replaced direct camera-to-printer connections.
