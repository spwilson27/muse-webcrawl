Warren and Wetmore

Warren and Wetmore was an American architectural firm based in New York City, founded in 1889 by Whitney Warren (1864–1943) and Charles D. Wetmore (1866–1941). Warren, trained at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris, was the firm's designer, while Wetmore, a lawyer by training, managed its business affairs and railroad connections.

Major works

The firm's most famous commission, won through Wetmore's ties to the New York Central Railroad, was Grand Central Terminal (1903–1913), designed in collaboration with Reed and Stem, for which Warren and Wetmore were responsible for the Beaux-Arts architectural treatment. Their railroad and hotel work across the United States and Canada included the Michigan Central Station in Detroit, the Ritz-Carlton and Biltmore hotels in New York, and numerous stations for the New York Central and other lines. In New York they also designed the New York Yacht Club clubhouse (1899), the Aeolian Building, and Chelsea Piers.

Legacy

Warren and Wetmore was among the most successful Beaux-Arts practices in the United States in the early 20th century. The partnership continued after the founders' deaths in the 1940s, and many of its buildings survive as designated landmarks.
