Barrios Altos massacre


Barrios Altos, Lima

The Barrios Altos massacre was an attack carried out on 3 November 1991 in the Barrios Altos neighbourhood of Lima, Peru, by the Grupo Colina, a death squad composed of members of the Peruvian Army acting on orders from the government of President Alberto Fujimori. Fifteen people, including an eight-year-old boy, were killed and four others wounded when masked gunmen burst into a pollada — a fundraising barbecue — on Jirón Huanta, apparently mistaking the gathering for a meeting of Shining Path sympathisers.

Investigation and amnesty

Judicial investigations pointed to the Grupo Colina and to Vladimiro Montesinos, head of the National Intelligence Service. In 1995, Congress passed an amnesty law covering military and police personnel for human rights abuses committed between 1980 and 1995, halting prosecutions. Journalist Gustavo Gorriti and others had earlier publicised evidence of the killings, and investigating magistrate Antonia Saquicuray was removed from the case.

Inter-American Court and trials

In its landmark judgment of 14 March 2001 in Barrios Altos v. Peru, the Inter-American Court of Human Rights held that the amnesty laws were incompatible with the American Convention and lacked legal effect, reopening prosecutions across Peru. Members of Grupo Colina were subsequently convicted, and in 2009 Fujimori himself was sentenced to 25 years' imprisonment for his role in the Barrios Altos and La Cantuta killings, the first time a democratically elected head of state was extradited and convicted of human rights crimes in his own country.
