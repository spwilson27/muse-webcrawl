Means of production

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The means of production are the physical, non-human inputs used in the production of economic value: land, labour facilities, machinery, tools, infrastructure and raw materials. Together with labour power — the human capacity to work — they constitute the productive forces of a society in Marxist analysis. Who owns the means of production is, for Marxists, the defining question of an economic system: private ownership by a capitalist class defines capitalism, while social or common ownership defines socialism and communism.

Marx analysed how the drive to accumulate capital transforms the means of production themselves — from hand tools, through manufacture, to mechanised factories — and argued that the social relations of production periodically come into conflict with the developing forces, producing crises and revolutionary transitions between modes of production. The concept's boundaries are debated in the digital economy: questions arise whether data, platforms and intellectual property are now decisive means of production, and who controls them.

Ownership forms

Means of production may be held privately (by individuals or corporations), collectively (by cooperatives or communities), or by the state. Marxist programmes have characteristically demanded the “socialisation” of the means of production; social-democratic and mixed economies have instead combined private ownership with regulation, public provision and some state ownership. Critics of the framework argue that entrepreneurship, knowledge and human capital resist classification as means of production, and that the concept neglects the coordinating function of markets and prices.
