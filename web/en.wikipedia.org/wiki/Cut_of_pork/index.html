The cuts of pork are the different sections into which a pig carcass is divided by butchers. The major wholesale or "primal" cuts in the American system are the shoulder, loin, leg, and belly (side), from which retail cuts such as chops, roasts, bacon, and ham are produced. Cutting schemes differ between countries: British, French, and Korean butchery divide the carcass differently and give different names to corresponding pieces.

Primal cuts

  • Boston butt — the upper part of the shoulder, well marbled, used for pulled pork and roasts.
  • Picnic shoulder — the lower shoulder, often smoked or braised.
  • Loin — the lean back section yielding loin chops, rib chops, tenderloin, and loin roasts; Canadian bacon is made from the loin eye.
  • Leg — usually cured and smoked as ham, or sold fresh for roasting.
  • Belly (side) — the fatty underside, cured as bacon or used fresh, as in chashu.
  • Spare ribs and baby back ribs — from the belly side and loin respectively.

Other cuts and products

Additional cuts include the jowl (used for guanciale), hocks and trotters, and the head. Ground pork, sausages, and charcuterie are produced from trimmings of the primal cuts. Fatback and leaf lard are rendered or used in pâtés and pastry.
