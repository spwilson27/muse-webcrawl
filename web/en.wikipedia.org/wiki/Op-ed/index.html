Op-ed

[Image: The opinion pages of a broadsheet newspaper]

An op-ed is an opinion piece in a newspaper, written by an outside contributor or a columnist, published on the page 'opposite the editorial' — the origin of the name when the New York Times formalised the modern op-ed page in 1970 under editor John Oakes. Op-eds are a principal venue where officials, experts and advocates argue policy in public.

Practice

Editors commission and edit op-eds for argument and timeliness, distinguishing them from the paper's unsigned editorials and from letters to the editor. Famous op-eds can move markets and politics; the form has migrated online as the guest essay.
