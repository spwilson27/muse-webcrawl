The World Jewish Congress (WJC) is an international federation of Jewish communities and organisations, founded in Geneva in 1936 to unite Jewish representation in the face of rising Nazism in Europe. Headquartered in New York, it acts as a diplomatic representative of Jewish communities in about one hundred countries before governments, the United Nations and other international bodies, and campaigns against antisemitism and for Holocaust remembrance and restitution.

History

The WJC grew out of earlier efforts, including the American Jewish Congress, to create a democratically elected world Jewish body. Its principal founders were the American rabbi Stephen Samuel Wise, its first president, and Nahum Goldmann, who led the organisation for decades. During and after the Holocaust the Congress worked to rescue Jews, pressed Allied governments for action, supplied evidence to the Nuremberg trials, and negotiated with Germany the 1952 Luxembourg reparations agreements through the affiliated Claims Conference. Under president Edgar M. Bronfman (1981–2007) the WJC achieved a high diplomatic profile.

Major campaigns

The Congress played a leading role in exposing the wartime record of UN Secretary-General and later Austrian president Kurt Waldheim in the 1980s. In the 1990s it led the campaign that produced the 1998 Swiss banks settlement of US$1.25 billion over dormant Holocaust-era accounts, and negotiated restitution of looted Jewish property across post-communist Europe. The WJC also championed the cause of Soviet Jewry, supports Jewish communities facing security threats, and organises the diplomatic corps of special envoys combating antisemitism.

Organisation

The Plenary Assembly, convening every four years with delegates from member communities, elects the president and governing board. Ronald S. Lauder, former US ambassador to Austria and heir to the Estée Lauder fortune, has been president since 2007. The WJC maintains offices in Jerusalem, Brussels, Geneva, Buenos Aires and elsewhere, and works through regional affiliates such as the European Jewish Congress and the Latin American Jewish Congress.
