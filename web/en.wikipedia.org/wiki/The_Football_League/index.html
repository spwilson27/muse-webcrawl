The Football League

The Football League was founded in England in 1888 and is the oldest association football league competition in the world. It was the top-level league in England until the formation of the Premier League in 1992, when its First Division clubs broke away; the remaining divisions continued as the Football League, which in 2016 was rebranded as the English Football League (EFL).

History

The league was founded by Aston Villa director William McGregor, who proposed a fixture list guaranteeing clubs regular competitive matches. Twelve founding clubs — including Accrington, Blackburn Rovers, Everton, Preston North End and West Bromwich Albion — began play in September 1888; Preston won the first championship unbeaten. A Second Division was added in 1892, Third Division in 1920 and a Fourth Division in 1958. The league also introduced the League Cup in 1960–61. In 1981 it adopted three points for a win.

After the Premier League

Since 1992 the League (now EFL) has comprised the Championship, League One and League Two — divisions two to four of the English pyramid — with promotion into the Premier League at the top and relegation to the National League at the bottom. Its clubs also contest the EFL Cup and EFL Trophy, and its end-of-season play-offs are among the most-watched fixtures in English football.
