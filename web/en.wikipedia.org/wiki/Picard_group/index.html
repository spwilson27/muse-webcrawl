Picard group

Elements of the Picard group can be thought of as isomorphism classes of line bundles.

In mathematics, the Picard group of a ringed space X, denoted Pic(X), is the group of isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves (or line bundles) on X, with the group operation being tensor product. This construction is a global version of the divisor class group of an algebraic variety and is fundamental in algebraic geometry and the theory of complex manifolds. The Picard group is named after Émile Picard.

Definition and examples

For a scheme or complex manifold X, the Picard group can also be defined as the first sheaf cohomology group H1(X, OX*) of the sheaf of nowhere-vanishing holomorphic (or regular) functions. For projective space, Pic is isomorphic to the integers, generated by the class of the hyperplane line bundle. For an elliptic curve, the Picard group decomposes into a copy of the integers (the degree) together with the curve itself parametrizing degree-zero line bundles.

The Picard scheme

For a family of varieties, the relative Picard group is represented (under suitable hypotheses) by the Picard scheme, a construction due to Alexander Grothendieck. The connected component of the identity in the Picard scheme of a smooth projective variety is an abelian variety called the Picard variety; its dual is the Albanese variety.

Relation to divisors

For a smooth variety, the Picard group is isomorphic to the group of Weil divisors modulo linear equivalence, tying the sheaf-theoretic definition to the classical theory of divisors. The Néron–Severi group is the quotient of the Picard group by the connected component Pic0, and its rank is the Picard number.
