The Yemeni civil war is an ongoing multilateral civil war that began in late 2014, chiefly between the Houthi movement (Ansar Allah), which controls the capital Sanaa and much of northern Yemen, and the internationally recognized Yemeni government. In March 2015 a coalition led by Saudi Arabia intervened militarily in support of the government, turning the conflict into a regional proxy struggle and producing what the United Nations has repeatedly called one of the world's worst humanitarian crises.

Background and outbreak

After the 2011 uprising forced President Ali Abdullah Saleh from power, his deputy Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi led a fragile transition. The Houthis, a Zaydi revivalist movement from the northern highlands that had fought the government in the 2000s, seized Sanaa in September 2014 and in early 2015 dissolved parliament and advanced south. Hadi fled to Aden and then to Saudi Arabia, which launched Operation Decisive Storm on 26 March 2015 with airstrikes and a naval blockade.

Course of the war

Coalition and government forces retook Aden and much of the south, but the front lines around Marib, Taiz, and Hodeidah hardened into stalemate. Saleh, who had allied with the Houthis, broke with them in December 2017 and was killed by Houthi fighters. The Southern Transitional Council, backed by the United Arab Emirates, separately seized Aden in 2019–2020, fracturing the anti-Houthi camp. A UN-brokered truce in April 2022 sharply reduced fighting, and in April 2022 Hadi transferred power to a Presidential Leadership Council. From late 2023 the Houthis attacked shipping in the Red Sea in professed solidarity with Palestinians in Gaza, drawing American and British airstrikes and widening the war's international reach.

Humanitarian situation

Years of fighting, blockade, and economic collapse have left millions displaced and dependent on aid, with cholera outbreaks and widespread food insecurity. Direct and indirect deaths have been estimated in the hundreds of thousands.
