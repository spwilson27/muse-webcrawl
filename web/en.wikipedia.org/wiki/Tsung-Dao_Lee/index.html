Tsung-Dao Lee (Chinese: 李政道; pinyin: Lǐ Zhèngdào; November 24, 1926 – August 4, 2024) was a Chinese-American physicist, known for his work on parity violation, the Lee–Yang theorem and particle physics more broadly. With Chen Ning Yang, he shared the 1957 Nobel Prize in Physics for their work on the violation of the parity law in weak interactions, becoming, at age 30, one of the youngest Nobel laureates in physics.

Early life and education

Lee was born in Shanghai and educated in China before receiving a fellowship to study in the United States in 1946. He completed his PhD at the University of Chicago in 1950 under Enrico Fermi.

Parity violation

In 1956 Lee and Yang proposed that parity might not be conserved in weak interactions and suggested experiments to test the idea. Chien-Shiung Wu's experiment with cobalt-60 confirmed parity violation in early 1957, a landmark result that transformed particle physics. Lee spent most of his career at Columbia University, where he became a professor in 1956 and University Professor in 1984.

Later work and legacy

Lee contributed to statistical mechanics, field theory, astrophysics and high-energy physics, and promoted scientific exchange and education in China, including the CUSPEA programme that brought Chinese graduate students to the United States. He died in New York City on 4 August 2024, aged 97.
