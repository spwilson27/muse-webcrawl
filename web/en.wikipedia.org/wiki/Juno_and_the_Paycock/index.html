Juno and the Paycock

Juno and the Paycock is a play by Seán O'Casey, first performed at the Abbey Theatre in Dublin in 1924. Set in a Dublin tenement during the Irish Civil War, it is the second of O'Casey's "Dublin trilogy," following The Shadow of a Gunman and preceding The Plough and the Stars.

Plot

The Boyle family lives in poverty in a two-room tenement. "Captain" Jack Boyle, the "paycock" (peacock) of the title, avoids work while his wife Juno struggles to hold the family together. When news arrives of an expected inheritance, the Boyles borrow heavily and furnish their rooms lavishly — but the legacy proves illusory. Meanwhile their son Johnny, maimed and traumatised by the wars, is dragged away and killed by former comrades, and their daughter Mary is abandoned pregnant.

Themes and reception

Mixing comedy and tragedy, the play indicts the destruction wrought by civil war and empty rhetoric. Juno's closing lament — "Sacred Heart o' Jesus, take away our hearts o' stone, and give us hearts o' flesh!" — is among the most famous speeches in Irish theatre. Alfred Hitchcock filmed the play in 1930.

Legacy

The play remains a cornerstone of the Irish repertoire and one of the most frequently revived Abbey Theatre productions worldwide.
