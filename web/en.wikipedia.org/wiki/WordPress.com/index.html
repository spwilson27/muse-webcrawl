WordPress.com is a blog hosting service owned by Automattic. It opened to beta testers on 8 August 2005 and opened to the public on 21 November 2005. It is powered by the open-source WordPress software, enhanced with various proprietary plug-ins, themes, and tools. Unlike installing the WordPress software on one's own server, WordPress.com sites are hosted by Automattic, which handles hosting, security, and updates; in exchange, free users accept limitations on customisation and may see advertisements on their sites.

History

Automattic founder Matt Mullenweg, a co-creator of WordPress, launched WordPress.com to make blogging accessible to users unwilling or unable to manage their own installations. The service grew rapidly, hosting blogs for major media organisations and businesses alongside personal writers. In 2010 it became the default host for blogs formerly on Windows Live Spaces. Tumblr, acquired by Automattic in 2019, has been migrated toward a WordPress-based back end.

Features and pricing

WordPress.com operates on a freemium model. The free tier includes a wordpress.com subdomain, basic themes, and limited storage. Paid plans add custom domain names, removal of ads, increased storage, advanced design customisation, e-commerce tools (via WooCommerce-related features), and, on higher tiers, the ability to install third-party themes and plug-ins and to add custom code. VIP (WordPress VIP) is the enterprise offering used by large publishers.

Differences from self-hosted WordPress

Self-hosted WordPress (often called WordPress.org) gives the owner full control over plug-ins, themes, advertising, and code, but requires the owner to arrange hosting, backups, and security. WordPress.com trades some of that flexibility for convenience; historically it forbade most third-party plug-ins and custom themes entirely, restrictions relaxed for higher-priced plans from 2017 onward.

Censorship and availability

WordPress.com has been blocked at various times in several countries, including China and Turkey, generally owing to content hosted on individual blogs. Automattic publishes transparency reports and has a record of contesting overbroad takedown demands.
