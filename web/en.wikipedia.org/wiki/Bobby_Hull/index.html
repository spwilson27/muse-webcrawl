Robert Marvin Hull (January 3, 1939 – January 30, 2023) was a Canadian professional ice hockey left winger, widely considered one of the greatest players of all time. Nicknamed "the Golden Jet" for his blond hair, blistering speed, and powerful slapshot, Hull starred for the Chicago Black Hawks of the NHL and later helped legitimize the rival World Hockey Association by signing a landmark contract with the Winnipeg Jets.

NHL career

Hull joined Chicago in 1957 and, with Stan Mikita, led the Black Hawks to the 1961 Stanley Cup, the franchise's first since 1938. He was the first NHL player to score more than 50 goals in a season (54 in 1965–66) and finished with 610 NHL goals. His curved stick blade and thunderous slapshot — once clocked at over 118 mph — changed goaltending and helped popularize the slapshot. He won the Hart Memorial Trophy as league MVP in 1965 and 1966 and the Art Ross Trophy three times.

WHA years

In 1972 Hull stunned the hockey world by jumping to the upstart WHA's Winnipeg Jets for a $1 million signing bonus (part of a $2.75 million package), the richest deal in hockey history at the time. His signing gave the new league instant credibility and forced NHL salaries upward. In Winnipeg he played alongside Swedish stars Anders Hedberg and Ulf Nilsson in one of hockey's most celebrated lines, scored 77 goals in 1974–75, and won two Avco Cups. He returned to the NHL after the 1979 merger, finishing his career with Winnipeg and Hartford in 1980.

Legacy

Hull was inducted into the Hockey Hall of Fame in 1983; his son Brett Hull also became a Hall of Fame goal scorer. Hull's personal life drew controversy, including reported remarks in a 1998 interview that he later said were taken out of context, and the Blackhawks ended his ambassador role in 2022. He died in January 2023, aged 84.
