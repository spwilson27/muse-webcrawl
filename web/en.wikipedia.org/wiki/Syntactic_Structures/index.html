Syntactic Structures

AuthorNoam Chomsky
Published1957
PublisherMouton & Co.
SubjectLinguistics, generative grammar

Syntactic Structures is a 1957 book by the American linguist Noam Chomsky, based on his lectures and his 1955 doctoral work The Logical Structure of Linguistic Theory. Though short — about a hundred pages — it is one of the most influential works in the history of linguistics, launching the program of generative grammar and contributing to the cognitive revolution in psychology.

Contents

Chomsky argues that each language can be described by a finite set of rules — a grammar — that generates the infinite set of its grammatical sentences. He shows that finite-state (Markov) models and simple phrase-structure grammars are inadequate, and introduces transformational rules that map underlying structures to surface forms. The book famously presents the grammatical but meaningless sentence "Colorless green ideas sleep furiously" to argue that grammaticality is independent of meaning and probability.

Influence

The work shifted linguistics away from behaviourist and purely taxonomic approaches toward the study of an innate, formal language faculty. Its ideas shaped subsequent theories including the Standard Theory, Government and Binding, and the Minimalist Program.
