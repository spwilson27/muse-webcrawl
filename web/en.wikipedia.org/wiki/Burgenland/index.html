Burgenland (German pronunciation: [ˈbʊʁɡn̩lant]; Hungarian: Őrvidék; Croatian: Gradišće) is the easternmost and least populous state (Land) of Austria. It stretches along the border with Hungary, Slovakia and Slovenia and consists of the region known as German West Hungary, transferred from Hungary to Austria after World War I. Its capital and largest city is Eisenstadt.

History

The area formed the western borderland of the Kingdom of Hungary for a thousand years. Following the dissolution of Austria-Hungary, the Treaties of Saint-Germain (1919) and Trianon (1920) assigned the German-speaking western strip of Hungary to Austria; after armed clashes and the Sopron plebiscite of 1921 — in which Sopron and its surroundings voted to remain in Hungary — the new state of Burgenland took effect in December 1921. The name, proposed the year before, derives from the four Hungarian counties (Burgen) from which it was formed: Bratislava/Pozsony, Moson, Sopron and Vas.

Geography

Burgenland is a long, narrow state divided into northern and southern parts joined only by a narrow corridor near Sieggraben. The north is largely lowland on the Pannonian Plain and includes Lake Neusiedl (Neusiedler See), a shallow steppe lake shared with Hungary and protected as a UNESCO World Heritage cultural landscape. The south is hillier, rising towards the foothills of the Alps.

Population and economy

The population is mostly German-speaking, with recognised Croatian (Burgenland Croats), Hungarian and Roma minorities, giving the state a distinctive multilingual character with bilingual place-name signs in many municipalities. Burgenland is Austria's premier red-wine region, noted for Blaufränkisch and the sweet wines of the Rust area, and tourism around Lake Neusiedl and its spa towns is a major industry. Historically one of Austria's poorest regions, it received substantial EU structural funding after 1995.
