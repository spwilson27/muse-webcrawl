ARD (full name: Arbeitsgemeinschaft der öffentlich-rechtlichen Rundfunkanstalten der Bundesrepublik Deutschland, "Consortium of public broadcasters in the Federal Republic of Germany") is a joint organisation of Germany's regional public-service broadcasters. Founded in 1950 in West Germany, it operates the national television channel Das Erste and numerous radio networks, and together with ZDF and Deutschlandradio forms the backbone of German public broadcasting.

Structure

ARD is a consortium rather than a single corporation: its members are nine independent regional broadcasting corporations established under the laws of the German Länder. They are Bayerischer Rundfunk (BR), Hessischer Rundfunk (HR), Mitteldeutscher Rundfunk (MDR), Norddeutscher Rundfunk (NDR), Radio Bremen, Rundfunk Berlin-Brandenburg (RBB), Saarländischer Rundfunk (SR), Südwestrundfunk (SWR) and Westdeutscher Rundfunk (WDR). The chairmanship rotates among the member directors-general. The federal structure was deliberately designed after the Second World War to prevent any centralised state control of broadcasting of the kind seen under the Nazi regime.

Programming

The members jointly programme Das Erste ("The First"), Germany's first national TV channel, contributing programmes in proportion to their size; each also runs its own regional "third" television channel and several radio stations. Flagship ARD productions include the news programme Tagesschau (produced by ARD-aktuell at NDR in Hamburg), the crime series Tatort, produced regionally since 1970, and extensive sports coverage. ARD also operates or co-operates thematic channels such as One and tagesschau24, the children's channel KiKa (with ZDF), and the streaming platform ARD Mediathek.

Funding

Like the rest of German public broadcasting, ARD is financed mainly by the Rundfunkbeitrag, a licence fee levied per household, supplemented by limited advertising. The system and the fee level have been the subject of recurring political and legal debate, including a dispute over a fee increase that reached the Federal Constitutional Court in 2021.

International services

ARD members are members of the European Broadcasting Union. Deutsche Welle, Germany's international broadcaster, is a member of ARD although it is financed by the federal government rather than the licence fee.
