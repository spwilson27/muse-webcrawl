Expletive (linguistics)

Image placeholder: Syntax tree with expletive "it"

In linguistics, an expletive is a word or phrase that performs a syntactic role but contributes no semantic content, or little of it, to a sentence. The term derives from the Latin explere, "to fill out". In everyday usage "expletive" often refers to profanity, but the linguistic sense covers function words such as the it in "It is raining" and the there in "There are two problems".

Syntactic expletives

English requires most clauses to have an overt subject. In sentences about the weather, time, or distance — "It is five o'clock", "It seems that he left" — the pronoun it has no referent and serves only to fill the subject position; such pronouns are called dummy pronouns or expletive pronouns. Similarly, existential there in "There is a book on the table" introduces the sentence without referring to a place. In generative grammar these items became important test cases for principles such as the Extended Projection Principle, which requires subjects in all clauses.

Other uses

In rhetoric, an expletive is a word or phrase inserted into a sentence for emphasis or metrical effect without changing its essential meaning. Expletive infixes, as in "fan-bloody-tastic", are a related phenomenon in word formation.
