Nā Pali Coast

The Nā Pali Coast is a stretch of rugged coastline on the northwestern shore of the Hawaiian island of Kauai. Its name comes from the Hawaiian nā pali, "the cliffs", referring to sea cliffs rising as much as 1,200 metres (4,000 feet) above the Pacific, cut by narrow hanging valleys and waterfalls.

Geography and access

The coast extends roughly 26 kilometres (16 miles) from Kēʻē Beach to Polihale State Park. No road crosses it; it is reached only by foot, boat or helicopter. The Kalalau Trail traverses 18 kilometres (11 miles) of the coast to Kalalau Beach, passing the Hanakāpīʻai and Hanakoa valleys, and is considered one of Hawaii's finest but most demanding hikes. Much of the area is protected within Nā Pali Coast State Wilderness Park and the Hono O Nā Pali Natural Area Reserve.

History

Native Hawaiians lived in the coastal valleys for centuries, farming taro in terraced plots watered by the streams; remains of house platforms, temples (heiau) and irrigation works survive at sites such as Nuʻalolo Kai. Isolated communities persisted into the twentieth century. The dramatic scenery has featured in films including King Kong (1976) and Jurassic Park.
