Barro Colorado Island is an island in Gatun Lake, in the middle of the Panama Canal. It was formed when the Chagres River was dammed (1911–1914) to create the lake, flooding the surrounding forest and isolating the former hilltop. Declared a nature reserve in 1923, it hosts one of the oldest tropical research stations in the world, administered since 1946 by the Smithsonian Institution (now the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute).

Research

With roughly 1,500 hectares of lowland tropical forest, Barro Colorado is among the most intensively studied tropical sites on Earth. Its 50-hectare Forest Dynamics Plot, established in 1980, maps and re-censuses every tree stem — a model replicated worldwide. Long-term studies there cover monkeys, bats, leaf-cutter ants, forest phenology, and the effects of climate and fragmentation.
