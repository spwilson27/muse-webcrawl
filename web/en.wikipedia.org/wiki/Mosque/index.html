A mosque is a place of worship for followers of Islam. The Arabic word is masjid ('place of prostration'). Mosques serve for prayer (especially the Friday noon congregation), education, information and community life, and are governed in their design by the need for a prayer hall oriented toward Mecca (the qibla, marked by a mihrab niche, beside which stands the minbar pulpit).

Typical features include a courtyard (sahn), ablution facilities, a minaret from which the call to prayer (adhan) is announced, and a dome — features elaborated in regional styles from Ottoman centralised domes to Persian iwans, Maghrebi square minarets, and Chinese or Sudano-Sahelian forms. The first mosque was the Prophet's Mosque in Medina (622 AD); the holiest is Masjid al-Haram in Mecca, followed by Medina and Al-Aqsa in Jerusalem. Non-figurative decoration — calligraphy, geometry, arabesque — predominates, reflecting aniconism in religious spaces.
