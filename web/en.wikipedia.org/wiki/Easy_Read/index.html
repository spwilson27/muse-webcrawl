Easy Read

Easy Read is an accessible format for presenting written information that is easier to understand for people with learning disabilities and others who find standard text difficult. It uses simple language, short sentences, a clear layout and supporting pictures or symbols placed alongside the text. Easy Read is widely used by governments, health services and public bodies in the United Kingdom and internationally to meet duties to provide accessible information.

Design principles

Easy Read documents typically follow guidelines first developed by organisations such as People First and the UK Department of Health, and promoted by the European association Inclusion Europe under its "Easy-to-Read" standards:

  • Use short, simple sentences with one idea per sentence.
  • Use everyday words and explain any difficult words.
  • Write in the active voice and address the reader directly.
  • Use a large, clear font (often 14 point or above) with left-aligned text and generous spacing.
  • Place a picture, photograph or symbol to the left of each sentence or paragraph to support the meaning.
  • Involve people with learning disabilities in producing and checking the document.

Applications

Easy Read versions are produced for benefit and voting information, health leaflets, tenancy agreements, consultation documents and accessible complaints procedures. In the UK, the Accessible Information Standard for NHS and social care services requires information to be provided in formats patients can understand, of which Easy Read is a principal example. Easy Read is distinct from, but related to, plain language and augmentative communication symbol systems such as Makaton and Widgit symbols.
