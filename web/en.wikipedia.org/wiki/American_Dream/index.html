American Dream

The American Dream is a national ethos of the United States in which freedom includes the opportunity for prosperity and success, and upward social mobility achieved through hard work in a society with few barriers.

History

The phrase was popularised by historian James Truslow Adams in The Epic of America (1931): a dream of a land where life should be better and richer and fuller for everyone. Its roots lie in the Declaration of Independence's promise of life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness.

Elements

Common elements include home ownership, education, entrepreneurship, and a better life for one's children. It has been a powerful draw for immigrants.

Criticism and measurement

Critics point to inequality, measured by the Gini coefficient, declining intergenerational mobility, and barriers of race and class — themes in literature from The Great Gatsby to Lorraine Hansberry. See Distribution of wealth.
