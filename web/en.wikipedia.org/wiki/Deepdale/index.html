Deepdale, home of Preston North End

Deepdale is a football stadium in Preston, Lancashire, England, and the home ground of Preston North End Football Club. It is widely regarded as the oldest football league ground in the world in continuous use, Preston having played there since 1878. The stadium takes its name from the Deepdale area of the city and the farm that previously occupied the site.

History

Deepdale was originally used for cricket and other sports before Preston North End took it over in 1878; the club's first Football League match there came in 1888, when North End won the inaugural league championship unbeaten as the "Invincibles." The ground was extensively rebuilt in the 1990s and early 2000s, producing four modern stands named after club legends: the Sir Tom Finney Stand, the Bill Shankly Kop, the Alan Kelly Town End and the Invincibles Pavilion, giving a capacity of about 23,400. A statue of Finney, "The Splash," inspired by a famous 1956 photograph, stands outside the stadium. Deepdale hosted matches of the 2005 UEFA Women's Championship and was for a time home to the National Football Museum (2001–2010) before it moved to Manchester.
