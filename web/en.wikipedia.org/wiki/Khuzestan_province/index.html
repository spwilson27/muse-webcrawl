Khuzestan province (Persian: استان خوزستان, Ostān-e Khūzestān) is one of the 31 provinces of Iran. Located in the southwest of the country, bordering Iraq and the Persian Gulf, its capital is Ahvaz. Covering an area of 63,238 square kilometres, Khuzestan is Iran's principal oil-producing region and one of its most historically ancient areas, home to the ancient city of Susa, one of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in the world.

History

Khuzestan was the heartland of the Elamite civilisation in the third and second millennia BCE and later formed part of the Achaemenid, Seleucid, Parthian, and Sasanian empires. After the Muslim conquest the region became known as Khuzestan, from the Khuz people. In the 20th century the discovery of oil at Masjed Soleyman in 1908 — the first commercial oil find in the Middle East — transformed the province. Khuzestan was a major battleground during the Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988), when cities such as Khorramshahr and Abadan suffered heavy damage.

Geography and economy

The province consists of a fertile alluvial plain in the south, watered by the Karun, Karkheh, Dez, and Marun rivers, and foothills of the Zagros Mountains in the north and east. Its economy is dominated by oil and gas production and refining — centred on Abadan, Ahvaz, and Masjed Soleyman — along with petrochemicals, steel, and agriculture, including sugarcane and dates. The province has a significant Arab population alongside Persians, Lurs, and others, and is home to the Mandaean religious minority along its rivers.
