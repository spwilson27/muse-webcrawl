Data Protection Directive

Full titleDirective 95/46/EC on the protection of individuals with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data
Adopted24 October 1995
Entered into force13 December 1995
Repealed byGeneral Data Protection Regulation (25 May 2018)

The Data Protection Directive, officially Directive 95/46/EC, was a European Union directive adopted in 1995 that regulated the processing of personal data within the European Union. It formed the framework for data protection law across the EU for more than two decades and is an important element of EU privacy and human rights law.

[ Hero image: The Berlaymont building in Brussels, seat of the European Commission ]

Principles

The directive rested on principles drawn from the OECD Guidelines of 1980: personal data should be processed fairly and lawfully, collected for specified and legitimate purposes, adequate and not excessive in relation to those purposes, accurate, and kept no longer than necessary. It distinguished ordinary personal data from "special categories" — data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, trade-union membership, health, or sex life — whose processing was generally prohibited subject to exceptions.

Rights and transfers

Data subjects were given rights of access, rectification, and objection, and rights concerning automated decisions. Transfers of personal data to third countries were permitted only where the destination ensured an "adequate level of protection", a mechanism that produced adequacy decisions and the EU–US Safe Harbor arrangement, later invalidated by the Court of Justice in the Schrems judgment of 2015. Each member state was required to establish an independent supervisory authority.

Replacement

As a directive, the instrument required transposition into national law, producing divergences between member states. It was repealed and replaced on 25 May 2018 by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), a directly applicable regulation that strengthened and harmonised the rules.
