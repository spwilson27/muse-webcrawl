Roadshow theatrical release

A roadshow theatrical release (or reserved seat engagement) was a presentation format used for major films, mainly from the 1910s through the early 1970s, in which a film was shown in a limited number of large theatres in major cities at higher ticket prices, with reserved seating, printed programmes and an intermission — modelled on a Broadway theatre experience rather than continuous cinema admission.

Characteristics

Roadshow engagements typically featured an overture, intermission with entr'acte music, and sometimes an exit music, mirroring stage productions. Films were often presented in large formats such as Cinerama, Todd-AO or 70 mm. Only after the roadshow run ended — sometimes a year or more later — did the film go into general release at regular prices.

History

Early epics such as The Birth of a Nation (1915) and Ben-Hur (1925) were roadshown. The format flourished in the 1950s and 1960s with titles including Oklahoma! (1955), The Ten Commandments (1956), Ben-Hur (1959), Lawrence of Arabia (1962), My Fair Lady (1964) and The Sound of Music (1965). Rising costs and a string of expensive failures in the late 1960s ended the era, though occasional modern releases — such as the 70 mm roadshow of The Hateful Eight (2015) — have revived the format as a special event.
