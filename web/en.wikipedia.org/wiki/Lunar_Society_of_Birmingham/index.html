The Lunar Society of Birmingham was a British dinner club and informal learned society of leading industrialists, natural philosophers, and intellectuals who met regularly in the English Midlands between about 1765 and 1813. Its members, sometimes called the "Lunaticks," were central figures of the Midlands Enlightenment and the Industrial Revolution.

Meetings and members

The society met on the Monday nearest the full moon — traditionally said to make the journey home safer by moonlight — at venues including Matthew Boulton's Soho House and Erasmus Darwin's home in Lichfield. Core members included Boulton, James Watt, Darwin, Joseph Priestley, Josiah Wedgwood, William Small, John Whitehurst, and Samuel Galton; Benjamin Franklin corresponded and visited.

Influence

Discussions ranged across chemistry, geology, medicine, canals, steam power, and education. The network helped commercialise Watt's steam engine through the Boulton & Watt partnership, advanced Priestley's pneumatic chemistry, and connected manufacturers with experimental science. The society faded after 1800 as members died or moved away, though a modern Lunar Society, founded in 1990, continues its name in Birmingham.
