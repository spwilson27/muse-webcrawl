Bill Viola (1951–2024) was an American video artist whose immersive installations explore universal human experiences — birth, death, emotion and consciousness — often using extreme slow motion and water imagery.

Career

Viola studied experimental studios at Syracuse University and worked at early video centres including Art/tapes/22 in Florence and WNET's Television Lab in New York. A near-drowning as a child, and influences from Zen Buddhism, Sufism and Christian mysticism, shaped his art. Works such as The Reflecting Pool (1977–79), Nantes Triptych (1992) and The Passing established his reputation.

Major works

He represented the United States at the 1995 Venice Biennale. Large-scale pieces including The Crossing, Five Angels for the Millennium and the permanent installations Martyrs (2014) and Mary (2016) at St Paul's Cathedral, London, use high-speed filming of actors plunging through water or fire to evoke spiritual transformation.
