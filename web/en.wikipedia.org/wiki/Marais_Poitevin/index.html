Marais Poitevin

LocationWestern France (Vendée, Deux-Sèvres, Charente-Maritime)
AreaAbout 100,000 hectares
DesignationRegional natural park (Parc naturel régional du Marais poitevin)

The Marais Poitevin (the "Poitevin Marsh") is a large area of marshland in western France, on the border of the regions of Nouvelle-Aquitaine and Pays de la Loire, stretching inland from the Atlantic coast near La Rochelle towards Niort. It is the former Gulf of Pictons, gradually silted up and reclaimed, and at roughly 100,000 hectares it is one of the largest wetlands in France.

Geography

The marsh divides into two contrasting zones. The marais desséché ("dried marsh") near the coast is protected from the sea by dikes and drained by sluices; it is used for grazing and grain farming. Further inland, the marais mouillé ("wet marsh"), flooded in winter, is a maze of canals shaded by pollarded ash and poplar trees, navigable by flat-bottomed boats called plates or batais. This lush landscape has earned it the nickname la Venise verte — "Green Venice". Villages such as Coulon, Arçais and La Garette are centres for boat hire and tourism.

History

Drainage works began under the abbeys of the region in the Middle Ages and were greatly expanded in the 17th century under Henri IV, who brought in Dutch engineers. The marsh is known for its market gardening, its white beans (mogettes), and the raising of Baudet du Poitou donkeys and Poitevin horses. After losing its earlier protected status amid drainage controversies, the area regained regional natural park status in 2014.
