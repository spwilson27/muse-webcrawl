Elections in Gibraltar

Elections in Gibraltar take place to elect the members of the Gibraltar Parliament, the legislature of the British Overseas Territory of Gibraltar. General elections are held every four years, or earlier if Parliament is dissolved before the end of its term.

System

The Gibraltar Parliament consists of 17 elected members and a Speaker. Voters may vote for up to ten candidates, and the seventeen candidates receiving the most votes are elected — a form of limited block voting that tends to favour organised party slates. Parties or alliances usually present full slates of ten candidates, and voters commonly vote along party lines.

Parties

The main parties in modern Gibraltarian politics are the Gibraltar Socialist Labour Party (GSLP), which has governed in alliance with the Gibraltar Liberal Party since 2011 under Chief Minister Fabian Picardo, and the Gibraltar Social Democrats (GSD), the main opposition party. The GSLP–Liberal alliance won the general elections of 2011, 2015, 2019 and 2023.

Referendums

Gibraltar has also held significant referendums, including the 1967 sovereignty referendum, in which Gibraltarians overwhelmingly voted to remain British; the 2002 referendum rejecting shared sovereignty with Spain; and the 2016 United Kingdom EU membership referendum, in which Gibraltar voted 96% to remain in the European Union. Gibraltar also voted in elections to the European Parliament as part of the South West England constituency until Brexit.
