Judea (Roman province)

Judea (Latin: Iudaea; Greek: Ἰουδαία) was a Roman province from 6 to 132 CE, encompassing the former territories of the Hasmonean and Herodian kingdoms in the southern Levant. It was created after the deposition of Herod Archelaus, when Judea, Samaria and Idumaea came under direct Roman administration from Caesarea Maritima, governed first by prefects and later by procurators.

History

The province was the setting for the ministry of Jesus and the early Church. Tensions between the Jewish population and Roman governors culminated in the First Jewish–Roman War (66–73 CE), ending with the destruction of the Second Temple in Jerusalem in 70 CE and the fall of Masada. After the Bar Kokhba revolt (132–135 CE), Hadrian merged Judea with neighbouring districts into the province of Syria Palaestina and refounded Jerusalem as the pagan colony Aelia Capitolina.

Administration

Governors resided at Caesarea; the Sanhedrin retained limited authority in religious matters. The province was garrisoned by Legio X Fretensis after 70 CE.
