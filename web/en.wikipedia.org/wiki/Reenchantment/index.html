Reenchantment

Reenchantment is a concept in sociology, religious studies, and cultural theory referring to the supposed return or persistence of magical, spiritual, and wonder-filled ways of experiencing the world in modern societies. It is generally framed as a counterpoint to Max Weber's thesis of the disenchantment (Entzauberung) of the world through rationalisation and secularisation.

Reenchantment describes the persistence of wonder within rationalised modernity. [Image placeholder]

Background

Weber argued that modern Western societies progressively displaced magical explanations with calculation and science, leaving the world "disenchanted". Critics have long questioned whether disenchantment was ever complete. From the late 20th century, scholars such as Morris Berman (The Reenchantment of the World, 1981), Christopher Partridge, and Jane Bennett (The Enchantment of Modern Life, 2001) argued that enchantment persists or has returned — through New Age spirituality, occultism, alternative medicine, consumer culture, fantasy media, and experiences of wonder at nature and technology.

Forms

Reenchantment has been argued to appear in several registers: explicitly religious revivals and new religious movements; "occulture" and the mainstreaming of formerly esoteric ideas; the sacralisation of nature in environmentalism; and even within consumerism and tourism, where experiences are marketed as magical or transformative. Some theorists speak instead of enchantment never having left, proposing that modernity is characterised by a continuing negotiation between disenchantment and reenchantment.

Criticism

Sceptics argue that the thesis is vague, that superficial uses of "magic" in marketing do not amount to genuine re-enchantment, and that secularisation trends in many countries remain strong.
