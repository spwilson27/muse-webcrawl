Distributism

Distributism
Type: Economic philosophy
Key figures: G. K. Chesterton, Hilaire Belloc
Influences: Catholic social teaching, Rerum novarum (1891)
Related: Subsidiarity, Guild

Distributism is an economic philosophy asserting that productive property should be widely owned rather than concentrated in the hands of the state (as in socialism) or a few individuals (as in concentrated capitalism).

Origins

Developed in early 20th-century England by Catholic thinkers G. K. Chesterton and Hilaire Belloc, distributism drew on Pope Leo XIII's encyclical Rerum novarum. Belloc's The Servile State (1912) argued both capitalism and socialism tend toward servitude.

Principles

Core ideas include private property as a right and safeguard of family independence, subsidiarity — decisions at the lowest competent level — guilds, cooperatives, antitrust measures and support for small farms and businesses.

Influence

Distributism influenced Catholic social thought, some agrarian and localist movements, and later thinkers such as E. F. Schumacher, though it was never implemented as a national system.
