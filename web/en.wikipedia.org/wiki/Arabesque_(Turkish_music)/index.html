Arabesque (Turkish: Arabesk) is a genre of Turkish popular music that emerged in the 1960s among migrants from rural Anatolia to Turkey's big cities. Characterised by melancholic lyrics about unrequited love, fate and hardship, and by lush string arrangements mixing Turkish folk instruments with Arabic melodic modes, it became one of the country's most popular — and officially disdained — musical styles.

History

Orhan Gencebay pioneered the style's signature sound, combining the bağlama with large violin sections. State broadcaster TRT long refused to air arabesque, regarding it as culturally degenerate, yet cassette sales made stars of Müslüm Gürses and Ferdi Tayfur. From the 1990s the genre blended with pop, rock and hip hop.

Themes

Arabesque songs are often described with the phrase acıların çocuğu ("child of sorrows"); their themes of fatalism and suffering resonated with the gecekondu (shantytown) experience of urban migration.
