Art Blakey

Arthur Blakey (October 11, 1919 – October 16, 1990) was an American jazz drummer and bandleader. He was also known as Abdullah Ibn Buhaina after his conversion to Islam in the late 1940s. Blakey made a name for himself in the big bands of Fletcher Henderson and Billy Eckstine, and led the Jazz Messengers for more than three decades, a group that became a proving ground for generations of young jazz musicians.

Early life and career

Born in Pittsburgh, Blakey taught himself piano before switching to drums. He played with Mary Lou Williams and Fletcher Henderson before joining Billy Eckstine's influential modern big band (1944–1947), which also featured Dizzy Gillespie, Charlie Parker, and Miles Davis. He then led a seventeen-piece group called the Seventeen Messengers and recorded with Thelonious Monk and other bebop pioneers.

The Jazz Messengers

In 1954 Blakey co-founded the Jazz Messengers with pianist Horace Silver; after Silver's departure Blakey led the band until his death. The Messengers became the archetypal hard bop group, blending bebop with blues and gospel, and their ranks included Clifford Brown, Lee Morgan, Wayne Shorter, Freddie Hubbard, Wynton Marsalis, and many others. Signature recordings include Moanin' (1958), whose title track by Bobby Timmons became a jazz standard.

Style and legacy

Blakey's drumming was powerful and swinging, characterised by thunderous press rolls, explosive accents, and a driving shuffle beat in which the hi-hat was snapped on the backbeat. Beyond his playing, his mentorship shaped the course of jazz: alumni of the Messengers led many of the music's important bands. He was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award posthumously.
