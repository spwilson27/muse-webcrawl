Éclair

An éclair is a French oblong pastry made with choux dough filled with a cream and topped with flavoured icing. The dough, the same as that used for profiteroles, is piped into an oblong shape with a pastry bag and baked until it is crisp and hollow inside. The pastry is typically filled with custard (crème pâtissière) flavoured with vanilla, coffee, or chocolate, or with whipped cream, and usually iced with chocolate fondant.

History

The éclair originated in France during the nineteenth century, where it was called pain à la Duchesse before 1850. Many food historians credit Antonin Carême, the celebrated French chef, with refining the recipe. The word éclair means "flash of lightning" in French; the pastry may be named because it is eaten quickly, or because of the shine of its icing.

Varieties

Classic flavours include chocolate, coffee, and vanilla, but modern pâtisseries offer salted caramel, pistachio, raspberry, and matcha éclairs. In North America, the Long John doughnut is a similar oblong pastry, and Boston cream doughnuts borrow the chocolate-and-custard combination.
