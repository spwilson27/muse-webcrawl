Neurological examination

A neurological examination is the assessment of sensory neuron and motor responses, especially reflexes, to determine whether the nervous system is impaired. It typically includes a physical examination and a review of the patient's history, and can be used both as a screening tool and as an investigative tool.

Components

The examination commonly assesses mental status, cranial nerves, motor function (strength, tone and bulk), reflexes, sensation, coordination and gait. Tests such as the Romberg test, the pronator drift test and examination of the pupils provide localising information about lesions in the brain, spinal cord or peripheral nerves.

Purpose

Screening examinations are performed on apparently healthy patients, while focused examinations are used to investigate symptoms such as weakness, numbness, seizures, headache or altered consciousness, and to follow the course of known neurological conditions.
