The Bhote Koshi is a trans-Himalayan river flowing from Tibet into Nepal, where it joins the Sun Koshi, a major tributary of the Koshi system that ultimately drains to the Ganges. In Tibet its upper course is known as the Poiqu. Fed by glacial meltwater, the river cuts a deep gorge through the Himalaya and is famous for some of the steepest commercially rafted whitewater in the world.

Course

The river rises from glaciers near the border in Tibet's Nyalam County, crosses into Nepal at the Kodari–Zhangmu border crossing on the Friendship Highway (the Araniko Highway on the Nepalese side), and runs south through Sindhupalchok District past Tatopani and Barabise. Below its confluence with tributaries near Dolalghat it continues as part of the Sun Koshi.

Tourism and hydropower

The Bhote Koshi's continuous steep rapids — graded IV–V — draw rafters and kayakers, typically on trips run from near Barabise. At The Last Resort, a camp above the gorge, one of the world's higher bungee jumps (about 160 metres) operates from a suspension bridge over the river. Hydropower schemes, notably the Upper Bhote Koshi project (45 MW), exploit the river's fall, sited near the border.

Hazards

The valley is prone to glacial lake outburst floods from Tibet — a major flood in 1981 destroyed bridges and sections of highway — and to landslides along the highway corridor. The 2015 Nepal earthquake devastated settlements in the valley, and the border crossing at Kodari was closed for several years afterwards.
