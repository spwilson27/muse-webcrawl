Cycladic art is the art produced by the ancient Cycladic civilisation of the Cyclades islands in the Aegean Sea, flourishing during the Early Bronze Age, roughly 3300 to 2000 BC. It is best known for its elegant marble figurines — schematic, minimalist human figures that influenced 20th-century modernist sculptors such as Brâncuși, Modigliani, and Henry Moore.

Figurines

The most characteristic Cycladic sculptures are female figures carved from white Parian marble, with folded arms, elongated heads tilted slightly back, and facial features reduced to a prominent nose; details such as eyes and hair were originally painted. The canonical "folded-arm figures" of the Spedos type are the most numerous. Other types include musicians — harpists and a double-flute player — and figures holding cups. Most excavated figurines come from graves, though their precise function (goddess images, ancestors, companions for the dead, or toys) remains debated.

Other arts

Cycladic craftsmen also produced marble vessels, pottery including the enigmatic decorated "frying pans", and metalwork in bronze, lead, and silver. Many artefacts were looted in the 20th century to supply collectors, destroying archaeological context; the illicit trade has made dating and interpretation difficult.
