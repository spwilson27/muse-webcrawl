The Queen is a 2006 British drama film directed by Stephen Frears and written by Peter Morgan. The film depicts the British royal family's response to the death of Diana, Princess of Wales, on 31 August 1997, and the tension between Queen Elizabeth II's instinct for privacy and tradition and Prime Minister Tony Blair's sense that the public needed to see the monarchy grieve.

Plot

Following Labour's victory in the 1997 general election, Tony Blair (Michael Sheen) becomes prime minister. Months later, Diana, Princess of Wales, dies in a car crash in Paris. The royal family, holidaying at Balmoral Castle, treats her death as a private family matter — Diana was no longer a member of the royal family — and declines to make a public statement or return to London. As public grief builds and the press turns hostile, Blair and his advisers press the Queen (Helen Mirren) to respond. The Queen eventually agrees to a live televised broadcast and a public funeral, and returns to London, where she inspects the floral tributes at Buckingham Palace.

Reception and awards

The film premiered at the Venice Film Festival, where Helen Mirren won the Volpi Cup for Best Actress. Mirren went on to win the Academy Award for Best Actress at the 79th Academy Awards, and the film was nominated for Best Picture. Peter Morgan's screenplay also received wide acclaim; Morgan later developed the material further into the play The Audience and the Netflix series The Crown.
