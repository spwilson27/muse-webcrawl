Hammer

A hammer is a tool, most often a hand tool, consisting of a weighted "head" fixed to a long handle that is swung to deliver an impact to a small area of an object. This can be, for example, to drive nails into wood, to shape metal (as with a forge), or to crush rock. Hammers are used for a wide range of driving, shaping, breaking and non-destructive striking applications.

History

The use of simple hammers dates to around 3.3 million years ago, as stone tools show evidence of being struck to shape other stones. Attached handles appeared by roughly 30,000 BCE during the middle of the Upper Paleolithic. Hammers of antler, bone, bronze, and iron followed, and the design — essentially a mass on a lever — has changed little since.

Design and physics

A hammer consists of a head, usually of metal, mounted on a handle of wood, fibreglass, or steel. The handle acts as a lever that amplifies the force of the swing, concentrating energy into the small face of the head so that the force per unit area — the pressure — is enough to drive a nail or deform a workpiece. Common sizes of hand hammer are graded by head weight, typically from a few ounces to several pounds.

Types

Specialized hammers include the claw hammer (with a forked claw for pulling nails), the ball-peen hammer used in metalworking, cross-peen and club hammers, the framing hammer with a long handle and milled face, the dead-blow hammer, soft-faced mallets, the sledgehammer for heavy work, and powered tools such as rotary hammers and jackhammers. Occupational variants include the geologist's rock hammer, the reflex hammer used in medical examinations, and the gavel used by judges and auctioneers.

Symbolism

The hammer is an enduring symbol of labour and industry, appearing with the sickle in the hammer and sickle emblem, in Norse mythology as Thor's hammer Mjölnir, and in the phrase "hammer and anvil".
