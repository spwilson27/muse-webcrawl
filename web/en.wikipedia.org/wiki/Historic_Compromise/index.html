Historic Compromise

The Historic Compromise (Italian: Compromesso storico) was a political strategy developed in the 1970s by Enrico Berlinguer, leader of the Italian Communist Party (PCI), proposing cooperation with the Christian Democracy party.

Context

Berlinguer proposed the strategy in 1973, partly in response to the Chilean coup against Salvador Allende, arguing that the left could not govern Italy on a narrow majority alone.

Outcome

From 1976 to 1979 the PCI supported Christian Democrat governments from outside the cabinet in a "national solidarity" arrangement. The strategy ended after the kidnapping and murder of Aldo Moro in 1978 and the PCI's return to opposition in 1979.
