Credit rating

A credit rating is an evaluation of the credit risk of a prospective debtor — an individual, a business, a corporation, or a government — predicting their ability to pay back a debt and the likelihood of default. Ratings are issued by credit rating agencies, of which the largest are S&P Global Ratings, Moody's, and Fitch Ratings.

Rating scales

Long-term ratings run from AAA/Aaa (highest quality, minimal risk) through BBB/Baa — the lowest investment-grade tier — to speculative grades BB, B, CCC and down to D (in default). Ratings strongly influence the interest rate an issuer must pay: higher-rated borrowers borrow more cheaply.

Uses

Ratings are used by investors to assess bonds, by regulators to set bank capital requirements, and in structured finance. Sovereign credit ratings grade governments' national debt; the loss of a AAA rating by the United States in 2011 (S&P) and 2023 (Fitch) drew wide attention.

Criticism

Agencies were heavily criticised after the 2008 financial crisis for assigning top ratings to mortgage-backed securities that defaulted en masse, and for the issuer-pays business model, which creates conflicts of interest. Reforms followed under the Dodd–Frank Act and EU regulation.
