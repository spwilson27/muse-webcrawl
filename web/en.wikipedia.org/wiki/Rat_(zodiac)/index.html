The Rat or Mouse (鼠) is the first of the repeating 12-year cycle of animals which appear in the Chinese zodiac, related to the Chinese calendar. The Year of the Rat is associated with the Earthly Branch symbol 子. In Chinese tradition the rat is a symbol of wealth, fertility, alertness and survival, and a sign of good luck in some regions, in contrast to its more ambivalent image in many Western cultures.

The Great Race legend

Folklore explains the rat's first position in the cycle with the story of the Great Race: the Jade Emperor invited the animals to race across a river, and the rat — too small to cross alone — hitched a ride on the Ox's head and leapt off just before the finish line to take first place, ahead of the Ox, Tiger, Rabbit and the other animals. Variants of the story explain the absence of the Cat, which the Rat is said to have pushed off the raft (or failed to wake), accounting for the legendary enmity between cats and rats.

Years and elements

People born in a Year of the Rat are traditionally said to be born under the sign. The year combines with the five-element cycle (wood, fire, earth, metal, water), so that each Rat year carries an element: for example, 1984 was the Wood Rat, 1996 the Fire Rat, 2008 the Earth Rat and 2020 the Metal (Gold) Rat. The Chinese New Year falls in January or February, so the zodiac year does not align exactly with the Gregorian calendar year.

Personality traits (traditional)

Traditional astrology characterises people born in the Year of the Rat as quick-witted, resourceful, adaptable, observant and thrifty, with strong survival instincts and a talent for accumulating wealth; less favourable ascribed traits include opportunism, suspicion, stinginess and a tendency to gossip. Compatibility charts traditionally pair the Rat most harmoniously with the Ox, Dragon and Monkey, and least with the Horse, which stands opposite the Rat in the cycle.

In other cultures

The Chinese zodiac and its rat year were adopted, with local variations, in Vietnam (where the cat replaces the rabbit, though the rat remains first), Japan, Korea and Tibet, and the animal's positive associations — prosperity, cleverness and fecundity — recur in East Asian art and New Year decoration, including red papercuts and gold ingot imagery.
