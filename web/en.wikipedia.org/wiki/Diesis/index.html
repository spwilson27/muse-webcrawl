Diesis

In classical music from Western culture, a diesis (roughly "difference") is a small musical interval with a ratio of 128:125, about 41 cents. It is the difference between three major thirds (each 5:4) and an octave: three major thirds fall short of the octave by a diesis.

History and usage

The term comes from Greek music theory, where it named the smallest interval in the enharmonic genus. Philolaus defined the diesis as the remainder after subtracting two whole tones from a perfect fourth. In medieval and Renaissance theory the word was also applied loosely to semitones and to the sharp sign (♯) in some languages: in Italian, a sharped note is called diesis, as in do diesis for C♯.

Greater and lesser diesis

The 128:125 interval is the greater diesis; the lesser diesis or enharmonic diesis (ratio 648:625, about 62.6 cents) separates other combinations of intervals in just intonation.
