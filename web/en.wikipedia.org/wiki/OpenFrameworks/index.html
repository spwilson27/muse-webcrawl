OpenFrameworks, stylised as openFrameworks, is an open-source toolkit written in C++ designed for "creative coding". The framework is intended to provide a general-purpose glue that wraps together several commonly used libraries, including OpenGL for graphics, OpenCV for computer vision, and libraries for audio, fonts, and video playback, behind a simplified, consistent interface. It runs on Microsoft Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, and Android.

History

OpenFrameworks was developed by Zachary Lieberman, Theo Watson, and Arturo Castro. It grew out of Lieberman's teaching and artwork at Parsons School of Design in New York, where students needed a way to work across media in C++ without the overhead of configuring many disparate libraries. The first public releases appeared in the mid-2000s, and the project has since become a mainstay of interactive art installations, experimental interfaces, prototyping, and education.

Design and addons

The core of openFrameworks provides classes for graphics, animation, audio, video, typography, communication (including OSC), and 3D. Its functionality is extended by a large ecosystem of community-contributed addons — for example ofxOpenCv, ofxKinect, ofxOsc, and ofxGui — which follow a common naming convention and can be added through the framework's project generator tool. Applications are structured around an application class with setup, update, and draw methods, an event-driven model familiar to users of Processing, but with the performance of native compiled C++.

Community

Because it is widely used in art and design schools, openFrameworks has an active community that shares examples, addons, and tutorials, notably through the openFrameworks forum and the "ofBook", a free community-written book for learning the framework.
