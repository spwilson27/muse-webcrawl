The Doge's Palace (Italian: Palazzo Ducale) is a palace built in Venetian Gothic style in Venice, Italy. It was the residence of the Doge of Venice, the supreme authority of the former Republic of Venice, and the seat of its government, courts and prisons.

Architecture

The present palace was constructed principally between the 14th and 15th centuries, its lagoon façade of white Istrian stone and pink Verona marble patterned above two tiers of open arcades. The Porta della Carta, sculpted by Giovanni and Bartolomeo Bon, leads to a courtyard and the Giant's Staircase, guarded by statues of Mars and Neptune by Sansovino.

Interior

The state rooms include the Chamber of the Great Council, decorated with Tintoretto's Paradise, among the largest oil paintings in the world, and a frieze of portraits of the doges — with the disgraced Marino Faliero's place blacked out. The palace connects via the Bridge of Sighs to the New Prison. Since 1923 it has operated as a museum, part of the Fondazione Musei Civici di Venezia.
