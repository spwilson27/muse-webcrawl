Nidderdale

Nidderdale is a valley in North Yorkshire, England, one of the Yorkshire Dales — although, unusually, it lies largely outside the Yorkshire Dales National Park. It is drained by the River Nidd, which rises on Great Whernside and flows south-east past Pateley Bridge to join the River Ouse near York.

Geography

The dale runs for roughly 30 miles. Its main settlements are Pateley Bridge, Glasshouses, Ramsgill, Lofthouse, Middlesmoor, Kirkby Malzeard and Masham at its lower end. Three reservoirs — Gouthwaite, Scar House and Angram — were built in the upper valley in the late 19th and early 20th centuries to supply Bradford with water.

Designation

Nidderdale was designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty (now a National Landscape) in 1994. It is known for gritstone moorland, the rock formations of Brimham Rocks, lead-mining remains, and Stump Cross Caverns.

History

The valley was historically part of the West Riding of Yorkshire and the liberty of Ripon. Lead mining, quarrying and textiles shaped its economy; the Nidderdale Railway (1907–1951) served the reservoir construction and local communities.
