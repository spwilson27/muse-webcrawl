Cassino (Italian pronunciation: [kasˈsiːno]) is a town and comune in the province of Frosinone, in the region of Lazio, southern Italy, at the foot of Monte Cairo and Monte Cassino, in the valley of the Liri river. It is best known worldwide for the Abbey of Monte Cassino, founded by Saint Benedict of Nursia around 529 AD, and for the Battle of Monte Cassino in the Second World War, during which the town was completely destroyed and the abbey reduced to rubble by Allied bombing in February 1944.

History

Ancient and medieval eras

The site originated as the Volscian and then Samnite settlement of Casinum, which became a Roman town on the Via Latina; remains include an amphitheatre, theatre and the tomb of Ummidia Quadratilla. In 529 Benedict founded his monastery on the acropolis above the town, writing the Rule that shaped Western monasticism. The abbey was repeatedly destroyed — by Lombards (c. 570), Saracens (883), earthquake (1349) and finally in 1944 — and rebuilt each time.

Second World War

In 1943–44 Cassino anchored the German Gustav Line across Italy. Between January and May 1944 four Allied battles were fought around the town and monastery hill, involving American, British, Indian, New Zealand, Polish, French and other forces. On 15 February 1944 Allied bombers destroyed the abbey in the mistaken belief it was a German observation post; German paratroopers then occupied the ruins. The town itself was obliterated by bombing on 15 March. Polish II Corps finally raised the Polish flag over the abbey ruins on 18 May 1944. War cemeteries of several nations surround the town.

Post-war

Cassino was rebuilt entirely on a new grid plan after the war and awarded the Gold Medal of Military Valour. The abbey was reconstructed "how it was, where it was" and reconsecrated by Pope Paul VI in 1964. The modern town is home to the University of Cassino (founded 1979) and a major Fiat (Stellantis) automobile plant at nearby Piedimonte San Germano.

Main sights

  • The Abbey of Monte Cassino, with the basilica, cloisters and museum.
  • The Polish, Commonwealth and German war cemeteries.
  • Remains of Roman Casinum: amphitheatre, theatre and mausoleum of Ummidia Quadratilla.
  • The Rocca Janula fortress above the town.
