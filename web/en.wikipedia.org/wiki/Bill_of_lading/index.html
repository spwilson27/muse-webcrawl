Bill of lading

A bill of lading (sometimes abbreviated as B/L or BoL) is a document issued by a carrier (or their agent) to acknowledge receipt of cargo for shipment. Historically used for sea transport, it is now used for many kinds of freight.

Bill of lading
Type: Shipping document
Functions: Receipt, evidence of contract of carriage, document of title

Functions

A bill of lading serves three functions: a receipt that goods were loaded, evidence of the contract of carriage, and — when negotiable / to order — a document of title allowing the holder to claim the goods.

Types

Types include straight, order, bearer, clean, claused, through, and electronic bills of lading. A sea waybill is similar but non-negotiable and not a document of title.

Legal framework

International carriage is governed by conventions such as the Hague-Visby Rules and national laws such as the UK Carriage of Goods by Sea Acts. Electronic bills have gained acceptance under laws such as UNCITRAL's MLETR.
