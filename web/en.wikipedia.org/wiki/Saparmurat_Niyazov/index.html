Overview

Saparmurat Atayevich Niyazov (1940–2006), known as Turkmenbashi, was the authoritarian leader of Turkmenistan from 1985 (as Communist Party chief) and president from independence in 1991 until his death in 2006. His rule was one of the world's most extreme personality cults: he renamed months and places after himself and his mother, erected a rotating gold statue of himself in Ashgabat (the Neutrality Monument), and made his spiritual guidebook, the Ruhnama, compulsory in schools, mosques and driving tests.

Rule

Niyazov's Turkmenistan combined gas wealth with total isolation and repression: opposition was crushed (especially after a 2002 assassination attempt, real or staged), independent media eliminated, opera, ballet and gold teeth banned as un-Turkmen, and hospitals outside the capital closed in 2005. He died of a heart attack in December 2006 and was succeeded by Gurbanguly Berdimuhamedow, who modestly rolled back some cult excesses while keeping the authoritarian system.
