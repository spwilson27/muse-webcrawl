State of matter

A state of matter is one of the distinct forms in which matter can exist, distinguished by the arrangement and energy of its particles. The four fundamental states observed in everyday life are solid, liquid, gas, and plasma.

Fundamental states

In a solid, particles are locked in a fixed structure; in a liquid they flow while maintaining a definite volume; in a gas they expand to fill their container; and in a plasma, gas is ionised, with free electrons and ions making it electrically conductive. Transitions between states — melting, vaporisation, condensation, sublimation — occur at characteristic temperatures and pressures.

Other states

Additional states appear under extreme conditions, including Bose–Einstein condensates and fermionic condensates near absolute zero, quark–gluon plasma at very high energies, and degenerate matter inside white dwarfs and neutron stars.
