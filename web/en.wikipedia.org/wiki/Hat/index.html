Hat

A hat is a head covering which is worn for various reasons, including protection against weather conditions, ceremonial or religious reasons, safety, or as a fashion accessory. Hats typically have a crown covering the top of the head and often a brim extending outward. In the past, hats were an indicator of social status.

History

One of the earliest known depictions of a hat is in a tomb painting in Thebes, Egypt, showing a man wearing a conical straw hat, dated to around 3200 BC. Felt hats became widespread in the Middle Ages, and centres of production such as Milan gave English the word "milliner." In the 19th and early 20th centuries, wearing a hat was near-universal for adults in Western societies.

Types

Common hat styles include the fedora, bowler, top hat, baseball cap, beanie, beret, boater, panama hat, and sun hat. Hats may be made of felt, straw, wool, cotton, leather, or synthetic materials. Specialised hats serve protective roles, such as hard hats in construction and helmets in sport and industry.

Cultural significance

Hats figure in idioms such as "throw one's hat into the ring" and "keep it under your hat." Religious head coverings, academic caps, and ceremonial crowns all belong to the broader family of headwear of which the hat is the everyday representative.
