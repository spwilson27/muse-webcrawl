Carol Susan Dweck (born 17 October 1946) is an American psychologist and the Lewis and Virginia Eaton Professor of Psychology at Stanford University. She is best known for her research on mindset—the distinction between a "fixed mindset," in which people believe intelligence and ability are innate traits, and a "growth mindset," in which they believe abilities can be developed through effort and learning.

Research

Dweck earned her Ph.D. from Yale University in 1972 and taught at the University of Illinois and Columbia University before joining Stanford in 2004. Her classic experiments with children showed that students praised for their intelligence ("You must be smart") tended to avoid challenges and crumble after failure, whereas students praised for their effort ("You must have worked hard") sought harder problems and improved. Her 2006 book Mindset: The New Psychology of Success brought these findings to a wide audience and made "growth mindset" a central idea in education, coaching, and management.

Criticism and replication

Dweck's work has also attracted scrutiny. Meta-analyses have found that mindset interventions produce, on average, small effects on achievement, concentrated among low-achieving and low-income students; a large 2019 national study led by David Yeager, with Dweck as a collaborator, found modest but real benefits for lower-achieving ninth graders. Dweck herself has warned against a "false growth mindset"—superficial praise of effort without genuine strategies or support—and has emphasized that mindsets are context-dependent rather than fixed personal labels.

Honors

Dweck is a member of the National Academy of Sciences and the American Academy of Arts and Sciences. In 2017 she was the inaugural recipient of the Yidan Prize for Education Research, one of the largest prizes in education.
