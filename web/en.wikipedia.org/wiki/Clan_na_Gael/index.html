Clan na Gael

Clan na Gael (Irish for "family/children of the Gaels") was an Irish republican organisation in the United States, founded in New York City in 1867 as the successor to the Fenian Brotherhood. It was the principal Irish-American support organisation for the Irish Republican Brotherhood (IRB) in Ireland, raising funds, lobbying American politicians, and at times financing and organising armed campaigns against British rule in Ireland. At its height it had tens of thousands of members organised in local "camps".

History

After the collapse of the Fenian Brotherhood's Canadian raids and internal splits, John Devoy and other exiled Fenians reorganised the movement as Clan na Gael in 1867. Under leaders such as Devoy and the Chicago-based Alexander Sullivan, the organisation developed close — and sometimes secret — links with the IRB. In 1876 Clan na Gael funded the celebrated Catalpa rescue, in which the whaler Catalpa freed six Fenian prisoners from Western Australia. In the early 1880s a faction of the Clan supported the "dynamite campaign" of bombings in Britain, which brought the organisation notoriety; Devoy opposed the policy, and the ensuing split in 1881, compounded by the 1889 murder of Dr Patrick Henry Cronin by Clan members in Chicago, badly damaged its reputation.

Reunified in 1900, the Clan revived as the American arm of the revolutionary movement. It funded the newspaper The Gaelic American, worked with Roger Casement to seek German support for Irish independence during the First World War, and raised money for the Easter Rising of 1916 and the subsequent Irish War of Independence. After the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921, the Clan split over the Treaty; Judge Daniel F. Cohalan's faction supported the Irish Free State position, while Joseph McGarrity's reorganised Clan na Gael backed the anti-Treaty side and later the Provisional IRA. During the Northern Ireland Troubles, McGarrity's organisation was a founding influence on NORAID and a channel of American support for the republican movement. Clan na Gael still exists in a reduced form as an Irish-American cultural and republican organisation.
