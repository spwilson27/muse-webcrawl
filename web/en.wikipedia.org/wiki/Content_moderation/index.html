Content moderation

Content moderation is the process by which online platforms — social media services, forums, marketplaces and other sites hosting user-generated content — monitor, review and act on content that violates their policies or the law. Actions range from leaving content up, to reducing its visibility ("downranking"), adding labels or warnings, removing it, and suspending or banning accounts. Moderation combines automated systems (classifiers, hash-matching databases) with human reviewers and, on some platforms, community governance such as Reddit's volunteer moderators or Wikipedia's editor community.

Methods

Pre-moderation reviews content before publication; post-moderation reviews it after posting, usually in response to user reports or automated flags; reactive moderation relies solely on reports. Automated tools match media against hashes of known illegal material (e.g., PhotoDNA for child sexual abuse material), detect spam and classify text and images with machine learning. Large platforms employ or contract tens of thousands of moderators, frequently through outsourcing firms in lower-wage countries — a workforce whose exposure to disturbing material has raised documented concerns about psychological harm and led to litigation and settlements.

Policy and law

Platforms set community standards covering hate speech, harassment, misinformation, graphic violence, sexual content and dangerous organizations, and typically publish transparency reports on enforcement. Legal frameworks include Section 230 of the U.S. Communications Decency Act (which generally shields platforms from liability for user content and for good-faith moderation), the EU Digital Services Act (2022), which imposes notice-and-action, appeals and risk-assessment duties especially on very large platforms, and Germany's earlier NetzDG. Political debate over moderation is polarized: critics alternately accuse platforms of censoring lawful speech and of failing to remove harmful content.

Challenges

Moderation operates at extreme scale — billions of posts daily — across languages and cultural contexts, and must navigate satire, counterspeech, journalism and documentation of abuses, all of which can resemble the content policies prohibit. The rise of generative AI has added synthetic media ("deepfakes") and AI-generated spam to moderators' workloads while also supplying new detection tools.
