Bernoulli number

In mathematics, the Bernoulli numbers Bn are a sequence of rational numbers with deep connections to number theory. They were discovered in connection with formulas for sums of like powers of the first integers, published posthumously by Jacob Bernoulli in his Ars Conjectandi (1713); the Japanese mathematician Seki Takakazu found them independently at about the same time.

Definition

The Bernoulli numbers are defined by the exponential generating function x / (ex − 1) = Σ Bn xn / n!. The first values are:

All odd Bernoulli numbers beyond B1 are zero.

Properties and applications

Bernoulli numbers give closed forms for sums of powers 1k + 2k + … + nk (Faulhaber's formula), appear in the Euler–Maclaurin summation formula, and determine the values of the Riemann zeta function at even positive integers, ζ(2n), through a formula of Euler. Their denominators are characterised by the von Staudt–Clausen theorem, and their numerators enter Kummer's criterion for regular primes in his approach to Fermat's Last Theorem.

Ada Lovelace's 1843 notes on the Analytical Engine contain an algorithm for computing Bernoulli numbers, often described as the first computer program. The sequence is indexed as A027641/A027642 in the OEIS.
