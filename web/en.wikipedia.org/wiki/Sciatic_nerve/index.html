Sciatic nerve

Sciatic nerve
FromLumbar and sacral plexuses (L4–S3)
ToTibial nerve, common fibular (peroneal) nerve
InnervatesPosterior thigh, most of the leg and foot

The sciatic nerve is a large nerve in the human body, the thickest nerve of all, formed from the ventral rami of spinal nerves L4 to S3 in the sacral plexus. It supplies most of the skin of the leg, the muscles of the back of the thigh, and the muscles of the whole leg and foot.

Course

The sciatic nerve leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen, usually below the piriformis muscle, crosses the back of the hip joint, and descends in the midline of the posterior thigh deep to the hamstring muscles. At the apex of the popliteal fossa behind the knee it divides into its two terminal branches: the tibial nerve, continuing down the calf, and the common fibular (peroneal) nerve, winding around the neck of the fibula.

Function

Through its branches the sciatic nerve provides motor supply to the hamstrings and all muscles below the knee, and sensory supply to the lateral and posterior leg and most of the foot, sparing a strip of medial skin supplied by the saphenous nerve.

Clinical significance

Irritation or compression of the sciatic nerve roots produces sciatica — pain radiating from the lower back or buttock down the back of the leg — most often caused by a prolapsed intervertebral disc. The nerve is also vulnerable to injury from hip surgery, fractures, and misplaced gluteal injections.
