Insurgency in Cabo Delgado
Date5 October 2017 – present
LocationCabo Delgado Province, northern Mozambique
StatusOngoing
BelligerentsMozambique armed forces; Rwandan and SADC forces; private military companies — versus Ansar al-Sunna (al-Shabaab), Islamic State affiliates

The insurgency in Cabo Delgado is an ongoing Islamist insurgency in the northern Mozambican province of Cabo Delgado, fought between Mozambican security forces (later supported by Rwandan troops and a Southern African Development Community mission) and the militant group Ansar al-Sunna, locally known as al-Shabaab, which pledged allegiance to the Islamic State and became part of its Central Africa Province.

Background

Cabo Delgado is rich in natural gas, rubies and graphite, yet it is one of Mozambique's poorest provinces; its predominantly Muslim population had long complained of marginalisation by the government in Maputo. The discovery of vast offshore liquefied natural gas reserves in the Rovuma Basin, and the granting of concessions to companies including TotalEnergies and ExxonMobil, sharpened local grievances about land, jobs and the distribution of resource wealth. A radical sect, Ansar al-Sunna wa Jamma, had preached against the secular state from about 2015.

Course of the conflict

The insurgency began on 5 October 2017 with coordinated attacks on police stations in Mocímboa da Praia. Violence escalated through 2019 and 2020: insurgents seized Mocímboa da Praia in August 2020 and, in March 2021, launched a major assault on the town of Palma, near the TotalEnergies LNG site, killing dozens including foreign contractors and forcing the suspension of the gas project. At its peak the conflict displaced more than 800,000 people and killed several thousand.

In July 2021 Rwanda deployed troops, followed by the SADC Mission in Mozambique (SAMIM). Joint operations retook Palma and Mocímboa da Praia and dismantled major insurgent bases, though attacks continued in smaller, mobile groups. SAMIM withdrew in 2024, leaving Rwandan and Mozambican forces as the principal counter-insurgency presence.

Humanitarian impact

The conflict produced one of southern Africa's largest displacement crises, with camps around Pemba and Metuge hosting hundreds of thousands. Aid agencies reported acute food insecurity, and insurgent attacks on aid convoys periodically cut off the northern districts.
