George Kenneth Griffey Jr. (born 21 November 1969), nicknamed "Junior" and "the Kid", is an American former professional baseball outfielder who played 22 years in Major League Baseball (MLB). He spent most of his career with the Seattle Mariners and Cincinnati Reds, along with a short stint with the Chicago White Sox. The first overall pick in the 1987 MLB draft, Griffey is widely regarded as one of the greatest baseball players of all time.

Career

Griffey debuted with the Mariners in 1989, playing alongside his father, Ken Griffey Sr.; in 1990 they became the first father-and-son duo to play on the same team and to hit back-to-back home runs. Renowned for his smooth swing, spectacular defensive plays in center field, and charismatic style — including his signature backwards cap — Griffey became the face of baseball in the 1990s. He won the American League Most Valuable Player Award in 1997 and led the AL in home runs four times.

In 2000 Griffey was traded to his hometown Cincinnati Reds, but his tenure there was marred by a series of injuries. He reached the 500-home-run milestone in 2004 and the 600 mark in 2008. After a brief stint with the White Sox, he returned to Seattle in 2009 before retiring in June 2010 with 630 career home runs, ranking seventh on the all-time list at the time.

Legacy

Griffey was inducted into the National Baseball Hall of Fame in 2016, receiving 99.3% of the vote — at the time the highest percentage in history. His number 24 was retired by the Mariners. He remains one of the most popular players of his era and is credited with helping save baseball in Seattle, particularly through the Mariners' run to the 1995 American League Championship Series.
