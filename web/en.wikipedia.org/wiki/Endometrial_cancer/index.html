Endometrial cancer

SpecialtyGynaecology, oncology
SymptomsAbnormal vaginal bleeding, pelvic pain
Risk factorsObesity, excess oestrogen exposure, age, diabetes

Endometrial cancer is a cancer that arises from the endometrium, the lining of the uterus. It is the most common gynaecological cancer in many high-income countries and most frequently occurs after menopause.

Signs and symptoms

The most common early symptom is abnormal vaginal bleeding, particularly postmenopausal bleeding, which prompts investigation in most cases and contributes to relatively early diagnosis. Other symptoms can include pelvic pain and abnormal discharge.

Causes and risk factors

Most endometrial cancers are endometrioid adenocarcinomas driven by prolonged exposure to oestrogen unopposed by progesterone. Risk factors include obesity, early menarche and late menopause, nulliparity, polycystic ovary syndrome, diabetes, and the drug tamoxifen. A minority of cases are associated with Lynch syndrome, an inherited cancer predisposition.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is typically by endometrial biopsy or dilation and curettage, with imaging used for staging. Treatment usually centres on surgery (hysterectomy with removal of the ovaries and fallopian tubes), sometimes followed by radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or hormonal therapy depending on stage and grade. Prognosis for early-stage disease is generally good.
