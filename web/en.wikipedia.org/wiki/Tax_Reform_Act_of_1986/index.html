Tax Reform Act of 1986

Enacted bythe 99th United States Congress
EffectiveOctober 22, 1986
CitationsPublic Law 99-514; 100 Stat. 2085
SignedPresident Ronald Reagan

The Tax Reform Act of 1986 (TRA) was passed by the 99th United States Congress and signed into law by President Ronald Reagan on October 22, 1986. It is often described as the second of two Reagan tax cuts, following the Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981 — although, unlike the 1981 act, it was designed to be revenue-neutral overall, shifting the tax burden from individuals to corporations.

The Act was a major reform of the Internal Revenue Code. It lowered the top marginal income tax rate, broadened the tax base by eliminating many deductions and tax shelters, simplified the rate structure, and removed several million low-income taxpayers from the income tax rolls.

Summary of changes

  • Rate reduction: The top marginal rate on individual income fell from 50% to 28% (with a transitional 38.5% rate in 1987 and a "bubble" rate of 33% phasing out deductions for upper-middle incomes). The number of brackets was reduced from 15 to two main brackets, 15% and 28%.
  • Base broadening: Many deductions and exclusions were curtailed or repealed, including the deduction for consumer interest (such as credit-card interest), the investment tax credit, and generous real-estate and passive-loss shelters. The Act's passive activity loss rules effectively ended the syndicated tax-shelter industry.
  • Capital gains: The preferential rate for long-term capital gains was repealed, taxing gains at the same rates as ordinary income.
  • Corporate taxes: The top corporate rate was cut from 46% to 34%, but the repeal of the investment tax credit and less generous depreciation meant corporations as a whole paid more; the reform shifted roughly $120 billion of tax burden onto corporations over five years.
  • Low-income relief: The standard deduction and personal exemption were increased and the Earned Income Tax Credit expanded, removing an estimated six million low-income Americans from the tax rolls.
  • Other provisions: The Act required dependents to have Social Security numbers (leading to millions fewer claimed dependents the following year) and equalised the tax treatment of many fringe benefits.

Legislative history

Reagan proposed tax reform in his 1984 State of the Union address, and the Treasury Department under Secretary Donald Regan produced a detailed plan. In Congress, the bill was shepherded by Dan Rostenkowski, Democratic chairman of the House Ways and Means Committee, and Bob Packwood, Republican chairman of the Senate Finance Committee. The final bill passed with bipartisan support — 292–136 in the House and 74–23 in the Senate in the final votes.

Effects

The Act is widely regarded as the most sweeping American tax reform of the late 20th century and a model of base-broadening, rate-lowering reform. It simplified filing for many households and closed prominent shelters, though subsequent legislation gradually raised rates again and restored various preferences, including a preferential capital gains rate in the 1990s.
