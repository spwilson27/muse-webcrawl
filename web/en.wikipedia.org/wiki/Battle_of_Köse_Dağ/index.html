Battle of Köse Dağ

The Battle of Köse Dağ was fought on 26 June 1243 between the Mongol Empire, commanded by the noyan Baiju, and the Sultanate of Rum under Sultan Kaykhusraw II, at Köse Dağ ("Bald Mountain"), a pass between Erzurum and Erzincan in northeastern Anatolia. The Mongol victory broke Seljuk power in Anatolia and reduced the Sultanate of Rum to a Mongol vassal state.

Background

After overrunning Khwarazm and the Caucasus, Mongol armies under Chormaqan and then Baiju pressed against the eastern frontier of the Sultanate of Rum, capturing Erzurum in 1242. Sultan Kaykhusraw II assembled a large coalition army — Seljuk troops, Georgian and Trapezuntine contingents, and Frankish mercenaries — and advanced to confront the Mongols in the mountain defiles where their cavalry might be confined. Critically, the sultan rejected the cautious advice of experienced commanders to hold a defensive position and await the Mongols, and instead allowed the army's vanguard to be drawn into an attack.

The battle

On 26 June 1243 the Seljuk vanguard, including the Georgian auxiliaries, charged the Mongol outposts, which executed the classic feigned retreat. The pursuit drew the coalition force into disorder across broken ground, whereupon Baiju's main body struck. The Seljuk army, its units engaging piecemeal and its command paralysed, disintegrated; Kaykhusraw fled the field to Antalya, leaving his camp, treasury and harem to the Mongols, who were reportedly so surprised by the scale of their victory that they waited two days before occupying the abandoned camp, fearing a ruse.

Aftermath

The Sultanate of Rum sued for peace and became a Mongol tributary: heavy annual payments, Mongol garrisons, and eventual direct Ilkhanid oversight followed. Seljuk decline opened Anatolia to the rise of the Turkish beyliks on the western marches — among them, by the end of the century, the small principality of Osman, founder of the Ottoman line. Köse Dağ is thus regarded as one of the turning points of Anatolian history.
