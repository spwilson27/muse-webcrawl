Transformer oil

Transformer oil or insulating oil is an oil that is stable at high temperatures and has excellent electrical insulating properties. It is used in oil-filled electrical power transformers, some types of high-voltage capacitors and switches, and fluorescent lamp ballasts.

Functions

The oil performs two main functions: it insulates the transformer's internal components, filling the spaces within the windings and preventing corona discharge and arcing, and it acts as a coolant, carrying heat from the core and windings to the tank walls and radiators. To serve these purposes the oil must have high dielectric strength, low viscosity, good thermal conductivity and chemical stability over decades of service.

Types

Most transformer oil is a highly refined mineral oil derived from petroleum, either paraffinic or naphthenic based. Because mineral oil is flammable and environmentally persistent, alternatives are also used, including silicone oils, synthetic esters and natural ester (vegetable oil-based) fluids, which offer higher fire points and ready biodegradability. Historically, polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs) were used for their fire resistance, but they were banned in most countries from the late 1970s because of their toxicity and persistence.

Testing and maintenance

The condition of transformer oil is monitored by tests of dielectric strength, acidity, moisture content and dissolved gas analysis, the last of which can reveal incipient faults inside the transformer. Oil can be reclaimed by filtration, degassing and drying to extend its life.
