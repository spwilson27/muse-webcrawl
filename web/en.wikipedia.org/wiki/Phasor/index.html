Phasor

In physics and engineering, a phasor (a portmanteau of phase vector) is a complex number representing a sinusoidal function whose amplitude, angular frequency, and initial phase are time-invariant. It is related to a more general concept called analytic representation, which decomposes a sinusoid into the product of a complex constant and a factor depending on time and frequency.

Phasors are used in electrical engineering to simplify analysis of alternating current circuits. By Euler's formula, a sinusoid can be represented as the real part of a complex exponential; the phasor captures its magnitude and phase so that differential equations become algebraic equations in the frequency domain.

Applications

Phasor analysis is central to the study of alternating current, impedance, resonance, and power systems. The technique was popularised by Charles Proteus Steinmetz in the 1890s.
