Timothy Treadwell (born Timothy William Dexter; April 29, 1957 – October 5, 2003) was an American bear enthusiast, environmentalist, and documentary filmmaker. For 13 summers he lived among coastal brown bears in Katmai National Park in Alaska, camping close to the animals and filming their behaviour. In October 2003, Treadwell and his girlfriend Amie Huguenard were killed and partly eaten by a bear at their campsite.

Life and work

Treadwell, a former bartender and self-described eco-warrior, first travelled to Alaska in 1989. He founded the organisation Grizzly People to promote bear protection and gave free presentations to schoolchildren. He co-authored the book Among Grizzlies: Living with Wild Bears in Alaska (1997) and appeared on television programmes discussing his experiences. Park officials and many bear researchers criticised his methods, arguing that he habituated bears to humans and put both at risk by camping without an electric fence or bear spray.

Death

On October 5, 2003, the day Treadwell and Huguenard were due to be collected by floatplane, a large male brown bear attacked their camp at Kaflia Bay. An audio recording from Treadwell's camera captured part of the attack. Rangers later killed the bear and recovered human remains. Their deaths were the first bear-caused fatalities in Katmai National Park's recorded history.

Legacy

Director Werner Herzog used roughly 100 hours of Treadwell's footage to make the documentary Grizzly Man (2005), which won wide critical acclaim and renewed debate about Treadwell's legacy.
