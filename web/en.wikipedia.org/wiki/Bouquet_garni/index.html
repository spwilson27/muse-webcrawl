Bouquet garni

Bouquet garni
TypeHerb bundle (seasoning)
Place of originFrance
Main ingredientsParsley, thyme, bay leaf
Used inSoups, stocks, stews, braises

The bouquet garni (French for "garnished bouquet"; plural: bouquets garnis) is a bundle of herbs usually tied together with string and mainly used to prepare soup, stock, casseroles and various stews. The bundle is cooked with the other ingredients and removed before consumption, so that the dish is flavoured by the herbs without loose leaves or stems being left behind.

Composition

There is no fixed recipe, but in French cuisine a bouquet garni typically consists of parsley (stems or leaves), thyme and a bay leaf. Depending on the dish it may also include basil, celery leaf, chervil, rosemary, savory, tarragon, marjoram, a strip of leek green (often used to wrap the bundle), peppercorns, or a piece of dried orange peel in Provençal cooking. Fresh herbs are tied with kitchen twine; dried herbs are often enclosed in a small square of muslin or cheesecloth, or placed in a tea infuser, and commercial dried bouquets garnis are sold in small porous bags.

Use

The bouquet garni is a standard aromatic component of classic preparations such as bœuf bourguignon, pot-au-feu, bouillon and many braised dishes. It is added early in cooking so the flavours infuse the liquid, and lifted out before serving. Its function is similar to that of the Indian potli of whole spices or a sachet d'épices.
