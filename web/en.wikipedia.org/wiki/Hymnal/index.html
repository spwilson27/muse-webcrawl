Hymnal

A hymnal or hymnary is a collection of hymns, usually in the form of a book, for use in congregational singing. Hymnals may also include order-of-worship materials, prayers, liturgies, creeds, and responsive readings. The term is commonly applied to the official songbooks of Christian denominations, such as the Lutheran Book of Worship, the United Methodist Hymnal, and Hymns Ancient and Modern in the Anglican tradition.

History

The earliest printed collections of vernacular hymns appeared in the Bohemian Brethren tradition (1501) and in the Lutheran Reformation; Martin Luther and his collaborators published the Achtliederbuch and the Erfurt Enchiridion in 1524. Reformed churches favoring exclusive psalmody produced metrical psalters, such as the Genevan Psalter (1562) and the Scottish Psalter. Isaac Watts's Hymns and Spiritual Songs (1707) and John Wesley's collections for the Methodist movement transformed English-language hymnody in the eighteenth century. The nineteenth century saw the classic Victorian hymnals — most famously Hymns Ancient and Modern (1861) — and in America the shape-note tunebooks of the Sacred Harp tradition.

Contents and organization

Hymnals are typically organized by liturgical season, theological topic, or biblical reference, with indexes of first lines, tunes, meters, authors, and composers. Each entry pairs a hymn text with a tune, often identified by a tune name (such as "Dix" or "Nicaea"), and modern hymnals usually print four-part harmonizations. Denominational committees revise hymnals periodically, balancing familiar standards with new compositions and global song, and wrestling with questions of language, including the revision of archaic or gendered wording.

Notable hymnals

  • Hymns Ancient and Modern (1861)
  • The English Hymnal (1906), edited by Ralph Vaughan Williams

  • The United Methodist Hymnal (1989)
