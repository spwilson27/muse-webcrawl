Law of Jersey

The law of Jersey is the legal system of the Bailiwick of Jersey, a Crown Dependency in the Channel Islands. It is a mixed legal system drawing on Norman customary law, English common law, and modern statute enacted by the States Assembly.

Sources

Jersey law derives from the customary law of Normandy, principally as recorded in the Très Ancien Coutume, together with legislation passed by the States Assembly and, in some areas, English statutes extended to the island. Royal charters and Orders in Council also form part of the constitutional framework.

Courts

The principal court is the Royal Court, presided over by the Bailiff, sitting with Jurats who act as judges of fact. Appeals lie to the Court of Appeal of Jersey and ultimately to the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in London.
