Palatinate (wine region)

The Palatinate (German: Pfalz) is a German wine-growing region (Anbaugebiet) in the area of the Palatinate in the state of Rhineland-Palatinate. With some 23,000 hectares of vines it is the second-largest wine region in Germany, after Rheinhessen. It lies along the eastern slopes of the Haardt mountains, the continuation of the Vosges, whose rain shadow gives the region one of Germany's warmest and driest climates, where almonds, figs and lemons also ripen.

Geography and the Wine Route

The vineyards stretch for about 85 kilometres along the German Wine Route (Deutsche Weinstraße), established in 1935 and commonly described as the oldest tourist wine route in the world, running from Schweigen at the French border north to Bockenheim. The region is divided into the Mittelhaardt/Deutsche Weinstraße and Südliche Weinstraße districts, and contains famous wine villages such as Deidesheim, Forst, Wachenheim and Bad Dürkheim, the latter home to the world's largest wine festival, the Dürkheimer Wurstmarkt, and the giant Dürkheimer Riesenfass barrel.

Wines

The region is best known for Riesling, which accounts for roughly a quarter of plantings and produces wines from dry (trocken) styles to celebrated wines from basalt and limestone sites such as Forster Jesuitengarten and Deidesheimer Kalkofen. Other significant varieties include Dornfelder (the leading red), Grauburgunder (Pinot gris), Weißburgunder (Pinot blanc), Spätburgunder (Pinot noir), Müller-Thurgau, Kerner and Scheurebe. The Palatinate is also a stronghold of German dry white wine production and of the "VDP" association of top estates.
