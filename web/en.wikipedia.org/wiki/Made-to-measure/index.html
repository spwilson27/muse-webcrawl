Made-to-measure

IndustryTailoring, fashion retail
PositionBetween ready-to-wear and bespoke
ProcessStandard pattern adjusted to individual measurements

Made-to-measure (MTM) refers to garments — most commonly men's suits — that are cut from a pre-existing standard pattern and then adjusted to fit an individual customer's measurements. It occupies the middle ground between off-the-rack ready-to-wear clothing and fully bespoke tailoring, in which a unique pattern is drafted from scratch for each client.

Process

A fitter takes the customer's key measurements — chest, waist, sleeve and trouser length, shoulder width and posture notes — and selects the closest stock size ("block") of a house pattern. The pattern is then modified within set limits: lengths can be shortened or lengthened, the waist suppressed, and details such as lapel style, buttons, vents and lining chosen from a menu of options. The garment is made up in a factory or workshop, usually over two to six weeks, and may receive one fitting and final alterations before delivery.

Comparison with bespoke

Bespoke tailoring involves creating an individual paper pattern, multiple fittings including a basted fitting, and extensive handwork, at a correspondingly higher price and longer timeline. Made-to-measure offers much of the fit benefit at a fraction of the cost, but adjustments are constrained by the base pattern, so extreme posture or proportion differences cannot always be accommodated. The rise of online MTM retailers in the 2000s — customers submitting self-measurements through websites — made the service mass-market, though fit quality depends heavily on the accuracy of measurements and the sophistication of the pattern system.
