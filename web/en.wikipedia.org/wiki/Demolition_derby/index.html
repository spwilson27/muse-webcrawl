Demolition derby

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A demolition derby is a type of motorsport, usually presented at county fairs and festivals, in which a number of drivers compete by deliberately ramming their vehicles into one another. The last driver whose vehicle is still operational is awarded the victory. Demolition derbies originated in the United States and quickly spread to other western nations; they are also popular in the United Kingdom, where they overlap with the related sport of banger racing.

History

Demolition derbies emerged in the 1940s and 1950s at American county fairs and stock car tracks. Promoter Don Basile is often credited with holding the first true demolition derby at Carrell Speedway in Gardena, California, in 1946, while driver Larry Mendelsohn popularised the format on Long Island, New York, in the late 1950s. National television coverage in the 1960s and 1970s — notably on ABC's Wide World of Sports — turned the derby into a mass spectacle. The sport declined somewhat as suitable older full-frame cars became scarce, but it remains a staple of rural American fairs.

Rules and format

Events are normally held in dirt or mud arenas enclosed by barriers. Drivers, wearing helmets and seatbelts, ram opponents — usually reversing into them to protect their own radiator and engine at the front — while avoiding deliberate driver's-door hits, which are prohibited for safety. A car is eliminated when it is immobilised, catches fire, rolls over, or fails to make contact within a time limit. Heats are followed by a feature event, and separate classes exist for compact cars, trucks, and even combine harvesters and buses.

Vehicles

Traditional derbies favour large, sturdy American sedans and station wagons from the 1960s and 1970s; the 1964–1966 Chrysler Imperial is so durable that it is banned from many events. Cars are stripped of glass, trim, and interior fittings; doors are welded or chained shut; radiators and fuel systems are modified or relocated for safety. After the event, the wrecks are usually sold for scrap.
