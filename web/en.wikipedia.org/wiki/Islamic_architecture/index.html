Islamic architecture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The Shah Mosque in Isfahan at dusk, its blue-tiled dome and minarets reflected in the plaza pool]
The Shah Mosque in Isfahan at dusk, its blue-tiled dome and minarets reflected in the plaza pool

Islamic architecture encompasses the architectural styles of buildings associated with Islam across fourteen centuries and three continents: mosques above all, but also madrasas, mausolea, palaces, caravanserais, baths and fortifications. Recurring elements — the Minaret, the dome, the iwan, the courtyard, pointed arches, muqarnas and the avoidance of images in religious settings — are combined with local materials and traditions from Andalusia to Indonesia.

Origins and the mosque

The Prophet's house-mosque at Medina set the courtyard template; the Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem (691–92), the first major Islamic monument, fused Byzantine and Persian craft. Mosque plans diversified: hypostyle halls (Cordoba), courtyard-and-iwan schemes in Persia, Ottoman domed-centralised designs perfected by Mimar Sinan (the Selimiye Mosque, his masterpiece, 1575), mud-brick Sudano-Sahelian towers (Djenné), and pagoda-like mosques in China.

Elements and engineering

The minaret evolved from Syrian church towers and lighthouse forms into regional signatures — pencil-thin Ottoman, square Maghrebi, spiral Malwiya at Samarra. Domes on pendentives and squinches, horseshoe and pointed arches (predating Gothic), intricate stalactite muqarnas, carved stucco, zellij tilework and girih geometry gave builders a shared ornamental grammar, while windcatchers, qanats and courtyards engineered comfort in hot climates centuries before mechanical cooling.

Modern era

Colonialism and oil wealth reframed Islamic architecture between revivalism and modernism: Hassan Fathy's vernacular revival, the Aga Khan Award for Architecture, and contemporary works (Louvre Abu Dhabi's light-filtering dome, Doha's Museum of Islamic Art by I. M. Pei) debate how tradition translates into the present — debates inseparable from questions of identity across the Muslim world.
