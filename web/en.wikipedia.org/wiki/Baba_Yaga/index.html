Baba Yaga

Baba Yaga (Russian: Ба́ба-Яга́) is a supernatural being from Slavic folklore who appears as a deformed or ferocious-looking old woman. She is one of the most famous figures of Slavic fairy tales, known for living deep in the forest in a hut that stands on chicken legs, and for flying in a mortar while steering with a pestle.

Characteristics

Baba Yaga is an ambiguous figure: in many tales she is a villain who threatens to eat travellers, especially children, but in others she acts as a donor who tests heroes and provides them with magical aid. She is often depicted with iron teeth, a long bony nose, and an appetite for human flesh. Her name is traditionally understood as "Grandmother Yaga", though the origin of the second element is debated.

The hut

Her dwelling is a hut perched on giant chicken legs, which can turn around in place. The hero must command it: "Hut, hut, turn your back to the forest and your front to me." The hut is guarded by a fence made of human bones topped with skulls whose eye sockets glow.

In folktales

Baba Yaga appears in many Russian fairy tales collected by Alexander Afanasyev, including "Vasilisa the Beautiful", in which a young girl is sent to fetch fire from Baba Yaga and succeeds through kindness and the help of a magic doll. She also figures in tales about Ivan Tsarevich and the Firebird. Folklorists such as Vladimir Propp interpreted her as a guardian of the boundary between the world of the living and the realm of the dead.

In modern culture

Baba Yaga appears widely in modern literature, film, animation, and games, often as a byword for a witch of the deep forest. She is also referenced in the John Wick film series, where the protagonist is nicknamed "Baba Yaga" as a feared assassin.
