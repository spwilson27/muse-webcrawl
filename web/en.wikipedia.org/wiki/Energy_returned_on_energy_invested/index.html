Energy returned on energy invested

In energy economics and ecological energetics, energy return on investment (EROI), also energy returned on energy invested (ERoEI), is the ratio of the amount of usable energy (exergy) delivered from a particular energy resource to the amount of energy used to obtain that energy resource. When the EROI of a resource is below or equal to one, that energy source becomes a net "energy sink" and can no longer be used as a source of energy.

Calculation

EROI = Usable energy delivered ÷ Energy required to deliver that energy. Estimates vary widely depending on system boundaries: oil and gas production historically enjoyed EROIs of around 100:1 in the early 20th century, falling to roughly 10–20:1 for many modern operations; hydropower often exceeds 40:1; wind and solar photovoltaics are commonly estimated in the range of 10–30:1; and some biofuels, such as corn ethanol, lie close to 1:1.

Significance

Researchers such as Charles A. S. Hall argue that a complex industrial society requires an average EROI well above unity — figures around 3:1 at the point of use as an absolute minimum, and higher to sustain infrastructure, healthcare and education — because the surplus energy above break-even is what powers everything else in the economy. Declining EROI of fossil fuels is discussed in relation to net energy analysis, the energy transition, and debates about peak oil.

Criticism

Critics note that EROI results are sensitive to methodological choices about what energy inputs to count, that the measure ignores energy quality differences and economics, and that it may not predict the viability of a technology whose costs fall rapidly.
