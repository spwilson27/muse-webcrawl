Aterui

Died:13 September 802, Kawachi Province, Japan
Occupation:Emishi chieftain
Known for:Resistance to Yamato expansion in the Tōhoku region

Aterui (阿弖流為, died September 13, 802) was a chief of the Emishi people of the Isawa region in northern Japan, in what is now southern Iwate Prefecture. He led the resistance of the Emishi against the imperial Yamato court during its campaigns to conquer the Tōhoku region in the late 8th century.

The Thirty-Eight Years' War

During the long war between the imperial court and the Emishi, Aterui's forces ambushed and defeated a large imperial army at the Battle of Sufuse Village in 789, inflicting heavy losses. However, subsequent campaigns under Sakanoue no Tamuramaro gradually overwhelmed Emishi resistance.

Surrender and death

In 802 Aterui surrendered with his ally More, on the understanding their lives would be spared. Tamuramaro argued in the capital for clemency, but court nobles insisted on execution, and Aterui was beheaded in Kawachi Province. His head was buried at a site in present-day Hirakata, Osaka, where a memorial stone stands today. Aterui is remembered as a heroic figure of the north, and his story features in regional festivals and a 2013 NHK historical drama.
