Nā Pali Coast State Park

LocationKauai, Hawaii, United States
Area6,175 acres (2,499 ha)
FeaturesSea cliffs, valleys, waterfalls, beaches

Nā Pali Coast State Park is a 6,175-acre state park on the northwestern shore of the island of Kauai in Hawaii. The Nā Pali ("the cliffs") coastline is famed for its towering emerald sea cliffs (pali) rising as much as 1,200 metres above the Pacific Ocean, deeply carved valleys, waterfalls, and remote beaches.

Access and trails

The park is inaccessible by road. It can be reached by hiking the 11-mile Kalalau Trail from Ke'e Beach, which traverses Hanakapi'ai and Hanakoa valleys to Kalalau Beach, or by boat and kayak landings (with permits) in summer. Camping is permitted at Kalalau and Hanakoa, and the park protects archaeological remains of ancient Hawaiian settlements, terraced taro fields, and native ecosystems including habitats for endangered seabirds and monk seals.

History

The Kalalau Valley was inhabited by Hawaiians for centuries, with communities remaining there into the early 20th century. The dramatic landscape has served as a filming location for numerous movies and is regarded as one of the most beautiful coastlines in the world.
