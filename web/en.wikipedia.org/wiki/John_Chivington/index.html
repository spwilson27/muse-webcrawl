John Milton Chivington (January 27, 1821 – October 4, 1894) was an American army officer and Methodist pastor who gained early fame in the American Civil War and permanent infamy for his role in the Sand Creek massacre of November 1864, in which Colorado volunteers under his command attacked a peaceful village of Cheyenne and Arapaho people, killing roughly 150 to 200, mostly women, children, and the elderly.

Early life and ministry

Chivington was born in Lebanon, Ohio, and spent part of his youth in Missouri. Ordained a Methodist minister, he served as a missionary and presiding elder in Nebraska and Colorado. A large, imposing man, he was nicknamed the "Fighting Parson" for combining frontier preaching with military service.

Civil War

At the outbreak of the Civil War, Chivington helped raise Colorado volunteer units. In the New Mexico campaign of 1862 he commanded the detachment that surprised and destroyed the Confederate supply train at the Battle of Glorieta Pass, an action sometimes called the "Gettysburg of the West." He was commissioned colonel of the 1st Colorado Cavalry and later commanded the District of Colorado.

Sand Creek massacre

On November 29, 1864, Chivington led about 675 men in an attack on a village under Cheyenne chief Black Kettle at Sand Creek in southeastern Colorado Territory. The village flew a United States flag and a white flag and considered itself at peace under military protection. The attack killed between 150 and 200 Native Americans, and soldiers mutilated many of the dead. Federal investigations condemned the massacre as a "foul and dastardly" act. Chivington resigned his commission before charges could be brought and was never prosecuted, though the massacre destroyed his career and reputation.

Later life and death

Chivington returned to Nebraska, then spent his final years in Denver, where he worked as a deputy sheriff and newspaper editor and remained an unrepentant defender of the Sand Creek attack. He died of cancer in 1894. The town of Chivington, Colorado, was named for him.
