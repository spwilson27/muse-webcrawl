USA for Africa (United Support of Artists for Africa) was a 1985 charity supergroup of 47 American recording artists who came together to record the single "We Are the World", written by Michael Jackson and Lionel Richie and produced by Quincy Jones, to raise money for famine relief in Africa, particularly Ethiopia, which was suffering a devastating famine in 1983–1985.

The recording

Inspired by Band Aid's "Do They Know It's Christmas?" and organised by Harry Belafonte and manager Ken Kragen, the song was recorded on the night of 28 January 1985 at A&M Recording Studios in Hollywood, immediately after the American Music Awards. Soloists included Stevie Wonder, Paul Simon, Kenny Rogers, Tina Turner, Billy Joel, Diana Ross, Dionne Warwick, Willie Nelson, Bruce Springsteen, Huey Lewis, Cyndi Lauper, and Bob Dylan, among many others.

Impact

Released on 7 March 1985, "We Are the World" became one of the best-selling singles of all time, with over 20 million copies sold, and won four Grammy Awards including Record and Song of the Year. Combined with the album, merchandise, and related events such as Hands Across America, USA for Africa raised more than $75 million for food, medicine, and development projects. The foundation continued to distribute funds for decades, supporting long-term development as well as emergency relief.

In 2010, following the Haiti earthquake, a new version, "We Are the World 25 for Haiti", was recorded by a new generation of artists.
