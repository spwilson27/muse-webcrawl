Infrastructure as a service

Infrastructure as a service (IaaS) is a form of cloud computing that provides virtualised computing resources over the internet — servers, storage, and networking — on a pay-as-you-go basis. An IaaS provider hosts and manages the physical hardware and virtualisation layer, while customers control their operating systems, applications, and data. It is one of the standard service models of cloud computing, alongside platform as a service (PaaS) and software as a service (SaaS).

Characteristics

Resources are offered as services that can be provisioned and released on demand through web interfaces or APIs, typically billed by consumption. Customers avoid buying and maintaining their own data-centre hardware while retaining more control than in PaaS or SaaS offerings. Under the shared-responsibility model, the provider secures the physical infrastructure and hypervisor, while the customer is responsible for securing the operating system, applications, and data.

Examples

Major IaaS offerings include Amazon Elastic Compute Cloud (EC2), Microsoft Azure Virtual Machines, and Google Compute Engine. Typical resources include virtual machines, block and object storage, load balancers, and virtual networks. IaaS underpins much of the modern internet economy, hosting everything from start-up websites to enterprise workloads.
