Llanuwchllyn (Welsh pronunciation: [ˌɬanɨuˈχəɬɨn]) is a village and community in Gwynedd, Wales, near the southern end of Bala Lake (Llyn Tegid). The name means "church of [Saint] Deiniol above the lake" — or more precisely, the parish church by the upper lake — llan meaning church enclosure, uwch meaning above, and llyn meaning lake.

The village lies on the A494 road between Bala and Dolgellau, in the historic county of Merionethshire. It is one of the most strongly Welsh-speaking communities in Wales, with a large majority of residents speaking Welsh as a first language.

History

The parish church of St Deiniol stands on a mound in the village and is a Grade II* listed building. The community includes the hamlets of Llangower and other scattered settlements around the southern shore of the lake. The area was historically part of the township system of Penllyn, and the region played a role in the cultural and religious life of Welsh-speaking Wales, including the Methodist revival and the 19th-century Sunday school movement.

Llanuwchllyn is known in Welsh cultural life for its strong tradition of eisteddfodau and for being the home of the Urdd (Welsh League of Youth) outdoor centre at Glan-llyn, on the shore of Bala Lake, which has introduced generations of Welsh schoolchildren to outdoor activities through the medium of Welsh.

Geography

The village sits at the south-western end of Llyn Tegid, the largest natural lake in Wales, at the point where the River Dee (Afon Dyfrdwy) leaves the lake on its journey towards Llangollen and Chester. The surrounding hills form part of the Aran range to the south, with Aran Benllyn and Aran Fawddwy among the highest peaks in southern Snowdonia (Eryri).

The narrow-gauge Bala Lake Railway runs along the eastern shore of the lake from Llanuwchllyn station to Bala, following part of the trackbed of the former Ruabon–Barmouth line, which closed in the 1960s.

Governance

An electoral ward of the same name exists, electing a county councillor to Gwynedd Council. The community council serves the village and surrounding rural area.

Notable people

  • O. M. Edwards (1858–1920), Welsh historian, educationist and writer, was born in Llanuwchllyn; he was a key figure in Welsh education and publishing, founding periodicals such as Cymru.
  • Ifan ab Owen Edwards (1895–1970), his son, founder of the Urdd (Welsh League of Youth), was also born in the village.
