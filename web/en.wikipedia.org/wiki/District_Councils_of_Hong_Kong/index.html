District Councils of Hong Kong

The District Councils, formerly District Boards until 1999, are the local councils for the 18 districts of Hong Kong. They are advisory bodies on district affairs and local issues within their communities, consulted by the government on matters affecting the well-being of local residents, and they allocate public funds for minor works and community activities.

History

District Boards were created in 1982 under Governor Murray MacLehose's District Administration Scheme, initially with a mix of appointed, ex officio, and elected members. The proportion of elected seats grew through the 1980s and 1990s, and after the 1997 handover the bodies were renamed District Councils. The 2019 elections, held amid the pro-democracy protests, saw a landslide victory for pro-democracy candidates and record turnout.

2023 reform

In 2023 the Hong Kong government overhauled the councils under the principle of "patriots administering Hong Kong". Directly elected seats were reduced to about one fifth of the total; the remaining seats are appointed by the Chief Executive or chosen by committees, and all candidates must pass a national-security vetting and nomination process. The first elections under the new system were held in December 2023, with sharply lower turnout.

Functions

Councils advise the government on the use of public facilities, the adequacy of local services, and community programmes; they also fund district minor works and cultural and recreational activities. They have no executive or legislative power over local government departments.
