The Neolithic Revolution, or First Agricultural Revolution, was the wide-scale transition of many human cultures during the Neolithic period from hunting and gathering to farming and settled life. It began independently in several regions — the Fertile Crescent from about 10,000 BC, and later China, Mesoamerica, the Andes, New Guinea and elsewhere.

Consequences

Domestication of wheat, barley, rice, maize and animals produced food surpluses, permanent villages and towns, population growth, pottery, weaving and eventually social hierarchies, specialised crafts and states. The archaeologist V. Gordon Childe coined 'Neolithic Revolution' to stress its transformative scale, comparable with the later Industrial Revolution.
