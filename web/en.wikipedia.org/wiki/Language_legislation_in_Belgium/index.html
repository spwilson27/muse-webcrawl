Language legislation in Belgium

Language legislation in Belgium governs the use of the country's official languages — Dutch, French, and German — in public administration, education, and law. Belgium is divided into four language areas: the Dutch-language area (Flanders), the French-language area (Wallonia), the bilingual Brussels-Capital area, and the German-language area in the east.

History

At Belgian independence in 1830, French dominated public life even though a majority of the population spoke Dutch dialects. The Flemish Movement campaigned for equality, achieving legal recognition of Dutch in stages during the late 19th and 20th centuries. The language border was formally fixed in 1962–63, and subsequent state reforms created the Communities and Regions of modern federal Belgium.

Principles

The system rests on territoriality: public services and state schools in each unilingual area operate in that area's language, while Brussels is officially bilingual. A number of municipalities along the language border provide "facilities" for speakers of the other language.
