Pierre Boulez, composer and conductor.

Pierre Boulez (26 March 1925 – 5 January 2016) was a French composer and conductor. One of the dominant figures of post-war classical music, he was a leading exponent of serialism and a formidable polemicist, and founded several institutions devoted to new music, most notably IRCAM in Paris.

Early career

Trained as a mathematician, Boulez studied composition at the Paris Conservatoire with Olivier Messiaen and privately with René Leibowitz, mastering the twelve-tone technique of Schoenberg and Webern. His early works — the Sonatine for flute and piano, the Second Piano Sonata and Structures — pushed serial organisation to every parameter of music, a phase known as integral serialism.

Major works

Le Marteau sans maître (1955), a setting of poems by René Char, established his international reputation. Later works include the vast Mallarmé portrait Pli selon pli, the explosive Éclat, the ensemble cycle Domaines, Répons for ensemble and live electronics, and the piano cycle Incises. His output remained small and endlessly revised; he joked that some works were "in progress" for half a century.

Conducting

Boulez became one of the most admired conductors of the twentieth century, famed for the clarity, precision and lack of visible gesture of his performances. He served as chief conductor of the BBC Symphony Orchestra and the New York Philharmonic in the 1970s, and was a regular guest with the Berlin and Vienna Philharmonics, the Chicago Symphony Orchestra and the Cleveland Orchestra. He conducted Wagner's Ring at Bayreuth (1976) in Patrice Chéreau's famous centenary production.

Institutions

In the 1950s he founded the Domaine musical concert series in Paris. At the invitation of President Georges Pompidou he created IRCAM (Institut de Recherche et Coordination Acoustique/Musique), which opened beneath the Centre Pompidou in 1977, and its resident group, the Ensemble Intercontemporain. In 2015 the Philharmonie de Paris opened with an auditorium later named in his honour.

Death and reception

Boulez died in Baden-Baden on 5 January 2016, aged 90. His influence as composer, conductor, teacher and administrator over post-war musical life is widely regarded as unmatched.
