Rain is water droplets that have condensed from atmospheric vapour and grown heavy enough to fall under gravity. It is the main way fresh water is delivered to land, driving rivers, agriculture and ecosystems, and a key part of the water cycle with evaporation and condensation.

Types and effects

Rain ranges from drizzle to downpours in thunderstorms and tornado-bearing supercells; monsoons bring seasonal rains to South Asia — Karachi among the cities affected — while deserts receive very little. Too much rain in too short a time causes flooding; its absence causes drought. Acid rain, from sulphur and nitrogen pollutants, damages forests, lakes and buildings.
