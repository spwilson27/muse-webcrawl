Anne Brontë (17 January 1820 – 28 May 1849) was an English novelist and poet, the youngest of the Brontë sisters. She wrote under the pen name Acton Bell.

Life

Anne was born at Thornton and raised at Haworth parsonage after her father became rector there. Unlike her sisters Charlotte and Emily, she spent several years working away from home as a governess, experiences that informed her fiction. She also worked for the Robinson family at Thorp Green, where her brother Branwell's disgrace contributed to the family's troubles.

Works

Her first novel, Agnes Grey (1847), drew on her governess years. The Tenant of Wildfell Hall (1848), about a woman who leaves her alcoholic husband, was a daring success and is now regarded as one of the first feminist novels. Her poetry includes hymns and reflective verse.

Death

Anne died of tuberculosis at Scarborough in May 1849, aged 29, months after the deaths of Branwell and Emily. After her death Charlotte prevented republication of The Tenant of Wildfell Hall for a time, which contributed to Anne's later, now reversed, relative obscurity.
