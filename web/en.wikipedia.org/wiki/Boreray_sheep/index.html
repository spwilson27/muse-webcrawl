Boreray sheep

Boreray
Origin: Boreray, St Kilda, Scotland
Status: Rare breed (RBST priority)

The Boreray is a breed of sheep originating on the island of Boreray in the St Kilda archipelago off the west coast of Scotland. It is one of the Northern European short-tailed sheep breeds and is among the rarest British sheep breeds. The breed descended from the sheep left behind when the human population of St Kilda was evacuated in 1930, which subsequently lived feral on Boreray.

Characteristics

Boreray sheep are small and hardy, adapted to the harsh, exposed conditions of the island. Both sexes are usually horned; rams may carry large spiral horns. The fleece is typically white or cream, sometimes with darker markings, and is shed naturally in summer, so the sheep often do not require shearing. Ewes are good mothers and lamb easily outdoors.

History and conservation

After the St Kilda evacuation, the sheep on Boreray — descendants of Scottish Blackface crosses with older Hebridean-type sheep — survived without management. A number were exported to the mainland from the 1970s onward to establish registered flocks. The Rare Breeds Survival Trust lists the Boreray as a priority breed, and registered breeding populations remain small, numbering only in the hundreds of ewes.

Uses

The breed is kept mainly for conservation grazing and by smallholders; its wool is used for tweeds and knitwear, and the meat is slow-grown and strongly flavoured.
