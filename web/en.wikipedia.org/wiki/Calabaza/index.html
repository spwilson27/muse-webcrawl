Calabaza is the generic Spanish name for squash and pumpkins, and in the Caribbean and Latin American context it refers particularly to the winter squash commonly grown in the West Indies, usually of the species Cucurbita moschata. It is also known as the West Indian pumpkin or calabasa.

Description

Calabaza plants are vigorous trailing vines. The fruits vary widely in shape and colour — round or pear-shaped, and ribbed or smooth, with skins ranging from green and buff to orange and mottled. The flesh is typically deep yellow to orange, sweet and flavourful when cooked, with a texture that holds up well in soups and stews.

Cultivation

The crop is well adapted to hot, humid tropical lowlands where many other squashes struggle. It is widely grown in Cuba, Puerto Rico, the Dominican Republic, Mexico and Central America, and in Florida, where it is a staple of Caribbean immigrant cuisine.

Culinary use

Calabaza is used in soups, stews, beans and rice dishes, and in sweet preparations. In Puerto Rican cooking it is a classic ingredient of sancocho and of bean stews; in Cuba it appears in caldoza and in the sweet boniatillo. The seeds and flowers are also eaten.
