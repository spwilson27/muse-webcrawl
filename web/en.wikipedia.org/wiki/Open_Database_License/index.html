Open Database License

The ODbL in brief: attribute, share alike, keep open.
AbbreviationODbL
AuthorOpen Data Commons (Open Knowledge Foundation)
Version1.0
TypeCopyleft database licence

The Open Database License (ODbL) is a copyleft licence agreement intended to allow users to freely share, modify and use a database while maintaining this same freedom for others. It was created by Open Data Commons, part of the Open Knowledge Foundation, and published in 2009. Because many jurisdictions treat databases as compilations protected by copyright, and the European Union additionally grants a sui generis database right, a dedicated licence is needed to handle rights that software licences such as the GPL do not address.

Terms

The licence's conditions are commonly summarised as:

  • Attribute — credit the source database and its licence for any public use.
  • Share-Alike — derivative databases made publicly available must be offered under the ODbL as well.
  • Keep open — if technological restrictions (such as DRM) are applied to a redistributed database, an unrestricted version must also be offered.

The ODbL covers the database as a whole; individual contents may carry their own licences (users often pair it with the Database Contents License, DbCL).

Users

The best-known adopter is OpenStreetMap, which in September 2012 completed a long and controversial migration from Creative Commons CC BY-SA to the ODbL, deleting data from contributors who did not agree to the new terms. Other users include Open Data Commons community datasets and various municipal open-data releases in combination with attribution licences.
