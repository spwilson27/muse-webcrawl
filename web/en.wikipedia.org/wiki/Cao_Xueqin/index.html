Cao Xueqin (Chinese: 曹雪芹; pinyin: Cáo Xuěqín; c. 1715 – 1763) was a Chinese novelist, poet and painter of the Qing dynasty, best known as the author of Dream of the Red Chamber, generally considered the greatest Chinese novel and one of the Four Great Classical Novels of Chinese literature.

Life

Many details of Cao's life are uncertain and have been reconstructed from the comments of his friends and from the semi-autobiographical traces in his novel. He was born into an eminent and wealthy Han Chinese family of the Plain White Banner that had served the Qing court for generations. His great-grandfather Cao Xi and grandfather Cao Yin were textile commissioners in Jiangning (Nanjing) and confidants of the Kangxi Emperor, who stayed at the family's residence on his southern tours. The family amassed and displayed enormous wealth.

When Cao Xueqin was still a boy — probably in 1728 — the Yongzheng Emperor confiscated the family's property on charges of financial mismanagement (and, it is thought, for backing the wrong prince in the succession struggle). The family fell into poverty and moved to Beijing. Cao spent his later years in straitened circumstances in the western suburbs of Beijing, selling his paintings and working at minor posts, drinking heavily and writing his novel. A note by his friend Dun Cheng records his grief at the death of a young son. He died in 1763 or 1764, leaving his great work unfinished.

Dream of the Red Chamber

Cao devoted at least a decade to Dream of the Red Chamber (紅樓夢, Hónglóu Mèng), also known as The Story of the Stone (石頭記). The novel chronicles the rise and fall of the aristocratic Jia family, modelled on his own, and centres on the love triangle among the sensitive hero Jia Baoyu and his two cousins: the frail, brilliant Lin Daiyu and the capable, pragmatic Xue Baochai. It contains an enormous cast — over 400 named characters — and an unmatched depiction of the domestic life, manners, medicine, cuisine, religion and bureaucracy of eighteenth-century China.

Cao wrote the first 80 chapters; the now-standard 120-chapter version was published in print in 1791–92 by Gao E and Cheng Weiyuan, who supplied the concluding 40 chapters, whether from Cao's drafts or their own composition remains a matter of scholarly debate. The novel circulated during Cao's lifetime in hand-copied manuscripts annotated by commentators signing as "Red Inkstone" (Zhiyanzhai), whose marginal notes are a major source for Cao's life and intentions.

Redology

The study of the novel and its author, called "Redology" (紅學, hóngxué), is a major field of Chinese scholarship. In the 1920s Hu Shih used textual and biographical evidence to establish Cao's authorship and the autobiographical basis of the novel, transforming Redology into a modern academic discipline. Scholars continue to debate Cao's birth year (1715 and 1724 are the leading candidates), his genealogy, and the authenticity of the last 40 chapters.

Legacy

Dream of the Red Chamber is routinely ranked the pinnacle of Chinese fiction; Mao Zedong claimed to have read it five times and recommended it as a portrait of feudal society. The Cao Xueqin Memorial in Beijing's Botanical Garden, at the site associated with his later years near the Western Hills, commemorates the novelist, and his former residence sites in Beijing and Nanjing are protected. His paintings, including a noted work of a queer-shaped rock, are also admired.
