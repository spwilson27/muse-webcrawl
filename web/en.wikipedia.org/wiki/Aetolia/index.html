Aetolia (Greek: Αἰτωλία) is a mountainous region of west-central Greece, bounded by the Achelous river to the west, Mount Panetoliko to the north and the Gulf of Corinth to the south. In antiquity its people, the Aetolians, rose from a tribal backwater to head the Aetolian League, one of the two great federal states of Hellenistic Greece.

Ancient history

In Homer and Thucydides the Aetolians appear as a rugged tribal people, some of whose inland communities were said to speak an unintelligible dialect and eat raw flesh. They organised themselves into a federation (koinon) with an annual assembly and elected strategos, meeting at the sanctuary of Apollo at Thermum. In the 3rd century BC the League expanded across central Greece, controlled Delphi, and won lasting prestige by repelling the Galatian invasion of 279 BC — celebrated in the Soteria festival. Aetolia clashed with Macedon, allied with Rome against Philip V, then turned against Rome and was crushed in the Roman–Syrian War settlement of 189 BC, after which it declined.

Geography and modern era

The region today forms, with neighbouring Acarnania, the regional unit of Aetolia-Acarnania, the largest by area in Greece, with its capital at Missolonghi — famous for its heroic exodus in the Greek War of Independence (1826) and as the place of Lord Byron's death — and its largest town at Agrinio, centre of a tobacco-growing plain.
