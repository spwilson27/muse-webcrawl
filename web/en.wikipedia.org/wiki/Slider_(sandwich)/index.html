Slider (sandwich)

A slider is an American term for a steam-grilled sandwich, typically made with a small hamburger. The term primarily refers to small hamburgers, but applies to any small sandwich made with a slider roll. White Castle trademarking "Slyders", sliders are thought to have been created there in 1921.

History and etymology

White Castle, founded in Wichita, Kansas, in 1921, sold its small, square, steam-grilled burgers for five cents for decades. The name "slider" is generally attributed to the burgers' small size and greasy ease going down; one popular account credits US Navy sailors, who called them sliders because they "slide down" easily, and the term spread from the 1940s. White Castle registered the spelling "Slyder" as a trademark in 1993.

Preparation

Classic sliders are cooked on a griddle over a bed of onions, so the thin patties steam in onion vapour; the small buns are placed on top to absorb the steam. In the 2000s the term broadened widely in American restaurants: "slider" now commonly denotes almost any miniature sandwich served on a small roll — pulled pork, fried chicken, meatball or vegetarian versions — usually served in multiples as appetisers or bar food.

Cultural references

Harold & Kumar Go to White Castle (2004) turned a late-night slider craving into a cult film plot. Sliders remain a staple of casual dining menus across the United States.
