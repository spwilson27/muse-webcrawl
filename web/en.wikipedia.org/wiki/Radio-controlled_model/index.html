Radio-controlled model

A radio-controlled model (or RC model) is a scale or sport model — of an aircraft, car, boat, helicopter or other vehicle — controlled remotely using a hand-held radio transmitter and an onboard receiver. RC modelling became widely available as a hobby after the 1960s as electronics miniaturised, and today ranges from inexpensive toy-grade models to competition machines.

Types

RC aircraft include fixed-wing planes, helicopters and multirotor drones, powered by electric motors or glow-fuel and petrol engines. Surface models include on- and off-road cars and trucks, tanks, and boats from sailboats to hydroplanes. Models are sold as ready-to-run (RTR), almost-ready-to-run, or as kits requiring assembly.

Control systems

Modern systems use 2.4 GHz spread-spectrum radio links, with proportional control of servos governing steering, throttle and flight surfaces. Digital protocols provide failsafes and telemetry. Earlier systems used AM and FM bands at frequencies such as 27, 40 and 72 MHz, with interchangeable crystals.

Organisations

National bodies such as the Academy of Model Aeronautics (United States) and the British Model Flying Association organise competitions, safety codes and insurance, and regulators increasingly register model aircraft alongside drones.
