Beta-glucan

Beta-glucans comprise a group of β-D-glucose polysaccharides naturally occurring in the cell walls of cereals, bacteria and fungi, with significantly differing physicochemical properties depending on source. They consist of glucose units joined by beta-glycosidic bonds, most commonly β(1,3) linkages with β(1,4) or β(1,6) side branches.

Sources and dietary role

Cereal beta-glucans, found in oats and barley, are soluble dietary fibres. Their consumption has been associated with reduced blood cholesterol, and health claims linking oat beta-glucan intake to a lowered risk of coronary heart disease have been authorised by regulators including the FDA and EFSA.

Biological effects

Beta-glucans from yeast and medicinal mushrooms are studied as biological response modifiers that can stimulate the innate immune system. Their biological activity depends on molecular weight, branching pattern and solubility.
