Oscar Wilde

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Oscar Wilde in the Napoleon Sarony photograph of 1882: velvet jacket, knee breeches, flowing hair and a languid pose]

Oscar Fingal O'Fflahertie Wills Wilde (16 October 1854 – 30 November 1900) was an Irish poet and playwright. After writing in different forms throughout the 1880s, he became one of the most popular playwrights in London in the early 1890s. He is best remembered for his epigrams and plays, his novel The Picture of Dorian Gray (1891), and his criminal conviction for gross indecency for homosexual acts.

The leading apostle of Aestheticism ('art for art's sake'), Wilde triumphed with the society comedies Lady Windermere's Fan (1892), An Ideal Husband and The Importance of Being Earnest (both 1895). His reckless libel suit against the Marquess of Queensberry collapsed into his own prosecution; sentenced to two years' hard labour, he wrote De Profundis in prison and The Ballad of Reading Gaol after release. He died in Paris, impoverished, at 46.
