Supayalat (Burmese: စုဖုရားလတ်; 1859–1925) was the last queen consort of Burma (Myanmar), as the chief wife of King Thibaw, the final monarch of the Konbaung dynasty. Ambitious and politically forceful, she is remembered in Burmese tradition — fairly or not — as a dominant influence on her husband and a central figure in the court's final, turbulent years before British annexation.

Rise to the throne

Supayalat was a daughter of King Mindon by Queen Hsinbyumashin (Sinpyumashin), who engineered the accession of Thibaw in 1878 after Mindon's death. Supayalat married her half-brother Thibaw and became his chief queen. Early in the reign, dozens of royal princes and relatives were executed in a notorious palace massacre intended to secure the succession; responsibility has traditionally been laid on Hsinbyumashin and Supayalat, though modern historians treat the queen's personal role with more caution.

Downfall

Thibaw's reign coincided with intensifying British pressure on Upper Burma. The Third Anglo-Burmese War of November 1885 ended in a swift British victory; Mandalay was occupied, and on 29 November 1885 the royal couple surrendered. Supayalat, then pregnant, went into exile with Thibaw to Ratnagiri on the west coast of India, where the former king died in 1916. The monarchy was abolished and Burma incorporated into British India.

Later life and legacy

After Thibaw's death, Supayalat was permitted to return to Burma in 1919, living in Rangoon on a pension until her death on 24 November 1925. She never renounced her royal status in manner or dress. Her dramatic life has inspired novels and plays, most famously in "The Queen of Burma" traditions of Burmese popular history, which cast her alternately as villainess and as a tragic defender of the throne. One of her daughters married an Indian door-keeper's son, a scandal of the exile years, while another became the subject of the memoir and film interest surrounding the family's fate in Ratnagiri.
