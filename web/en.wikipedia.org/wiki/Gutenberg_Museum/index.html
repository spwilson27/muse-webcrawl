Gutenberg Museum

Established1900
LocationLiebfrauenplatz 5, Mainz, Germany
TypePrinting museum

The Gutenberg Museum is a museum of the history of printing in Mainz, Germany, the city where Johannes Gutenberg developed printing with movable type in Europe. Founded in 1900, on the occasion of the 500th anniversary of Gutenberg's birth as reckoned at the time, it is one of the oldest printing museums in the world.

The Gutenberg Museum in Mainz

Collections

The museum's centrepieces are two original copies of the 42-line Gutenberg Bible of the 1450s, displayed in a vault — among the most valuable printed books in existence. Exhibits trace printing history from early block books and incunabula through hand-press and industrial printing to modern digital typography, and include presses, type-founding equipment, and collections devoted to bookbinding, paper, graphic arts and printing in non-European scripts, including East Asian printing that predates Gutenberg.

The museum occupies the historic Zum Römischen Kaiser building opposite Mainz Cathedral. It houses a working print shop where visitors can see historical printing demonstrated, along with a specialist library and the Gutenberg Shop. Planning for an expanded, modernised museum — the "Bibelturm" project and subsequent revised schemes — has been under discussion for years.
