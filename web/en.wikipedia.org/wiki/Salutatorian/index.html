Salutatorian

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[hero image: Salutatorian]
The salutatory address

A salutatorian is an academic title conferred upon the second-highest-ranking student in a graduating class at schools and universities in the United States, the Philippines, and some other countries. The title derives from the salutatorian's traditional role of delivering the salutatory address — the opening speech of the graduation ceremony that welcomes attendees — as opposed to the valedictorian, who delivers the closing (valedictory) address and is normally the highest-ranking student.

Selection and role

Salutatorians are usually selected by grade point average, sometimes weighted and sometimes combined with faculty votes, honours coursework, or extracurricular distinction. In many schools the ranking is determined after the final semester's grades. The salutatory historically was delivered in Latin at older institutions, a tradition retained in modified form at some universities, where a student delivers a Latin salutatory regardless of rank.

Practice and debate

The title is part of the Latin honours family that includes cum laude, magna cum laude, and summa cum laude. In recent decades some schools have debated or abandoned class rank and the valedictorian/salutatorian system, citing unhealthy competition and vanishingly small GPA differences caused by grade inflation; others retain the titles as motivators and as useful signals to colleges. In the Philippines, the salutatorian of primary and secondary schools is announced at graduation rites under the Department of Education's honours system, which since 2016 also uses the "with honours" categories.
