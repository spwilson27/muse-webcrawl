Lebanese Armed Forces

The Lebanese Armed Forces (LAF), also known as the Lebanese Army, are the military forces of the Republic of Lebanon. They consist of the Army, the Air Force, and the Navy. The President of Lebanon is the commander-in-chief, while operational command rests with the Commander of the Armed Forces. The LAF's stated mission is to defend Lebanon's territory, sovereignty, and population against external aggression and to assist in internal security and stability.

History

The origins of the Lebanese Armed Forces lie in the Legion of the Orient and the Troupes Spéciales du Levant raised under the French Mandate. On 1 August 1945, following Lebanon's independence, these units were formally transferred to Lebanese control, a date commemorated as Army Day. The LAF remained small during the early decades of independence, and during the Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990) it fractured along sectarian lines. After the Taif Agreement ended the war, the armed forces were rebuilt as a unified national institution with support from Syria and Western partners, principally the United States.

Composition

The Army is by far the largest branch, organized into brigades deployed across the country, supported by special forces units including the Airborne Regiment and the commando units. The Air Force operates a modest fleet of helicopters and fixed-wing aircraft, while the Navy patrols Lebanon's Mediterranean coastline with patrol craft. Total personnel numbers are generally estimated in the tens of thousands, making the LAF one of the larger employers in Lebanon.

Recent operations

Since 1990 the LAF has deployed in south Lebanon following the end of Israel's occupation in 2000 and the 2006 Lebanon War, fought the militant group Fatah al-Islam in the Nahr al-Bared refugee camp in 2007, and conducted operations along the Syrian border during the spillover of the Syrian civil war, including the 2017 campaign against Islamic State and al-Nusra Front positions near Arsal. The LAF is widely regarded in Lebanon as one of the few non-sectarian national institutions and receives substantial military assistance from the United States, France, and other countries.
