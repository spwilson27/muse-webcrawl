Glaser coupling

Glaser coupling is a chemical reaction in which two terminal alkynes are oxidatively coupled to form a conjugated diyne (a 1,3-diyne). It was reported by Carl Glaser in 1869 and is among the oldest known coupling reactions.

Conditions

The classic reaction uses a copper(I) salt, such as copper(I) chloride, in an ammonia-containing solvent exposed to air or oxygen, which oxidises the copper acetylide intermediate and joins the two alkyne units. Variants include the Eglinton coupling, using copper(II) acetate in pyridine, and the Hay coupling, which uses a CuCl–TMEDA catalyst under oxygen.

Applications

The reaction is used to build diyne-containing natural products, macrocycles, and carbon-rich materials such as graphdiyne precursors.
