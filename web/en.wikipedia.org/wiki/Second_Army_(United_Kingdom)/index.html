The British Second Army was a field army of the British Army active in both world wars. It is best remembered in the Second World War as the principal British formation in the Normandy landings and the subsequent campaign in North-West Europe.

First World War

Formed in France in December 1914 from the IV and V Corps of the British Expeditionary Force, the Second Army held the Ypres Salient for much of the war. Under General Sir Herbert Plumer from 1915, it won the Battle of Messines in June 1917 with a carefully prepared set-piece attack supported by mines, and fought in the Third Battle of Ypres. It was redesignated the Fourth Army briefly in 1918 before being re-formed, and ended the war advancing into Belgium.

Second World War

Re-formed in 1943 under Lieutenant-General Sir Miles Dempsey as part of the 21st Army Group, the Second Army landed on Gold, Juno, and Sword beaches on D-Day, 6 June 1944 (with the 1st Canadian Army elements under its command in the assault phase). It fought the grinding Battle for Caen, the breakout in Operation Bluecoat, the advance across France and Belgium, the failed airborne-ground Operation Market Garden in the Netherlands, the Battle of the Bulge's northern flank, and the crossing of the Rhine in Operation Plunder in March 1945. By VE Day its troops had reached the Baltic coast at Lübeck and Wismar. The headquarters was disbanded in 1945.

Dempsey is regarded as one of Britain's most capable army commanders of the war, though he served largely in the shadow of his army group commander, Field Marshal Bernard Montgomery.
