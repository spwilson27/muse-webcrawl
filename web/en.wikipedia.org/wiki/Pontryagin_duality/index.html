Pontryagin duality

Field: Harmonic analysis, topology

Named after: Lev Pontryagin

In mathematics, specifically in harmonic analysis and the theory of topological groups, Pontryagin duality is a duality between locally compact abelian groups and their character groups. It is named after the Soviet mathematician Lev Pontryagin, who introduced it in the 1930s.

The dual group

For a locally compact abelian group G, a character is a continuous group homomorphism from G to the circle group 𝕋 of complex numbers of modulus 1. The set of all characters, written Ĝ and called the dual group, is itself a locally compact abelian group under pointwise multiplication, with the compact–open topology.

The duality theorem

The Pontryagin duality theorem states that the natural evaluation map from G to its double dual is an isomorphism of topological groups: every locally compact abelian group is canonically identified with the dual of its dual. Important examples include: the dual of the real line ℝ is ℝ itself; the dual of the circle 𝕋 is the integers ℤ, and vice versa; the dual of a finite abelian group is (non-canonically) isomorphic to the group itself.

Fourier analysis

Pontryagin duality provides the foundation of abstract Fourier analysis: the Fourier transform maps functions on G to functions on Ĝ, unifying the classical Fourier transform on the real line, Fourier series (the case of the circle), and the discrete Fourier transform (finite groups). The Poisson summation formula is a consequence of the duality.
