The Office of Naval Intelligence (ONI) is the military intelligence agency of the United States Navy. Established in 1882 primarily to advance the Navy's modernization efforts, it is the oldest continuously operating member of the United States Intelligence Community. ONI's responsibilities include maritime intelligence concerning the development, operations, and capabilities of foreign navies and merchant shipping.

History

ONI was created by order of Secretary of the Navy William H. Hunt on March 23, 1882. Initially a small office collecting information on foreign navies, it expanded during the First and Second World Wars into counterintelligence, censorship, and operational intelligence. During the Cold War it tracked the Soviet Navy, and today it is headquartered at the National Maritime Intelligence Center in Suitland, Maryland, operating as part of the Naval Intelligence Enterprise alongside Fleet Cyber Command and the Naval Criminal Investigative Service.

Organisation

ONI produces analysis for Navy decision-makers and national policymakers through centres devoted to technical intelligence, operational intelligence, and information services, and it maintains the national capability for tracking merchant shipping worldwide.
