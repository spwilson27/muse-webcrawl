An Arctic roll is a British frozen dessert consisting of a cylinder of vanilla ice cream wrapped in a thin layer of sponge cake spread with raspberry-flavoured sauce or jam. It is served sliced, the cross-section showing a spiral of sponge around the ice cream.

History

The dessert became widely known in Britain from the 1950s onward as domestic freezers spread, and it was popularised commercially by the Birds Eye company in the 1970s and 1980s. Production of the Birds Eye version ceased in the 1990s after sales declined, but the dessert was relaunched in 2008 during the economic downturn, when demand for nostalgic, economical foods revived. Home cooks make Arctic roll by freezing ice cream into a log, coating it with jam, and rolling it in a thin fatless sponge, then returning it to the freezer until firm.

Variations

Chocolate and strawberry ice cream versions exist, and the sponge is sometimes replaced or supplemented with a chocolate coating. The dessert resembles the baked Alaska in combining sponge and ice cream, but is served entirely cold.
