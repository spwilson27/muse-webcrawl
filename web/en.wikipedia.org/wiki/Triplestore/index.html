Triplestore

A triplestore is a database designed for storing and querying data in the form of RDF triples — subject–predicate–object statements such as "Paris — capital of — France". Triplestores are the standard storage layer for the Semantic Web and for knowledge graphs, and are queried with the SPARQL language.

Design

Unlike relational databases, triplestores take a graph view of data, so merging datasets and adding new relationship types do not require schema changes. Implementations index permutations of the three components (SPO, POS, OSP, and so on) to answer pattern queries efficiently, and may store quads (triples plus a graph identifier) for named-graph support. Some systems are native stores built from scratch (for example GraphDB, Blazegraph, Virtuoso, Apache Jena TDB, Amazon Neptune, and Stardog), while others map triples onto relational or document databases.

Applications

Triplestores power public knowledge bases such as Wikidata and DBpedia, linked-data publishing by libraries and governments, and enterprise knowledge graphs used for data integration, drug discovery, and metadata management. Challenges include scaling to billions of triples, reasoning with OWL ontologies, and competing with property-graph databases, which dominate some commercial graph applications.
