Jean Shepherd

Jean Parker Shepherd Jr. (July 26, 1921 – October 16, 1999) was an American storyteller, humorist, radio and television personality, writer, and actor. He is best known for the semi-autobiographical stories collected in In God We Trust: All Others Pay Cash, which inspired the film A Christmas Story (1983), which he narrated and co-wrote.

BornJuly 26, 1921, Chicago, Illinois
DiedOctober 16, 1999, Sanibel, Florida
OccupationRadio storyteller, writer, humorist

Radio career

From 1955 to 1977, Shepherd hosted a late-night show on New York's WOR radio, spinning improvised tales of his Indiana childhood featuring his alter ego Ralphie Parker, alongside commentary on American life.

Legacy

A Christmas Story, with its leg lamp and Red Ryder BB gun, became a perennial holiday classic, and a Broadway musical and live television adaptations followed.
