National research and education network

Diagram of a typical NREN backbone

A national research and education network (NREN) is a specialised internet service provider dedicated to supporting the needs of the research and education communities within a country, usually providing high-performance connectivity between universities, research institutes, schools, libraries and public institutions.

Characteristics

NRENs typically operate national fibre-optic backbone networks with capacities far above commercial offerings of their time, pioneer new networking technologies such as IPv6 and multicast, and provide advanced services including federated identity (eduroam and eduGAIN), videoconferencing, and access to supercomputing and large scientific instruments. They are generally funded by governments and member institutions as not-for-profit organisations.

Examples

Well-known NRENs include Internet2 in the United States, JANET in the United Kingdom, DFN in Germany, SURFnet in the Netherlands, RENATER in France, AARNet in Australia, and CANARIE in Canada. Regional and international bodies link them together: GÉANT interconnects European NRENs, while organisations such as the Asia Pacific Advanced Network (APAN) coordinate interconnection in other regions. More than one hundred countries have an NREN of some form.

History

Research networking began with networks such as ARPANET in the United States and the academic networks of the 1980s, including NSFNET and BITNET. As the commercial internet grew, academic communities consolidated their own advanced infrastructures into national organisations, many coordinated internationally by bodies such as TERENA (later the GÉANT Association).
