Novaya Gazeta

Novaya Gazeta (Russian: Новая газета, "New Gazette") is a Russian independent newspaper known for its investigative journalism and critical coverage of Russian politics, corruption and human rights abuses. It was founded in Moscow in 1993, with funding help from former Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev, who used part of his Nobel Peace Prize money to buy the paper's first computers.

Journalism and journalists

The paper gained a reputation for fearless reporting on the Chechen wars, organised crime and official corruption. Several of its journalists have been murdered in connection with their work, most notably Anna Politkovskaya, assassinated in 2006, and also Yuri Shchekochikhin, Igor Domnikov, Anastasia Baburova and Natalia Estemirova (a contributor). Editor-in-chief Dmitry Muratov received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2021, sharing it with Maria Ressa.

Suspension and exile edition

Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022 and the passage of laws criminalising "false information" about the armed forces, Novaya Gazeta suspended publication in Russia in March 2022 after warnings from the regulator Roskomnadzor, and its media licence was later revoked by the courts. Journalists from the paper launched Novaya Gazeta Europe, based in Riga, Latvia, in April 2022, publishing in Russian and English; the outlet was later declared an "undesirable organisation" in Russia.
