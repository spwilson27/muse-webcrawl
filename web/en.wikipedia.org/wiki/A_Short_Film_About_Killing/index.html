A Short Film About Killing

A Short Film About Killing (Polish: Krótki film o zabijaniu) is a 1988 Polish drama film directed by Krzysztof Kieślowski, co-written with Krzysztof Piesiewicz. It is an expanded version of the fifth episode of Kieślowski's television series Dekalog, based on the Fifth Commandment, "Thou shalt not kill". The film interweaves three characters in Warsaw: Jacek, a young drifter; Waldemar, a taxi driver; and Piotr, a newly qualified lawyer idealistic about reforming the penal system.

Plot

Jacek wanders Warsaw aimlessly, engaging in petty acts of cruelty, before brutally murdering Waldemar, the taxi driver, in a prolonged and harrowing sequence. Piotr, who had argued in his bar examination against capital punishment, takes Jacek's case as his first defence brief but cannot save him. The film ends with an equally gruelling depiction of Jacek's execution by hanging, presented as a mirror of the murder itself.

Themes and reception

The film is a stark protest against the death penalty, arguing that state killing is no less murder than individual killing. It won the Jury Prize and the FIPRESCI Prize at the 1988 Cannes Film Festival and the first European Film Award for Best Film. The film is credited with contributing to the debate that led to a moratorium on executions in Poland in 1988 and the eventual abolition of capital punishment there in 1997. Critics have praised its desaturated, green-tinted cinematography by Sławomir Idziak, achieved with custom filters, which gives Warsaw a bleak, oppressive atmosphere.
