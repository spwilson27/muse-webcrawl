Crémant d'Alsace

Crémant d'Alsace is a French appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC) for sparkling wine made in the Alsace wine region of north-eastern France. Granted AOC status in 1976, it is produced by the traditional method of second fermentation in bottle, like Champagne, and has become the most widely produced crémant in France.

Production

The wines are made principally from Pinot Blanc, along with Auxerrois, Pinot Gris, Riesling, Chardonnay and Pinot Noir; rosé versions are made entirely from Pinot Noir. Grapes must be hand-harvested, and the finished wine must age on its lees for a minimum period before release.

Style

Crémant d'Alsace is typically dry (brut), fresh and delicate, with fine bubbles and flavours of white fruit and flowers, and is widely consumed in France as an aperitif and exported internationally.
