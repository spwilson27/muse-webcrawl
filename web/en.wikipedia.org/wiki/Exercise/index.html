Exercise

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Exercise is physical activity that is planned, structured and repetitive, performed to improve or maintain physical fitness and health. It includes aerobic exercise (running, cycling, swimming), resistance training (weights, bodyweight), flexibility and balance work. Regular exercise is one of the most consistently supported interventions in Medicine: it reduces the risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, several cancers, depression and dementia, and improves sleep, bone density and longevity.

Guidelines (WHO) recommend adults accumulate 150–300 minutes of moderate or 75–150 minutes of vigorous aerobic activity per week, plus muscle-strengthening on two days — targets most adults worldwide do not meet. Even far smaller doses yield measurable benefit; the steepest gains come from moving off zero.

Physiology

Exercise stresses systems productively: the heart and vessels remodel, muscles grow more mitochondria and capillaries, insulin sensitivity rises, and the brain releases factors (including BDNF) supporting mood and cognition. Adaptations are specific to the training imposed — the SAID principle.

Risks

Injury, overtraining and, rarely, cardiac events in susceptible people are the downsides; net population benefit is strongly positive. Exercise is prescribed as treatment ('exercise is medicine') for dozens of chronic conditions.
