Foundation Remembrance, Responsibility and Future

The Foundation Remembrance, Responsibility and Future (German: Stiftung Erinnerung, Verantwortung und Zukunft, EVZ) is a German federal foundation established in 2000 to provide compensation payments to former forced labourers and other victims of Nazi injustice, and to fund remembrance and education projects.

Establishment

The foundation was created by a law of the German Bundestag on 2 August 2000, following negotiations between the German government and German industry on one side, and the United States government, victim associations, and the governments of Central and Eastern European states on the other. Its initial capital of 10 billion Deutsche Marks (about €5.2 billion) was provided in equal shares by the German federal government and by German companies through the Foundation Initiative of German Industry.

Compensation payments

Between 2001 and 2007 the foundation, working through partner organisations in the countries concerned, paid compensation to approximately 1.66 million surviving former forced labourers and other victims in nearly 100 countries. Former concentration camp inmates and those deported to forced labour received the highest payments; other categories covered forced agricultural workers, children of forced labourers, and victims of personal injury or property loss.

Continuing work

After completing the payment programme in 2007, the EVZ continued as a funding foundation supporting remembrance of Nazi persecution, Holocaust education, human rights projects, and support for elderly survivors, financed from its remaining capital and investment income.
