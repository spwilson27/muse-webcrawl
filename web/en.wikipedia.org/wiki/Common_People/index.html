Common People

Album: Different Class
Released: 22 May 1995
Genre: Britpop
Length: 5:50 (album version)
Songwriter: Jarvis Cocker, Nick Banks, Steve Mackey, Russell Senior, Candida Doyle

"Common People" is a song by English alternative rock band Pulp, released in May 1995 as the lead single from their fifth studio album, Different Class. It reached number two on the UK Singles Chart and became one of the defining songs of the Britpop era.

Background and lyrics

The song's lyrics were inspired by an encounter frontman Jarvis Cocker had at Central Saint Martins College of Art and Design with a Greek student who told him she wanted to "live like common people". The narrator skewers the wealthy student's romanticized view of working-class life, building to the refrain that she will "never understand / how it feels to live your life / with no means and no control".

Reception and legacy

"Common People" was acclaimed on release and has repeatedly been voted among the greatest British songs of all time in critics' polls. Pulp's headline performance of it at the 1995 Glastonbury Festival is regarded as a landmark Britpop moment. In 2004, William Shatner released a well-received cover version, produced by Ben Folds with vocals from Joe Jackson, which brought the song to a new audience.
