Fort Caroline

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort Caroline was a French colonial settlement established in 1564 near present-day Jacksonville, Florida. Founded by René Goulaine de Laudonnière with French Huguenot colonists, it was the first French attempt at a permanent colony in what is now the continental United States. The triangular fort stood on the south bank of the St. Johns River, which the French named the River of May.

History

An earlier French expedition under Jean Ribault had erected a column claiming Florida for France in 1562. Laudonnière's party of some 200 settlers built Fort Caroline in June 1564 and traded with the local Timucua people. The colony struggled with food shortages and internal unrest; in 1565 Ribault returned with reinforcements and supplies.

Destruction

Spain, which claimed Florida, responded by founding St. Augustine under Pedro Menéndez de Avilés in 1565. After Ribault's fleet was wrecked by a hurricane, Menéndez marched overland and attacked Fort Caroline on 20 September 1565, killing most of its defenders. The fort was renamed San Mateo. A French counter-expedition in 1568 destroyed the Spanish garrison in reprisal. Today, the Fort Caroline National Memorial, part of the Timucuan Ecological and Historic Preserve, commemorates the site.
