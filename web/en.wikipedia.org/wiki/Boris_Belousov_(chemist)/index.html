Boris Belousov (chemist)

Boris Pavlovich Belousov (Russian: Бори́с Па́влович Белоу́сов; 19 February 1893 – 12 June 1970) was a Soviet chemist and biophysicist who discovered the Belousov–Zhabotinsky (BZ) reaction, one of the first known examples of a homogeneous oscillating chemical reaction.

Belousov came upon the reaction in the 1950s while trying to model the Krebs cycle with an inorganic analogue: a solution of citric acid, bromate and a cerium salt changed colour periodically between yellow and colourless. His attempts to publish were rejected by journal editors who considered such behaviour impossible for a homogeneous system, and he managed only to publish a brief abstract in an obscure proceedings volume in 1959. The work was taken up by Anatol Zhabotinsky in the 1960s and became a cornerstone of the science of non-equilibrium thermodynamics and self-organisation. Belousov was posthumously awarded the Lenin Prize in 1980.
