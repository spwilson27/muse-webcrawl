Heitor Villa-Lobos


Heitor Villa-Lobos
Born: 5 March 1887, Rio de Janeiro
Died: 17 November 1959, Rio de Janeiro
Occupation: Composer, conductor, cellist, guitarist

Heitor Villa-Lobos (March 5, 1887 – November 17, 1959) was a Brazilian composer, conductor, cellist, and classical guitarist described as "the single most significant creative figure in 20th-century Brazilian art music". Villa-Lobos has become the best-known and most significant Latin American composer to date. A prolific composer, he wrote numerous orchestral, chamber, instrumental and vocal works, totalling over 2,000 works by his death in 1959.

Life

Villa-Lobos was born in Rio de Janeiro, the son of Raúl Villa-Lobos, a librarian and amateur musician. He learned the cello and clarinet from his father, and absorbed popular Brazilian music playing in street ensembles (chorões). Around 1905–1912 he travelled widely in Brazil, collecting folk music. In the 1920s he spent extended periods in Paris, where his music attracted attention within avant-garde circles. Returning to Brazil in 1930, he became a central figure in music education under the Vargas regime, founding the Academia Brasileira de Música in 1945.

Music

His music combines Brazilian folk and popular elements with European classical forms and techniques, influenced by Bach, Debussy, and Stravinsky. Among his best-known works are the nine Bachianas Brasileiras (1930–1945), which fuse Bachian procedures with Brazilian melody and rhythm; the 14 Chôros (1920–1929); the operas and ballets Amazonas and Floresta do Amazonas; five piano concertos and two cello concertos; 17 string quartets; and extensive music for the guitar, including the 12 Études and the popular Chôros No. 1 and Prelude No. 1. His Bachianas Brasileiras No. 5 for soprano and cellos, with its wordless vocalise, is among his most frequently performed pieces.

Legacy

The Museu Villa-Lobos in Rio de Janeiro, founded in 1960, preserves his archive. His music helped establish a distinctly Brazilian school of classical composition and influenced generations of composers in Latin America and beyond.
