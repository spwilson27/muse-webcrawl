Museo Nacional de Antropología

The Museo Nacional de Antropología (National Museum of Anthropology) is a national museum of Mexico located in Chapultepec Park in Mexico City. Opened in 1964 and designed by architect Pedro Ramírez Vázquez, it is the largest and most visited museum in Mexico, housing the world's most important collection of pre-Hispanic Mexican art and artifacts, along with ethnographic exhibits on the country's Indigenous peoples.

Architecture

The museum is organized around a vast central courtyard partly covered by the famous "umbrella" (el paraguas), a huge roof supported by a single sculpted column with a waterfall cascading from it. Twenty-three halls surround the courtyard on two floors; the ground floor is devoted to archaeology, from the peopling of the Americas through the Mexica (Aztec) world, while the upper floor presents the cultures of contemporary Indigenous Mexico.

Collection highlights

Famous pieces include the Aztec Sun Stone (Calendar Stone), the colossal Olmec heads from Veracruz and Tabasco, the treasures of the Maya tomb of Pakal from Palenque, the Teotihuacan murals, and the giant Atlantean figures of Tula. The Mexica hall, at the courtyard's eastern end, forms the museum's centerpiece.

History

Mexico's national antiquities collections date to the late 18th century; the present museum was built under President Adolfo López Mateos and inaugurated on 17 September 1964. It is administered by the National Institute of Anthropology and History (INAH).
