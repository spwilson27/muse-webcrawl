Diplomatic mission

[Image: An embassy chancery building with flagpole and diplomatic plates on parked vehicles]

A diplomatic mission or foreign mission is a group of people from a state or international organisation present in another state to represent the sending state officially. The term usually refers to a resident mission — most commonly an embassy in the receiving state's capital — and to the building (chancery) in which it works. Diplomatic missions are governed principally by the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations of 1961.

Types of mission

An embassy is the principal mission, headed by an ambassador, handling the full political relationship. Between Commonwealth countries, the equivalent mission is called a high commission, headed by a high commissioner. Missions to international organisations (such as permanent missions to the United Nations) represent states within those bodies. A legation, headed by a minister, was historically a lower grade of mission; most were upgraded to embassies after the Second World War. Consulates (see Consulate) are formally consular rather than diplomatic missions but are often grouped with them in everyday usage.

Functions

The Vienna Convention lists the functions of a diplomatic mission as representing the sending state, protecting its interests and nationals, negotiating with the receiving government, ascertaining and reporting on conditions and developments, and promoting friendly relations, including economic, cultural and scientific ties. Missions also commonly provide consular services through a consular section.

Privileges and immunities

Mission premises are inviolable: agents of the receiving state may not enter without the head of mission's consent, and the receiving state must protect them. Diplomatic agents enjoy personal inviolability and immunity from criminal, and largely civil, jurisdiction. These privileges, which are reciprocal and rest on functional necessity, can be waived by the sending state; a diplomat who seriously abuses them may be declared persona non grata and expelled.

Non-resident and multiple accreditation

An ambassador may be accredited to several states simultaneously, residing in one of them, and small states frequently accredit a single mission to many countries. Some states maintain "embassies" that are in reality small liaison or interests sections, sometimes housed within the mission of a protecting power where direct relations are broken off.
