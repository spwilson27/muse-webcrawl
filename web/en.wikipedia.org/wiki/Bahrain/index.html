Bahrain, officially the Kingdom of Bahrain, is an island country in West Asia. It is situated on the Persian Gulf and comprises a small archipelago of some 50 natural islands and more than 30 artificial islands, centred on Bahrain Island. Bahrain is connected to Saudi Arabia by the King Fahd Causeway to the west and lies across the Gulf of Bahrain from Qatar. Its capital and largest city is Manama.

History

Bahrain was the centre of the ancient Dilmun civilisation, a trading entrepôt linking Mesopotamia with the Indus Valley as early as the third millennium BCE. The islands later came under Persian, Portuguese (1521–1602), and again Persian influence before the Al Khalifa family took control in 1783. During the 19th and 20th centuries Bahrain was a British protectorate; oil was discovered there in 1932, the first strike on the Arab side of the Gulf. Bahrain declared independence from Britain in 1971. In 2002 the country became a kingdom under King Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa. The 2011 uprising, part of the wider Arab Spring, was met with a government crackdown assisted by Gulf Cooperation Council forces.

Geography

Bahrain Island accounts for the great majority of the land area. The terrain is mostly low desert plain, rising gently to the central Jabal ad Dushan (Mountain of Smoke), about 134 metres high. The climate is arid, with extremely hot summers and mild winters. Fresh groundwater from the Dammam aquifer historically fed famous springs, giving the islands their name — "two seas" in Arabic — and supporting date palm groves along the northern coast.

Economy

Petroleum remains important, but Bahrain has diversified further than its neighbours into banking and financial services, aluminium production, and tourism. The Bahrain International Circuit in Sakhir hosts the Bahrain Grand Prix, a Formula One race first held in 2004. The United States Navy's Fifth Fleet is headquartered at Naval Support Activity Bahrain.

Demographics

About half the population are expatriate workers, principally from South Asia. Citizens are divided between Sunni and Shia Muslims, with Islam the state religion; the ruling family is Sunni. Arabic is the official language, while English is widely used in commerce.
