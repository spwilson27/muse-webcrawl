Classical republicanism

Classical republicanism, also known as civic republicanism or civic humanism, is a form of government and political ideology that stresses the common good, civic virtue, and participation of citizens in a republic.

Origins

Rooted in Aristotle, Cicero, and the Roman Republic, it was revived by Renaissance thinkers in Florence such as Leonardo Bruni and Niccolò Machiavelli, then transmitted through English republicans like James Harrington and Algernon Sidney.

Principles

  • Liberty as non-domination / freedom from arbitrary power
  • Mixed constitution balancing social orders
  • Civic virtue and active citizenship over private interest
  • Fear of corruption and standing armies

Influence

It profoundly influenced the American Founding Fathers and the American Revolution, alongside liberal ideas of natural rights, and has been revived by modern scholars such as Quentin Skinner and Philip Pettit.
