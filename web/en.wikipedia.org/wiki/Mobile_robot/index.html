Mobile robot

A mobile robot is an automatic machine that is capable of locomotion — moving around in its environment. Mobile robots contrast with fixed industrial robots, which are generally bolted to a workstation and manipulate objects with an arm.

Locomotion

Mobile robots move by various means: wheels and tracks on the ground, legs, propellers in the air (drones and other unmanned aerial vehicles), thrusters underwater (AUVs and ROVs), or swimming motions. Each medium presents distinct problems of stability, energy use, and control.

Navigation

Depending on their degree of autonomy, mobile robots may be teleoperated by a human, guided by sensors following lines or beacons, or fully autonomous. Autonomous navigation requires localization and mapping (often combined as SLAM — simultaneous localization and mapping), path planning, and obstacle avoidance using sensors such as lidar, cameras, sonar, and GPS.

Applications

Applications include warehouse automation, planetary rovers such as NASA's Mars rovers, military and bomb-disposal robots, domestic vacuum and lawn-mowing robots, agricultural robots, hospital delivery robots, and autonomous vehicles.
