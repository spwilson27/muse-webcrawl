Ensō

Ensō (円相)
Japanese円相
Literal meaning"circular form"
Associated withZen Buddhism, Japanese calligraphy

In Zen, an ensō (円相, "circular form") is a circle that is hand-drawn in one or two uninhibited brushstrokes to express a moment when the mind is free to let the body create. The ensō symbolises absolute enlightenment, strength, elegance, the universe, and mu (the void). It is characterised by a minimalism born of Japanese aesthetics.

Drawing

Drawing ensō is a meditative practice and a spiritual discipline in itself, related to the art of hitsuzendō ("the way of the brush"). The circle is usually drawn on silk or rice paper in a single fluid stroke of black ink. Some ensō are closed and complete; others are left open, with a gap in the circumference — a feature sometimes said to represent imperfection as an essential part of existence, or that the circle is not separate from everything else but part of something greater. Artists practise drawing ensō daily as a form of spiritual exercise.

Symbolism

As a sacred symbol in Zen Buddhism, the ensō is often understood to represent the universe and the void from which all things emerge. In Japanese calligraphy, where the ensō ranks among the deepest subjects, a single circle is taken to express the practitioner's state of mind at the moment of creation: it cannot be corrected afterwards, and so reveals the artist's true condition.
