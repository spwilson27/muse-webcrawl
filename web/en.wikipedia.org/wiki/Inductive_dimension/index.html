Inductive dimension

Field: Topology

Variants: Small inductive dimension (ind); large inductive dimension (Ind)

In the mathematical field of topology, the inductive dimension is a way of defining the dimension of a topological space by induction, based on the dimensions of the boundaries of small neighbourhoods of its points.

Definitions

The small inductive dimension ind(X) is defined recursively: the empty set has dimension −1, and ind(X) is the smallest n such that every point has arbitrarily small neighbourhoods whose boundaries have small inductive dimension at most n−1. The large inductive dimension Ind(X) uses closed sets and their separating neighbourhoods instead. The third classical notion is the Lebesgue covering dimension.

Relations

For separable metric spaces the three notions coincide, but in general spaces they can differ. The theory was developed in the early 20th century by Menger, Urysohn, Brouwer, and others.
