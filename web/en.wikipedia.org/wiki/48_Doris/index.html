48 Doris

48 Doris is one of the largest asteroids in the main belt.

48 Doris is one of the largest main-belt asteroids, with a diameter of about 222 kilometers. It was discovered on 19 September 1857 by German-French astronomer Hermann Goldschmidt from his balcony in Paris, and was named after Doris, a sea nymph and Oceanid in Greek mythology.

Physical characteristics

Doris is a dark, primitive carbonaceous body classified as a C-type asteroid (more specifically a CG-type). Its low albedo of around 0.06 and its spectrum suggest a surface composition similar to carbonaceous chondrite meteorites. Light-curve observations give a rotation period of about 11.9 hours. Occultation and imaging measurements indicate a slightly elongated, irregular shape.

Orbit

The asteroid orbits the Sun in the outer part of the main belt at a mean distance of about 3.1 AU, completing one orbit in roughly 5.5 years on a nearly circular path inclined about 6.6° to the ecliptic. It gives its name to the Doris family, a small grouping of asteroids sharing similar orbital elements, thought to be fragments from a past collisional breakup.
