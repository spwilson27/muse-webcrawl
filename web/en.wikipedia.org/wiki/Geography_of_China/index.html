The People's Republic of China is the world's third or fourth largest country by total area, covering approximately 9.6 million square kilometres. Its landscape is vast and diverse: the terrain descends in a broad staircase from the high plateaus and mountains of the west, dominated by the Tibetan Plateau — the "Roof of the World" — to the fertile plains and deltas of the east coast. China borders fourteen countries, more than any other state except Russia, and its coastline on the Pacific Ocean and its marginal seas runs some 14,500 kilometres.

Physical geography

Two great rivers dominate the settled east: the Yellow River (Huang He), 5,464 km long, the cradle of Chinese civilization on the North China Plain, and the Yangtze, Asia's longest river, which flows through the Sichuan Basin to the East China Sea at Shanghai. The Pearl River system drains the south. Deserts — the Taklamakan and Gobi — occupy much of the arid north and northwest, while the south is hilly and subtropical. The highest point, the summit of Mount Everest (Qomolangma) on the Nepal border, is the highest on Earth; the Turpan Depression in Xinjiang is among the lowest and hottest places in Asia.

Climate

China's climate ranges from subarctic in the far north of Heilongjiang to tropical in Hainan and Yunnan. The East Asian monsoon brings most precipitation in summer to the south and east; the southeast coast is subject to typhoons, while the northwest is dry continental. The Qinling–Huaihe line conventionally divides northern from southern China in climate, agriculture (wheat versus rice), and culture.

Population distribution

Population is heavily concentrated in the eastern third of the country. The Heihe–Tengchong line, proposed by geographer Hu Huanyong in 1935, divides China into a sparsely populated west (about 57 percent of the area but only a few percent of the people) and a densely populated east. Megacities such as Shanghai, Beijing, Chongqing, Guangzhou, and Shenzhen anchor the coastal urban belts.
