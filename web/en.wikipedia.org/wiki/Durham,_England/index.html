Durham is a cathedral city and the county town of County Durham in north-east England. The city stands on the River Wear, which loops around the rocky peninsula on which Durham Cathedral and Durham Castle stand — a site designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Durham is home to Durham University, one of England's oldest universities.

History

The city's history begins in 995, when monks of the Lindisfarne community, carrying the body of Saint Cuthbert, settled on the defensible peninsula in the Wear. A church was built to house Cuthbert's shrine, and pilgrims flocked to it. The present Norman cathedral was begun in 1093 and is regarded as one of the finest examples of Norman architecture in Europe; Cuthbert's shrine and the tomb of the Venerable Bede lie within it.

For centuries Durham was ruled by Prince-Bishops, who exercised extensive secular as well as religious authority over the County Palatine of Durham. Durham Castle, begun under William the Conqueror, served as the bishops' palace and now forms part of the university. The surrounding county became a major coal-mining region in the industrial era, an era commemorated by the Durham Miners' Gala, held annually since 1871.

Education

Durham University, founded in 1832, is a collegiate university modelled in part on Oxford and Cambridge, and its colleges and departments are spread throughout the city. It is consistently ranked among the leading universities in the United Kingdom.
