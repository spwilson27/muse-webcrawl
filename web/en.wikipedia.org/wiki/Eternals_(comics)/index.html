The Eternals are a fictional race of humanoids appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. They were created by artist and writer Jack Kirby and first appeared in The Eternals #1 (July 1976). The Eternals are an offshoot of humanity created millions of years ago by the cosmic Celestials, endowed with near-immortality and superhuman powers, and locked in a long conflict with their destructive cousins, the Deviants.

Publication history

Kirby, returning to Marvel after creating the Fourth World saga at DC Comics, developed the Eternals around themes of ancient astronauts and mythological gods — many Eternal names (Ikaris, Sersi, Thena, Makkari) echo figures of Greek myth, reflecting the idea that the Eternals inspired humanity's legends. The original series ran for 19 issues. The characters were later integrated fully into the Marvel Universe, featuring in series by Roy Thomas, Peter B. Gillis, Neil Gaiman and John Romita Jr. (2006), and Kieron Gillen (2021). A film adaptation, Eternals, was released in 2021 as part of the Marvel Cinematic Universe.

Fictional history

A million years ago the Celestials performed genetic experiments on early humans, producing the stable, long-lived Eternals and the unstable Deviants. Eternals live in hidden cities such as Olympia in Greece, are led by a Prime Eternal (Zuras, later Thena), and can merge into a collective entity called the Uni-Mind. Though sworn not to interfere with humanity, they have repeatedly fought the Deviants and prepared for the Celestials' return to judge Earth.

Powers and abilities

All Eternals possess superhuman strength, flight, telepathy, illusion casting and molecular manipulation powered by cosmic energy, as well as effective immortality — their minds are stored and can be restored to new bodies. Individual Eternals specialise: Ikaris in flight and senses, Sersi in transmutation, Makkari in speed, Gilgamesh in strength.
