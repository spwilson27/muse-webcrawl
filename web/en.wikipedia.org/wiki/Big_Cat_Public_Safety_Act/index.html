Big Cat Public Safety Act

Big Cat Public Safety Act
EnactedDecember 20, 2022 (signed by President Joe Biden)
CodifiedAmends the Lacey Act, 16 U.S.C. §§ 3371–3378
Public LawPub. L. 117–243

The Big Cat Public Safety Act is a United States federal law enacted in December 2022 that restricts the private possession, breeding, and commercial trade of big cats—including lions, tigers, leopards, jaguars, cheetahs, cougars, and hybrids of these species—and prohibits direct public contact with them, such as cub petting.

[Image: A tiger resting in an enclosure at an accredited sanctuary]

Background

Before the act, the keeping of big cats as pets or roadside attractions was governed by a patchwork of state laws; thousands of tigers were estimated to be kept privately in the United States. Safety incidents, escapes, and publicity surrounding facilities featured in the 2020 documentary series Tiger King intensified calls for federal legislation. Versions of the bill had been introduced in Congress repeatedly since 2012.

Provisions

The law amends the Lacey Act to ban breeding, purchasing, selling, and transporting big cats across state lines, with exemptions for licensed facilities such as accredited zoos, sanctuaries, universities, and wildlife rehabilitators. Existing owners could keep their animals if they registered them with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and did not breed, buy, or sell them, and are barred from allowing public contact. Violations can bring fines and imprisonment.

Reception

Animal welfare organizations such as the Humane Society of the United States and the Animal Legal Defense Fund supported the act, while some private owners and industry groups argued it was an overreach that burdened responsible keepers.
