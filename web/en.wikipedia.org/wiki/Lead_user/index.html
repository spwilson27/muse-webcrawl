Lead user

A lead user is a user of a product or service who currently experiences needs that will become general in a marketplace months or years later, and who stands to benefit significantly from obtaining a solution to those needs. The concept was developed by economist Eric von Hippel of the MIT Sloan School of Management, beginning with his work in the 1980s on user innovation.

Characteristics

Von Hippel identified two defining characteristics of lead users:

  • They face needs that are ahead of the trend — needs that most users in a market will not experience until much later.
  • They expect to obtain a high benefit from a solution to those needs, which motivates them to innovate on their own.

Because their needs are at the leading edge, lead users frequently develop their own modifications or entirely new products when manufacturers do not supply what they require. Studies in fields ranging from scientific instruments to extreme sports equipment and open-source software have found that many commercially important innovations originated with users rather than manufacturers.

Lead user method

The lead user method is a market research and product development technique in which firms systematically identify lead users, bring them into workshops, and work with them to develop new product concepts. It has been applied by companies such as 3M, where a comparison of lead user projects with conventionally generated projects found substantially higher projected revenues for the lead user concepts.

Relation to other concepts

Lead users are distinct from "early adopters" in the diffusion of innovations model: early adopters adopt existing products early, whereas lead users experience new needs early and often create the solutions themselves. The concept is a cornerstone of the user innovation and democratizing innovation literature.
