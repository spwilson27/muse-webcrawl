March on Washington for Jobs and Freedom

Marchers at the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool

The March on Washington for Jobs and Freedom, also known as simply the March on Washington or the Great March on Washington, was held in Washington, D.C., on Wednesday, August 28, 1963. The purpose of the march was to advocate for the civil and economic rights of African Americans. At the march, final speaker Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., standing in front of the Lincoln Memorial, delivered his historic "I Have a Dream" speech in which he called for an end to racism and racial segregation.

Background

The march was organized by a coalition of the "Big Six" civil rights organizations: the Southern Christian Leadership Conference, the Congress of Racial Equality, the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee, the NAACP, the National Urban League, and A. Philip Randolph's Brotherhood of Sleeping Car Porters. Randolph had first proposed a march on the capital in 1941, which was called off after President Franklin D. Roosevelt issued Executive Order 8802 banning discrimination in defense industries. Bayard Rustin served as chief organizer.

The march

An estimated 250,000 people gathered on the National Mall between the Washington Monument and the Lincoln Memorial, making it one of the largest political rallies for human rights in United States history. Speakers included Randolph, Rustin, Roy Wilkins, Whitney Young, John Lewis, and King. Performers included Mahalia Jackson, Marian Anderson, Bob Dylan, and Joan Baez. The march's ten demands included comprehensive civil rights legislation, desegregation of schools, a federal jobs program, and a minimum wage of $2.00 an hour.

Legacy

The march is credited with helping to pass the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965. King's "I Have a Dream" speech became one of the most famous speeches in American history. Anniversaries of the march were marked by further demonstrations, including events in 1983, 1993, 2013, and the Commitment March of 2020.
