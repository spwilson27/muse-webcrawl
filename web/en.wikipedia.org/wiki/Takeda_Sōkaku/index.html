Takeda Sōkaku (武田 惣角, 10 October 1859 – 25 April 1943) was a Japanese martial artist credited with founding the school of Daitō-ryū Aiki-jūjutsu. Known in his later years as the "little demon of Aizu", he travelled throughout Japan teaching his art to thousands of students, including police officers, military men, and martial artists.

Life

Born in the Aizu domain (modern Fukushima Prefecture) into a samurai family, Takeda learned martial arts from his father and grandfather and studied several traditional schools, including Hōzōin-ryū spear and Ono-ha Ittō-ryū swordsmanship. He claimed his jujutsu derived from arts preserved within the Takeda family of Aizu. After the Meiji Restoration he spent years travelling, training, and engaging in challenge matches.

Teaching and legacy

From the 1890s, Takeda taught openly, keeping enrolment books (eimeiroku) that recorded thousands of students. His most famous pupil was Morihei Ueshiba, founder of aikido, whose art grew out of Daitō-ryū. Other students founded or influenced lines such as Hakko-ryu, and Choi Yong-sool, founder of hapkido, claimed training under Takeda, though this is debated. Takeda's son Tokimune became his successor as head of the school in Hokkaido, where Takeda had taught many settlers and police.
