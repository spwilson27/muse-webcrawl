The Tōhoku region (東北地方, Tōhoku-chihō, literally "northeast region") is the northernmost region of Honshu, the largest island of Japan. It comprises six prefectures — Akita, Aomori, Fukushima, Iwate, Miyagi and Yamagata — with Sendai as its largest city. Historically known as the Michinoku region, Tōhoku is a largely rural, mountainous area famous for its rice production, heavy snowfall and summer festivals.

Geography

The Ōu Mountains run down the spine of the region, dividing the milder Pacific coast from the snowy Sea of Japan side, where districts such as the Shōnai plain and Akita's coast experience some of the heaviest snowfalls on earth. Important lowlands include the Sendai plain and the Fukushima basin. The region has a cool temperate climate with short summers, and its rugged Sanriku coast is marked by rias — drowned river valleys.

History

Tōhoku was the last part of Honshu brought under imperial control; its indigenous Emishi people resisted Yamato expansion for centuries until the campaigns of Sakanoue no Tamuramaro around 800 AD. In the 11th and 12th centuries the Northern Fujiwara ruled a semi-independent golden-age realm from Hiraizumi. During the Edo period the region was divided among powerful domains such as Sendai (Date clan) and Aizu. Tōhoku long remained one of Japan's poorer regions, and its farmers suffered badly in the Tenpō famine of the 1830s.

2011 earthquake and tsunami

On 11 March 2011 a magnitude 9.0–9.1 undersea earthquake off the Sanriku coast generated a devastating tsunami that inundated coastal communities in Miyagi, Iwate and Fukushima prefectures and triggered the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster. More than 15,000 people died across the region, and large-scale reconstruction has continued for over a decade.

Culture

The region is famed for its summer festivals: Aomori's Nebuta Matsuri, Akita's Kantō Matsuri, Sendai's Tanabata Matsuri and Yamagata's Hanagasa Matsuri, collectively known as the great festivals of Tōhoku. Its dialects are grouped as the Tōhoku-ben of Japanese.
