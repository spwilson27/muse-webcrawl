Benkei

Benkei, the loyal warrior monk of Japanese legend.

Saitō Musashibō Benkei (西塔武蔵坊弁慶, 1155–1189), popularly known simply as Benkei, was a Japanese warrior monk (sōhei) who lived in the latter years of the Heian period. In legend and popular tradition, he is the gigantic, immensely strong and unfailingly loyal retainer of the hero Minamoto no Yoshitsune. Benkei became one of the most beloved figures of Japanese folklore, celebrated in Noh and Kabuki theatre, chronicles and modern media.

Legendary life

According to tradition, Benkei was the son of a temple head priest and, in some tales, of a demon or a fierce mountain deity, accounting for his strength and his nickname Oniwaka ("young demon"). Trained as a monk on Mount Hiei, he is said to have roamed Kyoto collecting the swords of defeated samurai at the Gojō Bridge, vowing to gather a thousand blades. On the 999th sword, he was defeated in single combat by the slight young Minamoto no Yoshitsune, who outmatched him with dazzling swordsmanship. From that moment Benkei swore eternal loyalty to Yoshitsune.

Service to Yoshitsune

Benkei accompanied Yoshitsune through the Genpei War against the Taira clan, fighting in the great battles chronicled in The Tale of the Heike. When Yoshitsune was betrayed by his brother Yoritomo and forced to flee, Benkei disguised his master as a porter and bluffed their way through the barrier at Ataka — the episode dramatised in the Noh play Ataka and the Kabuki play Kanjinchō, in which Benkei strikes his own lord to preserve the disguise and weeps afterwards.

Death

Benkei's end is among the most famous scenes in Japanese literature. Cornered at the Koromogawa no Tate in 1189, Benkei held the bridge against Fujiwara forces alone while Yoshitsune took his own life within. Riddled with arrows, Benkei died standing upright, his body remaining a barrier even in death — the "standing death" (Benkei no tachijōgo) — and the attackers feared to approach him.
