Philip Bayard "Phil" Crosby (18 June 1926 – 18 August 2001) was an American businessman and author who contributed to management theory and quality management practices. He is best known for the concepts of "Do It Right The First Time" (DIRFT) and zero defects, and for his 1979 bestseller Quality Is Free, which popularized the idea that improving quality reduces total costs rather than increasing them.

Career

Crosby began his career in quality as an inspector and quality engineer, working at companies including Crosley, Bendix, and Martin-Marietta, where he developed the zero defects concept in the early 1960s on the Pershing missile program. He then served as corporate vice president of quality at ITT from 1965 to 1979. In 1979 he founded Philip Crosby Associates, Inc., a quality management consulting and training firm, and established the Quality College in Winter Park, Florida.

Quality philosophy

Crosby defined quality as "conformance to requirements" and argued that quality is measured by the "price of nonconformance" — the costs of doing things wrong. His four absolutes of quality management are: quality means conformance to requirements; the system of quality is prevention; the performance standard is zero defects; and the measurement of quality is the price of nonconformance. His 14-step quality improvement program and "quality management maturity grid" were widely used by corporations in the 1980s.

Works

Among his many books are Quality Is Free (1979), Quality Without Tears (1984), Running Things (1986), and The Absolutes of Leadership (1996).
