Tropomyosin receptor kinase

Structure of a Trk receptor in the cell membrane

The tropomyosin receptor kinases (Trk) are a family of receptor tyrosine kinases that are activated by neurotrophins, a family of growth factors involved in the development, survival and function of neurons. The family comprises TrkA, TrkB and TrkC, encoded by the genes NTRK1, NTRK2 and NTRK3.

Ligands and function

TrkA is the high-affinity receptor for nerve growth factor (NGF), TrkB binds brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and neurotrophin-4, and TrkC binds neurotrophin-3. Binding activates the intracellular kinase domain, triggering signalling cascades such as the MAPK/ERK, PI3K/Akt and PLCγ pathways, which promote neuronal differentiation and survival in both the central and peripheral nervous systems.

Clinical significance

Chromosomal rearrangements that fuse an NTRK gene to another gene can drive a range of cancers, producing constantly active Trk fusion proteins. These fusions occur in rare tumours such as infantile fibrosarcoma and secretory breast carcinoma, and at low frequency in many common cancers. Protein kinase inhibitors such as larotrectinib and entrectinib target these fusions and have been approved as tissue-agnostic cancer therapies. Mutations in NTRK1 also cause congenital insensitivity to pain with anhidrosis.
