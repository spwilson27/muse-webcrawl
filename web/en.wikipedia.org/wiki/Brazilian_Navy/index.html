The Brazilian Navy (Portuguese: Marinha do Brasil, MB) is the naval service branch of the Brazilian Armed Forces, responsible for conducting naval operations. It is the largest navy in Latin America and among the largest in the Americas, with a fleet that includes a helicopter carrier, frigates, corvettes, submarines (including, in development, a nuclear-powered boat), amphibious ships, patrol vessels, and an extensive riverine force for the Amazon and Pantanal. The navy also operates naval aviation and the Brazilian Marine Corps (Corpo de Fuzileiros Navais).

Created in 1822 upon Brazilian independence, the navy played a decisive part in the independence wars, the Cisplatine War, and the Paraguayan War (1864–70) — the largest conflict in South American history — and it participated in both World Wars, escorting convoys and hunting submarines in the South Atlantic during the Second World War. Today its missions include defending Brazil's 4.5 million km2 maritime jurisdiction (the "Amazônia Azul," or Blue Amazon), protecting offshore oil platforms, patrolling inland waterways, and supporting Antarctic research.

History

The Imperial Brazilian Navy was assembled in 1822 from Portuguese ships and foreign officers — most famously the British admiral Thomas Cochrane — and secured Brazil's coasts during the wars of independence. In the Cisplatine War (1825–28) it blockaded the Río de la Plata. During the Paraguayan War, the navy's victory at the Battle of Riachuelo (June 11, 1865), under Admiral Barroso, gave the Allies control of the rivers and is commemorated as the navy's principal battle honour. The service later endured crises such as the Revolta da Armada (1893–94) and the Revolt of the Lash (1910), in which sailors protested corporal punishment.

Brazil was the third country to operate dreadnought battleships, commissioning Minas Geraes and São Paulo from 1910, which briefly made its fleet among the world's strongest. In the First World War a Brazilian naval division served with the Royal Navy; in the Second, after German U-boats sank Brazilian merchant ships in 1942, Brazil declared war and its navy escorted Atlantic convoys and conducted anti-submarine patrols. Brazil operated the aircraft carrier Minas Gerais (1960–2001, ex-HMS Vengeance) and then NAe São Paulo (2001–2017, ex-French Foch), the latter decommissioned in 2017 and scuttled in the Atlantic in 2023.

Organisation

The navy is commanded by the Commander of the Navy under the Ministry of Defence in Brasília. Territory is divided into nine Naval Districts covering the coast and the Amazon/Pantanal river systems; operational forces include the Fleet Command (Comando-em-Chefe da Esquadra) at Rio de Janeiro, the Submarine Force, Naval Aviation Command, the Marine Corps general command, and riverine groups based at Manaus, Belém, Ladário, and elsewhere. The navy also administers Brazil's maritime authority functions — hydrography, aids to navigation, and merchant marine oversight — and the Capitania dos Portos (harbour captaincies).

Fleet and modernisation

The fleet's centrepiece is the multipurpose helicopter carrier NAM Atlântico (purchased from the United Kingdom in 2018). Surface forces include Niterói-class and Greenhalgh-class (ex-Royal Navy Type 22) frigates, Inhaúma and Barroso-class corvettes, and the new Tamandaré-class frigates — four ships based on the German MEKO design, the first launched in 2024 — intended to renew the escort force. The submarine arm operates Tupi-class (Type 209) boats and the new Riachuelo-class (Scorpène-derived) submarines built under the PROSUB programme with France; the programme's flagship goal is SN-BR Álvaro Alberto, a nuclear-powered attack submarine that would make Brazil the first non-nuclear-weapon state to field one, with completion not expected before the 2030s. Amphibious lift includes NDM Bahia (ex-French Siroco), and the navy maintains a large patrol fleet, including Amazonas-class ocean patrol vessels and Amazon river patrol ships with onboard medical facilities serving riverside communities.

Naval aviation and marines

Brazilian Naval Aviation flies AF-1 Skyhawk fighters (land-based, formerly embarked on São Paulo) and a range of helicopters, including Super Lynx, Seahawk, and Super Cougar types, for anti-submarine, search-and-rescue, and amphibious support missions. The Marine Corps (Fuzileiros Navais) numbers roughly 15,000–18,000 personnel and provides amphibious assault, riverine, and special operations capabilities, including the COMANF special forces battalion; Brazilian marines have served in UN peacekeeping, notably in Haiti (MINUSTAH).
