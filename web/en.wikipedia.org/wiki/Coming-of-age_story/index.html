Coming-of-age story

In genre studies, a coming-of-age story is a story whose main character grows from childhood or adolescence into adulthood. The protagonist's psychological and moral growth is the focus, often involving a loss of innocence and a transformative experience or journey of self-discovery.

Bildungsroman

The literary form is exemplified by the German Bildungsroman, or "novel of formation," whose archetype is Goethe's Wilhelm Meister's Apprenticeship (1795–96). Classic English-language examples include Dickens's Great Expectations, Mark Twain's Adventures of Huckleberry Finn, and J. D. Salinger's The Catcher in the Rye (1951).

In film

Coming-of-age films became a staple of cinema in the 20th century, including The 400 Blows (1959), Stand by Me (1986), the films of John Hughes, Boyhood (2014) — shot over 12 years as its lead actor aged — and Lady Bird (2017).

Characteristics

Typical elements include a young protagonist, a departure from home or a mentor figure, trials that test the character's values, first love, and a concluding moment of recognition in which the character accepts adult responsibilities or a new understanding of the world.
