The Association of Mineworkers and Construction Union (AMCU) is a trade union in South Africa. It was established in 1998 by veteran trade unionist Joseph Mathunjwa (a carpenter on the mines) and a group of likeminded unionists who were unhappy with the direction of the mine workers' unions. AMCU became famous in the mining industry when its members participated in a series of wildcat strikes at the Lonmin mine in Marikana, which led to a shootout on 16 August 2012. It was founded in Witbank, in Mpumalanga province.

AMCU was formally registered with the Department of Labour as a trade union in 2001. AMCU was admitted to the National Economic Development and Labour Council (NEDLAC) in 2018, and it had about 300,000 members in 2019, making it South Africa's second-biggest union.

History

AMCU arose in the aftermath of the Lonmin mine strike at Marikana in 2012, when members of the rival National Union of Mineworkers (NUM) clashed with strikers. The Marikana massacre of 16 August 2012, in which 34 miners were killed by police, saw AMCU supplanting NUM as the dominant union in the platinum belt after condemning NUM's handling of the strike. AMCU then led the longest strike in South African mining history in the platinum industry in 2014.

Leadership

Joseph Mathunjwa has served as the union's president since its founding. The union's slogan centres on representing workers' independent voice outside of formal political party alliances, in contrast to NUM's close relationship with the African National Congress (ANC) through the Congress of South African Trade Unions (COSATU).
