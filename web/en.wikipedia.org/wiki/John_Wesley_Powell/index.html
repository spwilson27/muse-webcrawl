John Wesley Powell (24 March 1834 – 23 September 1902) was an American geologist, U.S. Army soldier, explorer of the American West, and professor. He is famous for the 1869 Powell Geographic Expedition, a three-month river trip down the Green and Colorado rivers that included the first recorded passage by Europeans through the Grand Canyon.

Early life and Civil War

Powell was largely self-educated in natural history, making botanical and geological excursions along the Mississippi and Ohio rivers in his youth. Enlisting in the Union Army in 1861, he lost most of his right arm at the Battle of Shiloh in 1862 but continued to serve, rising to the rank of major.

Expeditions of 1869 and 1871

In May 1869 Powell set out with nine men in four boats from Green River, Wyoming. The expedition ran the Colorado through the canyons of Utah and Arizona; three men left the party shortly before its completion and were killed, but Powell and the remainder emerged below the Grand Canyon in August. A second expedition in 1871–72 mapped the region in detail. His narrative, The Exploration of the Colorado River and Its Canyons, remains a classic of exploration literature.

Survey and public service

Powell founded and directed the Bureau of Ethnology at the Smithsonian Institution, publishing classification work on Native American languages, and in 1881 succeeded Clarence King as the second director of the United States Geological Survey. His Report on the Lands of the Arid Region of the United States (1878) warned that the western public lands could not support conventional eastern farming and proposed irrigation districts organised by watershed — proposals largely ignored until the Dust Bowl era. Lake Powell, the reservoir behind Glen Canyon Dam, is named for him.
