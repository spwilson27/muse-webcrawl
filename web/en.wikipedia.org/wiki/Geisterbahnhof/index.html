Geisterbahnhof

Literal meaning"Ghost station"
LocationBerlin, Germany
Period1961–1989
NetworksBerlin U-Bahn, Berlin S-Bahn

Geisterbahnhof (plural Geisterbahnhöfe, literally "ghost station") is a German term for the underground stations of the Berlin U-Bahn and S-Bahn that were closed during the division of Berlin between 1961 and 1989, when the Berlin Wall separated the city.

Background

When the Wall was erected on 13 August 1961, Berlin's transport network was severed. Several lines originating in West Berlin passed for part of their route under East Berlin territory. These lines remained in operation for western passengers, but their trains passed through stations located in the East without stopping. The stations were closed, their entrances were sealed or demolished, and the dimly lit platforms were patrolled by East German guards and kept under surveillance to prevent escapes.

Stations affected

On the U-Bahn, the affected stations lay mainly on lines U6 and U8; on the S-Bahn, the Nord-Süd Tunnel stations were closed to eastern passengers, with the notable exception of Friedrichstraße station, which served as a controlled border crossing point. Nordbahnhof, Oranienburger Tor, Französische Straße, Stadtmitte and Potsdamer Platz were among the stations that became ghost stations.

Reunification

After the fall of the Berlin Wall on 9 November 1989, the ghost stations were progressively reopened, some within days and others after renovation, restoring a unified Berlin transport network by the mid-1990s.
