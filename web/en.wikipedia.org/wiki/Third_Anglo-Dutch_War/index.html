Third Anglo-Dutch War

Date: 1672–1674
Location: North Sea, English Channel, New York
Result: Treaty of Westminster (1674)
Combatants: Dutch Republic vs. Kingdom of England (with France, in the wider Franco-Dutch War)

The Battle of Texel, 1673

The Third Anglo-Dutch War (Dutch: Derde Engelse Zeeoorlog) was a naval conflict between England and the Dutch Republic lasting from 1672 to 1674. It was the third of the Anglo-Dutch Wars and formed part of the larger Franco-Dutch War, in which France invaded the Netherlands in the Rampjaar ("disaster year") of 1672.

Causes

Under the secret Treaty of Dover (1670), Charles II of England agreed with Louis XIV of France to attack the Dutch in return for French subsidies. England declared war in March 1672, shortly before the French invasion. Charles hoped to profit from Dutch trade and to settle scores from the Second Anglo-Dutch War, while Louis sought to conquer the Spanish Netherlands and break Dutch commercial power.

Course of the war

  • Battle of Solebay (7 June 1672) — indecisive clash in which the Dutch under Michiel de Ruyter frustrated the combined Anglo-French fleet.
  • Battles of Schooneveld (June 1673) — De Ruyter twice held off the allied fleet, preventing an invasion of the Dutch coast.
  • Battle of Texel (21 August 1673) — De Ruyter's victory ended the threat of an Anglo-French landing.
  • New Netherland — a Dutch expedition recaptured New York (renamed New Orange) in 1673, though it was returned by the peace.

On land, the Dutch survived by opening the dikes and flooding the Holland Water Line, halting the French advance. William III of Orange became stadtholder, and Grand Pensionary Johan de Witt was lynched by a mob.

Peace

Growing English opposition to the war and to the French alliance, together with fears of Charles's Catholic sympathies, forced Parliament to cut funding. The Treaty of Westminster (February 1674) restored the status quo: New Netherland returned to England, Suriname stayed Dutch, and trade regulations were modestly adjusted.
