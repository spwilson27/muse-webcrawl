Amazing Heroes was an American magazine about comic books, published by Fantagraphics Books from 1981 to 1992. Running for 204 regular issues (plus special issues and annuals), it was one of the principal professional news and criticism magazines of the comics direct-market era, combining news reporting, interviews with creators, reviews, and market coverage with a fan-friendly tone.

History and content

Launched in June 1981 under editor Michael Catron, Amazing Heroes initially appeared as a biweekly tabloid-sized magazine and later shifted formats and schedules as the market changed. Its pages mixed previews of upcoming comics, lengthy interviews with writers and artists, historical articles, and columns; the magazine was known for features such as its annual swimsuit specials drawn by comics artists and for the "Amazing Heroes" preview specials that catalogued upcoming releases. Editors over the run included Kim Thompson and others associated with Fantagraphics' critical flagship, The Comics Journal, though Amazing Heroes aimed at a broader readership of comics fans rather than critics.

Legacy

The magazine documented the transformation of the American comics industry during the 1980s — the rise of independent publishers, the direct market, and the graphic novel — and its interviews remain a primary source for comics historians. Declining advertising and the changing market led to its cancellation in 1992, by which time print fanzines and news magazines were beginning to be displaced by online coverage.
