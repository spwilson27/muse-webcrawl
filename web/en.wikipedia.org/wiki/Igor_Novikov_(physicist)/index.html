Igor Novikov (physicist)

Igor Dmitriyevich Novikov
Born: 10 November 1935, Moscow
Fields: Astrophysics, cosmology
Known for: Novikov self-consistency principle; black hole physics

Igor Dmitriyevich Novikov (born 1935) is a Russian theoretical astrophysicist and cosmologist. A student of Yakov Zeldovich, he worked at the Lebedev Physical Institute and the Space Research Institute in Moscow, and later directed the Theoretical Astrophysics Center in Copenhagen.

Work

With Zeldovich, Novikov predicted in 1966 that accreting black holes in binary systems should be detectable X-ray sources, and he contributed to the theory of black hole interiors, white holes and the early universe; his popular book Black Holes and the Universe reached a wide audience. He is best known to the general public for the Novikov self-consistency principle (1980s), which proposes that events in a spacetime containing closed timelike curves remain globally self-consistent — travellers to the past cannot change it, only fulfil it — an idea explored in his book The River of Time (1998).
