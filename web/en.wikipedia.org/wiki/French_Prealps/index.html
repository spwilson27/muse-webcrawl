French Prealps

The French Prealps (French: Préalpes françaises) are a group of mountain ranges in the western part of the Alps, located in France. They run roughly parallel to the main chain of the French Alps, to its west, and are separated from it by a chain of depressions. The Prealps are composed mainly of sedimentary limestone and marl, in contrast to the crystalline rocks of the central Alps, and their summits are generally lower than those of the high Alps.

Geography

The Prealps extend from Lake Geneva in the north to the Mediterranean hinterland in the south. In the north (the Savoy Prealps) they include the Chablais, Bornes, Bauges, Chartreuse and Vercors massifs. The southern Prealps include the ranges of Dauphiné and Provence, such as the Dévoluy, the Baronnies, the Montagne de Lure, the Luberon and the ranges behind Nice.

Geology

The Prealps consist of folded and faulted Mesozoic sedimentary rocks, chiefly limestones, that were deposited on the margin of the Alpine ocean and pushed westward during the Alpine orogeny. Their landscapes feature cliffs, plateaus, gorges and karst formations, including extensive cave systems in the Vercors and Chartreuse.
