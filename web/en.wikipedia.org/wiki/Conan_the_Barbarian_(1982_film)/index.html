Conan the Barbarian (1982 film)

Conan the Barbarian is a 1982 American epic sword-and-sorcery film directed by John Milius and written by Milius and Oliver Stone. Based on the character created by Robert E. Howard, it stars Arnold Schwarzenegger as Conan, with James Earl Jones as the villain Thulsa Doom.

Plot

As a boy, Conan's village is destroyed and his parents killed by Thulsa Doom, leader of a snake cult. Enslaved and forced to push the Wheel of Pain, Conan grows into a powerful gladiator and thief. Freed, he seeks revenge, aided by Subotai and Valeria, stealing from the cult and rescuing a princess before confronting Doom at his temple. Conan beheads Doom before his followers, who disperse.

Production and reception

Filmed in Spain, the production was noted for its elaborate sets, practical effects and Basil Poledouris's celebrated score. Its themes draw on Nietzsche and ideas of strength and will. The film was a commercial success, launched Schwarzenegger's film stardom, and was followed by the sequel Conan the Destroyer (1984). Its famous lamentation speech and the riddle of steel became enduring pop-culture references.
