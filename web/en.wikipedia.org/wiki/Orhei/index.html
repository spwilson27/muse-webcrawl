Orhei

Country: Moldova

District: Orhei District

Location: On the Răut River, ~46 km north of Chișinău

Nearby site: Old Orhei (Orheiul Vechi) archaeological complex

Orhei is a city and municipality in central Moldova, the administrative centre of Orhei District, situated on the Răut River about 46 kilometres north of the capital, Chișinău. It is one of the country's older towns, first attested in the late medieval period of the Principality of Moldavia.

History

The modern town grew after the older settlement — now the archaeological and museum complex of Old Orhei (Orheiul Vechi), some 18 km away near the villages of Trebujeni and Butuceni — was abandoned. Old Orhei preserves remains of Geto-Dacian fortifications, a medieval citadel built under Stephen the Great, cave monasteries cut into the limestone cliffs, and a Tatar-era bathhouse from the period of the Golden Horde, when the site was known as Shehr al-Jedid.

The new Orhei developed as a market town under Moldavian, then Russian imperial (from 1812), Romanian (1918–1940) and Soviet rule. Before the Second World War roughly half the town's population was Jewish; most were murdered or deported during the Holocaust after Romanian and German forces occupied Bessarabia in 1941.

Economy and culture

Orhei is a centre for food processing, wine and textiles. Old Orhei is Moldova's best-known cultural landscape and is on the country's tentative list for UNESCO World Heritage status; its cave monastery of the Dormition, still inhabited by monks, draws pilgrims and tourists.

Notable people

The town is associated with the controversial businessman and politician Ilan Shor, who served as mayor from 2015.
