Stone space

In topology and related areas of mathematics, a Stone space, also called a profinite space, is a compact, totally disconnected Hausdorff space. Stone spaces are named after Marshall Harvey Stone, who introduced them in his 1936 paper on the representation theory of Boolean algebras.

Definition and equivalences

The following conditions on a topological space are equivalent:

  • The space is a Stone space (compact, Hausdorff, totally disconnected);
  • The space is homeomorphic to an inverse limit of finite discrete spaces (a profinite space);
  • The space is compact and zero-dimensional (has a basis of clopen sets) and Hausdorff;
  • The space is the Stone space of some Boolean algebra.

Examples

The Cantor set, with its usual topology, is a Stone space. The one-point compactification of the discrete natural numbers, the space of p-adic integers, and profinite groups such as the profinite integers are further examples. Any finite Hausdorff space is discrete and hence a Stone space.

Stone duality

Stone spaces are the objects of one side of Stone duality: the category of Boolean algebras is dually equivalent to the category of Stone spaces and continuous maps. Under this duality, the Stone space of a Boolean algebra is its space of ultrafilters, and the Boolean algebra recovered from a Stone space is its algebra of clopen subsets.

Condensed mathematics

In the condensed mathematics programme of Dustin Clausen and Peter Scholze, profinite sets (Stone spaces) play a foundational role: condensed sets are defined as sheaves on the site of profinite sets.
