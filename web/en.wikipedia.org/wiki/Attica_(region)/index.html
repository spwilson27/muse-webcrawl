Attica (Greek: Αττική, Attikí) is a historical region of Greece that encompasses the city of Athens, the capital of the country. It is a triangular peninsula jutting into the Aegean Sea, bordered by Boeotia to the north and Megaris to the west. In modern administrative terms, Attica is one of the thirteen regions (peripheries) of Greece and contains the Athens metropolitan area, home to more than three million people — nearly a third of the Greek population.

The Attica peninsula in central Greece.

Geography

Attica is bounded by the Saronic Gulf to the south and the South Euboean Gulf to the northeast. Its interior is divided by mountain ranges — Hymettus, Pentelicus (Penteli), Parnes (Parnitha) and Aigaleo — into the plains of Athens, Eleusis (the Thriasian Plain), Marathon and the Mesogaia. The Cephissus and Ilissus rivers cross the Athenian plain. The region also includes the Saronic islands of Salamis, Aegina, Poros, Hydra, Spetses, Kythera and Antikythera administratively.

History

Attica was unified politically around Athens, traditionally under the legendary king Theseus, in a process called synoecism completed by about the 7th century BC. The reforms of Solon (594 BC) and Cleisthenes (508/7 BC), who divided Attica into demes grouped in ten tribes, laid the foundations of Athenian democracy. In classical times the population of Attica is estimated at 250,000–300,000. The silver mines at Laurion financed the Athenian fleet, and the Long Walls linked Athens to its port at Piraeus. After the classical period Attica passed through Macedonian, Roman, Byzantine and Ottoman rule before becoming the core of the modern Greek state in 1834, when Athens was chosen as capital.

Modern region

The administrative region of Attica was created in the 1987 administrative reform and restructured under the Kallikratis programme of 2011. It is subdivided into eight regional units. Beyond Athens and Piraeus, significant towns include Eleusis, Marathon, Lavrio, Megara and Markopoulo. The region is Greece's economic centre, generating close to half of national GDP.
