Potsdam


Labelled image placeholder: Sanssouci Palace, Potsdam

Potsdam (German pronunciation: [ˈpɔtsdam]) is the capital and largest city of the German state of Brandenburg. It is part of the Berlin/Brandenburg Metropolitan Region and lies on the River Havel, about 25 kilometres southwest of Berlin's city centre. Its population is roughly 180,000.

History

Potsdam grew from a Slavic settlement and was first mentioned in 993. It rose to prominence as a residence of the Prussian kings, above all Frederick the Great, who built the Sanssouci Palace and park in the mid-18th century. The city became a garrison town and a centre of royal administration. In 1945 Cecilienhof Palace hosted the Potsdam Conference, at which the Allied leaders settled the postwar order for Germany and Europe.

Sights

The Palaces and Parks of Potsdam and Berlin are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, including Sanssouci, the New Palace, Cecilienhof, Babelsberg Park, and the Glienicke Bridge, famous for Cold War exchanges of spies. The Dutch Quarter (Holländisches Viertel), built in the 1730s for Dutch craftsmen, is the largest collection of Dutch-style houses outside the Netherlands. The Einstein Tower, an expressionist solar observatory of 1921, stands on the Telegrafenberg science campus.

Economy and education

Potsdam is a significant centre of film production, home to Studio Babelsberg, founded in 1912 and considered the oldest large-scale film studio in the world. The University of Potsdam, founded in 1991, and numerous research institutes, including centres for astrophysics, geosciences, and climate research, make the city a leading science location.
