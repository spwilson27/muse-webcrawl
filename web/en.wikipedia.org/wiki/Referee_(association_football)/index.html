Referee (association football)


A referee cautioning a player with a yellow card.

The referee is the official in association football who enforces the Laws of the Game during a match. The referee has full authority to enforce the laws in connection with the match to which he or she has been appointed, and the referee's decisions on points of fact connected with play are final.

Duties and powers

Under Law 5, the referee:

  • enforces the Laws of the Game;
  • controls the match in cooperation with the other match officials;
  • acts as timekeeper and keeps a record of the match;
  • stops, suspends, or abandons the match for infringements or outside interference;
  • disciplines players and team officials, showing yellow cards for cautions and red cards for sending-off offences;
  • restarts play, including awarding free kicks and penalty kicks.

Assistant officials

The referee is assisted by two assistant referees (linesmen), a fourth official, and, in top competitions, a video assistant referee (VAR) team that reviews clear and obvious errors in goals, penalties, red cards, and mistaken identity.

History

Early football was officiated by team captains and off-field umpires; the single on-field referee with a whistle emerged in the late 19th century, with cards introduced by English referee Ken Aston and first used at the 1970 FIFA World Cup.
