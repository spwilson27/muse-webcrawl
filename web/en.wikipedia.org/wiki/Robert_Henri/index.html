Robert Henri (born Robert Henry Cozad; June 24, 1865 – July 12, 1929) was an American painter and teacher, a leading figure of the Ashcan School of American realism and an organiser of the landmark 1908 exhibition of "The Eight". He took the surname Henri (pronounced "Hen-rye") after his father, a gambler and land speculator, shot a man and the family assumed new identities.

Career

Henri studied at the Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts under Thomas Anshutz and at the Académie Julian in Paris. Teaching in Philadelphia and then New York, he encouraged students including George Bellows, Edward Hopper and Rockwell Kent to paint modern urban life directly and vigorously. In 1908 he and seven colleagues — including John Sloan, William Glackens and George Luks — exhibited together at the Macbeth Galleries in protest at the conservative National Academy of Design.

Legacy

His book The Art Spirit (1923), compiled from his teaching notes, remains a classic statement of artistic independence and is still widely read by art students. Henri's own portraits and landscapes, painted with a dark palette and vigorous brushwork, are held by major American museums.
