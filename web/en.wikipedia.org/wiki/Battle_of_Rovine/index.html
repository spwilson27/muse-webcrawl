The Battle of Rovine was fought in 1395 (dated by some historians to 1394) between the forces of Mircea the Elder, Voivode of Wallachia, and an invading Ottoman army led by Sultan Bayezid I. It is one of the most famous battles of medieval Romanian history, celebrated as a heroic Wallachian stand against the Ottoman advance.

Background

Wallachia, lying north of the Danube, stood in the path of Ottoman expansion into the Balkans. Mircea had allied with Sigismund of Hungary and resisted Ottoman demands, defeating an earlier raid at Karamov. In 1395 Bayezid led a major invasion in person, supported by Serbian vassal contingents under Stefan Lazarević and including, by tradition, figures such as Prince Marko and Constantine Dragaš.

The battle

Mircea, heavily outnumbered, avoided a frontal contest and drew the Ottomans into the marshy, wooded ground at Rovine, near the Argeș river. Wallachian archers harassed the advancing columns, and the fighting in the swamps turned into a bloody slogging match in which Ottoman numbers counted for little. Bayezid was forced to withdraw across the Danube, though Wallachian losses were also severe. Contemporary accounts, and the later epic tradition, describe the field as strewn with the dead of both armies; Serbian vassal lords including Marko and Dragaš were said to have been killed.

Aftermath

The battle checked the Ottoman advance for the moment and burnished Mircea's reputation as a defender of Christendom, but it did not end the threat: Bayezid returned to impose a rival voivode and Wallachia was drawn into tribute relations with the Porte. The battle was immortalised in Mihai Eminescu's poem Scrisoarea a III-a ("The Third Letter"), which imagines Mircea defying the Sultan before the fight.
