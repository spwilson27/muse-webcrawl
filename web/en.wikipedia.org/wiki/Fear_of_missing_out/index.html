Fear of missing out

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fear of missing out (FOMO) is the feeling of apprehension that one is potentially missing out on rewarding experiences that others are having, or that others might be having experiences from which one is absent. It is characterized by a desire to stay continually connected with what others are doing, and is closely associated with social media use.

Research

The term was popularized in the early 2000s and added to the Oxford English Dictionary in 2013. In a widely cited 2013 study, Andrew Przybylski and colleagues defined FOMO as "a pervasive apprehension that others might be having rewarding experiences from which one is absent" and developed a measurement scale for it. Research links higher FOMO with lower life satisfaction, heavier and more compulsive social media checking, distracted driving, and poorer sleep. Self-determination theory is often used to explain it: unmet needs for competence, autonomy, and especially relatedness make people more vigilant to social information.

Social media and marketing

Social networking platforms can amplify FOMO by making others' activities continuously visible. Marketers deliberately invoke it through limited-time offers, countdown timers, and scarcity appeals. A countervailing concept, the "joy of missing out" (JOMO), describes contentment with disconnecting, and digital-wellbeing guidance often recommends notification limits and scheduled time offline to reduce FOMO.
