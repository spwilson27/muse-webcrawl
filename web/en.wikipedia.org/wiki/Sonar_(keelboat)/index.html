Sonar (keelboat)

Sonar
Designer: Bruce Kirby
Year: 1980
Length overall: 7.01 m (23 ft)
Type: One-design keelboat
Crew: 3–5

The Sonar is a one-design keelboat designed by Canadian naval architect Bruce Kirby in 1980. It was conceived as a club racer and sail-training boat that could be sailed comfortably by a wide range of sailors, including those with physical disabilities.

Design

The Sonar has a large, self-bailing cockpit, a fractional sloop rig, and a fixed fin keel. Its controls are arranged so that the boat can be sailed without crew members needing to leave the cockpit, a feature that made it popular with adaptive sailing programs. The class is governed by strict one-design rules administered by the International Sonar Class Association.

Paralympic and competitive use

The Sonar was selected as the three-person keelboat for the Paralympic Games sailing competition, appearing from the Sydney 2000 Games — sailing's debut as a full Paralympic medal sport — through Rio 2016. It has also been used in world championships and in the Sailing Champions League and other fleet-racing events.

Related classes

Bruce Kirby also designed the Laser dinghy. Comparable keelboats include the J/22 and the Etchells.
