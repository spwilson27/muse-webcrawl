Standalone sequel

A standalone sequel is a work in a series that shares the fictional world, setting or premise of a previous work but tells a self-contained story with new characters, rather than continuing the earlier plot. A viewer or reader can follow the standalone sequel without having experienced the original, even though the two works exist in continuity with one another.

Characteristics

Unlike a direct sequel, which continues the story and usually requires knowledge of the preceding instalment, a standalone sequel resets the narrative stakes: a new protagonist, a new conflict and usually a new location within the same world. Connections to the original may be limited to setting, minor recurring characters, references, or thematic links. The format lets a franchise continue indefinitely without an accumulating burden of backstory, and lets new creative teams take over without being bound to earlier storylines.

Examples

In film, The Color of Money is a largely standalone continuation of The Hustler; the Mad Max films after the first change tone and context considerably; and many entries in long-running horror and action franchises function as standalone episodes. In video games, most Grand Theft Auto and Final Fantasy mainline titles are standalone sequels in shared or analogous worlds, numbering suggesting continuity of brand rather than of story. Anthology television series such as Black Mirror and Fargo apply a similar principle per season.

Related concepts

A standalone sequel contrasts with a prequel, a direct continuation, and a spin-off, which typically promotes a secondary character from an existing work to protagonist of a new one, often running concurrently rather than subsequently.
