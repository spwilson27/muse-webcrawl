Emmanuelle Charpentier

Emmanuelle Marie Charpentier (born 11 December 1968) is a French microbiologist, geneticist and biochemist. Together with Jennifer Doudna, she received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2020 "for the development of a method for genome editing", recognising their discovery that the bacterial CRISPR-Cas9 immune system could be repurposed as a programmable tool for cutting DNA.

Career

Charpentier was born in Juvisy-sur-Orge, France, and studied microbiology and biochemistry at Pierre and Marie Curie University in Paris, earning her PhD from the Pasteur Institute in 1995. After postdoctoral work in the United States, she held positions in Vienna, at Umeå University in Sweden, and at the Hannover Medical School, before becoming a director at the Max Planck Institute for Infection Biology in Berlin and founding director of the independent Max Planck Unit for the Science of Pathogens in 2018.

CRISPR-Cas9

Studying the bacterium Streptococcus pyogenes, Charpentier discovered the tracrRNA molecule, essential to the CRISPR-Cas9 defence system. In a landmark 2012 paper in Science, Charpentier and Doudna showed that Cas9, guided by a single engineered RNA, could be directed to cut DNA at chosen sites — the basis of modern genome editing, which has transformed biology, medicine and agriculture. She co-founded companies including CRISPR Therapeutics and ERS Genomics to develop and license the technology.

Honours

Besides the Nobel Prize, Charpentier has received the Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences (2015), the Canada Gairdner International Award, the Kavli Prize in Nanoscience (2018, for work on CRISPR), and numerous other distinctions, and has been elected to academies including the French Academy of Sciences and the Royal Society.
