Liver function tests

PurposeAssess liver health and detect liver injury or disease

Liver function tests (LFTs or LFs), also referred to as a hepatic panel, are groups of blood tests that provide information about the state of a patient's liver. These tests include prothrombin time (PT/INR), activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT), albumin, bilirubin (direct and indirect), and others. The liver transaminases aspartate transaminase (AST or SGOT) and alanine transaminase (ALT or SGPT) are useful biomarkers of liver injury in a patient with some degree of intact liver function.

Most liver diseases cause only mild symptoms initially, but these diseases must be detected early. Hepatic (liver) involvement in some diseases can be of crucial importance. Testing is performed on a patient's blood sample. Some tests are associated with functionality (e.g., albumin), some with cellular integrity (e.g., transaminase), and some with conditions linked to the biliary tract (gamma-glutamyl transferase and alkaline phosphatase).

Patterns of abnormality

Clinicians interpret LFTs by pattern. A hepatocellular pattern, in which transaminases predominate, suggests direct liver cell injury such as viral hepatitis, drug-induced injury or ischaemia. A cholestatic pattern, dominated by ALP and GGT elevation, suggests obstruction or impaired bile flow. An isolated rise in bilirubin may reflect haemolysis or inherited conditions such as Gilbert's syndrome. Reduced albumin and a prolonged INR indicate impaired synthetic function, as seen in advanced cirrhosis.

Limitations

Despite their name, most of these tests do not measure liver "function" directly; several measure enzyme leakage from damaged cells. Results can be normal in advanced liver disease and abnormal in people without liver disease — transaminases rise after strenuous exercise or with muscle injury, and ALP rises in pregnancy and bone disease. Abnormal results therefore require clinical correlation and often repeat testing, imaging or specialist referral.

Related investigations

LFTs are commonly combined with a full blood count, renal profile, viral hepatitis serology, liver ultrasound and, where indicated, non-invasive fibrosis scores such as FIB-4 or transient elastography (FibroScan).
