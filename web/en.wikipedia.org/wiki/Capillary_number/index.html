Capillary number

Common symbolCa
DimensionDimensionless
FieldFluid mechanics

In fluid dynamics, the capillary number (Ca) is a dimensionless quantity representing the relative effect of viscous drag forces versus surface tension forces acting across an interface between a liquid and a gas, or between two immiscible liquids. It is defined as

Ca = μV / σ

where μ is the dynamic viscosity of the liquid, V is a characteristic velocity, and σ is the surface tension (or interfacial tension) between the two fluid phases.

Interpretation

For low capillary numbers (typically less than 10−5), flow in porous media is dominated by capillary forces, whereas for high capillary numbers the capillary forces are negligible compared to viscous forces. The capillary number is a central parameter in enhanced oil recovery, where increasing Ca — by raising the viscosity or velocity of the displacing fluid, or lowering interfacial tension with surfactants — helps mobilise oil trapped in rock pores by capillary forces.

Applications

Capillary number governs droplet formation and deformation in microfluidics, coating flows (where it determines the thickness of films deposited in dip coating, in combination with the Landau–Levich law), and the displacement of one fluid by another in porous media, including groundwater remediation and carbon dioxide sequestration.
