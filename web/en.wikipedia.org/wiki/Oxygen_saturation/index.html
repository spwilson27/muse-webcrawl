Oxygen saturation

Oxygen saturation is the fraction of oxygen-saturated haemoglobin relative to total haemoglobin in the blood. It is a key measure of respiratory function, indicating how effectively oxygen is being delivered around the body.

Measurement

Arterial oxygen saturation (SaO2) is measured directly from an arterial blood sample. In routine practice it is usually estimated non-invasively by pulse oximetry (SpO2), a clip or sensor on a finger or earlobe that compares absorption of red and infrared light. Normal SpO2 at sea level is typically 95–100%; values below about 90% indicate hypoxemia requiring attention.

Physiology

The relationship between the partial pressure of oxygen and saturation is described by the sigmoid oxygen–haemoglobin dissociation curve, which is shifted by temperature, pH (the Bohr effect), carbon dioxide and 2,3-BPG. Venous oxygen saturation (SvO2) is lower, around 75%, reflecting oxygen extraction by the tissues, and is used as a marker of the balance between oxygen delivery and consumption in critical care.
