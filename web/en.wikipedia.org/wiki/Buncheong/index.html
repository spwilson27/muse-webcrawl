Buncheong

Buncheong
Type: Korean stoneware
Period: 15th–16th centuries (Joseon dynasty)
Also spelled: Punch'ong

Buncheong (Korean: 분청사기) is a form of Korean stoneware produced during the early Joseon dynasty, in the 15th and 16th centuries. The name is an abbreviation of bunjang hoecheong sagi, "grey-green ceramic ware decorated with powder," referring to the white slip coating applied to a grey stoneware body beneath a greenish-grey celadon-like glaze.

Techniques

Buncheong decoration employed stamped designs, incising, carving, inlay (sanggam), iron-brown painting, and brushed white slip (bunjang), sometimes leaving the whole vessel coated in slip. Motifs included peonies, lotuses, fish, and cranes, rendered with a spontaneity and humour often contrasted with the refinement of Goryeo celadon, from which buncheong developed.

Decline and legacy

Production waned in the later 16th century as white porcelain (baekja) rose to dominance and the Japanese invasions of 1592–98 devastated kilns; many Korean potters were taken to Japan, where buncheong-derived wares influenced Japanese ceramics such as Mishima ware. Buncheong experienced a celebrated revival among modern Korean studio potters.
