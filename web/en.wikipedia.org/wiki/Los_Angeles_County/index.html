Los Angeles County, officially the County of Los Angeles, is the most populous county in the United States, with about ten million residents in the 2020 census — more than any other county and more than 40 U.S. states. Its county seat and largest city is Los Angeles. The county contains 88 incorporated cities and many unincorporated areas, covering 4,083 square miles (10,570 km²) of land, and it is home to roughly a quarter of California's population.

Geography

The county stretches from the Pacific coast and the Channel Islands of San Clemente and Santa Catalina inland across the Los Angeles Basin, the San Fernando, San Gabriel and Santa Clarita valleys, and over the San Gabriel Mountains into the Antelope Valley portion of the Mojave Desert. Mount San Antonio (Mount Baldy), at 10,064 feet (3,068 m), is its highest point. The Los Angeles River and San Gabriel River drain the county's urban plain.

Government and economy

Los Angeles County is governed by a five-member Board of Supervisors, each representing a district of about two million people, and is one of the largest local governments in the world, with departments covering health services, sheriff, fire, public works and social services, and an annual budget exceeding that of many U.S. states. The county economy is driven by entertainment (Hollywood), international trade through the twin ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach — together the busiest container port complex in the Americas — aerospace, technology, healthcare and tourism.

Demographics

The county is one of the most ethnically diverse in the United States. Hispanic and Latino residents form the largest group (about 48 per cent in 2020), followed by non-Hispanic whites (about 26 per cent), Asian Americans (about 15 per cent) and African Americans (about 8 per cent). More than 200 languages are spoken, and over a third of residents were born outside the United States.
