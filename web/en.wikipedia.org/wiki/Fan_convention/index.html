Fan convention

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A fan convention (also known as a con) is an event at which fans of a particular topic — such as science fiction, comics, anime, gaming, or a television franchise — gather to participate in programmes, panels, exhibitions, and social activities. Fan conventions range from small, volunteer-run gatherings of a few hundred people to commercially produced spectacles drawing more than 100,000 attendees.

History

The first generally recognized science fiction convention was held in Leeds, England, in 1937, and the first World Science Fiction Convention (Worldcon) followed in New York in 1939, where some attendees wore the first recorded convention costumes. As fandom specialised, so did conventions: Star Trek conventions flourished in the 1970s, anime conventions from the 1980s and 1990s, and gaming conventions such as Gen Con grew alongside role-playing games. San Diego Comic-Con, founded in 1970, evolved into the largest popular-culture convention in North America.

Features

Typical features include panel discussions with creators and actors, film and episode screenings, dealers' rooms and artist alleys, gaming halls, autograph and photography sessions, and a masquerade or cosplay contest. Many fan-run conventions are organised by non-profit societies and staffed by volunteers; commercial conventions are produced by companies for profit. Both forms have generated their own traditions, slang ("con crud", "dead dog parties"), and awards ceremonies — most famously the Hugo Awards at Worldcon.
