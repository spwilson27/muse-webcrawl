Croup

Croup, also known as laryngotracheitis, is a viral respiratory infection that causes swelling of the upper airway, principally the larynx and trachea. It is characterised by a distinctive barking cough, a harsh grating sound on breathing in (stridor), hoarseness and varying degrees of respiratory distress. It most commonly affects children between six months and six years of age.

Cause

Croup is most often caused by parainfluenza viruses, especially type 1; other viruses, including influenza and respiratory syncytial virus, can produce the same syndrome. Symptoms typically worsen at night and last for a few days.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually clinical, based on the barking cough and stridor; neck X-rays may show the classic "steeple sign" of subglottic narrowing. Severity is commonly graded with the Westley score. Mild cases are managed at home with reassurance and cool night air. Moderate to severe cases are treated with corticosteroids such as dexamethasone, and nebulised epinephrine for significant stridor at rest. Most children recover fully, and hospital admission is uncommon.
