Tetranychus urticae

Common name: Two-spotted spider mite
Family: Tetranychidae
Genus: Tetranychus
Species: T. urticae Koch, 1836

Tetranychus urticae (common names include red spider mite and two-spotted spider mite) is a species of plant-feeding mite generally considered to be a major pest. It is the best-known member of the family Tetranychidae (spider mites) and has a worldwide distribution, feeding on hundreds of species of plants including many vegetables, fruit crops, ornamentals, and house plants.

Description

Adults are less than a millimetre long, oval, and vary in colour from greenish-yellow to reddish, typically with two dark spots on the body. Females overwinter in a bright orange-red diapause form. The mites feed by piercing plant cells, producing fine stippling and yellowing of leaves, and they spin characteristic silk webbing over infested foliage.

Life cycle

Development is rapid under warm, dry conditions: eggs hatch within a few days, and a generation can be completed in about a week at high temperatures. Females can lay well over a hundred eggs. The species is arrhenotokous — unfertilized eggs develop into males — which, together with its short generation time, contributes to a notorious ability to evolve resistance to acaricides.

Control

Management combines cultural methods, the predatory mite Phytoseiulus persimilis and other biological control agents, and selective chemical acaricides. T. urticae has developed resistance to more pesticides than almost any other arthropod, making integrated pest management essential.
