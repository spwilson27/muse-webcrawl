The Battle of Vesuvius was the first major engagement of the Third Servile War (73–71 BC), in which the escaped gladiator Spartacus and his followers defeated a Roman militia force on the slopes of Mount Vesuvius in southern Italy.

Background

In 73 BC about seventy gladiators escaped from the training school of Lentulus Batiatus at Capua, seizing kitchen implements and then military weapons. Led by Spartacus, Crixus and Oenomaus, the escapees took refuge on Mount Vesuvius, where their numbers swelled with runaway slaves from the surrounding countryside. Rome, not yet treating the revolt as a serious war, sent a militia force of some 3,000 men under the praetor Gaius Claudius Glaber.

The battle

Glaber besieged the rebels on the mountain, blocking the single known path down and expecting hunger to force a surrender. Instead, Spartacus's men fashioned ropes and ladders from wild vines and descended an unguarded cliff face by night, coming up behind the Roman camp. The rebels fell on the unsuspecting militia and routed it, capturing its supplies and equipment.

Consequences

The victory transformed a local slave breakout into a major revolt. Recruits flocked to Spartacus, whose army grew to tens of thousands and defeated several further Roman forces before Rome entrusted the war to Marcus Licinius Crassus, who finally crushed the rebellion in 71 BC.
