Corruption in South Africa

Corruption in South Africa is a persistent and widely documented problem affecting government, state-owned enterprises and the private sector. South Africa scores in the middle range of Transparency International's Corruption Perceptions Index. Public concern over corruption grew sharply during the presidency of Jacob Zuma (2009–2018), a period associated with the phenomenon of "state capture".

State capture

The term "state capture" describes the alleged systematic repurposing of state institutions by the Gupta family and associates of President Zuma to extract rents, influencing appointments and procurement at state-owned enterprises such as Eskom and Transnet. The scandal contributed to Zuma's resignation in February 2018. The Judicial Commission of Inquiry into State Capture, chaired by Raymond Zondo (the "Zondo Commission", 2018–2022), documented widespread corruption and recommended prosecutions and reforms.

Notable cases

  • The Arms Deal (1999), a multi-billion-rand strategic defence procurement dogged by bribery allegations.
  • Electricity utility Eskom's corruption-linked decline, contributing to chronic load shedding.
  • COVID-19 relief and PPE procurement scandals (2020).

Institutions

Anti-corruption bodies include the Public Protector, the National Prosecuting Authority, the Hawks and the Special Investigating Unit.
