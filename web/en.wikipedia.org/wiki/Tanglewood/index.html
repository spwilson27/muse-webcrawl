Tanglewood is a music venue and festival in the Berkshire Hills of western Massachusetts, straddling the towns of Lenox and Stockbridge. It is the summer home of the Boston Symphony Orchestra (BSO) and one of the most famous classical music festivals in the United States, drawing some 350,000 visitors each season.

History

The festival began in 1934 with concerts in the Berkshires; in 1936 the Tappan family donated their Tanglewood estate to the BSO, and the first concerts on the site took place in 1937. After a rainstorm interrupted a performance, funds were raised for the open-sided Music Shed (built 1938, later named for conductor Serge Koussevitzky), which seats about 5,100 with thousands more on the lawn. In 1940 Koussevitzky founded the Berkshire Music Center (now the Tanglewood Music Center) to train advanced young musicians; its alumni include Leonard Bernstein, who was associated with Tanglewood for fifty years.

Venues and programming

The Seiji Ozawa Hall, opened in 1994, hosts chamber and recital performances, and the Linde Center (2019) added year-round learning facilities. The summer season mixes BSO concerts, Tanglewood Music Center orchestral and vocal programs, chamber music and popular-artist concerts, alongside the Festival of Contemporary Music. The name "Tanglewood" derives from Nathaniel Hawthorne's Tanglewood Tales, written in a cottage nearby.
