Marian antiphons

The Marian antiphons are a group of four hymns of the Latin Church addressed to the Virgin Mary, sung at the close of Compline (night prayer) in the Liturgy of the Hours. Each antiphon is assigned to a season of the liturgical year.

The four antiphons

  • Alma Redemptoris Mater — from the first Sunday of Advent until Candlemas (2 February).
  • Ave Regina Caelorum — from Candlemas until the Wednesday of Holy Week.
  • Regina Caeli — during Eastertide, from Holy Saturday until Pentecost.
  • Salve Regina — from the day after Pentecost until the first Sunday of Advent.

History and music

The antiphons date from the eleventh and twelfth centuries; the Salve Regina is traditionally attributed to Hermann of Reichenau. They exist in both solemn and simple chant tones and have inspired countless polyphonic settings by composers of the Renaissance and Baroque. The practice of chanting one of them at the end of Compline spread widely through the influence of the mendicant orders. The chant melodies are also used as the basis of many organ works and motets.
