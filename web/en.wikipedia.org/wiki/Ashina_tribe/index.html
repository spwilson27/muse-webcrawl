Ashina tribe

Ashina (Chinese: 阿史那; pinyin: Āshǐnà)
Origin: Central Asia
Known for: Ruling clan of the Göktürks

The Eurasian steppe, heartland of the early Turkic khaganates ruled by the Ashina.

The Ashina tribe (also spelled Asena or Ašina) was a Turkic tribe and the ruling dynasty of the Göktürks, a nomadic confederation that dominated the Eurasian steppe from the 6th to the 8th centuries AD. Members of the Ashina clan held the title of khagan (emperor) over the First and Second Turkic Khaganates, and the clan's prestige derived in part from a legendary origin story involving descent from a she-wolf — a motif shared with the later Mongols.

Origins and legend

Chinese historical sources, including the Book of Sui and the Zhou Shu, record several accounts of the Ashina's origins. In the most famous legend, the ancestors of the Turks were destroyed by a neighbouring people, leaving only a single boy who survived with the help of a she-wolf; the wolf later bore him ten sons, one of whom was named Ashina and became the clan's eponymous ancestor. The she-wolf (Asena in some retellings) became a totemic symbol of the Turkic peoples.

Historically, the Ashina are thought to have emerged among the subject peoples of the Rouran Khaganate in the Altai Mountains region, where Turkic-speaking groups worked as blacksmiths for their Rouran overlords.

Rise to power

In 552 AD, Bumin Qaghan, a chieftain of the Ashina, overthrew the Rouran and founded the Turkic Khaganate. His brother Istämi ruled the western portion of the empire, and under successors such as Muqan Qaghan the khaganate stretched from Manchuria to the Black Sea, controlling key segments of the Silk Road and dealing diplomatically with both the Byzantine Empire and Sasanian Persia.

Decline and legacy

Internal succession disputes split the khaganate into eastern and western halves in the 580s. Both were eventually subdued by Tang China, though the Ashina-led Second Turkic Khaganate was restored in 682 under Ilterish Qaghan. The khaganate fell in 744 to the Uyghurs. Despite its political decline, the prestige of the Ashina name endured: elements claiming Ashina descent appear among the Khazars and other steppe peoples, and the clan's wolf legend remains central to Turkic national mythology today.
