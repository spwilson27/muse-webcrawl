Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park

Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park (Scottish Gaelic: Pàirc Nàiseanta Loch Laomainn is nan Tròisichean) is a national park in Scotland centred on Loch Lomond and the hills and glens of the Trossachs, together with several other ranges of hills. It was the first of the two national parks established by the Scottish Parliament in 2002, the second being the Cairngorms National Park. The park extends to cover much of the western part of the southern highlands, lying to the north of the Glasgow conurbation, and contains many mountains and glens.

Geography

The park covers 1,865 square kilometres and spans the Highland Boundary Fault, which crosses Loch Lomond and divides the lowland south from the mountainous north. Loch Lomond itself is the largest lake in Great Britain by surface area, at about 71 km², and contains more than 30 islands, including Inchmurrin and Inchcailloch. Ben Lomond (974 m), on the loch's eastern shore, is one of Scotland's most climbed Munros. The Trossachs, to the east, are a compact area of wooded hills and lochs — including Loch Katrine and Loch Venachar — popularised in the 19th century by Sir Walter Scott's poem The Lady of the Lake and his novel Rob Roy.

History

The idea of national parks for Scotland was discussed through the 20th century; the National Parks (Scotland) Act 2000 provided the legal framework, and the park was formally established on 19 July 2002, with the park authority assuming full powers in 2003. In 2017, byelaws extended camping management zones around the loch shores in response to littering and antisocial camping.

Wildlife and conservation

The park includes Atlantic oak woodland, blanket bog, and montane habitats supporting red deer, red squirrel, osprey, and golden eagle. The island of Inchcailloch is noted for its ancient oakwoods. The park authority balances its statutory aims of conservation, visitor experience, and sustainable rural development for the roughly 15,000 people who live within the park boundary.
