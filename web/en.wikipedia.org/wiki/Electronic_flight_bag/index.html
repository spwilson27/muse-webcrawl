Electronic flight bag


A tablet-based electronic flight bag mounted in a cockpit

An electronic flight bag (EFB) is an electronic information management device that helps flight crews perform flight management tasks more easily and efficiently with less paper providing the reference material often found in the pilot's carry-on flight bag, including the flight-crew operating manual, navigational charts, and other documents. In addition, the EFB can host purpose-built software applications to automate other functions normally conducted by hand, such as take-off performance calculations and weight-and-balance computation.

The EFB gets its name from the traditional pilot's flight bag, which typically weighs around 18 kg (40 lb) or more and contains the paper manuals and documentation pilots carry. Replacing the paper with an electronic device saves weight, reduces fuel burn, and allows documentation to be updated electronically rather than by replacing printed pages.

History

Early EFBs appeared in the 1990s when some operators began carrying laptops loaded with documentation. In 2003, the U.S. Federal Aviation Administration issued guidance (Advisory Circular 120-76) for the certification and use of EFBs. The widespread adoption of tablet computers, especially the Apple iPad from 2011 onward, led most major airlines to go "paperless" in the cockpit.

Classes and types

Regulators historically classified EFB hardware as portable or installed, and software applications by criticality: Type A applications present static information with no safety effect if misleading (for example, documentation viewers); Type B applications include dynamic, interactive charting and performance calculations; more demanding applications that replace primary navigation displays require certified installed avionics.

Benefits

Airlines report benefits in fuel savings from reduced weight, faster chart revisions, improved situational awareness through own-ship position display on airport diagrams, and reduced pilot workload.
