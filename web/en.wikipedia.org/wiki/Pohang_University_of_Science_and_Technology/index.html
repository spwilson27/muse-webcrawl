Pohang University of Science and Technology

The Pohang University of Science and Technology (POSTECH; Korean: 포항공과대학교) is a private research university in Pohang, South Korea, dedicated to research and education in science and technology. It was founded in 1986 by the steel company POSCO and its chairman Kim Tae-hyo's successor leadership under founding president Hogil Kim, modelled in part on the California Institute of Technology, with small classes, generous funding per student, and an emphasis on research from the undergraduate level up.

History

POSCO, then one of the world's most efficient steelmakers, established POSTECH to supply Korea with research-trained scientists and engineers. The university admitted its first undergraduates in 1987. It deliberately remained small, with an undergraduate intake of only a few hundred students per year, while building large research facilities, including the Pohang Accelerator Laboratory, whose PLS-II synchrotron light source serves researchers nationwide, and the Asia Pacific Center for Theoretical Physics, headquartered on its campus.

Academics

POSTECH offers undergraduate and graduate programmes through departments including Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Life Sciences, Materials Science and Engineering, Mechanical Engineering, Industrial and Management Engineering, Electrical Engineering, Computer Science and Engineering, Chemical Engineering, Environmental Science and Engineering, and convergence and interdisciplinary programmes. Instruction is bilingual in Korean and English, and a high proportion of graduates continue to doctoral study.

Rankings and reputation

Despite its youth and small size, POSTECH consistently ranks among the top universities in South Korea and in Asia, and it has placed highly in rankings of young universities worldwide — for several years it was ranked first in the Times Higher Education list of universities under 50 years old. Its research output per faculty member is among the highest in Korea, particularly in materials science, physics, and chemistry.

Campus

The compact, residential campus in the Hyoja-dong neighbourhood of Pohang brings laboratories, dormitories, and the Tae-Joon Park digital library together within walking distance; nearly all undergraduates live on campus. The university maintains close ties with POSCO's research institutes and with the nearby industrial complex.
