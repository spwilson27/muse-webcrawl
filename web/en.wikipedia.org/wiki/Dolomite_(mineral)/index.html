Dolomite is an anhydrous carbonate mineral composed of calcium magnesium carbonate, CaMg(CO3)2. It is the principal constituent of the sedimentary rock also historically called dolomite (now often termed dolostone to distinguish rock from mineral) and of metamorphic dolomitic marble. The mineral is named after the French geologist Déodat Gratet de Dolomieu, who first described the rock from the mountains of northern Italy that now bear his name — the Dolomites.

Properties

Dolomite crystallises in the trigonal-rhombohedral system, commonly forming rhombohedral crystals — frequently with curved, saddle-shaped faces — as well as granular and massive aggregates. It is slightly harder than calcite (Mohs 3.5–4 versus 3) and differs chemically in having ordered layers of calcium and magnesium ions between carbonate sheets. Unlike calcite, it reacts only weakly with cold dilute hydrochloric acid unless powdered, a standard field test for distinguishing the two. Iron substitution produces the variety ankerite; manganese and zinc analogues (kutnohorite and minrecordite) exist.

Formation: the dolomite problem

Vast thicknesses of dolostone occur in the geological record, especially in Precambrian and Palaeozoic sequences, yet dolomite rarely precipitates directly from modern seawater in the laboratory at surface conditions — the long-standing "dolomite problem." Most natural dolomite is thought to form by dolomitisation: magnesium-bearing fluids percolating through limestone replace part of its calcium with magnesium, converting calcite or aragonite to dolomite, often with an increase in porosity that makes dolostones important petroleum reservoirs. Proposed fluid sources include evaporated seawater (sabkha and reflux models), mixing zones of fresh and salt water, burial brines, and microbial mediation, which experiments since the 2000s have shown can overcome the kinetic barriers to precipitation.

Uses

Dolomite is quarried as aggregate and dimension stone, used as a flux in iron and steel making, calcined to produce refractory lining and magnesium compounds, and ground as an agricultural amendment ("dolomitic lime") that supplies both calcium and magnesium and neutralises acidic soils. Well-formed crystals from localities such as Eugui in Spain are prized by collectors.
