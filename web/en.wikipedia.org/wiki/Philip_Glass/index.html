Philip Glass

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Philip Glass at the piano in his New York studio in the 2010s, in a still of the composer in his eighties at work]

Philip Glass (born 31 January 1937) is an American composer and pianist, widely regarded as one of the most influential musicians of the late twentieth century. With Steve Reich, La Monte Young and Terry Riley he founded the style labelled minimalism — music of repetition, pulse and slow harmonic change — though Glass prefers 'music with repetitive structures'; his operas Einstein on the Beach (1976, with Robert Wilson), Satyagraha (1980) and Akhnaten (1983) remade opera as ritual spectacle.

Born in Baltimore, Glass studied flute at Peabody, philosophy and mathematics at Chicago, composition at Juilliard and with Nadia Boulanger in Paris, where transcribing Ravi Shankar's music for the film Chappaqua taught him the additive rhythmic processes of his mature style. He drove a New York taxi and worked as a plumber while his ensemble toured the art world.

From lofts to the world

The Philip Glass Ensemble (founded 1968) played galleries and lofts; Einstein on the Beach — five hours, no intermission, images and numbers in place of plot — sold out the Metropolitan Opera in 1976 and left its creators in debt and legendary. Commissions followed from every major house; film scores (Koyaanisqatsi, 1982; The Hours, 2002) carried his sound worldwide.

Later work

Glass has written more than a dozen operas, fourteen symphonies (by recent counts) and a mountain of chamber and piano music, including the Études completed in his seventies; his memoir Words Without Music (2015) tells the taxi-and-loft years drily. Minimalism's journey from downtown scandal to default film-score language is largely his biography writ large.
