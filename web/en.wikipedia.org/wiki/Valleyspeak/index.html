Valleyspeak

RegionSan Fernando Valley, California, United States
EraLate 1970s – 1980s; influence continues
Associated group"Valley girls"

Valleyspeak is the name given to a sociolect associated with the San Fernando Valley in Los Angeles, California, popularised in the early 1980s through the stereotype of the affluent teenage "Valley girl". Its hallmark features include the discourse marker like, quotative be like, emphatic totally and oh my God, expressive interjections such as gag me with a spoon, and a tendency toward uptalk (rising intonation at the ends of statements).

History

The dialect entered national consciousness with Frank and Moon Unit Zappa's 1982 hit song "Valley Girl", which satirised the speech of Sherman Oaks teenagers. Films such as Valley Girl (1983) and later Clueless (1995) fixed the stereotype in popular culture.

Features

Linguists note that many features branded as Valleyspeak — quotative like, uptalk, and discourse like — are part of broader changes in American English, particularly among young speakers, and are also found in the "California Vowel Shift". Though often mocked as ditzy or affected, Valleyspeak features have spread widely into mainstream North American speech.
