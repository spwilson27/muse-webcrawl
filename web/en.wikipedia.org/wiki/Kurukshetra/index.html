Kurukshetra (Sanskrit: कुरुक्षेत्र, "Field of the Kurus") is a city and pilgrimage region in the Indian state of Haryana. In Hindu tradition it is the land where the Kurukshetra War of the Mahabharata was fought between the Kauravas and the Pandavas, and where, before the battle, Krishna delivered the Bhagavad Gita to Arjuna. The wider sacred region is traditionally described as lying between the Sarasvati and Drishadvati rivers.

Tradition and history

Kurukshetra is named after King Kuru, ancestor of the Kuru clan, who is said to have performed asceticism and sown the "field of dharma" here. The Vedic Kuru kingdom centred on this region in the early first millennium BC, and the Brahmana texts praise the land as the sacrificial ground of the gods. The nearby town of Thanesar (Sthaneshvara) was an important early medieval capital under the Pushyabhuti dynasty of Harsha in the 7th century AD, described by the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang. Mughal-era and later structures, including the tomb of the Sufi saint Sheikh Chilli, dot the area.

Pilgrimage

The sacred circuit (48 kos parikrama) encompasses hundreds of tirthas; the most important are the Brahma Sarovar and Sannihit Sarovar tanks, where pilgrims bathe especially during solar eclipses — eclipse fairs at Kurukshetra draw enormous crowds — and at Jyotisar, revered as the spot of the Gita's delivery, marked by a great banyan tree and a marble chariot depicting Krishna and Arjuna. The annual International Gita Mahotsav festival celebrates the Gita's anniversary with recitations, fairs, and cultural events.

The modern district

Kurukshetra district is an agricultural region of Haryana with Kurukshetra University (founded 1956), the National Institute of Technology Kurukshetra, and a network of museums interpreting the Mahabharata, science, and regional history. The city lies on National Highway 44 (the historic Grand Trunk Road) north of Delhi.
