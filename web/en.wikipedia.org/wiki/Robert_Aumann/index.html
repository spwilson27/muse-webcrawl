Robert John Aumann (born 8 June 1930) is an Israeli-American mathematician and economist known for his foundational work in game theory. He is a professor emeritus at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem. In 2005 he shared the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Thomas Schelling "for having enhanced our understanding of conflict and cooperation through game-theory analysis."

Life

Aumann was born in Frankfurt am Main, Germany, to an Orthodox Jewish family that fled Nazi Germany for the United States in 1938. He grew up in New York City, studied mathematics at the City College of New York, and earned his doctorate from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1955 with a thesis on knot theory. After postdoctoral work at Princeton, he moved to Israel in 1956 and joined the mathematics department of the Hebrew University, where he spent his career.

Contributions

Aumann made major contributions to the theory of repeated games, showing how cooperation can emerge in long-run interactions (related to the folk theorem), and to the formalisation of common knowledge — his 1976 paper "Agreeing to Disagree" showed that rational agents with common priors cannot commonly know that they disagree. He also contributed to cooperative game theory, the theory of the core and value (including the Aumann–Shapley value), correlated equilibrium, and the mathematical foundations of decision under uncertainty. His two-volume collected works reflect a career spanning pure mathematics and economics.
