Michel Lambert

Bornc. 1610, Champigny-sur-Veude, France
DiedJune 29, 1696, Paris, France
OccupationSinger, theorbist, composer
EraBaroque

Michel Lambert (c. 1610 – June 29, 1696) was a French singer, theorbist and composer of the Baroque era, celebrated in his lifetime as the foremost French master of the air de cour.

Career

Lambert sang as a boy in the chapel of Gaston d'Orléans and came to Paris under the protection of the singer Pierre de Nyert. He became a musician in the household of Madame de Choisy and, around 1661, was appointed master of music of the King's Chamber under Louis XIV, sharing court musical duties with his son-in-law Jean-Baptiste Lully, who had married Lambert's daughter Madeleine in 1662. Lambert was famous as a singing teacher and as a performer who accompanied himself on the theorbo; his salons were centres of Parisian musical life.

Works

Lambert composed several hundred airs de cour and airs sérieux for one to three voices with basso continuo, many published in the collections of the Ballard firm. His settings of poets such as Benserade and Quinault were admired for their elegant melodic lines, expressive treatment of the text and refined ornamentation, and his airs circulated across Europe. Few of his songs were printed during his lifetime relative to the number he wrote, but modern recordings have revived his reputation as Lully's great counterpart in the intimate vocal music of the French court.
