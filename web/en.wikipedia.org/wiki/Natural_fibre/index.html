Natural fibre

Natural fibres or natural fibers (see spelling differences) are fibres that are produced by geological processes, or from the bodies of plants or animals. They can be used as a component of composite materials, where the orientation of fibres affects the properties. Natural fibres can also be matted into sheets to make paper or felt.

Plant fibres

Plant fibres include seed hairs, such as cotton; stem (bast) fibres, such as flax and hemp; leaf fibres, such as sisal; and husk fibres, such as coconut coir. Cotton is the most widely used natural fibre for clothing.

Animal fibres

Animal fibres consist largely of particular proteins. Examples include silk, produced by the larvae of the mulberry silkworm; wool, from sheep and other mammals; cashmere and mohair from goats; and angora from rabbits.

Mineral fibres

Mineral fibres include asbestos, a group of naturally occurring silicate minerals once widely used for insulation, now restricted in many countries because of its serious health hazards.

Properties and uses

Compared with synthetic fibres, natural fibres are generally biodegradable and renewable, and are valued for comfort and breathability in textiles. Their disadvantages include variability in quality, lower resistance to moisture in some cases, and susceptibility to insect and microbial attack. Natural fibres are also increasingly used in biocomposites for automotive and construction applications.
