Banks Lake

Banks Lake is a reservoir in Grant and Douglas counties in central Washington state, United States. It fills the northern part of the Grand Coulee, a dry coulee carved by Ice Age floods, and stretches about 43 kilometres (27 mi) between Grand Coulee Dam at its north end and Dry Falls Dam near Coulee City at the south. The lake is named for Frank A. Banks, the construction engineer who supervised the building of Grand Coulee Dam.

Columbia Basin Project

Banks Lake is the equalising reservoir of the Columbia Basin Project. Water is lifted about 85 metres (280 ft) from Franklin D. Roosevelt Lake behind Grand Coulee Dam by a pumping plant into a feeder canal that discharges into Banks Lake; from Dry Falls Dam the water enters the Main Canal to irrigate roughly 670,000 acres of farmland across the Columbia Basin. A pump-generating plant allows water to be pumped back during high demand to generate power.

Recreation and ecology

The lake, with about 91 miles of shoreline, supports fishing for walleye, bass, kokanee and rainbow trout, and boating and camping centred on Steamboat Rock State Park, whose 600-foot basalt butte rises from the lake. The surrounding shrub-steppe and wetlands provide habitat for waterfowl and other wildlife.
