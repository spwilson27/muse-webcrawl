Motor glider

A motor glider (or motorized glider) is a fixed-wing aircraft that can be flown under engine power like a conventional airplane or with the engine shut down like a glider. Motor gliders combine the ability to self-launch and cruise under power with the soaring efficiency of long, glider-like wings.

Types

Touring motor gliders have permanently installed engines and propellers and can take off and climb under their own power, resembling light aircraft with long wings. Self-launching sailplanes are high-performance gliders whose engine — often a retractable mast-mounted unit — is used mainly for takeoff and for sustaining flight to avoid outlandings; in pure gliding configuration the engine is stowed to minimize drag. Self-sustaining gliders have smaller engines sufficient only to maintain altitude in weak lift.

Characteristics

Typical engines range from about 40 to 120 horsepower; electric propulsion has become increasingly common. Propellers may be fixed, folding, or fully retractable. Many modern designs are built from composite materials. In many jurisdictions motor gliders can be flown with a glider pilot license with a motor glider endorsement, making them a popular, economical form of recreational flying and cross-country soaring.
