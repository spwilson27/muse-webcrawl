Canadian North

Canadian North is an airline headquartered in Yellowknife, Northwest Territories, Canada. Wholly owned by Inuit organizations, it operates scheduled passenger and cargo services throughout the Northwest Territories and Nunavut, along with charter services across Canada and beyond. It is one of the principal carriers serving Canada's Arctic communities, many of which have no road access and depend on air transport for food, mail, and medical travel.

History

The airline traces its origins to Air Norterra, formed in 1998 from the northern operations of Canadian Airlines. It adopted the Canadian North name in 2003. Ownership passed fully to Inuit organizations: the Inuvialuit Development Corporation, representing the Inuvialuit of the western Arctic, and Makivik Corporation, representing the Inuit of Nunavik in northern Quebec.

In 2019 Canadian North merged with First Air, the other major northern carrier owned by Makivik, following federal approval. The combined airline operated under the Canadian North name, integrating routes, fleets, and loyalty programs while committing to maintain service levels and northern employment.

Operations

Canadian North serves more than two dozen communities in the Northwest Territories and Nunavut, with southern gateways including Edmonton, Ottawa, and Montreal. Its fleet of Boeing 737s — including gravel-kit-equipped 737-200 combis historically used on unpaved northern strips — and ATR 42 turboprops carries passengers, fresh food under the Nutrition North program, freight, and charter traffic for resource projects.

The airline is a significant northern employer and offers training initiatives for Inuit pilots and maintenance staff.
