Methane emissions are the release of methane (CH4) into the Earth's atmosphere. Increasing methane emissions are a major contributor to the rising concentration of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere and are responsible for up to one-third of near-term global heating. Methane is a potent but relatively short-lived greenhouse gas: over a 100-year period its global warming potential is about 28 times that of carbon dioxide, and over 20 years more than 80 times.

Global methane emissions approached 580 million tonnes per year in the late 2010s, of which roughly 60% were anthropogenic. The largest human-caused sources are agriculture, fossil fuels, and waste.

Sources

Natural sources

Natural methane sources are dominated by wetlands, which account for roughly 20–40% of total global emissions. Other natural sources include termites, oceans, geological seeps, and methane hydrates.

Agriculture

Agriculture is the largest anthropogenic source. Enteric fermentation in ruminant livestock such as cattle and sheep produces methane as a by-product of digestion, released mainly through belching. Manure management, rice cultivation in flooded paddies, and the burning of agricultural residues also contribute.

Fossil fuels

The extraction, processing, and transport of coal, oil, and natural gas releases methane through venting, incomplete flaring, and fugitive leaks. Coal mining releases methane trapped in coal seams, while oil and gas systems leak at wells, pipelines, and processing plants. Satellite monitoring has revealed large "super-emitter" events from this sector.

Waste

Landfills and wastewater treatment generate methane as organic matter decomposes under anaerobic conditions.

Mitigation

Because methane is short-lived, cutting emissions produces rapid climate benefits. Measures include plugging leaking wells, capturing landfill gas, improved manure management, alternate wetting and drying in rice paddies, and feed additives for livestock. The Global Methane Pledge, launched at COP26 in 2021, commits signatories to reduce collective anthropogenic methane emissions by at least 30% from 2020 levels by 2030.
