Peter Ozsváth

BornOctober 20, 1967, Budapest, Hungary
OccupationMathematician
InstitutionsPrinceton University

Peter Steven Ozsváth (born October 20, 1967) is a Hungarian-born American mathematician, a professor at Princeton University, best known for creating, together with Zoltán Szabó, the Heegaard Floer homology, a powerful invariant in low-dimensional topology.

Career

Ozsváth earned his PhD from Princeton University in 1994 under John Morgan. He held positions at Caltech, the University of California, Berkeley, and Columbia University, where he was professor of mathematics, before returning to Princeton as a professor in 2015. With Szabó he introduced Heegaard Floer homology in the early 2000s, a theory that revolutionised the study of three- and four-dimensional manifolds and knot theory, providing tools that resolved longstanding conjectures and reframed Donaldson and Seiberg–Witten theory.

Honours

Together with Szabó, Ozsváth received the Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry in 2007, one of the highest honours in the field, for their work on Heegaard diagrams and Floer homology. He was also a co-author, with Szabó and Dylan Thurston, of foundational work on grid diagrams and knot Floer homology, and with Robert Lipshitz and Thurston on bordered Floer homology.
