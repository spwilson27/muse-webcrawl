Zoopraxiscope

The zoopraxiscope (initially named zoographiscope and zoogyriscope) is an early device for displaying moving images, considered a precursor of the movie projector. It was conceived by the photographic pioneer Eadweard Muybridge in 1879 and used publicly from 1880.

Design

The device projected images from rotating glass discs in rapid succession, creating the impression of motion. The images on the discs were derived from Muybridge's celebrated sequential photographs of animals and people in motion, though for projection they were often painted in silhouette or adapted by hand, distorting the photographic originals.

Legacy

Muybridge lectured with the zoopraxiscope across America and Europe, demonstrating his studies of animal locomotion — famously including the galloping horse whose legs all leave the ground — and the device is regarded as an important step in the prehistory of cinema.
