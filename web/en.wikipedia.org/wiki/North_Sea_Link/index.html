North Sea Link

North Sea Link (Norwegian: Nordsjøkabelen) is a 1,400 MW high-voltage direct current (HVDC) submarine power cable between Kvilldal in Norway and Blyth in Northumberland, England. At 720 kilometres (450 miles), it was the world's longest subsea power interconnector when it began commercial operation on 1 October 2021. The project was developed jointly by the British grid operator National Grid and the Norwegian transmission system operator Statnett.

Route and technology

The cable runs from a converter station at Kvilldal, near the head of Hylsfjorden in Rogaland, through the fjord and across the North Sea to Cambois beach near Blyth, where a second converter station connects to the British grid. It operates at ±525 kV using voltage-source converters, allowing power to flow in either direction.

Purpose

The link allows the two countries to trade electricity: Norway can export surplus hydropower when British wind generation is low, and Britain can export surplus wind power to Norway, where it can be stored effectively by holding back water in hydro reservoirs. It is part of a wider network of interconnectors linking the British grid to continental Europe.
