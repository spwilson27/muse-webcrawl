3200 Phaethon is an Apollo asteroid with an orbit that brings it closer to the Sun than any other named asteroid. It was discovered in 1983 in data from the IRAS satellite — the first asteroid found by a spacecraft — and named after Phaethon, son of the Greek sun god Helios. Phaethon is the parent body of the Geminid meteor shower.

Characteristics

Phaethon measures about 5.8 km across and rotates every 3.6 hours. Despite being classed as an asteroid, it behaves in some respects like a comet: it brightens and sheds dust near perihelion, where its surface temperature reaches roughly 800 °C, a phenomenon attributed to thermal fracturing and desiccation cracking, leading to its description as a "rock comet". It has an unusually blue colour, similar to the main-belt asteroid Pallas, from which it may have originated.

Geminids

The Geminid meteor shower, peaking each December, follows Phaethon's orbit, making it one of only two major showers associated with an asteroid rather than a comet.

Exploration

Phaethon made a close approach to Earth in December 2017 and was imaged by radar. JAXA's DESTINY+ mission is planned to fly by Phaethon to study its dust environment.
