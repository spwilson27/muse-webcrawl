George William Norris

Born: 11 July 1861, York Township, Ohio

Died: 2 September 1944, McCook, Nebraska

U.S. Senator from Nebraska (1913–1943)

U.S. Representative (1903–1913)

Party: Republican; later Independent

George William Norris (11 July 1861 – 2 September 1944) was an American politician from Nebraska who served in the United States House of Representatives from 1903 to 1913 and in the United States Senate from 1913 to 1943. A leading progressive, he is best known as a sponsor of the Tennessee Valley Authority.

Career

In the House, Norris led the 1910 revolt against Speaker Joseph Cannon that weakened the Speaker's control over the Rules Committee. In the Senate he opposed American entry into the First World War, championed public power, and co-sponsored the Norris–La Guardia Act of 1932, which restricted the use of injunctions in labour disputes.

Tennessee Valley Authority and other reforms

Norris's long campaign for public development of hydroelectric power at Muscle Shoals culminated in the creation of the Tennessee Valley Authority in 1933. He also authored the Twentieth Amendment to the United States Constitution, which moved the start of presidential and congressional terms (the "lame duck" amendment), and successfully campaigned for Nebraska's unicameral legislature, adopted in 1934.

Legacy

Norris was named one of the "Profiles in Courage" senators in John F. Kennedy's book of that name, and is remembered as a model of principled independence in Congress.
