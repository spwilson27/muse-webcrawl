Disaster of Yongjia

The Disaster of Yongjia (Chinese: 永嘉之亂) refers to events in 311 AD when Han-Zhao forces, associated with the Five Barbarians, captured and sacked Luoyang, the capital of the Western Jin dynasty. Emperor Huai of Jin was captured, and large numbers of officials, soldiers and civilians were killed.

Disaster of Yongjia
Date: 311 AD
Location: Luoyang
Result: Fall of Luoyang; capture of Emperor Huai

Background

The Jin dynasty had been weakened by the War of the Eight Princes (291–306), a devastating civil war among Sima princes. Meanwhile Wu Hu peoples who had settled in northern China rose in revolt; Liu Yuan founded Han-Zhao in 304 and proclaimed himself emperor.

The sack of Luoyang

In the fifth year of the Yongjia era (311), Han-Zhao generals including Liu Yao, Wang Mi and Huyan Yan advanced on Luoyang. Jin defences, starved and undermanned, collapsed. The invaders looted the city, burned palaces and tombs, and captured Emperor Huai, who was taken to Pingyang and later executed in 313 after being humiliated — famously made to serve wine at a banquet.

Aftermath

The disaster marked the effective end of Jin control in the north. Chang'an fell in 316 and Emperor Min was also captured, ending Western Jin. Sima Rui re-established the dynasty in the south as Eastern Jin in 318, ruling from Jiankang. Northern China entered the Sixteen Kingdoms period, while waves of aristocratic and common refugees migrated south across the Yangtze, transforming the demography and culture of southern China.
