Albemarle Sound is a large estuary on the coast of north-eastern North Carolina, one of the extensive drowned-river-valley sounds enclosed by the Outer Banks. It extends roughly 90 kilometres from east to west and is fed mainly by the Roanoke and Chowan rivers. Though called a sound, it has no direct opening to the Atlantic Ocean; its waters reach the sea through neighbouring Pamlico Sound and Oregon and Hatteras inlets, so it is predominantly fresh to brackish.

History

The shores of the sound were among the first areas of North America explored and settled by the English in the 1580s, when the Roanoke Colony was founded on Roanoke Island at its eastern end. The surrounding Albemarle region was the cradle of colonial North Carolina, and Edenton, on the sound's northern shore, served as an early colonial capital. The sound takes its name from George Monck, 1st Duke of Albemarle, one of the Lords Proprietors of Carolina.

Ecology and use

The sound supports important commercial and recreational fisheries, including river herring, striped bass (rockfish) and blue crabs, though it has suffered from nutrient pollution and algal blooms. It is crossed by long causeway bridges, including the Albemarle Sound Bridge, one of the longest in North Carolina.
