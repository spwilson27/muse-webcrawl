Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud (born 31 December 1935) is King of Saudi Arabia, reigning since 23 January 2015. A member of the House of Saud, he is a son of the kingdom's founder, Ibn Saud, and served as governor of Riyadh Province for nearly five decades before becoming crown prince in 2012.

Early life

Salman was born in Riyadh on 31 December 1935, the 25th son of Ibn Saud. His mother was Hussa bint Ahmed Al Sudairi, making him one of the so-called "Sudairi Seven", the influential full brothers who included the future King Fahd and Crown Prince Nayef. He was educated at the Princes' School in Riyadh.

Governor of Riyadh

Salman was appointed deputy governor of Riyadh Province in 1954 and became governor in 1963, a post he held until 2011. During his long tenure, Riyadh grew from a mid-sized desert town into one of the fastest-growing metropolitan areas in the Arab world. He was credited with attracting investment and tourism to the region and with managing the complex tribal and royal politics of the province.

Minister of defence and crown prince

In November 2011 Salman was appointed minister of defence following the death of his brother, Crown Prince Sultan. After Crown Prince Nayef died in June 2012, Salman was named crown prince and deputy prime minister, placing him first in line to the throne.

Reign

Salman acceded to the throne on 23 January 2015 on the death of his half-brother, King Abdullah. Early in his reign he launched a major government restructuring and, in March 2015, led a military intervention in Yemen. In June 2017 he removed his nephew Muhammad bin Nayef as crown prince and appointed his own son, Mohammed bin Salman, in his place — a significant break with precedent that positioned the younger prince as the kingdom's de facto day-to-day ruler and driver of the Saudi Vision 2030 reform programme.

Personal life

Salman has been married three times and has at least thirteen children. His sons include Prince Sultan, the first Arab and Muslim to fly in space, and Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman.
