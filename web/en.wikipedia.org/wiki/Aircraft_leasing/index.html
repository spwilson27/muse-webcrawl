Aircraft leasing is a form of financing in which airlines acquire the use of aircraft without buying them outright, instead renting them from specialist leasing companies (lessors). Leasing allows airlines to operate modern fleets with less capital, adjust capacity flexibly, and manage residual-value risk. Around half of the world's commercial airliner fleet is leased, and the largest lessors own more aircraft than the largest airlines.

Types of lease

In a dry lease, only the aircraft is provided; the airline supplies crew, maintenance and insurance and operates the aircraft under its own air operator's certificate. In a wet lease (ACMI: aircraft, crew, maintenance, insurance), the lessor operates the aircraft on the lessee's routes under the lessor's certificate — used for short-term capacity cover. A damp lease is a wet lease without cabin crew. Finance leases and operating leases differ in accounting and ownership terms: an operating lease is shorter and the aircraft returns to the lessor, while a finance lease effectively spreads the purchase cost over much of the aircraft's life.

Industry

The industry was pioneered by companies such as ILFC (founded 1973) and GECAS, and consolidated into giants such as AerCap — which acquired ILFC and later GECAS — alongside Avolon, SMBC Aviation Capital and BOC Aviation. Ireland became the dominant leasing centre thanks to its tax regime and treaty network, and a large share of the world's leased fleet is Irish-registered or managed from Dublin. A common transaction is the sale-and-leaseback, in which an airline sells aircraft to a lessor and leases them straight back.
