Sarah Grand

Sarah Grand (10 June 1854 – 12 May 1943), born Frances Elizabeth Bellenden Clarke, was an Irish-born British novelist and feminist campaigner. She is credited with coining the term "New Woman" in an 1894 essay exchange, shaping debates about women's roles in the late Victorian period.

Life and work

Married young to an army surgeon, she travelled in the Far East before leaving the marriage and settling in London to write. Her bestselling novel The Heavenly Twins (1893) attacked the sexual double standard and the dangers of syphilis, and was a publishing sensation. Other works include Ideala (1888) and The Beth Book (1897), a semi-autobiographical novel of female development.

Activism

Grand campaigned for women's suffrage and social purity reform, and served as president of a branch of the National Union of Women's Suffrage Societies. She later lived in Bath, where she was mayoress during the Second World War period, dying there in 1943.
