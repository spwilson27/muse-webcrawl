Ice cream parlor
TypeDessert restaurant
ProductsIce cream, sundaes, sodas, milkshakes

An ice cream parlor (American English) or ice cream parlour (British English) is a restaurant or shop that primarily serves ice cream and other frozen desserts, such as sundaes, banana splits, milkshakes, and ice cream sodas. Parlors traditionally feature a counter with swivel stools, soda fountains, and glass display freezers of many flavours.

History

Ice cream was sold in cafés in Paris and London from the late seventeenth century, but the dedicated parlor emerged in the United States in the nineteenth century. The classic soda fountain era peaked in the early-to-mid twentieth century, when parlors — often attached to drugstores — became central social spaces in American towns. During Prohibition, some former saloons and breweries converted to ice cream, further popularising the format.

Chains such as Baskin-Robbins, founded in 1945 and famous for its "31 flavors," and regional institutions like the parlors of Friendly's and Farrell's, spread the type widely. The modern "scoop shop" and gelateria continue the tradition, often emphasising small-batch or artisanal production.
