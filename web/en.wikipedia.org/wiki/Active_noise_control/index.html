Active noise control

Active noise control (ANC), also known as noise cancellation or active noise reduction, is a method for reducing unwanted sound by adding a second sound specifically designed to cancel the first. A microphone picks up the unwanted noise, electronics generate an "anti-noise" signal with the same amplitude but inverted phase, and a loudspeaker emits it; the two waves interfere destructively, reducing the perceived noise.

Principle and applications

The principle of superposition behind ANC was described in a 1936 patent by Paul Lueg, but practical systems awaited modern digital signal processing. ANC is most effective for low-frequency, repetitive sounds. It is widely used in noise-cancelling headphones, aircraft cabins, vehicle interiors, industrial ducting and transformer enclosures. Systems may be feedforward (using a reference microphone), feedback (using an error microphone), or hybrid combinations of both.
