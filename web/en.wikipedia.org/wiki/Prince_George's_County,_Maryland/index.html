Prince George's County, Maryland

County seat: Upper Marlboro
Largest city: Bowie
Founded: April 23, 1696
Named for: Prince George of Denmark
Population: about 967,000 (2020)

Prince George's County is a county located in the U.S. state of Maryland, bordering the eastern portion of Washington, D.C. As of the 2020 census its population was about 967,000, making it the second-most populous county in Maryland. Its county seat is Upper Marlboro, and its largest city is Bowie.

History

The county was created in 1696 from portions of Calvert and Charles counties and named for Prince George of Denmark, husband of Princess Anne (later Queen Anne). Once a tobacco-growing region dependent on enslaved labour, the county suburbanised rapidly after the Second World War as part of the Washington metropolitan area.

Geography and communities

The county includes College Park, home of the University of Maryland's flagship campus, Greenbelt, one of the New Deal-era planned "greenbelt towns", Hyattsville, Laurel, and National Harbor on the Potomac River. Major federal facilities include NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center, the USDA's Beltsville Agricultural Research Center, and Andrews Air Force Base (Joint Base Andrews).

Demographics

Prince George's County is notable as one of the most affluent majority African American counties in the United States, with a majority-Black population since the 1990s and growing Hispanic and immigrant communities.
