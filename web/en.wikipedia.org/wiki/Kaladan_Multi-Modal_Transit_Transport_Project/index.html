Kaladan Multi-Modal Transit Transport Project

The Kaladan Multi-Modal Transit Transport Project is a transport corridor being developed by India in Myanmar to connect the seaport of Kolkata with India's landlocked northeastern states through Myanmar, without relying solely on the narrow Siliguri Corridor. It combines sea, river and road legs along the Kaladan River.

Route

The project has three main components:

  • Sea leg: shipping from Kolkata to a deep-water port built by India at Sittwe on Myanmar's Rakhine coast, a distance of about 539 km.
  • River leg: an inland waterway of about 158 km up the Kaladan River from Sittwe to Paletwa in Chin State, served by a river terminal and dredging of the channel.
  • Road leg: a highway of about 110 km from Paletwa to Zorinpui on the India–Myanmar border, connecting onward to Lawngtlai in Mizoram and the Indian national highway network.

History

India and Myanmar signed a framework agreement in 2008. The Sittwe port was completed and received its first cargo vessel in May 2023. The road leg through Chin and Rakhine states, however, was delayed for years by difficult terrain, funding issues and conflict in Myanmar, including fighting involving the Arakan Army, leaving the corridor only partly operational.

Significance

The project is a centrepiece of India's "Act East" policy and aims to cut the transport distance between Kolkata and Mizoram roughly in half, boost trade with Myanmar, and develop the northeast region.
