Castella (Japanese: カステラ, kasutera) is a popular Japanese sponge cake made of sugar, flour, eggs and starch syrup (mizuame). It is a specialty of Nagasaki, where it was introduced by Portuguese traders and missionaries in the 16th century.

History

The cake derives from pão de Castela ("bread from Castile") brought by the Portuguese during the Nanban trade period. When Japan closed itself to most foreign contact under the Tokugawa shogunate, Nagasaki remained the sole trading port, and local confectioners refined the cake, using mizuame for a moist, dense texture. Castella became one of the classic meibutsu (famed local products) bought as a souvenir (omiyage).

Characteristics and varieties

Traditional Nagasaki castella is baked in wooden frames in long rectangular loaves, with a golden-brown crust and a pale yellow, springy, finely textured crumb; the bottom often carries a sprinkle of coarse zarame sugar. Varieties include matcha, chocolate and cheese castella, and the cake spread throughout East Asia, notably to Taiwan, where softer, soufflé-like versions became popular. Famous makers include Fukusaya (founded 1624) and Bunmeido.
