Brake pad

Brake pads are a component of disc brakes used in automotive and other applications. Brake pads are composed of a steel backing plate with friction material bound to the surface that faces the disc brake rotor. When the brakes are applied, the pads are pressed against the spinning rotor, converting kinetic energy into thermal energy through friction and slowing the vehicle.

Materials

Pad friction materials are commonly classed as organic (non-asbestos organic), semi-metallic, or ceramic. Semi-metallic pads contain metal fibers for strength and heat conduction; ceramic pads use ceramic fibers and copper, offering quieter operation and less dust. Historically, pads used asbestos, which has been phased out for health reasons.

Maintenance

Brake pads are a wear item and require periodic inspection and replacement. Many pads include a metal wear indicator that produces a squealing warning when the friction material is worn down.
