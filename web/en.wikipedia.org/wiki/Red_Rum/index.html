Red Rum

Red Rum (3 May 1965 – 18 October 1995) was an Irish champion Thoroughbred steeplechaser famous for winning the Grand National at Aintree three times — in 1973, 1974, and 1977 — a feat unmatched in the race's history. His name is "murder" spelled backwards.

Career

Bred in Ireland and initially unsuccessful on the flat, Red Rum was bought for trainer Ginger McCain, who famously trained him on the sands at Southport beach — the sea water helping a chronic foot condition (pedal osteitis). In the 1973 Grand National, ridden by Brian Fletcher, he hunted down the long-time leader Crisp in one of the race's most famous finishes. He won again in 1974, also winning the Scottish Grand National the same year, finished second in 1975 and 1976, and in 1977, at age twelve and ridden by Tommy Stack, he won by 25 lengths, cementing his status as a national hero.

Retirement and legacy

Red Rum was retired in 1978 after a hairline fracture discovered days before a planned sixth National appearance. He became a celebrity guest, switching on Blackpool illuminations and appearing at openings, and on his death in 1995 was buried beside the winning post at Aintree. Polls have repeatedly voted him the best-known racehorse in Britain.
