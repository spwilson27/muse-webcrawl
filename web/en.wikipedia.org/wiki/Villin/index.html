Villin

Villin is a 92.5 kDa tissue-specific actin-binding protein associated with the actin core bundle in the microvilli that make up the brush border of vertebrate intestinal and kidney epithelial cells. It is encoded by the VIL1 gene in humans.

Structure

Villin is composed of two major domains: a large N-terminal core composed of six homologous repeats similar to those found in gelsolin and severin, and a small C-terminal "headpiece" domain of about 8.5 kDa. The headpiece is a small, independently folding three-helix bundle that has become a popular model system for studying fast protein folding, because it folds in microseconds.

Function

Depending on calcium concentration, villin can bundle, cap, nucleate, or sever actin filaments. At low Ca2+ concentrations it bundles filaments into the parallel arrays of the microvillus core; at high Ca2+ it severs and caps them. This calcium-regulated activity is thought to contribute to the assembly and dynamic remodeling of the brush border.

Clinical significance

Because villin expression is largely restricted to gastrointestinal and renal epithelia, immunohistochemical staining for villin is used in diagnostic pathology to help identify adenocarcinomas of gastrointestinal origin among metastatic tumors of unknown primary site.
