Sun Quan (Chinese: 孫權; 182–252), courtesy name Zhongmou, was the founder of the state of Eastern Wu during the Three Kingdoms period of China. The second son of the warlord Sun Jian and younger brother of Sun Ce, he inherited control of the Jiangdong territories south of the Yangtze River in 200 AD at the age of eighteen and ruled the region for over fifty years, formally declaring himself emperor in 229.

Early rule and Red Cliffs

Advised by capable officials including Zhou Yu, Zhang Zhao and Lu Su, Sun Quan consolidated his brother's conquests and repelled incursions from the north. In the winter of 208–209, allied with Liu Bei, his forces won the decisive Battle of Red Cliffs against the far larger army of the northern warlord Cao Cao, ensuring the division of China between north and south. His later seizure of Jing Province and the execution of Liu Bei's general Guan Yu in 219 broke the alliance, and in 222 his forces defeated Liu Bei at the Battle of Yiling.

Reign as emperor

Sun Quan declared himself King of Wu in 222 and Emperor of Wu in 229, establishing his capital at Jianye (modern Nanjing). His long reign saw the development of the south's economy, the growth of maritime trade with Southeast Asia and the shipment of naval expeditions as far as Taiwan and Manchuria. His later years were marred by a bitter succession struggle between his sons Sun He and Sun Ba, which consumed the court and ended with Sun He deposed, Sun Ba forced to suicide, and the child Sun Liang installed as heir.

Legacy

Sun Quan was praised by contemporaries and later historians for his shrewd judgment of character and his ability to delegate to talented commanders such as Zhou Yu, Lü Meng and Lu Xun. Cao Cao is famously said to have remarked, "If you have a son, he should be like Sun Zhongmou."
