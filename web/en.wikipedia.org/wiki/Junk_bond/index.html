Junk bond

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: high-yield bond market chart]

A junk bond, formally a high-yield bond or speculative-grade bond, is a bond rated below investment grade by credit rating agencies. These bonds carry a higher risk of default than investment-grade bonds but offer higher interest rates (yields) to compensate investors.

Ratings

Bonds rated Ba1/BB+ or below by Moody's and Standard & Poor's are considered speculative grade. Issuers include companies with leveraged balance sheets, emerging firms without long track records, and "fallen angels" — once investment-grade companies that have been downgraded.

History

The modern high-yield market expanded dramatically in the 1980s, driven by Michael Milken at Drexel Burnham Lambert, and financed many leveraged buyouts and corporate takeovers. The market suffered a collapse in 1989–1990 amid the savings and loan crisis and Milken's prosecution, but later recovered and became a mainstream asset class.
