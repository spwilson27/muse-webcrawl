Michael Bay

Born: Michael Benjamin Bay, 17 February 1965, Los Angeles, California, U.S.

Occupations: Film director, producer

Years active: 1986–present

Michael Benjamin Bay (born February 17, 1965) is an American film director and producer. He is best known for making big-budget, high-concept action films characterized by fast cutting, stylistic cinematography, extensive use of special effects, and explosions — a style sometimes referred to as "Bayhem". His films, which include Bad Boys (1995), The Rock (1996), Armageddon (1998), Pearl Harbor (2001), and the first five entries of the Transformers film series, have grossed over US$7.8 billion worldwide, making him one of the most commercially successful directors in history.

Early life and career

Bay was born in Los Angeles and raised by adoptive parents. He became interested in filmmaking after an internship at Lucasfilm, where he filed storyboards for Raiders of the Lost Ark. He graduated from Wesleyan University in 1986 and began directing music videos and commercials through Propaganda Films, winning Clio Awards and directing the famous "Aaron Burr" installment of the Got Milk? advertising campaign. Jerry Bruckheimer and Don Simpson gave him his feature debut with Bad Boys.

Directing career

Armageddon was the highest-grossing film of 1998. Pearl Harbor (2001) was a major commercial success despite negative reviews. Bay directed The Island (2005), a rare box-office disappointment, before launching the Transformers franchise (2007–2017), whose five films earned over $4.3 billion. His later directorial works include the dark comedy Pain & Gain (2013), the war film 13 Hours: The Secret Soldiers of Benghazi (2016), the Netflix action film 6 Underground (2019), and Ambulance (2022).

Producing

Bay co-founded the production company Platinum Dunes in 2001, which produced horror remakes including The Texas Chainsaw Massacre (2003), The Amityville Horror (2005), Friday the 13th (2009), A Nightmare on Elm Street (2010), and the original A Quiet Place (2018). He has also produced the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles films and television series including Black Sails and The Last Ship.

Style and reception

Critics frequently fault Bay's films for spectacle over story, rapid editing, and product placement, but his technical craft — particularly his preference for practical explosions and in-camera effects — is widely acknowledged. The term "Bayhem", popularized by a 2014 video essay, describes his maximalist combination of orbiting camera moves, layered foreground and background action, and low-angle heroic shots.
