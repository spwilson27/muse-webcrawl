Sustainable design

Sustainable design, also called environmentally conscious design or ecodesign, is the philosophy of designing physical objects, buildings and services to comply with the principles of ecological sustainability and to minimise environmental impact over the whole life cycle.

Principles

Common principles include using low-impact and renewable materials, reducing energy and water consumption, designing for durability, repair, reuse and recyclability, eliminating toxic substances and waste, and considering the life cycle from manufacture through use to disposal ("cradle to cradle" rather than "cradle to grave"). Frameworks such as the Hannover Principles, formulated by William McDonough and Michael Braungart, codify these ideas.

Applications

In architecture, sustainable design is expressed through green building practices and rating systems such as LEED and BREEAM — passive solar design, efficient insulation, and renewable energy. In product design it covers packaging reduction, modular repairable electronics and circular-economy business models. Critics note that "sustainable" claims can amount to greenwashing when not backed by life-cycle assessment.
