The Decapoda are an order of crustaceans within the class Malacostraca, characterised by ten legs (five pairs of pereiopods). The group includes many familiar animals — crabs, lobsters, crayfish, shrimp and prawns — and is the largest order of crustaceans, with around 15,000 living species in some 2,700 genera.

Anatomy

Decapods have three pairs of thoracic appendages modified as maxillipeds for feeding and five pairs of walking legs, the first of which is often enlarged into claws (chelae). The head and thorax are fused into a cephalothorax covered by a carapace, beneath which lie the gills.

Classification and ecology

The order is divided into the suborders Dendrobranchiata (prawns) and Pleocyemata, the latter containing shrimps, lobsters, crayfish and the infraorder Brachyura (true crabs). Decapods occupy marine, freshwater and some terrestrial habitats, and many species support major commercial fisheries worldwide.
