Men in Black II (often styled MIIB) is a 2002 American science fiction action comedy film directed by Barry Sonnenfeld, the sequel to Men in Black (1997). Tommy Lee Jones and Will Smith reprise their roles as Agents K and J of the Men in Black, a secret organisation policing alien activity on Earth. The film was released by Columbia Pictures on 3 July 2002 and grossed over $440 million worldwide, though it received mixed reviews.

Plot

Twenty-five years after the alien Zarthan Laura Vasquez hid the mysterious "Light of Zartha" on Earth, the Kylothian criminal Serleena arrives seeking it; if the Light leaves or remains on Earth past a deadline, the planet faces destruction. Agent J discovers that only retired Agent K — now a postmaster in Truro, Massachusetts, his memory wiped by a neuralyzer — knows where the Light is hidden. J brings K back to MIB headquarters to restore his memory with the "deneuralyzer", fighting off Serleena and her henchmen, including the two-headed Scrad and Charlie. With the help of pizzeria worker Laura Vasquez, the agents uncover the truth: Laura herself is the Light, a Zarthan who must leave Earth aboard a departing ship. K and J defeat Serleena, and Laura departs, saving the planet. A closing gag reveals Earth itself to be stored inside a locker at an alien train station.

Cast

Tommy Lee Jones as Kevin Brown / Agent K; Will Smith as James Darrell Edwards III / Agent J; Lara Flynn Boyle as Serleena; Johnny Knoxville as Scrad and Charlie; Rosario Dawson as Laura Vasquez; Rip Torn as Chief Zed; Tony Shalhoub as Jeebs; and Frank the Pug, voiced by Tim Blaney. Michael Jackson makes a cameo appearance as an alien hoping to become an official MIB agent.

Reception

The film debuted at number one at the US box office but was criticised as a lighter, more derivative retread of the original, praised mainly for its stars' chemistry and Rick Baker's creature effects. A third film, Men in Black 3, followed in 2012.
