Eugene of Savoy

Prince Eugene of Savoy (French: Eugène de Savoie; 18 October 1663 – 21 April 1736) was a general of the Imperial Army and statesman of the Holy Roman Empire and the Austrian monarchy. Born in Paris, he grew up at the court of Louis XIV, who refused him a commission; he left France for Vienna and entered the service of the Habsburgs, under whom he became one of the most successful commanders in European history.

Career

Eugene first distinguished himself in the defence of Vienna and the Great Turkish War, culminating in his crushing victory over the Ottomans at Zenta (1697). In the War of the Spanish Succession he campaigned in Italy and, in alliance with the Duke of Marlborough, won the great victories of Blenheim (1704), Oudenarde (1708) and Malplaquet (1709). In the renewed war against the Ottomans he captured Belgrade in 1717, securing Habsburg power in southeastern Europe.

Statesman and patron

As president of the Imperial War Council and de facto chief minister to emperors Leopold I, Joseph I and Charles VI, Eugene shaped Austrian policy for three decades. He was also a great patron of the arts and architecture: he built the Belvedere Palace and the Stadtpalais in Vienna, amassed a famous library and art collection, and corresponded with Leibniz and Montesquieu. Napoleon considered him one of the seven greatest commanders in history.
