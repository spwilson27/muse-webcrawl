Andrés de Urdaneta

Born30 November 1498, Ordizia, Spain
Died3 June 1568, Mexico City, New Spain
OccupationAugustinian friar, explorer, navigator
Known forEstablishing the tornaviaje return route across the Pacific

Andrés de Urdaneta y Cerain (1508 – 3 June 1568) was a Spanish Augustinian friar, explorer and navigator best known for discovering the tornaviaje, the return route from the Philippines to Mexico across the Pacific Ocean, which enabled the Manila galleon trade.

Early expeditions

Urdaneta sailed with the Loaísa expedition of 1525 to the Spice Islands and spent years in the Moluccas before returning to Spain. He later took religious orders in Mexico, entering the Augustinian order.

The tornaviaje

In 1564 Urdaneta accompanied Miguel López de Legazpi's expedition to the Philippines as navigator and spiritual adviser. In 1565 he sailed from Cebu aboard the San Pedro, sailing north to catch the Kuroshio current and the westerlies, and reached Acapulco after a voyage of about four months. This northern route made regular trans-Pacific trade possible for the next 250 years.

Legacy

Urdaneta's route remained the standard passage for the Manila galleons until 1815, and he is regarded as one of the great navigators of the Age of Discovery.
