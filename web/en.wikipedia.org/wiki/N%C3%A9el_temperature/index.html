Néel temperature

Named afterLouis Néel
SymbolTN
Analogous toCurie temperature (ferromagnets)

The Néel temperature, symbol TN, is the temperature above which an antiferromagnetic material becomes paramagnetic — that is, the temperature at which thermal energy becomes large enough to destroy the material's microscopic magnetic ordering within a macroscopic volume. It is named after Louis Néel (1904–2000), who received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1970 for his work on antiferromagnetism and ferrimagnetism.

Description

Below the Néel temperature, adjacent atomic magnetic moments in an antiferromagnet align antiparallel, giving zero net magnetisation. As the temperature approaches TN from below, this ordered sublattice magnetisation decreases and vanishes at TN. Above it, the material's magnetic susceptibility follows a Curie–Weiss law with a negative Weiss constant.

Examples of Néel temperatures include manganese oxide (MnO, about 116 K), nickel oxide (NiO, about 523 K), chromium (about 311 K), and hematite (α-Fe2O3, about 950 K). The Néel temperature is commonly located experimentally by a cusp in the magnetic susceptibility and a peak in the heat capacity.
