Dhrishtadyumna is a character in the Hindu epic Mahabharata. He is the son of Drupada, king of Panchala, and the twin brother of Draupadi. Born from a sacrificial fire (yajna) specifically to kill the Kuru preceptor Drona, he serves as commander-in-chief (senapati) of the Pandava army in the Kurukshetra War.

Dhrishtadyumna rises fully armed from Drupada's sacrificial fire, destined to slay Drona.

Birth

Humiliated by Drona, his former friend, Drupada performed a great sacrifice to obtain a son who would kill him. From the sacrificial fire emerged Dhrishtadyumna, fully grown and armed, together with his sister Draupadi, who emerged as a young woman. Ironically, Drona himself later accepted Dhrishtadyumna as a student and taught him advanced military arts, knowing of the prophecy.

Kurukshetra War

At Kurukshetra, Krishna and the Pandavas appointed Dhrishtadyumna supreme commander. On the fifteenth day, after it was announced (falsely in part) that Drona's son Ashvatthaman had died, the disheartened Drona laid down his weapons and sat in meditation; Dhrishtadyumna then beheaded him, fulfilling his destiny — an act condemned by many, including Arjuna, as dishonourable toward a disarmed teacher.

Death

On the night after the eighteenth day of battle, Ashvatthaman raided the Pandava camp in revenge and dragged Dhrishtadyumna from his bed, trampling and beating him to death despite his plea to die by a weapon. The night raid, in which Ashvatthaman also slaughtered the remaining Panchala warriors and the Upapandavas, is one of the epic's darkest episodes.
