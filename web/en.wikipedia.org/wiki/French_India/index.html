French India

French India, formally the Établissements français dans l'Inde, was a French colony comprising five geographically separated enclaves on the Indian subcontinent that had initially been factories of the French East India Company. The enclaves were Pondicherry, Karikal and Yanam on the Coromandel Coast; Mahé on the Malabar Coast; and Chandernagore in Bengal.

History

The French East India Company was founded in 1664; its first factory in India was established at Surat in 1668, and Pondicherry was founded in 1674. Under governors such as Joseph François Dupleix in the 1740s, French influence expanded dramatically, but defeat in the Carnatic Wars and the Seven Years' War confined France to its coastal enclaves. After Indian independence, the enclaves were transferred to India — Chandernagore in 1950–52, and the remaining four in 1954 (de facto), ratified by treaty in 1956 and completed de jure in 1962. They now form most of the Union Territory of Puducherry.
