Film adaptation

A film adaptation is the transfer of a work or story, in whole or in part, to a feature film. Although often considered a type of derivative work, film adaptation has been conceptualized recently by academic scholars as a dialogic process. A common form of film adaptation is the use of a novel as the basis of a feature film.

Types

Adaptations may be loose, faithful, or somewhere in between; theorists distinguish transposition, commentary, and analogy. Other source materials include plays, short stories, comic books, video games, television series, and historical events.

Considerations

Because a film typically must compress a longer narrative, changes in plot, character, and theme are common, leading to frequent debate among audiences about fidelity to the source. Adaptations dominate many of the highest-grossing and most acclaimed films.
