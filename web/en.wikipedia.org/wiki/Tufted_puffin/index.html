Tufted puffin

Tufted puffin
Scientific name: Fratercula cirrhata
Family: Alcidae
Conservation status: Least Concern (IUCN)

The tufted puffin (Fratercula cirrhata), also known as the crested puffin, is a relatively abundant medium-sized pelagic seabird in the auk family (Alcidae) found throughout the North Pacific Ocean. It is easily recognised in breeding plumage by its glossy black body, white face, large red-and-yellow bill, and the long golden-yellow tufts of feathers curving back from behind its eyes.

Description and behaviour

Adults are about 35 cm long with a wingspan of roughly 70 cm. They nest in burrows dug into grassy clifftops and slopes on offshore islands, forming large colonies in places such as the Aleutian Islands, the Farallon Islands and the Commander Islands. Both parents incubate a single egg and feed the chick with small fish carried crosswise in the bill.

Distribution

The species ranges from northern California and British Columbia through Alaska and the Aleutians to Siberia and northern Japan, wintering far out at sea in the North Pacific. Populations at the southern edge of the range have declined, and the species was listed as endangered under Washington state law.
