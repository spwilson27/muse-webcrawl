Edith Head (October 28, 1897 – October 24, 1981) was an American costume designer who is considered one of the greatest figures in Hollywood costume design. Over a career spanning six decades, she won eight Academy Awards — more than any other woman in Oscar history — from a record 35 nominations.

Career

Trained as a language teacher, Head entered the film industry in 1923 at Paramount Pictures after answering an advertisement for a sketch artist, borrowing drawings for her portfolio. She rose to become Paramount's head designer in 1938 and remained a dominant figure there until 1967, when she moved to Universal.

Head dressed virtually every major star of the studio era and became the favourite designer of director Alfred Hitchcock, for whom she designed films including Rear Window, Vertigo and The Birds. Her Oscar-winning films include The Heiress, Samson and Delilah, All About Eve, A Place in the Sun, Roman Holiday, Sabrina and The Sting. Famous creations include Dorothy Lamour's sarong, the strapless gown for Elizabeth Taylor in A Place in the Sun, and Grace Kelly's icy wardrobe in Hitchcock's films.

Public image and legacy

With her severe bob haircut, dark glasses and tailored suits, Head cultivated a recognisable personal style and became a public figure in her own right, writing advice books and appearing on television. The Pixar character Edna Mode in The Incredibles is widely regarded as an homage to her.
