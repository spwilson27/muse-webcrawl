Austin Osman Spare (30 December 1886 – 15 May 1956) was an English artist and occultist who worked as both a draughtsman and a painter. Influenced by Symbolism and Art Nouveau, his art is characterised by the occult themes of sigils, automatic drawing, and the grotesque. Spare developed idiosyncratic magical techniques, including automatic writing and sigilisation based on his theories of the relationship between the conscious and unconscious self, and he is regarded as a forerunner of chaos magic.

Early life

Spare was born in London, the son of a City of London policeman. A precocious draughtsman, he became the youngest exhibitor at the Royal Academy summer exhibition in 1904, aged seventeen, attracting comparisons with Aubrey Beardsley. He published his first book of drawings, Earth: Inferno, in 1905.

Occult work

Spare was briefly involved with Aleister Crowley and the A∴A∴ but broke with them. In The Book of Pleasure (Self-Love): The Psychology of Ecstasy (1913) he set out his system: the "Zos" (the self, embodied) and "Kia" (the universal self), attained through ecstasy and the use of sigils — desire encoded into a glyph and launched into the unconscious at a moment of vacuity. He called his tradition the Zos Kia Cultus.

Later life

Spare served as an official war artist in the First World War. In the interwar years he lived in poverty in south London, selling startlingly accomplished drawings for small sums and holding exhibitions in pubs. A modest revival of interest followed the Second World War, partly through his friendship with Kenneth Grant. He died in 1956.

Legacy

Spare's sigil method became a foundational technique of chaos magic in the 1970s, and his vision of the artist-magician has influenced modern occultism, comic art, and surrealist drawing.
