ESG (investing)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The three pillars of ESG: environmental, social, and governance

ESG is an acronym for environmental, social, and governance, a framework used to assess how an organisation manages risks and opportunities related to sustainability and ethical impact. In investing, ESG criteria are used alongside traditional financial analysis to screen investments, allocate capital, and engage with companies. ESG is closely related to earlier traditions of socially responsible investing and corporate social responsibility.

The three pillars

  • Environmental — climate change, carbon emissions, resource depletion, pollution, biodiversity and waste management.
  • Social — labour standards, human rights, workplace diversity, health and safety, community relations and data privacy.
  • Governance — board composition, executive pay, shareholder rights, audit practices, bribery and corruption, and business ethics.

Development and criticism

The term gained currency after the 2004 UN Global Compact report Who Cares Wins and the launch of the Principles for Responsible Investment in 2006. Assets marketed as ESG-linked grew rapidly in the 2010s and early 2020s, accompanied by an industry of ratings agencies and disclosure frameworks such as GRI, SASB and the EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive. Critics dispute the consistency of ESG ratings, the evidence that ESG screening improves returns, and the risk of "greenwashing"; in some jurisdictions ESG has also become a politically contested label, prompting regulatory scrutiny of fund names and claims.
