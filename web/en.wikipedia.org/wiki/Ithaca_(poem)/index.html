Ithaca (poem)

AuthorConstantine P. Cavafy
Written1910; published 1911
LanguageGreek
SubjectOdysseus's voyage home

"Ithaca" (Greek: Ἰθάκη, Ithaki) is a 1911 poem by the Greek poet Constantine P. Cavafy of Alexandria, inspired by Odysseus's homeward journey in Homer's Odyssey. It is Cavafy's most famous and most translated poem.

Content

Addressing the traveller setting out for Ithaca, the speaker urges him to hope the road is long, full of adventure and discovery, and not to fear the mythic monsters — the Laestrygonians, Cyclopes and angry Poseidon — for he will not meet them unless he carries them in his soul. He should visit Egyptian cities and learn, gather sensual perfumes, coral, amber and ebony, and arrive rich in experience. Ithaca, the poem concludes, gave him the marvellous journey; wise as he has become, he will understand "what these Ithacas mean" — the destination matters less than the voyage.

Reception

Written in 1910 and published in 1911, "Ithaca" became a touchstone of modern Greek letters and a universally quoted text on life's journey. It was read at the funeral of Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis in 1994, and translations by Rae Dalven, Edmund Keeley and Philip Sherrard, and Daniel Mendelsohn spread its fame in English.
