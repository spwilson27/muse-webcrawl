Métis Nation

The Métis Nation is the political and cultural collectivity of the Métis people of Canada, an Indigenous people whose homeland — the Métis Homeland — stretches across the Prairie provinces (Manitoba, Saskatchewan and Alberta) and extends into parts of Ontario, British Columbia, the Northwest Territories and the northern United States. The Métis emerged as a distinct people in the historic Northwest during the fur trade era, from unions between European (mainly French and Scottish) fur traders and First Nations women, particularly Cree, Ojibwe and Dene.

History

Métis communities developed around fur-trade posts and the Red River Colony (present-day Manitoba). The Métis became renowned as buffalo hunters, traders, voyageurs and interpreters, developing a distinct culture symbolised by the infinity flag, the sash (ceinture fléchée), the Red River cart, fiddle music and jigging, and languages such as Michif — a unique mixed language combining French nouns with Cree verbs.

Under Louis Riel, the Métis formed a provisional government during the Red River Resistance of 1869–70, which led to the Manitoba Act, 1870 and the creation of the province of Manitoba. The failure of Canada to honour the Act's land provisions, and the flawed scrip system that followed, dispersed many Métis westward. In 1885, grievances erupted in the North-West Resistance, centred at Batoche, Saskatchewan; its defeat and Riel's execution in Regina were followed by decades of marginalisation, during which many Métis became known as the "road allowance people."

Recognition and rights

The Constitution Act, 1982 recognised the Métis as one of the Aboriginal peoples of Canada under section 35. In R v Powley (2003), the Supreme Court of Canada established a test for Métis harvesting rights, requiring self-identification, ancestral connection to a historic Métis community, and acceptance by the modern community. In Manitoba Metis Federation v Canada (2013), the Court held that the Crown had failed to implement the Manitoba Act land grant honourably, and in Daniels v Canada (2016) it declared that Métis and non-status Indians fall within federal jurisdiction under section 91(24).

Governance

The Métis National Council (MNC) was founded in 1983 to represent the Métis Nation nationally. Its governing members have included the Manitoba Métis Federation, the Métis Nation—Saskatchewan, the Métis Nation of Alberta, the Métis Nation British Columbia and the Métis Nation of Ontario. In 2019, Canada signed self-government recognition agreements with several Métis governments, and a Métis self-government bill was introduced in Parliament in 2023. Alberta hosts the eight Métis Settlements, established under provincial legislation in 1938 — the only constitutionally protected Métis land base in Canada.
