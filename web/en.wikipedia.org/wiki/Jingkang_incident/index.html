Jingkang incident

The Jingkang incident (Chinese: 靖康事變), also known as the humiliation of Jingkang, was the capture of the Northern Song capital Bianjing (modern Kaifeng) by the Jurchen Jin dynasty in the winter of 1126–27, during the Jin–Song wars. The Jin carried off the two emperors, Huizong and Qinzong, together with much of the imperial clan and court, bringing the Northern Song dynasty to an end.

Events

After the collapse of the Song–Jin alliance against the Liao, Jin armies invaded the Song in 1125. The retired emperor Huizong abdicated in favour of his son Qinzong, and a first Jin siege of Bianjing was bought off with enormous indemnities and the cession of territory. When the Song court attempted to recover the ceded prefectures, the Jin returned in late 1126; the city fell in January 1127. During the occupation the Jin systematically looted the capital, demanded huge quantities of gold, silver, silk and horses, and rounded up the two emperors, imperial princes and princesses, officials, artisans, musicians and thousands of palace women. In the spring of 1127 the captives were marched north to the Jin capital in Manchuria; many died on the journey, and the two emperors ended their lives in captivity in the far north-east.

Aftermath and memory

Prince Kang, the future Emperor Gaozong, escaped and re-founded the dynasty in the south as the Southern Song. The "shame of Jingkang" (靖康恥) became one of the most potent symbols of national humiliation in Chinese history, kept alive in the famous words attributed to the general Yue Fei in the lyric Man Jiang Hong: "The shame of Jingkang is not yet washed away." The incident remains a standard reference point in Chinese historical memory and patriotic education.
