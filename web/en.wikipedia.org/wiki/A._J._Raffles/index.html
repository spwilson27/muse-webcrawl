A. J. Raffles is a fictional character created by E. W. Hornung, brother-in-law of Arthur Conan Doyle. Raffles is a gentleman thief who lives in fashionable rooms at the Albany in London and supports his lavish lifestyle through burglary, aided by his former fag and loyal accomplice Bunny Manders, who narrates the stories. By day, Raffles is also a celebrated amateur cricketer, playing for the Gentlemen of England.

The stories

Raffles debuted in Cassell's Magazine in 1898 and appeared in the collections The Amateur Cracksman (1899), The Black Mask (1901), and A Thief in the Night (1905), as well as the novel Mr. Justice Raffles (1909). Hornung conceived Raffles partly as an inversion of Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson—a criminal and his chronicler—and dedicated The Amateur Cracksman to Conan Doyle. In the later stories Raffles is exposed as a thief, serves in the Boer War seeking redemption, and is killed at the Battle of Colenso.

Legacy

Raffles became the archetype of the "gentleman thief," influencing later characters such as Arsène Lupin rivals and countless fictional cracksmen. The stories have been adapted many times for stage, film, radio, and television, notably in the 1977 series Raffles starring Anthony Valentine.
