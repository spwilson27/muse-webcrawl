Battle of Varna

The Battle of Varna took place on 10 November 1444 near Varna, in eastern Bulgaria. The Ottoman army under Sultan Murad II defeated the Hungarian–Polish and Wallachian crusader army commanded by King Władysław III of Poland and Hungary and John Hunyadi. It was the final battle of the Crusade of Varna.

Background

After Hunyadi's successful Long Campaign of 1443, a peace treaty was signed at Szeged in 1444. Urged on by the papal legate Cardinal Julian Cesarini, who absolved the king of his oath, the crusaders repudiated the treaty and marched toward the Black Sea, hoping to link up with a Venetian and papal fleet blockading the Dardanelles. Sultan Murad II, who had retired to Anatolia, crossed back into Europe — according to tradition, carrying the broken treaty on a lance — and marched to meet them.

The battle

The crusader army took up a position between Lake Varna and the Franga plateau. Hunyadi's wings initially routed the Ottoman cavalry on both flanks, but King Władysław, against Hunyadi's advice, led a charge of some 500 Polish knights directly at the sultan's Janissary guard. The charge was stopped, the king was killed, and his head was displayed on a lance. The crusader army collapsed and was largely destroyed; Cardinal Cesarini also perished.

Aftermath

The defeat ended serious crusading efforts against the Ottomans in the Balkans and left Hungary in a succession crisis. Murad's victory secured Ottoman control of the region and set the stage for the fall of Constantinople in 1453 under his son Mehmed II. Władysław is commemorated in Bulgaria and Poland as "Władysław of Varna" (Warneńczyk).
