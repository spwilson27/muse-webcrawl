Trigeminal nerve

The trigeminal nerve (cranial nerve V, CN V) is the fifth and largest of the cranial nerves. It is responsible for sensation in the face and motor functions such as biting and chewing; its name derives from its three major branches.

Branches

  • Ophthalmic nerve (V1) – sensory, to the forehead, scalp, and upper eyelid.
  • Maxillary nerve (V2) – sensory, to the mid-face, upper teeth, and nasal cavity.
  • Mandibular nerve (V3) – mixed sensory and motor; sensation to the lower face and motor supply to the muscles of mastication.

Clinical significance

Disorders of the trigeminal nerve include trigeminal neuralgia, a condition of severe, stabbing facial pain, and cluster headache. Shingles (herpes zoster) may affect its ophthalmic division. See cranial nerves and trigeminal neuralgia.
