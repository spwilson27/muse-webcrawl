Imbolc

Imbolc
Date: 1 February (or 1–2 Feb)
Also called: Lá Fhéile Bríde, St Brigid's Day
Type: Gaelic festival, cross-quarter day

Imbolc or Imbolg (Irish: Lá Fhéile Bríde) is a Gaelic traditional festival held on 1 February, marking the beginning of spring, about halfway between the winter solstice and the spring equinox. It is one of the four Gaelic seasonal festivals, alongside Samhain, Beltane and Lughnasadh.

History and customs

Mentioned in early Irish literature, Imbolc was associated with the goddess Brigid and later with Saint Brigid of Kildare, whose feast day coincides. Traditions included weaving Brigid's crosses from rushes to hang for protection, making a brídeóg effigy carried from house to house, visiting holy wells, divination, and rituals welcoming Brigid into the home to bless livestock and the coming lactation of ewes — a possible origin of the name, from Old Irish i mbolg, 'in the belly', referring to pregnant ewes, or oimelc, 'ewe's milk'.

Modern observance

St Brigid's Day became a public holiday in Ireland in 2023. Neopagans, especially Wiccans, celebrate Imbolc as a sabbat, often linking it with fire and candles, akin to Candlemas.
