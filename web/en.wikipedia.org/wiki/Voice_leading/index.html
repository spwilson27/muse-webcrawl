Voice leading

Voice leading is the linear progression of individual melodic lines ("voices") and their interaction with one another to create harmony, as studied in counterpoint and tonal harmony pedagogy.

Principles

Traditional rules favour smooth, conjunct motion: voices should move by the smallest possible intervals, common tones between chords are held, and leaps should be balanced or resolved. Certain motions are traditionally prohibited in strict part-writing, notably parallel perfect fifths and octaves, because they compromise the independence of voices. Tendency tones such as the leading note resolve predictably (to the tonic), and dissonances are prepared and resolved.

Practice

Good voice leading underlies effective choral writing, orchestration and keyboard harmony, and the concept persists in jazz — where guide tones define chord progressions — and in modern voice-leading theory, which measures distances between pitch-class sets.
