El (also ʾIl, Ugaritic: 𐎛𐎍) is a Northwest Semitic word meaning "god" or "deity" and the proper name of the supreme god of the ancient Canaanite religion and the head of the pantheon attested in the texts of Ugarit. El was the father of humankind and of the gods, the aged and wise king who presided over the divine council.

Characteristics

In the Ugaritic texts, El is depicted as an elderly, bearded patriarch dwelling at the source of the rivers, in a tent or pavilion, far from human society. His epithets include "Bull El" (signifying strength and virility), "El the Creator of Creatures", "the Kindly One, El the Compassionate", and "Father of Years". He is generally benevolent but passive and sometimes indecisive; in the Baal Cycle he presides over the assembly of gods while younger deities such as Baal struggle for kingship among the gods.

Family and mythology

El's consort was the goddess Athirat (Asherah). Together they were credited with seventy divine children. El was the father of the gods Yam (Sea), Mot (Death) and, in some interpretations, Baal, who is more often called the son of Dagan yet addresses El as father in his capacity as head of the pantheon. El authorised Baal's palace, mourned his death, and rejoiced at his return.

El in the Hebrew Bible

The Hebrew word El and its plural Elohim became central terms for God in the Hebrew Bible. Many scholars hold that Israelite religion originally identified its god Yahweh with El, absorbing his epithets (such as El Shaddai, El Elyon, El Olam) and cultic titles. Distinct survivals include compound names in Genesis traditions — Bethel ("house of El") and personal names such as Ishmael and Israel.

Legacy

The divine name survives in theophoric names across Semitic languages, including Arabic ilāh (as in Allah, "the God") and names like Gabriel, Michael and Raphael in the Abrahamic traditions. Later Phoenician and Hellenistic writers, such as Philo of Byblos, identified El with the Greek Cronus.
