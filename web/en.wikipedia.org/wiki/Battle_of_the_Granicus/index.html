Battle of the Granicus

The Battle of the Granicus was fought in May 334 BC between the army of Alexander the Great and the forces of the Persian Empire in northwestern Anatolia, near the Granicus River (modern Biga Çayı, Turkey). It was the first of three major battles between Alexander and the Persians, and opened Asia Minor to Macedonian conquest.

Background

Alexander crossed the Hellespont in spring 334 BC to prosecute the pan-Hellenic war of revenge against Persia. The Persian satraps of Anatolia, rejecting Memnon's advice to adopt a scorched-earth strategy, resolved to fight, drawing up along the steep eastern bank of the Granicus with their cavalry forward — a dispositions later criticised, since it left their Greek infantry in reserve.

The battle

Against Parmenion's counsel to wait, Alexander attacked at once, advancing obliquely and leading the Companion cavalry in a charge across the river. In the mêlée on the bank Alexander was nearly killed, his helmet split by a Persian sword before Cleitus the Black saved his life. The Persian cavalry broke, and the abandoned Greek mercenaries were surrounded and mostly killed.

Aftermath

Alexander sent 300 Persian panoplies to Athens as an offering to Athena. The victory opened western Asia Minor: Sardis surrendered, and the coastal cities fell in succession, though Miletus and Halicarnassus required sieges. Alexander next met Darius in person at Issus in 333 BC.
