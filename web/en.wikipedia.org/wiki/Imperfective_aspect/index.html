Imperfective aspect

FieldLinguistics (grammar)

The imperfective (abbreviated IPFV) is a grammatical aspect that views a situation as ongoing, habitual or incomplete — from the inside, without regard to its boundaries. It stands in contrast to the perfective aspect, which views a situation as a single, completed whole. The distinction is fundamental in the verb systems of the Slavic languages, and is also found in Ancient Greek, Romance imperfect tenses, Mandarin Chinese and many other languages.

Meaning and uses

Imperfective forms typically express:

  • Progressive/ongoing situations: "She was walking home" (at the reference time).
  • Habitual situations: "He smokes" / "We visited them every summer."
  • Background situations in narration, against which perfective foreground events stand out.
  • General facts and states without endpoints in view.

In Slavic languages

In Russian, Polish and most other Slavic languages, nearly every verb exists in an imperfective–perfective pair. The imperfective is usually the basic form and the perfective is derived by prefixation or suffixation (Russian pisat' "to write (imperfective)" → napisat' "to write (perfective)"), though suppletion and stress shifts also occur. Choice of aspect is obligatory in nearly every verb form, including infinitives and imperatives, making aspect the most characteristic feature of the Slavic verb.

In other languages

Ancient Greek opposed the imperfective (present) stem to the aorist (perfective) stem. The Romance "imperfect" past tense is imperfective in meaning. Mandarin marks imperfective viewpoints with particles such as 在 zài and 着 zhe, while English approximates the distinction with the progressive construction "be + -ing" and habitual "used to".
