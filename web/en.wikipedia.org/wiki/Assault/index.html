Assault

Assault
Legal typeCrime and tort
Definition (common law)An act creating apprehension of imminent harmful or offensive contact

In criminal and civil law, assault is an attempt to initiate harmful or offensive contact with a person, or a threat to do so made in circumstances that create in the victim a reasonable apprehension of imminent harm. In common law systems, assault is often distinguished from battery, which is the actual infliction of the contact: the threat alone is assault even if no blow lands, while the completed act is battery. Jurisdictions vary, however, and many modern statutes use "assault" for both.

Elements

Traditionally, civil assault requires an intentional act by the defendant, a reasonable apprehension of imminent harmful or offensive contact on the part of the victim, and causation. Mere words are usually insufficient without an accompanying gesture or circumstance suggesting an immediate ability to carry out the threat. Importantly, the victim need not fear injury — apprehension means awareness and expectation of the contact, not fright.

Degrees and related offences

Many jurisdictions grade assault: simple assault; assault causing bodily injury; aggravated assault, typically involving a deadly weapon or intent to commit a serious crime; and sexual assault, which is separately defined in most modern codes. In England and Wales, common assault is a summary offence under the Criminal Justice Act 1988, with more serious conduct charged as assault occasioning actual bodily harm or grievous bodily harm under the Offences against the Person Act 1861. In the United States, definitions and grading vary by state, with several states merging assault and battery into a single offence. The term is unrelated in meaning to the military assault rifle, though both derive from the general sense of attack.
