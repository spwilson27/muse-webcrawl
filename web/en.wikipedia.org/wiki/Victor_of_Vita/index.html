Victor of Vita

Victor of Vita (Latin: Victor Vitensis; born c. 430) was an African bishop of the 5th century and the author of the Historia persecutionis Africanae provinciae ("History of the Persecution in the African Province"), the principal narrative source for the persecution of Nicene (Catholic) Christians under the Arian Vandal kings of North Africa, especially Huneric.

Manuscript of Victor's History of the Persecution

Life

Little is known of Victor's life beyond what can be inferred from his work. He was bishop of Vita, a small town in the province of Byzacena (in modern Tunisia), and by 484 he was in Carthage, where he witnessed the events he describes. He may have been exiled by the Vandals, as many Catholic bishops were, but his later fate is unknown.

The History of the Persecution

Written about 484–489, the Historia is divided into three books. The first covers the Vandal invasion and the reign of Gaiseric; the second and third treat the reign of Huneric (477–484), whose edicts — modelled pointedly on earlier imperial laws against heresy — demanded the conversion of Catholics to Arianism, closed churches, and exiled thousands of clergy. Victor's account includes quoted documents, such as Huneric's edict of 484 and the Catholic confession of faith presented at the Conference of Carthage that year, lending the work documentary value despite its openly partisan, rhetorical character.

Victor's dramatic stories of confiscations, forced rebaptisms and martyrdoms shaped later Christian memory of the Vandals and contributed to their reputation for destructive fanaticism. Modern historians read the work critically as both a source of genuine records and a work of persuasion aimed at winning support from Constantinople for the African Catholics.
