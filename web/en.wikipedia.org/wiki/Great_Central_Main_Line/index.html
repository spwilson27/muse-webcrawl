The Great Central Main Line (GCML), also known as the London Extension of the Manchester, Sheffield and Lincolnshire Railway, is a former railway line in England which opened in 1899 and ran between London Marylebone and Manchester London Road via Leicester, Nottingham, and Sheffield. It was the last main line to be built in Britain during the Victorian era.

History

The line was the project of Sir Edward Watkin, who intended it as part of a route from the industrial North to a future Channel Tunnel; unusually for a British line, it was built to the larger continental loading gauge. The London Extension opened to passengers on 15 March 1899, and the company renamed itself the Great Central Railway.

Closure and survival

Under the Beeching cuts the line was closed as a through route between September 1966 and May 1969, with most intermediate stations shut. The southern section from Marylebone to Aylesbury (and later Bicester and Banbury) survives as part of Chiltern Railways services, and sections are preserved as the heritage Great Central Railway in Leicestershire and the Great Central Railway (Nottingham). Parts of the route have repeatedly been studied for reopening and for high-speed rail proposals.
