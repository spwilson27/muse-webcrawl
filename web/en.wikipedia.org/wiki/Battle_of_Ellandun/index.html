The Battle of Ellandun or Ellendun was fought in 825 between King Egbert of Wessex and King Beornwulf of Mercia near present-day Wroughton in Wiltshire. Egbert's victory ended the long Mercian supremacy over southern England and marks a turning point in the political history of the Anglo-Saxon kingdoms, establishing Wessex as the dominant power in the south.

Background

Since the later eighth century Mercia, under Offa and his successors, had dominated the kingdoms south of the Humber, treating Kent, Sussex, and East Anglia as dependencies. Beornwulf, who seized the Mercian throne in 823, invaded Wessex in 825 in an attempt to reassert that dominance after Egbert's successes in the southwest.

The battle

Details of the fighting are sparse; the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle records simply that Egbert and Beornwulf fought at Ellandun and that Egbert won the victory, with great slaughter. Ellandun is generally identified with the area near Wroughton, south of Swindon.

Consequences

Following the victory, Egbert sent his son Æthelwulf into the southeast, where Kent, Surrey, Sussex, and Essex submitted to Wessex. Beornwulf was killed the same year fighting the East Angles, Mercia descended into instability, and its supremacy was never fully restored. Ellandun is thus often treated as the moment at which the balance of power in England shifted decisively toward Wessex.
