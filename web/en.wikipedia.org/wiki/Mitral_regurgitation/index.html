Mitral regurgitation

Other namesMitral insufficiency, mitral incompetence
SpecialtyCardiology
SymptomsShortness of breath, fatigue, palpitations; may be asymptomatic

Mitral regurgitation (MR), also known as mitral insufficiency, is a disorder of the heart in which the mitral valve does not close properly when the heart pumps out blood, allowing blood to flow backward from the left ventricle into the left atrium during systole.

Causes

Mitral regurgitation is divided into primary (degenerative) disease of the valve apparatus itself — most often mitral valve prolapse, but also rheumatic heart disease, infective endocarditis, and ruptured chordae tendineae — and secondary (functional) regurgitation, in which a structurally normal valve leaks because of left ventricular dilatation or dysfunction, for example after myocardial infarction.

Signs and symptoms

The hallmark sign is a holosystolic (pansystolic) murmur heard best at the cardiac apex, radiating to the axilla. Chronic MR may remain asymptomatic for years; as it progresses it can cause exertional dyspnoea, orthopnoea, fatigue, atrial fibrillation and ultimately left-sided heart failure. Acute severe MR, such as from papillary muscle rupture, presents abruptly with pulmonary oedema and shock.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is confirmed and severity quantified by echocardiography. Management depends on cause and severity and ranges from surveillance and medication to surgical mitral valve repair or replacement, and percutaneous edge-to-edge repair in selected patients.
