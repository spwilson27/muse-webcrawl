Formation (association football)

In association football, a formation describes how the players in a team are positioned on the pitch, excluding the goalkeeper. It is conventionally written as a series of numbers giving the count of players in each line from defence to attack — for example, 4-4-2 means four defenders, four midfielders and two forwards.

History

Nineteenth-century football was extremely attack-heavy: early England teams played formations close to 1-2-7. The 2-3-5 "pyramid" became standard in the late 1800s and lasted until the offside rule change of 1925 encouraged the W-M formation (3-2-2-3). Brazil's 4-2-4 won the 1958 World Cup, and subsequent decades saw the spread of 4-4-2, 4-3-3 and 3-5-2 systems.

Modern formations

Common modern formations include the balanced 4-4-2, the possession-oriented 4-3-3, the 4-2-3-1 with a supporting attacking midfielder, and back-three systems such as 3-5-2 and 3-4-3. Fluid modern teams often attack in one shape and defend in another, so a single label is only a starting point. Choice of formation depends on the players available, the opponent, and the desired balance between attack and defence.
