Comedy Central is an American cable channel owned by Paramount Global through its network division's Entertainment and Youth Group. Launched on April 1, 1991, through the merger of two rival comedy channels, Viacom's Ha! and HBO's The Comedy Channel, it was the first cable network devoted exclusively to comedy programming, carrying stand-up specials, syndicated sitcoms and original series.

History

The channel found its identity in the late 1990s with animated and topical originals: South Park (1997), whose crude animation and satire became a cultural phenomenon; The Daily Show (from 1996; reshaped under Jon Stewart from 1999 into an influential satire of news and politics); and Chappelle's Show (2003). Its roasts — the Comedy Central Roasts of celebrities — became a recurring franchise from 2003.

Programming

Signature series have included The Colbert Report, Inside Amy Schumer, Key & Peele, Broad City, Drunk History, Nathan for You and the long-running animated comedy South Park. The network also syndicates sitcoms such as The Office, Seinfeld and Futurama, and airs stand-up specials and the annual stand-up programming from its own tours and festivals. International versions of the channel operate in Europe, Latin America, Asia and elsewhere, with localised programming.
