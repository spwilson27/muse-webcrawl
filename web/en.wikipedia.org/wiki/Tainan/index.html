Tainan

Tainan, officially Tainan City, is a special municipality in southern Taiwan, facing the Taiwan Strait. It is the oldest city on the island and was Taiwan's capital during the Dutch colonial period, the Kingdom of Tungning, and much of Qing rule, until Taipei became the capital in the late 19th century.

History

The Dutch East India Company built Fort Zeelandia at Anping in 1624 and Fort Provintia nearby, making the settlement the centre of Dutch Formosa. In 1662 Koxinga expelled the Dutch and made the city the capital of his Kingdom of Tungning. Under Qing rule Tainan remained Taiwan's principal city and port. Its many temples, including Taiwan's oldest Confucian temple, reflect its long history.

Modern city

In 2010 the city merged with Tainan County to form the present special municipality, with a population of about 1.85 million. Tainan is known for its street food and snacks, historic sites such as Anping Fort and Chihkan Tower, and nearby technology industry in the Southern Taiwan Science Park.
