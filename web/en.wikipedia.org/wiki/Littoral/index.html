Littoral

Littoral refers to the shore or coastal region, and in a number of contexts specifically to the littoral zone of a sea, lake or river. The word derives from the Latin litus, litoris, meaning "shore".

Uses

  • Ecology — the littoral zone is the nearshore area where sunlight penetrates to the bottom, supporting rooted plants and dense animal life; in marine settings it overlaps with the intertidal zone.
  • Military — littoral warfare takes place in and near the shore; a littoral combat ship is a class of warship designed for operations close to shore.
  • Geography and place names — "Littoral" appears in the names of administrative regions, such as the Littoral Region of Cameroon, the Littoral Department of Benin, and the historical Austrian Littoral (Österreichisches Küstenland), a crown land of the Austrian Empire centred on Trieste. In Slovenia, the traditional region of Primorska is rendered in English as "the Littoral".
  • Law — littoral rights are the rights of landowners whose property borders large bodies of water, contrasted with riparian rights on rivers.

Littoral zone

In limnology and oceanography the littoral zone extends from the high-water mark to the depth at which rooted aquatic vegetation can grow. It is the most productive part of many lakes and coastal seas, providing habitat for fish, invertebrates and waterbirds, and it is also the zone most affected by human activity, pollution and shoreline development.
