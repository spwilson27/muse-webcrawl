Iraq War troop surge of 2007

The surge deployed more than 20,000 additional U.S. soldiers to Iraq, mostly to Baghdad and Al Anbar.

The Iraq War troop surge of 2007, commonly known as the surge, was a revised strategy for the Iraq War announced by U.S. President George W. Bush on January 10, 2007. It added more than 20,000 U.S. soldiers — five combat brigades — to Iraq, principally in Baghdad and Al Anbar Province, and paired the reinforcement with a new counterinsurgency strategy under General David Petraeus, who took command of Multi-National Force – Iraq in February 2007.

Background

By late 2006, Iraq was descending into sectarian civil war following the February 2006 bombing of the al-Askari Mosque in Samarra, and American public and congressional opinion had turned against the war. The bipartisan Iraq Study Group recommended a change of course, and the Bush administration adopted a strategy emphasising population security — clearing, holding, and building in Baghdad neighbourhoods — rather than transitioning rapidly to Iraqi forces.

Results and debate

Violence in Iraq fell dramatically during 2007 and 2008. Analysts credit the surge together with the Anbar Awakening — in which Sunni tribes turned against al-Qaeda in Iraq — a ceasefire by Muqtada al-Sadr's Mahdi Army, and the near-completion of sectarian cleansing in Baghdad. The relative weight of these factors remains debated. The additional brigades were withdrawn by mid-2008, returning U.S. troop levels to pre-surge figures.
