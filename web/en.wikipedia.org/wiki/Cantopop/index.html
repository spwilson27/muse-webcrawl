Cantopop

Cantopop (Chinese: 粵語流行音樂; Jyutping: jyut6 jyu5 lau4 hang4 jam1 ngok6), also spelled Canto-pop, is a genre of popular music originating from Hong Kong in the 1970s. The term is a contraction of "Cantonese popular music". Cantopop combines Cantonese lyrics with Western musical styles including pop, rock, jazz, and balladry, and it was a dominant force in Chinese-language popular culture during the 1980s and 1990s, when Hong Kong's entertainment industry reached its commercial and cultural peak.

History

Origins (1970s)

Before Cantopop, Hong Kong's popular music market was dominated by Mandopop from Taiwan and Shanghai, English-language pop, and Cantonese opera. In the early 1970s, singers such as Sam Hui began recording songs in colloquial Cantonese that reflected everyday Hong Kong life, most famously the theme song to the 1974 film Games Gamblers Play. Hui's witty, working-class lyrics are widely credited with establishing Cantonese as a legitimate language for modern pop music. Television dramas broadcast by TVB also popularised Cantonese theme songs, sung by performers such as Roman Tam and Jenny Tseng.

Golden age (1980s–1990s)

During the 1980s Cantopop entered its golden age. Superstars including Leslie Cheung, Anita Mui, Alan Tam, and the rock band Beyond achieved fame across East and Southeast Asia. Many hits were Cantonese adaptations of Japanese pop songs. The early 1990s were dominated by the "Four Heavenly Kings" — Jacky Cheung, Andy Lau, Aaron Kwok, and Leon Lai — whose popularity extended into film and advertising. Faye Wong, though Mandarin-speaking by birth, also became a major Cantopop star in this period.

Decline and evolution (2000s–present)

From the late 1990s, Cantopop's regional dominance waned due to music piracy, the Asian financial crisis, the growing appeal of Mandopop, and later K-pop. The industry contracted, and many Hong Kong singers turned to the mainland Chinese market. In the 2010s and 2020s, local acts such as the boy group Mirror sparked renewed interest in homegrown Cantonese pop among younger audiences.

Characteristics

Cantopop songs are typically slow to mid-tempo ballads with strong melodies, though dance-pop and rock subgenres exist. Because Cantonese is a tonal language with nine tones, lyricists must match melodic contours to lexical tones so words remain intelligible — a craft associated with lyricists such as James Wong, Lin Xi (Albert Leung), and Wyman Wong. Themes commonly include romance, personal perseverance, and Hong Kong identity.

Influence

Cantopop played a major role in shaping the modern Chinese pop industry and in projecting Hong Kong culture abroad. Its stars cross-promoted with the Hong Kong film industry, and Cantopop ballads remain standards at karaoke throughout the Chinese-speaking world.
