Kyphosis

Kyphosis
SpecialtyOrthopaedics, rheumatology
SymptomsRounded upper back, back pain, stiffness
CausesPosture, Scheuermann's disease, osteoporosis, congenital defects, degeneration

Kyphosis is an excessive outward (posterior) curvature of the thoracic spine, producing a rounded or hunched back. A normal thoracic kyphotic curve measures roughly 20° to 45°; curvature beyond about 50° is considered hyperkyphosis.

Types and causes

Postural kyphosis, the most common type in adolescents, results from poor posture and is flexible and correctable. Scheuermann's kyphosis is a rigid structural deformity of adolescence caused by wedging of the vertebrae. In older adults, kyphosis most often follows osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures, causing the so-called "dowager's hump". Other causes include congenital malformations, degenerative disc disease, infections such as tuberculosis (Pott's disease, producing a sharp gibbus), tumours, and neuromuscular conditions.

Diagnosis and treatment

Severity is measured by the Cobb angle on standing lateral radiographs. Mild cases are managed with physiotherapy, postural training and treatment of underlying osteoporosis; bracing may help adolescents with Scheuermann's disease. Severe or progressive deformity, pain, or neurological compromise may require spinal surgery.
