Queen ant

A queen ant is an adult, reproducing female ant in an ant colony; generally she will be the mother of all the other ants in that colony. Some female ants, such as Cataglyphis cursor, do not need to mate to produce offspring, reproducing through asexual parthenogenesis; all offspring will be female. Others, like those in the genus Crematogaster, mate in a nuptial flight.

A queen ant attended by workers

Life cycle

Ant queens begin life as winged virgin reproductives (alates). After leaving the nest on a nuptial flight and mating, a queen lands, sheds her wings, and excavates a small chamber where she begins laying eggs. In many species she seals herself in and raises the first brood of workers on stored body reserves, metabolizing her flight muscles.

Once the first workers emerge, the queen's role narrows to egg-laying and producing pheromones that regulate colony function. Queens are usually the largest caste in the colony and are fed and groomed by workers. Depending on the species, a colony may have a single queen (monogyny) or many (polygyny).

Lifespan

Ant queens are among the longest-lived insects: queens of the black garden ant Lasius niger have lived nearly 29 years in captivity, and queens of many species routinely live 10–20 years, far outliving their worker daughters, which may survive only weeks or months.
