Mapai (Hebrew: מַפָּא"י, an acronym for Mifleget Poalei Eretz Yisrael, "Workers' Party of the Land of Israel") was a democratic socialist political party in Israel and the dominant force in Israeli politics until its merger into the modern-day Israeli Labor Party in 1968. Mapai led every Israeli government from independence in 1948 until the merger.

History

Mapai was founded in 1930 by the merger of Ahdut HaAvoda and Hapoel Hatzair, the two principal Labor Zionist parties of the Yishuv. Under the leadership of David Ben-Gurion it became the leading party of the Jewish community in Mandatory Palestine, dominating the Histadrut trade union federation and the Jewish Agency.

After the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948, Ben-Gurion became the country's first prime minister, and Mapai won the first Knesset elections in 1949. The party absorbed many new immigrants and built the institutions of the young state, including its welfare system, the Israel Defense Forces, and the national water carrier.

Decline and merger

In the 1960s the party was weakened by the Lavon Affair and by internal conflict between Ben-Gurion's "young guard" and the party old guard. Ben-Gurion left in 1965 to found the Rafi party. In January 1968 Mapai merged with Ahdut HaAvoda and Rafi to form the Israeli Labor Party.

Prime ministers from Mapai

  • David Ben-Gurion (1948–1953, 1955–1963)
  • Moshe Sharett (1953–1955)
  • Levi Eshkol (1963–1968, continuing in the Labor Party until 1969)
