Cartomancy

Cartomancy
TypeDivination
ToolsPlaying cards, tarot cards, oracle cards

Cartomancy is the practice of fortune-telling or divination using a deck of cards. It most often uses standard playing cards, but the term also encompasses readings with tarot cards and purpose-made oracle decks. The reader interprets the meanings assigned to individual cards and their positions within a layout, or "spread."

History

Playing cards reached Europe from the Islamic world in the late 14th century, and their use in divination developed over the following centuries. Printed guides to card meanings appeared by the 18th century; the French occultist Jean-Baptiste Alliette ("Etteilla") published influential cartomantic systems from the 1770s and popularised tarot reading. In the 19th century, figures such as Marie Anne Lenormand, famed as Napoleon's card reader, lent their names to cartomancy decks still used today, notably the 36-card Lenormand deck.

Methods

In playing-card cartomancy, each suit and rank carries conventional meanings: hearts concern love and emotion, diamonds money and news, clubs work and enterprise, and spades conflict or misfortune. Readings may use spreads ranging from a single drawn card to elaborate layouts such as the 36-card Lenormand Grand Tableau. Tarot readers draw on the 78-card deck's Major and Minor Arcana, with spreads such as the Celtic Cross.

Criticism

Cartomancy is regarded by sceptics as a pseudoscience, with readings explained by cold reading, the Barnum effect, and subjective validation. Practitioners may frame readings as entertainment, counselling, or spiritual guidance.
