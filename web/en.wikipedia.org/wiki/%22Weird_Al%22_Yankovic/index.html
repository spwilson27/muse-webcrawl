"Weird Al" Yankovic

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Alfred Matthew "Weird Al" Yankovic (born October 23, 1959) is an American musician, comedian, and actor. He is best known for creating comedic songs that make light of pop culture, often performing parodies of specific contemporary songs by other artists, as well as original works in the style of pastiches of other artists' work.

Early life

Yankovic was born in Downey, California, and raised in nearby Lynwood. He took up the accordion as a child after a door-to-door salesman offered his parents a choice between accordion and guitar lessons. He sent homemade recordings to Dr. Demento's syndicated comedy radio program as a teenager, and one of his songs, "My Bologna", a parody of the Knack's "My Sharona", became a hit on the show in 1979.

Career

Yankovic has recorded more than 150 parody and original songs and sold more than 12 million albums. His best-known parodies include "Eat It" (a parody of Michael Jackson's "Beat It"), "Like a Surgeon" (after Madonna's "Like a Virgin"), "Amish Paradise", "White & Nerdy", and "Word Crimes". His album Mandatory Fun (2014) debuted at number one on the Billboard 200, the first comedy album to top the chart since 1960. He has won multiple Grammy Awards.

Yankovic wrote and starred in the film UHF (1989) and the television series The Weird Al Show (1997). A biographical parody film, Weird: The Al Yankovic Story, was released in 2022 starring Daniel Radcliffe. He is known for his Hawaiian shirts, curly hair, and for politely seeking permission from the artists he parodies, a courtesy not strictly required by law.
