Centrifugal fan

A centrifugal fan is a mechanical device for moving air or other gases. It uses a rotating impeller with blades mounted inside a spiral (volute) casing; air enters axially through the inlet and is thrown radially outward by centrifugal force, discharging at right angles to the intake. Centrifugal fans can achieve higher pressures than axial fans and are widely used in heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC), industrial ventilation, and dust collection.

Construction

The main parts are the impeller (fan wheel), the housing, inlet and outlet ducts, the drive shaft and bearings, and the drive mechanism. Impeller blades may be backward-curved, radial, or forward-curved ("squirrel cage"); backward-curved blades give the highest efficiency, while forward-curved wheels are compact and common in domestic and packaged HVAC equipment.

Performance

Fan performance is described by the fan laws: flow varies with rotational speed, pressure with the square of speed, and power with the cube of speed. Selection balances the required flow and static pressure against noise and energy consumption.
