Saint Cyril

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[hero image: Saint Cyril]
Saint Cyril, apostle to the Slavs

Saint Cyril may refer to several Christian saints. Most commonly the name denotes Cyril of Thessalonica (826/827 – 14 February 869), the Byzantine Greek monk and missionary who, with his brother Methodius, is credited with devising the Glagolitic alphabet and bringing Christianity and literacy to the Slavs. He is also the name of major figures such as Cyril of Alexandria and Cyril of Jerusalem.

Cyril of Thessalonica

Born Constantine in Thessalonica, he studied in Constantinople, where he became a professor of philosophy at the Magnaura School and earned the title "the Philosopher." In 862 the brothers were sent by the Byzantine emperor Michael III and Patriarch Photios to Great Moravia at the request of Prince Rastislav, who wanted missionaries able to preach in the Slavonic language. Cyril created the Glagolitic script, translated the liturgy and scriptures into what became Old Church Slavonic, and defended the use of the vernacular before the Pope in Rome, where he died in 869. Cyril and Methodius are venerated as "Apostles to the Slavs" and are co-patrons of Europe.

Other saints named Cyril

  • Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376 – 444), Patriarch of Alexandria, a leading figure of the Council of Ephesus (431) and a Doctor of the Church.
  • Cyril of Jerusalem (c. 313 – 386), Bishop of Jerusalem, author of the Catechetical Lectures and a Doctor of the Church.
  • Cyril of Turaw (1130–1182), Belarusian bishop and writer.
  • Cyril of Philea (c. 1015 – c. 1110), Byzantine monk and spiritual writer.
  • Cyril I of Moscow — not a canonised saint, but the modern Patriarch of Moscow and all Rus'.

Veneration

The feast of Saints Cyril and Methodius is celebrated on 14 February in the Roman Catholic Church and on 11 May in the Eastern Orthodox Church. The Cyrillic script, developed by the brothers' disciples in Bulgaria, bears Cyril's name and remains in use across much of Eurasia.
