Wushu (武術, "martial arts"), also known as modern wushu or contemporary wushu, is a standardised sport created in the People's Republic of China to systematise traditional Chinese martial arts. Competitive wushu comprises two disciplines: taolu, the performance of choreographed forms, and sanda (sanshou), full-contact sparring.

Taolu

Taolu events are judged on the quality, difficulty and artistry of routines performed bare-handed (changquan, nanquan, taijiquan) or with weapons such as the broadsword (daoshu), straight sword (jianshu), spear (qiangshu) and staff (gunshu). Modern competition routines incorporate acrobatic elements — aerial kicks, butterfly twists and difficult balances — known as nandu (difficulty movements), which earn bonus points.

Sanda

Sanda is a kickboxing-style combat sport fought on a raised platform (leitai), combining punches, kicks, sweeps and throws, with bouts decided by points or knockout.

Organisation and recognition

The International Wushu Federation (IWUF) was founded in Beijing in 1990 and stages the biennial World Wushu Championships. Wushu appears at the Asian Games and was showcased in a special tournament alongside the Beijing 2008 Olympics; taijiquan events were also included in the youth and multi-sport programmes, and wushu has campaigned for full Olympic inclusion.
