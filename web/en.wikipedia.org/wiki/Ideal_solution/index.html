Ideal solution

An ideal solution or ideal mixture is a solution that exhibits thermodynamic properties analogous to those of a mixture of ideal gases. The enthalpy of solution (or "enthalpy of mixing") is zero, as is the volume change on mixing; the closer these are to zero, the more "ideal" the behaviour of the solution becomes.

Definition

An ideal solution is one in which the fugacity of each component is proportional to its mole fraction across the whole range of composition, obeying Raoult's law: pi = xi p*i, where p*i is the vapour pressure of the pure component.

Physical basis

Ideality arises when the intermolecular interactions between unlike molecules are the same as those between like molecules, and the molecules are similar in size and shape. Mixtures of chemically similar substances, such as benzene and toluene, come close to ideality; deviations give rise to positive or negative departures from Raoult's law.
