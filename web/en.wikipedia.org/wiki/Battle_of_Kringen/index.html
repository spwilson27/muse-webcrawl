Battle of Kringen

The Battle of Kringen (Norwegian: Slaget ved Kringen) was fought on 26 August 1612 near Otta in the Gudbrandsdal valley of Norway, during the Kalmar War between Denmark–Norway and Sweden. A force of Scottish mercenaries marching to join Swedish service was ambushed and largely destroyed by Norwegian peasant militia.

Background

Some 300 Scots under George Sinclair had landed in western Norway intending to cross the country to Sweden. As they marched up the Gudbrandsdal, local farmers and militiamen gathered to resist them, setting an ambush at the narrow defile of Kringen, where logs and boulders were rolled down onto the column.

Outcome

Most of the Scots were killed; survivors were captured, and many were subsequently massacred, with only a handful — tradition says around 18 — spared. The battle became a celebrated event in Norwegian folk memory, commemorated in songs and the legend of the farm girl Prillar-Guri, who is said to have warned the militia with a lur horn.
