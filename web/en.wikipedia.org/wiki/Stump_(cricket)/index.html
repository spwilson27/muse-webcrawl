Stump (cricket)

A wicket: three stumps topped by two bails

In the game of cricket, a stump is one of the three vertical wooden posts that together form the wicket at each end of the pitch. The three stumps — traditionally made of ash — are hammered into the ground so that they stand 28 inches (71.1 cm) high, with a combined width of 9 inches (22.86 cm). Two bails rest in grooves on top of the stumps, completing the wicket.

Role in the game

The stumps are central to several modes of dismissal. A batsman is bowled if a delivery hits the stumps and dislodges a bail; stumped if the wicket-keeper breaks the wicket while the batsman is out of the crease; and run out if the wicket is broken while a batsman is short of the crease. "Breaking the wicket" requires that a bail be dislodged or a stump struck out of the ground.

Modern developments

Modern stumps may be fitted with stump cameras and microphones for broadcasting, and in many televised limited-overs matches LED "zing" bails and stumps light up on impact to aid run-out and stumping decisions. The three stumps are conventionally named off stump, middle stump and leg stump from the batsman's perspective.
