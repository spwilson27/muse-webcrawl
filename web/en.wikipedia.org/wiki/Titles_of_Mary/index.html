Mary, the mother of Jesus in Christian faith, is known by many different titles (Blessed Mother, Virgin Mary, Mother of God, Our Lady, Holy Virgin), epithets (Star of the Sea, Queen of Heaven, Cause of Our Joy), invocations (Panagia, Theotokos, Mater Dei), and names associated with places (Our Lady of Guadalupe, Our Lady of Lourdes, Our Lady of Fatima). Many of these titles derive from dogmatic, scriptural or liturgical sources, notably the Litany of Loreto; others arose from reported apparitions or local devotion.

Dogmatic titles

  • Theotokos (Greek: Θεοτόκος, "God-bearer") – affirmed at the Council of Ephesus in 431, affirming Mary as Mother of God.
  • Mother of God – the Latin Mater Dei, equivalent of Theotokos.
  • Ever-Virgin (Aeiparthenos) – affirming Mary's perpetual virginity.
  • Immaculate Conception – proclaimed as dogma in 1854, that Mary was conceived without original sin.
  • Assumption – the dogma of 1950 that Mary was assumed body and soul into heaven.

Biblical and liturgical epithets

Many titles come from Scripture and liturgy: Full of Grace (from the Annunciation), Blessed among Women, New Eve, Ark of the Covenant, Star of the Sea (Stella Maris), Queen of Heaven, Mother of the Church (proclaimed by Pope Paul VI in 1964), and the many invocations of the Litany of Loreto such as Mirror of Justice, Seat of Wisdom, Mystical Rose, Tower of David, House of Gold, and Refuge of Sinners.

Titles from apparitions and places

Numerous titles identify Mary with places of apparition or veneration, including Our Lady of Guadalupe (Mexico), Our Lady of Lourdes (France), Our Lady of Fátima (Portugal), Our Lady of Częstochowa (Poland), Our Lady of Aparecida (Brazil), and Our Lady of Walsingham (England).

Orthodox titles

In Eastern Orthodoxy, titles include Theotokos, Panagia ("All-Holy"), and the Platytera ("Wider than the Heavens"). The Akathist hymn supplies many poetic epithets, such as "Bride unwedded".
