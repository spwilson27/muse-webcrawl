Ill-posed problem

The mathematical term well-posed problem stems from a definition given by 20th-century French mathematician Jacques Hadamard. He believed that mathematical models of physical phenomena should have the properties that: (1) a solution exists; (2) the solution is unique; and (3) the solution's behaviour changes continuously with the initial conditions. A problem that violates any of these is called ill-posed.

Examples

Classic ill-posed problems include the backward heat equation (reconstructing an earlier temperature distribution — solutions depend discontinuously on the data) and most inverse problems, such as deblurring images, gravitational prospecting, and computing the derivative of noisy data, where measurement noise is amplified without bound.

Regularisation

Ill-posed problems are typically handled by regularisation, which imposes additional assumptions — smoothness, sparsity, or a prior distribution — to stabilise the solution. A standard method is Tikhonov regularization, named after Andrey Tikhonov, which balances fidelity to the data against a penalty on the solution's size or roughness. Bayesian formulations treat the same idea as maximum a posteriori estimation.
