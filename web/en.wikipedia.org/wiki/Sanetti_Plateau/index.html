Sanetti Plateau

The Sanetti Plateau is a high-altitude plateau in the Bale Mountains of southeastern Ethiopia, within Bale Mountains National Park. Lying at elevations between roughly 3,800 and 4,377 metres (the summit of Tullu Demtu, Ethiopia's second-highest mountain), it is one of the largest areas of Afroalpine habitat in Africa. A road crossing the plateau between Goba and Dolo Mena is among the highest all-weather roads on the continent.

Ecology

The plateau's Afroalpine moorland is characterised by giant lobelias, everlasting flowers (Helichrysum), and tussock grasses, dotted with small glacial tarns left by the last ice age. It is famous for its population of the Ethiopian wolf, the world's rarest canid, which hunts the plateau's abundant rodents — especially the giant mole-rat — and is the flagship species of the national park's highlands.

Wildlife

Besides the Ethiopian wolf, the plateau supports mountain nyala (more typical of the park's forests), giant mole-rats, Stark's hares, and numerous rodents, including the Blick's grass rat, that form the wolf's prey base. Raptors such as the bearded vulture (lammergeier), augur buzzard, and steppe eagle soar over the plateau, and the tarns attract blue-winged geese, ruddy shelducks, and spot-breasted lapwings, the latter endemic to Ethiopia.

Climate

Nights on the plateau are cold year-round, with temperatures frequently dropping below freezing, while intense solar radiation warms the days — a climate often described as "winter every night, spring every day". The area is a crucial water tower, feeding rivers that flow to the surrounding lowlands.
