Beats per minute

Beats per minute is used for both musical tempo and heart rate.

Beats per minute (BPM or bpm) is a unit used to measure tempo in music and heart rate. A BPM measurement of one beat per minute equals 1/60 Hz. The unit is most commonly encountered in music, where it quantifies the speed of a piece, and in medicine and fitness, where it describes the frequency of a heartbeat.

In music

In musical terminology, tempo is traditionally described with Italian words such as allegro or andante, but modern music — especially electronic dance music — is usually specified numerically in beats per minute. A metronome marking such as ♩ = 120 means that 120 quarter-note beats occur per minute. Different genres tend to occupy characteristic tempo ranges: hip hop often falls between 80 and 115 BPM, house music around 118–135 BPM, and drum and bass around 160–180 BPM.

Digital audio workstations, DJ software and streaming services routinely analyse and display the BPM of tracks, because matching tempos is essential for beatmatching and seamless mixing.

In medicine

A resting heart rate for a healthy adult typically lies between 60 and 100 beats per minute. Rates below 60 BPM in a resting person are termed bradycardia, and rates above 100 BPM tachycardia, although trained athletes commonly have resting rates well below 60 BPM. Maximum heart rate is often estimated with formulas such as 220 minus age.
