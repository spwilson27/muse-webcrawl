Special Olympics provides year-round sports training and competition for people with intellectual disabilities.

Special Olympics is a global organization that serves athletes with intellectual disabilities, providing year-round sports training and athletic competition in a variety of Olympic-type sports for children and adults. Founded in 1968 by Eunice Kennedy Shriver, it has grown to reach several million athletes in more than 170 countries, with regional and national programs offering local training and competitions in addition to the flagship World Games.

History

Shriver, whose sister Rosemary had an intellectual disability, began with a summer day camp, Camp Shriver, at her Maryland home in 1962. The first International Special Olympics Games were held at Soldier Field in Chicago on 20 July 1968, with about 1,000 athletes from the United States and Canada. Shriver announced the founding of Special Olympics, Inc. at the event. The movement is recognized by the International Olympic Committee — uniquely among organizations, it was granted permission in 1988 to use the word "Olympics" in its name.

World Games

The Special Olympics World Games alternate between summer and winter editions every two years. Summer Games have been held in cities including Los Angeles (2015) and Berlin (2023); Winter Games in Austria (2017) and Turin (2025). The Games feature opening ceremonies, a Law Enforcement Torch Run, and host-town programs, and are among the largest sporting events in the world by participation.

Programs

Beyond competition, Special Olympics runs Healthy Athletes (free health screening), Unified Sports (teams mixing athletes with and without disabilities), Young Athletes for ages 2–7, and athlete leadership programs. Its oath, "Let me win. But if I cannot win, let me be brave in the attempt," was coined by Shriver at the 1968 Games. The organization should not be confused with the Paralympics, which primarily serves elite athletes with physical disabilities.
