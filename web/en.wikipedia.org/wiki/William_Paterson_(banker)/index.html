William Paterson

BornApril 1658, Tinwald, Dumfriesshire, Scotland
Died22 January 1719 (aged 60), Westminster, London, England
OccupationMerchant, banker, writer
Known forFounding the Bank of England; the Darien scheme

William Paterson (April 1658 – 22 January 1719) was a Scottish trader and banker, best known as the founder of the Bank of England and as the promoter of the disastrous Darien scheme, Scotland's attempt to establish a colony in Panama.

Bank of England

Paterson made a fortune in trade, spending time in the Netherlands, Germany and the West Indies. In 1691 he proposed that the English government charter a bank whose subscribers would lend £1.2 million to the state in return for incorporation as the Governor and Company of the Bank of England. The scheme was enacted in 1694 amid the financial pressures of war with France, and Paterson served as a director during the Bank's first year before resigning after a dispute with his fellow directors.

Darien scheme

Paterson then turned to his native Scotland with a grander plan: a free-trade colony at Darien on the Isthmus of Panama, which he imagined as an entrepôt linking Atlantic and Pacific trade. The Company of Scotland raised an enormous subscription — a large fraction of Scotland's liquid wealth — and despatched expeditions in 1698 and 1699. Paterson sailed with the first expedition, on which his wife and child died. Starved of supplies, opposed by Spain, and refused English support, the colony collapsed with the loss of some two thousand lives and the savings of much of the Scottish nation.

Later years

The Darien disaster deepened the crisis that led to the Anglo-Scottish Union of 1707, which Paterson — calculating compensation for Scottish investors — supported. He spent his last years in London writing on trade, currency and public credit, and died in 1719.
