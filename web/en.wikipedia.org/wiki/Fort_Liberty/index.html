Fort Liberty

Fort Liberty is a United States Army installation in North Carolina, near Fayetteville and Spring Lake. Established in 1918 as Camp Bragg, it was known for most of its history as Fort Bragg, named for Confederate general Braxton Bragg. It was redesignated Fort Liberty on 2 June 2023 following the recommendations of the Congressional Naming Commission; in February 2025 it was renamed Fort Bragg once more, this time in honour of Private First Class Roland L. Bragg, a World War II paratrooper.

Whatever its name, the post is one of the largest military installations in the world by population, home to more than 50,000 military personnel. It is the headquarters of the U.S. Army Forces Command (FORSCOM), the U.S. Army Reserve Command, the XVIII Airborne Corps, the 82nd Airborne Division, the U.S. Army Special Operations Command and the Joint Special Operations Command, along with adjacent Pope Field.

History

Camp Bragg was established in 1918 as an artillery training ground, chosen for its mild climate, terrain and rail access. It became a permanent post, Fort Bragg, in 1922. During World War II it expanded enormously and became a centre of airborne training; the 82nd and 101st Airborne Divisions trained there. In the Cold War the post became synonymous with airborne and special operations forces: the Special Warfare Center (now the John F. Kennedy Special Warfare Center and School) was founded in 1952, and the 1st Special Forces Command traces its roots there.

Renaming history

The post's original namesake, Braxton Bragg, was a Confederate general widely criticised even by contemporaries. Under the 2021 National Defense Authorization Act, the Naming Commission recommended a value-based name, and on 2 June 2023 the installation became Fort Liberty — the only one of the nine renamed Army posts to take a value rather than a person as its name. On 10 February 2025, Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth directed the name restored to Fort Bragg, rededicating it to PFC Roland L. Bragg, who earned the Silver Star and Purple Heart at the Battle of the Bulge. Signage and official references changed accordingly; the 2025 change paralleled the one at Fort Benning.
