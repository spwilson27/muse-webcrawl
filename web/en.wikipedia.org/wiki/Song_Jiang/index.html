Song Jiang (Chinese: 宋江; died 1122) was a Chinese historical outlaw leader who became the principal hero of Water Margin, one of the Four Great Classical Novels of Chinese literature. The historical Song Jiang led a band of bandits in the final years of the Northern Song dynasty, active in the regions of modern Shandong, Henan, and Jiangsu, before surrendering to government forces around 1121–1122.

Historical figure

Historical sources, including the History of Song, record that Song Jiang and his 36 companions ranged widely and defeated government troops, causing the court considerable embarrassment before accepting amnesty. Some accounts associate him, after his surrender, with campaigns against the rebel Fang La — a connection the novel adopts. Details of his life and death are sparse and disputed.

In Water Margin

In the novel, Song Jiang is a clerk of Yuncheng County famed for generosity, earning the nicknames "Timely Rain" and "Filial and Righteous Dark Third Master". Forced to flee after killing his mistress Yan Poxi, he undergoes many adventures before joining the outlaw stronghold at Liangshan Marsh, which he eventually leads as chief of the 108 Stars of Destiny. Song Jiang's defining trait is loyalty to the Song emperor: instead of founding his own regime, he seeks and obtains imperial amnesty, then leads the Liangshan army against the Liao dynasty and the rebel Fang La at great cost. Afterwards, corrupt officials have him poisoned; he dies at Chuzhou, a tragic emblem of loyalty betrayed. Critics have long debated his character — folk hero of justice, or a feudal loyalist who squandered the rebellion.

Legacy

Song Jiang remains a staple figure of Chinese opera, storytelling, film, and television adaptations of Water Margin, and his Liangshan base in Shandong is a major tourist site. His story's folk motto, "enforcing justice on behalf of Heaven" (替天行道), is associated with his banner at Liangshan.
