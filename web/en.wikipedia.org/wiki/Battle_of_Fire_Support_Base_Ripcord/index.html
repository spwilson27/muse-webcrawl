The Battle of Fire Support Base Ripcord was a 23-day battle between elements of the U.S. 101st Airborne Division and two reinforced divisions of the People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN) in July 1970, during the Vietnam War.

Background

Fire Support Base Ripcord was established on a remote hilltop near the A Shau Valley as part of Operation Texas Star, intended to support a renewed offensive against North Vietnamese logistics routes.

The battle

From 1 July the base came under heavy mortar fire and repeated assaults. U.S. forces relied on artillery and massive air support, including B-52 strikes. On 23 July, after suffering heavy casualties, the Americans evacuated the base by helicopter; the position was then blasted by B-52s.

Aftermath

U.S. losses were over 70 killed; PAVN losses were estimated in the hundreds. The battle, little publicised at the time, was one of the last major engagements involving U.S. ground combat units in Vietnam.
