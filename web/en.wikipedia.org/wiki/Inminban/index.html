Inminban

Inminban (Korean: 인민반, "neighbourhood units" or "people's units") are neighbourhood-sized administrative and surveillance organizations in North Korea. Each inminban consists of roughly 20 to 40 families living in the same block or small area, headed by an inminbanjang ("people's unit head"), typically a middle-aged woman.

Functions

The inminban system organizes residents for local tasks such as cleaning, maintenance, and mobilization for public works, and distributes information and goods. It also keeps records on residents and visitors: households must report overnight guests, and the inminbanjang reports unusual activity to the authorities, making the system an important instrument of social control and monitoring.

Origins

The structure was established during and after the Korean War, drawing partly on colonial-era neighbourhood associations and on models in other socialist states, and it became a pervasive feature of everyday life in North Korean towns and cities.
