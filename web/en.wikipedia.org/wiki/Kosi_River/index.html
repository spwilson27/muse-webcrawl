Kosi River

The Kosi or Koshi is a transboundary river that flows through China, Nepal, and India. It is a tributary of the Ganges and drains the northern slopes of the Himalayas in Tibet and the southern slopes in Nepal. From a major confluence of tributaries north of the Chatra Gorge onwards, the Kosi River is also known as the Saptakoshi ("Seven Kosis") for its seven upper tributaries.

Geography

The river basin spans about 69,300 km² and includes some of the world's tallest mountains, among them Mount Everest, Kangchenjunga, Lhotse, Makalu, and Cho Oyu. Major tributaries include the Sun Kosi, Tama Kosi, Dudh Kosi, Arun, Tamor, Likhu, and Indravati. After debouching from the hills at Chatra, the river fans out across the plains of the Terai and northern Bihar in a huge alluvial megafan, one of the largest in the world.

Flooding

The Kosi has long been notorious for devastating floods and for shifting its course westward by more than 100 kilometres over the past two centuries, earning it the epithet "Sorrow of Bihar". In August 2008 a breach in the embankment at Kusaha in Nepal diverted most of the flow into an old channel, inundating districts of Nepal and Bihar and displacing millions of people.

Flood control and development

The Kosi Agreement of 1954 between India and Nepal led to construction of the Kosi barrage at Bhimnagar and embankments along the river. Irrigation and hydropower projects, including proposals for a high dam at Barahkshetra, remain subjects of bilateral negotiation and local debate.
