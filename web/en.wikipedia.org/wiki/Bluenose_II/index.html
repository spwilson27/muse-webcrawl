Bluenose II

Bluenose II under sail

Bluenose II is a replica of the famous Canadian racing and fishing schooner Bluenose, launched in 1963. Built at Lunenburg, Nova Scotia, in the same yard and from the same plans as the original, she serves as a sailing ambassador for Nova Scotia and as a memorial to the original vessel, which was wrecked off Haiti in 1946.

The original Bluenose

The original Bluenose was launched in 1921 and became famous as the undefeated champion of the International Fishermen's Trophy races against American schooners. She became a beloved Canadian symbol and has appeared on the Canadian dime since 1937. After her racing career she was sold as a freighter and lost on a reef near Haiti.

Construction and service

Bluenose II was built by the Oland family, owners of Moosehead Breweries, at a cost of about $300,000, and was presented to Nova Scotia in 1971 for one dollar. She is 49.7 metres (163 ft) long overall, wooden-hulled, and carries a crew that offers deck tours and sailing excursions from her summer home port of Lunenburg. She also represents the province at tall ship festivals and international events.

Refit

Between 2010 and 2012 Bluenose II underwent a major restoration — effectively a rebuild with new frames, planking, and systems — at Lunenburg. The project ran over budget and drew public scrutiny, but the schooner returned to service and continues her ambassadorial role, remaining one of Canada's most recognisable vessels.
