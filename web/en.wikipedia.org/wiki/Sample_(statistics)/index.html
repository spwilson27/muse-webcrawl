Sample (statistics)

Field: Statistics

Related concepts: Population, sampling, sample size, sampling bias

In statistics, a sample is a subset of individuals, items or observations drawn from a larger statistical population. Research typically studies a sample because measuring an entire population is impractical, expensive or impossible; the goal is to draw conclusions about the population from the sample.

Representative samples

For inferences to be valid, a sample should be representative of the population. In a simple random sample, every member of the population has an equal probability of selection, which avoids systematic sampling bias. Other designs include stratified sampling, cluster sampling and systematic sampling.

Sample size

The size of a sample, denoted n, affects the precision of estimates: larger samples generally yield smaller margins of error, though with diminishing returns. Sample size calculations balance statistical power, confidence level and the variability of the quantity being measured.

Statistics computed from samples

Quantities computed from sample data — such as the sample mean, sample variance and sample proportion — are called statistics and serve as estimates of the corresponding population parameters. The sampling distribution of a statistic describes how it would vary across repeated samples, and underlies confidence intervals and hypothesis tests.
