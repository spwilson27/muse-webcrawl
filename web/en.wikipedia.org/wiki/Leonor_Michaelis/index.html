Leonor Michaelis

Leonor Michaelis
Born: 16 January 1875, Berlin, German Empire
Died: 8 October 1949, New York City
Known for: Michaelis–Menten kinetics

Leonor Michaelis (16 January 1875 – 8 October 1949) was a German biochemist, physical chemist and physician. He is best known for his work with Maud Menten on enzyme kinetics, which produced the Michaelis–Menten equation, one of the foundational models of biochemistry.

Career

Michaelis studied medicine at Freiburg and Berlin, and worked in the laboratories of Paul Ehrlich and others before taking a post at Berlin's Charité hospital. Barred from a full professorship in Germany, he spent time as a professor at Nagoya University in Japan (1922–1926), then at Johns Hopkins University, and from 1929 at the Rockefeller Institute in New York, where he remained for the rest of his career.

Work

The 1913 paper by Michaelis and Maud Menten, "Die Kinetik der Invertinwirkung", applied rigorous mathematics to enzyme action, introducing the concept of an enzyme–substrate complex and the constant now denoted Km. Earlier, Michaelis had introduced the use of hydrogen-ion concentration (pH) buffers in biochemical measurement — Sorensen's pH scale followed his advocacy — and performed influential work on oxidation–reduction potentials in biological systems and on the permeability of cell membranes. He also described the Michaelis–Gutmann bodies of malakoplakia.
