Battle of Mosul (2016–2017)

Date16 October 2016 – 20 July 2017
LocationMosul, Nineveh Governorate, Iraq
ResultIraqi and coalition victory
BelligerentsIraq and allies vs Islamic State

The Battle of Mosul was a major military campaign launched by the Iraqi government forces and allied militias, the Kurdistan Regional Government, and international forces to retake the city of Mosul from the Islamic State (ISIL), which had seized it in June 2014. The operation, codenamed "We Are Coming, Nineveh", began on 16 October 2016, and Iraqi prime minister Haider al-Abadi declared the city liberated on 10 July 2017, after some nine months of fighting.

Course of the battle

A force of roughly 100,000 Iraqi-led troops advanced on the city from several axes, supported by coalition air strikes. Iraqi special forces (the Golden Division) entered eastern Mosul in November 2016; the eastern bank was fully secured by late January 2017 after months of block-by-block fighting. The offensive on western Mosul began in February, culminating in a grinding battle for the Old City and the al-Nuri Mosque, which ISIL destroyed in June 2017.

Humanitarian impact

The battle caused extensive destruction, especially in western Mosul, displaced around a million civilians, and killed thousands of civilians through fighting, airstrikes, and ISIL executions; estimates of total civilian deaths vary widely, from about 9,000 upward. It was among the largest urban battles since the Second World War.
