Marriage settlement

A marriage settlement was a legal arrangement, common among landed families in Britain from the 17th to the 19th century, by which property was settled on a couple at their marriage in order to provide for the wife, the children and the orderly descent of the estate. It was typically created by a deed of settlement executed before or shortly after the wedding.

Terms

A strict settlement combined the groom's family estate with the bride's dowry (portion), reserving a jointure or pin money for the wife, portions for younger children, and an entail keeping the core estate together for the eldest son. Trustees held the legal estate to enforce the scheme.

Decline

The system was reshaped by the Settled Land Acts of the Victorian era and largely superseded after the property legislation of 1925, though trusts of land remain common. "Marriage settlement" is also used more loosely for the financial terms agreed on marriage, comparable to a modern prenuptial agreement.
