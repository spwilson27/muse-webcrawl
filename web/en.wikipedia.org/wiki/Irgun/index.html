The Irgun (Hebrew: ארגון; full title: הארגון הצבאי הלאומי בארץ ישראל, HaIrgun HaTzva'i HaLe'umi BeEretz Yisra'el, lit. "The National Military Organization in the Land of Israel"), also known by the acronym Etzel (אצ״ל), was a Zionist paramilitary organisation that operated in Mandatory Palestine between 1931 and 1948. It was an offshoot of the older and larger Jewish paramilitary organisation Haganah.

History

The Irgun was founded in 1931 by Avraham Tehomi and others who broke away from the Haganah, objecting to its policy of restraint (havlagah) in response to Arab attacks on Jewish communities. Influenced by the Revisionist Zionism of Ze'ev Jabotinsky, the organisation held that a Jewish state should be established on both banks of the Jordan River and that armed force was a legitimate means to achieve it.

Activities

During the 1936–1939 Arab revolt in Palestine, the Irgun carried out reprisal attacks. After the publication of the British White Paper of 1939, which severely restricted Jewish immigration, the Irgun turned its attention to British targets. In 1944, under the command of Menachem Begin, it proclaimed a revolt against the British administration. The 1946 bombing of the King David Hotel in Jerusalem, the British administrative headquarters, killed 91 people and remains one of the most controversial episodes of the period. The British authorities and the Jewish Agency characterised the Irgun as a terrorist organisation, a label its members rejected, describing themselves as a national liberation movement.

The Saison and the Altalena affair

The mainstream Jewish Agency opposed the Irgun's campaign, and during the "Saison" (1944–45) the Haganah cooperated with the British against it. After the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948, the Irgun was ordered to disband and join the new Israel Defense Forces. The confrontation over the arms ship Altalena in June 1948, in which the IDF shelled the vessel, marked the violent end of the organisation as an independent force.

Legacy

After disbanding, Begin and other former members founded the Herut political party, a predecessor of the modern Likud party. The Irgun remains a subject of historical debate regarding the ethics and effectiveness of its campaign and its role in the end of the British Mandate.
