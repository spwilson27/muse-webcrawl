Hydroforming

Hydroforming is a metal forming process in which a high-pressure hydraulic fluid is used to press a workpiece — sheet or tube — into a die at room temperature. It allows complex, lightweight, structurally rigid shapes to be produced from a single piece of metal.

Processes

In tube hydroforming, a hollow tube is placed in a die and pressurised internally so it expands to the die's shape; it is widely used for automotive frame rails, engine cradles and exhaust parts. In sheet hydroforming, fluid pressure (or a fluid-filled bladder) presses sheet metal against a single-sided die, reducing tooling costs.

Advantages

Hydroforming can reduce part counts and welds, improve strength-to-weight ratios, and produce smooth contours, at the cost of slower cycle times and high-pressure equipment.
