Maremma

The Maremma is a coastal region of western Italy bordering the Tyrrhenian Sea, spanning southwestern Tuscany and northern Lazio. Once a malarial marshland, it is now known for agriculture, wine, beaches and the Maremma Regional Park.

Geography

The Maremma covers roughly the province of Grosseto and parts of Livorno and Viterbo provinces, drained by the Ombrone River. Hills such as the Colline Metallifere ("metal-bearing hills") rise inland, mined since Etruscan times.

History

Etruscans and Romans settled the area, but marshland and malaria drove decline in the Middle Ages. Drainage schemes begun under the Medici and completed in the 19th–20th centuries — celebrated in the works of the Grand Duke Leopold II — reclaimed the plain. The butteri, Maremma's cowboys, and the Maremmano horse and Maremma Sheepdog are emblems of the region.

Wine and tourism

The region produces Morellino di Scansano and other Tuscan wines, and its coastline and nature reserves attract visitors in summer. The Maremma Regional Park (Parco dell'Uccellina) protects dunes, pine forests and wild boar habitat.
