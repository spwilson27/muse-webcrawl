Bashir Gemayel

Bashir Pierre Gemayel (10 November 1947 – 14 September 1982) was a Lebanese politician and militia commander who led the Lebanese Forces during the Lebanese Civil War. Elected President of Lebanon in August 1982, he was assassinated before taking office.

Background

Bashir was the youngest son of Pierre Gemayel, founder of the Kataeb Party (Phalange). Trained as a lawyer, he rose to command the party's militia, and by 1980 had forcibly unified most Christian militias under the Lebanese Forces banner after the Ehden and Safra massacres eliminated rivals.

Presidency and assassination

Following Israel's 1982 invasion of Lebanon and the evacuation of the PLO, parliament elected Gemayel president on 23 August 1982. He sought to rebuild a strong central state and distance himself from his Israeli allies. On 14 September 1982, nine days before his inauguration, a bomb destroyed the Kataeb headquarters in Ashrafieh, killing him and dozens of others. Habib Shartouni, a member of the Syrian Social Nationalist Party, confessed to planting the bomb. Gemayel's death triggered the Israeli advance into West Beirut and the Sabra and Shatila massacre by Phalangist militiamen.

His elder brother Amine Gemayel was elected president in his place.
