Auto show

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An auto show, also known as a motor show or car show, is a public exhibition of current automobile models, debuts, concept cars, or out-of-production classics. Industry professionals, journalists and the general public attend. Auto shows are important to car manufacturers and local dealers as a public relations exercise, for advertising new products and promoting auto brands. The largest shows, coordinated by the Organisation Internationale des Constructeurs d'Automobiles (OICA), draw hundreds of thousands of visitors and extensive media coverage.

Major shows

The traditionally dominant "Big Five" shows were the North American International Auto Show in Detroit, the Geneva International Motor Show, the Frankfurt Motor Show (IAA, now held in Munich), the Paris Motor Show and the Tokyo Motor Show. Since the 2000s, the Beijing and Shanghai shows have grown with the Chinese market, and the industry's shift toward consumer electronics and direct-to-consumer sales has reduced some legacy shows' importance; Geneva was discontinued after 2024.

Format

Shows typically open with press days at which manufacturers stage reveals of production models and concepts, followed by public days. Displays range from mass-market cars that visitors can sit in to roped-off concept vehicles and heritage collections. Regional and specialist shows focus on classics, modified cars, commercial vehicles or electric vehicles.

History

The first motor shows date to the late 1890s — the Paris Salon de l'Automobile of 1898 is among the earliest — and the first New York Auto Show was held in 1900. National shows became fixtures of the industry calendar during the twentieth century, peaking in spectacle during the 1950s era of dream cars and elaborate manufacturer displays.
