Ayeyarwady River

The Ayeyarwady River, also spelled Irrawaddy River, is the largest river in Myanmar (Burma) and its most important commercial waterway. It flows from north to south through the centre of the country for about 2,170 kilometres (1,350 miles) before dividing into a wide delta that empties into the Andaman Sea.

Course

The river begins in Kachin State at the confluence of the N'mai and Mali rivers, themselves fed by glaciers in the mountains near the border with Tibet. It flows south past Myitkyina, Bhamo, and Mandalay, where it is joined by its main tributary, the Chindwin River. Below the ancient capitals around Mandalay and Sagaing — where the Ayeyarwady Bridge and Ava Bridge cross it — the river continues past Bagan and Magway before branching into the distributaries of the Irrawaddy Delta above Yangon.

Importance

The Ayeyarwady basin covers a large share of Myanmar's land area and population. The river supports irrigation for rice in its dry-zone middle course and in the fertile delta, a major rice-exporting region. It remains navigable for much of its length and has historically been the country's principal transport artery — the "Road to Mandalay" of Rudyard Kipling's poem, where the old Irrawaddy Flotilla Company steamers ran. The river is also home to the endangered Irrawaddy dolphin.

Dams

Several hydropower dams have been proposed or built on the river and its headwaters with Chinese involvement. The Myitsone Dam at the confluence of the N'mai and Mali was suspended by President Thein Sein in 2011 after widespread public protest, becoming a symbol of environmental and political contention in Myanmar.
