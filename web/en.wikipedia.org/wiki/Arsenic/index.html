Arsenic is a chemical element with the symbol As and atomic number 33. It occurs in many minerals, usually in combination with sulfur and metals, and as a pure elemental crystal. Arsenic is a metalloid, and its compounds — long notorious as poisons — have been used as pesticides, herbicides and alloying agents, as well as in the semiconductor gallium arsenide.


Native arsenic

Allotropes

The most stable form at room temperature is grey arsenic, a brittle, steel-grey semimetal. Yellow arsenic, made of As4 tetrahedra, and black arsenic are less common allotropes. Arsenic sublimes rather than melts at atmospheric pressure, passing directly to a garlic-smelling vapour at 616 °C.

Compounds and toxicity

Arsenic trioxide (As2O3), a white powder, was historically the poison of choice in homicides and was known as "inheritance powder." Inorganic arsenic compounds are classified as Group 1 carcinogens; chronic exposure through contaminated groundwater — a major public-health problem in Bangladesh and West Bengal — causes skin lesions and cancers of the skin, bladder and lung. Paradoxically, arsenic trioxide is also an effective drug for acute promyelocytic leukaemia.

Uses

Lead alloys for shot and battery grids are hardened with arsenic; chromated copper arsenate was long used to preserve timber (now restricted); and gallium arsenide is a key semiconductor for high-frequency electronics, LEDs and solar cells.
