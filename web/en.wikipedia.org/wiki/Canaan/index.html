Canaan
RegionSouthern Levant
PeriodBronze Age – Iron Age

Canaan was a Semitic-speaking civilisation and region in the ancient Near East, in the southern Levant, during the late second millennium BCE. The name appears in the Hebrew Bible, where its borders are described as extending from Sidon toward Gaza and the Dead Sea, and in Egyptian, Phoenician, and Mesopotamian sources.

History

During the Late Bronze Age, Canaan consisted of a patchwork of city-states — including Hazor, Megiddo, Shechem, and Jerusalem — that were vassals of the New Kingdom of Egypt and frequently appear in the Amarna letters of the fourteenth century BCE. The region's inhabitants, the Canaanites, spoke closely related Northwest Semitic languages and shared a common religious tradition centred on deities such as El, Baal, and Asherah, known in detail from the tablets of Ugarit.

Iron Age transition

Around 1200 BCE the Late Bronze Age collapse transformed the region: the Israelites emerged in the central highlands, the Philistines settled the southern coast, and the coastal Canaanite cities developed into the Phoenician civilisation. Modern genetic studies indicate substantial continuity between Bronze Age Canaanites and later Levantine populations.

Legacy

The Canaanite alphabetic script is the ancestor of the Phoenician, Hebrew, and ultimately Greek and Latin alphabets. In biblical tradition, Canaan is the son of Ham and grandson of Noah, and the land is promised to the descendants of Abraham.
