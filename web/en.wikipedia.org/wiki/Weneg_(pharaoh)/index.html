Weneg

Weneg (also rendered Uneg) was a shadowy ruler of the Early Dynastic Period of ancient Egypt, known from a handful of inscriptions. He may have reigned in the 2nd Dynasty, around the 28th century BCE, although his exact identity and position in the king list are disputed.

Evidence

Weneg's name appears on seal impressions and vessels, most famously on a black schist vessel found in the step pyramid complex of Djoser at Saqqara, and on sealings from the tombs of kings of the 2nd Dynasty. His Horus name, Weneg, is written with a distinctive sign of a "Weneg flower" (a type of papyrus). Some scholars have identified Weneg with the kings Raneb or Sekhemib, or considered him a short-lived ruler between them; others argue he is the same as the king recorded under the nebty name Wadjenes in later king lists.

Legacy

Because the evidence is so fragmentary, Weneg appears in most king lists only tentatively, and modern scholarly consensus remains divided about whether he was an independent king or an alternate name of a better-known ruler. His case illustrates the difficulties of reconstructing the chronology of Egypt's earliest dynasties from scattered seal impressions and later, sometimes contradictory, king lists.
