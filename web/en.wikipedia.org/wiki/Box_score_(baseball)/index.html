Box score (baseball)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A box score is a structured summary of the results of a sporting event. In baseball, the box score is a tabular record that recapitulates the essential statistics of a game: the runs scored in each inning, each player's batting performance, and the principal pitching lines. Baseball box scores have appeared in newspapers since the 19th century and remain the standard compact record of a game.

The box score concept was developed by Henry Chadwick, the pioneering baseball writer, who devised an early version in 1859 and refined it over the following decades; the modern form, with abbreviations for each statistic, became standard in newspapers by the early 20th century.

Contents

A baseball box score typically contains:

  • A line score showing runs per inning for each team, plus total runs (R), hits (H), and errors (E).
  • A batting table for each team listing players in batting order with at bats (AB), runs (R), hits (H), and runs batted in (RBI), along with footnotes for extra-base hits, home runs, stolen bases, and errors.
  • A pitching table listing innings pitched (IP), hits, runs, earned runs (ER), walks (BB), and strikeouts (SO), with the winning pitcher (W), losing pitcher (L), and save (S) noted.
  • Game information: umpires, time of game, and attendance.

Use in other sports

The term "box score" is also used in basketball and other sports for the statistical summary of a game, listing each player's points, rebounds, assists, and other statistics.
