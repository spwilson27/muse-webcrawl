President of Ireland

The president of Ireland (Irish: Uachtarán na hÉireann) is the head of state of Ireland and the supreme commander of the Defence Forces. The office was established by the Constitution of Ireland in 1937. The president is directly elected by Irish citizens for a seven-year term, renewable once, and resides at Áras an Uachtaráin in Dublin's Phoenix Park. Michael D. Higgins has held the office since 2011, winning re-election in 2018.

Functions

The role is mainly ceremonial, but the president has important discretionary powers: referring bills to the Supreme Court to test their constitutionality, refusing a dissolution to a taoiseach who has lost the support of Dáil Éireann, and convening meetings of either house of the Oireachtas. The president appoints the taoiseach on the nomination of the Dáil, appoints other ministers on the taoiseach's advice, signs bills into law, and represents Ireland abroad.

Election

Candidates must be citizens aged 35 or over, nominated by at least 20 members of the Oireachtas or by four county or city councils; former presidents may nominate themselves. Elections use instant-runoff voting. If only one candidate is nominated, no poll is held.

History

Douglas Hyde became the first president in 1938. Later holders include Éamon de Valera, Erskine Childers, Mary Robinson — whose 1990 election transformed the office's public role — and Mary McAleese, the first president from Northern Ireland.
