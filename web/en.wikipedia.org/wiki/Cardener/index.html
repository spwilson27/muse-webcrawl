Cardener

Country: Spain (Catalonia)
Length: ~87 km
Source: Near Castellar de la Ribera, Pyrenean foothills
Mouth: Llobregat at Castellgalí
Main towns: Cardona, Súria, Manresa

The Cardener is a river in Catalonia, Spain, and a right-bank tributary of the Llobregat. It rises in the pre-Pyrenean foothills near Castellar de la Ribera, in the comarca of Solsonès, and flows generally south-east for about 87 kilometres through the comarques of Solsonès, Bages, joining the Llobregat at Castellgalí near Manresa.

Course and towns

Along its course the Cardener passes the towns of Cardona — dominated by its castle and famous for the nearby salt mountain (Muntanya de Sal) — Súria and Callús, both historically associated with potash mining, and the outskirts of Manresa. The river is dammed at the Sant Ponç reservoir, one of the older large reservoirs in Catalonia, used for water supply, irrigation and hydroelectric power.

History and economy

From the 18th century onwards, textile colonies and factories were built along the Cardener and the Llobregat to exploit water power, shaping the industrial landscape of central Catalonia. In the 20th century the valley became a centre of potash mining around Cardona, Súria and Sallent, and the river suffered significant salinity problems from mining waste — an environmental issue addressed by pipelines carrying brine to the Mediterranean. Today the river supplies drinking water to a large part of the Barcelona metropolitan region via the Llobregat system.
