Wave function

In quantum physics, a wave function (or wavefunction) is a mathematical description of the quantum state of an isolated quantum system. The most common symbol is the Greek letter ψ. The wave function is a function of the degrees of freedom (such as positions) and, per Max Born's interpretation, its squared modulus gives a probability distribution for measurement outcomes.

The wave function evolves deterministically by the Schrödinger equation (or, in field theory and relativistic settings, the Dirac/Klein–Gordon equations); 'collapse' on measurement is a postulate of the Copenhagen interpretation and, in other interpretations (many-worlds, pilot wave), is explained or denied. Superposition of wave functions explains interference — the double-slit experiment writ large.
