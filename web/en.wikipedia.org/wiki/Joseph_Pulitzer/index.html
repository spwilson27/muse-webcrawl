Joseph Pulitzer

Joseph Pulitzer
Born10 April 1847, Makó, Hungary
Died29 October 1911
OccupationNewspaper publisher
[Image: A portrait of Pulitzer in his later years]

Joseph Pulitzer (1847–1911) was a Hungarian-American newspaper publisher who built the St. Louis Post-Dispatch and bought the New York World (1883), making it America's largest-circulation paper through crusades, stunts and sensational 'yellow journalism' in rivalry with Hearst. His will endowed Columbia's journalism school and the Pulitzer Prizes (first awarded 1917).

Career

Pulitzer emigrated in 1864, served in the Union cavalry, and rose in St. Louis politics and papers before New York. The World's campaigns (including funding the Statue of Liberty's pedestal by public subscription in 1885) mixed reform with showmanship; his later blindness and reclusiveness did not stop his editorial control by telephone and memo.
