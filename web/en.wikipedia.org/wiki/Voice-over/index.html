Voice-over

Voice-over (also known as off-camera or off-stage commentary) is a production technique where a voice — that is not part of the visual narrative — is used in a radio, television production, filmmaking, theatre, or other presentation. The voice-over is read from a script and may be spoken by someone who appears elsewhere in the production or by a specialist voice actor.

Uses

Voice-over is used in documentaries to explain or comment on footage, in film and television as narration that conveys a character's thoughts or backstory, in animation to give voices to characters, in advertising, and in news broadcasts. It is distinct from dialogue: the speaker is typically not seen speaking, and the track is added in post-production.

Techniques

In documentary filmmaking, an omniscient narrator may provide context. Dialogue replacement (dubbing) uses voice-over translation in foreign-language versions, a practice formalized in United Nations-style voice-over in news. Voice actors record in studios with close microphone technique, and editors synchronize the recording with the picture.

History

Radio drama pioneered the technique; early sound films such as newsreels adopted narrators in the 1930s. Film noir made first-person voice-over a signature device, and modern streaming media and video games have greatly expanded the market for professional voice talent.
