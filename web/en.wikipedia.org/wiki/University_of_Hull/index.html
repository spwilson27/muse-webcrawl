University of Hull

University
Founded: 1927 (as University College Hull); royal charter 1954
Location: Kingston upon Hull, England
Students: about 16,000
Motto: Lampada Ferens ("Bearing the Torch")

The University of Hull is a public research university in Kingston upon Hull, England. It was founded in 1927 as University College Hull and received its royal charter in 1954, becoming England's 14th university.

History

The college was established through the efforts of Thomas Ferens, a local industrialist and MP, who donated land and funds; the Duke of York (later George VI) laid the foundation stone in 1928. Early students took University of London external degrees until the 1954 charter allowed Hull to award its own.

Research and alumni

Hull is known for the invention of liquid crystal displays: George Gray's chemistry group developed the stable cyanobiphenyl compounds in the 1970s that made LCD screens practical. The university also houses the Wilberforce Institute for the study of Slavery and Emancipation. The poet Philip Larkin was its librarian from 1955 to 1985, and the Brynmor Jones Library holds his archive. Alumni include former Deputy Prime Minister John Prescott and broadcaster and computer scientist Muir Hunter's contemporaries in law.

Campus

The main campus is on Cottingham Road in northwest Hull; Hull York Medical School is run jointly with the University of York.
