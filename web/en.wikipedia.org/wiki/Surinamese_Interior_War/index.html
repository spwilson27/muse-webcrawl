Surinamese Interior War

The Surinamese Interior War (Dutch: Binnenlandse Oorlog) was a civil conflict fought in the rainforest interior of Suriname between 1986 and 1992. It pitted the military regime of Dési Bouterse against the Jungle Commando, a guerrilla group led by Ronnie Brunswijk and composed largely of Maroon fighters, principally from the Ndyuka people.

Course of the war

The war began in July 1986 when Brunswijk's men attacked an army post near Stolkertsijver. Fighting destroyed villages and infrastructure in eastern Suriname and displaced thousands of Maroons, many of whom fled across the Marowijne River into French Guiana. In November 1986 government forces massacred dozens of civilians, mostly women and children, at the village of Moiwana, an atrocity for which Suriname was later condemned by the Inter-American Court of Human Rights.

Resolution

Peace negotiations produced the Kourou accord in 1989, which later collapsed, but a final agreement was signed in August 1992, ending the war. Brunswijk and Bouterse both later became major figures in Surinamese electoral politics.
