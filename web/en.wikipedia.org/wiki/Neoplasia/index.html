Neoplasia

Neoplasia is the process of abnormal and excessive growth of tissue, and the resulting mass is called a neoplasm or tumour. The growth of a neoplasm is uncoordinated with that of normal tissues and persists in growing abnormally even after the stimulus that evoked it is removed.

Types

Neoplasms may be benign, which grow locally without invading surrounding tissue or spreading; potentially malignant; or malignant, which are known as cancers, invade and destroy adjacent structures and can metastasize to distant sites. The branch of medicine concerned with the study of neoplasms is oncology.

Causes

Neoplasia arises from genetic and epigenetic changes that disrupt the regulation of cell division, apoptosis and differentiation. Causes include carcinogenic chemicals, ionizing and ultraviolet radiation, certain viruses such as human papillomavirus, chronic inflammation and inherited mutations in tumour suppressor genes or oncogenes.
