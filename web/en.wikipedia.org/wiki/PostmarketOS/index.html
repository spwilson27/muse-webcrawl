PostmarketOS

postmarketOS running a mobile Linux interface on a smartphone.

postmarketOS (abbreviated pmOS) is a free and open-source operating system based on Alpine Linux, designed primarily for smartphones and other mobile devices. Announced in 2017, the project aims to give smartphones a ten-year life cycle, countering the short support periods of vendor Android builds by providing a conventional Linux distribution that can be maintained and updated like one for desktop computers.

Design

postmarketOS avoids Android's software stack in favour of standard Linux components, with device-specific packages built around a single kernel per device wherever possible and a long-term goal of running mainline Linux kernels. The system is installed using its pmbootstrap tool, which cross-compiles and flashes images from a computer. Users can choose between several mobile interfaces, including Phosh, KDE Plasma Mobile, Sxmo and GNOME-based environments, as well as console-only installs for development.

Device support

The project supports hundreds of devices in categories ranging from stable daily-driver use to testing and downstream forks of abandoned hardware. Support quality varies widely: on many devices basic functions such as calls, mobile data or cameras may be incomplete because of proprietary drivers and firmware. Devices designed for Linux, such as the PinePhone and Librem 5, are supported particularly well and have shipped with postmarketOS editions.

Reception and goals

The project has been noted in discussions of electronic waste and software sustainability: by decoupling security updates from the original manufacturer, it seeks to extend the usable life of phones that would otherwise be discarded once vendor support ends. It is developed by a community of volunteers and funded through donations.
