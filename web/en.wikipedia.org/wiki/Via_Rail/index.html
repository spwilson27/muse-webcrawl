Via Rail Canada Inc. (reporting mark VIA), operating as Via Rail or Via, is a Canadian Crown corporation mandated to operate intercity passenger rail service across Canada. It was created in 1977 by the federal government to take over the passenger services of Canadian National and Canadian Pacific, and it runs trains on roughly 12,500 km of track, most of it owned by freight railways.

Services

Via's busiest services run in the Quebec City–Windsor Corridor, where frequent trains link Quebec City, Montreal, Ottawa, Toronto and Windsor. Long-distance trains include The Canadian, the transcontinental service between Toronto and Vancouver famous for its stainless-steel dome cars, and The Ocean between Montreal and Halifax, one of the oldest continuously operated named trains in North America. Via also operates regional and remote services, such as trains to Churchill, Manitoba, which provide essential links for communities without road access.

History

Passenger rail in Canada declined sharply after the Second World War under competition from cars and airlines. The government consolidated the remaining services under Via in 1978, but major cuts in 1981 and especially in 1990 halved the network. Since then Via has focused on the Corridor, where it carries the great majority of its roughly four to five million annual passengers. A new fleet of Siemens-built trainsets began entering Corridor service in the 2020s, and the federal government has pursued plans for higher-frequency or high-speed rail in the corridor (the Alto project).
