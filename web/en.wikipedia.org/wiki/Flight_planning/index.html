Flight planning

Flight planning is the process of producing a flight plan to describe a proposed aircraft flight. It involves two safety-critical aspects: fuel calculation, to ensure that the aircraft can carry enough fuel to reach its destination safely, and compliance with air traffic control requirements, to minimise the risk of midair collision and to fit the flight efficiently into the airspace system.

In addition, flight planning for commercial aviation usually seeks to minimise flight cost through appropriate choice of route, height, speed and the amount of fuel carried. Flight plans are filed with air navigation service providers before departure and are used by air traffic controllers to provide separation and routing services.

Elements of a flight plan

  • Aircraft identification and type
  • Departure and destination aerodromes, and alternate aerodromes
  • Route of flight, including waypoints, airways and estimated times en route
  • Cruising altitude or flight level and airspeed
  • Fuel on board, expressed as endurance
  • Number of persons on board and emergency equipment

Fuel planning

Fuel planning regulations typically require carriage of taxi fuel, trip fuel, contingency fuel, alternate fuel, final reserve fuel and any extra fuel required by conditions or company policy. Modern computerised flight planning systems compute fuel burn using forecast winds and temperatures aloft, aircraft performance data and cost index values.

Route planning

General aviation pilots historically planned routes manually using aeronautical charts, plotters and flight computers such as the E6B. Today, electronic flight bags and flight-planning software generate navigation logs automatically. Airlines employ licensed flight dispatchers or flight operations officers who share legal responsibility with the captain for the safe dispatch of the flight.
