Isotopes of helium

Stable isotopes: ³He, ⁴He

Standard atomic weight: 4.002602

Known isotopes: ²He to ¹⁰He

Although there are nine known isotopes of helium (₂He), only helium-3 (³He) and helium-4 (⁴He) are stable. All radioisotopes are short-lived, the longest-lived being ⁶He with a half-life of about 807 milliseconds. In Earth's atmosphere, the ratio of ³He to ⁴He is about 1.37 parts per million, with ⁴He overwhelmingly dominant.

Helium-3 and helium-4

Helium-4, composed of two protons and two neutrons, is the product of alpha decay and of stellar fusion; it makes up about a quarter of the ordinary matter in the universe by mass. Helium-3 is rare on Earth but more abundant in lunar regolith. Both isotopes become superfluid at very low temperatures — ⁴He below 2.17 K and ³He below about 2.5 mK — and mixtures of the two power dilution refrigerators used in low-temperature physics.

Exotic isotopes

Helium-2 (the diproton) is an extremely unstable hypothetical isotope that decays almost immediately. The heavier isotopes ⁵He through ¹⁰He are particle-unbound or very short-lived and are produced only in nuclear reactions in laboratories.
