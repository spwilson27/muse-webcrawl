Subaru Telescope

The Subaru Telescope (Japanese: すばる望遠鏡) is an 8.2-metre (320 in) aperture optical-infrared telescope operated by the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan (NAOJ) at the Mauna Kea Observatory on the island of Hawaii. It saw first light in 1999 and is named after the Japanese word for the Pleiades star cluster.

LocationMauna Kea, Hawaii, United States

Design

Subaru's primary mirror is a single monolithic piece of ultra-low-expansion glass 8.2 metres across but only 20 cm thick; its figure is maintained by 261 computer-controlled actuators, giving the telescope exceptional image quality. The telescope supports instruments at four foci: prime, Cassegrain, and two Nasmyth foci.

Instruments and science

Its wide-field instruments, notably the Hyper Suprime-Cam and the earlier Suprime-Cam, have made it a leading facility for deep imaging surveys of distant galaxies, dark matter mapping through weak gravitational lensing, and the direct imaging of exoplanets with the SCExAO/CHARIS coronagraphic system.
