Heartbeat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A heartbeat is one complete cycle of contraction and relaxation of the heart — the periodic squeezing of the cardiac muscle that pumps blood around the body. Each heartbeat comprises systole, when the ventricles contract and eject blood, and diastole, when the heart relaxes and refills. In a resting adult the heart beats about 60–100 times per minute, roughly 100,000 times a day, driven by electrical impulses generated in the heart's own conduction system.

Electrical control

The heartbeat originates in the sinoatrial (SA) node, the heart's natural pacemaker, located in the right atrium. The impulse spreads across the atria, causing them to contract, then passes through the atrioventricular (AV) node — which delays it briefly so the ventricles can fill — and down the bundle of His and Purkinje fibres, coordinating a powerful contraction of the ventricles from apex to base. This electrical activity is recorded clinically as the electrocardiogram (ECG), whose characteristic P wave, QRS complex and T wave correspond to atrial depolarisation, ventricular depolarisation and ventricular repolarisation.

Regulation

Although intrinsically rhythmic, the heartbeat is modulated by the autonomic nervous system: sympathetic stimulation and adrenaline increase rate and contractility during exercise or stress, while the vagus nerve slows the rate at rest. Hormones, blood chemistry, temperature and fitness also influence rate; trained athletes may have resting rates below 50 beats per minute.

Heart rate and rhythm disorders

An abnormally slow heartbeat is bradycardia and an abnormally fast one tachycardia. Disturbances of rhythm — arrhythmias — range from benign ectopic beats to atrial fibrillation, which raises stroke risk, and ventricular fibrillation, which is rapidly fatal without defibrillation. Pacemakers and implantable defibrillators treat many such conditions.

Heart sounds

The familiar "lub-dub" of the heartbeat is produced by valve closure and is described in detail under heart sounds.
