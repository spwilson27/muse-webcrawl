Italian Air Force

The Italian Air Force (Italian: Aeronautica Militare) is the air force of the Italian Republic. It was established as an independent service on 28 March 1923 by King Victor Emmanuel III as the Regia Aeronautica, making Italy one of the first countries with an autonomous air arm, and it adopted its present name after the Second World War.

History

Italian military aviation began in the early 1900s, and Italian aircraft fought in the Italo-Turkish War of 1911 — the first war in which aeroplanes were used in combat — and in the First World War. Under the fascist regime the Regia Aeronautica was greatly expanded and took part in the conquest of Ethiopia and the Spanish Civil War. After 1945 the service was rebuilt within NATO, operating American and European aircraft during the Cold War.

Modern force

Today the Aeronautica Militare operates about 40,000 personnel and several hundred aircraft, including the Eurofighter Typhoon for air defence, the F-35 Lightning II — Italy hosts a final assembly facility at Cameri — the C-130J and C-27J transports, and AMX and Tornado strike aircraft being retired. Its aerobatic display team is the famous Frecce Tricolori. The service also shares responsibility for Italy's space and air defence command activities.
