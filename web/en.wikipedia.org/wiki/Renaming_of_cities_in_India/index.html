Renaming of cities in India

The renaming of cities in India began after the end of British colonial rule in 1947 and continues to the present day. Several major cities were renamed: Bombay became Mumbai, Madras became Chennai, Calcutta became Kolkata, and Bangalore became Bengaluru. Renamings generally sought to restore indigenous pronunciations or pre-colonial names, to honour local languages and deities, or to shed colonial-era spellings.

Process

A renaming proposal is typically initiated by a state government and requires approval by the central government, following clearance from agencies concerned with railways, postal services and mapping. Changes may then take years to be reflected in all official usage.

Notable renamings

  • Bombay → Mumbai (1995)
  • Madras → Chennai (1996)
  • Calcutta → Kolkata (2001)
  • Bangalore → Bengaluru (2014)
  • Allahabad → Prayagraj (2018)
  • Trivandrum → Thiruvananthapuram; Cochin → Kochi; Pondicherry → Puducherry

Controversy

Renamings are sometimes contested as political acts — associated with regional, linguistic or religious identity — and can impose costs on businesses and administration, while supporters argue they restore cultural identity.
