Four Questions

The Four Questions (Yiddish: פֿיר קשיות, fir kashios; Hebrew: מה נשתנה, Mah Nishtana, "What has changed?") are traditionally asked at the Passover Seder by the youngest child present, as part of the Magid section of the Haggadah. The questions prompt the retelling of the Exodus from Egypt, fulfilling the biblical injunction to explain the festival to one's children.

The questions

After the opening line "Why is this night different from all other nights?", the four differences are:

  • On all other nights we eat both leavened bread and matzah; tonight we eat only matzah.
  • On all other nights we eat all kinds of herbs; tonight we eat bitter herbs (maror).
  • On all other nights we do not dip even once; tonight we dip twice (parsley in salt water, maror in charoset).
  • On all other nights we eat sitting or reclining; tonight we all recline.

The Mishnah (Pesachim 10) originally framed the questions around the practices of its own time, including one about roasted meat which was removed after the destruction of the Temple in 70 CE. The Four Questions have a customary melody, and asking them is a widely recognized rite of passage for Jewish children. A traditional joking addition in some families is a "fifth question" — for example, in families of rabbis: "On all other nights, we are asked the Four Questions by our children; tonight, we are asked by our grandchildren."
