The Bala Lake Railway (Welsh: Rheilffordd Llyn Tegid) is a narrow-gauge heritage railway in Gwynedd, north Wales. It runs for 4.5 miles (7.2 km) along the southern shore of Llyn Tegid (Bala Lake) between Llanuwchllyn and the town of Bala, on part of the trackbed of the former standard-gauge Ruabon to Barmouth line of the Great Western Railway.

History

The original line through the Dee valley and along the lake opened in stages in the 1860s–1880s and was closed by British Railways in 1965. Local enthusiast George Barnes and others formed a company to rebuild part of the route as a narrow-gauge tourist railway; the first trains ran in 1972. The railway has since been extended to a station on the outskirts of Bala, with long-term ambitions to reach the town centre.

Operation

The line is worked mainly by a fleet of former industrial steam locomotives, notably Hunslet quarry engines from the slate quarries of north Wales, including Maid Marian, Holy War and Alice, supplemented by diesel locomotives. The headquarters, engine sheds and workshops are at Llanuwchllyn, where the station retains its original Great Western buildings and signal box. Trains operate mainly from spring to autumn, with special events at other times of year.
