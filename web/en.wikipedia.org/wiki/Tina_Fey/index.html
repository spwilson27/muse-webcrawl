Elizabeth Stamatina "Tina" Fey (born May 18, 1970) is an American actress, comedian, writer, and producer. Fey was a cast member and head writer for the NBC sketch comedy series Saturday Night Live from 1997 to 2006. After her departure from SNL, she created the NBC sitcom 30 Rock (2006–2013, 2020) and the Netflix sitcom Unbreakable Kimmy Schmidt (2015–2020).

Early life

Fey was born in Upper Darby Township, Pennsylvania, and discovered comedy through Monty Python, Saturday Night Live, and Second City recordings. She graduated from the University of Virginia in 1992 with a degree in drama, then moved to Chicago, where she trained and performed with The Second City alongside future collaborators including Amy Poehler.

Saturday Night Live

Hired as an SNL writer in 1997, Fey became the show's first female head writer in 1999 and joined the cast in 2000, co-anchoring "Weekend Update" with Jimmy Fallon and later Poehler. She returned as a guest in 2008 to play vice-presidential candidate Sarah Palin, an impersonation that won her an Emmy and became one of the show's most famous political portrayals.

30 Rock and film

30 Rock, loosely based on her SNL experience, starred Fey as head writer Liz Lemon opposite Alec Baldwin. The densely joked, critically beloved series won three consecutive Emmy Awards for Outstanding Comedy Series. Fey wrote the film Mean Girls (2004), adapted from the book Queen Bees and Wannabes, and later adapted it into a Broadway musical (2018) and a 2024 film adaptation of the musical. Her other films include Baby Mama (2008), Date Night (2010), Admission (2013), Whiskey Tango Foxtrot (2016), and voice work in Soul (2020). Her 2011 memoir Bossypants was a bestseller.

Honors

Fey has won nine Primetime Emmy Awards, three Golden Globes, and five Screen Actors Guild Awards. In 2010 she became the youngest recipient of the Mark Twain Prize for American Humor.
