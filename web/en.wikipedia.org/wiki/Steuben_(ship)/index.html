Steuben (ship)

Launched: 1922 (as München)

Fate: Torpedoed and sunk, 10 February 1945

Tonnage: 14,660 GRT

Steuben was a German passenger liner, built in 1922 as the München, that was later renamed General von Steuben and finally Steuben. During World War II she served as an accommodation and transport ship for the German Kriegsmarine. She was torpedoed and sunk by the Soviet submarine S-13 on 10 February 1945 while evacuating German troops and civilians from East Prussia, with the loss of several thousand lives.

History

The ship was launched in 1922 as the München for the Norddeutscher Lloyd line and served on transatlantic routes. After a fire and rebuilding, she was renamed General von Steuben in 1931, after Friedrich Wilhelm von Steuben, the Prussian officer who served in the American Revolutionary War. In 1938 her name was shortened to Steuben.

Sinking

In early 1945, as part of Operation Hannibal, the mass evacuation of German military personnel and civilians from East Prussia ahead of the advancing Red Army, Steuben sailed from Pillau bound for Kiel carrying an estimated 4,000 or more people, including wounded soldiers, medical staff, and refugees. On the night of 9/10 February 1945, the Soviet submarine S-13, under Alexander Marinesko — the same submarine that had sunk the Wilhelm Gustloff days earlier — torpedoed the ship in the Baltic Sea. The Steuben sank within minutes. Estimates of the death toll range from 3,000 to 4,500, making it one of the worst maritime disasters in history. Only a few hundred people were rescued.
