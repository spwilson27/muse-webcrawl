Western Qing tombs

Location: Yi County, Hebei, China
Built: 1730–1915
UNESCO World Heritage Site (part of the Imperial Tombs of the Ming and Qing Dynasties)

The Western Qing tombs (Chinese: 清西陵; pinyin: Qīng Xīlíng) are a necropolis of the Qing dynasty located in Yi County, Hebei, about 140 km southwest of Beijing. Together with the Eastern Qing tombs they contain the burials of the Qing emperors and their families; the western site was inscribed by UNESCO in 2000 as part of the Imperial Tombs of the Ming and Qing Dynasties.

History

The site was established after the Yongzheng Emperor broke with the eastern burial ground chosen by his father and selected a new site at Tai'ning Mountain. Thereafter Qing emperors alternated burial between the eastern and western sites under the so-called zhaomu system. The western tombs comprise four imperial mausolea — Tailing (Yongzheng), Changling (Jiaqing), Muling (Daoguang) and Chongling (Guangxu) — together with tombs for empresses, princes and princesses. Chongling, completed in 1915, was the last imperial tomb built in China; it was looted in 1938.
