Cloisonnism

Cloisonnism is a style of post-Impressionist painting with bold and flat forms separated by dark contours. The term was coined by the critic Édouard Dujardin on the occasion of the Salon des Indépendants in March 1888. The name derives from the cloisonné enamelling technique, in which wires are soldered to a metal surface to form compartments (cloisons) that are filled with coloured enamel paste.

Artists and works

Artists working in this style included Émile Bernard, Paul Gauguin, Louis Anquetin, and Paul Sérusier. Gauguin's Vision of the Sermon (1888), painted at Pont-Aven in Brittany, is a landmark example, with its flat areas of vivid colour bounded by heavy outlines. The style was closely related to Synthetism and influenced the Nabis and later developments in modern art.
