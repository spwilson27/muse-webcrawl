North–South divide

The North–South divide is a socio-economic and political division of Earth popularised in the late 20th century. The Global North broadly comprises wealthier, industrialised countries — North America, Europe, developed East Asia and Australasia — while the Global South comprises developing countries in Africa, Latin America and much of Asia.

Origins

The Brandt Line, proposed in the 1980 Brandt Report, drew a line around the world at approximately 30° north latitude separating rich North from poor South, with Australia and New Zealand as northern exceptions geographically in the south.

Criticism

The model is criticised as oversimplified, given rising incomes in many southern countries, inequality within countries, and the emergence of a "Global East".
