Bermondsey Abbey was an English Cluniac monastery in Bermondsey, in what is now the London Borough of Southwark. Founded in 1082 as a priory of St Saviour, it became an abbey in 1399 and survived until the Dissolution of the Monasteries in 1537. Its site lay just south of the Thames, opposite the City of London, where Bermondsey Square now stands.

Foundation and history

The priory was founded by Aylwin Child, a London merchant, who gave land at Bermondsey and invited monks from the great Cluniac house of La Charité-sur-Loire. William II granted the community the manor of Bermondsey, and the priory church was built at the end of the eleventh century. As an "alien priory" dependent on a French mother house, its estates were seized by the Crown during wars with France, but it escaped suppression and in 1399 Richard II allowed it to become a denizen abbey, ruled by its own abbot.

The abbey grew wealthy from rents and estates across southern England and was involved in reclaiming and embanking the marshy land along the Thames. Queen Elizabeth Woodville, widow of Edward IV, retired to the abbey in 1486 and died there in 1492; she was buried at Windsor. Thomas á Becket is said to have resided at Bermondsey before his election as archbishop.

Dissolution and after

The abbey surrendered to Henry VIII's commissioners in 1537, its income then valued at over £470 a year. The last abbot received a pension. The site was granted to Sir Thomas Pope, who demolished the church and built a mansion, Bermondsey House, on the grounds; the mansion in turn disappeared as Bermondsey developed into a leather-working and industrial district in the nineteenth century.

Archaeology

Excavations around Bermondsey Square and Abbey Street, notably between the 1980s and 2000s in advance of redevelopment, uncovered foundations of the abbey church (a cruciform building about 65 metres long), the chapter house, burials and medieval pottery and leather-working evidence. A small section of remains is preserved and displayed near the site of the modern Bermondsey Square, and the street names Abbey Street and Tower Bridge Road's vicinity retain the memory of the abbey's tower.
