History of Taiwan

The history of Taiwan dates back tens of thousands of years to the earliest known evidence of human habitation. The island's indigenous Austronesian peoples are thought to be the ancestors of the wider Austronesian expansion across the Pacific and Indian Oceans. Taiwan has successively been ruled by the Dutch and Spanish, the Ming loyalist regime of Koxinga, the Qing dynasty, the Empire of Japan, and the Republic of China.

Prehistory and indigenous peoples

Human settlement of Taiwan began at least 20,000 to 30,000 years ago. Neolithic cultures such as the Dapenkeng culture appeared around 6,000 years ago, associated with Austronesian-speaking peoples. Taiwan is widely regarded as the homeland of the Austronesian language family, from which peoples spread to the Philippines, Indonesia, Madagascar and the Pacific islands.

European colonisation

Portuguese sailors passing the island in 1544 called it Ilha Formosa, "beautiful island". The Dutch East India Company established a colony in the southwest from 1624, building Fort Zeelandia near present-day Tainan, while the Spanish occupied the north from 1626 to 1642. In 1662 the Ming loyalist Koxinga (Zheng Chenggong) expelled the Dutch and established the Kingdom of Tungning.

Qing rule

In 1683 Qing forces defeated Koxinga's grandson and annexed Taiwan, initially administering it as a prefecture of Fujian. Han Chinese immigration increased steadily, sometimes in conflict with indigenous communities. Taiwan became a province of the Qing Empire in 1887, with Taipei as its capital.

Japanese rule

Under the Treaty of Shimonoseki of 1895, following the First Sino-Japanese War, Qing China ceded Taiwan to Japan. Japanese rule lasted until 1945 and saw extensive infrastructure development, industrialisation and education, alongside policies of assimilation, especially during the kōminka movement of the late 1930s.

Republic of China

After Japan's defeat in 1945, Taiwan was placed under the administration of the Republic of China. Tensions between the new administration and the local population erupted in the February 28 incident of 1947. In 1949, following defeat in the Chinese Civil War, the ROC government retreated to Taiwan, bringing some two million refugees. Martial law was imposed and lasted until 1987.

Democratisation

From the late 1970s, opposition movements pressed for reform; the Democratic Progressive Party was founded in 1986 and martial law was lifted in 1987. The first direct presidential election was held in 1996. Taiwan has since developed into a multi-party democracy with a high-income, export-oriented economy dominated by technology industries such as semiconductors. Its political status remains disputed, as the People's Republic of China claims Taiwan as part of its territory.
