FIPS 140-3

FIPS PUB 140-3 is a U.S. government computer security standard used to approve cryptographic modules. The title is Security Requirements for Cryptographic Modules. It superseded FIPS 140-2 and was published by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) in March 2019, becoming effective in September 2019.

Overview

The standard specifies security requirements covering areas related to the secure design, implementation, and operation of a cryptographic module, including cryptographic module specification, ports and interfaces, roles and services, finite state model, physical security, operational environment, cryptographic key management, electromagnetic interference/compatibility, self-tests, life-cycle assurance, and mitigation of other attacks.

Security levels

As with its predecessor, FIPS 140-3 defines four increasing qualitative levels of security: Level 1 (basic requirements, production-grade components), Level 2 (adds tamper-evident coatings/seals and role-based authentication), Level 3 (adds tamper resistance, identity-based authentication, and physical/logical separation of data ports), and Level 4 (a complete protective envelope with environmental failure protection).

Relation to ISO/IEC 19790

Unlike earlier FIPS 140 versions, FIPS 140-3 references the international standard ISO/IEC 19790 for its requirements and ISO/IEC 24759 for test methods, with NIST specifying additional approved algorithms and techniques in annexes and in the SP 800-140 series of special publications.

Validation

Modules are validated under the Cryptographic Module Validation Program (CMVP), jointly operated by NIST and the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security. Testing of FIPS 140-2 submissions ended in 2022, after which all new validations proceed under FIPS 140-3.
