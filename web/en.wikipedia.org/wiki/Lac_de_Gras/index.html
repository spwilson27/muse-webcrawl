Lac de Gras

Lac de Gras is a lake in the Northwest Territories of Canada, roughly 300 kilometres northeast of Yellowknife. Covering about 633 square kilometres, it is the center of one of the world's richest diamond-mining districts: the kimberlite pipes near the lake yielded Canada's first and largest diamond mines.

LocationNorthwest Territories, Canada
Surface area~633 km²
Known forEkati and Diavik diamond mines

Diamond discoveries

In 1991, geologists Chuck Fipke and Stewart Blusson traced glacially scattered indicator minerals to kimberlite pipes beneath and around Lac de Gras, sparking a massive staking rush. The discovery led to the Ekati Diamond Mine (opened 1998), Canada's first diamond mine, and the Diavik Diamond Mine (opened 2003), built in the lake itself behind a dike that holds back the water from the open pits of an island in the lake's eastern arm. The mines transformed the economy of the Northwest Territories and made Canada one of the world's top diamond producers by value.

Geography

The lake lies in the barren-ground tundra of the Canadian Shield, drained northward by the Coppermine River system. It freezes for much of the year; the mines are supplied by an annual winter ice road from Yellowknife that crosses the frozen lake.
