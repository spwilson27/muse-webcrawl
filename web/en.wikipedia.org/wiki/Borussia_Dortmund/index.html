Ballspielverein Borussia 09 e. V. Dortmund, commonly known as Borussia Dortmund or BVB, is a German professional sports club based in Dortmund, North Rhine-Westphalia. Its football team plays in the Bundesliga and is one of Germany's most successful and best-supported clubs, famed for the 24,000-capacity standing terrace known as the "Yellow Wall" (Gelbe Wand) at its Westfalenstadion, the largest stadium in Germany.

History

The club was founded on 19 December 1909 by a group of young men who broke away from a church-sponsored team; "Borussia" is Latin for Prussia and also honoured a local brewery. Dortmund won their first national championship in 1956 and repeated it in 1957 and 1963, the last season before the Bundesliga. In 1966 they became the first German club to win a European trophy, the Cup Winners' Cup, beating Liverpool in the final.

The club's golden era came in the 1990s under Ottmar Hitzfeld: Bundesliga titles in 1995 and 1996 and the 1997 UEFA Champions League, won against Juventus in Munich. A third Bundesliga title followed in 2002, but reckless spending brought the club to the brink of bankruptcy in 2005. Rebuilt under Jürgen Klopp, Dortmund won back-to-back league titles in 2011 and 2012 (with a league-and-cup double) and reached the 2013 Champions League final, losing to Bayern Munich at Wembley; they reached the final again in 2024, losing to Real Madrid.

Honours

  • German champions: 8 (1956, 1957, 1963, 1995, 1996, 2002, 2011, 2012)
  • DFB-Pokal: 5 (1965, 1989, 2012, 2017, 2021)
  • UEFA Champions League: 1 (1997)
  • European Cup Winners' Cup: 1 (1966)
  • Intercontinental Cup: 1 (1997)

Rivalries

Dortmund's fiercest rivalry is the Revierderby with nearby Schalke 04; matches with Bayern Munich form Der Klassiker.
