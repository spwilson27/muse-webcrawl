Irish Distillers

Irish Distillers is Ireland's largest whiskey producer, a subsidiary of the French drinks group Pernod Ricard since 1988. It owns the Jameson brand — the world's best-selling Irish whiskey — along with Redbreast, Powers, Midleton Very Rare, and the Spot whiskeys.

History

The company was formed in 1966 by the merger of John Jameson & Son, John Power & Son, and the Cork Distilleries Company, in response to the long decline of the Irish whiskey industry. Production was consolidated at a new distillery at Midleton, County Cork, opened in 1975, while the Old Jameson Distillery in Dublin and Bushmills (later sold) became visitor attractions.

Operations

Nearly all Irish Distillers' whiskeys are produced at the Midleton Distillery, which underwent a major expansion in 2013 as global demand for Jameson surged. The group has been central to the Irish whiskey renaissance of the 21st century, which saw the number of distilleries on the island grow from a handful to dozens.
