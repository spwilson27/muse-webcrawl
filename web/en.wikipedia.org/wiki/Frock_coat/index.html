Frock coat

A frock coat is a formal coat for men, featuring a knee-length skirt cut all around the base, popular during the Victorian and Edwardian periods (1830s–1910s). The frock coat was characterised by a fitted bodice with a waist seam, unlike the earlier frock, which lacked a waist seam and was of more casual construction.

Description

The frock coat was worn as the standard daytime formal dress of gentlemen throughout the nineteenth century, for business, ceremonial and social occasions alike. It was typically made of dark wool, buttoned at the front (single- or double-breasted), worn with contrasting trousers, a waistcoat, and a top hat. By the later nineteenth century it had become the uniform of professionals, politicians and royalty; Prince Albert popularised it in Britain after his marriage to Queen Victoria.

Decline and survival

During the early twentieth century the frock coat was gradually displaced by the morning coat for formal day dress and by the lounge suit for everyday wear. It survived longest in conservative professions such as the law — English barristers traditionally wore frock coats in court — and in diplomatic and court dress. Today the frock coat appears mainly in historical re-enactment, traditional wedding attire in some cultures, and ultra-formal ceremonial uniforms.
