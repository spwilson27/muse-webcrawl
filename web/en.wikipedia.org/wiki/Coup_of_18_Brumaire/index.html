Coup of 18 Brumaire

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Coup of 18 Brumaire was the coup d'état by which General Napoleon Bonaparte overthrew the Directory and ended the French Revolution. It took place on 9 November 1799 — 18 Brumaire, Year VIII in the French Republican calendar — with the decisive confrontation occurring the following day at Saint-Cloud.

Events

The plot was directed by Emmanuel Joseph Sieyès, who sought a general's sword to revise the constitution; Napoleon, fresh from Egypt, proved more than a figurehead. On 18 Brumaire the legislature was moved to Saint-Cloud on the pretext of a Jacobin conspiracy, and the resignations of most Directors were secured. On 19 Brumaire hostile deputies of the Council of Five Hundred shouted Napoleon down, but troops under Lucien Bonaparte's direction cleared the chamber. A rump legislature then created the Consulate, with Napoleon as First Consul.

Significance

The coup is generally regarded as the end of the French Revolution and the beginning of Napoleon's rise to emperor. Karl Marx's 1852 essay The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte made "18 Brumaire" a byword for military-seizure coups.
