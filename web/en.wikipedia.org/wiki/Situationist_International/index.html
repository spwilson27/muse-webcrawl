The Situationist International (SI) was an international organisation of social revolutionaries, avant-garde artists, intellectuals and political theorists, active from its formation in 1957 to its dissolution in 1972. Rooted in Marxism, Dada, Surrealism and the Lettrist movement, the SI sought to overcome both advanced capitalism and the boredom of everyday life under what its leading theorist Guy Debord called "the society of the spectacle."

Origins

The SI was founded at a conference in Cosio d'Arroscia, Italy, in July 1957, through the merger of the Lettrist International, the International Movement for an Imaginist Bauhaus (including Asger Jorn and Giuseppe Pinot-Gallizio) and the London Psychogeographical Association. Debord's Report on the Construction of Situations served as a founding programme.

Key concepts

  • The spectacle — developed in Debord's The Society of the Spectacle (1967): social relations mediated by images and commodities, producing passive spectators.
  • Détournement — the hijacking and subversion of existing cultural material (comics, films, advertisements) to reveal its hidden meaning.
  • Dérive — a playful, unplanned drift through the city to experience its psychogeographic ambiences.
  • Psychogeography — the study of how geographical surroundings affect emotion and behaviour.
  • Unitary urbanism and the constructed situation — the creation of participatory, liberated life-moments and environments, exemplified by Constant's New Babylon project.

History

In its early years the SI focused on art, producing Jorn's modified paintings and Pinot-Gallizio's industrial painting. Around 1962, Debord expelled most of the artists (the so-called "Nashists" and Gruppe SPUR), steering the group toward political theory. Raoul Vaneigem's The Revolution of Everyday Life (1967) complemented Debord's book. Situationist slogans and tactics — graffiti, comic-strip détournements, calls for workers' councils — heavily influenced the student revolt of May 1968 in France, though the SI itself numbered only a few dozen members.

After 1968, internal purges shrank the group further. Debord and Gianfranco Sanguinetti dissolved the SI in 1972, recounting its history in The Veritable Split in the International.

Legacy

Situationist ideas influenced punk (via Malcolm McLaren and Jamie Reid's détourned graphics for the Sex Pistols), culture jamming, Adbusters magazine, and later movements such as Occupy. Debord's concept of the spectacle remains a standard reference in media theory and cultural criticism.
