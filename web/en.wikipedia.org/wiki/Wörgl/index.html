Wörgl

Wörgl is a city in the Austrian state of Tyrol, in the Kufstein district, about 20 kilometres (12 mi) west of Kufstein at the confluence of the Brixentaler Ache and the Inn. It is an important railway and road junction in the Lower Inn Valley and has around 14,000 inhabitants.

The Miracle of Wörgl

Wörgl is internationally famous for the "Miracle of Wörgl" (Wunder von Wörgl), a local currency experiment conducted during the Great Depression. In July 1932, under mayor Michael Unterguggenberger, the municipality issued stamped scrip — "certified compensation bills" — based on the ideas of Silvio Gesell, bearing a demurrage charge of 1 per cent per month. The scrip circulated rapidly, funded public works including roads, a bridge and a ski jump, and local unemployment fell while it rose elsewhere in Austria. The experiment was ended in September 1933 after the Austrian National Bank successfully sued to enforce its monopoly on currency issue.

History

The area has been settled since prehistory, with significant finds of the Urnfield culture and Roman-era remains. In 1809 the region saw fighting during the Tyrolean Rebellion against Bavarian and French occupation. Wörgl was severely damaged by bombing in the Second World War because of its railway yards. It received town status in 1951.
