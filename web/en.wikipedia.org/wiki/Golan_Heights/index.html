Golan Heights

The Golan Heights (Arabic: هضبة الجولان; Hebrew: רמת הגולן) is a basaltic plateau and disputed region in the Levant, bounded by the Yarmouk River to the south, the Sea of Galilee and the Hula Valley to the west, Mount Hermon to the north and the Raqqad valley to the east. Israel captured the area from Syria in the Six-Day War of 1967 and effectively annexed it in 1981; the international community, with the exception of the United States since 2019, regards it as Syrian territory under Israeli occupation.

Geography

The plateau is a volcanic field of basalt, sloping from about 1,000 metres in the north down toward the Sea of Galilee. It is strategically significant because it overlooks northern Israel and because the headwaters in the area, including the Banias spring, feed the Jordan River system. The climate ranges from snow on Mount Hermon — site of Israel's only ski area — to a Mediterranean climate suited to orchards, vineyards and cattle ranching.

History

The Golan was settled in antiquity and repeatedly contested. Under the French Mandate it became part of Syria, and from 1948 Syrian artillery on the heights shelled Israeli settlements below, a major grievance before 1967. Israel captured two-thirds of the plateau in June 1967; Syria retook ground in the Yom Kippur War of 1973 before a disengagement agreement in 1974 created a UN-monitored buffer zone (UNDOF). The Golan Heights Law of 1981 applied Israeli law to the area; UN Security Council Resolution 497 declared the annexation "null and void". In 2019 the United States recognised Israeli sovereignty over the Golan.

Population

After 1967 most Syrian inhabitants fled or were displaced; the remaining population is largely Druze, many of whom long retained Syrian citizenship and ambiguous status, alongside Jewish Israeli settlers in more than thirty settlements. The regional centre is the town of Katzrin. Following the fall of the Assad regime in Syria in December 2024, Israeli forces moved into additional parts of the buffer zone, a development whose long-term outcome remains unsettled.
