British Waterways
Founded: 1963 (as British Waterways Board)
Abolished: 2012
Successor: Canal & River Trust (England & Wales); Scottish Canals

British Waterways was a statutory corporation wholly owned by the UK Government, responsible for 2,200 miles (3,541 km) of canals and rivers, docks and reservoirs in England, Scotland and Wales.

History

Created by the Transport Act 1962 (operating from 1963) from the nationalised British Transport Commission's waterways, it shifted the network's focus from freight — in terminal decline — to leisure boating, angling and heritage restoration, overseeing major canal restorations including the Kennet and Avon.

Transfer

In 2012 its functions and assets in England and Wales transferred to the new charity Canal & River Trust, one of the largest charitable transfers in UK history; in Scotland it continued as Scottish Canals. See Canals of the United Kingdom.
