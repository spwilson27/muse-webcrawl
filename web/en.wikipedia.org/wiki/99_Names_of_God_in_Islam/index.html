99 Names of God in Islam

99 Names of God in Islam
Arabicأسماء الله الحسنى (Asmāʾ Allāh al-Ḥusnā)
Meaning"Allah's Most Beautiful Names"
SourceQur'an and hadith (notably a hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari and Muslim)

The 99 Names of God in Islam (Arabic: أسماء الله الحسنى, Asmāʾ Allāh al-Ḥusnā, "Allah's Most Beautiful Names") are the names and attributes of God in Islamic tradition, each expressing a particular quality of the divine — among them Ar-Raḥmān (the Most Gracious), Ar-Raḥīm (the Most Merciful), Al-Malik (the King), Al-Khāliq (the Creator) and As-Salām (the Source of Peace). Reciting and reflecting on the names is a widespread devotional practice.

Origins

The Qur'an refers to God's "most beautiful names" (e.g. 7:180, 17:110, 59:24) and contains many of them distributed through its text. A well-known hadith reports that God has ninety-nine names and that whoever learns them by heart will enter Paradise. Lists compiled by scholars differ slightly, since the canonical enumeration — commonly traced to a narration in al-Tirmidhi's collection — varies in a few entries.

Practice

The names are memorised, chanted in dhikr (remembrance), incorporated into personal names (e.g. Abd al-Rahman, "servant of the Most Gracious") and rendered in Islamic calligraphy and mosque decoration. In Sufi tradition they structure meditative practice and theology, each name being a locus for contemplating a divine attribute.
