Kronecker delta

In mathematics, the Kronecker delta (named after Leopold Kronecker) is a function of two variables, usually just non-negative integers. The function is 1 if the variables are equal, and 0 otherwise:

δij = 1 if i = j;   δij = 0 if i ≠ j

Equivalently, δij may be written with the Iverson bracket as δij = [i = j]. The Kronecker delta appears in many areas of mathematics, physics, and engineering, wherever a compact notation for the identity matrix or for orthogonality relations is needed.

The Kronecker delta, viewed as a matrix, is the identity matrix.

Properties

The following equations are satisfied by the Kronecker delta:

Σj δij aj = ai
Σi ai δij = aj
Σk δik δkj = δij

These express the "sifting" property: summing a sequence against the delta picks out a single term. The delta is also symmetric, δij = δji.

Relation to the identity matrix

If the indices are considered to range over a finite set of size n, the values δij are precisely the entries of the n × n identity matrix. In linear algebra, tensors built from the Kronecker delta act as the identity operator under contraction, and in tensor notation δ is used to raise and manipulate index expressions in the Einstein summation convention.

Digital signal processing

In digital signal processing, the same concept is represented by the unit impulse function, written δ[n], which equals 1 at n = 0 and 0 elsewhere. Any discrete signal can be decomposed into a sum of scaled, shifted impulses — the basis of convolution and of the sifting property of the discrete impulse.

Generalizations

The generalized Kronecker delta extends the symbol to multiple indices, giving the sign of a permutation when the indices are a permutation of one another and zero otherwise; it is used in expressing determinants, including the relation to the Levi-Civita symbol. In distribution theory and quantum mechanics, the continuous analogue is the Dirac delta function, which is not a function in the ordinary sense but is defined by its sifting integral.
