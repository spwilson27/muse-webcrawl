Washington Accord (credentials)

The Washington Accord is an international accreditation agreement for undergraduate professional engineering academic degrees and programmes. Under the Accord, the signatories recognise the substantial equivalency of programmes accredited by the engineering accreditation bodies of the signatory jurisdictions, and recommend that graduates of such programmes be recognised by other signatories as having met the academic requirements for entry to engineering practice.

History

The Accord was signed in 1989 by six founding signatories: the accreditation bodies of Australia, Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, and the United States. It has since grown to include many more jurisdictions across Asia, Africa, Europe, and the Americas, and is administered through meetings of the signatories under the framework of the International Engineering Alliance.

Membership

Signatory status requires an accreditation system judged to be substantially equivalent to those of existing signatories, following a review process. Provisional signatories are jurisdictions whose systems are considered to have the potential to reach signatory status. Companion agreements under the International Engineering Alliance include the Sydney Accord for engineering technologist qualifications and the Dublin Accord for engineering technician qualifications.

Effect

The Washington Accord facilitates the international mobility of engineers by allowing employers and registration bodies to accept accredited degrees from other signatory countries as academically equivalent, though it does not itself grant professional licensure, which remains subject to local requirements.
