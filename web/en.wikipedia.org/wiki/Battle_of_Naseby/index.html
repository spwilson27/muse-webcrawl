Battle of Naseby

The Battle of Naseby was fought on 14 June 1645 near Naseby, Northamptonshire, during the English Civil War. Parliament's New Model Army under Sir Thomas Fairfax and Oliver Cromwell destroyed King Charles I's main field army — the decisive battle of the war.

Background

After the Royalists stormed Leicester, Fairfax lifted the siege of Oxford and pursued the king. Charles chose to give battle rather than retreat north. The New Model Army, newly formed and professionally officered, deployed on high ground north of Naseby; Colonel Okey's dragoons lined a hedge on the Parliamentarian left.

Battle

Prince Rupert's cavalry routed Ireton's wing but, as at Edgehill, pursued to the baggage train. In the centre Royalist infantry initially pushed back Fairfax's foot before Fairfax counterattacked. Cromwell defeated Langdale's northern horse, kept his men in hand, and fell on the Royalist infantry's flank. The king's army collapsed; his artillery, baggage and some 5,000 men were captured.

Aftermath

Among the spoils was Charles's correspondence showing he sought Irish Catholic and foreign help, which Parliament published to damage his cause. The Royalists never fielded another comparable army; within a year the First Civil War ended.
