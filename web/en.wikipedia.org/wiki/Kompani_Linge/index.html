Kompani Linge

Kompani Linge (English: Linge Company) was a Norwegian special operations unit of the Second World War, formed in Britain in 1941 under the British Special Operations Executive (SOE) and named after its first commander, Captain Martin Linge. Its official British designation was the Norwegian Independent Company 1 (NORIC 1).

Operations

Volunteers were recruited from Norwegian forces and refugees in Britain and trained in Scotland. The company carried out raids and sabotage in German-occupied Norway, including the Måløy raid (Operation Archery, December 1941), in which Linge himself was killed, and later operations against power stations, shipping, and industrial targets. Members also served as radio operators and organisers for the Norwegian resistance.

Legacy

After the war the unit was disbanded, but its traditions were carried forward by Norwegian special forces. Captain Linge is commemorated by memorials in Norway and at Måløy, and the unit's name survives in Norway's modern special operations heritage; an army company of the Armed Forces' special command has borne the name Kompani Linge.
