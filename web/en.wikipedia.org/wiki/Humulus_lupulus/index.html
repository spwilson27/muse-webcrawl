Humulus lupulus


Hop cones of Humulus lupulus.

Humulus lupulus, the common hop or hops, is a species of flowering plant in the hemp family Cannabaceae, native to West Asia, Europe, and North America. It is a perennial, herbaceous climbing plant which sends up new shoots in early spring and dies back to a cold-hardy rhizome in autumn. It is dioecious (having separate male and female plants).

Uses

Common hop is best known as the principal flavoring and stability agent in beer, to which the female flower cones (hops) impart bitter, zesty, or citric flavors. Hops contain alpha acids, chiefly humulone, which isomerize during boiling to give beer its characteristic bitterness, as well as essential oils contributing aroma. Hops also have a mild sedative effect and are used in herbal medicine, for example in sleep aids, and the young shoots are eaten as a vegetable in some regions.

Cultivation

Numerous cultivars have been bred for brewing, differing in bitterness and aroma profile; major growing regions include Germany's Hallertau, the Czech Republic, and the Yakima Valley in the United States.
