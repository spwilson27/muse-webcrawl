Carlos Gardel (born Charles Romuald Gardès; 11 December 1890 – 24 June 1935) was a French-born Argentine singer, songwriter, composer, and actor, and the most famous figure in the history of tango. A baritone of extraordinary warmth and phrasing, Gardel created the tango-canción (tango song) and carried tango from the dance halls of Buenos Aires to international fame through hundreds of recordings and a series of films for Paramount Pictures. Argentines famously say that "Gardel sings better every day" (cada día canta mejor).

Early life

Gardel's birthplace was long disputed between Toulouse, France, and Tacuarembó, Uruguay; documentary evidence supports Toulouse, where he was born to a French laundress, Berthe Gardès, who emigrated with him to Buenos Aires when he was a small child. He grew up in the Abasto neighborhood of Buenos Aires and began singing in bars and at political rallies, performing in a celebrated duo with José Razzano from 1911.

Career

In 1917 Gardel recorded "Mi noche triste" ("My Sad Night"), generally considered the first tango-canción, transforming tango from dance music into a vehicle for sung, sentimental narrative. His partnership with lyricist Alfredo Le Pera produced many of his most beloved songs, including "El día que me quieras," "Volver," "Por una cabeza," and "Cuesta abajo." In the early 1930s he starred in Spanish-language films made by Paramount in France and the United States, including Melodía de arrabal, El tango en Broadway, and El día que me quieras, becoming a matinee idol across Latin America and Spain.

Death and legacy

Gardel died on 24 June 1935 in an airplane collision on the runway of the airport in Medellín, Colombia, while on tour. His death provoked an outpouring of grief across Latin America; his body was repatriated through several countries before burial in La Chacarita Cemetery in Buenos Aires, where his tomb remains a place of pilgrimage. In 2003 his recordings were inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World register.
