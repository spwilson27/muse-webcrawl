Cheryl Christina Crane (born July 25, 1943) is an American author and former model, best known as the daughter of actress Lana Turner and as the central figure in the 1958 killing of her mother's boyfriend, Johnny Stompanato, one of the most famous scandals of Hollywood's golden age.

The Stompanato killing

On the night of April 4, 1958, during a violent argument between Turner and Johnny Stompanato — a former Marine and associate of mobster Mickey Cohen — at Turner's rented home in Beverly Hills, the 14-year-old Crane stabbed Stompanato with a kitchen knife. At a coroner's inquest, the death was ruled justifiable homicide: the jury concluded that Crane had acted to protect her mother. Crane was made a ward of the court and placed in the custody of her grandmother.

Later life

After a troubled adolescence, Crane worked in modelling and later in the restaurant business and real estate. She chronicled her life in the memoir Detour: A Hollywood Story (1988) and co-authored Lana: The Memories, the Myths, the Movies (1982) about her mother. The Stompanato case has been dramatised in films and the novel L.A. Confidential drew on period Hollywood scandals in its fictional treatment.
