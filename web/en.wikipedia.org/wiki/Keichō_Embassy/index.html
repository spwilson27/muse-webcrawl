Keichō Embassy

Keichō Embassy
Dates: 1613–1620
Sent by: Date Masamune
Leader: Hasekura Tsunenaga
Destinations: New Spain, Spain, Rome

The Keichō Embassy (慶長遣欧使節, Keichō ken'Ō shisetsu) was a Japanese diplomatic mission to Europe and New Spain sent in 1613 by Date Masamune, daimyō of Sendai, and led by his retainer Hasekura Tsunenaga, accompanied by the Franciscan friar Luis Sotelo.

Voyage

The embassy sailed from Tsukinoura aboard the Japanese-built galleon San Juan Bautista, crossed the Pacific to Acapulco in New Spain, travelled overland to Veracruz, then crossed the Atlantic to Spain. Hasekura was received by King Philip III in Madrid and by Pope Paul V in Rome in 1615.

Return and aftermath

The mission returned to Japan in 1620 to find the country turning toward isolation under the Tokugawa shogunate and persecuting Christians. Hasekura died in 1622. The embassy remained largely forgotten until Japan's reopening in the 19th century.
