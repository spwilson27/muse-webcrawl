Purusha Sukta

The Purusha Sukta (Sanskrit: पुरुषसूक्तम्, Puruṣasūktam) is a hymn of the Rigveda (Mandala 10, Hymn 90) dedicated to the Purusha, the primordial cosmic being. It describes the creation of the universe through the ritual sacrifice and dismemberment of Purusha, whose body becomes the world and all its inhabitants. Versions of the hymn also appear in the other Vedas and later texts; it remains one of the most widely recited and commented Vedic hymns.

Content

The hymn opens by describing Purusha, thousand-headed, thousand-eyed, and thousand-footed, who pervades the earth yet extends beyond it; only one quarter of him is manifest in the world, while three quarters remain immortal in heaven. From his sacrifice by the gods, the hymn derives the seasons, the ritual formulas (the Rig, Sama, and Yajur verses), animals, and the elements of the cosmos: the moon from his mind, the sun from his eye, Indra and Agni from his mouth, the wind from his breath, the sky from his head, the earth from his feet.

The varna verse

One famous verse (10.90.12) states that the Brahmin (priestly class) came from Purusha's mouth, the Rajanya/Kshatriya (warriors and rulers) from his arms, the Vaishya (commoners) from his thighs, and the Shudra (servants) from his feet. This is the earliest textual reference to the four varnas and has been enormously influential — cited both to justify the caste hierarchy and, by reformers such as those in the bhakti tradition and modern reinterpretations, as a metaphor for the interdependence of society.

Dating and commentary

The tenth book of the Rigveda is generally considered later than the family books, and the Purusha Sukta's language and ideas reflect a later stage of Vedic composition. Its cosmic-man motif has parallels in other Indo-European traditions, such as the Norse Ymir and the Iranian Gayomart. The hymn is still chanted daily in many temples, especially in Vishnu worship, where Purusha is identified with Narayana.
