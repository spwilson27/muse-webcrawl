Ultra long-haul flight

An ultra long-haul flight is a non-stop passenger flight of exceptional duration or distance. There is no universally fixed definition, but the term is commonly applied to flights lasting more than about 12 hours, and in stricter usage to flights over roughly 14–16 hours or exceeding some 13,000–14,500 kilometres. Such flights became practical with long-range twin-engine aircraft such as the Airbus A350 and Boeing 777-200LR.

Characteristics

Ultra long-haul operations place unusual demands on airlines and passengers. Aircraft may carry fuel loads so large that payload or cargo must be restricted; purpose-built variants such as the A350-900ULR (Ultra Long Range) have enlarged fuel capacity for the purpose. Crews operate in augmented complements of three or four pilots with dedicated rest bunks, and cabin service is redesigned around sleep cycles, with special lighting and meal timing intended to reduce jet lag. Passengers face elevated risks of deep vein thrombosis, dehydration and fatigue on sectors that can approach 19 hours.

Singapore Airlines relaunched its Singapore–New York service in 2018 using the A350-900ULR, reclaiming the title of the world's longest scheduled flight; it had previously operated the route with the four-engine A340-500 until 2013.

Economics

Ultra long-haul routes command premium fares from time-sensitive travellers but burn large amounts of fuel simply to carry fuel, and historically have been vulnerable to fuel price rises. Several routes launched in the 2000s were withdrawn, while the efficiency of newer twinjets revived the category in the late 2010s. Qantas's "Project Sunrise" research flights in 2019 tested non-stop Sydney–London and Sydney–New York services of over 19 hours.
