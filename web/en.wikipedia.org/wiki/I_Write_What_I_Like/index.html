I Write What I Like

I Write What I Like is a posthumously published collection of the writings of South African anti-apartheid activist Steve Biko. First published in 1978, a year after Biko's death in police custody, the book gathers his columns, essays, and interviews written between 1969 and 1972, many of them originally published under the pseudonym "Frank Talk" in the journal of the South African Students' Organisation (SASO).

Content

The writings collected in the book articulate the philosophy of Black Consciousness, the movement Biko helped to found. Biko argued that the greatest weapon in the hands of the oppressor is the mind of the oppressed, and that black South Africans needed first to liberate themselves psychologically from internalized inferiority before political liberation could follow. The essays critique liberal white involvement in the anti-apartheid struggle, examine black identity, culture, and religion, and advocate for black self-reliance and pride.

The title comes from Biko's insistence, as a columnist writing under a pseudonym, on expressing his views without deference: he wrote what he liked.

Publication and reception

The book was edited by Father Aelred Stubbs, a friend and mentor of Biko, and was banned in South Africa under apartheid. It has been reissued several times and is regarded as one of the most important texts of the South African liberation struggle, widely quoted and studied for its rhetorical power and its formulation of Black Consciousness philosophy.
