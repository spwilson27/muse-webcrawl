Boeing 717

The Boeing 717 is an American five-abreast narrow-body airliner produced by Boeing. It was developed from the McDonnell Douglas MD-95, itself derived from the DC-9 family, and marketed to the 100-seat regional jet market. The 717 first flew in September 1998 and entered service in 1999 with AirTran Airways. Production ended in 2006 after 156 aircraft were built.

Design

The twinjet is powered by two rear-mounted Rolls-Royce BR715 engines and seats 106 passengers in a typical two-class layout, or up to 134 in high-density configuration. It has a range of about 2,060 nautical miles (3,820 km). A modern glass cockpit was adapted from the MD-11.

Operators

Major operators have included AirTran, Delta Air Lines, Hawaiian Airlines and QantasLink. The type remained in scheduled service into the 2020s, largely with Delta, which acquired AirTran's fleet after their merger.
