Stringybark Creek

The site of the 1878 shootout at Stringybark Creek

Stringybark Creek is a small watercourse in the Wombat Ranges of north-eastern Victoria, Australia, near Tolmie. It is famous as the site of a deadly confrontation on 26 October 1878 between a party of Victorian police and the Kelly Gang, led by the bushranger Ned Kelly. The incident, in which three policemen were killed, led to the Kelly gang being declared outlaws and triggered one of the largest manhunts in Australian history.

The shootout

In October 1878 a police party of four — Sergeant Michael Kennedy and Constables Thomas Lonigan, Michael Scanlan and Thomas McIntyre — camped at Stringybark Creek while searching for Ned and Dan Kelly, who were wanted for attempted murder of a trooper. On 26 October, while Kennedy and Scanlan were out scouting, the Kelly gang surprised the camp. Lonigan was shot dead; McIntyre surrendered. When Kennedy and Scanlan returned, a gunfight followed in which both were killed. McIntyre escaped on Kennedy's horse and raised the alarm at Mansfield.

Aftermath

The Victorian parliament passed the Felons Apprehension Act, declaring the four gang members outlaws who could be shot on sight, and placed a large reward on their heads. The gang went on to raid Euroa and Jerilderie before their final stand at Glenrowan in June 1880. The Stringybark Creek site, marked by memorials including the "Kelly Tree", remains a place of pilgrimage in the Kelly story, and debate continues over the exact locations of the camp and the shootings.
