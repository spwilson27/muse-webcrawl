Turno

Turno pacífico
Country: Spain (Restoration era)
Period: 1875–1923 (in practice)
Parties: Conservative Party and Liberal Party

The turno pacífico ("peaceful turn" or "peaceful alternation") was the system of planned alternation in power between the two dynastic parties of Restoration Spain, the Conservative Party of Antonio Cánovas del Castillo and the Liberal Party of Práxedes Mateo Sagasta, established after the Bourbon Restoration of 1875.

Operation

Under the turno, the monarch would appoint the leader of the incoming party as prime minister, who would then call elections that the government, working through local political bosses known as caciques, reliably arranged to win. The informal Pact of El Pardo (1885) regularised the alternation. The system's stability ended the cycle of military pronunciamientos that had plagued 19th-century Spain, but at the cost of genuine electoral competition and mass political engagement.

Decline

The turno frayed after the Disaster of 1898 and the deaths of its architects, and it collapsed entirely with the coup of Miguel Primo de Rivera in 1923. Historians regard it as a paradigmatic case of an elite-managed, oligarchic democracy.
