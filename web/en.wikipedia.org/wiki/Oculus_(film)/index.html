Oculus is a 2013 American supernatural psychological horror film directed by Mike Flanagan and written by Flanagan with Jeff Howard, expanding Flanagan's earlier short film Oculus: Chapter 3 – The Man with the Plan (2006). It stars Karen Gillan and Brenton Thwaites as siblings who believe an antique mirror, the Lasser Glass, was responsible for their parents' deaths. The film premiered at the 2013 Toronto International Film Festival and was released theatrically in April 2014 to positive reviews and commercial success.

Plot

Eleven years after their father Alan murdered their mother Marie and was himself shot by young Tim, Tim Russell is released from a psychiatric facility, convinced by years of therapy that the supernatural explanation the siblings once believed was false. His sister Kaylie, who works at an auction house, has located the mirror — the Lasser Glass — and sets up an elaborate recorded experiment in their childhood home to prove its guilt before it is shipped to a buyer, surrounding it with cameras, safeguards and a ceiling-mounted anchor as a "kill switch".

The film intercuts the adult experiment with flashbacks to 2002, when the mirror progressively drove Alan and Marie into madness. As the night proceeds, the mirror warps the siblings' perceptions so that past and present merge and their precautions fail; Tim, believing he is releasing the anchor to destroy the glass, instead kills Kaylie. Arrested as police arrive, he screams that the mirror made him do it, echoing the film's opening.

Production

Flanagan developed the feature with Intrepid Pictures and Blumhouse Productions; WWE Studios co-financed. Principal photography took place in Alabama in late 2012. The non-linear structure, interweaving two timelines, was central to Flanagan's design and was achieved largely through editing. The film was produced for about $5 million.

Reception

Oculus grossed roughly $44 million worldwide and was praised by critics for its performances and psychological tension; review aggregators report a majority of favourable reviews. It consolidated Flanagan's reputation in horror, leading to films such as Hush, Gerald's Game and Doctor Sleep.

Proposed sequel

Flanagan and the producers discussed a sequel exploring the mirror's earlier history, but it did not enter production.
