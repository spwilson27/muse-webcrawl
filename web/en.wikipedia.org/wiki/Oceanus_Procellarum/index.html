Oceanus Procellarum (Latin for "Ocean of Storms") is a vast lunar mare on the western edge of the near side of the Moon. It is the largest of the lunar maria, with an extent of more than 2,500 kilometres along its north–south axis, covering roughly 4 million square kilometres. Unlike the other large maria, Oceanus Procellarum may not lie within a single well-defined impact basin; its origin is debated, with proposals including a giant impact, mantle convection, or a rift-like "Procellarum basin".

Exploration

Several spacecraft have landed in or near Oceanus Procellarum. The Soviet Luna 9 made the first successful soft landing there in 1966, followed by Luna 13; NASA's Surveyor 1 and Surveyor 3 also landed in the region, and Apollo 12 landed near Surveyor 3 in 1969. China's Chang'e 5 landed in northern Oceanus Procellarum in 2020 and returned samples of some of the youngest lunar basalts ever dated, about two billion years old. The region is enriched in heat-producing elements (the Procellarum KREEP Terrane) and hosts wrinkle ridges, rilles and the prominent crater Copernicus on its eastern margin.
