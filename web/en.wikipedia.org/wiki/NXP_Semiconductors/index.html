NXP Semiconductors


NXP Semiconductors N.V.
Industry: Semiconductors
Founded: 2006 (as a Philips spin-off)
Headquarters: Eindhoven, Netherlands
Products: Microcontrollers, automotive and secure connectivity chips

NXP Semiconductors N.V. is a Dutch semiconductor designer and manufacturer headquartered in Eindhoven, Netherlands. The company employs approximately 34,000 people in more than 30 countries and is one of the world's largest suppliers of automotive semiconductors and secure connectivity solutions.

History

NXP originated as the semiconductor division of Philips, with roots going back to the 1950s (including the Mullard and Valvo businesses). In 2006, Philips sold a majority stake to a private equity consortium and the business was rebranded NXP, a name derived from "Next eXPerience". NXP went public on NASDAQ in 2010.

Freescale merger

In December 2015, NXP merged with Freescale Semiconductor in a deal valued at about $40 billion for the combined company, creating the world's largest automotive semiconductor supplier. In 2016–2018, Qualcomm attempted to acquire NXP for $44 billion, but the deal was abandoned after failing to win Chinese regulatory approval; Qualcomm paid NXP a $2 billion breakup fee.

Products

NXP's portfolio includes i.MX application processors, automotive radar and vehicle networking chips, NFC technology (co-invented with Sony), RFID products including MIFARE smart card chips used in transit systems worldwide, and secure elements for payment and identification.
