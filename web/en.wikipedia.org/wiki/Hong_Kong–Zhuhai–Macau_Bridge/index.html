The Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge (HZMB) is a 55-kilometre bridge–tunnel system across the Pearl River estuary in China, linking Hong Kong and Macau with Zhuhai in Guangdong province. It is the longest sea crossing and the longest open-sea fixed link in the world.

Structure

The system consists of a series of bridges, two artificial islands, and a 6.7 km undersea tunnel that allows ships to pass through the busy Lingdingyang channel. The main bridge section is 29.6 km long. The project was designed for a service life of 120 years and to withstand magnitude-8 earthquakes and super typhoons.

Construction and opening

Construction began in 2009 and cost an estimated ¥127 billion (about US$18.8 billion). The bridge was inaugurated by Xi Jinping and opened to traffic on 24 October 2018. It cut the journey between Hong Kong and Zhuhai from about three hours to roughly 45 minutes, and forms part of the Greater Bay Area integration plan.

Usage rules

Because Hong Kong and Macau drive on the left while mainland China drives on the right, vehicles switch sides at the boundary. Cross-boundary traffic requires special permits, and most travellers use shuttle buses between the port checkpoints.
