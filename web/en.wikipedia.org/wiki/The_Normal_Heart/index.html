Written byLarry Kramer

GenreDrama
SettingNew York City, 1981–1984

The Normal Heart is a largely autobiographical play by American playwright and activist Larry Kramer. Set in New York City between 1981 and 1984, it depicts the rise of the HIV/AIDS crisis through the eyes of writer Ned Weeks, the founder of a prominent HIV advocacy group, whose loud, confrontational style of activism frequently conflicts with the closeted, more cautious strategies of his fellow organizers. The play premiered Off-Broadway at The Public Theater on April 21, 1985, directed by Michael Lindsay-Hogg, and ran for 294 performances.

Synopsis

As a mysterious disease begins killing gay men in New York, writer Ned Weeks seeks help from Dr. Emma Brookner, a physician — herself confined to a wheelchair by polio — who is among the few doctors treating the mounting cases and who urges total sexual abstinence as the only safe course. Ned co-founds an advocacy organization (modelled on Gay Men's Health Crisis, which Kramer co-founded in 1982) and becomes its most outspoken voice, attacking the indifference of city government under Mayor Ed Koch, the silence of the media, and the caution of his own colleagues. His activism strains his relationships with his lover Felix Turner, a New York Times reporter who sickens and dies, with his brother Ben, and ultimately with the organization itself, which expels him. The play ends with Ned and Felix marrying in a hospital ceremony moments before Felix's death.

Productions and adaptation

The original production starred Brad Davis as Ned Weeks and D. W. Moffett as Felix; Davis was later succeeded by Joel Grey. The play was produced in London at the Royal Court Theatre in 1986 with Martin Sheen as Ned. A major Broadway staging finally arrived in 2011, directed by Joel Grey and George C. Wolfe, with Joe Mantello as Ned, Ellen Barkin as Dr. Brookner and Jim Parsons as Tommy Boatwright; it won the Tony Award for Best Revival of a Play, with acting awards for Barkin and John Benjamin Hickey. In 2014 HBO aired a television film adaptation directed by Ryan Murphy, starring Mark Ruffalo, Matt Bomer, Taylor Kitsch, Jim Parsons and Julia Roberts, which won the Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Television Movie.

Themes and reception

Part polemic, part elegy, The Normal Heart is both an indictment of institutional indifference during the early AIDS epidemic and a portrait of a community organising under catastrophic pressure. Its title derives from W. H. Auden's poem "September 1, 1939": "We must love one another or die." Critics have long recognised the play as a foundational work of AIDS literature and of American political theatre.
