Widmanstätten pattern

The Widmanstätten pattern, also called the Thomson structure, is the interlocking lamellar pattern of kamacite and taenite revealed when an iron meteorite is cut, polished and etched with dilute acid. It is named after Alois von Widmanstätten, who observed it in 1808; G. Thomson had described it earlier, in 1804.

Formation

The pattern forms as nickel–iron in a planetesimal core cools extremely slowly — degrees per million years — allowing taenite (high-nickel) to exsolve kamacite (low-nickel) plates along octahedral crystallographic planes. Such cooling rates are impossible in terrestrial rocks, making the pattern diagnostic of meteorites.

Use

Lamella width reflects cooling rate and nickel content and helps classify octahedrite iron meteorites (coarsest to finest). The pattern cannot be reproduced by heating and quenching on human timescales, a test against fakes.
