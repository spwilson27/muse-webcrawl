Synchronization

Synchronization is the coordination of events to operate a system in unison. For example, the conductor of an orchestra keeps the orchestra synchronized or in time. Systems that operate with all parts in synchrony are said to be synchronous or in sync — and those that are not are asynchronous.

Nature and physics

Synchronization of coupled oscillators was first observed by Christiaan Huygens in 1665, when two pendulum clocks mounted on the same beam gradually fell into step. Similar phenomena occur in fireflies flashing in unison, in coupled metronomes, and in the phase locking of electronic oscillators. The Kuramoto model is a widely used mathematical model of such synchronization.

Computing

In computer science, synchronization refers to the coordination of concurrent threads or processes so that shared data is not corrupted by race conditions. Primitives such as locks, semaphores, monitors and barriers enforce synchronization. Clock synchronization, such as via the Network Time Protocol, keeps the clocks of distributed machines in agreement.

Other uses

In film and television, synchronization of sound and picture is essential; in power systems, generators must remain synchronized to the grid frequency; in neuroscience, synchronized firing of neurons is associated with binding and with pathological states such as epilepsy.
