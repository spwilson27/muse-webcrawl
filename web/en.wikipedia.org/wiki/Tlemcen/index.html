Tlemcen


Great Mosque of Tlemcen, founded in the 11th century

Tlemcen is a city in northwestern Algeria, capital of Tlemcen Province, with a population of over 140,000. Situated inland south of the Mediterranean coast and near the Moroccan border, it was one of the great cities of the medieval Maghreb and is noted for its rich Hispano-Moorish architecture and its place in the history of Andalusi music and culture.

History

The site was a Roman settlement (Pomaria) before becoming a centre of the Berber kingdoms. Tlemcen flourished under the Almoravids, who founded its Great Mosque in 1082, and reached its zenith as capital of the Zayyanid (Abdalwadid) kingdom from the thirteenth to the sixteenth century, when it was a hub of trade between the Sahara and the Mediterranean and a renowned centre of learning. The city later came under Ottoman rule and was taken by France in 1842.

Culture

Tlemcen is a stronghold of the Andalusi musical tradition, particularly the gharnati school, and is associated with musicians such as Cheikh Larbi Bensari. Landmarks include the Great Mosque, the mosque and tomb of Sidi Boumediene — the city's patron saint — and the Mechouar palace complex.
