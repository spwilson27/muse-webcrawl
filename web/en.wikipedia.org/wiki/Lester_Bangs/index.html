Lester Bangs

Leslie Conway "Lester" Bangs (14 December 1948 – 30 April 1982) was an American music journalist and critic. Famous for his confrontational, stream-of-consciousness prose, he wrote for Rolling Stone, Creem and The Village Voice and championed punk rock and performers such as Lou Reed, Iggy and the Stooges, and the MC5.

Born14 December 1948, Escondido, California, US
Died30 April 1982, New York City, US
OccupationMusic journalist, critic

Career

Bangs began writing for Rolling Stone in 1969 after answering a reader call for reviews. Fired by Jann Wenner in 1973 for perceived disrespect toward musicians, he became editor of Creem in Detroit, where his wild style helped define the magazine. He later moved to New York and wrote for The Village Voice, championing the emerging punk and new wave scenes.

Style and legacy

Bangs's writing mixed gonzo energy, philosophical digression and ferocious honesty; his reviews of Lou Reed's work, notably of Metal Machine Music, are classics of the form. He died in 1982 from an accidental overdose of prescription medication. Posthumous collection Psychotic Reactions and Carburetor Dung (1987), edited by Greil Marcus, secured his reputation, and he was portrayed by Philip Seymour Hoffman in the film Almost Famous (2000).
