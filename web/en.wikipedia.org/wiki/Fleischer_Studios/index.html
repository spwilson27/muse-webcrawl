Fleischer Studios

Fleischer Studios was an American animation studio founded in 1929 by brothers Max and Dave Fleischer, who ran the business until 1942. It was one of the two leading animation studios in the United States during the 1930s, alongside Walt Disney Productions, and was the first to be based on the East Coast.

Betty Boop, created by Fleischer Studios

History

Max Fleischer was an important pioneer of animation, having invented the rotoscope, a device that allowed animators to trace over live-action film footage to produce realistic movement. The studio began with the Out of the Inkwell series featuring Koko the Clown, produced through earlier companies before Fleischer Studios was formally incorporated.

Characters and series

The studio's most famous creations were Betty Boop, introduced in 1930, and the animated adaptations of Popeye the Sailor, licensed from King Features Syndicate beginning in 1933. The Popeye cartoons became enormously popular, at times surpassing Disney's Mickey Mouse in cinema polls. The studio also produced the innovative Screen Songs series with its famous "bouncing ball" sing-along device.

Features and innovations

Fleischer produced two animated feature films: Gulliver's Travels (1939) and Mr. Bug Goes to Town (1941). It was also responsible for the first Superman theatrical cartoons (1941), noted for their lavish budgets and dramatic style. Technical innovations included the setback camera, which gave cartoon backgrounds a sense of three-dimensional depth.

Closure

Financial difficulties and a falling-out between the brothers led Paramount Pictures, the studio's distributor, to take control in 1942, reorganising it as Famous Studios. Max and Dave Fleischer left, and the New York studio's era ended, although its cartoons remained in circulation on television for decades.
