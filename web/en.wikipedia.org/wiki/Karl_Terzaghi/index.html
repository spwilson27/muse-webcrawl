Karl Terzaghi

Karl von Terzaghi (2 October 1883 – 25 October 1963) was an Austrian civil engineer and geologist, universally recognised as the father of soil mechanics — the engineering discipline concerned with the behaviour of soils as construction materials and foundations.

Career

Born in Prague, then in Austria-Hungary, Terzaghi studied mechanical and civil engineering at Graz. After field work in Croatia, Russia, and the United States, he taught at the American Robert College in Istanbul, where his experiments on soil behaviour led to his landmark book Erdbaumechanik (Earthwork Mechanics, 1925), which founded soil mechanics as a scientific discipline. He later held posts at MIT, the Technical University of Vienna, and, after emigrating in 1938, at Harvard University.

Contributions

Terzaghi introduced the principle of effective stress — the distinction between total stress in a soil mass and the pore-water pressure it contains — which explains consolidation, settlement, and soil strength. He developed theories of consolidation and bearing capacity that remain standard, and promoted the "observational method" of design based on monitoring construction in progress. His later works, Theoretical Soil Mechanics (1943) and, with Ralph Peck, Soil Mechanics in Engineering Practice (1948), codified the field.
