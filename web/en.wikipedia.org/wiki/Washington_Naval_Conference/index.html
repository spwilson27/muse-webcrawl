Washington Naval Conference

The Washington Naval Conference (also called the Washington Conference on the Limitation of Armament) was a disarmament conference held in Washington, D.C., from November 1921 to February 1922, convened by U.S. President Warren G. Harding. Nine nations participated: the United States, the United Kingdom, Japan, France, Italy, China, Belgium, the Netherlands and Portugal.

Treaties

The conference produced the Five-Power Treaty (the Washington Naval Treaty), which limited the capital-ship tonnage of the five principal navies in the famous ratio 5 : 5 : 3 : 1.75 : 1.75 for the United States, Britain, Japan, France and Italy respectively, capped individual battleships at 35,000 tons and 16-inch guns, and imposed a ten-year holiday on capital-ship construction. The Four-Power Treaty replaced the Anglo-Japanese Alliance with a consultative pact among the United States, Britain, Japan and France, while the Nine-Power Treaty affirmed China's sovereignty and the Open Door Policy. Japan also agreed to return the Shandong concessions to China.

Significance

The Washington system was the first major arms-control regime of the modern era and temporarily halted a naval arms race among the great powers. Its limits were extended by the London Naval Treaty of 1930, but the system collapsed in the mid-1930s as Japan demanded parity and withdrew, clearing the way for unrestricted rearmament before the Second World War.
