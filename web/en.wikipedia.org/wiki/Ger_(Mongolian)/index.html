Ger (Mongolian dwelling)

[Image: A ger on the Mongolian steppe, smoke rising from its central stove]

Overview

A ger is a portable, round tent covered with skins or felt, used as a dwelling by nomads in the steppes of Central Asia — the national dwelling of Mongolia (the Russian/Soviet-derived word "yurt" is common in the West and for the Kazakh and Kyrgyz equivalents). Its structure — lattice wall (khana), roof poles (uni), compression band and central crown ring (toono) — has changed little in a thousand years and can be assembled in well under an hour.

Life and culture

The ger's interior is cosmologically ordered: the hearth at the centre under the toono, the honoured north side (khoimor) opposite the south-facing door, men's and women's sides, and strict etiquette for guests. About a third of Mongolians still live in gers, including Ulaanbaatar's vast ger districts — whose coal-stove smoke drives the capital's severe winter air pollution, the central dilemma of this beloved dwelling today. The ger is inscribed in UNESCO-recognised heritage crafts of Mongolia and Kyrgyzstan.
