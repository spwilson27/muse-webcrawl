The Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB; Urdu: پاکستان کرکٹ بورڈ) is a sports governing body for cricket in Pakistan responsible for controlling and organising all tours and matches undertaken by the Pakistan national cricket team. A member of the International Cricket Council (ICC) since 1952, it represents the country's men's and women's national teams in international cricket and runs the country's domestic cricket competitions.

History

The board was founded on 1 May 1949 as the Board of Control for Cricket in Pakistan (BCCP), days before Pakistan's admission to the Imperial Cricket Conference. Pakistan played its first Test match in Delhi in October 1952. The board was renamed the Pakistan Cricket Board in the early 1970s. Under its administration Pakistan won the 1992 Cricket World Cup under Imran Khan, the 2009 ICC World Twenty20, and the 2017 ICC Champions Trophy.

Domestic cricket

The PCB oversees first-class competitions including the Quaid-e-Azam Trophy, the Pakistan Cup, and the National T20 Cup. In 2016 it launched the Pakistan Super League (PSL), a franchise-based Twenty20 league played by six city-based teams, which became a major commercial success and was progressively moved from the UAE to venues inside Pakistan.

International cricket in Pakistan

Following the 2009 attack on the Sri Lanka team bus in Lahore, touring teams declined to visit Pakistan for a decade, and the national team played its "home" matches mainly in the United Arab Emirates. International cricket gradually returned from 2015 onwards, with full tours resuming in the late 2010s and 2020s.
