Shazam (music app)

Shazam is an application that can identify music based on a short sample recorded through a device's microphone, using audio fingerprinting. It was developed by the British company Shazam Entertainment, founded in 1999, and launched as a telephone service in the United Kingdom in 2002 (via the short code 2580). Shazam was one of the first apps available in Apple's App Store in 2008 and became one of the most downloaded apps of all time. Apple acquired Shazam in 2018 for a reported $400 million and integrated its recognition technology into iOS, macOS, and Siri, while continuing the standalone app.

How it works

The app creates a spectrogram-based acoustic fingerprint of the recorded sample and searches a catalogue of fingerprints computed from millions of songs; the algorithm, described by co-founder Avery Li-Chun Wang in a widely cited 2003 paper, is robust to noise and distortion. A successful match returns the song title, artist, album, and links to streaming or purchase services, along with lyrics and related content.

Features and expansion

Shazam expanded from phones to wearables, desktop, and voice assistants. Under Apple it became ad-free, added offline tag queuing, an iOS Control Center toggle, and developer APIs (ShazamKit). The service also produced music charts from user "tags", and sister promotions under the "Shazaming" of TV broadcasts linked viewers to additional content.

Founders and history

Shazam Entertainment was founded by Chris Barton, Philip Inghelbrecht, Avery Li-Chun Wang, and Dhiraj Mukherjee. Before smartphones, users dialled 2580, held the phone to the music, and received an SMS identifying the track. The company raised substantial venture funding and operated the service at a loss for many years before profitability; Apple's acquisition closed in September 2018, and the European Commission cleared the deal after an in-depth investigation.
