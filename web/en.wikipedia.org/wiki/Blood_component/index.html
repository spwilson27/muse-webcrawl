Blood component

In transfusion medicine, blood components are the separable constituents of blood prepared from whole-blood donations or collected directly by apheresis. Component therapy allows one donation to treat several patients, each receiving only the fraction they need.

Main components

The principal components are packed red blood cells, used to treat anaemia; fresh frozen plasma, containing clotting factors; platelet concentrates for bleeding disorders and chemotherapy patients; and cryoprecipitate, rich in fibrinogen and factor VIII.

Preparation and storage

Donated blood is separated by centrifugation. Red cells are refrigerated for up to about 42 days, plasma is frozen for up to a year, and platelets are stored at room temperature with agitation for only a few days. Components are tested for transfusion-transmitted infections before release.
