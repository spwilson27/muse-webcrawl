Erdapfel

Creator: Martin Behaim
Date: 1492–1494
Location: Germanisches Nationalmuseum, Nuremberg

The Erdapfel (German: [ˈeːɐ̯tˌʔapfl̩], "earth apple") is a terrestrial globe produced by Martin Behaim from 1490 to 1492. It is the oldest surviving terrestrial globe, and is preserved in the Germanisches Nationalmuseum in Nuremberg. The word Erdapfel became a general German term for a globe; it also survives as a regional word for the potato.

Construction and content

The globe, about 51 cm in diameter, was made in the workshop of Behaim with the participation of the painter Georg Glockendon and other Nuremberg craftsmen. Its surface is painted on parchment strips stretched over a laminated linen ball. It reflects the late-medieval European view of the world: the Americas are absent (Columbus returned only in 1493), the Eurasian landmass is greatly elongated, and legendary places such as the island of Saint Brendan and the kingdom of Prester John appear, alongside extensive inscriptions drawn from sources including Marco Polo, Ptolemy, and the Nuremberg Chronicle.

Significance

The Erdapfel documents European geographical knowledge on the eve of the Age of Discovery and is inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World register. Conservation science, including multispectral imaging, has been used to read faded inscriptions.
