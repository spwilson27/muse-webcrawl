The Diocese of Bangor (Welsh: Esgobaeth Bangor) is a diocese of the Church in Wales, covering Anglesey, most of Gwynedd and the western part of Powys in north-west Wales. Its cathedral is Bangor Cathedral, dedicated to St Deiniol, and its bishop is the Bishop of Bangor.

History

The diocese traces its foundation to St Deiniol, who is traditionally said to have founded a monastery at Bangor around 546, making it one of the oldest religious sites in Britain still in continuous Christian use. The name Bangor derives from the Welsh for a wattled enclosure around the monastic settlement.

During the Middle Ages the diocese lay within the territory of the princes of Gwynedd and was frequently caught up in the conflicts between Wales and England. Following the Welsh Church Act 1914, the diocese became part of the disestablished Church in Wales when it came into being in 1920.

Structure

The diocese is divided into archdeaconries — Bangor, Meirionnydd and Anglesey — and, since the Church in Wales's reorganisation, its parishes are grouped into Ministry Areas served by teams of clergy and lay ministers.
