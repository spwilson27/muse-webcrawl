Annulus (mathematics)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, an annulus (Latin for "little ring", plural annuli) is the region bounded by two concentric circles. It is the two-dimensional analogue of a ring: a disc with a smaller, concentric disc removed from its interior. Annuli are open sets when defined with open discs, and they arise naturally in geometry, complex analysis, and topology.

Area

The area of an annulus with outer radius R and inner radius r is the difference of the areas of the two circles: A = π(R² − r²). This can also be written as π(R + r)(R − r), a form related to a classical result: the area depends only on the length of the longest chord tangent to the inner circle. Indeed, if a chord of the larger circle is tangent to the smaller one and has length 2L, the annulus's area is exactly πL², regardless of the individual radii — a fact used in the napkin ring problem.

In complex analysis and topology

In complex analysis, an annulus centred at a point a is the set of complex numbers with r < |z − a| < R. Every doubly connected domain in the complex plane is conformally equivalent to an annulus, and the ratio R/r (its modulus) is a conformal invariant. Laurent series expand holomorphic functions on annuli, generalising Taylor series. Topologically, an annulus is homeomorphic to a cylinder and to a punctured plane; it has fundamental group isomorphic to the integers, reflecting the hole at its centre.
