Ancient Greek dialects were the regional varieties of the Greek language spoken in the ancient world. Despite political fragmentation into city-states, the dialects remained mutually intelligible to a substantial degree, and over time particular dialects became associated with particular literary genres: epic with an artificial mixture based on Ionic, choral lyric with Doric colouring, personal lyric with Aeolic, and prose and drama with Attic.

Main groups

  • Ionic–Attic: spoken in Attica, Euboea, the Cyclades and the central Aegean coast of Asia Minor. Attic became the basis of the later Koine.
  • Doric: spoken in the southern and western Peloponnese (including Sparta), Crete, Rhodes, and colonies in Sicily and southern Italy; Northwest Greek (Phocian, Locrian, Elean) is often grouped with it.
  • Aeolic: spoken in Boeotia, Thessaly and Lesbos and the adjacent coast of Asia Minor; the dialect of Sappho and Alcaeus.
  • Arcadocypriot: spoken in Arcadia and Cyprus, and the closest relative of Bronze Age Mycenaean Greek.

Origins

The dialect map is traditionally explained by successive waves of Greek-speaking groups entering Greece, with Doric speakers arriving last and displacing or overlaying earlier varieties in the Peloponnese — hence landlocked Arcadia and overseas Cyprus preserved the older Arcadocypriot type. Mycenaean Greek, recorded in Linear B tablets from palaces such as Knossos and Pylos, shows features ancestral to Arcadocypriot.

Distinctive features

Dialects differed in phonology (for example, Doric retained long ā where Attic–Ionic shifted it to ē, hence Doric mātēr vs Attic mētēr, "mother"), in morphology (Doric third-person plural endings in -nti, Aeolic athematic infinitive forms), and in vocabulary. Inscriptions in local alphabets preserve the everyday dialect record, while literature is filtered through genre conventions.

Legacy

In the Hellenistic period the dialects were largely levelled into Koine Greek, though Doric survived in altered form in Tsakonian, still spoken in parts of the Peloponnese, and Italiot Greek in southern Italy preserves Doric features.
