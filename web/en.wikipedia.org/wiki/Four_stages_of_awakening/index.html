Four stages of awakening

The four stages of awakening in early Buddhism (and Theravada) are four progressive stages culminating in full awakening (bodhi) as an arahant. A person who reaches one of these stages is regarded as a noble person (ariya-puggala). The stages are defined by the successive abandonment of the ten fetters (samyojana) binding beings to samsara.

Four stages of awakening
1. Stream-entererSotāpanna — first 3 fetters abandoned
2. Once-returnerSakadāgāmi — lust and ill will weakened
3. Non-returnerAnāgāmi — first 5 fetters abandoned
4. ArahantAll 10 fetters abandoned; full liberation

The stages

  • Stream-enterer (sotāpanna) — has abandoned identity view, doubt and attachment to rites and rituals; will be reborn at most seven more times and cannot fall into lower realms.
  • Once-returner (sakadāgāmi) — has weakened sensual desire and ill will; will return to the human world only once more.
  • Non-returner (anāgāmi) — has abandoned the five lower fetters, including sensual desire and ill will; will not be reborn in the desire realm.
  • Arahant — has abandoned all ten fetters, including desire for form and formless existence, conceit, restlessness and ignorance; fully liberated.
