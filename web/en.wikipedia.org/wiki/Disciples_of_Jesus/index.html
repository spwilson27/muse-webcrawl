Disciples of Jesus

SubjectFollowers of Jesus in the Gospels
Inner circleThe Twelve Apostles
SourcesFour Gospels, Acts of the Apostles

In the New Testament, the disciples of Jesus were his primary followers during his ministry. The word "disciple" derives from the Latin discipulus, "learner", translating the Greek mathētēs. Beyond the inner group of the Twelve Apostles, the Gospels describe a wider circle of disciples, including the seventy (or seventy-two) sent out in Luke's Gospel and women followers such as Mary Magdalene.

The calling of the disciples

The Synoptic Gospels describe Jesus calling fishermen — Simon Peter, Andrew, James and John — by the Sea of Galilee with the words "Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men". Other calls include the tax collector Matthew (Levi) and, in John's Gospel, Philip and Nathanael.

Role in the ministry

Disciples travelled with Jesus, witnessed his teaching and miracles, and were sent out to preach and heal. The Gospels also portray their misunderstandings and failures, culminating in their flight at the arrest of Jesus and Peter's denial. After the resurrection appearances and Pentecost, the disciples — now generally called apostles — became the leaders of the early church in Jerusalem.

Discipleship

In Christian usage the term broadened to mean any follower of Jesus; the "cost of discipleship" — self-denial and taking up the cross — became a central theme of Christian spirituality.
