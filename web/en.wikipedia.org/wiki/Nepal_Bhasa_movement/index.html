Nepal Bhasa movement

The Nepal Bhasa movement is a broad social and cultural movement seeking the preservation, promotion and official recognition of Nepal Bhasa (Newari), the language of the Newar people of the Kathmandu Valley in Nepal. The movement arose in response to state policies that suppressed the language during the Rana regime and the Panchayat era.

History

Under the Rana regime (1846–1951), publishing and public use of Nepal Bhasa were restricted, and writers who produced works in the language faced imprisonment. The language experienced a literary renaissance in the early 20th century despite the repression, led by figures such as Nisthananda Bajracharya, Siddhidas Mahaju, Jagat Sundar Malla and Yogbir Singh Kansakar.

During the Panchayat period (1960–1990), the "one nation, one language" policy further marginalised Nepal Bhasa in education and media. Activists campaigned for mother-tongue education and broadcasting rights.

Modern developments

Following the restoration of democracy in 1990 and the federal restructuring of Nepal after 2015, Nepal Bhasa gained greater recognition. In 2024, the Bagmati Province government gave Nepal Bhasa official status for government work in the province, alongside Nepali and Tamang.
