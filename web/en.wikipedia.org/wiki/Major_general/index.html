Major general

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Major general (abbreviated Maj Gen) is a senior military rank used in many countries. It originated in the 17th century as the rank of sergeant-major general, the chief administrative officer subordinate to a lieutenant general. In most modern armies it is a two-star rank (NATO code OF-7), senior to a brigadier or brigadier general and junior to a lieutenant general, and is typically held by officers commanding a division or equivalent formation.

Usage by country

In the British Army and Royal Marines, a major-general commands a division and is a two-star rank, its insignia a pip over the crossed sword and baton. In the United States Army, Air Force, Marine Corps and Space Force, a major general wears two stars and commands divisions of 10,000–15,000 soldiers. In some countries, such as Germany (Generalmajor) and Russia (general-mayor), the equivalent rank is the lowest of the general-officer grades, corresponding to a one-star brigadier elsewhere — an anomaly arising because those systems lack a separate brigadier rank below it.

Etymology

The rank derives from "sergeant major general", contracted first to "major general": the "major" reflects the holder's role as chief of staff-like assistant to the lieutenant general, not superiority to that officer, which is why a major general ranks below a lieutenant general despite the names.
