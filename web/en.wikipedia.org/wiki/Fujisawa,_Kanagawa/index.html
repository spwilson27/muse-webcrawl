Fujisawa (藤沢市, Fujisawa-shi) is a city in Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan, on the Pacific coast of the Shōnan region, about 50 km southwest of Tokyo. It is best known for Enoshima, a small island joined to the mainland by a causeway, and for the beaches of the Shōnan coast, among Japan's most popular surfing and swimming destinations.

History

Fujisawa developed in the Edo period as Fujisawa-shuku, a post station on the Tōkaidō highway linking Edo and Kyoto. In 1192 the Yugyō-ji temple (Shōjōkō-ji) was founded here and became the head temple of the Jishū sect. The city was heavily damaged by the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake. Fujisawa received city status in 1940 and absorbed neighbouring towns and villages after the war.

Geography and landmarks

The city faces Sagami Bay. Landmarks include Enoshima island with its shrine and observation lighthouse, the Shōnan Enoshima Railway (a street-running line nicknamed the Enoden), the beaches of Kugenuma and Katase, and the campus of Keio University Shonan Fujisawa. The Tsujidō area is known for its aquarium, the Enoshima Aquarium.

Economy and transport

Fujisawa is a commercial and residential satellite of Tokyo and Yokohama, with manufacturing (including automotive and electronics plants) and a large seasonal tourism economy. It is served by the Tōkaidō Main Line, the Odakyū Enoshima Line, the Sagami Railway, and the Shōnan Monorail, the last of which links Ōfuna with Enoshima.

Sport

Fujisawa's Enoshima Yacht Harbour hosted the sailing events of the 1964 Tokyo Olympics and again for the 2020 Summer Olympics (held 2021), for which the harbour was redeveloped. The city is also associated with the Shonan Bellmare professional football club.
