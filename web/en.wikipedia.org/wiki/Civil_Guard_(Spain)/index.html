Civil Guard (Spain)

The Civil Guard (Spanish: Guardia Civil) is Spain's national gendarmerie force, founded in 1844. It is a military force charged with police duties, principally in rural areas, under the authority of the Ministry of the Interior and the Ministry of Defence.

Guardia Civil
Founded: 13 May 1844
Founder: Duke of Ahumada
Motto: El honor es mi divisa ("Honour is my emblem")
Personnel: c. 80,000

History

Created by the 2nd Duke of Ahumada to combat banditry, it became a defining — and under Franco, feared — institution. The February 1981 coup attempt, in which Lt. Col. Antonio Tejero led Civil Guards into Congress, marked a low point before democratic reform.

Roles

Duties include rural policing, highways, borders and coasts, environmental protection (SEPRONA), counter-terrorism, and specialised units such as mountain rescue and the GAR rapid-action group. It serves in EU and international missions and is part of the European Gendarmerie Force.

Organisation

The force is instantly recognisable historically by the tricorn hat (tricornio). It complements the National Police, which covers larger cities. See National Police Corps.
