Chess Records was an American record company established in Chicago in 1950 by Polish immigrant brothers Leonard and Phil Chess. It became one of the most important labels in the history of blues and rock and roll.

History and artists

The brothers took over the Aristocrat label and renamed it Chess in 1950. Recording at studios including 2120 South Michigan Avenue — memorialised in the Rolling Stones instrumental "2120 South Michigan Avenue" — Chess signed Muddy Waters, Howlin' Wolf, Little Walter, Chuck Berry, Bo Diddley, Etta James, Willie Dixon (as songwriter and producer), and many others. Producer and talent scout figures such as Dixon and later Roquel "Billy" Davis shaped the Chess sound.

Decline

The Chess brothers sold the label to General Recorded Tape (GRT) in 1969; Leonard died that year. The company folded in 1975. Its catalogue profoundly influenced British blues-rock groups such as the Rolling Stones and Led Zeppelin. The story was dramatised in the films Cadillac Records (2008) and Who Do You Love? (2008).
