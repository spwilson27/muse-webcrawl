Thameslink Programme


A Class 700 train, introduced under the Thameslink Programme

The Thameslink Programme, originally known as Thameslink 2000, was a £6.5 billion project to expand the Thameslink rail network in England, increasing capacity on the north–south route through central London. It upgraded stations, track, signalling and rolling stock, allowing longer and more frequent trains to run through the Thameslink core between London Bridge and St Pancras International.

Scope

The programme included the rebuilding of London Bridge station, the remodelling of the track layout at Bermondsey and the construction of the Borough Market Viaduct to remove a key bottleneck. Farringdon, Blackfriars and London Bridge stations were substantially enlarged, and platform extensions at many outlying stations allowed 12-car trains.

A new fleet of 115 Class 700 trains was procured from Siemens, and automatic train operation with ETCS signalling in the central core enabled frequencies of up to 24 trains per hour.

Completion

The core infrastructure was completed in 2018, when the full timetable was introduced — though the May 2018 timetable change caused severe disruption across the Thameslink and Great Northern networks, drawing criticism of the Department for Transport and operator Govia Thameslink Railway. The final elements of the programme were completed in 2019.
