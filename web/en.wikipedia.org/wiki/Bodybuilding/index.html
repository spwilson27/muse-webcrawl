Bodybuilding

Bodybuilding is the development and growth of the body's muscles through exercise, diet and rest, pursued competitively or recreationally. Competitive bodybuilders appear in lineups doing specified poses, and later individual routines, before a panel of judges who rank them on muscularity, symmetry, proportion, conditioning and presentation. The sport's most prestigious title is Mr. Olympia, created by Joe Weider in 1965.

History

Modern bodybuilding descends from 19th-century strongman culture and was systematised by Eugen Sandow, the "father of bodybuilding", who staged the first major contest at London's Royal Albert Hall in 1901. The sport grew through the physique magazines and gyms of the mid-20th century — Muscle Beach in California, the rivalry of governing bodies (AAU, NABBA, IFBB) — and reached mass audiences in the "golden era" of Arnold Schwarzenegger, whose seven Mr. Olympia titles and the 1977 film Pumping Iron made bodybuilding a cultural phenomenon.

Training and preparation

Bodybuilders use progressive resistance training to induce hypertrophy, follow high-protein diets, and cycle between "bulking" (caloric surplus) and "cutting" (fat loss) phases, depleting water and carbohydrates before contests to maximise definition. The use of anabolic steroids and other performance-enhancing drugs is widespread in untested competition and is a persistent health and ethics controversy; natural federations drug-test competitors.

Competitions and categories

The IFBB (now IFBB Professional League) sanctions Mr. Olympia, the Arnold Classic and divisions including Men's Open, 212, Classic Physique, Men's Physique, and women's divisions such as Ms. Olympia, Figure, Bikini and Wellness. Bodybuilding is distinct from powerlifting and Olympic weightlifting, which are judged on performance rather than appearance.
