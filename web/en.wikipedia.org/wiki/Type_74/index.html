Type 74 main battle tank

The Type 74 (74式戦車, nana-yon-shiki-sensha) is a main battle tank of the Japan Ground Self-Defense Force (JGSDF). It was built by Mitsubishi Heavy Industries as a replacement for the earlier Type 61. It was based on the best features of a number of contemporary designs, placing it in the same class as the US Patton series and the German Leopard 1.

History

The JGSDF began studies on a new tank design in 1962, as the Type 61 was already becoming obsolete compared with newer Soviet tanks such as the T-62. Features such as a hydropneumatic suspension system, allowing the tank to "sit", "stand", "kneel" or "lean", and the layout of the Leopard 1's hull were incorporated. The prototype was publicly demonstrated in 1971 and production began in 1975. A total of 873 were built between 1975 and 1989.

Design

The Type 74 is armed with a 105 mm main gun derived from the British Royal Ordnance L7. The hull is welded steel, and the turret is cast steel. The hydropneumatic suspension, developed by Mitsubishi, allows the driver to adjust the height and tilt of the hull — a feature especially useful on Japan's mountainous terrain. For secondary armament it carries a 12.7 mm M2 Browning anti-aircraft machine gun and a coaxial 7.62 mm machine gun.

Service

The Type 74 served as Japan's main battle tank until the introduction of the Type 90 in 1990, and it continued in service alongside the Type 90 and the later Type 10. Although planned for progressive retirement, the tank remained in front-line and reserve service for decades owing to its reliability and the slow pace of replacement.
