Trio (music)

A trio is a group of three musicians performing together, or a piece of music composed for such a group. It is one of the most common small-ensemble formats in Western classical music, and also appears in jazz, rock, and folk traditions.

Classical forms

Standard classical trios include the piano trio (piano, violin, and cello), the string trio (violin, viola, and cello), and combinations with a wind instrument such as the clarinet trio. In the Baroque era, the trio sonata — written on three staves but usually played by four musicians including continuo — was a dominant chamber form, cultivated by composers such as Corelli and Handel. Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven, Schubert, Mendelssohn, Brahms, and Dvořák all wrote major piano trios.

Other meanings

"Trio" also names the middle, contrasting section of a minuet or march (minuet and trio), a legacy of the Baroque practice of scoring that section for three instruments. In jazz, a trio typically consists of piano (or guitar), bass, and drums.
