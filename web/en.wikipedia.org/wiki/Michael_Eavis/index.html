Sir Athelstan Joseph Michael Eavis CBE (born 17 October 1935) is an English dairy farmer and the founder of the Glastonbury Festival, which takes place at his Worthy Farm near Pilton, Somerset.

Early life

Eavis was born in Pilton and educated at Wells Cathedral School and the Royal Naval College, Dartmouth; he briefly served as a midshipman before returning to run the family farm after his father's death, becoming a dairy farmer and Methodist local preacher. He also worked as a coal miner for a time in the Somerset coalfield.

Glastonbury Festival

Inspired by the 1970 Bath Festival of Blues and by the counterculture of the time, Eavis held the first Pilton Pop, Folk & Blues Festival on 19 September 1970 — the day after Jimi Hendrix died — charging £1 including free milk from the farm. It grew, with interruptions, into the Glastonbury Festival of Contemporary Performing Arts, the world's largest greenfield music and arts festival, attracting around 200,000 people. Eavis has been known for directing festival profits to charities including Oxfam, Greenpeace and WaterAid. His daughter Emily Eavis gradually took over the festival's organisation.

Honours and politics

Eavis stood for Parliament in Wells for the Labour Party in 1997 and later supported the Green Party and Jeremy Corbyn. He was appointed CBE in 2007 and received a knighthood in the 2024 New Year Honours for services to music and charity.
