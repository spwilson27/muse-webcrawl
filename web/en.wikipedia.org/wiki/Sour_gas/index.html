Sour gas

Sour gas is natural gas or any other gas mixture containing significant amounts of hydrogen sulfide (H2S). Natural gas is usually considered sour if it contains more than about 5.7 milligrams of H2S per cubic metre of gas, equivalent to roughly 4 parts per million by volume. Natural gas that does not contain significant hydrogen sulfide is called sweet gas.

Hazards

Hydrogen sulfide is a highly toxic, flammable, and corrosive gas with a characteristic rotten-egg odour at low concentrations. At higher concentrations it rapidly paralyses the sense of smell, removing the warning property; exposure to concentrations of several hundred parts per million can be immediately dangerous to life. Sour gas therefore demands rigorous safety procedures in drilling, production, and processing, including continuous H2S monitoring, breathing apparatus, and emergency planning for nearby communities.

In the presence of water, hydrogen sulfide forms a corrosive environment that can cause sulfide stress cracking of susceptible steels. Materials for sour service are specified by standards such as NACE MR0175/ISO 15156, which limit hardness and prescribe resistant alloys.

Processing

Before sour gas can be sold or used as fuel, the hydrogen sulfide and carbon dioxide must be removed in a process called gas sweetening, most commonly by absorption in aqueous amine solutions such as monoethanolamine or methyldiethanolamine. The acid gases stripped from the amine are typically sent to a Claus process unit, where hydrogen sulfide is converted into elemental sulfur, a saleable by-product. Sour crude oil similarly contains dissolved H2S and sulfur compounds and commands a price discount relative to sweet crude.

Occurrence

Major sour gas fields include the Shah and Bab fields of the United Arab Emirates, the Kashagan field in Kazakhstan, and fields in western Canada, where sour gas wells near populated areas have prompted strict regulation. The Lacq field in France, developed in the 1950s, was a landmark in handling very sour gas and gave rise to a large sulfur industry.
