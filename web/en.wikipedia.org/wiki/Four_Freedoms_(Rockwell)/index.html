Four Freedoms (Rockwell)

ArtistNorman Rockwell
Year1943
MediumOil on canvas
LocationNorman Rockwell Museum, Stockbridge, Massachusetts

The Four Freedoms is a series of four oil paintings by American artist Norman Rockwell, created in 1943 and published in The Saturday Evening Post alongside essays by prominent American writers. The paintings — Freedom of Speech, Freedom of Worship, Freedom from Want, and Freedom from Fear — were inspired by President Franklin D. Roosevelt's 1941 State of the Union address.

The paintings

Each canvas depicts an everyday American scene: a working man standing to speak at a town meeting; neighbors of varied faiths praying; a family gathered around a Thanksgiving turkey; and parents tucking their children into bed. Rockwell used his Arlington, Vermont neighbors as models.

War bond tour

The Post's printing of the series prompted an enormous public response. A nationwide tour of the originals, organized with the U.S. Treasury, drew over a million visitors and raised roughly 33 million in war bond sales. Reprints were distributed by the millions, making the images among the most widely reproduced American artworks of the war.

Legacy

The paintings now hang at the Norman Rockwell Museum in Stockbridge, Massachusetts, and remain iconic expressions of American democratic ideals, frequently referenced and reinterpreted by later artists.
