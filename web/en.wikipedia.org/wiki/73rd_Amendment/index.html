73rd Amendment

Constitution (73rd Amendment) Act, 1992
CountryIndia
Enacted1992; in force 24 April 1993
SubjectPanchayati Raj — rural local self-government
AddedPart IX and the Eleventh Schedule to the Constitution

The Seventy-third Amendment of the Constitution of India, enacted in 1992 and effective from 24 April 1993, gave constitutional status to the Panchayati Raj system of rural local self-government. It inserted Part IX (Articles 243 to 243-O) and the Eleventh Schedule, listing 29 subjects that may be devolved to panchayats.

Provisions

The amendment mandates a three-tier structure — Gram Panchayat at village level, Panchayat Samiti at block level, and Zila Parishad at district level (with exceptions for small states) — regular five-yearly elections conducted by State Election Commissions, and Gram Sabhas (village assemblies) as deliberative bodies. It reserves seats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in proportion to population, and not less than one-third of seats — and of chairperson offices — for women, a share several states have since raised to one-half.

Significance

The amendment created roughly a quarter-million elected local bodies and about three million elected representatives, among them well over a million women, making it one of the largest exercises in democratic decentralisation in the world. 24 April is observed in India as National Panchayati Raj Day. Its companion, the 74th Amendment, did the same for urban municipalities.
