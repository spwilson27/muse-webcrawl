The Encyclopaedia of Islam (EI) is a major scholarly encyclopaedia of the academic discipline of Islamic studies, published by Brill in Leiden. It covers the religion, peoples, cultures, geography, and history of the Muslim world from the rise of Islam to the present, and is regarded as a standard reference work in the field. Articles are signed by their authors and supplied with substantial bibliographies.

Editions

The first edition (EI1) was published between 1913 and 1938 under the managing editorship of M. Th. Houtsma and colleagues, appearing in English, French, and German versions, and amounting to four volumes plus a supplement. A shortened version was published in 1953 as the Shorter Encyclopaedia of Islam, intended chiefly for non-specialists.

The second edition (EI2), published from 1954 to 2005, grew to twelve volumes plus supplementary fascicles and an index volume. It was substantially larger and more authoritative than its predecessor and is the edition most often cited by scholars. Since 2007 the encyclopaedia has also been available online.

The third edition (EI3), begun in 2007, is published in English, initially online and in periodic printed installments. It broadens coverage to the twentieth and twenty-first centuries and to Muslim communities worldwide, including Europe and the Americas, with a renewed emphasis on social and cultural history.

Scope and reception

Entries range from short notices on terms and places to monograph-length articles on major figures, dynasties, and concepts. The encyclopaedia's transliteration system for Arabic, Persian, and Turkish has been widely adopted in Western scholarship. Together with its companion publications, including the Encyclopaedia of the Qurʾān and the Encyclopedia of Arabic Language and Linguistics, it forms the core reference apparatus of Islamic studies.
