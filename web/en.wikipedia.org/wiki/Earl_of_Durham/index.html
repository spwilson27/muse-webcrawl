Earl of Durham
Created: 1833
First holder: John Lambton, 1st Earl
Family: Lambton

Earl of Durham is a title in the Peerage of the United Kingdom, created in 1833 for the politician and colonial administrator John George Lambton, nicknamed "Radical Jack", together with the subsidiary titles Viscount Lambton and Baron Durham.

History

John Lambton had previously been raised to the peerage as Baron Durham in 1828. As Governor General of British North America he authored the 1839 Report on the Affairs of British North America (the Durham Report), which recommended responsible government and the union of Upper and Lower Canada. Subsequent earls included George Lambton, 2nd Earl, and the racing figure Frederick Lambton.

Seat

The family seat is Lambton Castle in County Durham, built around Harraton Hall by the Lambton family.
