Lanthanide contraction

The lanthanide contraction is the greater-than-expected decrease in atomic and ionic radii of the lanthanide elements as the atomic number increases, from lanthanum (atomic number 57) to lutetium (71). The ionic radius falls from about 103 pm for La³⁺ to 86 pm for Lu³⁺.

Cause

The contraction results from the poor shielding of nuclear charge by electrons in the 4f subshell. As protons are added across the series, the 4f electrons added at the same time do not shield each other effectively, so the effective nuclear charge felt by the outer electrons rises and pulls them closer to the nucleus.

Consequences

The contraction makes the later lanthanides much more similar in size to each other and to yttrium, complicating their chemical separation. It also causes the elements following the lanthanides, such as hafnium, to be smaller than expected — hafnium is nearly identical in radius to zirconium above it.
