Everton Football Club is a professional football club based in Liverpool, England, competing in the Premier League. Founded in 1878 as St Domingo's, the church team of a Methodist congregation, Everton were founder members of the Football League in 1888 and won their first league championship in 1891. Nicknamed "the Toffees", the club plays in royal blue shirts and white shorts.

History

Everton originally played at Anfield, but a rent dispute in 1892 led them to build Goodison Park across Stanley Park — while the landlord formed a new club, Liverpool F.C., at Anfield, creating one of football's great rivalries, the Merseyside derby. Everton's great pre-war sides featured the prodigious Dixie Dean, whose 60 league goals in 1927–28 remain an English record. Post-war highlights include the title-winning teams of Harry Catterick (1963, 1970) and Howard Kendall's side of the mid-1980s — champions in 1985 and 1987 and winners of the European Cup Winners' Cup in 1985, the club's only European trophy. The 1995 FA Cup was their last major honour for many years, and David Moyes's long tenure (2002–2013) restored stability without silverware.

Recent years

After investment by Farhad Moshiri from 2016 failed to bring success, Everton fought a series of relegation battles in the early 2020s and were docked points in 2023–24 for breaching the Premier League's profit and sustainability rules, surviving nonetheless. The Friedkin Group completed a takeover in December 2024, and David Moyes returned as manager in January 2025. In the summer of 2025 the club left Goodison Park after 133 years for a new 52,888-seat stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock on Liverpool's waterfront.

Honours

  • First Division champions: 9 (1890–91, 1914–15, 1927–28, 1931–32, 1938–39, 1962–63, 1969–70, 1984–85, 1986–87)
  • FA Cup winners: 5 (1906, 1933, 1966, 1984, 1995)
  • European Cup Winners' Cup: 1 (1985)
