The Nature of the Judicial Process

The Nature of the Judicial Process is a 1921 book by American jurist Benjamin N. Cardozo, based on the Storrs Lectures he delivered at Yale Law School. It is one of the most influential short works on how judges decide cases.

Contents

Cardozo argued that judging, especially in hard cases, is not merely mechanical logic. He identified four methods that guide judicial decision-making: the method of philosophy (logic and analogy), the method of evolution (history), the method of tradition (custom), and the method of sociology (considerations of justice, morals, and social welfare). He candidly acknowledged the role of the judge's own experience and subconscious preferences while insisting these forces are constrained by precedent and professional duty.

Influence

The book is a classic of legal realism-adjacent thought and remains widely read in law schools as an account of common-law adjudication and the growth of law through judicial decisions.
