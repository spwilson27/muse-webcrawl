Aquitaine (French: Aquitaine; Occitan: Aquitània) is a historical region of southwestern France, formerly an administrative region comprising the departments of Dordogne, Gironde, Landes, Lot-et-Garonne and Pyrénées-Atlantiques. On 1 January 2016 it merged with Limousin and Poitou-Charentes to form the region of Nouvelle-Aquitaine.

History

Roman Gallia Aquitania took its name from the Aquitani, an Iberian-related people described by Julius Caesar. In the Middle Ages the Duchy of Aquitaine, held famously by Eleanor of Aquitaine, passed to the English crown in 1152, making the region a battleground in the Hundred Years' War until France recovered it in 1453.

Geography and economy

The region stretches from the Atlantic coast — with the Côte d'Argent, the Dune of Pilat and the Landes forest — to the foothills of the Pyrenees. It is renowned for Bordeaux wine, oysters from Arcachon Bay, tourism and aerospace industry around Bordeaux.
