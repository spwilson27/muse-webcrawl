Bhajan

Bhajan
OriginBhakti movement, medieval India
InstrumentsHarmonium, tabla, mridangam, kartal, tanpura
Related formsKirtan, abhang, shabad

A bhajan is an Indian devotional song with a religious theme or spiritual ideas, in any language, though most commonly in Hindi and other languages of India. Bhajans are a central practice of the Bhakti movement in Hinduism, and are also found in Jainism, Sikhism, and other traditions of the Indian subcontinent.

Characteristics

The term bhajan derives from the Sanskrit root bhaj, meaning "to share, partake, or worship". Bhajans are usually sung in groups, with a lead singer and a chorus, accompanied by instruments such as the harmonium, tabla, dholak, mridangam, and kartal (cymbals). Lyrics typically invoke or narrate the deeds of a deity such as Krishna, Rama, Shiva, or the Goddess, or express longing for union with the divine.

History

Devotional singing has roots in the hymns of the Samaveda and in the verses of the Tamil Alvars. The bhakti movements of medieval India popularized vernacular devotional songs; celebrated composers include Mirabai, Kabir, Surdas, Tulsidas, and Tukaram. Their compositions remain the core repertoire of bhajan singing today.

Modern practice

Bhajans are sung in temples, homes, and public gatherings (satsangs), and by diaspora communities worldwide. Noted modern performers include Anup Jalota, Pandit Bhimsen Joshi, and Lata Mangeshkar. In the West, bhajan and kirtan singing were popularized by figures such as Krishna Das.
