Jan Evangelista Purkyně

Jan Evangelista Purkyně (Czech spelling; German: Johannes Evangelista Purkinje; 17 December 1787 – 28 July 1869) was a Czech anatomist and physiologist, one of the founders of modern experimental physiology. Numerous anatomical structures bear his name, including Purkinje cells of the cerebellum and Purkinje fibres of the heart.

Career

Purkyně studied medicine in Prague and became professor of physiology at the University of Breslau in 1823, where he founded one of the world's first physiological institutes. He returned to Prague in 1850 as professor at Charles University.

Discoveries

He described Purkinje cells (1837), Purkinje fibres in cardiac tissue (1839), the Purkinje effect (the shift of visual sensitivity toward blue light in dim conditions), and the Purkinje images reflected from the eye. He also proposed an early cell theory of plants and animals and introduced the terms plasma and protoplasm.
