The Alpine ibex (Capra ibex), also known as the steinbock, is a species of wild goat that lives in the mountains of the European Alps. It is sexually dimorphic: males are larger and have long, curved horns with prominent transverse ridges, while females have shorter, thinner horns. Its coat is brownish grey, darker in winter.

Behaviour and ecology

Ibex are superb climbers, favouring steep, rugged terrain above the treeline where they graze on grasses, herbs and shrubs; they descend to lower slopes in spring. Outside the winter rut, males and females live in separate herds. Their agility famously extends to near-vertical dam walls, which they climb to lick mineral salts, as at the Cingino Dam in Italy.

Conservation

By the early 19th century, hunting had reduced the Alpine ibex to a single population of around 100 animals in Italy's Gran Paradiso area, preserved as a royal hunting reserve. Careful protection and reintroductions across Italy, Switzerland, France, Austria, Germany and Slovenia rebuilt the population to tens of thousands, making the species a celebrated conservation success. All modern populations descend from that narrow genetic bottleneck, which leaves them vulnerable to disease and low genetic diversity.
