Agip

Agip (Azienda Generale Italiana Petroli, "Italian General Petroleum Company") is an Italian automotive gasoline, diesel, LPG, lubricants, and fuel retail brand of Eni. Founded in 1926, Agip was Italy's state petroleum company and became Eni's principal petrol station brand.

History

Agip was created by the Italian government in 1926 to reduce dependence on foreign oil companies, exploring for oil in Italy and its colonies and building a distribution network. After the Second World War, under Enrico Mattei, Agip's parent Eni expanded aggressively; the discovery of natural gas in the Po Valley in the late 1940s transformed Italy's energy supply.

Brand

The brand is famous for its logo, a black six-legged dog breathing flames, designed by sculptor Luigi Broggini for a 1952 competition and nicknamed "Supercortemaggiore's dog". Agip stations historically operated across Italy and in many other European and African countries; from around 2010 Eni progressively rebranded its stations under the Eni name, though Agip survives on lubricants and some networks.
