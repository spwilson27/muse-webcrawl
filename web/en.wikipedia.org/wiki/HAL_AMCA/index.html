HAL AMCA

The Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA) is an Indian programme to develop a fifth-generation, twin-engine, all-weather stealth multirole fighter aircraft for the Indian Air Force and the Indian Navy. The design is being developed by the Aeronautical Development Agency (ADA) and will be manufactured by Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL) together with private industry partners.

Development

The AMCA programme originated in studies for a medium combat aircraft in the 2000s and was formally pursued after 2010 as India's second indigenous fighter programme after the HAL Tejas. The design features internal weapons bays, serpentine air intakes, diverterless supersonic inlets, and extensive use of composites for low radar cross-section. The government of India approved full-scale development funding in 2024, with a first prototype flight targeted for the latter part of the decade.

Variants

The AMCA is planned in two marks. The Mark 1 is expected to fly with General Electric F414 engines, while the Mark 2 is intended to use a more powerful engine of about 110 kN thrust to be developed jointly with a foreign partner, enabling supercruise. A naval variant for carrier operations has also been studied.

Specifications (planned)

The aircraft is designed for a maximum take-off weight of roughly 25 tonnes, with internal fuel, a combat radius extended by internal weapons carriage, and a sensor suite centred on an AESA radar, infrared search and track, and sensor fusion.
