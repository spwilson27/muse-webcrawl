Longs Peak is a mountain in northern Colorado, the highest summit of Rocky Mountain National Park and the northernmost fourteener (peak over 14,000 feet) in the Rocky Mountains. Its elevation is 14,259 feet (4,346 m). The mountain was named in honour of explorer Stephen Harriman Long and is featured on the Colorado state quarter.

Climbing

The standard route, the Keyhole Route, is a demanding scramble rather than a hike: climbers pass through the Boulder Field, the Keyhole notch, and along the Ledges, the Trough and the Homestretch to the flat summit. Thousands attempt the peak each summer, and the mountain has a significant record of fatalities from falls, lightning and exposure, leading park rangers to stress early starts and careful preparation.

The sheer east face, known as the Diamond, is a major objective for technical rock climbing, with the Casual Route among its best-known climbs. The first recorded ascent of the summit was by John Wesley Powell's survey party in 1868; author and naturalist Isabella Bird climbed it in 1873 and described the ascent in A Lady's Life in the Rocky Mountains.
