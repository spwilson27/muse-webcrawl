Community service

Community service is unpaid work performed by a person or group of people for the benefit and betterment of their community without any form of compensation. Community service can be distinct from volunteering, since it is not always performed on a voluntary basis and may be compulsory or court-ordered. It may be performed for a variety of reasons, such as citizenship requirements, as an alternative sentence to imprisonment, or as a school graduation requirement.

Court-ordered community service

Courts in many jurisdictions may sentence offenders to perform unpaid work for the community instead of, or in addition to, other punishment. Common tasks include litter removal, graffiti cleaning, working in charity shops or food banks, and maintaining public spaces. In the United Kingdom this is known as "community payback" under a community order; in the United States it is frequently imposed for minor offences such as traffic violations or vandalism.

School and civic requirements

Many schools require students to complete a number of community service hours in order to graduate, and service-learning programs integrate community work with classroom instruction. Some countries also require community service as part of citizenship or national service obligations.
