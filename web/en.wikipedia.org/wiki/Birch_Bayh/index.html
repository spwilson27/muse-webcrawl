Birch Evans Bayh Jr. (January 22, 1928 – March 14, 2019) was an American Democratic politician who served as a United States Senator from Indiana from 1963 to 1981. He is the only non-Founding Father credited with authoring two amendments to the United States Constitution, and he was the principal Senate sponsor of Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972.

Constitutional amendments

Bayh chaired the Senate Subcommittee on Constitutional Amendments and authored the Twenty-fifth Amendment (1967), which established procedures for presidential succession and for filling a vacancy in the vice presidency, and the Twenty-sixth Amendment (1971), which lowered the voting age to 18 nationwide. He also championed the Equal Rights Amendment in the Senate.

Title IX

Working with Representative Edith Green and Representative Patsy Mink, Bayh was the key Senate author of Title IX, the 1972 law prohibiting sex discrimination in any educational programme receiving federal funds, which transformed women's participation in American school and college athletics and academics.

Later career

Bayh sought the Democratic presidential nomination in 1976, withdrawing early in the primaries. He was defeated for re-election to the Senate in 1980 by Dan Quayle, amid the Republican landslide of that year. His son, Evan Bayh, later served as Governor of Indiana and as a United States Senator from the same seat.
