Kayfabe

In professional wrestling, kayfabe is the portrayal of staged events as genuine or "real". Wrestlers historically maintained their personas in public, and the industry's scripted nature was treated as a closely guarded secret.

Origins

The term comes from carnival and wrestling carny slang; its exact etymology is debated, with one theory holding that it is pig Latin-like code for "be fake". For much of the twentieth century, performers protected kayfabe rigorously — heroes and villains would not be seen together in public.

Breaking kayfabe

The secrecy eroded in the late twentieth century, notably when Vince McMahon testified in 1989 that wrestling was sports entertainment rather than a legitimate contest. Modern promotions openly blend fiction and reality, and "breaking kayfabe" now usually means an unplanned departure from character.
