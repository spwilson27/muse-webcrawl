The Kukkiwon (Korean: 국기원), also known as the World Taekwondo Headquarters, is the official taekwondo governing organisation established by the South Korean government. It is located in the Gangnam District of Seoul and serves as the home of the World Taekwondo Academy and the centre for standardising taekwondo technique, forms (poomsae), and instructor certification worldwide.

History

The Kukkiwon was founded on 30 November 1972, with Kim Un-yong as its first president, and the main building — recognisable for its traditional Korean tiled roof combined with a modern gymnasium — was completed in 1973. In 1973, the Korea Taekwondo Association and the Kukkiwon helped establish the World Taekwondo Federation (now World Taekwondo), and the first World Taekwondo Championships were held at the Kukkiwon that year. The building was designated a special organisation under South Korea's Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism.

Functions

The Kukkiwon issues dan (black belt) and poom (junior black belt) certificates that are recognised internationally; official gradings above low dan ranks require testing under Kukkiwon standards. It maintains the official curriculum of poomsae, trains and certifies master instructors and examiners, conducts research on taekwondo history and technique, and hosts demonstrations and the Kukkiwon Demonstration Team's tours abroad. Its taekwondo museum and library collect artefacts and documents relating to the martial art's development.

Relationship with World Taekwondo

The Kukkiwon and World Taekwondo are distinct bodies: the Kukkiwon, a government-established institution, is responsible for technique, education, and certification (the "martial art" side), while World Taekwondo governs the competitive sport, including Olympic taekwondo. In practice Kukkiwon certification is required for participation in much of the WT competition structure.
