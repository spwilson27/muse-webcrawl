Al-Nasa'i

Abu Abd al-Rahman Ahmad ibn Shu'ayb al-Nasa'i (829–915 CE) was a Persian scholar of hadith and the compiler of Al-Sunan al-Sughra, also known as Sunan an-Nasa'i, one of the six canonical hadith collections (Kutub al-Sittah) of Sunni Islam.

Born in Nasa in Khorasan, he travelled through much of the Muslim world studying with numerous teachers and settled later in Egypt and Palestine. He is regarded as one of the most rigorous critics of hadith narrators, applying strict conditions to the traditions he accepted.

Al-Nasa'i also compiled a larger collection, Al-Sunan al-Kubra, from which the smaller, better-known Sunan was selected. He died in 915, in Ramla or Mecca according to differing accounts, reportedly after being beaten for refusing to speak against Ali ibn Abi Talib.
