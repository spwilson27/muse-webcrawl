Electronic viewfinder

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An electronic viewfinder (EVF) is a camera viewfinder in which the image captured by the lens is displayed on a miniature electronic display, typically an LCD or OLED microdisplay, rather than being viewed directly through an optical path.

The photographer views the display through an eyepiece magnifier, seeing a real-time representation of the image the sensor will record, including exposure, white balance and depth-of-field effects as applied by the camera's processing.

Advantages and disadvantages

Compared with optical viewfinders, EVFs can overlay shooting information, histograms and focus aids such as focus peaking and magnification; they show a WYSIWYG preview of exposure settings and function in video recording. Disadvantages historically included display lag, lower dynamic range than the optical image, resolution limits, and battery consumption — though successive generations of high-resolution, high-refresh OLED panels have narrowed these gaps.

Use

EVFs are standard on mirrorless interchangeable-lens cameras, bridge cameras and camcorders. Early consumer implementations appeared in the 1980s and 1990s on video cameras; still-camera EVFs became popular with the rise of mirrorless systems from around 2008 onward.
