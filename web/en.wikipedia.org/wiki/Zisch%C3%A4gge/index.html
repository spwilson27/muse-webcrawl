Zischägge

Origin: Ottoman Turkish çiçak, via Hungarian sisak

Period: 17th century

Also known as: Lobster-tailed pot helmet

The Zischägge is a type of combat helmet used across Europe in the 17th century, especially by cavalry and pikemen. In English it is commonly called the lobster-tailed pot helmet, from its articulated neck guard of overlapping plates resembling a lobster's tail.

Features

Derived from the Ottoman Turkish çiçak through the Hungarian sisak, the helmet typically combines a hemispherical skull (often fluted), a projecting peak, cheek pieces, and a sliding nasal bar for face protection. It was worn by harquebusiers, cuirassiers and dragoons during the Thirty Years' War and the English Civil War.

Decline

As body armour fell out of general use in the late 17th century, the Zischägge disappeared from Western European service, though variants survived longer in Eastern Europe.
