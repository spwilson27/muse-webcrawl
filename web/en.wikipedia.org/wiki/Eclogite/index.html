Eclogite

Eclogite
TypeMetamorphic rock
ProtolithBasalt or gabbro (mafic igneous rock)
Key mineralsGarnet (pyrope–almandine), omphacite (sodium-rich clinopyroxene)
FormationHigh to ultra-high pressure, typically in subduction zones
FaciesEclogite facies (roughly >1.2 GPa)

Eclogite is a dense, coarse-grained metamorphic rock composed principally of red-brown garnet (pyrope to almandine) and grass-green clinopyroxene (omphacite), giving it a striking red-and-green "Christmas tree" appearance. It forms when mafic igneous rocks such as basalt and gabbro are subjected to very high pressures — characteristic of the eclogite facies — usually as they are carried deep into the Earth in subduction zones.

Formation and significance

The defining reaction converts plagioclase-bearing basaltic rock into the denser garnet–omphacite assemblage at pressures typically above about 1.2 GPa (equivalent to depths of 40 km or more). Because eclogite is denser than the peridotite of the surrounding mantle, the transformation of oceanic crust to eclogite in a subducting slab adds negative buoyancy that helps drive plate tectonics. Some eclogites contain coesite or even micro-diamonds, evidence of ultra-high-pressure metamorphism at depths exceeding 100 km before the rocks were exhumed back to the surface.

Occurrences

Classic localities include the Western Gneiss Region of Norway, the Münchberg massif in Germany, the Zermatt–Saas zone of the Alps, Dabie–Sulu in China (a famous ultra-high-pressure terrane), and eclogite xenoliths carried to the surface in kimberlite pipes in southern Africa and Siberia. Eclogite is also of economic interest: some diamonds originate in eclogitic source rocks, and the rock type is a key record of ancient subduction in orogenic belts.
