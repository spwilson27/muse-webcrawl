MLA Handbook

The MLA Handbook (formerly the MLA Handbook for Writers of Research Papers) is a style guide published by the Modern Language Association of America (MLA). It sets out the MLA style system for documenting sources in scholarly writing, widely used in the humanities — particularly in literary studies, languages and cultural studies — and in school and undergraduate writing across North America. The ninth edition was published in 2021.

History

The MLA's guidance began as a short "MLA Style Sheet" published in 1951 to standardise the preparation of manuscripts for scholarly journals. A full handbook for student research papers followed in 1977, and successive editions expanded to cover electronic sources. The eighth edition (2016) substantially reformed the system, replacing rigid, format-specific templates with a flexible approach based on "core elements" and containers, designed to work for any kind of source. The ninth edition (2021) kept that framework while expanding guidance on inclusive language, digital media and citation of works in various formats.

MLA style

MLA style uses parenthetical in-text citations giving the author's surname and a page number — for example (Smith 42) — keyed to an alphabetical Works Cited list at the end of the paper. Works Cited entries are built from core elements in a fixed order: author, title of source, title of container (such as a journal, book or website), contributor, version, number, publisher, publication date and location (such as page numbers or a URL/DOI). Other conventions associated with MLA style include double-spaced text, a legible 12-point font, one-inch margins, page numbers with the author's surname in the header, and italics for titles of longer works.

Use

The Handbook is a companion to the MLA Style Manual, which addressed professional scholars and was discontinued after its third edition (2008), leaving the Handbook as the MLA's single style authority. An accompanying website, the MLA Style Center, provides answers to common citation questions. MLA style is one of the most widely taught citation systems in US secondary schools and colleges.
