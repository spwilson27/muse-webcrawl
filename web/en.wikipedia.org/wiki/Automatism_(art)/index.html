Automatism (art)

Automatism

Medium: Drawing, painting, writing
Associated movement: Surrealism
Key figures: André Breton, André Masson, Joan Miró

Automatism is a method of art-making in which the artist suppresses conscious control over the making process, allowing the unconscious mind to take over. In Surrealism, psychic automatism was defined by André Breton in the 1924 Surrealist Manifesto as the expression of thought free from rational control, moral concern or aesthetic judgement.

Origins

The term derives from spiritualist automatic writing and from psychological experiments with free association. André Masson began automatic drawings in 1924, letting the pen move rapidly without preconceived form, then discovering images within the resulting lines.

Techniques

Automatic methods include automatic drawing and writing, frottage, grattage, decalcomania and exquisite corpse. Joan Miró developed imagery from automatic beginnings, while later Abstract Expressionists adopted gestural automatism on a large scale.

Legacy

Automatism influenced Abstract Expressionism, Art Informel and later process art, and remains a reference point for art engaging chance and the unconscious.
