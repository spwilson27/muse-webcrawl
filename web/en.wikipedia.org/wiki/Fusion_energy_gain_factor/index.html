Fusion energy gain factor

The fusion energy gain factor, usually symbolized Q, is the ratio of fusion power produced to the power required to maintain the plasma in steady state. When Q = 1 the condition is known as breakeven; a self-sustaining burn with no external heating corresponds to infinite Q and is called ignition.

Definitions

Several variants are used: scientific breakeven compares fusion power to the heating power delivered to the plasma; engineering breakeven accounts for the efficiency of converting energy into the heating power; and commercial viability generally requires Q well above unity, often estimated at 20 or more for a practical power plant. The Lawson criterion relates the required combination of temperature, density and confinement time to these thresholds.

Records

The Joint European Torus (JET) achieved Q ≈ 0.67 transiently in 1997. In December 2022 the National Ignition Facility announced a shot producing more fusion energy than the laser energy delivered to the target (target gain above 1), a milestone in inertial confinement fusion. ITER is designed to reach Q = 10, producing 500 MW of fusion power from 50 MW of input heating power.
