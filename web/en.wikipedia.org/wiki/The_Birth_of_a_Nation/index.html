The Birth of a Nation

Directed by: D. W. Griffith
Released: February 8, 1915
Running time: approx. 187 minutes (varies by version)
Based on: The Clansman by Thomas Dixon Jr.

The Birth of a Nation, originally called The Clansman, is a 1915 American silent epic drama film directed by D. W. Griffith and starring Lillian Gish. Adapted from Thomas Dixon Jr.'s 1905 novel and play The Clansman: A Historical Romance of the Ku Klux Klan, the film chronicles the American Civil War and Reconstruction through two families, concluding with the formation of the Ku Klux Klan, whom it portrays heroically.

Significance and technique

The film was a landmark of cinema: at over three hours and with a pioneering orchestral score, it demonstrated the medium's narrative and commercial potential and popularized techniques such as cross-cutting, close-ups, and large-scale battle sequences. It was one of the highest-grossing films of the silent era and among the first screened at the White House.

Controversy

From its release, the film was condemned for its racist content. Its portrayal of Black people (many played by white actors in blackface) as unintelligent and sexually aggressive toward white women, and its glorification of the Klan, provoked protests by the NAACP and riots in some cities. Historians have credited the film's popularity with helping inspire the revival of the Ku Klux Klan in 1915. Despite — or because of — this legacy, it remains one of the most studied films in cinema history; it was inducted into the National Film Registry in 1992.
