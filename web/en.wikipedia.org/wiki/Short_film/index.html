Short film

A short film is an original motion picture that has a running time of less than 40 minutes, as defined by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (including all credits). In the United States, short films were generally termed short subjects from the 1920s into the 1970s when confined to two 35 mm reels (about 20 minutes); a third reel is termed a "three-reeler".

History

All films in cinema's first decades were short—often one minute or less—growing to "featurettes" as multi-reel features emerged in the 1910s. Short comedies by Chaplin, Keaton, and Laurel and Hardy, newsreels, cartoons, and serials were standard parts of theater programs until television displaced them in the 1950s.

Modern shorts

Today short films are a common calling card for new directors and a showcase at film festivals such as Sundance, Cannes, Clermont-Ferrand, and Annecy. The Academy Awards honor Best Live Action Short Film and Best Animated Short Film; the latter has been a proving ground for studios such as Pixar, whose shorts precede its features. Streaming platforms and the internet have greatly expanded audiences for short-form work.
