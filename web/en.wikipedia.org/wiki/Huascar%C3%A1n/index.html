Huascarán

Huascarán is a mountain in the Peruvian province of Yungay, in the Cordillera Blanca range of the Andes. Its southern summit, Huascarán Sur, rises to 6,768 metres (22,205 ft) and is the highest point in Peru and the fourth-highest mountain in South America. The mountain gives its name to Huascarán National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and its name is thought to derive from the Quechua waska ("chain") or a related term.

Geography and climbing

Huascarán has two summits separated by a high saddle, the Garganta: the higher southern peak (6,768 m) and Huascarán Norte (6,655 m). The mountain was first climbed in 1932 by a German-Austrian expedition. Because its summit is the point on Earth where the measured acceleration of gravity is lowest — owing to the combination of altitude and the equatorial bulge — Huascarán is sometimes cited in discussions of Earth's shape.

Disasters

The mountain is notorious for catastrophic avalanches and landslides. In 1962 a massive avalanche destroyed the town of Ranrahirca, killing thousands. On 31 May 1970, the great Ancash earthquake triggered an enormous debris avalanche from the north peak that buried the town of Yungay and killed more than 20,000 people — one of the deadliest natural disasters in Peruvian history. In 2012 the mountaineer and author of early ascents is remembered in the mountain's climbing history, which began with Annie Smith Peck's 1908 ascent of the north peak.
