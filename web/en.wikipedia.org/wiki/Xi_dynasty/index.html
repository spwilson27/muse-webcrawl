Xi dynasty

The Xi dynasty (Chinese: 西朝; pinyin: Xī cháo), officially known as Da Xi (大西), was a short-lived dynastic regime established by the peasant rebel leader Zhang Xianzhong during the collapse of the Ming dynasty. Based in Sichuan, it lasted from 1644 until 1647.

History

Zhang Xianzhong, a former soldier turned rebel leader, led a peasant army across China during the chaos of the Ming–Qing transition. In 1644 he captured Chengdu and proclaimed himself Emperor of the Great Xi, taking the era name Dashun. His regime issued its own coinage and established an administration in Sichuan.

Fall

Zhang's rule in Sichuan was marked by extreme violence and widespread destruction, and the province's population fell catastrophically — although historians debate how much of the devastation was directly attributable to his forces versus Qing armies, famine, and disease. In 1647, Zhang was killed in battle against Qing forces at Mount Fenghuang near Xichong. His remaining forces, under his adopted sons including Sun Kewang and Li Dingguo, retreated south and eventually allied with the Southern Ming resistance.

Legacy

The Xi regime is remembered largely through accounts of atrocities in Sichuan, including the possibly apocryphal "Seven Kill Stele". Sunken treasure recovered from the Min River at Jiangkou in 2017, including gold and silver ingots stamped with Zhang's regime, provided archaeological confirmation of aspects of the Xi court's history.
