Ulster fry

An Ulster fry is a cooked breakfast dish popular in Northern Ireland and parts of the Republic of Ireland. It is the local variant of the full breakfast found throughout Britain and Ireland, and is distinguished chiefly by the inclusion of soda bread and potato bread, both fried in the pan alongside the other ingredients.

Ingredients

A traditional Ulster fry typically consists of:

  • Back bacon
  • Pork sausages
  • A fried egg
  • Soda bread (usually soda farls, quartered and fried)
  • Potato bread (potato farls)
  • Black pudding and white pudding
  • Tomato (fresh, halved and fried, or tinned)
  • Mushrooms

Unlike other regional variants, baked beans are traditionally not part of the dish, although they are increasingly served. The whole meal is usually accompanied by strong tea, and in Ulster it may be eaten at any time of day rather than only at breakfast.

Preparation

Traditionally all the ingredients are fried in a single pan in lard or vegetable oil, with the breads added towards the end to soak up the cooking fat. Modern versions are often partly grilled or oven-baked for a lighter result. Soda farls and potato farls are the defining elements: soda bread is a quick bread leavened with bicarbonate of soda, cooked on a griddle and split into quarters; potato bread is made from mashed potato, flour and butter, rolled flat and likewise cut into farls.

Regional identity

The Ulster fry is regarded as a symbol of Northern Irish food culture and is widely available in cafés, hotels and bed-and-breakfast establishments. It differs from the full English breakfast mainly in its breads and the usual absence of beans and hash browns, and from the full Scottish breakfast in lacking Lorne sausage, haggis and tattie scones (of which potato farls are a close relative).
