Madison, Wisconsin

Madison is the capital city of the U.S. state of Wisconsin and the seat of Dane County. As of the 2020 census, its population was about 270,000, making it the state's second-largest city after Milwaukee. The city is built on an isthmus between Lakes Mendota and Monona, and its skyline is dominated by the Wisconsin State Capitol, modelled on the United States Capitol.

History

Madison was founded in 1836 when former federal judge James Duane Doty purchased land on the isthmus and persuaded the territorial legislature to make the planned city the capital. It was named for President James Madison. The first capitol was completed in 1838, and the University of Wisconsin was founded in 1848, the year Wisconsin became a state.

Government and economy

As the state capital, Madison is a centre of government employment. The University of Wisconsin–Madison, one of the leading public research universities in the United States, is the city's largest employer and a major cultural force. Health care, insurance and a growing technology sector also anchor the economy.

Culture

The city is known for the Dane County Farmers' Market around Capitol Square, one of the largest producers-only farmers' markets in the country, and for its extensive system of lakes, parks and bicycle paths.
