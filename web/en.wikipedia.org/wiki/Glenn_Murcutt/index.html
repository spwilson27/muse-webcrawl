Glenn Murcutt

Glenn Marcus Murcutt AO (born 25 July 1936) is an Australian architect and winner of the 2002 Pritzker Architecture Prize and the 2009 AIA Gold Medal. Working almost entirely alone and exclusively within Australia, he is celebrated for environmentally sensitive houses that respond precisely to landscape, climate, and light, summed up in his motto "touch the earth lightly."

Life and practice

Murcutt was born in London to Australian parents and raised partly in the Morobe Province of Papua New Guinea, where his father was a prospector and trader; the experience of building in a remote landscape shaped his self-reliant approach. He studied architecture at the Sydney Technical College (now the University of New South Wales), graduating in 1961, and established his own practice in 1969. Remarkably, he has always worked as a sole practitioner, without permanent staff, producing a modest number of intensely considered buildings, mostly private houses in New South Wales.

Architecture

Murcutt's buildings characteristically use economical materials — corrugated iron, steel, glass, timber, and brick — assembled with refined detailing. They are typically long, narrow pavilions raised off the ground, oriented to catch breezes and northern sun, with adjustable louvres, screens, and water-collecting roofs that let occupants tune the building to the weather. Notable works include the Marie Short House (1975), the Magney House at Bingie Point (1984), the Marika-Alderton House in Arnhem Land (1994), and the Arthur and Yvonne Boyd Education Centre at Riversdale (1999, with Wendy Lewin and Reg Lark). In 2019 the MPavilion in Melbourne, designed by Murcutt, opened to wide acclaim, and his Australian Islamic Centre in Newport, Melbourne, is his largest public work.

Recognition

Despite his small output and regional focus, Murcutt's influence on sustainable and place-responsive design is global. In awarding him the Pritzker Prize, the jury praised "an architecture of economy and ecology" produced in an era of large multinational practices.
