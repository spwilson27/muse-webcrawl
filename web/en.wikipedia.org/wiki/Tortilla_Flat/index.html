Tortilla Flat

Tortilla Flat is a novel by American author John Steinbeck, published in 1935. Set in Monterey, California, it follows a group of paisanos — unemployed, wine-loving friends of mixed Mexican, Spanish, Indian and Caucasian descent — in the years just after the First World War, and was Steinbeck's first clear commercial success.

Plot

Danny returns from the war to find he has inherited two houses in Tortilla Flat, the hillside district above Monterey. His friends — Pilon, Pablo, Jesus Maria Corcoran, the Pirate and his dogs, and Big Joe Portagee — gradually move in, and the narrative strings together comic episodes of scheming for wine, food and small mercies, loosely modelled on the tales of King Arthur and his knights, with Danny as Arthur and his house as the Round Table. The idyll darkens as Danny wearies of respectability; after his death in a drunken fall, the friends let the house burn and drift apart.

Reception and adaptation

The novel's warmth and humour established Steinbeck's reputation, though later critics have debated its portrayal of its paisano characters. It was adapted into a 1942 film starring Spencer Tracy, Hedy Lamarr and John Garfield.
