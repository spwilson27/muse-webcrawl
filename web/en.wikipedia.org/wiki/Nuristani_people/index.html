Nuristani people

RegionsNuristan, Laghman and Kunar provinces, Afghanistan; Chitral, Pakistan
LanguagesNuristani languages
ReligionSunni Islam (historically an Indo-Iranian polytheistic religion)
Related groupsKalash people, other Indo-Iranian peoples

The Nuristani people are an ethnic group native to the region of Nuristan in north-eastern Afghanistan and the adjoining Chitral district of Pakistan. They speak Nuristani languages, a small group forming its own branch of the Indo-Iranian languages. Numbering roughly 125,000 to 300,000, the Nuristanis are noted for their distinct culture, language, and history of isolation in the valleys of the Hindu Kush.

History

For centuries the Nuristanis practised an ancient polytheistic religion and fiercely resisted outside rule, which is why their homeland was known to neighbouring Muslims as Kafiristan, "land of the infidels". Their religion featured a pantheon headed by the creator god Imra, monumental carved ancestor statues, wooden temples, ritual specialists, and notable customs such as the use of chairs and carved house posts; wine-making was also prominent. In the winter of 1895–96 Emir Abdur Rahman Khan invaded and conquered the region, forcibly converting the population to Islam, after which the land was renamed Nuristan, "land of light". Some groups across the Durand Line, notably the Kalash of Chitral, retained their traditional religion.

Culture

Nuristani society is organised into clans and villages in steep mountain valleys. Traditional livelihoods combine terraced agriculture, goat herding and forestry. The Nuristanis are celebrated for their woodcarving — decorated doors, pillars, furniture and effigies — as well as embroidery and woven goods. They were described by travellers and ethnographers including George Scott Robertson, whose 1896 account The Kafirs of the Hindu-Kush remains a classic source. Because of the fair features of some Nuristanis, legends of descent from the soldiers of Alexander the Great have often been attached to them, though genetic and historical evidence links them with the ancient peoples of the region.
