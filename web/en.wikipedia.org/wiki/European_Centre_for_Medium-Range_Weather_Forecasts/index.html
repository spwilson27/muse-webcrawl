The European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) is an independent intergovernmental organisation supported by most European nations. Established in 1975 and headquartered at Shinfield Park, Reading, England, it is regarded as producing some of the most accurate medium-range weather forecasts in the world. It employs staff from more than 30 countries and operates one of the largest supercomputing facilities in Europe dedicated to weather prediction; its data centre moved to Bologna, Italy, in 2021.

Products

ECMWF's core product is the Integrated Forecasting System (IFS), a global numerical weather prediction model providing deterministic and ensemble forecasts out to about two weeks, with a high horizontal resolution of roughly 9 km for the high-resolution run. Its 51-member ensemble forecasting system, introduced in the 1990s, pioneered operational probabilistic prediction. The Centre also produces seasonal forecasts, atmospheric composition forecasts through the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service, and the ERA5 climate reanalysis, a widely used global dataset combining model data with observations from 1940 onward.

Role and funding

Member states fund the Centre and receive its forecasts for their national meteorological services; commercial licensing funds part of its budget. ECMWF forecasts are frequently cited publicly for their skill with major storms — for example, predicting the landfall of Hurricane Sandy in 2012 earlier than competing models. Since 2020 much of its data has moved toward open availability under EU policy.
