Emperor Thiệu Trị

Thiệu Trị (Vietnamese: [tʰiəw˧˨ʔ ʈi˧˨ʔ]; 6 June 1807 – 4 November 1847), born Nguyễn Phúc Miên Tông, was the third emperor of the Nguyễn dynasty of Vietnam. He reigned from 1841 until his death in 1847, succeeding his father, the Emperor Minh Mạng.

Reign

Thiệu Trị was the eldest son of Minh Mạng and was selected as heir despite court intrigues favouring a younger brother. His reign largely continued his father's conservative Confucian policies: maintaining a strong central bureaucracy, upholding orthodox administration, and restricting foreign contact and Catholic missionary activity, though enforcement of anti-Christian edicts was somewhat milder than under Minh Mạng.

Relations with France deteriorated over the persecution of missionaries. In 1847 a French naval force under Captain Lapierre attacked Vietnamese ships at Tourane (Da Nang) in the Bombardment of Tourane, demanding the release of imprisoned missionaries — an early episode in the growing French pressure that would lead to full-scale intervention under his successor.

Family and legacy

Thiệu Trị was a noted patron of literature and poetry, composing verse in classical Chinese. He had many consorts and a large number of children, among them Nguyễn Phúc Hồng Nhậm, who succeeded him as Emperor Tự Đức, and Nguyễn Phúc Hồng Y, father of later emperors. He died in November 1847 and was buried at the Xương Lăng tomb complex near Huế.
