United States Mint

The United States Mint is a bureau of the Department of the Treasury responsible for producing coinage for the United States. It manufactures circulating coins delivered to the Federal Reserve, commemorative and bullion coins such as the American Eagle series, and medals including Congressional Gold Medals.

History

The Coinage Act of 1792 established the Mint and the decimal dollar system. The first facility was built in Philadelphia, then the national capital, and David Rittenhouse served as its first director. Branch mints were later created to serve gold and silver producing regions, including Charlotte, Dahlonega, New Orleans, Carson City and San Francisco. Coins bear mint marks identifying the production facility.

Facilities and operations

Modern production facilities are located in Philadelphia, Denver, San Francisco and West Point. The headquarters is in Washington, D.C. The Mint designs and strikes coins, protects its production and storage sites through the United States Mint Police, sells numismatic products, and maintains the nation's gold and silver bullion reserves at locations including Fort Knox. Since 1999 the 50 State quarters and subsequent America the Beautiful and American Women quarters programmes have made circulating coin designs a major public showcase for people, places and events in American history.
