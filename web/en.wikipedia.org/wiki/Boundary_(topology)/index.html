Boundary (topology)

In topology and mathematics in general, the boundary of a subset S of a topological space X is the set of points in the closure of S not belonging to the interior of S. An element of the boundary of S is called a boundary point of S. The boundary of a set S is often denoted ∂S (using the partial derivative symbol).

Definitions

There are several equivalent definitions: the boundary of S is the set of points p in X such that every neighbourhood of p contains at least one point of S and at least one point not of S. Formally, ∂S = cl(S) \ int(S), where cl denotes closure and int denotes interior. Equivalently, ∂S = cl(S) ∩ cl(X \ S).

Properties

The boundary of a set is always closed. A set is closed if and only if it contains its boundary, and open if and only if it is disjoint from its boundary. The boundary of the boundary may be smaller than the original boundary (for example, the boundary of a closed disk in the plane is a circle, whose own boundary is empty). In three dimensions and above, boundaries interact with the notion of manifold boundary, though the two concepts differ: a closed ball has empty manifold boundary but its topological boundary (as a subset of Euclidean space) is its surface sphere.

Examples

The boundary of the rational numbers ℚ as a subset of ℝ is all of ℝ, since ℚ is dense with empty interior. The boundary of a disk is its circumference; the boundary of the empty set and of the whole space are both empty.
