Federico García Lorca

Born: 5 June 1898, Fuente Vaqueros, Spain
Died: 19 August 1936, near Granada
Occupation: Poet, playwright
Movement: Generation of '27

Federico del Sagrado Corazón de Jesús García Lorca (1898–1936) was a Spanish poet, playwright and theatre director, the most internationally acclaimed Spanish writer of the 20th century and a central figure of the Generation of '27.

Life and work

Born in Fuente Vaqueros near Granada, Lorca studied in Madrid, befriending Salvador Dalí and Luis Buñuel. Romancero gitano (Gypsy Ballads, 1928) made him famous. A year in New York produced the darker, surreal Poeta en Nueva York (published posthumously). Back in Spain he directed the travelling theatre La Barraca and wrote his great rural tragedies: Blood Wedding (1933), Yerma (1934) and The House of Bernarda Alba (1936).

Death and legacy

Days after the outbreak of the Spanish Civil War, Lorca was arrested by Nationalist forces in Granada and shot on 19 August 1936; his body has never been conclusively found. His work, steeped in Andalusian folk culture, duende, death and desire, was banned under Franco and is now celebrated worldwide. See Spanish Civil War.
