Unfederated Malay States

States: Johor, Kedah, Kelantan, Perlis, Terengganu
Period: Early 20th century – 1946
Status: British protected states

The Unfederated Malay States (Malay: Negeri-Negeri Melayu Tidak Bersekutu) were a grouping of five Malay states of the Malay Peninsula — Johor, Kedah, Kelantan, Perlis and Terengganu — that were British protectorates but, unlike the four Federated Malay States, were not joined in a federation.

History

Johor accepted a British adviser in 1914, completing the extension of British protection over the peninsula. Kedah, Kelantan, Perlis and Terengganu had been transferred from Siamese to British suzerainty under the Anglo-Siamese Treaty of 1909. Each unfederated state retained its own sultan and administration, receiving a British Adviser whose advice, in practice, carried less day-to-day weight than that of the Residents in the Federated Malay States, so the states preserved a greater measure of autonomy — and developed more slowly.

Dissolution

After the Japanese occupation of Malaya (1942–45), the unfederated states were incorporated into the short-lived Malayan Union in 1946 and then into the Federation of Malaya in 1948, whose successor states remain states of modern Malaysia.
