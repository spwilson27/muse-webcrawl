The Canadian Alliance formed the Official Opposition in the House of Commons from 2000 to 2003.

The Canadian Alliance (French: Alliance canadienne), formally the Canadian Reform Conservative Alliance, was a Canadian federal political party that existed from 2000 to 2003. It grew out of the Reform Party of Canada and the "Unite the Right" movement, which sought to merge Canada's conservative parties into a single alternative to the governing Liberals.

History

After the Reform Party's third-place finishes in the 1990s, leader Preston Manning promoted the creation of a broader conservative coalition. In a series of referendums, Reform members voted to dissolve their party and form the Canadian Alliance in March 2000. Stockwell Day, a former Alberta cabinet minister, won the leadership in an upset over Manning.

2000 election and Official Opposition

In the November 2000 federal election the Alliance won 66 seats and 25.5% of the popular vote, becoming the Official Opposition, but failed to make the hoped-for breakthrough in Ontario. Day's leadership was dogged by internal dissent; in 2001 several MPs left to sit as the Democratic Representative Caucus. Day resigned, and Stephen Harper won the leadership in March 2002.

Merger

In October 2003 Harper and Progressive Conservative leader Peter MacKay announced an agreement to merge their parties. Alliance members ratified the merger, and on 7 December 2003 the Canadian Alliance and the Progressive Conservative Party of Canada united to form the Conservative Party of Canada, which went on to win government under Harper in 2006.
