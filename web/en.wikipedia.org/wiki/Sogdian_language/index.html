Sogdian language

Sogdian
RegionSogdiana (Zeravshan valley, modern Uzbekistan and Tajikistan)
FamilyIndo-European → Indo-Iranian → Eastern Iranian
EraAttested c. 4th–11th centuries CE; descendant: Yaghnobi
ScriptsSogdian (Aramaic-derived), Manichaean, Syriac

The Sogdian language was an Eastern Iranian language spoken mainly in the Central Asian region of Sogdiana (in modern-day Uzbekistan and Tajikistan, centred on Samarkand and Bukhara) and for centuries the lingua franca of the Silk Road. Sogdian merchants established colonies as far east as China and Mongolia, and their language was written along the trade routes from the 4th to about the 11th century CE.

Corpus

Sogdian texts survive in three religious traditions — Buddhist, Manichaean, and Christian — as well as in secular documents: the Ancient Letters (c. 313 CE) found near Dunhuang, the letters and contracts of Mount Mugh (early 8th century), and inscriptions. Many works are translations from Sanskrit, Syriac, and Chinese. The corpus is written in a cursive script derived from Aramaic, or in Manichaean or Syriac alphabets.

Legacy

Sogdian died out as a vernacular after the Arab conquest and Persianisation of Transoxiana, but it survives in the modern Yaghnobi language spoken in Tajikistan's Yaghnob valley. The Sogdian script was adapted to write Old Uyghur, and through it ultimately Mongolian and Manchu — so the vertical scripts of those languages trace their ancestry to Sogdian. Sogdian loanwords enriched Middle Chinese and Persian.
