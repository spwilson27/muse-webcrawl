Influenza A virus subtype H5N1

Influenza A virus subtype H5N1
Family: Orthomyxoviridae
Genus: Alphainfluenzavirus
Host: Birds; occasionally mammals including humans
Common name: Bird flu

Influenza A virus subtype H5N1 (A/H5N1) is a subtype of the influenza A virus that can cause severe respiratory disease in birds and, occasionally, in humans and other mammals. A bird-adapted strain ("avian influenza" or "bird flu") was first identified in Guangdong, China in 1996 and caused its first known human infections in Hong Kong in 1997.

Overview

The subtype is named for its surface proteins: hemagglutinin type 5 and neuraminidase type 1. Wild aquatic birds are the natural reservoir. Highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI) H5N1 has caused mass mortality in poultry and wild birds; clade 2.3.4.4b viruses spread globally from 2020 onward, reaching the Americas and Antarctica and infecting many mammal species, including dairy cattle in the United States in 2024.

Human infection

Human cases have generally followed close contact with infected birds or contaminated environments. Reported symptoms range from conjunctivitis and mild respiratory illness to severe pneumonia. The reported case fatality rate in confirmed human cases historically has been around half, though mild cases may be undercounted. Sustained human-to-human transmission has not been observed.

Prevention and control

Control in poultry relies on surveillance, culling of infected flocks, biosecurity, and vaccination in some countries. Antivirals such as oseltamivir are used for treatment and prophylaxis, and several prepandemic vaccines have been stockpiled. Public health agencies monitor the virus for mutations or reassortment that could increase pandemic potential.
