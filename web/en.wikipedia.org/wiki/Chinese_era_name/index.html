Chinese era name

Era names appeared on coins, documents, and monuments

A Chinese era name (年號, niánhào) is a title used by an imperial Chinese ruler to designate a period of his reign for dating purposes. The system was inaugurated by Emperor Wu of Han, whose Jianyuan era began in 140 BC. Dates were expressed as the Nth year of a given era, and coins, official documents, and tomb inscriptions were dated by era name rather than by the emperor's personal name.

Usage

Early emperors adopted numerous era names during a single reign, changing them to mark auspicious events or dispel misfortune. From the Ming dynasty onward, emperors generally adopted a single era name for their entire reign, so they are commonly known by it—for example, the Yongle Emperor or the Kangxi Emperor. The era name was distinct from the emperor's personal name and from his posthumous temple name.

Influence

The era-name system spread throughout the Sinosphere: Japan continues the practice today (the current era being Reiwa, begun 2019), and Korea, Vietnam, and other states used era names historically. The Republic of China calendar, still used in Taiwan, numbers years from 1912 in a manner derived from the era system.
