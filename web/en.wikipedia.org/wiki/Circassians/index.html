The Circassians, who call themselves Adyghe, are an ethnic group native to Circassia in the northwestern Caucasus, along the northeastern Black Sea coast. Historically organised in independent tribes and principalities, they were celebrated for horsemanship and a strict code of honour and hospitality, the Adyghe Khabze. The majority of Circassians today live outside their homeland, the result of mass expulsion in the 19th century.

History

Circassian ancestors include the Maeotians and the medieval Zichi. From the 16th century they were gradually Islamised through contact with the Crimean Khanate and the Ottoman Empire. The Russo-Circassian War, fought from the 1760s until 1864 as part of the Russian conquest of the Caucasus, ended with the defeat of Circassian resistance. What followed is widely described by Circassians and many historians as a genocide and ethnic cleansing: the great majority of the population — estimates range from several hundred thousand to over a million — was deported or fled to the Ottoman Empire, with very large mortality from disease, starvation, and massacre. Georgia's parliament formally recognised the events as genocide in 2011.

Diaspora and homeland

Diaspora communities were founded across the Ottoman Empire, in today's Turkey (the largest community), Jordan — where Circassians helped found modern Amman in 1878 and have served in the royal guard — Syria, and Israel (the villages of Kfar Kama and Rehaniya). In the Russian Federation, Circassians live mainly in the republics of Adygea, Kabardino-Balkaria, and Karachay-Cherkessia, where Adyghe and Kabardian are official languages. Circassian activists commemorate 21 May, the anniversary of the 1864 expulsion, as a day of mourning, and campaigns for recognition and for diaspora repatriation continue.

Culture

Circassian culture prizes the Khabze code: respect for elders, hospitality to guests, courage, and restraint. Traditional dress includes the cherkesska, a long coat with cartridge pockets, worn across the Caucasus. Music and dance, including the Lezginka-like dances and the wuij gatherings, and a rich epic tradition (the Nart sagas, shared with neighbouring peoples) are hallmarks. Circassian cuisine is known for dishes such as shipsi and Circassian chicken.
