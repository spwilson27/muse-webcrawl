Spelman College is a private historically Black liberal arts college for women in Atlanta, Georgia, United States. Founded in 1881 as the Atlanta Baptist Female Seminary, it is a member of the Atlanta University Center Consortium and is widely regarded as one of the nation's top liberal arts colleges and a leading producer of Black women who go on to earn doctoral degrees in the sciences.

History

The school was founded in the basement of Friendship Baptist Church by Sophia B. Packard and Harriet E. Giles, two teachers from Massachusetts associated with the Woman's American Baptist Home Mission Society. Classes began on April 11, 1881, with eleven students, most of them formerly enslaved women.

In 1884 the school was renamed Spelman Seminary in honor of Laura Spelman Rockefeller, wife of philanthropist John D. Rockefeller, whose family provided crucial financial support. It became Spelman College in 1927. Over the twentieth century the college developed a strong liberal arts curriculum, and in 2020 MacKenzie Scott donated $20 million to the institution.

Academics

Spelman offers bachelor's degrees across the humanities, social sciences, natural sciences, and fine arts. Its most popular majors include psychology, biology, political science, economics, and computer science. The college emphasizes undergraduate research, global engagement, and leadership development for women of African descent.

Campus and student life

The 39-acre campus lies just west of downtown Atlanta and includes historic buildings such as Sisters Chapel, completed in 1927. Spelman students participate in the traditions of its brother institution, Morehouse College, including joint homecoming events. Student organizations include sororities of the National Pan-Hellenic Council, the Spelman College Glee Club, and numerous academic and service societies.
