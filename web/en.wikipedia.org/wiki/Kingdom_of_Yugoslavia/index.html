The Kingdom of Yugoslavia was a country in Southeast and Central Europe that existed from 1918 until the end of World War II in 1945. It was founded on 1 December 1918 by the unification of the provisional State of Slovenes, Croats and Serbs (itself formed from territories of the former Austria-Hungary) with the formerly independent Kingdom of Serbia. The Kingdom of Montenegro had united with Serbia five days earlier, while the regions of Kosovo, Vojvodina and Vardar Macedonia were parts of Serbia prior to the unification.

Formation

The state was initially named the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes. Peter I of Serbia was its first sovereign; the kingdom was proclaimed by his son, Prince Regent Alexander. The Corfu Declaration of 1917 had laid the groundwork for a unified South Slav state under the Serbian Karađorđević dynasty. The Vidovdan Constitution of 1921 established a unitary parliamentary monarchy, centralising power in Belgrade and provoking opposition from Croat and Slovene politicians who favoured federal arrangements.

The 1929 dictatorship and renaming

Political tensions culminated in 1928 when the Croat leader Stjepan Radić was shot in parliament and later died. On 6 January 1929 King Alexander I suspended the constitution, dissolved parliament and proclaimed a royal dictatorship. On 3 October 1929 the country was renamed the Kingdom of Yugoslavia ("Land of the South Slavs") and reorganised into nine banovinas (provinces) drawn to weaken historic regional identities.

Assassination and regency

Alexander I was assassinated in Marseille on 9 October 1934 by a Bulgarian gunman working with Croat Ustaše separatists. His eleven-year-old son succeeded as Peter II, and a regency under Prince Paul governed. In August 1939 the Cvetković–Maček Agreement created the autonomous Banovina of Croatia in an attempt to settle the "Croatian question".

World War II and collapse

Pressed to join the Axis Tripartite Pact, the government signed it in March 1941, triggering a coup d'état that deposed the regency. Germany invaded in April 1941; Yugoslavia capitulated within eleven days and was partitioned among the Axis powers. A government-in-exile sat in London while resistance movements — the royalist Chetniks and Tito's communist Partisans — fought the occupiers and each other. In 1945 the monarchy was abolished and the Federal People's Republic of Yugoslavia was proclaimed.
