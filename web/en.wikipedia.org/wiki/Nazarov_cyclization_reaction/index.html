Nazarov cyclization reaction

The Nazarov cyclization reaction (often called simply the Nazarov cyclization) is a chemical reaction used in organic chemistry for the synthesis of cyclopentenones from divinyl ketones. It is named after the Russian chemist Ivan Nikolaevich Nazarov, who first reported it in 1941.

Mechanism

Under acidic conditions, a divinyl ketone is protonated or coordinated by a Lewis acid to form a pentadienyl cation. This undergoes a conrotatory 4π electrocyclic ring closure to give an oxyallyl cation, which loses a proton to form an allyl enol and, after tautomerisation, the cyclopentenone product.

Applications

The reaction has been applied in the total synthesis of numerous natural products containing five-membered carbocycles, and modern catalytic, asymmetric and "interrupted" variants have broadened its scope considerably.
