Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan

Born6 February 1890, Utmanzai, British India
Died20 January 1988, Peshawar, Pakistan
Known forKhudai Khidmatgar movement; nonviolent resistance
NicknamesBacha Khan; Badshah Khan; "Frontier Gandhi"

Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (6 February 1890 – 20 January 1988), also known as Bacha Khan and Badshah Khan, was a Pashtun independence activist from the North-West Frontier Province of British India. A close ally of Mahatma Gandhi, he founded the Khudai Khidmatgar ("Servants of God") movement — the famous "Red Shirts" — which mobilised tens of thousands of Pashtuns in nonviolent resistance to British rule. He was nicknamed the "Frontier Gandhi" for his commitment to nonviolence.

Early life and education work

Ghaffar Khan was born in Utmanzai in the Peshawar Valley into a landed Pashtun family. Denied further study abroad by his mother, he turned to social reform among his own people, opening schools — beginning with a school at Utmanzai in 1910 and later the Azad Islamia school network — and founding the Pashtun journal Pakhtun to promote education, unity and reform among the frontier tribes.

Khudai Khidmatgar

In 1929 he founded the Khudai Khidmatgar, a disciplined, uniformed volunteer movement that adopted nonviolence as a creed grounded in Islam. Its members, conspicuous in red-dyed shirts, allied with the Indian National Congress. In April 1930, his arrest sparked protests in Peshawar during which British troops fired on unarmed demonstrators at Qissa Khwani Bazaar, killing hundreds — an event that made him a national figure. He spent much of his life in prison: roughly 15 years under the British and, after 1947, even longer under Pakistani governments.

Partition and later life

Ghaffar Khan opposed the partition of India, telling Congress leaders "you have thrown us to the wolves" when they accepted it. He demanded an autonomous Pashtunistan, boycotted the 1947 referendum, and after Pakistan's creation was repeatedly imprisoned and exiled for his opposition. He spent years in Afghanistan and returned to Pakistan in 1972. In 1987 he became the first non-Indian citizen to receive India's highest civilian honour, the Bharat Ratna. He died in Peshawar in 1988 and was buried, at his request, in Jalalabad, Afghanistan.
