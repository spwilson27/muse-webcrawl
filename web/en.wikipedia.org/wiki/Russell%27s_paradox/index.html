Russell's paradox

Russell's paradox is a paradox discovered by Bertrand Russell in 1901 that showed that the naive set theory of Gottlob Frege leads to a contradiction. It exposed inconsistencies in the informal notion of a set as any collection definable by a property, and it was a major impetus for the development of axiomatic set theory and Russell's own theory of types.

Statement

Consider the set R of all sets that are not members of themselves. If R is not a member of itself, then by definition it must be a member of itself; but if R is a member of itself, then it contradicts its own definition as the set of all sets that are not members of themselves. A popular illustration is the barber paradox: a barber who shaves all and only those men who do not shave themselves both shaves and does not shave himself.

Consequences

Russell communicated the paradox to Frege in 1902 just as the second volume of Frege's Grundgesetze was going to press, undermining its logical foundations. Responses included Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory, which restricts set formation through axioms such as separation and foundation, and type theory, which stratifies sets into levels so that self-membership cannot be formulated.
