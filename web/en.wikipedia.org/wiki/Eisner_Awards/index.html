The Will Eisner Comic Industry Awards, commonly shortened to the Eisner Awards, are awards for creative achievement in American comic books, sometimes referred to as the comics industry's equivalent of the Academy Awards. They are named in honour of the pioneering writer and artist Will Eisner, creator of The Spirit, who was a frequent participant in the ceremony until his death in 2005.

History

The Eisner Awards were created in 1988 by comics publisher Dave Olbrich and Fantagraphics Books, succeeding the Kirby Awards, which had been discontinued amid a dispute in 1987. The first ceremony was held in 1988, with awards covering work published in 1987. After a hiatus in 1990, the awards were taken over by San Diego Comic-Con in 1991, where they have been presented annually ever since, on the Friday evening of the convention.

Categories

Categories have changed over the years but typically include Best Writer, Best Artist, Best Continuing Series, Best Limited Series, Best New Series, Best Graphic Album, Best Publication for Teens, Best Publication for Kids, Best Humor Publication, Best Anthology, Best Lettering, Best Coloring, and Best Cover Artist. Winners are selected through a two-stage process: a panel of judges prepares nominations, and professionals in the comics industry vote on the final results.

Hall of Fame

The Will Eisner Award Hall of Fame honours comics creators for lifetime achievement. Inductees include Jack Kirby, Will Eisner himself, Carl Barks, Harvey Kurtzman, Osamu Tezuka, and many others. Each year judges select several inductees, with additional choices made by voters from a list of nominees.

Notable winners

Works that have won multiple Eisners include Alan Moore and Dave Gibbons's Watchmen, Neil Gaiman's The Sandman, Brian K. Vaughan and Fiona Staples's Saga, and John Lewis, Andrew Aydin and Nate Powell's March trilogy.
