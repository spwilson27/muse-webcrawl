Wehrmacht foreign volunteers and conscripts

Wehrmacht foreign volunteers and conscripts refers to the non-German personnel who served in the armed forces of Nazi Germany (the Wehrmacht, including the Army, Kriegsmarine and Luftwaffe) during the Second World War. Distinct from the foreign volunteers of the Waffen-SS, these men — numbering in the hundreds of thousands to over a million by some estimates — included genuine ideological volunteers, adventurers, anti-communists, prisoners of war recruited from camps, and conscripts drafted from annexed and occupied territories.

Categories

Volksdeutsche: ethnic Germans from Eastern and Central Europe were often conscripted directly. Osttruppen ("Eastern troops"): Soviet citizens — Russians, Ukrainians, Cossacks, Caucasians, Central Asians and others — served in battalions and in formations such as the Russian Liberation Army (ROA) under Andrey Vlasov. Hilfswillige ("Hiwis"): auxiliary volunteers, often former POWs, performed support roles. Western European volunteers served in national "legions", such as the French Legion of Volunteers against Bolshevism (LVF) and the Spanish Blue Division on the Eastern Front.

Conscription in occupied territories

In annexed areas such as Alsace-Lorraine, Luxembourg, and parts of Poland and Slovenia, local men were declared German citizens and conscripted; the Luxembourgers and Alsatians drafted this way are remembered as forced conscripts (the Malgré-nous in France). Latvians and Estonians were also conscripted into German service in large numbers in 1943–44, mostly into the Waffen-SS Latvian and Estonian Legions.

Aftermath

Motives and degrees of coercion varied widely, and post-war treatment ranged from execution or imprisonment (particularly for Soviet citizens repatriated under the Yalta agreements) to veteran recognition for conscripts in countries such as Luxembourg and France. The subject remains historically sensitive in several nations.
