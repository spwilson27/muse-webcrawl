Schwerin (German pronunciation: [ʃveˈʁiːn]) is the capital and second-largest city of the northeastern German state of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern. It is known for its picturesque old town, its many lakes — above all Lake Schwerin (Schweriner See) — and Schwerin Castle, the fairy-tale residence of the dukes of Mecklenburg set on an island in the lake. Despite being a state capital, it is Germany's smallest state capital by population.

History

A Slavic settlement of the Obotrites stood at the site by the early Middle Ages; the chronicle of Thietmar of Merseburg mentions the place in 1018. Henry the Lion conquered the stronghold in 1160, granting city rights and founding a bishopric; Schwerin thus claims to be one of the oldest cities in the region. From 1358 it belonged to the dukes of Mecklenburg, and it became the residence of the Duchy (later Grand Duchy) of Mecklenburg-Schwerin.

Grand Duke Friedrich Franz II rebuilt the castle in the mid-nineteenth century in a romantic historicist style inspired by French Renaissance châteaux such as Chambord. Schwerin escaped heavy destruction in the Second World War and was occupied by American troops in May 1945 before being handed over to the Soviet zone. In the German Democratic Republic it was a district capital noted for industry and its lakeside setting; after reunification in 1990 it became the capital of the restored state of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern.

Geography

Schwerin lies amid a landscape of lakes formed by the last Ice Age. Eleven lakes lie wholly or partly within the city area, the largest being Lake Schwerin, Germany's fourth-largest lake, which stretches north from the city. The old town sits between the Pfaffenteich pond and the southern shore of the lake.

Sights

  • Schwerin Castle (Schweriner Schloss), seat of the state parliament (Landtag), with its celebrated gardens; its ensemble was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2024 as part of the Schwerin Residence Ensemble.
  • Schwerin Cathedral, a major work of Brick Gothic architecture, founded in the twelfth century.
  • The Staatliches Museum Schwerin, noted for its collection of Dutch and Flemish Golden Age painting.
  • The old town's half-timbered houses, the market square, and the Arsenal and Marstall buildings.

Transport and economy

Schwerin Hauptbahnhof provides rail connections to Hamburg, Berlin, Rostock and Wismar. The city's economy is dominated by public administration, services, tourism, food processing and small-scale manufacturing; the surrounding lakes make it a centre for water sports and recreation.
