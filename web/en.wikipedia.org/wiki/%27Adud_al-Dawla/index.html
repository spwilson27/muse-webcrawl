ʿAḍud al-Dawla (Persian: عضدالدوله; 24 September 936 – 26 March 983), born Fanā (Panāh) Khusraw, was an emir of the Buyid dynasty, ruling Fars (949–983), Media (961–983), Kerman and Khuzistan (964–983), and Iraq (978–983). Widely regarded as the greatest ruler of the Buyid dynasty, he reunited its fragmented territories and presided over a flourishing of Persian and Arabic culture under Buyid rule.

Reign

The son of Rukn al-Dawla, founder of the Buyid amirate of Media, 'Adud al-Dawla inherited Fars from his uncle 'Imad al-Dawla in 949 while still a boy. Over three decades of campaigning and diplomacy he subdued rival Buyid cousins, annexed Kerman, Oman and parts of Mesopotamia, and in 978 entered Baghdad, deposing 'Izz al-Dawla and uniting Iraq with his other domains. He took the ancient Persian title Shahanshah ("King of Kings") on his coinage and in court ceremonial.

Patronage and works

His court at Shiraz attracted scholars, poets and scientists; he founded the famous 'Adudi hospital (al-Bimaristan al-'Adudi) in Baghdad in 982, built the Band-e Amir dam near Shiraz, and maintained an observatory and library. A Twelver Shi'a, he also sponsored major shrines in Najaf and Karbala and restored Persepolis-era monuments. He died in Baghdad in 983 and was buried in Najaf. After his death the Buyid realm again fragmented among his sons.
