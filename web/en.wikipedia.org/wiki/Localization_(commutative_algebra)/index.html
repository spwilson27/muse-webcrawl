Localization (commutative algebra)

In commutative algebra, localization is a formal construction that systematically adjoins multiplicative inverses to a ring with respect to a chosen multiplicatively closed set. Given a commutative ring R and a multiplicative set S ⊆ R (with 1 ∈ S and 0 ∉ S), the localization S−1R is a ring of "fractions" a/s, with a ∈ R and s ∈ S, universal among rings in which every element of S becomes a unit.

Construction

Elements are equivalence classes of pairs (a, s), written a/s, where (a, s) ~ (a′, s′) if there is some t ∈ S with t(s′a − sa′) = 0. Addition and multiplication follow the familiar rules for fractions. There is a canonical homomorphism R → S−1R, which is injective when R is an integral domain and S does not contain 0.

Important cases

  • Field of fractions — taking S = R ∖ {0} in a domain gives its fraction field, as the rationals arise from the integers.
  • Localization at a prime ideal — taking S = R ∖ 𝔭 yields a local ring R𝔭 with unique maximal ideal 𝔭R𝔭; studying a variety "locally" at a point corresponds to passing to this ring.
  • Single-element localization — S = {1, f, f², …} gives R[1/f], the functions defined where f ≠ 0.

Localization is an exact functor and underlies the definition of stalks of the structure sheaf in algebraic geometry, and the local–global principle of checking module properties prime by prime.
