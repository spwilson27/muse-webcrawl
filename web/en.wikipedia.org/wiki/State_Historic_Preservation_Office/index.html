State Historic Preservation Office

The State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) is a state governmental function created by the United States federal government in 1966 under Section 101 of the National Historic Preservation Act (NHPA). Each US state, territory, and the District of Columbia has a SHPO charged with carrying out the historic preservation responsibilities delegated by federal law.

Functions

SHPOs conduct surveys to identify historic properties, nominate properties to the National Register of Historic Places, review federal and state projects for their effects on historic resources under Section 106 of the NHPA, administer rehabilitation tax incentives, and provide technical assistance and grants to local governments, Certified Local Governments, and the public.

State Historic Preservation Officer

Each SHPO is headed by a State Historic Preservation Officer, appointed by the governor. The officer's staff typically includes archaeologists, architectural historians, and historians who meet federal professional qualification standards.
