P (complexity)

P
Definition: Decision problems solvable in polynomial time on a deterministic Turing machine
Contains: L ⊆ P ⊆ NP ⊆ PSPACE
Open question: P versus NP

In computational complexity theory, P is the class of decision problems solvable by a deterministic Turing machine in polynomial time — informally, problems efficiently solvable with known algorithms.

Properties

P is closed under complement and many operations, and is robust across reasonable models of computation (the Cobham–Edmonds thesis). Examples include primality testing (AKS), linear programming, sorting, and greatest common divisor.

P versus NP

Whether P equals NP — problems whose solutions are efficiently verifiable — is the most famous open problem in computer science and a Millennium Prize Problem. Most complexity theorists believe P ≠ NP.
