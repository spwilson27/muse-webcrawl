Treaty of Kraków

The Treaty of Kraków was signed on 8 April 1525 between the Kingdom of Poland, under King Sigismund I the Old, and Albert of Prussia, Grand Master of the Teutonic Order. The treaty ended the Polish–Teutonic War of 1519–1521 and dissolved the monastic state of the Teutonic Knights in Prussia.

Terms

Under the treaty, Albert secularized the Teutonic Order's Prussian territory and became the first Duke of Prussia, holding the new duchy as a fief of the Polish Crown. On 10 April 1525, two days after the signing, Albert paid homage to Sigismund I in the market square of Kraków — the famous Prussian Homage.

Significance

The treaty created the first Protestant state in Europe, since Albert had embraced Lutheranism and converted the duchy to the new faith. Ducal Prussia remained a Polish vassal until the mid-17th century, when the treaties of Wehlau and Bromberg (1657) granted the Hohenzollern electors full sovereignty — a step on the road to the later Kingdom of Prussia.
