Draugr


A draugr emerging from its burial mound.

A draugr (Old Norse: draugr, plural draugar) is an undead creature from Norse mythology and Icelandic folklore. Unlike the insubstantial ghosts of many traditions, the draugr is a corporeal revenant: the reanimated body of a dead person, typically inhabiting its grave mound and guarding the treasure buried with it. Draugar are described as immensely strong, able to swell in size, and possessed of a stench of decay.

Characteristics

In the Icelandic sagas, draugar retain human intelligence and often great malice. They can increase their size and weight at will, drive men mad, kill livestock, and crush or bite victims to death; some are said to kill by dragging victims down into their mounds. A draugr's skin may be described as black as Hel or corpse-blue, and it may be immune to ordinary weapons. Methods of destroying one include wrestling it back into its grave, decapitation followed by cremation, and scattering the ashes at sea or in a remote place.

In the sagas

Notable draugar in literature include Glámr in Grettis saga, whose curse afflicts the hero Grettir with fear of the dark; the mound-dweller Kárr the Old, whose sword Grettir claims; and the household of Þórólfr Twist-Foot in Eyrbyggja saga, whose hauntings force the community to hold a "door-doom" (duradómr), a legal court convened to banish the dead. Draugar appear prominently in the legendary and family sagas and in later Icelandic folk tales.

Sea draugar

Folklore also knows the sjódraugr, the drowned sailor returned from the sea, dripping with seaweed and appearing in open boats or on the shore as an omen of storms and death.

Modern popular culture

Draugar have been widely adopted in modern fantasy, appearing as undead enemies in role-playing games, novels and video games, including the Elder Scrolls series, where they populate ancient Nordic tombs.
