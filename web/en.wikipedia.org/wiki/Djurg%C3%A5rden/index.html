Djurgården

Djurgården is an island in central Stockholm, Sweden, forming part of the borough of Östermalm. It is one of the city's most popular recreational areas, home to museums, an amusement park, and extensive parkland within the Royal National City Park (Ekoparken).

History

The island was historically a royal hunting park — its name means "animal garden" — stocked with deer and elk from the 16th century. From the 18th century it developed into a fashionable leisure destination for Stockholmers.

Attractions

Djurgården hosts the open-air museum Skansen (opened 1891), the Vasa Museum housing the preserved 17th-century warship Vasa, the Nordic Museum, the ABBA museum, and the Gröna Lund amusement park (opened 1883). The Rosendal Palace and the Prince Eugen's Waldemarsudde gallery also stand on the island.

Access

The island is reached by bridges from Östermalm and the city centre, by the Djurgården ferry from Slussen, and by tram line 7.
