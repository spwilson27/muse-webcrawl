The Carolina Panthers are a professional American football team based in Charlotte, North Carolina. They compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the National Football Conference (NFC) South division, and play their home games at Bank of America Stadium. The franchise, representing both North and South Carolina, was awarded to Charlotte businessman Jerry Richardson in 1993 and began play in 1995.

History

The Panthers reached the NFC Championship Game in only their second season (1996) under coach Dom Capers, then an expansion-team record. Under coach John Fox and quarterback Jake Delhomme they reached Super Bowl XXXVIII after the 2003 season, losing 32–29 to the New England Patriots. Their most successful season came in 2015, when, coached by Ron Rivera and led by MVP quarterback Cam Newton, they went 15–1 before losing Super Bowl 50 to the Denver Broncos. Hall of Fame linebacker Sam Mills, whose "Keep Pounding" speech became the team's motto, is commemorated by the drum beaten before home games.

Ownership

In 2018, following workplace-misconduct allegations against Richardson, the team was sold to hedge-fund manager David Tepper for about $2.2 billion, then an NFL record. Recent seasons have brought frequent coaching changes, including the hiring of Dave Canales in 2024, and the development of quarterback Bryce Young, the first overall pick in the 2023 draft.
