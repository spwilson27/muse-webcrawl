Pan (moon)

Pan is the innermost named moon of Saturn. About 35 kilometres across and only 23 kilometres high, it orbits within the Encke Gap of Saturn's A Ring, where it acts as a ring shepherd, keeping the gap open and producing waves in the surrounding ring material. Its pronounced equatorial ridge gives it an appearance likened to a walnut or a ravioli.

Discovery and name

The existence of a moon in the Encke Gap was predicted from the wavy edges of the gap, and Pan was found in 1990 by Mark R. Showalter in re-examined Voyager 2 photographs from 1981. It is named after Pan, the Greek god of shepherds and flocks, in keeping with its shepherd-moon role.

Orbit and shape

Pan orbits Saturn in about 13.8 hours at a distance of roughly 133,584 kilometres. Images from the Cassini spacecraft in 2017 revealed its equatorial bulge in detail; the ridge is thought to have been built up by accretion of fine ring particles falling onto the moon's equator as it sweeps through the ring plane.
