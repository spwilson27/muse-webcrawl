Obturator internus muscle

Muscles of the gluteal and posterior femoral regions, showing the obturator internus.

The obturator internus is a muscle of the pelvis and hip. It arises from the inner surface of the obturator membrane and the surrounding pelvic bones, exits the pelvis through the lesser sciatic foramen, and inserts on the medial surface of the greater trochanter of the femur.

Function

Together with the obturator externus, the gemelli and quadratus femoris, it forms the lateral rotator group of the hip. It externally rotates the extended thigh and abducts the flexed thigh, and helps stabilise the femoral head in the acetabulum.

Innervation

The muscle is supplied by the nerve to obturator internus, from the sacral plexus (L5–S2). Its tendon is a landmark in the posterior approach to the hip and in pelvic surgery.
