Linkwitz–Riley filter

A Linkwitz–Riley (L-R) filter is an infinite impulse response filter used in audio crossovers. It is named after its inventors, Siegfried Linkwitz and Russ Riley. The filter is constructed by cascading two Butterworth filters of half the desired order, which gives the crossover outputs −6 dB gain at the crossover frequency and a summed response that is flat.

Properties

Linkwitz–Riley crossovers exist in even orders (LR2, LR4, LR8...). The very common fourth-order version (LR4) has 24 dB/octave slopes and requires the polarity of one output (typically the high-pass) to be inverted to achieve a flat summed magnitude response with no lobing error at the crossover point.

Applications

L-R crossovers are the standard choice in active loudspeaker systems and studio monitors because of their flat summed response and in-phase outputs at the crossover frequency.
