Leo III the Isaurian (Byzantine emperor)

Leo III the Isaurian (c. 685 – 18 June 741), also known as Leo the Syrian, was Byzantine emperor from 717 until his death in 741. He founded the Isaurian dynasty and is best known for repelling the Arab siege of Constantinople in 717–718 and for initiating the Byzantine iconoclasm controversy by prohibiting the veneration of religious images.

Leo rose through the military, becoming commander (strategos) of the Anatolic theme. He deposed Theodosius III and was crowned in March 717, just months before a massive Umayyad army and fleet besieged Constantinople. Using Greek fire and aided by a severe winter and Bulgarian intervention, Leo forced the Arabs to abandon the siege in August 718, a victory often compared in significance to the Battle of Tours. In 726 he issued the first edicts against the veneration of icons, opening a controversy that would divide the empire for over a century. His law code, the Ecloga, simplified and humanized aspects of Roman law. In 740 his forces won the Battle of Akroinon, halting Arab raids into Anatolia.

Pope Leo III

Pope Leo III (died 12 June 816) was bishop of Rome from 795 until his death. His pontificate is famous for the coronation of Charlemagne as emperor in St. Peter's Basilica on Christmas Day 800, an event that laid the foundation for the Holy Roman Empire and deepened the estrangement between Rome and Constantinople. Attacked and nearly blinded by Roman aristocrats in 799, Leo fled to Charlemagne's court at Paderborn and was restored under Frankish protection. He is venerated as a saint in the Catholic Church.

Leo III of Armenia

Leo III was king of the Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia, reigning from 1269 (or 1270) until his death in 1289, following the reign of his father Hethum I. His reign was dominated by the struggle against the Mamluks of Egypt, despite alliances with the Mongol Ilkhanate.
