Bending moment

SymbolM
SI unitNewton-metre (N·m)
Related quantitiesShear force, deflection, curvature
Governing relationdV/dx = −w; dM/dx = V

A bending moment is the internal moment in a structural element, such as a beam, that resists bending when external loads are applied. At any cross-section of a beam, the bending moment equals the algebraic sum of the moments about that section of all forces acting on one side of it. It causes one face of the beam to be compressed and the other put in tension: for a simply supported beam loaded from above, the top fibres shorten and the bottom fibres stretch ("sagging"); over an interior support of a continuous beam the reverse, "hogging", occurs.

Theory

In Euler–Bernoulli beam theory, the bending moment is related to the curvature κ of the beam by M = EIκ, where E is the Young's modulus and I the second moment of area of the cross-section. The resulting normal (bending) stress varies linearly across the section depth: σ = My/I, maximum at the outermost fibres. The shear force V and distributed load w are connected to the moment by dV/dx = −w and dM/dx = V, relations used to draw shear-force and bending-moment diagrams — standard tools for locating the section of maximum moment, which governs a beam's design strength.

Sign conventions and examples

Two sign conventions are in use: in structural engineering a moment causing compression in the top fibres (sagging) is usually taken as positive, whereas in mechanical engineering and some texts the opposite applies; either is consistent if applied uniformly. For a simply supported beam of span L with a central point load P, the maximum moment is PL/4 at midspan; under a uniformly distributed load w it is wL²/8. A cantilever with an end load P has a fixed-end moment of PL.
