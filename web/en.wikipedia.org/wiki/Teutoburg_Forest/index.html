Teutoburg Forest

The Teutoburg Forest (German: Teutoburger Wald) is a range of low, forested hills in the German states of Lower Saxony and North Rhine-Westphalia. It is famed as the presumed site of the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest in AD 9, in which an alliance of Germanic tribes under Arminius ambushed and annihilated three Roman legions commanded by Publius Quinctilius Varus — a defeat that halted Roman expansion east of the Rhine.

Geography

The ridge runs roughly 105 km from near Osnabrück southeast toward Paderborn. Its highest elevation is the Barnacken at about 446 metres. Much of the area is protected within the TERRA.vita nature park and the Teutoburg Forest/Egge Hills Nature Park.

Landmarks

The Hermannsdenkmal, a colossal statue of Arminius completed in 1875 near Detmold, commemorates the battle. The Externsteine, a distinctive sandstone rock formation near Horn-Bad Meinberg, is another landmark with a long history of religious associations. Excavations at Kalkriese, north of Osnabrück, have uncovered a Roman battlefield widely identified with Varus's defeat.
