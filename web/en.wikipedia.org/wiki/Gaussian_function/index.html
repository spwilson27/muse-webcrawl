Gaussian function

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, a Gaussian function, often simply referred to as a Gaussian, is a function of the base form

f(x) = a · exp( −(x − b)2 / (2c2) )

and with parametric extension for arbitrary real constants a, b and non-zero c. The graph of a Gaussian is a characteristic symmetric "bell curve" shape. The parameter a is the height of the curve's peak, b is the position of the centre of the peak, and c (the standard deviation, sometimes called the RMS width) controls the width of the "bell".

Gaussian functions are often used to represent the probability density function of a normally distributed random variable with expected value μ = b and variance σ2 = c2, in which case the Gaussian is of the form:

g(x) = (1 / (σ√(2π))) · exp( −(x − μ)2 / (2σ2) )

Properties

Gaussian functions arise in many areas of the natural sciences, social sciences, mathematics and engineering. Some examples include:

  • In statistics and probability theory, Gaussian functions appear as the density function of the normal distribution, which is a limiting probability distribution of many complex processes in accordance with the central limit theorem.
  • A Gaussian function is the wave function of the ground state of the quantum harmonic oscillator.
  • Gaussian functions are used to define some types of artificial neural networks.
  • In signal processing they serve to define Gaussian filters, such as in image processing where two-dimensional Gaussians are used for Gaussian blurs.
  • The Fourier transform of a Gaussian function is another Gaussian function — Gaussians are eigenfunctions of the Fourier transform.
  • The integral of a Gaussian is the error function, erf(x).

Integral

The definite integral of the unnormalised Gaussian over the whole real line is ∫−∞∞ exp(−x2) dx = √π. This famous result, the Gaussian integral, can be proved by squaring the integral and converting to polar coordinates.

Multidimensional Gaussians

In n-dimensional space, the Gaussian generalises using a mean vector and a covariance matrix, giving the multivariate normal distribution. Anisotropic Gaussians, with different widths along different axes, are widely used in computer graphics (for example in 3D Gaussian splatting), geostatistics and machine learning kernels.

History

The function is named after Carl Friedrich Gauss, who used it in his 1809 work on the theory of errors and celestial mechanics, although the curve had appeared earlier in the work of Abraham de Moivre (1738) as an approximation to the binomial distribution.
