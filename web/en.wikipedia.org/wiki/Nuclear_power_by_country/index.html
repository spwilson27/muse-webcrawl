Nuclear power by country

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Countries by nuclear power usage.

Nuclear power plants operate in about 32 countries worldwide. As of the mid-2020s, roughly 440 reactors with a combined capacity of about 390 gigawatts generate around 9–10% of the world's electricity. The United States operates the largest fleet, while France derives the highest share of its electricity — about two-thirds — from nuclear energy.

Status categories

Countries fall broadly into groups: those operating and building reactors (such as China, India, Russia and South Korea, with China having the largest construction pipeline); those operating but not building; those with plans under consideration, including newcomer states such as Poland, Egypt (with reactors under construction at El Dabaa), Turkey (Akkuyu) and Bangladesh (Rooppur); and those that have phased out nuclear power or never adopted it. Germany completed its phase-out in April 2023, shutting its last three reactors, while Italy abandoned nuclear power after referendums following the Chernobyl disaster, and countries such as Austria never started their completed plant.

Trends

After growth in the 1970s and 1980s, global capacity plateaued; the 2011 Fukushima Daiichi accident led Japan to idle its fleet and accelerated Germany's exit. In the 2020s, interest revived over energy security and climate policy, with life extensions of existing plants, large new builds in Asia, and development of small modular reactors (SMRs). The United Arab Emirates' Barakah plant made it the first Arab state with commercial nuclear power.
