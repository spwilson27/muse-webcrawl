Secretariat (horse)

Secretariat (March 30, 1970 – October 4, 1989), also known as Big Red, was an American Thoroughbred racehorse who, in 1973, became the first Triple Crown winner in 25 years. His record-breaking victory in the Belmont Stakes, which he won by 31 lengths, is widely regarded as one of the greatest races in history. During his racing career, he won five Eclipse Awards, including Horse of the Year honours at ages two and three. He was nominated to the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in 1974.

Racing career

Secretariat was bred in Virginia by Meadow Stud and raced in the blue-and-white checked silks of Meadow Stable under owner Penny Chenery. After a championship two-year-old season in 1972, he was syndicated for a then-record $6.08 million before beginning his three-year-old campaign. In the Kentucky Derby he set a track record of 1:59⅖ that still stands, running each quarter-mile faster than the one before. He won the Preakness Stakes and then, on 9 June 1973, produced his immortal performance in the Belmont Stakes, covering the mile and a half in 2:24 — a world record that still stands — and winning by 31 lengths.

Stud career and death

Retired to Claiborne Farm in Kentucky, Secretariat sired the champion filly Lady's Secret and the Preakness and Belmont winner Risen Star, and became an influential broodmare sire through daughters such as Weekend Surprise, dam of A.P. Indy. He was euthanised on 4 October 1989 after developing laminitis. His heart, on examination, was found to be far larger than that of an average horse — estimated at 22 pounds (10 kg) — a possible source of his extraordinary stamina.

Legacy

Secretariat remains the standard against which American racehorses are measured. He was ranked second, behind Man o' War, in The Blood-Horse's list of the top 100 racehorses of the 20th century, and his Belmont victory is routinely cited among the greatest sporting performances of all time.
