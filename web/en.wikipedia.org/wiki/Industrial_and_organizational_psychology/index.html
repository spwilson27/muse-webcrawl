Industrial and organizational psychology

Industrial and organizational psychology (I/O psychology) is the scientific study of human behaviour in organisations and the workplace. It applies psychological theories and methods to problems of recruitment, selection, training, performance appraisal, motivation, job satisfaction, leadership, and employee well-being, aiming both to improve organisational effectiveness and to enhance the quality of working life.

Two sides of the field

The industrial (or personnel) side focuses on individual differences and the fit between people and jobs: job analysis, employee selection and testing, performance measurement, and training. The organizational side focuses on the social context of work: motivation, attitudes, group dynamics, leadership, organisational culture and structure, and change. In practice most I/O psychologists work across both.

History

The field emerged in the early 20th century from the work of figures such as Walter Dill Scott and Hugo Münsterberg, who applied experimental psychology to advertising, selection, and vocational guidance. It expanded rapidly during the two world wars, when psychologists developed tests for selecting and placing millions of military personnel, and through the Hawthorne studies of the 1920s and 1930s, which highlighted the importance of social factors and attention in worker productivity.

Methods and practice

I/O psychologists use surveys, psychometric tests, experiments, and statistical techniques such as meta-analysis and criterion validation. They work in consulting firms, corporations, government, and universities. In the United States the field is represented by the Society for Industrial and Organizational Psychology (SIOP), a division of the American Psychological Association.
