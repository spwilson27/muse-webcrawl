Medical law

Medical law is the branch of law that governs the responsibilities of medical professionals and the rights of patients. It encompasses issues such as medical malpractice, informed consent, confidentiality, and the licensing and regulation of practitioners.

Major topics

  • Medical malpractice: professional negligence by a healthcare provider in which treatment falls below the accepted standard of care and causes injury or death. Claims typically require proof of a duty of care, a breach of that duty, causation, and damages.
  • Informed consent: the legal and ethical requirement that a patient agree to treatment only after being told of its nature, benefits, risks, and alternatives. Exceptions exist for emergencies and patients lacking capacity.
  • Confidentiality and privacy: duties protecting patient information, codified in the United States by the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and in Europe by data protection law.
  • Licensing and discipline: state or national bodies license physicians and may sanction misconduct, from reprimand to revocation.
  • End-of-life law: advance directives, living wills, powers of attorney for healthcare, and the law of assisted dying where permitted.
  • Reproductive law: abortion, contraception, fertility treatment, and surrogacy.

The doctor–patient relationship

The legal relationship between a physician and patient generates duties on both sides: the physician owes a standard of care, loyalty, and confidentiality; the patient is generally free to accept or refuse recommended treatment. When the relationship ends improperly, liability for abandonment may arise.

Regulation of practice

Medical law also covers the regulation of hospitals and insurers, public health powers such as quarantine and mandatory reporting, and the legal framework for clinical research and organ transplantation.
