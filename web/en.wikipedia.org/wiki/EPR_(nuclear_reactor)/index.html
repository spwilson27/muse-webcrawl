The EPR is a Generation III+ pressurized water reactor (PWR) design originally developed by Areva NP (a joint venture of Framatome and Siemens) and later by EDF and Framatome. The name originally stood for European Pressurized Reactor and later Evolutionary Power Reactor. It is designed to produce about 1,650 megawatts of electricity and to meet post-2000 safety standards with improved efficiency and reduced waste per unit of energy.

Design

The EPR features a double-walled containment building resistant to aircraft impact, four independent redundant trains of safety systems, a "core catcher" to retain molten fuel in a severe accident, and passive and active cooling systems. Its large output and 60-year design life were intended to lower generation costs, and its core can run with a substantial fraction of MOX fuel.

Construction projects

The first projects — Olkiluoto 3 in Finland and Flamanville 3 in France — became notorious for long delays and large cost overruns; Olkiluoto 3, begun in 2005, entered regular operation in 2023, and Flamanville 3 was connected to the grid in 2024. In contrast, two units at Taishan in China were completed faster and entered service in 2018 and 2019, Taishan 1 being the first EPR to operate. Two EPRs are under construction at Hinkley Point C in England, and a further pair is planned at Sizewell C. An optimised, cheaper variant, the EPR2, is planned for new reactors in France.

Reception

Supporters describe the EPR as among the safest reactor designs in the world. Critics argue that its size and complexity made it slow and expensive to build in Europe, damaging the economics of nuclear new-build, and the troubled first projects prompted industry efforts to simplify the design and improve construction management.
