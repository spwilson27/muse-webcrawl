List of nuclear weapons tests of France

France conducted a total of 210 nuclear tests between 1960 and 1996, first in Algeria and later in French Polynesia. The programme began under President Charles de Gaulle as part of the development of an independent French nuclear deterrent, the force de frappe.

Algerian tests (1960–1966)

The first French test, codenamed Gerboise bleue ("Blue Jerboa"), took place on 13 February 1960 at Reggane in the Algerian Sahara, with a yield of about 70 kilotons — at the time the largest first test of any nuclear power. Three further atmospheric tests (Gerboise blanche, rouge and verte) followed at Reggane. After Algerian independence, testing moved underground at In Ekker in the Hoggar mountains, where 13 tests were carried out between 1961 and 1966. The Béryl incident of 1 May 1962 accidentally vented radioactive material when an underground shot was not fully contained.

Pacific tests (1966–1996)

From 1966, testing moved to the Mururoa and Fangataufa atolls in French Polynesia, administered by the Centre d'expérimentation du Pacifique. Early atmospheric tests included the first French thermonuclear device, Canopus, detonated over Fangataufa on 24 August 1968 with a yield of about 2.6 megatons. France conducted 46 atmospheric tests in the Pacific before moving underground in 1975, partly in response to international pressure and a case brought by Australia and New Zealand at the International Court of Justice.

Underground tests continued at Mururoa and Fangataufa until 1996. President Jacques Chirac announced a final series of tests in 1995, provoking widespread international protest; the last test took place on 27 January 1996 at Fangataufa. France signed the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty later that year.
