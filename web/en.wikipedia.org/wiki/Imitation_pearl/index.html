Imitation pearl

Imitation pearls are artificial beads made to resemble natural or cultured pearls, typically consisting of a core of glass, plastic, or shell coated with a pearlescent substance. They have been produced for centuries as an affordable alternative to genuine pearls and are used in costume jewelry, clothing, and decorative work.

Imitation pearls are usually coated glass or plastic beads.

Manufacture

Traditionally, imitation pearls were made by coating glass beads with essence d'orient ("oriental essence"), a pearlescent paste prepared from guanine crystals taken from fish scales — a process attributed in its classic form to the 17th-century French rosary maker Jacquin. Earlier methods filled hollow glass beads with wax. Modern production uses glass, ceramic, or plastic cores dipped or sprayed with coatings containing guanine, bismuth oxychloride, or titanium-dioxide-coated mica, then sometimes finished with lacquer or a thin layer of acetate for durability.

A well-known premium type is the shell pearl (often marketed under names such as "Majorica"), in which a core of mother-of-pearl shell is coated with layers of ground shell and resin, giving weight and luster closer to real nacre.

Distinguishing from real pearls

Genuine pearls have a slightly gritty feel when rubbed gently against the teeth, owing to the crystalline structure of nacre, whereas most imitations feel smooth and glassy. Imitations also tend to be perfectly uniform in size, shape, and color, and their coating may chip or peel around drill holes; real pearls show natural surface variation and a characteristic depth of luster (orient).

History and use

Glass "pearls" were treasured already in Roman times, and Venetian glassmakers perfected pearl-like beads in the Renaissance. In the 20th century, inexpensive imitation pearls became staples of fashion jewelry, popularized by designers such as Coco Chanel, who mixed faux pearls with real gems. Trade regulations in many countries require that artificial pearls be sold with a qualifier such as "imitation" or "simulated" to avoid confusing them with natural or cultured pearls.
