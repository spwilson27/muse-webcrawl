Juliana Berners

Juliana Berners, O.S.B. (born c. 1388), also known as Dame Juliana Berners, was an English writer on heraldry, hawking and hunting, said to have been prioress of the Priory of St Mary of Sopwell, near St Albans in Hertfordshire. She is best known as the purported author of treatises on field sports collected in the Boke of St Albans (1486), the earliest known book on hunting written in English.

Life

Very little is known of Berners' life. Tradition holds that she was the daughter of Sir James Berners (died 1388), a member of parliament for Essex who was executed as a favourite of Richard II. She is said to have become prioress of Sopwell Priory, a Benedictine nunnery near St Albans, and to have been a woman of great learning who was fond of field sports.

Because documentary evidence is so scarce, some scholars have doubted whether she existed at all, or whether the works attributed to her were written by someone else. She has nevertheless entered literary history as one of the earliest named women writers in English.

The Boke of St Albans

The treatises on hunting, hawking and heraldry attributed to Berners were first printed at St Albans in 1486 in the volume known as the Boke of St Albans. The hunting treatise, written partly in rhyming verse and partly in prose, describes the beasts of the chase, the terminology of the hunt, and the rules of venery. It also includes the famous list of collective nouns for animals — "terms of venery" — such as a pride of lions and an exaltation of larks, many of which remain in use today.

A second edition printed by Wynkyn de Worde in 1496 added a treatise on fishing, the Treatyse of Fysshynge wyth an Angle, which describes rods, lines and artificial flies and is often regarded as the earliest substantial English work on angling. Its authorship is also traditionally, though disputedly, assigned to Berners.

Legacy

Berners' name is commemorated in Juliana, a suburb of Canberra, Australia, and she has been celebrated in works on the history of sport and of women's writing. A blue plaque tradition and literary histories continue to associate her with Sopwell Priory.
