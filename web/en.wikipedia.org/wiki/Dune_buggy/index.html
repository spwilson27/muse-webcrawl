Dune buggy

A dune buggy, also known as a beach buggy, is a recreational motor vehicle with large wheels and wide tires, designed for use on beaches and sand dunes. The original dune buggies were typically built on the chassis of a Volkswagen Beetle, whose rear-mounted, air-cooled engine, light weight, and simple construction made it well suited to modification. Most dune buggies are open-topped, with minimal bodywork and a roll cage for safety.

History

Hobbyists in California were converting cars for use on dunes from the 1950s. The breakthrough design came in 1964, when Bruce Meyers built the Meyers Manx, a fibreglass-bodied buggy on a shortened Beetle floorpan. The Manx won the inaugural Mexican 1000 race in 1967 and became a cultural icon of the California beach lifestyle, spawning thousands of copies and imitations despite Meyers losing a patent battle over the design. Dune buggy racing, including events such as the Baja 1000, developed alongside recreational use.

Modern buggies range from street-legal kit cars to purpose-built sand rails with powerful engines and long-travel suspension. The form also influenced military light vehicles, such as the Desert Patrol Vehicle used by United States special forces.
