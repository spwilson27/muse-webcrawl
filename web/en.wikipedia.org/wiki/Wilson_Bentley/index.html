Wilson Alwyn Bentley (February 9, 1865 – December 23, 1931), known as the Snowflake Man, was an American farmer from Jericho, Vermont, who was the first known person to photograph individual snowflakes. Over his lifetime he captured more than 5,000 images of snow crystals on glass plates, and his work provided the first visual evidence that no two snowflakes are exactly alike.

Early life

Bentley was born on the family farm in Jericho, in Vermont's "snow belt" west of Lake Champlain. Educated at home and fascinated by the natural world, he received a microscope from his mother for his fifteenth birthday and began examining snowflakes, sketching what he saw before they melted.

Photomicrography

Determined to record the crystals permanently, Bentley devised a way to combine a bellows camera with a compound microscope. On January 15, 1885, working in a cold shed, he produced the world's first photomicrograph of a single snow crystal, catching flakes on a velvet-covered tray, transferring them with a splinter of wood, and exposing glass plates before the crystals evaporated.

Over the following decades Bentley photographed more than 5,000 crystals, most of them six-sided (hexagonal), each one unique in his records. He also photographed frost, dew and water drops, and kept daily weather records for over forty years.

Recognition and legacy

Initially ignored, Bentley's work eventually attracted scientific attention; in 1899 his images began appearing in journals, and he collaborated with the physicist George Henry Perkins and the meteorologist William J. Humphreys. In 1931 the Smithsonian Institution published his monograph Snow Crystals, containing more than 500 of his photographs — the book remains in print. In the same year the Smithsonian acquired a set of his original glass plates, and other collections are held by institutions in Buffalo and Jericho.

Bentley died of pneumonia in December 1931, reportedly after walking home through a blizzard. The children's book Snowflake Bentley (1998), illustrated by Mary Azarian, won the Caldecott Medal and introduced his story to a wide audience.
