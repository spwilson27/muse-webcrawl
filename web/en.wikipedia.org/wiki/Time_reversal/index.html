Time reversal

Time reversal is the theoretical ability to travel backwards in time, or more generally, the concept of reversing the direction of the flow of time. In physics, it is closely associated with T-symmetry or time reversal invariance, the symmetry of physical laws under the transformation that replaces the time coordinate t with −t. Most of the fundamental laws of physics — including Newton's laws of motion, Maxwell's equations of electromagnetism, and the Schrödinger equation of quantum mechanics — are invariant under time reversal, meaning that a film of a physical process run backwards would depict a process that is itself permitted by the laws of physics.

T-symmetry in physics

The microscopic laws of physics are, with small but significant exceptions, symmetric under time reversal. Classical mechanics, electromagnetism, gravitation, and the strong interaction all respect T-symmetry. The principal known exception arises in the weak interaction: CP violation, observed in neutral kaon and B-meson systems, implies — through the CPT theorem — that T-symmetry must also be violated. Direct observation of time-reversal violation was achieved by the BaBar experiment in 2012, which measured an asymmetry in B-meson transitions consistent with T violation.

Thermodynamics and the arrow of time

Although microscopic dynamics are largely time-symmetric, macroscopic phenomena display a pronounced arrow of time. The second law of thermodynamics states that the entropy of an isolated system never decreases, giving time a preferred direction at the macroscopic scale. This asymmetry is statistical in origin: processes that increase entropy are overwhelmingly more probable than those that decrease it, a point first developed by Ludwig Boltzmann.

Time reversal in signal processing and acoustics

In signal processing, time reversal refers to the operation of recording a signal and playing it back in reverse. In acoustics, time-reversal mirrors record a wave field, time-reverse it, and retransmit it so that it refocuses on its source, a technique developed by Mathias Fink and used in medical imaging, lithotripsy, and underwater communication.

In fiction

Time reversal and backward time travel are longstanding themes in science fiction, from H. G. Wells's The Time Machine (1895) to modern films in which characters experience events in reverse temporal order.
