William Bainbridge

William Bainbridge (1774–1833), United States Navy commodore.

William Bainbridge (7 May 1774 – 27 July 1833) was a commodore in the United States Navy. During his long career he served under six presidents, took part in the Quasi-War with France, the Barbary Wars and the War of 1812, and commanded several famous American warships, including USS Constitution.

Early career

Born in Princeton, New Jersey, Bainbridge went to sea in the merchant service as a teenager and was commanding ships by 19. Commissioned a lieutenant in the new U.S. Navy in 1798, he endured early misfortunes: he was forced to surrender the schooner Retaliation to French frigates, and in 1803 the frigate Philadelphia, which he commanded, ran aground off Tripoli and was captured, leaving him a prisoner for 19 months.

War of 1812

Bainbridge's reputation was redeemed in the War of 1812. In command of USS Constitution, he engaged the British frigate HMS Java off Brazil on 29 December 1812 and captured her after a hard fight in which he was twice wounded. Java was too badly damaged to keep and was burned, but the victory made Bainbridge a national hero.

Later years

After the war Bainbridge commanded squadrons in the Mediterranean and helped establish naval shore infrastructure, including service associated with the Charlestown Navy Yard. Several U.S. Navy destroyers and the nuclear-powered cruiser USS Bainbridge were named for him.
