Ruth Ellis

Ruth Ellis (née Neilson; 9 October 1926 – 13 July 1955) was a Welsh nightclub hostess and the last woman to be executed in the United Kingdom. She was hanged at Holloway Prison, London, for the murder of her lover, racing driver David Blakely.

Background and the murder

Ellis was born in Rhyl, Wales, and worked as a nude model, film extra, and nightclub hostess in London, rising to manage the Little Club in Knightsbridge. She had two children from early relationships. In 1953 she met Blakely through racing driver Mike Hawthorn; their relationship was passionate and violent, and Blakely repeatedly beat her — ten days before the killing she suffered a miscarriage after he punched her in the stomach. On Easter Sunday, 10 April 1955, Ellis waited outside the Magdala public house in Hampstead and shot Blakely repeatedly with a .38 Smith & Wesson revolver, then calmly asked a bystander to call the police.

Trial and execution

At the Old Bailey on 20 June 1955 the jury took about twenty minutes to convict after Ellis stated, when asked what she intended, "It's obvious when I shot him I intended to kill him." The mandatory death sentence followed. A petition for reprieve bearing some 50,000 signatures was refused by Home Secretary Gwilym Lloyd George, amid questions — never fully answered — about who supplied Ellis with the gun. She was executed by Albert Pierrepoint on 13 July 1955.

Legacy

The case became a landmark in the campaign against capital punishment in Britain; the last execution in the UK followed in 1964, and the death penalty for murder was suspended in 1965 and abolished in 1969. Ellis's story was dramatised in the film Dance with a Stranger (1985) and the television series A Cruel Love: The Ruth Ellis Story (2025). Her family has continued to seek a posthumous pardon, arguing she should have been convicted of manslaughter by reason of provocation and diminished responsibility.
