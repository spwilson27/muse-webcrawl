Electrical conduction system of the heart

Cardiac conduction system
Components of the cardiac conduction system
ComponentsSinoatrial node, atrioventricular node, bundle of His, bundle branches, Purkinje fibres
FunctionGenerates and conducts electrical impulses coordinating the heartbeat

The electrical conduction system of the heart is a network of specialised cardiac muscle cells that initiates and distributes the electrical impulses responsible for the coordinated contraction of the heart. It consists of the sinoatrial (SA) node, the atrioventricular (AV) node, the bundle of His, the right and left bundle branches, and the Purkinje fibres.

Structure and function

The heartbeat originates in the SA node, located in the wall of the right atrium near the opening of the superior vena cava. Its pacemaker cells depolarise spontaneously, normally setting a resting rate of 60–100 beats per minute. The impulse spreads across both atria, causing atrial contraction, and converges on the AV node in the interatrial septum. The AV node delays the impulse by roughly 0.1 seconds, allowing the ventricles to fill, before passing it to the bundle of His.

From the AV node the signal travels down the bundle of His, which divides into right and left bundle branches running along the interventricular septum, and finally through the Purkinje fibre network, which rapidly distributes the impulse across the ventricular myocardium so the ventricles contract from the apex upward.

Electrocardiography

The activity of the conduction system is recorded clinically by the electrocardiogram (ECG): the P wave reflects atrial depolarisation, the QRS complex ventricular depolarisation, and the T wave ventricular repolarisation. Abnormalities of conduction — heart blocks, bundle branch blocks, and arrhythmias such as atrial fibrillation — produce characteristic ECG changes.

Clinical significance

Disease of the conduction system can cause abnormally slow (bradycardia) or fast (tachycardia) rhythms, or irregular rhythms. Artificial pacemakers and implantable defibrillators treat many of these disorders by delivering timed electrical impulses to the heart.
