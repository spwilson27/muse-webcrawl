Henry H. Arnold

Henry Harley "Hap" Arnold (June 25, 1886 – January 15, 1950) was an American general officer who served as Commanding General of the United States Army Air Forces during the Second World War. He is the only U.S. Air Force general to hold five-star rank, as General of the Air Force, and the only officer to hold five-star rank in two different services (the Army and the Air Force).

Career

Arnold learned to fly from the Wright brothers in 1911, becoming one of the U.S. Army's first military aviators. Between the wars he was a disciple of Billy Mitchell and an advocate of independent air power, weathering a court-martial-era controversy for supporting Mitchell. As Chief of the Air Corps from 1938, he directed the immense expansion of American air power from a small corps into the Army Air Forces of some 2.4 million personnel and tens of thousands of aircraft.

During the war he sat on the Joint Chiefs of Staff and oversaw global air operations, including the strategic bombing campaigns and the B-29 operations against Japan led from the Marianas. He was promoted to General of the Army in 1944 and redesignated General of the Air Force in 1949. Arnold Air Force Base in Tennessee and the Arnold Engineering Development Center are named for him.
