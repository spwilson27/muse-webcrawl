Universal Monsters

The Universal Monsters (also known as Universal Classic Monsters) are a series of horror, suspense, and science fiction films made by Universal Pictures between the 1920s and the 1950s. The series established enduring screen versions of characters from Gothic literature, including Dracula, Frankenstein's monster, the Mummy, the Invisible Man, the Wolf Man, and the Creature from the Black Lagoon.

Golden age

The cycle began in the silent era with The Hunchback of Notre Dame (1923) and The Phantom of the Opera (1925), both starring Lon Chaney. The arrival of sound brought Dracula and Frankenstein (both 1931), which made stars of Bela Lugosi and Boris Karloff and set the template for horror cinema. Sequels and crossovers followed, including Bride of Frankenstein (1935), The Wolf Man (1941) with Lon Chaney Jr., and the monster-rally films of the 1940s; the series closed with Creature from the Black Lagoon (1954) and its sequels.

Legacy

Makeup artist Jack Pierce's designs for Frankenstein's monster and the Mummy became the default pop-culture images of those creatures. The monsters have been continually merchandised and revived; Universal attempted a shared-universe reboot, the Dark Universe, beginning with The Mummy (2017), which was abandoned after poor reception, and later found success with the standalone The Invisible Man (2020).
