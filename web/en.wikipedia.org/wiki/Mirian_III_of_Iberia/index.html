Mirian III of Iberia

Mirian III was a king of Iberia in the Caucasus (Kartli, present-day eastern Georgia) who reigned in the late 3rd and early 4th centuries AD. He is remembered as the first Georgian king to accept Christianity, traditionally in 337, following the mission of Saint Nino.

Reign

According to the Georgian chronicles, Mirian was of Iranian Mihranid origin, installed as king with Persian backing when he was a child. His long reign saw Iberia manoeuvre between the Roman Empire and Sasanian Persia.

Conversion

The traditional account tells that Queen Nana was healed by the captive Christian woman Nino, and that Mirian, lost in darkness while hunting, prayed to Nino's God and was delivered, leading him to declare Christianity the state religion. Iberia thus became one of the earliest Christian kingdoms. Mirian and Nana are venerated as saints by the Georgian Orthodox Church.
