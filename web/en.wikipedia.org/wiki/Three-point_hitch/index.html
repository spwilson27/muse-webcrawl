Three-point hitch
InventorHarry Ferguson
Patented1926 (Ferguson system)
ComponentsTwo lower lift arms, one top link, hydraulic lift
StandardISO 730; categories 0–4

The three-point hitch (or three-point linkage) is a widely used system for attaching implements to an agricultural tractor. The implement is connected at three points — two lower hitch arms and a single upper link — forming a rigid triangle that lets the tractor's hydraulic system raise, lower, and control the implement as if it were part of the tractor itself.

History

The system was developed by Irish engineer Harry Ferguson in the 1920s and was demonstrated on the Ferguson-Brown tractor of 1936. It became famous on the Ford-Ferguson 9N of 1939 and the Ferguson TE20. Because the arrangement transfers some of the implement's draft force onto the tractor's rear wheels, it improves traction and automatically moderates ploughing depth.

Categories

Hitches are standardized in categories 0 through 4 according to tractor power, with larger categories using wider spacing and heavier pins. Nearly all modern tractors and implements worldwide use the three-point linkage, making it one of the most successful standards in agricultural engineering.
