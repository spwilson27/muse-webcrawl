Resultant

In mathematics, the resultant of two polynomials is a polynomial expression of their coefficients that equals zero if and only if the polynomials have a common root (possibly in an extension field), or equivalently a common factor of positive degree. It is a fundamental tool of elimination theory, introduced in the context of solving systems of polynomial equations.

Definition

Given two polynomials of degrees m and n, their resultant can be computed as the determinant of the Sylvester matrix, a square matrix of size (m+n) built from the coefficients of the two polynomials. Equivalently, up to sign, the resultant equals the product of all pairwise differences of the roots of the two polynomials, multiplied by powers of their leading coefficients. The resultant is closely related to the discriminant, which is essentially the resultant of a polynomial and its derivative.

Applications

Resultants are used to eliminate variables from systems of polynomial equations, in computer algebra systems, in algebraic geometry (for example in implicitisation of curves and surfaces), and in checking whether polynomials share factors. Generalisations such as the multivariate resultant extend the concept to several polynomials in several variables.

In vector physics, the word "resultant" also denotes the single vector obtained by adding two or more vectors together — the resultant force or resultant vector.
