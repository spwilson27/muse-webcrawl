Adolf Frederick (Swedish: Adolf Fredrik; 14 May 1710 – 12 February 1771) was King of Sweden from 1751 until his death in 1771. He was the son of Christian August of Holstein-Gottorp, Prince of Eutin, and Albertina Frederica of Baden-Durlach, and had been Prince-Bishop of Lübeck before being elected crown prince of Sweden in 1743 under Russian pressure.

Reign

Adolf Frederick's reign fell during Sweden's Age of Liberty, when power rested with the Riksdag of the Estates and the rival Hat and Cap parties; the king had little real authority. An attempted royal coup in 1756, backed by his energetic wife Louisa Ulrika, sister of Frederick the Great, failed and further reduced the monarchy's standing.

Death

He died on 12 February 1771 in Stockholm after a lavish meal — famously including lobster, caviar, sauerkraut, kippered herring, and champagne, finished with many servings of his favourite dessert, semla in hot milk — and is sometimes humorously called "the king who ate himself to death." He was succeeded by his son Gustav III.
