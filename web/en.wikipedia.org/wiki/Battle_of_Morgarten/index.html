Battle of Morgarten

The Battle of Morgarten was fought on 15 November 1315, when a force of Swiss Confederates from the cantons of Uri, Schwyz and Unterwalden ambushed and defeated a much larger army of Habsburg Austria led by Duke Leopold I. The battle, fought near the Morgarten Pass between Lake Ägeri and Schwyz, is regarded as one of the foundational victories of the Old Swiss Confederacy and a landmark in the history of infantry warfare.

Background

Tensions between the forest cantons and the Habsburgs had grown over control of trade routes through the Gotthard Pass and over a long-running dispute between Schwyz and Einsiedeln Abbey. In January 1314, men of Schwyz raided the abbey, a Habsburg protectorate, prompting Duke Leopold to march against them in late 1315. The immediate political context was the disputed imperial election of 1314, in which the Swiss supported Louis of Bavaria against the Habsburg candidate Frederick the Fair.

The battle

Leopold's army advanced along the shore of Lake Ägeri toward Schwyz. Warned in advance, the Swiss prepared an ambush in the defile at Morgarten, blocking the road with felled trees and abatis. As the Austrian column of mounted knights was compressed in the narrow pass, the Swiss rolled boulders and logs down the slope and attacked from above with halberds. Unable to deploy or retreat, the knights were slaughtered; many were driven into the lake and drowned. Leopold escaped with difficulty.

Aftermath

In December 1315 the three cantons renewed their alliance in the Pact of Brunnen, consolidating the Confederacy. Morgarten became a symbol of Swiss resistance and is still commemorated annually. Historians note the battle as an early demonstration that disciplined infantry fighting on favourable terrain could defeat feudal heavy cavalry — a pattern repeated at later Swiss victories such as Sempach (1386) and against Burgundy in the 1470s.
