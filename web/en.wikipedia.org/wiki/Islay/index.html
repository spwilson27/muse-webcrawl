Islay (Scottish Gaelic: Ìle) is the southernmost island of the Inner Hebrides of Scotland. Known as "The Queen of the Hebrides", it lies in Argyll and Bute just south-west of Jura and around 40 km (25 mi) north of the Northern Irish coast. The island's capital is Bowmore, known for its distinctive round church, and the largest settlement is Port Ellen.

Geography

Islay is 40 km (25 mi) from east to west and 35 km (22 mi) from north to south, with a coastline of some 209 km (130 mi) deeply indented by sea lochs, principally Loch Gruinart in the north and Loch Indaal in the south. The terrain is predominantly low-lying peat moorland; the highest point, Beinn Bheigeir, reaches 491 m. The Rhinns of Islay form a hammer-headed peninsula in the west.

History

Humans have lived on Islay since the Mesolithic. In the early medieval period the island was a centre of the Gaelic kingdom of Dál Riata, and later it became the seat of the Lords of the Isles, whose council met on an island in Loch Finlaggan. The MacDonald lordship was forfeited to the Scottish crown in 1493. In the 19th century the island was developed by the Campbell lairds of Shawfield, who founded planned villages such as Bowmore (1768) and Port Ellen (1821); the population peaked above 15,000 before emigration and clearance reduced it sharply.

Whisky

Islay is world-famous for its single malt Scotch whiskies, traditionally characterised by peat smoke imparted by drying malted barley over peat fires, together with maritime notes from coastal warehouses. The island's distilleries include Ardbeg, Lagavulin and Laphroaig on the south coast (the "Kildalton" distilleries), Bowmore, Bruichladdich, Bunnahabhain, Caol Ila and Kilchoman, with the revived Port Ellen distillery reopening in 2024. The annual Fèis Ìle festival each May draws thousands of whisky enthusiasts.

Wildlife

The island is an important site for overwintering barnacle geese and Greenland white-fronted geese, which feed on the pastures around Loch Gruinart, where the RSPB maintains a reserve. Choughs, hen harriers and golden eagles also breed on Islay.
