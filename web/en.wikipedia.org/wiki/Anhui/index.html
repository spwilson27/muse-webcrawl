Anhui

Anhui (Chinese: 安徽; pinyin: Ānhuī) is a landlocked province of the People's Republic of China, part of the East China region. Its provincial capital and largest city is Hefei. The province's name derives from the names of two cities: Anqing and Huizhou (now Huangshan City). The abbreviation for Anhui is 皖 (Wǎn), from the historical Wan state and Mount Wan.

Geography

Anhui is bisected by the Huai River and the Yangtze River, dividing it into three natural regions: north, central, and south of the Yangtze. The north is flat alluvial plain, the centre is low hills around Chaohu Lake, and the south is mountainous, home to the Huangshan (Yellow Mountains), a UNESCO World Heritage Site famous for its granite peaks and pine trees. The province borders Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Hubei, Henan, and Shandong.

History

Anhui was created as a province in the Qing dynasty, in 1667, from the division of Jiangnan province. The old Huizhou prefecture in the south produced famed merchants whose wealth funded distinctive architecture, woodcarving, and the Xin'an school of painting. In the 20th century Anhui was an early centre of rural reform: in 1978 the village of Xiaogang secretly pioneered the household responsibility system that transformed Chinese agriculture. Conversely, the province suffered severely in the Great Chinese Famine of 1959–61.

Economy and culture

Traditionally agricultural, Anhui has industrialised rapidly, with Hefei becoming a hub for science and technology, home to the University of Science and Technology of China. Culturally the province is divided: the north belongs to the Central Plains cultural sphere, while the south preserves the distinct Hui culture, including Huizhou cuisine, ink making, and Anhui (Hui) opera traditions that contributed to Peking opera.
