Open educational resources

Term coined: UNESCO Forum, 2002

Framework: The "5 Rs" — retain, reuse, revise, remix, redistribute

Typical licenses: Creative Commons licenses

Open educational resources (OER) are teaching, learning and research materials in any medium — digital or otherwise — that reside in the public domain or have been released under an open license that permits no-cost access, use, adaptation and redistribution by others with no or limited restrictions.

History

The term "open educational resources" was first adopted at UNESCO's 2002 Forum on the Impact of Open Courseware for Higher Education in Developing Countries, funded by the William and Flora Hewlett Foundation. The movement grew out of open courseware initiatives, most prominently MIT OpenCourseWare, launched in 2001, which published MIT course materials freely online.

The 2007 Cape Town Open Education Declaration and the 2012 Paris OER Declaration, adopted at the UNESCO World OER Congress, called on governments and institutions to support open licensing of publicly funded educational materials. In 2019 UNESCO adopted a formal Recommendation on OER.

Licensing and the 5 Rs

David Wiley formulated the "5R permissions" that define openness in educational content: the right to retain (make and own copies), reuse, revise, remix, and redistribute the material. Most OER are released under Creative Commons licenses or dedicated to the public domain.

Examples

Notable OER projects include OpenStax, which publishes free peer-reviewed textbooks; the Khan Academy; Wikibooks and other Wikimedia projects; the Saylor Academy; CK-12; and national initiatives such as Poland's open textbook program and the US Department of Labor's Trade Adjustment Assistance Community College and Career Training grants, which required open licensing of funded materials.

Impact and criticism

Advocates cite cost savings for students, improved access in developing countries, and the ability of teachers to adapt materials to local contexts. Research reviews have generally found learning outcomes with OER to be comparable to commercial textbooks. Challenges include sustainable funding models, discoverability and quality assurance, and limited awareness among faculty.
