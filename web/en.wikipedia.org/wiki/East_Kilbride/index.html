East Kilbride (Scottish Gaelic: Cill Bhrìghde an Ear) is a town in South Lanarkshire, Scotland, about 13 kilometres southeast of Glasgow. It was designated in 1947 as the first — and Scotland's largest — New Town, intended to relieve overcrowding and poor housing in Glasgow. Today it is one of the largest settlements in Scotland without city status, with a population of roughly 75,000.

History

The original village of East Kilbride grew around a church dedicated to St Bride (Kilbride means "church of Bride"), with weaving and later mining and agriculture as local trades. Following the New Towns Act, East Kilbride was designated on 6 May 1947, and the East Kilbride Development Corporation master-planned an entirely new town of neighbourhoods — including Murray, Westwood, Calderwood, Greenhills, St Leonards and Stewartfield — around a purpose-built town centre, separated by landscaped parkland and a network of roads notable for their many roundabouts, which have earned the town the nickname "Polo Mint City".

Economy and amenities

The town attracted engineering, electronics and government employers; a major UK Government administration centre (Centre 1) handled tax work for decades. East Kilbride Shopping Centre, developed from the original town centre precinct, became one of the largest covered shopping centres in Scotland, with an ice rink and cinema. Cultural facilities include the Village Theatre and the East Kilbride Arts Centre; the National Museum of Rural Life stands at Kittochside on the town's edge, and Calderglen Country Park and James Hamilton Heritage Park provide open space.

Transport and sport

East Kilbride railway station is the terminus of a branch line from Glasgow Central. The A725 and A726 link the town to the M8, M74 and M77 motorways. The town's senior football club, East Kilbride F.C., plays in the Lowland League, while the town is also known for its ice hockey and curling at the town-centre ice rink.
