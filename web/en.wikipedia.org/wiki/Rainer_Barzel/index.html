Rainer Barzel

Rainer Candidus Barzel (20 June 1924 – 26 August 2006) was a German politician of the Christian Democratic Union (CDU). He was federal leader of the CDU from 1971 to 1973 and its candidate for Chancellor in the 1972 federal election, which he lost to Willy Brandt. Earlier the same year Barzel tabled the first constructive vote of no confidence in the Federal Republic's history against Brandt — over the Chancellor's Ostpolitik treaties — and failed by two votes, in a ballot later shown to have been influenced by East German bribery. He later served as President of the Bundestag (1983–1984).

Early life

Barzel was born in Braunsberg, East Prussia, into a Catholic family; his father was a senior civil servant. He served as a naval aviator in the Second World War, studied law at Cologne, and joined the CDU in North Rhine-Westphalia, becoming a member of the Bundestag in 1957. From 1962 to 1963, in Konrad Adenauer's last cabinet, he was Federal Minister for All-German Affairs (later styled Intra-German Relations) — at 38, the youngest minister in the government.

Opposition leader and the no-confidence vote

After the CDU/CSU lost power in 1969, Barzel led the parliamentary group and in October 1971 succeeded Kurt Georg Kiesinger as CDU federal chairman. Opposing Brandt's Ostpolitik — the treaties with Moscow and Warsaw recognising post-war borders — Barzel moved a constructive vote of no confidence on 27 April 1972, proposing himself as Chancellor. The motion received 247 votes, two short of the 249 needed. It later emerged that the East German Stasi had bribed at least one CDU deputy, Julius Steiner, to abstain or vote against. The government subsequently lost its working majority anyway, and Brandt called an early election for November 1972.

The 1972 election and after

In the November 1972 federal election — the "Willy election", fought largely over Ostpolitik — the SPD for the first time outpolled the CDU/CSU, and Brandt remained Chancellor. Barzel resigned the party leadership in May 1973 and was succeeded by Helmut Kohl. He remained a respected elder of the CDU, chaired Bundestag committees, and served as President of the Bundestag from March 1983 until October 1984, resigning amid the Flick donations affair inquiries (he was cleared of personal wrongdoing). He died in Munich in 2006; Chancellor Helmut Schmidt's era overlapped his later parliamentary career.
