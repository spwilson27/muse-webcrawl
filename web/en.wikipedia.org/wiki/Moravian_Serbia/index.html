Moravian Serbia

Moravian Serbia (Serbian: Моравска Србија, Moravska Srbija) was the largest and most powerful of the Serbian principalities that emerged from the collapse of the Serbian Empire in the second half of the 14th century. Centred on the valley of the Morava River, it existed from roughly 1371 until 1403, when it evolved into the Serbian Despotate.

History

Its founder and ruler was Prince Lazar Hrebeljanović, who consolidated control of the Morava basin with Kruševac as his capital and founded the monasteries of Ravanica and Lazarica. Lazar was killed at the Battle of Kosovo in 1389 fighting the Ottoman Turks, becoming the central martyr-hero of Serbian epic tradition. His widow Milica governed as regent, then his son Stefan Lazarević succeeded, accepting Ottoman vassalage, leading Serbian heavy cavalry at Nicopolis (1396) and Ankara (1402), and receiving the title of despot from Byzantium in 1402.

Culture

Despite the political fragmentation and Ottoman pressure, Moravian Serbia saw a flourishing of church building in the distinctive "Morava school" of Serbian medieval architecture — exemplified by Ravanica, Ljubostinja, Kalenić, and Manasija — combining Byzantine, Romanesque, and local elements with rich sculptural decoration.
