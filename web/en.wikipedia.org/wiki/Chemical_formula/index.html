Chemical formula

A chemical formula is a way of presenting information about the chemical proportions of atoms that constitute a particular chemical compound or molecule, using chemical element symbols, numbers, and sometimes other symbols such as parentheses, dashes, brackets and plus (+) and minus (−) signs.

Types of formula

The empirical formula gives the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms in a compound — CH2O for glucose. The molecular formula gives the actual number of each type of atom in a molecule — C6H12O6 for glucose. A structural formula shows how the atoms are arranged and bonded, as in a chemical structure diagram, while a condensed formula compresses that information into a single line of text, such as CH3CH2OH for ethanol.

Ionic compounds and other notations

For ionic compounds the formula lists the cations and anions in their simplest ratio, e.g. NaCl for sodium chloride. Parentheses group polyatomic units, as in Ca(OH)2. Dots indicate water of crystallisation in hydrates (CuSO4·5H2O), and isotope labels can be prefixed, as in D2O for heavy water.
