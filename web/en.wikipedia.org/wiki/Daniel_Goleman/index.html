Daniel Goleman

Daniel Goleman (born 7 March 1946) is an American psychologist, author, and science journalist. He is best known for his 1995 book Emotional Intelligence, which popularised the concept worldwide.


Born: 7 March 1946, Stockton, California, US
Education: Amherst College, Harvard University (PhD)
Known for: Emotional intelligence
Notable work: Emotional Intelligence (1995)

Career

Goleman earned his PhD at Harvard under David McClelland and wrote for Psychology Today and The New York Times, covering brain and behavioural science. His reporting drew on research by Peter Salovey and John Mayer, who coined "emotional intelligence".

Works and ideas

Emotional Intelligence argued that self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, and social skills matter as much as IQ for success. Follow-ups included Working with Emotional Intelligence, Social Intelligence, Focus, and, with the Dalai Lama, works on destructive emotions and meditation (including Altered Traits with Richard Davidson). He co-founded the Collaborative for Academic, Social, and Emotional Learning (CASEL).

Reception

His books were bestsellers translated into many languages, though academics debate the measurement and distinctiveness of emotional intelligence as popularised.
