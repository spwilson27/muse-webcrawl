Fighting in ice hockey

Fighting in ice hockey is an established, if controversial, tradition in North American professional hockey. Fighting is prohibited by the rules of most leagues, yet in the National Hockey League (NHL) and other North American professional leagues it is penalized with a five-minute major penalty rather than ejection, and officials generally allow combatants to fight until one falls to the ice. In international and European hockey fighting usually results in ejection and suspension.

History and role

Fighting has been part of the sport since the 19th century and grew with its roughness; the NHL introduced the major-penalty rule in 1922. Supporters argue fighting polices on-ice conduct, deters cheap shots against star players, and energizes fans. Teams historically employed "enforcers" or "goons" whose primary role was to fight and protect teammates. Famous incidents, such as bench-clearing brawls in the 1970s "Broad Street Bullies" era, entrenched its place in the game's culture.

Decline and debate

Fighting rates have fallen sharply since the 1990s as speed and skill grew in value, rostering rules changed, and concerns about head injuries and chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) mounted. In 2023 the Quebec Maritimes Junior Hockey League effectively banned fighting. Critics argue staged fights add no strategic value and endanger players; defenders counter that it is safer than stick fouls and remains part of the sport's identity. Rules penalize instigators and players who remove helmets, and leaving the bench to join a fight draws automatic suspension.
