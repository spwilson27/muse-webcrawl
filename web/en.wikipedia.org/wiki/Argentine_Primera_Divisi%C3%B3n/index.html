The Argentine Primera División is the top professional football league in Argentina. Organised by the Argentine Football Association (AFA) through the Liga Profesional de Fútbol, it is one of the oldest football leagues in the world, having first been contested in 1891, and is regularly ranked among the strongest leagues globally.

History

The first championship was held in 1891, making Argentina the first country outside the British Isles to establish a football league. The league turned professional in 1931. For decades the competition was dominated by the so-called "Big Five" clubs: River Plate, Boca Juniors, Independiente, Racing Club and San Lorenzo.

From 1967 to 1985 the championship was split into Metropolitano and Nacional tournaments. Later formats included the Apertura and Clausura short tournaments, and in the 2010s the league experimented with expanded formats involving as many as 30 teams.

Competition format

In recent seasons the Primera División has featured around 28 teams. The season typically combines a league tournament (Torneo) with a cup-style competition (Copa de la Liga Profesional). Relegation is determined by a coefficient table (promedios) averaging points over several seasons, together with the annual table.

Champions

River Plate is the most successful club in the competition's history, followed by Boca Juniors. Their rivalry, the Superclásico, is regarded as one of the fiercest derbies in world football. Other historically successful clubs include Independiente, Racing, San Lorenzo, Vélez Sarsfield and Estudiantes de La Plata.

International competition

Primera División clubs qualify for the Copa Libertadores and Copa Sudamericana. Argentine clubs have won the Copa Libertadores more times than those of any other nation, with Independiente holding a record seven titles.
