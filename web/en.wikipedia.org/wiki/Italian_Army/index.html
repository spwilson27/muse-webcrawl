Italian Army

The Italian Army (Italian: Esercito Italiano) is the land warfare branch of the Italian Armed Forces. Its origins go back to the Royal Sardinian Army of the Kingdom of Sardinia, which became the Royal Italian Army (Regio Esercito) on the unification of Italy in 1861; the current name dates from the birth of the Italian Republic in 1946.

History

The army fought the Wars of Italian Independence, colonial campaigns in Africa, and the First World War — including the battles of the Isonzo and the victory at Vittorio Veneto in 1918. In the Second World War it fought in Greece, North Africa, the Soviet Union and the Balkans before the armistice of 1943 split it between the co-belligerent forces and those of the Italian Social Republic. During the Cold War it stood guard on the north-eastern border within NATO; since 2005 it has been an all-volunteer professional force after the suspension of conscription.

Organisation

The army fields mechanised and armoured brigades such as the "Ariete" armoured brigade, alpine brigades of the Alpini, the Folgore parachute brigade, cavalry, artillery, engineers and special forces (including the 9th Paratroopers Assault Regiment "Col Moschin"). Main equipment includes the Ariete main battle tank, Centauro tank destroyer, Dardo and Freccia infantry fighting vehicles and Mangusta attack helicopters. Italian troops have served extensively in peacekeeping and coalition operations in the Balkans, Afghanistan, Iraq, Lebanon and Africa.
