Eighth Crusade
Date1270
LocationTunis, Hafsid Ifriqiya (modern Tunisia)
BelligerentsKingdom of France and allies vs Hafsid Sultanate
ResultTreaty of Tunis; death of Louis IX

The Eighth Crusade was the second crusade launched by King Louis IX of France, in 1270. Instead of Egypt or the Holy Land, Louis — influenced, it is thought, by his brother Charles of Anjou and hopes that the Hafsid caliph would convert to Christianity — sailed against Tunis.

Course

The fleet landed near ancient Carthage in July 1270 and occupied the ruins while awaiting Charles. Disease — dysentery or typhus — devastated the camp in the summer heat. Louis himself died on 25 August 1270, reportedly with the word "Jerusalem" on his lips. His son Philip III succeeded him.

Treaty and aftermath

Charles of Anjou negotiated the Treaty of Tunis: the Hafsids paid an indemnity, doubled tribute to Sicily, and granted Christian merchants privileges and freedom of worship. The crusaders withdrew. Edward of England (later Edward I), arriving late, sailed on to Acre for what is sometimes counted the Ninth Crusade. Louis was canonised in 1297.
