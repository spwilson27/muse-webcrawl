Seychelles

The Seychelles, officially the Republic of Seychelles, is an island country and archipelagic state in the Indian Ocean, northeast of Madagascar. It consists of 155 islands — granitic inner islands around Mahé and coral outer islands — and is Africa's smallest country by area and population (about 120,000). Its capital is Victoria, one of the world's smallest capitals.

The Seychelles were uninhabited until French settlement in the 1770s, then British until independence in 1976. They are famed for pristine beaches regularly ranked among the world's best (Anse Source d'Argent), giant Aldabra tortoises, the coco de mer palm's enormous seeds, and a tourism- and tuna-based economy. About half the country's land is protected — the highest proportion in the world — and it pioneered a debt-for-nature swap to fund marine protection.
