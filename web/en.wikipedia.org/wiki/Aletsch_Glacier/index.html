The Aletsch Glacier (German: Aletschgletscher) is the largest glacier in the Alps. It is situated in the canton of Valais in south-western Switzerland, within the Jungfrau-Aletsch protected area, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The glacier is about 23 kilometres long and covers roughly 80 square kilometres, holding an estimated 11 billion tonnes of ice — about a fifth of Switzerland's total glacier ice volume.

Geography

The glacier forms at Konkordiaplatz, a broad ice field where three great tributary firns (the Grosse Aletschfirn, Jungfraufirn and Ewigschneefäld) converge. From there it flows south-east, flanked by peaks including the Aletschhorn and Eggishorn, before its meltwaters feed the Massa gorge and ultimately the Rhône. Distinctive dark medial moraines mark the junctions of the tributary ice streams.

Retreat

Like most Alpine glaciers, the Aletsch has retreated substantially since the Little Ice Age maximum around 1860, losing several kilometres of length and thinning rapidly in recent decades. Projections suggest that even under moderate warming scenarios most of its volume will disappear by 2100.

Tourism

The glacier is a major attraction, viewed from the Jungfraujoch, Bettmerhorn and Eggishorn, and skirted by the historic Villa Cassel nature centre of Pro Natura.
