Donepezil, sold under the brand name Aricept among others, is a medication used to treat dementia of the Alzheimer's type. It appears to result in a small benefit in mental function and ability to function, but has not been shown to change the progression of the disease. It is taken by mouth or via a transdermal patch.

Medical uses

Donepezil is used to treat mild to severe dementia in Alzheimer's disease. There is no evidence that donepezil or other similar agents alter the course or progression of Alzheimer's disease; however, studies have shown modest combined benefits on cognition, activities of daily living, and clinician global assessments. It has also been studied in other types of dementia, including Lewy body dementia and vascular dementia.

Mechanism of action

Donepezil is a centrally acting reversible inhibitor of the enzyme acetylcholinesterase. By blocking the breakdown of acetylcholine, it increases cholinergic neurotransmission, which is diminished in Alzheimer's disease owing to the loss of cholinergic neurons. It is highly selective for the central nervous system form of the enzyme.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include nausea, diarrhea, insomnia, vomiting, muscle cramps, fatigue, and loss of appetite. More serious effects may include bradycardia (slow heart rate), stomach ulcers, and seizures in people with a prior history of seizures.

History

Research leading to donepezil began in 1983 at the Japanese pharmaceutical company Eisai, and clinical trials began in Japan in 1992. It was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 1996, co-promoted with Pfizer. Generic versions have been available since 2010. A combination product with memantine is also marketed. On the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines, donepezil is listed as an antidementia medication.
