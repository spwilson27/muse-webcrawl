Seven-spot ladybird

Scientific classification
KingdomAnimalia
OrderColeoptera
FamilyCoccinellidae
SpeciesCoccinella septempunctata

The seven-spot ladybird (Coccinella septempunctata) is the most common ladybird in Europe and one of the most widespread ladybird beetles in the world. It was introduced into North America for biological control of aphids and is now established there.

Description

Adults are 6.5–8 mm long, with bright red elytra bearing three black spots on each wing case and one at the base where the two cases meet, making seven in total. The pronotum is black with white or pale markings at the front corners. The colouration serves as a warning (aposematism): the beetles secrete bitter-tasting alkaloids from their leg joints when threatened.

Biology

Both adults and larvae are voracious predators of aphids, a single larva consuming hundreds during its development. Females lay clusters of yellow eggs near aphid colonies. There are typically one or two generations a year in temperate climates, and adults overwinter in sheltered places, sometimes aggregating in houses. The species inhabits a wide range of habitats, from gardens and farmland to woodland edges and meadows.
