Distinguished Flying Cross (United States)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Distinguished Flying Cross medal

The Distinguished Flying Cross (DFC) is a military decoration of the United States Armed Forces. The medal was established by the Air Corps Act passed by the United States Congress on 2 July 1926. It is awarded to any officer or enlisted member of the United States Armed Forces who distinguishes himself or herself by single acts of heroism or extraordinary achievement while participating in aerial flight.

In the order of precedence of U.S. military awards, the Distinguished Flying Cross is a higher decoration than the Bronze Star Medal, but ranks below the Legion of Merit. The medal was designed by Elizabeth Will and Arthur E. DuBois.

Criteria

The Distinguished Flying Cross is awarded in the name of the President of the United States to any office or enlisted member of the Armed Forces of the United States who, after 6 April 1917, distinguishes himself or herself by single acts of heroism or extraordinary achievement while participating in aerial flight. The performance of the act must be evidenced by voluntary action above and beyond the call of duty and must have resulted in an accomplishment so exceptional and outstanding as to clearly set the individual apart from his or her comrades or from other persons in similar circumstances.

Appearance

The medal is a cross pattée made of bronze, depicting an ornamental propeller in the center, with a sunburst behind it. The reverse of the medal bears the words "For Heroism" and "For Achievement" arranged in a circle around the center. The ribbon consists of a central stripe of navy blue flanked by stripes of white and red.

Notable recipients

  • Charles Lindbergh, for his solo transatlantic flight in 1927
  • Amelia Earhart, the first woman to receive the award
  • Jimmy Doolittle, aviation pioneer and leader of the Doolittle Raid
  • George H. W. Bush, later President of the United States
  • John McCain, U.S. naval aviator and later Senator
  • Buzz Aldrin, astronaut and second man on the Moon
