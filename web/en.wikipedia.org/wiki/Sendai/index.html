Sendai

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sendai (仙台市, Sendai-shi) is the capital city of Miyagi Prefecture, the largest city in the Tōhoku region of northern Honshu, Japan. With a population of roughly 1.1 million, it is one of Japan's designated cities. Sendai is nicknamed the City of Trees (Mori no Miyako) for its wide, zelkova-lined boulevards.

History

Although the area had been inhabited for thousands of years, Sendai was effectively founded in 1600 by the powerful feudal lord Date Masamune, who built Sendai Castle (Aoba Castle) on Aobayama hill and laid out the castle town below. Masamune sent the Keichō Embassy under Hasekura Tsunenaga to Europe in 1613, one of the earliest Japanese diplomatic missions to the West.

Sendai became a city in 1889 and was designated a city by government ordinance in 1989. The city suffered heavy damage in World War II air raids in 1945, and its coastal districts were devastated by the tsunami following the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake of 11 March 2011, though the elevated city centre was largely spared.

Culture

Sendai is famous for the Tanabata Festival, held each August, one of the largest in Japan. Local specialities include gyūtan (grilled beef tongue), zunda (sweet edamame paste), and sasakamaboko fish cake. Tohoku University, founded in 1907, is one of Japan's leading research universities.
