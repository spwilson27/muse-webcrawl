Tetrameter

Tetrameter is a metre in poetry. It refers to a line consisting of four metrical feet. The word derives from the Greek tetra- ("four") and metron ("measure"). Tetrameter is a common line length in English verse, appearing in ballads, hymns, children's verse, and song lyrics, as well as in longer narrative and dramatic poems.

Types

Like other metres, tetrameter is named by its foot type. Iambic tetrameter (four iambs, da-DUM ×4) is used in works such as John Milton's companion poems "L'Allegro" and "Il Penseroso" and Andrew Marvell's "To His Coy Mistress." Trochaic tetrameter (DUM-da ×4) drives Henry Wadsworth Longfellow's The Song of Hiawatha and William Blake's "The Tyger." Anapestic tetrameter is the metre of Clement Clarke Moore's "'Twas the Night Before Christmas" and many of Dr. Seuss's books. Catalectic variants drop the final unstressed syllable, giving a strong line ending.

Effect

Shorter than pentameter, tetrameter tends to feel quicker, lighter, and more songlike, which is why it is favored for narrative momentum, satire, and verse intended to be sung or read aloud.
