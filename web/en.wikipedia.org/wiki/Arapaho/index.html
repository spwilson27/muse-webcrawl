The Arapaho (Arapaho: Hinono'eino, "our people"; French: Arapahos) are a Native American people historically living on the plains of eastern Colorado and Wyoming. They speak the Arapaho language, a member of the Algonquian family, closely related to Gros Ventre. In the 19th century the tribe split into two divisions: the Northern Arapaho, now of the Wind River Reservation in Wyoming, which they share with the Eastern Shoshone, and the Southern Arapaho, federally recognised together with the Southern Cheyenne in Oklahoma.

History

Arapaho-speaking peoples migrated from the western Great Lakes region onto the northern plains by the early 18th century, adopting a nomadic buffalo-hunting culture organised in bands with age-graded men's societies. They were close allies of the Cheyenne and, later, of the Lakota. Treaties at Fort Laramie (1851 and 1868) recognised Arapaho territory, but gold rushes and railroad expansion brought conflict; Southern Arapaho were among the victims of the Sand Creek massacre of 1864, in which Colorado volunteers under John Chivington killed scores of Cheyenne and Arapaho, mostly women and children. The Northern Arapaho were settled on the Wind River Reservation in 1878, initially as guests of the Shoshone.

Culture

The Arapaho ceremonial centre is the Sun Dance (Offerings Lodge), and sacred objects include the Sacred Flat Pipe, kept by designated pipe-keepers. Traditional crafts include intricate quillwork and beadwork with geometric designs. The Ghost Dance movement of 1889–90 spread widely among the Arapaho, whose delegates visited the prophet Wovoka in Nevada.

Language

The Arapaho language is severely endangered: most fluent first-language speakers are elderly, though immersion preschools and school programmes at Wind River and in Oklahoma are working to revitalise it. Linguist Andrew Cowell and others have produced extensive documentation, including a dictionary and conversation materials.

Modern tribes

  • Northern Arapaho Tribe of the Wind River Reservation, Wyoming (enrolled population over 10,000)
  • Cheyenne and Arapaho Tribes of Oklahoma, headquartered at Concho
