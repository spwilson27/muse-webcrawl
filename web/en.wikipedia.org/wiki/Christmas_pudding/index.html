Christmas pudding

Christmas pudding
Alternative names: Plum pudding, figgy pudding
Origin: England
Main ingredients: Suet, breadcrumbs, dried fruit, spices, treacle
Served: Christmas Day, flamed with brandy

Christmas pudding is a steamed British dessert traditionally served on Christmas Day. Despite the name it contains no plums in the modern sense — 'plum' formerly meant raisins. It is dark, rich and dense with dried fruit and spices.

History

The pudding evolved from medieval pottages and plum porridge. By the Victorian era it took its canonical form, popularised in literature including Charles Dickens's A Christmas Carol (1843). Traditionally families stir the mixture on 'Stir-up Sunday', the Sunday before Advent, and hide a silver coin in it for luck.

Preparation

The mixture of suet, breadcrumbs, flour, eggs, brown sugar, treacle, raisins, currants, candied peel, spices and ale or brandy is steamed for many hours, matured for weeks, then steamed again, decorated with holly, flamed with warmed brandy and served with brandy butter, rum sauce or custard.
