World War II in Yugoslavia

Dates: 6 April 1941 – 25 May 1945
Location: Yugoslavia
Result: Partisan victory; establishment of Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia

World War II in Yugoslavia began on 6 April 1941 with the Axis invasion of the Kingdom of Yugoslavia. The country was partitioned between Germany, Italy, Hungary and Bulgaria, with puppet states including the Independent State of Croatia.

Occupation and resistance

Two resistance movements emerged: the Chetniks, largely Serb royalist forces, and the communist-led Partisans under Josip Broz Tito. The Partisans grew into a large multi-ethnic liberation army, recognised by the Allies from 1943.

Major operations

Axis offensives including the Battles of Neretva and Sutjeska in 1943 attempted to destroy Partisan forces. In autumn 1944, Partisans with Soviet Red Army support liberated Belgrade. Fighting continued until May 1945.

Human cost

Yugoslavia suffered very high losses relative to population — around one million dead by many estimates — including massacres, the Holocaust, and atrocities by occupying and collaborationist forces, notably in the Independent State of Croatia.

Aftermath

The monarchy was abolished and a socialist federation proclaimed in 1945, with Tito as leader.
