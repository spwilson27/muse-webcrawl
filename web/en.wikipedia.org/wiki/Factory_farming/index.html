Factory farming

Factory farming, also known as intensive animal farming or industrial livestock production, is a type of intensive agriculture designed to maximise production while minimising costs, in which large numbers of animals — cattle, pigs, poultry and fish — are raised in high-density confinement.

Factory farming
Also calledIntensive animal farming, CAFO (US)
AnimalsCattle, pigs, chickens, fish
IssuesAnimal welfare, pollution, antibiotics, climate

Practices

Methods include battery cages for laying hens, gestation crates for sows, feedlots for beef cattle, and indoor broiler sheds holding tens of thousands of chickens. Animals are fed formulated diets, often with antibiotics for growth promotion and disease prevention in crowded conditions, a practice increasingly restricted over antimicrobial resistance concerns.

History

Intensive methods expanded after the Second World War with mechanisation, synthetic fertilisers, cheap grain and vertical integration of meat companies, spreading from the United States and Europe worldwide.

Impacts and debate

Supporters cite efficiency, lower consumer prices and land use per unit of output. Critics cite animal suffering, water and air pollution from manure, greenhouse gas emissions, zoonotic disease risk, and effects on rural communities. Reform efforts include cage-free commitments, welfare labelling and alternative proteins.
