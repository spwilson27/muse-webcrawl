Basil Anthony Moreau

Basil Anthony Marie Patrice Moreau, CSC (11 February 1799 – 20 January 1873) was a French priest who founded the Congregation of Holy Cross (Congregatio a Sancta Cruce, CSC), a religious congregation of priests, brothers, and sisters devoted to education and parish ministry.

Early life and priesthood

Moreau was born in Laigné-en-Belin during the turmoil of the French Revolution, when Catholic practice was suppressed. Ordained a priest for the Diocese of Le Mans in 1821, he taught theology at the seminary and worked to renew Christian education in a region where schooling had been devastated.

Foundation of Holy Cross

In 1835 Moreau assumed leadership of the Brothers of St. Joseph, founded by Jacques Dujarié, and in 1837 united them with a group of auxiliary priests he had gathered at Sainte-Croix, near Le Mans — the origin of the Congregation of Holy Cross. A community of sisters, the Marianites of Holy Cross, followed. Moreau sent missionaries abroad, most famously Father Edward Sorin, who in 1842 founded the University of Notre Dame in Indiana.

Later years and veneration

Moreau resigned as superior general in 1866 and spent his final years in relative obscurity, dying in Le Mans in 1873. He was beatified by Pope Benedict XVI on 15 September 2007, with the ceremony held in Le Mans. His feast day is 20 January.
