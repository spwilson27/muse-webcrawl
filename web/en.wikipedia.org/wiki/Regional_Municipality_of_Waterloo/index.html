Regional Municipality of Waterloo


Country: Canada
Province: Ontario
Created: 1 January 1973
Population: over 600,000

The Regional Municipality of Waterloo (Waterloo Region) is a metropolitan area and regional municipality in Southern Ontario, Canada. It comprises the cities of Kitchener, Waterloo, and Cambridge, and the townships of Woolwich, Wellesley, Wilmot, and North Dumfries, with a population exceeding 600,000.

History

The region replaced Waterloo County on 1 January 1973 as part of Ontario's creation of two-tier regional governments. The area was settled in the early 1800s by Pennsylvania German Mennonites; Kitchener was called Berlin until 1916.

Economy and institutions

Waterloo Region is a major Canadian technology hub, anchored by the University of Waterloo, Wilfrid Laurier University, and Conestoga College, and historically by BlackBerry (Research In Motion). The region is also a centre for insurance and manufacturing. The Grand River flows through it, and the area includes the preserved Mennonite countryside around St. Jacobs.

Government and transit

The upper-tier regional council delivers policing (Waterloo Regional Police Service), transit (Grand River Transit), water, waste management, and public health. The Ion light rail system opened in 2019, linking Waterloo and Kitchener.
