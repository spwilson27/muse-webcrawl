The World Summit on the Information Society (WSIS) was a two-phase United Nations summit on information, communication and the information society, held in Geneva in 2003 and in Tunis in 2005. It brought together governments, the private sector and civil society to address the global digital divide and the governance of the Internet.

Geneva phase (2003)

The first phase adopted a Declaration of Principles and a Plan of Action setting targets for connecting villages, schools, health centres and governments to information and communication technologies by 2015, aligned with the Millennium Development Goals.

Tunis phase (2005)

The second phase produced the Tunis Commitment and the Tunis Agenda for the Information Society. Controversially, it left oversight of the Internet's naming and numbering system largely unchanged but created the Internet Governance Forum (IGF), a multistakeholder body for policy dialogue, and called for "enhanced cooperation" on international Internet public policy.

Follow-up

WSIS follow-up is coordinated through the WSIS Forum, held annually, and a ten-year review (WSIS+10) by the UN General Assembly in 2015, with a WSIS+20 review scheduled for 2025.
