Supreme Court of the Netherlands

The Supreme Court of the Netherlands (Dutch: Hoge Raad der Nederlanden), officially the High Council of the Netherlands, is the highest court in the civil, criminal, and tax law systems of the European Netherlands and of the Caribbean constituent countries of the Kingdom of the Netherlands. Established in 1838, it sits in The Hague.

Role: cassation

The Court is principally a court of cassation: it does not re-examine the facts of a case but reviews whether the lower court (usually one of the four courts of appeal) applied the law correctly and observed proper procedure. If it finds an error, it quashes the judgment and typically refers the case back for rehearing. Its rulings promote the uniform application and development of Dutch law.

Composition and appointment

The Court consists of a president, vice-presidents, and justices (raadsheren) organised into civil, criminal, and tax chambers, supported by the independent Procurator General's office, whose advocates general issue advisory opinions (conclusions) on cases. Justices are formally appointed by royal decree on the nomination of the House of Representatives, from a shortlist drawn up by the Court itself — a co-optation-based system designed to insulate appointments from politics. Justices serve until the mandatory retirement age of 70.

Constitutional position

Unlike constitutional courts in some countries, the Hoge Raad may not review Acts of Parliament against the Dutch Constitution (Article 120 prohibits constitutional review of statutes), although it can and does test legislation against self-executing provisions of international treaties such as the European Convention on Human Rights. Administrative law cases are heard not by the Hoge Raad but by the Council of State's Administrative Jurisdiction Division and other specialised bodies.

History

The Court traces its origins to earlier high councils of the Dutch Republic and was instituted in its modern form with the judicial organisation of 1838. Its seat since 2016 is a purpose-built building on the Korte Voorhout in The Hague.
