Resource depletion

Resource depletion is the consumption of a resource faster than it can be replenished. Natural resources are commonly divided between renewable resources and non-renewable resources. The use of either of these forms of resources beyond their rate of replacement is considered to be resource depletion. The value of a resource is a result of its ability to be used, and depletion of resources has the potential to change the way an economy functions.

Depletion accounting

In an attempt to make the cause of resource depletion undesirable, economists developed the idea of depletion accounting, now more commonly called green accounting. Depletion accounting attempts to account for the value of natural resources in a similar way to how financial accounting accounts for other assets. Depletion accounting is one of several ways to account for the costs of using precious resources in the field of environmental accounting.

Mineral depletion

Minerals are required to provide food, clothing, and housing for the world's population. The extraction of minerals often proceeds at a rate that exhausts deposits over time, giving rise to concerns about peak minerals and the long-run availability of critical materials such as rare earth elements, phosphorus, and fossil fuels.
