Cao Shuang

Cao Shuang (Chinese: 曹爽; died 9 February 249), courtesy name Zhaobo, was a Chinese military general and regent of the state of Cao Wei during the Three Kingdoms period of China. He was the eldest son of Cao Zhen, a prominent Wei general. After the death of Emperor Cao Rui in 239, Cao Shuang shared the regency for the young emperor Cao Fang with the veteran statesman Sima Yi. Over the following decade Cao Shuang monopolised power, sidelining Sima Yi and enriching his own faction, until Sima Yi overthrew him in the Incident at Gaoping Tombs in 249 and had him and his associates executed.

Regency

Emperor Cao Rui's deathbed appointments in 239 made Cao Shuang and Sima Yi co-regents. Cao Shuang initially treated Sima Yi with deference but, advised by cronies such as He Yan, Deng Yang, and Ding Mi, gradually stripped him of real authority — promoting him to the honorary but powerless post of Grand Tutor in 247 while concentrating military and civil appointments in his own clan's hands. In 244, Cao Shuang led an expensive and unsuccessful invasion of Shu Han, defeated at the Battle of Xingshi, which damaged his prestige.

Incident at Gaoping Tombs

On 5 February 249, Cao Shuang accompanied Emperor Cao Fang out of Luoyang to visit the Gaoping Tombs. Sima Yi, who had feigned illness, staged a coup in the capital, seizing the arsenal and the city gates. Persuaded by the promise of safety conveyed through the official Xu Yun and others, Cao Shuang surrendered rather than fleeing to Xuchang to resist. Within days, on 9 February 249, Cao Shuang and his brothers and associates were charged with treason and executed along with their families — exterminated to the third degree of kinship. The coup transferred effective control of Wei to the Sima clan, paving the way for the founding of the Jin dynasty.
