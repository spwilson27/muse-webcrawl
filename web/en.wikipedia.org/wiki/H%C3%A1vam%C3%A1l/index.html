Hávamál
Type: Old Norse poem
Source: Codex Regius, Poetic Edda
Speaker: Odin
Meaning: "Words of the High One"

Hávamál ("Words of the High One") is an Old Norse poem from the Poetic Edda, preserved in the 13th-century Codex Regius, presenting advice for living, hospitality, and wisdom attributed to the god Odin.

Contents

The poem combines gnomic wisdom — proverbial stanzas on conduct, friendship, drinking, and caution — with mythic episodes, including Odin's self-sacrifice on the world tree Yggdrasil to gain the runes, and his winning of the mead of poetry. It contains about 164 stanzas.

Notable verses

Famous lines include stanza 77: "Cattle die, kindred die, / every man is mortal, / but the good name never dies / of one who has done well." The poem remains a key source for Norse ethics and mythology.
