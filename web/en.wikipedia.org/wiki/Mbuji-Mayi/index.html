Mbuji-Mayi

Mbuji-Mayi (formerly Bakwanga) is a city in south-central Democratic Republic of the Congo and the capital of Kasaï-Oriental province. It is one of the largest cities in the country, with a population estimated in the millions, and the centre of one of the world's richest alluvial diamond deposits. The name derives from the Tshiluba words for "goat water" — a local name associated with goat rearing and the Mbuji stream in the area.

History

The settlement grew from a trading and diamond-mining post at Bakwanga. Diamonds had been discovered in the region in the early 20th century, and mining was organised on an industrial scale by the Société Minière de Bakwanga (MIBA). During the Congo Crisis of 1960, Bakwanga became the capital of the breakaway "Mining State of South Kasai" under Albert Kalonji. Hundreds of thousands of Luba people expelled from Katanga in the early 1990s swelled the city, and it grew rapidly despite limited infrastructure.

Diamonds and economy

The Mbuji-Mayi deposits produce mainly industrial-grade diamonds; the region is often described as the industrial diamond capital of the world. Alongside the state-backed MIBA operation, large numbers of artisanal diggers (creuseurs) work the gravels, and diamond trading dominates the local economy. The city has long suffered from poor road and rail links — most goods arrive by air or by long overland routes — and from unreliable electricity and water supplies, despite the wealth extracted from its soil.
