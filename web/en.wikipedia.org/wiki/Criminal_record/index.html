Criminal record

A criminal record (also called a police record or informally a rap sheet) is a record of a person's criminal history maintained by a government agency. The information typically includes arrests, charges, convictions, sentences, and fingerprint and photographic identifiers, though the exact contents and rules of access vary widely by jurisdiction.

Contents and systems

Modern records are held in centralised databases. In the United States, the FBI's Interstate Identification Index ("Triple I") links state repositories, and a "rap sheet" (from Record of Arrests and Prosecutions) summarises an individual's encounters with the criminal justice system. The United Kingdom maintains the Police National Computer, and the European Union operates ECRIS for exchanging conviction information between member states.

Uses

Criminal records are used by courts for sentencing, by police for investigations, and — controversially — by employers, landlords, licensing bodies and immigration authorities in background checks. Because arrests that did not lead to conviction often appear on records, many jurisdictions distinguish conviction records from arrest records and restrict their use.

Expungement and rehabilitation

Many legal systems provide for records to be sealed, expunged, or treated as "spent" after a rehabilitation period. The UK Rehabilitation of Offenders Act 1974 allows most convictions to become spent after a defined period, after which they need not generally be disclosed. In the United States, expungement and record-sealing laws vary by state, and "ban the box" policies restrict when employers may ask about criminal history. Juvenile records are commonly sealed on reaching adulthood.

Criticism

Research links criminal-record screening to reduced employment and housing opportunities for people with old or minor convictions, with disproportionate effects on racial minorities; these collateral consequences are a major theme of criminal-justice reform debates.
