Lindow Man, also known as Lindow II and popularly nicknamed "Pete Marsh", is the preserved body of a man discovered in a peat bog at Lindow Moss near Wilmslow in Cheshire, England, on 1 August 1984. He is one of Britain's most famous bog bodies and is exhibited in the British Museum.

Discovery and remains

Peat cutters found the remains during commercial peat extraction; a year earlier the same bog had yielded a female skull (Lindow Woman) that briefly featured in a murder investigation. Lindow Man's body preserved skin, hair and several internal organs. He was a healthy man in his mid-twenties, about 1.68 m tall, with a trimmed beard and moustache and manicured nails suggesting he had not done heavy manual work.

Death

The body shows evidence of a violent death sometimes described as a ritual "triple death": he was struck on the head, strangled with a knotted cord (interpreted by some as a garrotte), and his throat was cut before he was placed face-down in the bog pool. His last meal included griddle cake made from wheat and barley, with traces of mistletoe pollen, which has fuelled theories of Druidic human sacrifice, though his manner of death and its interpretation remain debated. Radiocarbon dating places him in the Iron Age, around the time of the Roman conquest of Britain.
