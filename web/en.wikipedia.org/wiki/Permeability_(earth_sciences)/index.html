Permeability (earth sciences)

Permeability in earth sciences describes how readily a porous rock or sediment transmits fluids under a pressure gradient. Together with porosity — the fraction of void space — it determines whether a formation can store and deliver groundwater or hydrocarbons.

Controls

Grain size, sorting, packing, cementation, and fracturing all affect permeability. Coarse, well-sorted sands and gravels are highly permeable; shales and unfractured crystalline rocks are not. Permeability is often anisotropic, varying with direction, especially in layered sedimentary rocks and fractured media.

Applications

Hydrogeologists use permeability in Darcy's law to predict aquifer yields and contaminant migration. Petroleum geologists combine it with porosity to evaluate reservoir quality; typical reservoir rocks range from tens to thousands of millidarcies, while cap rocks that trap oil and gas must have very low permeability.
