Italian Committee of National Liberation

Partisans of the Italian resistance movement, coordinated by the CLN, in 1945.

The Committee of National Liberation (Italian: Comitato di Liberazione Nazionale, CLN) was a political umbrella organisation and the main representative body of the Italian resistance movement fighting against Nazi Germany's occupation of Italy and the collaborationist Italian Social Republic during the Second World War.

Formation

The CLN was founded in Rome on 9 September 1943, the day after the announcement of the Armistice of Cassibile, by six anti-fascist parties: the Communist Party, the Socialist Party, the Action Party, the Christian Democracy, the Labour Democratic Party, and the Liberal Party. Its first chairman was the elderly former prime minister Ivanoe Bonomi. A counterpart body, the CLNAI (Committee of National Liberation for Upper Italy), directed the resistance in the German-occupied north from Milan.

Role in the war and postwar transition

The CLN coordinated the partisan brigades, organised strikes and uprisings, and in April 1945 ordered the general insurrection that liberated Turin, Milan, and Genoa before Allied forces arrived. After the war, governments formed under CLN auspices governed Italy through the transition from monarchy to republic: Bonomi, Ferruccio Parri, and Alcide De Gasperi headed CLN-based cabinets until 1947, when the coalition broke up at the onset of the Cold War. The CLN formally dissolved that year, but its parties went on to write the Italian Constitution of 1948.
