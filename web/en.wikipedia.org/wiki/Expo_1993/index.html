Expo 1993, officially the Taejŏn International Exposition, was a specialised world exposition held in Daejeon, South Korea, from 7 August to 7 November 1993. Its theme was "The Challenge of a New Development Path", with sub-themes devoted to traditional and modern science and technology and the rational use of resources for sustainable development. It was the first world exposition held in a developing country and in South Korea.

Background

The exposition was sanctioned by the Bureau International des Expositions (BIE) and staged at the Expo Science Park near the Daedeok Science Town, South Korea's principal research and development district. The event was timed to showcase the country's rapid industrial and technological transformation in the decades since the Korean War, and to promote Daejeon as a centre of science.

The fair

A total of 108 countries and 33 international organisations participated. National and corporate pavilions presented displays on space technology, telecommunications, transport, energy and the environment. Landmark structures included the Hanbit Tower, a 93-metre observation tower whose design recalled the observatory of the Joseon-dynasty astronomer, and the Expo Bridge across the Gapcheon river. Over its 93 days the fair attracted approximately 14 million visitors, exceeding expectations.

Legacy

After the exposition closed, the site was retained as the Expo Science Park, South Korea's first science theme park. Much of the complex was later redeveloped; the Hanbit Tower and the Expo Bridge remain prominent landmarks of Daejeon. The event is remembered as a milestone in South Korea's emergence as a technologically advanced nation, preceding the country's hosting of the 2002 FIFA World Cup and the Expo 2012 in Yeosu.
