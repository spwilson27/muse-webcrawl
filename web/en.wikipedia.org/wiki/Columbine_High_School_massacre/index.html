The Columbine High School massacre was a school shooting and attempted bombing that occurred on April 20, 1999, at Columbine High School in Columbine, Colorado, United States. The perpetrators, twelfth-grade students Eric Harris and Dylan Klebold, murdered twelve students and one teacher, injured twenty-four others, and exchanged fire with police before dying by suicide in the school library.

Background

Harris and Klebold had planned the attack for well over a year, intending it primarily as a bombing: two propane bombs placed in the cafeteria were meant to kill hundreds, after which the pair would shoot fleeing survivors. The bombs failed to detonate, and the attack became a shooting. Both left extensive writings, recordings and journals expressing anger and violent fantasies, which investigators and journalists have studied extensively in debates over motive.

The attack

At approximately 11:19 a.m., the pair opened fire outside the school before entering and moving through the building. The deadliest phase occurred in the library, where ten students were killed. The perpetrators roamed the school, taunting victims and throwing small bombs, before returning to the library and taking their own lives around 12:08 p.m. SWAT teams entered the building later in the afternoon.

Aftermath and legacy

At the time, Columbine was the deadliest high school shooting in U.S. history. The massacre reshaped American school security — giving rise to lockdown drills, zero-tolerance policies and changed police tactics toward immediate engagement with active shooters. It also provoked intense debates over gun control, violent entertainment, bullying and mental health, and became a cultural reference point, with some later attackers citing it as an influence. The school library was demolished and rebuilt; a permanent memorial opened in Clement Park in 2007.
