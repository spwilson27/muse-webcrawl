The Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) is the research and development agency of the Ministry of Defence of the Government of India, responsible for the development of technology for use by the Indian Armed Forces. Headquartered in New Delhi, it operates a network of more than 50 laboratories across India working in fields including aeronautics, armaments, missiles, electronics, combat vehicles, naval systems and advanced computing.

History

DRDO was formed in 1958 by merging the Technical Development Establishments of the Indian Army and the Directorate of Technical Development and Production with the Defence Science Organisation. It grew substantially in the 1980s with the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme under A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, which produced the Prithvi, Agni, Akash, Trishul and Nag missiles.

Major programmes

Notable DRDO products and programmes include the Agni and Prithvi ballistic missile families, the BrahMos supersonic cruise missile (developed jointly with Russia), the Light Combat Aircraft Tejas, the Arjun main battle tank, the INSAS rifle system, airborne early warning systems, radars such as the Rajendra and Swordfish, and the multi-layered ballistic missile defence programme. DRDO has also developed technologies for nuclear, biological and chemical protection, life sciences and high-altitude agriculture for defence personnel. Its work is frequently criticized for delays and cost overruns, and reforms have periodically been proposed to restructure the organisation.
