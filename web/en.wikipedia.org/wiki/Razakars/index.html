Razakars

This article is about the Hyderabad militia. For the Bangladesh Liberation War collaborators, see Razakars (Pakistan).

The Razakars (Urdu for "volunteers") were a paramilitary force organised by Qasim Razvi, leader of the Majlis-e-Ittehadul Muslimeen, in the princely state of Hyderabad in the years before its accession to India. Their stated aim was to uphold the rule of the Nizam and resist Hyderabad's integration into the Indian Union.

Activities

By 1947–48 the Razakars numbered in the tens of thousands (estimates vary widely) and were accused of widespread violence against the state's Hindu majority and against pro-accession activists, particularly in rural districts. Their actions formed part of the justification for India's "police action", Operation Polo, in September 1948, which ended Hyderabad's independence; the Razakars were disbanded and Razvi imprisoned.

Later use of the term

During the Bangladesh Liberation War of 1971, the name "Razakar" was revived for an East Pakistani paramilitary force that collaborated with the Pakistan Army; in Bangladesh the word remains a potent term of abuse for traitors.
