Scouting BSA

Scouting BSA (formerly Boy Scouting) is the flagship program of Scouting America (formerly the Boy Scouts of America, BSA). Founded in 1910, it serves young people through outdoor adventure, leadership development, and character education. In February 2019 the program opened membership to girls, and its name was changed from Boy Scouting to Scouts BSA; in February 2025 the parent organisation itself was renamed Scouting America.

Program and structure

Members, called Scouts, are organised into troops sponsored by community organisations such as schools, churches and civic groups. Within a troop, Scouts form patrols of roughly eight to twelve members. Youth leadership is central: the senior patrol leader runs troop activities with guidance from adult Scoutmasters. Scouts are typically 11 to 17 years old (from fifth grade through age 18), having graduated from Cub Scouting or joined directly.

Advancement

Scouts progress through ranks — Scout, Tenderfoot, Second Class, First Class, Star, Life, and Eagle Scout — by demonstrating Scout skills, outdoor competence, citizenship, physical fitness, community service and leadership. The Eagle Scout rank, first awarded in 1912, is the program's highest achievement and requires, among other things, earning at least 21 merit badges and planning and leading a substantial service project. Merit badges, of which there are more than 130, cover subjects from first aid and camping to robotics and citizenship; a core set (including First Aid, Citizenship in the Community/Nation/World, Personal Fitness, and Environmental Science or Sustainability) is required for Eagle.

Methods and ideals

The program's stated methods include the patrol method, outdoor programs, advancement, adult association, personal growth, leadership development, and the uniform. Its ideals are expressed in the Scout Oath ("On my honor I will do my best to do my duty to God and my country...") and the twelve points of the Scout Law: trustworthy, loyal, helpful, friendly, courteous, kind, obedient, cheerful, thrifty, brave, clean and reverent. Signature activities include weekly troop meetings, monthly campouts, summer camp at council camps, and high-adventure bases such as Philmont Scout Ranch, Sea Base, Summit Bechtel Reserve and Northern Tier.

History

The American movement traces to William D. Boyce's 1910 incorporation of the BSA after encountering Scouting in London, drawing on Robert Baden-Powell's Scouting for Boys and the woodcraft of Ernest Thompson Seton and Daniel Carter Beard. Over the following century more than 130 million Americans participated. Membership declined from its 1970s peak amid social controversy and competition; notable policy changes included admitting openly gay youth (2013) and leaders (2015), transgender boys (2017), and girls (2018–2019). The organisation restructured through a bankruptcy process (2020–2023) arising from historical sexual abuse claims, emerging with a large survivor compensation trust and strengthened youth-protection requirements.
