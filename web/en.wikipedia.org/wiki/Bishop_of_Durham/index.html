The Bishop of Durham is the ordinary of the Church of England Diocese of Durham in the Province of York. The see is one of the oldest and most prestigious in England; the bishop ranks fourth in the Church of England hierarchy, after the Archbishops of Canterbury and York and the Bishop of London, and — with London and Winchester — is one of three bishops entitled to sit in the House of Lords regardless of seniority.

History

The see traces its origins to the Diocese of Lindisfarne, founded by Saint Aidan in 635. Driven out by Viking invasions, the community of St Cuthbert eventually settled at Durham in 995, and the bishop's seat was established there. From the Norman Conquest until the 19th century the bishops of Durham were Prince Bishops, wielding extensive temporal powers in the County Palatine of Durham — minting coinage, raising armies and administering justice — as a buffer against Scotland. Much of the palatinate authority was removed in 1836, and the palatinate courts were abolished in 1971.

The bishop's historic official residence is Auckland Castle in Bishop Auckland, and the cathedral church is Durham Cathedral, a UNESCO World Heritage Site containing the shrine of St Cuthbert. Famous bishops include Antony Bek, Cuthbert Tunstall, Joseph Barber Lightfoot, Brooke Foss Westcott, David Jenkins and Justin Welby, who served as Bishop of Durham (2011–2013) before becoming Archbishop of Canterbury. The bishop signs with the traditional signature "N. Dunelm", from the Latin Dunelmensis.
