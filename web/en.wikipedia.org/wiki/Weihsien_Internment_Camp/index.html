Weihsien Internment Camp

The Weihsien Internment Camp (officially the Weihsien Civil Assembly Centre) was a Japanese-run internment camp in Weihsien (now Weifang), Shandong, China, during the Second World War. From March 1943 to October 1945 it held more than 2,000 Allied civilians — missionaries, businesspeople, teachers, and children from schools such as the Chefoo School — in a former American Presbyterian mission compound.

Life in the camp

Internees organised their own schools, hospital, bakery, and committees despite severe crowding, poor sanitation, and food shortages. Notable internees included Olympic sprinter and missionary Eric Liddell, who died in the camp in February 1945, and theologian Langdon Gilkey, whose memoir Shantung Compound describes camp society.

Liberation

The camp was liberated in August 1945 by an American OSS rescue team (the "Duck Mission") parachuted in after Japan's surrender announcement; internees were repatriated over the following weeks.
