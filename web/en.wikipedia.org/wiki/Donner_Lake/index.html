Donner Lake

Donner Lake is a freshwater lake in the Sierra Nevada of northeastern California, near the town of Truckee in Nevada County. The lake is about 2.7 miles (4.3 km) long and 0.6 miles (1 km) wide, with a maximum depth of about 238 feet (73 m). It is named after the Donner Party, the group of emigrants who were trapped by snow near the lake in the winter of 1846–47.

Geography

Donner Lake sits in a glacial trough along the route of present-day Interstate 80, below Donner Pass. Donner Creek drains the lake eastward into the Truckee River. The north shore carries the old Lincoln Highway (Donner Pass Road) and the transcontinental railroad line, which crosses the lake's outlet near the original Central Pacific route over the Sierra.

History

In the winter of 1846–47, the ill-fated Donner Party camped at the east end of the lake (then called Truckee Lake), where many members died of starvation and exposure. Donner Memorial State Park, at the lake's eastern end, commemorates the pioneers with a museum and the tall Pioneer Monument, dedicated in 1918, whose pedestal height of 22 feet is said to mark the depth of the snow that winter.

Recreation

The lake is popular for boating, fishing (including lake trout and kokanee salmon), swimming and camping. The state park and the public piers along the north shore provide access, and the surrounding shoreline is lined with cabins and residences.
