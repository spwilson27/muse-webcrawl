Stardust (spacecraft)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stardust (spacecraft)
OperatorNASA / JPL
Launch7 February 1999
TargetsComet Wild 2; asteroid Annefrank; Comet Tempel 1
Sample return15 January 2006
Stardust at comet Wild 2 (artist's concept).

Stardust was a NASA Discovery-class spacecraft launched in 1999 to collect dust from the coma of comet Wild 2 and return it to Earth — the first sample ever brought back from beyond the Moon's orbit, and the first from a comet. It captured particles in tiles of aerogel, a ghost-light silica foam, as it flew through the comet's coma in January 2004.

The sample return capsule parachuted into the Utah desert in January 2006 at the fastest reentry speed of any human-made object at the time (about 46,400 km/h). The comet grains it delivered — including minerals that must have formed in the hot inner solar system — reshaped understanding of how comets mix materials from across the young solar nebula. The bus flew on as Stardust-NExT to re-image comet Tempel 1 in 2011.

Interstellar dust

Stardust also carried a collector exposed to the stream of interstellar dust flowing through the solar system; analysis later identified a small number of candidate interstellar grains among the aerogel tracks — painstaking work aided by a citizen-science project, Stardust@home, which enlisted volunteers to scan microscope imagery.
