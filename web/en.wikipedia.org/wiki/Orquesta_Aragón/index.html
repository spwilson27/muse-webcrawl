Orquesta Aragón

GenresDanzón, cha-cha-chá, charanga

Orquesta Aragón, Cuba's emblematic charanga francesa.

Orquesta Aragón is a Cuban musical band formed in 1939 by Orestes Aragón Cantero in Cienfuegos, Cuba. The band, a charanga francesa built around European classical violins and a French flute combined with an Afro-Cuban rhythm section, originally played danzones. Under the leadership of flautist and violinist Rafael Lay, Orquesta Aragón became one of the most popular and influential dance orchestras in Cuba, central to the development of the cha-cha-chá and the nueva onda style, with its strings playing tightly synchronised rhythmic riffs.

History

Founded as Rítmica 39 and renamed Orquesta Aragón the following year, the band moved to Havana in the 1940s, broadcasting regularly on radio and recording for RCA Victor. When founder Orestes Aragón fell ill in 1949, Rafael Lay took over direction and led the orchestra for more than three decades, until his death in a car accident in 1982; his son Rafael Lay Jr. later assumed leadership. The orchestra's signature sound combined soaring violins, Lay's virtuoso flute improvisations, and the vocal trio of Pepe Olmos, Joseíto Beltrán, and later Felo Bacallao and Ernesto Bacallao.

In the 1950s the Aragón helped turn the cha-cha-chá — created largely by Enrique Jorrín's Orquesta América — into an international craze, recording signature numbers such as "El Bodeguero" and "Los Marcianos." The band toured widely in Latin America, Africa, and Europe, and it remained a flagship of Cuban popular music after the revolution, continuing to record and tour into the twenty-first century under state sponsorship.

Legacy

Orquesta Aragón is widely regarded as the archetypal Cuban charanga, and its recordings are staples of Latin dance music. The orchestra received a Latin Grammy nomination for its album La Charanga Eterna and is often billed, simply, as "La Aragón" — the orchestra against which other charangas are measured.
