Coin

A coin is a small object, usually round and flat, mainly used as a medium of exchange or legal tender. Coins are standardized in weight and produced in large quantities at a mint to facilitate trade, and are most often issued by a government.

History

The first coins were struck in Lydia (in modern Turkey) around the 7th century BC from electrum, a natural alloy of gold and silver. Coinage spread through the Greek world, Rome — whose denarius became a standard for centuries — and independently in China, where cast bronze coins with square holes circulated for two millennia.

Modern coins

Modern coins are typically made of inexpensive alloys such as cupronickel, and frequently cost more to produce in low denominations than their face value. Milled edges, introduced to deter clipping and counterfeiting, remain a common feature.

Collecting

The study and collection of coins is called numismatics. Rare coins can command prices far above face value based on rarity, condition and historical significance.
