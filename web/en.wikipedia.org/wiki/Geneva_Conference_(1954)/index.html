Geneva Conference (1954)

Geneva Conference
Dates26 April – 21 July 1954
LocationPalais des Nations, Geneva
ParticipantsFrance, the Viet Minh, the USSR, China, the UK, the US, the State of Vietnam, Cambodia, Laos, and others
OutcomeGeneva Accords; partition of Vietnam

The Geneva Conference (April 26 – July 20, 1954) was a conference of major powers and involved states held in Geneva, Switzerland, to attempt to find a way to settle outstanding issues in Asia: the Korean War and, above all, the First Indochina War, then reaching its climax at the Battle of Dien Bien Phu.

Korean phase

The conference opened with discussions of Korea, involving the UN Command states, South Korea, North Korea and China. No agreement on unification was reached, and the Korean sessions ended in deadlock in June.

Indochina phase

Negotiations on Indochina began on 8 May 1954, the day after the French garrison at Dien Bien Phu fell to the Viet Minh. Delegations led by figures including China's Zhou Enlai, the USSR's Vyacheslav Molotov, Britain's Anthony Eden, France's Georges Bidault and then Pierre Mendès France, and the Viet Minh's Phạm Văn Đồng hammered out the settlement. Mendès France had staked his premiership on achieving a ceasefire by 20 July, and the Geneva Accords were concluded in the early hours of 21 July, temporarily dividing Vietnam at the 17th parallel and ending the First Indochina War.
