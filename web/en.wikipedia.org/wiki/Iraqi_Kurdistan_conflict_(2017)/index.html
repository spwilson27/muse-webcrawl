Iraqi Kurdistan conflict (2017)

Date: October 2017
Location: Northern Iraq
Result: Iraqi federal control restored over Kirkuk and disputed territories

The 2017 Iraqi Kurdistan conflict was a brief military confrontation between the federal government of Iraq and the Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG) that followed the Kurdish independence referendum of 25 September 2017, in which an overwhelming majority voted for independence from Iraq. Baghdad declared the referendum unconstitutional and demanded the KRG cede control of border crossings, airports, and the disputed territories.

Course of the conflict

On 15–16 October 2017, Iraqi security forces and allied Popular Mobilization Forces advanced on Kirkuk, a multi-ethnic, oil-rich city held by Kurdish Peshmerga since 2014. Amid divisions between the Kurdistan Democratic Party and the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan, Peshmerga units withdrew from much of the city, and federal forces took control of Kirkuk, its oil fields, and the surrounding areas. In the following days Iraq reasserted control over most of the disputed territories, including Sinjar, Makhmour, and areas near Mosul, with only limited clashes.

Aftermath

The loss of Kirkuk's oil revenue was a severe economic blow to the KRG. President Masoud Barzani resigned effective 1 November 2017, and the independence bid was frozen as Erbil entered negotiations with Baghdad. Casualties on both sides were reported in the dozens.
