Bush tucker

Bush tucker, also called bush food, is any food native to Australia and historically eaten by Aboriginal Australians and Torres Strait Islander peoples, including fruits, seeds, nuts, greens, and animals such as kangaroo, emu and witchetty grubs. It also describes any native fauna or flora used for culinary or medicinal purposes, regardless of the continent.

Examples

Well-known bush foods include the quandong, wattleseed, macadamia, lemon myrtle, finger lime, Kakadu plum (notable for extremely high vitamin C content), bush tomato, muntries, and the honey of native stingless bees (sugarbag). Many traditional foods require preparation such as leaching or roasting to remove toxins, as with cycad seeds and some yams.

Modern use

Interest in bush foods revived from the 1980s, and native ingredients increasingly appear in Australian restaurants and commercial products, raising questions about Indigenous ownership and benefit-sharing from traditional knowledge.
