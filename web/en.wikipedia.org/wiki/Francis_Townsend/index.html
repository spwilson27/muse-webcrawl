Francis Everett Townsend (13 January 1867 – 1 September 1960) was an American physician and political activist who, during the Great Depression, proposed the Townsend Plan — a popular old-age pension scheme that mobilised millions of elderly Americans and helped spur the creation of the United States Social Security system.

The Townsend Plan

Townsend was a retired country doctor in Long Beach, California, when, in September 1933, he published his plan in a letter to a local newspaper. The Townsend Plan proposed that the federal government pay every retired person over sixty a pension of $200 per month — at the time roughly a working wage — on condition that they retire from work and spend the entire amount within thirty days. The pensions would be financed by a 2% national sales (transactions) tax. Townsend argued the plan would end the Depression by putting purchasing power in circulation, opening jobs for younger workers, and ending old-age poverty at a stroke.

Movement and influence

The proposal spawned a mass movement: by 1935 there were some 7,000 Townsend Clubs, millions of members, and petitions bearing millions of signatures delivered to Congress. Economists overwhelmingly judged the plan unworkable — $200 a month for every senior would have consumed about half the national income, and the tax could not have raised it — but its political weight was undeniable. Together with Huey Long's Share Our Wealth campaign, Townsendism formed a pincer of populist pressure on President Franklin D. Roosevelt, and historians credit it with accelerating the Social Security Act of 1935, whose old-age insurance was far more modest than Townsend demanded. Townsend denounced the Act as inadequate, backed the Union Party ticket in 1936, and was later convicted of contempt of Congress (a conviction for which Roosevelt pardoned him in 1937) after walking out of a hostile committee hearing. His movement dwindled as Social Security payments began, but Townsend campaigned for his plan until his death in Los Angeles in 1960, aged 93.
