The Algorithmic Beauty of Plants

Authors: Przemysław Prusinkiewicz, Aristid Lindenmayer

Published: 1990

Publisher: Springer-Verlag

Subject: Computer graphics, plant modelling

The Algorithmic Beauty of Plants is a 1990 book by Polish computer scientist Przemysław Prusinkiewicz and Hungarian biologist Aristid Lindenmayer. It presents the mathematical modelling and computer visualisation of plant development using L-systems (Lindenmayer systems), the rewriting grammars Lindenmayer had introduced in 1968 to model the growth of algae and plants.

Contents

The book develops L-systems step by step: from simple string-rewriting models of filamentous organisms, through bracketed L-systems that generate branching structures interpreted by a "turtle" in the LOGO sense, to parametric and context-sensitive systems capable of modelling flowers, inflorescences, leaves and whole plants. Further chapters treat the modelling of plant organs, the generation of realistic images, phyllotaxis (the arrangement of leaves and florets, related to the golden angle), and the use of fractals and iterated function systems in plant-like structures.

Influence

The book is a landmark of computer graphics and procedural modelling: its strikingly realistic simulated plants demonstrate that complex biological forms can arise from compact formal rules. It has been widely cited in research on virtual plants, computer-generated imagery for film and games, and mathematical biology, and it popularised L-systems well beyond their original biological context. A companion volume, The Algorithmic Beauty of Seaweeds, Sponges and Corals (2001), extended the approach to marine organisms.
