Hubert Lyautey

Louis Hubert Gonzalve Lyautey (17 November 1854 – 21 July 1934) was a French Army general and colonial administrator who served as the first Resident General of the French protectorate in Morocco from 1912 to 1925. He was made a Marshal of France in 1921 and elected to the Académie française in 1912.

Early career

Born in Nancy into a military family, Lyautey graduated from Saint-Cyr and served in Algeria before postings to Indochina under Joseph Gallieni, whose methods of "peaceful penetration" — combining military presence with markets, schools and administration — profoundly shaped his own. In Madagascar he put those methods into practice as Gallieni's subordinate, and his 1900 essay on the social role of the officer (Du rôle social de l'officier) made his reputation as a thinker as well as a soldier.

Morocco

As Resident General, Lyautey directed the French consolidation of Morocco while professing respect for Moroccan institutions: he ruled through Sultan Moulay Youssef, preserved the old medinas, and built new European-style cities beside them under the town planning of Henri Prost. He promoted ports, railways and the port of Casablanca, and staged colonial exhibitions and fairs such as that at Casablanca in 1915. During the First World War he briefly served as Minister of War (1916–17). The Rif War under Abd el-Krim from 1921 onward strained the protectorate; Lyautey was replaced by Marshal Pétain in 1925 and retired to France. He organised the great Colonial Exhibition of 1931 in Paris and died at Thorey in 1934; in 1961 his remains were transferred to Les Invalides.
