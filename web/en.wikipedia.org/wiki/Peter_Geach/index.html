Peter Thomas Geach (March 29, 1916 – December 21, 2013) was a British philosopher who worked in the fields of logic, metaphysics and the philosophy of religion. He was Professor of Logic at the University of Leeds from 1966 until his retirement in 1981 and a Fellow of the British Academy.

Life and work

Geach studied at Balliol College, Oxford, where he met his wife, the philosopher Elizabeth Anscombe; both became converts to Roman Catholicism, and both were influenced by Ludwig Wittgenstein. Geach produced the first English translation, with Max Black, of selections from the writings of Gottlob Frege, helping to establish Frege's reputation in the English-speaking world. His own books include Mental Acts (1957), Three Philosophers (1961, with Anscombe), Reference and Generality (1962), God and the Soul (1969) and Logic Matters (1972).

Contributions

Geach is known for the "Frege point" in moral philosophy — the argument that a predicate must have the same meaning in asserted and unasserted contexts, deployed against expressivist theories of ethics — and for his critique of the identity of indiscernibles, his defence of relative identity, and his work on Aquinas. He also lent his name to the "Geach–Kaplan sentence", a sentence used in a celebrated debate about quantification and "some" statements. He was awarded a papal knighthood for his services to philosophy and the Catholic Church.
