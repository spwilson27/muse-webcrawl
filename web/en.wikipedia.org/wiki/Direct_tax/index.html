Direct tax

A direct tax is a tax imposed directly on individuals or organisations, as opposed to an indirect tax imposed on a transaction. In principle, a direct tax cannot be shifted to another party: the person or entity liable for the tax pays it directly to the government. Income tax, corporation tax, capital gains tax, inheritance tax and property taxes are standard examples.

United States constitutional usage

In United States constitutional law the term has a specific technical meaning deriving from the requirement that "direct taxes" be apportioned among the states by population. In Hylton v. United States (1796) the Supreme Court treated the category narrowly, and in Pollock v. Farmers' Loan & Trust Co. (1895) it held taxes on income from property to be direct taxes requiring apportionment. The Sixteenth Amendment (1913) removed that requirement for income taxes, authorising Congress to tax incomes "without apportionment among the several States".

Economics

Economists debate the true incidence of nominally direct taxes — corporation tax, for instance, may in practice be partly borne by workers or consumers. Direct taxes are generally considered easier to make progressive than indirect taxes and form the core of most modern revenue systems.
