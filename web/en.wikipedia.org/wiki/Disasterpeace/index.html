Richard Vreeland (born June 29, 1986), better known by his stage name Disasterpeace, is an American composer and electronic musician. He is best known for composing the soundtracks to the video game Fez and the horror film It Follows.

Career

Vreeland grew up in Staten Island, New York, and began releasing music as Disasterpeace in the mid-2000s, drawing on the chiptune scene and video game music. His early albums and the score to the puzzle-platformer Fez (2012) brought him wide attention: the Fez soundtrack, built from ambient synth textures and chiptune-derived sounds, was widely praised and became one of the best-known indie game scores of its era.

Director David Robert Mitchell, having heard the Fez music, hired Vreeland to score the horror film It Follows (2014). The score's dissonant, pulsing synthesizer writing was singled out by critics as central to the film's dread and became highly influential on subsequent horror scoring. Vreeland went on to compose for Mitchell's Under the Silver Lake (2018) and for the action film Triple Frontier (2019), while continuing to work in games, including Hyper Light Drifter (2016) and Mini Metro.

Style

His music blends chiptune timbres and arpeggiated synth patterns with minimalist composition and, in his film work, abrasive noise and clusters influenced by twentieth-century concert music. He frequently uses constrained software tools, including tracker-style workflows and the music application he helped develop, January.
