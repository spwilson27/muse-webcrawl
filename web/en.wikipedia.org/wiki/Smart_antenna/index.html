Smart antenna

Smart antennas (also known as adaptive array antennas, digital antenna arrays, multiple antennas and, recently, MIMO) are antenna arrays with smart signal processing algorithms used to identify spatial signal signatures such as the direction of arrival (DOA) of the signal, and use them to calculate beamforming vectors which are used to track and locate the antenna beam on the mobile or target. Smart antennas are widely used in wireless communication systems, radar, and radio astronomy.

Techniques

Smart antenna systems are commonly divided into two broad classes:

  • Switched beam systems have a finite set of fixed, predefined patterns or beams. The system detects signal strength and selects the beam best aligned with the user.
  • Adaptive array systems adjust the weights applied to each element in near real time, steering a main lobe toward the desired signal and placing nulls toward interference. Algorithms such as LMS (least mean squares) and RLS (recursive least squares) are used to update the weights.

Advantages

By concentrating radiated energy and rejecting interference spatially, smart antennas increase the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio, extend range, and improve the capacity of cellular networks: the same frequency can be reused at shorter distances, an arrangement called spatial division multiple access (SDMA). Multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems extend the idea by using spatially separated antennas at both ends of the link to multiply throughput, and form the basis of beamforming in 4G LTE and 5G NR base stations.

Applications

Smart antennas are used in cellular base stations, Wi-Fi access points, satellite communications, radar tracking, electronic warfare and radio astronomy arrays. Massive MIMO, in which a base station uses tens or hundreds of elements, is a key enabling technology of 5G.
