U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) is a federal law enforcement agency under the U.S. Department of Homeland Security. Its mission includes enforcing immigration laws, investigating transnational crime, and preventing terrorism.

History

ICE was created in 2003 under the Homeland Security Act of 2002, combining the investigative and interior enforcement functions of the former Immigration and Naturalization Service and the criminal investigation arm of the U.S. Customs Service.

Directorates

The agency's two main operational directorates are Enforcement and Removal Operations (ERO), responsible for identifying, arresting and removing noncitizens subject to removal, and Homeland Security Investigations (HSI), the department's principal investigative arm, which targets smuggling, human trafficking, financial crime, and export violations.

Controversy

ICE has been a recurring focus of political debate over immigration enforcement, particularly regarding deportations, detention practices and family separations; the agency's detention system is among the largest in the world, and it operates with an annual budget of roughly $8–9 billion and more than 20,000 personnel.
