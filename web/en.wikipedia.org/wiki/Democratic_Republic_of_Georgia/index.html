The Democratic Republic of Georgia (DRG) was the first modern establishment of a republic of Georgia, existing from May 1918 to February 1921. Recognized by many European states, it was ruled by the Georgian Social Democratic Party (Mensheviks) and formed a multi-party parliamentary system.

History

The republic was proclaimed on 26 May 1918 following the collapse of the Russian Empire and the dissolution of the Transcaucasian Democratic Federative Republic. Its first prime minister was Noe Ramishvili, succeeded by Noe Zhordania. The DRG bordered the Russian SFSR to the north, the Ottoman Empire and the Republic of Armenia to the south, and the Azerbaijan Democratic Republic to the southeast.

The republic faced border disputes and wars with Armenia, incursions by Denikin's White forces, and internal unrest. In May 1920 it signed a treaty with Soviet Russia recognizing its independence and borders. Nevertheless, in February 1921 the Red Army invaded, and after the fall of Tbilisi on 25 February 1921 the republic collapsed; the Georgian Soviet Socialist Republic was proclaimed.

Legacy

The DRG is remembered for its democratic constitution of 1921, universal suffrage including women (among the first countries to grant it), land reform, and cultural development. The modern Republic of Georgia regards it as its predecessor state, and 26 May is celebrated as Independence Day. The government-in-exile continued in France until 1933.
