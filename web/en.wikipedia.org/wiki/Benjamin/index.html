Benjamin

Benjamin (Hebrew: Binyamin, "son of the right hand" or "son of the south") was the twelfth and youngest son of the patriarch Jacob and the second and last son of Rachel in the Hebrew Bible. He is the founder of the Israelite Tribe of Benjamin. Unlike his brothers, Benjamin was born in Canaan rather than Mesopotamia.

Biblical narrative

According to Genesis, Rachel died giving birth to Benjamin near Bethlehem; with her last breath she named him Ben-Oni ("son of my sorrow"), but Jacob renamed him Benjamin. During the famine narrative, Joseph, then a ruler in Egypt, demanded that Benjamin be brought to him, tested his brothers' loyalty by planting a silver cup in Benjamin's sack, and then revealed himself. Jacob's blessing in Genesis 49 compares Benjamin to a ravenous wolf.

Tribe of Benjamin

Benjamin's descendants formed one of the twelve tribes of Israel, allotted a small territory north of Judah, including Jericho and part of Jerusalem's vicinity. The tribe features prominently in the Book of Judges, where a civil war nearly destroyed it, and it later produced Israel's first king, Saul, as well as, in the New Testament's account, the apostle Paul.

Name usage

Benjamin has been a longstanding given name in Jewish and Christian cultures, with bearers including Benjamin Franklin and numerous heads of government. It also appears in the phrase "Benjamin's mess", an especially large helping of food, reflecting the fivefold portion Benjamin received at Joseph's table.
