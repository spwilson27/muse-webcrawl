Martial arts

Martial arts are codified systems and traditions of combat practised for a number of reasons, such as self-defence, military and law enforcement applications, competition, physical and mental development, entertainment, and the preservation of a nation's intangible cultural heritage. The term derives from Latin ars Martialis, "the arts of Mars", the Roman god of war.

Varieties

Martial arts may be divided into armed and unarmed systems, and by technique into striking arts (such as karate, taekwondo, and boxing), grappling arts (such as judo, wrestling, and Brazilian jiu-jitsu), and weapon-based arts (such as kendo, fencing, and archery). Many systems, including the Chinese martial arts collectively called kung fu or wushu, combine all three.

History

Formalised fighting systems arose in every warrior culture. In Asia, traditions were shaped by Indian, Chinese, and Japanese influences; Shaolin kung fu, Japanese budo, and Korean arts each developed distinctive philosophies emphasising discipline and self-cultivation alongside combat. In the 20th century, martial arts spread worldwide, were codified into sports with rules and federations, and were popularised by film stars such as Bruce Lee and Jackie Chan.

Modern practice

Today millions train for fitness, sport, and self-defence. Mixed martial arts (MMA) competitions, beginning with the UFC in 1993, test techniques across disciplines under a unified ruleset. Several martial arts, including judo, taekwondo, karate, and wrestling, are Olympic sports.
