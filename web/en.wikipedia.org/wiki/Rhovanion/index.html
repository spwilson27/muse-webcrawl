Rhovanion

Rhovanion, also called the Wilderland, is a large region in J. R. R. Tolkien’s fictional world of Middle-earth. It lies east of the Misty Mountains and west of the Sea of Rhûn, encompassing Mirkwood, the upper Anduin valley, the plains around the River Running and lands extending toward the Iron Hills.

Setting Middle-earth
Major places Mirkwood, Lothlórien’s eastern approaches, the Lonely Mountain, Dale, Lake-town, Beorn’s country and the Gladden Fields
Peoples Wood-elves, Northmen, Dwarves, Hobbit ancestors and Easterlings

Geography

The Great River Anduin flows south through western Rhovanion. Mirkwood dominates the centre, once called Greenwood the Great before Sauron’s presence at Dol Guldur darkened it. North-east of the forest stand Erebor, the Lonely Mountain, and the human town of Dale; farther east the River Running leads from Lake-town toward Dorwinion and Rhûn.

History

Ancient Elves crossed or settled parts of the region during their westward journeys, and the ancestors of the Hobbits once lived in the Anduin valley. During the Third Age, Northmen founded kingdoms allied with Gondor, most notably the Kingdom of Rhovanion. Its prince’s marriage into Gondor’s royal line influenced later claims and alliances. Plague, invasion by the Wainriders and internal pressures eventually destroyed that kingdom, though descendant cultures persisted in Dale, Rohan and elsewhere.

In the narratives

Much of The Hobbit crosses Rhovanion, from Beorn’s house through Mirkwood to Lake-town and Erebor. In The Lord of the Rings, the Fellowship passes its western edge, and later events include the battles around Dale and Erebor during the War of the Ring. After Sauron’s fall, Thranduil’s realm and the renewed kingdoms of Dale and Erebor brought recovery to the region.

“Wilderland” is used especially in The Hobbit and can denote the untamed country beyond the edge of the Wild, closely overlapping Rhovanion but shaped by narrative perspective.
