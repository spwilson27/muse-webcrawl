Banditaccia Necropolis

LocationCerveteri, Lazio, Italy
CultureEtruscan
Period9th–3rd centuries BCE
DesignatedUNESCO World Heritage Site (2004), with Tarquinia

The Banditaccia Necropolis (Italian: Necropoli della Banditaccia) is an ancient Etruscan burial ground at Cerveteri, northwest of Rome, Italy. Covering about 400 hectares, of which some 10 hectares form the protected visitor area, it contains thousands of tombs used from the Villanovan period (9th century BCE) into the later Etruscan era. In 2004 it was inscribed, together with the Monterozzi necropolis at Tarquinia, on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the "Etruscan Necropolises of Cerveteri and Tarquinia".

Tombs

The necropolis is laid out like a city, with streets and neighbourhoods of tombs. Many are covered by large earth tumuli, while others are cut into the rock in rows of cube-shaped "dice" tombs. The interiors imitate Etruscan houses, with carved ceilings, beams, columns, beds and household objects. Notable tombs include the Tomb of the Shields and Chairs, the Tomb of the Reliefs — whose walls are decorated with painted stucco reliefs of weapons and household goods — and the Tomb of the Five Chairs.

Significance

Cerveteri (Etruscan Caisra) was one of the wealthiest cities of Etruria, and the Banditaccia tombs preserve the most extensive surviving evidence of Etruscan domestic architecture and burial customs. Grave goods from the site, including bucchero pottery, gold jewellery and the famous Sarcophagus of the Spouses, are held in the Museo Nazionale Etrusco at Villa Giulia in Rome and the Louvre in Paris.
