Oahu

Oahu (Hawaiian: Oʻahu), known as "The Gathering Place", is the third-largest of the Hawaiian Islands and the most populous island in the U.S. state of Hawaii. It is home to roughly two-thirds of Hawaii's population and to the state capital and largest city, Honolulu.

Geography

Oahu is 44 miles (71 km) long and 30 miles (48 km) across. Like the other Hawaiian islands, it was formed by volcanic activity; its landscape is dominated by two parallel mountain ranges, the Waiʻanae Range in the west and the Koʻolau Range in the east, the eroded remains of two shield volcanoes. Between them lies a broad central valley and the Pearl Harbor embayment on the south shore. The island's highest point is Mount Kaʻala in the Waiʻanae Range.

The southeastern shore is indented by the famous tuff cone of Diamond Head (Lēʻahi), which overlooks Waikīkī Beach. Other notable features include Hanauma Bay, a marine sanctuary in a flooded volcanic crater; the North Shore, renowned for big-wave surfing at places such as Waimea Bay and the Banzai Pipeline; and the windward coast around Kāneʻohe Bay.

History

Polynesian voyagers settled Oahu roughly a thousand years ago. In 1795 the island was conquered by Kamehameha I at the Battle of Nuʻuanu, a decisive step in the unification of the Hawaiian Kingdom. Honolulu, with its protected harbor, grew into the kingdom's capital and a major Pacific port serving whalers and traders.

On 7 December 1941, the naval base at Pearl Harbor and other military installations on Oahu were attacked by Imperial Japanese forces, bringing the United States into the Second World War. The USS Arizona Memorial now marks the site.

Economy and culture

Tourism, defense, and agriculture are the pillars of Oahu's economy. Waikīkī draws millions of visitors annually, while Joint Base Pearl Harbor–Hickam and other installations make the U.S. military one of the island's largest employers. Landmarks include ʻIolani Palace, the only royal palace on American soil, the Bishop Museum, and the Polynesian Cultural Center.
