Ampex Corporation is an American electronics company founded in 1944 by Alexander M. Poniatoff as a manufacturer of electric motors; its name is an acronym of its founder's initials and "excellence" (Alexander M. Poniatoff Excellence). Ampex pioneered commercial magnetic tape recording in the United States.

Tape recording

After World War II, Ampex engineer Harold Lindsay and singer Bing Crosby's technical director Jack Mullin adapted captured German Magnetophon technology into the Ampex Model 200, introduced in 1948 — the first commercially successful American tape recorder. Crosby invested in the company and adopted tape for his network radio shows, allowing broadcasts to be recorded and edited. Multitrack Ampex machines, including the "Sel-Sync" recorders used by Les Paul, transformed music production.

Video

In 1956 Ampex introduced the VRX-1000, the first practical videotape recorder, using the quadruplex format with a rotating four-head assembly. It revolutionized television by enabling high-quality recording and time-zone delay of broadcasts, and earned Ampex an Emmy Award and an Academy Award. Later products included helical-scan recorders that influenced the VCR, and digital data recorders. Ampex's recording businesses declined with the digital transition, though the Ampex name continued in data-storage ventures.
