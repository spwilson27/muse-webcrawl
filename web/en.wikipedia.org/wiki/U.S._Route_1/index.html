U.S. Route 1

U.S. Route 1 (US 1) is a major north–south United States Numbered Highway that serves the East Coast of the United States. It runs 2,370 miles (3,813 km) from Key West, Florida, north to Fort Kent, Maine, at the Canadian border, making it the longest north–south road in the United States.

Length2,370 mi (3,813 km)

North endFort Kent, Maine (Canadian border)
Formed1926

Route description

US 1 generally parallels the Atlantic coastline and Interstate 95 through Florida, Georgia, the Carolinas, Virginia, the District of Columbia, Maryland, Delaware, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, New York, Connecticut, Rhode Island, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, and Maine. It passes through many of the largest cities of the East Coast, including Miami, Washington, D.C., Baltimore, Philadelphia, New York City, and Boston.

History

The route was created in 1926 as part of the original U.S. Numbered Highway System, assembled from a collection of earlier auto trails and state roads, including parts of the Dixie Highway and the Atlantic Highway. Its southern terminus was extended to Key West in 1938, crossing the Overseas Highway. Large portions of its long-distance traffic were later taken over by Interstate 95, but US 1 remains a vital local and regional arterial.
