Sleigh

A sleigh is a land vehicle that slides across a surface, usually ice or snow, supported on smooth runners rather than wheels. Sleighs are typically drawn by horses, reindeer, or dogs, though human- and motor-powered versions exist. The word comes from the Dutch slee, and the terms sleigh, sled, and sledge are used with regional variation: in North America a sleigh is usually a horse-drawn passenger vehicle, while a sled is smaller and often used for sport or by children.

Design and use

A traditional sleigh has a pair of long runners shod with steel, an open body with bench seating, and shafts for a horse in harness; bells on the harness warned other travellers on silent snowy roads — a custom recalled in the song "Jingle Bells," which celebrates sleigh racing in a one-horse open sleigh. Before paved roads and automobiles, sleighs were essential winter transport in Scandinavia, Russia (where the three-horse troika became emblematic), Canada, and the northern United States.

Cultural associations

Sleighs are strongly associated with Christmas through Santa Claus, who in legend travels the world in a reindeer-drawn sleigh. Horse-drawn sleigh rides remain popular tourist attractions in winter resorts, and dog sleds continue in use for travel and racing in Arctic regions. In sport, the luge, bobsleigh ("bobsled"), and skeleton are specialized racing sleighs on ice tracks.
