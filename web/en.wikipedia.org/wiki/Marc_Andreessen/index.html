Marc Andreessen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Marc Andreessen
Born9 July 1971, Cedar Falls, Iowa, US
Known forCo-creating Mosaic; co-founding Netscape and Andreessen Horowitz
[Image: Portrait photograph of Marc Andreessen, a tall bald man in a dark shirt, speaking at a technology conference]

Marc Lowell Andreessen (born 1971) is an American businessman and software engineer. In 1993, as an undergraduate staff member at the National Center for Supercomputing Applications (NCSA) at the University of Illinois, he co-authored Mosaic — with Eric Bina, the first widely used graphical web browser — the program that introduced images inline in web pages and brought the World Wide Web to the general public.

After graduating he moved to California and co-founded Netscape Communications with Jim Clark, leading the development of Netscape Navigator. Netscape's 1995 IPO made him, at 24, the public face of the Internet generation (a barefoot appearance on the cover of Time followed). After AOL acquired Netscape, he served as its chief technology officer, then co-founded Loudcloud/Opsware (sold to HP for about $1.6 billion in 2007), co-created the Ning platform, and in 2009 co-founded the venture capital firm Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) with Ben Horowitz, which became one of Silicon Valley's most influential firms.

Andreessen is also known as an essayist: his 2011 'Software Is Eating the World' is among the most quoted technology essays, and his 2023 'Techno-Optimist Manifesto' argued for unapologetic technological acceleration — positions that keep him a central, and contested, figure in debates about the industry's direction.
