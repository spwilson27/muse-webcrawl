Aerospace

[Image: Aerospace spans aircraft and spacecraft engineering]

Overview

Aerospace is the human effort in science, engineering and business to fly in the atmosphere of Earth (aeronautics) and surrounding space (astronautics). Aerospace organisations design, manufacture, operate and maintain aircraft, spacecraft, missiles, satellites and launch vehicles. The term, coined in the late 1950s from "aero" + "space", embraces both civil and defence industries.

Industry

The aerospace industry is dominated by large primes — Boeing and Airbus in airliners; Lockheed Martin, RTX and Northrop Grumman in defence; SpaceX and United Launch Alliance in launch — supplied by engine makers (GE Aerospace, Rolls-Royce, Safran) and vast component chains, and regulated by bodies such as the FAA and EASA. Research agencies (NASA, ESA, JAXA, ISRO, CNSA) drive technology. Employment is concentrated in the US, Europe, China, Russia, Canada, Brazil (Embraer) and Japan. The sector's products range from airliners and fighter jets to weather and communications satellites and interplanetary probes; unmanned aerial vehicles and commercial human spaceflight are its fastest-changing segments.
