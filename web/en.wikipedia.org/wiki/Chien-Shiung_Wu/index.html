Chien-Shiung Wu (Chinese: 吳健雄; 31 May 1912 – 16 February 1997) was a Chinese-American particle physicist and experimental physicist who made significant contributions in the fields of nuclear and particle physics. Wu worked on the Manhattan Project, where she helped develop the process for separating uranium into uranium-235 and uranium-238 isotopes by gaseous diffusion.

The Wu experiment

Wu is best known for conducting the Wu experiment (1956–57), which proved that parity is not conserved in weak interactions, contradicting the law of conservation of parity. Her collaborators Tsung-Dao Lee and Chen-Ning Yang, who had proposed the experiment, won the 1957 Nobel Prize in Physics for the theory work, while Wu's exclusion from the prize has been widely cited as an injustice; she received the inaugural Wolf Prize in Physics in 1978 in recognition of the work.

Later life

Wu spent most of her career at Columbia University, where she became a full professor in 1958 and the first Michael I. Pupin Professor of Physics in 1973. She was a fellow of the National Academy of Sciences and served as president of the American Physical Society in 1975. She died in New York in 1997.
