The New York City Housing Authority (NYCHA) is a public development corporation that provides public housing in New York City. Founded in 1934 during the administration of Mayor Fiorello La Guardia, it is the largest public housing authority in North America. NYCHA administers conventional public housing in more than 330 developments across the five boroughs — including Queensbridge Houses, the largest public housing development in the United States — and administers the city's Section 8 housing voucher program. Altogether, roughly one in sixteen New Yorkers lives in NYCHA housing or uses a NYCHA-administered voucher.

History

NYCHA's first development, First Houses on the Lower East Side, opened in 1935. Construction expanded rapidly under the influence of Robert Moses and federal housing programs; landmark projects include East Harlem's East River Houses and the vast Queensbridge and Red Hook developments of 1939–1940. Building continued through the postwar decades, producing the characteristic red-brick towers-in-the-park that house generations of working-class New Yorkers. From the 1970s onward, federal disinvestment, fiscal crises, and aging infrastructure produced a growing capital-needs backlog estimated in the tens of billions of dollars.

Challenges and oversight

In the 2010s NYCHA faced scandals over lead paint inspections, heating and hot-water outages, and mold, culminating in a 2018 federal complaint and a 2019 agreement between the city, the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, and federal prosecutors that imposed a federal monitor. Programs such as PACT (Permanent Affordability Commitment Together), which converts developments to Section 8 funding with private management partners, have been advanced to fund repairs.
