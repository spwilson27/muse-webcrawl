Walter Weston

The Reverend Walter Weston (25 December 1861 – 27 March 1940) was an English clergyman and mountaineer. A missionary of the Church Missionary Society in Japan, he is best remembered for popularising mountaineering in Japan and for introducing the Japanese Alps to the Western world through his book Mountaineering and Exploration in the Japanese Alps (1896). He is often called the "father of mountaineering in Japan."

Life in Japan

Weston was educated at Derby School and Clare College, Cambridge, and was ordained in the Church of England. He went to Japan in 1888 as a missionary of the Church Missionary Society, working in Nagasaki, Kumamoto and later Kobe. During three periods of residence in Japan (1888–1895, 1902–1905 and 1911–1915), he travelled widely in the Japanese Alps, climbing peaks including Mount Yari, Mount Hotaka and Mount Fuji, often accompanied by Japanese guides and porters.

Mountaineering and writings

Before Weston's arrival, mountain climbing in Japan was largely a religious or official practice rather than a sport. Weston promoted recreational climbing, gave lectures, and in 1896 published Mountaineering and Exploration in the Japanese Alps, which introduced the term "Japanese Alps" — his name for the Hida, Kiso and Akaishi mountain ranges — to English readers. He later wrote The Playground of the Far East (1918) and Japan (1926). Weston was a fellow of the Royal Geographical Society and a member of the Alpine Club; in 1937 he became an honorary member of the Japanese Alpine Club, which he had helped inspire in 1905.

Legacy

A memorial plaque to Weston stands in the Kamikōchi valley, a gateway to the Northern Alps, and a Weston Festival is held there each June. His advocacy helped establish mountaineering as a popular recreation in Japan and spread international awareness of the Japanese mountains.
