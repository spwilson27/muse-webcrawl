Unsolved problems in computer science

This article surveys major unsolved problems in computer science: questions in theoretical computer science, algorithms, cryptography, and related fields that have resisted solution despite sustained research effort. Some are famous open conjectures with monetary prizes attached; others are engineering and systems challenges where no fully satisfactory solution is known.

Complexity theory

  • P versus NP — whether every problem whose solution can be verified in polynomial time can also be solved in polynomial time. One of the seven Millennium Prize Problems, carrying a US$1,000,000 prize from the Clay Mathematics Institute.
  • P versus PSPACE, NP versus co-NP, and the relationships among the classes BPP, NC, and others remain unresolved.
  • Unique games conjecture — a conjecture about the hardness of approximation with major implications for optimisation problems.
  • Exponential time hypothesis and fine-grained complexity questions about the exact running times of basic algorithms.
  • Graph isomorphism — whether the problem admits a polynomial-time algorithm; the best known algorithm runs in quasi-polynomial time.

Algorithms and data structures

  • Whether integer factorisation can be done in polynomial time on a classical computer.
  • The optimal complexity of matrix multiplication; the exponent ω is known to be below 2.373 but its exact value is open.
  • Dynamic graph problems, including fully dynamic connectivity and shortest paths with optimal update times.

Cryptography and security

  • Proving the existence of one-way functions, which would imply P ≠ NP.
  • Constructing fully satisfactory obfuscation for general programs (indistinguishability obfuscation).
  • Achieving practical, provably secure systems against adaptive adversaries, and post-quantum cryptographic standards for all use cases.

Distributed systems and other areas

  • Tight bounds for Byzantine agreement and consensus under various synchrony assumptions.
  • The verification of large concurrent and machine-learning systems.
  • Open questions in quantum computing, including the exact power of quantum complexity classes such as QMA.
