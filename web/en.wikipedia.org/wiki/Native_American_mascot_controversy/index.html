The Native American mascot controversy concerns the use of names, images and stereotypes of Native American people and culture by sports teams, schools and brands in the United States and elsewhere. Native American organizations have campaigned against such mascots since the 1960s, arguing they are disrespectful, perpetuate stereotypes and harm Indigenous youth.

Background

The National Congress of American Indians launched its campaign against derogatory mascots in 1968. Psychological research, including studies cited by the American Psychological Association in a 2005 resolution, found that Indigenous mascots harm the self-esteem and development of Native students and foster prejudice among non-Native students.

Changes in professional sports

After decades of protest, Washington's NFL team retired its "Redskins" name in 2020, becoming the Commanders in 2022, and Cleveland's baseball team replaced "Indians" with "Guardians" after the 2021 season and retired its Chief Wahoo logo. Some teams, such as the Kansas City Chiefs and Atlanta Braves, have retained their names while modifying rituals and imagery.

Schools and policy

The NCAA's 2005 policy restricted "hostile or abusive" mascots in postseason play, prompting many universities to change names or gain tribal consent to keep them. Several states, including Maine, Colorado and Washington, have enacted laws prohibiting Indigenous mascots in public schools, with exceptions for tribal partnerships.
