In monotheistic belief systems, God is usually viewed as the supreme being, creator, and principal object of faith. God is typically conceived as being omnipotent, omniscient, omnipresent, and omnibenevolent, as well as having an eternal and necessary existence. God is most often held to be incorporeal, with related conceptions of transcendence or immanence.

In theism, God is the creator and sustainer of the universe, while in deism, God is the creator, but not the sustainer, of the universe. In pantheism, God is the universe itself. Atheism rejects the belief in any God, while agnosticism holds that the existence of God is unknown or unknowable.

Etymology and usage

The earliest written form of the Germanic word God comes from the 6th-century Christian Codex Argenteus. The English word itself is derived from the Proto-Germanic gudą. The reconstructed Proto-Indo-European form ǵhu-tó-m was likely based on the root ǵhau(ə)-, which meant either "to call" or "to invoke".

Conceptions of God

Conceptions of God vary widely among religions and cultures. Monotheists hold that there is only one God, and may give him characteristics such as those described in the Hebrew Bible and other scriptures. In Christianity, God is held to be a Trinity of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. In Islam, God (Allah) is strictly one and indivisible. Judaism affirms one God, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

Existence of God

The existence of God is a subject of debate in the philosophy of religion. A wide variety of arguments for and against the existence of God can be categorized as logical, empirical, metaphysical, subjective, or scientific. Philosophical positions include theism, atheism, agnosticism, deism, pantheism, and panentheism.

Approaches to God

Approaches to God include prayer, meditation, ritual, and mystical experience. Many traditions hold that God can be known through revelation as recorded in sacred texts, or through reason and observation of the natural world, an approach known as natural theology.
