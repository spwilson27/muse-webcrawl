Purmerend

Purmerend (Dutch: [ˌpʏrməˈrɛnt]) is a city and municipality in the province of North Holland in the Netherlands. It is surrounded by the polders Beemster, Purmer, and Wormer, and lies about 15 kilometres north of Amsterdam. The municipality has a population of roughly 80,000, the great majority in the city itself.

History

Purmerend began as a fishing and trading settlement between the lakes Purmer, Beemster, and Wormer. It received city rights in 1410 from Count William VI of Holland. After the surrounding lakes were drained in the early 17th century — the Beemster in 1612 and the Purmer in 1622 among them — the town became an important market centre for the rich new agricultural land, handling cheese, cattle, and other produce at its Koemarkt (cattle market).

For much of its history Purmerend remained a modest market town of a few thousand inhabitants. In the 1960s it was designated a growth centre for Amsterdam's overflow population, and new districts such as Overwhere, De Gors, and Purmer-Noord expanded it rapidly. Purmer-Zuid, the newest district, was built from the 1990s onward.

Transport and economy

The city has three railway stations — Purmerend, Purmerend Weidevenne, and Purmerend Overwhere — on the line from Amsterdam to Hoorn and Enkhuizen, and bus connections throughout the Waterland region. The historic livestock markets have largely ended, and most residents now work in services locally or commute to Amsterdam.
