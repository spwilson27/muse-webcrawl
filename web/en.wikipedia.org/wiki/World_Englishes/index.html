World Englishes

World Englishes
Key figureBraj Kachru
Key modelThree Circles of English (1985)
JournalWorld Englishes (founded 1982 as World Language English)

World Englishes is a term for the emerging localised forms of the English language used in territories influenced by British and American colonisation and in the subsequent spread of English as a global lingua franca. Study of World Englishes examines how English has been indigenised in different societies, producing varieties such as Indian English, Nigerian English and Singapore English, each with distinctive phonology, vocabulary and grammar.

Kachru's Three Circles

The linguist Braj Kachru proposed the influential Three Circles model (1985). The Inner Circle comprises countries where English is the primary native language (e.g. the United Kingdom, United States, Australia). The Outer Circle comprises postcolonial countries where English holds institutional status (e.g. India, Nigeria, Singapore). The Expanding Circle comprises countries where English is learned as a foreign language (e.g. China, Japan, Russia).

Debates

Scholars debate the status of local norms: whether Outer Circle varieties should be recognised as legitimate standards in their own right, and how English language teaching should treat the diversity of world Englishes. The related field of English as a lingua franca (ELF) studies communication between non-native speakers.
