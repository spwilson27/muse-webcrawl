Larry Laudan

Larry Laudan (October 16, 1941 – August 23, 2022) was an American philosopher of science and epistemologist. He is best known for his work on scientific rationality and theory change, notably the books Progress and Its Problems (1977) and Science and Values (1984).

Work

In Progress and Its Problems, Laudan proposed an account of scientific progress in terms of problem-solving effectiveness: a theory is progressive if its research tradition solves more and weightier empirical problems than its rivals, replacing earlier notions of truth-approximation in the debates following Kuhn and Lakatos. He is particularly famous for the pessimistic meta-induction (or "pessimistic induction"), advanced in his 1981 paper "A Confutation of Convergent Realism": since many once-successful past scientific theories contained terms (such as ether, phlogiston and caloric) that we now take to refer to nothing, we have inductive grounds to expect that our current theories will likewise prove false — an argument that became a central challenge to scientific realism.

Laudan taught at the University of Pittsburgh, Virginia Tech, the University of Hawaii, and the National Autonomous University of Mexico, among others. Later he wrote extensively on legal epistemology, including Truth, Error, and Criminal Law (2006), arguing that much of the legal system is structured to avoid error types asymmetrically.
