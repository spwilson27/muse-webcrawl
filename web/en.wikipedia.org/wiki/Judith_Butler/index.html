Judith Butler (born 1956) is an American philosopher and theorist at the University of California, Berkeley, whose work on gender, power, language and violence has made them one of the most influential and debated thinkers in the humanities. Their Gender Trouble: Feminism and the Subversion of Identity (1990) introduced the theory of gender performativity and became a founding text of queer theory, reshaping feminist philosophy and the study of sex and gender worldwide.

Gender Trouble and performativity

Drawing on Beauvoir, Foucault, speech-act theory and psychoanalysis, Butler argued that gender is not the expression of an inner essence but is performed — constituted by the stylised repetition of acts under social constraint — and that even "sex" is apprehended through gendered discourse. The drag performance, in their analysis, exposes the imitative structure of all gender. The book's dense prose and radical conclusions drew both acclaim and criticism (including a famous 1999 "bad writing" prize and defences of their style); the clarifications in Bodies That Matter (1993) stressed that performativity is not theatrical choice but constrained citation of norms — a distinction popular summaries often miss. Butler, who uses they/them pronouns, extended the argument in Undoing Gender (2004) and Who's Afraid of Gender? (2024), the latter analysing the global political movements mobilised against "gender ideology".

Later work

After 2001 Butler turned to war, precarity and ethics: Precarious Life (2004) and Frames of War (2009) ask which lives are publicly grievable; Notes Toward a Performative Theory of Assembly (2015) theorises protest; The Force of Nonviolence (2020) defends an egalitarian nonviolence. Their writings on Israel/Palestine, including Parting Ways (2012), have been a further, sharp site of controversy. Prizes and honours include the Adorno Prize (2012) and election to national academies; their lectures draw large crowds and, occasionally, protests, and their ideas remain a live battleground in cultural and political debate.
