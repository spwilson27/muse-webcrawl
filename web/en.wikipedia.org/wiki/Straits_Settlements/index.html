Straits Settlements

Existed: 1826–1946
Capital: Singapore
Components: Singapore, Penang, Malacca (later Labuan, Christmas Island, Cocos)

The Straits Settlements were a group of British territories on the Malay Peninsula and surrounding islands, established in 1826 under the East India Company and becoming a Crown Colony in 1867.

Composition

The core settlements were Penang (founded 1786), Singapore (founded by Stamford Raffles in 1819), and Malacca (acquired from the Dutch in 1824). Dindings, Labuan, Christmas Island, and the Cocos (Keeling) Islands were added at various times.

Dissolution

After the Japanese occupation (1942–1945), the Settlements were dissolved in 1946: Singapore became a separate Crown Colony (later the Republic of Singapore), while Penang and Malacca joined the Malayan Union, a predecessor of Malaysia.
