Central Europe

Central Europe is a geographical and cultural region of Europe situated between Eastern and Western Europe, whose boundaries are variously defined. Countries most commonly considered part of it include Austria, Croatia, the Czech Republic, Germany, Hungary, Poland, Slovakia, Slovenia and Switzerland, with parts of neighbouring states sometimes included. The region has a population of roughly 180 million.

Definitions

Definitions of Central Europe vary with geography, history and politics. German conceptions of Mitteleuropa, Anglo-American geopolitical usage, and the statistical schemes of the United Nations (which place Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary and Slovenia in differing subregions) do not coincide. Common threads in most definitions are the historical lands of the Holy Roman Empire and the Habsburg monarchy, Catholic and Protestant cultural legacies, and, after 1945, the Iron Curtain's division of the region between East and West.

History

The region was shaped by the Holy Roman Empire, the Habsburg and Ottoman frontier, the partitions of Poland, and Prussian and Austrian rivalry within the German Confederation. After the First World War it was reorganised into nation states; after the Second, much of it fell within the Soviet sphere as the Eastern Bloc. The Revolutions of 1989 brought democratic transitions and reintegration with Western institutions: Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary and Slovenia joined the European Union in 2004 (Croatia in 2013), and the Visegrád Group (Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary) coordinates regional policy.

Geography and culture

Central Europe spans the North European Plain, the Bohemian and Pannonian basins, and the Alps and Carpathians; the Danube is its defining river. Its cities — Vienna, Prague, Budapest, Warsaw, Berlin, Kraków — are noted for Gothic, Baroque and Habsburg-era architecture, a coffeehouse culture, and classical music traditions associated with composers from Mozart and Beethoven to Chopin, Liszt and Dvořák.
