Proto-Balto-Slavic language

RegionEastern Europe
Erac. 2500–1500 BC (disputed)
ParentProto-Indo-European
DescendantsBaltic languages, Slavic languages

Proto-Balto-Slavic (PBS) is the reconstructed proto-language which is the common ancestor of the Baltic and Slavic languages, themselves descendants of Proto-Indo-European. Although no records survive, its features are reconstructed by the comparative method applied to Lithuanian, Latvian, Old Prussian and the Slavic languages.

Evidence for the grouping

Baltic and Slavic share numerous exclusive innovations: a common definitive adjective formation, the genitive singular of o-stems in -ā, a shared vocabulary stratum, satem development of the Indo-European palatovelars, and similar accentual developments. A majority of scholars therefore accept a period of common development, while a minority view the parallels as the result of prolonged later contact between separate branches.

Features

Proto-Balto-Slavic is reconstructed with the Winter's law lengthening of vowels before plain voiced stops, the loss of syllabic resonants' distinctiveness (yielding ir/ur/il/ul groups), a free but mobile pitch accent related to the Hirt's law retraction, and a largely conservative nominal system with seven cases. It later split into Proto-Baltic and Proto-Slavic's ancestor, Proto-Slavic.

Homeland

The speech community is generally placed in the forest zone of north-eastern Europe, often associated with the middle Dnieper and the area between the Vistula and the upper Volga, in contact with early Finnic speakers — a contact reflected in loanwords shared with Proto-Finnic.
