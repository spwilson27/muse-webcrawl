The Flying Burrito Brothers are an American country rock band formed in Los Angeles in 1968 by Gram Parsons and Chris Hillman, both formerly of the Byrds. They are widely credited with founding the country rock genre and exerting a lasting influence on alternative country.

History

The band's debut album, The Gilded Palace of Sin (1969), fused traditional country music with rock, soul, and psychedelia — Parsons' self-described "Cosmic American Music" — and featured the band in ornate Nudie suits. The follow-up, Burrito Deluxe (1970), included the first released recording of the Jagger–Richards song "Wild Horses." Parsons left in 1970 and died in 1973; Hillman departed the following year, and various line-ups continued under the Burrito name for decades.

Legacy

Though commercially modest in their original run, the Burritos' albums became touchstones for later artists including the Eagles and generations of alt-country musicians, and The Gilded Palace of Sin is regularly listed among the greatest albums of its era.
