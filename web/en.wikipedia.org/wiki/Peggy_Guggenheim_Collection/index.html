Location: Palazzo Venier dei Leoni, Dorsoduro, Venice, Italy
Type: Art museum
Founded: 1980 (opened to the public)
Administered by: Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation

Peggy Guggenheim Collection

The Peggy Guggenheim Collection is a museum of modern art on the Grand Canal in Venice, Italy. It is one of the most visited attractions in Venice and houses the personal art collection of the American heiress and patron Peggy Guggenheim (1898–1979), who lived in the palazzo for the last thirty years of her life.

The palazzo

The museum occupies the Palazzo Venier dei Leoni, an 18th-century palace of white Istrian stone that, unusually for the Grand Canal, was built only one storey high. Peggy Guggenheim purchased the palazzo in 1949 and opened her collection to the public there during the summers from 1951. She is buried in the museum's sculpture garden alongside her beloved Lhasa Apso dogs.

The collection

The collection is one of the most important assemblages of early 20th-century European and American modern art in Italy. It includes major works of Cubism, Futurism, Surrealism, and Abstract Expressionism by artists such as Pablo Picasso, Salvador Dalí, René Magritte, Joan Miró, Paul Klee, Constantin Brâncuși, and Jackson Pollock, whom Guggenheim championed early in his career. The museum also displays the Nasher Sculpture Garden and hosts temporary exhibitions.

Administration

Since Guggenheim's death in 1979 the museum has been administered by the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation, which also runs the Guggenheim Museum in New York.
