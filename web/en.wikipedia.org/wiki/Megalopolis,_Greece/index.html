Megalopolis (Greek: Μεγαλόπολη, "Great City") is a town in the southwestern part of the regional unit of Arcadia in the Peloponnese, Greece. It is situated in a wide plain at about 430 metres above sea level, on the banks of the Alpheios river. The modern town stands near the site of the ancient city of Megalopolis, founded in the 4th century BC, and is today known chiefly for its lignite-fired power station, which for decades was one of the main sources of electricity in Greece.

Ancient city

The ancient Megalopolis was founded between 371 and 368 BC by the Theban general Epaminondas as the federal capital of the Arcadian League, created to counterbalance the power of Sparta after the Battle of Leuctra. Inhabitants from some forty surrounding villages and towns were relocated (synoecised) into the new city, which was laid out on both banks of the river Helisson. Megalopolis possessed the largest theatre in ancient Greece, seating about 20,000 spectators, adjacent to the Thersilion, the assembly hall of the Arcadian League's council of ten thousand. The city was the birthplace of the historian Polybius and of Philopoemen, the celebrated last strategos of the Achaean League.

The city declined after the Roman conquest of Greece and was largely deserted by the time the travel writer Pausanias visited it in the 2nd century AD, describing it as mostly in ruins. Excavations have uncovered remains of the theatre, the Thersilion, sanctuaries of Zeus Soter and other public buildings.

Modern town

The modern town grew up around the village of Sinanou and was renamed Megalopolis in the 19th century. Its recent history is dominated by lignite (brown coal) mining and the Public Power Corporation's power stations, commissioned from the 1960s onwards, which transformed the local economy and landscape. The units have been progressively retired as Greece phases out lignite generation. On 13 December 1969, eleven people were killed when a Fairchild aircraft crashed near the town during a storm.

Geography

Megalopolis lies in a basin surrounded by the mountains of Mainalo to the northeast, Taygetus to the southwest and Lykaion to the west. The Alpheios, the longest river of the Peloponnese, flows past the town. The Greek National Road 7 (Tripoli–Kalamata) passes to the east, and the A7 motorway connects the town with Tripoli and Corinth.
