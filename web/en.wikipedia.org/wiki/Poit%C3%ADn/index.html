Poitín

Poitín
Type: Spirit
Country of origin: Ireland
Alcohol by volume: traditionally 40–90%

Poitín (Irish pronunciation: [ˈpˠətʲiːnʲ]), anglicised as poteen or potcheen, is a traditional Irish distilled beverage. Historically produced illicitly in small pot stills — from which it takes its name, pota meaning "pot" — it was traditionally made from malted barley, grain, potatoes, or whey, and could reach very high strengths. It is one of the strongest spirits in the world when made traditionally.

History

Poitín has been distilled in Ireland since at least the early medieval period. The Crown made the private distillation of spirits illegal in 1661, driving production underground for more than three centuries and giving poitín a central place in Irish folklore and song. In 1997 the Irish Revenue Commissioners ended their long opposition, and poitín was granted protected geographical indication (GI) status by the European Union in 2008, allowing legal commercial production under defined standards.

Production

Traditional poitín was distilled in remote locations, often signalled by smoke from the still. Modern legal producers use pot stills and a variety of bases, including barley, potatoes, and apples, and typically bottle at lower strengths than the illicit original.
