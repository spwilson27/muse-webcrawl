Mundell–Fleming model

The Mundell–Fleming model, also known as the IS-LM-BoP model, is an economic model first set out by Robert Mundell and Marcus Fleming in the 1960s. It extends the closed-economy IS–LM model to a small open economy, adding a balance-of-payments (BoP) condition that describes equilibrium in international trade and capital flows.

Implications

The model's best-known result is the impossible trinity (or trilemma): a country cannot simultaneously maintain a fixed exchange rate, free capital movement and an independent monetary policy; it must choose two of the three. Under a floating exchange rate with perfect capital mobility, monetary policy is highly effective (it works through exchange-rate depreciation) while fiscal policy is impotent. Under a fixed exchange rate the conclusions reverse: fiscal policy is effective while monetary policy is entirely absorbed by the need to defend the peg.

Influence

The model remains a standard textbook framework for analysing open-economy macroeconomic policy and exchange-rate regimes, and Mundell's work on it contributed to his Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 1999.
