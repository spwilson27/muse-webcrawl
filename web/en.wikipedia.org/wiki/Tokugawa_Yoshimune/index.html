Tokugawa Yoshimune (November 27, 1684 – July 12, 1751) was the eighth shōgun of the Tokugawa shogunate of Japan, ruling from 1716 until his abdication in 1745. He is considered one of the dynasty's ablest rulers, famed for the Kyōhō Reforms.

Rise to power

Yoshimune was the fourth son of Tokugawa Mitsusada, daimyō of Kii Domain, and governed Kii successfully before being chosen to succeed the childless Ietsugu over the main line, a succession that stirred controversy.

Kyōhō Reforms

Faced with fiscal crisis caused by the shogunate's rice-based income, Yoshimune cut expenditure, raised land productivity, encouraged new crops such as sweet potato, reformed the rice market, and set up the Metsuke and suggestion box (meyasubako) to hear commoners' grievances. He also relaxed the ban on importing foreign books, opening the way for Dutch learning (Rangaku).

Legacy

Nicknamed the "Rice Shōgun" (Kome Shōgun), Yoshimune restored the shogunate's finances for a generation and became a popular hero of period drama, notably the long-running television series Abarenbō Shōgun.
