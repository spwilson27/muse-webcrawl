Formula E

Formula E
Category: Single-seater electric racing
Country: International
Inaugural season: 2014–15
Sanctioning body: FIA

Formula E, officially the ABB FIA Formula E World Championship, is a single-seater motorsport championship for electric cars. Conceived in 2011 by FIA president Jean Todt and businessman Alejandro Agag, the series held its first race in Beijing in September 2014. It races mainly on temporary street circuits in major cities, aiming to promote electric mobility and serve as a testbed for EV technology. In 2020–21 it became the first single-seater series besides Formula One to receive FIA world championship status.

The cars

All teams use a common chassis and battery, with manufacturers developing their own powertrains (motor, inverter, gearbox, and software). The first-generation Spark-Renault SRT_01E (2014–2018) had limited range, so drivers swapped cars mid-race. The Gen2 car (2018–2022) doubled usable energy, ending car swaps. The Gen3 car, introduced for the 2022–23 season, is lighter and more powerful, with a front powertrain used for regenerative braking and combined regeneration of up to 600 kW; a further evolution, Gen3 Evo, followed in 2024–25.

Race format

Events ("ePrix") are usually one-day affairs on street circuits, with races lasting a set number of laps plus additional laps for interruptions. Distinctive features include Attack Mode, in which drivers arm a period of higher power by driving through an activation zone off the racing line, and (in earlier seasons) FanBoost, a fan vote granting extra power. Energy management is central: drivers must finish within their allocated energy, making lift-and-coast and regenerative braking key skills.

Champions

The first drivers' champion was Nelson Piquet Jr. (2014–15), followed by Sébastien Buemi, Lucas di Grassi, Jean-Éric Vergne (twice), António Félix da Costa, Nyck de Vries, Stoffel Vandoorne, Jake Dennis, and Pascal Wehrlein. Notable teams have included Audi, DS Techeetah, Jaguar, Porsche, Nissan, and Mahindra.
