Trouton's rule

Trouton's rule is an empirical regularity in physical chemistry stating that the entropy of vaporisation of most liquids at their normal boiling points is approximately constant, around 85–88 J/(mol·K). In other words, the molar enthalpy of vaporisation is roughly proportional to the absolute boiling temperature. The rule was formulated by Frederick Thomas Trouton in 1884.

QuantityEntropy of vaporisation ΔSvap
Typical value≈ 85–88 J/(mol·K)
Formulated1884, by Frederick Trouton

Rationale and exceptions

The rule works because vaporisation converts a disordered liquid into a much more disordered gas, and the increase in disorder is similar for many substances at their boiling points, where the vapour has a comparable molar volume. Liquids with hydrogen bonding, such as water and ethanol, deviate positively (≈ 109 J/(mol·K) for water) because their liquids are unusually ordered; very light molecules such as hydrogen and helium deviate negatively.
