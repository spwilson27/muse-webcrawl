Chiefs of Staff Committee

The Chiefs of Staff Committee is a committee of the British Armed Forces, consisting of the professional heads of the Royal Navy, the British Army, the Royal Air Force and Strategic Command. It is responsible for providing military advice to the British government and is chaired by the Chief of the Defence Staff.

Membership

The committee comprises the Chief of the Defence Staff (chair), the Vice-Chief of the Defence Staff, the First Sea Lord and Chief of the Naval Staff, the Chief of the General Staff, the Chief of the Air Staff, and the Commander of Strategic Command (formerly Joint Forces Command). The Permanent Under-Secretary of the Ministry of Defence may also attend.

History

The committee was established in 1923 as a subcommittee of the Committee of Imperial Defence, reflecting the need for coordinated advice among the three services between the world wars. It played a central role in British strategy during the Second World War, working alongside the War Cabinet. In 1959 the position of Chief of the Defence Staff was created as its chairman, and subsequent reforms — notably the creation of the Ministry of Defence in 1964, the Levene reforms, and the formation of Joint Forces Command (later Strategic Command) in 2012 — reshaped its membership and role.
