Marian Adam Rejewski (16 August 1905 – 13 February 1980) was a Polish mathematician and cryptologist who, in late 1932, reconstructed the Nazi German military Enigma cipher machine and developed techniques for defeating it. His work with Jerzy Różycki and Henryk Zygalski at the Polish Cipher Bureau laid the foundations for the Allied Ultra codebreaking effort at Bletchley Park during the Second World War.

Breaking the Enigma

Rejewski studied mathematics at the University of Poznań and attended a secret cryptology course run by the Polish General Staff. Working for the Cipher Bureau in Warsaw, he applied permutation theory to the Enigma — an approach that treated the cipher as a problem in pure mathematics. Using intelligence material supplied by French intelligence (derived from the German agent Hans-Thilo Schmidt), he deduced the internal wiring of the military Enigma's rotors in December 1932, the first successful attack on the machine.

Methods and machines

Rejewski and his colleagues devised a succession of aids: the cyclometer, the card catalogue of rotor characteristics, Zygalski's perforated sheets, and the electromechanical bomba kryptologiczna ("cryptologic bomb") of 1938, which automated the search for daily settings. In July 1939, at a meeting in the Kabaty Woods near Warsaw, Poland shared its Enigma knowledge and replica machines with Britain and France, weeks before the German invasion.

Later life

After the invasion Rejewski worked in France and, following hazardous clandestine work, reached Britain in 1943, where he worked on other German ciphers but was kept from Bletchley Park's Enigma work. He returned to Poland in 1946, worked in industry, and only late in life was able to write about his achievements; the full Polish role in breaking Enigma became public in the 1970s. He died in Warsaw in 1980 and has since been widely honoured in Poland and internationally. See also Enigma machine, Bletchley Park, and Ultra.
