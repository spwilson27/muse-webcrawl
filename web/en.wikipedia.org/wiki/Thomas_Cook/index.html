Thomas Cook

Thomas Cook
Born: 22 November 1808, Melbourne, Derbyshire, England
Died: 18 July 1892, Leicester, England
Known for: Founder of the Thomas Cook travel agency

Thomas Cook (22 November 1808 – 18 July 1892) was an English businessman and founder of the travel agency Thomas Cook & Son, which became one of the world's best-known travel brands. He is regarded as the inventor of modern mass tourism.

The first excursion

Cook, a Baptist preacher and temperance campaigner, organised his first railway excursion on 5 July 1841: a trip from Leicester to a temperance rally in Loughborough, carrying about 500 passengers for a shilling each. Encouraged by its success, he arranged further excursions and in 1845 ran his first commercial tour, to Liverpool, complete with a printed handbook — a forerunner of the modern holiday brochure.

Growth of the business

Cook expanded into tours of Scotland, then continental Europe. He organised travel to the Great Exhibition of 1851 in London and the Paris Exhibition of 1855. In 1865 he opened a London office, and his son John Mason Cook became a partner, bringing strong commercial management. The firm pioneered many fixtures of modern travel: hotel coupons, traveller's cheques ("Cook's Circular Notes"), escorted round-the-world tours (the first in 1872–73), and cruises on the Nile. Cook himself escorted his first party to Egypt and the Holy Land in 1869.

Later years and legacy

Cook gradually withdrew from the business in the late 1870s, leaving it to his son. He died in Leicester in 1892. The company he founded passed through family ownership, railway nationalisation, and private hands; the UK business collapsed in September 2019, but the name was relaunched as an online travel agency by the Chinese group Fosun, which had acquired the brand.
