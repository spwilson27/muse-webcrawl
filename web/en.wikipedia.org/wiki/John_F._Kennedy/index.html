John Fitzgerald Kennedy (May 29, 1917 – November 22, 1963), often referred to by his initials JFK, was an American politician who served as the 35th president of the United States from 1961 until his assassination in 1963. The second son of Joseph P. Kennedy Sr., he represented Massachusetts in Congress from 1947 to 1960 before narrowly defeating Richard Nixon in the 1960 presidential election. At 43, he was the youngest person elected president and the first Roman Catholic to hold the office.

Early life and war service

Kennedy graduated from Harvard in 1940 and joined the U.S. Navy, commanding the patrol torpedo boat PT-109 in the Pacific. When the boat was rammed and sunk by a Japanese destroyer in 1943, Kennedy's actions to save his crew made him a war hero. His political rise was rapid: elected to the House in 1946 and the Senate in 1952, he won a Pulitzer Prize for Profiles in Courage (1956) and married Jacqueline Bouvier in 1953.

Presidency

Kennedy's "New Frontier" administration was defined by Cold War crises: the failed Bay of Pigs invasion (1961), the Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962, which brought the world close to nuclear war before a negotiated settlement, and the building of the Berlin Wall. He established the Peace Corps, escalated U.S. involvement in Vietnam, pressed for civil rights legislation, and set the goal of landing a man on the Moon by the end of the decade. His inaugural address — "Ask not what your country can do for you — ask what you can do for your country" — became one of the century's most quoted lines, and his glamorous White House, nicknamed "Camelot," made him a lasting cultural icon.

Assassination and legacy

Kennedy was shot and killed in Dallas, Texas, on November 22, 1963, while riding in a motorcade with his wife and Texas Governor John Connally. Lee Harvey Oswald was arrested for the murder but was himself shot by Jack Ruby two days later; the Warren Commission concluded Oswald acted alone, a finding that has remained controversial. Vice President Lyndon B. Johnson succeeded him. Kennedy is remembered as a symbol of youth and optimism, buried at Arlington National Cemetery beneath an eternal flame; his death set in motion the chain of events that led, a decade later, to United States v. Nixon and the Ethics in Government Act.
