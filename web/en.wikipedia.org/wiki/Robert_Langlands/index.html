Robert Langlands

Robert Phelan Langlands (born October 6, 1936) is a Canadian mathematician. He is best known as the founder of the Langlands program, a vast web of conjectures and theorems that link number theory and representation theory, as well as for his work on L-functions and the general theory of automorphic forms. He is professor emeritus at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, New Jersey. In 2018 he received the Abel Prize "for his visionary program connecting representation theory to number theory."

Biography

Langlands was born in New Westminster, British Columbia, and studied at the University of British Columbia before taking his PhD at Yale in 1960 on Lie groups. After posts at Princeton, Yale and Berkeley, and a formative year in Turkey learning Turkish, he joined the Institute for Advanced Study in 1972.

The Langlands program

In January 1967 Langlands wrote a famous seventeen-page letter to André Weil setting out sweeping conjectures: that motivic Galois representations correspond to automorphic forms, governed by L-groups, and that all L-functions of number theory are automorphic L-functions ("functoriality"). The resulting program has driven decades of research; partial cases underlie Andrew Wiles's proof of Fermat's Last Theorem and Laurent Lafforgue's Fields Medal work. Langlands himself proved base change for GL(2) and, with Maria José Soares, developed the theory of Eisenstein series, and later proposed an extension "beyond endoscopy".

Honours

He shared the Wolf Prize in Mathematics in 1995/96, won the inaugural Shaw Prize in 2007 with Richard Taylor, and received the Abel Prize in 2018.
