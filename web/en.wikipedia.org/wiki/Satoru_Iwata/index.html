Satoru Iwata

Born: December 6, 1959, Sapporo, Japan
Died: July 11, 2015 (aged 55), Kyoto, Japan
Occupation: Video game programmer, producer, businessman
Known for: President and CEO of Nintendo (2002–2015)

Satoru Iwata, fourth president of Nintendo.

Satoru Iwata (岩田 聡, December 6, 1959 – July 11, 2015) was a Japanese video game programmer, designer and businessman who served as the fourth president and chief executive officer of Nintendo from 2002 until his death in 2015. He was the first Nintendo president not related by blood or marriage to the company's founding Yamauchi family.

Career

Iwata began his career at HAL Laboratory, where he worked on games in the Kirby series and helped rescue the company from the brink of bankruptcy as its president in the 1990s. As a programmer he contributed to titles such as EarthBound and the Pokémon series, famously reprogramming Pokémon Gold and Silver to include the Kanto region and optimising the battle code of Pokémon Stadium.

As Nintendo's president he oversaw the launch of the Nintendo DS and the Wii, consoles that greatly expanded the video game audience, and later the Nintendo 3DS and Wii U. He was widely known to the public through the "Iwata Asks" interview series and his appearances in Nintendo Direct presentations. In 2015 he announced Nintendo's entry into mobile games and the development of the console codenamed NX, released after his death as the Nintendo Switch.

Death and legacy

Iwata died on July 11, 2015, of complications from a bile duct growth. He was succeeded by Tatsumi Kimishima. Tributes across the video game industry remembered his philosophy that "video games are meant to be just one thing: fun for everyone," and his self-description as a game developer at heart.
