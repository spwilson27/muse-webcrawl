Neutron cross section

The neutron cross section measures the probability that a neutron will interact with an atomic nucleus, expressed as an effective area, usually in barns (1 barn = 10−28 m2). Cross sections are defined for specific reactions — scattering, capture, fission and others — and depend strongly on neutron energy and target nuclide.

Energy dependence

In the low-energy (thermal) region many absorption cross sections vary inversely with neutron velocity (the 1/v law). At intermediate energies, sharp resonance peaks appear where neutrons are captured into compound-nucleus energy levels; at high energies the cross section varies more smoothly.

Importance

Accurate cross-section data are essential for nuclear reactor design, radiation shielding, dosimetry and astrophysics. Evaluated libraries such as ENDF compile experimental and theoretical cross sections for all stable and many unstable nuclides.
