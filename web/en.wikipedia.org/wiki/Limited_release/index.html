Limited release

Limited release is a film distribution strategy of releasing a new film in a small number of theaters in major markets, usually in the largest cities of a country, before expanding more widely. A limited release is planned and executed carefully, because a film's performance in its opening venues — measured by per-theater average gross — determines whether distributors expand it to more screens. It contrasts with a wide release, which opens simultaneously on thousands of screens.

Purpose

Limited releases are typically used for independent, foreign-language and art films whose appeal is uncertain or niche. Strong reviews, festival acclaim and word of mouth during the limited run build demand and press coverage before a wider rollout. A platform release is a related strategy in which the film opens in very few theaters and expands in stages, riding awards-season momentum.

Qualifying runs

In the United States, a limited run in Los Angeles County and New York City before the end of the calendar year is also used to qualify films for Academy Awards consideration, a practice behind many late-December openings of prestige films.

Other media

The term is also used for limited releases of albums, books, and video games sold initially through selected outlets or in small print runs, and for software distributed to a restricted group of users before general availability.
