Messerschmitt Bf 109

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A Messerschmitt Bf 109 fighter in flight with Luftwaffe markings]
A Bf 109 in flight.

The Messerschmitt Bf 109 is a German Second World War single-seat fighter aircraft designed by Willy Messerschmitt and Robert Lusser. First flown in 1935 and blooded in the Spanish Civil War, it was the Luftwaffe\'s backbone throughout the war: more than 34,000 were built — the most-produced fighter in history — and the highest-scoring aces of all time (Erich Hartmann, 352 victories) flew it. Advanced for 1935 with an all-metal monocoque, enclosed cockpit and inverted V12 engine, it duelled the Spitfire through the Battle of Britain and evolved through E, F, G and K models.

Design

Automatic leading-edge slats, a narrow-track outward-retracting undercarriage (a source of many takeoff/landing accidents) and a compact airframe around the Daimler-Benz DB 601/605 defined the type. Armament centred on engine-mounted cannon firing through the spinner in later marks, plus wing guns in some variants.

Service

Bf 109s served with Germany and its allies and neutrals (Finland, Hungary, Romania, Croatia, Switzerland, Spain — whose Hispano \'Buchón\' versions, re-engined with Merlins, flew into the 1960s and starred in the 1969 film Battle of Britain). Captured and post-war versions (Avia S-199) even equipped the young Israeli Air Force in 1948.

Legacy

Survivors fly with the RAF\'s Battle of Britain Memorial Flight opponents at airshows and in museums worldwide. The Bf 109\'s production total and ace associations make it the archetypal Axis fighter — a superb weapon constrained by short range in 1940 and by Germany\'s pilot-training collapse later.
