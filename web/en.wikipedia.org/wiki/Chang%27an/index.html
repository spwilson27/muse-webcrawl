Chang'an

Chang'an (長安, "perpetual peace") was the capital of several Chinese dynasties, located on the site of modern Xi'an in Shaanxi province. It served as the capital of the Western Han dynasty and, most famously, of the Tang dynasty, when it was among the largest cities in the world, with a population of around one million within its walls.

History

The Han capital was laid out south of the Wei River from 202 BC. Under the Tang (618–907), Chang'an was rebuilt on a grand grid plan as Daxing under the Sui and renamed Chang'an; its walled rectangle, roughly 9.7 by 8.7 kilometres, was divided into wards and two great markets, and was the eastern terminus of the Silk Road.

Legacy

Chang'an's grid influenced the design of later capitals, including Japan's Heijō-kyō (Nara) and Heian-kyō (Kyoto) and Beijing. Its name is still used poetically for Xi'an.
