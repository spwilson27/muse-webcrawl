Glass transition

The glass transition is the gradual and reversible transition in amorphous materials (or in amorphous regions within semicrystalline materials) from a hard and relatively brittle "glassy" state into a viscous or rubbery state as the temperature is increased. The temperature at which it occurs is called the glass transition temperature, denoted Tg.

Characteristics

Unlike a melting transition in a crystalline material, the glass transition is not a true phase transition: there is no latent heat and no abrupt change in volume, but properties such as heat capacity, thermal expansion and viscosity change markedly over a temperature interval. Below Tg, molecular motion is largely frozen; above it, segments of the material can move cooperatively.

Factors affecting Tg

The glass transition temperature depends on molecular weight, cross-linking, the presence of plasticizers, and the rate at which the material is cooled or heated. For common polymers, Tg ranges from about −120 °C for silicone rubber to over 300 °C for some high-performance plastics. Ordinary soda-lime glass has a transition around 500–600 °C.

Measurement

Tg is commonly measured by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) or dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA).
