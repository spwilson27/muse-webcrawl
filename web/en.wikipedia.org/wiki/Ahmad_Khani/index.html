Ahmad Khani

Ahmad Khani (Kurdish: Ehmedê Xanî; 1650 – 1707) was a Kurdish intellectual, scholar, mystic and poet. He is regarded as one of the founders of Kurdish literature and an early voice of Kurdish national sentiment. His masterpiece is the romantic epic Mem û Zîn (Mem and Zin), written in Kurmanji Kurdish.

Life and work

Khani was born into the Khani tribe in the Hakkari region and studied in madrasas in Mesopotamia and Persia before settling in Doğubayazıt (Bayazid), where he worked as a cleric and teacher and opened a school that taught in Kurdish. Besides Mem û Zîn (completed 1692), he wrote Nûbihara Biçûkan ("The Spring of Children"), an Arabic–Kurdish rhymed dictionary for students, and the Eqîdeya Îmanê, a creed in verse.

Mem û Zîn

The epic tells the tragic love of Mem and Zîn, thwarted by the scheming Beko. In its famous preface Khani laments the disunity and subjugation of the Kurds — divided between the Ottoman and Persian empires — and explains that he wrote in Kurdish to prove its worth as a literary language. The preface is often cited as one of the earliest expressions of Kurdish national consciousness. Khani's tomb near Doğubayazıt remains a place of pilgrimage.
