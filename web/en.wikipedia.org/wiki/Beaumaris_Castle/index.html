Beaumaris Castle

Beaumaris Castle on Anglesey, regarded as a masterpiece of concentric design.

Beaumaris Castle (Welsh: Castell Biwmares), in Beaumaris, Anglesey, Wales, was built as part of Edward I's campaign to conquer north Wales after 1282. Construction began in 1295 under the direction of the master mason James of St George, but the castle was never fully completed: work slowed as funds were diverted to wars in Scotland, and the upper storeys of the inner ward were never built.

Architecture

Beaumaris is considered the most technically perfect of Edward's Welsh castles. It is a concentric castle of near-perfect symmetry, with an inner ward surrounded by twelve towers and two gatehouses, enclosed in turn by an outer curtain wall with fifteen towers, and a moat once connected to the sea so that supply ships could sail right up to the castle walls. The name derives from the Norman French beau marais, "fair marsh".

History

The castle saw little action: it was held for the king during the English Civil War and surrendered to Parliamentary forces in 1648. Afterwards it fell into picturesque ruin before being acquired by the state in the twentieth century. It now forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd" and is managed by Cadw.
