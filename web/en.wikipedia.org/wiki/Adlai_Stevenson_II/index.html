Adlai Stevenson II

Adlai Ewing Stevenson II (February 5, 1900 – July 14, 1965) was an American politician and diplomat. A Democrat, he served as the 31st governor of Illinois (1949–1953) and twice won his party's nomination for President of the United States, losing to Dwight D. Eisenhower in 1952 and 1956. From 1961 until his death he was the United States ambassador to the United Nations under Presidents John F. Kennedy and Lyndon B. Johnson.

Born: February 5, 1900, Los Angeles, California
Died: July 14, 1965, London, England
Offices: Governor of Illinois; US Ambassador to the UN

Early life

Stevenson came from a prominent Illinois political family: his grandfather Adlai Stevenson I had been Vice President under Grover Cleveland. He graduated from Princeton University and Harvard Law School, practised law in Chicago, and held a series of posts in the Roosevelt administration, including work connected with the founding of the United Nations in 1945.

Governor and presidential campaigns

Elected governor of Illinois in 1948, Stevenson won a reputation as a reformer who fought corruption and modernised state government. Drafted by Democrats in 1952, his eloquent, witty campaign style delighted intellectuals but could not overcome Eisenhower's popularity; he lost again, by a wider margin, in 1956.

Ambassador to the United Nations

Kennedy appointed Stevenson ambassador to the UN in 1961. He is best remembered for his televised confrontation with Soviet ambassador Valerian Zorin during the Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962, presenting photographic evidence of Soviet missile sites in Cuba and declaring he was prepared to wait "until hell freezes over" for an answer. Stevenson died of a heart attack in London in July 1965 while still in office.
