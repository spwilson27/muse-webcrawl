Chest tube

A chest tube (also called an intercostal drain, chest drain, or tube thoracostomy) is a flexible plastic tube inserted through the chest wall into the pleural space to remove air, blood, or fluid, and to allow the lung to re-expand. It is a standard treatment for pneumothorax, haemothorax, and large pleural effusions, and is routinely placed after thoracic surgery.

Insertion

The tube is typically inserted in the "triangle of safety" in the fifth intercostal space in the mid-axillary line, under local anaesthesia and sterile conditions, using blunt dissection or a Seldinger technique. The external end connects to a drainage system, usually an underwater-seal bottle or a chamber that maintains a one-way valve, often with gentle suction. A chest X-ray confirms position.

Management and removal

Drainage volume and the presence of an air leak guide management; bubbling in the water seal indicates air leaving the pleural space. The tube is removed when the lung has re-expanded, drainage has fallen, and there is no ongoing air leak — commonly during a breath-hold or Valsalva manoeuvre. Complications include malposition, infection (empyema), bleeding, and injury to the lung or intercostal vessels.
