Pribilof Islands

The Pribilof Islands (Russian: Острова Прибылова) are a group of four volcanic islands in the Bering Sea, about 200 miles (320 km) north of Unalaska Island and 200 miles south of Cape Newenham, Alaska. The two main islands are Saint Paul and Saint George; the smaller Otter Island and Walrus Island lie nearby. The total land area is about 75 square miles (194 km²). The islands are administered by the State of Alaska and are home to Unangax̂ (Aleut) communities.

History

The islands were named for the Russian navigator Gavriil Pribylov, who reached them in 1786 while searching for the breeding grounds of the northern fur seal reported by the crew of earlier expeditions. Russian promyshlenniki soon established seasonal hunting camps, and Unangax̂ people from the Aleutian Islands were relocated to work in the fur seal harvest. After the Alaska Purchase of 1867, the United States leased the sealing rights to private companies, beginning with the Alaska Commercial Company and later the North American Commercial Company.

Fur seals and wildlife

The Pribilofs host one of the world's largest breeding colonies of northern fur seals, with rookeries concentrated on Saint Paul and Saint George. Uncontrolled pelagic sealing in the late nineteenth century devastated the herd and led to the North Pacific Fur Seal Convention of 1911 among the United States, Russia, Japan, and Great Britain (for Canada) — one of the earliest international wildlife conservation treaties. The islands are also renowned for seabird colonies, including tufted puffins, crested auklets, and red-legged kittiwakes, and are a noted destination for birdwatching.

Communities

The two inhabited islands have year-round communities: St. Paul, the largest, and St. George. Residents are predominantly Unangax̂, and the local economy combines subsistence hunting and fishing with commercial halibut and crab fisheries and support services.
