The Giant Magellan Telescope (GMT) is an extremely large ground-based telescope under construction at Las Campanas Observatory in the Atacama Desert of northern Chile. When completed, it will combine seven of the largest telescope mirrors ever made into a single optical system with the resolving power of a 24.5-metre (80-foot) aperture, producing images up to ten times sharper than those of the Hubble Space Telescope.

Design

Unlike other extremely large telescopes, which tile many small segments, the GMT arranges seven 8.4-metre borosilicate mirrors — one central and six off-axis — in a "flower" pattern. They are cast and polished at the University of Arizona's Richard F. Caris Mirror Lab, beneath its football stadium, using a spin-casting technique in a rotating furnace. Seven matching 1.05-metre secondary mirrors form an advanced adaptive optics system that corrects atmospheric turbulence in real time, aiming to deliver diffraction-limited images in the near-infrared. The telescope is a Gregorian design mounted on an altitude-azimuth fork inside a rotating enclosure about 22 storeys tall.

Science goals

The GMT is designed to study exoplanet atmospheres and search for signs of life, trace the formation of the first stars and galaxies, probe dark matter and dark energy, and observe the environments of black holes. Its first-generation instruments include high-resolution spectrographs and imagers contributed by partner institutions in the United States, Australia, Brazil, South Korea and elsewhere.

Project and construction

The project is led by the GMTO Corporation, a consortium whose founding members include the Carnegie Institution for Science, the University of Arizona, Harvard University and the Smithsonian Institution (through the Center for Astrophysics), the University of Chicago, the University of Texas at Austin and several international partners. The site, near the existing Magellan telescopes at Las Campanas, offers exceptionally dark, dry and stable air. Ground was broken in 2015, and the telescope's completion is planned for the early 2030s, together with the Thirty Meter Telescope as part of the proposed US Extremely Large Telescope Program.
