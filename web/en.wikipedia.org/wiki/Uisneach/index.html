The Hill of Uisneach (Irish: Cnoc Uisnigh) is a hill and ancient ceremonial site in County Westmeath, Ireland. Though only 182 metres (597 ft) high, it occupies a prominent place in Irish mythology and history as the traditional centre of Ireland and the meeting point of its ancient provinces.

The Catstone, said to mark the meeting point of the provinces of Ireland.

Mythology and history

In Irish mythology Uisneach was the seat of the fire festival of Bealtaine, where a great fire was lit and from it, tradition held, all the fires of Ireland were kindled. It was associated with the god Lugh and with the goddess Ériu, said to be buried beneath the great glacial erratic known as the Catstone or Ail na Mireann ("stone of the divisions"), from which the provinces of Ireland were said to radiate. The legendary high king Tuathal Techtmar is said to have established assemblies at Uisneach in the 1st or 2nd century AD and to have annexed the central district of Mide around it.

The site contains numerous archaeological monuments: ringforts, enclosures, a holy well dedicated to St Patrick, and the remains of ancient roadways. Uisneach is part of the Royal Sites of Ireland, proposed for UNESCO World Heritage listing along with Tara, Navan Fort, and others.

Modern revival

In recent years the Bealtaine fire festival has been revived at Uisneach, drawing thousands of visitors each May. The site, in private ownership, is open to guided tours and is promoted as the symbolic and geographical heart of Ireland.
