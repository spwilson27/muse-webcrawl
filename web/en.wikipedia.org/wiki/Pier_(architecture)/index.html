Pier (architecture)

A pier, in architecture, is an upright support for a structure or superstructure such as an arch or bridge. Sections of structural walls between openings (bays) can function as piers. Unlike a column, which is typically round and slender and has a base and capital, a pier is usually a solid masonry or concrete mass, often rectangular in plan, used where heavy loads must be carried.


Compound piers in a Gothic cathedral nave.

Uses

Church architecture

In Romanesque and Gothic churches, piers carry the nave arcades and the vaults above. Simple piers may be square or round; compound piers (piliers composés) have clusters of attached shafts (responds) rising to the transverse arches, diagonal ribs and arcade arches, expressing the lines of force of the Gothic structural system. The piers of Durham Cathedral, with their incised geometric patterns, are famous Romanesque examples.

Bridges

In bridge engineering, a pier is an intermediate support for the spans of a multi-span bridge, founded in the riverbed or ground; the end supports are called abutments. Bridge piers may incorporate cutwaters (starlings) to deflect flowing water and ice.

Other uses

The term also applies to the masonry supports of aqueducts, viaducts and raised roadways, to the portions of a wall between windows and doors, and in foundation engineering to drilled or driven vertical supports ("pier foundations").

Related terms

  • Column — a slender, usually cylindrical upright with base and capital.
  • Pilaster — a shallow pier or rectangular feature projecting slightly from a wall.
  • Buttress — an external support resisting lateral thrust.
  • Respond — a half-pier set against a wall.
  • Abutment — the end support of a bridge or arch.
