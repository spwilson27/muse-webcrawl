Duncan I (c. 1001 – 14 August 1040) was King of Scots from 1034 until his death in battle in 1040. The grandson of Malcolm II through his mother Bethóc, he succeeded his grandfather in accordance with the king's arrangements, setting aside the older tanistry custom of succession within a wider royal kindred. He is best known today as the elderly, gracious king murdered in Shakespeare's Macbeth — a portrayal that bears little resemblance to the historical Duncan, who was a relatively young man and a war leader.

Reign

Before becoming king, Duncan ruled Strathclyde as its king under Malcolm's overlordship. As king of Scots he campaigned in the north and south; in 1039 he led an unsuccessful siege of Durham in England. In 1040 he invaded Moray, whose mormaer was Macbeth, and was defeated and killed at the Battle of Bothnagowan (Pitgaveny), near Elgin, on 14 August 1040. Macbeth succeeded him and reigned for seventeen years.

Family and legacy

Duncan married a woman sometimes named Suthen, a relative of Siward, Earl of Northumbria. His sons Malcolm (later Malcolm III, "Canmore") and Donald (later Donald III) both eventually became kings after Macbeth's death. Shakespeare's depiction of Duncan as a venerable, saintly old man derives from later chronicle traditions, especially Holinshed's Chronicles, which the playwright transformed for dramatic purposes.
