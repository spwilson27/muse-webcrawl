Edessa (Syriac: Ūrhāy) was an ancient city in Upper Mesopotamia, located at the site of modern Şanlıurfa in southeastern Turkey. It was an important centre of Syriac Christianity, the capital of the kingdom of Osroene, a Roman and Byzantine frontier city, and later the capital of the first Crusader state.

History

The city was refounded by Seleucus I Nicator around 302 BC and named after Edessa in Macedonia. From 132 BC it was ruled by the Abgarid dynasty of Osroene, nominally under Parthian and then Roman suzerainty. According to Christian tradition, King Abgar V corresponded with Jesus and received the Image of Edessa, a cloth bearing Christ's likeness, making Edessa one of the first kingdoms associated with Christianity.

Edessa became a major centre of Syriac learning and literature; its dialect of Aramaic became the classical Syriac language. The theologian Ephrem the Syrian taught there in the fourth century. The city passed between Roman/Byzantine and Persian hands, was taken by Arab Muslims in 639, and was later ruled by various Turkish dynasties.

County of Edessa

In 1098, during the First Crusade, Baldwin of Boulogne became Count of Edessa, founding the first Crusader state in the Levant. The county fell to the Turkish commander Zengi in 1144, an event that prompted the Second Crusade.
