Somali (Somali: Af Soomaali) is an Afroasiatic language of the Cushitic branch, spoken by ethnic Somalis in Somalia, Somaliland, Djibouti, Ethiopia and Kenya, and by a large global diaspora. It is the national language of Somalia and, with around 24 million speakers, the most widely spoken Cushitic language.

Classification and history

Somali belongs to the East Cushitic branch of the Afroasiatic family, closely related to Afar and Saho within the Lowland East Cushitic group. Though Somalis have been literate in Arabic for religious purposes for centuries, Somali itself was long primarily an oral language with a rich tradition of poetry, proverb and oral history.

Writing system

Several scripts were devised for Somali, including the Osmanya, Borama and Kaddare alphabets. In 1972 the Somali government adopted a Latin-based orthography developed by linguist Shire Jama Ahmed, which became the standard for administration and education and remains in use today. The alphabet lacks the letters p, v and z, and uses digraphs such as dh, kh, sh and c for the voiced pharyngeal fricative.

Grammar and phonology

Somali is a tonal, agglutinative language with pitch accent distinguishing grammatical functions. It marks focus with particles such as baa and ayaa, has a complex system of gender polarity in which nouns switch gender marking between singular and plural, and typically follows subject–object–verb word order. The consonant inventory includes ejective and implosive consonants as well as pharyngeals borrowed through contact with Arabic.

Dialects

The main dialect groups are Northern (Standard) Somali, Benadir (coastal) Somali, and Maay, spoken in the south; Maay is sometimes considered distinct enough to be a separate language (Digil-Mirifle). Standard Somali, based on northern varieties, is used in national media.
