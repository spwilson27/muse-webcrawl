Third Carlist War

The Third Carlist War (Spanish: Tercera Guerra Carlista) was the last of the Carlist civil wars in Spain, fought between 1872 and 1876 between the supporters of the pretender Carlos, Duke of Madrid (Don Carlos, "Carlos VII" to his followers), and the governments of Spain — successively those of Amadeo I, the First Spanish Republic and the restored Bourbon monarchy of Alfonso XII. The Carlists, drawing their strength from Navarre, the Basque provinces and rural Catalonia, at their height controlled much of the north-east and besieged Bilbao, but the restoration of the monarchy in December 1874 deprived them of their cause's urgency, and by February 1876 the war ended in government victory. A principal consequence was the abolition of the Basque fueros (1876).

Course of the war

Don Carlos called his supporters to arms in April 1872 after disputed elections, but the initial rising was crushed at Oroquieta in May, and the pretender fled to France, signing the Convention of Amorebieta. The abdication of Amadeo I and the proclamation of the Republic in February 1873 gave Carlism its great opportunity: under commanders such as Dorregaray, the Carlists won victories at Eraul and Udave, captured Estella — which became Don Carlos's court — and by 1874 held most of Navarre and the Basque interior, besieging Bilbao (February–May 1874) until government forces relieved it. In the east, Carlist forces in Catalonia and the Maestrazgo, nominally under the veteran Ramón Cabrera (who never took the field), fought a parallel guerrilla war.

End of the war

The pronunciamiento of General Martínez Campos at Sagunto (29 December 1874) restored the Bourbon monarchy in the person of Alfonso XII, rallying moderate opinion against both republicans and Carlists and drawing off Carlist support. With a greatly enlarged army, the government systematically reduced the Carlist north: victories at Treviño and the fall of strongpoints through 1875, then the final offensive of early 1876. Estella fell in February 1876, Don Carlos crossed into France on 28 February 1876, and Alfonso XII entered Pamplona proclaiming the war over. Total deaths are commonly estimated in the tens of thousands.

Aftermath

By the law of 21 July 1876 the traditional fueros of the Basque provinces were abolished (Navarre's had already been modified in 1841), integrating them into the Spanish fiscal and military system — though the economic agreements (conciertos económicos) of 1878 preserved a measure of fiscal autonomy. Defeated Carlism persisted as a political movement of traditionalist Catholicism, influential in Navarre into the twentieth century and part of the Nationalist coalition in 1936.
