Friedrich Paschen

Louis Carl Heinrich Friedrich Paschen (1865–1947).

Louis Carl Heinrich Friedrich Paschen (22 January 1865 – 25 February 1947) was a German physicist known for his work on electrical discharge and atomic spectra. He is best known for Paschen's law, which describes the breakdown voltage of a gas as a function of pressure and gap length, and for the Paschen series of spectral lines of hydrogen in the infrared.

Paschen's law

In 1889 Paschen showed that the voltage required to spark across a gas gap depends not on pressure and distance separately but on their product, yielding the characteristic U-shaped Paschen curve with a minimum breakdown voltage for each gas. The law is fundamental to the design of high-voltage equipment, gas-discharge lamps and plasma technology.

Spectroscopy

Paschen also discovered, in 1908, the series of hydrogen emission lines in the infrared corresponding to electron transitions ending at the third energy level, now called the Paschen series — the infrared counterpart to the visible Balmer series. He later discovered the Paschen–Back effect, the behaviour of spectral lines in strong magnetic fields, in collaboration with Ernst Back. Paschen was director of the Physikalisch-Technische Reichsanstalt from 1924 to 1933 and professor at the University of Tübingen.
