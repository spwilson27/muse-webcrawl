Battle of Jemappes

The Battle of Jemappes was fought on 6 November 1792 near Jemappes in the Austrian Netherlands (modern Belgium), between the French Revolutionary army under Charles François Dumouriez and the Austrian army under Duke Albert of Saxe-Teschen. The first major offensive battle of the French Revolutionary Wars, it opened the Austrian Netherlands to French occupation and marked the debut of the mass armies of the Revolution.

Background

After checking the allied invasion of France at Valmy (20 September 1792), Dumouriez took the offensive into Belgium, intending to "liberate" the Austrian Netherlands and win the natural frontiers for France. The Austrians, much outnumbered, stood in entrenched positions on the heights west of Mons around Jemappes and Cuesmes, guarding the fortress.

The battle

Dumouriez attacked along the whole Austrian line in great columns of volunteers and regulars. On the French right, General Beurnonville's assaults on Jemappes village were repeatedly repulsed with loss; in the centre, the young Duke of Chartres (the future King Louis Philippe) rallied broken battalions under heavy fire — an episode that made his military reputation. The turning point came on the left, where French troops forced their way up the heights at Cuesmes and rolled up the Austrian position, while renewed attacks carried Jemappes itself. Saxe-Teschen ordered an orderly retreat on Mons, which the Austrians evacuated the next day.

Aftermath

Within weeks nearly all of Belgium was in French hands, and Brussels fell on 14 November. The occupation soon soured as the French imposed revolutionary policies and requisitions, but Jemappes, like Valmy, electrified revolutionary Europe: a citizen army animated by patriotic fervour had beaten professional troops in the open field. The battle is named on the Arc de Triomphe in Paris.
