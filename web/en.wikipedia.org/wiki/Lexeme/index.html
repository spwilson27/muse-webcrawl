Lexeme

Lexeme
FieldLinguistics, lexicology, morphology
Related unitsMorpheme, word form, lemma

A lexeme is a unit of lexical meaning that underlies a set of words that are related through inflection. For example, the English word forms run, runs, ran, and running are all forms of the same lexeme, conventionally written RUN. A lexeme is an abstraction: it is the basic unit of the lexicon, roughly corresponding to a dictionary headword, whereas the individual inflected variants are its word forms (or grammatical words).

Lexemes and lemmas

The lemma is the citation form chosen to represent a lexeme — in English, typically the infinitive for verbs and the singular for nouns. In lexicography, each dictionary entry corresponds (approximately) to one lexeme, and a lexeme may include multi-word units such as phrasal verbs (put up with).

Distinguishing lexemes

Homographs with unrelated meanings are separate lexemes (e.g. bank "financial institution" versus bank "river edge"), while polysemous senses of one form belong to a single lexeme. Derivational processes, by contrast with inflection, create new lexemes: runner and running (the noun, as in "the running of the bulls") are distinct from RUN.
