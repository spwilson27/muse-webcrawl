Maxilla

Maxilla
Part ofSkull, upper jaw
ArticulationsFrontal, zygomatic, palatine, lacrimal, nasal and other facial bones
FeaturesMaxillary sinus; alveolar process; infraorbital foramen

The maxilla (plural: maxillae) is the upper jawbone of vertebrates. In humans the two maxillae fuse at the midline to form the upper jaw. The maxilla forms most of the midface: it contributes to the floor of the orbit, the walls of the nasal cavity and the roof of the mouth, and its alveolar process holds the upper teeth.

Structure

Each maxilla consists of a pyramidal body and four processes: the frontal process, which rises toward the forehead; the zygomatic process, extending toward the cheekbone; the palatine process, which forms most of the hard palate; and the alveolar process, which contains the tooth sockets. Inside the body lies the maxillary sinus, the largest of the paranasal sinuses, which drains into the nasal cavity through an opening high on its medial wall — a feature that makes sinus infections slow to clear.

Development

The maxilla ossifies from membrane bone in the first arch of the embryo. Its secondary centre, the premaxilla (incisive bone), carries the incisor teeth and fuses with the rest of the bone early in life; failure of the palatine processes to fuse produces a cleft palate.

Clinical significance

The maxilla is commonly fractured in midface trauma, classically described by the Le Fort classification. It is also central to dentistry and maxillofacial surgery, including implant placement and orthognathic (jaw-correcting) operations.
