Pantera

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pantera was an American heavy metal band from Arlington, Texas, formed in 1981 by the Abbott brothers (guitarist Dimebag Darrell and drummer Vinnie Paul), and currently composed of vocalist Phil Anselmo, bassist Rex Brown, and touring musicians Zakk Wylde and Charlie Benante. The group's best-known lineup consisted of the Abbott brothers along with Brown and Anselmo, who joined in 1986.

Pantera began as a glam metal band releasing four independent albums in the 1980s. After recruiting Anselmo in 1986, the band signed to Atco Records and released Cowboys from Hell (1990), which redefined their sound toward a heavier groove metal style. Their following album Vulgar Display of Power (1992) is widely considered a landmark of heavy metal. Subsequent albums Far Beyond Driven (1994), which debuted at number one on the Billboard 200, The Great Southern Trendkill (1996), and Reinventing the Steel (2000) cemented their influence.

History

Pantera was formed in 1981 by brothers Darrell and Vincent Abbott with bassist Rex Brown. The band released glam metal albums Metal Magic (1983), Projects in the Jungle (1984), I Am the Night (1985), and Power Metal (1988), the last featuring Phil Anselmo, who replaced Terry Glaze in 1986.

With producer Terry Date, Cowboys from Hell introduced the band's groove-oriented sound. Vulgar Display of Power followed in 1992, featuring songs such as "Walk", "Mouth for War", and "This Love". Far Beyond Driven (1994) became the first extreme metal album to top the Billboard 200. Internal tensions, exacerbated by Anselmo's drug use, led to the band's breakup in 2003.

On December 8, 2004, Dimebag Darrell was shot and killed on stage while performing with his new band Damageplan in Columbus, Ohio. Vinnie Paul died in 2018. In 2022, Anselmo and Brown re-formed Pantera with Zakk Wylde and Charlie Benante for a reunion tour.

Musical style and influence

Pantera is generally credited with popularizing groove metal, a subgenre emphasizing mid-tempo, syncopated riffs and rhythmic heaviness over speed. Dimebag Darrell's guitar playing, combining blues-influenced solos with aggressive riffing, has influenced generations of metal guitarists. The band cited Black Sabbath, Kiss, Van Halen, Iron Maiden, Judas Priest, and hardcore punk among their influences.
