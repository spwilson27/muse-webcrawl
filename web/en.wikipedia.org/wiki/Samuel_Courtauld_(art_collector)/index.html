Samuel Courtauld (art collector)

Born: 7 May 1876, Bocking, Essex, England

Died: 1 December 1947 (aged 71), London, England

Occupation: Industrialist, art collector

Known for: Founder of the Courtauld Institute of Art; Courtauld Gallery collection

Samuel Courtauld (7 May 1876 – 1 December 1947) was a British industrialist and art collector. A member of the family that built the Courtaulds textile empire, he is remembered as the founder of the Courtauld Institute of Art in London and as one of the greatest British collectors of Impressionist and Post-Impressionist painting.

Business career

Courtauld joined the family firm, which pioneered the manufacture of artificial silk (viscose rayon), and became its chairman in 1921. Under his leadership Courtaulds became one of Britain's largest textile companies.

Art collection

From the 1920s, advised by the critic Roger Fry and others, Courtauld assembled an outstanding collection of French modern art, including major works by Manet, Monet, Renoir, Degas, Cézanne, Van Gogh, Gauguin and Seurat. Among its most famous paintings are Manet's A Bar at the Folies-Bergère and Cézanne's The Card Players. In 1923 he also established a fund to buy modern French works for British national collections.

The Courtauld Institute

In 1932 Courtauld co-founded the Courtauld Institute of Art, the first institution in Britain devoted to the academic study of art history, endowing it with his London home, Home House in Portman Square. On his death he bequeathed his collection to the Institute; it is now displayed in the Courtauld Gallery at Somerset House.
