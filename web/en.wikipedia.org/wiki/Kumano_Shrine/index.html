Kumano Shrine

A Kumano shrine (熊野神社, Kumano Jinja) is a type of Shinto shrine in Japan associated with the worship of the Kumano mountains and the three grand shrines of the Kumano region in the Kii Peninsula: Kumano Hongū Taisha, Kumano Hayatama Taisha, and Kumano Nachi Taisha. There are more than 3,000 Kumano shrines throughout Japan, making the Kumano faith one of the most widely distributed cults in the country.

History

The Kumano cult originated in the sacred mountainous region of Kumano, straddling present-day Wakayama and Mie prefectures. The three grand shrines, collectively known as the Kumano Sanzan, became major pilgrimage destinations from the Heian period onward, attracting imperial pilgrims — the retired Emperor Shirakawa made his first pilgrimage in 1090, and the cloistered emperors' journeys became so frequent that the roads to Kumano were said to resemble "pilgrimages of ants" (ari no Kumano mode).

Kumano worship blended Shinto with Shugendō mountain asceticism and esoteric Buddhism; its deities, the Kumano Gongen, were regarded as manifestations of Buddhas and bodhisattvas. As pilgrims and traveling priests (bikuni and Kumano yamabushi) spread the faith, branch shrines were founded across the country through the kanjō process of dividing and transferring the deity's spirit.

Worship

Kumano shrines enshrine the twelve Kumano Gongen, of whom the chief three correspond to the main deities of the Sanzan. Kumano was traditionally associated with the afterlife and salvation, and was unusual in welcoming women and people considered impure, who were excluded from many other sacred sites. The cult is also linked with the yatagarasu, the three-legged crow said to have guided Emperor Jimmu through the Kumano mountains.

Notable Kumano shrines

  • Kumano Hongū Taisha — head shrine at Hongū, Wakayama
  • Kumano Hayatama Taisha — Shingū, Wakayama
  • Kumano Nachi Taisha — Nachikatsuura, Wakayama, near the Nachi Falls

The Kumano Sanzan and the pilgrimage routes to them were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2004 as part of the "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range".
