Cold fusion

Cold fusion is a hypothesised type of nuclear reaction that would occur at, or near, room temperature. It would contrast markedly with the "hot" fusion known to take place naturally within stars and in hydrogen bombs, which requires temperatures of millions of degrees.

The 1989 announcement

Electrochemists Martin Fleischmann and Stanley Pons reported that electrolysis of heavy water on a palladium electrode produced excess heat they attributed to nuclear fusion. The announcement drew worldwide attention but other laboratories failed to reproduce the effect, and reviews by the US Department of Energy in 1989 and 2004 concluded the evidence was not convincing.

Later work

A small community continues research under the label LENR (low-energy nuclear reactions). A 2019 Google-funded multi-institution study published in Nature found no evidence of conventional cold fusion but reported renewed interest in the underlying materials science.
