The Xiongnu were a tribal confederation of nomadic peoples who formed a vast steppe empire centred on the Mongolian plateau from the late 3rd century BC. Unified by Modu Chanyu in 209 BC, they became Han China's principal northern rival for three centuries.

History

Modu defeated the Donghu and Yuezhi and forced Han Gaozu, after the Battle of Baideng (200 BC), into a heqin policy of marriage alliances and gifts. Under Emperor Wu, Han armies under generals such as Huo Qubing and Wei Qing campaigned deep into the steppe from 133 BC. The Xiongnu split in 53 BC into southern (Han-aligned) and northern branches; Ban Chao and Dou Xian's campaigns (89–91 AD) destroyed the northern confederation, after which many groups migrated west, while southern Xiongnu settled inside China — their descendants later founded states in the Sixteen Kingdoms period.

Society

Xiongnu society was organised in a decimal military hierarchy under the chanyu and kings of the Left and Right. Their language remains debated. Rich burials such as Noin-Ula contained Chinese silks and lacquer alongside steppe art, reflecting intensive exchange and conflict.
