Dyspraxia

Dyspraxia (developmental co-ordination disorder)
SpecialtyNeurology; paediatrics; occupational therapy
SymptomsPoor motor co-ordination, clumsiness, difficulty planning movements
OnsetEarly childhood

Dyspraxia, formally known as developmental co-ordination disorder (DCD), is a neurodevelopmental condition characterised by marked difficulty acquiring and executing coordinated motor skills. It affects fine and gross motor control — tasks such as handwriting, tying shoelaces, catching a ball and riding a bicycle — to a degree that interferes with daily life and schooling.

Features

Children with dyspraxia may appear clumsy or "out of sync" with their environment, have poor balance and posture, and struggle to plan and organise movements (praxis) even when they understand the task. Difficulties with speech articulation (verbal dyspraxia) and with organisation and sequencing can also occur. Intelligence is not inherently affected. The condition frequently co-occurs with ADHD, dyslexia and autism spectrum conditions.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is made when motor difficulties are substantially below age expectations, are not better explained by another medical condition, and significantly affect everyday functioning; standardised motor tests and clinical history are used. There is no cure, but occupational therapy, physiotherapy and structured practice help build skills and strategies, and support in school is commonly recommended. The condition persists into adulthood in many cases.
