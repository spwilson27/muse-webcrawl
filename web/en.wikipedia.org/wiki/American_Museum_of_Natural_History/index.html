The American Museum of Natural History (AMNH) is a natural history museum on the Upper West Side of Manhattan in New York City, facing Central Park. It is one of the largest natural history museums in the world, comprising dozens of exhibition halls across a complex of more than twenty interconnected buildings, with scientific collections of over 34 million specimens of plants, animals, fossils, minerals, meteorites, and human cultural artifacts — only a small fraction of which can be displayed at once.

Exhibitions

Highlights include the dinosaur halls of the fourth floor, including Tyrannosaurus rex and the long-necked Titanosaur; the Milstein Hall of Ocean Life with its life-size blue whale model; the Akeley Hall of African Mammals with its habitat dioramas; the Hayden Planetarium within the Rose Center for Earth and Space (opened 2000); the Hall of Biodiversity; and the Richard Gilder Center for Science, Education, and Innovation, opened in 2023. The museum's Theodore Roosevelt Memorial and Rotunda commemorate the conservationist president, whose father was a co-founder.

Research and history

Founded in 1869 and opened at its current site in the 1870s, the museum conducts research in anthropology, paleontology, zoology, earth and planetary sciences, and astrophysics, and runs a doctoral program through the Richard Gilder Graduate School. Its expeditions — from Roy Chapman Andrews's Central Asiatic Expeditions in the Gobi Desert to Franz Boas's Jesup North Pacific Expedition — built its collections. The museum was the setting of the film Night at the Museum (2006).
