Stan Getz

Stan Getz (born Stanley Gayetsky; February 2, 1927 – June 6, 1991) was an American jazz saxophonist. Playing primarily the tenor saxophone, Getz was known as "The Sound" because of his warm, lyrical tone, rooted in the style of Lester Young. He first gained fame as a soloist with Woody Herman's band in the late 1940s and went on to lead a long career spanning bebop, cool jazz, and bossa nova; his recordings with Charlie Byrd and Astrud Gilberto introduced bossa nova to American audiences and produced the hit "The Girl from Ipanema".

Early life and career

Getz was born in Philadelphia to Ukrainian Jewish immigrant parents and raised in New York City. A prodigy, he was playing professionally in his teens with Jack Teagarden and Stan Kenton before joining Woody Herman's Second Herd in 1947. His solo on "Early Autumn" (1948), alongside Zoot Sims, Al Cohn, and Serge Chaloff in the celebrated "Four Brothers" saxophone section, made him famous. In the 1950s he led successful small groups, recorded landmark albums such as Stan Getz Plays and West Coast Jazz, and became a central figure of cool jazz, despite personal struggles with addiction that interrupted his career.

Bossa nova

In 1962 Getz recorded Jazz Samba with guitarist Charlie Byrd, whose rendition of "Desafinado" became a hit and ignited a bossa nova craze in the United States. The following year, Getz/Gilberto (1963), with João Gilberto, Antônio Carlos Jobim, and Astrud Gilberto, won the Grammy Award for Album of the Year, and "The Girl from Ipanema" became one of the most recorded songs in history. Getz's feathery tone proved an ideal foil for the Brazilian rhythm.

Later life

Getz continued to evolve, recording with Chick Corea and leading a quartet that launched vibraphonist Gary Burton, experimenting with fusion in the 1970s and returning to acoustic jazz in the 1980s with pianist Kenny Barron. He performed almost until his death from liver cancer in 1991. He won multiple Grammy Awards across four decades and is consistently ranked among the greatest tenor saxophonists in jazz history.
