Sphalerite

Sphalerite is a sulfide mineral with the chemical formula (Zn,Fe)S and is the most important ore of zinc. It consists largely of zinc sulfide in crystalline form, with variable amounts of iron substituting for zinc; when the iron content is high, the dark, opaque variety is called marmatite. Nearly pure, iron-free sphalerite is pale yellow to honey-coloured and may be called cleiophane. The name derives from the Greek sphaleros, "treacherous", because the mineral's appearance deceived miners — it resembles galena but yields no lead.

Properties and structure

Sphalerite crystallises in the cubic system, commonly as tetrahedra and dodecahedra with perfect dodecahedral cleavage in six directions. Its crystal structure — the zinc blende structure — is a prototype for many semiconductor compounds, with zinc and sulfur atoms each tetrahedrally coordinated. Its high refractive index (about 2.37) gives transparent crystals great brilliance and dispersion, and gem-quality material is occasionally faceted for collectors, though its low hardness and perfect cleavage make it unsuitable for everyday jewellery.

Occurrence and uses

Sphalerite occurs in hydrothermal veins and Mississippi Valley-type deposits, commonly associated with galena, pyrite, chalcopyrite, calcite, dolomite, and fluorite. Major deposits are found in Australia, China, Peru, the United States, Canada, and Mexico. The mineral is the world's principal source of zinc, used for galvanising steel, brass, and die-casting alloys, and is also an important source of by-product metals including cadmium, indium, gallium, and germanium, which are recovered during smelting.
