The Battle of Mirbat took place on 19 July 1972 during the Dhofar Rebellion in Oman, when guerrillas of the Popular Front for the Liberation of the Occupied Arabian Gulf (PFLOAG), known locally as the Adoo, attacked the coastal town of Mirbat. A small British Army Training Team detachment of nine men of the Special Air Service (SAS), commanded by Captain Mike Kealy, together with Omani gendarmes and local firqat irregulars, held the town against a force of several hundred attackers until reinforcements and air support arrived. The battle is regarded as one of the most celebrated actions in the history of the SAS.

Background

The Dhofar Rebellion began in the 1960s against Sultan Said bin Taimur. After Sultan Qaboos came to power in 1970, British-supported reforms and military operations intensified. British Army Training Teams, built around SAS squadrons, were stationed in Dhofari towns such as Mirbat and Taqah, training local forces and running civil aid programmes.

The battle

At dawn on 19 July 1972, in the monsoon mist, around 250–400 PFLOAG fighters attacked Mirbat, expecting to overrun its small garrison. The defenders fought from the BATT house and a fort, manning a 25-pounder gun, mortars and machine guns. Trooper Talaiasi Labalaba famously fired the 25-pounder single-handedly after its Omani gunner was wounded, and was killed at the gun. Captain Kealy led a small group across open ground to reinforce the gun pit. Strikemaster jets of the Sultan of Oman's Air Force strafed the attackers, and a relief force with elements of G Squadron SAS arriving by helicopter later in the day drove the guerrillas off.

Aftermath

Two SAS soldiers, Labalaba and Trooper Tommy Tobin, were killed. Kealy received the Distinguished Service Order, and other members of the detachment were decorated. The defeat was a major blow to PFLOAG morale and marked a turning point in the Dhofar campaign, which ended in government victory in 1976.
