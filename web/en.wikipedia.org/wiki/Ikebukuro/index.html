Ikebukuro (池袋) is a commercial and entertainment district in Toshima, Tokyo, Japan. It centres on Ikebukuro Station, one of the busiest railway stations in the world, served by JR East, the Seibu Ikebukuro Line, the Tobu Tojo Line, and several Tokyo Metro lines. Along with Shinjuku and Shibuya, it is one of the three major sub-centres (fukutoshin) on the western side of central Tokyo.

Overview

The east side of the station is dominated by the Sunshine City complex, opened in 1978, whose Sunshine 60 skyscraper was for a time the tallest building in Asia; it contains an aquarium, planetarium, museum, and the Ancient Orient Museum. The area is notable for its concentration of shops serving the otaku subculture, especially "Otome Road", a cluster of shops selling manga and goods aimed at women, complementing Akihabara's male-oriented scene.

The west side hosts the Tokyo Metropolitan Theatre, Rikkyo University, and the former site of Sugamo Prison, where Sunshine City now stands. Ikebukuro also has a significant Chinese community and many Chinese restaurants and shops.

History

The name is said to derive from a pond (ike) that once stood west of the station, shaped like a bag (fukuro). The station opened in 1903, and the district grew rapidly after the Great Kanto earthquake of 1923 and again after the Second World War, when black markets flourished around the station.
