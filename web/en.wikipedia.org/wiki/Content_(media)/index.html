Content (media)

In media, content is information and experience directed at an end-user or audience. Content is "something that is to be expressed through some medium, as speech, writing or any of various arts". Examples include books, articles, images, video, audio, interactive media and other forms of digital media distributed by publishers, broadcasters and creators.

Terminology

The word originates in Latin from the verb continere, meaning "held together" or "contained". With the growth of the World Wide Web in the 1990s, "content" became a standard industry term for the material published on websites, in contrast to the design, technology and delivery platform. Users and industry commentators sometimes criticise the term as reducing creative works to a commodity to be distributed; the media theorist Bob Stein and others have discussed this usage, while creators of individual works may prefer terms such as "writing", "photography" or "art".

Content production and delivery

Content is produced by authors, journalists, photographers, filmmakers, musicians and other creators, and increasingly by ordinary users (user-generated content). Organisations manage content through content management systems, and distribute it via websites, streaming services, social media and traditional publishing channels.

Value and business models

Content may be monetised through direct sales, subscriptions, advertising or licensing. Debates over the economics of digital content include the slogan "content is king", popularised in a 1996 essay by Bill Gates, which argued that content would be where much of the money on the internet would be made, comparable to broadcasting.

Criticism

The generic label "content" has been criticised for flattening distinct art forms and for framing creative work primarily as material to fill platforms and attract attention. Writers such as Emily VanDerWerff have discussed the term's spread in entertainment journalism and production.
