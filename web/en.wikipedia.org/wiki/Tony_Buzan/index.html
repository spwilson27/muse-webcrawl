Anthony Peter "Tony" Buzan (2 June 1942 – 13 April 2019) was an English author and educational consultant. He is best known as the populariser of mind mapping, a diagram technique for visualising ideas, which he presented in his BBC television series and book Use Your Head (1974) and developed through a large body of self-help books on memory, reading and learning.

Early life and education

Buzan was born in Palmers Green, London, in 1942 and moved with his family to Canada as a child. He studied at the University of British Columbia in Vancouver, graduating in 1964 with studies in psychology, English, mathematics and general sciences. While at university he became interested in how the brain processes information and in techniques for improving memory and study skills, partly through involvement with Mensa, the high-IQ society, whose magazine he later edited.

Career

In the early 1970s Buzan worked with the BBC on the television series Use Your Head, accompanied by the book of the same name, which introduced mind mapping to a wide audience. He founded the Brain Trust charity and the Brain Foundation, and co-founded, with chess grandmaster Raymond Keene, the World Memory Championships in 1991, helping to establish competitive memorisation ("memory sports") as an organised pursuit. He also founded the World Speed Reading Championships and the World Mind Mapping Championships.

Buzan wrote or co-wrote more than 80 books, translated into many languages, including Use Your Memory, Master Your Memory, Speed Reading, The Mind Map Book (with his brother Barry Buzan) and Buzan's Book of Mental World Records. He lectured and consulted internationally on creativity and learning, and licensed mind-mapping training and software around the world.

Mind mapping

Buzan described mind maps as radiant, tree-like diagrams in which a central concept branches into associated topics, using colour, images and single key words on curved lines. He claimed the format mirrors the brain's associative processes and presented it as a tool for note-taking, planning, revision and brainstorming. Academic assessments of the specific learning benefits he claimed have been mixed, though the technique remains widely used in education and business.

Death

Buzan died on 13 April 2019, aged 76, after a heart attack.
