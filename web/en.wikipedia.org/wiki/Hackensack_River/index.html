The Hackensack River is a river, approximately 45 miles (72 km) long, in the U.S. states of New York and New Jersey, emptying into Newark Bay, a back chamber of New York Harbor. The river was named after the Hackensack, a band of the Lenape (Delaware) people who inhabited the region.

Course

The river rises in Rockland County, New York, near Lake DeForest and the Lake Tappan reservoir, and flows south into New Jersey through the suburban communities of Bergen and Hudson counties. Four reservoirs — Lake DeForest, Lake Tappan, Lake Woodcliff, and the Oradell Reservoir — impound its upper course and provide drinking water to roughly a million people in northern New Jersey and Rockland County.

The lower river is tidal and widens into the New Jersey Meadowlands, a large complex of wetlands that separates Hudson and Bergen counties from Newark. It joins the Passaic River at Newark Bay near Kearny Point, passing the Port Newark–Elizabeth Marine Terminal area. The estuary is crossed by numerous road and rail bridges, many of them movable spans including the famous PATH and Amtrak Portal and Dock bridges.

Ecology and pollution

Heavy industrialisation of the lower valley in the 19th and 20th centuries left the river severely polluted, including mercury contamination from former processing plants at Berry's Creek — one of the most mercury-contaminated sites in the world. Nevertheless, the Meadowlands estuary supports surprising wildlife, including herons, egrets, diamondback terrapins, and recovering fish populations. Parts of the river and adjacent wetlands are protected as the Hackensack Meadowlands District, and portions of the lower river have been proposed for federal Superfund listing.
