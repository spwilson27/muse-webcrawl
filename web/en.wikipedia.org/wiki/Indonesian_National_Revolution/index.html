Indonesian National Revolution

The Indonesian National Revolution, or the Indonesian War of Independence, was an armed conflict and diplomatic struggle between the Republic of Indonesia and the Dutch Empire and, to a lesser extent, an internal social revolution. It took place between Indonesia's proclamation of independence on August 17, 1945, and the Netherlands' transfer of sovereignty on December 27, 1949.

Background

Indonesia had been the Dutch East Indies colony. During World War II Japan occupied the archipelago (1942–45), destroying Dutch prestige and encouraging Indonesian nationalism. Two days after Japan's surrender, nationalist leaders Sukarno and Mohammad Hatta proclaimed independence. The returning Dutch, supported initially by British forces, refused to recognise the Republic and sought to restore colonial rule through a federal structure.

The struggle

Fighting erupted across Java and Sumatra: the Battle of Surabaya in November 1945, in which British and Indian troops clashed with Indonesian forces after the killing of Brigadier Mallaby, became an iconic episode commemorated as Heroes' Day. The Dutch launched two major offensives, euphemistically called "police actions," in 1947 and 1948, seizing much territory and capturing Sukarno and Hatta at Yogyakarta in December 1948 — but guerrilla resistance under General Sudirman continued, and attacks such as the March 1, 1949 general offensive on Yogyakarta demonstrated the Republic's survival. Internally, the revolution also included social revolutions against local aristocracies and a communist uprising at Madiun in 1948, suppressed by Republican forces.

Transfer of sovereignty

International pressure, especially from the United States and the United Nations, forced the Netherlands to negotiate. At the Round Table Conference in The Hague in 1949 the Dutch agreed to transfer sovereignty to a federal United States of Indonesia, which in 1950 was dissolved into the unitary Republic of Indonesia. Estimates of Indonesian deaths range from 45,000 to 100,000 fighters and far higher numbers of civilians; Dutch military deaths were around 5,000.
