Radon

Radon is a chemical element with symbol Rn and atomic number 86 — a colourless, odourless, radioactive noble gas formed by the decay of radium in rocks and soil. Radon-222, its longest-lived common isotope, has a half-life of about 3.8 days.

Radon seeps into buildings from the ground and can accumulate indoors; its decay products, inhaled, irradiate lung tissue, and radon is recognised as the second-leading cause of lung cancer overall (after smoking) and the leading cause among non-smokers, according to health agencies such as the WHO and US EPA. Homes in granite-rich areas are tested with simple kits and remediated by sub-slab ventilation. Friedrich Ernst Dorn identified the gas in 1900, following the Curies' observation of radioactive gas from radium compounds.
