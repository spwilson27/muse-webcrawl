Operation Bagration (Russian: Операция Багратион, Operatsiya Bagration) was the codename for the 1944 Soviet Byelorussian strategic offensive operation, a military campaign fought between 22 June and 19 August 1944 in Soviet Byelorussia in the Eastern Front of World War II. The operation was named after the Georgian prince Pyotr Bagration, a general of the Imperial Russian Army who received a fatal wound at the Battle of Borodino in 1812.

The operation resulted in the almost complete destruction of the German Army Group Centre and the loss of three German armies. It is considered one of the largest and most consequential operations of the war, enabling the Red Army to advance to the Vistula River and the borders of East Prussia, within reach of Berlin itself.

Background

By the summer of 1944, Germany had been pushed back from much of the Soviet Union. Army Group Centre held a large salient in Byelorussia, and German intelligence expected the main Soviet summer offensive to fall in Ukraine against Army Group North Ukraine. Soviet deception operations (maskirovka) reinforced this expectation, drawing German armoured reserves south and leaving Army Group Centre critically weakened in tanks and aircraft.

The offensive

The offensive opened on the third anniversary of the German invasion of the Soviet Union, Operation Barbarossa. Four Soviet fronts — the 1st Baltic Front, and the 1st, 2nd and 3rd Byelorussian Fronts, coordinated by Marshals Georgy Zhukov and Aleksandr Vasilevsky — attacked along a front of more than 1,000 kilometres. After a massive artillery and air bombardment, Soviet forces broke through at Vitebsk, Bobruisk, Mogilev and Orsha, encircling and destroying large German formations. Minsk was liberated on 3 July 1944, trapping some 100,000 German troops east of the city.

Aftermath

German losses were catastrophic — commonly estimated at around 450,000 killed, wounded and captured, including 57,000 prisoners paraded through Moscow on 17 July 1944. Army Group Centre effectively ceased to exist and had to be rebuilt with divisions transferred from other theatres. The Red Army advanced some 600 kilometres, liberating the Baltic states and eastern Poland and reaching the outskirts of Warsaw by early August. Together with the Allied landings in Normandy, Bagration placed Nazi Germany in an inescapable two-front vice.
