Divine providence

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


An allegory of Divine Providence

Divine providence, in theology, is God's intervention in the universe. The term Divine Providence (usually capitalized) is also used as a title of God. A distinction is usually made between "general providence", which refers to God's continuous upholding of the existence and natural order of the universe, and "special providence", which refers to God's extraordinary intervention in the life of people.

In Christian theology

Traditional Christian teaching holds that God not only created the world but continually sustains and governs it. Thinkers such as Thomas Aquinas taught that providence extends to all things, including particular events, while working through secondary causes and respecting human free will. The doctrine has raised classic questions about the problem of evil, the relation between providence and prayer, and the extent of divine foreknowledge and determination, debated between traditions such as Calvinism, with its strong doctrine of predestination, and Arminianism, which emphasizes free will and God's conditional providence.

In other traditions

Analogous ideas appear in Judaism, where divine providence is discussed as hashgachah, and in Islam, in the doctrine of qadar (divine decree), as well as in Stoic philosophy's concept of a rational order governing the cosmos.
