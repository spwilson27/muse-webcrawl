Aris Velouchiotis

Aris Velouchiotis (1905–1945), chief captain of ELAS.

Aris Velouchiotis (27 August 1905 – 16 June 1945), born Athanasios Klaras, was a Greek Communist journalist and guerrilla leader who founded and commanded the Greek People's Liberation Army (ELAS), the largest resistance organisation in Axis-occupied Greece. Under his nom de guerre — from Ares, the god of war, and Mount Velouchi — he became the most celebrated and controversial figure of the Greek Resistance.

Resistance leadership

A member of the Communist Party of Greece (KKE), Klaras was dispatched to the mountains of central Greece in 1942 to organise armed resistance for the National Liberation Front (EAM). He proclaimed the formation of ELAS at Domnista in June 1942 and led its forces in operations including the blowing up of the Gorgopotamos viaduct (November 1942), carried out with EDES guerrillas and British SOE officers — the most spectacular act of sabotage in occupied Greece. By 1944 ELAS controlled most of the Greek countryside.

Death

Velouchiotis opposed the Varkiza Agreement of February 1945, by which EAM agreed to disarm, and returned to the mountains to continue the struggle. Disowned by the KKE leadership under Nikos Zachariadis, he was hunted by government forces and died in June 1945 near Mesounta in Arta — by suicide or killed, according to differing accounts; his head was publicly displayed in Trikala. He remains a hero of the Greek left and a symbol of the resistance, while critics associate ELAS under his authority with violence against rival resistance groups.
