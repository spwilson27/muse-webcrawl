The Battle of Chappar Chiri was fought on 12 May 1764 between the Sikh Misls and the Afghan forces of the Durrani Empire under Zain Khan Sirhindi, the governor of Sirhind. It is remembered as one of the great Sikh victories of the eighteenth century and as retribution for the massacres of Sikhs carried out during the Afghan invasions of Ahmad Shah Durrani.

Background

In 1762 the Sikhs had suffered the Vadda Ghalughara ("Great Holocaust"), in which tens of thousands were killed by Durrani forces. Zain Khan, appointed governor of Sirhind, continued harsh measures against the Sikhs. In early 1764 the combined misls resolved to attack Sirhind itself, first defeating its governor in the field.

Battle

The Sikh forces, led by sardars including Jassa Singh Ahluwalia, met Zain Khan's army near the village of Chappar Chiri, close to modern Fatehgarh Sahib. In the ensuing battle the Afghan force was overwhelmed and Zain Khan was killed. The victory left Sirhind open to the Sikhs, who sacked the city in January of the following campaigning season.

Legacy

The battlefield is commemorated by the Fateh Burj ("Tower of Victory") at Chappar Chiri, among the tallest victory towers in India, completed in 2011 near the site where Banda Singh Bahadur had earlier defeated Wazir Khan at the Battle of Sirhind in 1710.
