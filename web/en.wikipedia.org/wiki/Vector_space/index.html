Vector space

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Two arrows (vectors) in a plane being added tip-to-tail]

A vector space (also called a linear space) is a set whose elements, called vectors, may be added together and multiplied ('scaled') by numbers called scalars, subject to axioms (associativity, commutativity, identity elements, distributivity) abstracted from geometric vectors.

Scalars come from a field — typically the real or complex numbers. Examples range from arrows in the plane and n-tuples of numbers to matrices, polynomials, and function spaces, which is why the concept pervades mathematics: linear algebra studies vector spaces and the linear maps between them.

Structure

Key notions include linear combinations, span, linear independence, basis (a minimal spanning independent set), and dimension (the number of basis vectors, invariant across bases). Subspaces are subsets closed under the operations; quotient spaces and dual spaces complete the basic landscape.

With an inner product, a vector space gains lengths and angles (geometry); normed and Hilbert spaces extend the structure into analysis, underlying functional analysis and quantum mechanics, where states are vectors in a complex Hilbert space.
