Upper motor neuron

The corticospinal tract, the principal upper motor neuron pathway.

Upper motor neurons (UMNs) are motor neurons that originate in the cerebral cortex (or brainstem) and carry motor commands down to the spinal cord or brainstem nuclei, where they synapse — directly or through interneurons — on lower motor neurons, which in turn innervate muscles. The term was introduced by William Gowers in the 19th century.

Pathways

The best-known UMN pathway is the corticospinal tract: cell bodies in the primary motor cortex (including the giant Betz cells of layer V) send axons through the internal capsule, cerebral peduncle, pons, and medullary pyramids, where most fibers decussate to form the lateral corticospinal tract. The corticobulbar tract supplies motor nuclei of the cranial nerves. Other descending systems — reticulospinal, vestibulospinal, rubrospinal, and tectospinal tracts — modulate posture, tone, and gross movement.

Upper motor neuron lesions

Damage to UMNs produces a characteristic syndrome distinct from lower motor neuron injury: weakness in a "pyramidal" distribution, spasticity (velocity-dependent increased tone), hyperreflexia, clonus, an extensor plantar response (Babinski sign), and loss of fine dexterity — without significant muscle atrophy or fasciculations. Causes include stroke, multiple sclerosis, spinal cord compression, and motor neuron diseases such as primary lateral sclerosis and the upper-motor-neuron component of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS).

Clinical significance

Distinguishing UMN from LMN signs is a cornerstone of neurological localization, indicating whether pathology lies in the central descending pathways or in the final common pathway to muscle.
