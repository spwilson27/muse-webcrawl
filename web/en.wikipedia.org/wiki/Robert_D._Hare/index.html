Robert D. Hare

Robert David Hare
Born: 1 January 1934, Calgary, Alberta, Canada
Field: Criminal psychology
Known for: Hare Psychopathy Checklist (PCL-R)

Robert David Hare (born 1934) is a Canadian forensic psychologist, known for his research on the psychology of psychopathy. He is a professor emeritus of psychology at the University of British Columbia and the developer of the Hare Psychopathy Checklist (PCL-R).

The PCL-R

The Psychopathy Checklist–Revised (1991, revised 2003) is a 20-item clinical rating scale scored from interviews and records, covering interpersonal/affective traits such as glibness, callousness and lack of remorse, and lifestyle/antisocial features such as impulsivity and criminal versatility. It is widely used in forensic settings to assess risk of reoffending, though Hare has cautioned against its misuse, including in labelling defendants in court.

Books and influence

His popular book Without Conscience (1993) brought psychopathy research to general readers, and with Paul Babiak he wrote Snakes in Suits (2006) on psychopaths in the workplace. Hare's two-factor model shaped modern research, while debates continue over the construct's boundaries and its relation to antisocial personality disorder.
