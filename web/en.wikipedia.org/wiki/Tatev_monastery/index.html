Tatev monastery

The Tatev Monastery (Armenian: Խաթթ՜ ջանօ) is a 9th-century Armenian Apostolic monastery located on a large basalt plateau near the village of Tatev in Syunik Province, southern Armenia, above the gorge of the Vorotan River. In the 14th and 15th centuries it hosted one of the most important medieval Armenian universities.

History

Founded in the 9th century on the site of an older shrine, the monastery became the seat of the bishops of Syunik and grew wealthy on its extensive lands. Its principal church of Saints Paul and Peter was built between 895 and 906. In the 14th century, under Hovhan Vorotnetsi and Grigor Tatevatsi, the University of Tatev flourished as a centre of philosophy, theology, science and manuscript illumination. The monastery declined after invasions and the 1931 Zangezur earthquake, and has been extensively restored since the late 20th century.

Notable features

The Gavazan is a remarkable octagonal pillar topped by a khachkar, erected in 904; it is hinged in such a way that it tilts in response to earth tremors or vibrations — possibly as an early seismic warning device — and returns to vertical. Since 2010 the monastery has been reached by the Wings of Tatev, at 5.7 km then the world's longest reversible aerial tramway, spanning the Vorotan gorge from Halidzor.
