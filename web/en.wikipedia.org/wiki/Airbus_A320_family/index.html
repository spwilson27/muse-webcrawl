Airbus A320 family

Airbus A320 family
Type: Narrow-body airliner family
Manufacturer: Airbus
First flight: 22 February 1987
Introduction: 18 April 1988 with Air France
Status: In production (as A320neo family)
Number built: Over 11,000 (all variants, by mid-2020s)

The Airbus A320 family is a series of narrow-body airliners developed and produced by Airbus. The A320 was launched in March 1984, first flew on 22 February 1987, and was introduced in April 1988 by Air France. The family pioneered fly-by-wire flight controls and side-stick controls in a commercial airliner.

Members

The original family (often called A320ceo, for current engine option) comprises:

  • A318 — shortened, 107–132 seats, introduced 2003.
  • A319 — shortened, 124–156 seats, introduced 1996.
  • A320 — baseline, 150–180 seats.
  • A321 — stretched, 185–236 seats, introduced 1994.

The re-engined A320neo family (new engine option), launched in 2010 and introduced by Lufthansa in 2016, offers improved fuel efficiency with CFM LEAP-1A or Pratt & Whitney PW1000G engines and Sharklet wingtips. It includes the long-range A321LR and A321XLR.

Development

Airbus sought to compete with the Boeing 737 and McDonnell Douglas MD-80 in the single-aisle market. The A320 introduced a digital fly-by-wire system, a glass cockpit, and a wider fuselage cross-section than the 737, allowing wider seats and larger overhead bins.

Production and competition

Final assembly takes place in Toulouse and Hamburg, Mobile, Alabama, and Tianjin, China. The A320 family surpassed the Boeing 737 to become the best-selling airliner family, with orders exceeding those of any other jet airliner programme.
