Bruges (Dutch: Brugge; French: Bruges) is the capital of the province of West Flanders in northwestern Belgium. Its remarkably preserved medieval centre, laced with canals, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Europe's most visited tourist destinations; the city is often called the "Venice of the North".

History

Bruges flourished from the 12th to the 15th century as one of the great commercial cities of northern Europe, linked by the Zwin channel to the North Sea and trading with the Hanseatic League, England and the Mediterranean. It was a centre of the Flemish cloth trade, of early banking — its Bourse gave its name to stock exchanges — and of Early Netherlandish painting, home to Jan van Eyck and Hans Memling. As the Zwin silted up, trade shifted to Antwerp and Bruges declined into a picturesque backwater, revived by tourism after Georges Rodenbach's novel Bruges-la-Morte (1892). The port of Zeebrugge, connected by canal in 1907, restored a maritime role.

Sights

Landmarks include the 13th-century Belfry on the Markt, the Church of Our Lady (whose brick tower is among the world's tallest, and which houses Michelangelo's Madonna and Child), the Basilica of the Holy Blood, the Beguinage, and the Groeningemuseum's collection of Flemish Primitives. The historic centre escaped major damage in both World Wars.
