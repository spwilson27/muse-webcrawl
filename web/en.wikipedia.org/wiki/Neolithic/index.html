Neolithic

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Reconstruction of a Neolithic farming village with round stone houses and early wheat fields]

The Neolithic (from Greek neos, 'new', and lithos, 'stone') is the final division of the Stone Age, characterised by the development of agriculture, the domestication of animals, and the establishment of permanent settlements. It began in the Fertile Crescent of the Near East around 10,000 BC, in what archaeologist V. Gordon Childe called the Neolithic Revolution, and spread — or arose independently — in China, South Asia, Mesoamerica and other regions. The period ends with the widespread use of metal tools in the Copper Age and Bronze Age.

Origins

As the last Ice Age ended, warming climates allowed communities in the Levant and upper Mesopotamia to intensify their use of wild cereals. Natufian hunter-gatherers built some of the first permanent villages; by about 9500 BC, sites such as Jericho and Göbekli Tepe show substantial settled life. Wheat and barley were domesticated in the Fertile Crescent, rice and millet in China, maize in Mesoamerica, and tubers in New Guinea. Sheep, goats, cattle and pigs were tamed for meat, milk, wool and labour.

Spread and society

Farming spread from Anatolia into Europe along the Danube and around the Mediterranean, reaching Britain by about 4000 BC, whether by migration of farmers or adoption by local foragers — most evidence now favours substantial migration. Neolithic villages built in mudbrick, stone and timber produced pottery, polished stone axes, woven textiles and megalithic monuments such as Stonehenge and the temples of Malta. Surpluses supported craft specialists, trade over hundreds of kilometres (obsidian, salt, shells), and growing social inequality visible in burials.

Consequences

Agriculture supported far denser populations than foraging, but early farmers were often shorter and less healthy than their hunter-gatherer ancestors, dependent on few crops and vulnerable to famine and new crowd diseases caught from livestock. The Neolithic nevertheless laid the foundations for cities, states and civilisation in the following Bronze Age.
