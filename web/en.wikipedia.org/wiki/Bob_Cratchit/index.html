Robert "Bob" Cratchit is a fictional character in Charles Dickens's 1843 novella A Christmas Carol. The abused, underpaid clerk of Ebenezer Scrooge, Cratchit embodies the cheerful, long-suffering poor of Victorian London and provides the emotional centre of the story through his family, especially his youngest son, the ill Tiny Tim.

Role in the story

Cratchit works in a frigid office for fifteen shillings a week, supporting a wife and six children. Scrooge reluctantly grants him Christmas Day off. In Stave Three, the Ghost of Christmas Present shows Scrooge the Cratchits' modest but joyful Christmas dinner, at which Bob toasts Scrooge as "the Founder of the Feast". The Ghost of Christmas Yet to Come reveals a future in which Tiny Tim has died. After his transformation, Scrooge raises Cratchit's salary and becomes "a second father" to Tiny Tim.

Interpretation

Dickens used Cratchit to dramatise the plight of the working poor and the moral responsibility of employers; the character's meagre wage and his employer's refusal to heat the office were pointed social commentary in 1843.

Portrayals

Cratchit has been played by many actors in adaptations, including Gene Lockhart (1938), Mervyn Johns (1951), David Collings (1977), Richard Wilson (1999) and Gary Oldman (voice, 2009). Kermit the Frog played the role in The Muppet Christmas Carol (1992).
