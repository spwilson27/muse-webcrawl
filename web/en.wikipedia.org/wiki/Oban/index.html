Oban

Oban (Scottish Gaelic: An t-Òban, meaning "the little bay") is a resort town within the Argyll and Bute council area of Scotland. Despite a population of only around 8,500, it is the largest town in Argyll and serves as an important port and ferry hub for the Hebrides.

History

The site has been inhabited since Mesolithic times; cave shelters around the bay have yielded some of the earliest evidence of human settlement in Scotland. The modern town dates largely from the late eighteenth century, growing around its distillery (founded 1794, before the town itself) and its fine natural harbor. The arrival of the railway in 1880 made Oban a fashionable Victorian resort, and it styled itself "the Charing Cross of the Highlands".

Above the town stands McCaig's Tower, a colossal granite folly built from 1895 by banker John Stuart McCaig, modeled on the Colosseum and intended as a monument and museum; it was left unfinished at his death.

Transport and tourism

Caledonian MacBrayne ferries sail from Oban to the islands of Mull, Coll, Tiree, Colonsay, Lismore, and Barra, making the town the principal mainland gateway to the Inner and Outer Hebrides. The town is also a rail terminus on the scenic West Highland Line from Glasgow. Oban Distillery produces a well-known single malt Scotch whisky, and the town markets itself as the "Seafood Capital of Scotland" for its harbor-side restaurants and stalls.
