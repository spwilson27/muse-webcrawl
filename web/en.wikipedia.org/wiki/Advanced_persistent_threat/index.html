Advanced persistent threat
Domain: Computer security
Typical actors: State-sponsored groups
Goals: Espionage, data theft
Methods: Spear phishing, zero-day exploits, long-term persistence

An advanced persistent threat (APT) is a stealthy, prolonged cyberattack in which an intruder gains and maintains unauthorised access to a network, remaining undetected for an extended period in order to steal data rather than cause immediate damage.

Characteristics

APTs are typically conducted by well-resourced, often state-sponsored groups targeting governments, defence contractors, financial institutions and critical infrastructure. Attackers commonly enter through spear phishing or zero-day exploits, establish persistence, escalate privileges, and exfiltrate information gradually.

Named groups

Security firms designate groups with labels such as APT1, APT28 (Fancy Bear) and APT29 (Cozy Bear). Defence against APTs relies on layered security, monitoring, and incident-response practices.
