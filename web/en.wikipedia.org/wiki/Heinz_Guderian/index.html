Heinz Wilhelm Guderian (17 June 1888 – 14 May 1954) was a German general, a pioneer of armoured warfare and a leading proponent of the tactics later called Blitzkrieg.

Early career

Commissioned in 1908, Guderian served in signals in World War I and in the interwar Reichswehr, studying motorised transport and tank theory, notably in his book Achtung – Panzer! (1937).

World War II

He commanded panzer corps in the invasions of Poland and France — his drive through the Ardennes to the Channel in 1940 was decisive — and the 2nd Panzer Group/Army in Operation Barbarossa, reaching the approaches to Moscow before being dismissed in December 1941 after disputes with Hitler.

Later war

Recalled in 1943 as Inspector General of Armoured Troops, he became Chief of the Army General Staff in July 1944 after the 20 July plot, serving until March 1945.

Postwar

Detained until 1948 but not tried at Nuremberg, Guderian wrote the memoir Panzer Leader, influential yet criticised for contributing to the myth of the 'clean Wehrmacht'.
