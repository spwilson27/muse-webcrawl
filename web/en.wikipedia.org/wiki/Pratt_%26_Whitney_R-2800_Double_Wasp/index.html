The Pratt & Whitney R-2800 Double Wasp is an American twin-row, 18-cylinder, air-cooled radial aircraft engine with a displacement of 2,800 cubic inches (46 litres). It is part of the long-lived Wasp family of radial engines.

Design and use

First run in 1939, the R-2800 produced roughly 2,000 horsepower in early versions and considerably more in later ones with water injection. It powered many of the most important American combat aircraft of the Second World War, including the F4U Corsair, the P-47 Thunderbolt, and the F6F Hellcat, as well as twin-engine types such as the B-26 Marauder.

Legacy

More than 125,000 R-2800s were built between 1939 and 1960. Post-war, it powered airliners such as the Douglas DC-6 and Martin 4-0-4, and examples remain in service in warbirds and utility aircraft.
