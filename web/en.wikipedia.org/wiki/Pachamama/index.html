Pachamama

Pachamama is a goddess revered by the indigenous peoples of the central Andes, including the Quechua and Aymara peoples. She is an earth and fertility deity — a "Mother Earth" figure who presides over planting and harvesting, and who embodies the mountains and the earth itself.

Worship

In Andean cosmology Pachamama is one of the most important deities, paired with the sun god Inti. Offerings known as pago or challa — including coca leaves, alcohol, food and llama fat — are made to her to ensure good harvests and protection, and August is traditionally her feast period, beginning with the Día de la Pachamama on 1 August.

Modern significance

Pachamama remains central to Andean identity and spirituality, often in syncretic form alongside Catholic practice. Her name has entered modern politics: Bolivia's 2009 constitution and its 2010 Law of the Rights of Mother Earth invoke Pachamama, and the concept of the rights of nature is widely associated with her cult.
