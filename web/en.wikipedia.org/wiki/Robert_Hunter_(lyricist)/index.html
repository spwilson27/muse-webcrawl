Robert C. Hunter (born Robert Burns; June 23, 1941 – September 23, 2019) was an American lyricist, singer-songwriter, translator, and poet, best known for his collaborations with Jerry Garcia and the Grateful Dead. Though not a performing member, he was considered an integral part of the band and was inducted with them into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1994.

Work with the Grateful Dead

Hunter met Jerry Garcia in Palo Alto in 1961 and played in early folk and jug bands with him. From 1967 he began sending lyrics to the Grateful Dead, beginning an almost three-decade partnership that produced many of the band's most enduring songs, among them "Dark Star", "Uncle John's Band", "Ripple", "Truckin'", "Friend of the Devil", "Terrapin Station", "Scarlet Begonias", "Touch of Grey", and "Box of Rain" (the last written for his dying father). Hunter typically wrote lyrics first, setting Garcia free to compose music to them; he also collaborated with other band members, including Bob Weir on songs such as "Playing in the Band" (in part) and with Phil Lesh.

Other work

Hunter released solo albums, including Tales of the Great Rum Runners (1974) and Tiger Rose (1975), performed his songs on tour, and wrote lyrics for artists including Bob Dylan (notably on Together Through Life, 2009), Bruce Hornsby, and Jim Lauderdale. He published poetry collections and translations of Rilke and Lorca. In 2015 he and Garcia were inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame. Hunter died at his home in San Rafael, California, in 2019.
