Max Dehn

Max Wilhelm Dehn (November 13, 1878 – June 27, 1952) was a German mathematician best known for his work in geometry, topology, and geometric group theory. Born in Hamburg, he was a student of David Hilbert at Göttingen.

Contributions

In 1900 Dehn solved Hilbert's third problem, proving that two polyhedra of equal volume are not necessarily scissors-congruent; he introduced the Dehn invariant to show that a cube and a regular tetrahedron of equal volume cannot be dissected into congruent pieces. The solution made him the first to resolve one of Hilbert's 23 problems. In topology he formulated Dehn's lemma (proved by Christos Papakyriakopoulos in 1957) and developed Dehn surgery, a fundamental construction of 3-manifolds. In group theory he posed the word, conjugacy, and isomorphism problems for finitely presented groups (1911), founding geometric group theory; Dehn's algorithm solves the word problem for surface groups. He also proved the Dehn–Nielsen theorem on mapping class groups.

Later life

Dehn taught at Frankfurt from 1921 until the Nazis forced him out in 1935. He emigrated via Scandinavia to the United States in 1940, teaching at Idaho Southern Branch, the Illinois Institute of Technology, St. John's College, and finally Black Mountain College in North Carolina, where he remained until his death in 1952.
