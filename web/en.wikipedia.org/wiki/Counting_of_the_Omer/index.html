Counting of the Omer

The Counting of the Omer (Hebrew: Sefirat HaOmer) is a Jewish ritual in which a verbal counting of each of the forty-nine days between the festivals of Passover and Shavuot is performed. The mitzvah is derived from Leviticus 23:15–16, which commands counting seven full weeks from the day after the Sabbath of Passover, when an omer (a measure) of barley was offered in the Temple.

The ritual

Each night after nightfall, a blessing is recited and the day is counted both in days and in weeks and days ("Today is twenty-three days, which is three weeks and two days of the Omer"). The period is traditionally one of semi-mourning — weddings and haircuts are commonly avoided — in memory of a plague said to have killed the students of Rabbi Akiva, except on Lag BaOmer, the 33rd day, when mourning is lifted. Kabbalistic tradition associates each week and day with combinations of the seven lower sefirot, making the count a period of spiritual refinement.
