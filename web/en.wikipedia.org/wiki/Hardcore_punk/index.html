Hardcore punk
Origin: Late 1970s, United States and United Kingdom
Features: Fast tempos, shouted vocals, short songs, DIY ethic
Key bands: Black Flag, Minor Threat, Bad Brains, Dead Kennedys, Discharge

Hardcore punk (often just hardcore) is a punk rock subgenre that emerged in the late 1970s, played faster, harder and more aggressively than earlier punk, with an emphasis on do-it-yourself ethics.

Origins

Scenes formed in Washington D.C., California, New York and elsewhere around bands such as Bad Brains, Black Flag, Minor Threat and the Dead Kennedys, and in the UK around Discharge. Independent labels like SST and Dischord, fanzines and all-ages shows built an underground network.

Characteristics and culture

Songs are often under two minutes, with shouted vocals and themes of politics, anti-authoritarianism and personal integrity. Subcultures included straight edge — abstinence from alcohol and drugs, associated with Minor Threat — as well as skate punk and youth crew.

Evolution

Hardcore influenced metalcore, post-hardcore, emo, grindcore and thrash metal, and later waves from 1990s to today keep the DIY touring and label model.
