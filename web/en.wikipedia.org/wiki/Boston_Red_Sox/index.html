Boston Red Sox

The Boston Red Sox are an American professional baseball team based in Boston. They compete in Major League Baseball as a member club of the American League East Division. Founded in 1901 as one of the American League's eight charter franchises, the team has played its home games at Fenway Park since 1912.

Boston Red Sox
Established: 1901

History

The Red Sox dominated early baseball, winning five of the first fifteen World Series, with stars such as Cy Young and Babe Ruth. After selling Ruth to the New York Yankees following the 1918 season, the club entered an 86-year championship drought attributed to the "Curse of the Bambino". Legends of the drought era included Ted Williams, Carl Yastrzemski, and Carlton Fisk, whose 12th-inning home run won Game 6 of the 1975 World Series.

The curse ends

In 2004, the Red Sox came back from a 3–0 deficit against the Yankees in the American League Championship Series — the first such comeback in MLB history — and swept the St. Louis Cardinals in the World Series. Further titles followed in 2007, 2013, and 2018.

Fenway Park

Fenway Park, the oldest ballpark in Major League Baseball, is famed for the Green Monster, the 37-foot left-field wall. The Red Sox' rivalry with the New York Yankees is among the most intense in American sport.
