Bofors

Founded1646 (ironworks); 1873 (AB Bofors-Gullspång)
HeadquartersKarlskoga, Sweden

FateDefence business split; parts now in Saab Bofors Dynamics and BAE Systems Bofors; steel in Outokumpu

Bofors AB is a Swedish arms manufacturer based in Karlskoga, whose name has been associated with the iron and steel industry since 1646 and with artillery since the 1880s. Its most famous product, the Bofors 40 mm anti-aircraft gun introduced in the 1930s, was used by nearly every combatant in the Second World War and remains in service in updated forms worldwide. Alfred Nobel owned the company from 1894 until his death in 1896 and reshaped it from an ironworks into a modern armaments and chemical concern.

History

The Bofors works originated in a hammer mill established at Bofors in 1646. In the late 19th century the firm moved from iron into steel and, after 1883, into guns under managing director Carl Danielsson. Acquired by Nobel in 1894, Bofors expanded into explosives and high-quality ordnance steel. During the interwar period it developed the 40 mm L/60 gun, licensed and copied internationally, and supplied artillery to many nations while Sweden remained neutral.

Later developments

In the 1980s the company was at the center of the Bofors scandal in India, in which allegations of kickbacks on a 1986 howitzer contract became a major political affair and contributed to the fall of Rajiv Gandhi's government. In 1991 Bofors merged its explosives interests into Nobel Industries and later Celsius; the defence business was eventually divided between Saab (missiles, as Saab Bofors Dynamics) and United Defense/BAE Systems (guns, as BAE Systems Bofors), while the stainless steel operations became part of Outokumpu.
