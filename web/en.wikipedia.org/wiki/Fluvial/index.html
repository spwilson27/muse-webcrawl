Fluvial

Fluvial processes
The term fluvial refers to processes associated with rivers and streams.

In geography and geology, fluvial is a term used to refer to processes associated with rivers and streams and the deposits and landforms created by them. When streams or rivers are associated with glaciers, ice sheets, or ice caps, the term glaciofluvial or fluvioglacial is used.

Fluvial processes

Fluvial processes include the motion of sediment and erosion or deposition on the river bed. Erosion by moving water can happen in two ways. Firstly, the movement of water across the stream bed exerts a shear stress directly onto the bed. If the cohesive strength of the substrate is lower than the shear exerted, or the bed is composed of loose sediment which can be mobilized by such stresses, then the bed will be lowered purely by clearwater flow. Secondly, abrasion by the sediment load carried by the current wears the bed down.

Sediment is transported in three ways: as bed load, rolling and sliding along the bottom; as suspended load, carried within the water column; and as dissolved load (solute), carried in solution. The capacity of a stream to transport sediment depends on its discharge and velocity, while the particle sizes it can move constitute its competence. Where flow slows — on the inside of a meander, in a lake or delta, or across a floodplain during floods — deposition occurs, building landforms.

Fluvial landforms

  • Meanders and oxbow lakes
  • Floodplains and natural levees
  • Deltas
  • Alluvial fans and bajadas
  • Braided rivers and anastomosing channels
  • River terraces
  • Waterfalls and rapids
  • V-shaped valleys, gorges, and canyons
  • Point bars and slip-off slopes

Fluvial deposits

Material deposited by a river is called alluvium. Fluvial deposits are typically sorted by energy: coarse gravels in channel bars and lags, sands on point bars and levees, and silts and clays in overbank floodplain settings. Stacked fluvial deposits in the geological record preserve evidence of ancient river systems, and fluvial sandstones and conglomerates form important aquifers and hydrocarbon reservoirs. In placer mining, dense minerals such as gold are concentrated in fluvial gravels.

Fluvial geomorphology

The study of rivers and their landforms is called fluvial geomorphology. It examines drainage basin evolution, channel patterns (straight, meandering, braided), the graded profile of the long profile, and the response of rivers to changes in climate, base level, and tectonics. Foundational concepts include William Morris Davis's cycle of erosion and the quantitative work of Luna Leopold and others in the mid-twentieth century, which related channel form to discharge and sediment load.
