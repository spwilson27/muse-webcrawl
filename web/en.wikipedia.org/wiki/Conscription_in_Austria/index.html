Conscription in Austria

Austria maintains universal male conscription for the Austrian Armed Forces (Bundesheer). Under the Austrian constitution and the Defence Act (Wehrgesetz), all male citizens are liable for military service from the age of 17 until 50 (with practical call-up concentrated in the years after turning 18); the basic service obligation is six months of compulsory military training (Grundwehrdienst), after which conscripts pass into the militia reserve and may be recalled for exercises and, in a crisis, for service.

Alternative civilian service

Since 1974, recognized conscientious objectors may perform alternative civilian service (Zivildienst) instead of military service. The civilian service lasts nine months — longer than military service — and is carried out mainly in social and health institutions, hospitals, care homes, the Red Cross and other emergency services, and civil protection organizations. Women are not conscripted but may volunteer for military service on equal terms since 1998.

The 2013 referendum

The future of conscription was settled by a national referendum on 20 January 2013 — the first consultative referendum of its kind in the Second Republic. Voters were asked to choose between retaining conscription (with civilian service) and replacing it with a professional volunteer army and a voluntary social year. About 59.7 per cent voted to keep conscription, and the system was retained, with subsequent reforms adjusting training and the militia structure.

History

Modern Austrian conscription descends from the universal service introduced in the Habsburg monarchy in 1868 and was re-established after the restoration of Austrian sovereignty by the 1955 State Treaty, which permitted a conscript army. During the Cold War the Bundesheer was built around a large militia mobilization system designed for territorial defence and Austrian neutrality; service length, once as long as eight months, was shortened in stages to six months by 2006.
