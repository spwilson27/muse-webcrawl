Pastry cream

Vanilla pastry cream (crème pâtissière).

Pastry cream, in French crème pâtissière, is a thick custard made from milk or cream, egg yolks, sugar, and a starch thickener such as flour or cornstarch, usually flavoured with vanilla. It is one of the foundational preparations of French pâtisserie and is used as a filling for éclairs, fruit tarts, mille-feuille, choux pastry, and many other desserts.

Preparation

Milk is heated with vanilla (often a split pod). Egg yolks are whisked with sugar until pale, then the starch is mixed in. Hot milk is poured over the yolk mixture while whisking (tempering), and the combined custard is returned to the heat and boiled briefly — unlike a stirred custard such as crème anglaise, pastry cream must come to a full boil so the starch gelatinises and the enzymes in the yolk that would thin the cream are deactivated. The finished cream is strained, its surface covered directly with film to prevent a skin, and chilled.

Variations

  • Crème légère — pastry cream lightened with whipped cream.
  • Crème mousseline — pastry cream enriched with butter, used in Paris-Brest and fraisier.
  • Crème chiboust — pastry cream combined with Italian meringue, the classic filling of the Saint Honoré cake.
  • Frangipane-style blends — pastry cream mixed with almond cream for fruit tarts and galette des rois.

Because pastry cream contains eggs and milk, it is perishable: it is typically kept refrigerated and used within a few days, and fillings containing it should not be frozen.
