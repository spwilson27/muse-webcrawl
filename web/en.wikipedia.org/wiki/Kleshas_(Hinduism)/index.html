Kleshas (Hinduism)

The five kleshas are the root afflictions that bind the self to samsara, according to Patanjali's Yoga Sutras.

Kleshas (Sanskrit: क्लेश, romanized: kleśa) is a Sanskrit term meaning "affliction" or "defilement". In Hindu philosophy, particularly in the Yoga school as presented in Patanjali's Yoga Sutras (2.3), the kleshas are five afflictions of the mind that are the root cause of suffering and of bondage to samsara, the cycle of rebirth. They are said to colour (or "taint") the mind and to obstruct the discernment of the true self (purusha).

The five kleshas

  • Avidya — ignorance or misapprehension of reality; mistaking the non-eternal for the eternal, the impure for the pure, suffering for happiness, and the non-self for the self. It is called the "field" in which the other four grow.
  • Asmita — egoism or I-am-ness; the identification of the seer with the instrument of seeing (mind, body, senses).
  • Raga — attachment, desire, and craving that follow from remembered pleasure.
  • Dvesha — aversion and hatred that follow from remembered pain.
  • Abhinivesha — the clinging to life and fear of death, present instinctively even in the learned.

Role in yoga practice

The Yoga Sutras describe the kleshas as existing in four states: dormant, attenuated, interrupted, and active. They are weakened by kriya yoga — austerity (tapas), self-study (svadhyaya), and devotion to Ishvara (Ishvara-pranidhana) — and are finally burnt away, "like seeds roasted", through discriminative discernment (viveka), so that they can no longer produce karma and rebirth.

Comparison with Buddhism

A cognate concept exists in Buddhism, where kleshas (Pali: kilesa) are mental states that cloud the mind, such as greed, hatred, and delusion, and that drive the wheel of becoming. The Hindu and Buddhist lists and treatments overlap but differ in detail and in the metaphysical framework — Buddhism classically enumerates the three poisons and ten fetters, whereas the yogic tradition organises them under the fivefold scheme of Patanjali.
