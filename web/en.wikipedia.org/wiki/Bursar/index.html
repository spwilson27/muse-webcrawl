A bursar (derived from the Latin bursa, meaning "purse") is a professional financial administrator in a school or university. In the United States, bursars are usually officials at the highest level of a university, accounting for tuition bills and other fees, while in the United Kingdom and other Commonwealth countries the bursar of a college or independent school is often a senior administrative officer responsible for the institution's finances, estates and non-academic operations.

Responsibilities

Depending on the institution, a bursar may oversee student billing and accounts receivable, manage budgets and payroll, administer endowment and fee income, supervise catering, maintenance and grounds, and advise the governing body on financial planning. At the collegiate universities of Oxford and Cambridge, the bursar is typically a fellow of the college with charge of its finances and domestic affairs; titles such as "domestic bursar" and "estates bursar" divide operational and property duties.

History

The office originated in medieval monasteries and colleges, where the bursar was the officer entrusted with the common purse, receiving revenues and disbursing funds for food, stipends and building works. The related term "bursary" denotes a scholarship or grant awarded to a student, reflecting the same root.
