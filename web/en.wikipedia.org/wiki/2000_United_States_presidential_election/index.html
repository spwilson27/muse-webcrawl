The 2000 United States presidential election was the 54th quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, 7 November 2000. Republican candidate George W. Bush, the governor of Texas and son of former president George H. W. Bush, defeated incumbent Democratic Vice President Al Gore. It was one of the closest elections in American history and the first since 1888 in which the winner of the Electoral College lost the national popular vote by a clear margin.

The Florida recount

The election hinged on Florida's 25 electoral votes. On election night the networks first called Florida for Gore, retracted it, then called it for Bush before retracting again. Bush's official lead shrank to a few hundred votes, triggering an automatic machine recount and then weeks of manual recounts, lawsuits and arguments over "hanging chads" on punch-card ballots and the confusing Palm Beach County "butterfly ballot", which apparently diverted thousands of Gore votes to Reform Party candidate Pat Buchanan. Green Party candidate Ralph Nader's 97,488 Florida votes also exceeded the final margin many times over.

Bush v. Gore and outcome

On 12 December 2000 the US Supreme Court, in Bush v. Gore, ruled 5–4 that the Florida Supreme Court's order for a statewide manual recount of undervotes violated the Equal Protection Clause and, with the federal safe-harbour deadline that day, effectively ended the recount. Gore conceded the next day. The certified Florida result gave Bush the state by 537 votes out of nearly six million cast. In the Electoral College, Bush won 271 votes to Gore's 266 (one District of Columbia elector abstained). The election spurred the Help America Vote Act of 2002 and a lasting overhaul of voting technology.
