Jackson Pollock

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Jackson Pollock at work in 1950: moving around a canvas spread on the floor, dripping and flinging black enamel paint from a stick

Paul Jackson Pollock (28 January 1912 – 11 August 1956) was an American painter and a major figure in the abstract expressionist movement. He was widely noticed for his 'drip technique' of pouring or splashing liquid household paint onto a horizontal surface, enabling him to view and paint his canvases from all angles — a method associated with 'action painting'.

Working in Springs, Long Island, Pollock produced his most famous drip paintings between 1947 and 1950, including Number 1A, 1948, Autumn Rhythm (Number 30) and Lavender Mist. Life magazine in 1949 asked whether he was 'the greatest living painter in the United States'. He died at 44 in an alcohol-related car crash. His drip paintings have since achieved some of the highest prices in art.
