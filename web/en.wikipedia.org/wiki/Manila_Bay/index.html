Manila Bay (Filipino: Look ng Maynila) is a natural harbour that serves the Port of Manila in the Philippines. Strategically located around the capital city, the bay facilitated trade and commerce between the Philippines and neighbouring countries, becoming the gateway for the country's socio-economic development. The bay is considered one of the finest natural harbours in Southeast Asia and was used as a waterway well before the Spanish colonial period.

Geography

The bay is 48 km wide at its entrance and 19 km at its widest, bordered by the provinces of Cavite and Metro Manila to the east, Bulacan and Pampanga to the north, and Bataan to the west. The island of Corregidor guards the entrance, dividing it into the North Channel and South Channel. Major rivers, including the Pasig and Pampanga, drain into the bay.

History

Manila Bay was the setting of the Battle of Manila Bay in 1898, in which Commodore George Dewey's U.S. Asiatic Squadron destroyed the Spanish Pacific Squadron at the start of the Spanish–American War. During the Second World War, Corregidor and the bay saw heavy fighting in 1942 and again in 1945. Today the baywalk along Roxas Boulevard is famous for its sunsets, while reclamation projects and water pollution remain subjects of continuing public debate and rehabilitation programmes.

Ecology

The bay supports fisheries and coastal wetlands important for migratory birds, such as the Las Piñas–Parañaque Critical Habitat. Years of industrial and domestic pollution led to government clean-up drives, including the "Battle for Manila Bay" rehabilitation launched in 2019.
