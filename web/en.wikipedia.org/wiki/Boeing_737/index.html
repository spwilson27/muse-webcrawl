Boeing 737

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A Boeing 737 in Ryanair-style livery on final approach]
A 737 landing.

The Boeing 737 is an American narrow-body jet airliner produced by Boeing Commercial Airplanes, and the best-selling airliner family in history, with over 12,000 delivered since 1967. Designed as a short-haul complement to the 707 and 727, it evolved through the Original, Classic and Next Generation (737NG) series to the re-engined 737 MAX — whose two fatal crashes (2018, 2019) and global grounding (2019–20) caused one of the worst crises in Boeing\'s history.

Evolution

The 737-100/200 (1967–68) put engines under a low wing on a short fuselage; the Classic (-300/-400/-500) added CFM56 turbofans; the NG (-600 to -900ER, 1997) brought a new wing and glass cockpit. Ryanair, Southwest (its launch-era champion) and dozens of others built low-cost empires on the type.

737 MAX and crisis

The MAX (2016) added larger LEAP-1B engines; to handle their pitch effects Boeing fitted MCAS flight software, whose single-sensor activation caused Lion Air 610 (2018) and Ethiopian 302 (2019), killing 346 people. The 20-month grounding, recertification, criminal and civil settlements, and production scrutiny — renewed after a 2024 door-plug blowout on a 737 MAX 9 — reshaped Boeing and FAA oversight.

Today

The MAX family (7/8/8200/9/10, the -7 and -10 still uncertified as of the mid-2020s) competes with the Airbus A320neo family, which overtook the 737 in orders and, in 2025, in cumulative deliveries of a single family line according to industry trackers. Military versions (P-8 Poseidon, E-7) extend the line.
