Conan the Barbarian (also known as Conan the Cimmerian) is a fictional sword and sorcery hero created by American author Robert E. Howard in 1932 for a series of fantasy stories published in Weird Tales magazine. The character was popularized through books, comics, films, television programs, video games, and role-playing games, and is regarded as the archetypal barbarian hero and a foundational figure of the sword-and-sorcery subgenre.

The character

Conan is born in Cimmeria, a northern land of the fictional Hyborian Age, set after the sinking of Atlantis. Across the stories he rises from tribal youth to thief, outlaw, pirate, and mercenary, and eventually seizes the throne of Aquilonia, the most powerful civilized kingdom of the age, deposing and killing its king. Howard's Conan is quick-witted, multilingual, and a gifted leader, in addition to being a formidable swordsman.

Stories and legacy

Howard wrote 21 Conan works in his lifetime, four of them novels or novellas including The Hour of the Dragon. After his death in 1936, authors such as L. Sprague de Camp and Lin Carter edited, completed, and extended the series. Frank Frazetta's paperback covers in the 1960s fixed Conan's visual image, and the 1982 film Conan the Barbarian, starring Arnold Schwarzenegger, made the character a global pop-culture icon.
