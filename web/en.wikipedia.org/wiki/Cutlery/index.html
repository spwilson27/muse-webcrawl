Cutlery

[Image: A formal table setting with knife, forks, and spoons arranged by course]

Cutlery (also called silverware, flatware, or tableware in some usages) comprises the hand instruments used for preparing, serving, and eating food in Western culture, principally the knife, fork, and spoon. The word comes from the Old French coutellerie, from coutel, "knife". Historically the term also covered the cutler's craft of making edged tools generally.

History

Knives and spoons are ancient, but the table fork spread through Europe only from the Byzantine and Italian courts of the late Middle Ages, reaching general use in Britain and North America in the 18th century. Standardised place settings with multiple specialised pieces — fish knives, dessert spoons, oyster forks — flourished in the Victorian era. Sheffield in England and Solingen in Germany became great centres of cutlery manufacture; Sheffield gave its name to Sheffield plate and pioneered stainless steel cutlery after Harry Brearley's 1913 invention.

Materials and manufacture

Cutlery was traditionally made of silver or silver plate for the wealthy, and of pewter, iron, or steel for others. Modern cutlery is overwhelmingly stainless steel, graded by composition (18/8 or 18/10 chromium-nickel content); plastic and wooden disposable cutlery appeared in the twentieth century, and biodegradable alternatives are now promoted on environmental grounds.

Etiquette

Formal Western etiquette arranges cutlery in the order of use, working inward course by course, with the knife blade facing the plate and dessert implements above. Styles of holding the fork differ between the European (fork in left hand throughout) and American ("zigzag") methods.
