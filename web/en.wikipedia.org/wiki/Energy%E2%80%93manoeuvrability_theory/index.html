Energy–manoeuvrability theory

DeveloperJohn Boyd, Thomas Christie
FieldAir combat, fighter design

Energy–manoeuvrability theory (E–M theory) is a model of aircraft performance developed by United States Air Force Colonel John Boyd and mathematician Thomas P. Christie in the 1960s. It describes an aircraft's ability to change its energy state and manoeuvre as a function of altitude, speed, and configuration.

Core concepts

E–M theory centres on specific energy — the total energy of the aircraft per unit weight — and specific excess power (Ps), the rate at which the aircraft can gain or loses energy at a given flight condition. Plotting Ps contours on "doghouse" diagrams lets designers and pilots compare fighters across their envelopes and identify the altitudes and speeds at which each performs best.

Influence

Analysis using E–M theory, informed by combat experience in Korea and Vietnam, underpinned the "Fighter Mafia" critique of contemporary American fighter design and influenced the requirements for the F-15 Eagle and especially the lightweight F-16 Fighting Falcon.
