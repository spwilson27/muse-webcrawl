Seljuk Empire

The Seljuk Empire (Turkish: Büyük Selçuklu İmparatorluğu; Persian: امپراتوری سلجوقی), also known as the Great Seljuk Empire, was a high medieval empire founded by the Oghuz Turks under Tughril and Chaghri Beg. At its height in the late 11th century it controlled a vast area stretching from western Anatolia and the Levant to the Hindu Kush and from Central Asia to the Persian Gulf, covering some 3.9 million square kilometres.

History

The Seljuks were a clan of the Oghuz Turks who migrated from the Eurasian steppe into Persia. After defeating the Ghaznavids at the Battle of Dandanqan (1040), Tughril entered Baghdad in 1055, where the Abbasid caliph recognised him as sultan. His successor Alp Arslan defeated the Byzantine Empire at the Battle of Manzikert in 1071, opening Anatolia to Turkish settlement. Under Malik-Shah I and his vizier Nizam al-Mulk, the empire reached its zenith; Nizam founded the network of Nizamiyya madrasas and wrote the Siyasatnama (Book of Government).

Decline and legacy

After Malik-Shah's death in 1092 the empire fragmented into competing sultanates and atabeg states, including the Sultanate of Rum in Anatolia. The last Great Seljuk sultan of Persia, Toghrul III, was killed in 1194 by the Khwarazmshah. Seljuk rule fostered Persianate culture, monumental architecture such as the Friday Mosque of Isfahan, and the careers of scholars including Omar Khayyam, who helped reform the calendar under Malik-Shah.
