Bardstown is a city in Nelson County, Kentucky, United States. The population was approximately 13,700 at the 2020 census. It is the county seat of Nelson County and styles itself the "Bourbon Capital of the World", hosting the annual Kentucky Bourbon Festival and numerous bourbon whiskey distilleries in and around the town.

History

First settled in 1780, Bardstown was chartered in 1788 and named for the Bard brothers, William Bard and David Bard, who obtained the land for settlement. It was one of the earliest towns in Kentucky and an early center of Roman Catholicism west of the Appalachian Mountains; the Basilica of St. Joseph Proto-Cathedral, completed in 1819, was the first Catholic cathedral west of the Alleghenies.

Geography

Bardstown lies in the rolling hills of central Kentucky, roughly 35 miles southeast of Louisville. The historic downtown is organized around Court Square, with the Nelson County Courthouse at its center, and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Bourbon industry

The area's limestone water, corn supply, and climate made it a center of whiskey production from the late 18th century. Distilleries in or near Bardstown include Heaven Hill, Barton 1792, Willett, and Lux Row. The town is a stop on the Kentucky Bourbon Trail, and the Kentucky Bourbon Festival has been held there each September since 1991.

Landmarks

My Old Kentucky Home State Park preserves Federal Hill, the plantation house associated with composer Stephen Foster's ballad "My Old Kentucky Home". Other landmarks include the Oscar Getz Museum of Whiskey History, the Old Talbott Tavern (1779), and St. Joseph Proto-Cathedral.
