Rice transplanter

A rice transplanter is a specialised agricultural machine fitted to transplant rice seedlings onto a paddy field. It replaces the labour-intensive hand transplanting of seedlings from a nursery bed, dramatically reducing the labour required to establish a rice crop.

Operation

Seedlings are raised in dense mats in a nursery and loaded onto the machine as trays or mats. As the transplanter moves across the flooded, levelled paddy, mechanical planting arms pick small clumps of seedlings from the mat and press them into the mud at regular spacing and shallow depth. Precise planting depth and spacing improve tillering and yield compared with broadcasting seed.

Types

Two main types exist: the riding type, an engine-powered machine with four wheels on which the operator sits, used for large fields; and the walking type, guided by an operator walking behind it, suited to smaller paddies and terraces. Modern Japanese and Korean manufacturers such as Kubota, Yanmar and Iseki dominate the market, and high-end models add GPS guidance and automatic steering.

Adoption

Mechanised transplanting is standard in Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and much of China, and is spreading in South and Southeast Asia as rural labour becomes scarcer and more expensive. It requires carefully levelled fields and synchronised nursery production of seedlings at the correct age.
