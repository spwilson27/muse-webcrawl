American Alpine Journal

The American Alpine Journal is an annual magazine, and journal of record, published by the American Alpine Club. It is the world's most significant annual record of major new climbs and expeditions, documenting ascents in the greater ranges of the world.

History

The journal was first published in 1929 and has appeared annually ever since. Its "Climbs and Expeditions" section collects reports of significant new routes and first ascents from mountain regions worldwide, written largely by the climbers themselves, while feature articles present longer expedition narratives and essays. Long-serving editors have included H. Adams Carter, who edited the journal for several decades, and Christian Beckwith.

Role in mountaineering

Because it functions as the reference of record for Himalayan, Andean, Alaskan, and other high-mountain activity, the journal is a key source for verifying first ascents and route histories, and is used by researchers, guidebook authors, and databases such as the Himalayan Database. In recent years the club has digitised its back catalogue and made reports searchable online.
