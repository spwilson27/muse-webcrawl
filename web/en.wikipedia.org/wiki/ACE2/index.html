ACE2

Angiotensin-converting enzyme 2
Gene: ACE2 (human)
Chromosome: X (Xp22.2)
Function: carboxypeptidase; receptor for SARS-CoV and SARS-CoV-2

Angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) is an enzyme that can be found either attached to the membrane of cells in the intestines, kidney, testis, gallbladder, and heart, or in a soluble form. ACE2 is an entry receptor for some coronaviruses, including HCoV-NL63, SARS-CoV, and SARS-CoV-2, the virus that causes COVID-19.

Function

ACE2 is a zinc-containing metalloenzyme and carboxypeptidase. As part of the renin–angiotensin system (RAS), it converts the hormone angiotensin II into angiotensin (1–7), a vasodilator, thereby counterbalancing the effects of the related enzyme ACE. Through this activity ACE2 helps regulate blood pressure and fluid balance and has protective effects in the cardiovascular and respiratory systems.

Role in coronavirus infection

The spike (S) protein of SARS-CoV and SARS-CoV-2 binds to ACE2 on the cell surface, and this binding, together with priming by host proteases such as TMPRSS2, allows the virus to enter the cell. The distribution of ACE2 in the human body helps explain the tissues most affected by COVID-19.

Clinical significance

Recombinant human ACE2 has been investigated as a decoy treatment for COVID-19, intended to bind the virus before it reaches cells. ACE2 levels and activity are also studied in hypertension, diabetes, and heart failure.
