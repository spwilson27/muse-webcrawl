HITECH Act

The Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health Act, abbreviated as the HITECH Act, is a United States federal law enacted as Title XIII of the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009 and signed by President Barack Obama on February 17, 2009. It allocated tens of billions of dollars to promote the adoption and "meaningful use" of electronic health records (EHRs) and strengthened the privacy and security provisions of HIPAA.

Provisions

The Act created incentive programs under Medicare and Medicaid that paid hospitals and clinicians for adopting certified EHR technology and demonstrating "meaningful use" — later reorganised under the Merit-based Incentive Payment System. It established the Office of the National Coordinator for Health Information Technology in statute, funded regional extension centres and health information exchanges, and imposed direct liability on business associates under HIPAA, with tiered civil penalties and mandatory breach notification.

Impact

EHR adoption among US hospitals rose from under 10% in 2008 to the overwhelming majority within a decade, reshaping clinical documentation, billing and health data exchange. The program has also been criticised for usability burdens, vendor lock-in and "information blocking", addressed in part by the 21st Century Cures Act of 2016.
