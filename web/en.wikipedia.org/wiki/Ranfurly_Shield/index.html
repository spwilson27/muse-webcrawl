The Ranfurly Shield, colloquially known as the "Log o' Wood," is a trophy in New Zealand rugby union and one of the country's most revered sporting prizes. It operates on a challenge system: the holding provincial union must defend the Shield in home matches against challengers, and any challenger that wins takes the Shield away.

History

The Shield was presented in 1902 by the Earl of Ranfurly, Governor of New Zealand, and first competed for in 1904, when Wellington became its inaugural holder by virtue of the best record that season; Auckland won the first actual challenge match in 1905. Originally decorated with an image of association football by mistake (later corrected), the trophy is a large wooden shield adorned with small silver shields recording each tenure.

Traditions and notable tenures

Shield matches are famous for dramatic last-minute challenges and for the joy of smaller unions "lifting the Log." Auckland's tenure from 1985 to 1993, spanning 61 successful defences, is the longest in history, while Canterbury, Waikato, and Wellington have also compiled famous eras. Famous moments include Hawke's Bay's long 1920s tenure and Taranaki's and Southland's celebrated wins. The Shield is contested during the National Provincial Championship season, and holders are obliged to accept challenges, including from unions in the Heartland Championship in pre-season matches.
