Osso buco

Osso buco or ossobuco (Italian for "bone with a hole", from osso "bone" and buco "hole") is a Milanese speciality of cross-cut veal shanks braised with vegetables, white wine and stock, usually garnished with gremolata and traditionally served with risotto alla milanese. The cut is taken across the leg bone, so each portion contains meat surrounding a section of bone whose marrow is considered a delicacy and is often eaten with a small spoon or fork.

Preparation

The shanks are seasoned, dredged in flour and browned, then braised slowly with onion, carrot, celery, tomatoes (in modern versions), white wine and meat stock until the meat is tender enough to fall from the bone. In the older Milanese style (in bianco) tomatoes are omitted and the dish is flavoured with cinnamon, bay and gremolata—a chopped garnish of lemon zest, garlic and parsley added at the end of cooking to cut the richness of the meat and marrow.

History

Osso buco is generally dated to the 19th century and is associated with the osterie of Milan. It became a canonical dish of Lombard cuisine, paired with saffron risotto; the combination is so standard that ossobuco is rarely served in Milan with any other accompaniment. Outside Italy the name is often applied to similar braised shank dishes made with beef or pork.
