Scandinavian cross

Nordic flags displaying the Scandinavian (Nordic) cross.

A Scandinavian cross, also called a Nordic cross, is a flag pattern in which a cross extends to the edges of the flag, with the vertical bar shifted toward the hoist side. The design is borne by the national flags of all five Nordic countries — Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Finland and Iceland — as well as by many regional, municipal and other flags in Northern Europe.

Origins

The oldest of the designs, the Dannebrog of Denmark, dates in legend to 1219 and is the oldest continuously used national flag. The cross symbolised Christianity, and Denmark's flag served as the model for the others: Sweden adopted its yellow cross on blue in the 16th century, Norway combined the Danish cross with Swedish colours in 1821, Finland adopted its blue cross on white in 1918, and Iceland its own design in 1915 (civil) and 1944.

Use beyond the national flags

The pattern appears in the flags of the autonomous territories of Åland, the Faroe Islands and (unofficially) Greenland proposals, in many Norwegian and Swedish municipal and county flags, in German flags such as that of Nordfriesland and Oldenburg, in Scottish flags such as Shetland and Orkney, and in proposed flags for regions with Norse heritage. The shared design is a potent symbol of Nordic unity and the Nordic Council.
