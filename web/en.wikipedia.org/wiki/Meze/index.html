Meze (also spelled mezze) is a selection of small dishes served as appetizers or as a light meal in parts of the Middle East, the Balkans, and the eastern Mediterranean, in cuisines including Turkish, Greek, Lebanese, Armenian, and Cypriot cooking. The word derives from the Turkish meze, itself from the Persian maza (مزه), meaning "taste" or "snack."

A traditional meze spread shared at the table.

Customs

Meze are typically served to accompany alcoholic drinks such as rakı, ouzo, or wine, or as the opening course of a larger meal. In Turkey, a rakı sofrası (rakı table) may feature dozens of cold and hot meze shared over several hours. In Greece, small plates are often ordered communally, while in the Levant a meze spread can constitute an entire meal.

Common dishes

  • Hummus — chickpea and tahini dip
  • Baba ghanoush / melitzanosalata — smoked eggplant dip
  • Tzatziki / cacık — yogurt, cucumber, and garlic
  • Dolma / dolmades — stuffed vine leaves
  • Tabbouleh and fattoush — Levantine salads
  • Halloumi and beyaz peynir cheeses, olives, and cured meats
  • Hot items such as sigara böreği (cheese rolls), falafel, grilled octopus, and köfte

Regional variations

Turkey

Turkish meze include haydari (thick yogurt with herbs), ezme (spicy tomato and pepper salad), şakşuka, olives, and white cheese, followed by hot starters (ara sıcak) such as paçanga böreği.

Greece

Greek mezedes feature taramasalata, melitzanosalata, fava, spanakopita, and grilled seafood, served in tavernas alongside ouzo or tsipouro.

Levant

Lebanese and Syrian spreads emphasise fresh salads, kibbeh, muhammara (walnut and pepper dip), and labneh, typically with pita bread.
