The Knowledge

The Knowledge is the test of London's streets and places of interest that must be passed by drivers of licensed black cabs (Hackney carriages) in London, England. Introduced in 1865 and administered today by Transport for London, it is widely considered the most demanding taxi-driver examination in the world.

Scope

Candidates — known as Knowledge students — must memorise every street within a six-mile radius of Charing Cross, an area containing roughly 25,000 streets, together with thousands of "points of interest" such as hospitals, hotels, stations, theatres, government buildings and places of worship. They must learn 320 standard routes ("runs") set out in the official Blue Book, and be able to produce, on demand, the shortest legal route between any two points.

Training and examination

Most students spend three to four years preparing, traditionally criss-crossing the city on a moped with a clipboard. Examinations proceed through a series of one-to-one oral tests called "appearances," scored by examiners, typically beginning with written and map tests and progressing through progressively harder stages.

Neuroscience

The Knowledge became famous in neuroscience through studies by Eleanor Maguire and colleagues showing that licensed London taxi drivers have enlarged posterior hippocampi — brain regions involved in spatial navigation — compared with control subjects, and that the enlargement correlates with years of driving experience. The findings are a landmark demonstration of adult neuroplasticity.
