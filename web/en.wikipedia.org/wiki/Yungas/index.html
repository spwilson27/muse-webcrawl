Yungas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Yungas are a band of tropical and subtropical moist forests along the eastern slopes of the Andes in Peru, Bolivia, and northern Argentina. The name derives from the Quechua word yunka, meaning "warm valley." Lying between the cold highlands (puna) and the hot Amazonian lowlands, the Yungas form a transition zone of exceptional biodiversity.

Geography and ecology

Moist air rising from the Amazon basin condenses on the eastern Andean slopes, producing persistent cloud cover, heavy rainfall (often over 3,000 mm annually), and lush montane cloud forest. Depending on classification and altitude, the Yungas are divided into ecoregions including the Peruvian Yungas, Bolivian Yungas, and Southern Andean Yungas of Bolivia and Argentina. The forests harbour orchids, tree ferns, and bamboos, and fauna such as the spectacled bear, jaguar, ocelot, and the Andean cock-of-the-rock.

Human geography

The Bolivian Yungas, in the departments of La Paz and Cochabamba, are famed for coca, coffee, citrus, and banana cultivation, with towns such as Coroico serving as centres of Afro-Bolivian culture. The North Yungas Road from La Paz to Coroico — long notorious as one of the world's most dangerous roads — descends from over 4,600 m to about 1,200 m through the cloud forest.

Conservation

The Yungas are threatened by deforestation for agriculture and coca cultivation. Protected areas include Manú National Park in Peru and Amboró and Carrasco national parks in Bolivia.
