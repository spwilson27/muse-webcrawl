Seung-Hui Cho (January 18, 1984 – April 16, 2007) was a South Korean-born American mass murderer who perpetrated the Virginia Tech shooting on April 16, 2007, killing 32 people and wounding 17 others before taking his own life. At the time it was the deadliest school shooting in United States history.

Background

Cho was born in Asan, South Korea, and immigrated to the United States with his family in 1992, settling in Centreville, Virginia; he was a U.S. permanent resident. In middle and high school he was diagnosed with severe anxiety disorder and selective mutism. At Virginia Tech, where he was an undergraduate English major, his behaviour in his final years disturbed classmates and teachers: his violent-themed creative writing led to his removal from one class, and he was investigated for stalking two female students. In December 2005 a court ordered him to outpatient mental-health treatment after he expressed suicidal thoughts, but he never received follow-up care.

The attack

On the morning of April 16, 2007, Cho shot two students in a dormitory. More than two hours later, having mailed a package of images, video and writings to NBC News, he chained shut the doors of Norris Hall and shot students and faculty in classrooms across the building's second floor, killing 30 more people before killing himself as police closed in. The subsequent official review panel criticised the university's delayed warning and the fragmentation of Cho's mental-health records, and the case prompted changes to campus emergency notification and to the reporting of mental-health adjudications to the federal firearms background-check system.
