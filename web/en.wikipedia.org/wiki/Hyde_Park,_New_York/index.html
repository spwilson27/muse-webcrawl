Hyde Park, New York

Hyde Park is a town in Dutchess County, New York, United States, on the eastern bank of the Hudson River just north of Poughkeepsie. The population was about 21,500 at the 2020 census. The town is world-famous as the hometown of President Franklin D. Roosevelt.

History

The area was settled by the Dutch in the 18th century and named for Edward Hyde, an early colonial governor of New York. The town was formed in 1821 from part of Clinton. In the Gilded Age, wealthy families built great estates along the Hudson, including the Vanderbilt Mansion (built for Frederick W. Vanderbilt, 1896–99) and Staatsburgh (the Ogden Mills estate).

Historic sites

Hyde Park hosts an unusual concentration of National Park Service sites: the Home of Franklin D. Roosevelt National Historic Site ("Springwood", Roosevelt's lifelong home and burial place), the adjacent Franklin D. Roosevelt Presidential Library and Museum (the first presidential library, dedicated in 1941), Val-Kill (Eleanor Roosevelt's cottage and later home), and Top Cottage. The town is also home to the Culinary Institute of America, headquartered since 1970 in a former Jesuit seminary, St. Andrew-on-Hudson.
