Humulus

Humulus is a genus of flowering plants in the hemp family Cannabaceae, native to temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. The best-known species is Humulus lupulus, the common hop, whose female flowers — hop cones — are used to flavour and preserve beer.

Description

Hops are vigorous perennial climbing plants (bines) that twine clockwise up supports and die back to a cold-hardy rhizome each winter. They are dioecious, with male and female flowers on separate plants; only the unfertilised female cones are harvested. Glands in the cones produce lupulin, containing alpha acids such as humulone that give beer its bitterness, and aromatic oils.

Species and uses

Accepted species include H. lupulus, H. japonicus (Japanese hop, grown as an annual ornamental) and H. yunnanensis. Hop shoots are eaten as a delicacy in some regions, and hop extracts have a long history in herbal medicine as a sedative.
