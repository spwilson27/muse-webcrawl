Productive efficiency

Field: Microeconomics

Condition: Production at minimum average total cost / on the production possibility frontier

Related concepts: Allocative efficiency, Pareto efficiency, technical efficiency

Productive efficiency (also known as production efficiency) is a situation in which the economy or an economic system, such as a firm, industry or country, cannot produce more of one good without sacrificing production of another good and without improving the technology available. In other words, goods are being produced at the lowest possible average total cost.

In the firm

A firm is productively efficient when it produces at the minimum point of its long-run average cost curve, using the least-cost combination of inputs. Under perfect competition, firms are driven to this point in the long run, since any firm producing at higher cost cannot survive. In monopoly and other imperfect markets, firms generally do not produce at minimum average cost, resulting in productive inefficiency.

In the economy

For an economy, productive efficiency corresponds to any point on its production possibility frontier (PPF): resources are fully and efficiently employed, and more of one good can be obtained only by giving up some of another. Points inside the frontier indicate productive inefficiency. Productive efficiency is a necessary but not sufficient condition for allocative efficiency, which additionally requires that the mix of goods produced matches consumer preferences.
