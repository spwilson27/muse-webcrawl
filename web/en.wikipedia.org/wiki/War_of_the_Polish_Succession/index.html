War of the Polish Succession

The War of the Polish Succession (1733–1738) was a major European conflict sparked by the death of King Augustus II of Poland and the disputed election of his successor. France supported the former king Stanisław Leszczyński, father-in-law of Louis XV, while Austria and Russia backed Augustus's son, Augustus III.

Course of the war

Leszczyński was elected by the Polish nobility in September 1733, but Russian troops intervened and secured the election of Augustus III. Leszczyński fled to Danzig (Gdańsk), which was besieged and taken by Russian and Saxon forces in 1734 after the famous defence and the flight of the king in disguise. France, joined by Spain and Sardinia, fought Austria in the Rhineland and in Italy, where battles at Parma and Guastalla were fought in 1734; in the south, Spanish forces conquered Naples and Sicily for Don Carlos.

Settlement

The preliminary Peace of Vienna (1735), ratified in 1738, left Augustus III on the Polish throne. Leszczyński received the Duchy of Lorraine, which reverted to France on his death in 1766. Francis Stephen of Lorraine, the future Emperor Francis I, was compensated with Tuscany, while Don Carlos was recognized as King of Naples and Sicily. The war further exposed the weakness of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and the growing dominance of Russia in its affairs.
