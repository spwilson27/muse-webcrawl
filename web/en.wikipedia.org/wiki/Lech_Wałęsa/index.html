Lech Wałęsa

Lech Wałęsa (born 29 September 1943) is a Polish statesman, dissident, and Nobel Peace Prize laureate. An electrician at the Gdańsk Shipyard, he co-founded and led Solidarity (Solidarność), the first independent trade union in a Warsaw Pact country, whose strikes of 1980–81 and survival through martial law made it the central movement in the collapse of communism in Poland. Following the partially free elections of 1989, Wałęsa served as President of Poland from 1990 to 1995.

Early life and activism

Wałęsa was born in Popowo into a carpenter's family and trained as an electrician, beginning work at the Lenin Shipyard in Gdańsk in 1967. He took part in the 1970 protests in which troops killed dozens of shipyard workers, and he was repeatedly detained during the 1970s as he moved into opposition activity with the Workers' Defence Committee (KOR) and the Free Trade Unions of the Coast. Dismissed from the shipyard in 1976, he worked at other firms while continuing underground organising.

Solidarity

In August 1980, strikes over food prices spread to the Gdańsk Shipyard. Wałęsa famously climbed over the shipyard fence to join the strike and became chairman of the Interfactory Strike Committee. The resulting Gdańsk Agreement conceded the workers' central demand: the right to independent trade unions. Solidarity was registered in September 1980 and soon claimed some 10 million members — a third of Poland's working-age population. When General Jaruzelski imposed martial law on 13 December 1981, Wałęsa was interned for eleven months; Solidarity was driven underground but survived. In 1983 Wałęsa received the Nobel Peace Prize, which his wife Danuta accepted in Oslo on his behalf, fearing he would not be allowed back into Poland if he travelled.

Transition and presidency

Facing renewed strikes, the regime negotiated the Round Table Talks of early 1989, leading to semi-free elections on 4 June 1989 that Solidarity swept. Wałęsa backed Tadeusz Mazowiecki as the first non-communist prime minister in the Soviet bloc, then — amid the "war at the top" that split Solidarity — ran for president himself, winning the 1990 election. His presidency saw Poland's market transition and withdrawal of Soviet troops, but also political instability and conflicts with parliament; he narrowly lost re-election in 1995 to Aleksander Kwaśniewski, a former communist minister, and failed again in a comeback bid in 2000.

Later life and controversies

Wałęsa remains an international symbol of peaceful resistance and lectures worldwide. In Poland his legacy has been contested, notably by allegations from security-service files that in the early 1970s he had been registered as an informer codenamed "Bolek" — claims he has always denied as fabricated, which historians continue to debate and which Poland's Institute of National Remembrance investigations have not settled to universal satisfaction.
