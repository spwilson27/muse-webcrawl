Zumbi

Zumbi (1655 – 20 November 1695), also known as Zumbi dos Palmares, was a Brazilian quilombola leader and one of the pioneers of resistance to the enslavement of Africans by Portuguese colonists in Brazil. He was the last of the kings of the Quilombo dos Palmares, a settlement of Afro-Brazilian escaped slaves in the present-day state of Alagoas.

Life

Zumbi was born free in Palmares but captured as a child and raised by a priest, who taught him Portuguese and Latin. He escaped at fifteen and returned to Palmares, where he became a renowned military strategist and eventually succeeded his uncle Ganga Zumba, whose treaty with the Portuguese Zumbi rejected as accepting continued slavery for others.

Death and legacy

After years of attacks, Palmares was destroyed by bandeirante forces under Domingos Jorge Velho in 1694. Zumbi survived in hiding but was betrayed and killed on 20 November 1695. In Brazil, 20 November is commemorated as Black Consciousness Day, and Zumbi is a national hero of Afro-Brazilian resistance.
