Celtic calendar

The Celtic calendar is a compilation of pre-Christian Celtic systems of timekeeping, including the Gaulish Coligny calendar, used by Celtic countries to define the beginning and length of the day, week, month, seasons, quarter days and festivals.

Celtic calendar
Type: Lunisolar (Coligny)
Key festivals: Samhain, Imbolc, Beltane, Lughnasa
Evidence: Coligny calendar (1st–2nd c. AD)

Coligny calendar

The Coligny calendar, a bronze tablet found in France in 1897, is a lunisolar calendar of 62 months over five years, dividing months into bright and dark halves and marking days as favourable or unfavourable.

Festivals

In Ireland and Scotland the year was marked by four seasonal festivals: Samhain (1 November), Imbolc (1 February), Beltane (1 May) and Lughnasa (1 August), with the day traditionally beginning at sunset. Modern Celtic neopaganism revived these festivals within the Wheel of the Year.
