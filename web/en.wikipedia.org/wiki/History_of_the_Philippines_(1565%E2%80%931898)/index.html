History of the Philippines (1565–1898)

The history of the Philippines from 1565 to 1898 covers the period of Spanish colonisation, beginning with the arrival of Miguel López de Legazpi's expedition and the establishment of the Captaincy General of the Philippines under the Spanish Empire, and ending with the Philippine Revolution and the Spanish–American War, which transferred the islands to the United States under the Treaty of Paris.

Conquest and administration

Legazpi founded settlements in Cebu (1565) and Manila (1571), which became the capital. The colony was administered from Mexico as part of New Spain until 1821 and thereafter directly from Madrid. The encomienda system, the galleon trade between Manila and Acapulco, and the work of Catholic religious orders reshaped Philippine society; friars became powerful local figures, and Catholicism spread widely.

Nineteenth century and revolution

Opening to world trade in the nineteenth century enriched an educated elite (the ilustrados) and nourished reformist and then separatist movements. José Rizal's novels inspired the Propaganda Movement; the Katipunan, founded by Andrés Bonifacio, launched the Philippine Revolution in 1896. Rizal's execution that year intensified the revolt. In 1898 the United States defeated Spain in the Spanish–American War, and Spain ceded the Philippines for 0 million, even as Emilio Aguinaldo proclaimed Philippine independence.
