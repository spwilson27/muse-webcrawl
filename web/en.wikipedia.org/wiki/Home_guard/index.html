Home guard

A home guard is a military or paramilitary organization raised locally for the defense of a community, region, or country, typically composed of part-time volunteers who are not members of the regular armed forces. Home guards have historically been mobilized in emergencies and wars, and permanent home guard forces form part of the military reserve structures of several countries, especially in Scandinavia and the Baltic states.

British Home Guard

The best-known example is the British Home Guard of the Second World War. Announced by Secretary of State for War Anthony Eden on 14 May 1940, in the face of threatened German invasion, it was initially called the Local Defence Volunteers and renamed the Home Guard by Winston Churchill that July. Around 1.5 million men — mainly those too old, too young, or in reserved occupations for regular service — volunteered. The force guarded infrastructure, manned anti-aircraft and coastal defenses, and was intended to delay an invasion until regular forces could respond; it was stood down in December 1944 and disbanded in 1945. The force is affectionately remembered in British culture, notably through the television sitcom Dad's Army.

Other home guards

Several countries maintain standing volunteer home guards: the Danish Home Guard (Hjemmeværnet), the Swedish Home Guard (Hemvärnet), the Norwegian Home Guard (Heimevernet), the Lithuanian National Defence Volunteer Forces, and the Estonian Defence League (Kaitseliit) all recruit local volunteers for territorial defense and support to civil authorities in crises. In India, the Home Guards is a civil-defense volunteer auxiliary to the police. During the American Civil War and both world wars, various U.S. state home guard units substituted for National Guard forces deployed overseas.
