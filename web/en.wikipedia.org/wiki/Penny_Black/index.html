Penny Black

The Penny Black was the world's first adhesive postage stamp used in a public postal system. It was first issued in the United Kingdom on 1 May 1840 but was not valid for use until 6 May. The stamp features a profile of Queen Victoria.

In 1837, British postal rates were high, complex and anomalous. To simplify matters, Rowland Hill proposed an adhesive stamp to indicate pre-payment of postage. At the time it was normal for the recipient to pay postage on delivery, charged by the sheet and by distance travelled. By contrast, the Penny Black allowed letters of up to half an ounce (14 grams) to be delivered at a flat rate of one penny, regardless of distance.

Background

The idea of an adhesive stamp to indicate prepayment was part of Rowland Hill's 1837 proposals for postal reform, set out in his pamphlet Post Office Reform: Its Importance and Practicability. Hill proposed a uniform rate of one penny per half ounce, prepaid by the sender. Following a public competition that attracted some 2,600 entries but no entirely suitable design, the Treasury adopted a design based on Hill's own ideas, using the profile of the young Queen Victoria taken from the City Medal by William Wyon, struck for her visit to the City of London in 1837.

Design

The stamp bears the profile of Victoria, then aged 15 in the portrait used, on a black background. The word "POSTAGE" appears at the top and "ONE PENNY" at the bottom. In the lower corners are letters indicating the stamp's position in the printed sheet — for example, A-A for the top-left stamp — which served as a check against forgery. No country name appears on the stamp; the United Kingdom remains the only country that does not print its name on its stamps, using the monarch's portrait instead.

Printing

The stamps were printed by Perkins, Bacon & Co. using line engraving on steel plates, in sheets of 240 (20 rows of 12). A total of 720,000 sheets were printed from 11 plates (plate 1 was printed in two states), giving about 68.8 million stamps. Because the stamps were imperforate, they had to be cut from the sheet with scissors.

Cancellation

The Penny Black was cancelled with a Maltese cross postmark, initially in red ink. However, the red cancellation was difficult to see on the black stamp and could be cleaned off and the stamp reused, so in 1841 the colour scheme was reversed: the Penny Red was issued and cancelled in black. The Penny Black was officially withdrawn after less than a year in use.

Rarity and collecting

Despite being the world's first postage stamp, the Penny Black is not especially rare because so many were printed and preserved; used examples can be bought for modest sums, though fine mint examples and stamps from plate 11 are considerably more valuable. It remains one of the most famous and iconic stamps in philately.
