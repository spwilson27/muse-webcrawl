Tangent circles

FieldEuclidean geometry
Related topicsProblem of Apollonius, Ford circles, Steiner chain

In geometry, tangent circles are circles in a common plane that intersect at exactly one point. If the point of tangency lies on the line joining their centres, the circles are tangent; they are internally tangent if one circle lies within the other, and externally tangent if they lie outside each other.

Configurations

Classical problems involving tangent circles include the problem of Apollonius — constructing circles tangent to three given circles — and Descartes' theorem, which relates the curvatures of four mutually tangent circles. Chains of tangent circles inscribed between two given circles are called Steiner chains, and tangent circles packed in a strip give rise to Ford circles in number theory.

Applications

Tangent-circle constructions appear in gear geometry, in the design of arches and Gothic tracery, and in circle-packing problems in materials science and communications.
