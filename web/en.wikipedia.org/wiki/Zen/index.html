Zen is a school of Mahayana Buddhism that originated in China as Chan during the Tang dynasty and spread to Vietnam, Korea and Japan, where as Zen it took its best-known forms. It emphasises direct experience over scripture and doctrine: awakening (satori, kensho) is pursued through seated meditation (zazen), paradoxical koan stories ('What is the sound of one hand clapping?'), and the guidance of a master, aiming at seeing one's true nature.

History and influence

Legend traces Zen to the Buddha's silent 'flower sermon' and to Bodhidharma, who brought it to China. In Japan, Eisai founded the Rinzai school (koan-centred) and Dogen the Soto school ('just sitting', shikantaza), whose writings are philosophical classics. Zen deeply marked Japanese arts — ink painting, tea ceremony, gardens, haiku, martial arts — and, from the writings of D. T. Suzuki and the Beats onward, Western art, literature and psychology; secular mindfulness derives in part from it.
