European Pressurized Reactor

Type: Pressurized water reactor (Generation III+)

Designer: Areva / Framatome, EDF, Siemens

Electrical output: ~1,650 MWe

Thermal output: ~4,500 MWth

The European Pressurized Reactor, marketed internationally as the EPR, is a Generation III+ pressurized water reactor (PWR) design developed by the French company Areva (now Framatome) together with Électricité de France (EDF) and the German utility Siemens. It evolved from the French N4 and German Konvoi reactor lines, aiming at higher output, improved fuel efficiency and substantially enhanced safety relative to the reactors built in the 1970s and 1980s.

Design

The EPR produces about 4,500 MW of thermal power and roughly 1,650 MW of electricity, making it among the most powerful reactor designs in the world. Safety features include a double-wall containment building designed to withstand an aircraft impact, four independent trains of safety systems in separate buildings, a "core catcher" that spreads and cools molten core material in the event of a severe accident, and additional protection against hydrogen explosions. The design target was to reduce the probability of core damage and of large radioactive releases by an order of magnitude compared with earlier plants.

Construction projects

The first EPR projects became notorious for cost overruns and delays. Olkiluoto 3 in Finland, begun in 2005, entered regular electricity production only in 2023. Flamanville 3 in France, begun in 2007, was connected to the grid in 2024. The two units at Taishan in China, started later, were the first to operate, in 2018 and 2019. In the United Kingdom, two EPRs are under construction at Hinkley Point C in Somerset, with a further pair planned at Sizewell C. A simplified derivative, the EPR2, is planned for France's programme of new reactors.
