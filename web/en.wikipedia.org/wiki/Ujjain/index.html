Ujjain

Ujjain is a city in the Indian state of Madhya Pradesh, situated on the eastern bank of the Shipra River. One of the most ancient cities of India, it is one of the Sapta Puri — the seven sacred cities of Hinduism — and hosts the Simhastha Kumbh Mela, one of the largest religious gatherings in the world. Ujjain is home to the Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga, one of the twelve revered shrines of the god Shiva.

History

Known in antiquity as Ujjayini, the city was the capital of the Avanti kingdom, one of the sixteen mahajanapadas of the 6th century BCE, and a major center on early trade routes. It was associated with the legendary king Vikramaditya and, under the Guptas, became a renowned center of learning; the poet Kalidasa celebrated it in his works. Ujjain was long treated as the prime meridian of Indian astronomy — the "Greenwich of India" — and the 18th-century ruler Jai Singh II built one of his Jantar Mantar observatories here. It later passed through Maratha rule under the Scindias before becoming part of Madhya Pradesh.

Religion and festivals

The Mahakaleshwar Temple, rebuilt in the Maratha period over far older foundations, draws pilgrims year-round; its pre-dawn Bhasma Aarti, in which the deity is adorned with sacred ash, is famous. Every twelve years the Simhastha festival brings tens of millions of devotees to bathe in the Shipra. The city also preserves the Harsiddhi Temple, the Kal Bhairav temple, and the observatory Vedh Shala.

Geography and economy

Ujjain lies on the Malwa plateau at an elevation of about 494 metres, with a population of roughly 700,000 in the city and over 2 million in the district. It is a regional commercial and educational center, home to Vikram University (founded 1957), with an economy based on agriculture, textiles, and religious tourism.
