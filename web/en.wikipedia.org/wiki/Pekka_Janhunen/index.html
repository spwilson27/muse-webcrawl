Pekka Janhunen is a Finnish space physicist at the Finnish Meteorological Institute, best known as the inventor of the electric solar wind sail, or E-sail.

Electric sail

Janhunen proposed the E-sail in 2006 as a method of propellantless spacecraft propulsion. A spacecraft deploys a fan of long, thin, electrically charged tethers; the fast-moving ions of the solar wind are deflected by the electrostatic field around the tethers, and the momentum transferred pushes the craft. The concept promises continuous gentle thrust suitable for asteroid missions, solar-wind monitoring, and eventually fast missions to the outer Solar System. Related to it is the plasma brake, a small charged tether that increases drag in low Earth orbit so that small satellites can de-orbit quickly at the end of their lives.

Testing

The concept has been tested on small satellites, notably the Estonian ESTCube-1 (2013), whose tether experiment was hampered by a deployment problem, and Finland's Aalto-1 (2017), which carried a plasma brake. Subsequent European projects and CubeSat missions have continued development of both technologies.
