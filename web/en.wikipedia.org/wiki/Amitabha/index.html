Amitābha (Sanskrit: अमिताभ, "Infinite Light") is a celestial Buddha described in the scriptures of Mahāyāna Buddhism. He is the principal Buddha of Pure Land Buddhism, a major tradition in East Asia. According to the Larger Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra, Amitābha was once a monk named Dharmākara who made a series of vows — most famously the eighteenth, or "Primal Vow" — resolving to create a purified Buddha-field, Sukhāvatī, where beings could be reborn through faith in him and thereafter attain enlightenment without falling back.

Doctrine

Amitābha presides over Sukhāvatī, the Western Pure Land, a realm described as adorned with jeweled trees, lotus ponds, and continuous teaching of the Dharma. Devotees seek rebirth there by reciting his name — the practice known as nianfo in Chinese and nembutsu in Japanese ("Namu Amida Butsu", "Homage to Amitābha Buddha"). In Japanese Pure Land schools founded by Hōnen and Shinran, this recitation, understood as relying entirely on Amitābha's "Other Power" (tariki) rather than one's own effort, became the central practice.

Origins and scriptures

The earliest texts devoted to Amitābha, including the Pratyutpanna Samādhi Sūtra and the two Sukhāvatīvyūha sūtras, were composed in India around the 1st–2nd centuries CE, possibly in the Gandhāra or Mathurā regions. The name Amitābha means "Infinite Light"; he is also called Amitāyus, "Infinite Life." Some scholars have suggested his cult may have been influenced by Iranian ideas of light, though this remains debated.

Iconography

Amitābha is usually depicted in red, the colour of the setting sun in the west, seated in meditation (dhyāna mudrā) with an alms bowl, or displaying the vitarka or welcoming (raigō-in) gesture. He is commonly flanked by the bodhisattvas Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta, forming the "Three Saints of the West." Famous images include the Great Buddha of Kamakura (Amida Daibutsu) in Japan, cast in bronze in the mid-13th century.

Role in other traditions

In Vajrayāna (Tibetan) Buddhism, Amitābha is one of the Five Tathāgatas (Five Wisdom Buddhas), associated with the west, the colour red, and the transformation of desire into discriminating wisdom. The Panchen Lamas are considered his emanations in Tibetan tradition.
