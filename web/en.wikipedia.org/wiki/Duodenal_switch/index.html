Duodenal switch
Other names: Biliopancreatic diversion with duodenal switch (BPD/DS)
Specialty: Bariatric surgery

The duodenal switch (formally biliopancreatic diversion with duodenal switch, BPD/DS) is a weight-loss surgery procedure composed of a restrictive and a malabsorptive aspect. It is generally considered the most effective bariatric operation for sustained weight loss and resolution of type 2 diabetes, but it is also the most technically complex and carries the highest risk of nutritional deficiencies.

Procedure

The restrictive part involves removing roughly 70–80% of the stomach along the greater curvature (a sleeve gastrectomy), preserving the pylorus. The malabsorptive part reroutes a lengthy portion of the small intestine: the duodenum is divided just past the pylorus and reconnected to the distal ileum, creating a short common channel (typically 75–100 cm) where food mixes with digestive juices, greatly reducing absorption of fat and calories.

Outcomes and risks

Patients commonly lose 70–80% of excess weight, with high rates of diabetes remission. Risks include protein malnutrition, fat-soluble vitamin and mineral deficiencies, diarrhoea and malodorous flatulence from fat malabsorption, and the surgical complications common to bariatric surgery. Lifelong supplementation and monitoring are required. A related single-anastomosis variant, SADI-S, simplifies the intestinal rerouting.
