Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy condition

CFL condition

[Image: diagram of a numerical domain of dependence contained within the physical domain of dependence]
FieldNumerical analysis
Named afterRichard Courant, Kurt Otto Friedrichs, Hans Lewy
Published1928

The Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy condition (usually called the CFL condition) is a necessary condition for the stability and convergence of numerical methods that solve time-dependent partial differential equations, especially explicit finite difference and finite volume schemes. It states, roughly, that information must not travel more than one grid cell per time step: the numerical domain of dependence must contain the true, physical domain of dependence.

Formulation

For a one-dimensional problem with wave speed u, grid spacing Δx and time step Δt, the condition is expressed in terms of the Courant number C:

C = u Δt / Δx ≤ Cmax

where Cmax depends on the scheme and is often 1 for simple explicit methods. In multiple dimensions the restriction involves a sum over the coordinate directions and can be considerably stricter. Violating the condition typically causes errors to grow explosively, making the simulation useless.

History

The condition was described in a 1928 paper by Richard Courant, Kurt Friedrichs and Hans Lewy on difference equations of mathematical physics, written long before electronic computers made large-scale simulation routine. In computational fluid dynamics practice, the CFL condition is the principal constraint on the time step of explicit simulations; implicit methods can relax it for stability but may still need comparable steps for accuracy.
