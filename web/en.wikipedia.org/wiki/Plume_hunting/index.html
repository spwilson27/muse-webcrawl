Plume hunting

Plume hunting is the hunting of wild birds to harvest their feathers, especially the more decorative plumes which were sold for use as ornamentation, particularly in hat-making (millinery). The movement against the plume trade in the United Kingdom was led by the Royal Society for the Protection of Birds (RSPB).

History

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, demand for feathers in fashion — especially in Paris, London and New York — led to the slaughter of millions of egrets, herons, grebes, birds of paradise and other species. Plumes from snowy egrets (aigrettes) were at times worth more than their weight in gold. Colonies of wading birds in Florida and elsewhere were devastated.

Conservation response

Opposition to the trade helped launch the modern conservation movement. In Britain, Emily Williamson founded the Society for the Protection of Birds in 1889; in the United States, Harriet Hemenway and Minna Hall founded the Massachusetts Audubon Society in 1896. Legislation including the US Lacey Act (1900), the Weeks-McLean Act (1913) and the UK's Importation of Plumage (Prohibition) Act 1921 curtailed the trade.
