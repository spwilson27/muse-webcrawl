Hans Lewy (20 October 1904 – 23 August 1988) was an American mathematician, known for his work on partial differential equations and complex analysis, and for the celebrated Lewy example of a smooth linear partial differential equation with no solutions at all — not even in the sense of distributions.

Life

Lewy was born in Breslau and studied at the University of Göttingen, earning his doctorate in 1926 under Richard Courant. He became a Privatdozent at Göttingen, but after the Nazi seizure of power in 1933 he emigrated, first to France and then to the United States. After positions at Brown University and the University of Minnesota, he joined the University of California, Berkeley, in 1935, where he remained until his retirement in 1972.

Work

With Courant and Friedrichs, Lewy co-authored the 1928 paper introducing what is now called the CFL condition (Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy condition), a fundamental stability criterion for numerical solutions of hyperbolic partial differential equations. His 1957 "Lewy example" showed that a first-order linear partial differential equation with analytic coefficients need not have any solution — a result that reshaped the theory of linear PDEs and spurred the development of conditions (such as those of Hörmander and Nirenberg–Trèves) characterising solvability.

Lewy also contributed to the theory of free boundary problems, water waves, and complex analysis in several variables. He shared the 1984/85 Wolf Prize in Mathematics with Kunihiko Kodaira and received the Steele Prize in 1979.
