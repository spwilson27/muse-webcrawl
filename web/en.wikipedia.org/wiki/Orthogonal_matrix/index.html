Orthogonal matrix

In linear algebra, an orthogonal matrix, or orthonormal matrix, is a real square matrix whose columns and rows are orthonormal vectors. Equivalently, a matrix Q is orthogonal if its transpose is equal to its inverse:

QTQ = QQT = I,

where I is the identity matrix. This leads to the equivalent characterization: a matrix Q is orthogonal if its transpose is equal to its inverse, QT = Q−1.

Properties

An orthogonal matrix is necessarily invertible, with inverse equal to its transpose, and unitary. Its determinant is either +1 or −1. As a linear transformation, an orthogonal matrix preserves the inner product of vectors, and therefore acts as an isometry of Euclidean space, such as a rotation, reflection or rotoreflection. Orthogonal matrices with determinant +1 form the special orthogonal group, which represents rotations; those with determinant −1 include reflections.

The set of n × n orthogonal matrices forms a group, the orthogonal group O(n), under matrix multiplication. Orthogonal matrices are important in numerical methods because they do not amplify errors: multiplication by an orthogonal matrix preserves the Euclidean norm of a vector, so algorithms based on orthogonal factorizations, such as the QR decomposition and the singular value decomposition, are numerically stable.

Examples

The 2 × 2 rotation matrix through an angle θ,

[cos θ, −sin θ; sin θ, cos θ],

is orthogonal for every θ. Permutation matrices, Householder reflections, and Givens rotations are also orthogonal. Over the complex numbers, the analogous concept is the unitary matrix.
