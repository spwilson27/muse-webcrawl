NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS) football is the highest level of college football in the United States. The FBS comprises the largest schools in the National Collegiate Athletic Association; as of the 2024 season it comprises 134 schools organised into ten conferences plus a small number of independents. Unlike other NCAA divisions, the FBS championship was historically determined not by an NCAA-run tournament but by polls and, since 2014, by the College Football Playoff.

FBS programmes play in the largest stadiums in American sport, several holding over 100,000 spectators.

Structure

FBS schools may award up to 85 full football scholarships. Teams play a 12-game regular season, mostly against conference opponents, followed by conference championship games and postseason bowl games. The most powerful leagues — the "Power Four" after the 2023–24 realignment: the SEC, Big Ten, ACC, and Big 12 — dominate revenue and playoff access, after the Pac-12 collapsed as most of its members departed.

Determining a champion

For most of its history, the FBS "national champion" was crowned by polls such as the AP Poll and Coaches Poll, often producing disputed split titles. The Bowl Championship Series (1998–2013) matched the top two teams in a single title game. The College Football Playoff, introduced in 2014 with four teams, expanded to twelve teams for the 2024 season, with a selection committee seeding conference champions and at-large teams.

Economics and change

FBS football generates billions of dollars in television revenue and is the financial engine of most major athletic departments. Since 2021, players have been permitted to profit from their name, image, and likeness (NIL), and court settlements in the mid-2020s moved the system toward direct revenue sharing with athletes and liberalised transfer rules through the transfer portal — changes that have reshaped roster building and sparked continuing debate about the amateur model.
