In machine learning and statistics, feature selection, also known as variable selection, attribute selection or variable subset selection, is the process of selecting a subset of relevant features (variables, predictors) for use in model construction. Feature selection techniques are used to simplify models, shorten training times, mitigate the curse of dimensionality, reduce overfitting, and improve interpretability and generalisation.

Rationale

The central premise is that data contain many features that are either redundant or irrelevant, and that can thus be removed without incurring much loss of information. An optimal feature subset improves accuracy while producing models that are cheaper to train and easier to explain — important in domains such as genomics, text classification and medical diagnosis, where datasets may have tens of thousands of variables and few samples.

Approaches

Filter methods

Filter methods rank variables by intrinsic statistical properties, independent of any learner: correlation, mutual information, chi-squared tests, ANOVA F-values, or ReliefF scores. They are fast and scalable but ignore interactions with the model.

Wrapper methods

Wrapper methods search the space of subsets (e.g. forward selection, backward elimination, recursive feature elimination) using the target model's cross-validated performance as the criterion. They capture feature interactions but are computationally expensive and prone to overfitting the validation process.

Embedded methods

Embedded methods perform selection within model training. The canonical example is LASSO regression, whose L1 penalty shrinks some coefficients exactly to zero; decision-tree ensembles (random forests, gradient boosting) likewise provide built-in variable-importance measures used for selection.

Evaluation

Selected subsets should be evaluated with nested cross-validation to avoid leakage of selection into performance estimates, and stability (agreement of selections across resamples) is a concern in high-dimensional applications such as biomarker discovery.
