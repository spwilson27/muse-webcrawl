Cosmonaut

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cosmonaut
Russianкосмонавт (kosmonavt)
EtymologyGreek kosmos (universe) + nautes (sailor)
FirstYuri Gagarin (1961)
Star City training.

Cosmonaut is the title given to space travellers trained and flown by the Soviet and Russian space programmes — in Russian, kosmonavt, from the Greek kosmos (universe/order) and nautes (sailor). The word is not interchangeable with astronaut in formal usage: cosmonauts are Russian (or Soviet) programme fliers; China's are taikonauts; others, astronauts — all three terms denote the same job in different flags.

The cosmonaut corps centred on Star City (Zvyozdny Gorodok) outside Moscow, where Yuri Gagarin trained and where candidates still study. Soviet crews were drawn heavily from military pilots and engineers, with a strong contingent of flight engineers — a division of labour (commander/pilot versus research engineer) inherited by Soyuz operations today.

Language notes

The term's prestige is such that Russia maintains it as a state honour title ('Pilot-Cosmonaut of the USSR/Russian Federation'). Western media often use 'astronaut' generically for everyone; specialist writing preserves the distinction, as does this crawl. Visiting fliers on Russian vehicles who are not Russian professionals are typically 'spaceflight participants' or their own programme's astronauts.
