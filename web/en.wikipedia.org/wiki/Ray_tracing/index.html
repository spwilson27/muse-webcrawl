Ray tracing

In 3D computer graphics, ray tracing is a technique for modelling light transport for use in a wide variety of rendering algorithms for generating digital images. On a spectrum of computational cost and visual fidelity, ray tracing-based rendering techniques, such as ray casting, recursive ray tracing, distribution ray tracing, photon mapping and path tracing, are generally slower and higher fidelity than scanline rendering methods.

Principle

Ray tracing traces the path of light backwards from the camera through each pixel into the scene, computing intersections with objects and recursively following reflected, refracted and shadow rays. Because it simulates the physical transport of light, it naturally produces soft shadows, reflections, refraction, caustics and global illumination effects that rasterisation approximates only with difficulty.

History and adoption

Recursive ray tracing was described by Turner Whitted in 1979, and path tracing was formalised with the rendering equation by James Kajiya in 1986. Long confined to offline rendering in film because of its cost, ray tracing entered real-time graphics with hardware-accelerated GPUs from 2018 onwards, and modern game engines combine rasterisation with ray-traced effects.
