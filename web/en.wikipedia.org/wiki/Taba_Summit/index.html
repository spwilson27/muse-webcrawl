The Taba Summit (also known as the Taba Talks) were negotiations between Israel and the Palestinian Authority held at Taba, on Egypt's Sinai coast, from 21 to 27 January 2001. The talks followed the failed Camp David Summit of July 2000 and the outbreak of the Second Intifada, and were based on the parameters proposed by United States President Bill Clinton in December 2000. They are widely considered the closest the two sides came to a final-status agreement in that era.

Background

After the collapse of the Camp David Summit, violence escalated with the Second Intifada. In December 2000, Clinton presented bridging proposals ("the Clinton Parameters") covering territory, Jerusalem, refugees and security. Israeli Prime Minister Ehud Barak and Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat both accepted the parameters as a basis for negotiation, with reservations, and the Taba meeting was convened to try to close the remaining gaps before Israeli elections in February 2001.

Negotiations

The Israeli delegation was led by Foreign Minister Shlomo Ben-Ami, with Yossi Beilin a prominent participant; the Palestinian side included Ahmed Qurei (Abu Ala) and Yasser Abed Rabbo. The European Union's envoy Miguel Moratinos documented the discussions in a summary later known as the "Moratinos non-paper." The parties discussed:

  • Territory: a Palestinian state in the Gaza Strip and most of the West Bank, with land swaps for settlement blocs; discussion converged on figures in the range of 94–97% of the West Bank plus swaps.
  • Jerusalem: division along the principle that Arab neighbourhoods would be Palestinian and Jewish neighbourhoods Israeli, with special arrangements for the Old City and holy sites.
  • Refugees: formulas combining return primarily to the Palestinian state with compensation and limited admission to Israel.
  • Security: Israeli early-warning stations, international forces, and phased withdrawal timetables.

Outcome

On 27 January 2001 the two delegations issued a joint statement saying they had "never been closer to reaching an agreement" and that the remaining gaps could be bridged after the Israeli elections. Barak, however, lost the February 2001 election to Ariel Sharon, who declined to continue the negotiations, and the Taba understandings were never converted into a treaty. Participants and historians have since debated how close a deal truly was, but the Taba positions remain a reference point for later proposals, including the Geneva Initiative of 2003.
