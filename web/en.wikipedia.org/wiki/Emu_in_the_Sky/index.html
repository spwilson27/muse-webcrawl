Emu in the Sky

The Emu in the Sky is a constellation in Australian Aboriginal astronomy that is defined by dark nebulae — dark dust lanes in the Milky Way — rather than by stars. The Emu's head is the Coalsack Nebula, next to the Southern Cross; its body is the dark dust lanes trailing along the Milky Way through Scorpius toward Sagittarius, and its legs extend further down the galactic band.

Cultural significance

For many Aboriginal groups, including the Kamilaroi and Euahlayi peoples of northern New South Wales, the Emu in the Sky is a creator ancestor and an important seasonal calendar marker. Its orientation in the evening sky at different times of year signals when emus are mating, when they are laying eggs, and when the eggs can be collected, as well as marking times for ceremony. The figure is connected to the earth by the Emu in the Waterhole, a reflection or corresponding figure in sacred sites.

Recognition

The Emu in the Sky is one of the best-known examples of "dark cloud constellations", shared in different forms by Indigenous peoples of Australia and by cultures in South America, such as the Inca, whose astronomy also identified figures in the Milky Way's dark lanes. It appears in contemporary Australian public art and astronomy outreach as a symbol of Aboriginal astronomical knowledge.
