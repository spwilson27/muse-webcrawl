Cotton

Cotton
PlantGossypium species
ProductSoft staple fibre for textiles
Centres (history)India, Egypt, American South
[Image: A field of cotton plants with white fluffy bolls open]

Cotton is a soft, fluffy staple fibre that grows in a boll around the seeds of cotton plants, spun into yarn for textiles. Domesticated independently in Asia, Africa and the Americas, cotton cloth was an Indian speciality exported for millennia — through ports near Karachi among others.

Industry and ethics

The cotton gin (1793) and Lancashire mills made cotton the Industrial Revolution's emblematic commodity — and entrenched slavery in the American South and colonial extraction elsewhere. Today cotton clothes much of humanity; its thirst for water and pesticides drives organic and recycled alternatives, debated alongside silk and synthetics like plastic fibres.
