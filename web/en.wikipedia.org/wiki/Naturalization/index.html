Naturalization

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Naturalization (or naturalisation) is the legal act or process by which a non-citizen of a country acquires citizenship or nationality after birth. It typically follows a period of lawful residence and requires some combination of language and civics knowledge, good character, an oath or declaration of allegiance, and renunciation (or not) of prior citizenship, depending on the country's rules on Dual citizenship.

Residence requirements range from about three years (often for spouses of citizens) to five years (the United States and many European states) or ten or more. The United States naturalises several hundred thousand people in a typical year under the Immigration and Nationality Act, with an English and civics test and the Oath of Allegiance; ceremonies are public and symbolically important. Fees, processing backlogs and test difficulty are recurring policy questions, as is “citizenship by investment” (golden passports), under which states such as Malta have sold naturalisation to wealthy applicants — a practice the European Union has challenged in court for its own member states.
