Bodawpaya

Bodawpaya (Burmese: ဘိုးတော်ဘုရား; 1745 – 1819), also known as Bodawhpaya, was the sixth king of the Konbaung Dynasty of Burma, reigning from 1782 to 1819. Born Maung Shwe Waing, he was a son of Alaungpaya, the dynasty's founder. Bodawpaya came to the throne after deposing his nephew Phaungkaza Maung Maung, and moved the capital to Amarapura. His long reign saw the conquest of Arakan in 1785, bringing Burma into direct contact and conflict with British India, as well as a major programme of religious works.

Reign

Bodawpaya seized the throne in 1782 after a brief succession struggle, executing the young king Phaungkaza Maung Maung. He founded the new capital of Amarapura in 1783. In 1785 his forces conquered the coastal kingdom of Arakan (Rakhine), carrying off the revered Mahamuni Buddha image to his capital; the annexation created a volatile frontier with British Bengal and led to decades of friction, refugee crises and ultimately contributed to the First Anglo-Burmese War under his successor.

Religious works

A devout but autocratic Theravada Buddhist, Bodawpaya proclaimed himself the future Buddha Maitreya and undertook enormous merit-making projects. He began the Mingun Pahtodawgyi, a colossal stupa at Mingun intended to be the world's largest, but it was left unfinished — deliberately, after a prophecy foretold the king's death upon its completion — and was later cracked by the 1839 earthquake. He also cast the Mingun Bell in 1808, one of the largest ringing bells in the world. He sponsored a new recension of the Buddhist canon and collected thousands of manuscripts.

Administration and legacy

Bodawpaya ordered comprehensive revenue surveys and censuses, and his reign saw Burma's population and royal administration documented in unusual detail. His interference in Siam and Assam, and the pressure his Arakan conquest placed on the British frontier, set the stage for conflict with the East India Company. He died in 1819 and was succeeded by his grandson Bagyidaw.
