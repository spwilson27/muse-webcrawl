The Cahn–Ingold–Prelog (CIP) priority rules are a set of rules in organic chemistry for naming the stereoisomers of a molecule. A molecule may contain any number of stereocentres and any number of double bonds, and each gives rise to two possible dispositions. The CIP rules assign an unambiguous ordering of substituents so that descriptors such as R and S (for tetrahedral stereocentres) and E and Z (for double bonds) can be applied consistently.

Background

The system was developed by Robert Sidney Cahn, Christopher Kelk Ingold and Vladimir Prelog, and published in a series of papers culminating in 1966. It forms part of the IUPAC nomenclature of organic chemistry and is the standard method for specifying absolute configuration.

The sequence rules

Priorities are assigned to the substituents of a stereocentre according to a set of sequence rules:

  1. Atomic number rule. The atoms directly attached to the stereocentre are ranked by atomic number; the atom with the higher atomic number receives higher priority.
  2. First point of difference. If two attached atoms are identical, the lists of atoms attached to each of them are compared, sorted by atomic number, and compared element by element until a difference is found.
  3. Multiple bonds. Atoms connected by double or triple bonds are treated as duplicated or triplicated ("phantom") atoms. For example, a carbon doubly bonded to oxygen is treated as a carbon bonded to two oxygens.
  4. Isotopes. When isotopes are compared, the atom with the greater atomic mass receives higher priority (e.g. deuterium outranks hydrogen).
  5. Lone pairs are treated as phantom atoms of atomic number zero and therefore receive the lowest priority.

Assigning R and S

With the substituents prioritised 1–4, the stereocentre is viewed with the lowest-priority group (4) pointing away from the observer. If the sequence 1 → 2 → 3 runs clockwise, the configuration is R (Latin rectus); if anticlockwise, it is S (Latin sinister).

E and Z descriptors

For alkenes, the two substituents on each carbon of the double bond are ranked by CIP priority. If the two higher-priority groups lie on opposite sides of the double bond the descriptor is E (entgegen, "opposite"); if on the same side, Z (zusammen, "together").

Extensions

Later extensions of the system handle pseudoasymmetric centres (descriptors r and s), helicity (M and P), and planar chirality.
