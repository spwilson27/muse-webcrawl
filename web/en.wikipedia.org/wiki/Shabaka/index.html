Shabaka

Shabaka (also Shabaqo) was a pharaoh of the Twenty-fifth (Kushite) Dynasty of ancient Egypt, reigning roughly 705–690 BC (dates vary by chronology). A king of Kush from Nubia, he succeeded his brother Piye and consolidated Kushite rule over all Egypt.

Reign

Unlike Piye, who ruled Egypt from Napata, Shabaka moved the royal residence to Memphis and presented himself as a restorer of ancient Egyptian tradition. He campaigned against the Delta prince Bakenranef of Sais, ending the rival 24th Dynasty. In foreign policy he sheltered the fugitive Iamani of Ashdod from the Assyrian king Sargon II at first, but extradited him around 706 BC to avoid war.

Building and the Shabaka Stone

Shabaka built and restored temples at Thebes, Memphis and elsewhere. He is best remembered for the Shabaka Stone (now in the British Museum), a basalt slab inscribed on his orders with the “Memphite Theology” — a creation account centred on the god Ptah — which Shabaka claimed to have copied from a worm-eaten ancient papyrus. The stone was later damaged when reused as a millstone.

Death

Shabaka was buried in a pyramid at el-Kurru in Nubia and succeeded by Shebitku, during whose reign Egypt clashed with Assyria under Sennacherib.
