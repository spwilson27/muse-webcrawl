The Young Hegelians (German: Junghegelianer), also called the Left Hegelians, were a group of German intellectuals who, in the decade after G. W. F. Hegel's death in 1831, radicalised his philosophy into critiques of religion, the Prussian state and received morality. Where the "Old" or Right Hegelians defended Hegel as a philosopher of reconciliation with Protestant Christianity and the Prussian order, the Young Hegelians treated his dialectic as a revolutionary solvent: everything existing, they argued, was destined to be dissolved and superseded.

Development

The split began in theology. David Friedrich Strauss's The Life of Jesus, Critically Examined (1835) interpreted the Gospel miracles as myth, scandalising orthodox readers. Ludwig Feuerbach's The Essence of Christianity (1841) went further, arguing that God is the projection of human essence — theology is anthropology. Bruno Bauer's criticism denied the historicity of large parts of the New Testament and championed an atheism of "self-consciousness". Around figures such as Arnold Ruge, editor of the Hallische and Deutsche Jahrbücher, the movement turned openly political, demanding a rational, democratic state.

Marx, Engels and Stirner

Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels emerged from the Berlin Young Hegelian milieu (the "Doctors' Club", and the bohemian circle caricatured as "Die Freien"). Their works of the 1840s were written largely as arguments with former comrades: The Holy Family (1845) against Bruno Bauer, and The German Ideology (1845–46) against Feuerbach and Max Stirner, whose egoist manifesto The Ego and Its Own (1844) attacked all fixed ideas — God, Man, morality — as "spooks". Marx's break with the circle produced historical materialism: criticism of heaven transformed into criticism of earth, of theology into politics and economics.

Legacy

State repression — Bauer was dismissed from his university post, Ruge's journals suppressed — and the defeat of the 1848 revolutions dispersed the movement. Its legacy runs through Marxism, the higher criticism of the Bible, and modern atheist and existential thought.
