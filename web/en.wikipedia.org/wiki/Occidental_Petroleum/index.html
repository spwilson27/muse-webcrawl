Occidental Petroleum Corporation, often abbreviated to Oxy, is an American energy and chemicals company headquartered in Houston, Texas. Founded in 1920 in California, it is one of the largest oil and gas producers in the United States, with major operations in the Permian Basin, the Rocky Mountains, and the Gulf of Mexico, as well as international production in the Middle East and Latin America.

History

Occidental drilled its first well in California in 1920. Under Armand Hammer, who became chief executive in 1957, the company expanded dramatically, making major discoveries in Libya in the 1960s and the North Sea in the 1970s. In 2019, Occidental outbid Chevron to acquire Anadarko Petroleum for about $38 billion, a deal financed in part by an investment from Berkshire Hathaway, greatly expanding its position in the Permian Basin.

Operations

Beyond oil and gas exploration and production, Occidental's OxyChem subsidiary is a major producer of chlorine, caustic soda, and vinyls. The company has also invested in direct air capture of carbon dioxide through its subsidiary 1PointFive, building large-scale capture facilities in Texas.

Controversies

The company faced criticism over its operations in Peru, where its subsidiary's decades of oil production in the Amazon were alleged to have contaminated indigenous Achuar land, and over the 1988 Piper Alpha disaster in the North Sea, in which its subsidiary operated the platform where 167 men died.
