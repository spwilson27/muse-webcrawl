Melissus of Samos

Melissus of Samos (fl. c. 440 BCE) was a Greek pre-Socratic philosopher, the third and last member of the Eleatic school founded by Parmenides. He was also a statesman and admiral: as commander of the Samian fleet, he defeated the Athenians under Pericles in 441 BCE. His philosophical work, of which substantial fragments survive, defended and modified Parmenides' doctrine of the One.

Philosophy

Like Parmenides, Melissus argued that what exists is one, unchanging, ungenerated and imperishable, and that motion, plurality and change are illusions of the senses. But he differed from his master in two important ways. First, he held that Being is infinite (apeiron) rather than limited like a sphere, arguing that if it had a limit it would be limited by nothingness, which cannot be. Second, he concluded that Being is incorporeal — it has no body or thickness — because a body would have parts, and parts would mean plurality.

Melissus' arguments were taken seriously by later philosophers: Aristotle engaged with them at length, and the atomists Leucippus and Democritus framed their theory of atoms and the void partly as a response to Eleatic logic.
