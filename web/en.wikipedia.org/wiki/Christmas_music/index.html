Christmas music comprises the songs, hymns, carols and larger works associated with the Christmas season. It ranges from sacred music celebrating the Nativity to secular songs about winter weather, feasting and gift-giving. Radio stations in many countries switch to all-Christmas formats each December, and a small canon of recordings returns to the charts annually.

Carols and hymns

The Christmas carol grew from medieval dance-songs and vernacular religious verse. Many familiar carols date from the 18th and 19th centuries: "O Come, All Ye Faithful", "Hark! The Herald Angels Sing" (words by Charles Wesley, music adapted from Mendelssohn) and "Silent Night" (1818, by Joseph Mohr and Franz Gruber). Carol services, most famously the Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols from King's College, Cambridge, broadcast since 1928, remain a seasonal institution.

Classical music

Large-scale works include Handel's Messiah (1741, now a Christmas staple though written for Easter), Bach's Christmas Oratorio (1734), Berlioz's L'enfance du Christ, and Tchaikovsky's ballet The Nutcracker (1892), set on Christmas Eve.

Popular music

The 20th century produced a canon of popular standards: "White Christmas" (Irving Berlin, popularised by Bing Crosby, among the best-selling singles of all time), "Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer", "Jingle Bell Rock", "Rockin' Around the Christmas Tree" and "Last Christmas" by Wham!. Mariah Carey's "All I Want for Christmas Is You" (1994) has topped charts in numerous countries each December since streaming-era chart rules allowed recurrent hits. Novelty and comedy songs, from "Grandma Got Run Over by a Reindeer" to charity singles such as Band Aid's "Do They Know It's Christmas?", also form part of the tradition.
