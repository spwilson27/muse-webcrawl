Histogram

A histogram is a graphical representation of the distribution of numerical data. It is an estimate of the probability distribution of a continuous variable, first introduced by Karl Pearson. The data are divided into intervals called bins, and a rectangle is drawn over each bin with height (or area) proportional to the number of observations in it.

Construction

The choice of the number and width of bins strongly affects the appearance of a histogram. Common rules for selecting bin width include Sturges' rule, the square-root choice, Scott's rule, and the Freedman–Diaconis rule. Histograms differ from bar charts in that they represent continuous data over intervals rather than discrete categories.

Uses

Histograms are used in statistics, quality control, and image processing, where the distribution of pixel intensities (an image histogram) guides exposure and contrast adjustments.
