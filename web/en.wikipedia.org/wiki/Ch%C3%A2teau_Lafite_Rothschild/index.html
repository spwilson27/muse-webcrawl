Château Lafite Rothschild

The château and vineyards at Pauillac.

Château Lafite Rothschild is a wine estate in the Pauillac commune of the Médoc region of Bordeaux, France, owned by members of the Rothschild family since the 19th century. Classified as a Premier Grand Cru Classé (First Growth) in the Bordeaux Wine Official Classification of 1855, Lafite is one of the most famous and expensive red wines in the world, and its name is generally considered the foremost of the Bordeaux first growths.

History

The estate's history reaches back to the 17th century under the Ségur family, when the vineyard took shape on a gravelly rise whose name derives from the Gascon la hite, "the hillock". Lafite wines were celebrated at the court of Versailles, and Thomas Jefferson was among its early American admirers. In 1868 Baron James de Rothschild bought the estate; from 1949 Baron Éric de Rothschild led a renovation of the property, and the estate is today managed through Domaines Barons de Rothschild (Lafite), with Saskia de Rothschild succeeding her father as chair in 2018.

Vineyard and wines

The vineyard covers roughly 112 hectares of deep gravel over limestone and clay near the northern edge of Pauillac, planted predominantly with Cabernet Sauvignon (about 70%), along with Merlot, Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot. The grand vin, Château Lafite Rothschild, is typically around 90% Cabernet Sauvignon, aged in new oak barrels made in part by the estate's own cooperage, and renowned for its aromatic finesse and longevity. A second wine, Carruades de Lafite, has been produced since the 1980s.

Lafite vintages have repeatedly set auction records; a bottle of the celebrated 1869 vintage sold in Hong Kong in 2010 for a then-record price, and demand from China in the 2000s made Lafite an emblem of luxury wine in Asian markets, a status reflected in prices for the 1982 vintage.
