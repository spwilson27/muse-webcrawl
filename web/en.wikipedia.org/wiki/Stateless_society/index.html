Stateless society

Stateless society
ConceptPolitical organisation without a state
ExamplesMany hunter-gatherer and segmentary societies; medieval Iceland (historical)
Associated thoughtAnarchism, anarcho-syndicalism, some forms of libertarianism

A stateless society is a society that is not governed by a state — a centralised political organisation claiming a monopoly on the legitimate use of force over a territory. In a stateless society there is little or no concentration of authority; functions such as dispute resolution, defence and coordination are handled by diffuse institutions such as kinship networks, customary law, voluntary associations and assemblies.

Historical examples

For most of human prehistory, people lived in stateless bands and tribes, and many documented societies — including segmentary lineage systems such as the Nuer of South Sudan, studied by E. E. Evans-Pritchard — maintained order without rulers. Historical examples often cited include the Icelandic Commonwealth (930–1262), with its Althing and chieftains but no executive, and various frontier and highland societies described by James C. Scott in The Art of Not Being Governed as deliberately evading state control.

Political theory

Anarchist thinkers such as Pierre-Joseph Proudhon, Mikhail Bakunin and Peter Kropotkin advocated a stateless society organised through free federation, mutual aid and voluntary association; the International Workingmen's Association was an early arena for these debates. Some libertarian and anarcho-capitalist writers similarly propose replacing state functions with markets and private law. Critics question whether complex, large-scale societies can provide defence, infrastructure and welfare without a state.
