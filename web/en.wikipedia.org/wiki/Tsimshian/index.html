Tsimshian

The Tsimshian (Sm'algyax: Ts'msyen) are an Indigenous people of the Pacific Northwest Coast, whose traditional territories lie around the lower Skeena River, the Nass River estuary and the coastal islands of northwestern British Columbia, with a community at Metlakatla, Alaska. They speak Sm'algyax, a Tsimshianic language, and are renowned for monumental totem poles, crest (adaawx) traditions, and the potlatch feast system.

Society and culture

Traditional Tsimshian society is matrilineal and organised into four exogamous clans or crests (pts'aan): Killerwhale, Wolf, Raven and Eagle. Rank, names, crests and resource sites were validated publicly through feasts. In 1887, about 800 Tsimshian followed missionary William Duncan to found New Metlakatla on Annette Island in Alaska, the only Indian reserve in that state.
