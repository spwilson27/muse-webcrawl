Ismail Gasprinsky (Crimean Tatar: İsmail Gaspıralı; 20 March 1851 – 24 September 1914) was a Crimean Tatar intellectual, educator, publisher and politician. He was one of the first Muslim intellectuals in the Russian Empire to call for the education and social reform of Turkic and Muslim peoples, and the founder of the Jadid ("new method") movement in education.

Biography

Gasprinsky was born in the village of Avjyköy near Bakhchisarai in Crimea, the son of an officer in Russian service. He was educated at the Simferopol gymnasium and at military schools in Voronezh and Moscow. Unsuited to a military career, he travelled to Paris and Constantinople, where he worked as a journalist, before returning to Crimea in the late 1870s. In 1879 he was elected mayor of Bakhchisarai.

Terciman

In 1883 Gasprinsky received permission to publish the newspaper Terciman ("The Interpreter", also known in Russian as Perevodchik), printed in both Russian and a simplified Turkic written language intended to be intelligible across the Turkic world. The paper became the most influential publication among Russia's Muslims and promoted his slogan "Unity in language, thought and action" (Dilde, fikirde, işte birlik).

Jadidism

Gasprinsky promoted the usul-i jadid ("new method") of teaching in Muslim primary schools, using phonetic methods to teach literacy rapidly and adding secular subjects such as arithmetic, geography and Russian to the curriculum. By 1914 thousands of Jadid schools operated across the Volga region, Central Asia and the Caucasus. He also advocateded women's education and in 1893 founded a school for girls run by his daughter Şefiqa.

Legacy

Gasprinsky died in Bakhchisarai in 1914 and was buried in the grounds of the Zincirli Madrasa. He is regarded as the father of modern Crimean Tatar national consciousness, and his ideas influenced reformers throughout the Turkic world.
