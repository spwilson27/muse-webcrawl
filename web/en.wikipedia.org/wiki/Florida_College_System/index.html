Florida College System

The Florida College System (FCS) is the system of 28 public state and community colleges in the U.S. state of Florida, governed by the Florida State Board of Education. Formerly known as the Florida Community College System, it enrolls roughly three-quarters of a million students annually and is a principal point of entry into Florida higher education.

History and mission

Florida's public junior colleges grew from a 1957 state master plan that placed a college within commuting distance of most residents. The system's colleges offer the Associate in Arts degree, whose holders are guaranteed admission to a state university under Florida's long-standing 2+2 articulation system, as well as Associate in Science degrees, workforce certificates, adult education, and — since 2001 — a growing number of four-year bachelor's degrees in high-demand fields such as nursing, education, and business. Many institutions dropped "community college" from their names as they added baccalaureate programs, and in 2009 the system was officially renamed the Florida College System.

Notable institutions

The largest members include Miami Dade College — among the largest colleges in the United States — Valencia College, Broward College, Palm Beach State College, and Santa Fe College, the latter two of which have won national recognition including the Aspen Prize for Community College Excellence (Santa Fe College, 2015).
