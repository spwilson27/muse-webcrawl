Quasisimple group

In mathematics, specifically group theory, a quasisimple group is a group that is a perfect central extension of a simple group. Equivalently, a group G is quasisimple if it equals its commutator subgroup (G = [G,G], i.e. it is perfect) and the quotient of G by its center is simple. Quasisimple groups are also called covering groups or Schur covers of simple groups in some contexts.

Examples

The special linear group SL(2,5) is quasisimple: it is a double cover of the simple alternating group A5. More generally, the universal central extensions of finite simple groups are quasisimple. The binary icosahedral group is another classical example.

Classification

Finite quasisimple groups are closely tied to the classification of finite simple groups: every finite quasisimple group is a central extension of a finite simple group, and the possible centers are governed by the Schur multiplier of the simple group. Quasisimple groups appear naturally as components in the generalized Fitting subgroup of a finite group.
