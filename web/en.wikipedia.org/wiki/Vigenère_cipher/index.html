Vigenère cipher

The Vigenère cipher is a method of encrypting alphabetic text using a series of interwoven Caesar ciphers based on the letters of a keyword. It is a form of polyalphabetic substitution.

History

Described by Giovan Battista Bellaso in 1553 and later misattributed to Blaise de Vigenère, it resisted cryptanalysis until the 19th century, when Babbage and Kasiski developed methods to break it.

Description

The cipher uses a Vigenère square: each plaintext letter is shifted by the corresponding keyword letter, repeating the keyword as needed.
