Curator

A curator (from Latin curare, "to take care") is a manager or overseer of a cultural heritage institution such as a museum, gallery, library or archive. Curators care for collections and are responsible for selecting, researching, interpreting and presenting objects, typically through exhibitions and publications.

Role

Curatorial work includes acquiring and documenting objects, conducting scholarly research on the collection, designing exhibitions, writing catalogues and labels, and supervising conservation. Senior curators hold specialised subject expertise, often with advanced degrees in art history, archaeology, natural history or a related field, together with museum studies training.

Contemporary practice

Since the late twentieth century the role of the independent exhibition curator has grown in contemporary art, with figures who shape biennials and thematic shows as authors in their own right. The verb "to curate" has also spread widely beyond museums to describe the selection and organisation of content in media, retail and social platforms.
