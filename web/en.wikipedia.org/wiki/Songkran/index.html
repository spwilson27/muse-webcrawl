Songkran

Songkran
Observed by: Thailand, Laos and other Southeast Asian countries
Type: Cultural / New Year
Date: 13–15 April (varies)
Related to: Thingyan, Lao New Year, Cambodian New Year

Songkran is the Thai New Year's national holiday, celebrated annually from 13 to 15 April, though festivities in many cities begin earlier and run longer. The word derives from the Sanskrit saṃkrānti, meaning "astrological passage" — the movement of the sun from one zodiac sign to another. Songkran falls at the end of the dry season and coincides with the traditional New Year celebrations of several South and Southeast Asian cultures.

History and significance

The festival has roots in ancient Hindu calendrical traditions transmitted through Southeast Asia. In Thailand the holiday is deeply intertwined with Theravada Buddhism: the period is a time for making merit, visiting temples, pouring water over Buddha statues, and honouring elders. Pouring water symbolises purification and the washing away of the past year's misfortunes.

Celebrations

The most famous aspect of Songkran is the water festival: streets fill with people armed with water pistols, buckets and hoses, and passers-by are drenched in good-natured fun. Major celebrations take place in Bangkok (especially along Khao San Road and Silom), Chiang Mai, Pattaya and Phuket. Temples host ceremonies in which sand pagodas are built and decorated. Families clean their homes, and younger people pour scented water over the palms of elders to receive blessings.

Regional counterparts

Related New Year water festivals include Thingyan in Myanmar, Pi Mai in Laos, and Chaul Chnam Thmey in Cambodia. In December 2023, UNESCO inscribed Songkran in Thailand on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
