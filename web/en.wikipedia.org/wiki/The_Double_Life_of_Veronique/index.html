The Double Life of Veronique (French: La double vie de Véronique; Polish: Podwójne życie Weroniki) is a 1991 drama film directed by Krzysztof Kieślowski and written by Kieślowski and Krzysztof Piesiewicz. It stars Irène Jacob in a dual role as Weronika, a Polish choir soprano, and Véronique, a French music teacher, two women who share a mysterious spiritual connection.

Plot

Weronika, living in Kraków, has a beautiful voice and a heart condition she ignores. While visiting Kraków's Main Square she glimpses her French double aboard a tourist bus. Soon afterward, during a concert, Weronika collapses and dies on stage. In France, Véronique, suddenly overwhelmed by grief, gives up her singing career without knowing why. Through a puppeteer's stories and a marionette made in her likeness, she comes to understand that she once had a double who "used up" the burden of her fragility. The film exists in a slightly different version for the American market, with an altered ending.

Reception

The film premiered at the 1991 Cannes Film Festival, where Irène Jacob won Best Actress. It is praised for Sławomir Idziak's luminous, golden-green cinematography and Zbigniew Preisner's score, attributed in the film to the fictitious composer Van den Budenmayer. It is regarded as one of Kieślowski's finest works, exploring themes of identity, intuition, free will, and unseen bonds between lives.
