Hans Scharoun

Bernhard Hans Henry Scharoun (20 September 1893 – 25 November 1972) was a German architect best known for designing the Berlin Philharmonie concert hall (home of the Berlin Philharmonic) and the Schminke House in Löbau, Saxony. He was one of the leading figures of organic architecture and of the modern movement in Germany.

Early work

Born in Bremen, Scharoun studied architecture in Berlin and was influenced by the expressionist circle around Bruno Taut and the Glass Chain (Gläserne Kette), for which he produced visionary watercolours after the First World War. In the 1920s he built celebrated modernist housing, including contributions to the Weissenhof Estate in Stuttgart (1927) and the Siemensstadt housing estate in Berlin.

Postwar work

Under the Nazi regime Scharoun received few commissions and worked quietly. After 1945 he helped plan Berlin's reconstruction and taught at the Technical University of Berlin. His masterpiece, the Berliner Philharmonie (1960–1963), pioneered the "vineyard" arrangement of terraced seating around a central orchestra — a layout widely imitated in concert halls worldwide. His other late works include the Berlin State Library (Staatsbibliothek) and the German Maritime Museum in Bremerhaven.
