Player versus player

Player versus player (PvP) is a type of multiplayer interactive conflict within a game between human players. This is in contrast to player versus environment (PvE), in which players compete against computer-controlled opponents or solve content cooperatively.

Description

PvP can take many forms: one-on-one duels, team deathmatches, capture-the-flag modes, large-scale faction warfare in massively multiplayer online games (MMOs), battle royales, and structured esports competitions. Some games are built entirely around PvP, while others make it optional—confining it to designated zones, arenas, or separate game modes, or requiring players to "flag" themselves as open to attack.

In role-playing games, PvP is sometimes controversial. Unrestricted PvP allows "player killing" (PKing) of weaker characters, which can lead to griefing—deliberately harassing other players. Richard Bartle's taxonomy of player types identified "killers" as players motivated primarily by imposing themselves on others. Developers often balance PvP with safe zones, level restrictions, or penalties for killing much weaker opponents.

History

Early examples of PvP appeared in multi-user dungeons (MUDs) in the late 1970s and 1980s, and in networked games such as the mainframe game Empire. The genre expanded with competitive first-person shooters in the 1990s (Doom, Quake), real-time strategy games, and MMORPGs such as Ultima Online (1997), notorious for its open PvP, and EverQuest, which largely separated PvP onto dedicated servers. Modern esports titles—including fighting games, MOBAs such as League of Legends and Dota 2, and tactical shooters—are fundamentally PvP experiences, and PvP modes are a staple of online play generally.

Types

  • Duels – consensual one-on-one fights, often for honour or practice.
  • Arena/team modes – instanced matches between balanced teams.
  • Open-world PvP – combat possible anywhere, sometimes with looting of the defeated.
  • Faction warfare – large groups aligned to in-game factions contest territory.
  • Battle royale – many players compete until one remains.
