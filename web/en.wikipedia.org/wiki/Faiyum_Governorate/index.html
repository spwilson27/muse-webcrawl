Faiyum Governorate (Arabic: محافظة الفيوم) is one of the governorates of Egypt, located in the middle of the country southwest of Cairo. Its capital is the city of Faiyum. The governorate occupies much of the Faiyum Oasis, a large depression fed by the Bahr Yussef canal from the Nile, lying close to Lake Qarun.

Geography

The Faiyum depression sits about 40–50 metres below sea level in places and is one of Egypt's oldest agricultural regions. Lake Qarun, a saline remnant of the ancient Lake Moeris, lies in its northwestern part, while the brackish lakes of Wadi El Rayan lie to the southwest. Irrigation channels radiating from the Bahr Yussef support cultivation of cereals, vegetables, olives, and figs.

History and archaeology

The region has been inhabited since prehistoric times and is noted for Middle Kingdom projects of the 12th Dynasty, the Hawara and Lahun pyramids, and Greco-Roman sites such as Karanis and the famed Faiyum mummy portraits. North of the lake, the Wadi Al-Hitan ("Valley of the Whales") fossil site, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, preserves Eocene whale fossils including Basilosaurus.
