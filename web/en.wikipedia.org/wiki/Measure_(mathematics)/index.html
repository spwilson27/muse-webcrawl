Measure (mathematics)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, the concept of a measure is a generalisation of length, area or volume. Informally, a measure is a function that assigns a number to subsets of a given set, intended to represent their size. This concept is important in mathematical analysis and probability theory.

Definition

Formally, a measure μ on a set X is defined on a σ-algebra Σ of subsets of X and takes values in [0, ∞], satisfying: (1) the empty set has measure zero; (2) countable additivity — for a countable collection of pairwise disjoint sets in Σ, the measure of their union equals the sum of their measures. The triple (X, Σ, μ) is called a measure space. A probability measure is a measure with total measure 1.

Examples

  • Lebesgue measure on ℝ, extending the length of intervals, the foundation of the Lebesgue integral.
  • Counting measure, which counts the elements of a set.
  • Dirac measure, concentrated at a single point.
  • Haar measure on locally compact groups, and Hausdorff measures in geometry.

Properties and theory

Measures are monotone and countably subadditive, and continuous from below and (when finite) from above. Sets not assigned a size by a construction — non-measurable sets such as Vitali sets for Lebesgue measure — motivated the restriction to σ-algebras. Measure theory, developed by Henri Lebesgue, Émile Borel, Johann Radon and others around 1900, underlies modern integration, probability (via Kolmogorov's axioms), ergodic theory and functional analysis.
