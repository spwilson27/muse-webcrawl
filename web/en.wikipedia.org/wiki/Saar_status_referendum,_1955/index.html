A referendum on the status of the Saar was held in the Saar Protectorate on 23 October 1955. Voters were asked whether to approve the "Saar Statute" agreed between France and West Germany, which would have given the territory a European status under the Western European Union while remaining economically tied to France.

Voting in Saarbrücken, October 1955.

Background

After the Second World War the Saar was separated from Germany and became a French protectorate, economically integrated with France. In 1954, as part of the Paris Agreements settling West Germany's sovereignty, Chancellor Konrad Adenauer and French Premier Pierre Mendès France agreed on a statute under which the Saar would become a European territory. The plan required approval in a referendum.

Campaign and result

The pro-German parties, campaigning for reunification with the Federal Republic, urged a No vote as a vote for Germany. On a turnout of roughly 96 percent, 67.7 percent of voters rejected the statute. The result was widely interpreted as a vote for return to Germany and led to the fall of the Saar's pro-autonomy government of Minister-President Johannes Hoffmann.

Aftermath

France and West Germany negotiated the Saar Treaty (Luxembourg Treaty) of 1956, under which the Saarland acceded to the Federal Republic of Germany on 1 January 1957, with a transitional period of economic integration lasting until 1959. The day of the referendum, 23 October, was later commemorated in the Saarland.
