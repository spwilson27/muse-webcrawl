Wiki

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Screenshot-style image of a wiki article page with an Edit tab at the top]

A wiki is a form of online hypertext publication that is collaboratively edited and managed by its own audience directly through a web browser. A typical wiki contains multiple pages meant for the general public, and allows any visitor to add and change content, usually without programming knowledge.

The first wiki, WikiWikiWeb, was created by Ward Cunningham in 1995 to let programmers share patterns and ideas; he named it after the Hawaiian word wiki, meaning 'quick', which he had encountered on the Wiki Wiki shuttle bus at Honolulu airport. Wiki software tracks every revision, making collaboration self-correcting: bad edits can be reverted as easily as they were made.

The most famous wiki is Wikipedia (2001), the free encyclopedia whose open editing produced one of the largest reference works in history — and the site this page imitates. Wikis also thrive inside companies as knowledge bases, in fan communities documenting fictional universes in extraordinary depth, and as documentation for software projects. The wiki idea — that a page and its edit button are the same thing — was one of the conceptual foundations of Web 2.0.
