Machito

Machito (born Francisco Raúl Gutiérrez Grillo, 16 February 1908 – 15 April 1984) was a Latin jazz musician who helped refine Afro-Cuban jazz and create both Cubop and salsa music. He was raised in Havana alongside his foster sister, the singer Graciela, with whom he later performed.

Life and career

Machito arrived in New York City in 1937 and worked with several Latin bands before forming his own orchestra, the Afro-Cubans, in 1940. With his brother-in-law Mario Bauzá as musical director, the Afro-Cubans were among the first bands to fuse the formal structures and harmonies of American big-band jazz with authentic Afro-Cuban rhythms played on congas, bongos and timbales. Machito sang and played maracas, fronting the band with great charisma.

The band's signature works include "Tanga" (1943), often credited as the first Afro-Cuban jazz composition, composed by Bauzá. After Bauzá left in 1976, Machito continued leading the orchestra; in later years his son Mario Grillo took over direction. The band won a Grammy Award in 1983 for the album Machito & His Salsa Big Band '82. Machito died in London in 1984, on tour.

Legacy

Together with Bauzá and Dizzy Gillespie, Machito is regarded as a founding father of Latin jazz. His orchestra was a training ground for many leading players, and a corner in East Harlem, New York, is named Machito Square in his honour.
