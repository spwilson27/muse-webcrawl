Franciscus Donders

Franciscus (Frans) Cornelis Donders FRS FRSE (27 May 1818 – 24 March 1889) was a Dutch ophthalmologist. During his career, he was a professor of ophthalmology and physiology in Utrecht. He was internationally regarded as an authority on eye diseases and directed the first ophthalmic hospital in the Netherlands, the Nederlandsch Gasthuis voor Ooglijders.

Work

Donders made foundational contributions to the scientific study of vision, including the laws governing refraction and accommodation of the eye and the rational prescription of lenses for astigmatism and other refractive errors. His monograph On the Anomalies of Accommodation and Refraction of the Eye (1864) established ophthalmology on a quantitative basis.

He is also remembered in psychology for his work on the speed of mental processes: using reaction-time experiments, he proposed the subtraction method — sometimes called Donders' method — for inferring the duration of distinct stages of mental processing, a foundational idea of mental chronometry and of modern cognitive psychology.
