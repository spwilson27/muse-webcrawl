Fred Astaire

Fred Astaire (born Frederick Austerlitz; May 10, 1899 – June 22, 1987) was an American dancer, actor, singer, choreographer and presenter, widely regarded as the greatest popular-music dancer in the history of film and television. His career in stage, film and television spanned 76 years.

Early life and stage career

Astaire was born in Omaha, Nebraska, to an Austrian immigrant father and a mother of German descent. He began performing in vaudeville as a child in a celebrated brother-and-sister act with his elder sister Adele. The Astaires starred on Broadway and in London in shows such as Lady, Be Good! (1924) and Funny Face (1927), becoming defining stars of 1920s musical theatre. After Adele retired in 1932, Fred went to Hollywood.

Film career

After a famous (and probably apocryphal) screen-test report — "Can't act. Can't sing. Slightly bald. Can dance a little" — Astaire was signed by RKO. His partnership with Ginger Rogers in ten films, including The Gay Divorcee (1934), Top Hat (1935) and Swing Time (1936), revolutionised the film musical: full-body framing, minimal cuts and dance integrated into the plot.

Later films include Holiday Inn (1942) and Easter Parade (1948) with Judy Garland, The Band Wagon (1953), Funny Face (1957) with Audrey Hepburn, and Silk Stockings (1957). Astaire insisted on rehearsing for weeks and on shooting dances in as few takes as possible, with the camera following the dancers. He received an Honorary Academy Award in 1950 "for his unique artistry and his contributions to the technique of musical pictures".

Later work and death

Astaire continued acting into old age, earning an Academy Award nomination for The Towering Inferno (1974) and winning acclaim for On the Beach (1959) and Ghost Story (1981). His television specials with Barrie Chase won multiple Emmy Awards. He died of pneumonia in Los Angeles in 1987, aged 88.

Legacy

Astaire influenced generations of dancers, from Gene Kelly to Michael Jackson. The American Film Institute ranked him the fifth-greatest male screen legend of all time, and he was inducted into the Hall of Fame of the Academy of Television Arts and Sciences.
