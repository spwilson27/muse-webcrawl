UFC 1: The Beginning was the first mixed martial arts event by the then-newly created Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC), held at the McNichols Sports Arena in Denver, Colorado, on 12 November 1993. The event was broadcast live on pay-per-view and later released on home video.

Background

The event was conceived by advertising executive Art Davie and Brazilian jiu-jitsu master Rorion Gracie as an eight-man, single-elimination tournament to determine the most effective martial art in a real fight. Fighters represented styles including Brazilian jiu-jitsu, boxing, kickboxing, karate, sumo, savate, and shootfighting. There were few rules: no biting or eye-gouging, no weight classes, no rounds, and bouts ended only by submission, knockout, or a corner throwing in the towel.

Tournament

Royce Gracie, Rorion's younger brother and the smallest competitor, won the tournament by defeating Art Jimmerson, Ken Shamrock, and Gerard Gordeau, finishing all three by submission. In the opening bout, Gordeau knocked out sumo wrestler Teila Tuli in 26 seconds, in a finish famous for sending one of Tuli's teeth into the crowd. The winner received $50,000.

Legacy

UFC 1 drew approximately 86,000 pay-per-view buys and launched what became the world's premier mixed martial arts promotion. Gracie's victories showcased the effectiveness of Brazilian jiu-jitsu and grappling, profoundly changing martial arts training worldwide and laying the foundations for the modern sport of mixed martial arts.

Results

  • Final: Royce Gracie def. Gerard Gordeau via submission (rear-naked choke)
  • Semifinals: Gracie def. Ken Shamrock; Gordeau def. Kevin Rosier
  • Quarterfinals: Gracie def. Art Jimmerson; Shamrock def. Patrick Smith; Gordeau def. Teila Tuli; Rosier def. Zane Frazier
