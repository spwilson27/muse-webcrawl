Retreat of the Republic of China to Taiwan

The retreat of the government of the Republic of China to Taiwan refers to the exodus of the remnants of the Kuomintang-ruled government of the Republic of China (ROC) to the island of Taiwan in December 1949 at the end of the Chinese Civil War. The Kuomintang (Chinese Nationalist Party), its officers, and approximately two million Nationalist troops and civilians took part in the retreat, in addition to many civilians and refugees, fleeing the advance of the People's Liberation Army of the Chinese Communist Party.

Background

After the resumption of the Chinese Civil War in 1946, Communist forces steadily gained the upper hand. In 1949 the Communists captured Nanjing, the ROC capital, in April, and proclaimed the People's Republic of China in Beijing on 1 October. The Nationalist government moved successively to Guangzhou, Chongqing, and Chengdu before Chiang Kai-shek and the remaining government organs withdrew to Taipei. On 7 December 1949, the ROC government announced the relocation of the capital to Taipei.

The evacuation

Along with personnel, the Nationalists transported to Taiwan much of China's gold reserves, foreign currency reserves, and the finest holdings of the Palace Museum and other cultural treasures, which later formed the core collections of the National Palace Museum in Taipei. Taiwan had been returned to ROC administration in 1945 after fifty years of Japanese colonial rule; the mass arrival of mainlanders reshaped the island's politics and society for decades.

Aftermath

The ROC maintained its claim to be the legitimate government of all China, a position supported for a time by its retention of China's seat at the United Nations until 1971. Martial law in Taiwan, declared in 1949, remained in force until 1987. The political status of Taiwan remains contested, with the island governed by the ROC while the People's Republic of China claims it as part of its territory.
