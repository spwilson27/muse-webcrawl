Lombardy

Lombardy (Lombardia)
Country: Italy
Capital: Milan
Area: 23,844 km²
Population: approx. 10 million

Lombardy (Italian: Lombardia) is an administrative region of Italy located in the northern part of the country. With about 10 million inhabitants, it is the most populous region of Italy, and its capital, Milan, is the country's economic and financial centre. Lombardy borders Switzerland to the north and the Italian regions of Piedmont, Emilia-Romagna, Veneto and Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol.

Geography

The region's landscape ranges from the Alps in the north — including parts of the Bernina, Ortler and Adamello ranges — through the pre-Alpine lakes (Lake Garda, Lake Como, Lake Maggiore and Lake Iseo) to the fertile plains of the Po Valley in the south, crossed by the Po River and its tributaries.

History

The region takes its name from the Lombards (Longobards), a Germanic people who invaded Italy in 568 and established a kingdom with its capital at Pavia. Lombard rule was ended by Charlemagne in 774. In the Middle Ages the cities of the Lombard League resisted the emperors Frederick Barbarossa and Frederick II. Milan later became a powerful duchy under the Visconti and Sforza families, and Lombardy passed through Spanish, Austrian and Napoleonic rule before Italian unification in the 19th century.

Economy

Lombardy is the economic engine of Italy, producing roughly a fifth of national GDP. Milan is a global centre of fashion, design, finance and media, while the region also has strong manufacturing, engineering, agri-food and tourism sectors.

Provinces

Lombardy comprises the provinces of Bergamo, Brescia, Como, Cremona, Lecco, Lodi, Mantua, Milan, Monza and Brianza, Pavia, Sondrio and Varese.
