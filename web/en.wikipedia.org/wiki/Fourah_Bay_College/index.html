Fourah Bay College is a public university college in Freetown, Sierra Leone. Founded on 18 February 1827, it is the oldest university college in modern sub-Saharan Africa and the first western-style university built in West Africa. It is a constituent college of the University of Sierra Leone.

During the colonial era, Fourah Bay College educated students from across British West Africa and beyond, earning Sierra Leone the nickname "the Athens of West Africa" for its role in higher education and scholarship.

History

The college was founded by the Anglican Church Missionary Society (CMS) to train African schoolmasters, catechists and clergy. Its name derives from Fourah Bay, the bay east of Freetown near its original site. The college later moved to its present location on Mount Aureol.

In 1876 the college became affiliated with Durham University in England, an arrangement that allowed its students to earn Durham degrees and lasted until 1967. In 1972, Fourah Bay College joined with Njala University College to form the University of Sierra Leone.

Academics and campus

The college comprises faculties of arts, engineering, pure and applied sciences, and social sciences and law, together with the Institute of Public Administration and Management (IPAM) and other institutes. Its campus on Mount Aureol overlooks Freetown and the Sierra Leone River estuary.

The college was badly affected during the Sierra Leone Civil War (1991–2002), when its facilities were damaged and occupied, but it has since been rehabilitated.
