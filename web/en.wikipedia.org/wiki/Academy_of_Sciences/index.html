Academy of sciences

Academy of sciences
TypeLearned society / national academy
Earliest examplesAccademia dei Lincei (1603), Royal Society (1660), French Academy of Sciences (1666)
FunctionsResearch, advice to government, publishing, honours

An academy of sciences is a type of learned society founded to promote science and research, usually at the national level. National academies of sciences typically elect distinguished scientists as members, advise governments on scientific matters, publish journals, and award prizes and medals. The name "academy" derives from Plato's Academy in ancient Athens.

History

The earliest scientific academies appeared in Italy, beginning with the Accademia dei Lincei in Rome (1603), whose members included Galileo Galilei. The Royal Society of London (founded 1660) and the French Academy of Sciences (founded 1666 under Louis XIV) became models for state-sponsored academies across Europe, including the Prussian Academy of Sciences (1700), the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences (1739) and the Russian Academy of Sciences (1724).

Roles

In many countries the academy functions as an honorific body of leading researchers, while in others — notably the former Soviet Union and China — academies directly direct large networks of research institutes. The United States National Academy of Sciences (founded 1863) is a private, non-profit body charged with advising the federal government; its reports are produced through the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine.
