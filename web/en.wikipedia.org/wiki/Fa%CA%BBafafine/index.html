Faʻafafine

RegionSamoa and the Samoan diaspora
Literal meaning"In the manner of a woman" (Samoan)

Faʻafafine are people who identify themselves as a third gender in Samoa and the Samoan diaspora. The Samoan word combines faʻa- ("in the manner of") with fafine ("woman"). Faʻafafine are assigned male at birth but embody feminine gender traits and occupy a recognised social role distinct from both men and women.

Social role

Traditionally, faʻafafine contribute to family and village life through caregiving, domestic work, hospitality and entertainment, and they are a familiar presence in Samoan churches, schools and public life. Families may raise a child as faʻafafine, though many also come to the identity themselves. The related term faʻafatama describes people assigned female at birth with masculine roles.

Recognition

Faʻafafine are widely visible in Samoan society, including pageants and public figures, and the concept is frequently discussed in Pacific studies as an example of an indigenous, culturally embedded gender category predating European contact.
