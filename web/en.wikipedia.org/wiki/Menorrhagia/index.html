Heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB), previously known as menorrhagia, is a menstrual period with excessively heavy flow. It is a type of abnormal uterine bleeding. Abnormal uterine bleeding can be caused by structural abnormalities in the reproductive tract, anovulation, bleeding disorders, hormonal issues (such as hypothyroidism), or cancer of the reproductive tract.

Heavy menstrual bleeding affects about 10–35% of women at some point and can significantly affect quality of life, work attendance, and iron stores, sometimes causing iron-deficiency anemia.

Definition

Traditionally, menorrhagia was defined as menstrual blood loss of more than 80 mL per cycle. In clinical practice, the modern definition is based on the patient's perception of excessive bleeding that interferes with physical, social, emotional, or material quality of life, as recommended by bodies such as FIGO and NICE.

Causes

The FIGO PALM-COEIN classification divides causes into structural (Polyp, Adenomyosis, Leiomyoma (fibroid), Malignancy and hyperplasia) and non-structural (Coagulopathy, Ovulatory dysfunction, Endometrial, Iatrogenic, Not otherwise classified).

Diagnosis

Evaluation includes a history, blood tests (full blood count, ferritin, thyroid function, coagulation studies), pelvic examination, ultrasound, and sometimes endometrial biopsy or hysteroscopy, particularly in women over 45 or with risk factors for malignancy.

Treatment

First-line options include the levonorgestrel intrauterine system, combined hormonal contraception, tranexamic acid, and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. Surgical options — endometrial ablation, uterine artery embolization, myomectomy, or hysterectomy — are considered when medical therapy fails or is declined.
