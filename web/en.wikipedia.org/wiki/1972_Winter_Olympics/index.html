The 1972 Winter Olympics, officially the XI Olympic Winter Games, were held from 3 to 13 February 1972 in Sapporo, Japan. They were the first Winter Olympics held outside Europe and North America, and Japan's first Olympic Games of any kind since the cancelled 1940 editions.

Highlights

Norwegian cross-country skier Magne Myrmo won the last Olympic gold awarded for racing on wooden skis, in the 50 kilometres. Dutch speed skater Ard Schenk won three gold medals, while Soviet cross-country skier Galina Kulakova also won three golds. Japanese ski jumpers achieved a celebrated clean sweep of the normal hill event, led by Yukio Kasaya, before a huge home crowd. The Soviet Union topped the medal table, ahead of East Germany and Switzerland.

Controversy

The Games were overshadowed by a dispute over amateurism: Austrian alpine star Karl Schranz was expelled by the IOC under president Avery Brundage for alleged commercial endorsements, while state-sponsored "full-time amateurs" from the Eastern Bloc competed freely — a contradiction that fuelled growing criticism of the Olympic amateur code.

Legacy

Sapporo's successful staging showcased post-war Japan's organisational capability two years before the 1964 Tokyo Summer Games' successor events and eight years after them, and the city remains a candidate-grade winter sports centre, home of the annual Sapporo Snow Festival, which grew in prominence around the Games.
