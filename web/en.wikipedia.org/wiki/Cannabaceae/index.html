Cannabaceae

Cannabaceae is a small family of flowering plants in the order Rosales. It includes about 170 species in 11 genera, among them Cannabis (hemp and marijuana) and Humulus (hops).

Genera

Recognised genera include Aphananthe, Cannabis, Celtis (hackberries), Chaetachme, Gironniera, Humulus, Lozanella, Parasponia, Pteroceltis, Trema and others. The large genus Celtis, formerly placed in the elm family, accounts for most of the family's diversity; many members are trees of temperate and tropical regions.

Characteristics

Members are erect or climbing herbs, shrubs or trees, often with simple, frequently palmately lobed or compound leaves with stipules. Flowers are small, greenish and usually unisexual, with plants often dioecious. The fruit is an achene or drupe.

Uses

Cannabis sativa is grown for fibre (hemp), seed, oil and cannabinoids. Humulus lupulus provides hops for brewing beer. Parasponia is notable as a non-legume that forms nitrogen-fixing root nodules with rhizobium bacteria.
