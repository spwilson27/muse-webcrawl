Global surveillance disclosures (2013–present)

Global surveillance disclosures
DateBegan June 2013
SourceEdward Snowden
Agencies exposedNSA (US), GCHQ (UK) and Five Eyes partners
Key programmesPRISM, XKeyscore, Tempora, bulk telephony metadata

The global surveillance disclosures are a series of revelations, beginning in June 2013, about mass surveillance programmes conducted by the United States National Security Agency (NSA), the United Kingdom's GCHQ and their Five Eyes allies. The disclosures were based on classified documents leaked by former NSA contractor Edward Snowden and reported initially by journalists Glenn Greenwald, Laura Poitras and Barton Gellman.

Revelations

The first stories exposed a secret court order compelling Verizon to hand over Americans' telephone metadata in bulk, and the PRISM programme, under which the NSA obtained data from major US technology companies. Further documents described XKeyscore, a system for searching intercepted internet data; GCHQ's Tempora programme tapping fibre-optic cables; the tapping of allied leaders' phones, including Angela Merkel's; and efforts to undermine encryption standards.

Reactions and aftermath

The disclosures ignited a worldwide debate over privacy and state power. Snowden fled to Hong Kong and then Moscow, where he remains, charged in the US under the Espionage Act. In 2015 the USA FREEDOM Act ended bulk telephony metadata collection by the NSA, technology companies greatly expanded end-to-end encryption, and courts in the US and Europe ruled some mass-surveillance practices unlawful.
