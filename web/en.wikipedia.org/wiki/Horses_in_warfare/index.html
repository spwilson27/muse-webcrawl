Horses in warfare


Mounted warriors in battle

Horses have been used in warfare for most of recorded history. The earliest evidence of horses in war is the use of chariots in the ancient Near East and Eurasian steppe from around 2000 BC. As riding equipment and horse breeding improved, mounted cavalry became central to armies from antiquity through the early 20th century, while horses also remained indispensable for transport, artillery, and logistics until mechanisation.

Antiquity and the Middle Ages

Steppe peoples such as the Scythians and later the Mongols built empires on horse archery. In the classical world, cavalry played supporting roles beside infantry phalanxes, while Alexander the Great's Companion cavalry delivered decisive charges. In medieval Europe, the armoured knight on a heavy warhorse dominated battlefields, aided by the stirrup and high-cantled saddle, until gunpowder weapons and disciplined pike infantry reduced the knight's supremacy.

Early modern era to the World Wars

From the 16th century, cavalry evolved into dragoons, hussars, cuirassiers, and lancers, used for shock action, reconnaissance, and pursuit. Horses hauled artillery and supply wagons in enormous numbers: millions served and died in the First World War, and even the largely mechanised armies of the Second World War—especially Germany's Wehrmacht—relied heavily on horse transport. The last great cavalry charges occurred early in the Second World War.

Modern use

Today horses serve mainly ceremonial, police, and special-purpose roles, including mounted patrols, search and rescue in mountainous terrain, and occasional use by special forces, as in Afghanistan in 2001. Warhorse welfare and commemoration, such as Britain's Animals in War Memorial, reflect the scale of their historical contribution.
