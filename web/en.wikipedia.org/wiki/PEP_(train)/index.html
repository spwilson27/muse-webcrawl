PEP (train)

PEP (Prototype Electro-Pneumatic) was a series of prototype electric multiple unit trains built by British Rail Engineering Limited (BREL) in the early 1970s. Three prototype units were built: the two-car Class 445 and the four-car Class 446. They were designed to test features for a new generation of suburban trains for British Rail.

Design

The PEP units introduced several features that became standard on later British suburban trains: aluminium body construction, integral body design, sliding pocket doors with wide openings for rapid loading, and the ability to work in multiple with automatic couplers. The stock ran on 750 V DC third rail in the Southern Region trials.

Legacy

Although the prototypes themselves were withdrawn and scrapped after testing, their design directly led to the large fleet of production "PEP-derived" classes built from the mid-1970s, including Classes 313, 314, and 315 (dual-voltage and AC units) and Classes 507 and 508. These trains served suburban routes around London, Liverpool, and Glasgow for decades.
