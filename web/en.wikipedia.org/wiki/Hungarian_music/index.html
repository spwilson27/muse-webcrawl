Music of Hungary

Hungarian music encompasses a rich folk tradition and a major contribution to European classical music. Hungarian folk music, with its distinctive scales and rhythms, was collected and studied in the early 20th century by Béla Bartók and Zoltán Kodály, whose fieldwork transformed ethnomusicology and profoundly influenced their own compositions.

Folk traditions

Folk styles include the verbunkos, originally a military recruiting dance, the csárdás, and the songs of the Romani bands whose virtuoso performances inspired Franz Liszt's Hungarian Rhapsodies. The táncház (dance house) movement of the 1970s revived village music and dance for urban audiences and was later recognised by UNESCO.

Classical and modern music

Hungary produced composers such as Liszt, Bartók, Kodály, Ernő Dohnányi and György Ligeti, and conductors including Georg Solti and Eugene Ormandy. In popular music, the band Omega achieved international success, and Hungary has a notable electronic and folk-metal scene.
