Mojmir I

Mojmir I (also Mojmír I; c. 795 – 846) was the first known ruler of the Slavic principality of Moravia and the founder of the House of Mojmir, which governed Great Moravia in the ninth century. Around 833 he expelled Prince Pribina of Nitra and united the Moravian and Nitran principalities, creating the polity that historians call Great Moravia.

Reign

Mojmir's Moravia emerged in the aftermath of the Frankish destruction of the Avar khaganate. The principality appears in the Conversio Bagoariorum et Carantanorum and Frankish annals as a neighbour and tributary of the East Frankish kingdom. In 833 Mojmir drove Pribina from Nitra; Pribina fled to the Franks and eventually founded the Balaton principality. Mojmir is traditionally credited with accepting Christianity — the church at Mikulčice and other early Moravian churches date from his era — though the scale of Christianization under him is debated. In 846 Louis the German invaded Moravia; Mojmir died around this campaign (the circumstances are unclear), and Louis installed his nephew Rastislav as ruler.

Legacy

As the founder of the first durable West Slavic state, Mojmir is a foundational figure of Slovak and Czech historical memory. For the fuller article on his rule within the Moravian principality, see Mojmir I of Moravia.
