Bank of Puerto Rico

The Bank of Puerto Rico (Spanish: Banco de Puerto Rico) was a bank of issue in Puerto Rico during the final decades of Spanish colonial rule. It was authorised in 1888 and opened in San Juan in 1890, holding the privilege of issuing banknotes for the island.

Founded1888 (opened 1890)
HeadquartersSan Juan, Puerto Rico
TypeBank of issue

History

Before the bank's creation, Puerto Rico relied on Spanish and foreign coin, supplemented by private tokens and credit instruments. The new institution issued peso notes backed by silver, giving the island its first systematic paper currency.

After 1898

Following the Spanish–American War, Puerto Rico passed to the United States. The island's currency was converted to the US dollar at a rate of 60 cents per peso, and the bank lost its issuing role in the new monetary order, winding down its note circulation in the early 20th century.

Legacy

The bank's neoclassical headquarters in Old San Juan is a notable landmark of the period of Spanish colonial finance.
