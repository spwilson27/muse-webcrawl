E-ZPass

Type: Electronic toll collection system
Launched: 1993
Region: Eastern and Midwestern United States
Operator: E-ZPass Interagency Group

E-ZPass is an electronic toll collection system used on toll roads, bridges, and tunnels in the Eastern and Midwestern United States. The system allows drivers to pay tolls automatically from a prepaid account using a radio-frequency transponder mounted inside the vehicle, without stopping to pay cash at a toll booth.

E-ZPass was first deployed in 1993 by the seven toll agencies then forming the E-ZPass Interagency Group (IAG) in the New York metropolitan area. It has since grown to include agencies in states from Maine to Florida and west to Minnesota and Kentucky — over 40 agencies in some 20 states — making it the largest interoperable toll system in the world, with tens of millions of transponders in service.

Operation

Each vehicle carries a transponder, typically mounted on the windshield, which communicates with overhead or roadside antenna readers as it passes through a toll gantry or plaza. The toll is debited from the user's prepaid account, which replenishes automatically from a linked credit card or bank account. Many facilities now operate with all-electronic tolling (gantries only, no cash lanes), and vehicles without transponders are billed by licence-plate image ("Pay-By-Plate" / Tolls by Mail), usually at a higher rate and with administrative fees.

Interoperability

E-ZPass accounts work across all member agencies. A 2012 federal law (MAP-21) directed nationwide electronic toll interoperability, and E-ZPass has expanded compatibility with systems such as Florida's SunPass; it remains incompatible with some regional systems elsewhere, though multi-protocol transponders increasingly bridge the gaps.

Privacy and enforcement

Because the system records the time and place of vehicle movements, E-ZPass data has raised privacy concerns: toll records have been sought and used in court proceedings, including divorce and criminal cases, and state laws vary in how they restrict disclosure. Violations for unpaid tolls can result in fines and registration blocks.
