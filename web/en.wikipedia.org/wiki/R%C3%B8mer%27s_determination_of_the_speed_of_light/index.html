Rømer's determination of the speed of light

Rømer's determination
Date1676
AstronomerOle Rømer
Observed bodyIo (moon of Jupiter)

Rømer's determination of the speed of light was the first quantitative measurement of the speed of light, made in 1676 by the Danish astronomer Ole Rømer at the Paris Observatory. By timing the eclipses of Jupiter's moon Io, Rømer demonstrated that light travels at a finite speed, contrary to the then-common belief that it propagated instantaneously.

Observations

Rømer noticed that the observed intervals between Io's eclipses were shorter when Earth was moving towards Jupiter and longer when it was moving away. He concluded that light took time to cross the changing distance between the planets, and predicted correctly that an eclipse in November 1676 would occur about ten minutes later than calculated from earlier observations.

Value obtained

From Rømer's data, Christiaan Huygens derived a value of roughly 220,000 km per second — about three quarters of the modern value of 299,792 km/s — the first estimate of the correct order of magnitude for one of nature's fundamental constants.
