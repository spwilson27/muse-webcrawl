Qurbani

Qurbani is the ritual animal sacrifice performed by Muslims during Eid al-Adha. The practice commemorates the willingness of Ibrahim, known in English as Abraham, to sacrifice his son in obedience to God, and the provision of a ram in his place in Islamic tradition.

Practice

The sacrifice is normally carried out after the Eid prayer on the days of Eid al-Adha. Animals traditionally include sheep, goats, cattle and camels, subject to rules about age and health. The meat is commonly divided among the household, relatives and people in need, making charity a central part of the observance.

Interpretation

Qurbani is associated with devotion, gratitude and sharing rather than with the meat itself. Many contemporary Muslims arrange sacrifices through charities that distribute meat in other communities. Local regulations govern slaughter, animal welfare and food safety, and observance varies by legal school and region.

The festival context is connected with Meccan themes also recalled in Quraysh.
