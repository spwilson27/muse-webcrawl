Semantic loan

A semantic loan is a process of borrowing the meaning of a word from a foreign language rather than borrowing the word itself. The native word takes on an additional sense modelled on the foreign term, because the two words share at least one meaning. Semantic loans are a common result of language contact and are closely related to, but distinct from, loanwords and calques (loan translations).

Distinction from related processes

In a loanword, both form and meaning are imported (e.g. English kindergarten from German). In a calque, the foreign expression is translated morpheme by morpheme (e.g. English skyscraper → French gratte-ciel). In a semantic loan, only a new meaning is added to a pre-existing native word.

Examples

English mouse acquired the meaning "computer pointing device" on the model of its existing metaphor, a development paralleled in many languages that extended their native word for the animal. In French, réaliser traditionally meant "to make real", but under English influence it also came to mean "to become aware" (from realize) — a change frequently criticised by purists. German kontrollieren originally meant "to check/verify", but has been extended towards the English sense of control, "to command". Many semantic loans arose in the early medieval period as vernacular languages absorbed concepts from Latin.
