The Korean Central News Agency (KCNA) is the state news agency of North Korea. Founded on December 5, 1946, the agency portrays the views of the North Korean government and the Workers' Party of Korea for both domestic and foreign audiences. It is headquartered in Pyongyang and has correspondents and exchange agreements with news agencies in several countries.

Overview

KCNA is the sole source of news for all North Korean media, including the newspapers Rodong Sinmun and Minju Choson and Korean Central Television. It publishes daily reports in Korean and in translations including English, Spanish, Chinese, Russian, and Japanese. Its output consists largely of official statements, reports of the activities of the country's leadership, and articles praising the state's policies, alongside strongly worded commentary directed at South Korea, the United States, and Japan.

Foreign access

Foreign news organizations closely monitor KCNA dispatches as a primary window into official North Korean positions. The agency launched a website in 2010. South Korean access to KCNA content has historically been restricted under the National Security Act, though journalists and officials are granted special permission to monitor it. In 2011 the Associated Press opened a bureau in Pyongyang and became one of the few Western agencies with a presence in the country.
