Mary Elizabeth Truss (born 26 July 1975) is a British politician who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom and Leader of the Conservative Party from September to October 2022. Her premiership of 49 days is the shortest in British history. She was Member of Parliament for South West Norfolk from 2010 until losing her seat in 2024.

Early life and career

Truss grew up in Paisley and Leeds, attended Roundhay School, and read Philosophy, Politics and Economics at Merton College, Oxford, where as a Liberal Democrat she supported abolishing the monarchy. After switching to the Conservatives in 1996, she worked for Shell and Cable & Wireless and was deputy director of the think tank Reform. She entered Parliament in 2010 and rose through junior ministerial posts.

Cabinet career

Truss served as Secretary of State for the Environment (2014–2016), Justice Secretary and first female Lord Chancellor (2016–2017), Chief Secretary to the Treasury, and International Trade Secretary (2019–2021), in which role she negotiated post-Brexit trade agreements. As Foreign Secretary from 2021 she took a hard line in support of Ukraine after Russia's invasion and was involved in the dispute over the Northern Ireland Protocol.

Premiership

Truss defeated Rishi Sunak in the Conservative leadership election following Boris Johnson's resignation. Her government launched an "energy price guarantee" to cap household bills, but the "mini-budget" of 23 September 2022, delivered by Chancellor Kwasi Kwarteng, announced large unfunded tax cuts that triggered a collapse in the pound, a spike in gilt yields and emergency Bank of England intervention. Kwarteng was sacked, most of the measures were reversed, and Truss resigned on 20 October after losing the confidence of her MPs. She was succeeded by Sunak.

After office

Truss published the memoir Ten Years to Save the West (2024) and has campaigned on free-market and populist-conservative themes internationally. At the 2024 general election she lost South West Norfolk, becoming one of a record number of former prime ministers to lose their seat.
