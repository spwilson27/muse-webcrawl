John Worrall (born 1946) is a British philosopher of science, professor at the London School of Economics, known principally for originating the position of structural realism in the scientific realism debate.

Work

Worrall studied at the LSE, where he was influenced by Imre Lakatos and John Watkins, and spent his career there, editing the British Journal for the Philosophy of Science and serving as president of the British Society for the Philosophy of Science. In his 1989 paper "Structural Realism: The Best of Both Worlds?" he proposed that scientific theories reveal the mathematical or structural content of the world even when their ontological claims are discarded — citing the survival of Fresnel's equations within Maxwell's theory as a case where structure, not substance, was retained across theory change. The position aimed to answer both the pessimistic meta-induction against realism and the "no miracles" argument in its favour.

Worrall has also written extensively on the methodology of scientific research programmes, on evidence in medicine — where he has been a prominent critic of weak clinical-trial methodology and of alternative medicine — and on issues ranging from Bayesianism to the history of optics.
