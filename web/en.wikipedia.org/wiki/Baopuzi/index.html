The Baopuzi (Chinese: 抱朴子, "The Master Who Embraces Simplicity") is a Chinese text written by the Jin dynasty scholar and alchemist Ge Hong (283–343), completed around 320 CE. It is one of the most important surviving sources for early Chinese alchemy and religious Daoism.

Structure

The work consists of two parts. The twenty "Inner Chapters" (neipian) deal with Daoist practice: the quest for immortality through alchemy (waidan), meditation, breath control, dietary regimens and charms. The fifty "Outer Chapters" (waipian) are essays on Confucian social and literary topics, politics and Ge Hong's autobiography.

Alchemy

The Inner Chapters give detailed recipes for elixirs, many based on cinnabar (mercuric sulfide) and gold, which Ge Hong believed could confer longevity and supernatural powers on those who consumed them. The text also discusses the classification of immortals and includes practical lore on herbs, minerals and talismans. Despite the toxicity of many ingredients, the Baopuzi preserves valuable early evidence of Chinese chemical knowledge.
