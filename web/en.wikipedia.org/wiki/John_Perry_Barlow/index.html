John Perry Barlow (3 October 1947 – 7 February 2018) was an American poet, essayist, cattle rancher and lyricist for the Grateful Dead. A founding member of the Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF), he was one of the most prominent early theorists and defenders of the free internet.

Life and work

Barlow was raised on the Bar Cross Ranch in Wyoming and educated at Wesleyan University. From the early 1970s he wrote lyrics for the Grateful Dead, collaborating chiefly with guitarist Bob Weir on songs such as "Cassidy", "Mexicali Blues", "Black-Throated Wind" and "Throwing Stones". He also ran the family cattle ranch, which he sold in 1988.

Internet activism

An early participant in the WELL online community, Barlow co-founded the EFF in 1990 with Mitch Kapor and John Gilmore to defend civil liberties in the digital sphere. In 1996, responding to the US Communications Decency Act, he published "A Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace" from Davos, Switzerland, arguing that governments of the industrial world had no sovereignty over the global social space being built online. His 1994 essay "The Economy of Ideas" anticipated debates over intellectual property in the digital age. Barlow died in his sleep in San Francisco in 2018.
