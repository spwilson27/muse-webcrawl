Assab (Tigrinya: ኣሰብ) is a port city in the Southern Red Sea Region of Eritrea, on the west coast of the Red Sea near the Bab-el-Mandeb strait. It was the site of Italy's first colonial acquisition in Africa and long served as one of Ethiopia's two principal seaports. Since the Eritrean–Ethiopian war of 1998–2000, the port has been largely idle, as landlocked Ethiopia shifted its trade to Djibouti.

History

In 1869 the Italian explorer Giuseppe Sapeto arranged the purchase of land at Assab Bay for the Rubattino shipping company, intending a coaling station on the new Suez Canal route; the Italian government took over the possession in 1882, making Assab the nucleus of the colony of Italian Eritrea (proclaimed 1890), before the colonial capital moved to Massawa. Under Italian rule and later under Ethiopian administration, Assab's port was expanded with Soviet assistance, including an oil refinery built in the 1960s, and handled a large share of Ethiopia's imports, especially during the Ogaden War era.

After Eritrean independence

Assab became part of independent Eritrea in 1993, but Ethiopia continued to use it duty-free under bilateral arrangements until the border war erupted in 1998. The refinery shut down, shipping ceased, and the city declined sharply. Periodic reports of renewed interest — including a United Arab Emirates military logistics presence at Assab during the Yemen war from about 2015 — have drawn attention, while Ethiopian statements since 2023 about regaining Red Sea access have revived regional debate over the port's future.

Geography

Assab lies in one of the hottest and driest coastal zones in the world, at the southern end of Eritrea close to the Djibouti border, surrounded by volcanic desert terrain of the Danakil Depression. Its road link runs northwest to Massawa and inland toward the Ethiopian highlands.
