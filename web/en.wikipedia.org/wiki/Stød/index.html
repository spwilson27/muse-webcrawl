Stød

Stød appears as a period of creaky, irregular phonation in the syllable rime

The stød (Danish: [ˈstøð], literally "thrust" or "push") is a suprasegmental phonetic feature of the Danish language, a laryngealisation — often realised as creaky voice or a glottal stop-like constriction — that occurs on certain stressed syllables. It is phonemic: it distinguishes word pairs such as hun [hun] "she" versus hund [hunˀ] "dog".

Phonetics and distribution

Stød can only occur in a syllable with a long vowel, or a short vowel followed by a sonorant consonant — the so-called stød basis — and within that domain its presence is partly predictable from morphology and partly lexical. In standard Danish roughly speaking, stød has parallels in the word accents of Norwegian and Swedish, from which it is historically related; it likely developed around the 12th–13th centuries and is absent from some Danish dialects, such as those of Bornholm and parts of southern Denmark, which use tonal distinctions instead.

Notation

In Danish lexicography stød is marked with a small superscript vertical stroke or an apostrophe-like sign after the syllable (in the IPA transcription, [ˀ]). The Dania transcription system uses a dedicated stød mark, and Danish dictionaries indicate it systematically because of its role in distinguishing words.
