The Rocky Horror Picture Show is a 1975 musical comedy horror film produced by Lou Adler and Michael White, directed by Jim Sharman, and distributed by 20th Century Fox. It is an adaptation of Richard O'Brien's 1973 stage musical The Rocky Horror Show, with screenplay by O'Brien and Sharman. The film stars Tim Curry (in his feature debut), Susan Sarandon and Barry Bostwick, with appearances by O'Brien, Patricia Quinn, Nell Campbell, Jonathan Adams, Peter Hinwood, Meat Loaf and Charles Gray.

Plot

Newly engaged Brad Majors and Janet Weiss, stranded by a flat tyre on a rainy November night, seek help at a castle inhabited by Dr. Frank-N-Furter, a flamboyant, corset-clad "sweet transvestite from Transsexual, Transylvania" — in reality an alien scientist — who is unveiling Rocky, the muscular creature he has created. Through a night of songs, seductions, the murder of the delivery boy Eddie, and a floor show that descends into chaos, the castle is revealed to be a spacecraft; Frank's servants Riff Raff and Magenta mutiny, kill Frank and Rocky, and depart for their home planet, leaving Brad, Janet and the wheelchair-using Dr. Scott amid the wreckage as the narrator ("The Criminologist") concludes that mankind is "just insects crawling on the planet's face."

Songs

The score, a pastiche of rock and roll and B-movie science fiction and horror, includes "Science Fiction/Double Feature," "Dammit Janet," "The Time Warp," "Sweet Transvestite," "I Can Make You a Man," "Hot Patootie – Bless My Soul," "Touch-a, Touch-a, Touch-a, Touch Me," "Rose Tint My World" and "I'm Going Home."

Cult following

The film was initially a commercial and critical disappointment but, beginning at New York's Waverly Theater in 1976, became the defining midnight movie: audiences returned weekly in costume, shouted scripted responses at the screen, and threw props (rice, toast, water, toilet paper) at the appropriate moments, while "shadow casts" acted the film out beneath the screen. 20th Century Fox withdrew it from regular release yet it has remained in limited release ever since — the longest-running theatrical release in cinema history — grossing some $170 million. In 2005 the film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry by the Library of Congress as "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant." A television remake, The Rocky Horror Picture Show: Let's Do the Time Warp Again, aired on Fox in 2016.
