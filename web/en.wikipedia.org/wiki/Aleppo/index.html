Aleppo (Arabic: حلب, Ḥalab) is a city in northern Syria and one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, occupied since at least the 6th millennium BC. Before the Syrian civil war it was Syria's largest city and its commercial and industrial hub, famed for its covered souks, soap, textiles and cuisine.

History

Aleppo stood at the crossroads of trade routes linking Mesopotamia, Anatolia and the Mediterranean. It was an Amorite and Hittite city, then Aramaean, Persian, and a Hellenistic foundation refounded as Beroea. It flourished under Byzantine and then Islamic rule; the great Citadel, on a mound in the city centre, was rebuilt largely under the Ayyubids and Mamluks. Under the Ottomans, Aleppo was the empire's third city, a terminus of the Silk Road hosting European consulates and trading houses ("factories") — the setting for part of Shakespeare's Macbeth allusion and Agatha Christie's arrival on the Orient Express.

The old city

The Ancient City of Aleppo, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1986, contains the Great Mosque (Umayyad Mosque, founded 715, with its celebrated 11th-century minaret), some 17 km of covered souks — among the largest in the world — plus khans, hammams, mosques and historic houses, and the landmark Aleppo soap tradition of laurel-oil soap.

Civil war and aftermath

From 2012 to 2016 Aleppo was a principal battlefield of the Syrian civil war, divided between government-held west and rebel-held east; the siege and bombardment destroyed large parts of the old city, including the Umayyad Mosque's minaret (2013) and much of the souks. Government forces retook the city in December 2016. Aleppo was again affected by the February 2023 earthquake, and the city changed hands in late 2024 as the Assad government fell.
