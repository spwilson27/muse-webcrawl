Wheel profile

The wheel profile is the shape of the cross-section of a railway wheel where it contacts the rail. The profile of the running surface — including the tread and the flange — governs how the wheelset steers through curves, how wear develops, and how stable the vehicle is at speed.

Geometry

A typical profile consists of a slightly coned tread (historically a conicity of about 1 in 20), a flange on the inner side that provides guidance on curves and through switches, and a fillet between tread and flange. The difference in rolling radius between the two wheels of a rigid axle, produced by the coned treads, allows the wheelset to steer itself along curves and to center itself on straight track.

Wear and maintenance

In service, wheels wear into "hollow" or otherwise degraded profiles that increase contact stresses and the risk of hunting oscillation. Profiles are restored by turning the wheels on an underfloor or bench lathe. Standard profiles, such as the S1002 and P8 profiles used in Europe, are specified by railway standards.
