Trinity of Carnatic music

The Trinity of Carnatic music (the sangita trimurti) are the three composer-musicians credited with defining the modern form of Carnatic music, the classical music of South India: Tyagaraja (1767–1847), Muthuswami Dikshitar (1775–1835), and Syama Sastri (1762–1827). All three were born in Thiruvarur in present-day Tamil Nadu and were near-contemporaries.

The composers

Tyagaraja, a devotee of Rama, composed thousands of kritis in Telugu, mostly in praise of his deity, including the celebrated Pancharatna Kritis ("five gems"), still performed at the annual Tyagaraja Aradhana festival at Thiruvaiyaru. Muthuswami Dikshitar composed in Sanskrit, often incorporating references to temple deities, astrology, and philosophy; his Navagraha kritis on the nine planets and his use of Hindustani ragas reflect his travels, including time in Varanasi. Syama Sastri, the eldest and least prolific of the three, composed intricate kritis in Telugu and Sanskrit to the goddess Kamakshi, and is noted for rhythmic complexity, including his use of unusual talas and the raga Chintamani.

Legacy

The trinity's kritis form the core repertoire of Carnatic concerts, and their compositions systematised raga forms that performers continue to elaborate today.
