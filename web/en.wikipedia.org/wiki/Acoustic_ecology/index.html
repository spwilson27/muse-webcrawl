Acoustic ecology

Acoustic ecology, sometimes called ecoacoustics or soundscape studies, is a discipline studying the relationship, mediated through sound, between human beings and their environment. It draws on the concept of the soundscape — the totality of sounds in a place — developed by Canadian composer and scholar R. Murray Schafer and the World Soundscape Project at Simon Fraser University in the 1970s.

Soundscapes

Schafer distinguished "hi-fi" soundscapes, where individual sounds can be heard clearly, from "lo-fi" soundscapes dominated by overlapping noise. Researchers classify sounds as geophony (wind, rain, rivers), biophony (animal vocalisations) and anthrophony (human-made sound), following the taxonomy later developed by Bernie Krause.

Applications

Acoustic ecology informs soundscape design, noise policy, conservation biology — where passive acoustic monitoring tracks biodiversity — and composition, including soundscape composition and field recording practices.
