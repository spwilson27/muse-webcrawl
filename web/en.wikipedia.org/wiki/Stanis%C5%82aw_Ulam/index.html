Stanisław Marcin Ulam (1909–1984) was a Polish-American mathematician who played a central role in the Manhattan Project and in the development of the hydrogen bomb. With John von Neumann he pioneered the Monte Carlo method of statistical sampling using computers.

Career

Trained in the Lwów School of Mathematics, Ulam emigrated to the United States in 1935. At Los Alamos he worked on hydrodynamics and neutron diffusion, and in 1951, with Edward Teller, devised the Teller–Ulam design that made practical thermonuclear weapons possible.

Other work

Ulam contributed to set theory, ergodic theory and cellular automata, and noticed the Ulam spiral pattern in prime numbers. His memoir Adventures of a Mathematician appeared in 1976.
