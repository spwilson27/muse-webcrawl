Metta Sandiford-Artest (born Ronald William Artest Jr.; November 13, 1979), previously known as Ron Artest and Metta World Peace, is an American former professional basketball player. Known for his defensive intensity, he played for six NBA teams and won an NBA championship with the Los Angeles Lakers in 2010.

Career

Artest played college basketball at St. John's University and was drafted 16th overall by the Chicago Bulls in 1999. He later played for the Indiana Pacers, where he won the NBA Defensive Player of the Year Award in 2004, the Sacramento Kings, and the Houston Rockets before joining the Lakers in 2009. In Game 7 of the 2010 NBA Finals he scored 20 points and hit a key late three-pointer to help defeat the Boston Celtics.

Controversies and advocacy

Artest received the longest suspension in NBA history (86 games) for his role in the "Malice at the Palace" brawl in 2004. In 2011 he legally changed his name to Metta World Peace, explaining that "Metta" is a Buddhist word for loving-kindness; he changed it again to Metta Sandiford-Artest in 2020. He has been an outspoken advocate for mental health awareness.
