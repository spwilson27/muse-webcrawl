Truckee, California

Truckee is an incorporated town in Nevada County, California, United States, in the Sierra Nevada near the Nevada border. The population was 16,729 at the 2020 census. Located along the Truckee River and Interstate 80, the town is a gateway to the Lake Tahoe ski country and the Donner Pass area.

History

The town takes its name from a Paiute chief known as Truckee, who guided emigrant parties across the Sierra in the 1840s. The area became notorious through the Donner Party tragedy of 1846–47, which occurred near present-day Donner Lake just west of town. Truckee grew as a stop on the First Transcontinental Railroad, completed through Donner Pass in 1868, and became an important lumber and ice-harvesting centre, shipping Sierra ice to San Francisco. Its early Chinatown was among the largest in the West before anti-Chinese violence, including the "Truckee method" of boycott and expulsion, drove residents out in the 1880s.

Geography and climate

Truckee sits at an elevation of about 5,817 feet (1,773 m) and is one of the coldest towns in the contiguous United States, frequently recording the lowest overnight temperatures in California due to cold-air pooling in its mountain valley. Winters bring heavy snow, supporting major ski resorts nearby such as Northstar California, Palisades Tahoe, Sugar Bowl and Boreal, while summers are dry and mild.

Transportation

Interstate 80 and Amtrak's California Zephyr serve the town; the historic depot stands in the preserved commercial row of old brick and stone buildings in downtown Truckee.
