Ballet company

A company performing a classical full-length ballet

A ballet company is a type of dance troupe that performs classical ballet, neoclassical ballet and contemporary ballet works. A major company typically employs dozens of dancers organised in a hierarchy — principals, soloists and corps de ballet — led by an artistic director and supported by ballet masters, choreographers, an orchestra and production staff.

Organisation and repertoire

Companies perform seasons of full-length story ballets such as Swan Lake, The Nutcracker, Giselle and The Sleeping Beauty, together with mixed programmes of one-act works, including new commissions. Many leading companies run attached schools that train students in their house style — the Vaganova, Cecchetti, Bournonville and Royal Academy of Dance methods among them. Historically, the oldest companies grew out of court and opera-house ensembles: the Paris Opera Ballet traces its origins to 1669, while other celebrated troupes include the Mariinsky and Bolshoi ballets of Russia, The Royal Ballet in London, New York City Ballet, American Ballet Theatre, the Stuttgart Ballet and the Australian Ballet. Touring is central to the model: companies perform in residence at a home theatre and tour nationally and internationally.
