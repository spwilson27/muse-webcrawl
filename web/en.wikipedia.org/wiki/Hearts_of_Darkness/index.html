Hearts of Darkness

Hearts of Darkness: A Filmmaker's Apocalypse is a 1991 American documentary film about the production of Apocalypse Now (1979), Francis Ford Coppola's Vietnam War epic. It was directed by Fax Bahr and George Hickenlooper, based on footage shot by Coppola's wife Eleanor Coppola during the film's production in the Philippines in 1976–77, and takes its title from Heart of Darkness, the Joseph Conrad novella on which Apocalypse Now was loosely based.

Content

The documentary chronicles the extraordinarily troubled production of Apocalypse Now: a typhoon destroyed expensive sets and halted filming; lead actor Harvey Keitel was fired and replaced by Martin Sheen, who then suffered a near-fatal heart attack; Marlon Brando arrived overweight and underprepared; and the shoot ballooned far beyond its schedule and budget, with Coppola financing overruns personally and risking financial ruin. The film includes Eleanor Coppola's behind-the-scenes footage and her private audio recordings of her husband, who at one point despairs that the production is "a failure" spiralling out of control.

Reception

Hearts of Darkness premiered at the 1991 Cannes Film Festival and was released theatrically the same year. It was widely praised by critics and is often cited as one of the greatest documentaries about filmmaking. It won several awards, including two Emmy Awards and a Directors Guild of America award.

Legacy

The film helped establish the legend of Apocalypse Now as one of the most famously difficult productions in cinema history, and Eleanor Coppola's companion book, Notes: The Making of Apocalypse Now (1979), is drawn upon throughout.
