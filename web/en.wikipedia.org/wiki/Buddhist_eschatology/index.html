Buddhist eschatology

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image placeholder: Painting of Maitreya, the Buddha of the future age]
Maitreya, who is prophesied to appear at the end of the present age

Buddhist eschatology, like that of other major religious traditions, concerns teachings about the end of the present age and the future of the world. Because Buddhism conceives of time as cyclical, unfolding over immense kalpas (aeons) of arising and dissolution, Buddhist eschatology does not generally posit a single final end of the universe. Instead, it describes the decline of the Buddha's teaching (the Dharma), the degeneration of human morality and lifespan, the eventual destruction and re-formation of the world system, and the coming of a future Buddha, Maitreya.

Decline of the Dharma

Early Buddhist texts such as the Cakkavatti-Sihanada Sutta describe a future period in which human beings, having abandoned moral conduct, see their lifespans shrink to as little as ten years, accompanied by violence, famine, and social collapse. At the lowest point, survivors withdraw, repent, and begin to practise virtue, after which lifespans and prosperity gradually increase again. Later traditions in India, China, and Japan developed schemes of the decline of the Dharma into successive ages; in Japanese Buddhism the final age is known as mappo, the "Latter Day of the Law," during which the teaching is held to be in irreversible decline and liberation through one's own effort becomes extremely difficult — a doctrine that strongly influenced Pure Land and Nichiren Buddhism.

Maitreya

Maitreya (Pali: Metteyya) is the future Buddha prophesied to appear on earth after the teaching of Gautama Buddha has been completely forgotten. According to the tradition, Maitreya currently resides in the Tusita heaven, awaiting the proper time. When human lifespans have again risen to tens of thousands of years and the world is ruled by a righteous wheel-turning monarch, Maitreya will be born, attain awakening, and teach the Dharma anew. Devotion to Maitreya has been widespread in both Theravada and Mahayana traditions, and at times millenarian movements have arisen in expectation of his imminent arrival.

End of the world system

Buddhist cosmology describes world systems that pass through phases of formation, endurance, destruction, and emptiness over a great kalpa. In the phase of destruction, a second sun appears in the sky, followed by further suns — up to seven — which progressively dry up the rivers and oceans, burn the earth, and consume the world up to the lower heavens. After a long empty interval, the world re-forms. This cyclical picture means that "the end of the world" in Buddhism is a recurring phase rather than a final terminus.

In East Asian Buddhism

In China, the three-ages scheme (the ages of the True Dharma, the Semblance Dharma, and the Final Dharma) structured much medieval Buddhist thought. Belief in the Final Dharma age motivated new movements emphasising faith in Amitabha Buddha's vows, and in Japan the doctrine of mappo was central to the teachings of Honen, Shinran, and Nichiren during the Kamakura period.
