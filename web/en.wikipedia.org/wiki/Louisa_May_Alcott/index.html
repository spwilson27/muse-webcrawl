Louisa May Alcott

Louisa May Alcott (November 29, 1832 – March 6, 1888) was an American novelist, short story writer, and poet best known as the author of the novel Little Women (1868) and its sequels Little Men (1871) and Jo's Boys (1886). Raised in New England by transcendentalist parents, she grew up among many of the luminaries of the day, including Ralph Waldo Emerson, Nathaniel Hawthorne, Henry David Thoreau and Henry Wadsworth Longfellow.

Early life

Alcott was born in Germantown, Pennsylvania, the second of four daughters of the transcendentalist educator Amos Bronson Alcott and the social worker Abigail May Alcott. The family moved to Boston and then to Concord, Massachusetts, and briefly joined the utopian commune at Fruitlands. Poverty pressed on the family throughout her childhood, and Alcott worked from a young age as a teacher, seamstress, governess and domestic servant to help support them, while writing stories and poems.

Early writing and the Civil War

Her first book, Flower Fables, appeared in 1854. Under the pen name A. M. Barnard she wrote sensational "blood and thunder" tales for popular magazines. During the American Civil War she served as a nurse at a Union hospital in Georgetown in 1862–63 until typhoid fever forced her home; her letters from the hospital were published as Hospital Sketches (1863) and brought her first critical recognition.

Little Women and fame

In 1868 her publisher Thomas Niles urged her to write a book for girls. Alcott wrote Little Women, based on her own coming-of-age with her three sisters, in a matter of weeks. Published in September 1868, it was an immediate success and has never been out of print. Its heroine, Jo March, modelled on Alcott herself, became one of the most beloved characters in American literature. The second part followed in 1869, and the later sequels Little Men and Jo's Boys extended the March family saga. Other novels include An Old Fashioned Girl, Eight Cousins and Rose in Bloom.

Later life and beliefs

Alcott was an abolitionist and a feminist; she supported women's suffrage and was the first woman to register to vote in Concord for a school committee election. She never married. When her sister Elizabeth died in 1858 and her youngest sister May died in 1879, Alcott adopted May's daughter, Louisa May Nieriker ("Lulu"). Her health, damaged by her wartime illness and by mercury-based calomel treatment, declined in her later years, and she died in Boston on March 6, 1888, two days after her father's death. She is buried in Sleepy Hollow Cemetery in Concord.
