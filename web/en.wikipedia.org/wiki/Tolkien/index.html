J. R. R. Tolkien

John Ronald Reuel Tolkien
Born: 3 January 1892, Bloemfontein, Orange Free State
Died: 2 September 1973, Bournemouth, England
Occupation: Writer, philologist, professor
Notable works: The Hobbit; The Lord of the Rings; The Silmarillion

John Ronald Reuel Tolkien (3 January 1892 – 2 September 1973) was an English writer and philologist, Rawlinson and Bosworth Professor of Anglo-Saxon and later Merton Professor of English Language and Literature at the University of Oxford. He is the author of the high-fantasy works The Hobbit (1937) and The Lord of the Rings (1954–55), and is widely regarded as the father of modern fantasy literature.

Life

Born in South Africa and raised in Birmingham, Tolkien served as a signals officer at the Battle of the Somme in the First World War. At Oxford he formed the Inklings literary group with friends including C. S. Lewis. His academic work included the notable lecture "Beowulf: The Monsters and the Critics" (1936) and editions of medieval texts.

Works and legacy

Tolkien built the extensive legendarium of Middle-earth over his lifetime, inventing languages such as Quenya and Sindarin that preceded the stories. The Silmarillion and other posthumous works were edited by his son Christopher. His books have sold hundreds of millions of copies, been adapted into acclaimed films, and profoundly shaped the fantasy genre.
