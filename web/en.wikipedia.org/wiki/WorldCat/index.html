WorldCat is a union catalogue that itemises the collections of tens of thousands of libraries in more than 100 countries which participate in the OCLC global cooperative. It is operated by OCLC, Inc. The subscribing member libraries collectively maintain WorldCat's database, the world's largest bibliographic database. Including print and electronic items, it records over 500 million bibliographic records in 483 languages, representing over 3 billion physical and digital assets.

History

OCLC was founded in 1967 as the Ohio College Library Center, and its online shared cataloguing system went live in 1971 under founder Frederick G. Kilgour. The union catalogue grew from a regional utility into a global resource as libraries contributed original cataloguing and holdings. A public-facing version appeared on the web (FirstSearch and the open WorldCat.org in 2006), allowing anyone to search and locate items at nearby libraries. In 2022 OCLC relaunched the public service as WorldCat.org, and in 2023 the address moved to search.worldcat.org.

Contents and use

WorldCat covers books, journals, maps, sound recordings, films, archival materials, e-books, and digital objects. Records use MARC format and are identified by OCLC numbers. Member libraries use the database for copy cataloguing, interlibrary loan, and collection analysis; services built on it include WorldCat Discovery, FirstSearch, and the WorldCat knowledge base. WorldCat data also underpins VIAF, the virtual international authority file linking name authority records across national libraries.

Access and controversies

Full database access is subscription-based for institutions, while the public interface is free. OCLC's record-use policies have periodically drawn controversy, notably a proposed 2008 policy on the transfer and use of WorldCat records that was withdrawn and revised after community criticism. Open-data initiatives, such as OCLC's release of FAST (Faceted Application of Subject Terminology) data, coexist with the cooperative's licensing model.
