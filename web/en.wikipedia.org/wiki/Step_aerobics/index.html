Step aerobics

Step aerobics, also simply called step, is a form of aerobic exercise that involves stepping on and off a rectangular, square or circular platform of adjustable height, usually 4 to 12 inches, often to music and with choreographed movements. It was created in the late 1980s by American fitness instructor Gin Miller and became one of the most popular group-fitness formats of the 1990s, distributed worldwide through programmes such as Reebok Step and Les Mills BodyStep.

History

Miller developed the workout in 1986 while rehabilitating a knee injury, following a doctor's advice to strengthen the joint by stepping up and down on a milk crate. Working with Reebok, she launched the Step Reebok programme in 1989 with an adjustable plastic bench. Research commissioned from exercise physiologists (notably Lorna and Peter Francis) reported that step training at appropriate heights and tempos provided cardiovascular benefits comparable to running at lower impact, helping to drive its rapid adoption in health clubs.

Practice

A class follows an instructor through combinations built from basic steps — the basic step, V-step, A-step, knee lift, repeater, turnstep, over-the-top and straddle — typically counted in 32-beat musical phrases at around 118–128 beats per minute. Platform height and movement intensity are adjusted for fitness level. Choreography ranges from simple "squared" patterns to dance-like advanced routines; athletic variants emphasise power moves and conditioning intervals.

Benefits and risks

Step aerobics improves cardiovascular fitness, lower-body muscular endurance, balance and coordination, and burns a comparable number of calories to other moderate-to-vigorous aerobic activities. The main risks are knee and Achilles tendon strain from excessive platform height, fast tempos or poor foot placement; guidelines recommend keeping knee flexion within safe angles and stepping with the whole foot on the platform.
