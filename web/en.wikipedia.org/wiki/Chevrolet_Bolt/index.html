Chevrolet Bolt

The Chevrolet Bolt EV is a battery-electric subcompact hatchback developed and manufactured by General Motors in partnership with LG of South Korea. Launched in late 2016, it was the first affordable long-range electric car sold in the United States, offering an EPA-rated range of 238 miles (383 km) at a price below US$40,000. A crossover-styled companion model, the Bolt EUV, followed in 2021.

History

The Bolt was previewed as a concept at the 2015 North American International Auto Show and went into production at GM's Orion Assembly plant in November 2016. It won numerous awards, including the 2017 Motor Trend Car of the Year, North American Car of the Year and Green Car of the Year. In Europe it was sold briefly as the Opel Ampera-e.

Battery recall

In 2020–2021, GM recalled every Bolt ever built because of a risk of battery fires caused by defective LG cells; production was halted for months and GM replaced battery modules across the fleet, with LG reimbursing most of the roughly US$2 billion cost.

Discontinuation and return

GM ended Bolt production in December 2023 as it shifted to its Ultium platform, but following strong demand announced that the Bolt nameplate would return on Ultium architecture.
