Demand curve

Demand curve
Type: Economic model
Field: Microeconomics
Related: Supply and demand, Price elasticity

A demand curve is a graph depicting the relationship between the price of a commodity or service and the quantity demanded for a given period of time. Typically price appears on the vertical axis and quantity on the horizontal axis.

Law of demand

The curve normally slopes downward, reflecting the law of demand: as price rises, quantity demanded falls, other things equal, and vice versa. This follows from diminishing marginal utility and income and substitution effects.

Shifts and movements

A change in the good's own price causes a movement along the curve. Changes in income, tastes, prices of substitutes and complements, expectations, or number of buyers shift the whole curve left or right.

Elasticity

The steepness relates to price elasticity of demand. A flat curve indicates elastic demand; a steep curve indicates inelastic demand. See also Supply curve and Market equilibrium.

Individual and market demand

Market demand is the horizontal sum of individual demand curves. Firms and governments use estimated demand curves for pricing, taxation and welfare analysis.
