Incarnation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Incarnation is the central Christian doctrine that God the Son, the second person of the Trinity, "became flesh" and was conceived in the womb of the Virgin Mary and born as Jesus of Nazareth, being fully God and fully human in one person. The word derives from the Latin incarnatio, from in carne, "in flesh." The doctrine rests principally on the prologue of the Gospel of John: "The Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us" (John 1:14).

Doctrine

The Incarnation is traditionally dated to the Annunciation, when the angel Gabriel announced Christ's conception to Mary, and is celebrated on 25 March, nine months before Christmas. The Council of Chalcedon (451) defined that Christ is one person (hypostasis) in two natures, divine and human, "without confusion, change, division, or separation." Alternative accounts — that Christ only appeared to be human (Docetism), or that he was a created being (Arianism) — were rejected as heresies by the early councils.

In other traditions

The concept of a deity taking bodily form appears elsewhere, notably in the Hindu doctrine of the avatar, in which gods such as Vishnu descend to earth in embodied form. In Islam, by contrast, the incarnation is emphatically denied: Jesus is honoured as a prophet and born of a virgin, but God does not become human.
