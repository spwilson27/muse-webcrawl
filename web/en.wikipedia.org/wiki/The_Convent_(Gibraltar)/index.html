The Convent has been the official residence of the Governor of Gibraltar since 1728.

The Convent is the official residence of the Governor of Gibraltar, located at the southern end of Main Street in Gibraltar. Originally a convent of Franciscan friars, it was completed in 1531. Following the Anglo-Dutch capture of Gibraltar in 1704 and the Treaty of Utrecht in 1713, the building became the residence of the British governor in 1728.

History

Franciscan friars arrived in Gibraltar during the Spanish period and built a church and convent dedicated to Our Lady. After the British capture, the friars departed and the building was converted, becoming the governor's residence in 1728. Much of the present building dates from the 18th and 19th centuries, including a Georgian facade added when the front was rebuilt in the 19th century. The adjacent King's Chapel (originally the convent church, known in Spanish times as Nuestra Señora de los Remedios) serves as the garrison church.

Features and traditions

The building contains a ballroom added in the early 19th century and is guarded by members of the Royal Gibraltar Regiment; the Ceremony of the Keys is performed weekly outside the residence. The Convent is said to be haunted by the "Grey Lady," the ghost of a nun allegedly walled up alive for attempting to elope. The governor's residence should not be confused with the Convent Guard Room, opposite, or the former convent in other parts of the town.
