Farewell My Concubine (film)

Farewell My Concubine (Chinese: 霸王别姬; pinyin: Bàwáng Bié Jī) is a 1993 Chinese historical drama film directed by Chen Kaige, starring Leslie Cheung, Zhang Fengyi and Gong Li. Adapted from the novel by Lilian Lee, it spans roughly fifty years of twentieth-century Chinese history through the lives of two Peking opera performers.

Plot

Cheng Dieyi and Duan Xiaolou are trained together from childhood in a harsh Peking opera school; Dieyi specialises in female roles (dan) and Xiaolou in heroic male roles. Their signature piece is the opera Farewell My Concubine, in which Dieyi plays the concubine Yu Ji to Xiaolou's King of Chu. Dieyi's devotion blurs the boundary between stage and life. When Xiaolou marries the former prostitute Juxian (Gong Li), a jealous triangle forms, and the trio are swept through the Second Sino-Japanese War, the Chinese Civil War and the Cultural Revolution, during which they are forced to denounce one another.

Reception and awards

The film shared the Palme d'Or at the 1993 Cannes Film Festival — the first (and to date only) Chinese-language film to win the prize — and received the FIPRESCI prize. It was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film and won Golden Globe and BAFTA awards in that category. It is frequently ranked among the greatest films ever made, noted for Leslie Cheung's performance and its sweeping treatment of modern Chinese history. The film was briefly banned in China before release.
