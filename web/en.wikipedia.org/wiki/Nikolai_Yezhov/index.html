Nikolai Ivanovich Yezhov (1 May 1895 – 4 February 1940) was a Soviet secret police official under Joseph Stalin. As head of the NKVD from 1936 to 1938, he directed the most intense phase of the Great Purge — a period so identified with him that it is known in Russian as the Yezhovshchina. Of very short stature (about 151 cm), he was nicknamed "the Bloody Dwarf".

Rise

Yezhov, a foundry worker's son with minimal formal education, joined the Bolsheviks in 1917 and rose through party personnel work, earning a reputation as a meticulous and ruthless administrator loyal to Stalin. In September 1936 he replaced Genrikh Yagoda as People's Commissar for Internal Affairs, with Stalin's charge to intensify the hunt for alleged enemies.

The Great Purge

Under Yezhov the NKVD ran mass operations against "former kulaks", national minorities, officials and the military leadership. Order No. 1 of July 1937 set quotas for arrests and executions, which local organs competed to exceed. In 1937–38 roughly 1.5 million people were arrested and about 680,000–750,000 executed, alongside the staged Moscow Trials of Old Bolsheviks. Yezhov personally signed execution lists and was briefly among the most powerful men in the USSR.

Downfall

In late 1938 Stalin reined in the terror and made Yezhov its scapegoat. He was demoted, arrested in April 1939, and — after torture and a secret trial — shot on 4 February 1940. Soviet censorship then removed him from published photographs, most famously an image beside Stalin at the Moscow Canal, making him an iconic example of the airbrushed "vanishing commissar".
