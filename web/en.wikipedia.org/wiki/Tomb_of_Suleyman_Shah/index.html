Tomb of Suleyman Shah

The Tomb of Suleyman Shah (Turkish: Süleyman Şah Türbesi) is the mausoleum of Süleyman Shah, the grandfather of Osman I, founder of the Ottoman Empire. By treaty, the tomb's site is Turkish sovereign territory administered as an exclave within Syria, guarded by Turkish soldiers.

History

According to Ottoman tradition, Süleyman Shah drowned crossing the Euphrates around 1236 and was buried near the castle of Qal'at Ja'bar. Under the 1921 Treaty of Ankara between France and Turkey, the tomb remained Turkish property. In 1973, when the site was threatened by the rising waters of Lake Assad, the tomb was moved to a new location near Karakozak village, north of Manbij.

2015 relocation

In February 2015, amid the Syrian civil war and threats from ISIL, Turkey launched Operation Shah Euphrates: a large armoured column entered Syria overnight, evacuated the roughly 40 guarding soldiers, removed the remains, and demolished the compound. The tomb was temporarily brought to Turkey and then re-established at the Syrian village of Ashme, just 180 metres from the Turkish border, pending an eventual return to its previous site.
