The Library of Congress Subject Headings (LCSH) comprise a thesaurus (in the information science sense, a controlled vocabulary) of subject headings, maintained by the United States Library of Congress, for use in bibliographic records. It is the most widely used subject heading system in the English-speaking world and is employed by libraries of all sizes in many countries.

History

Subject headings were first used by the Library of Congress in 1898, when the Library published its List of Subject Headings Used in the Dictionary Catalogues. The system grew out of the cataloging practices developed by Charles Ammi Cutter and others in the late nineteenth century. It was published in print form for many years as large red volumes, commonly called the "red books." The vocabulary is now maintained as an online database and is freely available through the Library of Congress Authorities website and as linked data.

Structure

LCSH headings are pre-coordinated: catalogers combine main headings with subdivisions to build complex subject strings. Subdivisions may be topical (such as "History"), geographic, chronological, or form subdivisions. Headings follow the inverted and direct forms, and the vocabulary includes cross-references guiding users from unused terms to the authorized form, from broader to narrower terms, and between related terms.

Use

LCSH is applied in MARC bibliographic records, principally in the 650 field for topical terms. It underpins the subject cataloging of the Library of Congress's own collections of more than 170 million items, and because LC records are distributed to other libraries, LCSH headings appear in catalogs worldwide. Critics have noted that the vocabulary has historically contained biased or outdated terminology; the Library of Congress works through a formal proposal process, involving the American Library Association's Subject Analysis Committee, to revise headings.
