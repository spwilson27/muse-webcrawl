Mighty Mouse

Mighty Mouse is an American animated cartoon character created by Isadore Klein and produced by Terrytoons. The character is a superhero mouse, originally created as a parody of Superman, who flies to the rescue of other mice menaced by cats and other villains, often singing his approach in operatic style: "Here I come to save the day!"

History

The character first appeared in the 1942 theatrical short The Mouse of Tomorrow, initially named "Super Mouse" — he appeared under that name in seven cartoons before being renamed Mighty Mouse in 1944's The Wicked Wolf, after a pre-existing comic-book character's name became an issue. He went on to star in more than 80 theatrical shorts through 1961.

Television

Mighty Mouse became a fixture of early television: Mighty Mouse Playhouse ran on CBS Saturday mornings from 1955 to 1967, repackaging the theatrical shorts. Later series included Filmation's The New Adventures of Mighty Mouse and Heckle & Jeckle (1979–1980) and Ralph Bakshi's Mighty Mouse: The New Adventures (1987–1988), the latter notable for launching the careers of animators including John Kricfalusi — and for a notorious controversy in which a scene of the character sniffing a powder was alleged by a religious broadcaster to depict cocaine use.

In popular culture

Andy Kaufman's famous Saturday Night Live lip-sync of the Mighty Mouse theme song in 1975 helped cement the character's catchphrase in pop culture. In 2005 Apple named its first multi-button mouse the "Mighty Mouse", licensing the name from CBS.
