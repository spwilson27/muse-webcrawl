Pachygyria

Pachygyria is a congenital malformation of the cerebral hemispheres characterised by abnormally thick, broad and flat gyri (convolutions) of the cerebral cortex, with shallow sulci between them. The name derives from the Greek pachy, meaning thick, and gyria, the folds of the brain.

Causes

Pachygyria results from disordered neuronal migration during foetal development, when neurons fail to reach their proper positions in the cortex. It forms part of the lissencephaly spectrum: lissencephaly proper ("smooth brain") shows few or no gyri, while pachygyria shows a small number of broad, thickened gyri, and polymicrogyria, by contrast, shows excessive small folds. Genetic causes include mutations in genes such as LIS1, DCX and tubulin genes; congenital infections and other developmental insults can also be responsible.

Clinical features

Affected children typically have intellectual disability, developmental delay, epilepsy and motor problems, with severity depending on the extent of cortex involved. Diagnosis is by neuroimaging, principally MRI. There is no cure; treatment is supportive, including anti-epileptic medication and developmental therapies.
