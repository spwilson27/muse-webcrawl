Indian human spaceflight programme

The Indian human spaceflight programme is an initiative of the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) to develop the technology needed to launch crewed orbital spacecraft. Its first mission, Gaganyaan, aims to carry a crew of three to low Earth orbit aboard an Indian-built spacecraft launched on the human-rated LVM3 rocket (HLVM3) from Sriharikota.

Development

Preliminary studies began in the 2000s; the programme was formally announced in 2018 by Prime Minister Narendra Modi. Key milestones include the Crew Module Atmospheric Re-entry Experiment (CARE, 2014), pad abort tests, and the TV-D1 in-flight abort demonstration in October 2023. Uncrewed Gaganyaan test flights precede the first crewed flight. Four astronaut candidates, all Indian Air Force test pilots, were announced in 2024 and trained in Russia and India.

Context

The first Indian in space was Rakesh Sharma, who flew aboard a Soviet Soyuz in 1984. A successful Gaganyaan mission would make India the fourth nation to independently launch humans into space, after the USSR/Russia, the United States and China. Longer-term plans include the Bharatiya Antariksha Station space station.
