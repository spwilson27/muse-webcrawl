Blaise reaction

The Blaise reaction is an organic chemical reaction that forms a β-keto ester from the reaction of zinc metal with an α-bromo ester in the presence of a nitrile. It was reported by the French chemist Edmond Blaise in 1901.

Mechanism

Zinc inserts into the carbon–bromine bond to give an organozinc intermediate analogous to a Reformatsky reagent. This adds to the carbon–nitrogen triple bond of the nitrile; aqueous work-up hydrolyses the resulting imine to the ketone, furnishing the β-keto ester.

Applications

The reaction is a useful route to β-keto esters bearing substituents derived from the nitrile component. A related transformation, the Blaise–Maire reaction, converts acid chlorides into ketones using organozinc compounds.
