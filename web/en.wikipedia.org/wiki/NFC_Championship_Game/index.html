NFC Championship Game

The NFC Championship Game is the annual championship game of the National Football Conference (NFC) and one of the two semi-final playoff games of the National Football League (NFL), the largest professional American football league in the world. The game is played on the last Sunday in January by the two remaining playoff teams in the NFC, the Sunday before the Super Bowl. The winner receives the George Halas Trophy, named after the founder of the Chicago Bears, and advances to face the winner of the AFC Championship Game in the Super Bowl.

History

The game was created after the AFL–NFL merger of 1970, when the old National Football League's teams were organised into the National Football Conference. The first NFC Championship Game was played following the 1970 season, with the Dallas Cowboys defeating the San Francisco 49ers. From 1970 to 1977 the playoff format was smaller; the modern tournament expanded over the years, and since the 2020 season seven teams per conference qualify, with the championship game contested by the survivors of the wild-card, divisional rounds. From 1970 through 1978 the NFL rotated the home team among division winners; since 1979 home-field advantage has gone to the team with the better playoff seeding and record.

Records

The Dallas Cowboys and San Francisco 49ers have each won eight NFC championships, the most in the conference; the 49ers have appeared in the game the most times (19 appearances). Other frequent winners include the Green Bay Packers, Washington, the New York Giants, Philadelphia Eagles and Los Angeles/St. Louis Rams. Famous editions include the 1981 game, decided by "The Catch" — Joe Montana's touchdown pass to Dwight Clark for the 49ers against Dallas — and the 2014 game, remembered for the 49ers–Seahawks rivalry and Richard Sherman's postgame interview. The NFC champion has gone on to win the Super Bowl in roughly half of the meetings.
