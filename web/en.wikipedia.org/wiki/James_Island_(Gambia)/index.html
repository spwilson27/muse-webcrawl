James Island (Gambia)

Location: River Gambia, The Gambia

Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (2003)

James Island is an island in the Gambia River, in The Gambia, about 30 kilometres (19 miles) from the river's mouth. It is the site of Fort James, a former trading post and slave-trading station, and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2003 as part of the "Kunta Kinteh Island and Related Sites" listing.

History

The island was first occupied by Portuguese traders in the 15th century. In 1651 the Duchy of Courland and Semigallia (present-day Latvia) established Fort Jacob there; the island was later taken by the Dutch, then by the English, who renamed it James Island after King James II. The fort changed hands repeatedly between Britain and France during the 17th and 18th centuries as a base for the trade in enslaved people, gold, and ivory.

World Heritage

James Island, along with other sites connected to the slave trade in the region, was inscribed by UNESCO in 2003. The island's ruins — including parts of the fort, a storehouse, and a gun battery — are eroding into the river and are the subject of conservation efforts. The site is associated in popular memory with Kunta Kinte, the protagonist of Alex Haley's novel Roots, although the historical connection is debated.
