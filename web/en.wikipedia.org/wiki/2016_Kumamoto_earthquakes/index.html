The 2016 Kumamoto earthquakes were a series of powerful earthquakes that struck Kumamoto Prefecture on the Japanese island of Kyushu in April 2016. A magnitude 6.2 foreshock on the evening of 14 April was followed in the early hours of 16 April by a magnitude 7.0 mainshock, the largest to strike inland Kyushu in the instrumental era.

Sequence

The earthquakes occurred along the Futagawa and Hinagu fault zones at shallow depth beneath the city of Kumamoto and the town of Mashiki, producing shaking of the maximum intensity 7 on the Japanese seismic scale — recorded twice in the same locality, a first in Japanese records. Thousands of aftershocks followed, and seismic activity migrated north-east toward Ōita Prefecture and the Mount Aso area.

Impact

Fifty people were killed directly by the shaking and landslides, and official figures eventually counted 277 deaths including disaster-related deaths among evacuees. More than 180,000 people evacuated at the peak. Mashiki was devastated, parts of Kumamoto Castle's stone walls and turrets collapsed, the Aso Shrine's worship hall was destroyed, and landslides severed roads, bridges and the Kyushu Shinkansen line in the Aso valley. Supply chains for Japan's car and electronics industries were disrupted nationwide.

Significance

The disaster led to revisions in Japanese building and disaster-response practice, notably because many deaths occurred in the second, larger shock in buildings damaged by the first, and because of the toll taken by prolonged evacuation in cars ("economy class syndrome").
