Balsas River

The Balsas River (Spanish: Río Balsas) is a major river of south-central Mexico, flowing about 771 km from the mountains of Puebla through Guerrero and Michoacán to the Pacific Ocean at Lázaro Cárdenas. Its basin, the Balsas Depression, is famed as the region where maize was first domesticated from teosinte thousands of years ago.

Course

Formed by the Atoyac and other streams in Puebla, the river is known in its upper reaches as the Atoyac, then the Mezcala, before becoming the Balsas proper. It cuts deep canyons between the Sierra Madre del Sur and the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt, forming part of the Guerrero–Michoacán border. Major dams include La Villita and El Infiernillo, among Mexico's largest hydroelectric projects, and the Carlos Ramírez Ulloa (El Caracol) dam.

Ecology and history

The dry Balsas basin is a centre of endemism for cacti, bursera trees, and freshwater fish. Archaeologically, the region's caves, such as Guilá Naquitz nearby in Oaxaca, inform debates on early agriculture; genetic studies point to the Balsas valley as the homeland of wild Zea mays subsp. parviglumis, the ancestor of maize.
