Patriot Guard Riders
Founded: 2005, Mulvane, Kansas, USA
Type: Nonprofit organization
Purpose: Attending funerals of military personnel, veterans, and first responders at family invitation

The Patriot Guard Riders (PGR) is an American nonprofit organization, composed largely of motorcycle riders, whose members attend the funerals of United States military personnel, veterans, and first responders at the invitation of the deceased's family. The organization was formed in 2005 in Mulvane, Kansas, initially in response to protests by the Westboro Baptist Church at military funerals.

Activities

At a funeral mission, PGR members form a line holding American flags, acting as a respectful screen and, where necessary, drowning out disruptive noise with motorcycle engines or patriotic singing. Participation is strictly by invitation of the family, is non-confrontational, and is open to non-riders and non-veterans. The group also conducts welcome-home and send-off events for deployed troops, escorts for traveling memorials such as the Vietnam Traveling Wall, and assistance programs for veterans through its Help on the Homefront initiative.

Legal context

The activities of funeral protesters, and the Patriot Guard's counter-presence, prompted legislation restricting protests at funerals, including the federal Respect for America's Fallen Heroes Act (2006) and state laws. In Snyder v. Phelps (2011), the U.S. Supreme Court upheld the protesters' First Amendment rights, while continuing to praise peaceful counter-presences such as the Patriot Guard.
