2020 Beirut explosion
Date: 4 August 2020, ~18:08 EEST
Location: Port of Beirut, Beirut, Lebanon
Cause: Fire detonating ~2,750 tonnes of stored ammonium nitrate
Deaths: 218+
Injured: ~7,000
Damage: ~US$15 billion; ~300,000 homeless

On 4 August 2020, a massive explosion occurred at the Port of Beirut in Lebanon. The blast was caused by the detonation of approximately 2,750 tonnes of ammonium nitrate that had been stored unsafely in a warehouse for six years after being confiscated from the cargo ship MV Rhosus.

The explosion

A fire in Warehouse 12, apparently involving fireworks stored nearby, preceded two explosions; the second, at about 18:08 local time, was one of the largest non-nuclear explosions in history, estimated at around 0.5–1.1 kilotons of TNT equivalent. It generated a seismic event of magnitude 3.3 and a shockwave felt as far away as Cyprus. The port's grain silos partially shielded western Beirut but were destroyed.

Consequences

At least 218 people were killed, about 7,000 injured, and some 300,000 left homeless, with damage estimated at $15 billion. The disaster, occurring amid an economic crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic, triggered mass protests; the government of Hassan Diab resigned days later. Investigations into official negligence continued for years amid political dispute.
