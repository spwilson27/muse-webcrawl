Warm-up

A warm-up is a period of light, progressive physical activity performed before exercise or sport to prepare the body for more intense effort. It typically involves raising body temperature and heart rate, increasing blood flow to the muscles, mobilizing the joints through their range of motion, and rehearsing movement patterns specific to the activity that follows.

Physiology

Warming up raises muscle temperature, which speeds metabolic reactions, reduces the stiffness of muscles and connective tissue, and improves the speed and force of contraction. It also stimulates the production of synovial fluid in the joints and enhances nerve conduction and coordination. A typical warm-up proceeds from general aerobic activity, such as jogging, to dynamic stretching and sport-specific drills of gradually increasing intensity.

Evidence and practice

Structured warm-up programmes — such as the FIFA 11+ in football — have been shown in controlled studies to reduce the incidence of certain injuries, particularly in team sports. Static stretching performed alone before exercise has less support and may temporarily reduce power output; it is generally recommended after activity or as part of a broader routine. A complementary cool-down after exercise aids gradual recovery of heart rate and breathing.
