Gemcitabine

Gemcitabine — Trade name: Gemzar. Chemotherapy medication. IUPAC: 2′,2′-difluoro-2′-deoxycytidine. CAS: 95058-81-4. On the WHO List of Essential Medicines.

Gemcitabine, sold under the brand name Gemzar, among others, is a chemotherapy medication used to treat cancers. It is used in the treatment of testicular cancer, breast cancer, ovarian cancer, non-small cell lung cancer, pancreatic cancer, and bladder cancer. It is given by injection into a vein. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

Gemcitabine is used, often in combination regimens, for pancreatic adenocarcinoma (where it became a standard of care following a 1997 trial showing improved survival and clinical benefit over fluorouracil), non-small cell lung cancer (with cisplatin), bladder cancer, ovarian cancer, breast cancer (with paclitaxel), and other malignancies.

Side effects

Common side effects include bone marrow suppression (low blood cell counts), liver and kidney problems, nausea, fever, rash, shortness of breath, mouth sores, diarrhea, neuropathy, and hair loss. Use during pregnancy may result in harm to the baby. Rare but serious effects include capillary leak syndrome, posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome, and haemolytic uraemic syndrome.

Mechanism of action

Gemcitabine is a nucleoside analogue of deoxycytidine in which two fluorine atoms replace hydrogens on the 2′ carbon. Inside cells it is phosphorylated to active diphosphate and triphosphate metabolites: the triphosphate is incorporated into growing DNA, causing masked chain termination, while the diphosphate inhibits ribonucleotide reductase, depleting the deoxynucleotide pools needed for DNA synthesis.

History

Gemcitabine was patented in 1983 and approved for medical use in 1995; it was developed by Eli Lilly and Company. It is available as a generic medication.
