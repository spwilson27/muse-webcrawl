George Batchelor

George K. Batchelor

Born: 8 March 1920, Melbourne, Australia

Died: 30 March 2000, Cambridge, England

Known for: Turbulence theory, An Introduction to Fluid Dynamics

George Keith Batchelor FRS (8 March 1920 – 30 March 2000) was an Australian applied mathematician and fluid dynamicist, and one of the leading figures in the study of turbulence in the 20th century.

Life and career

Batchelor studied engineering and mathematics at the University of Melbourne. During the Second World War he worked on aerodynamics at the Aeronautical Research Laboratory in Melbourne. In 1945 he moved to Cambridge to work under Geoffrey Ingram Taylor, and remained there for the rest of his career. He founded the Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics (DAMTP) at Cambridge in 1959 and headed it for decades.

Work

His book The Theory of Homogeneous Turbulence (1953) systematised the statistical theory of turbulent flows. His textbook An Introduction to Fluid Dynamics (1967) is renowned for its physical insight and clarity and remains widely used. Later research addressed low-Reynolds-number flows, microhydrodynamics and the behaviour of suspensions, including the Batchelor scale in scalar turbulence mixing.

Journal of Fluid Mechanics

In 1956 Batchelor founded the Journal of Fluid Mechanics and edited it for over forty years, shaping it into the leading journal of the field. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1957.
