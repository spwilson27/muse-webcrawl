Forth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Forth is a procedural, stack-oriented programming language and interactive environment designed by Charles H. "Chuck" Moore and first released publicly in the early 1970s. Known for its extreme simplicity and extensibility, Forth uses reverse Polish notation, in which operands are pushed onto a data stack and operations ("words") manipulate the stack.

Design

A Forth system consists of a small kernel and a dictionary of words defined in terms of simpler words. There is no fixed syntax or reserved keyword set; programmers build application-specific vocabularies. Forth code is typically compiled incrementally into threaded code, combining interactive development speed with execution efficiency.

Uses

Forth was initially developed for controlling radio telescopes at observatories and became popular in astronomy, embedded systems, and control applications. It powered systems ranging from the Atari portfolio of early computer games by the development house that became known for its use, to NASA spacecraft instruments, and it survives today in boot firmware (Open Firmware), embedded controllers, and implementations such as Gforth.
