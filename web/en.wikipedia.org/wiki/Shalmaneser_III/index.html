Shalmaneser III (Akkadian: Šulmānu-ašarēdu, "the god Shulmanu is pre-eminent") was king of the Neo-Assyrian Empire from 859 BC to 824 BC. The son and successor of Ashurnasirpal II, he is best known for his long series of military campaigns recorded on monuments such as the Black Obelisk, the Kurkh Monoliths and the gates of Balawat. His reign saw Assyria expand westward to the Mediterranean and come into repeated conflict with the kingdoms of Syria and Israel.

Reign

Shalmaneser ascended the throne in 859 BC and campaigned almost annually. His annals, organised by regnal year, describe expeditions against Bit-Adini, Carchemish and the states of the Syrian coast, from which he extracted heavy tribute in gold, silver, tin, bronze and exotic goods including monkeys and elephants.

Battle of Qarqar

In 853 BC Shalmaneser faced a coalition of twelve western kings at the Battle of Qarqar on the Orontes River. The Kurkh Monolith records the alliance, which included Hadadezer of Damascus, Irhuleni of Hamath and Ahab of Israel, the latter contributing, according to the Assyrian account, 2,000 chariots and 10,000 soldiers. Shalmaneser claimed victory, though the coalition continued to resist him for several more years; the battle is notable as the first certain mention of an Israelite king in an extra-biblical source.

Later campaigns

In 841 BC Shalmaneser received the tribute of Jehu of Israel, an event depicted on the Black Obelisk, where Jehu (or his envoy) is shown prostrating before the Assyrian king — the only known contemporary depiction of an Israelite or Judean monarch. He also campaigned against Urartu, Que (Cilicia), and into the Zagros mountains against the Medes and Persians, whose names appear for the first time in his records.

Final years and succession

The last years of the reign were troubled by a major revolt led by Shalmaneser's eldest son Ashur-danin-pal, which began around 827 BC and drew in many of Assyria's great cities. Shalmaneser died in 824 BC and was succeeded by his younger son Shamshi-Adad V, who eventually crushed the rebellion.

Monuments

Shalmaneser's reign is unusually well documented. The Black Obelisk, discovered at Nimrud (ancient Kalhu), depicts tribute scenes from five foreign rulers. The Kurkh Monoliths record his early campaigns including Qarqar, while the bronze bands of the Balawat gates illustrate his victories in relief. A large statue of the king was found at Nimrud, and his annals were inscribed on the throne base and other monuments at Kalhu, his capital.
