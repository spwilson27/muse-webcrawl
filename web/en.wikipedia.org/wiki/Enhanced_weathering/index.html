Enhanced weathering

Enhanced weathering, or enhanced rock weathering (ERW), is a proposed carbon dioxide removal technique that accelerates natural silicate weathering. Finely ground silicate rock—commonly basalt—is spread on land (especially cropland) or in the oceans; reactions with CO2 and water convert carbon into dissolved bicarbonate that eventually reaches the ocean, storing it for long timescales.

Process

Weathering of silicate minerals naturally consumes atmospheric CO2. Crushing rock increases reactive surface area. On farmland, ERW may also raise soil pH, supply nutrients such as potassium and silicon, and improve yields, offsetting costs.

Potential and costs

Modelling studies estimate removal potential of roughly 0.5–4 gigatonnes of CO2 per year globally by mid-century, depending on rock supply, grinding energy and deployment scale. Mining, grinding and transport emissions must be subtracted, and verification of actual carbon uptake in field soils remains challenging.

Criticisms and research

Issues include ecosystem effects of trace metals (e.g. nickel and chromium in some rocks), land and energy demands, and measurement-reporting-verification. Field trials in the US Corn Belt, UK, and elsewhere, and companies applying carbon-credit financing, expanded markedly in the early 2020s.
