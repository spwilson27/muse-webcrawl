Effort justification

Effort justification is an idea in social psychology, rooted in cognitive dissonance theory, that people value outcomes more highly when they have put significant effort into achieving them, even if the outcome is objectively disappointing.

Classic study

Aronson and Mills (1959) found women who underwent a severe (embarrassing) initiation to join a discussion group rated the dull group more favourably than those with mild or no initiation — they justified the effort by inflating the reward's value.

Applications

The effect helps explain hazing, costly rituals, sunk-cost perseverance, and why hard-won memberships feel more valuable. It relates to the IKEA effect, where people value self-assembled products more.
