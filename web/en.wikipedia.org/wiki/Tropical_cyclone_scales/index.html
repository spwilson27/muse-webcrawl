Tropical cyclone scales
Main scalesSaffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale (Atlantic/NE Pacific); Australian tropical cyclone intensity scale; Japan Meteorological Agency typhoon scale; IMD cyclonic storm scale
Measured byMaximum sustained wind speed (1-minute or 10-minute averages, varying by agency)
CategoriesTropical depression, tropical storm, then numbered categories of hurricane/typhoon/cyclone strength

Tropical cyclone scales are the classification systems used by meteorological agencies to rate the intensity of tropical cyclones, principally by their maximum sustained winds. Different ocean basins use different scales, and the same storm may receive different ratings from different agencies because averaging periods (one minute in the United States, ten minutes in most other basins) and thresholds differ.

Saffir–Simpson scale

The Saffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale, used in the Atlantic and eastern/central North Pacific, classifies hurricanes into Categories 1–5, from winds of 119 km/h (74 mph) to 252 km/h (157 mph) and above for Category 5. Storms below hurricane strength are termed tropical storms or tropical depressions.

Other basins

Australia uses a five-category scale of tropical lows and tropical cyclones. The Japan Meteorological Agency classifies typhoons by 10-minute winds into typhoon, very strong typhoon and violent typhoon classes above the basic threshold. The India Meteorological Department grades North Indian Ocean systems from depression up to super cyclonic storm. The South-West Indian Ocean scale used by Météo-France runs from tropical disturbance to intense tropical cyclone.
