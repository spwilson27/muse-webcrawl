Duke of Leuchtenberg

Duke of Leuchtenberg was a title created by Maximilian I Joseph of Bavaria on 14 November 1817 for his son-in-law Eugène de Beauharnais, Napoleon's stepson and former Viceroy of Italy, together with the principality of Eichstätt and the landgraviate of Leuchtenberg in Bavaria, as compensation for the loss of his Italian possessions after the fall of Napoleon.

Holders

Eugène's son Auguste de Beauharnais, 2nd Duke of Leuchtenberg (1810–1835), married Queen Maria II of Portugal and became her prince consort, but died only months after the marriage. His younger brother Maximilian de Beauharnais, 3rd Duke (1817–1852), married Grand Duchess Maria Nikolaevna, eldest daughter of Tsar Nicholas I of Russia; their descendants, the Dukes of Leuchtenberg of the Russian line, were members of the Russian imperial family with the style of Serene Highness and the surname Romanowsky. The family held the Leuchtenberg Palace in St Petersburg and later lived in Bavaria and France. The senior male line continued through Nicholas Maximilianovich, 4th Duke, a noted mineralogist, and his successors into the twentieth century.

Legacy

The ducal family's art collection, assembled by Eugène and housed in the Palais Leuchtenberg in Munich — built for him by Leo von Klenze and one of the grandest private palaces of its era — was partly dispersed after the First World War. The title is also borne in historical literature as "Duke of Leuchtenberg, Prince of Eichstätt".
