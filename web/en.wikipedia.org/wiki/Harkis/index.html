Harkis

Harkis
Context: Algerian War (1954–1962)
Role: Algerian Muslim auxiliaries of the French Army
Aftermath: Massacres in Algeria; camps and marginalisation in France

Harkis were Algerian Muslims who served as auxiliaries (harkas) with the French Army during the Algerian War. Tens of thousands served, from motives including pay, protection and local loyalties.

After independence

After the Évian Accords and Algerian independence in 1962, France largely refused to evacuate the harkis despite warnings. Many thousands were killed in reprisals in Algeria. Those who reached France were often housed for years in remote camps such as Rivesaltes and Saint-Maurice-l'Ardoise, and faced discrimination.

Recognition

Descendants campaigned for recognition. French presidents gradually acknowledged France's abandonment; a 2021 law asked forgiveness and provided reparations measures, following President Macron's apology.
