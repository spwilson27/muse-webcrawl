Jotunheimen


Jotunheimen
Location: Innlandet and Vestland, Norway
Highest point: Galdhøpiggen (2,469 m)
Area: approx. 3,500 km²
Designation: Jotunheimen National Park (est. 1980)

Jotunheimen (Norwegian for "the home of the Jotnar", the giants of Norse mythology) is a mountainous area of roughly 3,500 km² in southern Norway and the country's most important range, containing the 29 highest mountains in Norway, including Galdhøpiggen (2,469 m) and Glittertind.

Geography

The range lies between the Sognefjord to the west and the Gudbrandsdalen valley to the east, forming part of the Scandinavian Mountains. Much of the area is protected as Jotunheimen National Park, established in 1980, alongside the neighbouring Utladalen landscape protection area.

Tourism and culture

Jotunheimen is Norway's most popular area for hiking and mountaineering, with a network of lodges run by the Norwegian Trekking Association (DNT); the Besseggen ridge, immortalised in Henrik Ibsen's Peer Gynt, is among the country's most walked trails. The poet Aasmund Olavsson Vinje gave the area its name in the 19th century.
