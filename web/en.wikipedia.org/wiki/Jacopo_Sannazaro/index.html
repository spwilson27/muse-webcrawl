Jacopo Sannazaro (28 July 1458 – 27 April 1530) was an Italian poet and humanist of the Renaissance, attached to the court of Naples. His pastoral prose romance Arcadia established a literary fashion that spread across Europe and influenced writers from Sir Philip Sidney to the pastoral dramatists of the following century.

Life

Born in Naples into a noble family, Sannazaro studied under the humanists of the Neapolitan academy and took the academic name Actius Syncerus. He served the Aragonese kings of Naples loyally, following Frederick of Naples into exile in France (1501–1504) after the kingdom's collapse. Returning to Naples, he spent his last years at his villa at Mergellina, where he built the church of Santa Maria del Parto.

Arcadia

The Arcadia, published in complete form in 1504 after circulating in manuscript, alternates prose narrative with verse eclogues. Set in an idealized Greek countryside of shepherds and nymphs, it tells of the lovesick Sincero's journey and his eventual return to Naples. Its mixture of prose and verse — modeled partly on Boccaccio — and its lush description of an idealized landscape made it one of the most imitated books of the sixteenth century.

Other works

Sannazaro also wrote Latin works prized by contemporaries, including the sacred poem De partu Virginis (1526) on the birth of Christ, the Piscatory Eclogues set among Neapolitan fishermen, and elegies and epigrams. His Italian lyric poems and the unfinished chivalric romance Gliuommene illustri also circulated widely.
