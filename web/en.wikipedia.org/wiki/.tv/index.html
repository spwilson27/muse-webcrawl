.tv is the Internet country code top-level domain (ccTLD) for Tuvalu, a Polynesian island nation in the Pacific Ocean. Because "TV" is also the abbreviation of television, the domain has been marketed worldwide for use by media, video and live-streaming websites, and the licensing of the domain has become a significant source of revenue for Tuvalu's government.

History

The domain was delegated in 1996. In the late 1990s, the Tuvaluan government, recognising the commercial value of the .tv suffix, entered into licensing agreements with private companies to operate the registry. A deal with the Canadian company .tv Corporation — later acquired by Verisign — provided Tuvalu with millions of dollars in royalties, which funded infrastructure, scholarships and the country's membership of the United Nations, which it joined in 2000 partly using .tv income. At times, domain royalties have accounted for a substantial share of Tuvalu's national revenue, a rare case of a small state profiting significantly from its assigned country code.

Use

Registrations are open to anyone, with no requirement of a connection to Tuvalu. The suffix is popular with broadcasters, video platforms and esports and streaming services; the live-streaming platform Twitch operates at twitch.tv. Premium one- and two-character names have been sold at high prices. The agreement to operate the registry was retendered in the 2020s, with GoDaddy Registry taking over operation of the domain.

Economic significance

For Tuvalu — one of the world's smallest and most remote economies, and one threatened by rising sea levels — .tv revenue is a vital income stream. The government has explored how digital assets, including its domain, might help preserve Tuvaluan statehood and fund adaptation, including plans to create a digital copy of the nation.
