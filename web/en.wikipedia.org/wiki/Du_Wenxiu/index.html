Du Wenxiu

Du Wenxiu (1823–1872), leader of the Panthay Rebellion

Du Wenxiu (Chinese: 杜文秀; pinyin: Dù Wénxiù; 1823 – 27 December 1872) was a Chinese Muslim leader of Hui ethnicity who led the Panthay Rebellion, a prolonged Muslim uprising against the Qing dynasty in the province of Yunnan. At its height, his regime — the Pingnan Guo, often called the Dali Sultanate in Western accounts — controlled much of western Yunnan.

Early life

Du was born in Yongchang (present-day Baoshan) in western Yunnan to a Hui (Chinese Muslim) family. He received an education in both Chinese classics and Islamic learning, and as a young man he became a vocal critic of the corruption and anti-Muslim discrimination of local Qing officials. Tensions between Hui and Han communities in Yunnan had been growing for decades, aggravated by competition between mining communities and by official partiality toward Han plaintiffs in legal disputes.

The rebellion

In 1856, after a massacre of Muslims at Kunming sanctioned by provincial authorities, Hui communities across Yunnan rose in revolt. Du Wenxiu emerged as the rebellion's most capable leader and established his capital at Dali. He adopted the title of Sultan (rendered in English accounts as "Sultan Suleiman") and proclaimed the Pingnan Guo ("Pacified Southern Kingdom"), which at its greatest extent governed roughly half of Yunnan and maintained relations with the British in Burma.

Du's administration was notable for its attempts at intercommunal governance: his government included Hui, Han and indigenous (Yi and Bai) officials, and he issued edicts prohibiting the slaughter of oxen out of respect for Hindu-influenced customs among some of his subjects, while also relaxing certain Islamic prohibitions to accommodate his non-Muslim population. Rebel forces besieged Kunming several times but never took it permanently.

Defeat and death

After the Qing suppressed the Taiping Rebellion and other uprisings, imperial forces under commanders such as Cen Yuying concentrated on Yunnan. The rebellion was gradually worn down; Dali fell in late 1872. Du Wenxiu is said to have taken poison before surrendering himself to the Qing camp in an attempt to spare the city's population, and he was executed on 27 December 1872. Large-scale massacres of Muslims followed the suppression of the rebellion, and many Hui fled into Burma and Siam, forming the communities known in Burma as the Panthays.

Legacy

Du Wenxiu is remembered by the Hui people as a hero of resistance, while official Qing historiography treated him as a rebel. Modern scholarship generally regards his regime as one of the more organised and pragmatic of the great mid-nineteenth-century Chinese rebellions. Descendants of his followers survive among the Chinese Muslim (Panthay) communities of Myanmar.
