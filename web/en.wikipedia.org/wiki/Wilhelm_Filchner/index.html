Wilhelm Filchner

Wilhelm Filchner (13 September 1877 – 7 May 1957) was a German explorer, geographer and travel writer, known for expeditions to Central Asia and for leading the Second German Antarctic Expedition (1911–12).

Antarctic expedition

Filchner's ship Deutschland penetrated the Weddell Sea, discovered the Filchner Ice Shelf (named for him, the Filchner–Ronne Ice Shelf in part) and Luitpold Coast, and landed at Vahsel Bay. The ship was beset and drifted in the ice for nine months in 1912 before escaping — an ordeal that preceded Shackleton's similar Endurance drift. A mutinous atmosphere and later disputes marred accounts of the expedition.

Central Asia and later work

He travelled in the Pamirs and led geomagnetic survey expeditions in Tibet and Nepal in the 1920s–30s, producing maps of regions little known in Europe. He died in Zürich in 1957.
