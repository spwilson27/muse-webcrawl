Tomara dynasty

The Tomara dynasty was a Rajput dynasty that ruled parts of present-day Delhi and Haryana in northern India during the early medieval period, principally from the eighth to the twelfth centuries CE.

History

The Tomaras initially appear as feudatories of the Gurjara-Pratihara empire. Tradition credits Anangpal Tomar with founding the settlement of Dhillika—modern Delhi—around the eighth century, and with building the Lal Kot fortifications in the eleventh century, remnants of which survive in the Mehrauli area near the Qutub Minar complex. The Surajkund reservoir near Faridabad is also attributed to the Tomara king Surajpal.

Decline

In the mid-twelfth century the Tomaras were displaced by the Chahamanas (Chauhans) of Shakambhari under Vigraharaja IV, who annexed Delhi; the Tomaras thereafter continued as subordinate rulers before fading from the record. Their period is significant as the formative era of Delhi's rise as a political centre, immediately preceding its establishment as capital of the Delhi Sultanate.
