Georges Picquart

Marie Georges Picquart (6 September 1854 – 19 January 1914) was a French army officer and Minister of War. He is best known for his central role in the Dreyfus affair: as head of the army's intelligence service, he discovered the evidence proving that Alfred Dreyfus was innocent and that the real spy was Ferdinand Walsin Esterhazy — and was persecuted by his own superiors for insisting on the truth.

Early career

Born in Strasbourg, Picquart entered the École spéciale militaire de Saint-Cyr and graduated near the top of his class. He served in French Indochina and North Africa and taught at the École supérieure de guerre, where Alfred Dreyfus was among his students. In 1895, aged 40, he was promoted to lieutenant-colonel and appointed head of the Statistical Section — the French army's counter-intelligence service — the youngest officer of his rank in the army.

Discovery of the truth

In March 1896, Picquart's agents recovered a torn-up pneumatic telegram, the petit bleu, addressed by a German military attaché to Major Esterhazy. Investigating further, Picquart obtained a sample of Esterhazy's handwriting and recognized it as identical to that of the bordereau, the document for which Dreyfus had been convicted and sent to Devil's Island. He reported his findings to his superiors, expecting the error to be corrected.

Persecution

Instead, the General Staff resolved to protect the original verdict. Picquart was transferred to a posting in Tunisia, while his deputy, Major Hubert-Joseph Henry, forged documents to shore up the case against Dreyfus. Picquart confided the truth to his friend, the lawyer Louis Leblois, through whom it reached Senator Auguste Scheurer-Kestner, vice-president of the Senate, who took up Dreyfus's cause. Removed from the army and arrested on trumped-up charges, Picquart spent nearly a year in prison. When Henry's forgeries were exposed in 1898 and Henry killed himself, the revision of the case became unstoppable.

Rehabilitation and later career

After Dreyfus's full exoneration in 1906, Picquart was reinstated and promoted to brigadier general. In October 1906 he became Minister of War in the government of Georges Clemenceau — entering the ministry, as Clemenceau observed, through the same door by which Dreyfus's persecutors had left. He served until 1909 and later commanded an army corps at Amiens, where he died in 1914 after a fall from a horse.

In popular culture

Picquart is the protagonist of Robert Harris's 2013 novel An Officer and a Spy and of Roman Polanski's 2019 film adaptation, J'accuse, in which he is played by Jean Dujardin.
