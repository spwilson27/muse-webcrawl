Elizabeth (also spelled Elisabeth; Greek: Ἐλισάβετ, from Hebrew Elisheva, "my God is an oath") is a figure in the New Testament, described in the Gospel of Luke as the wife of the priest Zechariah and the mother of John the Baptist. She was a relative — traditionally described as a cousin — of Mary, the mother of Jesus, and is venerated as a saint in several Christian traditions.

Biblical account

According to Luke 1, Elizabeth was a descendant of Aaron and, like her husband, was "righteous in the sight of God." The couple were childless and advanced in years when the angel Gabriel appeared to Zechariah in the Temple to announce that Elizabeth would bear a son, to be named John. In the sixth month of Elizabeth's pregnancy, Mary visited her — the episode known as the Visitation — whereupon the child in Elizabeth's womb leapt, and Elizabeth, "filled with the Holy Spirit," greeted Mary with the words "Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb," which form part of the Hail Mary prayer. Elizabeth's own song of thanksgiving appears in some manuscripts, though it is usually attributed to Mary as the Magnificat. When her son was born, Elizabeth insisted on the name John, against the expectation that he would be named after his father.

Veneration

Elizabeth is commemorated as a saint in the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and Anglican traditions. Her feast day is 5 November in the Roman calendar and 5 September in the Orthodox calendar; she is also remembered with Zechariah. Tradition places her home at Ein Karem near Jerusalem, where the Church of the Visitation commemorates Mary's visit.

In Islam

Elizabeth is not named in the Qur'an, but Islamic tradition honours her as the wife of Zechariah (Zakariya) and mother of John (Yahya), and commentators discuss her in connection with the family of Imran mentioned in Surah Al Imran.
