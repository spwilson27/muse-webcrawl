Launch vehicle

A heavy-lift launch vehicle at liftoff.
TypeRocket-powered space vehicle
UsesCarrying payloads to orbit or beyond

A launch vehicle is a rocket-powered vehicle used to carry a payload — a satellite, spacecraft or space station module — from Earth's surface to outer space, usually to Earth orbit or beyond. Launch vehicles are classified by the mass they can deliver to low Earth orbit (LEO): small-lift, medium-lift, heavy-lift and super heavy-lift.

Design

Most launch vehicles use multiple stages that are discarded as their propellant is exhausted, overcoming the limits of the rocket equation. Historically most were expendable launch systems, flown only once; reusable systems, pioneered operationally by the Space Shuttle and later SpaceX's Falcon family, recover first stages or boosters for refurbishment and reflight, aiming to reduce cost.

Notable vehicles

Famous launch vehicles include Saturn V, which carried Apollo astronauts to the Moon and remains among the most powerful ever flown; the Soviet/Russian Soyuz, the most-flown family in history; Europe's Ariane series; and modern vehicles such as Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy, China's Long March family, and NASA's Space Launch System.
