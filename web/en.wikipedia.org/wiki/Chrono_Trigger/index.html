Chrono Trigger is a 1995 role-playing video game developed and published by Square for the Super Nintendo Entertainment System. It was created by a team dubbed the "Dream Team" — Final Fantasy creator Hironobu Sakaguchi, Dragon Quest creator Yuji Horii, and Dragon Ball artist Akira Toriyama — with music primarily by Yasunori Mitsuda, supplemented by Nobuo Uematsu.

Gameplay and story

The game follows Crono and a party of adventurers who travel through time — from prehistory to a post-apocalyptic future — to prevent a global catastrophe caused by the parasitic entity Lavos, which emerges in 1999 AD. Battles occur seamlessly on the exploration map using an evolution of the Active Time Battle system, with characters able to combine techniques in double and triple "Techs." The game was pioneering in offering multiple endings (over a dozen variants) and a New Game+ mode, along with side quests resolvable in different eras whose consequences ripple through time.

Development and release

Development began in 1992; the large team of over 50 staff produced a 32-megabit cartridge, among the largest and most expensive of its era. Chrono Trigger was a commercial success, selling more than two million copies in its first two months in Japan. It has been re-released on the PlayStation (1999, with anime cutscenes), Nintendo DS (2008, with extra dungeons and a new ending linking to its sequel Chrono Cross), and PC and mobile platforms (2018 "Ultimate Edition" updates).

Reception and legacy

The game is routinely ranked among the greatest video games of all time, praised for its story, pacing, character design, music and technical ambition. It influenced later time-travel narratives in games and popularised New Game+ as a standard feature of the genre.
