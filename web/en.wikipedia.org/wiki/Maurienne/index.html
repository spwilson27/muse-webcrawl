Maurienne

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The Maurienne is the valley of the river Arc in Savoie, France.

The Maurienne (French pronunciation: [mɔʁjɛn]) is a valley of the river Arc in the French Alps, in the department of Savoie in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region. It is one of the two principal valleys of the former province of Savoy, the other being the Tarentaise, and forms the French side of the historic route to Italy over the Mont Cenis pass. The valley is some 120 kilometres long, running from the Col de l'Iseran to the confluence of the Arc with the Isère at Aiton.

History

The valley was settled in antiquity by the Celtic Medulli. In the Middle Ages it was the heartland of the County of Savoy, whose counts bore the title Count of Maurienne. The valley's strategic importance grew with the road over the Mont Cenis pass, a major medieval crossing of the Alps between France and Italy. The Maurienne was annexed by France with the rest of Savoy in 1860.

Economy and tourism

Historically industrialised around aluminium smelting at Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne, the valley's economy today is dominated by tourism. The Maurienne hosts some of the world's largest ski resorts, including Val Thorens, Les Menuires and Val Cenis, as well as the high mountain passes of the Col du Galibier, the Col de la Croix de Fer and the Col de l'Iseran, famous from the Tour de France. The valley is also served by the Fréjus rail tunnel and the Mont Cenis railway line.
