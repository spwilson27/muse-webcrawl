End credits

End credits, also known as closing credits, are a list of the cast and crew of a particular motion picture, television programme, or video game. While opening credits appear at the beginning of a work, end credits appear close to, or at the very end of a work. A full set of credits can include the cast and crew, but also production sponsors, distribution companies, works of music licensed or written for the work, various legal disclaimers, and other information. Usually, the end credits are shown over a blank background, or superimposed over the final scene.

History

In early Hollywood, films carried only brief credits at the beginning, and most crew went uncredited. From the 1970s onward — a shift often associated with blockbusters such as Star Wars (1977), which placed essentially all credits at the end — end credits grew progressively longer as union rules and contractual obligations expanded the list of credited personnel. Modern major-studio films may have end credits running ten minutes or more, listing thousands of names, including digital effects artists around the world.

Post-credits scenes and conventions

Since the late 20th century, filmmakers have frequently added post-credits scenes — short extra sequences, teasers, or jokes placed during or after the credits — a practice popularised by comedies and expanded by franchise films such as the Marvel Cinematic Universe, encouraging audiences to remain seated. Credits conventions are governed in part by guild rules, which regulate the size, order, and duration of certain credits, especially for writers and directors.
