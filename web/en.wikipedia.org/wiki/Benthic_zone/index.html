Benthic zone

TypeAquatic ecological region
LocationLowest level of a body of water
IncludesSeabed, lakebed, sediment surface and sub-surface layers
OrganismsBenthos (benthic organisms)

The benthic zone is the ecological region at the lowest level of a body of water such as an ocean, lake, or stream, including the sediment surface and some sub-surface layers. Organisms living in this zone are called benthos and include microorganisms (such as bacteria and fungi) as well as larger invertebrates, such as crustaceans and polychaetes. Organisms here generally live in close relationship with the substrate, and many are permanently attached to the bottom. The benthic boundary layer, which includes the bottom layer of water and the uppermost layer of sediment directly influenced by the overlying water, is an integral part of the benthic zone, as it greatly influences the biological activity that takes place there.

In contrast, the pelagic zone is the water column above the benthic zone. The region of the seafloor that lies on continental shelves is sometimes distinguished as the littoral or sublittoral benthic environment, while the deep-ocean floor forms the abyssal benthic zone.

Characteristics

The benthic region of the ocean begins at the shore line (the intertidal or littoral zone) and extends downward along the surface of the continental shelf out to sea. It thus starts at the zone of shallow water, near the shore, and extends down into the deep water of the abyssal plains. Continental shelves have benthic zones extending from the shore to depths of roughly 200 metres, below which the continental slope descends toward the deep sea.

Light does not penetrate very deep ocean water, so the energy source for most deep benthic ecosystems is organic matter from higher up in the water column that drifts down to the depths. This dead and decaying matter sustains the benthic food chain; most organisms in the benthic zone are scavengers or detritivores. In shallow waters, by contrast, sunlight can reach the bottom, allowing photosynthesizing organisms such as seagrasses and benthic algae to grow.

Organisms

Benthos have many important roles in aquatic ecosystems. Examples of benthic organisms include bivalves such as clams, mussels, and oysters; sea stars, brittle stars, and sea cucumbers; crabs and other crustaceans; polychaete worms; sponges; corals; and bottom-dwelling fish such as flounder, sole, and many species of shark and ray. Microbial communities in the sediment carry out much of the decomposition and nutrient cycling of the ecosystem, including processes such as nitrification and denitrification.

Benthic habitats

Benthic habitats range from rocky reefs and coral reefs to soft mud and sand flats, seagrass meadows, mangrove sediments, kelp forests, and the sediments of the abyssal plains. The character of the substrate — rock, gravel, sand, or mud — is a major determinant of which organisms can live in a given benthic habitat.
