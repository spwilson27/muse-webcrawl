John, King of England

John (24 December 1166 – 19 October 1216) was King of England from 1199 until his death. The youngest son of Henry II and Eleanor of Aquitaine, he was nicknamed “Lackland” because he initially inherited no territory. His reign saw the loss of most English lands in France and the sealing of Magna Carta.

Reign

John succeeded his brother Richard I, defeating his nephew Arthur's claim — Arthur later disappeared in John's custody. War with Philip II of France cost Normandy by 1204. Heavy taxation, a dispute with Pope Innocent III (leading to interdict and John's submission, making England a papal fief), and failed campaigns angered the barons.

Magna Carta and civil war

In June 1215 rebellious barons forced John to seal Magna Carta at Runnymede, limiting royal power. John repudiated it with papal support, sparking the First Barons' War; French Prince Louis invaded. John died of dysentery at Newark in October 1216 and was succeeded by his nine-year-old son Henry III.

Legacy

Traditionally portrayed as a villain — notably in Robin Hood legends — modern historians note his administrative ability but judge his personality and failures harshly.
