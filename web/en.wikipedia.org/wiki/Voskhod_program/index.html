Voskhod programme

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Voskhod programme
CountrySoviet Union
Flights1964–1965 (2 crewed)
PurposeMulti-crew firsts; first spacewalk
SuccessorSoyuz
Voskhod with its airlock.

The Voskhod programme was the Soviet Union's second crewed spaceflight programme, an interim effort that squeezed spectacular firsts out of a modified Vostok capsule to stay ahead of America's Project Gemini. Its two crewed flights achieved the first multi-person crew (Voskhod 1, 1964) and the first spacewalk (Voskhod 2, 1965), after which the programme was cancelled in favour of Soyuz.

To fit three men into a capsule designed for one, Voskhod 1's crew flew without spacesuits, ejection seats or any escape system — a gamble that appalled engineers and worked. Voskhod 2 added an inflatable airlock for Alexei Leonov's walk, at the cost of similar risk.

Assessment

Historians generally judge Voskhod a propaganda-driven improvisation: Korolev pushed it to harvest firsts while the real next-generation ship, Soyuz, matured. Its cancellation after Korolev's death — and the fact its crews survived two very dangerous missions — closed the USSR's early lead as Gemini demonstrated rendezvous, docking and endurance methodically.
