John Harvey Kellogg

John Harvey Kellogg (26 February 1852 – 14 December 1943) was an American businessman, inventor, physician, and advocate of progressive medicine and healthy living. He was the director of the Battle Creek Sanitarium in Michigan, and is best known for inventing the breakfast cereal known as corn flakes.

Battle Creek Sanitarium

A Seventh-day Adventist, Kellogg took charge of the church's Western Health Reform Institute in Battle Creek in 1876 and built it into the world-famous Battle Creek Sanitarium, which treated thousands of patients, including celebrities and industrialists, with a regimen of vegetarian diet, hydrotherapy, exercise, and fresh air.

Corn flakes

In 1894, Kellogg and his brother Will Keith Kellogg developed flaked wheat and then corn flakes as a wholesome, ready-to-eat breakfast food for sanitarium patients. Their partnership fractured, and W. K. Kellogg founded the Kellogg Company in 1906, leading to decades of legal battles between the brothers over the Kellogg name.

Views and legacy

Kellogg wrote extensively on health, advocating vegetarianism, abstinence, and what he called "biologic living." He also promoted eugenics and racial segregation late in life, views that have been widely criticized. He died in Battle Creek in 1943.
