480i

Interlaced scanning: alternate fields carry the odd and even lines of each frame.

480i is the shorthand name for a standard-definition video mode with 480 horizontal lines of visible resolution displayed using interlaced scanning. The "480" denotes the vertical resolution and the "i" denotes interlacing, in which each frame is drawn in two passes, or fields, of alternating lines.

Technical details

The 480i format is derived from the analog NTSC television system used in North America, Japan, and other regions: a full frame consists of 525 total scan lines, of which 480 (sometimes 486) carry picture information. Fields are transmitted at 59.94 per second in color NTSC practice (originally 60 in monochrome), giving 29.97 complete frames per second. Horizontal resolution depends on bandwidth and source, and the standard aspect ratio is 4:3, though anamorphic widescreen 16:9 variants were widely used on DVD.

Usage

480i was the standard format for broadcast television, VHS, LaserDisc, and DVD-Video in NTSC countries for decades, and it remains the native output of many retro video game consoles and legacy cameras. Its 50 Hz counterpart in PAL and SECAM regions is 576i, which offers more vertical lines at a lower field rate. In digital standards such as ATSC and DVB, 480i is retained as a standard-definition broadcast format, and it is carried over HDMI, component, S-Video, and composite connections.

Because the two fields of a frame are captured at slightly different instants, moving objects display comb-like artifacts when the signal is shown on progressive displays without deinterlacing.
