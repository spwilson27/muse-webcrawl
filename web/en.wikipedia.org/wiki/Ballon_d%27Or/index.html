The Ballon d'Or (French for "Golden Ball") is an annual football award presented by the French magazine France Football since 1956 to honour the player judged to have performed the best over the previous season (originally calendar year). It is widely regarded as the most prestigious individual award in football.

History

The award was created by sports writers Gabriel Hanot and Jacques Ferran. Initially restricted to European players at European clubs, eligibility was widened in 1995 to all players at European clubs and in 2007 to players worldwide. Between 2010 and 2015 the award merged with FIFA's World Player of the Year as the FIFA Ballon d'Or, before the partnership ended.

Winners

Lionel Messi has won the award a record eight times, followed by Cristiano Ronaldo with five. Johan Cruyff, Michel Platini and Marco van Basten each won three times; Platini, Cruyff and others also feature in the Ballon d'Or Dream Team announced in 2020. A women's award, the Ballon d'Or Féminin, has been presented since 2018.

Voting

The winner is chosen by a jury of football journalists, one from each of the top-ranked FIFA nations, who rank five players and award points on a 6-4-3-2-1 basis.
