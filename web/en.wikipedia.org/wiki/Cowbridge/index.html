Cowbridge


Cowbridge High Street

Cowbridge (Welsh: Y Bont-faen) is a market town in the Vale of Glamorgan, Wales, approximately 12 miles (19 km) west of Cardiff. Its name in Welsh means "the bridge of the cow field", and the English name is a translation of an earlier Welsh form. Cowbridge is twinned with Clisson in France.

History

The town has Roman origins: excavations have revealed a substantial Roman settlement, possibly the fort-site of Bovium, on the route west from Cardiff. Cowbridge was one of very few medieval walled towns in Wales, receiving charters in the 13th century, and parts of its town wall and the south gate survive. In the 18th century it was a prosperous market and social centre for the landed gentry of the Vale, with coaching inns on the main road to west Wales.

Today

Cowbridge is known for its independent shops, restaurants, and fashion boutiques, and is frequently described as one of the most affluent small towns in Wales. The town's independent school, Cowbridge Comprehensive, and the Physic Garden (restored in 2004) are local landmarks. The Cowbridge Food and Drink Festival is held annually.
