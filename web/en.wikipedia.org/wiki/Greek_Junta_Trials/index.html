Greek junta trials

[Image: Greek junta trials]

The Greek junta trials (Greek: Οι δίκες της Χούντας) were the trials of the leaders of the Greek military junta of 1967–1974, held after the fall of the regime during the Metapolitefsi. They are generally considered landmark proceedings in the restoration of democracy in Greece.

The trials

The principal trial of the junta's instigators began on 28 July 1975 at Korydallos Prison. Georgios Papadopoulos, Stylianos Pattakos, and Nikolaos Makarezos were sentenced to death for high treason and insurrection; the sentences were commuted to life imprisonment by the government of Konstantinos Karamanlis. Separate trials addressed the Athens Polytechnic uprising of November 1973 and the systematic torture practised by the military police (the ESA trials).

Legacy

The trials purged the armed forces and security services of junta collaborators and are regarded as a model of transitional justice conducted through ordinary courts.
