Peter Diamond

Born: Peter Arthur Diamond, 29 April 1940, New York City, U.S.

Field: Labour economics, public economics

Institution: MIT

Award: Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences (2010)

Peter Arthur Diamond (born 29 April 1940) is an American economist known for his work on labour market search and matching, social security and optimal taxation. He is an Institute Professor Emeritus at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. In 2010 he shared the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Dale T. Mortensen and Christopher A. Pissarides "for their analysis of markets with search frictions".

Work

Diamond's 1965 overlapping-generations model of national debt extended Samuelson's framework and remains a staple of macroeconomics. With James Mirrlees he developed the Diamond–Mirrlees production efficiency theorem in optimal tax theory. His models of job search — including the "coconut model" of an island economy — helped found the Diamond–Mortensen–Pissarides (DMP) framework used to analyse unemployment, vacancies and wage bargaining.

A leading authority on Social Security, Diamond co-authored Saving Social Security (2004) with Peter Orszag, proposing reforms to the U.S. pension system.

Federal Reserve nomination

In 2010 President Barack Obama nominated Diamond to the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve. After the nomination was blocked repeatedly in the Senate, Diamond withdrew in June 2011, writing a widely discussed opinion piece criticising the confirmation process.
