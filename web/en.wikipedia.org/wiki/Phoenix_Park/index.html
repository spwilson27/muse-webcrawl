The Phoenix Park (Irish: Páirc an Fhionnuisce) is an urban park in Dublin, Ireland, lying 2–4 kilometres (1.2–2.5 mi) west of the city centre, north of the River Liffey. Its 11 kilometres (6.8 mi) perimeter wall encloses 707 hectares (1,750 acres); it is one of the largest enclosed recreational spaces within any European capital city. It includes large areas of grassland and tree-lined avenues, and since the 17th century has been home to a herd of wild fallow deer.

The park is home to Áras an Uachtaráin, the official residence of the President of Ireland, as well as Dublin Zoo, the official residence of the United States Ambassador to Ireland, and numerous other historic buildings and monuments.

History

The park was originally formed as a royal deer park in the 1660s under James Butler, 1st Duke of Ormond, Lord Lieutenant of Ireland. Its name derives not from the mythical phoenix but from a corruption of the Irish fionn uisce, meaning "clear water", referring to a spring near the site. In 1745 the park was opened to the public by Philip Stanhope, 4th Earl of Chesterfield.

Prominent features include the Wellington Monument, a 62-metre (203 ft) obelisk completed in 1861 to commemorate the victories of Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington, who was born in Dublin; the Papal Cross, erected for the visit of Pope John Paul II in 1979, when over a million people attended an open-air Mass; and the Phoenix Column, topped by a gilded phoenix.

Phoenix Park murders

On 6 May 1882, the Chief Secretary for Ireland, Lord Frederick Cavendish, and the Permanent Under-Secretary, Thomas Henry Burke, were stabbed to death in the park by members of the Irish National Invincibles, in an event known as the Phoenix Park murders — one of the most notorious political assassinations of the Victorian era in Ireland.

Flora and fauna

About 30% of the park is covered by trees, mainly broadleaf species such as oak, ash, lime, beech, sycamore, and horse chestnut. The herd of fallow deer, numbering around 400–600 animals, descends from the herd introduced in the 1660s. The park contains numerous sports grounds, including the oldest cricket ground in Ireland, and hosts concerts and the annual Great Ireland Run.
