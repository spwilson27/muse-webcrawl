The 1982 FIFA World Cup was the 12th edition of the FIFA World Cup, held in Spain from 13 June to 11 July 1982. The tournament was won by Italy, who defeated West Germany 3–1 in the final at the Santiago Bernabéu Stadium in Madrid. It was Italy's third World Cup title, the first since 1938.

Italy captain Dino Zoff, at 40 the oldest World Cup-winning captain, lifts the trophy in Madrid.

Tournament summary

The field was expanded from 16 to 24 teams for the first time. The format featured two group stages followed by semi-finals. The tournament is remembered for one of the greatest matches in World Cup history, Italy's 3–2 victory over a celebrated Brazil side in the second group stage in Barcelona, in which Paolo Rossi scored a hat-trick. Rossi, who had only recently returned from a two-year ban, won the Golden Boot with six goals and the Golden Ball as the tournament's best player.

Other notable episodes included West Germany's controversial 1–0 win over Austria in Gijón — the "Disgrace of Gijón" — after which FIFA changed the scheduling of final group matches; the semi-final between West Germany and France, won by the Germans on penalties after a 3–3 draw; and the violent foul by German goalkeeper Harald Schumacher on France's Patrick Battiston, which went unpunished.

Final

After a scoreless first half, Italy struck three times in the second half through Rossi, Marco Tardelli and Alessandro Altobelli. Paul Breitner scored a late consolation for West Germany. Tardelli's exuberant goal celebration became one of the tournament's enduring images. Italian coach Enzo Bearzot's side included 40-year-old goalkeeper and captain Dino Zoff, veteran defenders Gaetano Scirea and Claudio Gentile, and midfielder Giancarlo Antognoni.
