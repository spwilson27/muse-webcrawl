Pseudorandom number generator

Common algorithms: Mersenne Twister, LCG, PCG, xorshift
Cryptographic: CSPRNG (e.g. ChaCha-based)
Key property: Deterministic sequence from a seed

A pseudorandom number generator (PRNG), also known as a deterministic random bit generator, is an algorithm that produces a sequence of numbers whose properties approximate those of random numbers, even though the sequence is fully determined by an initial value called a seed. PRNGs are essential to simulations, games, randomized algorithms, and Monte Carlo methods.

Characteristics

A PRNG's quality is judged by its period (length before repetition), its statistical uniformity and independence, and its speed. Simple linear congruential generators have short periods and lattice structure; the Mersenne Twister, long the default in many languages, has a period of 219937−1. Modern generators such as PCG and xoshiro256** offer better statistics with smaller state.

Cryptographic security

General-purpose PRNGs are predictable once their state is observed, so security applications require cryptographically secure PRNGs (CSPRNGs), which mix in entropy from the operating system and are designed so that outputs reveal nothing about future or past values.
