Sight (device)

A sight is an aiming device used to align a weapon — typically a firearm, bow, or crossbow — or a surveying or optical instrument with its target. Sights range from simple beads and notches to sophisticated optical and electronic systems.

Types

  • Iron sights: metal front and rear elements (post, bead, notch, or aperture) aligned by eye; standard on most firearms.
  • Telescopic sights ("scopes"): magnifying optics with a reticle, used for precision shooting at longer ranges.
  • Reflex and red-dot sights: non-magnifying optics projecting an illuminated aiming point onto a lens, enabling fast target acquisition with both eyes open.
  • Holographic sights: use a laser-generated hologram of a reticle.
  • Laser sights: project a visible dot directly onto the target.

Principles

Sights must be "zeroed" — adjusted so that the line of sight intersects the projectile's trajectory at a chosen range. Adjustments for elevation and windage compensate for bullet drop and drift. Historical predecessors include the sights of artillery and the alidade used in surveying.
