El Centro is a city in and the county seat of Imperial County, California, United States, in the Imperial Valley near the Mexican border. It is the largest city in the valley and its commercial and administrative centre. El Centro lies below sea level and has a hot desert climate, with some of the highest summer temperatures in the United States.

El Centro is the economic centre of California's Imperial Valley.

History

The city was founded in 1906, when the developer W. F. Holt established it as a rail and agricultural centre for the newly irrigated Imperial Valley, and it was incorporated in 1908. Irrigation water from the Colorado River turned the surrounding desert into one of California's most productive farming regions, growing lettuce, carrots, melons, alfalfa and other crops, and agriculture remains the foundation of the local economy, together with cross-border trade and government services.

Earthquakes

The Imperial Valley is seismically active. El Centro was heavily damaged by the 1940 Imperial Valley earthquake (magnitude 6.9), whose strong-motion record became a landmark dataset in earthquake engineering, and again affected by the 1979 Imperial Valley earthquake.

Geography and climate

El Centro is located about 113 miles east of San Diego and 15 miles north of Mexicali, Mexico. Naval Air Facility El Centro, just northwest of the city, is the winter training home of the U.S. Navy's Blue Angels flight demonstration squadron.
