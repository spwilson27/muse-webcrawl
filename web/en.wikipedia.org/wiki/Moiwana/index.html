Moiwana

Moiwana is a Maroon village in eastern Suriname, in the Marowijne District near the border with French Guiana. It is home to members of the Ndyuka (Aukan) people, descendants of Africans who escaped enslavement in the colonial period and formed independent communities in the Surinamese interior.

The 1986 massacre

On 29 November 1986, during the Surinamese Interior War between the government of Desi Bouterse and the Jungle Commando of Ronnie Brunswijk, soldiers of the Surinamese National Army attacked Moiwana searching for Brunswijk. The soldiers killed at least 39 villagers — mostly women, children and elderly people — and burned the village to the ground. Survivors fled across the Marowijne River to French Guiana, where many lived in refugee camps for years.

Inter-American Court case

Suriname failed to investigate or prosecute those responsible. In 2005, the Inter-American Court of Human Rights decided the case of the Moiwana Community v. Suriname, finding the state responsible for violating the rights to life, humane treatment, property, freedom of movement and judicial protection. The Court ordered Suriname to investigate the massacre, recover the remains of the victims, pay reparations, and formally apologise to the community. A monument commemorating the massacre was unveiled at Moiwana in 2008, and the state issued an official apology, although a full criminal investigation remained outstanding for many years.
