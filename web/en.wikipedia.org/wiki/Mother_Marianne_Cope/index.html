Marianne Cope (January 23, 1838 – August 9, 1918), also known as Saint Marianne of Molokaʻi, was a German-born American religious sister of the Sisters of St. Francis of Syracuse. She spent 35 years caring for people with leprosy (Hansen's disease) in Hawaii, first in Honolulu and then in the isolated settlement of Kalaupapa on Molokai, where she worked alongside, and then succeeded, Saint Damien de Veuster.

Early life

Born Maria Anna Barbara Koob in Hesse, she emigrated with her family to Utica, New York, as a child. After working in a factory to support her family, she entered the Sisters of St. Francis in 1862, taking the name Marianne. She taught, then became a pioneering hospital administrator in Syracuse, helping to found two hospitals open to patients regardless of creed or ability to pay.

Work in Hawaii

In 1883 Mother Marianne led a group of sisters to Hawaii at the request of King Kalākaua to care for leprosy patients. She ran the receiving hospital in Honolulu and the Bishop Home for girls at Kalaupapa, improving sanitation, dignity and education for patients. Remarkably, none of her sisters contracted the disease.

Death and canonization

She died at Kalaupapa in 1918. Beatified in 2005, she was canonized by Pope Benedict XVI on October 21, 2012, becoming one of the few American saints.
