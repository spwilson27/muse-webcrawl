Freedom and Independence

Freedom and Independence (WiN) was a Polish underground organisation.

Freedom and Independence (Polish: Wolność i Niezawisłość, WiN) was a Polish underground anti-communist organisation founded on September 2, 1945, in Warsaw. It grew out of the structures of the disbanded Home Army (Armia Krajowa) and the Delegatura Sił Zbrojnych (Armed Forces Delegation).

Activities

WiN aimed to resist the Soviet-backed communist takeover of Poland through political action, intelligence gathering, propaganda, and — where its members judged necessary — armed struggle. It maintained contacts with the Polish government-in-exile in London and passed information to the West, including documentation of Soviet and Polish communist repression. Its successive commands were penetrated and arrested by the Ministry of Public Security (UB); the organisation's fourth and final headquarters was dismantled in 1947, though a deception operation run by the security services pretended WiN structures survived into the early 1950s to entrap supporters and channel Western aid.

Legacy

Members of WiN are commemorated in Poland among the "cursed soldiers" (Żołnierze wyklęci) — anti-communist partisans persecuted under the communist regime and rehabilitated after 1989.
