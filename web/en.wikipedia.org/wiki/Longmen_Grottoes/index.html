Longmen Grottoes
LocationSouth of Luoyang, Henan, China
CreatedFrom AD 493 (Northern Wei) onward
Contentsc. 2,300 caves, 110,000 statues
UNESCOWorld Heritage Site (2000)

The Longmen Grottoes (Chinese: 龙门石穴; pinyin: Lóngmén Shíkū, "Dragon's Gate Grottoes") are one of the finest examples of Chinese Buddhist art, comprising some 2,300 caves and niches carved into the steep limestone cliffs along the Yi River south of Luoyang, Henan province. They house as many as 110,000 Buddhist stone statues, more than 60 stupas, and 2,800 inscriptions.

History

Carving began in 493 AD when Emperor Xiaowen of Northern Wei moved the capital to Luoyang, and continued through the Tang dynasty and beyond. The largest figure, the 17-metre Vairocana Buddha of the Fengxian Temple (completed 676), is said to have been modelled on Empress Wu Zetian, who patronised the site. Guyang Cave, the oldest, contains more than 800 niches.

Many sculptures were damaged or removed during periods of persecution, vandalism, and early-20th-century art collecting, and heads of numerous statues are now in Western museums. The grottoes were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000.
