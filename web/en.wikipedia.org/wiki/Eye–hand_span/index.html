Eye–hand span

The eye–hand span is the distance, measured in notes, characters, or time, between where a performer's eyes are looking and where their hands are playing or typing during sight-reading. It is analogous to the eye–voice span in reading aloud. A larger span indicates that the performer is reading further ahead of their execution, a skill that develops with expertise and allows smoother, faster sight-reading.

Research

Studies using eye-tracking in music reading, associated with researchers such as Michael Furneaux, Shaaron Ainsworth, and others, show that skilled sight-readers maintain a span of several notes or beats, that the span shrinks at difficult passages and phrase boundaries, and that it expands with the predictability of the music. The span is limited by working memory and by the time needed to convert notation into motor commands. Related measures are used in typing research, where the eye leads the fingers by a variable number of characters.
