Market price

The market price is the current price at which an asset or service can be bought or sold. In economic theory, the market price tends toward the point where the quantity supplied equals the quantity demanded — the intersection of the supply and demand curves, known as the equilibrium price.

Determination

Where a market is competitive, no single buyer or seller can dictate the price; instead the price emerges from countless transactions. If demand rises while supply is unchanged, buyers bid the price up; if supply increases while demand is flat, sellers accept lower prices. The resulting price signals producers how much to make and consumers how much to buy.

Market price and value

Economists distinguish market price from value. The market price of a good can fluctuate daily with weather, fashion, or speculation, while its long-run cost of production changes more slowly. In financial markets, the market price of a share is simply the price of the most recent trade, which investors compare with their estimates of intrinsic value.

Price discovery

Exchanges and auction markets perform price discovery: they aggregate bids and offers so that a single transparent market price forms. Where markets are thin or monopolised, prices may be administered by the seller rather than discovered, and economists treat the outcome as a monopoly price rather than a competitive market price.
