The Bucharest Early Intervention Project (BEIP) is a long-running randomised controlled trial examining the effects of early institutional deprivation on child development and the benefits of high-quality foster care. It began in Bucharest, Romania, in 2000 and is the only randomised trial of foster care as an intervention for children raised in institutions.

Background and design

Under Nicolae Ceaușescu, Romania's pro-natalist policies and poverty filled state institutions with tens of thousands of children who were raised with very low caregiver-to-child ratios and little individual attention. In the BEIP, 136 children aged 6 to 31 months living in institutions were randomly assigned either to remain in care as usual or to be placed in foster families recruited and supported by the project. A comparison group of never-institutionalised children was also followed.

Findings

The project demonstrated that institutional rearing causes measurable deficits in IQ, attachment, brain electrical activity and growth, and that placement into foster care — especially before about two years of age — substantially improves outcomes in cognition, attachment security and psychopathology, though some effects of early deprivation, such as elevated rates of ADHD, persist. The results have influenced child-welfare policy internationally, supporting deinstitutionalisation and early family placement.
