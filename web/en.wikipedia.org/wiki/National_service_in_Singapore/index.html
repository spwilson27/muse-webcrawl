National service in Singapore

National service in Singapore is the statutory conscription of male Singaporean citizens and permanent residents, introduced in 1967 shortly after independence. Liable males serve two years of full-time service in the Singapore Armed Forces (SAF), the Singapore Police Force or the Singapore Civil Defence Force, followed by reservist obligations (Operationally Ready National Service) typically until age 40 for other ranks and 50 for officers.

History

The National Service (Amendment) Act of 1967 was passed as Singapore built up its own defence capability following separation from Malaysia and ahead of the announced British military withdrawal. The first intakes were trained with Israeli advisory assistance.

Enlistment and service

Males are required to register at age 16½ and are enlisted from age 18. Most serve in the SAF. Conscripts undergo basic military training before vocation posting. Exemptions are rare and mainly on medical grounds; defaulters face imprisonment. Female citizens may volunteer but are not conscripted.
