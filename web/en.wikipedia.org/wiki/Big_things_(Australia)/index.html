Big things (Australia)

The Big Banana, Coffs Harbour — the original Australian "big thing" (1964)

The big things of Australia are a loosely defined set of large novelty structures, mostly found on the roadside throughout the country. There are estimated to be more than 230 such objects. Typically built of steel, fibreglass or concrete and representing local produce, wildlife or industries — fruit, animals, tools, bottles and more — they function as tourist attractions, advertisements and landmarks, and have become a recognised piece of Australian folk culture.

History and notable examples

The phenomenon began with the Big Banana at Coffs Harbour, New South Wales, erected in 1964 by banana plantation owner John Landi to draw motorists to his roadside stall. Early followers included the Big Merino at Goulburn (a 15-metre concrete ram), the Big Pineapple at Woombye, Queensland (1971), and the Big Lobster at Kingston SE, South Australia. Other famous examples include the Big Prawn at Ballina, the Big Koala at Dadswells Bridge, the Big Mango at Bowen, the Big Golden Guitar at Tamworth, and Larry the Lobster. Many big things are tied to a roadside business whose fortunes rise and fall with them, and some have been demolished, moved or repeatedly restored, prompting heritage listings and public campaigns.

Cultural significance

Big things are a staple of the Australian road trip and a frequent subject of photography, comedy and popular lists. They are often affectionately regarded as kitsch, and "collecting" visits to them is a hobby in itself. Comparable traditions exist in the United States and Canada, where giant roadside objects have advertised businesses since the early 20th century.
