Quintus Hortensius Hortalus (114 – 50 BC) was a Roman orator and advocate, the leading pleader of the Roman courts in the generation before Cicero and Cicero's chief professional rival. He was consul in 69 BC.

Life and career

Hortensius began his public career as a young man during the Social War and rose quickly as an advocate. By his thirties he dominated the Roman bar, appearing in many of the most celebrated trials of the late Republic. He served as quaestor, aedile (75 BC), praetor (72 BC), and finally consul (69 BC), after which he declined a provincial command in favour of a life of wealth and luxury in Rome and at his villas.

He is best remembered as Cicero's great rival. In 70 BC Hortensius led the defence of the corrupt governor Verres against Cicero's prosecution; Cicero's devastating speeches (the Verrines) were a triumph and marked the point at which Cicero displaced Hortensius as Rome's foremost orator. Thereafter the two often appeared on the same side — as in the defence of Murena — and developed a relationship of professional respect and genuine friendship.

Style and reputation

Hortensius was the leading exponent in Rome of the florid "Asiatic" style of oratory, noted for its elaborate rhythm, elegant delivery, and graceful gesture. He possessed a famously retentive memory and a meticulously organised method of marshalling arguments. Cicero devoted much of his dialogue Brutus (46 BC) to an affectionate assessment of Hortensius's career, tracing both his brilliance and the decline of his powers in later life.

None of Hortensius's speeches survives. He was also a wealthy and ostentatious connoisseur, famous for his fishponds and for watering a prized plane tree with wine.

Family

Hortensius married first Lutatia, daughter of Quintus Lutatius Catulus, and then Marcia, whom he famously "lent" to his friend Cato the Younger — Marcia returned to Hortensius after Cato, taking a substantial fortune with her. His daughter Hortensia was herself renowned for a public speech against a tax on wealthy women, delivered in 42 BC and recorded by Appian.
