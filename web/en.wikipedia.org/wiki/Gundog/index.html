Gundog

Gundogs, or gun dogs, are types of dogs developed to assist hunters in finding and retrieving game, usually birds. They are divided by function into three main groups: retrievers, flushing dogs (spaniels), and pointing breeds. Kennel clubs in the United Kingdom classify these breeds in the Gundog Group, while the American Kennel Club places them in the Sporting Group.

Types

  • Retrievers — such as the Labrador Retriever, Golden Retriever, and Chesapeake Bay Retriever — specialise in retrieving shot game, particularly waterfowl, and typically have soft mouths and water-resistant coats.
  • Spaniels — English Springer Spaniels, Cocker Spaniels, and others — work close to the hunter, quartering ground and flushing game into the air within shooting range.
  • Pointing breeds — Pointers, English and Gordon Setters, and the versatile continental breeds (German Shorthaired Pointer, Brittany, Weimaraner) — range widely and freeze in a "point" on scenting game.

Training and work

Gundog training emphasises steadiness to shot, response to whistle and hand signals, and retrieving to hand without damaging the bird. In Britain, working gundogs are central to driven and walked-up shooting, and their skills are tested in field trials and working tests. Many traditional gundog breeds are also popular family pets, though working strains often retain strong hunting instincts.

History

Dogs assisted hunters with nets and bows long before firearms; the specialised gundog breeds emerged in the 18th and 19th centuries as wing shooting with shotguns became popular, with British breeders systematising the retriever, spaniel, and setter types.
