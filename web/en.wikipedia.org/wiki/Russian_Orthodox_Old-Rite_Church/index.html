Russian Orthodox Old-Rite Church

The Russian Orthodox Old-Rite Church is an Eastern Orthodox church belonging to the priestly branch of the Old Believers, who separated from the official Russian Orthodox Church after the liturgical reforms of Patriarch Nikon in the seventeenth century. It is the largest of the priestly Old Believer jurisdictions.

History

The Old Believers rejected Nikon's corrections of liturgical books and practices — such as making the sign of the cross with two fingers rather than three — holding that the old Russian rites were inviolable. The resulting schism (Raskol) led to centuries of persecution and self-exile. Priestless groups, lacking bishops, developed their own chapels, while priestly Old Believers received fugitive priests from the state church.

In 1846 the defrocked Greek metropolitan Ambrose (Pappa-Georgopoulos) joined the Old Believers at Belaya Krinitsa in Austrian Bukovina and consecrated bishops, establishing a full hierarchy — the Belokrinitskaya hierarchy — from which the Old-Rite Church descends. After periods of tolerance (1905) and Soviet persecution, the church's metropolitanate settled in Moscow at the Rogozhskoye Cemetery, historically its spiritual centre. Its head bears the title Metropolitan of Moscow and All Rus'.
