The Diocese of Oxford is a Church of England diocese covering the counties of Oxfordshire, Berkshire, and Buckinghamshire, an area of some 2,280 square miles with a population of over two million. It is one of the largest dioceses in England by number of parishes and church schools.

History

The diocese was created in 1542 under Henry VIII, initially with its seat at Osney Abbey and from 1546 at Christ Church, Oxford. Its boundaries changed several times in the nineteenth century, gaining Berkshire and Buckinghamshire from neighbouring dioceses.

Organisation

The diocesan Bishop of Oxford is assisted by area bishops of Dorchester, Buckingham, and Reading, each overseeing an episcopal area matching one of the three counties, together with archdeacons and a diocesan office in Oxford. The diocese maintains several hundred parishes and a large family of church schools.
