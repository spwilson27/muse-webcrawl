Bishop of London

The Bishop of London is the ordinary of the Church of England Diocese of London in the Province of Canterbury. Viewed from the perspective of the Bishop of Oxford, London is the senior neighbour: the Bishop of London ranks third in the Church of England's order of precedence, immediately after the Archbishops of Canterbury and York, and serves ex officio as Dean of the Chapels Royal and a Privy Counsellor.

Role and history

The see of London is ancient, with a bishop recorded as early as AD 314 at the Council of Arles; the modern diocese covers most of Greater London north of the Thames. Its cathedral is St Paul's Cathedral. Unlike most diocesan bishops, the Bishop of London sits immediately in the House of Lords as a Lord Spiritual, whereas the Bishop of Oxford, like the bishops of Durham and Winchester, follows by seniority among the privileged group of five.

Relations with Oxford

The two sees have long exchanged personnel and influence: both govern large, historic dioceses adjoining one another along the Thames, and their bishops work together on national church governance, including the House of Bishops and the Crown Nominations Commission.
