The Bishop of Oxford is the ordinary of the Church of England's Diocese of Oxford in the Province of Canterbury. The diocese covers the counties of Oxfordshire, Berkshire, and Buckinghamshire—one of the largest in England by number of parishes—and the bishop's seat is at Christ Church Cathedral in Oxford, which uniquely also serves as the chapel of Christ Church college.

History

The see was founded by Henry VIII in 1542, one of the new dioceses carved from the Diocese of Lincoln at the English Reformation; its first cathedral was Osney Abbey before the seat moved to Christ Church in 1546. Notable bishops include Samuel Wilberforce (1845–1869), a leading Victorian churchman known as "Soapy Sam," and Richard Harries (1987–2006), a prominent broadcaster and ethicist. The diocese is divided into four episcopal areas—Oxford, Dorchester, Buckingham, and Reading—each served by an area bishop.
