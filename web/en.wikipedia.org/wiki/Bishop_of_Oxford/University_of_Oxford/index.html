The University of Oxford is a collegiate research university in Oxford, England. Teaching existed there by 1096, making it the oldest university in the English-speaking world and the world's second-oldest university in continuous operation after Bologna.

Organisation

Oxford is a federation of 39 self-governing colleges and a number of permanent private halls, together with central departments, libraries, and museums. Students are members of a college as well as the university, and much undergraduate teaching takes place in tutorials — small-group sessions with a tutor that are a hallmark of the Oxford system.

History and reputation

The university grew rapidly from 1167, when Henry II banned English students from Paris. Alumni include numerous British prime ministers, heads of state, Nobel laureates, and writers. The Bodleian Library is one of the oldest libraries in Europe and a legal deposit library for the United Kingdom. Oxford consistently ranks among the top universities in the world.
