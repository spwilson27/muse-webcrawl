Christ Church Cathedral, Oxford

LocationChrist Church, Oxford
DioceseOxford
Founded12th century (cathedral since 1546)
Unique featureBoth a college chapel and a diocesan cathedral

Christ Church Cathedral is the cathedral of the Diocese of Oxford and simultaneously the chapel of Christ Church, a constituent college of the University of Oxford. This dual role makes it unique in England.

History

The site was originally the church of the Augustinian priory of St Frideswide, founded on the place associated with Oxford's patron saint. When Henry VIII refounded the college in 1546, the priory church became the cathedral of the new Diocese of Oxford, transferring the seat from Osney Abbey. Though one of England's smallest cathedrals, it retains fine Norman architecture, the shrine of St Frideswide, and notable stained glass including windows by Edward Burne-Jones.

Music

The cathedral choir, with its lay clerks and choristers educated at Christ Church Cathedral School, is internationally renowned for its recordings and broadcasts, singing daily services in term time.
