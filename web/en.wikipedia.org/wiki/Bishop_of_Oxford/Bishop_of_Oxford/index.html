The Bishop of Oxford is the ordinary of the Church of England Diocese of Oxford in the Province of Canterbury. The diocese covers the counties of Berkshire, Buckinghamshire and Oxfordshire — an area that coincides with the Thames Valley — together with parts of neighbouring counties.

History

The see was founded by Henry VIII in 1542 as part of the creation of new dioceses during the English Reformation. The cathedra was initially placed at Osney Abbey before moving in 1546 to Christ Church, whose chapel serves the unusual dual role of college chapel and cathedral. The bishop's official residence is Bishop's Lodge at Kidlington, north of Oxford.

The modern diocese is one of the largest in the Church of England by population and is divided into four episcopal areas — Oxford, Buckingham, Dorchester and Reading — each served by an area bishop. The Diocesan Bishop of Oxford since 2016 has been Steven Croft, formerly Bishop of Sheffield.

Notable bishops

Famous holders of the see include Samuel Wilberforce, the Victorian bishop known for his debate with T. H. Huxley over evolution in 1860, and Richard Harries, who later became a life peer as Lord Harries of Pentregarth.
