Bishop of Truro

The Bishop of Truro is the ordinary of the Church of England Diocese of Truro in the Province of Canterbury. In relation to the Bishop of Oxford, Truro represents the younger generation of Victorian dioceses: where Oxford's see dates to 1542, Truro's was created only in 1876, when the historic archdeaconry of Cornwall was separated from the Diocese of Exeter.

Role and history

The diocese covers the county of Cornwall, the Isles of Scilly and a small part of Devon. The bishop's cathedral is Truro Cathedral, begun in 1880 as the first entirely new Anglican cathedral built in England since St Paul's. The first bishop, Edward White Benson, later became Archbishop of Canterbury and is credited with devising the Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols while at Truro.

Comparison with Oxford

Both bishops are diocesan ordinaries in the Province of Canterbury and members of the House of Bishops. Oxford, as one of the five privileged sees, reaches the Lords Spiritual by right of seniority far sooner than Truro, whose bishop must wait for a vacancy under the general seniority rules; Oxford's diocese also covers three counties, while Truro's ministry is shaped by Cornwall's distinct Celtic heritage and dispersed rural parishes.
