Occupational safety and health

Occupational safety and health (OSH), also called occupational health and safety (OHS), is a multidisciplinary field concerned with the safety, health and welfare of people at work. Its goals include fostering a safe working environment, preventing work-related injury and ill health, and protecting co-workers, employers, customers and the public who might be affected by the workplace.

Hazards

Workplace hazards are commonly grouped as safety hazards (machinery, falls, electricity), biological hazards, physical hazards (noise, radiation, temperature), ergonomic hazards, chemical hazards and psychosocial hazards such as stress and workplace violence. Risk management typically follows a hierarchy of controls: elimination, substitution, engineering controls, administrative controls and personal protective equipment (PPE).

Regulation

Modern OSH systems emerged from 19th-century factory legislation. In the United States the Occupational Safety and Health Act of 1970 created OSHA and NIOSH; the United Kingdom has the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the HSE; the European Union sets framework directives, and the International Labour Organization promotes global conventions. The ILO estimates that nearly 3 million people die each year from work-related causes worldwide.
