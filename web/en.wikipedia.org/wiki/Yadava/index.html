Yadava

The Yadavas (Sanskrit: Yādava, "descendants of Yadu") were an ancient Indian clan (or group of clans) who claimed descent from Yadu, a legendary king of the Lunar dynasty (Chandravansha). They figure prominently in the Sanskrit epics and Puranas, above all as the people of the god-hero Krishna, and are associated with the regions of Mathura, Saurashtra and Dvaraka.

Origins and clan structure

According to the traditional genealogies, Yadu was the eldest son of King Yayati by Devayani, cursed by his father to lose the royal succession, passing the throne to his younger brother Puru. The Yadavas consequently spread westward and southward rather than ruling the central Kuru realm. By the time of the epics they comprised several allied clans — notably the Vrishnis, Andhakas, Bhojas and Satvatas — organised in a confederacy (sangha) at Mathura and later at Dvaraka on the Kathiawar coast, in which power was shared among clan chiefs rather than held by a single king.

In the epics

Krishna, of the Vrishni clan, is the central Yadava figure of the Mahabharata: counsellor and charioteer of Arjuna, and, in later tradition, an avatar of Vishnu. His brother Balarama and the Yadava heroes Satyaki and Kritavarma also play important roles in the Kurukshetra war; Kritavarma fought for the Kauravas while most Yadava forces and Krishna sided with the Pandavas.

The Mausala Parva of the Mahabharata recounts the self-destruction of the Yadavas 36 years after the war: cursed by sages, the clan destroyed itself in a drunken brawl at Prabhasa using reeds turned to iron clubs (mausala), after which Krishna departed the world and Dvaraka was swallowed by the sea.

Historical traces and later claims

Historically, Yadava identity was claimed by or attributed to several later dynasties, including the Seuna (Yadava) dynasty of Devagiri (c. 1187–1317) in the Deccan, and communities and ruling houses in western and central India. In modern India, "Yadav" is a widespread community name, and many groups trace their ancestry to the epic Yadavas and to Krishna.
