Eris (minor-planet designation 136199 Eris) is the most massive and second-largest known dwarf planet in the Solar System. It is a trans-Neptunian object in the scattered disc, with a high-inclination orbit, and has one known moon, Dysnomia.

Discovery and naming

Eris was discovered by a Palomar Observatory-based team led by Mike Brown. Images were taken in October 2003 and the object identified in January 2005; the discovery was announced in July 2005. Initially nicknamed "Xena", it was at first reported as possibly larger than Pluto. The object was named Eris, after the Greek goddess of strife and discord, in September 2006.

Role in redefining "planet"

The discovery of Eris forced astronomers to reconsider the definition of a planet. In 2006 the International Astronomical Union adopted a definition under which both Eris and Pluto are classified as dwarf planets rather than planets.

Physical characteristics

Eris has a diameter of about 2,326 kilometres, slightly smaller than Pluto, but about 27% more massive, determined from the orbit of Dysnomia. Its surface is covered in methane ice, giving it a high albedo, and its orbit carries it as far as about 97 astronomical units from the Sun.
