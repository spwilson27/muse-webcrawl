New media

New media are communication technologies that enable or enhance interaction between users and content, typically digital and networked: websites, social media, streaming audio and video, blogs, podcasts, wikis, video games, mobile apps, and virtual and augmented reality. The term contrasts with “old” media such as print, radio, and broadcast television.

Characteristics

Scholars such as Lev Manovich identify key principles of new media: numerical representation, modularity, automation, variability, and transcoding. New media are typically interactive, hypertextual, networked, and allow user-generated content and many-to-many communication rather than one-way broadcasting.

Social impact

New media have transformed journalism, politics, marketing, education, and entertainment, enabling citizen journalism, social movements organised online, and on-demand consumption, while raising concerns about misinformation, filter bubbles, privacy, algorithmic bias, and the attention economy.

History

The term gained currency in the 1990s with the CD-ROM, the World Wide Web, and digital multimedia, and expanded with Web 2.0 participatory platforms in the 2000s. Academic programs in new media studies examine both the technologies and their cultural effects.
