Josh Gibson

Joshua Gibson (December 21, 1911 - January 20, 1947) was an American baseball catcher in the Negro leagues. Regarded by many as one of the greatest power hitters of all time, he was elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1972.

Early life

Gibson was born in Buena Vista, Georgia, and moved with his family to Pittsburgh as a boy, where his father worked in the steel mills. He played sandlot and industrial-league baseball and was working as an elevator operator and playing for a department-store team when he was spotted by the Homestead Grays.

Career

Gibson joined the Grays in 1930, famously debuting as an emergency substitute catcher. He starred for the Pittsburgh Crawfords in the mid-1930s alongside Satchel Paige, then returned to the Grays, leading them to nine consecutive Negro National League pennants from 1937 to 1945. Contemporary accounts credited him with close to 800 home runs over his career, against Negro league, semipro and exhibition competition, and with batting averages well over .300; later statistical research confirms averages around .370 in league play. He also played winter ball in Mexico and the Caribbean. Because of baseball's color line, Gibson never played in the major leagues.

Death and legacy

Gibson's health declined in the 1940s; he was diagnosed with a brain tumor and suffered from alcohol problems. He died of a stroke in January 1947, aged 35, just three months before Jackie Robinson broke the major league color barrier. Gibson was elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1972, and in 2020 Major League Baseball recognized the Negro leagues as major leagues, with Gibson's records incorporated into official statistics.
