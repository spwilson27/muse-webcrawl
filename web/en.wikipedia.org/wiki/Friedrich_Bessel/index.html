Friedrich Bessel

Friedrich Wilhelm Bessel (22 July 1784 – 17 March 1846) was a German astronomer, mathematician, physicist and geodesist. He was the first astronomer to measure the parallax of a star, providing the first reliable distance to a star beyond the Sun, and he systematised the Bessel functions important in physics.


Born: Minden, Prussia
Known for: Stellar parallax of 61 Cygni, Bessel functions

Career

A self-taught clerk turned calculator of orbits, Bessel became director of the Königsberg Observatory in 1810 and professor at the university there.

Contributions

In 1838 Bessel announced the parallax of 61 Cygni, about 0.31 arcseconds, implying a distance of roughly ten light-years. His precise stellar positions, work on geodesy and the Bessel ellipsoid, and analysis of pendulum motion set new standards. The dark companion he inferred for Sirius from its wobble was later observed. See Fred Hoyle for later stellar astronomy.
