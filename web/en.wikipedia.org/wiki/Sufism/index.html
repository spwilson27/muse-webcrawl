Sufism (Arabic: Tasawwuf) is the mystical tradition of Islam, emphasising the inward search for God and purification of the heart from ego. Sufis seek experiential knowledge of the divine through remembrance (dhikr), meditation, music and poetry, usually within an order (tariqa) tracing a chain of teachers back to the Prophet Muhammad.

History and influence

Early ascetics such as Rabia of Basra (8th century) taught disinterested love of God; Al-Ghazali reconciled Sufism with orthodox theology. The great orders — Qadiriyya, Naqshbandiyya, Chishtiyya, Mevleviyye (the 'whirling dervishes' founded around Rumi) — spread Islam across Africa and Asia. Sufi poets — Rumi, Hafez, Attar, Kabir — produced some of the world's most beloved religious literature; Rumi's Masnavi is often called 'the Quran in Persian'. Modern reformist movements sometimes condemn Sufi shrine practices, yet Sufism remains a major, often moderate, force in Muslim life.
