Maintenance

The technical meaning of maintenance involves functional checks, servicing, repairing, or replacing of necessary devices, equipment, machinery, building infrastructure, and supporting utilities in industrial, business, and residential installations. Over time, the term has come to encompass multiple wordings describing various cost-effective practices to keep equipment operational, including preventive, predictive, corrective, and reliability-centred approaches. Maintenance is distinct from repair in that it includes the routine activities intended to prevent failure in the first place.


A technician performing scheduled maintenance on industrial machinery.

Types

Preventive maintenance

Preventive maintenance (PM) is performed at predetermined intervals or according to prescribed criteria — calendar time, running hours, cycles — in order to reduce the probability of failure. Examples include lubrication, filter changes, inspections, and calibration.

Corrective maintenance

Corrective maintenance is carried out after a fault is recognised, to restore an item to a state in which it can perform its required function. Where the failure stops production this becomes emergency or breakdown maintenance, generally the most expensive strategy per event.

Predictive maintenance

Predictive maintenance (PdM) uses condition-monitoring techniques — vibration analysis, thermography, oil analysis, ultrasound — to determine when maintenance should be performed, intervening only when measurements indicate deterioration.

Reliability-centred maintenance

Reliability-centred maintenance (RCM), developed in the aviation industry in the 1960s and 1970s, selects maintenance tasks based on the consequences of each failure mode, prioritising safety- and mission-critical functions.

Sectors

Maintenance practices apply across sectors: aircraft maintenance is regulated in detail by aviation authorities; facilities maintenance covers buildings and their systems; vehicle maintenance ranges from owner checks to fleet programmes; and software maintenance denotes the modification of software after delivery to correct faults, improve performance, or adapt to a changed environment.

Economics

Maintenance typically accounts for a substantial share of the operating cost of industrial plant. Optimising the maintenance strategy balances the cost of planned work against the far higher cost of unplanned downtime, secondary damage, and safety incidents; under-maintenance increases failure rates, while over-maintenance wastes resources and can itself introduce defects.
