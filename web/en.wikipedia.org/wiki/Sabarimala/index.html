Sabarimala

Sabarimala is a Hindu pilgrimage centre located in the Periyar Tiger Reserve in the Western Ghat mountain ranges of Pathanamthitta District, Kerala, India. It is one of the largest annual pilgrimages in the world, with an estimated 40 to 50 million devotees visiting every year. The temple is dedicated to the Hindu celibate god Ayyappa, also known as Dharma Sastha, who according to belief is the son of Shiva and Mohini, the feminine incarnation of Vishnu.

The temple is situated on a hilltop at an altitude of about 468 metres (1,535 feet) above mean sea level, surrounded by 18 hills in the Periyar Tiger Reserve. The temple is open for worship only during the days of Mandalapooja (approximately 15 November to 26 December), Makaravilakku (14 January) and Maha Vishuva Sankranti (14 April), and the first five days of each Malayalam month.

Pilgrimage

Devotees traditionally observe a period of austerity, known as vratham, lasting 41 days before undertaking the pilgrimage. They wear black or saffron clothing, abstain from alcohol, meat and certain other indulgences, and walk barefoot. The pilgrims carry on their heads a bundle, the irumudikettu, containing offerings such as coconuts filled with ghee. The trek to the shrine involves climbing the hill, and the final approach to the sanctum is by a flight of 18 gold-plated steps known as the Pathinettam Padi, which hold symbolic significance in Ayyappa worship.

Makaravilakku

The most important festival at Sabarimala is Makaravilakku, celebrated on Makara Sankranti (usually 14 January). Devotees believe that a divine light, the Makara Jyothi, appears on the Ponnambalamedu hill opposite the temple at dusk, marking the celestial event associated with Ayyappa.

Women's entry controversy

For decades, women between the ages of 10 and 50 were not permitted to enter the temple, a restriction defended by the temple authorities on the grounds that Ayyappa is worshipped there as a celibate deity. In September 2018, the Supreme Court of India ruled that the restriction was unconstitutional and that women of all ages could enter the temple. The verdict sparked large-scale protests in Kerala, and the matter was later referred to a larger bench for review.
