Simple interest

In economics and finance, simple interest is interest calculated only on the principal amount of a loan or deposit, without compounding — that is, interest is not charged on previously accumulated interest. It contrasts with compound interest, in which interest is periodically added to the principal.

Calculation

Simple interest is computed with the formula:

I = P · r · t

where I is the interest, P is the principal, r is the interest rate per period, and t is the number of periods. The total amount after t periods is therefore A = P (1 + r t), a linear function of time. For example, $1,000 lent at 5% simple interest for 3 years earns $150 in interest, for a total of $1,150.

Use

Simple interest is common in short-term lending, some car loans and consumer loans, and in the calculation of interest accrued between coupon payments on bonds. Because it grows linearly rather than exponentially, it is generally favourable to borrowers relative to compound interest at the same nominal rate.
