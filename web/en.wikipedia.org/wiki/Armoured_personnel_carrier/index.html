Armoured personnel carrier

An armoured personnel carrier (APC) is a type of armoured military vehicle designed to transport infantry personnel to the battlefield. APCs are usually armed only for self-defence and are not intended to take part in direct-fire combat; more heavily armed variants are classed as infantry fighting vehicles.

History

The concept emerged in the First World War with vehicles such as the British Mark IX tank, and matured in the Second World War with half-tracks like the German Sd.Kfz. 251 and American M3. Cold War designs such as the American M113 and Soviet BTR series moved to fully enclosed, often amphibious hulls.

Design

APCs may be wheeled or tracked, and their armour typically protects against small arms fire and shell splinters. Modern designs emphasise protection against mines and improvised explosive devices, with V-shaped hulls and blast-attenuating seats.
