History of Zionism

MovementJewish national movement
Key figureTheodor Herzl (1860–1904)
MilestonesFirst Zionist Congress 1897; Balfour Declaration 1917; State of Israel 1948

The history of Zionism traces the Jewish national movement that sought to re-establish a Jewish homeland in the Land of Israel, from its 19th-century origins through the founding of the State of Israel in 1948 and beyond.

Precursors

Religious attachment to Zion was ancient, but modern Zionism arose from 19th-century European nationalism and antisemitism. Precursors included Rabbis Alkalai and Kalischer, Moses Hess's Rome and Jerusalem (1862), and the Hibbat Zion movement and First Aliyah (1882) spurred by pogroms in Russia.

Political Zionism

Theodor Herzl, shocked by the Dreyfus affair, published Der Judenstaat (1896) and convened the First Zionist Congress at Basel in 1897, creating the World Zionist Organization. Rival strands — cultural Zionism (Ahad Ha'am), socialist Zionism, revisionist Zionism (Jabotinsky) — debated the movement's character.

Mandate and statehood

The Balfour Declaration (1917) and the League of Nations Mandate gave Zionism international recognition. Jewish immigration built the Yishuv's institutions in Palestine; the Holocaust lent urgency to statehood demands. The UN partition plan of 1947 and the declaration of the State of Israel on 14 May 1948 fulfilled Zionism's central aim, while the ensuing war displaced hundreds of thousands of Palestinian Arabs — the Nakba — a grievance at the heart of the continuing conflict.
