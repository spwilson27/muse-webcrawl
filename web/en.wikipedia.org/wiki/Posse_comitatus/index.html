Posse comitatus

Posse comitatus (Latin for "power of the county") is the common-law power of a county sheriff or other authority to summon able-bodied men to assist in keeping the peace, pursuing felons, or executing legal process. Historically it referred both to the power itself and to the body of men so summoned — the "posse" of Western tradition.

History

The institution derives from English common law, under which the sheriff could raise the posse comitatus of the county, a duty rooted in the Anglo-Saxon fyrd and later systematised in statutes such as the Statute of Winchester (1285). It was carried into American law, where posses played a prominent role in frontier law enforcement. Sheriffs in most U.S. states retain statutory authority to summon assistance.

Posse Comitatus Act

The term is also associated with the United States Posse Comitatus Act of 1878, which (as later amended) generally prohibits the use of the U.S. Army and Air Force as domestic law enforcement, with exceptions such as the Insurrection Act. The broader principle limits military involvement in civilian policing.
