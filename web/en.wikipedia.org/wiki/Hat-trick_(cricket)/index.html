Hat-trick (cricket)

In cricket, a hat-trick occurs when a bowler takes three wickets with three consecutive deliveries. The deliveries may be interrupted by an over bowled by another bowler from the other end, or by the other team's innings, but must be consecutive deliveries by the individual bowler in the same match. It is a rare and celebrated feat; only a few dozen have been achieved in over 2,000 Test matches.

Origin of the term

The term is generally traced to 1858, when the bowler H. H. Stephenson took three wickets in three balls for the All-England Eleven against Hallam at Sheffield. A collection was taken for him and the proceeds used to buy a hat, which was presented to him — giving rise to the expression, which later spread to other sports including association football and ice hockey.

Notable hat-tricks

Jimmy Matthews of Australia is the only bowler to take two hat-tricks in the same Test match, against South Africa in 1912. In limited-overs cricket, Lasith Malinga of Sri Lanka famously took four wickets in four balls in a One Day International against South Africa in 2007, and repeated a similar feat in Twenty20 internationals. Hat-tricks have also decided major finals, including Chetan Sharma's in the 1987 World Cup — the first in that tournament — offset by his being on the receiving end of famous last-ball hitting in an earlier final.

Variations

A "double hat-trick" is sometimes used informally for four wickets in four balls. Wickets taken across two innings of the same match, or across the end of one innings and the start of the next, still qualify provided the bowler's deliveries remain consecutive for that bowler.
