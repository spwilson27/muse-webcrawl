Tallahassee, Florida

Downtown Tallahassee and the Florida State Capitol.

Tallahassee is the capital city of the U.S. state of Florida. It is the county seat of Leon County and the principal city of the Tallahassee metropolitan area. As of the 2020 census, the population was 196,169, making it the eighth-largest city in Florida. Tallahassee is known for its rolling hills and canopy roads, features unusual in a state known for flat terrain.

The name "Tallahassee" is a Muskogean word often translated as "old fields" or "old town", reflecting the area's history of Apalachee and later Seminole settlement.

History

Tallahassee was chosen as the territorial capital in 1824, in part because it lay midway between the older Spanish colonial capitals of St. Augustine and Pensacola. It was incorporated in 1825. During the American Civil War, Tallahassee was the only Confederate state capital east of the Mississippi River not to be captured by Union forces. In the 20th century the city grew around state government and its two major universities.

Education

Tallahassee is home to Florida State University and Florida A&M University, one of the largest historically Black universities in the United States, as well as Tallahassee State College. The large student population gives the city a notably young demographic profile for a state capital.

Government

As the state capital, Tallahassee hosts the Florida Capitol complex, including the 22-story Capitol tower completed in 1977 and the preserved Historic Capitol, now a museum. State government is the city's largest employer.
