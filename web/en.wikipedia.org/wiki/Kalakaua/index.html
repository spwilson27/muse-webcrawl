Kalākaua

Kalākaua (David Laʻamea Kamananakapuu Mahinulani Naloiaehuokalani Lumialani Kalākaua; 16 November 1836 – 20 January 1891), sometimes called the Merrie Monarch, was the last king and penultimate monarch of the Kingdom of Hawaii, reigning from 12 February 1874 until his death in 1891. He was succeeded by his sister, Liliʻuokalani.

Reign

Kalākaua was elected king by the legislature in 1874 after the death of Lunalilo, defeating Queen Emma in a contested election that sparked rioting. His reign saw the Reciprocity Treaty of 1875 with the United States, which boosted Hawaii's sugar industry, and a revival of Hawaiian culture, including the hula, which he encouraged after it had been suppressed by missionaries. He composed the song "Hawaiʻi Ponoʻī", now the state anthem.

World tour and later years

In 1881 Kalākaua travelled around the world, becoming the first reigning monarch to circumnavigate the globe, visiting Asia, the Middle East, Europe and the United States. In 1887 he was forced under threat of violence to sign the so-called Bayonet Constitution, which reduced the power of the monarchy and disenfranchised many Native Hawaiians and Asian subjects. He died in San Francisco in January 1891 while seeking medical treatment.

Legacy

Kalākaua is remembered for his patronage of Hawaiian music and dance; the Merrie Monarch Festival, the world's premier hula competition held annually in Hilo, is named in his honour.
