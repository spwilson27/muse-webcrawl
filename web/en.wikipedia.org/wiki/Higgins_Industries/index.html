Higgins Industries


Higgins boat production, WWII

Higgins Industries was an American shipbuilding company based in New Orleans, Louisiana, founded by Andrew Jackson Higgins. The company is best known for designing and mass-producing the Landing Craft, Vehicle, Personnel (LCVP), universally known as the "Higgins boat," which became the principal Allied landing craft of World War II.

History

Higgins began building shallow-draft wooden boats for oil exploration, logging, and trapping in the Louisiana bayous in the 1920s. His "Eureka boat," with its spoonbill bow and protected propeller, could run in shallow water and beach itself — qualities that attracted the attention of the United States Marine Corps, which was searching for a practical landing craft.

World War II production

During the war, Higgins Industries grew from a small firm into a major manufacturer employing some 20,000 workers in New Orleans — notable at the time for its racially integrated workforce. The company produced more than 20,000 boats, including LCVPs, PT boats, and other craft. General Dwight D. Eisenhower later credited Higgins as "the man who won the war for us," saying that without his landing craft the whole strategy of the war would have been different. Higgins also designed ambitious projects such as the airborne lifeboat and a small cargo plane (the Higgins A-1 "Commando" helicopter concept among them).
