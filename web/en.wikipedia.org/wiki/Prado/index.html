Museo del Prado

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Prado in Madrid: the Villanueva building's colonnade with the statue of Velázquez before it]

The Museo del Prado, officially known as Museo Nacional del Prado, is the main Spanish national art museum, located in central Madrid. It is widely considered to house one of the world's finest collections of European art, dating from the 12th century to the early 20th century, based on the former Spanish Royal Collection, and unquestionably the best single collection of Spanish art.

Opened in 1819 in a building designed by Juan de Villanueva, the Prado's glories are Velázquez — above all Las Meninas (1656) — Goya, from the court portraits to the terrifying Third of May 1808 and the Black Paintings, and El Greco, plus the largest holdings of Bosch (including The Garden of Earthly Delights), Titian and Rubens. The museum anchors Madrid's 'Golden Triangle of Art' with the Reina Sofía and Thyssen-Bornemisza museums.
