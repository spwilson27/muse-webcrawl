Home run

A home run scores the batter and any runners on base

In baseball, a home run (abbreviated HR) is scored when the ball is hit in such a way that the batter is able to circle the bases and reach home plate safely in one play without any errors being committed by the defensive team. A home run is usually achieved by hitting the ball over the outfield fence between the foul poles (or hitting one of the foul poles) without the ball first touching the ground, resulting in an automatic home run. Inside-the-park home runs, where the ball remains in play, are rarer.

Scoring

A home run scores the batter and any runners already on base: a solo home run (one run), a two-run homer, a three-run homer, and a grand slam (four runs, with the bases loaded). Home runs are among the most celebrated plays in the sport, and leading home run hitters are among the most famous—and often highest-paid—players.

Types and terminology

Common types include the walk-off home run, which ends the game when hit by the home team in the bottom of the final inning; back-to-back home runs by consecutive batters; and the inside-the-park home run. In softball, as in baseball, a home run over the fence awards the batter and all runners a free circuit of the bases.

Records

The all-time Major League Baseball career record is 762 home runs, held by Barry Bonds, who also holds the single-season record with 73 in 2001. Babe Ruth (714) and Hank Aaron (755) held the career mark for decades; Aaron's record was the most celebrated in American sport when he broke Ruth's in 1974. In Japan's Nippon Professional Baseball, Sadaharu Oh hit 868 career home runs, the world professional record. Josh Gibson is credited with the most in the Negro leagues.
