Akra–Bazzi method

Recursion tree of a divide-and-conquer recurrence

In computer science, the Akra–Bazzi method, or Akra–Bazzi theorem, is used to analyze the asymptotic behavior of divide-and-conquer recurrences whose subproblems have substantially different sizes. It is a generalization of the master theorem for divide-and-conquer recurrences, which assumes subproblems are of equal size. The method was described by Mohamad Akra and Louay Bazzi in 1998.

Formulation

The method applies to recurrences of the form T(x) = g(x) + Σ ai T(bi x + hi(x)), where the subproblem sizes bi x differ by small perturbations hi. One first finds the unique real number p such that Σ ai bip = 1. The solution then is T(x) = Θ( xp ( 1 + ∫1x g(u)/up+1 du ) ).

Application

The Akra–Bazzi method handles recurrences arising from algorithms whose splits are unbalanced or randomized, such as quickselect variants or recurrences in which the problem is divided into unequal pieces — for example a split into one-third and two-thirds subproblems with polynomial overhead. Because the master theorem requires all subproblems to have the same size, such recurrences previously demanded ad-hoc analysis; Akra–Bazzi gives a single formula covering both the balanced and unbalanced cases.
