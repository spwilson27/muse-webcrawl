World peace, or peace on Earth, is the concept of an ideal state of peace within and among all people and nations on Earth. Different cultures, religions, philosophies and organisations hold differing views on how such a state could be achieved. Historically, world peace has been pursued through international law, disarmament, international organisations such as the League of Nations and the United Nations, and through movements advocating nonviolence.

Theories

Proposed routes to world peace include the democratic peace theory, which holds that democracies rarely if ever go to war with one another; capitalist peace theory, emphasising trade interdependence; and Immanuel Kant's idea of a federation of free states set out in his 1795 essay Perpetual Peace. Other frameworks stress international institutions, arms control, or a world government.

Measurement

The Institute for Economics and Peace publishes the annual Global Peace Index, ranking countries by indicators of internal and external conflict, societal safety and militarisation. Iceland has consistently ranked among the most peaceful countries since the index began.

Religious and cultural views

Many world religions contain teachings on peace, and the dove and the olive branch are longstanding symbols of peace in Western culture. The concept is also the goal of many peace movements and of the annual International Day of Peace, observed on 21 September.
