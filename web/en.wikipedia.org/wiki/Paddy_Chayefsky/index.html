Paddy Chayefsky

Sidney Aaron "Paddy" Chayefsky (January 29, 1923 – August 1, 1981) was an American playwright, screenwriter and novelist. He is the only person to have won three solo Academy Awards for Best Screenplay, for Marty (1955), The Hospital (1971), and Network (1976).

Early life

Chayefsky was born in the Bronx to Russian-Jewish immigrant parents. He attended DeWitt Clinton High School and the City College of New York, graduating in 1943 with a degree in accounting. He served in the United States Army during the Second World War, where he was wounded by a land mine near Aachen; the nickname "Paddy" was bestowed on him during his service.

Career

Chayefsky became a leading figure of the "Golden Age of Television," writing naturalistic dramas for anthology series such as Studio One and The Philco Television Playhouse. His teleplay Marty, about a lonely Bronx butcher, was adapted into the 1955 film that won the Academy Award for Best Picture and the Palme d'Or at Cannes.

His later screenplays turned more satirical and rhetorical. The Hospital (1971) satirized the American medical system, while Network (1976), about a television news anchor who becomes a ratings-driven prophet on air, is widely regarded as one of the greatest screenplays ever written. He also wrote the novel Altered States (1978) and its film adaptation, from which he had his name removed after disputes with director Ken Russell.

Style and legacy

Chayefsky's early work was celebrated for its kitchen-sink realism and dialogue drawn from ordinary speech; his later work used extended monologue and satire to attack institutional corruption and media sensationalism. He died of cancer in 1981 at the age of 58.
