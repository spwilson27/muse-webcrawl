Alta California ("Upper California") was a province of the Spanish Empire and subsequently a territory of Mexico that encompassed roughly the present-day U.S. state of California, together with adjacent parts of the modern Southwest. It was distinguished from Baja California ("Lower California"), the peninsula to the south; the two Californias were formally separated in 1804.

History

Although the coast had been explored by Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo as early as 1542, permanent Spanish settlement began only in 1769, when the "Sacred Expedition" of Gaspar de Portolá and Junípero Serra founded the presidio and mission of San Diego. Over the following decades, Franciscan missionaries under Serra and his successors built a chain of 21 missions from San Diego to Sonoma, supported by four presidios (military forts) and a few civilian pueblos, including San José and Los Angeles. Native Californians, drawn or compelled into the mission system, suffered catastrophic population decline from disease and disruption.

Following Mexican independence in 1821, Alta California became a Mexican territory. In the 1830s the missions were secularized and their vast lands distributed as ranchos, giving rise to the Californio ranchero society. American immigration increased in the 1840s; during the Mexican–American War, U.S. forces occupied the territory (1846–47), and the Bear Flag Revolt proclaimed a short-lived California Republic. By the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848), Mexico ceded Alta California to the United States — just days after gold was discovered at Sutter's Mill, triggering the California Gold Rush. California became the 31st U.S. state in 1850.
