Military parade

Military parade
Type: Ceremonial display
Elements: March-past, flypast, equipment display
Famous examples: Bastille Day (Paris), Victory Day (Moscow)

A military parade is a formation of soldiers whose movement is restricted by close-order manoeuvring, typically presented for ceremonial purposes, to display military capability, or to commemorate national occasions. Parades may include marching troops, military bands, flags and colours, armoured vehicles, artillery and aircraft flypasts.

Famous parades

Notable annual parades include the Bastille Day military parade in Paris on 14 July — the oldest regular military parade in the world, held since 1880 — Russia's Victory Day parade in Red Square, Moscow, on 9 May, China's National Day parades in Beijing, India's Republic Day parade in New Delhi, and Pakistan Day and North Korea's mass parades in Pyongyang. Britain's Trooping the Colour marks the monarch's official birthday.

Purposes

Military parades serve to honour traditions and fallen soldiers, boost national pride and morale, mark anniversaries, and signal strength to domestic and foreign audiences — a function sometimes described as deterrence or, by critics, intimidation. The scale of equipment displayed can make parades significant sources for open-source intelligence analysts.
