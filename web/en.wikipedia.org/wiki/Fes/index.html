Fes (Arabic: فاس, romanized: Fās; French: Fès) is a city in northern inland Morocco and the capital of the Fès-Meknès administrative region. It is one of Morocco's four imperial cities and is widely regarded as the country's spiritual and cultural capital. Its old medina, Fes el-Bali, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the world's largest surviving medieval urban areas, famed for being largely car-free.

History

Fes was founded on the banks of the Jawhar river by Idris I in 789 and expanded by his son Idris II in 809, receiving waves of Muslim refugees from Córdoba and Kairouan who gave quarters of the city their names (al-Andalusiyyin and al-Qarawiyyin districts). The University of al-Qarawiyyin, founded in 859 by Fatima al-Fihri, is recognized by UNESCO as one of the oldest existing universities in the world. Under the Almoravids the two settlements were unified; the city flourished under the Marinids (13th–15th centuries), who built Fes el-Jdid, the Royal Palace, and celebrated madrasas such as Bou Inania and Al-Attarine. Fes remained a religious, scholarly, and craft center through the Saadian and Alaouite periods, serving as capital at various times until the French protectorate moved the administrative capital to Rabat in 1912.

Landmarks

Sights include the Bab Bou Jeloud gate, the Bou Inania Madrasa, the Al-Qarawiyyin mosque and university, the Al-Attarine Madrasa, the Zaouia of Moulay Idris II, the Royal Palace (Dar al-Makhzen), and the Chouara Tannery, where leather is still dyed in stone vats as in medieval times. The Mellah (historic Jewish quarter) and the Ville Nouvelle (French-built new town) lie outside the old walls.

Culture

Fes is renowned for traditional crafts—embroidery, pottery (blue-and-white Fassi ware), brasswork, and leather—and for Andalusian music. It hosts the annual Fes Festival of World Sacred Music, founded in 1994.
