The panokseon (Korean: 판옥선; hanja: 板屋船, "plank-roofed ship") was the main class of warship used by the Korean navy during the Joseon dynasty, most famously in the Japanese invasions of Korea (1592–1598), where fleets under Admiral Yi Sun-sin used them to great effect.

Design

The panokseon was a large, flat-bottomed vessel built of pine, with a high castle-like superstructure of two or more decks. Its elevated fighting platforms allowed Korean soldiers and archers to fire down on enemy ships and made the vessel difficult to board — a crucial advantage, since Japanese naval tactics centred on grappling and boarding, while the Koreans preferred to stand off and bombard with cannon. Typical examples were roughly 30–36 metres long and carried crews of around 125 men, including rowers, sailors, and fighting men.

Armament

Panokseon mounted multiple cannon of graded calibres — known as cheonja (heaven), jija (earth), hyeonja (black), and hwangja (yellow) chongtong — capable of firing large wooden arrows, iron shot, and fragmentation projectiles at ranges exceeding those of Japanese muskets. This artillery advantage underpinned victories such as the Battle of Hansan Island (1592), won alongside the better-known turtle ships (geobukseon).

Role and limitations

Forming the backbone of the Joseon fleet, panokseon served as command ships and line-of-battle vessels for two centuries after the Imjin War. Their high-sided, flat-bottomed hulls suited Korea's tidal coasts but made them relatively slow and unwieldy in rough open seas.
