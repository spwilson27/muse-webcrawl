Spanish Riding School

LocationHofburg, Vienna, Austria
Founded1572 (first mention)
Building completed1735
Breed usedLipizzaner

The Spanish Riding School (German: Spanische Hofreitschule) is an Austrian institution in Vienna dedicated to the preservation of classical dressage and the training of Lipizzaner horses. It is the oldest riding academy of its kind in the world, and its public performances in the baroque Winter Riding School of the Hofburg Palace are a major tourist attraction. Classical horsemanship at the school was inscribed by UNESCO on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2015.

History

The school takes its name from the Spanish horses that formed the foundation of the Lipizzaner breed, established at the imperial stud at Lipica (in present-day Slovenia) in 1580. A riding school at the Vienna court was first documented in 1572; the Winter Riding School itself was commissioned by Emperor Charles VI and built by Joseph Emanuel Fischer von Erlach between 1729 and 1735. The institution survived the fall of the Habsburg monarchy in 1918, and at the end of the Second World War the stallions were evacuated to Upper Austria; their protection in 1945, with the involvement of the U.S. General George S. Patton, was later dramatized in the Disney film Miracle of the White Stallions.

Training and performances

Riders (Bereiter) train for many years, first on experienced school horses and then bringing on young stallions through work on the long rein, work in hand, and the "airs above the ground" — the levade, courbette, and capriole — derived from the haute école of classical equitation. The stallions are bred at the Piber Federal Stud in Styria. In performances the riders wear traditional brown tailcoats, buckskin breeches, and bicorne hats.
