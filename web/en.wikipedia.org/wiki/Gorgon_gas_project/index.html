The Gorgon gas project is a multi-decadal liquefied natural gas (LNG) project in Western Australia involving the development of the Greater Gorgon gas fields, subsea gas production facilities and a liquefaction plant on Barrow Island. It includes a domestic gas component. The project is operated by Chevron and is one of the world's largest natural gas developments.

Development

The Greater Gorgon gas fields, located offshore in the Carnarvon Basin, contain an estimated 40 trillion cubic feet of gas. The project comprises three LNG trains with a combined capacity of 15.6 million tonnes per annum, together with a domestic gas plant supplying the Western Australian market. Construction began in 2009, and the first LNG cargo was shipped in March 2016.

Carbon capture and storage

Gorgon includes one of the world's largest carbon capture and storage (CCS) systems, designed to inject reservoir carbon dioxide into a deep formation beneath Barrow Island, reducing the project's emissions. The CCS system began operation in 2019 after delays.

Environment

Barrow Island is a Class A nature reserve, and the project operates under strict quarantine and environmental conditions to protect its fauna, including flatback turtles that nest on the island's beaches.
