Real Valladolid Club de Fútbol, S.A.D. is a Spanish professional football club based in Valladolid, Castile and León. Founded in 1928 from the merger of Real Unión Deportiva and Club Deportivo Español, the club plays in purple and white striped shirts and has spent much of its history moving between La Liga and the Segunda División, earning a reputation as a quintessential "yo-yo club".

History

Valladolid first reached La Liga in 1948 and reached the Copa del Rey final in 1950, losing to Athletic Bilbao. The club's most successful modern period came in the 1980s and 1990s, including winning the Copa de la Liga in 1984 and qualifying for European competition. Its best league finish is fourth place, achieved in 1962–63. In the 1990s the club, under Colombian and Spanish managers, developed a distinctive possession-based style associated with players such as Fernando Hierro, who began his career at the club.

Recent years

In September 2018 former Brazilian striker Ronaldo Nazário became the club's majority shareholder and president. Valladolid were relegated from La Liga in 2021, promoted again in 2022, relegated in 2023 and promoted once more in 2024, continuing their oscillation between the top two divisions.

Stadium

The club plays at the Estadio José Zorrilla, opened in 1982 for the FIFA World Cup, named after the Valladolid-born poet José Zorrilla. The stadium is known for its cold, windy conditions in winter.

Honours

  • Copa de la Liga: 1984
  • Segunda División championships: several, including 1947–48, 1958–59 and 2006–07
  • Copa del Rey runners-up: 1949–50, 1988–89
