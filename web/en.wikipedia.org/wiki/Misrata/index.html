Misrata (Arabic: مصراتة) is a city in northwestern Libya, on the Mediterranean coast about 187 km (116 mi) east of Tripoli. It is the capital of the Misrata District and Libya's third-largest city. Misrata is the country's principal commercial port city, home to the Libyan Iron and Steel Company, one of the largest steelmakers in North Africa, and to a free trade zone around its deep-water port at Qasr Ahmad.

History

The area has been settled since antiquity, near the Roman-era site of Cephalae, and Misrata developed as a caravan and maritime trading post. Under Ottoman and then Italian colonial rule it remained a regional market town. After Libyan independence in 1951 it grew rapidly as an industrial and commercial centre with a reputation for an entrepreneurial merchant class.

The 2011 siege

Misrata became internationally known during the Libyan Civil War of 2011, when it rose against Muammar Gaddafi's government and endured a brutal three-month siege by regime forces, who shelled the city and mined its port. Supplied by sea and supported by NATO air strikes, Misratan brigades held the city and later played a major role in the war, including the 2016 campaign that drove the Islamic State from Sirte. The city's powerful militias have remained central actors in Libya's politics and conflicts since.

Economy

Besides steel, the economy rests on the port, textiles, food processing and trade. Misrata University is among Libya's larger universities, and the city has its own international airport.
