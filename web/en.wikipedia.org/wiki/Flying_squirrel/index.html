Flying squirrel

Flying squirrels (tribe Pteromyini) are a group of about 50 species of squirrels that glide through the air using a furry membrane, the patagium, stretched between their limbs; they do not truly fly like bats or birds.

Behaviour

Nocturnal and arboreal, flying squirrels launch from trees, steer with their limbs and flattened tail, and can glide over 100 metres in the largest species, the woolly flying squirrel of the Himalayas.

In North America the northern and southern flying squirrels are common; southern flying squirrels fluoresce pink under ultraviolet light.

Ecology

They eat nuts, seeds, fungi, fruit, and insects, and often nest in tree cavities, sometimes communal in winter.
