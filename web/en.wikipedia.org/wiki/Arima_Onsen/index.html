Arima Onsen (有馬温泉) is an onsen (hot spring) resort town in Kita-ku, Kobe, Hyōgo Prefecture, Japan, nestled in a valley north of Mount Rokkō. It is one of Japan's oldest hot spring resorts, counted among the "Three Ancient Springs" (Nihon San Ko-yu) alongside Dōgo Onsen and Shirahama Onsen, and its springs are mentioned in the Nihon Shoki and the Man'yōshū.

Waters

Arima is unusual for having two chemically distinct types of spring water emerging close together:

  • Kinsen ("gold spring") — iron- and salt-rich water that oxidises to a reddish-brown, cloudy gold colour; noted for warmth retention and said to help with neuralgia and poor circulation.
  • Ginsen ("silver spring") — clear water containing radium (radon) and carbonate; said to aid recovery and circulation.

History

According to legend, the springs were discovered by the gods Ōnamuchi and Sukunahikona. The Buddhist monk Gyōki is said to have founded a temple at Arima in 724 after a dream. The warlord Toyotomi Hideyoshi, a devoted visitor, rebuilt the resort after an earthquake in 1596 and developed the tea ceremony there with his tea master Sen no Rikyū. During the Edo period Arima ranked first on the official list of hot springs, and the town flourished under direct shogunal administration.

Today

The town retains narrow lanes of ryokan inns, souvenir shops selling its famous tansan senbei (carbonated rice crackers, made using the carbonated spring water), and public bathhouses including Kin no Yu and Gin no Yu. Arima is reached from Kobe by the Arima Line and ropeway over Mount Rokkō.
