United States Virgin Islands

USVI
Capital: Charlotte Amalie
Largest islands: Saint Croix, Saint Thomas, Saint John
Status: Unincorporated U.S. territory

The United States Virgin Islands (officially the Virgin Islands of the United States) are a group of Caribbean islands and an unincorporated and organized territory of the United States. The islands are geographically part of the Virgin Islands archipelago in the Leeward Islands of the Lesser Antilles.

Geography

The main islands are Saint Thomas, Saint John, and Saint Croix, along with the smaller Water Island and numerous islets. Much of Saint John is protected as Virgin Islands National Park. The capital, Charlotte Amalie, on Saint Thomas, is a major cruise ship port.

History

The islands were colonized by Denmark from the 17th century as the Danish West Indies, with an economy based on sugar plantations worked by enslaved Africans. The United States purchased the islands from Denmark in 1917 for $25 million in gold, fearing German submarine bases, and Transfer Day is still commemorated on March 31. Residents were granted U.S. citizenship in 1927.

Government and economy

The territory elects a governor and a 15-member unicameral legislature, and sends a non-voting delegate to the U.S. House of Representatives. Tourism is the primary economic activity, supplemented by rum production and petroleum transshipment on Saint Croix.
