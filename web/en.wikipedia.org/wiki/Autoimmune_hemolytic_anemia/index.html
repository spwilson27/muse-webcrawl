Autoimmune hemolytic anemia

Autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA) is a rare autoimmune disorder in which autoantibodies directed against the body's own red blood cells cause their premature destruction (hemolysis), leading to anemia.

Classification

AIHA is divided by the temperature at which the autoantibody is most active: warm AIHA, mediated usually by IgG antibodies reactive at 37 °C, and cold agglutinin disease, mediated by IgM antibodies reactive at low temperatures that fix complement. Mixed types and paroxysmal cold hemoglobinuria also occur.

Signs and diagnosis

Symptoms include fatigue, pallor, jaundice and splenomegaly; laboratory findings include low hemoglobin, raised reticulocytes, LDH and bilirubin, low haptoglobin, spherocytes on the blood film, and a positive direct antiglobulin (Coombs) test. AIHA may be primary (idiopathic) or secondary to lymphoproliferative disorders, infections, drugs or other autoimmune diseases such as systemic lupus erythematosus.

Treatment

First-line treatment for warm AIHA is corticosteroids; rituximab and splenectomy are used for relapsed or refractory disease. Cold agglutinin disease is managed with cold avoidance, rituximab-based regimens and complement inhibitors such as sutimlimab.
