Western Desert language
Region:Western Desert of Australia (WA, SA, NT)
Family:Pama–Nyungan → Wati
Speakers:Roughly 7,000–10,000 (various dialects)
Major dialects:Pitjantjatjara, Yankunytjatjara, Ngaanyatjarra, Pintupi, Martu Wangka, Luritja

The Western Desert language (or Wati) is a dialect cluster of Australian Aboriginal languages spoken across the Western Desert region of Australia — the largest language grouping on the continent by area. Its named dialects, including Pitjantjatjara, Yankunytjatjara, Ngaanyatjarra and Pintupi, are largely mutually intelligible.

Dialects

Dialect names often derive from their word for "this" or from distinctive vocabulary: Pitjantjatjara, for instance, has pitjantja ("coming/going") in its verb forms. The continuum covers communities from the Warburton ranges to Balgo and from Uluru to the Great Victoria Desert — an area of well over 600,000 km².

Features

Like most Australian languages, Western Desert varieties have three vowels, no fricatives, ergative case marking, and free word order. Pitjantjatjara was one of the first Aboriginal languages to be written down extensively and is used in Bible translation, broadcasting and school programmes.

Status and culture

The Western Desert bloc is also a cultural region: its people share Dreaming (Tjukurpa) law, ceremony and kinship, and the area produced the Papunya Tula art movement, which launched the Western Desert acrylic painting style now celebrated worldwide. The language group retains more first-language speakers than most Australian Aboriginal languages, though transmission varies by community.
