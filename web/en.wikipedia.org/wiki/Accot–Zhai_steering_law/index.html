Accot–Zhai steering law

The Accot–Zhai steering law is a predictive model in human-computer interaction that describes the time required for a user to steer a pointer through a constrained path, such as a tunnel or a cascading menu. It was proposed by Johnny Accot and Shumin Zhai in 1997 as a generalisation of Fitts's law to steering tasks.

Formulation

For a straight tunnel of length A and width W, the average movement time MT is given by MT = a + b (A / W), where a and b are empirically determined constants. For curved or more complex paths, the law takes an integral form in which the index of difficulty accumulates the ratio of path length elements to local tunnel width along the trajectory.

Applications

The steering law is widely used to model navigation through hierarchical menus, drawing and handwriting input, driving simulations, and teleoperation tasks. It complements Fitts's law, which models discrete pointing at targets rather than continuous path following.
