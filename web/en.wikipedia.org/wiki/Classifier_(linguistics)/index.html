Classifier (linguistics)

A classifier, in linguistics, is a word or affix that accompanies a noun in certain grammatical contexts, categorising the noun according to some salient property — typically its shape, animacy, function, or social status. The best-known type is the numeral classifier, required in languages such as Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, Thai, Malay, and Bengali when a noun is counted: rather than "three books", Mandarin uses the pattern numeral + classifier + noun, as in sān běn shū ("three [volume-classifier] book"). Classifiers typically classify nouns by shape (long, flat, round objects), animacy (people, animals), arrangement, or function (vehicles, machines), and most classifier languages have dozens, sometimes hundreds, of them.

Types of classifiers

  • Numeral classifiers appear with numbers and demonstratives, as in East and Southeast Asian languages.
  • Noun classifiers accompany nouns regardless of counting and form a looser association; systems of this kind occur in some Australian Aboriginal and Amazonian languages.
  • Possessive classifiers, common in Austronesian languages, classify the relationship between possessor and possessed — for example, distinguishing things possessed for eating, drinking, or general use in Fijian or Hawaiian.
  • Verbal classifiers incorporate the classification of an argument into the verb itself; they are also central to signed languages, where "classifier constructions" use handshapes representing classes of objects (such as vehicles or upright persons) moving and located in signing space.

Properties and distribution

Classifiers are often historically derived from ordinary nouns: the Mandarin general classifier gè, for example, originally referred to bamboo stems, while Japanese hon (for long cylindrical objects) derives from a word for a tree trunk or stem. Many languages use a general classifier for nouns that lack a specific one. Classifier systems are a strong areal feature of mainland Southeast Asia and East Asia, and also occur in Mesoamerican languages such as Tzotzil and in many languages of West Africa and the Pacific. Linguists debate whether classifier languages' nouns are inherently uncountable "mass-like" entities that classifiers individualise, or whether classifiers are a largely grammatical agreement device. In the study of cognition, classifier systems have been used to test whether language influences how speakers categorise objects.
