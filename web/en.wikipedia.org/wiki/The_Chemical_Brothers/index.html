The Chemical Brothers

The Chemical Brothers are an English electronic music duo formed by Ed Simons and Tom Rowlands in Manchester in 1989. They were among the pioneers, alongside the Prodigy, Fatboy Slim, and the Crystal Method, of the big beat genre in the 1990s, and are regarded as one of the most successful acts in electronic music history, with six UK number-one albums and multiple Grammy Awards. They are equally renowned for their spectacular live shows, developed with visual collaborators including Adam Smith.

History

Rowlands and Simons met studying history at the University of Manchester and began DJing together, taking the name the Dust Brothers in homage to the American production duo. Their early remixes and tracks such as "Song to the Siren" (1992) built a following; legal pressure from their namesakes forced a change to the Chemical Brothers. The debut album Exit Planet Dust (1995) fused hip-hop breaks, techno, and psychedelia. Dig Your Own Hole (1997) reached number one in the UK and produced the hits "Setting Sun" (with Noel Gallagher), "Block Rockin' Beats", and "The Private Psychedelic Reel". Surrender (1999) moved toward house music and yielded "Hey Boy Hey Girl" and "Let Forever Be".

Later albums include Come with Us (2002), Push the Button (2005) — featuring "Galvanize", a Grammy winner — We Are the Night (2007), Further (2010), Born in the Echoes (2015), No Geography (2019), which won two Grammy Awards, and For That Beautiful Feeling (2023). Their catalogue also includes film soundtracks and collaborations with artists from Beth Orton and Richard Ashcroft to Q-Tip and Beck.

Style and influence

The duo's sound combines heavy sampled breakbeats, distorted basslines, siren-like synths, and guest vocalists, arranged with a rock band's dynamics. With the Prodigy and Fatboy Slim they carried electronic dance music into arenas and the mainstream charts in the 1990s, and their live productions set a benchmark for audio-visual performance.
