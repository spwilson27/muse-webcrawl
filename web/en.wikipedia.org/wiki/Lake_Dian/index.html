Lake Dian (Chinese: 滇池; pinyin: Diān Chí), also known as Kunming Lake, is a large freshwater lake on the Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau immediately south of Kunming, the capital of Yunnan Province, China. It is the largest lake in Yunnan and one of the largest freshwater lakes in China, covering about 300 square kilometres.

Geography and history

The lake sits at about 1,886 metres above sea level and is overlooked by the Western Hills (Xishan) on its western shore. Its name is associated with the ancient Dian Kingdom, a Bronze Age culture centred on the lake's shores in the first millennium BC, famous for its elaborate bronze work, which was absorbed into the Han dynasty in the 1st century BC. The outflow of the lake feeds into the Jinsha River system.

Environment

Rapid urbanisation and industrial growth around Kunming made Lake Dian one of China's most severely polluted major lakes in the late twentieth century, with heavy nutrient loading, algal blooms, and the loss of much of its native aquatic life, including endemic fish and the lake's once-noted biodiversity. Large-scale and costly remediation programmes — wastewater treatment, wetland restoration around the shore, and diversion of cleaner water — have been under way since the 1990s, with gradual improvements in water quality reported.
