Gulf Cartel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Gulf Cartel (Spanish: Cártel del Golfo, CDG) is a criminal syndicate and drug cartel based in Matamoros, Tamaulipas, Mexico, that primarily smuggles cocaine and marijuana into the United States across the lower Rio Grande valley. Founded in the 1930s as a liquor- and contraband-smuggling group, it was for decades one of Mexico's two dominant cartels, and is notorious for creating Los Zetas — originally its paramilitary enforcement arm — from deserters of the Mexican special forces.

History

The organisation traces its origins to Juan Nepomuceno Guerra, who smuggled whisky into Texas during Prohibition, and his nephew Juan García Ábrego, who in the 1980s built a cocaine-trafficking empire in partnership with Colombian suppliers and protected by corrupt officials. García Ábrego, the first trafficker placed on the FBI's Ten Most Wanted list, was arrested in 1996 and extradited to the United States.

The Zetas era and decline

His successor, Osiel Cárdenas Guillén, recruited the special-forces deserters who became Los Zetas as his private army. After Cárdenas's arrest in 2003 and extradition in 2007, friction between the cartel and the Zetas erupted into open war in 2010, devastating Tamaulipas and Nuevo León. The Gulf Cartel itself then splintered into rival factions (the Metros and the Rojos, among others), losing much of its territory and importance while remaining active in Matamoros, Reynosa and the border corridor.

Activities

Beyond drugs, the cartel and its factions are involved in migrant smuggling, extortion, kidnapping and fuel theft. The US Treasury has designated its leaders under the Kingpin Act, and successive capos — including Jorge Eduardo Costilla Sánchez ("El Coss", arrested 2012) — have been captured or killed.
