Wiggler (synchrotron)

A wiggler is an insertion device in a synchrotron or free-electron laser consisting of a periodic array of magnets that forces a beam of charged particles, usually electrons, to undergo strong sinusoidal oscillations, causing them to emit intense synchrotron radiation. Wigglers are distinguished from undulators by their stronger magnetic fields and larger deflection of the particle trajectory, described by a large dimensionless parameter K (much greater than 1).

A wiggler's alternating magnetic field bends the electron beam into a sinusoidal path.

Characteristics

Because the deflection in a wiggler is large, the radiation emitted at each bend adds incoherently, producing a broad, continuous spectrum similar to that of a bending magnet but with an intensity enhanced roughly in proportion to twice the number of magnetic poles. Wigglers shift and extend the critical energy of the emitted spectrum to higher photon energies compared with ordinary bending magnets, making them valuable sources of hard X-rays for experiments at synchrotron light sources.

Applications

Wigglers are used to generate high-flux X-ray beams for materials science, protein crystallography, and medical imaging research. Wavelength-shifter and multipole wiggler variants are also used to tailor the radiation spectrum and, in damping rings, to increase radiation damping of particle beams.
