Shamakhi (Azerbaijani: Şamaxı) is a city in Azerbaijan and the administrative centre of the Shamakhi District. The city lies in the eastern foothills of the Greater Caucasus, about 122 km west of Baku. It is famous as the former capital of the kingdom of the Shirvanshahs, for its winemaking tradition, and as the birthplace of many Azerbaijani poets.

History

Shamakhi was an ancient city, mentioned by the Greco-Roman geographer Ptolemy. From the 9th to the 16th centuries it was the capital of the Shirvan kingdom ruled by the Shirvanshahs. It was an important city on the Silk Road and a centre of silk production and carpet weaving. The city was repeatedly devastated by earthquakes, notably in 1667, 1859, and 1902; after the 1859 quake the provincial capital was moved to Baku. In the 18th century it was the capital of the Shamakhi Khanate.

Climate

Shamakhi has a humid subtropical to semi-arid climate with warm summers and cool winters, influenced by its mountain foothill location.

Culture

The city is the birthplace of poets such as Sabir, Seyid Azim Shirvani, and Imadaddin Nasimi is associated with the region. The Juma Mosque of Shamakhi, originally founded in 743–744, is one of the oldest mosques in the Caucasus. The Shamakhi Astrophysical Observatory, founded in 1960 in nearby Pirqulu, is a major scientific institution of Azerbaijan.

Economy

Viticulture and winemaking, carpet weaving, and agriculture form the basis of the local economy.
