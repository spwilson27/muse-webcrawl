Delacorte Theater

The Delacorte Theater is an open-air amphitheater in Central Park, Manhattan, New York City, home to the Public Theater's Shakespeare in the Park summer festival. Opened in 1962, the theater seats 1,800 and overlooks Turtle Pond with Belvedere Castle visible beyond the stage. Admission to its productions is free, with tickets distributed on the day of performance.

History

Producer Joseph Papp began presenting free Shakespeare with his New York Shakespeare Festival in the 1950s, fighting a celebrated legal battle with parks commissioner Robert Moses over the right to perform in Central Park. Philanthropist George T. Delacorte Jr. funded construction of a permanent theater, which opened on June 18, 1962 with The Merchant of Venice starring George C. Scott. Since then the Delacorte has hosted more than 150 productions and some five million audience members.

Productions and legacy

The Delacorte's stages have launched or showcased productions that transferred to Broadway and film, including the original 1967 "Naked" Hair workshops era of the Public, The Pirates of Penzance (1980), and celebrated revivals featuring actors such as Meryl Streep, Morgan Freeman, Al Pacino, Anne Hathaway, and Lupita Nyong'o. The theater underwent an extensive renovation in the 2020s, reopening with improved accessibility, backstage facilities, and a restored façade. Free distribution now combines in-person queues — a New York ritual — with digital lotteries.
