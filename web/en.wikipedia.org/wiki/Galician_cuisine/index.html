Galician cuisine

Galician cuisine is the traditional cooking of Galicia, an autonomous community in northwestern Spain. It is renowned throughout Spain for the quality of its seafood, drawn from the Atlantic Ocean and the region's rías (flooded river valleys), as well as for hearty inland dishes based on pork, potatoes and greens.

Signature dishes

The most famous Galician dish is pulpo a la gallega (Galician-style octopus), in which tender boiled octopus is cut into slices, dressed with olive oil, coarse salt and smoked paprika (pimentón), and traditionally served on a wooden board with boiled potatoes (cachelos) and crusty bread. It is the centrepiece of the O Carballiño octopus festival.

Other emblematic dishes include:

  • Empanada gallega — a large savoury pie filled with tuna, cod with raisins, pork, cockles or scallops.
  • Caldo gallego — a broth of turnip greens (grelos), potatoes, beans and pork fat.
  • Lacón con grelos — cured pork shoulder served with turnip tops, potatoes and chorizo, typical of Carnival.
  • Zamburiñas and vieiras — variegated and queen scallops, baked in their shells with breadcrumbs.
  • Percebes — gooseneck barnacles harvested from wave-battered rocks.
  • Tarta de Santiago — an almond cake dusted with icing sugar in the shape of the Cross of Saint James.

Bread and cheese

Pan gallego, a rustic crusty loaf often made with corn or rye mixed with wheat, accompanies every meal. The best-known cheese is queso tetilla, a soft cow's-milk cheese moulded into a distinctive conical, breast-like shape.

Wines

Galicia produces celebrated white wines, above all Albariño from Rías Baixas, along with Godello, Treixadura and Ribeira Sacra reds based on Mencía. The fiery grape spirit orujo is the base of the ritual queimada, a punch set alight while an incantation (conxuro) against evil spirits is read.
