J. Robert Oppenheimer (22 April 1904 – 18 February 1967) was an American theoretical physicist and the director of the Los Alamos Laboratory during the Manhattan Project, often called the 'father of the atomic bomb'. Watching the Trinity test on 16 July 1945, he later recalled the Bhagavad Gita line: 'Now I am become Death, the destroyer of worlds.'

Oppenheimer made research contributions to quantum mechanics (the Born–Oppenheimer approximation, early black-hole theory with the Oppenheimer–Volkoff limit) and built Berkeley into a leading physics school. After the war he chaired the Atomic Energy Commission's advisory committee and opposed the crash programme for the hydrogen bomb; in 1954, amid McCarthy-era suspicion of his pre-war left-wing associations, his security clearance was revoked after a controversial hearing — a decision the US Department of Energy formally vacated in 2022. He spent his final years directing the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton.
