PUMA (robot)

The PUMA (Programmable Universal Machine for Assembly, or Programmable Universal Manipulation Arm) is an industrial robotic arm developed by Victor Scheinman at Unimation in the late 1970s, initially for General Motors.

Design

The PUMA was one of the first widely used articulated robot arms, with a serial chain of revolute joints modelled on the human arm — waist, shoulder, elbow and wrist. Models such as the PUMA 560, driven by electric servo motors, became standard equipment in robotics research laboratories and in university teaching, as well as in light assembly work.

Significance

The PUMA 560 is famous in medical robotics history: in 1985 a PUMA 560 was used to position a needle for a brain biopsy under CT guidance, one of the first robot-assisted surgical procedures. PUMA robots remained common in research for decades, and the arm's kinematics are a standard textbook example.
