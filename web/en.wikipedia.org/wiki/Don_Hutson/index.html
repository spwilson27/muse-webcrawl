Donald Montgomery Hutson (January 31, 1913 – June 26, 1997) was an American professional football player and coach for the Green Bay Packers of the National Football League (NFL). He is widely regarded as the first modern wide receiver and one of the greatest receivers in NFL history.

Career

After starring at the University of Alabama, Hutson joined the Packers in 1935 and played his entire 11-season career in Green Bay under Curly Lambeau. In an era of low-scoring, run-dominated football, he led the NFL in receptions eight times, receiving yards seven times and touchdown catches nine times — records central to his fame. He retired holding 18 NFL records, including 488 receptions and 99 touchdown catches, the latter standing for 44 years. He also played safety, intercepting 30 passes, and kicked extra points and field goals.

Honours

Hutson was a charter member of the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1963, and the Packers' indoor practice facility is named the Don Hutson Center.
