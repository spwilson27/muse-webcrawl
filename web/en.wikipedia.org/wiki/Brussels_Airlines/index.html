Brussels Airlines

Brussels Airlines
IATA: SN · ICAO: BEL · Callsign: BEELINE
Founded: 7 November 2006
Hub: Brussels Airport
Alliance: Star Alliance
Parent: Lufthansa Group
Headquarters: Diegem, Machelen, Belgium

Brussels Airlines is the flag carrier and largest airline of Belgium, based and headquartered at Brussels Airport. It operates to over 100 destinations in Europe, North America and Africa and also offers charter services, maintenance and crew training. It is a member of the Star Alliance and is a subsidiary of the Lufthansa Group.

History

Brussels Airlines was created following the merger of SN Brussels Airlines (itself the successor to Sabena, Belgium's former national carrier, which ceased operations in 2001) and Virgin Express. The new airline was announced in 2006 and began operations on 25 March 2007 under the Brussels Airlines name, adopting the IATA code SN inherited from Sabena.

In 2008 Lufthansa announced it would acquire a 45% stake in the airline, with an option to acquire the remainder; the acquisition was completed in stages, and from January 2017 Brussels Airlines became a wholly owned subsidiary of the Lufthansa Group. It was integrated into the Eurowings Group for a time before being repositioned as Lufthansa Group's African specialist and a network carrier in its own right.

Destinations and fleet

The airline is known for its extensive network in sub-Saharan Africa, inherited from Sabena's historical network, serving destinations such as Kinshasa, Dakar, Abidjan, Douala, Entebbe and Kigali. Its long-haul fleet consists of Airbus A330 aircraft, while short- and medium-haul services use Airbus A319, A320 and A320neo aircraft. During the summer season it also operates wet-leased regional aircraft on thinner European routes.

Branding

In November 2021 the airline unveiled a refreshed brand identity with a new logo of dotted circles forming a "b". Its aircraft include special-liveried "Belgian Icons" planes celebrating Belgian culture, including liveries dedicated to Tintin, the Smurfs, Pieter Bruegel the Elder, René Magritte and the Tomorrowland festival.
