Coastal taipan

The coastal taipan (Oxyuranus scutellatus) is a highly venomous snake native to coastal northern and eastern Australia and the island of New Guinea. It is one of the largest venomous snakes in Australia, commonly reaching about 2 metres in length.

Behaviour and venom

The coastal taipan is generally shy but will defend itself vigorously if cornered, delivering multiple rapid bites. Its venom contains potent neurotoxins and procoagulants; before a specific antivenom was developed in 1955, bites were nearly always fatal. It hunts mammals in cane fields, grasslands and open forest.
