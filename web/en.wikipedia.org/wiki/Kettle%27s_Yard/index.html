Kettle's Yard

Kettle's Yard is an art gallery and former home in Cambridge, England, associated with the University of Cambridge. It is renowned for its intimate domestic atmosphere and for its collection of modern British and European art, arranged not as a conventional museum but as a living space in which paintings, sculptures, furniture and natural objects are placed in conversation with one another.

History

The house was created between 1956 and 1970 by Jim Ede, a former curator at the Tate Gallery, and his wife Helen. Ede, who had befriended many of the artists of the British avant-garde, converted a group of cottages into a home where works by artists such as Ben Nicholson, Barbara Hepworth, Henry Moore, Joan Miró and Henri Gaudier-Brzeska were displayed alongside pebbles, shells, plants and simple furniture. Students from the university were welcomed to visit in the afternoons, a tradition that shaped the gallery's character. In 1966 the Edes gave the house and its collection to the University of Cambridge, and a modern gallery wing was added in 1970.

The collection

The permanent display remains much as Jim Ede left it, with works carefully positioned in relation to light and space. A major redevelopment completed in 2018 added new gallery and education spaces while preserving the original house.
