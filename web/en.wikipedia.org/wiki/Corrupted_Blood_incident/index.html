Corrupted Blood incident

The Corrupted Blood incident was a virtual plague that affected the massively multiplayer online role-playing game World of Warcraft in September 2005. Caused by an unintended game mechanic, the incident spread a damaging debuff across the game world, killing large numbers of characters and disrupting play for days; it later attracted research interest as a model for the study of real-world epidemics.

The incident

On 13 September 2005, Blizzard Entertainment introduced the raid dungeon Zul'Gurub. Its final boss, Hakkar the Soulflayer, cast "Corrupted Blood," a debuff that drained health over time and could be passed to nearby characters — intended to be confined to the encounter. However, players discovered the debuff could be carried out of the dungeon on hunter pets and warlock minions, which could be dismissed and resummoned in crowded cities. Released in capitals such as Ironforge and Orgrimmar, the plague spread rapidly between characters, non-player characters (who could carry but not die from it) and pets.

High-level characters survived the damage, but low-level characters died within seconds, and city streets were littered with skeletons. Players reacted in recognisably human ways: some fled to the countryside, some with healing abilities volunteered to cure the infected, and others deliberately spread the disease. Blizzard attempted quarantines and hotfixes, and finally reset the servers and fixed the pet mechanic to end the outbreak.

Research significance

Epidemiologists, notably Eric Lofgren and Nina Fefferman, proposed that virtual outbreaks like Corrupted Blood could inform models of human behaviour during real pandemics, capturing variables — curiosity, altruism, malicious spread, flight — that mathematical models usually omit. The incident has been compared with later real-world outbreaks and is frequently cited in discussions of emergent behaviour in virtual worlds and of the ethics of using games as research environments. A later, deliberate in-game plague event, the 2008 "Zombie Plague" preceding Wrath of the Lich King, echoed the incident by design.
