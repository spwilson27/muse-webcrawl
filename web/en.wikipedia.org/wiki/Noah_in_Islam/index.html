Noah in Islam

Noah, known as Nūḥ (Arabic: نُوْحٌ) in Islam, is recognised as a prophet and messenger of God. He is one of the five prophets of firm resolve (ulu al-azm) and the subject of the Quran's 71st chapter, Surah Nuh.

Quranic account

According to the Quran, Noah was sent to warn his people against idolatry and wickedness. He preached for 950 years, but most rejected him and mocked his building of the Ark. God then sent the Flood, from which Noah, his family and the believers — along with pairs of animals — were saved. One of Noah's sons refused to board and drowned, and Noah's plea for him is answered by the teaching that kinship is by faith, not blood.

Role in Islamic tradition

Noah is regarded as the second father of mankind, since humanity after the Flood descends from his surviving sons. Traditions identify the Ark's resting place with Mount Judi (identified with a mountain near Cizre in modern Turkey). Numerous shrines claimed as Noah's tomb exist across the Middle East.
