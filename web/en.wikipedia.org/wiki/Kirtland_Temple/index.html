Kirtland Temple

[Image placeholder: the Kirtland Temple, Kirtland, Ohio]

The Kirtland Temple is a historic building in Kirtland, Ohio, built between 1833 and 1836 by members of the Latter Day Saint movement under the direction of Joseph Smith. It was the first temple built by the movement and served as a house of worship, school, and administrative centre rather than as a temple in the later Latter-day Saint sense of a place for ordinances. It is a National Historic Landmark.

Construction and dedication

The Latter Day Saints, gathered at Kirtland after 1831, were commanded in revelation to build a "house of the Lord". Construction began in 1833 despite the poverty of the small community; men quarried stone and worked on the walls while women made clothing and curtains. The exterior combined elements of Greek Revival and Gothic Revival styles, with stucco mixed with crushed glass that gave the walls a distinctive lustre. The temple was dedicated on 27 March 1836, in a ceremony at which Joseph Smith offered the dedicatory prayer now canonised as Doctrine and Covenants section 109. A week later, on 3 April 1836, Smith and Oliver Cowdery reported visions in the temple, including of Jesus Christ, Moses, Elias, and Elijah (Doctrine and Covenants 110).

Ownership and preservation

After most Latter Day Saints left Ohio in 1838, ownership of the temple was disputed. The Reorganized Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints (now Community of Christ) obtained clear title in the late nineteenth century and maintained the building as a historic site and place of worship for over a century. In March 2024, Community of Christ sold the Kirtland Temple to the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints as part of a larger transfer of historic sites and manuscripts; the temple remains open to the public for guided tours.
