Northeast Frontier Railway zone

The Northeast Frontier Railway (abbreviated NFR) is one of the zones of Indian Railways, responsible for rail operations in the north-eastern states of India and parts of West Bengal and Bihar. Its headquarters are at Maligaon in Guwahati, Assam. The zone was formed on 15 January 1958, after the reorganisation of the former North Eastern Railway, to serve the strategically important and geographically difficult frontier region beyond the narrow Siliguri Corridor.

Organisation

The zone is divided into five divisions: Alipurduar, Katihar, Rangiya, Lumding, and Tinsukia. It operates a network of roughly 4,000 route kilometres serving Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, Meghalaya, and Sikkim—terrain that includes the Brahmaputra floodplains and steep hill sections prone to landslides and monsoon washouts.

Development

Historically much of the region was served by metre-gauge lines; large-scale gauge conversion to broad gauge has connected all the north-eastern state capitals progressively since the 1990s, with Agartala reached in 2016 and new lines advancing towards Aizawl, Imphal, Kohima, and Gangtok. Major engineering works include the Bogibeel Bridge over the Brahmaputra, opened in 2018. The zone also operates the UNESCO-listed Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, a narrow-gauge mountain line, in its Katihar division. See also Indian Railways, Guwahati, and Darjeeling Himalayan Railway.
