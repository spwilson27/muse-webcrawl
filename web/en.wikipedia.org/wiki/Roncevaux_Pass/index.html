Roncevaux Pass

The Roncevaux Pass (Basque: Ibañeta; Spanish: Puerto de Ibañeta) is a high mountain pass in the Pyrenees on the border between France and Spain, near the village of Roncevaux (Roncesvalles) in Navarre. At about 1,057 metres (3,468 ft) above sea level, it has been a principal route across the western Pyrenees since antiquity.

History

The pass was used by Roman roads connecting Gaul with Hispania and later became a main crossing for pilgrims travelling the French Way of the Camino de Santiago to Santiago de Compostela.

It is celebrated above all as the setting of the Battle of Roncevaux Pass in 778, in which the rearguard of Charlemagne's army, returning from a campaign in Spain, was ambushed and destroyed by Basque forces. Among the dead was Roland, Charlemagne's paladin, whose legendary last stand — in the later tradition against Saracen armies — became the subject of the Chanson de Roland, one of the most famous of the Old French chansons de geste.

Pilgrimage

The collegiate church and pilgrims' hostel at Roncesvalles, founded in the 12th century, still serve walkers crossing the pass at the start of the Camino Francés. A modern chapel stands at the summit of Ibañeta.
