Automotive engineering

Automotive engineering, along with aerospace engineering and naval architecture, is a branch of vehicle engineering, incorporating elements of mechanical, electrical, electronic, software and safety engineering as applied to the design, manufacture and operation of motorcycles, automobiles and trucks and their respective engineering subsystems. It also includes modification of vehicles.

Disciplines

Automotive engineering covers numerous specialized fields. Powertrain engineers develop engines, transmissions and electric drive units; chassis engineers work on suspension, steering and braking; body engineers design the structure and manage crashworthiness; NVH (noise, vibration and harshness) specialists refine refinement; and electronics and software engineers develop the growing body of control systems, from engine management to driver assistance. Vehicle dynamics, aerodynamics, materials science and manufacturing engineering all play central roles.

Development process

Modern vehicle development follows a systems-engineering V-model, from concept definition through simulation, prototyping and validation testing, with regulatory compliance for safety and emissions verified before production. The shift to electric vehicles has added battery engineering, power electronics and thermal management as core competencies, while software-defined vehicle architectures have made software engineering one of the largest disciplines in the field.
