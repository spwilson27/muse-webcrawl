The River Cocker is a river in the Lake District in North West England, in the county of Cumbria. It gives its name to the town of Cockermouth, where it joins the River Derwent.

Course

The Cocker flows from the outflow of Crummock Water, having earlier passed through Buttermere as the Buttermere Dubs. It flows north through the Lorton Vale, passing the villages of Low Lorton and High Lorton, before reaching Cockermouth, where it joins the River Derwent on the edge of the town centre. The river is a tributary system fed by streams from the fells around Honister Pass, including the waters draining Buttermere and Crummock Water.

Etymology

The name Cocker is of Brittonic origin and is thought to derive from a word meaning "crooked" or "winding", describing the river's course. The same name is borne by other English rivers, including the Cocker in Lancashire.

Flooding

Cockermouth was badly affected by the Cumbria floods of November 2009, when the Cocker and Derwent both burst their banks, inundating the town centre. Further flooding occurred during Storm Desmond in December 2015, after which new flood defences, including a self-closing flood barrier, were completed in the town.
