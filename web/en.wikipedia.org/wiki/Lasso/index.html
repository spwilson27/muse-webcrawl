Lasso

A lasso or lariat is a loop of rope designed to be thrown around a target and tightened when pulled. It is a well-known tool of the North American cowboy, used for catching cattle and horses, and derives from the Spanish la reata and the ranching traditions of Mexico's vaqueros. The word "lasso" comes from the Spanish lazo, meaning a noose or snare.

Construction and use

A lariat is made from stiff rope — traditionally rawhide or hemp, today usually nylon or polyester — so that the loop stays open when thrown. One end is tied in an eyelet or fitted with a metal or rawhide "honda" through which the rope slides to form the loop. The roper swings the loop overhead and casts it over the animal's head, horns, or legs; the other end may be dallied (wrapped) around the saddle horn to secure the catch. Working styles differ between traditions: Texas-style ropers typically use shorter, lighter ropes and tie hard and fast, while the Californio and Great Basin buckaroo tradition favours longer rawhide reatas and dallying.

Rodeo and trick roping

Lassoing is central to rodeo events such as calf roping (tie-down roping), team roping, and steer roping. Trick roping — spinning the rope in flat loops, wedding rings, and other figures around the body — became a popular performance art through Wild West shows, most famously by Will Rogers. A lasso is also carried by the fictional superhero Wonder Woman as the "Lasso of Truth".

Related tools

Analogous looped catching tools exist worldwide, including the Mongolian uurga (a lasso on a long pole) used by herders, and bolas used in South America for hunting and cattle work.
