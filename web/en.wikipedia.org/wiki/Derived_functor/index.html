Derived functor

In mathematics, certain functors may be derived to obtain other functors closely related to the original. This operation, while fairly abstract, unifies a number of constructions in mathematics — for example, the Ext and Tor functors of homological algebra arise as derived functors. The notion comes from the fact that many functors of interest (such as tensor product or Hom) are exact on only one side: a left exact functor fails to preserve surjectivity, and its derived functors measure that failure.

Motivation and construction

Let F be a left exact functor between abelian categories with enough injectives. To derive F, replace an object A by an injective resolution

0 → A → I0 → I1 → I2 → ⋯

apply F to the resolution (deleting A), and take cohomology of the resulting complex. The resulting functors RiF are the right derived functors of F; R0F recovers F itself. Dually, a right exact functor can be derived using projective resolutions, giving left derived functors LiF.

Examples

  • The Ext functors Exti(A, −) are the right derived functors of Hom(A, −).
  • The Tor functors Tori(A, −) are the left derived functors of the tensor product A ⊗ −.
  • Sheaf cohomology is the right derived functor of the global sections functor.
  • Group cohomology is the right derived functor of the invariants functor.

Long exact sequences

The principal application of derived functors is that a short exact sequence of objects gives rise to a long exact sequence involving all the derived functors, allowing computations to propagate through diagrams. In the modern language of derived categories, a derived functor is simply a functor defined on complexes, of which the classical derived functors are the cohomology groups.
