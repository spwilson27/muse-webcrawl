Case mix

Case mix is a term used in health care to describe the diversity, complexity, and severity of the patients treated by a health care provider, hospital, or other organization over a given period. It is used to compare performance and resource use between providers fairly, since an institution treating more complex patients will naturally incur higher costs and longer stays.

Measurement

The best-known measure is the case mix index (CMI), the average relative weight of diagnosis-related groups (DRGs) treated by a hospital under systems such as the United States Medicare prospective payment system. A higher CMI indicates a more complex, resource-intensive patient population. Case-mix adjustment is also central to risk adjustment in outcomes research and hospital report cards.

Applications

  • Setting reimbursement rates under DRG-based payment systems.
  • Benchmarking hospitals on cost, length of stay, and outcomes.
  • Planning staffing, beds, and services.
  • Research on variation in practice and quality of care.

Criticism

Case-mix measures can be gamed through upcoding ("DRG creep"), and incomplete adjustment may penalize providers caring for disadvantaged populations.
