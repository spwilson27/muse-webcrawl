2014 Crimean status referendum

Crimean status referendum
Date: 16 March 2014
Location: Autonomous Republic of Crimea and Sevastopol, Ukraine
Official result: ~97% in favour of joining Russia (official figures)
Recognition: Not recognised by Ukraine or the UN General Assembly

The 2014 Crimean status referendum was a referendum held on 16 March 2014 by the legislature of the Autonomous Republic of Crimea and the city of Sevastopol, following the seizure of the peninsula by unmarked Russian forces during the Russo-Ukrainian crisis that followed the Ukrainian revolution of 2014.

The vote

Voters were offered two choices: joining the Russian Federation, or restoring the 1992 Crimean constitution and Crimea's status within Ukraine. There was no option to maintain the status quo. Official results announced turnout of around 83% in the Autonomous Republic of Crimea with about 97% voting to join Russia.

Legality and reaction

The referendum was held under Russian military occupation, was considered illegal under the Ukrainian constitution, and was boycotted by many Crimean Tatars and pro-Ukrainian residents. The United Nations General Assembly adopted Resolution 68/262 affirming Ukraine's territorial integrity and declaring the referendum invalid. Most countries continue to recognise Crimea as part of Ukraine, though Russia formally annexed the peninsula on 18 March 2014 and administers it as federal territory.
