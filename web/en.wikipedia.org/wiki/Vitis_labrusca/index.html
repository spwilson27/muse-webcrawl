Vitis labrusca

Vitis labrusca, the fox grape, is a species of grape native to eastern North America and is the source of many famous grape cultivars, including Catawba, Concord, Delaware, Isabella, Niagara, and many hybrid grape varieties such as Agawam, Alexander and Onaka. Among the characteristics of this vine species in contrast to the European wine grape Vitis vinifera are its "slip skin" fruit, meaning the skin of the grape berries easily slips off when squeezed, and the presence of tendrils on every node of the cane.

Characteristics

The fox grape has large, rough, dark-green leaves with a pale undersurface covered in fine hairs. Its berries have a distinctive musky, "foxy" flavour — the source of the grape's common name and of the characteristic taste of Concord grape juice and jelly — attributed in part to the compound methyl anthranilate.

History and use

Vitis labrusca was one of the North American grape species used in attempts to combat the phylloxera epidemic that devastated European vineyards in the 19th century, serving as resistant rootstock and in hybridisation programmes. In North America, Concord and related cultivars became the basis of the grape juice and jelly industry, notably that of Welch's.

Unlike V. vinifera, labrusca varieties are comparatively cold-hardy and resistant to many grape diseases, making them well suited to the continental climate of eastern North America.
