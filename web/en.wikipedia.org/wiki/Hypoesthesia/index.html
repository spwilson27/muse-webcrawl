Hypoesthesia

Hypoesthesia (also spelled hypesthesia) is a reduced sense of touch or sensation, or a partial loss of sensitivity to sensory stimuli. It is one of several sensory disturbances that also include anaesthesia (total loss of sensation), hyperesthesia (increased sensitivity) and paresthesia (abnormal sensations such as tingling).

Causes

Hypoesthesia can result from damage anywhere along the sensory pathways, from the peripheral nerves to the spinal cord and brain. Common causes include diabetic peripheral neuropathy, nerve compression or entrapment, vitamin B12 deficiency, stroke, multiple sclerosis, leprosy and side effects of certain medications, including some chemotherapy agents. Local anaesthetics produce a temporary, localised hypoesthesia or anaesthesia.

Diagnosis

Sensory examination compares light touch, pinprick, temperature, vibration and proprioception between the affected area and the corresponding area on the opposite side of the body. The distribution of the deficit — for example a "glove and stocking" pattern or a single dermatome — helps localise the lesion. Nerve conduction studies and imaging may be used to identify the underlying cause.

Treatment

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause, such as glycaemic control in diabetes or decompression of an entrapped nerve. Because reduced sensation increases the risk of unnoticed injury, patients are often advised to inspect and protect affected areas, particularly the feet.
