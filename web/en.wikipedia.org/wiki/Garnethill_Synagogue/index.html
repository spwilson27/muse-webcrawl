Garnethill Synagogue is an Orthodox synagogue in the Garnethill area of Glasgow, Scotland. Opened in 1879, it was the first synagogue built in Scotland and remains the city's principal historic synagogue, serving the Glasgow Hebrew Congregation. The building is Category A listed.

History

Glasgow's Jewish community, established in the early 19th century, previously worshipped in rooms and converted premises in the city centre. As the community grew and moved west, a purpose-built synagogue was commissioned on Hill Street in Garnethill, then a fashionable residential suburb. The foundation stone was laid in 1877 and the synagogue was consecrated on 9 September 1879. It served a congregation drawn largely from immigrants arriving from Eastern Europe in the following decades, and remained the centre of Glasgow Jewish life for much of the 20th century.

Architecture

The synagogue was designed by the Glasgow architect John McLeod in association with Nathan Solomon Joseph of London, a noted synagogue architect. The exterior is in a Romanesque Revival style with Moorish and Byzantine touches. The interior features a basilica-like plan with galleries on columns for women worshippers, a central bimah, an ornate ark (aron ha-kodesh) beneath a large stained-glass window, and rich stencilled decoration; the building is noted for its sculptural details including distinctive Moorish-style capitals and carved timber work.

Scottish Jewish Archives Centre

Since 1987 the synagogue has housed the Scottish Jewish Archives Centre (SJAC), which collects and preserves the records of Scotland's Jewish communities — including documents, photographs, newspapers and artefacts relating to Jewish settlement across Scotland since the 18th century — and runs exhibitions, a library and a study centre on the site.
