Franz Anton Beckenbauer (11 September 1945 – 7 January 2024) was a German professional football player, manager, and official. Nicknamed der Kaiser ("the Emperor"), he is widely regarded as one of the greatest footballers of all time and is credited with popularizing the modern attacking sweeper, or libero, role.

Playing career

Born in Munich, Beckenbauer joined Bayern Munich as a youth and spent most of his career there, winning four Bundesliga titles and three consecutive European Cups (1974–1976). He later played for the New York Cosmos in the North American Soccer League and for Hamburger SV, winning another Bundesliga title.

For West Germany he earned 103 caps, captaining the team to victory at UEFA Euro 1972 and the 1974 FIFA World Cup on home soil. He won the Ballon d'Or in 1972 and 1976.

Managerial career

As manager of West Germany, Beckenbauer led the team to the 1986 World Cup final and victory at the 1990 FIFA World Cup, becoming one of only three men to win the World Cup as both player and manager. He also managed Olympique de Marseille and Bayern Munich, and later served as president of Bayern and of the German Football Association, heading the organizing committee for the 2006 FIFA World Cup.

Death

Beckenbauer died on 7 January 2024 in Salzburg, Austria, at the age of 78.
