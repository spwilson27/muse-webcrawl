Lepidoptera is an order of insects that includes butterflies and moths — about 180,000 described species in 126 families, making it one of the largest insect orders. The name derives from the Greek lepis (scale) and pteron (wing), referring to the tiny flattened scales that cover the wings and bodies of adults and produce their colours.

Biology

Lepidopterans undergo complete metamorphosis: egg, larva (caterpillar), pupa (chrysalis or cocoon) and adult. Most adults have a coiled proboscis for drinking nectar and are important pollinators; the larvae, with chewing mouthparts, are predominantly herbivorous, and some are major agricultural pests, while others — the silkworm Bombyx mori — are economically vital. Wing colours arise both from pigments and from structural coloration in the scales.

Diversity

Moths greatly outnumber butterflies (roughly 160,000 moth species versus some 17,500–19,000 butterflies, which form the clade Rhopalocera within the order) and are mostly nocturnal, while butterflies are mainly diurnal. Sizes range from micromoths with wingspans of a few millimetres to the Atlas moth and Queen Alexandra's birdwing, exceeding 25 cm. Lepidoptera are found on every continent except Antarctica and serve as key indicators of ecosystem health.
