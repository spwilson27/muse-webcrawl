Boundary Ranges

The Boundary Ranges, also known in the local Tlingit language as the Sierra al Boundario, are the largest and northernmost sub-range of the Coast Mountains of British Columbia, Canada, and the adjacent Alaska Panhandle of the United States. They run along the international boundary — from which their name derives — from the Nass River in the south to the Yukon border region near the Alsek River, and are part of the Pacific Cordillera.

Geography

The Boundary Ranges cover an area of roughly 87,000 km² and are characterised by vast icefields, among them the Juneau, Stikine, Cambria and Andrei icefields, and some of the largest temperate-zone glaciers in the world. Peaks include Mount Ratz (3,090 m), among the most prominent summits in Canada, along with Kates Needle and Devils Thumb, the latter famous in mountaineering literature. Rivers such as the Stikine, Taku and Whiting cut deep gorges through the range from the interior plateau to the Pacific.

The ranges are remote, largely roadless and sparsely inhabited; much of the area lies within protected areas including the Stikine River Provincial Park and parts of the Tongass National Forest in Alaska.
