U.S. Route 101 (US 101) is a major north–south United States Numbered Highway in the state of California, running for just over 800 miles from Los Angeles to the Oregon border. It is one of the longest U.S. Routes in California and largely follows the historic El Camino Real, the road that linked the Spanish missions of Alta California.

Route description

From its southern terminus at the East Los Angeles Interchange, US 101 heads northwest through the San Fernando Valley as the Ventura Freeway, then along the coast through Ventura and Santa Barbara. On the Central Coast it turns inland through San Luis Obispo and the Salinas Valley — a stretch sometimes called "the Salad Bowl of the World" for its agriculture — before reaching San Jose and the San Francisco Peninsula.

In San Francisco the highway crosses the Golden Gate Bridge as a combined US 101 / State Route 1 alignment. North of the bay it passes through the wine country of Sonoma County, then winds through the redwood forests of Mendocino and Humboldt counties, where the parallel Avenue of the Giants follows an older alignment. The highway ends at the Oregon state line north of Crescent City.

History

US 101 was established in 1926 as part of the original U.S. Numbered Highway System, originally running from San Diego to Olympia, Washington. The San Diego–Los Angeles segment was decommissioned in the 1964 California highway renumbering and replaced by Interstate 5. Much of the route overlays the mission-era El Camino Real, commemorated by distinctive bell-shaped markers along the roadside.
