Shanghai Metro

The Shanghai Metro (Chinese: 上海地铁) is the rapid transit system of Shanghai, China. Having opened in 1993, it has grown into one of the largest metro networks in the world, with over 800 kilometres of lines and more than 500 stations, and it carries upwards of ten million passengers on busy days — among the highest ridership of any metro on earth.

History

Construction of Line 1 began in 1990 with German assistance; the first section opened in 1993 and the full line between Xinzhuang and Shanghai Railway Station was completed by 1997. Expansion accelerated dramatically in the 2000s, driven by the city's growth and preparations for Expo 2010, for which eleven lines were in service. Subsequent phases added suburban extensions, the airport Maglev connection (a separate Transrapid line opened in 2004), and fully automated lines such as Line 10 and the Pujiang line.

Network

The system comprises around twenty lines, including the ring Line 4 and radial lines reaching Jiading, Songjiang, Lingang and Chongming connections via Line 2's extension network. Line 2 links Pudong International Airport with Hongqiao Airport across the city. Fares are distance-based and paid primarily through the Shanghai Public Transportation Card or mobile apps.

Records

Shanghai's network has traded the title of world's longest metro system with Beijing's subway as both have expanded; annual ridership has exceeded 3.5 billion journeys. The system is operated by Shanghai Shentong Metro Group.
