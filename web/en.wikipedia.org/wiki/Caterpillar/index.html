Caterpillar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Hero image placeholder: a monarch butterfly caterpillar on milkweed]

Caterpillars are the larval stage of members of the order Lepidoptera — the butterflies and moths. The word derives from the Old French catepelose, "hairy cat". Caterpillars are mostly herbivorous and often brightly coloured or camouflaged; they are voracious feeders, and many species are significant agricultural pests, while a few, such as the silkworm, are economically valuable.

Anatomy

A caterpillar has a soft, segmented body with a tough head capsule bearing simple eyes (stemmata) and short antennae. Behind the head are three thoracic segments, each with a pair of jointed true legs, followed by abdominal segments, typically with fleshy unjointed prolegs that grip with tiny hooks called crochets. Caterpillars grow by moulting their cuticle, usually four or five times, the stages between moults being called instars.

Defences

Many caterpillars are protected by camouflage, warning (aposematic) colouration advertising chemical defences derived from their food plants, urticating hairs, or startling eyespots. Some, such as the larvae of the death's-head hawkmoth, can even produce sounds. Others live socially in silk webs or tents.

Metamorphosis

At the end of the larval stage the caterpillar pupates — in butterflies forming a chrysalis — and undergoes complete metamorphosis, emerging as a winged adult. This four-stage life cycle (egg, larva, pupa, adult) is shared with other holometabolous insects.
