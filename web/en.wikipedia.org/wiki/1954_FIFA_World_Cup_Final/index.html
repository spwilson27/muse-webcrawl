West Germany 3–2 Hungary

The 1954 FIFA World Cup Final was played at the Wankdorf Stadium in Bern, Switzerland, on 4 July 1954. West Germany, who had been thrashed 8–3 by Hungary in the group stage, came from 2–0 down to beat the Hungarians 3–2 in one of the greatest upsets in football history. The match is known in Germany as the Miracle of Bern (Das Wunder von Bern) and is often regarded as a founding moment of post-war German identity.

Background

Hungary's "Mighty Magyars", captained by Ferenc Puskás and coached by Gusztáv Sebes, were unbeaten in 32 matches over four years and were Olympic champions. In the group match between the sides, West German coach Sepp Herberger had rested key players and accepted a heavy defeat, misleading the Hungarians and securing an easier route through the play-off. Puskás was injured in that game by a tackle from Werner Liebrich and was only partially fit for the final.

Match summary

In driving rain — "Fritz Walter weather", ideal for the German captain who had suffered malaria during the war — Hungary raced into a 2–0 lead within eight minutes through Puskás and Zoltán Czibor. Max Morlock pulled one back almost immediately, and Helmut Rahn equalised on 18 minutes. Thereafter German goalkeeper Toni Turek made a series of saves, Hungary hit the woodwork repeatedly, and in the 84th minute Rahn scored the winner from the edge of the box. Puskás had a late goal disallowed for offside by Welsh linesman Mervyn Griffiths. Radio commentator Herbert Zimmermann's call of the winning goal — "Rahn müsste schießen..." ("Rahn should shoot...") — became iconic in Germany.

Legacy and controversy

The victory, achieved only nine years after the Second World War and before West Germany had regained full sovereignty, was celebrated as a symbol of national recovery ("Wir sind wieder wer" — "We are somebody again"). Decades later, allegations emerged that German players had received injections — officially of vitamin C, allegedly in fact of the methamphetamine Pervitin — a controversy that remains debated by historians.
