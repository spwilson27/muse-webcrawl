A Caesar is a cocktail created and consumed primarily in Canada. It typically contains vodka, a clam and tomato juice blend (Clamato), hot sauce, and Worcestershire sauce, and is served with ice in a large glass rimmed with celery salt, garnished with a stalk of celery and a wedge of lime. What distinguishes it from a Bloody Mary is the clam-tomato juice, which gives the drink its savoury, seafood-tinged character. The Caesar is one of Canada's most popular drinks — an estimated 350 million or more are consumed annually — and is sometimes described as the country's unofficial national cocktail, remarkable precisely because it never achieved comparable popularity in the United States.

History

The Caesar was invented in 1969 by restaurant manager Walter Chell of the Calgary Inn (now the Westin Calgary) in Calgary, Alberta. Asked to create a signature drink for the inn's new Italian restaurant, Marco's, Chell drew on the flavours of spaghetti alle vongole (spaghetti with clams): he mashed clams into a nectar, mixed it with tomato juice, vodka, Worcestershire, and spices, and named it for Julius Caesar. The drink spread first through Calgary, then nationally after the Mott's company developed and marketed Clamato juice, which made the Caesar easy to prepare consistently. Chell also founded the still-running Caesar Chell Society traditions of remembrance in Calgary bars.

In 2009, for the drink's 40th anniversary, Calgary officially proclaimed Caesar Day on the Thursday before Victoria Day in May, and a petition and parliamentary motion in 2009 sought formal recognition of the Caesar as Canada's national cocktail. Vancouver bartenders have since popularised ever more elaborate garnishes — sliders, onion rings, whole fried items, and even full meals skewered on top — turning the "extreme Caesar" into a brunch centrepiece.

Preparation

The standard build: rim a highball or pint glass with lime and celery salt; fill with ice; add 1–1½ oz vodka, two dashes each of hot sauce (usually Tabasco) and Worcestershire sauce, a pinch of salt and pepper (or Montreal steak spice in some regions), and top with Clamato. Garnish with a celery stalk and lime wedge; common additions include pickled beans or asparagus, olives, pepperoncini, bacon, and prawns. Variations include the Virgin Caesar (no vodka), the gin or tequila Caesar, the beer-and-Caesar ("Red Eye" style), and regional tweaks such as horseradish in Atlantic Canada.

Comparison with the Bloody Mary

Both drinks are savoury vodka-and-tomato cocktails served as hair-of-the-dog or brunch drinks, but the Bloody Mary uses plain tomato juice and is the international standard, while the Caesar's clam broth adds umami depth and its celery-salt rim and celery garnish are fixed conventions. Mott's has marketed Clamato in the United States for decades without the Caesar displacing the Mary, a fact often cited in Canadian cultural commentary as a marker of distinct national taste.
