Spix's macaw

Conservation statusExtinct in the wild (IUCN)
Scientific nameCyanopsitta spixii
FamilyPsittacidae
Native toCaatinga, Bahia, Brazil

The Spix's macaw (Cyanopsitta spixii), also known as the little blue macaw, is a small blue parrot native to the caatinga dry forests of northeastern Brazil. It is the only member of the genus Cyanopsitta. The species was declared extinct in the wild in 2019, following a study that found no confirmed sightings in its natural habitat since 2000.

Description

The bird is about 55–57 cm long with predominantly pale blue plumage, a grey-blue head, and bright blue underparts. It has a black bill, yellow eyes, and grey legs. It feeds mainly on the seeds and nuts of caraiba trees and other caatinga plants.

History

The species is named after the German naturalist Johann Baptist von Spix, who collected a specimen in 1819. Habitat destruction, trapping for the pet trade, and the invasion of Africanized bees drove its decline through the twentieth century. A single wild male was famously paired with a released female blue-headed macaw in the 1990s.

Conservation and reintroduction

A captive population of several hundred birds was maintained by breeding centres in Brazil, Germany, and Qatar. In June 2022, the first group of Spix's macaws was released back into the wild in Bahia, marking the first step of an ambitious reintroduction programme. The bird also inspired the animated film Rio.
