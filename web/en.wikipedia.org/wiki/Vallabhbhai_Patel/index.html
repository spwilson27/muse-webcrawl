Vallabhbhai Patel

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
Born: 31 October 1875, Nadiad, Gujarat
Died: 15 December 1950, Bombay
Office: First Deputy Prime Minister of India (1947–1950)

Vallabhbhai Jhaverbhai Patel (31 October 1875 – 15 December 1950), commonly known as Sardar Patel, was an Indian statesman and a leading figure in the country's independence movement. He served as India's first Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Home Affairs, and is remembered as the "Iron Man of India" for his role in unifying the country after Partition.

Early life and freedom struggle

Born in Nadiad, Gujarat, Patel travelled to England to study law and became a successful barrister in Ahmedabad. Drawn into Mahatma Gandhi's movement, he organised the Kheda Satyagraha (1918) and led the Bardoli Satyagraha of 1928, whose success earned him the title Sardar ("chief"). He presided over the Indian National Congress's Karachi session in 1931, was repeatedly imprisoned, and played a central organisational role in the Quit India movement.

Integration of the princely states

At independence in 1947 India was divided between British-administered provinces and more than 560 princely states. As head of the States Department, working with V. P. Menon, Patel persuaded nearly all the rulers within India's territory to accede to the Union — by diplomacy in most cases, and by police action in the case of Hyderabad in 1948. He also created the All India Services, calling the civil service the "steel frame" of the nation.

Legacy

Patel died in Bombay on 15 December 1950. He was posthumously awarded the Bharat Ratna in 1991. The Statue of Unity in Gujarat — at 182 metres the world's tallest statue — was inaugurated in his honour in 2018, and his birthday, 31 October, is observed as National Unity Day.
