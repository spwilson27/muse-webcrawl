Mother Teresa

BornAnjezë Gonxhe Bojaxhiu, 26 August 1910, Skopje, Ottoman Empire (now North Macedonia)
Died5 September 1997 (aged 87), Calcutta (now Kolkata), India
Venerated inRoman Catholic Church
Canonised4 September 2016, by Pope Francis
Known forFounding the Missionaries of Charity

Mary Teresa Bojaxhiu, better known as Mother Teresa, was an Albanian-Indian Catholic nun and missionary. Born in Skopje, she moved to Ireland and then to India, where she lived most of her life and founded the Missionaries of Charity, a religious congregation dedicated to serving, in her words, the poorest of the poor.

[Image: Mother Teresa in her blue-bordered white sari, in later life]

Early life

She was born Anjezë Gonxhe Bojaxhiu in Skopje in 1910. At 18 she left home to join the Sisters of Loreto in Ireland, and in 1929 she arrived in India, teaching at a school in Calcutta for many years.

Missionaries of Charity

In 1946 she reported experiencing a calling to work among the poor while living in Calcutta. She began working in the slums, gained Indian citizenship, and in 1950 founded the Missionaries of Charity. The congregation opened hospices and homes for people dying of diseases including HIV/AIDS, leprosy and tuberculosis, as well as orphanages and schools. Its work expanded worldwide, recognised by the blue-bordered white sari adopted as its habit.

Recognition and criticism

She received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979 — announced on 17 October that year — among many international honours. Her work also attracted criticism, including over the standard of care in her homes, her views on abortion and contraception, and the sources of some donations.

Death and sainthood

She died in Calcutta on 5 September 1997. She was beatified by Pope John Paul II in 2003 and canonised — recognised as Saint Teresa of Calcutta — by Pope Francis in 2016.
