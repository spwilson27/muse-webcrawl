Lean is a proof assistant and programming language based on the calculus of constructions with inductive types. It is used to write formal, machine-checked mathematical proofs as well as verified software. Lean was originally developed by Leonardo de Moura at Microsoft Research beginning in 2013; the current version, Lean 4, released in 2021, reimplements the system largely in Lean itself and is designed to be both an efficient theorem prover and a practical general-purpose programming language.

Overview

In Lean, mathematical statements are expressed as types and proofs as terms, following the Curry–Howard correspondence. Users prove theorems interactively using tactics, and the Lean kernel checks every proof. Lean 4 compiles to C and supports metaprogramming: users can write new tactics and commands in Lean itself.

Mathlib

The community library Mathlib contains formalisations of large parts of undergraduate and research mathematics — algebra, analysis, topology, number theory, category theory and more — together with a library of tactics. Landmark formalisations in Lean include the liquid tensor experiment, which verified a central result of Peter Scholze's condensed mathematics, completed in 2022, and work verifying properties of Fermat's Last Theorem for regular primes.

Use in research and AI

Lean is increasingly used as a benchmark environment for automated theorem proving and AI systems, which generate Lean proofs that the kernel can verify. In 2023, stewardship of Lean passed to the Lean Focused Research Organization (Lean FRO), a nonprofit supporting its development.
