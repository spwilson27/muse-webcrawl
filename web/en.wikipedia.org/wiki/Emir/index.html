Emir

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Emir (Arabic: أمير, ʾamīr, "commander" or "prince") is a word of Arabic origin that can refer to a male monarch, aristocrat, holder of high-ranking military or political office, or other person possessing actual or ceremonial authority. The title has been used throughout the Muslim world and in some non-Muslim contexts.

Etymology and early use

The word derives from the Arabic root ʾ-m-r, "to command". In the early Islamic period, an amīr was a commander or governor; the caliph's supreme title Amīr al-Muʾminīn ("Commander of the Faithful") remains one of the most prestigious in Islam. The English word "admiral" descends from the Arabic amīr al-baḥr ("commander of the sea") via medieval European languages.

Rulers and states

A state ruled by an emir is an emirate. Today, emirs reign in Kuwait, Qatar and several constituent states of the United Arab Emirates. Historically, notable emirates included Córdoba, Granada, Bukhara, Afghanistan, Diriyah and Transjordan. In the Ottoman Empire and elsewhere, emir could also denote a prince of a ruling house, and the title is borne by leaders of some religious movements, such as the Ahmadiyya caliph's title Ameer-ul-Momineen.

Military and honorific use

In various periods emir ranked generals and provincial governors; in Nigeria, emirs are traditional rulers of the northern Muslim states, such as the Emir of Kano. The title is also used honorifically within some Sufi orders and organisations.
