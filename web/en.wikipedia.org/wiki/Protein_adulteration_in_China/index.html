Protein adulteration in China

Adulterant: Melamine
Principal incidents: 2007 pet food recalls; 2008 milk scandal
Reason: Inflating apparent protein content measured by nitrogen-based tests

Protein adulteration in China refers to the deliberate addition of nitrogen-rich substances, above all the industrial chemical melamine, to diluted or low-quality food and feed products in order to fool standard protein tests, which infer protein content from total nitrogen. The practice came to worldwide attention in 2007 and 2008 through two major scandals.

2007 pet food incident

In 2007, melamine and cyanuric acid were found in wheat gluten and rice protein exported from China for pet food in North America. The combination formed crystals that damaged pets' kidneys, prompting one of the largest pet-food recalls in history and the deaths of many cats and dogs.

2008 milk scandal

In 2008, melamine was found in infant formula and milk products produced by Sanlu Group and other Chinese dairies. Watered-down raw milk had been dosed with melamine to pass protein tests. Around 300,000 infants were affected, with at least six deaths, leading to executions, long prison terms, the bankruptcy of Sanlu, and sweeping reforms of Chinese food-safety law.

Aftermath

China revised its Food Safety Law in 2009 and again in 2015, consolidated regulators, and imposed stricter testing, yet melamine-tainted stock resurfaced in smaller seizures in later years.
