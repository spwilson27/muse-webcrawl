Saluki

OriginMiddle East (Fertile Crescent)
TypeSighthound
Height58–71 cm (23–28 in)
CoatSmooth or feathered; many colours
Life spanAbout 12–14 years

The Saluki is a standardised breed developed from the ancient landrace sighthounds of the Middle East and Central Asia. One of the oldest recognisable types of dog, Salukis were bred by nomadic peoples to hunt by sight and course game such as gazelle across open desert and steppe. They are also known as the Persian greyhound or Arabian hound.

History

Dogs of Saluki type appear in art from ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia, and the breed's ancestors spread along trade routes from Persia to China. Treasured by Bedouin tribes, who regarded them as a gift from God (el hor, "the Noble One"), Salukis were traditionally never sold but given as marks of honour. They were introduced to Europe in the 19th century, and the breed was formally recognised in Britain in 1923.

Characteristics

Salukis are tall, slim, deep-chested sighthounds built for speed and endurance, capable of sustaining pace over long distances in heat. Two coat varieties exist: smooth, and feathered, with silky feathering on the ears, tail and backs of the legs. Colours include white, cream, fawn, red, grizzle and black, in solid or combination patterns. Temperament is typically gentle, dignified, aloof with strangers and independent; like other sighthounds they have a strong instinct to chase.
