Cumae (Ancient Greek: Κύμη, Kyme; Italian: Cuma) was the first ancient Greek colony on the mainland of Italy, founded by settlers from Euboea in the 8th century BC. It became one of the most powerful cities of Magna Graecia, dominating the coast of Campania and spreading Greek culture — including the alphabet, adapted by the Etruscans and Romans — throughout Italy.

History

According to tradition, Cumae was founded around 750 BC by colonists from Chalcis and Cyme in Euboea, led by Megasthenes and Hippocles. The city grew rich from trade and agriculture and founded its own colonies, including Neapolis (Naples). In 524 and 504 BC the Cumaeans, led by the tyrant Aristodemus, repelled Etruscan invasions, and in 474 BC Cumae and Syracuse defeated the Etruscans at the Battle of Cumae. The city fell to the Samnites around 421 BC and later became a Roman civitas sine suffragio in 338 BC.

The Cumaean Sibyl

Cumae was famous throughout the ancient world for the Cumaean Sibyl, a prophetess who presided over the oracle of Apollo. Her cave — a trapezoidal tunnel cut into the rock of the acropolis — can still be visited. In Virgil's Aeneid, the Sibyl guides Aeneas into the underworld. The Sibylline Books, collections of her prophecies, were kept in Rome and consulted by the Senate in times of crisis.

Archaeology

The archaeological park preserves the acropolis with the temples of Apollo and Jupiter, the Cave of the Sibyl, and extensive Roman remains including baths and an amphitheatre. The city was finally destroyed in 1207 by Neapolitan forces after it had become a stronghold of Saracen pirates.
