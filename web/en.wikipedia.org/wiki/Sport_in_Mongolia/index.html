Sport in Mongolia

Sport in Mongolia is centred on the traditional "three games of men"—Mongolian wrestling, horse racing and archery—which form the core of the annual Naadam festival, held across the country each July and inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. These sports descend from the martial and pastoral traditions of the nomadic peoples of the Mongolian steppe.

Traditional sports

Mongolian wrestling (bökh) is the most popular spectator sport in the country; wrestlers wear distinctive open-chested jackets, and champions earn animal-themed titles such as falcon, elephant, lion and titan. Child jockeys race horses over cross-country distances of 15–30 km at Naadam, while archers shoot at targets with composite bows. Ankle-bone shooting (shagai) is another traditional pastime played at festivals.

Modern sports

Mongolia first competed at the Olympic Games in 1964 and won its first medals in wrestling, judo and shooting at Mexico City in 1968. The country's first Olympic gold medals came at Beijing 2008, through judoka Naidangiin Tüvshinbayar and boxer Enkhbatyn Badar-Uugan. Mongolian athletes are internationally successful in judo, freestyle wrestling, boxing and shooting. Mongolian sumo wrestlers have achieved extraordinary success in Japan, where Hakuhō, Asashōryū, Harumafuji and Terunofuji—all Mongolian-born—reached the rank of yokozuna, the highest in the sport. Basketball, football and ice hockey (including the traditional bandy) also have growing followings, and Mongolia hosted and won early editions of the East Asian Youth Games.
