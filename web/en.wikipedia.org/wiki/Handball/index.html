Handball

Handball (also known as team handball, European handball or Olympic handball) is a team sport in which two teams of seven players each (six outcourt players and a goalkeeper) pass a ball using their hands with the aim of throwing it into the goal of the opposing team. A standard match consists of two periods of 30 minutes, and the team that scores more goals wins.

Gameplay

Handball is played on a court 40 by 20 metres (131 by 66 ft), with a goal in the centre of each end. The goals are surrounded by a 6-metre zone where only the defending goalkeeper is allowed. Players may dribble the ball, take up to three steps holding it, and pass to teammates. Goals must be scored from outside the zone or while "diving" into it.

History

Modern handball was codified in northern Europe — chiefly Denmark, Germany, Norway and Sweden — in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Field handball, played outdoors with eleven players a side, appeared at the 1936 Berlin Olympics; the indoor seven-a-side game became an Olympic sport for men in 1972 and for women in 1976. The sport is governed internationally by the International Handball Federation (IHF), founded in 1946.

Competitions

The IHF World Men's and Women's Handball Championships are held every two years. The sport is most popular in Europe, where professional leagues such as the German Bundesliga and the EHF Champions League draw large audiences.
