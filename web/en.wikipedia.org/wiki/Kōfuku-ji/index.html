Kōfuku-ji (Japanese: 興福寺) is a Buddhist temple in Nara, Japan, that served as the family temple of the powerful Fujiwara clan and was one of the Seven Great Temples of Nara. It is the head temple of the Hossō school of Japanese Buddhism and is renowned for its five-storied pagoda, the second tallest wooden pagoda in Japan.

History

The temple was founded in 669 by Kagami no Ōkimi, wife of Fujiwara no Kamatari, at Yamashina in Kyoto, and was moved to the new capital of Nara in 710. Under Fujiwara patronage it grew immensely rich and powerful, at times rival to the state. Many of its buildings were destroyed by fire over the centuries and repeatedly rebuilt.

Treasures

Kōfuku-ji's National Treasure Museum houses masterpieces of Japanese Buddhist sculpture, including the famous dry-lacquer Ashura statue and the Ten Great Disciples from the Nara period. Structures include the Five-storied Pagoda (Gojū-no-tō, rebuilt 1426), the Tōkondō (Eastern Golden Hall), the Hokuendō (Northern Octagonal Hall) and the rebuilt Central Golden Hall (Chūkondō), reopened in 2018.
