1715 Treasure Fleet

The 1715 Treasure Fleet was a Spanish treasure fleet returning from the New World to Spain. At two in the morning on Wednesday, 31 July 1715, seven days after departing Havana, Cuba, under the orders of General Don Juan Esteban de Ubilla, eleven of the twelve ships of this fleet were lost in a hurricane near present-day Vero Beach, Florida. Because the fleet carried a large cargo of silver, it is also known as the Silver Fleet or Plate Fleet (plata being Spanish for silver).

The disaster

The fleet combined the New Spain fleet of Ubilla with the Tierra Firme fleet of Antonio de Echeverz, carrying gold, silver, jewels, Chinese porcelain and other riches accumulated over two years, as war in Europe had delayed sailings. Delayed in Havana and sailing into hurricane season, the convoy was struck by a powerful hurricane on 31 July that drove the ships onto the reefs of Florida's Atlantic coast. Around a thousand people drowned, though many survivors reached shore and established camps.

Salvage

Spanish salvage operations recovered a substantial portion of the treasure using divers, including enslaved and indigenous labourers, and a salvage camp was established whose site is now commemorated at the McLarty Treasure Museum in Sebastian Inlet State Park. News of the wrecks attracted English privateers and pirates, including Henry Jennings, whose raids on the salvage camp helped spark the crackdown on piracy in the Caribbean. In the twentieth century, treasure hunter Kip Wagner and his Real Eight Company located the wrecks and recovered large quantities of gold and silver, making the stretch of coast famous as Florida's "Treasure Coast". Coins from the fleet still wash ashore after storms.
