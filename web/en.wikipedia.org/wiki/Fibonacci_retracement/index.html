Fibonacci retracement

Fibonacci retracement levels on a price chart
TypeTechnical analysis indicator
Key levels23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%
Based onFibonacci numbers, golden ratio

Fibonacci retracement is a method of technical analysis used by traders to identify potential support and resistance levels on a price chart. It is based on the idea that markets will retrace a predictable portion of a move, after which they will continue to move in the original direction.

Method

The analyst selects two extreme points on a chart, usually a significant peak and trough, and draws horizontal lines at Fibonacci ratios of the vertical distance between them. The most commonly used levels are 23.6%, 38.2%, 61.8%, and 78.6%; the 50% level is also commonly included although it is not strictly a Fibonacci ratio. These ratios derive from the Fibonacci sequence, in which each number is the sum of the two preceding ones, and from the golden ratio (approximately 1.618), to which ratios of successive Fibonacci numbers converge.

Use

Traders use retracement levels to anticipate where a pullback in a trend may end, placing entry orders, stop-loss orders, or price targets near the levels. Related techniques include Fibonacci extensions, arcs, fans, and time zones.

Criticism

Studies of the technique's effectiveness are mixed; some research finds no evidence that prices reliably stop or reverse at Fibonacci levels more often than chance would predict, while other analyses find limited support. The method remains popular in charting software and trading practice.
