José Victoriano Huerta Márquez (Spanish: [biktoˈɾjano ˈweɾta]; 22 December 1850 – 13 January 1916) was a Mexican general and politician who served as the 35th President of Mexico from 1913 to 1914. He came to power in a coup d'état — the Decena Trágica ("Ten Tragic Days") of February 1913 — against the democratically elected government of Francisco I. Madero, and his regime is widely regarded as a military dictatorship.

Coup and presidency

Huerta, commander of government forces in Mexico City, conspired with rebel general Félix Díaz and U.S. ambassador Henry Lane Wilson in the Pact of the Embassy. During ten days of fighting in the capital, Madero was forced to resign and was murdered days later along with his vice president, José María Pino Suárez. Huerta assumed the presidency on 19 February 1913.

His government was recognised by several European powers but not by U.S. President Woodrow Wilson, who pursued a policy of "watchful waiting" and moral condemnation. Huerta dissolved Congress in October 1913 and ruled by decree, while revolutionary forces under Venustiano Carranza, Emiliano Zapata, and Pancho Villa rose against him under the Plan of Guadalupe.

Downfall and death

Following the U.S. occupation of Veracruz in April 1914 and defeats by Constitutionalist armies, Huerta resigned on 15 July 1914 and fled into exile. He conspired with German agents to regain power, was arrested by U.S. authorities in New Mexico in 1915 for violating American neutrality laws, and died in custody-adjacent detention in El Paso, Texas, in January 1916, officially of cirrhosis.
