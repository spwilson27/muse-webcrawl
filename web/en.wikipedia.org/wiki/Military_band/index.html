Military band

A military band is a group of musicians performing musical duties for military functions, usually for the armed forces. A typical military band consists of brass, woodwind and percussion instruments, and performs at ceremonies, parades, official functions and concerts. Field music — drums, fifes, bugles and bagpipes — historically served the battlefield role of transmitting commands and regulating the soldier's day.

History

Military music is ancient: drums and horns accompanied armies in the ancient world, and the Ottoman mehter bands of the Janissaries, with their booming drums, cymbals and Turkish crescents, profoundly influenced European military music after the 17th century. European armies developed wind bands for ceremony and morale, a tradition codified in the 18th and 19th centuries with the addition of valved brass instruments. John Philip Sousa's marches made the American military band world-famous in the early 20th century.

Functions

Military bands perform for state ceremonies, troop reviews, military funerals, tattoos and public concerts. Instruments and formations vary by country: British-style bands feature brass and woodwind with a drum major and corps of drums; the United States fields premier ensembles such as "The President's Own" United States Marine Band, founded in 1798. Many bands also maintain jazz, popular and ceremonial sub-units, and serve a public-relations and recruiting role.
