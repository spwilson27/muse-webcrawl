Information assurance

Information assurance (IA) is the practice of assuring information and managing risks related to the use, processing, storage and transmission of information. It is a broader discipline than information security alone, encompassing not only protection against attack but also the reliability, recoverability and correct handling of information throughout its life cycle.

Pillars

IA is commonly described through five pillars: confidentiality, integrity, availability, authenticity and non-repudiation. The first three form the classic CIA triad of information security; authenticity and non-repudiation extend the model to cover proof of identity and proof of origin for messages and transactions.

Process

An information assurance programme typically proceeds by classifying information assets, assessing threats and vulnerabilities, analysing risk, implementing controls (technical, procedural and organisational), and continuously monitoring effectiveness. Standards and frameworks used in IA include the ISO/IEC 27000 family, the NIST Risk Management Framework and COBIT. In government and defence contexts, IA also covers certification and accreditation of systems before they are authorised to operate.
