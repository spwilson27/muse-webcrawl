Business Process Model and Notation

Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN) is a graphical representation for specifying business processes in a business process model, maintained by the Object Management Group (OMG). It provides a standard notation that is intended to be understandable by all business users — business analysts, technical developers, and business managers alike — while also being precise enough to map to execution languages.

Overview

BPMN diagrams are built from a small set of core elements:

  • Flow objects — events (circles), activities/tasks (rounded rectangles), and gateways (diamonds) that control divergence and convergence of flow.
  • Connecting objects — sequence flows, message flows, and associations.
  • Swimlanes — pools (representing participants) and lanes (roles within a pool).
  • Artifacts — data objects, groups, and annotations.

Processes may be modelled at different levels, from high-level descriptive diagrams to executable models exchanged in a standardized XML format.

History

BPMN was originally developed by the Business Process Management Initiative (BPMI), with version 1.0 released in 2004; BPMI merged with the OMG in 2005. BPMN 2.0, released in January 2011, renamed the standard "Business Process Model and Notation", added an XML serialization and execution semantics, and defined conformance classes for process modelling, process execution, and choreography. It was published as the ISO standard ISO/IEC 19510:2013.
