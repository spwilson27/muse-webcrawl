1994 FIFA World Cup

The Rose Bowl, Pasadena, hosted the 1994 World Cup Final before over 94,000 spectators.

The 1994 FIFA World Cup was the 15th edition of the FIFA World Cup, hosted by the United States from 17 June to 17 July 1994. Twenty-four teams competed in nine stadiums. Despite the host country's limited football tradition at the time, the tournament set all-time World Cup attendance records, drawing a total of nearly 3.6 million spectators at an average of almost 69,000 per match — records that still stand.

Tournament summary

Brazil, led by the attacking pair of Romário and Bebeto and coached by Carlos Alberto Parreira, won their group and defeated the United States, the Netherlands (in a celebrated 3–2 quarter-final), and Sweden to reach the final. Italy, inspired by Roberto Baggio, struggled through the group stage but advanced on the strength of Baggio's goals in the knockout rounds. Surprise packages included Sweden (third place) and Bulgaria (fourth), whose star Hristo Stoichkov shared the Golden Boot with Russia's Oleg Salenko, who scored five goals in one match against Cameroon — a World Cup record.

Final

The final, at the Rose Bowl in Pasadena on 17 July, was the first in World Cup history to finish goalless after extra time, and the first to be decided by a penalty shoot-out. Brazil won the shoot-out 3–2 after Italy's Franco Baresi, Daniele Massaro, and Roberto Baggio missed their kicks, Baggio famously sending the decisive penalty over the bar. Brazil thus won a record fourth World Cup title, which the team dedicated to the memory of racing driver Ayrton Senna, killed earlier that year.

Incidents and legacy

The tournament was overshadowed by the expulsion of Argentina's Diego Maradona after he tested positive for ephedrine, and by the murder of Colombian defender Andrés Escobar, shot in Medellín days after his own goal contributed to Colombia's elimination. Off the field, the World Cup's commercial success helped launch Major League Soccer in 1996 and established the United States as a major market for the sport. It was also the first World Cup to award three points for a win in the group stage.
