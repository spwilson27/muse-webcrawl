Marie Louise, Duchess of Parma

Marie Louise (12 December 1791 – 17 December 1847) was an Austrian archduchess who became Empress of the French as the second wife of Napoleon (married 1810), bore him his long-awaited heir — the King of Rome, later Napoleon II — and after his fall ruled as Duchess of Parma, Piacenza and Guastalla from 1814 until her death.

Marriage to Napoleon

After the divorce of Joséphine de Beauharnais, Napoleon sought a dynastic bride and a Habsburg alliance; Metternich — whose state had just been beaten at Wagram — promoted the match. Marie Louise, raised to regard Napoleon as the Antichrist, married him by proxy in Vienna in March 1810 and in person at the Louvre. The marriage was, by most accounts, companionable; in March 1811 she gave birth to a son, Napoleon François, styled King of Rome. She served as regent during the campaigns of 1813–14 but, on her father's instruction, did not join Napoleon on Elba.

Duchess of Parma

The Congress of Vienna assigned her Parma for life, with her Habsburg officer — and soon husband — Count Neipperg as her guide (and morganatic spouse from 1821). She ruled Parma mildly and was reasonably popular, building theatres and infrastructure, while her son by Napoleon — the "Eaglet" — was raised at the Viennese court as Duke of Reichstadt, dying there in 1832. Marie Louise died in Parma in 1847.
