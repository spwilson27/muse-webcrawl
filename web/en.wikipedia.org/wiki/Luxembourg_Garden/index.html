Luxembourg Garden

Jardin du Luxembourg
Location: 6th arrondissement, Paris, France
Area: about 25 hectares
Created: 1612
Commissioned by: Marie de' Medici

The Luxembourg Garden (French: Jardin du Luxembourg) is a public park in the 6th arrondissement of Paris, France, created in 1612 by Marie de' Medici, widow of King Henry IV, to accompany the Luxembourg Palace she built as her new residence. It covers roughly 25 hectares between the Latin Quarter and Saint-Germain-des-Prés.

Layout

The garden combines formal French terraces and gravel walks around a central octagonal basin, the Grand Bassin, with an English-style landscaped section added in the nineteenth century. It contains over a hundred statues, monuments, and fountains, including the Medici Fountain, a long grotto and pool built around 1630.

Features

Visitors can rent model sailboats on the Grand Bassin, use tennis courts and chess tables, and visit an apiary and an orchard of heritage fruit varieties. A small-scale replica of the Statue of Liberty stands in the garden, and the park also hosts photography exhibitions on its railings and concerts at its bandstand.

Management

The garden is owned and maintained by the French Senate, which meets in the Luxembourg Palace at its northern edge.
