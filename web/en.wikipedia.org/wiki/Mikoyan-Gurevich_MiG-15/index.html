Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-15

The Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-15 (NATO reporting name: "Fagot") is a jet fighter aircraft developed by the Soviet Union in the late 1940s. One of the most successful early swept-wing jet fighters, it first flew on 30 December 1947 and entered service in 1949. Approximately 13,000 were built in the USSR, with total production including licence-built versions exceeding 18,000, making it among the most-produced jet aircraft in history.

Design

The MiG-15 used a 35-degree swept wing with wing fences and was powered by the Klimov VK-1, a development of the Rolls-Royce Nene engine whose technology Britain had sold to the USSR in 1946. Armament was heavy for its size: one 37 mm N-37 cannon and two 23 mm NR-23 cannon, optimised for destroying bombers.

Korean War

The MiG-15's combat debut over Korea in November 1950 shocked United Nations forces: flown by Soviet, Chinese and North Korean pilots from bases in Manchuria ("MiG Alley"), it outperformed straight-wing Allied jets and could contest even the F-86 Sabre, though the Sabre's better-trained pilots achieved a favourable exchange ratio. The improved MiG-15bis followed, and a two-seat trainer, the MiG-15UTI, became the standard jet trainer across the Eastern Bloc.

Legacy

MiG-15s served with some 40 air forces and saw combat in the Suez Crisis, the Taiwan Strait crises and numerous other conflicts. Hundreds survive as museum pieces and warbirds.
