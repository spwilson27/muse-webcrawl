Cinnamic acid

Cinnamic acid
FormulaC9H8O2
Molar mass148.16 g/mol
AppearanceWhite crystalline solid
Melting point133 °C (271 °F)

Cinnamic acid is an organic compound with the formula C6H5–CH=CH–COOH. It is a white crystalline compound that is slightly soluble in water and freely soluble in many organic solvents. Classified as an unsaturated carboxylic acid, it occurs naturally in a number of plants, including cinnamon, from which it takes its name.

Occurrence and production

Cinnamic acid is obtained from oil of cinnamon and balsams such as storax and shea butter. Industrially it is synthesised by the Perkin reaction between benzaldehyde and acetic anhydride, or by the Knoevenagel condensation of benzaldehyde with malonic acid. In plants it is biosynthesised from phenylalanine by the enzyme phenylalanine ammonia-lyase and is a central intermediate in the phenylpropanoid pathway leading to lignin, flavonoids and coumarins.

Uses

Cinnamic acid is used in flavourings, cosmetics and perfumery, partly through its esters such as methyl cinnamate and ethyl cinnamate. It is also a precursor in the manufacture of pharmaceuticals and of the sweetener aspartame, and has been investigated for antimicrobial and other biological activities.
