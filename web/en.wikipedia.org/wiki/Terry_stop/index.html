Terry stop

A Terry stop (or "stop and frisk") is a brief detention and search by police in the United States under the doctrine of the Supreme Court case Terry v. Ohio, 392 U.S. 1 (1968). It permits an officer who has reasonable suspicion that a person has committed, is committing, or is about to commit a crime to stop and briefly investigate, and to pat down the person's outer clothing if the officer reasonably suspects the person is armed and dangerous.

Legal standard

Reasonable suspicion is a lower standard than the probable cause required for an arrest or a full search. Evidence found in a lawful frisk may support arrest; stops exceeding the brief investigative scope require more. Related doctrines cover vehicle stops and the "plain feel" rule from Minnesota v. Dickerson (1993).

Controversy

Stop-and-frisk practices, notably in New York City, have drawn litigation and criticism over racial disparities and Fourth Amendment rights; in 2013 a federal court found the city's application unconstitutional as applied.
