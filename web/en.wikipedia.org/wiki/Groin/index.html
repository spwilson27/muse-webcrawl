Groin

In human anatomy, the groin (or inguinal region) is the junctional area between the abdomen and the thigh on either side of the pubic bone. It corresponds to the crease where the lower abdomen meets the leg and overlies the inguinal canal.

Anatomy

The groin contains the inguinal ligament (running from the anterior superior iliac spine to the pubic tubercle), the inguinal canal — through which the spermatic cord passes in males and the round ligament in females — and important structures including the femoral artery, vein, and nerve, and clusters of inguinal lymph nodes.

Clinical significance

The region is clinically important because of inguinal hernias, in which abdominal contents protrude through weaknesses in the canal wall; femoral hernias occur just below the ligament. "Pulled groin" in sport usually denotes a strain of the adductor muscles of the thigh, a common injury in ice hockey, football, and soccer. Swollen inguinal lymph nodes may signal infection in the leg or genital region.

Other uses

In architecture and coastal engineering, the word (also spelled groyne) refers to a protective structure: a vault's groin is the curved line where two vaults intersect, and a coastal groyne is a barrier built out into the water to limit longshore drift of sediment.
