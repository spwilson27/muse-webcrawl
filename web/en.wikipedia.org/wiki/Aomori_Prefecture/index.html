Aomori Prefecture

Aomori Prefecture is the northernmost prefecture of Honshu, the main island of Japan. Its capital and largest city is Aomori. The prefecture faces Hokkaido across the Tsugaru Strait and is bounded by Iwate Prefecture and Akita Prefecture to the south. It is known for heavy snow, apples, seafood and summer festivals.

Geography

Aomori has coastlines on the Sea of Japan, the Pacific Ocean and Mutsu Bay. The Hakkōda Mountains rise in the centre, while the Shirakami-Sanchi beech forest, a UNESCO World Heritage site, lies in the west. Lake Towada and the Oirase Stream are major natural attractions near the border with Akita.

Economy and culture

Agriculture and fisheries are important, and Aomori is Japan's leading apple-producing prefecture. The prefectural capital hosts the Aomori Nebuta Matsuri each August, while Hirosaki is noted for its castle and cherry blossoms. The Seikan Tunnel links Aomori with Hokkaido, and the Tōhoku Shinkansen serves Shin-Aomori Station.

History

The area was historically inhabited by Emishi peoples before coming under Japanese control. During the Edo period much of it was ruled by the Hirosaki and Morioka domains. Aomori Prefecture took roughly its modern form after the Meiji Restoration.
