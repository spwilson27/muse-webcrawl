The 1994 San Marino Grand Prix was a Formula One motor race held on 1 May 1994 at the Autodromo Enzo e Dino Ferrari in Imola, Italy. It was the third race of the 1994 season and is remembered as one of the darkest weekends in the history of the sport: Austrian driver Roland Ratzenberger was killed in a crash during Saturday qualifying, and three-time world champion Ayrton Senna died in a crash during the race itself.

The Imola circuit was extensively redesigned following the events of the 1994 weekend.

Weekend of tragedy

The weekend began with a heavy accident for Rubens Barrichello in Friday practice, from which he escaped with relatively minor injuries. On Saturday, Simtek driver Roland Ratzenberger crashed fatally at the Villeneuve corner after his front wing failed; he was the first driver killed at a Grand Prix weekend since 1982. Despite the accident, Senna took pole position.

Race

The race was marred at the start by a collision involving JJ Lehto and Pedro Lamy that injured spectators and a policeman. On the restart, Senna led Michael Schumacher; on lap 7 Senna's Williams left the road at the Tamburello corner at high speed and struck a concrete wall. He died from his injuries later that day, aged 34. The race was restarted and won by Schumacher, who went on to win the 1994 championship.

Aftermath

The deaths of Ratzenberger and Senna prompted sweeping safety reforms in Formula One, including the reformation of the Grand Prix Drivers' Association, redesigned circuits with slowed corners at Imola, improved crash structures and head protection, and a lasting programme of medical and safety research under FIA medical delegate Sid Watkins. Senna's death led to an extended legal process in Italy and remains a defining moment in the sport's history.
