Evolute

In the differential geometry of curves, the evolute of a given curve is the locus of all its centres of curvature. Equivalently, it is the envelope of the normals to the curve. The original curve is the involute of its evolute.

History

The concept was introduced by Apollonius of Perga in Book V of his Conics, but the first systematic treatment, including the name, is due to Christiaan Huygens in his Horologium Oscillatorium (1673), where evolutes arose in the design of the cycloidal pendulum clock.

Definition and properties

For a plane curve parametrised by arc length, the evolute is traced by the centres of the osculating circles. Cusps of the evolute correspond to vertices of the original curve, points where the curvature is locally maximal or minimal. The length of an arc of the evolute equals the difference of the radii of curvature at the corresponding endpoints of the original curve.

Examples

The evolute of a parabola is a semicubical parabola (Neil's parabola); the evolute of an ellipse is a stretched astroid-like curve; the evolute of a cycloid is a congruent cycloid, a property exploited by Huygens; the evolute of a circle degenerates to its centre point.
