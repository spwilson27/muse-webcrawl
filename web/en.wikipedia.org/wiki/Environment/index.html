Environment

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Photograph of a forested river valley under a clear sky]

The environment, in the sense used in environmental science and policy, means the surroundings in which organisms live — the natural world of air, water, land, ecosystems and climate, together with the human influences upon them. Environmental studies examine how human activity affects these systems and how damage can be limited or reversed.

Issues

Major environmental issues include climate change, air and water pollution, deforestation, biodiversity loss, desertification, ocean acidification and waste. International responses include the Paris Agreement on climate change and conventions on biodiversity and endangered species trade.

Environmentalism

The modern environmental movement grew in the 1960s–70s, helped by works such as Rachel Carson's Silent Spring (1962) and the first Earth Day in 1970. Governments created environment agencies and laws on clean air and water, and concepts such as sustainability and sustainable development entered mainstream policy.
