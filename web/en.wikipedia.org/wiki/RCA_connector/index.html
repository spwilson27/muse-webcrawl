TypeElectrical connector (audio/video)

The RCA connector, also called a phono connector or Cinch connector, is a type of electrical connector commonly used to carry audio and video signals. Developed by the Radio Corporation of America in the late 1930s for internal connections in record players, it became the standard consumer connector for analogue audio and composite video for most of the 20th century.

Design

The connector consists of a central pin surrounded by a metal ring with spring fingers, mating with a jack containing a central hole and an outer contact. Plugs are usually colour-coded: red and white (or black) for right and left stereo audio, yellow for composite video, and red/green/blue sets for component video.

Usage

RCA leads connect turntables, CD players, cassette decks, amplifiers, televisions and games consoles; the same connector carries coaxial digital audio in the S/PDIF format. Although HDMI and other digital interfaces have displaced it in new equipment, the RCA connector remains ubiquitous on audio gear.
