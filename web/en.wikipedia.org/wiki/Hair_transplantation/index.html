Hair transplantation

Hair transplantation is a surgical technique that removes hair follicles from a donor site, usually the back or sides of the scalp, and implants them into a bald or balding recipient site. It is primarily used to treat male pattern baldness and female pattern hair loss.

Techniques

The two main harvesting methods are follicular unit transplantation (FUT, strip harvesting) and follicular unit extraction (FUE), in which individual follicular units are removed with small punches. Grafts are then placed into slits or holes in the recipient area, respecting natural hair direction and density.

History

Modern hair transplantation began in Japan in the 1930s and was developed in the United States in the 1950s by Norman Orentreich, who established the principle of donor dominance. Large plug grafts gave way to mini- and micrografts and then to follicular unit grafting in the 1990s.

Outcomes and risks

Transplanted hair typically sheds within weeks and regrows over 6–12 months. Risks include infection, scarring, bleeding, unnatural appearance and continued loss of native hair, sometimes requiring medication such as minoxidil or finasteride or further sessions.
