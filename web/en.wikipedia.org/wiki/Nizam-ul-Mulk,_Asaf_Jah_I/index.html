Nizam-ul-Mulk, Asaf Jah I

Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan Siddiqi
Born: 11 August 1671
Died: 1 June 1748
Reign as Nizam of Hyderabad: 1724–1748
Dynasty founded: Asaf Jahi (Nizams of Hyderabad)

Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan Siddiqi (11 August 1671 – 1 June 1748), known by his titles Chin Qilich Khan, Nizam-ul-Mulk, and Asaf Jah I, was the founder of the Asaf Jahi dynasty and the first Nizam of Hyderabad State in the Deccan. A leading nobleman of the late Mughal Empire, he established a virtually independent state that lasted until 1948.

Early career

Born into a family of Turkic origin from Samarkand that had settled in India, Qamar-ud-din entered Mughal service under Aurangzeb and distinguished himself in the Deccan wars. He served as governor of the Deccan and of Malwa, and briefly as Grand Vizier (wazir) of the empire under Muhammad Shah from 1722, where his attempts to reform the court were frustrated by factional intrigue.

Foundation of Hyderabad

Resigning the vizierate, he returned to the Deccan in 1723. When the Mughal viceroy Mubariz Khan refused to yield authority, Nizam-ul-Mulk defeated and killed him at the Battle of Shakar Kheda (11 October 1724), an event generally taken as the foundation of Hyderabad State. The emperor, unable to resist, confirmed him as viceroy of the Deccan with the title Asaf Jah. Though nominally a Mughal governor, he ruled autonomously — appointing officials, making war and peace, and conducting diplomacy with the Marathas, the French, and the English.

Later years

His last decades were occupied with the defence of the Deccan against Maratha expansion under Peshwa Baji Rao I, who defeated him at Palkhed (1728) and Bhopal (1737), and with the catastrophe of Nader Shah's invasion of Delhi in 1739, where he served as a negotiator. He died at Burhanpur in 1748, aged 77, leaving a succession contested among his sons and grandsons.

Legacy

Asaf Jah I is remembered as an able administrator and the shrewdest Indian statesman of his generation. He founded a dynasty of seven ruling Nizams whose state became the largest and wealthiest of princely India.
