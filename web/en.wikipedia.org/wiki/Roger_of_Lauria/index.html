Roger of Lauria (c. 1245 – 17 January 1305), in Catalan Roger de Llúria, was an Italian-Aragonese admiral who commanded the fleets of the Crown of Aragon during the War of the Sicilian Vespers. Victorious in every major engagement he fought — Malta, the Gulf of Naples, Les Formigues, Cape Orlando and Ponza among them — he is regarded as one of the greatest naval commanders of the Middle Ages, and is quoted as boasting that no fish could raise its head in the Mediterranean without bearing the arms of Aragon.

Early life

Roger was born at Lauria in Basilicata, in the Kingdom of Sicily, the son of Richard of Lauria, a loyal officer of Manfred of Sicily. After Charles of Anjou conquered the kingdom at Benevento (1266), the family fled to Barcelona, where Roger was raised at the court of Peter III of Aragon and grew up alongside the future Alfonso III; he married Margarita Lancia, of another exiled Sicilian family.

War of the Sicilian Vespers

After the Sicilian Vespers revolt of 1282 and Peter III's intervention in Sicily, Roger was made admiral of the Aragonese-Sicilian fleet. In 1283 he destroyed the Angevin fleet off Malta; in June 1284, in the Gulf of Naples, he captured Charles of Salerno, the Angevin heir, after feigning flight; in 1285 he annihilated the French fleet of Philip III at Les Formigues during the Aragonese Crusade; and in 1287 he won another crushing victory at the Battle of the Counts off Naples. His galleys, crewed by Catalan and Sicilian sailors and armed with skilled crossbowmen, dominated the western Mediterranean.

Later career

After the Peace of Caltabellotta settlement took shape, Roger quarrelled with Frederick III of Sicily and transferred his allegiance wholly to James II of Aragon, even fighting against his former Sicilian allies — defeating them at Cape Orlando (1299) and Ponza (1300). Rewarded with lands in the Kingdom of Valencia, including Cocentaina, he retired there and died at Valencia on 17 January 1305. He is commemorated by statues in Barcelona and Tarragona, and Spanish warships have borne his name.
