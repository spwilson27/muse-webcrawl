Gesta Hungarorum

The Gesta Hungarorum (Latin for "The Deeds of the Hungarians") is the earliest surviving — though not the first — book about Hungarian history. It was written by an author known only as Anonymus, who styled himself "P., called master, formerly notary of the most glorious King Béla", and is therefore often called the Anonymous Notary of King Béla. The work was probably completed in the late twelfth or early thirteenth century, around the year 1200. It survives in a single manuscript, discovered in the Ambras Castle library and kept since the eighteenth century in the Austrian National Library in Vienna.

Contents

The Gesta narrates the origins of the Hungarians (Magyars), their legendary ancestors Hunor and Magor, their departure from Scythia, and their conquest of the Carpathian Basin (the Honfoglalás) under the seven chieftains led by Álmos and his son Árpád. Anonymus describes the peoples and rulers the Hungarians found and defeated in the basin — including figures such as Gelou in Transylvania, Glad in the Banat and Menumorut in Bihar — and derives many Hungarian place names from invented personal names and events.

Historical value

Historians treat the Gesta with caution. Written some three centuries after the conquest, it projects the political geography and ethnic relations of the author's own time back onto the ninth century, and many of its named opponents appear to be fictional or anachronistic. At the same time, it preserves genuine traditions, titles (such as gyula and kende), and descriptions of customs, and it remains a foundational text of Hungarian national historiography. The debate over whether rulers such as Gelou actually existed has been especially charged because of its implications for the early history of Transylvania.

Anonymus

The author's identity is unknown; King Béla in question is usually identified as Béla III (r. 1172–1196). A statue of the hooded, faceless Anonymus by Miklós Ligeti (1903) stands in Vajdahunyad Castle in Budapest's City Park and is one of the city's best-known monuments.
