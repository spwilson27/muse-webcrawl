Shechem

Shechem (Hebrew: שְׁכֶם, Šəḵem, "shoulder") was an ancient city in the southern Levant, located in the mountain pass between Mount Gerizim and Mount Ebal, at the site of Tell Balata just east of the modern city of Nablus in the West Bank. It was an important Canaanite city-state and later a major Israelite centre.

History

Shechem is attested in Egyptian texts of the 19th century BC and in the Amarna letters, whose ruler Labaya expanded his influence across the central hill country. Excavations revealed massive Middle Bronze Age fortifications, including a cyclopean wall and a fortress-temple. In the Hebrew Bible, Shechem is where Abraham first built an altar in Canaan, where Jacob bought land and where his daughter Dinah was avenged by her brothers; Joseph's bones were said to be buried there, at the site still venerated as Joseph's Tomb.

After Solomon's death, the northern tribes met at Shechem and crowned Jeroboam, making it the first capital of the Kingdom of Israel before the seat moved to Tirzah and Samaria. Following the Assyrian destruction of the north, the area became the centre of the Samaritan community, whose temple stood on Mount Gerizim. The Hellenistic city was destroyed by John Hyrcanus around 108 BC; the Romans built the new city of Neapolis — modern Nablus — nearby.
