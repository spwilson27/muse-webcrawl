Seal (emblem)

A seal is a device used to make an impression in wax, clay, paper or another medium, and also the impression so produced; in heraldic and governmental usage, a seal is an official emblem of a state, office, institution or person, used to authenticate documents. Seals have been used since the earliest civilisations of Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley.

History

Cylinder seals rolled across wet clay were characteristic of ancient Mesopotamia, while stamp seals were used in Egypt, the Indus Valley and China, where the seal (yìnzhāng) became both an instrument of authority and an art form. In medieval Europe the seal, impressed in wax and appended to charters, was the principal means of authenticating documents for rulers, nobles, towns and ecclesiastical bodies; great seals of state, with obverse and reverse designs, remain in ceremonial use, as with the Great Seal of the United Kingdom and the Great Seal of the United States (adopted 1782).

Modern usage

Today seals serve as official symbols — the Seal of the President of the United States, university seals, corporate seals — and as embossing or stamping devices that certify authenticity. The study of seals is called sigillography or sphragistics.
