Electrical double layer

Electrical double layer
Field: electrochemistry, colloid science
Key length: Debye length
Models: Helmholtz, Gouy–Chapman, Stern

An electrical double layer is the structure of ions and charges that forms at the interface between a charged surface and an electrolyte solution. The surface charge attracts a layer of counter-ions, producing two parallel layers of opposite charge that govern the behaviour of colloids, electrodes, and biological membranes.

Structure

In the Stern model, the double layer consists of an inner compact layer of specifically adsorbed ions (the Stern or Helmholtz layer) and an outer diffuse layer in which ion concentrations grade smoothly into the bulk solution, as described by the Gouy–Chapman theory. The thickness of the diffuse layer is characterised by the Debye length, typically a few nanometres in aqueous solutions.

Zeta potential

When a particle moves through a liquid, the potential at the slipping plane between the bound and free fluid is called the zeta potential. It determines the stability of colloidal dispersions: particles with a large zeta potential repel one another and remain suspended.

Applications

Double-layer effects underlie supercapacitors (electric double-layer capacitors), electrokinetic phenomena such as electrophoresis and electro-osmosis, and the charging of mineral surfaces in water treatment and soil science.
