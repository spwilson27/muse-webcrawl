Scrotum

SystemReproductive system
ContentsTestes, epididymides, spermatic cords
PrecursorLabioscrotal swellings

The scrotum is a sac of skin and smooth muscle that hangs outside the body, behind and below the penis, and contains the testes and associated structures in most terrestrial male mammals. Its position outside the abdominal cavity keeps the testes at a temperature a few degrees below core body temperature, which is required for normal sperm production.

Structure

The scrotum is divided into two compartments by a septum; externally the division is marked by a ridge of skin called the raphe. Beneath the skin lies the dartos fascia, whose contraction wrinkles the scrotal skin and draws the testes closer to the body in cold conditions, while the cremaster muscle raises or lowers each testis, together providing temperature regulation.

Development and function

The scrotum develops from the labioscrotal swellings, which are homologous to the labia majora in females. During development the testes descend from the abdomen into the scrotum through the inguinal canals. Failure of descent (cryptorchidism) is a recognised clinical condition.

Clinical significance

Conditions affecting the scrotum include hydrocele (fluid accumulation), varicocele (enlarged veins of the pampiniform plexus), epididymitis, testicular torsion — a surgical emergency — and the rare but serious skin infection Fournier gangrene.
