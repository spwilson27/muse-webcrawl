G4 nations
MembersBrazil, Germany, India, Japan
Formed2004
PurposeMutual support for permanent seats on the UN Security Council

The G4 nations, comprising Brazil, Germany, India, and Japan, are four countries which support each other's bids for permanent seats on the United Nations Security Council. The group was formed in 2004, and its members jointly proposed a framework resolution on Security Council reform in 2005, ahead of the UN's 60th anniversary.

Proposal

The G4 proposal called for expanding the Security Council from 15 to 25 members, adding six new permanent seats — the four G4 countries plus two African states — and four new non-permanent seats. New permanent members would initially forgo the veto, accepting a review after fifteen years. Unlike the current five permanent members, the G4 countries are among the largest financial contributors to the UN and major regional powers not represented permanently on the Council.

Opposition

The bids face opposition from the Uniting for Consensus group (including Italy, Pakistan, South Korea, Argentina and Mexico), which favours expanding only non-permanent seats, arguing that new permanent seats would entrench hierarchy. Regional rivalries — China toward Japan and India, Pakistan toward India, Argentina and Mexico toward Brazil, and Italy and Spain toward Germany — have also hindered consensus. Despite repeated joint statements and renewed pushes, Security Council reform has remained stalled in intergovernmental negotiations.
