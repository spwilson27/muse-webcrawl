Académie Colarossi

The Académie Colarossi was an art school in Paris founded by the Italian sculptor Filippo Colarossi. Located first on the Île de la Cité and later at 10 rue de la Grande-Chaumière in Montparnasse, it was one of the most important alternatives to the official École des Beaux-Arts in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

Teaching

Unlike the École des Beaux-Arts, which long barred women from life drawing, the Colarossi admitted women on equal terms and allowed them to draw from nude models — a principal reason for its popularity with foreign and women students. Fees were modest and paid weekly; instruction was given by visiting professors who corrected students' work several times a week, and twice-weekly croquis (sketch) sessions were held in the evenings.

Notable students

  • Paul Gauguin
  • Camille Claudel
  • Amedeo Modigliani
  • Émile Bernard
  • Helen Hyde and many other international artists

Teachers associated with the academy included the sculptors Jean-Antoine Injalbert and, for summer courses at the associated Grande Chaumière, Antoine Bourdelle. The school closed in the 1930s; its premises were absorbed by the neighbouring Académie de la Grande Chaumière.
