The Black Army (Hungarian: Fekete sereg) was the standing mercenary army of King Matthias Corvinus of Hungary in the second half of the fifteenth century. It is regarded as one of the first professional standing armies in Europe since Roman times and was instrumental in making Hungary a major military power of the era.

Formation and organisation

Rather than relying on the unreliable levies of the nobility, Matthias built a paid force of mercenaries recruited from Hungarians, Czechs, Poles, Germans and Serbs. At its peak the core force numbered around 28,000 men, combining heavy and light cavalry, infantry — including handgunners and crossbowmen — artillery and a Danube war flotilla. The name "Black Army" is associated with the black armour and insignia associated with its soldiers, and with the nickname of one of its commanders, John Haugwitz.

Campaigns

The Black Army held the southern frontier against the Ottoman Empire, famously supporting the victory at the Battle of Breadfield (1479), and carried Matthias's ambitions north-westward: it conquered much of the Austrian hereditary lands, occupying Vienna in 1485 and Wiener Neustadt in 1487, as well as parts of Bohemia, Moravia, Silesia and Lusatia in the struggle with the Bohemian crown.

Disbandment

The army depended entirely on Matthias's extraordinary taxation. After his death in 1490 the estates elected the weaker Vladislaus II, revenues collapsed, and the unpaid mercenaries turned to plundering before being defeated and formally disbanded by 1494. Hungary's loss of a standing royal army contributed to its later vulnerability, culminating in the disaster at Mohács in 1526.
