The Prinsengracht is a canal in Amsterdam, the Netherlands. It is the third and outermost of the three main canals of the city's canal belt (grachtengordel), alongside the Herengracht and the Keizersgracht. Together the three canals form concentric belts around the old centre of the city.

The Prinsengracht was named after the Prince of Orange. Construction of the canal belt began in 1612 as part of a major expansion of Amsterdam during the Dutch Golden Age, and the Prinsengracht was dug at the western and southern edges of the growing city.

Landmarks

Many notable buildings line the Prinsengracht. The most famous is the Anne Frank House at Prinsengracht 263, where Anne Frank and her family hid during the Second World War and where she wrote her diary. Nearby stands the Westerkerk, the church whose tower is mentioned in the diary.

Other sights along the canal include the Houseboat Museum, the historic Noorderkerk near its northern end, and numerous monumental canal houses, warehouses and houseboats. The canal runs roughly 3.2 kilometres from the Brouwersgracht in the north to the Amstel river in the east.

Canal belt

In 2010 the seventeenth-century canal ring area of Amsterdam, including the Prinsengracht, was placed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, in recognition of its pioneering town planning and its distinctive gabled canal houses.
