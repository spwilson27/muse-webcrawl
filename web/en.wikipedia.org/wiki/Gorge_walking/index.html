Gorge walking

Gorge walking is an outdoor activity involving travelling through a steep-sided river gorge on foot, typically by walking, scrambling, climbing, swimming and jumping into plunge pools along the course of the stream. It is commonly offered as a group adventure activity, particularly in upland areas of the United Kingdom such as Wales, the Lake District and Scotland.

Description

Participants usually wear wetsuits, buoyancy aids, helmets and sturdy footwear, and progress upstream or downstream along the watercourse, negotiating waterfalls, slides, rock steps and deep pools. Organised sessions are led by qualified instructors and graded by difficulty; the activity differs from canyoning mainly in emphasis and terminology, gorge walking in Britain generally involving ascending or traversing a gorge rather than descending with ropes, though the terms overlap.

Safety

Key hazards include cold water, slippery rock, rising water levels after rain and jumping into water of unknown depth. Providers typically check river levels, equip participants with protective clothing, and use supervised jumps and roped sections where necessary.
