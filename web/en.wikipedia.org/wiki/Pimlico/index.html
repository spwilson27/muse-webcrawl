Pimlico

Pimlico is an area of Central London in the City of Westminster, built as a southern extension to neighbouring Belgravia during the 19th century. It is known for its garden squares and grand Regency-style stucco terraces, many now protected as listed buildings within a conservation area.

Geography and history

Pimlico lies south of Victoria station, bounded by the River Thames to the south, Vauxhall Bridge Road and Victoria to the north, and Chelsea to the west. The land was largely marshy before the master builder Thomas Cubitt developed it from the 1820s onwards for the Grosvenor estate; streets such as St George's Square, Warwick Square, and Eccleston Square were laid out with tall white stucco houses. The name "Pimlico" is of uncertain origin and appears in the 17th century, possibly from a once-popular ale or a local landowner.

Landmarks

Tate Britain stands on Millbank at Pimlico's southern edge, and the area is served by Pimlico tube station on the Victoria line, opened in 1972 — the last station added to that line. Dolphin Square, a large 1930s block of flats, and the Pimlico District Heating Undertaking, which pioneered district heating in Britain using waste heat from Battersea Power Station, are also notable.
