Manitoulin Island is a Canadian island in Lake Huron, in the province of Ontario. With an area of 2,766 square kilometres, it is the largest freshwater island in the world — an island in a lake that is itself large enough to contain more than a hundred lakes of its own, including Lake Manitou, the largest lake on a freshwater island.

Geography

The island forms part of the Niagara Escarpment and separates the main body of Lake Huron from Georgian Bay and the North Channel. It is connected to the mainland by the Little Current swing bridge, one of the oldest swing bridges of its kind, and by a seasonal ferry from Tobermory.

History and communities

Manitoulin's name derives from Manidoowaaling, "cave of the spirit", in the Ojibwe language. Anishinaabe peoples have lived on the island for millennia. Today it contains several First Nations reserves, including Wikwemikong, the only officially unceded Indigenous territory in Canada, as well as towns such as Little Current, Gore Bay and Mindemoya. Tourism, farming and fishing are the main industries.
