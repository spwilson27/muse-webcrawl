Consumer Financial Protection Bureau

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) is an independent agency of the United States government responsible for consumer protection in the financial sector. It was created by the Dodd–Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act of 2010 in response to the financial crisis of 2007–2008.

History

The bureau was proposed by then–Harvard Law professor Elizabeth Warren, who chaired the congressional oversight panel for the Troubled Asset Relief Program. Richard Cordray became its first Senate-confirmed director in 2013. The CFPB began operations on July 21, 2011, consolidating consumer-protection functions previously spread across seven federal agencies.

Functions

The CFPB writes and enforces rules for financial products such as mortgages, credit cards, student loans and payday loans; supervises large banks and nonbank financial companies; takes consumer complaints; and conducts financial education programmes. Its enforcement actions have returned billions of dollars to consumers.

Structure and funding

The bureau is led by a single director appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate for a five-year term. It is funded through transfers from the Federal Reserve rather than congressional appropriations, a design intended to insulate it from political pressure. In Seila Law LLC v. CFPB (2020), the Supreme Court held that the director must be removable by the President at will.
