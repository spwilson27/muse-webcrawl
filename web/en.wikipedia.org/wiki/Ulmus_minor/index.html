Ulmus minor

Kingdom: Plantae
Family: Ulmaceae
Genus: Ulmus
Species: U. minor Mill.
Common names: Field elm, smooth-leaved elm complex

Ulmus minor Mill., the field elm, is an elm species native to southern and central Europe, North Africa and southwestern Asia. It is a highly variable and taxonomically complex species, encompassing many regional forms formerly treated as separate species, including the English elm (U. procera) and the Atinian elm.

Description

The field elm is a deciduous tree typically growing to 20–30 metres, often reproducing vigorously by root suckers, which form hedgerow thickets. Its leaves are smaller and smoother than those of the wych elm, with a glossy upper surface. The samaras are notched at the tip, with the seed positioned close to the notch.

History and disease

Field elms were planted for millennia around the Mediterranean and were carried across Europe by human activity; the English elm was introduced to Britain, probably in the Roman era, and became a defining feature of the lowland landscape. Because suckering produces genetically identical clones, populations proved catastrophically vulnerable to Dutch elm disease, which destroyed most mature trees in the twentieth century.

Cultivation

Hedgerow suckers continue to regenerate in many areas but usually succumb to disease before reaching maturity. Disease-resistant field elm selections, such as those released in Spain, are used in replanting programmes.
