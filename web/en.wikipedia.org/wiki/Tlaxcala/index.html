Country: Mexico
Capital: Tlaxcala de Xicohténcatl
Largest city: Vicente Guerrero
Area: 3,991 km² — smallest state
Population: ~1.34 million (2020)

Tlaxcala (Nahuatl: Tlaxcallān, 'place of maize tortillas') is one of the 32 federal entities of Mexico. It is the smallest state by area, located in east-central Mexico, bordered by Puebla, Hidalgo and the State of Mexico.

History

Pre-Columbian Tlaxcala was a confederation of four altepetl — Tizatlan, Ocotelolco, Quiahuiztlan and Tepeticpac — that resisted Aztec domination and was never conquered by the Mexica. In 1519 the Tlaxcalans allied with Hernán Cortés against the Aztec Empire, supplying thousands of warriors crucial to the fall of Tenochtitlan. In return they retained privileges under Spanish rule, including their own cabildo and exemption from some encomienda demands.

Geography

The state lies on the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt. Its highest point is the dormant volcano La Malinche (Matlalcueitl), 4,462 m, shared with Puebla and protected as a national park.

Economy and culture

Economy centres on agriculture, textiles, and manufacturing near Puebla. Tlaxcala is famous for its colonial capital — among the earliest Spanish cities in Mexico — its bullfighting tradition, pulque production, and the vast sawdust-and-flower carpets (alfombras) of Huamantla's La Noche Que Nadie Duerme festival.
