Soil mark


Soil mark — a discoloration in the soil revealing a buried archaeological feature.

A soil mark is a difference in the colour or texture of a soil surface that indicates the presence of a buried archaeological feature. Soil marks are one of the principal ways in which archaeologists detect sites that are otherwise invisible on the ground, particularly in ploughed fields and in aerial photographs.

Formation

When a pit, ditch, or other feature is dug into the ground and later filled in, the fill usually differs from the surrounding natural soil. A backfilled ditch may contain darker, humus-rich material, while a buried wall or road surface may lie over stony or pale deposits. When ploughing gradually levels the surface, these contrasting fills appear as visible patterns at the surface: darker marks typically indicate deeper features such as ditches and pits, and lighter marks indicate shallower features such as walls, foundations, or surfaces.

Detection

Soil marks are most visible in freshly ploughed fields, especially after rain or in raking light, and they form a class of evidence used alongside cropmarks and shadow marks in aerial archaeology. Fieldwalking and geophysical survey are often used to test marks identified from the air.
