Anthozoa

Anthozoa is a class of marine invertebrates in the phylum Cnidaria that includes the sea anemones, stony corals, soft corals, and sea pens. Unlike other cnidarians, anthozoans exist only in the polyp stage throughout their lives — they have no medusa (jellyfish) phase. The name comes from the Greek anthos ("flower") and zoa ("animals"), referring to the flower-like appearance of the polyps.

Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Cnidaria
Class: Anthozoa Ehrenberg, 1834
Subclasses: Hexacorallia, Octocorallia, Ceriantharia

Anatomy

An anthozoan polyp has a cylindrical body topped by an oral disc ringed with tentacles surrounding a central mouth. The mouth leads through a flattened pharynx (stomodaeum), often with a ciliated groove called a siphonoglyph, into a gastrovascular cavity partitioned by complete or incomplete septa (mesenteries) that increase surface area for digestion and bear the gonads.

Diversity and ecology

The Hexacorallia (with sixfold symmetry) include stony corals and sea anemones; the Octocorallia (eightfold symmetry) include soft corals, sea fans, and sea pens. Reef-building stony corals host symbiotic photosynthetic algae (zooxanthellae) and construct the calcium carbonate skeletons underlying coral reefs. Anthozoans number more than 6,000 described species and are distributed in oceans worldwide, from intertidal zones to abyssal depths.
