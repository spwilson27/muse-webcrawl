Dwarf (Middle-earth)

Dwarves
Created by: J. R. R. Tolkien
Names: Khazâd (Khuzdul), Hadhodrim
Homelands: Khazad-dûm, Erebor, Iron Hills

In the fantasy of J. R. R. Tolkien, the Dwarves are a race inhabiting Middle-earth, central to The Hobbit and a major presence in The Lord of the Rings. Short, stocky, and bearded, they are famed as miners, smiths, and craftsmen, dwelling in great underground halls such as Khazad-dûm and the Lonely Mountain.

Origins

Unlike Elves and Men, the Dwarves were created by the Vala Aulë, impatient for the coming of Ilúvatar's Children; Ilúvatar adopted them but decreed they sleep until after the Firstborn had awakened. Seven Fathers of the Dwarves founded the seven clans; the best known is Durin's Folk, the Longbeards. Their maker gave them great endurance, resistance to evil domination, and lifespans of some 200–300 years.

Culture

Dwarves speak Khuzdul, a language kept largely secret, and use the Cirth runes for writing. They are fiercely loyal, remember both friendships and grievances for generations, and prize gold, gems, and fine workmanship — traits that repeatedly draw them into conflict with Elves and dragons. Notable Dwarves include Thorin Oakenshield, Balin, Gimli son of Glóin (a member of the Fellowship of the Ring), and Durin the Deathless.

Sources

Tolkien drew his Dwarves chiefly from Norse mythology, including the dwarf-names listed in the Völuspá, most of which he assigned to Thorin's company in The Hobbit.
