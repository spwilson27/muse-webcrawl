A working dog is a dog trained to perform practical tasks, as opposed to a companion or show dog. Working dogs carry out a wide range of jobs for humans, exploiting canine senses, strength, speed and trainability; many breeds were developed specifically for a working role and are grouped by kennel clubs into working or pastoral groups.

A Border Collie working sheep

Types of work

Herding dogs such as the Border Collie gather and move livestock, while livestock guardian dogs — including the Great Pyrenees and Anatolian Shepherd — live with flocks and deter predators. Hunting dogs divide into retrievers, pointers, setters, spaniels and hounds. Sled dogs haul freight and people in Arctic regions. Detection dogs use their sense of smell, estimated to be vastly more sensitive than a human's, to find explosives, narcotics, diseases, cadavers and contraband for customs, police and military forces. Service dogs assist people with disabilities, including guide dogs for the blind, hearing dogs and mobility assistance dogs, and dogs also work in search and rescue, therapy, and conservation.

Characteristics

Working breeds are typically intelligent, energetic and strongly motivated by work itself, which makes them rewarding for experienced handlers but often challenging as sedentary pets. Modern working lines are frequently bred separately from show lines, prioritising health, drive and working ability over appearance.
