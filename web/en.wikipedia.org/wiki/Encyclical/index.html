Encyclical

An encyclical is a letter sent by the pope to bishops and the faithful of the Catholic Church — sometimes addressed more broadly to all people of good will — teaching on matters of doctrine, morals, or discipline. The word derives from the Greek enkyklios, "circular." In the Eastern Orthodox Church and the Anglican Communion, the term has also been used for letters sent by bishops as a group.

Usage and authority

Modern papal encyclicals begin with Pius IX's Ubi Primum (1849); Leo XIII issued a record number on social and philosophical questions, including Rerum Novarum (1891), the foundational document of Catholic social teaching. Encyclicals rank below dogmatic definitions and apostolic constitutions in authority but are an important expression of the pope's ordinary magisterium. They are usually titled by their opening Latin words.

Notable encyclicals

Famous examples include Pacem in Terris (1963) on peace, Humanae Vitae (1968) on contraception, Laborem Exercens (1981) on work, Evangelium Vitae (1995), and Pope Francis's Laudato si' (2015) on care for the environment and Fratelli tutti (2020) on fraternity. Benedict XVI's Deus Caritas Est (2005) addressed Christian love.
