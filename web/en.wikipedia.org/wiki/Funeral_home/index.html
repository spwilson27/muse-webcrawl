Funeral home

A funeral home, funeral parlor or mortuary, is a business that provides burial and funeral services for the dead and their families. These services may include a prepared wake and funeral, the provision of a chapel for the funeral, preparation and care of the body, and arrangements for interment or cremation.

Services

Funeral homes employ licensed funeral directors and embalmers who arrange services according to the wishes of surviving family members and the deceased. Facilities typically include viewing rooms or parlors for visitation, a chapel, a selection room displaying coffins and urns, preparation rooms, and vehicles such as hearses and limousines for the funeral procession.

History

Before the late 19th century, funerals were usually held in the home of the deceased. As embalming became common during and after the American Civil War, specialist establishments emerged; many early funeral homes grew out of furniture stores and livery stables, which supplied coffins and horses. By the mid-20th century the purpose-built funeral home had become the standard venue for funeral services in North America.

Industry

The industry includes both independent, family-owned businesses and large corporate chains. In the United States, the Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule requires funeral providers to give consumers itemized price information and prohibits misrepresentations about legal requirements such as embalming.
