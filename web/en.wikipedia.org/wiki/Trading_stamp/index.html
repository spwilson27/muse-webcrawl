Trading stamp

Trading stamps were small paper stamps given to customers by merchants in loyalty programmes in the United States and other countries from the 1890s through the 1980s. Customers collected the stamps, typically pasting them into booklets, and redeemed filled books for merchandise from the stamp company's catalogue or redemption centre. They are considered an early form of the modern loyalty programme.

S&H Green Stamps pasted into a saver book.

History

Retailers experimented with stamp and coupon premiums from the late 19th century, but the industry was transformed by the Sperry & Hutchinson Company, founded in 1896, whose S&H Green Stamps became the dominant American scheme. Supermarkets, petrol stations, and department stores bought stamps from S&H and gave shoppers one stamp per ten cents spent. At their peak in the 1960s, S&H printed more stamps than the U.S. Post Office issued postage stamps, and its catalogue was one of the largest publications in the country. Competitors included Top Value Stamps, Gold Bond Stamps, and Plaid Stamps.

In the United Kingdom, Green Shield Stamps (founded 1958) followed a similar model; its founder Richard Tompkins later applied the catalogue-redemption idea to the Argos chain of catalogue shops.

Decline

Trading stamps declined during the 1970s as inflation, the energy crisis, and price competition pushed retailers toward discount pricing instead of premiums, and several U.S. states had earlier tried to regulate or tax the schemes. S&H Green Stamps faded by the 1980s, though the brand was briefly revived online as "greenpoints". Loyalty functions passed to frequent-flyer miles, credit-card points, and store loyalty cards.

Economics

Merchants paid roughly 2–3% of sales for stamps, betting that the programme would attract and retain customers rather than simply discounting prices. Critics argued the cost was passed on in higher prices, and economists debated whether stamps distorted competition — a debate that reached the courts and the Federal Trade Commission.
