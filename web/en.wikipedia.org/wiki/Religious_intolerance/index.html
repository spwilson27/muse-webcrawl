Religious intolerance

Religious intolerance is intolerance of another's religious beliefs, practices and adherents, ranging from mild bigotry to mass violence. It differs from religious discrimination in that intolerance describes an attitude or social condition, whereas discrimination refers to concrete unequal treatment, though the two frequently overlap. Religious intolerance may be expressed by individuals, social groups, or states, and may target both religious minorities and non-religious people.

Historical examples

Instances include the Roman persecution of Christians, the expulsion of Jews and Muslims from Spain, the European wars of religion, the Inquisition, and modern episodes of communal violence. In the 20th century, state atheism in some communist countries produced intolerance toward religion generally, while elsewhere religious nationalism has fuelled conflict.

Contemporary issues

Scholars distinguish levels of intolerance, from intolerance of belief, of practice, and of the presence of the other group. Reports by bodies such as the Pew Research Center and the United Nations Special Rapporteur on freedom of religion or belief track government restrictions and social hostilities involving religion worldwide. Efforts to combat intolerance include interfaith dialogue, education, and legal guarantees of religious freedom.
