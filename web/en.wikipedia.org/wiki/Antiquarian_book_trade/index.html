Antiquarian book trade

The antiquarian book trade is the branch of the bookselling trade concerned with rare, collectible, out-of-print and historically significant books and manuscripts. Antiquarian booksellers typically deal in first editions, fine bindings, illustrated books, incunabula, maps, prints and ephemera, and their customers include private collectors, libraries and museums.

History

The trade developed alongside printing itself: by the 16th century, auction sales of libraries were common in the Netherlands and England. London, Paris, Amsterdam and later New York became major centres. In the 19th and 20th centuries the trade formalised through associations such as the Antiquarian Booksellers' Association (founded 1906 in Britain) and the International League of Antiquarian Booksellers (ILAB, founded 1948).

Practices

Dealers describe books using a shared bibliographical vocabulary covering edition, impression, binding, condition and provenance. Prices depend on scarcity, condition, completeness, association value and market demand. Sales take place through shops, printed and online catalogues, book fairs and auctions. Since the 1990s, internet marketplaces and meta-search sites have transformed the previously localised trade.

Notable dealers

Historically significant firms include Maggs Bros and Quaritch in London, and the Strand and Argosy in New York. The trade has also attracted notable forgers and thieves, making provenance research and security marking an important part of professional practice.
