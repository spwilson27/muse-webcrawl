Black walnut

Scientific nameJuglans nigra
FamilyJuglandaceae
Native rangeEastern North America
UsesTimber, edible nuts, dye

The black walnut (Juglans nigra) is a species of deciduous tree in the walnut family, native to eastern North America. It is prized both for its dark, straight-grained timber — among the most valuable North American hardwoods — and for its edible nuts.

Description

Black walnut grows to 30-40 metres tall on good sites, with pinnate leaves and rough, dark bark. The nuts ripen inside green husks in autumn; the kernel is rich and strongly flavoured but the shell is exceptionally hard. The roots, leaves, and husks produce juglone, a compound that inhibits the growth of many other plants (allelopathy), which gardeners notice when tomatoes and other sensitive plants fail near walnut trees.

Uses

The wood is used for furniture, cabinetry, gunstocks, and veneer. The nuts are gathered wild and from selected cultivars for food, and the husks yield a dark brown dye historically used for cloth and hair.
