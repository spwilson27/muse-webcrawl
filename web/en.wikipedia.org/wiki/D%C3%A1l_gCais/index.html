Dál gCais

The Dál gCais (English: Dalcassians) were a Gaelic Irish dynasty and regional kingdom in the region now known as County Clare and neighbouring parts of Munster, in the south-west of Ireland. Rising to prominence in the 10th century, they produced Ireland's most famous high king, Brian Boru, who defeated the combined forces of the Norse of Dublin and the King of Leinster at the Battle of Clontarf in 1014.

Origins

According to tradition, the Dál gCais traced their descent from Cas, a son of Conall Echlúath, and belonged to the Déisi Tuisceart, the northern Déisi, who settled east of the River Shannon. Their ancestral territory, later known as Thomond (Tuadmumu, "North Munster"), lay around the modern County Clare.

Rise to power

Under kings such as Cennétig mac Lorcáin and his sons Mathgamain and Brian, the Dál gCais expanded rapidly in the 10th century, capturing Cashel, the traditional seat of the Kings of Munster, in 967. Brian Boru went on to become High King of Ireland, breaking the centuries-long monopoly of the Uí Néill dynasties on the high kingship.

Later history

After Brian's death at Clontarf, his descendants, who took the surname Ó Briain (O'Brien), remained Kings of Thomond for centuries. The kingdom survived as a semi-independent lordship until it was surrendered to the Tudor crown in 1543, when Murrough O'Brien became the first Earl of Thomond. Other families claiming Dalcassian descent include the MacMahons, Kennedys, and Considines.
