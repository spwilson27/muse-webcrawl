Interconnector

An interconnector (also intertie) in electricity supply is a high-voltage link, often a submarine or underground cable, connecting the electricity grids of two countries or regions so that power can be traded and shared between them.

Interconnectors improve energy security, allow balancing of variable renewable generation across wider areas, and enable price convergence between markets. Examples include the IFA link between Great Britain and France, the Viking Link between Britain and Denmark (the world's longest land and subsea interconnector when it opened in 2023), and the North Sea Link between Britain and Norway.

Most long-distance interconnectors use high-voltage direct current (HVDC) technology to reduce losses and connect asynchronous grids.
