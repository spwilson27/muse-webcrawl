Jacob Mincer (July 15, 1922 – August 20, 2006) was a Polish-born American economist, widely regarded as the father of modern labor economics. A longtime professor at Columbia University, he pioneered the empirical study of earnings, education, and training.

Life and career

Mincer was born in Tomaszów Lubelski, Poland. He survived imprisonment in concentration camps in Czechoslovakia and Germany during World War II, emigrated to the United States in 1948, and received his PhD from Columbia University in 1957. He spent most of his career at Columbia and the National Bureau of Economic Research.

Contributions

Mincer's 1974 book Schooling, Experience, and Earnings introduced the Mincer earnings function, which models log earnings as a function of years of schooling and years of labor-market experience (with a quadratic experience term). The equation remains one of the most widely estimated relationships in empirical economics and underpins the human capital framework developed alongside Gary Becker and Theodore Schultz.

He also made foundational contributions on female labor supply, on-the-job training, and the economics of the family. In 2004 he received the first IZA Prize in Labor Economics.

Selected works

"Investment in Human Capital and Personal Income Distribution" (1958); Schooling, Experience, and Earnings (1974); "Intercountry Comparisons of Endowment and Returns to Schooling" and related papers on labor supply.
