Appeal of 18 June

Appel du 18 Juin
Date: 18 June 1940
Speaker: Charles de Gaulle
Medium: BBC Radio, London

The Appeal of 18 June (Appel du 18 juin) was a speech by French general Charles de Gaulle broadcast by BBC Radio from London on 18 June 1940, calling on the French to resist Nazi occupation after the fall of France.

Content

De Gaulle declared that France had lost a battle but not the war, invoking the resources of the French Empire, British support, and American industry: the flame of French resistance must not and will not go out.

Legacy

Few heard it live, but a poster version spread in occupied France and it became the founding act of Free France. UNESCO listed the appeal in Memory of the World. No recording of the original broadcast survives; de Gaulle re-recorded a version later.
