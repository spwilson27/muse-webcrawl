Atiyah–Singer index theorem

The Atiyah–Singer index theorem, proved by Michael Atiyah and Isadore Singer in 1963, is a fundamental result in differential geometry stating that for an elliptic differential operator on a compact manifold, the analytical index (related to the dimension of the space of solutions) is equal to the topological index (defined in terms of some topological data). It includes many other theorems, such as the Riemann–Roch theorem, as special cases, and has applications in theoretical physics.

Statement

Let D be an elliptic differential operator between vector bundles over a compact manifold X. The analytical index is the difference dim ker D − dim coker D, a purely analytic quantity defined by solving a differential equation. The theorem states that this equals a topological quantity computed from the characteristic classes of the bundles and the tangent bundle of X, integrated over X:

index(D) = (−1)n ∫X ch(σ(D)) · Td(TX ⊗ ℂ)

where σ(D) is the symbol of the operator, ch the Chern character and Td the Todd class.

History

The theorem grew out of work by Israel Gelfand, who observed around 1960 that the index seemed to be a homotopy invariant and asked for a topological formula. Atiyah and Singer announced the result in 1963 and published proofs using cobordism theory and then K-theory. Later proofs using pseudodifferential operators and, in the 1980s, supersymmetric quantum mechanics and heat-kernel methods (notably those of Ezra Getzler and others following Edward Witten's insight) made the theorem a bridge between analysis, topology and physics.

Special cases and consequences

  • The Riemann–Roch theorem and its generalisation by Hirzebruch to complex manifolds.
  • The Hirzebruch signature theorem for the signature operator.
  • The Gauss–Bonnet theorem via the Euler characteristic.
  • The counting of zero modes of Dirac operators, later central to the theory of anomalies in quantum field theory.

Recognition

Atiyah and Singer received the Abel Prize in 2004 "for their discovery and proof of the index theorem, bringing together topology, geometry and analysis, and their outstanding role in building new bridges between mathematics and theoretical physics."
