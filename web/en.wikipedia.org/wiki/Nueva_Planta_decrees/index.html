Nueva Planta decrees

The Nueva Planta decrees (Spanish: Decretos de Nueva Planta; Catalan: Decrets de Nova Planta) were a series of decrees issued between 1707 and 1716 by King Philip V of Spain, the first Bourbon king of Spain, during and after the War of the Spanish Succession. They abolished the traditional laws, institutions, and privileges (fueros) of the territories of the Crown of Aragon — Aragon, Valencia, Mallorca, and Catalonia — which had supported Philip's rival, Archduke Charles of Austria, and imposed a centralized administration modelled on Castile and absolutist France.

Provisions

The decrees dissolved the representative institutions of the Aragonese territories (the Corts and the Generalitat), replaced viceroys with captains-general, introduced Castilian law and the Castilian language in administration and the courts, and reorganized taxation and local government along Castilian lines. The University of Barcelona was closed and replaced by the University of Cervera. Philip justified the measures by right of conquest and by the territories' "rebellion".

Consequences

The decrees effectively created a unitary Spanish state for the first time, ending the composite monarchy in which the Aragonese lands had governed themselves under their own laws. They remain a grievance in Catalan nationalism, where the fall of Barcelona on 11 September 1714 — commemorated as Catalonia's national day, the Diada — symbolizes the loss of Catalan self-government.
