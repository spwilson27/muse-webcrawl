Big Tech

Big Tech
Core companiesAlphabet (Google), Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft
AcronymsGAFAM; FAANG (with Netflix)
Focus of debateMarket dominance, antitrust, privacy, content moderation

Big Tech is a term for the largest and most influential technology companies, generally the five American firms Alphabet (parent of Google), Amazon, Apple, Meta, and Microsoft — collectively known by the acronym GAFAM. A related grouping, FAANG, instead lists Facebook (Meta), Amazon, Apple, Netflix, and Google.

Dominance and criticism

Big Tech companies dominate online search, e-commerce, social media, mobile operating systems, cloud computing, and digital advertising, and rank among the most valuable public companies in the world. Their scale has drawn criticism over market power, tax practices, data privacy, misinformation and content moderation, treatment of workers, and environmental impact.

Regulation

Governments have responded with antitrust investigations and lawsuits (including U.S. cases against Google and Meta, and earlier cases against Microsoft), and new regulatory regimes such as the European Union's Digital Markets Act and Digital Services Act, which impose obligations on designated "gatekeeper" platforms. Congressional hearings in the United States and inquiries in the UK, Australia, and elsewhere have examined the firms' competitive practices.
