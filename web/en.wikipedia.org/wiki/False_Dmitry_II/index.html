False Dmitry II

The pretender's camp at Tushino

False Dmitry II (died 21 December 1610), also known as the Tushino Thief, was the second of three pretenders who claimed to be Tsarevich Dmitry Ivanovich, the youngest son of Ivan the Terrible, during Russia's Time of Troubles. His true identity is unknown. He appeared in 1607, the year after the first False Dmitry was killed in Moscow, claiming to have miraculously survived that uprising as well.

The Tushino camp

Backed by Polish magnates and Cossacks, the pretender gathered an army and in 1608 advanced on Moscow. Unable to take the capital, he established a fortified camp at the village of Tushino, some 12 km away, where he maintained a rival court and government for about eighteen months with boyars who defected from Vasily Shuisky — earning him the nickname "the Thief (or Brigand) of Tushino". Marina Mniszech, widow of False Dmitry I, publicly "recognised" him as her miraculously saved husband and secretly married him, strengthening his claim in Polish eyes.

Downfall and death

The stalemate broke when King Sigismund III of Poland openly invaded Russia and besieged Smolensk in 1609, causing the pretender's Polish supporters to desert to the king. False Dmitry fled Tushino in disguise in December 1609 and re-established himself at Kaluga, where he retained Cossack support. There, on 21 December 1610, he was murdered by a Tatar princeling in his service, Pyotr Urusov, apparently in revenge for an insult. His death did not end the Troubles, which continued until the election of Michael Romanov in 1613.
