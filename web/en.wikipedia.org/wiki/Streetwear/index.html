Streetwear is a style of casual clothing which became global in the 1990s. It grew from New York hip hop fashion and Californian surfskate culture to encompass elements of sportswear, punk, and Japanese street fashion. Eventually haute couture became an influence; conversely, streetwear brands and aesthetics were absorbed by luxury fashion.

History

Early pioneers included Shawn Stüssy, whose surfboard-logo T-shirts from 1980 onward helped define the genre, and James Jebbia, who founded Supreme in New York in 1994. In Japan, designers such as Nigo (A Bathing Ape, founded 1993) and Hiroshi Fujiwara developed an influential parallel scene centred on Harajuku. Sneaker culture, limited "drops", and collaborations between street labels and global sportswear companies (notably Nike and Adidas) turned scarcity itself into a marketing model, spawning a large resale market.

Luxury crossover

By the 2010s streetwear had entered high fashion: Louis Vuitton's 2017 collaboration with Supreme was a landmark, and in 2018 Virgil Abloh—founder of the streetwear label Off-White—became artistic director of Louis Vuitton menswear. Major houses adopted hoodies, sneakers and drop-based releases, while streetwear labels opened flagship stores worldwide.

Characteristics

Streetwear typically features comfortable, oversized or boxy fits; prominent logos and graphics; workwear and sportswear borrowings; and limited production runs that create exclusivity. Its distribution historically relied on independent boutiques, pop-ups and online drops rather than department stores.
