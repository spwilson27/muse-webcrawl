Fluoride varnish

Fluoride varnish
CompositionTypically 5% sodium fluoride (22,600 ppm F) in a resin base
PurposePrevention of dental caries
FrequencyUsually 2–4 times per year
SpecialtyDentistry

Fluoride varnish is a highly concentrated form of topical fluoride applied to the tooth surface by a dental or medical professional to prevent tooth decay (dental caries). Most products contain 5% sodium fluoride, equivalent to 22,600 parts per million of fluoride ion, suspended in an alcohol-based resin (colophony) that sets on contact with saliva and adheres to the teeth for several hours.

Mechanism and effectiveness

The varnish holds fluoride against the enamel far longer than gels or rinses, allowing uptake into demineralized enamel and the formation of calcium fluoride-like deposits that release fluoride during acid challenges. Fluoride promotes remineralization and inhibits bacterial metabolism. Systematic reviews by the Cochrane Collaboration have found that fluoride varnish applied two to four times yearly reduces caries increment in permanent teeth by roughly 40% and in primary teeth by roughly 37%.

Application

Application is quick and does not require drying of the teeth or special equipment: the teeth are wiped clean, and a thin layer of varnish is painted on with a small brush. Patients are usually advised to avoid hard or hot foods for the remainder of the day. Because only a small amount is used and it sets rapidly, the risk of ingestion is low, making varnish the preferred topical fluoride for young children.

Recommendations

The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force recommends that primary care clinicians apply fluoride varnish to the primary teeth of all infants and children starting at the age of primary tooth eruption. Dental organizations recommend risk-based application for children and adults at elevated caries risk, including people with root exposure, dry mouth, or orthodontic appliances.
