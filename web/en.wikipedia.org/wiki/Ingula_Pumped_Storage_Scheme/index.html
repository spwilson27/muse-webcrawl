Ingula Pumped Storage Scheme

Ingula Pumped Storage Scheme
Location: Drakensberg escarpment, KwaZulu-Natal/Free State, South Africa
Operator: Eskom
Capacity: 1,332 MW (4 × 333 MW)
Status: Operational (from 2017)

The Ingula Pumped Storage Scheme is a pumped-storage hydroelectric power station operated by Eskom on the Drakensberg escarpment near Ladysmith, on the border of KwaZulu-Natal and the Free State in South Africa. With an installed capacity of 1,332 megawatts from four reversible turbine-generator units, it stores energy for the national grid by moving water between an upper and a lower reservoir.

Design

The upper reservoir, Bedford Dam, sits on the escarpment in the Free State, while the lower reservoir, Bramhoek Dam, lies in the valley below in KwaZulu-Natal; the two are separated by a vertical drop of roughly 470 metres and connected by waterways to an underground powerhouse. During periods of low demand, electricity pumps water uphill; at peak demand the water is released back through the turbines to generate power.

History and environment

Construction began in the mid-2000s and the units were synchronised to the grid in 2016–2017, adding peaking capacity to South Africa's strained electricity supply. The scheme lies near important wetlands and the range of the endangered white-winged flufftail; conservation measures, including the creation of a protected area around the lower reservoir, formed part of the project's environmental commitments.
