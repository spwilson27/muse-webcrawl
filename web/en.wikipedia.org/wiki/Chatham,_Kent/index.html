Chatham is a town in Kent, England, on the River Medway, forming part of the Medway Towns conurbation with Rochester and Gillingham. For roughly four centuries Chatham was one of the Royal Navy's principal dockyard towns: Chatham Dockyard, founded in the mid-16th century, built and maintained warships through the age of sail and into the 20th century, and the town's growth and identity were shaped by the naval establishment until the yard closed in 1984.

History

The name Chatham derives from the Old English Cetham, "the homestead in the wood". A naval storehouse was established on the Medway in 1547, and the dockyard proper developed under Elizabeth I, refitting ships against the Spanish Armada. By the 18th century Chatham was among the most important naval bases in Britain; HMS Victory, Nelson's flagship at Trafalgar, was launched here in 1765. The yard's vulnerability was exposed in June 1667, when the Dutch fleet sailed up the Medway in one of the worst defeats in Royal Navy history.

Extensive fortifications, including Fort Amherst and the Chatham Lines, were built to defend the yard. In the 19th and 20th centuries the dockyard shifted from shipbuilding to refit and repair, especially of submarines. Charles Dickens lived in Chatham as a boy and later at nearby Gad's Hill; the town appears in his fiction and memory as a formative landscape.

The town today

Following the dockyard's closure in 1984, the site became the Chatham Historic Dockyard museum, preserving roperies, dry docks, and historic warships, and part of the yard was redeveloped as a university campus and marina. The former naval barracks area now hosts the Universities at Medway. Chatham remains a retail and transport center for the Medway Towns, though regeneration since the dockyard closure has been a continuing project.
