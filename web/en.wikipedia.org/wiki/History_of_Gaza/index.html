History of Gaza

The history of Gaza and of Gaza City, one of the oldest cities in the world, spans some 4,000 years. Located on the Mediterranean coast at the junction of trade routes between Egypt and the Levant (the ancient Via Maris), Gaza has been ruled in succession by Egyptians, Philistines, Israelites, Assyrians, Persians, Greeks, Romans, Byzantines, Arab caliphates, Crusaders, Mamluks, Ottomans, the British and Egyptians, before coming under Israeli occupation in 1967 and partial Palestinian self-rule in 1994.

Ancient Gaza

Known in Egyptian records as early as the 15th century BCE, Gaza served as an Egyptian administrative centre in Canaan. It later became one of the five cities of the Philistine pentapolis and figures in the biblical accounts of Samson. Gaza was besieged by Alexander the Great in 332 BCE, flourished as a Hellenistic and Roman trading city, and was an early centre of Christianity; its bishop Porphyry is credited with converting the city around 400 CE.

Islamic and Crusader periods

Gaza surrendered to Arab Muslim forces in 637 CE. It became a prosperous market town, noted as the birthplace of Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i, founder of one of the four Sunni schools of law. Crusaders captured Gaza in 1149 and built a castle there, but Saladin retook it in 1187. Under the Mamluks it was capital of a province, and under the Ottomans (from 1516) it prospered as a caravan stop on the road between Egypt and Syria.

Modern era

British forces captured Gaza after hard fighting in 1917, and it was included in the Mandate for Palestine. In the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, Egypt occupied the coastal strip around Gaza, which became home to large numbers of Palestinian refugees. Israel captured the Gaza Strip in the 1956 Suez Crisis, returned it to Egyptian administration, and captured it again in the Six-Day War of 1967. Under the Oslo Accords, the Palestinian Authority assumed administration of most of the Strip in 1994. Israel withdrew its settlements and troops in 2005; Hamas won the 2006 Palestinian legislative elections and took control of Gaza in 2007, after which Israel and Egypt imposed a blockade, and the territory has been the scene of repeated conflicts.
