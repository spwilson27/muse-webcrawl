Nuclear energy in Brazil

Nuclear energy in Brazil accounts for a small share—around 2–3 percent—of the country's electricity, which is dominated by hydropower. Brazil operates two pressurised water reactors at the Angra Nuclear Power Plant in Rio de Janeiro state, with a third unit long under construction. Brazil possesses large uranium reserves and a full domestic nuclear fuel-cycle programme, including uranium enrichment at Resende, and a naval nuclear-propulsion programme.

History

Brazil's nuclear ambitions date to the 1950s. A 1975 agreement with West Germany envisaged eight reactors and technology transfer, but only Angra 2 resulted directly. Angra 1, a Westinghouse unit, suffered a troubled early life. Angra 3's construction began in 1984, stopped in 1986, resumed in 2010, and was halted again amid cost overruns and the Lava Jato corruption scandal; the government has repeatedly announced plans to finish it.

Fuel cycle and navy programme

Indústrias Nucleares do Brasil (INB) mines uranium (notably at Caetité and Santa Quitéria) and operates the Resende enrichment plant using centrifuge technology developed with the Brazilian Navy. The Navy's nuclear programme aims at a nuclear-powered submarine (the Álvaro Alberto), making Brazil the first non-nuclear-weapon state to pursue naval nuclear propulsion; Brazil is a party to the Non-Proliferation Treaty and the Treaty of Tlatelolco, and its facilities are subject to ABACC and IAEA safeguards arrangements.
