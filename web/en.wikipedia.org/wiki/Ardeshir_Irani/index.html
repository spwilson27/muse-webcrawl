Khan Bahadur Ardeshir Irani (5 December 1886 – 14 October 1969) was a writer, director, producer, actor, film distributor, and cinematographer of Indian cinema, best known as the maker of Alam Ara (1931), the first Indian sound film ("talkie"). He is regarded as one of the founding figures of the Indian film industry and of the talkie era in India.

Early career

Irani was born in Pune to a Parsi (Zoroastrian) family and began as a schoolteacher before entering the entertainment business as a representative of Western film companies. With Bhogilal Dave he set up exhibition ventures and, in 1920, produced his first film. In 1924 he founded the Imperial Film Company in Bombay, which became one of the largest studios of the silent era, releasing films in several languages and giving early breaks to stars such as Prithviraj Kapoor and Mehboob Khan.

Alam Ara and the coming of sound

Having seen Universal's part-talkie Show Boat, Irani raced rival producers to bring sound to Indian screens. Using the Tanar sound-on-film system and recording songs live with a harmonium and tabla placed out of frame, he completed Alam Ara, based on a Parsi stage play, starring Master Vithal and Zubaida. Premiered at Bombay's Majestic Cinema on 14 March 1931, it was an enormous popular success despite its technical crudity, and its seven songs established music as the backbone of Indian popular cinema. Its prints are now lost, making it one of the most famous lost films of India.

Later work

Irani went on to make India's first colour film, Kisan Kanya (1937), processed with the Cinecolour method, and produced films in Hindi, Urdu, Tamil (the first Tamil talkie, Kalidas, was made under his banner in 1931), Telugu, and Persian — he also directed the first Persian talkie, Dokhtar-e Lor (1933). In a career of more than 150 films he shaped the early Indian studio system; he retired as sound technology and the industry transformed after the Second World War.
