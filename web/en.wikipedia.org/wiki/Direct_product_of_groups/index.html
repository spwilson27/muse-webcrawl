Direct product of groups

In mathematics, specifically in group theory, the direct product is an operation that takes two groups G and H and constructs a new group, usually denoted G × H. The underlying set is the Cartesian product of the two groups, and the group operation is defined componentwise: (g₁, h₁)(g₂, h₂) = (g₁g₂, h₁h₂). The construction generalises to products of arbitrarily many groups.

Properties

The factors G and H embed in G × H as normal subgroups isomorphic to the originals, they intersect trivially, and every element of the product can be written uniquely as a product of an element of G and an element of H which commute with each other. Conversely, a group expressible as an internal direct product of two normal subgroups has these properties. If G and H are abelian (or finite), so is their product, with order |G|·|H| in the finite case.

Examples

The Klein four-group is the direct product of two cyclic groups of order 2. The group of units of the ring of integers modulo mn, when m and n are coprime, is the direct product of the unit groups modulo m and modulo n — a consequence of the Chinese remainder theorem. Finite abelian groups are classified as direct products of cyclic groups of prime-power order.
