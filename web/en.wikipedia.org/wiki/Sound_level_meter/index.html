Sound level meter

A sound level meter is an instrument used to measure sound pressure levels, commonly in decibels. It consists of a calibrated microphone, amplifier, frequency-weighting networks, and a display, and is used for occupational noise assessment, environmental noise monitoring, and acoustics research.

Weightings and measurements

Meters typically apply A-weighting (dBA), which approximates human hearing sensitivity, as well as C and Z weightings. They measure quantities such as the equivalent continuous sound level (LAeq), peak levels, and statistical levels over time. Time weightings — Fast, Slow, and Impulse — control the meter's response to fluctuating sounds.

Standards and classes

Performance is specified by the international standard IEC 61672, which defines Class 1 (precision) and Class 2 (general purpose) instruments. Occupational exposure measurements follow standards set by bodies such as OSHA and NIOSH in the United States. Integrating meters and personal noise dosimeters are used to assess workers' daily noise exposure.
