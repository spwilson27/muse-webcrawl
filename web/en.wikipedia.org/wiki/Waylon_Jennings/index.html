Waylon Arnold Jennings (June 15, 1937 – February 13, 2002) was an American country music singer, songwriter, and musician. A pioneer of the outlaw country movement, he began his career as a bassist for Buddy Holly during the 1958–59 "Winter Dance Party" tour; he famously gave up his seat on the ill-fated flight that killed Holly, Ritchie Valens, and J. P. "The Big Bopper" Richardson on February 3, 1959 — "the Day the Music Died".

Career

After working as a disc jockey and recording for independent labels, Jennings signed with RCA Victor in 1965. Frustrated by the restrictive Nashville sound, he fought for and won creative control of his recordings in the early 1970s, producing albums such as Lonesome, On'ry and Mean (1973) and Honky Tonk Heroes (1973). With Willie Nelson, Tompall Glaser, and his wife Jessi Colter, he recorded Wanted! The Outlaws (1976), country music's first platinum album. Duets with Nelson, including "Mammas Don't Let Your Babies Grow Up to Be Cowboys" (1978), became signature songs. Jennings also narrated and sang the theme of the television series The Dukes of Hazzard.

Later life and legacy

In 1985 Jennings formed the supergroup The Highwaymen with Nelson, Johnny Cash, and Kris Kristofferson. He was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 2001. After years of health problems aggravated by diabetes, he died on February 13, 2002, in Chandler, Arizona. His phasing-shifted guitar style, baritone voice, and insistence on artistic independence made him one of country music's defining figures.
