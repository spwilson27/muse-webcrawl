Massawa (Tigrinya: ምጽዋዕ, Məṣṣəwaʿ; Arabic: مصوع) is a port city in the Northern Red Sea region of Eritrea, located on the Red Sea coast at the northern end of the Gulf of Zula. It has represented Eritrea's main port for centuries and served as the capital of the Italian colony of Eritrea from 1890 until 1897. Spread across the mainland and two islands — Massawa Island and Taulud — joined by causeways, the old town is noted for its Ottoman-era coral-stone architecture, much of it damaged in the Eritrean War of Independence.

History

Massawa was an important trading port from the medieval period, linked with the Dahlak Archipelago offshore and with trade routes to Arabia, India, and the Ethiopian highlands. The Ottoman Empire took control in 1557 and administered the area (as part of the Habesh Eyalet) for three centuries, before influence passed to Egypt. Italy occupied Massawa in 1885 and made it the nucleus of its colony of Eritrea, building the railway to Asmara (completed in stages to 1911) that climbs from the coast to the highland capital.

British forces captured Massawa in 1941 during the East African Campaign. The city suffered heavy damage in 1990 during Operation Fenkil, when Eritrean People's Liberation Front forces captured the port from Ethiopia. Since Eritrean independence in 1991–93, Massawa has again been the country's principal port, though trade has fluctuated with regional politics, including the long closure of the border with Ethiopia after the 1998–2000 war.

Geography and climate

Massawa is one of the hottest coastal cities in the world, with summer temperatures regularly exceeding 40 °C (104 °F) and high humidity from the Red Sea; annual rainfall is low, around 180 mm. The port handles most of Eritrea's imports and exports and is connected to Asmara, about 115 km inland, by road and by the historic narrow-gauge Eritrean Railway, parts of which have been restored.
