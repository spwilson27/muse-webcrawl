Axial skeleton

The axial skeleton is the part of the skeleton that consists of the bones of the head and trunk of a vertebrate. In humans it is made up of 80 bones and consists of the skull (including the hyoid bone and the auditory ossicles), the vertebral column, and the thoracic cage (ribs and sternum). Together with the appendicular skeleton (126 bones), it forms the complete human skeleton.

Components

The skull comprises 22 bones of the cranium and face, plus the six auditory ossicles of the middle ears and the hyoid bone. The vertebral column consists of 33 vertebrae in children, of which five sacral and (typically) four coccygeal vertebrae fuse in adults, leaving 26 distinct bones; it protects the spinal cord and supports the head and trunk. The thoracic cage is formed by 12 pairs of ribs and the sternum, protecting the heart and lungs and assisting in breathing.

Function

The axial skeleton provides the central framework of the body: it protects the brain, spinal cord and thoracic organs, supports the weight of the upper body, and provides attachment surfaces for muscles that move the head, neck and trunk and that participate in respiration.
