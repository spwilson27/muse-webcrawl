Time-domain astronomy

Time-domain astronomy is the study of astronomical objects that change with time — variable stars, supernovae, novae, gamma-ray bursts, active galactic nuclei, exoplanet transits and other transient events.

History

The field has roots in early records of "guest stars" and variable-star observing, but was transformed by photographic and then digital sky surveys that repeatedly image large areas of sky.

Surveys

Major projects include the Palomar Transient Factory, Pan-STARRS, the Zwicky Transient Facility, ASAS-SN, and the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST), which is expected to issue millions of alerts per night. Time-domain data underpin multi-messenger astronomy, linking optical transients to gravitational-wave and neutrino events.

Phenomena

Timescales range from milliseconds (fast radio bursts) to decades (AGN variability). Classification increasingly relies on machine learning applied to alert streams.
