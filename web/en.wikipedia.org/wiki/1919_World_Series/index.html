1919 World Series

The 1919 World Series matched the Cincinnati Reds against the Chicago White Sox, with the Reds winning the best-of-nine series five games to three. It is remembered chiefly for the Black Sox Scandal, in which eight White Sox players were accused of deliberately losing in exchange for payments from gamblers.

The fix

Disgruntled by the frugal salary policies of owner Charles Comiskey, several White Sox players — orchestrated by first baseman Chick Gandil and approached by gamblers including figures connected to Arnold Rothstein — agreed to throw the series. Pitcher Eddie Cicotte hit the first batter he faced as the agreed signal. The Reds, a strong team in their own right, won decisively.

Aftermath

Eight players, including star outfielder Shoeless Joe Jackson, were indicted in 1920; although acquitted in court in 1921, they were banned for life by new Commissioner Kenesaw Mountain Landis. The scandal led baseball to appoint its first commissioner and remains the most famous gambling scandal in American sport.
