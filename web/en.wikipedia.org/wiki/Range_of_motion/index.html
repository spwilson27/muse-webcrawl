Range of motion

Range of motion (or ROM) is the linear or angular distance that a moving object may normally travel while properly attached to another. In the case of the human body, it is the full movement potential of a joint, usually its range of flexion and extension, measured in degrees with an instrument called a goniometer. Range of motion is a core concept in anatomy, physical therapy, orthopedics and exercise science.

Measuring a joint's range of motion with a goniometer

Types

  • Active range of motion (AROM) — movement produced by the individual's own muscles acting on the joint.
  • Passive range of motion (PROM) — movement produced entirely by an external force, such as a therapist's hands; normally slightly greater than AROM.
  • Active-assistive range of motion (AAROM) — movement in which the individual assists their own muscles with an external aid.

Measurement and typical values

Clinicians measure ROM using goniometers, inclinometers or visual estimation, comparing values with standard norms: for example, shoulder flexion about 180°, elbow flexion about 150°, knee flexion about 135°, and ankle dorsiflexion about 20°. Limited range of motion, sometimes called hypomobility, may result from injury, arthritis, muscle shortening, swelling, pain or neurological conditions such as spasticity; excessive range is termed hypermobility.

Clinical importance

ROM assessment guides rehabilitation after fractures, joint replacements and strokes. Range-of-motion exercises help maintain joint health, prevent contractures in immobilised patients, and restore function. In strength training, the term also describes how far a resistance exercise is performed — full versus partial range of motion — which influences muscle activation and hypertrophy.
