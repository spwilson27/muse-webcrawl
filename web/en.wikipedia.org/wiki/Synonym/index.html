Synonym

A synonym is a word or phrase that means exactly or nearly the same as another word or phrase in the same language. Examples include begin/start, happy/glad, and car/automobile. Words that are synonyms are said to be synonymous, and the state of being a synonym is synonymy. The word derives from Ancient Greek syn ("with") and onoma ("name").

Degrees of synonymy

Linguists distinguish absolute synonyms — expressions interchangeable in every context with identical meaning, which are rare and often unstable — from near-synonyms, which overlap in meaning but differ in connotation, register, collocation or dialect. Thus buy and purchase differ mainly in formality; slim and skinny share a denotation but differ in evaluation. Some words are synonymous in only one sense: deep and profound coincide for thought but not for water.

Uses

Synonyms are collected in thesauri, beginning with Roget's Thesaurus (1852), and are central to good writing, allowing variation and precision of tone. They matter in lexicography, translation, and computational linguistics, where resources such as WordNet group words into synonym sets ("synsets") to support search and language understanding.

Related concepts

The opposite of a synonym is an antonym. Other sense relations include hypernyms and hyponyms (general and specific terms), homonyms (same form, unrelated meaning) and polysemy (one word with several related senses). In biological taxonomy, "synonym" has a technical meaning: different scientific names applied to the same taxon.
