Informal settlement

An informal settlement is a residential area where housing has been constructed on land to which the occupants have no legal claim, or which they occupy illegally, and which typically does not comply with planning and building regulations. Informal settlements are known by many local names, including shanty towns, squatter settlements, favelas in Brazil, townships' informal areas in South Africa, and colonias or barrios elsewhere.

Characteristics

Common characteristics include insecure tenure, self-built dwellings of salvaged or inexpensive materials, overcrowding, and inadequate access to clean water, sanitation, drainage, electricity and paved roads. Settlements often arise on marginal land — steep hillsides, floodplains, railway reserves and the peripheries of cities — as migrants arrive faster than formal housing can be provided. Despite poor conditions, settlements may develop strong community organisation, informal economies and, over time, incremental consolidation of housing.

Scale and policy

UN-Habitat estimates that around one billion people — roughly a quarter of the world's urban population — live in slums and informal settlements. Policy responses have shifted over time from clearance and relocation toward in-situ upgrading: regularising tenure, extending infrastructure and services, and supporting community-led improvement, exemplified by programmes such as Brazil's favela-bairro projects and slum upgrading in Kenya and India.
