University admission

University admission or college admission is the process through which students enter tertiary education at universities and colleges. Systems vary widely across countries and institutions, ranging from centralized national examinations to holistic review of individual applications.

Systems by country

In China, admission is dominated by the Gaokao national entrance examination; India uses examinations such as the JEE and NEET for engineering and medicine, alongside the newer Common University Entrance Test. In the United Kingdom, applications are centralized through UCAS, with offers typically conditional on A-level results. In the United States, many colleges use holistic admissions considering grades, coursework, essays, recommendations, and activities, and a large group of institutions share the Common Application. Germany traditionally admits students on the basis of the Abitur school-leaving certificate, with local restrictions (Numerus Clausus) for oversubscribed subjects.

Issues

Admissions processes are periodically debated over fairness and access, including the role of standardized testing, legacy preferences, affirmative action (curtailed in the United States by the Supreme Court's 2023 decision in Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard), and corruption, as exposed by the 2019 US "Varsity Blues" scandal.
