The ABRSM (Associated Board of the Royal Schools of Music) is a United Kingdom-based music examination board and registered charity. Founded in 1889, it is one of the largest providers of graded music examinations in the world, assessing hundreds of thousands of candidates each year in more than ninety countries.

History

The Associated Board was established in 1889 by the Royal Academy of Music and the Royal College of Music, with Alexander Mackenzie and George Grove among its leading advocates, to provide a standardised system of examinations for local music students. The Royal Manchester College of Music and the Royal Scottish Academy of Music and Drama joined shortly afterwards, and the board's four partner schools remain its owners. In 2009 the organisation rebranded from "Associated Board of the Royal Schools of Music" to the shorter name ABRSM.

Examinations

ABRSM offers graded examinations from an introductory level through Grade 8 in instrumental and vocal performance, together with diplomas (DipABRSM, LRSM, and FRSM) for advanced performers, teachers, and directors. Practical assessments traditionally test prepared pieces, scales and arpeggios, sight-reading, and aural skills; separate written examinations cover music theory. Since 2020 the board has also offered digitally assessed performance grades based on recorded performances.

Publishing

Through its publishing arm, ABRSM Publishing, the board issues editions of examination pieces, theory workbooks, and educational materials used by teachers and candidates worldwide.
