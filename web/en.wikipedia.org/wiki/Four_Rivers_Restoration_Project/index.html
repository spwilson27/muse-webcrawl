Four Rivers Restoration Project

The Four Rivers Restoration Project, also known as the Four Major Rivers Project, was a multi-purpose green growth project on the Han River, Nakdong River, Geum River and Yeongsan River in South Korea. Launched by the government of President Lee Myung-bak in 2009 and substantially complete by 2012, it involved dredging the rivers, building sixteen new weirs (barrages), constructing reservoirs and creating hundreds of kilometres of bicycle paths and riverside parks.

Aims

The government presented the project, costing about 22 trillion won (roughly US$17–19 billion), as a means of securing water resources, preventing floods, improving water quality, restoring river ecosystems and creating jobs in the wake of the 2008 financial crisis.

Controversy

The project was highly controversial. Critics argued the weirs slowed flows, worsened algal blooms and damaged ecosystems, and questioned the costs and the lack of environmental assessment; supporters pointed to flood control and recreation benefits. A government audit in 2018 found serious water-quality problems, and the subsequent administration opened some weirs and considered dismantling several of them.
