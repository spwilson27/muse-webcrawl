The Tatars are a group of Turkic peoples speaking languages of the Kipchak branch. The name most often refers to the Volga Tatars, the largest Tatar group, who are indigenous to the Volga-Ural region of Russia and form a majority of the population of Tatarstan. Other major groups include the Crimean Tatars, Siberian Tatars, Lipka Tatars, and Astrakhan Tatars, whose histories and languages differ substantially.

History

The Volga Tatars descend from the population of Volga Bulgaria, a medieval state that adopted Islam in 922, and from Kipchak-speaking peoples of the Golden Horde, which succeeded it. After the Russian conquest of the Khanate of Kazan in 1552, Tatars lived under pressure of Christianisation and Russification but preserved their language and Sunni Islam. Tatars became prominent merchants across the Russian Empire and played a key role in the Jadid educational and cultural reform movement of the late 19th century.

Following the Russian Revolution, the Tatar Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic was created in 1920. In 1990 Tatarstan declared state sovereignty, and a 1992 referendum and subsequent treaty with Moscow gave the republic broad autonomy within the Russian Federation.

Culture

Tatar culture is centred on the Tatar language, a rich literary tradition (poets such as Ğabdulla Tuqay), distinctive cuisine (such as öçpoçmaq and çäkçäk), and the harvest festival Sabantuy, inscribed on UNESCO's heritage lists. The deportation of the Crimean Tatars to Central Asia in 1944 and their return after 1989 remain central themes of Crimean Tatar identity and politics.
