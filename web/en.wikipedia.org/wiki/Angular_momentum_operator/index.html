Angular momentum operator

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In quantum mechanics, the angular momentum operator is one of several related operators analogous to classical angular momentum. It plays a central role in atomic and nuclear physics and in the theory of rotations. The total angular momentum operator J combines orbital angular momentum L and spin S, and its algebraic structure explains the quantised spectra of atoms.

Orbital angular momentum

The orbital angular momentum operator is defined by analogy with the classical expression L = r × p, with position and momentum replaced by their operators. In position representation it becomes a differential operator acting on the wavefunction. Its components obey the commutation relations [Lx, Ly] = iħ Lz (and cyclic permutations), which means no two components can be measured simultaneously with complete precision.

Ladder operators and eigenvalues

Because J² commutes with each component of J, conventionally Jz, the two can be diagonalised together. The raising and lowering operators J+ and J− move between eigenstates of Jz with the same value of j. Requiring the spectrum of m to be bounded yields the eigenvalue conditions: J² has eigenvalues ħ² j(j+1), where j is a non-negative integer or half-integer, and Jz has eigenvalues ħm with m ranging from −j to j in steps of one. Integer j corresponds to orbital angular momentum; half-integer values arise for spin.

Spin

Spin angular momentum is an intrinsic property of particles with no classical analogue as a spatial differential operator; for spin-½ particles it is represented by the Pauli matrices multiplied by ħ/2. The total angular momentum J = L + S obeys the same commutation algebra, and its coupling underlies fine structure, the addition of angular momenta, and the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients.
