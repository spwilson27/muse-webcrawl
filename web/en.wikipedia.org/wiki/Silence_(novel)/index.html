Silence (novel)

Silence (Chinmoku), first published in 1966.

Silence (Japanese: 沈黙, romanized: Chinmoku) is a 1966 novel of historical fiction by Japanese author Shūsaku Endō, published in English by Peter Owen Publishers. It is Endō's most famous work and is generally considered his masterpiece. The story of a Jesuit missionary sent to 17th-century Japan, which has officially renounced Christianity and where the Tokugawa shogunate persecutes the small remaining community of Kakure Kirishitan ("Hidden Christians"), it explores the silence of God in the face of human suffering, the conflict between faith and apostasy, and the clash between Western Christianity and Japanese culture.

Plot summary

The young Portuguese Jesuit Sebastião Rodrigues travels with his companion Francisco Garupe to Japan, where Christianity has been outlawed and its adherents tortured and forced to apostatize by trampling on a fumie, an image of Christ. The priests seek news of their former teacher, Cristóvão Ferreira, who is rumoured to have apostatized after being tortured. Rodrigues is betrayed by the cowardly fisherman Kichijirō, captured, and confronted with the apparent silence of God as Japanese believers are tortured before his eyes. Eventually Rodrigues himself tramples the fumie, hearing — or imagining — Christ's voice telling him it was for him that Christ came into the world. He lives out his life in Japan under a Japanese name, outwardly apostate yet, in Endō's telling, not wholly stripped of faith.

Themes

The novel's central theme is the silence of God amid suffering, a theme that resonated with Endō's own experience as a Catholic in a country where Christians were a tiny minority. It also examines whether Christianity could take root in the "swamp" of Japan, the moral status of outward apostasy undertaken to save others from torture, and the figure of the weak, repeatedly failing believer embodied by Kichijirō.

Reception and adaptations

Silence won the Tanizaki Prize in 1966 and has been translated into many languages. Graham Greene praised Endō as one of the finest writers alive. Masahiro Shinoda adapted the novel into the 1971 film Chinmoku, and Martin Scorsese, who had long sought to direct it, released his adaptation Silence in 2016, starring Andrew Garfield as Rodrigues and Adam Driver as Garupe. The novel also inspired an opera by Teizō Matsumura.
