Rhinoceros poaching

Rhinoceros poaching is the illegal killing of rhinoceroses, principally for their horns, which are highly valued in some traditional medicine markets and as status symbols. The sharp escalation of poaching in Africa and Asia during the 2000s and 2010s drove several rhino populations toward critically endangered status.

Causes

Rhino horn is composed of keratin, the same material as fingernails, but demand persists because of beliefs in its medicinal properties in parts of East and Southeast Asia and its use as a luxury gift. A kilogram of horn has at times fetched prices comparable to gold or cocaine on black markets, making poaching attractive to organized criminal syndicates.

Impact

South Africa, home to the majority of the world's white rhinoceroses, lost more than a thousand animals per year at the peak of the crisis around 2014. Species such as the western black rhinoceros were declared extinct, and the northern white rhinoceros was reduced to a handful of individuals under armed guard.

Countermeasures

Responses have included militarized anti-poaching patrols, dehorning of rhinos to reduce their value to poachers, demand-reduction campaigns, DNA databases of horn stockpiles, and proposals for a legalized trade in horn harvested from live animals. Conservationists remain divided on which strategies are most effective.
