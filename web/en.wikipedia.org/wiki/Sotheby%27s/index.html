Sotheby's is a British-founded American multinational auction house, one of the world's largest brokers of fine and decorative art, jewellery, real estate, and collectibles. Founded in London in 1744 by bookseller Samuel Baker, it is a principal rival of Christie's and has salerooms in New York, London, Hong Kong, Paris, and other cities.

History

The firm began with a book auction in 1744 and grew through the 19th and 20th centuries into a dominant dealer in art and antiquities. It expanded to New York in 1955 and became a public company in 1988. In 2019 the company was taken private by French-Israeli telecom magnate Patrick Drahi in a deal valued at about $3.7 billion.

Operations

Sotheby's conducts auctions and private sales across dozens of categories, from Old Master paintings and Impressionist art to contemporary art, wine, watches, and classic cars. Notable sales include some of the most expensive artworks ever auctioned. The company also offers financial services, including art-secured lending, and operates the Sotheby's International Realty brand under licence.
