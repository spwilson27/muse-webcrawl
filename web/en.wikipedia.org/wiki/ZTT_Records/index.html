ZTT Records

ZTT Records is a British record label founded in 1983 by producer Trevor Horn, his wife and music business manager Jill Sinclair, and journalist Paul Morley. The name ZTT is an abbreviation taken from Filippo Tommaso Marinetti's futurist sound poem "Zang Tumb Tumb". The label became famous in the mid-1980s for its lavish, sample-driven productions and provocative marketing of acts such as Frankie Goes to Hollywood, Propaganda and Art of Noise.

History

ZTT grew out of Trevor Horn's production success with Dollar, ABC and Malcolm McLaren. Its first release, in 1983, was "Into Battle with the Art of Noise". The label's breakthrough came with Frankie Goes to Hollywood: after the BBC ban on "Relax" in early 1984 propelled the single to number one for five weeks, "Two Tribes" and "The Power of Love" also topped the UK charts that year, making Frankie only the second act in UK chart history to reach number one with their first three singles. Propaganda's "Dr. Mabuse" and A Secret Wish, Grace Jones's Slave to the Rhythm and releases by Seal — whose 1990 debut album was a major success — followed.

The label's practices, including long-term contracts and control of publishing and merchandising, produced celebrated disputes: Holly Johnson successfully sued to escape his contract in a landmark 1988 case that the judge ruled an unreasonable restraint of trade. ZTT later had success with 808 State and revived interest in its catalogue through compilations and reissues, including the Action Series and extensive Frankie Goes to Hollywood retrospectives. In 2017 the label's catalogue passed to Universal Music Group.

Legacy

ZTT is regarded as one of the defining labels of 1980s pop, notable for treating singles, packaging, slogans and videos as parts of a single artwork — Morley's "Frankie Say" T-shirts became an iconic fashion item of 1984.
