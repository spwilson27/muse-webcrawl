Loch Long is a body of water in Argyll and Bute, Scotland. The sea loch extends 20 miles (32 km) from the Firth of Clyde at its southwestern end, to Arrochar. The village of Arrochar stands at the head of the loch. It separates the Rosneath Peninsula from the Arrochar Alps.

The loch's name comes from the Gaelic for "ship lake" (long being the Gaelic word for a ship). During the Second World War the loch was used as a testing ground for torpedoes, and a torpedo range with a range control building survives near Arrochar.

Description

The loch is long and narrow, flanked by steep hills and mountains, including Ben Arthur (the Cobbler) and Beinn Narnain at its head. The eastern shore is formed by the Rosneath Peninsula; near the mouth of the loch lie the villages of Cove and Kilcreggan. On the western shore are the settlements of Lochgoilhead (at the head of the branch inlet Loch Goil), Coulport, and Ardentinny further south.

At Coulport, on the eastern shore of the loch, is the Royal Naval Armaments Depot at Coulport, part of His Majesty's Naval Base Clyde, where the warheads of the United Kingdom's Trident nuclear deterrent are stored and loaded onto submarines.

History

Loch Long was raided by Viking longships in the Middle Ages; the name is said to recall a Viking fleet's anchorage. In later centuries the loch was an important route for transporting goods and cattle from the Highlands to the Clyde.
