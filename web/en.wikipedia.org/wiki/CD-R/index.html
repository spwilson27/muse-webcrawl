CD-R

CD-R (Compact Disc-Recordable) is a digital optical disc storage format. A CD-R disc is a compact disc that can be written once and read arbitrarily many times.

CD-R discs (CD-Rs) are readable by most plain CD readers manufactured prior to the introduction of CD-R, usually by using the same technology as that in CD players. CD-R discs are based on the same ISO standard as read-only (CD-ROM) discs. CD-R discs have similar capacities to audio CDs: typically 650–700 MB (74–80 minutes of audio) for standard 12 cm discs.

History

The CD-R was originally named CD-WO (write-once) in drafts of the Orange Book standard, published in 1989 by Philips and Sony. Commercial CD recorders capable of writing CD-R discs first appeared in the early 1990s; by the late 1990s prices had fallen enough for widespread consumer adoption. CD-R became a popular medium for music compilation discs, data backup, and software distribution.

Technical details

A CD-R disc uses an organic dye recording layer (cyanine, phthalocyanine, or azo dye) between the polycarbonate substrate and a reflective layer of silver or gold. When written ("burned"), a laser heats the dye, changing its optical properties to create marks equivalent to the pits and lands of a pressed disc. Unlike CD-RW, a CD-R cannot be erased or rewritten.
