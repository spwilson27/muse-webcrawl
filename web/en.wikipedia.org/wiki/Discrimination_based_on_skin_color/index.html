Discrimination based on skin color

Discrimination based on skin color, also known as colorism, is a form of prejudice or discrimination in which people are treated differently based on the social meanings attached to their skin color. Unlike racism, which typically operates between racial groups, colorism frequently operates within them: people with lighter skin may receive preferential treatment over darker-skinned members of the same racial or ethnic group, although it can also occur across groups.

History and manifestations

Historians trace colorism in many societies to colonialism and slavery, in which colonizers and slaveholders granted relative privileges to lighter-skinned people, fostering durable hierarchies of status, beauty, and opportunity. Empirical studies in the United States have found skin tone associated with outcomes such as wages, educational attainment, sentencing severity, and marriage patterns, even after controlling for race and background. Similar patterns have been documented in Latin America, the Caribbean, South and East Asia, and parts of Africa, often reinforced by large markets for skin-lightening products — a global industry that has drawn public-health and ethical criticism.

Causes and analysis

Researchers link colorism to the internalization of Eurocentric beauty standards, media representation, and institutional practices. Sociologists distinguish colorism from racism while noting how the two interact with gender, class, and caste. In India, for example, debates connect skin-tone preference to caste history and to the marketing of fairness creams, which prompted advertising regulators and brands in the 2020s to drop "fairness" branding.

Responses

Activists and policymakers have addressed colorism through anti-discrimination law (skin color is a protected characteristic in some jurisdictions), media and beauty-industry campaigns celebrating darker skin, and education. In the United States the concept gained wider attention through works such as Alice Walker's writings, which popularized the term "colorism" in the early 1980s.
