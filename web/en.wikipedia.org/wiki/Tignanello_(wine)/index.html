Tignanello is an Italian red wine produced by the Marchesi Antinori family from a single vineyard of the same name in the Chianti Classico area of Tuscany. First released with the 1971 vintage, it is regarded, along with Sassicaia, as one of the founding "Super Tuscan" wines — innovative Tuscan reds that deliberately broke the rules of the official appellations and came to be celebrated worldwide.

History

Tignanello originated under Piero Antinori, working with oenologist Giacomo Tachis. Departing from Chianti tradition, the wine omitted white grapes, used French barriques for aging, and from 1975 included Cabernet Sauvignon and later Cabernet Franc alongside the native Sangiovese. Because it violated the Chianti Classico DOC regulations, it was initially sold merely as a vino da tavola (table wine), and later as a Toscana IGT. Its success helped reform Italian wine law and elevate the international standing of Tuscan wine.

The wine

The modern blend is typically around 80% Sangiovese with about 15% Cabernet Sauvignon and 5% Cabernet Franc, aged roughly a year in French and Hungarian oak. Tignanello is made only in better vintages, and has been praised for combining the freshness and structure of Sangiovese with the depth of Bordeaux varieties.
