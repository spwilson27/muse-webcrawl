A mature Brandywine tomato, noted for its large size and rich flavour.

The Brandywine tomato is an heirloom cultivar of tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) known for its large, pink, beefsteak-type fruit and its rich, complex flavour. It is widely regarded as one of the best-tasting tomato varieties, though it is also known for relatively low yields and a long growing season.

Description

The plant is an indeterminate vine with distinctive potato-leaf foliage. Fruit typically weigh from 340 to 680 grams (12 to 24 ounces), ripen to a dusky pink, and take around 80 to 90 days from transplanting to mature. The flesh is dense and juicy with low acidity and a sweet, old-fashioned tomato flavour prized in taste trials.

History

Brandywine was first listed commercially in 1885 by the Philadelphia seed firm Johnson & Stokes. It rose to prominence in the heirloom seed movement of the 1980s, when the Ohio gardener Ben Quisenberry obtained seed said to have come from Tennessee; the best-known strain, 'Sudduth', was selected for superior flavour and productivity. Numerous variants — including yellow and red forms — are now grown under the Brandywine name.
