Clique-width

FieldGraph theory
Introduced byCourcelle, Engelfriet and Rozenberg (1990)

In graph theory, clique-width is a parameter describing the structural complexity of a graph. It is the minimum number of labels needed to construct the graph using operations that create a labelled vertex, take disjoint unions, add all edges between two label classes, and relabel vertices.

Properties

Graphs of bounded clique-width include cographs (clique-width at most 2) and complete graphs. Clique-width generalises treewidth in the sense that bounded treewidth implies bounded clique-width, but not conversely — cliques have clique-width 2 but unbounded treewidth. Many NP-hard problems become tractable on graphs of bounded clique-width when an expression is given.

Related concepts

A closely related measure is rank-width, which is functionally equivalent: each is bounded by a function of the other.
