Tempranillo

Tempranillo is a black grape variety widely grown to make full-bodied red wines in Spain, where it is the principal grape of Rioja and Ribera del Duero. Its name derives from temprano ("early"), as it ripens several weeks earlier than most Spanish red grapes. In Portugal it is known as Tinta Roriz and Aragonez.

SpeciesVitis vinifera
OriginSpain
Notable regionsRioja, Ribera del Duero, Toro

Characteristics

The vine has thick-skinned berries giving deeply coloured wines with flavours of plum, cherry, leather and tobacco, especially after ageing in American oak. Tempranillo has a neutral profile that takes oak character well; traditionally Rioja wines were aged long in barrel before release.

Distribution

By the 2000s Tempranillo was among the world's most planted grape varieties, with plantings also in Argentina, Australia, California and Texas.
