Undetectable = untransmittable

Undetectable = untransmittable (U=U) is a health information campaign asserting that people living with HIV who achieve and maintain an undetectable viral load through antiretroviral therapy cannot sexually transmit the virus to others. The slogan was launched in 2016 by the Prevention Access Campaign and has since been endorsed by health authorities including the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, the World Health Organization, and UNAIDS.

Evidence

The campaign rests on large clinical studies. The HPTN 052 trial showed that early antiretroviral therapy dramatically reduced transmission within serodiscordant couples, while the PARTNER, PARTNER2, and Opposites Attract studies observed zero linked transmissions across tens of thousands of condomless sex acts when the partner with HIV had a suppressed viral load. "Undetectable" generally means fewer than 200 copies of the virus per millilitre of blood.

Impact

Advocates credit U=U with reducing stigma around HIV, encouraging treatment adherence and testing, and informing debates over the criminalisation of HIV non-disclosure. The message applies specifically to sexual transmission and does not cover transmission through breastfeeding or shared needles, where risks, though reduced, may not be zero.
