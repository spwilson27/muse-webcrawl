Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe

ArtistÉdouard Manet
Year1863
MediumOil on canvas
LocationMusée d'Orsay, Paris

Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe (The Luncheon on the Grass) is a large oil painting by the French artist Édouard Manet, created in 1862–1863. It depicts a nude woman and a partially clothed bather picnicking with two fully dressed men in a wooded setting.

Reception

Rejected by the jury of the Paris Salon of 1863, the painting was exhibited instead at the Salon des Refusés, where it caused a sensation and scandal. Critics objected both to the subject — a contemporary nude in ordinary company, gazing directly at the viewer — and to Manet's flat, sketch-like technique, which departed from academic finish.

Sources and influence

Manet drew the central figural group from an engraving after Raphael, and the pastoral setting recalls Giorgione and Titian's Pastoral Concert. Now regarded as a landmark of modern art, the painting helped inaugurate avant-garde painting and influenced generations of artists.
