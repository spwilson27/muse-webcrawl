Montmorillonite

CategoryPhyllosilicate (smectite group)
Formula(Na,Ca)0.33(Al,Mg)2(Si4O10)(OH)2·nH2O
Hardness1–2 (Mohs)
ColourWhite, grey, pink, yellowish

Montmorillonite is a very soft phyllosilicate group of minerals that are typically microscopic crystals that form a clay. It is named after Montmorillon in France. Montmorillonite, a member of the smectite group, is a 2:1 clay, meaning that it has two tetrahedral sheets of silica sandwiching a central octahedral sheet of alumina.

Properties

The particles are plate-shaped with an average diameter around 1 micrometre and a thickness of 0.96 nanometres; magnification of around 25,000× (using an electron microscope) is required to "see" individual clay particles. Members of this group include saponite, nontronite, and beidellite.

Montmorillonite is notable for its ability to absorb large amounts of water into the space between its sheets, swelling to many times its dry volume; sodium montmorillonite swells much more than the calcium form. Its high cation-exchange capacity and enormous specific surface area make it useful as an adsorbent and catalyst.

Occurrence and uses

Montmorillonite is the main constituent of bentonite, formed by the weathering of volcanic ash, and of fuller's earth. It is used in drilling muds, foundry sands, cat litter, water treatment, and as a component of barrier systems for landfills and repositories. Modified ("organo-") montmorillonite clays are used as nanofillers in polymer nanocomposites. Montmorillonite has also been studied in prebiotic chemistry as a catalyst that promotes the polymerisation of RNA building blocks.
