Non-RAID drive architectures

Non-RAID drive architectures are storage arrangements that use multiple disk drives without the redundancy or striping of RAID. Well-known tongue-in-cheek acronyms include JBOD, SPAN, MAID and others, often defined as backronyms in contrast to RAID ("redundant array of independent disks").

JBOD and SPAN

JBOD ("just a bunch of disks") can mean drives operated independently, each appearing as its own volume, or drives concatenated by a controller or volume manager into one large logical volume (SPAN/BIG) with no redundancy: failure of one drive can lose the data stored on it and complicate recovery of the whole volume.

MAID

MAID ("massive array of idle drives") keeps most disks spun down or powered off, spinning them up only on access. It saves power and extends drive life for rarely accessed archival data, at the cost of access latency.

Comparison with RAID

Unlike RAID levels 1, 5 or 6, these architectures provide no fault tolerance; they are chosen for simplicity, cost, or power savings. Filesystem-level approaches such as copy-on-write filesystems with built-in pooling are sometimes grouped in the same discussion.
