Four-leaf clover

The four-leaf clover is a rare variation of the common three-leaf clover (shamrock), traditionally regarded as a symbol of good luck. The extra leaflet results from genetic and environmental factors in clover plants, most often white clover (Trifolium repens). Estimates of its frequency vary widely, from about 1 in 5,000 to 1 in 10,000 clovers; clovers with even more leaflets exist, and the world record exceeds fifty leaves.

Folklore

According to tradition, each leaf represents something: the first is for faith, the second for hope, the third for love, and the fourth for luck. In Irish and broader Western folklore, finding a four-leaf clover is said to bring fortune to the finder, especially if found accidentally rather than sought. The rarity of the plant has made it a popular motif in jewellery, charms, logos, and branding—famously, the Italian carmaker Alfa Romeo's Quadrifoglio badge.

Cultivation

Because the trait is partly heritable, some growers breed and sell four-leaf clovers as novelty gifts, sometimes preserved in resin or plastic. Researchers have identified genetic markers associated with the production of the fourth leaflet, though expression depends on environmental conditions.
