Mystic chord

In music, the mystic chord or Prometheus chord is a six-note synthetic chord and its associated scale, most often linked to the work of Alexander Scriabin. It consists of fourths — augmented, diminished and perfect — and is frequently written C, F♯, B♭, E, A, D.

Scriabin used the chord prominently in Prometheus: The Poem of Fire, Op. 60, and it underpins much of his late harmonic language, functioning as a dominant-like sonority that resists traditional resolution.

Structure

Interpreted as a quartal chord, it can also be seen as an altered dominant chord with raised fourth, raised fifth and lowered ninth extensions.
