Outlaw

An outlaw, in historical legal systems, is a person declared outside the protection of the law. In pre-modern societies, all legal protection was withdrawn from the criminal, so anyone was legally empowered and permitted, or even rewarded, to kill or capture them. Outlawry was thus one of the harshest penalties in the legal system.

In early Germanic law, the death penalty was not used for most offences; instead a person convicted was declared an outlaw. In England, offenders who refused to submit to arrest or fled justice could be outlawed. The concept survives in the famous outlaws of the American West, such as Billy the Kid and Jesse James, and in folklore figures like Robin Hood.

History

Outlawry dates to Roman law and Norse and Anglo-Saxon traditions. In Iceland, outlawry (skóggangur) featured prominently in the sagas. By the 19th and 20th centuries common-law outlawry was largely abolished in England and the United States.
