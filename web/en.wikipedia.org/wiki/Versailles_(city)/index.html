Versailles (French pronunciation: [vɛʁsɑj]) is a commune in the department of Yvelines in the Île-de-France region of north-central France, about 19 km southwest of central Paris. It is world-famous for the Palace of Versailles, seat of the French court and government under Louis XIV from 1682 until the French Revolution, and it remains the prefecture of Yvelines and an important administrative and university centre.

History

Versailles was a small village until Louis XIII built a hunting lodge there in 1623. His son Louis XIV transformed it into the largest palace in Europe, moving the court and government there in 1682 and making the town the de facto capital of France for over a century. In October 1789 the royal family was forced back to Paris by the Women's March on Versailles, and the town lost its capital status.

Versailles repeatedly returned to the centre of French history: the Treaty of Versailles ending the Franco-Prussian War was accompanied in 1871 by the proclamation of the German Empire in the Hall of Mirrors, and the Treaty of Versailles of 1919 formally ended the First World War. The French parliament still meets in congress at the palace to revise the constitution.

Geography and cityscape

The city was laid out on a planned grid radiating from the palace along three great avenues. Its historic quarters — Notre-Dame and Saint-Louis — contain the royal stables, the Potager du roi (the king's kitchen garden), and Versailles Cathedral. The palace, its park and gardens form a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of France's most visited attractions.

Transport

Versailles is served by several railway stations, notably Versailles-Château–Rive Gauche on RER line C, linking the palace directly with central Paris, as well as commuter lines to Paris Montparnasse and La Défense.
