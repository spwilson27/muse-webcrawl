Arvid Lindau

Arvid Vilhelm Lindau (1892–1958)

Arvid Vilhelm Lindau (23 July 1892 – 7 September 1958) was a Swedish pathologist and bacteriologist. He is best known for his 1926 doctoral thesis describing the association between retinal angiomas and cerebellar hemangioblastomas, the condition now named von Hippel–Lindau disease.

Work

In his study of cystic tumours of the cerebellum ("Studien über Kleinhirncysten"), Lindau showed that patients with cerebellar hemangioblastomas often also had the retinal lesions described earlier by Eugen von Hippel, and recognised the syndrome as a systemic, hereditary phakomatosis also involving the kidneys, pancreas, and other organs.

Career

Lindau spent much of his career in Lund, where he became professor of bacteriology and later of pathology, and he also worked on tuberculosis and founded Sweden's BCG vaccination work against it.
