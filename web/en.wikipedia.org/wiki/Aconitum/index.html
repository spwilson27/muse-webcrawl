Aconitum

Aconitum, commonly known as aconite, monkshood, wolfsbane or devil's helmet, is a genus of over 250 species of flowering plants in the buttercup family, Ranunculaceae. Most species are herbaceous perennials native to mountainous regions of the Northern Hemisphere, growing in moist but well-drained soils of mountain meadows.

Description

The plants have dark green leaves that are palmately lobed, and tall erect stems bearing large racemes of flowers in shades of purple, blue, pink, yellow or white. The upper sepal of each flower develops into a large, helmet-shaped structure that gives the genus its common name monkshood.

Toxicity

All parts of the plant, especially the roots and seeds, contain potent alkaloids such as aconitine. Even small quantities can cause severe and potentially fatal poisoning, affecting the heart and nervous system; aconite was historically used as an arrow poison and features in classical mythology and folklore.

Cultivation

Several species and hybrids, including Aconitum napellus and Aconitum carmichaelii, are popular ornamental garden plants valued for their late-season blue and purple spires, though gardeners are advised to handle them with gloves.
