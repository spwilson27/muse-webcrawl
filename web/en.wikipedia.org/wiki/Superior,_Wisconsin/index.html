Superior is a city in northwestern Wisconsin and the county seat of Douglas County. Located at the western tip of Lake Superior, it forms with Duluth, Minnesota, the Twin Ports — together one of the largest inland ports in the world, shipping iron ore, coal, grain and other bulk cargo from the heart of North America via the Great Lakes and Saint Lawrence Seaway.

History

The area was long inhabited by Ojibwe peoples and visited by French fur traders in the 17th century. Superior was founded in the 1850s by eastern investors anticipating a rail and shipping terminus; it was incorporated as a city in 1889. Grain elevators, ore docks and shipyards drove its growth, and it vied with Duluth as the head-of-the-lakes port.

Geography and economy

The city sits on low ground south of Superior Bay and the St. Louis River estuary. Its economy rests on port operations, including the large Midwest Energy Resources coal terminal, an oil refinery operated by Cenovus, railways, and the University of Wisconsin–Superior, founded in 1893. The Richard I. Bong Veterans Historical Center commemorates the city's most famous son, Second World War ace Richard Bong, the United States' top-scoring fighter pilot.
