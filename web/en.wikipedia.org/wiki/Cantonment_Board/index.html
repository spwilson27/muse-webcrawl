Cantonment Board

A Cantonment Board is a municipal and civic administration body in India and Pakistan that governs a cantonment — a permanent military station and its associated civilian population. Cantonment Boards operate under the control of the Ministry of Defence (in India, through the Directorate General of Defence Estates) and are constituted under the Cantonments Act (in India, the Cantonments Act, 2006, replacing the Act of 1924).

Functions

Cantonment Boards perform the functions of a municipality within the cantonment area: water supply, sanitation, roads, public health, primary education, street lighting and town planning, together with the levy of taxes and fees. Because cantonments are defence land, land use is tightly regulated and the boards work alongside the station commander and defence estates officers.

Composition

Boards combine elected civilian members with nominated and ex officio military members; the station commander serves as president and the chief executive officer, appointed by the central government, is the board's executive head. Cantonments are categorised by civilian population, which determines the size of the board.

Cantonments today

India has more than sixty cantonments, including Delhi Cantonment, Pune Cantonment and Secunderabad Cantonment, many of which have grown into substantial towns within larger metropolitan areas. Their separate administration, military land restrictions and unelected character are periodically debated, and the Government of India has moved to excise civilian enclaves from some cantonments and merge them with neighbouring municipalities.

Pakistan retains a similar system under its own Cantonments Act, with cantonment boards in garrison cities such as Rawalpindi and Lahore.
