Severn barrage

Severn barrage (proposed)
Proposed alignment between Cardiff and Weston-super-Mare
LocationSevern Estuary, between Wales and England
PurposeTidal power generation
Proposed capacityUp to ~8,640 MW (Cardiff–Weston scheme)
StatusProposed; repeatedly studied, not built

The Severn barrage is a long-standing proposal for a tidal barrage across the River Severn estuary in south-western Britain, between England and Wales, intended to harness the estuary's exceptional tidal range — the second highest in the world, at up to about 15 metres — to generate electricity.

Proposals

Schemes have been studied since the 19th century and in government reports of 1925, 1933, 1945 and 1981 (the Bondi report). The best-known modern scheme, examined by the Severn Tidal Power Group, would run roughly 16 km from Lavernock Point near Cardiff to Brean Down near Weston-super-Mare, with some 200 turbines producing an estimated 8,640 MW — around 5% of the United Kingdom's electricity demand. Smaller schemes, including tidal lagoons such as the proposed Swansea Bay lagoon, have also been advanced.

Arguments and opposition

Supporters cite predictable, renewable generation and a design life of over a century. Opponents cite the very high capital cost (tens of billions of pounds), and severe environmental impacts: the estuary is a designated conservation area (SPA, SAC and Ramsar site) whose intertidal mudflats feed tens of thousands of migratory birds, and a barrage would alter habitats, impede fish migration including salmon and eels, and affect ports upstream. A 2010 government feasibility study concluded there was no strategic case for public investment at that time, and later reviews kept the scheme under consideration without approval.
