Lujiazui (Chinese: 陆家嘴; pinyin: Lùjiāzuǐ) is a locality in Pudong, Shanghai, China, on a peninsula formed by a bend in the Huangpu River. Since the early 1990s, Lujiazui has been developed as Shanghai's new financial district and one of the leading financial centres of China and the world. It is famous for its skyline of supertall skyscrapers, visible across the river from the Bund, which symbolises China's economic rise.

Geography

Lujiazui lies on the eastern bank of the Huangpu River in Pudong New Area, directly opposite the Bund and the old financial district of Shanghai on the western bank. The Lujiazui Finance and Trade Zone, formally established in 1990 as part of the opening of Pudong, covers about 31.8 km² (12.3 sq mi), of which the central skyscraper cluster occupies a much smaller core.

Landmarks

The district contains three of China's tallest buildings clustered together: the Shanghai Tower (632 m, the tallest building in China), the Shanghai World Financial Center (492 m, nicknamed the "bottle opener" for the trapezoid aperture at its top), and the Jin Mao Tower (421 m). The distinctive Oriental Pearl Radio & Television Tower (468 m), completed in 1994, was the first landmark of the new district and remains its most recognisable symbol. Lujiazui also hosts the Shanghai Stock Exchange, the Shanghai Futures Exchange, and the China Financial Futures Exchange, together with hundreds of Chinese and foreign banks and financial institutions.

Transport

The district is served by Lujiazui station on Shanghai Metro Lines 2 and 14, by the Bund Sightseeing Tunnel beneath the Huangpu River, and by ferry piers connecting to the Bund. A ring-shaped pedestrian footbridge (Lujiazui Circular Pedestrian Bridge, opened 2011) links the skyscrapers above street level and has become a popular viewpoint.
