Anne Sullivan (born Johanna Mansfield Sullivan; 14 April 1866 – 20 October 1936) was an American teacher best known as the instructor and lifelong companion of Helen Keller, who had been left blind and deaf by illness in infancy.

Early life

Sullivan herself nearly lost her sight to trachoma as a child, spent part of her childhood in the almshouse at Tewksbury, and regained partial vision after operations. She graduated from the Perkins School for the Blind in 1886 and was recommended to the Keller family of Tuscumbia, Alabama.

The "miracle worker"

Arriving in March 1887, Sullivan taught the six-year-old Keller by spelling words into her palm. The breakthrough came at the water pump, when Keller connected the letters w-a-t-e-r with the substance flowing over her hand — a scene immortalised in the play and film The Miracle Worker. Sullivan remained Keller's teacher, interpreter and closest companion for nearly fifty years.
