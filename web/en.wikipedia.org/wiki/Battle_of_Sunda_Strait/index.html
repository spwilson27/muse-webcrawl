Battle of Sunda Strait

The Battle of Sunda Strait was a naval battle of the Pacific War during the Second World War, fought on the night of 28 February – 1 March 1942 in the Sunda Strait between Java and Sumatra. The American heavy cruiser USS Houston and the Australian light cruiser HMAS Perth, retreating after the Allied defeat at the Battle of the Java Sea, ran into a Japanese invasion convoy landing troops in Bantam Bay and fought a hopeless action against overwhelming odds. Both cruisers were sunk.

The battle

Expecting the strait to be clear, Perth and Houston stumbled into the Japanese Western Java invasion force at close range shortly before midnight. In a confused night action lasting into the early hours, the two cruisers sank or damaged several transports and escorts — some Japanese vessels fell victim to their own side's Long Lance torpedoes — but, outnumbered and out of main ammunition, both were torpedoed and sunk. Captains Rooks and Waller went down with their ships.

Aftermath

About 675 men from Houston and 375 from Perth died; the survivors were taken prisoner, and many perished in captivity, including on the Burma Railway. The action was the last stand of the ABDA naval forces; Java surrendered days later. The wrecks of both cruisers lie in the Sunda Strait and are protected war graves.
