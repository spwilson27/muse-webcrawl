Survivalism

Survivalism is a social movement of individuals or groups (called survivalists or preppers) who proactively prepare for emergencies, including natural disasters, as well as disruptions to the social, political, or economic order. Preparations may include learning self-defense and self-sufficiency skills, stockpiling food and water, preparing to become self-sufficient, and building structures such as survival retreats or underground shelters that may help survivalists endure a catastrophe.

History

The origins of the modern survivalist movement in the United Kingdom and the United States can be traced to government policies and fears of nuclear war during the Cold War, the writings of economists such as Howard Ruff, and the oil crisis of the 1970s. Kurt Saxon is credited with coining the term "survivalist" in the mid-1970s. Interest in the movement surged around the year 2000 amid Y2K concerns, and again after the September 11 attacks, the 2008 financial crisis, and during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Practices

Common survivalist practices include storing long-shelf-life foods and water purification equipment, maintaining a bug-out bag for rapid evacuation, acquiring first-aid and medical training, growing food, and developing alternative power sources. Some survivalists prepare for short disruptions such as storms or blackouts, while others anticipate long-term societal collapse. Modern participants often prefer the term "prepper", which carries fewer of the political and isolationist connotations associated with the survivalist label of the 1970s and 1990s.

In popular culture

Survivalism has been depicted in television series such as Doomsday Preppers and in a large body of post-apocalyptic fiction. Interest in preparedness has also been promoted through online forums, podcasts and social media.
