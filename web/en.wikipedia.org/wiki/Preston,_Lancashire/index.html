Preston is a city on the north bank of the River Ribble in Lancashire, England. It is the administrative centre of the county and of the City of Preston borough. Preston and its surroundings have provided evidence of ancient Roman activity, largely long-distance Roman roads which led towards the north of England. The name derives from the Old English prēosta tūn, "priests' settlement".

History

The town received a market charter in 1179 and later became an important centre of the Lancashire cotton trade during the Industrial Revolution, though conditions in its mills contributed to the Preston Strike and lockout of 1853–54, which inspired Charles Dickens's novel Hard Times. The Battle of Preston (1648) in the Civil War and the Battle of Preston (1715) in the Jacobite rising both took place in or near the town. Preston was granted city status in 2002, during the Golden Jubilee of Elizabeth II, becoming England's 50th city.

Geography and transport

Preston stands on the lowest bridging point of the River Ribble, roughly 27 miles (43 km) north-west of Manchester. The M6 motorway passes to the east of the city, and Preston railway station is a major stop on the West Coast Main Line. The Lancaster Canal also runs through the city.

Culture and sport

The city is home to the University of Central Lancashire, one of the largest universities in the United Kingdom. Preston North End F.C., a founder member of the Football League in 1888 and winners of the first league championship, play at Deepdale, one of the oldest continuously used football stadiums in the world. The Preston Guild, a civic celebration held once every twenty years, dates from 1179; the most recent was in 2012, giving rise to the Lancashire phrase "once every Preston Guild".
