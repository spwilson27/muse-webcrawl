Service medal

A service medal is a military decoration awarded to members of the armed forces for participation in a specific campaign, operation, or period of service, rather than for individual acts of gallantry or meritorious achievement.

Characteristics

Service medals are typically awarded to all personnel who meet defined criteria, such as serving a minimum number of days in a theatre of operations. They are often accompanied by clasps or devices denoting particular battles, campaigns, or additional periods of service. Examples include the World War II Victory Medal of the United States and the general service medals issued by the United Kingdom.

Distinction from other awards

Unlike gallantry decorations, which recognise specific acts of bravery, service medals are essentially commemorative of qualifying service. Campaign medals are a closely related category tied to particular wars or operations.
