Izzy Asper

Israel Harold "Izzy" Asper OC OM QC (August 11, 1932 – October 7, 2003) was a Canadian tax lawyer, politician, and media magnate. He founded Canwest Global Communications, which grew from a single television station into one of Canada's largest media companies.

Early life and politics

Asper was born in Minnedosa, Manitoba, to Jewish immigrant parents, and raised in Winnipeg. He earned law degrees from the University of Manitoba and became a leading tax lawyer, writing a newspaper column on taxation. Entering politics, he became leader of the Manitoba Liberal Party in 1970 and was elected to the provincial legislature for Wolseley in 1972; he resigned as leader in 1975 and returned to law and business.

Canwest

In 1977 Asper purchased the Winnipeg station KCND-TV's assets and relaunched it as CKND-TV. From this base he assembled the Global Television Network across Canada and expanded internationally, acquiring TV3 in New Zealand, Network Ten in Australia, and stakes in British and Irish media. In 2000 Canwest bought the Hollinger newspaper chain, including the National Post, making Asper Canada's dominant private media owner. His hard-driving style, political activism, and support for Israel and free-market causes made him a prominent and controversial public figure.

Legacy

Asper was a major philanthropist, endowing the Asper Foundation and championing the creation of the Canadian Museum for Human Rights in Winnipeg, which opened in 2014. He died in 2003; his children, notably Leonard Asper, succeeded him at Canwest, which entered creditor protection in 2009 following heavy acquisition debt.
