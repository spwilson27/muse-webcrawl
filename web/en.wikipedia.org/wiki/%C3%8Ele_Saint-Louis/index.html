Île Saint-Louis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Île Saint-Louis is one of two natural islands in the Seine in the centre of Paris, France — the other being the Île de la Cité. It lies just east of its larger neighbour, to which it is connected by the Pont Saint-Louis, and is part of the 4th arrondissement of Paris.

History

Unlike the Île de la Cité, settled since antiquity, the Île Saint-Louis remained pastureland — used for grazing cattle and storing timber — until the 17th century. Two smaller islets, the Île Notre-Dame and the Île aux Vaches, were merged and developed under a project begun in 1614 under Marie de' Medici and completed in the reign of Louis XIII, whose name the joined island took. Developer Christophe Marie built its regular street grid, quays, and the bridge that still bears his name, the Pont Marie (1635).

The island was developed with elegant stone townhouses, the hôtels particuliers, many designed by architect Louis Le Vau, including the Hôtel Lambert (1640s), famous for its painted Gallery of Hercules. The Church of Saint-Louis-en-l'Île was built between 1664 and 1775.

Today

The Île Saint-Louis is a quiet, largely residential enclave known for its narrow streets, boutiques, and the celebrated Berthillon ice-cream shop on the Rue Saint-Louis-en-l'Île. It has been home over the centuries to artists, writers, and politicians, and forms part of the UNESCO-listed Banks of the Seine World Heritage site.
