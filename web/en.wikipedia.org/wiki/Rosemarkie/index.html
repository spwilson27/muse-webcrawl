Rosemarkie

Rosemarkie (Scottish Gaelic: Ros Maircnidh, meaning "Ross of the horse river") is a village on the south coast of the Black Isle peninsula in Ross-shire, in the Highland council area of Scotland. It lies on the shore of the Moray Firth, roughly 14 miles (23 km) north-east of Inverness, and forms a linked settlement with the neighbouring village of Fortrose, with which it shares a parish.

History

The area around Rosemarkie is rich in Pictish archaeology. Numerous carved Pictish stones have been recovered in and around the village, including fragments of cross-slabs and symbol stones; a significant collection is displayed at the Groam House Museum in the village, which specialises in Pictish and early medieval art. The finds suggest that Rosemarkie was the site of an important Pictish monastery, possibly founded in the 7th or 8th century and associated with Saint Moluag or Saint Boniface (Curitan), to whom the parish church is traditionally linked.

Rosemarkie was once the seat of the bishops of Ross before the episcopal seat moved to Fortrose, where the remains of Fortrose Cathedral stand. The medieval church of Rosemarkie stood on the site of the present parish church, built in the early 19th century.

Geography and attractions

The village has a long sandy beach along Rosemarkie Bay and is a popular spot for watching the bottlenose dolphins of the Moray Firth, which can often be seen close inshore near Chanonry Point between Rosemarkie and Fortrose. The Fairy Glen, a wooded gorge inland from the village managed as a nature reserve, contains waterfalls and is associated in local folklore with fairies and wishing rituals. To the north-east along the coast lie a series of sea caves, including the large cave known as Caird's Cave.

Rosemarkie is home to the Rosemarkie Camanachd Club, a shinty team, and to facilities including a golf course shared with Fortrose. The village lies within the civil parish of Rosemarkie, in the historic county of Ross-shire.
