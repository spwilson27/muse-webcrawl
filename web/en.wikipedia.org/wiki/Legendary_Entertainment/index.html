Legendary Entertainment

Legendary Entertainment, LLC, also known as Legendary Pictures or simply Legendary, is an American film production and mass media company based in Burbank, California, founded by Thomas Tull in 2000. The company has collaborated with major studios including Warner Bros. Pictures and Universal Pictures on large-scale genre films. Legendary is best known for producing the MonsterVerse franchise (including Godzilla and Kong: Skull Island), Christopher Nolan's The Dark Knight trilogy (as co-producer/financier), Pacific Rim, Jurassic World (co-financed), Dune and Dune: Part Two, Pokémon Detective Pikachu, and A Minecraft Movie. In 2016, the Chinese conglomerate Wanda Group acquired a majority stake in the company for a reported $3.5 billion; in 2024, Legendary bought out Wanda's remaining stake, returning the company to majority control by its management and Apollo Global Management.

History

Founding and the Warner Bros. era

Thomas Tull, a former private equity executive, founded Legendary in 2000 and raised capital from private equity firms to finance studio films while retaining a share of ownership—an unusual model at the time. In 2005, Legendary signed a landmark deal with Warner Bros. to co-produce and co-finance dozens of films. Early fruit of the partnership included Batman Begins (2005), Superman Returns (2006), 300 (2007), Watchmen (2009), The Hangover (2009), Inception (2010), and The Dark Knight (2008) and The Dark Knight Rises (2012), among the highest-grossing films of their era. The company built a reputation for fan-oriented, large-scale genre properties and for savvy use of audience data.

Universal deal and the MonsterVerse

In 2013, Legendary ended its Warner Bros. arrangement and signed a distribution and co-financing deal with Universal Pictures beginning in 2014. With Warner Bros., however, it retained the Godzilla property: Godzilla (2014), directed by Gareth Edwards, launched the MonsterVerse, a shared universe of giant-monster films continued with Kong: Skull Island (2017), Godzilla: King of the Monsters (2019), Godzilla vs. Kong (2021), and Godzilla x Kong: The New Empire (2024), plus the Apple TV+ series Monarch: Legacy of Monsters. Under Universal, Legendary co-financed hits such as Jurassic World (2015) and produced Pacific Rim (2013, with Warner Bros.) and its sequel, as well as Warcraft (2016), which performed strongly in China.

Wanda acquisition and later developments

In January 2016, Dalian Wanda Group announced its acquisition of Legendary for approximately $3.5 billion, then the largest acquisition of an American media company by a Chinese firm. Thomas Tull departed in 2017. The company later shifted distribution back to Warner Bros. for key franchises, scoring major successes with Dune (2021) and Dune: Part Two (2024), both directed by Denis Villeneuve and adapted from Frank Herbert's novel, and with Pokémon Detective Pikachu (2019). In 2022, Apollo Global Management made a $760 million minority investment. In October 2024, Legendary bought out Wanda's remaining interest, ending Chinese majority ownership. Its 2025 release A Minecraft Movie, based on the video game, became one of the highest-grossing films of the year.

Divisions

Legendary operates subsidiaries including Legendary Television, which has produced series such as Monarch: Legacy of Monsters, The Expanse (later seasons), and Carnival Row; Legendary Comics, a graphic-novel imprint; and Legendary East, focused on the Chinese market. The company also maintains an analytics and digital division rooted in Tull's data-driven approach to audience targeting.

Selected filmography

Notable productions include The Dark Knight trilogy (2005–2012), 300 (2007), Inception (2010), Pacific Rim (2013), Godzilla (2014), Interstellar (2014, co-production), Jurassic World (2015, co-financing), Straight Outta Compton (2015), The Great Wall (2016), Kong: Skull Island (2017), Pokémon Detective Pikachu (2019), Enola Holmes (2020), Dune (2021), Godzilla vs. Kong (2021), Dune: Part Two (2024), and A Minecraft Movie (2025).
