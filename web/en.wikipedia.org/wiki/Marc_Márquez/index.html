Marc Márquez Alentà (born 17 February 1993) is a Spanish Grand Prix motorcycle road racer widely regarded as one of the greatest riders of all time. He has won eight Grand Prix World Championships, six of them in the premier MotoGP class, and in 2013 became the youngest-ever MotoGP world champion at 20 years 266 days, as well as the first rookie to win the title since Kenny Roberts in 1978. Nicknamed the "Ant of Cervera," he is famed for an extreme riding style — extraordinary lean angles, elbow-down cornering, and an uncanny ability to save front-end slides.

Career

Márquez won the 125cc world title in 2010 and the Moto2 crown in 2012 before stepping up to MotoGP with Repsol Honda in 2013. He won back-to-back titles in 2013–14 (including ten consecutive wins to open 2014), then, after a crash-strewn 2015, four straight championships from 2016 to 2019, often clinching with rounds to spare.

Injury and comeback

A broken right humerus in the 2020 season opener at Jerez began a four-year ordeal of surgeries and complications, including a fourth operation in 2022 at the Mayo Clinic to rotate the bone back into alignment, compounded by recurring diplopia. After Honda's competitiveness collapsed, Márquez left for the satellite Gresini Ducati team in 2024, returned to winning that season, and for 2025 joined the factory Ducati Lenovo team alongside Francesco Bagnaia.

Style and records

Márquez's dominance blended qualifying speed (a record tally of premier-class pole positions), aggressive overtaking, and unrivalled crash-saving reflexes. Together with his younger brother Álex Márquez, a Moto3 and Moto2 champion, he forms the most successful sibling pairing in Grand Prix history.
