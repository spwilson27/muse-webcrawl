Nuptial flight

A nuptial flight is an important stage in the reproduction of most ant, termite, and some bee species. During the flight, virgin queens and males (alates) emerge in large numbers from many colonies simultaneously, mate in the air or on the ground, and disperse to found new colonies.

Process

Flights are typically triggered by suitable weather — warm, humid, calm conditions, often after rain — and synchronised across colonies of a species in a region, which swamps predators and promotes outcrossing. Males usually die shortly after mating. The fertilised queen sheds or bites off her wings, excavates a first chamber, and raises the initial brood on stored body fat and, in some species, a fungus garden starter carried in her mouth.

Ecology

Nuptial flights provide a seasonal food bonanza for birds, dragonflies, bats and other predators. In some regions the swarming of termites is so predictable that people collect the alates as food. The phenomenon is sometimes colloquially called "flying ant day" in Britain.
