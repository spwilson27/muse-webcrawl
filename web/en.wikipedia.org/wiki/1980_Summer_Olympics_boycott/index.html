The 1980 Summer Olympics boycott was a protest led by the United States against the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan. President Jimmy Carter announced that the United States would not send a team to the Moscow Games unless Soviet forces withdrew by February 1980. When the deadline passed, the United States Olympic Committee voted to boycott, and dozens of countries either stayed away or competed under restrictions.

Scope

Sixty-five invited nations did not participate, though not all absences were for boycott reasons. Some governments allowed athletes to choose; several Western European teams marched under the Olympic flag rather than their national flags. Athletes who lost their only Olympic opportunity became the boycott's most visible cost. Alternative meets, including the Liberty Bell Classic in Philadelphia, were arranged for some sports.

Consequences

The Soviet Union and its allies retaliated by boycotting the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics. Historians debate the Moscow boycott's effect on Soviet policy; it did not end the Afghan war, but it damaged the prestige of the Moscow Games, deepened Cold War sporting division and strengthened later arguments that mega-event boycotts mainly punish athletes.
