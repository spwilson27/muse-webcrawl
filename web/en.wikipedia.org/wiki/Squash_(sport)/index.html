Squash (sport)

Squash
Highest governing body: World Squash Federation
First played: c. 1830, Harrow School, England
Players: 2 (singles) or 4 (doubles)
Equipment: Squash racket, squash ball

Squash is a racket sport played by two players (or four for doubles) in a four-walled court with a small, hollow rubber ball. Players alternate striking the ball against the front wall, and may use the side and back walls, aiming to make the ball bounce twice before the opponent can return it. The sport was formerly called squash rackets, referring to the soft, "squashable" ball used, in contrast to the harder ball of the related game of rackets.

History

Squash developed at Harrow School in London around 1830, when pupils discovered that a punctured rackets ball, which "squashed" on impact with the wall, produced a game with a greater variety of shots. The sport spread through schools, clubs and the British military; the first purpose-built courts appeared in the nineteenth century, and the game was codified in the twentieth. The World Squash Federation, founded in 1967, governs the sport internationally, and squash was added to the Olympic programme for the Los Angeles 2028 Games.

Play and scoring

The ball is served diagonally into the opposite service box, and rallies continue until a player fails to make a valid return. Modern scoring is point-a-rally to 11, with a two-point margin required at 10–all. Balls are graded by speed (marked with coloured dots), with slower balls bouncing less and demanding greater fitness and skill. Shots include drives, boasts off a side wall, drops, lobs and volleys, and players must give their opponent fair access to the ball, under the "let" and "stroke" rules.

Equipment and courts

The standard singles court measures 9.75 m by 6.4 m. Rackets are lightweight, typically under 200 grams strung, and the small rubber ball warms up and becomes bouncier as play proceeds. Professional events are often staged on all-glass show courts.
