Radio Cymru is the main Welsh-language radio station, based in Cardiff.

BBC Radio Cymru is a Welsh-language radio network owned and operated by BBC Cymru Wales, a division of the BBC. It is the main radio service broadcasting entirely in Welsh, carrying news, sport, music, drama and entertainment for Welsh speakers. Its English-language sister station is BBC Radio Wales.

History

Welsh-language programmes had been broadcast regionally by the BBC since the 1930s within the Welsh Home Service, but campaigners long demanded a dedicated station. Radio Cymru launched on 3 January 1977, shortly before its Scottish Gaelic counterpart services were expanded, although its founding was controversial: some Welsh-language activists had campaigned for a combined radio and television authority, and the launch of the station while Welsh television remained limited to opt-outs on BBC Wales helped fuel the campaign that produced the television channel S4C in 1982. A second service aimed at younger listeners, Radio Cymru 2, began in 2018, initially as a breakfast-time opt-out and later expanding its hours.

Programming

The station's output mixes news bulletins and current affairs (including the morning programme Dros Frecwast and midday's Dros Ginio), extensive Welsh-language music, rugby and football coverage, comedy, and coverage of cultural festivals such as the National Eisteddfod and the Urdd Eisteddfod. Radio Cymru is an important outlet for contemporary Welsh-language popular music and has launched or supported many Welsh bands and presenters.

Audience

Radio Cymru reaches roughly 100,000–125,000 listeners per week, a significant share of Wales's fluent Welsh-speaking population, and is regarded as a cornerstone of Welsh-language media alongside S4C.
