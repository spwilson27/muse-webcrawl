"Heroes" is the twelfth studio album by the English musician David Bowie, released on 14 October 1977 through RCA Records. Recorded in collaboration with the musician Brian Eno and the producer Tony Visconti, it is the second instalment of Bowie's so-called "Berlin Trilogy", following Low (1977) and preceding Lodger (1979). It was the only album of the trilogy to be recorded entirely in Berlin, at Hansa Tonstudio, a studio close to the Berlin Wall.

Background and recording

Bowie had moved to West Berlin in late 1976 to escape a drug addiction and to work in a less pressurised environment. The sessions for "Heroes" took place in July and August 1977 and reunited much of the personnel from Low, including Carlos Alomar, Dennis Davis and George Murray. The guitarist Robert Fripp, formerly of King Crimson, was brought in and recorded his parts within a few days, often playing along to tracks he had not previously heard.

Music and lyrics

The album's second side continues the experimental, largely instrumental approach of Low, featuring ambient pieces such as "Sense of Doubt", "Moss Garden" and "Neuköln". Side one contains more conventional, though still avant-garde, rock songs. The title track, "Heroes", tells of two lovers meeting at the Berlin Wall; inspired in part by producer Tony Visconti embracing his girlfriend by the Wall, it has become one of Bowie's signature songs, though it was only a modest chart success on release.

Reception and legacy

On release, "Heroes" was well received by critics and reached number 3 on the UK Albums Chart, though it performed less strongly in the United States. It has since been regarded as one of Bowie's finest works. The album cover photograph, like that of Iggy Pop's The Idiot, was inspired by Erich Heckel's painting Roquairol. In later years the title track was widely embraced in popular culture, notably following Bowie's death in 2016, when it charted highly in several countries.
