Flerovium

Flerovium is a superheavy chemical element with the symbol Fl and atomic number 114. It is an extremely radioactive, synthetic element: it has only been created in particle accelerators and no macroscopic quantity has ever been produced. The element is named after the Flerov Laboratory of Nuclear Reactions in Dubna, Russia, itself named after physicist Georgy Flyorov.

Discovery

Flerovium was first reported in 1999 by a team at the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research (JINR) in Dubna, who bombarded plutonium-244 targets with calcium-48 ions. Later experiments, including work at the Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, confirmed additional isotopes. The name flerovium was officially adopted by IUPAC in 2012.

Properties

In the periodic table flerovium sits in group 14, below lead, and was once expected to behave as a heavy homologue of lead. However, relativistic effects on its electron configuration are predicted to make it unusually inert: calculations and early gas-phase chemistry experiments suggest flerovium may be a volatile metal, possibly even behaving somewhat like a noble gas. All known isotopes are highly unstable; the longest-lived known isotope, 289Fl, has a half-life of about 2.6 seconds.

Island of stability

With 114 protons, flerovium lies close to the centre of the theorised "island of stability", a region of the chart of nuclides where superheavy nuclei are predicted to have longer half-lives due to filled nuclear shells. Interest in flerovium and its neighbours is driven partly by the search for such relatively stable superheavy nuclei.
