Bespoke

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bespoke is an adjective meaning made to order, or custom-made to a particular buyer's specifications. The term originates in British tailoring: a cloth was said to be "bespoken" once a customer had reserved it. It is most strongly associated with the bespoke tailoring tradition of Savile Row in London, where a bespoke suit is cut from a pattern drafted entirely from scratch for the individual client, as distinct from made-to-measure garments altered from a standard pattern and off-the-peg (ready-to-wear) clothing.

Bespoke tailoring

A fully bespoke garment traditionally involves the drafting of an individual paper pattern, a baste fitting in unfinished cloth so the cutter can correct the fit, and two or more subsequent fittings before the garment is completed largely by hand. The Savile Row Bespoke Association sets standards for member tailors, including a minimum number of hours of handwork and the requirement to keep individual patterns for customers.

Wider usage

By extension, "bespoke" has spread beyond clothing to describe custom software ("bespoke software"), furniture, bicycles and other goods and services made or adapted for a specific customer rather than mass-produced. In British business usage it is often contrasted with "off-the-shelf" offerings. The word's popularity in marketing has broadened its meaning so much that some writers regard it as a fashionable synonym for "custom".
