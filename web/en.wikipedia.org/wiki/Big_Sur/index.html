Big Sur is a rugged and mountainous section of the Central Coast of California, where the Santa Lucia Mountains rise abruptly from the Pacific Ocean. Its name derives from the Spanish el país grande del sur, "the big country of the south".

Geography

The region stretches roughly from Carmel south to San Simeon along Highway 1, famous for the Bixby Creek Bridge and sweeping ocean views. Cone Peak rises to about 1,570 m within a few kilometres of the shore. State parks including Pfeiffer Big Sur, Julia Pfeiffer Burns (with McWay Falls) and Andrew Molera protect redwood canyons and beaches.

Culture

Sparsely populated, Big Sur attracted writers and artists — Henry Miller lived there, Jack Kerouac wrote the novel Big Sur, and the Esalen Institute, founded in 1962, became a centre of the human potential movement. Tourism is the main industry, bringing millions of drivers along Highway 1 each year, alongside recurring wildfires and landslides that periodically close the road.
