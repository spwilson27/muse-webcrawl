Jack Yates Senior High School is a public high school located at 3650 Alabama Street in Houston, Texas, serving grades 9 through 12. Named for Reverend John Henry "Jack" Yates, a former slave, Baptist minister, and community leader in Houston's Fourth Ward, the school is a historically African American institution in the Third Ward near Texas Southern University, and is part of the Houston Independent School District (HISD).

History

The school traces its origins to Yates Colored High School, established in 1892, the second African American high school in Houston. The campus moved to its current Third Ward location in 1926, when a new building opened on Alabama Street; the original building became part of what is now James D. Ryan Middle School. A modern replacement campus was built adjacent to the historic building and opened in 2018, preserving the original structure. Yates was long a centre of Black academic, athletic, and civic life in Houston during the era of segregation.

Athletics

The Yates Lions are renowned for their basketball program, which won consecutive Texas Class 4A state championships in 2009 and 2010; the 2009–10 team, which scored over 100 points in many games, was ranked among the top teams in the nation. The football program has also produced successful seasons and numerous collegiate and professional players.

Programs and alumni

Yates houses a magnet program in communications, learnings in print, radio, and television broadcast media, and has partnerships with nearby Texas Southern University. Notable alumni include members of the R&B group H-Town, NFL players, and numerous educators and civil rights figures in Houston.
