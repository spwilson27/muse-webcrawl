The Croatian Parliament (Croatian: Hrvatski sabor, "Croatian assembly") is the unicameral legislature of the Republic of Croatia. Under the Constitution it exercises legislative power, adopts the state budget, decides on war and peace, and oversees the Government. It has 151 members, elected for four-year terms: 140 from ten multi-member electoral districts within Croatia, three by the Croatian diaspora, and eight by national minorities.

History

The Sabor traces its origins to medieval assemblies of the Croatian nobility and clergy; the first documented Sabor met in Zagreb in 1273, and the assembly at Cetin in 1527 elected Ferdinand of Habsburg as king of Croatia after the catastrophe of Mohács. The Sabor preserved an institutional continuity through the Habsburg, and later Austro-Hungarian, periods, notably in 1848 when Ban Josip Jelačić convened it amid demands for national rights, and in 1918 when it severed ties with Austria-Hungary. During socialist Yugoslavia its role was reduced, but the first multi-party elections of 1990 restored it as the legislature of an independent Croatia, whose constitution it adopted on 22 December 1990.

Organisation

The Parliament is led by a Speaker assisted by deputy speakers, and works through standing committees. It was bicameral between 1990 and 2001, when constitutional amendments abolished the Chamber of Counties. Sessions are held at the Sabor Palace in Zagreb's Upper Town, on St. Mark's Square, opposite the seat of the Government.
