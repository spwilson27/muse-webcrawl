The Magic Roundabout in Hemel Hempstead, Hertfordshire, England, is the familiar name given to the Plough Roundabout, a large ring junction constructed in 1973. It consists of six smaller roundabouts (mini-roundabouts) arranged in a ring, through which traffic may flow in both directions around the central island. The junction connects five approach roads and a former sixth arm, at the intersection near the River Gade and the town's railway station.

Design

The junction works as a gyratory system: drivers may travel clockwise or anticlockwise around the ring, yielding at each mini-roundabout. This two-way circulation multiplies the possible routes through the junction, which can be confusing for unfamiliar drivers but keeps traffic moving, since vehicles need not complete a full circuit to reach their exit. The arrangement was designed to handle heavy flows between the A414 and other routes serving the new town.

Name and reputation

The name "Magic Roundabout" was borrowed from the children's television programme The Magic Roundabout, and from the better-known Swindon Magic Roundabout, which had opened the previous year. The Hemel Hempstead junction is unusual in having six mini-roundabouts rather than five. It regularly appears in surveys and popular lists of Britain's most intimidating junctions, though local drivers generally regard it as efficient once its logic is understood.
