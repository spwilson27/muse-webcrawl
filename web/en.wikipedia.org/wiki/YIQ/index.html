YIQ

YIQ color space
Used byNTSC analog color television
ComponentsY (luma), I (in-phase), Q (quadrature)
Introduced1953 (NTSC color standard)

YIQ is the color space used by the analog NTSC color television system, formerly the standard in North America and parts of Asia. It encodes a color image as one luminance component (Y) and two chrominance components: I (in-phase), roughly orange-to-cyan, and Q (quadrature), roughly purple-to-green.

Design

The YIQ encoding was designed in the early 1950s so that color broadcasts remained compatible with existing black-and-white receivers, which simply displayed the Y signal. The axes were chosen to match the human eye's color sensitivities: viewers are most sensitive to changes along the orange–cyan (I) axis, so I was allotted more bandwidth (about 1.3 MHz) than Q (about 0.4 MHz), while fine detail was carried entirely by Y.

Relation to other spaces

YIQ is a linear rotation and scaling of the YUV space used by PAL: the I and Q axes are the U and V axes rotated by 33 degrees. Conversion between RGB and YIQ is a simple matrix multiplication, which made YIQ cheap to implement in analog circuitry and in early digital image processing. Although analog NTSC broadcasting has ended in most regions, YIQ transformations still appear in image-processing software and in some compression research.
