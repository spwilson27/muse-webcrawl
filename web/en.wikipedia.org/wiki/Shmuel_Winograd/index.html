Shmuel Winograd

Shmuel Winograd (January 4, 1936 – March 25, 2019) was an Israeli-American computer scientist, noted for his contributions to computational complexity theory and arithmetic computing. He spent much of his career at IBM's Thomas J. Watson Research Center.

Biography

Winograd was born in Tel Aviv. He studied electrical engineering at the Technion – Israel Institute of Technology, earning his bachelor's and master's degrees, and received his Ph.D. in electrical engineering and computer science from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1968. He joined IBM Research in 1961 and remained there for the bulk of his career.

Work

Winograd is best known for the Winograd algorithm for fast matrix multiplication, which in 1970 showed that matrix multiplication could be performed with O(n2.81) operations, the first improvement over the naive cubic algorithm's exponent since Strassen's work. With Don Coppersmith, he later developed the Coppersmith–Winograd algorithm (1987), which held the record for the fastest known matrix multiplication exponent (O(n2.376)) for over two decades. He also developed the Winograd Fourier transform algorithm and Winograd's minimum convolution algorithm, widely used in signal processing and in convolutional neural network libraries (the "Winograd convolution" used to accelerate CNN inference).

Honors

Winograd was elected to the National Academy of Engineering and the National Academy of Sciences, and was a Fellow of the IEEE and the Association for Computing Machinery. He co-authored, with Shlomo S. Allan, the book Arithmetic Complexity of Computations (1980).
