Ned Washington (born Edward Michael Washington, 15 August 1901 – 20 December 1976) was an American lyricist who wrote songs for vaudeville, Broadway, and especially for Hollywood films, winning three Academy Awards for Best Original Song. He is regarded as one of the great lyricists of the Great American Songbook era.

Career

Born in Scranton, Pennsylvania, Washington began writing for vaudeville and revues in New York in the 1920s, contributing songs to the Earl Carroll's Vanities. With the arrival of talking pictures he moved to Hollywood, where he wrote lyrics for dozens of films.

His best-known songs include "When You Wish Upon a Star" (with Leigh Harline), written for Walt Disney's Pinocchio (1940), which won the Academy Award for Best Original Song and became Disney's signature tune; the title song from High Noon (1952), "Do Not Forsake Me, Oh My Darlin'" (with Dimitri Tiomkin), another Oscar winner; and "The Nearness of You" (with Hoagy Carmichael), a standard recorded by generations of jazz and pop singers. With Tiomkin he also won an Oscar for the score of The High and the Mighty (1954), and the pair wrote the themes for Rawhide and other television series.

Other well-known Washington lyrics include "My Foolish Heart", "Stella by Starlight", "Town Without Pity", "I Don't Stand a Ghost of a Chance with You" and "I'm Getting Sentimental Over You". He was nominated for Academy Awards twelve times and was inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 1972.

Death

Washington died of a heart ailment in Beverly Hills, California, in December 1976, aged 75.
