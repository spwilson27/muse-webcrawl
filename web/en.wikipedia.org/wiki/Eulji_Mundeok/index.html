Eulji Mundeok (Korean: 을지문덕) was a military leader and statesman of Goguryeo, one of the Three Kingdoms of Korea, famed for defeating the invasion of Sui dynasty China at the Battle of Salsu in 612. He is revered in Korea as one of its greatest national heroes.

Background

Little is known of his early life; he rose to the post of prime minister of Goguryeo by the time Emperor Yang of Sui launched his invasions. The Sui campaigns of 612 mobilised, by Chinese accounts, over a million men against Goguryeo.

The 612 campaign and Salsu

Eulji employed feigned retreat and scorched-earth tactics, visiting the Sui camp under a flag of truce and composing a famous mocking poem to the Sui general Yu Zhongwen urging withdrawal. As the starving Sui army—having overrun its supplies—retreated toward the Salsu River (the Chongchon River), Eulji's forces dammed the river and released the water at the moment of crossing, then attacked from all sides. Chinese histories report catastrophic losses; of some 305,000 troops in the detached column, only about 2,700 allegedly returned. The disaster contributed to the fall of the Sui dynasty in 618.

Legacy

Eulji Mundeok's victory is a cornerstone of Korean military memory: a major avenue in Seoul (Euljiro) bears his name, South Korea's Order of Military Merit Eulji and a joint US–South Korean exercise were named Ulchi in his honour, and his pre-battle poem remains a classic of Korean literature.
