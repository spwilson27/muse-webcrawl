Songhua River

Length: 1,434 km (891 mi)
Basin size: 557,180 km²
Mouth: Amur River at Tongjiang

The Songhua River (Chinese: 松花江; pinyin: Sōnghuā Jiāng) is the largest tributary of the Amur River, flowing about 1,434 kilometres (891 mi) from the Changbai Mountains on the China–North Korea border through northeast China (Manchuria). It drains a basin of roughly 557,000 square kilometres in Heilongjiang, Jilin, and Inner Mongolia provinces, and passes the major cities of Jilin City, Harbin, and Jiamusi before joining the Amur at Tongjiang.

The frozen Songhua River at Harbin, site of the annual ice festival.

Course

The river rises on the slopes of the Changbai Mountains near Heaven Lake (Tianchi) on the border with North Korea. Its main tributaries include the Nen River, which joins it near Da'an and contributes the larger share of flow, the Mudan River, and the Hulan River. Below the Nen confluence the Songhua flows northeast past Harbin, then east and northeast to its confluence with the Amur (Heilongjiang) on the Russian border.

History and events

In November 2005, an explosion at a petrochemical plant in Jilin City released about 100 tonnes of benzene and nitrobenzene into the river, creating an 80-kilometre toxic slick. Water supplies to Harbin, a city of several million, were suspended for days, and the pollution travelled downstream into the Amur, causing diplomatic friction with Russia. In July 2010, flooding washed thousands of barrels of chemicals into the river near Jilin City. The frozen river at Harbin is a centrepiece of the Harbin International Ice and Snow Festival, where ice blocks cut from the river are used to build illuminated sculptures.

Navigation and development

The Songhua is navigable by medium-sized vessels as far as Harbin, and historically served as a major transport artery for Manchuria, especially during the period of Russian and Japanese influence in the early twentieth century. Dams on the upper river, including the Fengman Dam near Jilin City, provide hydroelectric power and flood control.
