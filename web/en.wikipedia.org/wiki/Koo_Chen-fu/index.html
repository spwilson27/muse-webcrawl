Koo Chen-fu (Chinese: 辜振甫; January 6, 1917 – January 3, 2005) was a Taiwanese businessman and diplomat. He led the Koos Group (now KGI-affiliated family conglomerate) and served as chairman of Taiwan's Straits Exchange Foundation (SEF), representing the Republic of China in negotiations with the People's Republic of China.

Business career

Koo was born into the wealthy Lukang Koo family while Taiwan was under Japanese rule, and studied in Japan. After the Second World War he became one of Taiwan's most prominent industrialists, building the family's cement, finance, and manufacturing interests—centered on Taiwan Cement—into one of the island's major business groups. He also served in senior roles in organizations such as the Chinese National Association of Industry and Commerce.

Cross-strait diplomacy

In 1990 Koo became the first chairman of the Straits Exchange Foundation, the semi-official body handling relations with mainland China. In April 1993 he met Wang Daohan, chairman of Beijing's Association for Relations Across the Taiwan Straits, in Singapore—the first high-level contact between the two sides since 1949. The Koo–Wang talks produced four agreements on practical matters such as document verification and registered mail, and are remembered as a landmark in cross-strait relations. Koo met Wang again in Shanghai in 1998. He died in Taipei in January 2005.
