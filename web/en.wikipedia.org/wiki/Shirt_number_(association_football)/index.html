Shirt number (association football)

In association football, shirt numbers (squad numbers) identify and distinguish players on the field. Numbers were first worn in the 1920s — notable early uses include Arsenal and Chelsea in England in August 1928 — and became standard with the adoption of the 2–3–5 formation numbering: the goalkeeper wore 1, defenders 2–3, half-backs 4–6, and forwards 7–11.

Traditional numbering

In the classic system still influential today: 1 is the goalkeeper; 2 and 3 are full-backs; 4, 5 and 6 are central defenders and defensive midfielders; 7 and 11 are wingers; 8 is a central midfielder; 9 is the centre-forward or main striker; and 10 is the playmaker or second striker, a number made iconic by players such as Pelé and Diego Maradona. Regional variations exist, particularly in South America, where numbering developed along different lines.

Squad numbers

From the 1990s, most major leagues introduced fixed squad numbers for the whole season, allowing players to keep personal numbers regardless of position, and enabling unusual choices — such as Ivan Zamorano's "1+8" at Inter Milan. Competitions like the FIFA World Cup allocate numbers 1–26 (or the squad size) per tournament. Retired numbers honour club legends, though many leagues restrict or discourage the practice.
