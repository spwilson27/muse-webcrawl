Silicon is the chemical element with the symbol Si and atomic number 14. It is a hard, brittle, blue-grey metalloid and the second most abundant element in Earth's crust, mostly as silica (sand, quartz) and silicate minerals.

Silicon is the backbone of modern electronics: purified single-crystal silicon wafers are doped with traces of other elements to make the transistors and integrated circuits of computers — hence 'Silicon Valley'. It is also the main ingredient of glass, concrete and silicones.

[Image: A polished silicon wafer with a rainbow sheen, and quartz crystals]

History

Jöns Jacob Berzelius first prepared amorphous silicon in 1824 and is credited with its discovery; the name comes from the Latin silex (flint). Crystalline silicon was obtained by Henri Sainte-Claire Deville in 1854.

Chemistry

Like carbon, silicon has four valence electrons, but it forms much weaker double bonds and prefers single-bonded networks. Its dioxide, SiO₂, is one of the most common compounds on Earth.
