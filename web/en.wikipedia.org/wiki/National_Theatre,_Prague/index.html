National Theatre, Prague

National Theatre (Národní divadlo)
LocationPrague, Czech Republic
Opened1881; reopened 1883
ArchitectJosef Zítek; Josef Schulz (reconstruction)
StyleNeo-Renaissance

The National Theatre (Czech: Národní divadlo) in Prague is the Czech Republic's principal stage for drama, opera and ballet and a national cultural monument. Standing on the bank of the Vltava River, it is known as the "alma mater of Czech opera" and a symbol of the Czech national revival.

Construction and fire

The theatre was funded by donations from across the Czech lands; its foundation stones, brought from across Bohemia and Moravia, were laid in 1868. Designed by Josef Zítek in neo-Renaissance style, it opened on 11 June 1881 with Smetana's opera Libuše — only to be devastated by fire later that summer. A nationwide collection rebuilt it within two years under architect Josef Schulz, and it reopened in 1883, again with Libuše.

Companies

The theatre is home to drama, opera and ballet ensembles, which also perform at the State Opera, the Estates Theatre and the modern Nová scéna (New Stage, opened 1983). Its repertoire centres on Czech works by Smetana, Dvořák and Janáček alongside the international canon.

Architecture

The building's gilded roof, sculptures by Bohuslav Schnirch and paintings by the "National Theatre Generation" — including Mikoláš Aleš and František Ženíšek — make it a landmark of 19th-century Czech art. Its motto, "Národ sobě" ("The Nation to Itself"), is inscribed above the stage.
