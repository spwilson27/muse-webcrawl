cdmaOne

cdmaOne was the brand name for the IS-95 family of CDMA standards.

cdmaOne is the commercial brand name for a family of second-generation (2G) digital cellular standards based on code-division multiple access (CDMA), developed by Qualcomm and standardized by the Telecommunications Industry Association as IS-95. It was one of the principal 2G technologies deployed worldwide, competing with GSM in markets such as North America, South Korea, Japan, China, and parts of Latin America.

History

Qualcomm demonstrated CDMA cellular technology in the early 1990s, arguing that spread-spectrum techniques could support many more subscribers per unit of spectrum than the analog AMPS system and the time-division systems then in use. The IS-95 standard, later revised as IS-95A and IS-95B, was published in 1993, and commercial cdmaOne networks launched in the mid-1990s, notably in Hong Kong and South Korea in 1996 and in the United States by carriers including PrimeCo and Sprint.

Technical characteristics

cdmaOne systems use a 1.25 MHz carrier in which all users share the same channel, distinguished by unique spreading codes. Key features included soft handoff between cells, power control to mitigate the near–far problem, rake receivers to exploit multipath propagation, and variable-rate vocoders such as QCELP and EVRC. IS-95A supported circuit-switched data at 14.4 kbit/s, while IS-95B added packet data at rates up to about 115 kbit/s.

Evolution

The cdmaOne family evolved into the third-generation CDMA2000 standards (IS-2000), including 1xRTT and EV-DO, which offered higher data rates while retaining backward compatibility with IS-95 handsets and spectrum. Most cdmaOne and CDMA2000 networks were shut down in the early 2020s as carriers refarmed spectrum for LTE and 5G.
