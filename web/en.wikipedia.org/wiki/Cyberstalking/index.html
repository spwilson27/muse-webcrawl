Cyberstalking

Cyberstalking is the use of the Internet or other electronic means to stalk or harass an individual, group, or organization. It may include false accusations, defamation, slander and libel, monitoring, identity theft, threats, vandalism, solicitation for sex, or gathering information that may be used to threaten, embarrass or harass the victim.

Definition and behaviour

Cyberstalking is generally defined by experts as a course of conduct, conducted online, that causes a reasonable person to feel fear or substantial emotional distress. Behaviour may include repeated unwanted messages, posting personal information (doxxing), impersonation, threats of violence, soliciting others to harass the victim, and using technology such as spyware or GPS tracking to monitor the victim. Unlike one-off online harassment, cyberstalking is characterised by persistence and by a focus on a specific victim; in many cases the offender is a former intimate partner, and online stalking may accompany or escalate to offline stalking.

Prevalence and effects

Surveys in several countries find that a significant minority of internet users — with women and young people disproportionately affected — report experiencing cyberstalking or related online abuse. Victims commonly report anxiety, depression, sleep disturbance and changes to their daily routines and online behaviour.

Legal responses

Many jurisdictions have extended stalking and harassment laws to electronic communications. In the United States, federal law (18 U.S.C. § 2261A) criminalises interstate cyberstalking, and nearly all states have stalking or harassment statutes covering online conduct; California enacted the first cyberstalking law in 1999. In the United Kingdom, the Protection from Harassment Act 1997 and the Malicious Communications Act 1988 are commonly used, supplemented by the Online Safety Act 2023. Enforcement is complicated by anonymity, cross-border offending and the volume of reports.

Prevention and safety

Advice to victims typically includes preserving evidence, blocking and reporting the offender on platforms, tightening privacy and location settings, seeking platform and police assistance, and in serious cases obtaining protective orders. Platforms increasingly offer safety tools and dedicated reporting routes for stalking behaviour.
