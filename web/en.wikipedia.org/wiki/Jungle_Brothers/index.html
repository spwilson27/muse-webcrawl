Jungle Brothers

The Jungle Brothers are an American hip hop duo composed of Michael Small (Mike Gee), Nathaniel Hall (Afrika Baby Bam), and DJ Sammy Burwell (DJ Sammy B). Known as the pioneers of the fusion of jazz, hip-hop and house music, they were the first hip-hop group to collaborate with a house-music producer — a link that helped establish the subgenre of hip house. They were foundational members of the Native Tongues collective alongside De La Soul and A Tribe Called Quest.

History

The group formed in the mid-1980s in New York and released their first single, "Jimbrowski", in 1987. Their debut album, Straight out the Jungle (1988), was released on an independent label and included the landmark track "I'll House You", produced by Todd Terry, which fused rap with house music. The album's Afrocentric themes, jazz-inflected samples and positive outlook helped define the Native Tongues sound.

Their follow-up, Done by the Forces of Nature (1989), released on Warner Bros., was critically acclaimed and features dense sampling, African rhythms and socially conscious lyrics on songs such as "Beyond This World" and "What 'U' Waitin' '4'?".

Later work

The group's third album, J Beez wit the Remedy (1993), emerged after a troubled production history. They later released Raw Deluxe (1997), V.I.P. (2000) and All That We Do (2002). Members also collaborated with the British electronic act Propellerheads on the 1998 hit "History Repeating" (featuring Shirley Bassey), on which Mike Gee appeared. The group has continued to tour and record intermittently, and their pioneering blend of samples, uplift and dance music remains influential in alternative hip hop.
