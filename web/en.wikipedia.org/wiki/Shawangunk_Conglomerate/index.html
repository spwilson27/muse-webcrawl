Shawangunk Conglomerate

TypeGeological formation
AgeSilurian
LithologyQuartz conglomerate and sandstone
RegionNew York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania

The Shawangunk Conglomerate, also known as the Shawangunk Formation, is a Silurian geological formation of hard quartz conglomerate and sandstone that caps the Shawangunk Ridge in New York and its continuation, Kittatinny Mountain, in New Jersey and Pennsylvania.

Geology

The formation consists of well-rounded white quartz pebbles in a silica-cemented matrix, deposited by braided rivers at the margin of an ancient sea. Its extreme hardness makes it highly resistant to erosion, so it forms the prominent ridge that stands above the surrounding shale valleys.

Climbing and landscape

The cliffs of the Shawangunk Ridge, known as "the Gunks", are a famous rock-climbing area, prized for the conglomerate's horizontal fractures and solid holds. The formation also underlies sites such as Sam's Point and the protected lands of Minnewaska State Park.
