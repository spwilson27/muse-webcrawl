Slip angle

In vehicle dynamics, slip angle or sideslip angle is the angle between the direction in which a wheel is pointing and the direction in which it is actually traveling (i.e., the angle between the longitudinal velocity vector and the vector sum of wheel forward and lateral velocities). This slip angle results in a force, the cornering force, which is in the plane of the contact patch and perpendicular to the direction of the intersection of the contact patch and the wheel midplane.

How it arises

A pneumatic tyre is elastic: when a lateral force is applied, the tread in the contact patch deforms and the tyre's actual travel direction lags behind its heading. The resulting cornering force rises roughly linearly with slip angle at small angles (the slope is the tyre's cornering stiffness), then saturates as the contact patch begins to slide, typically peaking around 6–10 degrees depending on the tyre and load.

Role in handling

The balance of front and rear slip angles determines whether a car understeers or oversteers: if the front slip angles exceed the rear ones, the car turns less than commanded (understeer); if the rears are larger, it turns more (oversteer). Racing drivers deliberately exploit large rear slip angles when drifting. The term "slip angle" is also used in aerodynamics and sailing for the angle between a reference line and the airflow or travel direction.
