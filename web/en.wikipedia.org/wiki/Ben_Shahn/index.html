Ben Shahn

Ben Shahn (September 12, 1898 – March 14, 1969) was a Lithuanian-born American artist best known for his works of social realism, his left-wing political views, and his activity as a photographer for the Farm Security Administration during the Great Depression.

Life and work

Shahn immigrated with his family to Brooklyn, New York, in 1906 and was apprenticed to a lithographer before studying art. In the early 1930s he gained fame with a series of paintings on the Sacco and Vanzetti case, The Passion of Sacco and Vanzetti, which established his reputation as a socially engaged painter.

Photography and public art

Working alongside Walker Evans and Dorothea Lange, Shahn documented rural poverty across the American South and Midwest for the federal government. He also designed posters for the Office of War Information and executed murals, including those for the Bronx General Post Office and the community centre of Jersey Homesteads (later Roosevelt), New Jersey, where he settled.

Later career

After the war, Shahn developed a more symbolic, calligraphic style, accepted major commissions including a mosaic at his namesake gallery, taught at Harvard, and represented the United States at the Venice Biennale in 1954 alongside Willem de Kooning.
