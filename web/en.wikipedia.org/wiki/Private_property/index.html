Private property

Private property is legally designated for the exclusive use of its owner.

Private property is a legal designation for the ownership of property by non-governmental legal entities — individuals and corporations. It is distinguished from public property, owned collectively by a community or by the state, and from personal property in socialist theory, which reserves "private property" for productive assets (capital, land, means of production) as opposed to personal belongings.

History

Ideas of individual land ownership developed in ancient Near Eastern and Mediterranean societies; Roman law gave the concept its classic form in dominium, with extensive rights of use and abuse. In medieval Europe, land was held through feudal tenures layered with overlapping claims. John Locke's labour theory of property (1689) justified private ownership by the mixing of one's labour with nature, subject to the proviso that "enough and as good" remain for others, and became foundational for liberal political thought. Enclosure movements in Britain converted common land into private holdings between the 16th and 19th centuries.

Justifications and criticisms

Defenders argue private property secures liberty, encourages stewardship and investment, and solves allocation problems through markets; economists add that well-defined property rights internalise externalities (the Coase theorem) and that privatisation can avert the "tragedy of the commons". Critics from Proudhon ("property is theft") to Marx argued that private ownership of the means of production produces exploitation and class division; socialist states in the 20th century abolished or severely restricted it, while most modern economies maintain mixed systems with regulation, taxation, and eminent-domain powers.

Legal forms

Private property law covers real property (land and fixtures), personal property (chattels), and intangible property (intellectual property, shares, financial assets). Ownership is typically a "bundle of rights" that can be divided — for example by leases, easements, liens, and trusts.
