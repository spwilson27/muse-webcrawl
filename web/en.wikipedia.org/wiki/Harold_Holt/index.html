Harold Holt

Harold Edward Holt (5 August 1908 – 17 December 1967) was an Australian politician who served as the 17th prime minister of Australia from 1966 until his disappearance in 1967. He held office as leader of the Liberal Party.

Prime Minister of Australia: 26 January 1966 – 17 December 1967

Predecessor: Robert Menzies

Successor: John McEwen

Born: 5 August 1908, Stanmore, New South Wales

Died: 17 December 1967 (aged 59), Cheviot Beach, Victoria (presumed drowned)

Early life and career

Born in Sydney and educated in Melbourne, Holt studied law at the University of Melbourne and was elected to the House of Representatives in 1935, aged 27. He served as a minister under Robert Menzies, most notably as Minister for Immigration (1949–1956), overseeing the post-war immigration programme, and as Treasurer (1958–1966), in which role he helped establish the Reserve Bank of Australia and introduced decimal currency.

Prime ministership

Holt succeeded Menzies on his retirement in January 1966 and led the Coalition to a landslide victory at the 1966 election. His government expanded Australia's involvement in the Vietnam War, famously declaring in Washington that he was "all the way with LBJ"; relaxed aspects of the White Australia policy; and supported the successful 1967 referendum on Aboriginal affairs. Holt also began reorienting Australia's trade and defence outlook toward Asia and the United States as Britain turned toward Europe.

Disappearance

On 17 December 1967, Holt went swimming at Cheviot Beach near Portsea, Victoria, in rough conditions and disappeared. An extensive search failed to recover his body. A 2005 coronial inquest formally ruled that he had drowned in accidental circumstances. His disappearance, unique among national leaders, generated numerous conspiracy theories, none substantiated. He was succeeded as prime minister briefly by John McEwen and then by John Gorton.

In a much-noted irony, the Harold Holt Memorial Swimming Centre in Melbourne is named in his honour.
