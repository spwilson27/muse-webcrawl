Marienburg Castle

Marienburg Castle, Hanover region

Marienburg Castle (Schloss Marienburg) is a Gothic Revival castle in Lower Saxony, Germany, near Hanover. It was a summer residence of the House of Hanover, built between 1858 and 1867 for Queen Marie of Hanover as a birthday present from her husband, King George V of Hanover.

History

George V commissioned the castle as a surprise birthday gift for his wife, Marie of Saxe-Altenburg, in 1858. The Hanoverian court architect Georg Ludwig Friedrich Laves designed it in the Gothic Revival style, drawing on English castles such as Windsor; Conrad Wilhelm Hase took over construction after Laves's death. The royal family never lived there long: Hanover was annexed by Prussia in 1866 after the Austro-Prussian War, and the king and queen went into exile in Austria.

Later history

The castle passed to the deposed royal family's estate trust, and after the Second World War it was used by the widowed Princess Viktoria Luise. It remained in the hands of the House of Hanover until the 2010s, when the then-head of the house, Ernst August, placed it under a foundation, and the castle was later transferred to the state of Lower Saxony for renovation. It is open to visitors, with exhibits on the Hanoverian monarchy, and its name — "Mary's castle" — honours Queen Marie.
