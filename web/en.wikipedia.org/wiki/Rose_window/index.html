Rose window

Rose window is a generic term applied to a circular window, but is especially used for those found in Gothic cathedrals and churches. The windows are divided into segments by stone mullions and tracery, and filled with stained glass. The term originated with the French rosace.

History

Circular windows (oculi) were used in Roman architecture and in Romanesque churches. The rose window proper developed in the 12th century with Gothic architecture in northern France; notable early examples include the west front of Chartres Cathedral. Rose windows reached their zenith in the great French cathedrals such as Notre-Dame de Paris, Amiens, and Reims.

Symbolism

The rose window is often associated with the Virgin Mary, to whom many cathedrals are dedicated, and its radiating design has been read as a symbol of divine order and of the sun. In the Gothic Revival of the 19th century, rose windows returned to widespread use in churches and public buildings.
