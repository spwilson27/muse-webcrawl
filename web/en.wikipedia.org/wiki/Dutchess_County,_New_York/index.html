Dutchess County, New York

Dutchess County is a county in the U.S. state of New York, located in the Hudson Valley region midway between New York City and Albany. As of the 2020 census, the population was approximately 295,911. The county seat is the city of Poughkeepsie. The county was named for Mary of Modena, Duchess of York ("Dutchess" being an archaic spelling of "Duchess").

History

Dutchess was one of the twelve original counties of the Province of New York, created in 1683 and formally organized in 1713. It was a centre of the wheat and agricultural trade of the Hudson Valley and later of manufacturing along its riverfronts and railroads. Franklin D. Roosevelt's home at Hyde Park, now a National Historic Site, is in the county.

Geography and communities

The county is bordered by the Hudson River to the west and Connecticut to the east. It contains two cities (Poughkeepsie and Beacon), twenty towns, and eight villages. The Taconic State Parkway and U.S. Route 9 run north–south, and Interstate 84 crosses the southern part of the county. Institutions include Vassar College, Bard College, Marist University, and the Culinary Institute of America.
