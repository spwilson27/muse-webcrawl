Textile industry in Bangladesh

The textile and garment industry is the backbone of Bangladesh's economy and its largest source of export earnings, accounting for more than 80% of total exports and employing around four million workers, the majority of them women. Bangladesh is the world's second-largest exporter of ready-made garments after China, supplying major Western retail brands.

History

The modern industry began in the late 1970s, aided by quota access under the Multi Fibre Arrangement and technology transfer from South Korea's Daewoo, which trained Bangladeshi workers in 1978–79. Production expanded around Dhaka and Chattogram, moving from woven garments into knitwear, denim and home textiles.

Labour and safety

Low wages and poor factory safety long drew criticism. The collapse of the Rana Plaza building in April 2013, which killed more than 1,100 garment workers, prompted international accords on fire and building safety, factory remediation programmes and gradual minimum-wage increases, though concerns about wages, union rights and enforcement persist.
