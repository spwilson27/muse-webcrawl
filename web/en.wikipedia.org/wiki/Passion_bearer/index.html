Passion bearer

In the Eastern Orthodox Church, a passion bearer (Russian: страстотерпец, strastoterpets) is one of the various customary titles used to glorify saints. The term describes a person who faced death in a Christ-like manner — not for the Christian faith itself, which would make them a martyr, but in a spirit of meekness, non-resistance to evil, and forgiveness of their murderers.

Saints Boris and Gleb, the archetypal passion bearers.

Definition

Unlike martyrs, passion bearers were not killed because of their Christian confession, and unlike confessors they did not suffer prolonged persecution for the faith. Their sanctity lies in the manner of their death: they accepted murder without resistance, imitating Christ's voluntary suffering and praying for their killers. The title is especially characteristic of the Russian Orthodox tradition.

Notable passion bearers

  • Boris and Gleb — sons of Prince Vladimir of Kiev, murdered in 1015 by agents of their brother Sviatopolk; they declined to raise arms against their elder brother and are regarded as the first saints canonised in Kievan Rus'.
  • Nicholas II of Russia and his family — the last Russian emperor, Empress Alexandra, and their five children, executed in 1918, were glorified by the Russian Orthodox Church in 2000 as passion bearers (the Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia had canonised them in 1981 as martyrs).
  • Dmitry of Uglich — the young tsarevich who died in 1591, venerated as a passion bearer.
  • Alexander Schmorell — a member of the White Rose resistance group, executed by the Nazi regime in 1943 and glorified in 2012.

Distinction from other titles

Eastern Orthodox hagiography distinguishes several "ranks" of sanctity: apostles, martyrs, great martyrs, hieromartyrs (martyred clergy), confessors, unmercenaries, righteous, and passion bearers among them. A passion bearer's death is typically political or personal in motivation on the murderer's side, and the saint's holiness is manifested in humility and non-resistance rather than public confession of faith.
