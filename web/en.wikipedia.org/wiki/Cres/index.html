Cres (Croatian pronunciation: [tsrɛ̂ːs]; Italian: Cherso; German: Kersch) is an Adriatic island in Croatia, in the northern part of the Kvarner Gulf. With an area of about 405.7 square kilometres it is, together with Krk, one of the two largest Croatian islands. The island's main settlement is the town of Cres on its western coast.

Cres is known for the freshwater Lake Vrana (Vransko jezero), which supplies drinking water to both Cres and the neighbouring island of Lošinj, and for being one of the last refuges of the griffon vulture in Croatia.

Geography

The island is long and narrow, stretching roughly 66 km from north to south. Its highest peak is Gorice (648 m). The northern part is covered in sub-Mediterranean forest of downy oak, while the south is characterised by Mediterranean pasture and macchia. Lake Vrana, whose surface lies about 13 metres above sea level while its bottom reaches some 60 metres below it, is a phenomenon of karst hydrology and a protected water source.

History

Cres has been inhabited since the Palaeolithic. In antiquity it formed, together with Lošinj, the island group known to the Greeks as the Apsyrtides, named for Apsyrtus of the Argonaut myth. The island passed through Roman, Byzantine, Croatian, Venetian, Austrian, Italian and Yugoslav rule before becoming part of independent Croatia in 1991. The town of Cres preserves a Venetian-era old town with a loggia, clock tower and the remains of town walls.

Settlements and wildlife

Besides the town of Cres, villages include Beli in the north — below which griffon vultures nest on sea cliffs — Valun, known for the Valun tablet, an early Glagolitic inscription, and Osor, the town on the narrow channel separating Cres from Lošinj. The griffon vulture colony, among the last in the region, is protected, and an eco-centre in Beli cares for injured birds.
