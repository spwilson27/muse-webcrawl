Gymnasium (Germany)

Gymnasium (German plural: Gymnasien) is a type of secondary school in Germany that provides advanced academic education and prepares students for university study. It is the most academically selective of the traditional German school types, alongside the Realschule and Hauptschule, and culminates in the Abitur examination, which qualifies graduates for university admission.

A traditional Gymnasium building

Structure

Pupils typically enter Gymnasium after four years of primary school (age ten, later in some states) and study for eight years (the "G8" model) or nine years ("G9"), depending on the Land. The curriculum traditionally emphasises languages — usually two or three foreign languages, often including Latin or Greek in the humanistic branch — mathematics, natural sciences, history and the arts. In the final two or three years (the gymnasiale Oberstufe) students choose courses with varying weightings leading to the Abitur.

History and debate

The Gymnasium descends from the humanist schools of the Reformation era and was shaped by Wilhelm von Humboldt's early nineteenth-century reforms, which made the Abitur the gateway to university and the civil service. About a third or more of German pupils now attend a Gymnasium, and the institution is central to debates about selection at age ten, social mobility, and the balance between comprehensive and tracked schooling in Germany's federal education system.
