Per Erik Rutger Martin-Löf (born 8 May 1942) is a Swedish logician, philosopher and mathematical statistician. He is internationally renowned as the father of Martin-Löf type theory, a foundational system for constructive mathematics and the basis of several proof assistants, and for his definition of algorithmic randomness.

Work

Martin-Löf studied at Stockholm University and spent time in Moscow working with Andrey Kolmogorov. In the 1960s he gave the first satisfactory definition of a random infinite sequence — now called Martin-Löf randomness — using effectively null sets from computability theory.

From the 1970s he developed intuitionistic (constructive) type theory, presented most fully in his 1984 Bibliopolis monograph Intuitionistic Type Theory. The theory treats propositions as types, so that a proof of a proposition is an object of the corresponding type (the Curry–Howard correspondence extended to dependent types). It underlies the proof assistants Agda, and influenced Coq and the homotopy type theory programme. He also made contributions to the philosophy of language, the theory of judgments and the foundations of probability and statistics.

Other interests

Martin-Löf is also a noted ornithologist and birdwatcher, and has published on bird identification, including work on the first records of certain species for Sweden.
