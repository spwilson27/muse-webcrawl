Emergencies Act

The Emergencies Act (French: Loi sur les mesures d'urgence) is a statute passed by the Parliament of Canada in 1988 that allows the federal government to take extraordinary temporary measures in response to urgent and critical situations that seriously threaten the health, safety, or security of Canadians and that exceed the capacity of the provinces to deal with them.

Provisions

The Act distinguishes four categories of emergency: public welfare emergencies, public order emergencies, international emergencies, and war emergencies. A declaration of emergency must be reviewed by Parliament, is subject to the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, and expires after a fixed period unless renewed. The Act replaced the War Measures Act, which had been invoked during both World Wars and the October Crisis of 1970.

First invocation

The Act was invoked for the first time on February 14, 2022, by the government of Prime Minister Justin Trudeau in response to the Canada convoy protests and blockades at border crossings. The public order emergency was revoked on February 23, 2022. A public inquiry, the Public Order Emergency Commission led by Justice Paul Rouleau, subsequently examined the use of the Act and reported in 2023 that the legal threshold for invocation had been met, with reluctance noted by the commissioner.
