Emil Zátopek (19 September 1922 – 22 November 2000) was a Czech long-distance runner best known for winning three gold medals at the 1952 Summer Olympics in Helsinki — the 5,000 metres, 10,000 metres and the marathon, an unprecedented triple that has never been repeated. Nicknamed the "Czech Locomotive", he pioneered interval training methods and was famous for his pained running expression and his sportsmanship.

Career

Zátopek took up running while working at the Bata shoe factory in Zlín. At the 1948 London Olympics he won gold in the 10,000 m and silver in the 5,000 m. In Helsinki in 1952 he won both events and then entered the marathon — the first he had ever run — and won it as well, setting Olympic records in all three.

Style and legacy

Zátopek revolutionised training with high-volume interval workouts and was known for talking to fellow competitors mid-race; at the 1952 marathon he famously asked Jim Peters whether the pace was too slow before going on to win. His wife Dana Zátopková won the Olympic javelin title on the same day he won the 5,000 m. After supporting the 1968 Prague Spring he was expelled from the army and Communist Party and given menial jobs, before being rehabilitated after 1989.
