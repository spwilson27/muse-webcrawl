Jabal al-Druze (Arabic: جبل الدروز, "Mountain of the Druze"), also known as Jabal Hauran or Jabal al-Arab, is an elevated volcanic region in the As-Suwayda Governorate of southern Syria, near the border with Jordan. The region forms the western part of the Harrat Ash Shaam volcanic field and rises to 1,803 metres at Tell Qeni. It takes its name from the Druze religious community that has inhabited it since the late seventeenth and eighteenth centuries.

Geography

The mountain massif consists of overlapping basaltic flows and scoria cones erupted over several million years. Its dark volcanic soils, where clear of boulders, are fertile, and the region is known for vineyards, orchards, and wheat. The principal towns include As-Suwayda, the governorate capital, as well as Shahba and Salkhad, which preserve Roman-era remains from when the area formed part of the province of Arabia Petraea.

History

Druze families migrating from Mount Lebanon settled the mountain from the late 17th century onward, displacing or absorbing earlier inhabitants and establishing a largely autonomous highland society. Under Ottoman rule the region resisted taxation and conscription, and relations with the authorities were frequently tense.

During the French Mandate for Syria and Lebanon, the region was administered as the autonomous Jabal Druze State (1921–1936), with its own flag and government under French supervision. In 1925 the Great Syrian Revolt began in Jabal al-Druze under the leadership of Sultan al-Atrash, whose forces defeated French columns at al-Kafr and al-Mazraa before the uprising spread to Damascus. The mountain was later reintegrated into the Syrian state in 1936.

Population

The inhabitants are predominantly Druze, with Christian and Sunni Muslim minorities in some towns. The region has historically been known for its clan structure, hospitality customs, and the political influence of families such as the al-Atrash.
