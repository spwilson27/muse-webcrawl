The Jewish Encyclopedia

The Jewish Encyclopedia is an English-language encyclopedia containing over 15,000 articles on the history, culture and state of Judaism up to the beginning of the 20th century. It was published in twelve volumes by Funk & Wagnalls in New York between 1901 and 1906, under the editorship of Isidore Singer, and remains a landmark of Jewish scholarship.

Production and scope

Singer conceived the project in Europe before bringing it to America, where it was produced with the help of an editorial board of leading rabbis and scholars and contributions from more than 400 collaborators worldwide. The encyclopedia covers biblical, talmudic, medieval and modern Jewish history, biography, geography, liturgy, folklore and the contemporary situation of Jewish communities in every country. Its scholarly standards reflected the Wissenschaft des Judentums ("science of Judaism") movement, and it drew on and cited an extensive multilingual literature, often reproducing source material and statistics that are otherwise hard to find.

Legacy and availability

Although inevitably dated — its final volumes predate both world wars, the Holocaust and the State of Israel — the Jewish Encyclopedia is still consulted for its detailed biographies of obscure figures, its community-by-community population data and its illustrations. Because it is in the public domain, the entire text was placed online at jewishencyclopedia.com, with scanned page images; its articles are a frequent source for later reference works, and thousands of its entries formed a seed for coverage of Jewish topics elsewhere. Abridged and successor projects include the Universal Jewish Encyclopedia (1939–43) and the Encyclopaedia Judaica (1971–72, second edition 2007).
