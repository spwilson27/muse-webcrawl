In digital photography, the crop factor, also known as the focal length multiplier, is the ratio of a camera's sensor diagonal to the diagonal of 35 mm film (the "full frame" format, 36 × 24 mm). It describes how much of the image circle a smaller sensor "crops", and hence how the field of view of a lens on that sensor compares with the same lens on full frame.

Calculation

Crop factor = 43.3 mm ÷ sensor diagonal. Multiplying a lens's focal length by the crop factor gives the 35 mm-equivalent focal length in terms of field of view: a 50 mm lens on a Canon APS-C body (×1.6) frames like an 80 mm lens on full frame, although the lens's actual focal length, and its depth of field for a given aperture, do not change.

Effects

  • Field of view: narrower on smaller sensors for the same focal length — an advantage for telephoto work (sports, wildlife) and a disadvantage for wide-angle photography.
  • Depth of field: for the same framing and aperture, smaller sensors give greater depth of field.
  • Noise: larger sensors generally gather more light per image and show less noise at high ISO.
  • Equivalence: matching framing, aperture × crop factor and ISO × crop factor² gives comparable depth of field and noise across formats.

Common formats

Medium-format digital sensors larger than full frame have crop factors below 1 (e.g. ×0.79 for the 44 × 33 mm format). Compact cameras and smartphones have much larger factors — a typical 1/2.3-inch sensor is about ×5.6, and main smartphone sensors commonly range around ×3–4.
