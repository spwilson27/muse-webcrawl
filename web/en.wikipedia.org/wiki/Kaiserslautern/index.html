Kaiserslautern

Kaiserslautern (Palatine German: Lautre) is a city in Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany, at the edge of the Palatinate Forest. With a population of roughly 100,000, it is best known internationally as the home of football club 1. FC Kaiserslautern and for the large United States military community in the surrounding area, the Kaiserslautern Military Community, the largest American military community outside the United States.

History

The name derives from Emperor (Kaiser) Frederick Barbarossa, who built a palace here in the 12th century and enjoyed hunting in the surrounding forests; the river Lauter gives the second element. The imperial palace's ruins, the Casimirschloss and the Gothic Stiftskirche, remain landmarks. The city suffered heavily in the Thirty Years' War and in French occupations, and was largely rebuilt after severe Second World War bombing.

American presence

After 1945 the area became a major hub of the U.S. armed forces in Europe. Nearby Ramstein Air Base, Landstuhl Regional Medical Center and installations within the city itself support tens of thousands of American service members, civilian employees and family members, giving Kaiserslautern — nicknamed "K-Town" — an unusually American character for a German city.

Sport

1. FC Kaiserslautern, founded in 1900, won the German championship in 1951, 1953, 1991 and, famously as a newly promoted side, in 1998. Its home, the Fritz-Walter-Stadion on the Betzenberg hill, hosted matches at the 2006 FIFA World Cup. The club supplied several players to West Germany's 1954 World Cup "Miracle of Bern" team, including captain Fritz Walter.

Economy and education

The city is home to the University of Kaiserslautern-Landau (RPTU) technical campus, several Fraunhofer institutes (including for industrial mathematics and software engineering), and plants of Opel (Stellantis), Pfaff sewing machines and other manufacturers.
