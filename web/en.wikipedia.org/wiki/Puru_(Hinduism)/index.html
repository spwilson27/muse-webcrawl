Puru (Hinduism)

Puru (Sanskrit: पूरु, romanized: Pūru) is a legendary king in Hindu tradition, the youngest son of King Yayati and his wife Sharmishtha, and the ancestor of the Puru dynasty, one of the principal lineages of the Lunar dynasty (Chandravamsha). His story is told in the Mahabharata and several Puranas.

Legend

Yayati, cursed by the sage Shukra with premature old age, asked each of his sons in turn to exchange their youth for his decrepitude. His elder sons — including Yadu, ancestor of the Yadavas — refused, but Puru agreed, serving his father for a thousand years before the exchange was reversed. In gratitude, Yayati named Puru his heir over his elder brothers, and from Puru's line descended the Pauravas, among them Bharata, after whom India is traditionally called Bhāratavarṣa, and ultimately the Kuru clan and the royal houses of the Mahabharata.

Historical associations

The Purus are also named in the Rigveda as one of the tribes of Vedic India, figuring in the Battle of the Ten Kings, and some scholars connect the epic genealogy with this ancient tribe. A later King Puru has sometimes been tentatively associated in popular tradition with Porus, the king who fought Alexander the Great — though the two figures belong to different traditions.
