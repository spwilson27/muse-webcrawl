The Lake Pontchartrain Causeway (French: Chaussée du lac Pontchartrain), also known simply as The Causeway, is a fixed link of two parallel bridges crossing Lake Pontchartrain in southeastern Louisiana, United States. The longer of the two bridges is 23.83 miles (38.35 km) long. The southern terminus of the Causeway is in Metairie, Louisiana, and its northern terminus is in Mandeville.

The bridges are supported by 9,500 concrete pilings. They feature a bascule drawbridge span 8 miles (13 km) south of the north shore to allow marine traffic. For much of its length the shore is invisible from the roadway — for about 8 miles of the crossing, drivers can see no land in any direction.

History

The idea of a bridge across Pontchartrain dates to the early 19th century, and ferries operated from the 1830s. The modern Causeway was built by the Louisiana Bridge Company and opened on 30 August 1956 as a two-lane span. A parallel second span, slightly longer, opened on 10 May 1969, converting the pair to one-way traffic on each bridge. The southbound span was listed by Guinness World Records as the longest bridge over water in the world until 2011, when the title for continuous length over water passed to China's Jiaozhou Bay Bridge; the Causeway remains the longest bridge over water in the United States and holds the record as the longest bridge over water (continuous) in some listings of specific categories.

Operation and safety

The Causeway is operated by the Greater New Orleans Expressway Commission. Police patrol the bridge continuously, and a system of crossovers allows disabled vehicles to be escorted. Fog on the lake has caused multi-vehicle accidents; in poor weather, convoys with police escort cross at reduced speed. In 2005 Hurricane Katrina damaged the nearby I-10 Twin Span but the Causeway survived with minor damage and reopened quickly, becoming a vital evacuation and recovery route.
