The Israel–Jordan peace treaty (formally the "Treaty of Peace Between the State of Israel and the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan"), sometimes called the Wadi Araba Treaty, is an agreement that ended the state of war between the two countries that had existed since 1948 and established mutual diplomatic relations. It was signed on 26 October 1994 at the southern border crossing between the countries in the Arava valley.

Background

Jordan and Israel had maintained secret contacts for decades. The 1993 Oslo Accords between Israel and the PLO, including the Israel–PLO Declaration of Principles, opened the way for a public Jordanian track; in July 1994 King Hussein and Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin issued the Washington Declaration ending the state of belligerency.

Terms

The treaty fixed the international boundary along the Jordan and Yarmouk rivers, with minor land swaps and special regimes for the Baqura/Naharayim and Tzofar/Al Ghamr enclaves (the lease arrangements over these areas ended in 2019). It allocated water from the Jordan and Yarmouk rivers, recognised Jordan's historic role as custodian of Muslim holy shrines in Jerusalem, and provided for cooperation on security, trade, transport and the environment.

Aftermath

The treaty made Jordan only the second Arab country, after Egypt, to formally make peace with Israel. Diplomatic relations, open borders and security cooperation followed; relations cooled at times over the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, but the peace has endured. US President Bill Clinton attended the signing ceremony.
