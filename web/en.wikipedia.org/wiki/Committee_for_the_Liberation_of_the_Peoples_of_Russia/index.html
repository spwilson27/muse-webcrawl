The Committee for the Liberation of the Peoples of Russia (Russian: Комитет освобождения народов России, Komitet osvobozhdeniya narodov Rossii, abbreviated KONR) was a political body formed in Prague on 14 November 1944 by Soviet citizens opposed to the regime of Joseph Stalin. Led by the captured Red Army general Andrey Vlasov, the committee proclaimed the Prague Manifesto, which called for the overthrow of Stalinism and the establishment of a democratic order in Russia, while cooperating with Nazi Germany during the final months of the Second World War.

Background

Millions of Soviet prisoners of war and civilians in German-occupied territory faced starvation and forced labour. Vlasov, captured in 1942, had issued appeals for a Russian liberation movement, but German authorities long refused to permit a unified political organisation. Only in autumn 1944, as Germany's military position collapsed, did Heinrich Himmler authorise the committee's formation and the raising of its armed forces.

The Prague Manifesto

The manifesto proclaimed aims including the abolition of collective farms and forced labour camps, freedom of speech, press, and religion, and national self-determination for the peoples of the Soviet Union. It stopped short of condemning Germany's war aims and was shaped by the committee's dependence on German sponsorship, a dependence that undermined its credibility among Russians and the Western Allies alike.

Armed forces

The committee's army, the Russian Liberation Army (ROA), formally absorbed the various Russian volunteer formations. Its 1st Division, under Sergei Bunyachenko, briefly fought the Red Army on the Oder front in April 1945 before turning against German forces during the Prague uprising in May 1945.

Aftermath

After the war, Vlasov and the committee's leading members were repatriated to the Soviet Union, tried for treason, and executed in Moscow on 1 August 1946. Rank-and-file members were imprisoned in the Gulag or, in the West, became part of the post-war anti-communist émigré movement.
