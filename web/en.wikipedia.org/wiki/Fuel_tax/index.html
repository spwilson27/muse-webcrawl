Fuel tax

A fuel tax (also known as a petrol, gasoline or diesel tax or excise) is an excise tax imposed on the sale of fuel. In most countries it is levied on fuels used for transport, historically to fund road construction and maintenance on the user-pays principle, and sometimes to price in environmental externalities.

Rates and use

Rates vary widely. The United States federal gasoline tax has been 18.4 cents per gallon since 1993, supplemented by state taxes that vary from under 10 to nearly 70 cents per gallon. European countries levy much higher duties—often the equivalent of several dollars per gallon—plus value-added tax, so fuel prices are far higher than in the US. Revenues commonly flow to highway trust funds, though many jurisdictions now treat them as general environmental or revenue measures. Aviation fuel and marine fuel are often exempt or lightly taxed internationally by treaty and practice, a long-standing point of debate.

Issues

Fuel taxes are criticized as regressive and as burdening rural drivers, and defended as among the most efficient climate policies because they price carbon and congestion indirectly. Because the tax base shrinks with improved efficiency and electric vehicles—which use roads without paying fuel tax—policymakers have explored road-user charges and per-mile fees as replacements. Protests over fuel taxes, such as France's gilets jaunes movement from 2018, show their political sensitivity.
