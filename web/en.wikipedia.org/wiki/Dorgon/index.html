Dorgon (Manchu: ᡩᠣᡵᡤᠣᠨ, Chinese: 多爾袞; 17 November 1612 – 31 December 1650), formally known as Prince Rui, was a Manchu prince and regent of the early Qing dynasty. The fourteenth son of Nurhaci, founder of the Later Jin, Dorgon became the most powerful figure in China after the death of his brother Huang Taiji in 1643.


Dorgon, Prince-Regent of the Qing dynasty.

Regency

In the succession crisis of 1643, Dorgon stepped aside and engineered the enthronement of Huang Taiji's young son Fulin (the Shunzhi Emperor), with himself as prince-regent. In 1644 he seized the opportunity of the Ming collapse, accepting the defection of the Ming general Wu Sangui and leading Qing forces through Shanhai Pass to capture Beijing, defeating the rebel Li Zicheng.

Conquest and policies

As regent, Dorgon directed the Qing conquest of China proper, ordering campaigns that subdued most of the country by 1650. His policies included the controversial queue order of 1645, which forced Han Chinese men to adopt the Manchu hairstyle on pain of death, provoking fierce resistance. He died on a hunting trip in 1650 and was posthumously honoured as an emperor, though his titles were soon stripped amid a purge of his faction; he was rehabilitated decades later by the Qianlong Emperor.
