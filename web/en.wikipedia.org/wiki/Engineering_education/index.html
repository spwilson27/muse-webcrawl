Engineering education

FocusKnowledge and skills for engineering practice

Engineering education is the activity of teaching the knowledge and principles underlying the professional practice of engineering. It encompasses undergraduate and postgraduate university programmes, accreditation systems, and continuing professional development.

Curriculum

Engineering degrees combine mathematics, natural science, and discipline-specific subjects such as mechanics, thermodynamics, circuits, and materials, with design projects, laboratory work, and increasingly topics in computing, sustainability, ethics, and project management. Capstone design projects commonly integrate these elements in the final year.

Accreditation and licensure

Programmes are accredited by national bodies — for example ABET in the United States and the Engineering Council in the United Kingdom — and mutual recognition agreements such as the Washington Accord allow accredited qualifications to be recognised internationally. In many jurisdictions, professional licensure (such as the Professional Engineer licence in the US) builds on an accredited education plus supervised experience.

History

Formal engineering schools emerged in the 18th and 19th centuries, including the École Polytechnique in Paris (1794) and Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute in the United States (1824), as the profession shifted from apprenticeship to scientific training.
