Dialogue

Dialogue is a written or spoken conversational exchange between two or more people, and a literary and theatrical form that depicts such an exchange. The term derives from the Greek dialogos, meaning conversation.

In literature and philosophy

The philosophical dialogue was perfected by Plato, whose works present Socrates in discussion with interlocutors as a means of exploring truth. Later practitioners include Cicero, Hume and Galileo. In fiction and drama, dialogue advances plot, reveals character and conveys information, and is conventionally distinguished from monologue and soliloquy.

As a practice

In modern usage, dialogue also denotes structured communication aimed at mutual understanding, as in interfaith dialogue, diplomatic dialogue and the educational theories of Paulo Freire and David Bohm, who proposed dialogue as a method of collective inquiry.
