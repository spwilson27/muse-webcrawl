Fatel Razack

TypeSailing ship
Historic voyageMadras (Chennai) to Trinidad, 1845
SignificanceFirst ship to bring Indian indentured labourers to the Caribbean

The Fatel Razack (also written Fath Al Razack, meaning "Victory of Allah the Sustainer") was the ship that carried the first group of Indian indentured labourers to the Caribbean, arriving in Trinidad on 30 May 1845.

The voyage

After the abolition of slavery in the British Empire, plantation owners sought a new labour force and turned to indentured workers from British India. The Fatel Razack sailed from Calcutta (or, in some accounts, Madras) with more than 200 Indian men, women, and children aboard and, after a voyage of over three months, reached the Gulf of Paria, Trinidad. The date of its arrival, 30 May, is commemorated annually in Trinidad and Tobago as Indian Arrival Day, a public holiday.

Legacy

The arrival of the Fatel Razack began a system that, until 1917, brought roughly 147,000 Indians to Trinidad and hundreds of thousands more to British Guiana, Jamaica, Suriname, Fiji, Mauritius, and Natal. Its passengers were the ancestors of much of today's Indo-Trinidadian community. A replica display and monument near the shore at Cedros/Waterloo commemorate the landing, and the ship's name derives from an Arabic phrase invoking God as provider.
