Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric

The Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric (FLRW) is a metric in general relativity that describes a homogeneous, isotropic, expanding (or contracting) universe. It is the geometric basis of the standard Big Bang cosmological model and of the Friedmann equations.


Named after: Alexander Friedmann, Georges Lemaître, H. P. Robertson, A. G. Walker

Form

The metric introduces a time-dependent scale factor a(t) multiplying a spatial geometry that may be flat, positively curved (spherical) or negatively curved (hyperbolic). Matter and energy content determine how a(t) evolves through the Friedmann equations.

History

Friedmann derived expanding solutions in 1922–24; Lemaître independently did so in 1927 and proposed a primeval atom; Robertson and Walker put the metric on a rigorous footing in the 1930s. Fred Hoyle opposed the resulting Big Bang picture with steady-state theory.

Applications

The FLRW framework underlies measurements of cosmic expansion, dark energy, the cosmic microwave background and distance–redshift relations.
