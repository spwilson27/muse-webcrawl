Cotai Strip

The Cotai Strip, Cotai, Macau

The Cotai Strip is the central area of Cotai, a district of reclaimed land in Macau, a special administrative region of China, that joins the islands of Taipa and Coloane. Modelled in concept on the Las Vegas Strip, it is lined with large integrated casino resorts, hotels, shopping malls, and entertainment venues, and has become the centre of Macau's gaming industry since the mid-2000s.

Development

After Macau's casino monopoly held by Stanley Ho's Sociedade de Turismo e Diversões de Macau ended in 2002, foreign operators including Las Vegas Sands, Wynn Resorts, and MGM entered the market. Las Vegas Sands coined the name "Cotai Strip" for the reclaimed strip of land between Taipa and Coloane and opened The Venetian Macao there in 2007, at the time the largest casino in the world and one of the largest buildings on Earth.

Major properties

Major resorts on or near the strip include The Venetian Macao, The Parisian Macao (with its half-scale Eiffel Tower replica), The Londoner Macao, Sands Cotai Central, City of Dreams, Studio City, Galaxy Macau, Wynn Palace, and MGM Cotai. Together they offer tens of thousands of hotel rooms and a substantial share of Macau's gaming revenue, which has at times exceeded that of the Las Vegas Strip several times over.

Transport

The Cotai Strip is served by the Taipa line of the Macau Light Rapid Transit, which opened in 2019, and by the Lotus Bridge crossing to Hengqin Island in Zhuhai, mainland China. Shuttle buses connect the resorts with the Macau Ferry Terminal, the airport, and the border gates.
