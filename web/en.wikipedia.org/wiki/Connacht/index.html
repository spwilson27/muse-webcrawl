Connacht (Irish: Connachta [ˈkɔn̪ˠəxt̪ˠə], meaning "descendants of Conn"), formerly anglicised as Connaught, is one of the four provinces of Ireland, situated in the west of the country. It comprises the counties of Galway, Leitrim, Mayo, Roscommon and Sligo, and the city of Galway is its largest urban centre. With a population of about 590,000, it is the least populous of the Irish provinces. The River Shannon forms much of its eastern boundary, and its Atlantic coastline includes Connemara, the Aran Islands and Clew Bay.

History

The province takes its name from the Connachta, the legendary descendants of Conn Cétchathach, a mythical high king of Ireland. In the early Middle Ages the province was dominated by the Uí Briúin Ai dynasty, and Cruachan (Rathcroghan) in Roscommon was its ancient royal seat; Queen Medb of Connacht is a central figure of the Ulster Cycle epic Táin Bó Cúailnge. After the Norman invasion in the 12th–13th centuries, Connacht was granted to the de Burgh (Burke) family, and the region became largely Gaelicised. The Cromwellian settlement of the 1650s transplanted dispossessed landowners from the rest of Ireland "to Hell or to Connacht", making the phrase proverbial. The province suffered severely during the Great Famine of 1845–1852, losing a large share of its population to death and emigration, and parts of it remain among Ireland's most rural and economically disadvantaged regions.

Geography and culture

Connacht contains extensive blanket bog, the mountains of Connemara and Nephin, and islands including Inishmore and Achill. Its Gaeltacht districts — in west Galway (Conamara) and Mayo — hold the largest remaining populations of daily Irish speakers. Galway city is a cultural centre known for arts festivals and traditional music; the province is also known for sites such as Knock Shrine, Croagh Patrick (the pilgrimage mountain in Mayo), Kylemore Abbey and the Cliffs of Moher–era Atlantic scenery. In sport, Connacht is represented by the professional rugby team Connacht Rugby, based in Galway, which won the Pro12 title in 2016.
