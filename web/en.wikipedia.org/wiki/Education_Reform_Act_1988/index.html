Education Reform Act 1988

Long titleAn Act to amend the law relating to education
Citation1988 c. 40
Introduced byKenneth Baker, Secretary of State for Education and Science
Royal assent29 July 1988
Territorial extentEngland and Wales (main provisions)

The Education Reform Act 1988 is widely regarded as the most important piece of education legislation in England and Wales since the Education Act 1944. Introduced by Education Secretary Kenneth Baker under the government of Margaret Thatcher, it fundamentally restructured state schooling.

Main provisions

  • National Curriculum: introduced a prescribed curriculum for state schools in England and Wales, with core subjects (English, mathematics and science) and foundation subjects, organised into four Key Stages with attainment targets and testing at ages 7, 11, 14 and 16.
  • Local management of schools: delegated budgets to individual schools, with funding largely following pupil numbers ("formula funding").
  • Open enrolment: allowed parents to choose schools beyond their immediate neighbourhood, subject to capacity.
  • Grant-maintained schools: permitted schools to opt out of local authority control by parental ballot and be funded directly by central government.
  • City Technology Colleges: created a new category of independent state-funded secondary schools.
  • Higher education: abolished academic tenure for newly appointed staff and restructured the funding of universities and polytechnics.

Legacy

The Act introduced market mechanisms and parental choice into state education, and its National Curriculum and testing regime remain the framework of English schooling, though subsequently revised many times. The grant-maintained experiment was ended by the School Standards and Framework Act 1998.
