Goguryeo–Tang War

Date645–668
LocationLiaodong and the northern Korean Peninsula
BelligerentsTang China and Silla against Goguryeo (with Baekje)
ResultFall of Goguryeo, 668

The Goguryeo–Tang War was a series of conflicts between 645 and 668 between the Korean kingdom of Goguryeo and Tang dynasty China, which ultimately, in alliance with the southern Korean kingdom of Silla, destroyed Goguryeo after nearly seven centuries of its existence and redrew the political map of Northeast Asia.

Background and first campaigns

The Tang founder's successor, Emperor Taizong, resented Goguryeo's defiance and its strongman Yŏn Kaesomun, who had seized power in a coup in 642. In 645 Taizong personally led a large invasion across the Liao River, winning field battles and capturing fortresses, but his siege of the fortress of Ansi failed against the stubborn defence traditionally credited to the commander Yang Manch'un, and the onset of winter forced a costly withdrawal. Further Tang campaigns in 647 and 648, smaller in scale, harassed Goguryeo's frontiers without decisive result, and Taizong died in 649 while preparing another invasion.

Fall of Goguryeo

Under Emperor Gaozong, Tang formed an alliance with Silla. Their combined forces destroyed Goguryeo's ally Baekje in 660. Repeated Tang–Silla invasions of Goguryeo in 661–662 were repelled, but after Yŏn Kaesomun's death in 666 his sons quarrelled over the succession and one defected to Tang. In 668 a final Tang and Silla offensive captured Pyongyang, the last king surrendered, and Goguryeo was annexed into the Tang protectorate system, although much of its southern territory passed to Silla and many Goguryeo people were resettled in China. Resistance movements briefly revived Goguryeo successor states, and former Goguryeo territories in Manchuria later formed part of Balhae.
