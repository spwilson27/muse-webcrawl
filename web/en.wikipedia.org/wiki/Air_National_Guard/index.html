Air National Guard

The Air National Guard (ANG), also known as the Air Guard, is a federal military reserve force of the United States Air Force, as well as the air militia of each U.S. state, the District of Columbia, the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico, and the territories of Guam and the U.S. Virgin Islands. Together with the Army National Guard, it forms the National Guard of the United States. Air National Guard units typically operate under state control, answering to the governor through the state adjutant general, but may be federalized by order of the President of the United States.

Overview

The Air National Guard was established under the National Security Act of 1947 as a separate reserve component of the new U.S. Air Force. It maintains more than 90 flying wings and numerous support units across the country. Its members are "citizen-airmen" who generally serve part-time — one weekend a month and two weeks a year — while holding civilian jobs, although a substantial full-time cadre (Active Guard and Reserve and Air Reserve Technician personnel) sustains day-to-day operations.

Missions

The ANG has a dual mission. Its federal mission is to maintain trained and equipped units available for prompt mobilization in war or national emergency; Guard units have deployed extensively, from the Korean War Berlin Crisis call-ups through Operations Desert Storm, Enduring Freedom, and Iraqi Freedom. Its state mission, under the governor's control, includes disaster relief, search and rescue, and support to civil authorities during hurricanes, floods, wildfires, and other emergencies. Distinctive continuing missions include the air defense alert mission for the continental United States, flown largely by ANG fighter units, and Operation Noble Eagle, the post-2001 air defense of American airspace.

Organization

Air National Guard forces are administered at the federal level by the National Guard Bureau, whose Air National Guard directorate coordinates training, funding, and readiness standards with the active-duty Air Force. Flying units are "gained" by major commands — principally Air Combat Command, Air Mobility Command, Air Force Special Operations Command, and Pacific Air Forces — when mobilized. The ANG operates front-line aircraft including the F-15, F-16, F-22, F-35, A-10, KC-135, KC-46, C-130, C-17, and HH-60, as well as remotely piloted aircraft and space and cyber units.
