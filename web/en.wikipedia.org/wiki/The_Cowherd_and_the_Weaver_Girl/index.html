The Cowherd and the Weaver Girl

OriginChinese folk tale
CharactersNiulang (the Cowherd), Zhinü (the Weaver Girl)
FestivalQixi Festival (Double Seventh)
StarsAltair and Vega

Magpies form a bridge across the Milky Way so the lovers can meet.

The Cowherd and the Weaver Girl is a Chinese folk tale. The story tells of the romance between Zhinü (the Weaver Girl, symbolizing the star Vega), the youngest daughter of a goddess, and Niulang (the Cowherd, symbolizing the star Altair). Their love was not allowed, and they were banished to opposite sides of the Heavenly River (the Milky Way). Once a year, on the seventh day of the seventh lunar month, a flock of magpies forms a bridge to reunite the lovers for one day. It is one of China's Four Great Folktales.

The tale

In most versions, Niulang, a kind-hearted orphan who herded his family's ox, was directed by the ox (itself a banished deity) to a spring where heavenly maidens bathed. He hid Zhinü's clothes, and the two fell in love, married, and had children. When the Jade Emperor or the Queen Mother of the West discovered the marriage, Zhinü was recalled to heaven to resume her weaving of colorful clouds. In her grief, the Queen Mother scratched the sky with a hairpin, creating the Milky Way between them. Moved by their devotion, she allowed them to meet once a year on the Magpie Bridge.

Festival and variations

The tale is the origin of the Qixi Festival, celebrated on the seventh day of the seventh month of the Chinese lunar calendar, sometimes called Chinese Valentine's Day. Variants of the story exist across East and Southeast Asia, including the Japanese Tanabata story of Orihime and Hikoboshi and the Korean Chilseok legend. The story has been told in Chinese literature for over two thousand years, with early references in the Classic of Poetry to the stars, and fuller narrative forms appearing by the Han dynasty.
