The Chief of the General Staff (CGS) is the title of the professional head of the British Army since 1964, previously titled Chief of the Imperial General Staff (CIGS) from 1909 to 1964.

History

The post was created in 1904 following the Esher reforms. As CIGS, holders such as Sir William Robertson and Sir Alan Brooke served as the senior military adviser to the government in the two world wars.

Role

The CGS is a member of the Defence Council and the Army Board, and is responsible for the fighting effectiveness, efficiency and morale of the Army.
