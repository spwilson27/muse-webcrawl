Young Men and Fire

Young Men and Fire is a non-fiction book by American author Norman Maclean, published posthumously in 1992 by the University of Chicago Press. It recounts the Mann Gulch fire of August 5, 1949, in Montana, in which 13 firefighters — most of them young smokejumpers — were killed when a wildfire blew up in a narrow gulch.

Content

Maclean, author of A River Runs Through It, spent many years researching the disaster: interviewing the two surviving smokejumpers, studying Forest Service records, and working with scientists to model the fire's behaviour using modern fire science. The book reconstructs the crew's final minutes and explores the tragedy of young men racing a fire uphill and losing.

Reception and legacy

The book won the National Book Critics Circle Award for nonfiction and is regarded as a classic of disaster literature. Maclean died in 1990 before completing his final revisions; the book was edited and published by the University of Chicago Press. His research helped renew attention to the Mann Gulch fire and influenced wildland fire training and safety culture in the United States.
