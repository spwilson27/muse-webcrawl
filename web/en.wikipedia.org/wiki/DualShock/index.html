DualShock

The original DualShock (SCPH-1200)

The DualShock is a line of gamepads developed by Sony for the PlayStation family of consoles. The name derives from the controller's dual vibration motors, which provide force feedback. Introduced in Japan in November 1997 for the original PlayStation, the DualShock line — in successive generations DualShock 2, 3 and 4 — defined the standard PlayStation controller layout for more than two decades before being succeeded by the DualSense in 2020.

DualShock (original)

The first DualShock combined two features that had appeared separately on earlier Sony controllers: the twin analogue sticks of the Dual Analog Controller and built-in vibration feedback. It used two vibration motors of different sizes to vary the intensity and character of the rumble. The design, with its symmetrical sticks and four shoulder buttons, became the template for all later models. It won an Emmy Award for Engineering in 2007 alongside Nintendo's controllers for contributions to game control.

DualShock 2

Released with the PlayStation 2 in 2000, the DualShock 2 was visually similar to its predecessor but in black, with all face and shoulder buttons (except Start, Select and the analogue mode button) being analogue pressure-sensitive — a feature used by games such as Gran Turismo 3 and Metal Gear Solid 2.

DualShock 3

The DualShock 3 (2007) restored vibration to the PlayStation 3's controller line-up after the launch Sixaxis controller had omitted it during a patent dispute over haptic technology. It added Bluetooth wireless connectivity and motion sensing inherited from the Sixaxis.

DualShock 4

Launched with the PlayStation 4 in 2013, the DualShock 4 added a clickable two-point touch pad, a light bar (used for player identification and PlayStation VR tracking), a Share button, a built-in speaker and a headset jack. It is also widely used as a PC controller.
