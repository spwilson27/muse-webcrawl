The French Forces of the Interior (French: Forces françaises de l'Intérieur, FFI) were the formally unified military arm of the French Resistance during the final phase of the Second World War. Created in February 1944, the FFI merged the principal resistance armies — the Gaullist Armée Secrète, the communist Francs-Tireurs et Partisans, and others — under a single command answerable to General de Gaulle's French Committee of National Liberation.

Formation and role

General Marie-Pierre Kœnig was appointed commander in March 1944, with the FFI placed under the operational umbrella of Allied headquarters for the Liberation. As Allied forces landed in Normandy in June 1944 and in Provence in August, FFI units sabotaged rail and communications, ambushed German columns, and rose in open revolt in many regions. FFI fighters played a central role in the Liberation of Paris in August 1944 and in freeing much of southwestern and central France largely by their own efforts.

Integration

After the Liberation, some 140,000 FFI volunteers were amalgamated into the regular French Army ("l'amalgame"), forming new units that fought in the final campaigns of 1944–45, including the reduction of the German-held Atlantic pockets. Total FFI strength reached roughly 400,000 by October 1944.
