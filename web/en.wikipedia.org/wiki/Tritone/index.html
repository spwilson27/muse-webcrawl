Tritone

A tritone is a musical interval spanning three whole tones (six semitones) — an augmented fourth or, enharmonically, a diminished fifth. It divides the octave exactly in half and is strongly dissonant, playing a central role in the tension and resolution of Western harmony.

Properties

In twelve-tone equal temperament the tritone's frequency ratio is √2:1. It is the inversion of itself: inverting a tritone yields another tritone. Within the dominant seventh chord, the tritone between the third and seventh scale degrees drives resolution to the tonic in functional harmony, and tritone substitution in jazz exploits the interval's symmetry.

"Diabolus in musica"

The tritone acquired a reputation as the diabolus in musica ("the Devil in music"), said to have been banned in medieval sacred music. Modern scholarship treats the "ban" as exaggerated, though theorists from Guido of Arezzo onward marked the interval as difficult and unstable in solmisation. It features prominently in works from Wagner's Tristan und Isolde to Bernstein's "Maria" in West Side Story, as well as in heavy metal and film scores.
