North American Regional Broadcasting Agreement

The North American Regional Broadcasting Agreement (NARBA) is an international treaty governing the allocation of AM radio frequencies in North America. Signed in Havana in 1937 and implemented on 29 March 1941, it reorganised the standard broadcast band among the United States, Canada, Mexico, Cuba, and other signatories.

NARBA
Signed: 1937, Havana
Implemented: 29 March 1941
Scope: AM band allocations in North America

Provisions

NARBA extended the AM band to 1600 kHz, classified stations into clear-channel, regional, and local categories, and assigned exclusive high-power clear channels among the signatory nations. The 1941 implementation forced frequency changes on some 1,300 stations in a single night — remembered in US radio as "moving day."

Legacy

The agreement's channel classifications still underlie North American AM regulation, although bilateral agreements and later treaties have updated many details. Its clear-channel system allowed a handful of 50,000-watt stations to be heard across much of the continent at night.
