eBird is an online database of bird observations providing scientists, researchers and amateur naturalists with real-time data about bird distribution and abundance. Launched in 2002 by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology at Cornell University and the National Audubon Society, it has grown into one of the world's largest biodiversity citizen-science projects, with well over a billion observations contributed by hundreds of thousands of participants.

How it works

Birders submit "checklists" — lists of species seen or heard at a place and time, with counts and effort information — through the eBird website or mobile app. Automated filters flag unusual records for review by regional volunteer experts. The companion app Merlin Bird ID helps users identify birds by description, photo or sound, and its observations feed into the same ecosystem.

Scientific use

eBird data power species range maps, migration animations, the annual eBird Status and Trends products, and hundreds of peer-reviewed studies in conservation, ecology and climate-change research. Events such as the Global Big Day mobilise hundreds of thousands of observers in a single day.
