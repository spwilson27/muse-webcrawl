Alternating group

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, an alternating group is the group of even permutations of a finite set. The alternating group on a set of n elements is called the alternating group of degree n, or the alternating group on n letters, and is denoted An.

Definition

Every permutation can be written as a product of transpositions (swaps of two elements); although the decomposition is not unique, its parity — whether the number of transpositions is even or odd — is fixed. The even permutations form a subgroup of the symmetric group Sn: the identity is even, the product of two even permutations is even, and every even permutation has an even inverse. An is the kernel of the sign homomorphism sgn: Sn → {±1}, hence a normal subgroup of index 2, with exactly n!/2 elements.

A cycle of length k decomposes into k − 1 transpositions, so a permutation is even precisely when it contains an even number of even-length cycles in its cycle decomposition. The group An is generated by the 3-cycles.

Examples

  • A1 and A2 are trivial; A3 is cyclic of order 3.
  • A4 has order 12 and acts as the rotation group of the tetrahedron; it is solvable and contains the Klein four-group as a normal subgroup.
  • A5 has order 60, acts as the rotation group of the icosahedron, and is the smallest non-abelian simple group.

Simplicity

A landmark theorem of Galois theory states that An is simple — has no nontrivial normal subgroups — for n ≥ 5. This non-solvability is the group-theoretic reason there is no general formula by radicals for polynomial equations of degree five or higher (the Abel–Ruffini theorem): the solvability of a polynomial by radicals corresponds to the solvability of its Galois group, and a solvable group cannot have A5 as a composition factor.

Relations

An is the commutator subgroup of Sn (for n ≥ 2), and its automorphism group is Sn except for n = 1, 2, 6; A6 famously admits an exceptional outer automorphism. The Schur cover of An underlies the theory of projective representations of symmetric groups.
