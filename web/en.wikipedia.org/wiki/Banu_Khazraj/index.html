Banu Khazraj

The Banu Khazraj (Arabic: بنو الخزرج) were one of the two great Arab tribes of Yathrib — the oasis city later known as Medina — in the Hejaz, the other being the Banu Aws. Of South Arabian (Yemeni) origin, the Khazraj migrated north in late antiquity and, with the Aws, became the agricultural people of the oasis, living alongside the Jewish tribes of Banu Qaynuqa, Banu Nadir, and Banu Qurayza.

Before Islam

The Khazraj and Aws fought a long series of feuds, culminating in the Battle of Bu'ath (about 617 CE), in which both tribes suffered heavily and neither could dominate the oasis. Traditions hold that the Khazraj's leading families, particularly the Banu Najjar, were related to the family of Muhammad through his great-grandmother Salma bint Amr.

Role in early Islam

Men of the Khazraj were prominent in the two pledges of al-Aqaba (621 and 622 CE) by which leading men of Yathrib invited Muhammad to settle among them as arbiter. After the Hijra (622), the Khazraj, with the Aws, formed the Ansar — the "Helpers" — who hosted and supported the Meccan emigrants (Muhajirun). Leading Khazraj companions included Sa'd ibn Ubada, their chief; Ubayy ibn Ka'b; and Abu Ayyub al-Ansari, in whose house Muhammad first lodged in Medina. At Muhammad's death in 632, the Ansar, led by Sa'd ibn Ubada of the Khazraj, gathered at the Saqifa of the Banu Sa'ida to consider the succession, but the caliphate passed to Abu Bakr of Quraysh. Members of the tribe later spread across the Islamic world, and Khazraj descendants are attested in al-Andalus and North Africa.
