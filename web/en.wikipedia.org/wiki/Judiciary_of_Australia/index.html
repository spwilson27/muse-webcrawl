Judiciary of Australia

The judiciary of Australia comprises judges in courts exercising federal and state judicial power under the Constitution of Australia. Chapter III vests the judicial power of the Commonwealth in the High Court of Australia and such other federal courts as Parliament creates, and guarantees the separation of judicial power at the federal level.

Hierarchy

The High Court, established in 1903, is the final court of appeal. Federal courts include the Federal Court of Australia, the Federal Circuit and Family Court, and the Family Court. Each state and territory has a Supreme Court, intermediate district or county courts, and magistrates' courts. Judges are appointed by the executive and hold tenure until a mandatory retirement age — 70 for federal judges — and can be removed only by an address from both Houses of Parliament.

Doctrine

The Boilermakers' Case (1956) entrenched the separation of federal judicial power, while the Kable doctrine limits state parliaments' ability to compromise the institutional integrity of state courts exercising federal jurisdiction.
