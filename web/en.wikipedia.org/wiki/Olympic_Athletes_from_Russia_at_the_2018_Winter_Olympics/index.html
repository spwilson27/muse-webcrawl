Olympic Athletes from Russia (OAR) was the designation used for the team of Russian athletes who competed at the 2018 Winter Olympics in Pyeongchang, South Korea, after the International Olympic Committee suspended the Russian Olympic Committee over the state-sponsored doping scandal surrounding the 2014 Sochi Games.

Background

Following the McLaren Report's findings of a state-run doping system, the IOC suspended the Russian Olympic Committee on 5 December 2017. Individual Russian athletes with clean records could be invited to compete as neutral "Olympic Athletes from Russia," wearing neutral uniforms, competing under the Olympic flag, and hearing the Olympic Hymn instead of the Russian anthem at medal ceremonies. An IOC panel reviewed applicants and ultimately cleared 168 athletes.

Medals

The OAR team won 17 medals. Gold medals came from figure skater Alina Zagitova, who won the ladies' singles at age 15 ahead of teammate Evgenia Medvedeva, and from the men's ice hockey team, which defeated Germany 4–3 in overtime in the final — a game in which the neutral athletes sang the Russian anthem over the Olympic Hymn during the medal ceremony. Silver medals included the figure skating team event and skeleton athlete Nikita Tregubov; bronzes included skier Alexander Bolshunov, who won four medals in total, the most of any OAR athlete.

Doping cases at the Games

Two OAR athletes failed doping tests during the Games: curler Alexander Krushelnitsky, who was stripped of the mixed doubles bronze medal he had won with Anastasia Bryzgalova after testing positive for meldonium, and bobsledder Nadezhda Sergeeva, who tested positive for trimetazidine. Partly as a consequence, the IOC decided not to lift the Russian Olympic Committee's suspension before the closing ceremony, so the team marched under the Olympic flag; the suspension was lifted three days later, on 28 February 2018.

Legacy

The OAR designation was a one-off precursor to later neutral arrangements: Russian athletes competed as "ROC" (Russian Olympic Committee) at the Tokyo 2020 and Beijing 2022 Games under sanctions imposed by the Court of Arbitration for Sport following the World Anti-Doping Agency's four-year ban, reduced on appeal to two years.
