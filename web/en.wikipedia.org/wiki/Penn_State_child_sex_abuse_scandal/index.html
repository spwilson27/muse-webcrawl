Penn State child sex abuse scandal
Date: Revealed November 2011
Location: Pennsylvania State University, University Park, Pennsylvania
Key figure: Jerry Sandusky (convicted 2012)
Outcome: University fined, football sanctions, officials prosecuted

The scandal centred on Penn State's football facilities

The Penn State child sex abuse scandal concerned allegations and criminal convictions arising from the sexual abuse of young boys over at least fifteen years by Jerry Sandusky, a former assistant football coach at Pennsylvania State University, and the failure of senior university officials to report the abuse to police. The scandal became public in November 2011 with a grand jury presentment, shaking one of the most storied programmes in college sports.

Background and charges

Sandusky, defensive coordinator under head coach Joe Paterno until his 1999 retirement, founded The Second Mile, a charity for underprivileged youth through which he met many of his victims. A 2001 incident witnessed by graduate assistant Mike McQueary in a campus shower was reported up the chain to Paterno, athletic director Tim Curley, and vice president Gary Schultz, but not to law enforcement. In November 2011 Sandusky was arrested and charged with dozens of counts of child sexual abuse. In June 2012 he was convicted on 45 counts and later sentenced to 30 to 60 years in prison.

Consequences

University president Graham Spanier and Paterno were removed within days of the arrests; Paterno died in January 2012. The Freeh Report, commissioned by the university's trustees and released in July 2012, concluded that the university's most senior leaders had concealed Sandusky's activities to protect the institution's reputation. The NCAA imposed severe sanctions on the football programme, including a $60 million fine, a four-year postseason ban, scholarship reductions, and the vacating of all wins from 1998 to 2011 — later substantially mitigated, with wins restored in 2015. Curley, Schultz, and Spanier were each convicted of child endangerment. The university has paid well over $100 million in settlements to victims.
