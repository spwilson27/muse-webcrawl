Brian Randolph Greene (born February 9, 1963) is an American theoretical physicist, mathematician, and string theorist. He is a professor of physics and mathematics at Columbia University, where he has also served as director of the university's Center for Theoretical Physics. Greene is widely known for his popular science books, most notably The Elegant Universe (1999), which brought string theory to a general audience, and for co-founding the World Science Festival in 2008.

Early life and education

Greene was born in New York City, the son of Alan Greene, a vaudeville performer who later worked as an acting coach. A mathematical prodigy, Greene could multiply 30-digit numbers by the age of five and was tutoring university students while still at school. He graduated from Stuyvesant High School and entered Harvard University in 1980, completing his undergraduate degree in physics. He then went to Magdalen College, Oxford, as a Rhodes Scholar, earning his doctorate in 1987 with research on superstring theory.

Career

After a postdoctoral position at Harvard, Greene joined the faculty of Cornell University in 1990, becoming a full professor in 1995. In 1996 he moved to Columbia University, where he has remained. His research has centred on string theory and its mathematical structure, including work on mirror symmetry — with Ronen Plesser he demonstrated the symmetry relating distinct Calabi–Yau manifolds — and on the geometry of extra dimensions, including the "Greene–Shapere–Vafa–Yau" work on stringy cosmic strings. With David Morrison and others he studied topology change in string theory, showing that spacetime geometry can evolve smoothly between topologically distinct forms.

Popularisation of science

Greene's first book, The Elegant Universe: Superstrings, Hidden Dimensions, and the Quest for the Ultimate Theory (1999), was a finalist for the Pulitzer Prize and won the Royal Society Prize for Science Books; it was adapted into a three-hour PBS Nova miniseries hosted by Greene in 2003. His subsequent books include The Fabric of the Cosmos (2004), also adapted for television; Icarus at the Edge of Time (2008), a retelling of the Icarus myth for children; The Hidden Reality (2011), on the multiverse; and Until the End of Time (2020), on cosmology and consciousness. In 2008 Greene and his wife, the television producer Tracy Day, founded the World Science Festival, an annual event in New York City. He received the Andrew Gemant Award from the American Institute of Physics in 2003 for his contributions to bringing physics to the public.

Personal life

Greene is married to Tracy Day, with whom he has two children. He has been a vegetarian since childhood and is a vegan. He has appeared frequently as a guest on television talk shows and has made cameo appearances in film and television, including in the sitcom The Big Bang Theory.
