Đinh Bộ Lĩnh

Đinh Bộ Lĩnh Reign name: Đinh Tiên Hoàng ("First Emperor")
Born: 22 March 924, Gia Viễn, Ninh Bình
Died: October 979 (aged 55), Hoa Lư
Reign: 968 – 979
Dynasty: Đinh
State: Đại Cồ Việt
Statue of Đinh Bộ Lĩnh at Hoa Lư.

Đinh Bộ Lĩnh (924 – 979), posthumously known by his imperial name Đinh Tiên Hoàng, was the founding emperor of the Đinh dynasty and the first emperor of an independent Vietnam after a millennium of Chinese domination. In 968 he unified the country after the Anarchy of the 12 Warlords, proclaimed himself emperor, and gave the state the name Đại Cồ Việt.

Born in the village of Đại Hoàng (in present-day Ninh Bình province), Đinh Bộ Lĩnh grew up during the collapse of Tang authority in the former province of Annan. As a young warlord's lieutenant he built his own following, and by 968 he had defeated or absorbed the eleven rival warlords who had divided the country.

Reign and death

Đinh Tiên Hoàng established his capital at Hoa Lư, amid the limestone karsts of Ninh Bình, organised a court and administration modelled partly on the Chinese pattern, appointed generals and officials, and concluded a tributary relationship with the Song dynasty that secured recognition of his state while preserving its internal independence. His reign saw the first steps of an independent Vietnamese monarchy: a royal army, a hierarchy of mandarins, and legal codes. In 979 he and his eldest son were assassinated by an official, Đỗ Thích, plunging the young state into a succession crisis that was resolved by the rise of Lê Hoàn and the Early Lê dynasty.
