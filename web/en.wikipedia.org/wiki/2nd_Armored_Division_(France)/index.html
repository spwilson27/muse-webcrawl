The French 2nd Armored Division (French: 2e Division Blindée, abbreviated 2e DB), commanded by General Philippe Leclerc, was one of the most famous French formations of the Second World War. It is best known for its role in the Normandy campaign and the liberation of Paris in August 1944.

Formation

The division grew out of Leclerc's Free French column that had marched from Chad and fought in North Africa, capturing Kufra in 1941 and fighting in Tunisia. Formally created in August 1943 and equipped largely with American vehicles, including M4 Sherman tanks and M10 tank destroyers, it included French troops, colonial soldiers and Spanish Republican exiles — among them the Nueve company, the first Allied unit to enter Paris.

France and Germany, 1944–45

Landing in Normandy in August 1944, the division fought at Alençon and Argentan before being chosen to liberate Paris, entering the capital on 24–25 August 1944. It went on to liberate Strasbourg in November, fulfilling Leclerc's "Oath of Kufra" to fight until the French flag flew over Strasbourg cathedral. In 1945 the division fought in the Colmar Pocket and ended the war in Bavaria, reaching Berchtesgaden.

Legacy

Disbanded in 1946, the division was re-formed in 1977 and finally dissolved in 1999, its traditions carried on by the 2nd Armored Brigade. Numerous streets, squares and monuments across France commemorate the 2e DB and its Sherman tanks.
