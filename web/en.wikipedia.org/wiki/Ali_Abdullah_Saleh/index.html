Ali Abdullah Saleh (21 March 1947 – 4 December 2017) was a Yemeni politician and military officer who ruled Yemen for more than three decades. He became president of the Yemen Arab Republic (North Yemen) in 1978, and on unification in 1990 became the first president of the Republic of Yemen, a position he held until 2012.

Rule

Saleh governed through a web of patronage and balancing among tribes, the military and Islamist groups, a style he described as "dancing on the heads of snakes". His rule saw the unification of North and South Yemen, a civil war in 1994 that preserved the union, a long insurgency by the Houthis in the north, and an al-Qaeda presence in the south. Yemen remained one of the poorest Arab countries and was plagued by corruption.

Downfall and death

Mass protests during the Arab Spring of 2011 forced Saleh to sign a Gulf Cooperation Council agreement transferring power to his deputy, Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi, in February 2012. Granted immunity, Saleh remained a power broker and later allied with the Houthis in the civil war that began in 2014. When the alliance collapsed, Houthi fighters killed him in Sana'a on 4 December 2017 as he attempted to flee the capital.
