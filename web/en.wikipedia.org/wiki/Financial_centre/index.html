Financial centre

A financial centre or financial hub is a location with a concentration of participants in banking, asset management, insurance or financial markets, business infrastructure facilitating these activities, and a workforce of skilled financial professionals. Financial centres compete with each other and are ranked by indices such as the Global Financial Centres Index.

Types

International financial centres such as New York City, London, and Hong Kong serve clients worldwide and host the headquarters of major banks and exchanges. Regional centres serve a particular geographic area, while offshore financial centres — including the Cayman Islands, Jersey, Singapore, and Luxembourg — provide financial services to non-residents, often with low taxation and light regulation. Traditional centres grew around ports, trade routes, and legal systems; the common-law framework, deep capital markets, and time-zone position are commonly cited advantages of London and New York.

Historical development

Early financial centres developed in Renaissance Italy (Florence, Genoa, Venice), then shifted to Amsterdam in the seventeenth century, London in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, and New York after the Second World War. Asian centres such as Tokyo, Singapore, Hong Kong, and Shanghai grew in prominence in the late twentieth and twenty-first centuries.
