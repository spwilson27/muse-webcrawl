Arthrodesis

Arthrodesis
SpecialtyOrthopedic surgery
Other namesJoint fusion, artificial ankylosis
UsesSevere arthritis, joint instability, deformity, failed joint replacement

Arthrodesis, also known as artificial ankylosis or joint fusion, is a surgical procedure in which a joint is fused in a fixed position so that the bones on either side grow together into one. The operation sacrifices movement at the joint in exchange for stability and relief of pain, and is most often used when a joint has been destroyed by arthritis, infection, trauma or congenital deformity and cannot be salvaged or replaced.

Technique

The surgeon removes the remaining articular cartilage and shapes the bone ends so that they fit closely together in the position of greatest function. The surfaces are held in compression with screws, plates, rods, staples or an external fixator, and bone graft — the patient's own bone, donor bone, or a synthetic substitute — may be packed around the site to encourage union. Over the following weeks to months the bones bridge with new bone, a process monitored by X-ray. Some procedures, such as ankle arthrodesis, can be performed arthroscopically through small incisions.

Sites and indications

Common sites include the ankle, the joints of the foot (including triple arthrodesis of the hindfoot), the wrist, the thumb and fingers, the big toe, and the spine, where fusion of vertebrae is a long-established operation. In the hip and knee, arthrodesis is now uncommon because joint replacement (arthroplasty) usually gives better function, but fusion remains an option for young manual workers, severe infection, or failed replacements, particularly in the ankle, where replacement is less predictable.

Outcomes and complications

Successful fusion reliably relieves pain, but the loss of motion transfers stress to neighbouring joints, which may develop arthritis over time. Complications include failure of the bones to unite (non-union), malposition, infection and nerve injury. Smoking and diabetes increase the risk of non-union.

In veterinary medicine

Arthrodesis is also widely used in animals — for example, fusion of the carpus or hock in dogs and horses with severe injury or degenerative disease.
