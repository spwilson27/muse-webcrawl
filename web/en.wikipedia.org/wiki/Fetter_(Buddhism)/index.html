Fetter (Buddhism)

In Buddhism, a fetter (Pali: saṃyojana; Sanskrit: saṃyojana) is a mental bond or chain that binds beings to saṃsāra, the cycle of suffering and rebirth. The canonical lists identify ten fetters, which are progressively abandoned on the path to awakening; the stage of spiritual attainment of a practitioner is traditionally defined by which fetters they have destroyed.

The ten fetters

The ten fetters in the Pali Canon are:

  1. Belief in a self (sakkāya-diṭṭhi) — the view that a permanent self exists.
  2. Doubt (vicikicchā) — sceptical uncertainty about the Buddha, Dhamma, and Sangha.
  3. Attachment to rites and rituals (sīlabbata-parāmāsa).
  4. Sensual desire (kāma-rāga).
  5. Ill will (vyāpāda).
  6. Lust for material existence (rūpa-rāga) — attachment to rebirth in the form realm.
  7. Lust for immaterial existence (arūpa-rāga) — attachment to formless realms.
  8. Conceit (māna) — the subtle sense of "I am".
  9. Restlessness (uddhacca).
  10. Ignorance (avijjā).

The four stages of awakening

Abandoning the first three fetters makes one a stream-enterer (sotāpanna), destined for awakening within at most seven rebirths. Weakening sensual desire and ill will marks the once-returner (sakadāgāmi); destroying the first five fetters makes a non-returner (anāgāmi), who will not be reborn in the sensual world. Destroying all ten fetters, one becomes an arahant, fully liberated and free from rebirth.

In Mahāyāna Buddhism

Mahāyāna Abhidharma lists present the fetters somewhat differently and relate them to the defilements abandoned along the bodhisattva path, but the tenfold scheme remains widely taught across traditions.
