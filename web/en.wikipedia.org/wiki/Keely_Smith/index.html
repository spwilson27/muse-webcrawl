Keely Smith

Smith during her years performing with Louis Prima

Keely Smith (born Dorothy Jacqueline Keely; March 9, 1928 – December 16, 2017) was an American jazz and popular music singer, who performed and recorded extensively in the 1950s with her then-husband Louis Prima and throughout her career as a solo artist.

Early life

Smith was born in Norfolk, Virginia, of Irish and Cherokee ancestry. She sang with a naval air station band as a teenager and was singing professionally by age fifteen. In 1947 she joined Louis Prima's band after answering his search for a new female vocalist.

Career with Louis Prima

Smith married Prima in 1953, and their musical partnership became one of the most popular lounge acts in Las Vegas and on television. Their contrasting stage personas — Prima's exuberant clowning against Smith's deadpan, unmoving delivery — were a comic sensation. Their recording of "That Old Black Magic" won the Grammy Award for Best Performance by a Vocal Group or Chorus at the first Grammy Awards in 1959. She also appeared with Prima in the film Hey Boy! Hey Girl! (1959) and sang "That Old Black Magic" in Senior Prom (1958).

Solo career

After divorcing Prima in 1961, Smith signed with Reprise Records and recorded a series of solo albums, including I Wish You Love (1957, for Capitol), whose title track became her signature song. Her 1965 album The Intimate Keely Smith, recorded with arrangements influenced by Frank Sinatra's style, was a notable success. She retired from performing in the 1980s but returned in the 2000s with well-received albums and concerts, including Swing, Swing, Swing (2000) and Keely Sings Sinatra (2001).

Death

Smith died of apparent heart failure in Palm Springs, California, on December 16, 2017, at the age of 89.
