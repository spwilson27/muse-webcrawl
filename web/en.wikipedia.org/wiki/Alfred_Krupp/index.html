Alfred Krupp (26 April 1812 – 14 July 1887) was a German industrialist who transformed his family's small Essen steelworks into one of the largest industrial enterprises in Europe, supplying steel, railway material and artillery to states around the world. He was nicknamed "the Cannon King" (der Kanonenkönig).

Building the firm

Krupp took over the cast-steel factory founded by his father Friedrich after the latter's death in 1826, when he was only fourteen. He travelled Europe as a salesman, promoted seamless railway tyres — the three interlocked rings that became the firm's emblem — and from the 1850s developed breech-loading steel cannon that proved superior to bronze guns. Krupp steel artillery armed Prussia and many other nations, and the firm's exhibits, including a 50-ton cast steel block, caused sensations at world exhibitions.

Social policy and legacy

A paternalistic employer, Krupp built housing estates, schools, hospitals and pension funds for his workers while fiercely opposing trade unions and social democracy. By his death the company employed around 20,000 people. His son Friedrich Alfred succeeded him, and the dynasty's role as Germany's premier arms maker continued into the 20th century.
