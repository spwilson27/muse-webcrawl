Collapse: How Societies Choose to Fail or Succeed is a 2005 book by the American geographer and historian Jared Diamond. A sequel in theme to his Pulitzer Prize-winning Guns, Germs, and Steel, the book examines how environmental factors, and societies' responses to them, contributed to the collapse of past civilisations, and draws lessons for the modern world.

Summary

Diamond proposes a five-point framework of factors that can contribute to societal collapse: environmental damage, climate change, hostile neighbours, loss of friendly trade partners, and — most importantly — the society's own response to its problems. Case studies of failed societies include the Norse settlements of Greenland, the Maya civilisation, the inhabitants of Easter Island, and the Anasazi of the American Southwest. Contrasting successes, where societies recognised problems and adapted, include Iceland, Tokugawa Japan's forestry management, and the highland farmers of New Guinea. Diamond also applies the framework to modern cases such as Rwanda, Haiti and Montana.

Reception

The book was a bestseller and was widely reviewed. Some scholars praised its scope and readability, while others criticised its environmental determinism and the accuracy of particular case studies, notably the account of Easter Island's "ecocide". The question of how far collapse is driven by environmental versus political and economic choices remains debated among archaeologists and historians.
