Breguet 14

Role: Bomber / reconnaissance aircraft

Manufacturer: Breguet Aviation

First flight: 21 November 1916

Number built: about 7,800

The Breguet 14 was a French biplane bomber and reconnaissance aircraft of the First World War. Designed by Louis Breguet, it was one of the most successful French warplanes of the conflict, notable for its extensive use of aluminium in its structure — unusual at a time when most aircraft were built of wood and fabric.

Service

Entering service in 1917, the Breguet 14 served with French, Belgian, and American squadrons in day-bomber (B.2) and reconnaissance (A.2) versions, powered typically by a Renault V-12 engine. It remained in use after the war in colonial and civil roles, including the pioneering Aéropostale mail routes.
