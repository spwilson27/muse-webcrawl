Gay slang

TypeSlang / argot of LGBT communities
Notable sourcesPolari, ball culture, drag culture, camp

Gay slang is a set of slang and argot used predominantly by gay people and wider LGBT communities. It has changed a great deal since the beginning of the 20th century, when it served partly as a secret language allowing gay people to identify one another and speak freely in public under conditions of criminalisation and stigma.

Origins

Early gay slang drew on several sources: Polari, the cant of British travelling performers and fairground workers later adopted by gay men in Britain; the vocabulary of theatre and female impersonation; and the African-American and Latino ball culture of New York City, which produced a rich lexicon around drag houses, categories, and performance — terms such as "reading", "shade", "werk", "slay", and "spill the tea". Ball culture was documented in the film Paris Is Burning (1990) and later popularised worldwide by the television series RuPaul's Drag Race.

Function and spread

Like other argots, gay slang signals in-group identity and solidarity, and shades readily into the humorous, theatrical register known as camp. From the late 20th century onward, many terms crossed into mainstream popular culture and internet slang, often losing their original connotations in the process.
