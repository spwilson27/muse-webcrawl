Prince William Augustus, Duke of Cumberland

Born: April 15, 1721, London

Died: October 31, 1765, London

Father: George II

Prince William Augustus, Duke of Cumberland (April 15, 1721 – October 31, 1765) was the third and youngest son of King George II of Great Britain and Caroline of Ansbach. A military commander, he is best remembered for his defeat of the Jacobite rising at the Battle of Culloden in 1746 — and for the harsh reputation that campaign earned him in Scotland, where he is still known as "Butcher Cumberland".

Military career

Cumberland showed early military enthusiasm, serving at the Battle of Dettingen (1743) and commanding allied forces at the Battle of Fontenoy (1745) during the War of the Austrian Succession. When Charles Edward Stuart ("Bonnie Prince Charlie") raised the Jacobite standard in 1745, Cumberland was recalled to Britain, took command of the government army, and crushed the Jacobites at Culloden near Inverness on April 16, 1746 — the last pitched battle fought on British soil.

Aftermath and later life

The severe repression that followed in the Highlands, together with legislation disarming the clans and proscribing Highland dress, fixed his controversial reputation. His later career was less successful: defeat by the French at Lauffeld (1747) and the Convention of Klosterzeven (1757) during the Seven Years' War damaged his standing, and he resigned his military offices. He remained an influential political figure and adviser to his nephew George III until his death in 1765.

The flower sweet William is popularly said to have been named in his honour — and, in Scotland, the weed ragwort is sometimes called "stinking Billy" in counterpoint.
