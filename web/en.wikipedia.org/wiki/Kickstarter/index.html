Kickstarter

Kickstarter is an American public benefit corporation based in Brooklyn, New York, that operates a global crowdfunding platform for creative projects. Launched on 28 April 2009 by Perry Chen, Yancey Strickler and Charles Adler, it allows creators to raise money from "backers" in exchange for rewards, under an all-or-nothing model: a project receives its pledges only if it reaches its funding goal by its deadline. Kickstarter has hosted some of the most famous crowdfunding campaigns, including the Pebble smartwatch, the Veronica Mars film, the Exploding Kittens card game and the Coolest Cooler.

Model

Creators set a funding goal and deadline of up to 60 days, and offer tiered rewards. If the goal is met, backers' cards are charged and Kickstarter collects a 5% fee; if not, no money changes hands. Kickstarter stresses that it is a funding platform, not a store: backers support a project's creation and are not guaranteed delivery, which has led to controversy over failed or delayed high-profile projects. By the early 2020s, more than $6 billion had been pledged to hundreds of thousands of projects, with a success rate of roughly 40%.

History

The company grew rapidly after 2010, expanding internationally and reincorporating as a public benefit corporation in 2015, pledging to consider public benefit alongside profit. Its workers unionised in 2020, a first among major US tech firms. In 2021 Kickstarter announced — and later quietly shelved — plans to build a blockchain-based crowdfunding protocol, following criticism.

Notable projects

  • Pebble Time (2015) — over $20 million, among the largest campaigns ever.
  • Coolest Cooler (2014) — $13 million raised; later became a prominent failure case.
  • Exploding Kittens (2015) — most-backed project by number of backers at the time.
  • Kingdom Death: Monster 1.5 and the Brandon Sanderson 2022 campaign — record-setting tabletop and publishing projects.
