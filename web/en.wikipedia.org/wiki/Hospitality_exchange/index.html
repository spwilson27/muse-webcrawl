Hospitality exchange

A hospitality exchange network or hospitality service is a form of social networking service in which hosts offer travellers accommodation in their homes, usually free of charge, in exchange for cultural exchange rather than money. Participants typically arrange stays through a central website, and most networks operate on a principle of generalized reciprocity — guests are expected to host others in turn, not necessarily their own hosts.

History

Organized hospitality exchange began in 1949 with Servas International, founded by Bob Luitweiler to promote peace through intercultural contact, and grew with printed host directories. The internet transformed the practice: Hospitality Club (2000) and then Couchsurfing (founded 2004 by Casey Fenton) brought it to millions of members. Other networks include BeWelcome (a non-profit, volunteer-run successor community), Warm Showers (for touring cyclists), Pasporta Servo (for Esperanto speakers), and WWOOF, which exchanges lodging and food for farm work.

How it works and trust

Members create profiles, send stay requests, and afterwards leave public references for one another. Reputation systems, vouching, and identity verification substitute for the trust that payment and contracts give commercial lodging. Researchers describe the networks as exemplars of the sharing economy and of gift exchange, while noting safety concerns and controversies — including Couchsurfing's conversion to a for-profit company in 2011 and its introduction of a mandatory membership fee in 2020, which drove growth of non-profit alternatives.
