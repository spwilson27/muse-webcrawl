Macintosh 128K

The Macintosh 128K, originally released as the Apple Macintosh, is the original Apple Macintosh personal computer. It was introduced in January 1984 and sold for US$2,495. Its beige case consists of a 9-inch (23 cm) CRT monitor and comes with a keyboard and mouse.

Overview

The Macintosh was launched with the famous "1984" television advertisement directed by Ridley Scott, aired during Super Bowl XVIII. It was the first mass-market personal computer featuring a graphical user interface and built-in display screen, making it more accessible to non-technical users than the command-line systems then dominant. It used a Motorola 68000 microprocessor running at 7.8 MHz, had 128 KB of RAM (hence the later name "128K"), and a 3.5-inch floppy disk drive. It shipped with MacPaint and MacWrite, which demonstrated its graphical capabilities.

Legacy

The Macintosh line became one of the most successful personal computer families in history; the 128K was succeeded by the Macintosh 512K later in 1984.
