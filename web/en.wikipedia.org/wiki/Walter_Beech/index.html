Walter Beech

Walter Herschel Beech (January 30, 1891 – November 29, 1950) was an American aviator and aviation entrepreneur. He was the co-founder of the Beech Aircraft Corporation (now Beechcraft) in Wichita, Kansas, and one of the formative figures in American general aviation.

Early life and career

Born in Pulaski, Tennessee, Beech built and flew a glider at age 14. He served as a pilot and instructor in the U.S. Army during the First World War, then became a test pilot and barnstormer. In the 1920s he joined the Swallow Airplane Company and then the Travel Air Manufacturing Company in Wichita, winning prominent air races, including the 1926 Ford Reliability Tour. In 1929 he set a record by winning the National Air Races in a Travel Air.

Beech Aircraft

In 1932, in the depth of the Great Depression, Beech and his wife Olive Ann Beech founded the Beech Aircraft Corporation. Their first product, the Model 17 Staggerwing, was a fast, luxurious biplane that became a classic of executive aviation. During the Second World War, Beech built thousands of training and transport aircraft, including the AT-7 Navigator and the C-45 Expeditor. In 1947 the company introduced the Bonanza, a single-engine business aircraft that remains in production in evolved form. After Walter Beech's death in 1950, Olive Ann Beech led the company for more than three decades.
