David Lean

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: David Lean directing on location in Jordan for Lawrence of Arabia (1962), squinting across the desert light he waited hours to capture]

Sir David Lean (25 March 1908 – 16 April 1991) was an English film director, producer, screenwriter and editor, celebrated for epics of landscape and longing: The Bridge on the River Kwai (1957), Lawrence of Arabia (1962), Doctor Zhivago (1965) and A Passage to India (1984) won him two Best Director Academy Awards and places at the summit of British cinema, while his early intimate films — Brief Encounter (1945) above all — are just as revered.

Lean began as a tea boy at Gaumont, became Britain's best editor, co-directed with Noël Coward (In Which We Serve, 1942), and directed the great Dickens adaptations Great Expectations (1946) and Oliver Twist (1948).

The epic years

From Kwai onward Lean worked in 70mm on three continents with vast crews and an editor's patience: Lawrence of Arabia, made in Jordanian and Spanish deserts with Peter O'Toole's debut in the title role, tops the American Film Institute's epic list and most directors' private canons (Spielberg calls it a miracle and studied it shot by shot). Doctor Zhivago, though critics carped, became one of the highest-grossing films ever made.

Late career

The failure of Ryan's Daughter (1970) and hostile notices silenced Lean for fourteen years; A Passage to India (1984), his Forster adaptation, restored him, and he died preparing Conrad's Nostromo. Knighthood came in 1984. His perfectionism — waiting days for a cloud — is film-set legend, and his influence on the epic form, from Spielberg to Denis Villeneuve, is direct and acknowledged.
