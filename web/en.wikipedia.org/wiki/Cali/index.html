Santiago de Cali, usually known simply as Cali (Spanish pronunciation: [ˈkali]), is the capital of the Valle del Cauca department and the most populous city in south-western Colombia, with a metropolitan population of roughly 2.8 million. It is Colombia's third-largest city and a major economic, industrial and cultural centre, located in the Cauca Valley between the Western and Central ranges of the Colombian Andes at about 1,000 metres above sea level.

History

Cali was founded on 25 July 1536 by the Spanish conquistador Sebastián de Belalcázar, making it one of the oldest European-founded cities in the Americas. It remained a modest provincial town through the colonial era and into the 19th century, before rapid growth in the 20th century driven by sugar cane agriculture, industry, and completion of the railway and road links to the Pacific port of Buenaventura. The city hosted the 1971 Pan American Games, which spurred major infrastructure development. From the 1980s Cali was also associated with the Cali Cartel, at its height one of the world's most powerful drug-trafficking organisations, whose dismantling in the mid-1990s reshaped the city's recent history.

Culture

Cali is widely known as the “world capital of salsa”, with a distinctive fast-footwork salsa style, numerous dance schools and clubs, and the annual Feria de Cali festival held between Christmas and New Year. Landmarks include the Cristo Rey statue overlooking the city, the colonial church of La Ermita, the San Antonio neighbourhood, and the Cali Zoo, regarded as one of the best in Latin America. The city is home to two major football clubs, América de Cali and Deportivo Cali, who contest the Valle del Cauca derby at the Estadio Olímpico Pascual Guerrero and Deportivo Cali's Palmaseca stadium.
