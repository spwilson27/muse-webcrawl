Erich Moritz von Hornbostel
Born: 25 February 1877, Vienna, Austria-Hungary
Died: 28 November 1935 (aged 58), Cambridge, England
Fields: Ethnomusicology, musicology
Known for: Hornbostel–Sachs instrument classification

Erich Moritz von Hornbostel (25 February 1877 – 28 November 1935) was an Austrian ethnomusicologist and musicologist, regarded as a founder of comparative musicology (the field now called ethnomusicology). He is best known, together with Curt Sachs, for the Hornbostel–Sachs system of musical instrument classification, published in 1914 and still the most widely used scheme of its kind.

Life and work

Born in Vienna, Hornbostel studied chemistry and natural science before turning to music psychology under Carl Stumpf in Berlin, where he became director of the Berlin Phonogramm-Archiv, an archive of early commercial and field recordings of music from around the world. He analysed recordings of music from many cultures, published pioneering studies of non-European tuning systems and melody — including work on the music of Indigenous peoples of North America and of Thailand and Japan — and argued against the assumption that European tonality was a universal standard.

Hornbostel–Sachs classification

In "Systematik der Musikinstrumente" (1914), Hornbostel and Sachs extended Victor-Charles Mahillon's scheme, dividing instruments according to the material that vibrates to produce sound: idiophones (the instrument itself vibrates), membranophones (a membrane vibrates), chordophones (strings vibrate), and aerophones (air vibrates), with electrophones added later. The decimal subdivision system, modelled on the Dewey Decimal Classification, remains standard in organology and museum cataloguing.

Later life

Of partly Jewish background, Hornbostel was forced out of his Berlin positions when the Nazis came to power in 1933; he emigrated via Switzerland and New York to Cambridge, England, where he died in 1935.
