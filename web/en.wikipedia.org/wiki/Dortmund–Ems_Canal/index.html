Dortmund–Ems Canal

The Dortmund–Ems Canal (German: Dortmund-Ems-Kanal) is a 269-kilometre (167 mi) long canal in Germany between the inland port of Dortmund and the seaport of Emden. The artificial waterway was opened in 1899 to reduce the dependence of the Ruhr industrial region on foreign ports and railways for the transport of coal and steel. At the northern end, the canal connects the port of Emden on the Ems estuary with the German inland waterway network; near Datteln it links with the Rhine–Herne Canal, the Wesel–Datteln Canal, and the Datteln–Hamm Canal.

Features

A notable structure on the canal is the Henrichenburg boat lift, first opened in 1899 and later replaced by a modern ship lift. The canal passes through Münster and crosses the Ems river on an aqueduct near Greven. It remains an important freight route for bulk goods serving the eastern Ruhr.
