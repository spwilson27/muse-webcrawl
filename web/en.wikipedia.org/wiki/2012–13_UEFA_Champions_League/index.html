The 2012–13 UEFA Champions League was the 58th season of Europe's premier club football tournament organised by UEFA, and the 21st since it was renamed the UEFA Champions League. Bayern Munich won the competition for the fifth time, defeating fellow German club Borussia Dortmund 2–1 in the first all-German final, held at Wembley Stadium in London on 25 May 2013. Arjen Robben scored the winning goal in the 89th minute.

Road to the final

Bayern, runners-up the previous season, dominated their semi-final against Barcelona, winning 7–0 on aggregate, while Dortmund overcame Real Madrid 4–3 on aggregate, inspired by Robert Lewandowski's four goals in the first leg. In the final, Mario Mandžukić gave Bayern the lead on the hour, İlkay Gündoğan equalised from the penalty spot, and Robben — who had missed a penalty in the 2012 final — decided the match late on. The victory completed a treble for Jupp Heynckes's Bayern, who also won the Bundesliga and the DFB-Pokal.

Group stage and knockout rounds

Thirty-two teams contested the group stage, including debutants Montpellier, Málaga and Nordsjælland; Málaga reached the quarter-finals in their first campaign. Defending champions Chelsea became the first holders to be eliminated in the group stage, dropping into the Europa League, which they went on to win. Cristiano Ronaldo finished as top scorer with 12 goals.
