Tiffany & Co. (colloquially Tiffany's) is an American luxury jewellery and specialty design house headquartered on Fifth Avenue in Manhattan, New York City. It sells jewellery, sterling silver, china, crystal, stationery, fragrances, watches, and personal accessories.

History

The company was founded in 1837 by Charles Lewis Tiffany and John B. Young as a stationery and fancy goods store in New York. Tiffany's reputation grew through its silverware, its design of the Great Seal of the United States still used on the one-dollar bill, and its jewellery for wealthy clients including the families of American presidents. The firm's flagship store, opened in 1940, was immortalized in the 1961 film Breakfast at Tiffany's.

Brand

Tiffany is strongly identified with its trademarked robin's-egg blue colour, used on its packaging since the 19th century, and with diamond engagement rings, notably the Tiffany Setting introduced in 1886. In 2021 the company was acquired by the French luxury group LVMH in a deal worth about US$15.8 billion.
