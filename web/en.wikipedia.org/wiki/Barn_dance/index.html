Barn dance

A barn dance is a social dance event traditionally held in a barn, featuring folk or country music and dances such as square dances, contra dances, reels, and circle dances directed by a caller. Barn dances were a staple of rural social life in North America and the British Isles — in Scotland and Ireland the related institution is the cèilidh.

History and format

In nineteenth-century farming communities, barn dances celebrated barn-raisings, harvests, and weddings, with fiddlers providing music. A caller announces the figures so that beginners can join without rehearsal; partners frequently change, making the event strongly communal. The term also names a specific progressive couple dance, "The Barn Dance", popularised in the 1890s.

Modern usage

Today barn dances are held as weddings, fundraisers, and community events, often with live folk bands. The phrase became widely known in Britain through radio and later television programmes evoking rural dance traditions.
