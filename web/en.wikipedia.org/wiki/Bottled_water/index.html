Bottled water

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bottled water is usually packaged in PET plastic or glass bottles (image placeholder)

Bottled water is drinking water (e.g. well water, distilled water, deionised water, mineral water or spring water) packaged in plastic or glass water bottles. It may be carbonated or not, and ranges in size from small single-serving bottles to large carboys for water coolers. Global sales reached several hundred billion litres annually by the 2020s.

Types

Regulators distinguish categories such as spring water, drawn from an underground formation to the surface; mineral water, containing a characteristic level of dissolved minerals and protected from contamination; purified water, produced by distillation, deionisation or reverse osmosis; and sparkling water, containing carbon dioxide. In the United States the Food and Drug Administration regulates bottled water as a packaged food, while municipal tap water is regulated by the Environmental Protection Agency.

History

Water was bottled and sold at spas from the 17th and 18th centuries — early examples include the waters of Évian, San Pellegrino and Saratoga Springs — initially for perceived health benefits. Large-scale commercial growth came in the late 20th century with cheap PET bottles and brands such as Perrier, Evian, Poland Spring, Dasani and Aquafina; the latter two are purified municipal water.

Environmental concerns

Bottled water is criticised for the energy and water used in production, greenhouse-gas emissions from transport, and plastic waste: most single-use bottles are not recycled, and the product typically costs hundreds to thousands of times more per litre than tap water. Several cities, universities and events have restricted its sale, and some jurisdictions have banned extraction or sales outright. Defenders cite convenience, taste, emergency relief and safety where tap water is contaminated.

Health

Bottled water generally contains no fluoride, which is added to many municipal supplies for dental health, and quality standards and testing frequency differ between bottled and tap regimes in ways that remain debated.
