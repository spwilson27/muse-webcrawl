HNLMS De Ruyter (Dutch: Hr.Ms. De Ruyter) was a light cruiser of the Royal Netherlands Navy, named after the seventeenth-century admiral Michiel de Ruyter. Designed under tight budgetary limits during the Depression as the intended flagship of the fleet in the Dutch East Indies, she was a unique ship of about 6,450 tons, armed with seven 150 mm (5.9 in) guns and only lightly armoured.

Design

Built by Wilton-Fijenoord at Schiedam, De Ruyter sacrificed armour and a fourth turret position to cost constraints; her main armament was mounted in three twin turrets and one single mount, and she carried a single Fokker C.XI-W floatplane. She commissioned in October 1936 and sailed for the East Indies the following year.

Second World War

After the Japanese invasion of the Dutch East Indies in early 1942, De Ruyter became flagship of Rear-Admiral Karel Doorman's Combined Striking Force of the American-British-Dutch-Australian (ABDA) Command. She fought in the battles of Balikpapan's approaches, Badung Strait (19–20 February 1942) and the Java Sea.

At the Battle of the Java Sea on 27 February 1942, the Allied force engaged a Japanese invasion convoy's escorts. During the night action, De Ruyter was struck by a Type 93 "Long Lance" torpedo from the heavy cruiser Haguro at about 23:30; she sank in the early hours of 28 February with the loss of 367 men, including Admiral Doorman and her captain, Eugène Lacomblé. The wreck, located in 2002, was declared a war grave; surveys in 2016 found the wreck had been largely removed by illegal salvage.
