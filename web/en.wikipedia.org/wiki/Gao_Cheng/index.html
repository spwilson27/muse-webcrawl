Gao Cheng

Gao Cheng (521–549), courtesy name Zihui, formally Prince Wenxiang of Bohai, was the eldest son of Gao Huan, the paramount general and de facto ruler of the Chinese Eastern Wei dynasty. As his father's chief deputy and eventual successor, Gao Cheng consolidated the Gao family's control of the Eastern Wei court, though his arrogance and violent temper made him many enemies. He was assassinated in 549 before he could usurp the throne himself; his younger brother Gao Yang completed the transition, founding the Northern Qi dynasty in 550.

Life

Born in 521, Gao Cheng was made his father's heir and rose quickly through military and civil office. After Gao Huan's death in 547, Gao Cheng took over the regency, famously humiliating the puppet Emperor Xiaojing and executing members of the imperial clan who plotted against him. His administration combined harsh discipline with able officials such as Cui Xian, and his reform of the legal code influenced later dynasties.

Death and legacy

In 549, while plotting to seize the throne, Gao Cheng was stabbed to death by his cook Lan Jing, a former prisoner of war he had repeatedly insulted. His brother Gao Yang suppressed news of the death, purged the conspirators, and deposed the Eastern Wei emperor the following year.
