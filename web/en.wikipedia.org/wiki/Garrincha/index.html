Manuel Francisco dos Santos (28 October 1933 – 20 January 1983), nicknamed Garrincha ("the little bird"), was a Brazilian professional footballer who played as a right winger. Regarded as one of the greatest players of all time and perhaps the greatest dribbler in football history, he won the FIFA World Cup with Brazil in 1958 and 1962, and was the outstanding player of the 1962 tournament after Pelé was injured.

Early life

Garrincha was born in Pau Grande, Rio de Janeiro state, into poverty. He had congenital deformities: his spine was warped, his right leg bent inward and his left leg was six centimetres shorter and curved outward — legs that nonetheless gave him an uncanny, unpredictable dribbling style. His nickname came from a small wren his siblings thought he resembled.

Club career

He spent almost his entire career with Botafogo of Rio de Janeiro, playing 612 games and scoring 245 goals between 1953 and 1965, winning the Campeonato Carioca three times. His twisting runs made him a beloved idol, the Alegria do Povo — "Joy of the People". After leaving Botafogo his career declined through spells at Corinthians, Portuguesa, Flamengo and Olaria, amid mounting alcoholism.

International career

Garrincha earned 50 caps for Brazil and lost only one match in national colours — his very last, against Hungary at the 1966 World Cup. At the 1958 World Cup in Sweden, he and Pelé were introduced in the third match against the USSR and electrified the tournament. In Chile in 1962, with Pelé injured, Garrincha carried Brazil to the title, finishing as joint top scorer and winning the Golden Ball; he remains the only player to win the World Cup's Golden Ball, Golden Boot and the World Cup itself at one tournament.

Later life and legacy

Garrincha's later years were marked by alcoholism and poverty; he died in Rio de Janeiro in 1983, aged 49, of cirrhosis. Hundreds of thousands attended his funeral procession. The national stadium in Brasília, the Estádio Nacional Mané Garrincha, is named after him, and he is routinely ranked among the ten greatest footballers of the 20th century.
