King's Men (playing company)

The King's Men was the acting company to which William Shakespeare belonged for most of his career. Formerly the Lord Chamberlain's Men, the company received a royal patent from James I in 1603 and took its new name.

History

The company performed at the Globe and Blackfriars theatres and at court. Leading members included Richard Burbage, John Heminges and Henry Condell, who after Shakespeare's death edited the First Folio of his plays (1623). The company dissolved when the London theatres were closed in 1642.
