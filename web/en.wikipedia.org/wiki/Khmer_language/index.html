Khmer (ខ្មែរ, khmê), also known as Cambodian, is the official and national language of Cambodia, spoken by the Khmer people and by Khmer minorities in southern Vietnam and north-eastern Thailand. It is the second most widely spoken Austroasiatic language after Vietnamese, with around 16–17 million speakers, and is notable for its large phonemic inventory and its abugida writing system.

History

Khmer is one of the earliest recorded languages of mainland Southeast Asia: inscriptions in Old Khmer date from the 7th century CE, from the era of the Chenla and later Khmer Empire (Angkor) kingdoms. The language was heavily influenced by Sanskrit and Pali through Hinduism and Buddhism, which supplied much of its learned and religious vocabulary. Middle Khmer (14th–18th centuries) saw major phonological restructuring, including the loss of voicing contrasts in consonants and the development of a complex vowel system.

Phonology and grammar

Modern Khmer is an analytic, non-tonal language — unusual among its neighbours — though it distinguishes a very large set of vowel qualities and diphthongs. Word order is subject–verb–object, and grammar relies on word order and particles rather than inflection. Like other languages of the region, Khmer uses numeral classifiers and elaborate registers of speech that vary according to the social status of the listener, particularly in religious and royal contexts.

Script

The Khmer script descends from the Pallava script of southern India via Old Khmer inscriptions. It is an abugida in which each consonant carries an inherent vowel, modified by dependent vowel signs; consonants appear in two series (a-series and o-series) reflecting an earlier voicing distinction. The script has one of the largest alphabets of any language, with 33 consonants, 23 dependent vowels and additional independent vowels and diacritics.

Dialects

Major varieties include Central Khmer (the standard, based on Phnom Penh and the Battambang region), Northern Khmer (Surin Khmer, spoken in north-eastern Thailand), Khmer Krom (spoken in Vietnam's Mekong Delta), and the conservative Cardamom Khmer of the western mountains.
