Gig economy

[Image: a food delivery courier on a bicycle with a branded backpack in city traffic]

The gig economy is a labour market characterised by short-term, freelance or platform-mediated work rather than permanent jobs: ride-hailing and delivery (Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, Deliveroo), task platforms (TaskRabbit), freelance marketplaces (Upwork, Fiverr) and similar arrangements in which an app matches workers to customers, sets or steers prices, and rates performance.

Debates

Platforms classify most gig workers as independent contractors, not employees — the central legal fight of the sector, from California's AB5 law and Proposition 22 to the UK Supreme Court's 2021 ruling that Uber drivers are 'workers'. Supporters stress flexibility, low entry barriers and consumer convenience; critics cite insecure income, no sick pay or pension, algorithmic Management and misclassification shifting risk onto workers. Measuring the gig economy is hard — most participants earn supplementary income a few hours a month — but its model has spread into care, logistics and professional services, making it a test case for the future of labour law in Labour economics.
