Type: Sparkling white wine (DOCG)
Region: Piedmont, Italy
Grape: Moscato Bianco (Muscat Blanc à Petits Grains)
Style: Sweet, lightly sparkling, low alcohol

Asti (formerly Asti Spumante) is a sweet sparkling white wine produced in the province of Asti and neighbouring areas of Piedmont in north-western Italy, made from the Moscato Bianco grape. It holds DOCG status, the highest classification for Italian wines, shared with the still or lightly sparkling Moscato d'Asti.

Production

Asti is made by a single fermentation in pressurised tanks — the Charmat or "Asti method" — halted by chilling and filtration to preserve the grape's aroma and natural sweetness, typically yielding around 7-9.5% alcohol. Moscato d'Asti is only lightly sparkling (frizzante) and sweeter.

History

The wine's modern production dates to the 19th century, when Carlo Gancia applied sparkling-wine methods to Moscato in Canelli. It became hugely popular in the United States in the post-war decades.
