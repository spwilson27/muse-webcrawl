Irenaeus (Greek: Εἰρηναῖος, Eirēnaios; c. 130 – c. 202 AD) was a Greek bishop and theologian, the second bishop of Lugdunum in Gaul (modern Lyon, France). A leading figure of early Christianity, he is best known for his five-volume work Against Heresies (full title On the Detection and Overthrow of the So-Called Gnosis), a systematic refutation of Gnosticism written around 180 AD. In 2022 Pope Francis declared him a Doctor of the Church with the title "Doctor of Unity."

Life

Irenaeus was born in Smyrna in Asia Minor, where in his youth he heard the preaching of Polycarp, himself said to have been a disciple of John the Apostle — a link to the apostolic generation that Irenaeus prized. He came to Gaul as a presbyter of the church at Lugdunum; after Bishop Pothinus died in the persecution of 177, Irenaeus succeeded him. He also intervened, unsuccessfully in the short term, to moderate Pope Victor I's threatened excommunication of the Quartodeciman churches of Asia over the date of Easter.

Theology

Against the Gnostic systems of Valentinus and others, Irenaeus argued for the unity of God as creator of both Testaments, the goodness of creation, and the public "rule of faith" preserved in the churches through apostolic succession — the first clear surviving statement of that doctrine. His theology of recapitulation held that Christ "recapitulates" in himself the whole of human history, succeeding where Adam failed. Against Heresies survives complete only in an early Latin translation, with substantial fragments in Greek and Armenian.
