Boston Consulting Group (BCG) is an American global management consulting firm founded in 1963 by Bruce Henderson. Together with McKinsey & Company and Bain & Company, it is regarded as one of the "Big Three" strategy consulting firms.

History

BCG began as the consulting arm of the Boston Safe Deposit and Trust Company, with Henderson as its sole consultant. It became independent in the 1970s through an employee buyout. Henderson's short "Perspectives" essays, circulated to clients, introduced concepts that shaped strategic management.

Concepts

BCG is known for the experience curve and the growth-share matrix (the "BCG matrix"), which classifies business units as stars, cash cows, question marks, and dogs according to market growth and relative market share. Though simplified, the matrix became a fixture of business education and corporate planning.

Operations

The firm advises corporations, governments, and nonprofits in more than 50 countries, with practices spanning strategy, digital transformation (through BCG Digital Ventures and BCG X), and sustainability. It consistently ranks among the most sought-after employers for business school graduates.
