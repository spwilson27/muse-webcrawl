Paul John Keating (born 18 January 1944) is an Australian former politician who served as the 24th prime minister of Australia from 1991 to 1996, holding office as the leader of the Australian Labor Party (ALP). He previously served as treasurer under Prime Minister Bob Hawke from 1983 to 1991.

Early life

Keating was born in the working-class Sydney suburb of Bankstown to a family of Irish Catholic descent. He left school at fourteen, worked as a clerk, and became involved in the labour movement and the Labor Party's youth organisations. He was elected to the House of Representatives for the seat of Blaxland in 1969, at the age of 25.

Treasurer

Following Labor's victory at the 1983 federal election, Keating became Treasurer in the Hawke government. Working closely with Hawke, he presided over a sweeping programme of economic reform: the floating of the Australian dollar in December 1983, deregulation of the financial system, reductions in tariffs, corporatisation and privatisation of public assets, and the introduction of enterprise bargaining. His warning in 1986 that Australia risked becoming a "banana republic" became one of the most famous phrases in Australian political history, spurring further reform.

Prime Minister

After two leadership challenges, Keating deposed Hawke in December 1991 and became prime minister. His government legislated the Native Title Act 1993 in response to the High Court's Mabo decision, pursued closer engagement with Asia, promoted an Australian republic, and advanced a "big picture" cultural and social agenda. Against most predictions, Keating led Labor to victory at the 1993 election — the "sweetest victory of all" — defeating the Coalition's Fightback! programme. His government was defeated heavily at the 1996 election by John Howard's Coalition.

Later life

Keating resigned from Parliament shortly after the 1996 defeat. He has since worked as a company director and consultant, served on international advisory bodies including for Chinese development banks, and remains a prominent and often controversial commentator on Australian politics, economic policy, and foreign affairs, particularly regarding Australia's relationship with China and the AUKUS security pact, of which he has been a fierce critic.
