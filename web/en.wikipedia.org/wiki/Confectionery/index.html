Confectionery

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A confectioner's display of candies

Confectionery is the art of making confections, or sweet foods. Confections are divided into two broad groups: bakers' confections, which include sweet pastries, cakes, and doughnuts, and sugar confections, which include candies, chewing gum, chocolates, and other principally sugar-based sweets.

Generally speaking, sugar confections are based on sugar or honey and include many types: hard candies, caramels, toffees, fudges, marshmallows, jelly candies, nougats, licorice, marzipan, and chocolate. Confectioners use flavorings, colorings, nuts, fruits, and spices to produce an enormous variety of products.

History

Sweet preparations are ancient: honey-based sweets were made in Egypt, Greece, China, and India long before refined sugar became widely available. Sugar confectionery expanded in medieval Europe through trade with the Islamic world, where sugar craft was highly developed. The Industrial Revolution brought mechanization and mass production, creating famous confectionery companies in the 19th and 20th centuries, and chocolate — transformed by conching and the milk chocolate innovations of the 1870s — became a dominant category.

Production and industry

Modern confectionery is a major global industry, with multinational producers and seasonal peaks around holidays such as Easter, Halloween, and Christmas. Sugar boiling to precise stages (thread, soft ball, hard crack) remains fundamental to texture, and enrobing, panning, and depositing are common industrial processes.
