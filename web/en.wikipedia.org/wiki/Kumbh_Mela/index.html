Kumbh Mela (Hindi: कुम्भ मेला, "festival of the sacred pitcher") is a major pilgrimage and festival in Hinduism, and the largest peaceful gathering of pilgrims in the world. It is celebrated in a cycle of approximately twelve years at four river-bank sites: Prayagraj (at the confluence of the Ganges, Yamuna, and the mythical Sarasvati), Haridwar (Ganges), Nashik (Godavari), and Ujjain (Shipra). Pilgrims gather to bathe in the sacred rivers, believing the waters cleanse them of sins and free them from the cycle of rebirth.

Mythology

The festival is rooted in the Hindu myth of the Samudra Manthana, the churning of the ocean of milk by the gods and demons to obtain amrita, the nectar of immortality. In the struggle over the kumbh (pitcher) containing the nectar, drops are said to have fallen at the four festival sites. The twelve-year cycle corresponds to the twelve divine days of the battle, each equalling a human year.

Types and cycle

The gathering at each site is determined by astrological positions of the Sun, Moon, and Jupiter (Brihaspati). The Maha Kumbh at Prayagraj, held after twelve twelve-year cycles (144 years in tradition), is considered the most significant. The Ardh (half) Kumbh is held midway, every six years, at Prayagraj and Haridwar. The 2013 Prayagraj Kumbh Mela drew an estimated 100–120 million pilgrims over its duration, with some 30 million bathing on the single busiest day, making it the largest gathering of people on Earth. The 2025 Maha Kumbh in Prayagraj was officially reported to have drawn crowds on an even larger scale.

Rituals

The central rite is the shahi snan (royal bath), in which the ascetic orders (akharas) process to the river in grand processions, with Naga sadhus — ash-smeared, naked ascetics — traditionally entering the water first. Other observances include religious discourses, devotional singing, mass feeding of pilgrims and the poor, and debates on doctrine. The festival is administered as a vast temporary city, with pontoon bridges, tented accommodation, sanitation, and hospitals built for the event.

History

River festivals are ancient in India; the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang described a great gathering at Prayaga in the 7th century CE during the reign of King Harsha. The modern Kumbh Mela cycle and mythology took shape over subsequent centuries, and the festival grew enormously under British colonial administration and after independence. In 2017, UNESCO inscribed Kumbh Mela on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
