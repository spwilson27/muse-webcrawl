Elverum is a municipality in Innlandet county, Norway. It is located in the traditional district of Østerdalen. The administrative centre of the municipality is the town of Elverum. Other settlements in the municipality include Heradsbygd, Sørskogbygda, and Jømna. The town is situated at an important crossroads: Hamar lies 30 kilometres (19 mi) to the west, Kongsvinger 73 kilometres (45 mi) to the south, and Trysil 73 kilometres (45 mi) to the northeast. The municipality is bordered by Åmot in the north, Trysil in the east, Våler in the south, and Løten in the west.

History

Elverum was established as a municipality on 1 January 1838 (see formannskapsdistrikt law). During the April 1940 Norwegian campaign of the Second World War, Elverum briefly served as the seat of the Norwegian government. On 9 April 1940, King Haakon VII and the government fled to Elverum as German forces invaded; the Elverum Authorization, passed by the Storting on 9 April, granted the government full powers for the duration of the war. On 11 April 1940, the town centre was bombed and destroyed by the Luftwaffe.

Geography

The Glomma river, Norway's longest, flows through the municipality. Forests dominate the landscape, and forestry has historically been a major industry, reflected in the town's past role as a centre of the timber trade.

Economy and education

Elverum is home to a campus of the Inland Norway University of Applied Sciences (formerly Hedmark University College), and the Norwegian Museum of Forestry (Norsk Skogmuseum) is located in the town. The local football club is Elverum Fotball, and the handball club Elverum Håndball has won multiple Norwegian championships.
