The Great Lakes region of Northern America is a binational Canadian–American region centred on the Great Lakes — Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie, and Ontario — and includes the U.S. states of Illinois, Indiana, Michigan, Minnesota, New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania, and Wisconsin, together with the Canadian province of Ontario.

Economy

The region is one of the world's largest economic areas, with a combined GDP exceeding US$6 trillion. It developed as an industrial heartland — steel, automobiles, chemicals, and machinery — with shipping on the lakes and the St Lawrence Seaway moving iron ore, coal, and grain. Chicago and Toronto are the region's financial centres.

Geography and environment

The lakes contain about 21% of the world's surface freshwater. Joint management through institutions such as the International Joint Commission addresses water quality, levels, and invasive species. The watershed supports extensive agriculture, and winter "lake-effect" snow shapes the climate of downwind shores.
