Renal papilla

Part ofKidney (renal medulla)
SystemUrinary system

The renal papilla is the location where the renal pyramids in the medulla empty urine into the minor calyx in the kidney. Histologically it is marked by medullary collecting ducts converging to form a papillary duct to channel the fluid. Transitional epithelium begins to be seen as the papilla forms.

At the tip (apex) of the papilla, the papillary ducts (ducts of Bellini) open onto a perforated region called the area cribrosa. Urine released here passes into a minor calyx, then into a major calyx, the renal pelvis, and finally the ureter.

Clinical significance

Some chemicals in the kidney can harm the renal papillae, in a process known as renal papillary necrosis. Damage can result from analgesic nephropathy (chronic use of certain painkillers such as NSAIDs), diabetes mellitus, sickle cell disease, and urinary tract obstruction or infection. When portions of the papilla become necrotic and then detach, they can pass into the urine or obstruct the urinary tract, causing pain and possible renal colic. Papillary necrosis can impair the kidney's ability to concentrate urine and, when extensive, contribute to chronic kidney disease.
