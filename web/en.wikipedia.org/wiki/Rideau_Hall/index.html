Rideau Hall is the official residence in Ottawa of both the Canadian monarch and the Governor General of Canada. Located at 1 Sussex Drive in the New Edinburgh neighbourhood, it has been the home of every governor general since Confederation in 1867. The main building contains about 175 rooms across some 9,500 square metres, set in 36 hectares of landscaped grounds.

History

The original villa was built in 1838 by Thomas McKay, a wealthy stonemason and contractor who had worked on the Rideau Canal, from which the house takes its name. The Crown leased the property in 1864 as a residence for Viscount Monck, Governor General of British North America, and purchased it outright in 1868. Successive additions — including the Minto Wing, the Tent Room, and the long entrance facade completed in 1914 — transformed the villa into a stately residence. The ballroom and the Tent Room, with its striped tent-like ceiling, are among its best-known state rooms.

Role and use

Rideau Hall hosts investitures of the Order of Canada, citizenship and awards ceremonies, state dinners, and the swearing-in of prime ministers and cabinets. Foreign heads of state are received here on official visits. The grounds, open to the public year-round, include a Canadian Heritage Garden, the sentry-guarded ceremonial drive, a skating rink in winter, and trees planted by visiting dignitaries.
