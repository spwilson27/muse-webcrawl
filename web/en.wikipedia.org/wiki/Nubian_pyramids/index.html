Nubian pyramids

The Nubian pyramids were built by the rulers of the ancient Kushite kingdoms in present-day Sudan — at El-Kurru, Nuri, Jebel Barkal and Meroë — numbering about 255, more than in Egypt.

Nubian pyramids
Location: Sudan (Nubia)
Built: ~800 BC – AD 350
Main sites: Meroë, Nuri, El-Kurru, Jebel Barkal

Construction

Smaller and more steeply angled than Egyptian pyramids, built of sandstone blocks over royal tombs, often with a mortuary chapel on the eastern face decorated with reliefs. Building began under the Napatan kings who ruled Egypt as the 25th Dynasty.

Sites and damage

Meroë has the largest concentration, in its northern and southern cemeteries. In the 1830s treasure hunter Giuseppe Ferlini demolished the tops of several pyramids searching for gold. The sites are UNESCO World Heritage (Island of Meroe, 2011). See Kingdom of Kush, Meroë.
