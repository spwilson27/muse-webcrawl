Federal State of Austria

The Federal State of Austria (1934–1938) was the Austrofascist one-party state that succeeded the First Austrian Republic.

The Federal State of Austria (German: Bundesstaat Österreich) was a continuation of the First Austrian Republic between 1934 and 1938, the years of the one-party state rule of the Fatherland Front (Austrofascism). The regime's leader was Chancellor Engelbert Dollfuss, and after his assassination in July 1934, Kurt Schuschnigg. The state was formally abolished after the Anschluss, Austria's annexation by Nazi Germany in March 1938.

Establishment

In March 1933, Dollfuss suspended the National Council, exploiting a procedural crisis known as the "self-elimination of Parliament", and began ruling by emergency decree. After the brief Austrian Civil War of February 1934 against the Social Democrats, the regime promulgated the authoritarian May Constitution of 1934, which renamed the republic the "Federal State of Austria" and reorganised it as a corporatist state modelled loosely on Italian Fascism and Catholic social teaching.

The Fatherland Front regime

All political parties were dissolved into the Fatherland Front, presented as a supra-party national movement. The regime suppressed both the Social Democrats and Austrian Nazis, while relying on the paramilitary Heimwehr. An attempted Nazi coup in July 1934 succeeded in murdering Dollfuss but failed to seize power; Schuschnigg succeeded him and tried to preserve Austrian independence through agreements with Italy and, increasingly unsuccessfully, by concessions to Germany, including the July Agreement of 1936.

Anschluss

Under mounting pressure, Schuschnigg announced a plebiscite on Austrian independence in March 1938. Hitler responded with an ultimatum; Schuschnigg resigned, and German troops entered Austria on 12 March 1938. Austria was annexed into the German Reich, ending the Federal State.
