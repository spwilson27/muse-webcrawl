Sokollu Mehmed Pasha

Sokollu Mehmed Pasha (Ottoman Turkish: صقللي محمد پاشا; Serbian: Мехмед-паша Соколовић, Mehmed-paša Sokolović; c. 1505 – 11 October 1579) was an Ottoman statesman of Serb origin, most notable for being the Grand Vizier of the Ottoman Empire for 14 years, from 1565 to 1579, serving under three sultans: Suleiman the Magnificent, Selim II and Murad III. He was arguably the most powerful grand vizier of the empire's classical age.

Early life

Born as Bajica Sokolović into a Serbian Orthodox family near Rudo in Bosnia, he was taken as a youth through the devşirme child levy, converted to Islam and educated in the palace school at Edirne and Istanbul. He rose through naval and provincial commands, becoming Kapudan Pasha (grand admiral) and then Beylerbey of Rumelia.

Grand Vizierate

Appointed grand vizier in June 1565, he managed the empire through Suleiman's death during the Szigetvár campaign — concealing it until Selim II could secure the throne. Under the indolent Selim he was effectively ruler of the empire. His tenure saw the conquest of Cyprus (1570–71), the Ottoman defeat at Lepanto and the fleet's rapid rebuilding, the conquest of Tunis, and campaigns in Yemen and the Caucasus. He promoted vast public works and trade, planned canals at Suez and between the Don and Volga, and maintained diplomatic correspondence with European powers.

Assassination and legacy

After Selim's death his power waned under Murad III. On 11 October 1579 he was stabbed by a mentally disturbed assassin while receiving petitions. His endowments, designed by the architect Sinan, include mosques in Istanbul and the famed Mehmed Paša Sokolović Bridge in Višegrad — the subject of Ivo Andrić's Nobel-winning novel The Bridge on the Drina.
