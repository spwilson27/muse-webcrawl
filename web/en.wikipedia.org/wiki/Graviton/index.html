Graviton

CompositionElementary particle (hypothetical)
StatusHypothetical
Mass0 (theoretical)
Electric charge0
Spin2

In theories of quantum gravity, the graviton is the hypothetical elementary particle that mediates the force of gravitational interaction. There is no complete quantum field theory of gravitons due to the outstanding mathematical problem of renormalization in general relativity, but the graviton is expected to be a massless spin-2 boson.

If it exists, the graviton must be massless because gravity has an infinite range, and a spin-2 boson because the source of gravitation is the stress–energy tensor, a second-order tensor (compared with electromagnetism's spin-1 photon, sourced by the four-current). In string theory, gravitons emerge as closed-string vibrational modes, and they are a natural prediction of the theory.

Detection

Unambiguous detection of individual gravitons is considered practically impossible: the coupling between gravitons and matter is so weak that any conceivable detector would be overwhelmed by noise. Gravitational-wave detectors such as LIGO detect coherent waves composed of enormous numbers of gravitons rather than single quanta. Experimental bounds place the graviton mass below roughly 10−22 eV/c².
