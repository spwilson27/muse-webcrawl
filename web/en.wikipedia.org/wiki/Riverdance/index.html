Riverdance is a theatrical show consisting mainly of traditional Irish music and dance. With music composed by Bill Whelan, it originated as a seven-minute interval performance during the Eurovision Song Contest 1994 in Dublin and was expanded into a full-length show which opened at the Point Theatre, Dublin, on 9 February 1995. The show propelled Irish dancing to worldwide fame and has been seen live by tens of millions of people.

Origins

The interval act was commissioned by producer Moya Doherty and directed by John McColgan, with choreography by Michael Flatley and Jean Butler and choral music by the choir Anúna. Broadcast to an estimated 300 million viewers, the seven-minute piece, "Riverdance", received a standing ovation and was released as a single that entered the Irish charts at number one. Whelan's soundtrack album won the Grammy Award for Best Musical Show Album in 1997.

The show

The full show presents a loose celebration of Irish culture and the Irish diaspora, organised around the power of nature and the river of its title. Its numbers include "Reel Around the Sun", "The Countess Cathleen", "Thunderstorm" (an a cappella hard-shoe dance), "Trading Taps" — contrasting Irish dance with African-American tap — "Russian Dervish", flamenco elements, and the massed ensemble finale "Heart's Cry" and "Riverdance" itself, with its celebrated line of dancers advancing in unison.

Flatley departed the production in 1995 after a dispute and created Lord of the Dance; Colin Dunne replaced him as principal male dancer alongside Butler. Multiple touring companies have taken Riverdance to North America, Europe, Asia and Australia; a 25th-anniversary production toured from 2020, and an animated film, Riverdance: The Animated Adventure, was released in 2021.

Impact

Riverdance transformed Irish dance from a competitive and cultural niche into a global entertainment industry, boosting enrolment in dance schools worldwide and paving the way for shows such as Lord of the Dance and Feet of Flames.
