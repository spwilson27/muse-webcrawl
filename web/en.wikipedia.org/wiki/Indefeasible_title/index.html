Indefeasible title


Land title registration

Indefeasibility of title is a central principle of the Torrens system of land registration used in Australia, New Zealand, Canada, Singapore, Malaysia, and other jurisdictions. Under the principle, the person registered as proprietor of land holds title that is immune from challenge based on earlier defects, fraud by predecessors, or unregistered claims, subject to limited statutory exceptions.

Operation

The register is said to be a "mirror" of title: a purchaser may rely on the register alone without investigating the chain of historical transactions. Indefeasibility is generally "immediate" in most Australian jurisdictions — even a transferee under a forged instrument may obtain good title upon registration — though some jurisdictions apply "deferred" indefeasibility, protecting only later purchasers in good faith. Exceptions commonly include fraud by the registered proprietor, prior registered interests, overriding statutory interests, easements omitted by error, and short-term tenancies.

Rationale and assurance funds

The principle trades certainty of title and cheap conveyancing against the possibility that a true owner may lose land through fraud or error. Torrens systems therefore maintain state assurance (compensation) funds from which persons deprived of their land through the operation of the system may claim compensation.
