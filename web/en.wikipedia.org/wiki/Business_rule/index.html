Business rule

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A business rule is a statement that defines or constrains some aspect of a business, intended to assert business structure or to control or influence the behaviour of the business. Business rules describe the operations, definitions, and constraints that apply to an organisation in achieving its goals — for example, "a customer who places an order over a certain value qualifies for free shipping."

Types

Rules are commonly classified as structural facts (definitions and relationships among business terms), action restrictions (constraints that govern when actions may occur), and derivations (rules by which new facts are inferred from known ones). Policies, regulations, and standards are common sources of business rules.

Management and automation

The business rules approach separates rules from application code so they can be maintained by business analysts. Business rules management systems (BRMS) and rule engines execute rules during business processes, while standards such as Decision Model and Notation (DMN) provide a common notation for modelling decisions and their rules.
