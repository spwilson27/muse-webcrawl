Gibberish

Gibberish resembles language but carries no meaning

Gibberish, also known as jibber-jabber or gobbledygook, is speech or writing that is (or appears to be) nonsense: it may include speech sounds that are not actual words, pseudowords, language games, or specialised jargon that seems unintelligible to the uninformed. Unlike mere mumbling, gibberish often mimics the rhythm, intonation and structure of real language while conveying no meaning.

Uses

Gibberish occurs in many contexts: the babbling of infants learning to talk; improvisational theatre and comedy, where performers may speak fluent-sounding nonsense; scat singing in jazz; religious contexts such as glossolalia ("speaking in tongues"); and deliberately in literature — most famously Lewis Carroll's poem "Jabberwocky", which uses invented words whose grammar makes them partly intelligible. Simlish, the fictional language of The Sims video games, is a designed gibberish used to avoid the cost and repetition of real dialogue.

Etymology

The word gibberish is first recorded in the 16th century and is probably imitative of the sound of unintelligible speech, possibly influenced by jabber. A folk etymology connects it with the 8th-century alchemist Jabir ibn Hayyan (Geber), whose technical writings were said to be incomprehensible, but this is not supported.
