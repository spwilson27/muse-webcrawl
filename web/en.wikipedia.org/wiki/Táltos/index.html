Táltos

The táltos ([ˈtaːltoʃ]) is a figure in Hungarian mythology, a person with supernatural power similar to a shaman. According to most accounts the táltos was traditionally believed to have been chosen by the gods or spirits before birth or in early childhood. Signs of a future táltos included being born with extra bones, such as teeth, an extra finger, or a caul.

Characteristics

A táltos was typically discovered in childhood, often through unusual behaviour, precocious strength, or the physical signs present at birth. The child might be taken away by older táltosok ("táltoses") and initiated into the spiritual world. During initiation the táltos underwent a ritual in which his body was "dismembered" in a trance state and reassembled, an experience common in shamanic traditions worldwide.

The táltos could enter a trance or ecstasy (called révülés) in order to travel to other worlds, heal the sick, predict the weather, find lost objects, or fight other táltosok in the form of animals, flames, or wheels. Battles between táltosok were believed to determine the weather and the abundance of the harvest: storms were explained as the visible trace of such clashes in the sky.

Role in society

Unlike witches (boszorkány), who were feared as malevolent, the táltos was generally regarded as a benevolent helper of the community, though an ambivalent figure whose powers could also be dangerous. The tradition persisted in Hungarian folk belief into the twentieth century, and trials of táltosok were recorded in the early modern period.
