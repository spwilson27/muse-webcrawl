Jones calculus

Jones calculus, invented by R. Clark Jones in 1941, is a mathematical formalism describing fully polarised light. The polarisation state is represented by a two-component complex Jones vector, and each optical element (polariser, waveplate, mirror) by a 2×2 complex Jones matrix; the effect of a system is found by multiplying the matrices acting on the input vector.

Jones calculus handles interference between polarisation components but only applies to completely polarised, coherent light; partially polarised light requires the more general Mueller calculus. It is widely used in polarimetry, liquid-crystal display design and fibre optics.
