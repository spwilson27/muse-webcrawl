The National Register of Historic Places (NRHP) is the official list of the United States federal government of districts, sites, buildings, structures, and objects deemed worthy of preservation for their historical significance. Established by the National Historic Preservation Act of 1966 and administered by the National Park Service, the Register contains more than 98,000 separate listings, representing well over a million individual resources when historic districts are counted.

Criteria

Properties are generally at least 50 years old and must meet one or more of four criteria: association with events that made a significant contribution to broad patterns of history; association with significant persons; distinctive characteristics of a type, period, or method of construction, or the work of a master; or the potential to yield important information in history or prehistory. Cemeteries, religious properties, moved structures, and reconstructed buildings are ordinarily excluded unless they meet special requirements.

Effects of listing

Listing is largely honorary and does not, by itself, restrict what private owners may do with a property. However, listed properties may qualify for federal historic preservation tax incentives, grants (when available), and consideration in the planning of federal or federally assisted projects under Section 106 of the NHPA. Many states and municipalities maintain their own registers and stronger local protections.

Process

Nominations are usually prepared by State Historic Preservation Offices or Federal Preservation Officers and reviewed at the state level before forwarding to the Keeper of the National Register. Roughly 30,000 properties are added in a typical decade, and about 1,200 per year in recent times.
