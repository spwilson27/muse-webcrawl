An obligation is a legal bond (vinculum iuris) by which one person, the obligor or debtor, is bound to render a performance — to give something, to do something, or to refrain from doing something — in favour of another, the obligee or creditor, who has a corresponding right to demand that performance. The law of obligations is a central branch of private law in civil law systems, encompassing contract, delict (tort), and unjust enrichment.

History

The concept derives from Roman law. Justinian's Institutes define obligatio as "a legal bond whereby we are constrained, by the law of our state, to render something." Roman jurists classified obligations by their source — contract, quasi-contract, delict and quasi-delict — a scheme still echoed in modern civil codes such as the French Code civil and the German Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch.

Sources of obligations

  • Contract — obligations voluntarily assumed by agreement.
  • Delict / tort — obligations to compensate for wrongful harm.
  • Unjust enrichment — obligations to restore a benefit obtained at another's expense without legal ground.
  • Statute and other events — obligations imposed directly by law, such as duties of maintenance between relatives.

Elements

Every obligation involves two parties, a determinate or determinable performance, and a legal bond enforceable by action. The creditor's side of the relationship is an asset (a claim or chose in action); the debtor's side is a liability. Obligations are relative rights: they bind only the parties, unlike property rights, which are effective against the world.

Performance and breach

An obligation is normally discharged by performance in accordance with its terms. Failure to perform — delay, defective performance or non-performance — gives the creditor remedies which may include enforced performance, damages, or termination of the contract. In common law systems the equivalent doctrines are distributed between contract and tort and no single general part of "obligations" is codified, though scholars increasingly analyse the two together.

Extinction

Obligations are extinguished by performance, release, set-off, novation, impossibility of performance, merger of the positions of creditor and debtor, and prescription (the expiry of the limitation period).
