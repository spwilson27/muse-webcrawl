Carex

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Plantae
Order:Poales
Family:Cyperaceae
Genus:Carex L.

Carex is a vast genus of grass-like plants in the family Cyperaceae, commonly known as sedges (particularly the "true sedges"). With roughly 2,000 accepted species, it is one of the largest genera of flowering plants. Sedges in this genus occur on all continents except Antarctica and are especially diverse in temperate wetlands, forests and tundra of the Northern Hemisphere.

Description

Like other sedges, Carex species typically have triangular stems ("sedges have edges"), with leaves arranged in three ranks. The flowers are small and wind-pollinated, borne in spikes; each female flower is enclosed in a flask-shaped bract called a perigynium, a characteristic feature of the genus used in identification. The fruit is a lens-shaped or three-sided achene.

Ecology and uses

Carex species are ecologically important in wetlands, where they stabilise soils and provide food and cover for wildlife. Some, such as Carex morrowii and Carex oshimensis, are widely grown as ornamental ground cover; others are used in ecological restoration and stormwater plantings.
