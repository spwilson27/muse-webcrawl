Climate of Japan

The climate of Japan is predominantly temperate, but varies greatly from north to south. The archipelago stretches over some 3,000 kilometres, from the subarctic island of Hokkaido to the subtropical Ryukyu Islands, and its mountainous spine divides the country into a Pacific side and a Sea of Japan side with contrasting seasons.

[Image: Heavy snow in the "snow country" along the Sea of Japan coast]

Regions

Hokkaido has cold winters with abundant snow and cool summers. Northern Honshu (Tohoku) is similar but milder. Central and southern Honshu, Shikoku, and Kyushu have hot, humid summers and mild winters with little snow on the Pacific side. The Sea of Japan coast is one of the world's snowiest inhabited regions, as cold Siberian air picks up moisture crossing the sea. Okinawa and the Ryukyu Islands are subtropical, with warm winters and frequent typhoons.

Seasons and rainfall

Most of Japan has a rainy season (tsuyu or baiu) in early summer, brought by the seasonal monsoon front, followed by hot humid weather and a typhoon season peaking in August and September. Annual precipitation averages about 1,700 mm, roughly double the global land average, though it is unevenly distributed through the year.

Extremes

Japan's highest recorded temperature is 41.1 °C, reached at Kumagaya and Hamamatsu; the lowest is −41.0 °C at Asahikawa, Hokkaido. Mount Fuji's summit has an alpine climate with sub-zero average temperatures for much of the year.

Climate change

Japan has warmed by more than 1 °C over the past century, with more days of extreme heat, heavier short-duration rainfall, and shifting cherry-blossom flowering dates, which now occur earlier than in historical records.
