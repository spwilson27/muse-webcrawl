Kingdom of Bavaria

The Kingdom of Bavaria (German: Königreich Bayern) was a German state that existed from 1806 to 1918. It succeeded the Electoral Palatinate and the Electorate of Bavaria and was ruled by the House of Wittelsbach. Its capital was Munich.

History

The kingdom was proclaimed on 1 January 1806 when Elector Maximilian IV Joseph, an ally of Napoleon, assumed the title King Maximilian I Joseph following the Treaty of Pressburg and the dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire. Bavaria gained and lost territories in the Napoleonic reorganisation, switched sides in time to join the allies in 1813, and emerged from the Congress of Vienna as the third-largest German state.

Under Ludwig I, Munich became a centre of art and architecture; his abdication followed the revolutions of 1848. His grandson Ludwig II, the "Fairy Tale King", is famous for the castles of Neuschwanstein, Linderhof and Herrenchiemsee and for his patronage of Richard Wagner. After the Austro-Prussian War of 1866, Bavaria allied with Prussia and joined the German Empire in 1871, retaining certain reserved rights including its own army, railways and postal service in peacetime.

End

The kingdom ended in November 1918 amid defeat in the First World War: Ludwig III fled Munich and was deposed as Kurt Eisner proclaimed the People's State of Bavaria during the German revolution — the first German monarchy to fall. Bavaria continued as a constituent state (Free State) of the Weimar Republic.
