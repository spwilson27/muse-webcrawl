Stratigraphy is a branch of geology concerned with the study of rock layers (strata) and layering (stratification). It is primarily used in the study of sedimentary and layered volcanic rocks, and it underpins the construction of the geologic time scale. Stratigraphic principles are also used in archaeology to date layers of human occupation.

Principles

Nicolas Steno formulated the foundational laws in the 17th century: the law of superposition (in an undisturbed sequence, lower layers are older), the principle of original horizontality, and the principle of lateral continuity. William Smith later showed that strata could be identified and correlated across distances by their characteristic fossils — the principle of faunal succession.

Branches

Lithostratigraphy classifies rock bodies by physical properties; biostratigraphy uses fossil assemblages; chronostratigraphy relates strata to geologic time; and sequence stratigraphy interprets depositional patterns controlled by sea-level change. Radiometric dating and magnetostratigraphy provide numerical ages for stratigraphic boundaries.
