Oktoberfest (German pronunciation: [ɔkˈtoːbɐˌfɛst]; Bavarian: Wiesn, d'Wiesn) is the world's largest Volksfest, featuring a beer festival and a travelling carnival. It is held annually in Munich, Bavaria, from mid- or late-September to the first Sunday in October, with more than six million international and national visitors attending the event. Locally, it is called d'Wiesn, after the colloquial name for the fairgrounds, Theresienwiese.

History

The festival began on 12 October 1810, when Crown Prince Ludwig of Bavaria married Princess Therese of Saxe-Hildburghausen and the citizens of Munich were invited to celebrate on the fields — named Theresienwiese in the bride's honour — in front of the city gates. Horse races closed the festivities and were repeated the following year, starting the tradition. Agricultural shows, carnival booths and eventually beer stands followed; by the late nineteenth century the small stands had grown into vast brewer-run tents.

The festival today

Only beer brewed within Munich's city limits by its six great breweries — Augustiner, Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu, Löwenbräu, Paulaner and Spaten — and conforming to the Reinheitsgebot may be served. It is a specially brewed Märzen-style Oktoberfest beer, around 5.8–6.3% alcohol, served by the litre in glass Maß steins. The festival opens when the mayor taps the first keg at noon and cries "O'zapft is!" ("It's tapped!"). Costume and riflemen's parades, fairground rides, and foods such as roast chicken (Hendl), pork knuckle, giant pretzels and Steckerlfisch complete the event. Despite its name, most of Oktoberfest takes place in September; it has been cancelled only some two dozen times in its history — for cholera, wars, hyperinflation, and in 2020–21 for the COVID-19 pandemic.

Impact

Oktoberfest contributes well over a billion euros to Munich's economy in a typical year and has inspired imitations worldwide, the largest outside Germany being in Kitchener-Waterloo, Canada, and Blumenau, Brazil.
