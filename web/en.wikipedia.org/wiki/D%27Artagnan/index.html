Charles d'Artagnan is a fictional character created by French novelist Alexandre Dumas, first published in the novel The Three Musketeers (1844). He is a young man from Gascony who leaves home to travel to Paris in the hope of joining the Musketeers of the Guard, and befriends the three inseparable musketeers Athos, Porthos and Aramis — "the three musketeers" of the title.

Dumas based the character loosely on the life of Charles de Batz de Castelmore d'Artagnan (c. 1611–1673), a real captain of the Musketeers who served Louis XIV and died at the Siege of Maastricht in 1673. Dumas drew on the semi-fictionalised memoirs published by Gatien de Courtilz de Sandras.

In the novels

D'Artagnan is the protagonist of Dumas's d'Artagnan Romances: The Three Musketeers, Twenty Years After and The Vicomte of Bragelonne: Ten Years Later, the last of which includes the episode of the Man in the Iron Mask. Across the trilogy he rises from an impoverished Gascon cadet to marshal's rank through loyalty, courage and intrigue.

Portrayals

The character has been played on screen by many actors, including Gene Kelly, Michael York, Chris O'Donnell and Logan Lerman.
