John Edensor Littlewood

John Edensor Littlewood (9 June 1885 – 6 September 1977) was a British mathematician who worked on analysis, number theory, and dynamical systems. He is best known for his long collaboration with G. H. Hardy, for the Hardy–Littlewood conjectures on prime numbers, and for results including the first Hardy–Littlewood tauberian theorem and work on the Riemann zeta function.

Life and career

Littlewood was educated at St Paul's School and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he was Senior Wrangler. He taught at Manchester under Ernest Rutherford's faculty, then returned to Cambridge, becoming Rouse Ball Professor. During the First World War he worked on ballistics for the Royal Garrison Artillery.

Mathematics

With G. H. Hardy, Littlewood developed methods in analytic number theory, partitions, and Fourier series. Their first and second conjectures concern prime pairs and prime counting. He proved that the difference between π(x) and li(x) changes sign infinitely often (related to Skewes's number), collaborated with Mary Cartwright on differential equations leading toward chaos theory, and wrote the popular A Mathematician's Miscellany. The Littlewood–Paley theory and three principles of Littlewood bear his name.
