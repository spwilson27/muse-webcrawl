The Acheron (Ancient Greek: Ἀχέρων, "river of woe") is a river in the region of Epirus in northwestern Greece, some 52 km long, flowing into the Ionian Sea near the village of Ammoudia. In Greek mythology, Acheron was one of the five rivers of the underworld, across which, in later tradition, the ferryman Charon carried the souls of the dead.

The real river

The river rises in the mountains of the Ioannina regional unit and flows southwest through a dramatic gorge, the Gates of Hades of local tradition, before reaching a fertile coastal plain. Part of its course runs underground, which strengthened its association with the underworld. Near the village of Mesopotamos stood the Necromanteion, an ancient sanctuary believed to be an oracle of the dead where visitors sought to communicate with departed souls. The river's cold, clear springs are today popular for rafting and hiking.

In mythology

In Homer's Odyssey, the Acheron is a river of Hades into which flow the Pyriphlegethon and the Cocytus; Odysseus visits its banks to consult the dead. Acheron was also personified as a river god, a son of Oceanus, who was said to have been punished by Zeus for giving water to the Titans during their war with the Olympians. In Dante's Inferno, the Acheron forms the boundary of Hell proper, and Charon ferries damned souls across it.

Other uses

The name has been given to a river in New Zealand's Canterbury region, a lake and river system in Victoria, Australia, and various works of art and music inspired by the river of the dead.
