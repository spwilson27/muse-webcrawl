The Youth Olympic Games (YOG) are an international multi-sport event for athletes between 14 and 18 years old, organised by the International Olympic Committee (IOC). They alternate between summer and winter editions every four years, offset so that a Youth Olympic event is held every two years. The Games combine high-level competition with a Culture and Education Programme intended to teach Olympic values, healthy living and social responsibility.

History

The concept was proposed by IOC president Jacques Rogge and approved in 2007. The first Summer Youth Olympic Games were held in Singapore in 2010, drawing about 3,600 athletes from 204 National Olympic Committees, and the first Winter Youth Olympic Games followed in Innsbruck, Austria, in 2012. Subsequent summer editions were held in Nanjing (2014) and Buenos Aires (2018), while winter editions went to Lillehammer (2016), Lausanne (2020) and Gangwon (2024). The 2026 Summer Youth Olympics were awarded to Dakar, Senegal — the first Olympic event scheduled for Africa — after being postponed from 2022.

Format and features

The sports programme largely mirrors the Olympic Games but with adapted formats, including mixed-gender and mixed-nationality team events, and newer disciplines such as 3x3 basketball, futsal, breakdancing and sport climbing that later featured at the senior Games. The events are deliberately smaller in scale than the Olympics, with host cities encouraged to use existing venues. Beyond competition, athletes take part in workshops, cultural exchanges and mentoring sessions with elite "Athlete Role Models".
