NorNed is a 580-kilometre (360 mi) long high-voltage direct current (HVDC) submarine power cable between Feda in Norway and the seaport of Eemshaven in the Netherlands. When it entered service in 2008 it was the longest subsea power cable in the world. It connects the electricity grids of Norway and the Netherlands, allowing the two countries to trade electricity — in particular, exporting Norwegian hydropower and importing Dutch (and wider continental) power when prices and conditions favour it.

Technical description

The cable has a transmission capacity of 700 MW and operates as a monopolar HVDC link at a voltage of 450 kV, using a mass-impregnated paper-insulated cable with a metallic return conductor. The converter stations at each end convert between direct current in the cable and the alternating current of the respective national grids. The Norwegian terminal is at Feda in Kvinesdal municipality, Agder, and the Dutch terminal is at Eemshaven in Groningen province.

History

The project was developed by the transmission system operators Statnett (Norway) and TenneT (Netherlands) and their joint venture. Construction began in 2006; the cable was laid across the North Sea floor, in places buried for protection. Commercial operation started on 6 May 2008. The total cost was approximately €600 million. The cable held the record for the world's longest subsea power interconnector until the commissioning of longer links such as NordLink (Norway–Germany, 2021) and the North Sea Link (Norway–United Kingdom, 2021).

Operation

Power flows in both directions depending on market prices in the Nordic and continental European electricity markets: typically, Norway exports hydroelectric power during periods of high continental demand and imports when Norwegian reservoir levels are low or wind generation on the continent is abundant. The link has experienced occasional outages, including cable faults requiring marine repair operations lasting several weeks.
