The Cloud of Unknowing

The Cloud of Unknowing (Middle English: The Cloude of Unknowyng) is an anonymous work of Christian mysticism written in Middle English in the latter half of the 14th century. It is a spiritual guide to contemplative prayer, addressed by an older monk to a young student, teaching that God cannot be grasped by the intellect but only approached through love.

Teaching

The author instructs the contemplative to place all created things beneath a "cloud of forgetting" and to reach toward God, who dwells beyond a "cloud of unknowing," with a "sharp dart of longing love." The intellect must be abandoned in favour of a blind, loving impulse toward God. The work belongs to the apophatic, or negative, tradition of theology, drawing on Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite and echoing earlier mystics such as Meister Eckhart. The author also wrote related shorter treatises, including The Book of Privy Counselling.

Influence

The Cloud is a central text of English medieval spirituality, alongside the writings of Julian of Norwich and Walter Hilton. It influenced the modern Centering Prayer movement of the twentieth century, whose practitioners, including Thomas Keating, drew directly on its method. The phrase also lent its title to the English electronic musician Burial's influences and to numerous works of literature and music.
