Josip Jelačić

Josip Jelačić von Bužim (16 October 1801 – 19 May 1859) was a Croatian general and politician who served as Ban (viceroy) of Croatia from 1848 until his death. He is remembered as a Croatian national hero for abolishing serfdom in Croatia and for his campaigns during the Revolutions of 1848.

Early life

Jelačić was born in Petrovaradin into a Croatian noble military family. He was educated at the Theresian Military Academy in Vienna and entered the Austrian imperial army, rising through the ranks while also writing poetry in Croatian.

Ban of Croatia

Appointed Ban in March 1848 amid revolutionary upheaval in the Habsburg monarchy, Jelačić proclaimed the abolition of serfdom in Croatia on 25 April 1848. He broke relations with the revolutionary Hungarian government, which sought to Magyarize Croatia, and cut ties between Croatia and Hungary.

Revolution of 1848

In September 1848 Jelačić led an army into Hungary in support of the Habsburg emperor against the Hungarian revolution. His forces fought at Pákozd and later joined the imperial campaign that retook Vienna from revolutionaries. After the revolution's defeat, Croatia nevertheless came under absolutist rule from Vienna, disappointing many of his supporters.

Legacy

Zagreb's central square, Ban Jelačić Square, bears his name, along with its famous equestrian statue, first erected in 1866, removed in 1947, and restored in 1990. He remains a central figure in Croatian national identity.
