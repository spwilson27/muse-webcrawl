Signing bonus

A signing bonus or sign-on bonus is a sum of money paid to a new employee (including a professional sports person) by a company as an incentive to join that company. They are often given as a way of making a compensation package more attractive to the employee (e.g., if the annual salary is lower than they desire). It also lowers the risk to the company, as it is a one-time payment; for example, if the employee underperforms, the company does not have to continue paying a higher salary. Signing bonuses are often used in professional sports, and to recruit people to jobs where scarce skills are in high demand, such as the technology industry.

In professional sports

Signing bonuses are used in professional sports to lure highly sought-after players to a team. In leagues with salary caps, such as the National Football League (NFL) and the National Basketball Association (NBA), signing bonuses have been used strategically because, in some systems, the bonus can be prorated over the life of the contract for salary-cap purposes, allowing a team to pay a player a large sum while limiting the annual cap impact. In Major League Baseball, top draft picks and international free agents frequently receive large signing bonuses.

Clawback provisions

Employers sometimes include clawback or repayment provisions requiring the employee to return some or all of the bonus if they leave the company within a specified period, commonly one or two years. Such provisions vary in enforceability by jurisdiction.

Taxation

In the United States, signing bonuses are generally treated as supplemental wages for federal income tax purposes and are subject to withholding, as well as Social Security and Medicare taxes.
