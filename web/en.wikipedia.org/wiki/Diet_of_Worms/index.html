The Diet of Worms (German: Reichstag zu Worms) was an imperial diet (assembly) of the Holy Roman Empire held at Worms, Germany, from 28 January to 25 May 1521. Presided over by the newly crowned Emperor Charles V, it is most famous for the appearance of Martin Luther, who was summoned to renounce or reaffirm his writings and refused, and for the resulting Edict of Worms, which declared Luther an outlaw and banned his works. Despite its name, the "diet" was a political assembly, not a meal — though the coincidence of names has made it a staple of historical humour, it concerned no food at all.

Background

Luther's Ninety-five Theses of 1517 had sparked a growing theological and political controversy. After disputations at Heidelberg (1518) and Leipzig (1519), Pope Leo X issued the bull Exsurge Domine (1520) condemning Luther's teachings, and Luther responded by publicly burning the bull. On 3 January 1521 the Pope excommunicated him by the bull Decet Romanum Pontificem. Charles V, needing the support of the German princes but committed to defending the Catholic faith, granted Luther a safe conduct to appear before the Diet and answer for himself.

Luther before the Diet

Luther arrived in Worms on 16 April 1521 to popular acclaim. On 17 April he was shown a table of his books and asked two questions: whether they were his, and whether he would recant their contents. Asking for time to consider, he was granted one day. On 18 April, in a celebrated speech delivered first in German and then in Latin, Luther acknowledged the books as his but refused to recant, arguing that he could not retract unless convinced by Scripture or plain reason, since acting against conscience was neither right nor safe. The speech later became attached to the famous words "Here I stand, I can do no other" (Hier stehe ich, ich kann nicht anders), though this exact phrase does not appear in the earliest records of the proceedings.

Edict of Worms

After private negotiations failed, Charles V announced his determination to proceed against Luther as a notorious heretic. The Edict of Worms, drafted by the papal nuncio Girolamo Aleandro and issued on 25 May 1521, declared Luther an outlaw: his writings were banned, and no one was to give him food or shelter, while anyone was permitted to kill him without legal consequence.

Aftermath

On his journey home Luther was "kidnapped" in a staged ambush arranged by his protector, Frederick the Wise, Elector of Saxony, and hidden in the Wartburg Castle, where he translated the New Testament into German. The Edict was never effectively enforced across the Empire — many princes sympathised with reform or resented imperial and papal authority — and the confrontation at Worms is conventionally seen as a decisive moment in the Protestant Reformation.
