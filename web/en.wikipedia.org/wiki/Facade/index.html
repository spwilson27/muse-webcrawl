Facade

OriginFrom French façade, from Italian facciata ("face")
FieldArchitecture

A facade is generally the front of a building, especially its principal, most architecturally elaborate exterior face, typically containing the main entrance. The term, derived from the French façade, can also refer to any exterior wall of a building.

In architecture

Facades are often the most expressive element of a building's design, communicating its style, period and function through materials, ornament and fenestration. In historic districts, facade alterations are frequently regulated to preserve streetscape character, and "facadism" — retaining only a historic front while rebuilding everything behind it — is a controversial preservation practice.

Metaphorical use

By extension, "facade" denotes a superficial appearance concealing a different reality, as in a person or organisation presenting a false front.
