Freixenet (Catalan pronunciation approximately "fresh-eh-NET") is a Spanish producer of cava and other wines, based in Sant Sadurní d'Anoia in the Penedès region of Catalonia. It is one of the largest producers of traditional-method sparkling wine in the world, best known for its Cordon Negro, sold in a distinctive frosted black bottle.

History

The company grew from the union of two wine families: Francesc Sala, whose family had made wine since 1861, and Dolores Sala Vivé. Their son Pedro Ferrer married into the Sala family, and in 1914 the house released its first sparkling wine under the name Freixenet, derived from "La Freixeneda," the family estate named for its ash trees (freixe in Catalan). The company expanded internationally after the Spanish Civil War and became a global brand in the 1970s and 1980s.

Products and ownership

Freixenet's cavas are made by the traditional (Champagne) method from the Catalan grape varieties Macabeo, Xarel·lo, and Parellada. In 2018 Germany's Henkell & Co. (part of the Oetker group) acquired a majority stake, creating the Henkell Freixenet group, one of the world's largest sparkling wine companies.
