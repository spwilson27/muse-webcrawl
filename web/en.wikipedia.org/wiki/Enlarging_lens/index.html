Enlarging lens

An enlarging lens is a specialised photographic lens designed to be used in an enlarger to project an image of a photographic negative onto photographic paper. Unlike a normal camera lens, it is optimised for close conjugates — a short object distance (the negative) and a longer image distance (the paper) — and for flatness of field across the whole projected area, so that enlargements are sharp from centre to corner.

Characteristics

Enlarging lenses are usually of moderate aperture (f/2.8 to f/5.6) and focal lengths matched to the negative format: about 50 mm for 35 mm film, 75–80 mm for medium format, and 135–150 mm for 4×5 in large format negatives. Better examples use six-element designs (sold under names such as El-Nikkor, Rodagon and Componon) that minimise distortion and chromatic aberration at the small working apertures (typically f/8 or f/11) used for printing. Most mount to the enlarger's lens board with a 39 mm Leica thread.

Use

The lens is set wide open for focusing and composition, then stopped down for the exposure. Because enlarging lenses lack shutters and focusing mounts of their own, they are simple and inexpensive relative to their optical quality, and are sometimes adapted for macro photography and copying work.
