Annealing (metallurgy)

Annealing in metallurgy is a heat treatment in which a material is heated to a suitable temperature, held there, and then cooled at a controlled rate. It alters microstructure and properties such as hardness, ductility, residual stress and electrical conductivity.

Process

For steels, full annealing commonly involves heating into or near the austenite range and cooling slowly in a furnace to produce a soft, coarse pearlitic structure. Process annealing and stress-relief annealing use lower temperatures. In cold-worked metals, annealing permits recovery, recrystallisation and grain growth, restoring ductility lost through strain hardening.

Applications

Annealing is used before machining or forming, after welding, in wire and sheet production, and in the manufacture of copper, aluminium, brass, glass and semiconductor materials. The exact cycle depends on composition, section size and the properties required.
