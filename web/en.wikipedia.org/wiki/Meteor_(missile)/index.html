Meteor missile on a Gripen fighter

The Meteor is a European beyond-visual-range air-to-air missile (BVRAAM) developed by MBDA for the air forces of the United Kingdom, Germany, Italy, Spain, France, and Sweden. It entered service first with the Swedish Air Force's Saab JAS 39 Gripen in 2016. Its defining feature is a throttleable ducted-rocket ramjet engine that sustains propulsion through much of the flight, giving it a large no-escape zone compared with conventional rocket-powered missiles.

Development

The Meteor programme began in the 1990s as the UK-led BVRAAM requirement, with MBDA selected in 2002 and a six-nation contract following. The propulsion system, based on a variable-flow ducted rocket developed by Bayern-Chemie (now part of MBDA), uses a boron-loaded solid fuel whose combustion is sustained by air scooped through intakes, allowing the thrust to be throttled according to flight conditions and preserving energy for the terminal phase.

Characteristics

The missile is 3.65 m long, weighs about 190 kg, and exceeds Mach 4. It navigates mid-course by inertial guidance updated via a two-way datalink from the launch aircraft or third parties, and homes in the terminal phase with an active radar seeker derived from the Aster/4A family. It carries a blast-fragmentation warhead with impact and proximity fuzes. Meteor is integrated on the Eurofighter Typhoon, Dassault Rafale, and Gripen, and integration with the F-35 has been pursued by the UK and Italy.

Operators

Operators include the partner nations (UK, Germany, Italy, Spain, France, Sweden) and export customers such as India, Qatar, Brazil, South Korea, and others.
