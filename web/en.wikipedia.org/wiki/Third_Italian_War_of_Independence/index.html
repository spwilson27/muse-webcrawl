The Third Italian War of Independence was a war between the Kingdom of Italy and the Austrian Empire fought between June and August 1866. It formed the Italian theatre of the Austro-Prussian War (the Seven Weeks' War), Italy having allied itself with Prussia in the hope of completing the unification of Italy by annexing the Austrian-held region of Venetia.

Background

When the Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed in 1861, it did not include Venetia, then part of the Austrian Empire, nor Rome and the remaining Papal States. The desire to acquire Venetia — a long-standing goal of Italian nationalists (irredentismo) — led Prime Minister Alfonso La Marmora to conclude an alliance with Prussia on 8 April 1866. Under its terms, Italy would attack Austria from the south while Prussia invaded from the north, and Italy would receive Venetia in return.

Course of the war

Italy invaded Venetia and the Trentino in June 1866. On land, the Italian army suffered defeats at the Battle of Custoza (24 June) and, at sea, at the Battle of Lissa (20 July), where Admiral Wilhelm von Tegetthoff's Austrian fleet defeated a larger Italian force. However, volunteers under Giuseppe Garibaldi won the engagement at Bezzecca in the Trentino, and decisively, Prussia's crushing victory over Austria at the Battle of Königgrätz (Sadowa) on 3 July forced Austria to seek peace.

Outcome

Under the Treaty of Vienna (October 1866), Austria ceded Venetia to France, which promptly transferred it to Italy; a plebiscite in Venetia confirmed the annexation. Although Italy's military performance had been poor, its strategic goal was achieved thanks to its Prussian ally. Rome and Lazio remained outside the kingdom until their capture in 1870, which completed Italian unification.
