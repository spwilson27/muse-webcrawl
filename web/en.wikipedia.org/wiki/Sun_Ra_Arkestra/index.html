The Sun Ra Arkestra is an American jazz ensemble formed in Chicago in the mid-1950s by the composer, pianist and bandleader Sun Ra (Herman Poole Blount, 1914–1993). One of the most distinctive large ensembles in jazz history, the Arkestra fused swing-era big band arrangements, bebop, modal and free improvisation, electronic keyboards and theatrical space-age pageantry, all filtered through Sun Ra's self-created mythology blending ancient Egyptian imagery, science fiction and Afrocentric philosophy ("space is the place").

History

Sun Ra assembled the band — at various times billed as the Solar Arkestra, the Myth Science Arkestra and other variants — from Chicago musicians including saxophonists John Gilmore, Pat Patrick and Marshall Allen. The group relocated to New York in 1961, where it became a fixture of the avant-garde loft scene, then to Philadelphia in 1968, where its members lived communally in a house on Morton Street for decades. The Arkestra released dozens of albums, many self-issued on Sun Ra's own Saturn label with hand-drawn covers, now highly collectible.

After Sun Ra's death in 1993, the Arkestra continued under tenor saxophonist John Gilmore until 1995, and then under alto saxophonist Marshall Allen, who had joined in 1958. Under Allen — who turned 100 in 2024 — the band has kept touring and recording, releasing the Grammy-nominated album Swirling in 2020 and continuing Sun Ra's vision for a new generation.

Style and influence

Arkestra performances mix tightly arranged swing numbers, chants such as "We Travel the Space Ways", percussion processions through the audience and extended free improvisation, with members in glittering robes and headdresses. The ensemble is regarded as a foundational influence on Afrofuturism and on generations of experimental jazz and rock musicians.
