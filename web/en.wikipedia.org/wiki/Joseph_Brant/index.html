Joseph Brant

Thayendanegea, commonly known as Joseph Brant (March 1743 – November 24, 1807), was a Mohawk military and political leader, based in present-day New York and later Ontario, who was closely associated with Great Britain during and after the American Revolutionary War. Perhaps the best known Native American of his generation in the Atlantic world, he met many of the era's most significant figures, including George III and George Washington.

Early life

Brant was born in the Ohio Country during a hunting expedition and named Thayendanegea. His sister Molly Brant formed a lasting union with Sir William Johnson, the British superintendent of Indian affairs, and Joseph was educated at Eleazar Wheelock's Moor's Charity School in Connecticut, learning English and Western subjects. As a teenager he served with Britain in the French and Indian War.

American Revolutionary War

Brant travelled to London in 1775–76 and returned to lead Mohawk and Loyalist forces in the New York frontier war, including the Siege of Fort Stanwix and the Battle of Oriskany (1777), the Cherry Valley massacre (1778), and Sullivan's Expedition (1779). Americans remembered him as the "Monster Brant" of the frontier raids, though historians note his role in atrocities was often exaggerated. He held a British captain's commission and worked to preserve a Mohawk homeland through alliance with the Crown.

Later life in Canada

After the war, Brant led his people to the Grand River in Ontario, on land granted by the Haldimand Proclamation of 1784, founding the reserve of the Six Nations. He travelled again to London and to Philadelphia, worked on translating Anglican religious texts into Mohawk, and built a home at Burlington Beach, where he died in 1807. The city of Brantford, Ontario, is named after him.
