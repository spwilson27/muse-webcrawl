Corporate communication

Corporate communication is the set of activities involved in managing and orchestrating all internal and external communications aimed at creating favourable standing with stakeholders on which a company depends. It functions as an integrative framework that coordinates public relations, investor relations, marketing communications, internal communication, government affairs and crisis communication under a consistent corporate identity and brand.

Scope

Internally, corporate communication covers employee communication, change management, leadership communication and the articulation of vision, mission and values, often through intranets, town-hall meetings and internal publications. Externally it includes media relations, financial communication to investors and analysts, corporate advertising, sponsorship, public affairs and lobbying, and reputation and crisis management.

Development

The field emerged in the late 20th century as companies consolidated previously separate departments — press offices, marketing PR, and investor relations — into integrated communication functions. Scholars such as Cees van Riel and Joep Cornelissen systematized the discipline, emphasizing alignment between communication and corporate strategy and the building of reputational capital.
