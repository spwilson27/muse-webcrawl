Cello Concerto (Dvořák)

The Cello Concerto in B minor, Op. 104, B. 191, is the last solo concerto by Antonín Dvořák. It was written in 1894–95 for his friend, the cellist Hanuš Wihan, while Dvořák was director of the National Conservatory of Music in New York City. However, it was premiered in London on 19 March 1896 by the English cellist Leo Stern. The concerto is widely regarded as one of the greatest works in the cello repertoire; Brahms is reported to have said, on seeing the score, that had he known such a concerto could be written he would have written one himself long ago.

Background

Dvořák had long resisted requests to write a cello concerto, considering the cello ill-suited to the role of solo instrument outside the middle register. Hearing Victor Herbert's Cello Concerto No. 2 in New York helped change his mind. He composed the work between November 1894 and February 1895, and revised its ending in June 1895 after news of the death of his sister-in-law Josefina Čermáková, whom he had once courted; the finale's coda quotes her favourite of his songs, "Leave Me Alone."

Structure

  1. Allegro (B minor) — a sonata-form movement opening with a long orchestral exposition, the main theme announced by the clarinets and later given a famous horn statement.
  2. Adagio, ma non troppo (G major) — a lyrical slow movement incorporating the melody of Dvořák's song "Leave Me Alone," Op. 82 No. 1.
  3. Finale: Allegro moderato — Andante — Allegro vivo (B minor to B major) — a rondo with a reflective coda recalling themes from the earlier movements.

Wihan made suggested changes and had expected to give the premiere, but a scheduling conflict — and Dvořák's insistence on keeping the coda unchanged — led to Leo Stern performing the first performance with the composer conducting. Dvořák stipulated that the ending should not be altered: "the finale closes gradually diminuendo, like a breath... then there is a crescendo, and the last measures are taken up by the orchestra, ending stormily. That was my idea, and I cannot depart from it."
