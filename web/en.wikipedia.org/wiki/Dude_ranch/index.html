Dude ranch

Dude ranch (guest ranch)
Type: Vacation resort
Origin: American West, late 19th century

A dude ranch, also called a guest ranch, is a type of ranch oriented towards visitors and tourism. It originated in the American and Canadian West, where working cattle ranches began taking in paying guests — "dudes", in cowboy slang for tenderfoot easterners — who wanted to experience ranch life, horseback riding and the outdoors.

History

The tradition grew in the 1880s–1920s as cattle ranching became less profitable and railroads brought eastern tourists west. Theodore Roosevelt's time at the Maltese Cross and Elkhorn ranches in Dakota Territory in the 1880s popularised the idea of the restorative ranch holiday. The Dude Ranchers' Association was founded in 1926 to set standards for member ranches in the northern Rockies.

Types

Working dude ranches let guests join cattle drives and ranch chores; resort ranches emphasise comfort, with pools, spas and guided activities alongside riding; many are now luxury destinations in Wyoming, Montana, Colorado, Arizona and Alberta. Horseback riding remains the core activity, supplemented by fishing, hiking, rodeos and campfire entertainment.

In culture

Dude ranches entered popular culture through films such as City Slickers (1991) and remain an emblem of the mythic American West adapted to tourism.
