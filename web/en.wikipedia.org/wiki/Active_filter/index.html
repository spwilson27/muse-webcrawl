Active filter

An active filter is a type of electronic filter that uses active components, such as an operational amplifier, in addition to resistors and capacitors, but not inductors. Active filters can implement low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, and band-stop responses with controlled gain and quality factor (Q), and are fundamental building blocks in audio equipment, instrumentation, communications, and anti-aliasing and reconstruction stages of data converters.

Advantages over passive filters

Inductors are bulky, heavy, expensive, and non-ideal, especially at low frequencies. Replacing them with amplifier-based circuits gives filters that are compact and cheap, provide voltage gain, offer high input and low output impedance (so stages can be cascaded without interacting), and allow Q values and filter shapes that would require impractical components in a passive design. Their limitations include the need for a power supply, the finite gain-bandwidth and slew rate of the amplifier, and restricted voltage and frequency ranges.

Common topologies

  • Sallen–Key — a widely used second-order section using a single non-inverting amplifier with unity or modest gain.
  • Multiple feedback (MFB) — an inverting topology giving good high-Q performance.
  • State-variable filters — provide simultaneous low-pass, band-pass, and high-pass outputs with independently adjustable frequency and Q.
  • Biquad filters — versatile second-order sections that can realise any standard response.

Design

Higher-order filters are built by cascading second-order (and one first-order) sections whose poles are placed according to standard approximations such as Butterworth (maximally flat), Chebyshev (steeper roll-off with passband or stopband ripple), and Bessel (linear phase). Active filters dominate at audio and low radio frequencies; at higher frequencies, op-amp limitations favour passive LC, ceramic, SAW, or digital filters.
