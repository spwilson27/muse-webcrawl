Sherman's March to the Sea

Sherman's March to the Sea (also known as the Savannah Campaign) was a military campaign of the American Civil War conducted through Georgia from November 15 until December 21, 1864, by William Tecumseh Sherman, major general of the Union Army.

Campaign

After capturing Atlanta, Sherman's force of about 62,000 men marched some 285 miles (459 km) southeast to the port of Savannah, living off the land and destroying military targets, industry, infrastructure and civilian property along a swath of countryside. Sherman described the deliberate destruction as an application of total war intended to break the Confederacy's capacity and will to fight.

The campaign ended with the capture of Savannah on December 21, 1864, which Sherman offered to President Abraham Lincoln as a "Christmas gift". It was followed in early 1865 by the Carolinas Campaign.

Legacy

The march remains one of the most famous — and in the South, infamous — episodes of the war, and is commemorated in the song "Marching Through Georgia". Historians continue to debate its military necessity and its effects on civilians.
