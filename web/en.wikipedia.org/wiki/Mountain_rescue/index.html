Mountain rescue

Mountain rescue refers to search and rescue operations in mountainous terrain, where casualties may be injured, ill, lost or stranded in dangerous environments far from road access. Techniques include stretcher evacuations over rough ground, rope and cableway rescues on steep faces, avalanche searches with dogs and transceivers, and helicopter winching where flying conditions permit.

Organisation

In many countries mountain rescue is provided by volunteers: in England and Wales, independent Mountain Rescue teams coordinated by Mountain Rescue England and Wales respond as a free emergency service alongside the police; in Scotland, Scottish Mountain Rescue teams operate similarly. In the Alps, services such as France's PGHM, Switzerland's Rega and Air Zermatt, and Austria's Bergrettung combine professional and volunteer rescuers. In the United States, county sheriff's offices and National Park Service rangers lead rescues, supported by volunteer groups.

History

Organised mountain rescue grew out of alpine clubs in the late 19th and early 20th centuries and expanded greatly after the Second World War with the spread of recreational mountaineering, climbing and skiing. Modern teams train in casualty care, technical rope work, winter skills and incident management, and increasingly respond to floods, missing-person searches and severe weather in lowland areas as well.
