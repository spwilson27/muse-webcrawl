Night

Night is the period of ambient darkness from sunset to sunrise, during which a given location on Earth experiences darkness because that part of the planet is facing away from the Sun. Night is the counterpart of day and is interrupted by the transitional periods of twilight at dusk and dawn.

Opposite: Day

Astronomy and physics

The duration of night varies with latitude and season. At the equator it lasts roughly half of each 24-hour period, while near the poles the polar night of winter can last for weeks or months, offset by the midnight sun in summer. Complete darkness for astronomical observation occurs only when the Sun is more than 18 degrees below the horizon (astronomical night).

Effects on life

The alternation of day and night drives circadian rhythms in most organisms. Many species are nocturnal, active primarily at night, while diurnal species such as humans traditionally rest and sleep during it. Artificial lighting has significantly altered the human experience of night, and light pollution now obscures the night sky for a large fraction of the world's population.

Cultural significance

Night has been associated in mythology and religion with darkness, rest, danger and the divine; personifications include Nyx in Greek mythology. It has inspired extensive treatment in literature, painting and music, from van Gogh's The Starry Night to the nocturnes of Chopin.
