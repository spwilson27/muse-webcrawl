Taxane

Taxanes are a class of diterpenoid compounds originally identified from plants of the genus Taxus (yews), characterised by a taxadiene core skeleton. They are among the most important classes of anticancer drugs: the taxanes paclitaxel (Taxol) and docetaxel (Taxotere), and the later derivative cabazitaxel, are widely used chemotherapy agents for breast, ovarian, lung, prostate, and other cancers.

Paclitaxel (Taxol), the prototypical taxane anticancer drug, first isolated from Pacific yew bark.

History

Paclitaxel was isolated in the 1960s–70s from the bark of the Pacific yew (Taxus brevifolia) through a US National Cancer Institute screening programme, and its unusual structure was published in 1971. Its mechanism — promoting the assembly of microtubules and stabilising them against depolymerisation, thereby blocking cell division — was identified by Susan Horwitz's laboratory in 1979. Because harvesting bark killed the slow-growing trees, supply became a celebrated problem in chemistry; it was solved by semi-synthesis from 10-deacetylbaccatin, an abundant precursor in European yew needles, and by the total syntheses achieved by the Holton and Nicolaou groups in 1994. Paclitaxel gained FDA approval for ovarian cancer in 1992 and for breast cancer in 1994.

Mechanism of action

Whereas vinca alkaloids prevent microtubule assembly, taxanes bind to β-tubulin within assembled microtubules and prevent their dynamic disassembly. The frozen spindle cannot complete mitosis, and rapidly dividing cancer cells undergo arrest and apoptosis.

Clinical use and side effects

Taxanes are used against ovarian, breast, non-small-cell lung, prostate, gastric, and head and neck cancers, among others. Characteristic toxicities include myelosuppression, peripheral neuropathy, hypersensitivity reactions (managed by premedication and, for paclitaxel, formulation in Cremophor EL; an albumin-bound form, nab-paclitaxel, avoids the solvent), fluid retention (docetaxel), and alopecia.

Related compounds

More than 300 taxane diterpenoids have been characterised from yew species. Beyond the three approved drugs, numerous analogues have been investigated to overcome drug resistance mediated by efflux pumps such as P-glycoprotein.
