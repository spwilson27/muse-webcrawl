Israel Joshua Singer (Yiddish: ישראל יהושע זינגער; 30 November 1893 – 10 February 1944) was a Polish-American Yiddish novelist, playwright and journalist. He was the elder brother of the Nobel laureate Isaac Bashevis Singer and of the novelist Esther Kreitman.

Life

Singer was born in Biłgoraj, the son of a Hasidic rabbi, and raised in Warsaw, where he began writing for the Yiddish press. His early novellas, including Pearls (1922) and the novel Steel and Iron (1927), established his reputation. The novel Yoshe Kalb (1932), serialised in the New York Yiddish newspaper Forverts, was dramatised successfully on stage.

The Brothers Ashkenazi

In 1934 Singer emigrated to the United States. His great family saga The Brothers Ashkenazi (1936), chronicling the rise and decline of Jewish industrial Łódź, became a bestseller in English translation and is often considered the finest historical novel in Yiddish literature. Later works include The River Breaks Up (1938), East of Eden (1939) and the posthumous The Family Carnovsky (1943; English 1969).

Death and legacy

Singer died of a heart attack in New York in 1944. His memoir Of a World That Is No More appeared posthumously. Though overshadowed in English-language fame by his younger brother, he is regarded as the more accomplished craftsman by many critics of Yiddish literature.
