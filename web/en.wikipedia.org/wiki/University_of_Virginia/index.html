University of Virginia

The University of Virginia (UVA) is a public research university in Charlottesville, Virginia. It was founded in 1819 by Thomas Jefferson, who designed its Academical Village — centered on the Rotunda and the Lawn — which is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a landmark of early American architecture.

History

Jefferson conceived the university as a secular institution, unusual for its time, with no religious instruction and with students electing their courses of study. The first classes met in 1825. James Madison and James Monroe served as rectors, and Edgar Allan Poe was briefly a student. Jefferson considered the university among his greatest achievements, listing it on his tombstone alongside the Declaration of Independence and the Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom.

Student life and honor

UVA is known for its Honor System, dating to 1842, under which students pledge not to lie, cheat, or steal, and for student self-governance. Secret societies, including the Seven Society and the IMP Society, are part of campus lore. The grounds (students and faculty say "Grounds," never "campus") include the Lawn rooms, whose residents are selected as a senior honor.

Athletics

The Virginia Cavaliers (or "Wahoos") compete in the Atlantic Coast Conference.
