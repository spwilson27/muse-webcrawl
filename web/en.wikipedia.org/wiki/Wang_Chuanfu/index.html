Wang Chuanfu

Wang Chuanfu (Chinese: 王传福; born April 1966) is a Chinese chemist, entrepreneur and billionaire who founded the technology and automotive company BYD, serving as its chairman and president. Under his leadership BYD grew from a rechargeable-battery maker into one of the world's largest producers of electric vehicles.

Early life and career

Wang was born to a poor farming family in Wuwei County, Anhui, and was orphaned as a teenager, being raised by his elder brother and sister-in-law. He studied metallurgical physical chemistry at Central South University and earned a master's degree in 1990, afterwards working as a researcher and deputy director at a state battery research institute in Beijing.

BYD

In 1995 Wang left his secure research post, borrowed money from his cousin Lü Xiangyang, and founded BYD in Shenzhen to make rechargeable batteries by labour-intensive methods that undercut Japanese rivals. BYD became the world's largest maker of rechargeable batteries and a major supplier of handset components; in 2003 it entered the automobile business, and its plug-in hybrid and battery-electric cars eventually made BYD the world's best-selling electric-vehicle brand, overtaking Tesla in global sales. Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway bought a 10% stake in BYD in 2008. Wang has been ranked among China's wealthiest people and is noted for his engineering-focused, hands-on management style.
