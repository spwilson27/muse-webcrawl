Salzach

Length225 km (140 mi)
SourceKrimml Alps, Austrian Alps
MouthInn, near Braunau am Inn / Passau region
CountriesAustria, Germany

The Salzach is a river in Austria and Germany, 225 kilometres (140 mi) long, and the most important tributary of the Inn. Its name derives from Salz, German for salt, reflecting the historic salt trade along its course. It drains much of the Austrian state of Salzburg and forms part of the Austria–Germany border in its lower reaches.

Course

The Salzach rises in the Krimml Alps near Krimml in the Pinzgau district, not far from the famous Krimml Waterfalls on its tributary the Krimmler Ache. It flows east through Zell am See's district, past St. Johann im Pongau, then turns north through the narrow Lueg Pass gorge at Golling and reaches Hallein and the city of Salzburg, which it bisects between the Altstadt and the newer right bank. Below Salzburg it forms the frontier with Bavaria, passing Laufen and Burghausen, before joining the Inn near Braunau am Inn.

History and use

For centuries the Salzach was the artery of the Salzburg salt trade, carrying salt from the Hallein mines downstream to the Inn and Danube — the basis of the prince-archbishops' wealth. Today the river is harnessed by a chain of run-of-river hydroelectric power stations, and its valley carries the Tauern railway and major roads. The Salzachöfen gorge near Golling and the river's rafting sections are popular with visitors.
