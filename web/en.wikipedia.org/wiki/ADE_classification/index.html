ADE classification

In mathematics, the ADE classification is a scheme that classifies certain kinds of objects — most famously the simply laced Dynkin diagrams — into two infinite families, An and Dn, and three exceptional cases, E6, E7, and E8. Objects following the ADE classification appear throughout mathematics and physics, in contexts that at first seem unrelated.

Classified objects

The ADE pattern governs, among other things: the simply laced Dynkin diagrams and corresponding simple Lie algebras; finite subgroups of SU(2) (via the McKay correspondence); the simple singularities of complex surfaces (Du Val singularities); the Platonic solids and their symmetry groups; modular invariants in conformal field theory; and certain quiver representations of finite type.

McKay correspondence

The McKay correspondence, observed by John McKay in the late 1970s, relates the finite subgroups of SU(2) to the extended (affine) ADE Dynkin diagrams: the irreducible representations of such a subgroup form a graph that is exactly an extended ADE diagram. This correspondence was later understood geometrically through resolutions of quotient singularities.
