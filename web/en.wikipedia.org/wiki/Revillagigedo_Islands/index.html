Revillagigedo Islands

The Revillagigedo Islands (Spanish: Islas Revillagigedo) are a remote group of four volcanic islands in the eastern Pacific Ocean, administered by Mexico. They lie several hundred kilometres southwest of the Baja California Peninsula and are celebrated for exceptionally rich marine life.

Principal islands
Socorro, San Benedicto, Roca Partida and Clarion.
Status
Biosphere reserve and UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Geography

Socorro is the largest island and contains Mount Evermann, an active shield volcano. San Benedicto was dramatically reshaped by the 1952 eruption of Bárcena volcano. Roca Partida is a tiny steep rock, while Clarion is the westernmost and second-largest island. Fresh water is scarce, and the islands are exposed to Pacific hurricanes.

Ecology

Isolation has produced endemic plants and animals, including the Socorro dove, Socorro mockingbird and distinctive lizards and seabirds. The surrounding waters support giant manta rays, sharks, tuna, dolphins and humpback whales. Divers know the archipelago—often called “Socorro”—for close encounters with large pelagic animals.

History and administration

Spanish explorers sighted the islands in the sixteenth century. They were later named for Juan Vicente de Güemes, Count of Revillagigedo and viceroy of New Spain. Mexico maintains naval and scientific personnel, especially on Socorro and Clarion; there is no ordinary civilian population or commercial tourism by land.

Conservation

The islands were designated a biosphere reserve in 1994 and inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2016. In 2017 Mexico created the Revillagigedo National Park, one of the largest fully protected marine areas in North America, prohibiting fishing throughout the reserve. Invasive species control remains vital to protect endemic land species.
