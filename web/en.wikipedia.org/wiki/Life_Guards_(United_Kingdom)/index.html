The Life Guards (LG) is the most senior regiment of the British Army and part of the Household Cavalry, along with the Blues and Royals. The regiment has served in an armoured reconnaissance and ceremonial role, guarding the sovereign and royal residences in London.

History

The Life Guards trace their origins to 1660, when Charles II raised troops of Horse Guards from Royalist exiles before the Restoration. The regiment fought at Waterloo in 1815 as part of the Household Brigade, and later served in Egypt, the Boer War, both world wars, and more recent operations including Iraq and Afghanistan.

Role

In its operational role the regiment serves as an armoured cavalry unit, while at home it mounts the King's Life Guard at Horse Guards in Whitehall and takes part in state ceremonial occasions such as Trooping the Colour. Its soldiers famously wear scarlet tunics and metal cuirasses with white plumed helmets on mounted duty.
