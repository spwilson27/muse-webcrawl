Sarmizegetusa

Sarmizegetusa Regia was the capital and the most important military, religious, and political centre of the Dacians, located in the Orăștie Mountains of present-day Romania at an altitude of about 1,200 metres. It was the stronghold of King Decebalus until its capture by the Romans in Trajan's Dacian Wars (101–102 and 105–106 CE).

The site

The fortress was part of a system of Dacian hill-forts in the Orăștie Mountains. Its sacred precinct contains circular and horseshoe-shaped temple sanctuaries, including a famous circular calendar-sanctuary of andesite pillars, and the site is notable for its sophisticated water supply system of terracotta and wooden pipes. After the conquest, the Romans founded a new capital, Colonia Ulpia Traiana Augusta Dacica Sarmizegetusa, some 40 km away in the Hațeg basin.

Legacy

The ruins of Sarmizegetusa Regia are inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the Dacian Fortresses of the Orăștie Mountains. The site has also attracted attention because of gold Dacian bracelets and coins looted from the area and partly recovered by Romanian authorities.
