Nord Stream 1 (German: Nord Stream 1, formerly the North European Gas Pipeline) is a pair of natural gas pipelines running on the bed of the Baltic Sea from Vyborg in Russia to Greifswald in Germany. It was the first of the two Nord Stream pipelines, built between 2010 and 2012 and operated by Nord Stream AG, a consortium majority-owned by Gazprom. Its twin strings gave Germany and Western Europe a direct gas supply route from Siberian fields that did not pass through Ukraine or Poland.

History

The agreement to build the pipeline was signed in 2005, construction of the offshore section began in April 2010, and the first string was inaugurated on 8 November 2011 by German Chancellor Angela Merkel and Russian President Dmitry Medvedev. The second string entered service in October 2012, bringing total capacity to 55 billion cubic metres of gas per year.

Shutdown and destruction

Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Gazprom progressively reduced flows through Nord Stream 1, citing turbine maintenance issues, and shut the pipeline down entirely at the end of August 2022. On 26 September 2022, underwater explosions destroyed both strings of Nord Stream 1 near Bornholm, Denmark, along with one string of Nord Stream 2. The pipelines have not operated since, and the incident remains under investigation and international dispute.
