Forced labour

Forced labour, or unfree labour, is any work relation in which people are employed against their will under the threat of destitution, detention, violence, compulsion, or other extreme hardship to themselves or members of their families. It includes all forms of slavery and related practices, such as debt bondage, serfdom, and human trafficking.

Definitions and law

The International Labour Organization (ILO) Forced Labour Convention, 1930 (No. 29) defines forced or compulsory labour as "all work or service which is exacted from any person under the menace of any penalty and for which the said person has not offered himself voluntarily". Exceptions include military service, normal civic obligations, prison labour under certain conditions, and work in emergencies. The 2014 Protocol to the Convention requires states to take measures for prevention, protection of victims, and access to compensation.

Scale and forms

The ILO estimated in 2022 that around 27.6 million people were in forced labour on any given day, including forced commercial sexual exploitation, state-imposed forced labour, and forced labour imposed by private agents, frequently connected with poverty, discrimination, and migration. Historical systems of forced labour include the transatlantic slave trade, the corvée, the gulag system of the Soviet Union, and the labour camps of Nazi Germany. Modern forms include debt bondage in South Asia, forced labour in supply chains, and the exploitation of migrant workers under sponsorship systems such as kafala.
