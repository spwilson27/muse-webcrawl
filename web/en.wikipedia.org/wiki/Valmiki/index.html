Valmiki, the Adi Kavi

Valmiki (Sanskrit: वाल्मीकि, Vālmīki) is a revered sage in Hindu tradition, traditionally regarded as the author of the Ramayana, one of the two Sanskrit Itihasas (epics) of ancient India. He is honoured as the Adi Kavi, the "first poet", credited with inventing the shloka metre in which the epic is composed. His life and the date of the Ramayana's composition are matters of tradition and scholarly debate; the epic's core is generally placed in the first millennium BCE.

Legend

According to tradition, Valmiki was once a highway robber named Ratnakara, who was transformed by an encounter with the sage Narada: meditating for so many years on the name of Rama that anthills (valmika) grew over his body, he emerged reborn as Valmiki, "he who comes from the anthill". The Ramayana itself narrates how the sage, witnessing a hunter kill one of a pair of mating krauncha birds, spontaneously uttered a curse in verse — the first shloka — and was then charged by the god Brahma with composing Rama's life story, which Narada had already related to him.

Role in the Ramayana

Valmiki also appears within his own epic: after Rama exiles the pregnant Sita, it is Valmiki who shelters her in his hermitage, where she bears the twins Lava and Kusha. The sage teaches the boys the Ramayana, and they recite it before Rama himself — the frame through which the poem presents itself. The final book (Uttara Kanda) describing these events is widely considered a later addition to the epic.

Veneration

Valmiki is venerated across India; temples dedicated to him are found in many regions, and his birthday, Valmiki Jayanti, is celebrated on the full moon day of the month of Ashwin. He holds a special place for the Valmiki (Balmiki) community, many of whose members regard him as their patron saint and ancestor; he also features in Sikh tradition, where Bhai Gurdas recounts his redemption.
