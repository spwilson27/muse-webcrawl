Ctenoid scale
Found inAdvanced teleost fish (e.g., perch, bass)
Distinguishing featureComb-like ctenii on the exposed edge

A ctenoid scale is a type of fish scale found in most bony fishes of the more advanced teleost groups, such as perch, bass, and many other spiny-rayed fishes. The name derives from the Greek kteis ("comb"): the exposed posterior edge of the scale bears small tooth-like projections called ctenii, which give the fish a rough texture when stroked from tail to head.

Ctenoid scales are one of the two main forms of the cosmoid-derived elasmoid scales; the other is the cycloid scale, which has a smooth posterior edge. Like cycloid scales, ctenoid scales overlap like roof tiles and grow by adding concentric rings, allowing age estimation by counting annuli. The ctenii are thought to improve hydrodynamics or provide protection. Some fish, such as flounders, bear ctenoid scales on one side of the body and cycloid scales on the other.
