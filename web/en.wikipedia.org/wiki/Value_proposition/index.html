Value proposition


The Value Proposition Canvas links customer needs to product benefits

A value proposition is a statement that identifies the clear, measurable and demonstrable benefits a customer gets from buying a product or service. It summarises why a consumer should choose one offering over alternatives: the bundle of products, services and benefits that a company promises to deliver to a chosen customer segment, and the reason that offer is preferable to competitors' offerings.

Concept and development

The term emerged in management consulting in the 1980s — it is often credited to McKinsey & Company consultant Michael Lanning, whose 1988 paper treated the value proposition as the core of a "value delivery system" aligning product design, pricing and communication. In marketing theory the value proposition sits at the heart of market orientation and positioning: a strong proposition is specific, customer-focused and differentiated, articulating functional benefits (what the product does), economic benefits (what it saves or earns) and emotional benefits (how it makes the customer feel).

Frameworks

Several practical frameworks structure the concept. In Alexander Osterwalder's Business Model Canvas, the value proposition is the central building block linking customer segments to channels, revenue streams and the firm's resources; his later Value Proposition Canvas maps customer "jobs", pains and gains against the pain relievers, gain creators and products of the offer. Similar ideas appear in the "value proposition statement" formats used in sales and product marketing ("For [customer] who [need], our [product] is a [category] that [key benefit], unlike [alternative]...").

Applications

Beyond products, the term is used for employer value propositions in human-resources branding, for investor pitches, and for public-sector services. Critics note that the phrase is sometimes used loosely to mean any marketing slogan; in its stricter sense it demands evidence that the claimed benefits are delivered and valued.
