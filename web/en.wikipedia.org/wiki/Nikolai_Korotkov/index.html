Nikolai Korotkov

Born26 February 1874, Kursk, Russian Empire
Died14 March 1920, Petrograd
Known forKorotkov sounds; auscultatory blood-pressure method

Nikolai Sergeyevich Korotkov (1874–1920) was a Russian surgeon who in 1905 described the sounds — now called Korotkov sounds — heard through a stethoscope over an artery below an inflated cuff, making possible the standard auscultatory measurement of systolic and diastolic blood pressure.

Life and work

Korotkov graduated in medicine from Moscow University in 1898 and worked at the Imperial Military Medical Academy in St Petersburg. Serving during the Russo-Japanese War, he studied collateral circulation in wounded soldiers. Working with the Riva-Rocci sphygmomanometer cuff, he reported to the academy in November 1905 that characteristic sounds appear and disappear as cuff pressure falls: the first sound marks systolic pressure, the last marks diastolic.

Legacy

Briefly published and initially little noticed, especially outside Russia, the method became the worldwide clinical standard for non-invasive blood-pressure measurement for more than a century. Korotkov died of tuberculosis in 1920.
