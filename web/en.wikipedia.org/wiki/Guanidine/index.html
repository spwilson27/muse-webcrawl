Guanidine

FormulaCH₅N₃
Molar mass59.07 g/mol
AppearanceColourless solid

Guanidine is a nitrogenous organic compound with the formula HN=C(NH₂)₂. It is a strong base, and its protonated form, the guanidinium cation, is resonance-stabilised with the positive charge delocalised over the three nitrogen atoms.

Occurrence and significance

The guanidinium group forms the side chain of the amino acid arginine, giving it the highest basicity of the common amino-acid side chains and an important role in protein structure and enzyme active sites. Guanidine occurs naturally as a breakdown product of protein metabolism and was first isolated from guano, from which it takes its name.

Uses

Guanidinium salts such as guanidinium chloride are widely used as chaotropic agents to denature proteins in biochemistry. Guanidine derivatives include disinfectants, resins, and pharmaceutical compounds such as metformin, whose structure contains a related biguanide group.
