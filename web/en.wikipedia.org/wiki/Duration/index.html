Duration

Duration is the length of time something continues or exists — the interval between two events or points in time. The term is used across many fields with related but distinct technical meanings.

In music

In music, duration refers to how long a pitch, note, or phrase sounds. Note values — whole notes, half notes, quarter notes and so on — specify relative durations, which are realised in performance according to tempo. Duration is one of the basic parameters of a musical sound, along with pitch, timbre, and loudness, and twentieth-century composers such as Olivier Messiaen and the serialists organised duration systematically in their works.

In finance

In finance, duration — most commonly Macaulay duration and modified duration — measures the sensitivity of a bond's price to changes in interest rates, expressed in years. It is the weighted average time until a bond's cash flows are received, and it is a standard tool of portfolio and risk management for fixed-income securities.

In philosophy and other fields

The philosopher Henri Bergson made durée (duration) central to his thought, arguing that real time is a qualitative, flowing continuity that cannot be captured by the spatialised, measurable time of clocks. In linguistics, duration (quantity) is the length of a speech sound, phonemic in languages such as Finnish and Japanese. In project management and computing, duration is the elapsed time a task or process takes to complete.
