The Guinness Storehouse is a tourist attraction at St. James's Gate Brewery in Dublin, Ireland. Since opening in 2000, it has received over twenty million visitors. The Storehouse covers seven floors surrounding a glass atrium shaped in the form of a pint of Guinness; the ground floor introduces the beer's ingredients, and displays the 9,000-year lease Arthur Guinness signed on the brewery site in 1759.

The building

The building was erected in 1902 as the brewery's fermentation plant, where beer was fermented before storage, and was the first multi-storey steel-framed building in Ireland. It served as a fermentation house until 1988. Its conversion into a visitor centre, replacing the earlier Guinness Hop Store attraction, cost some £35 million.

The experience

Exhibitions trace brewing ingredients and process, the cooper's craft, Guinness transport history, and famous advertising campaigns (including the toucan and the "Guinness is Good for You" slogan). A tasting experience and the Guinness Academy teach visitors to pour the two-part pint. The top-floor Gravity Bar offers a complimentary pint with 360-degree views over Dublin. The Storehouse is consistently Ireland's most popular fee-charging visitor attraction, drawing well over a million visitors in peak years, and the brewery itself sits west of the medieval core near the former western edge of the Pale.
