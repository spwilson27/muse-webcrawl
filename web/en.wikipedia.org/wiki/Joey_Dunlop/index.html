William Joseph Dunlop OBE (25 February 1952 – 2 July 2000) was a Northern Irish motorcycle racer, widely regarded as one of the greatest road racers of all time. Known as the "King of the Roads", he won the Isle of Man TT 26 times, a record that stood for more than two decades, and was a five-time Formula TT world champion. He was equally celebrated for his humanitarian work, delivering aid to orphanages in Romania and Bosnia in his own van.

Career

Dunlop began racing in 1969 and made his TT debut in 1976. He achieved his first TT victory in 1977 and went on to dominate road racing through the 1980s and 1990s, winning races on machinery ranging from 125cc machines to superbikes, often run on a shoestring by his own small team. He won the Ulster Grand Prix 24 times and the North West 200 13 times. At the 2000 TT, aged 48, he achieved a hat-trick of wins, including the Senior TT.

Death and legacy

Weeks after the 2000 TT, Dunlop was killed in a crash during a race in Tallinn, Estonia, when he lost control in wet conditions. His funeral in Ballymoney drew tens of thousands of mourners. Statues stand in his honour in Ballymoney and on the Isle of Man, where a memorial garden and the Joey Dunlop Foundation continue his charitable work. The Dunlop racing dynasty continued through his brother Robert and nephews Michael and William Dunlop; Michael surpassed his uncle's TT record in 2024.
