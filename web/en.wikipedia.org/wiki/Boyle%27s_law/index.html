Boyle's law, also referred to as the Boyle–Mariotte law, is an experimental gas law that describes the relationship between pressure and volume of a confined gas. It states that the pressure (p) of a given mass of an ideal gas is inversely proportional to its volume (V) at a constant temperature: pV = constant.

History

The law was first published by the Anglo-Irish natural philosopher Robert Boyle in 1662, based on experiments with trapped air performed with the assistance of Robert Hooke. The French physicist Edme Mariotte independently stated the same relationship in 1676, adding the requirement of constant temperature, and the law is known in France by his name.

Applications

Boyle's law explains the operation of syringes, the expansion of gases in the lungs during breathing, and the behaviour of air in scuba diving. Together with Charles's law and Gay-Lussac's law it forms the combined gas law, generalised by the ideal gas law.
