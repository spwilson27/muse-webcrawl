Real property

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In English common law, real property, realty or immovable property comprises land and anything permanently attached to it — buildings, structures and fixtures — together with the legal interests in them (freehold and leasehold estates). The term contrasts with personal property (chattels and choses in action). Historically the distinction turned on the remedy: “real” actions recovered the land itself, while personal actions gave only damages.

Interests in land are conveyed by deed and, in most modern systems, recorded in public registries (in England and Wales, HM Land Registry; Torrens title registration, originating in South Australia in 1858, operates in much of the Commonwealth). Ownership is subject to planning and zoning law, environmental rules, taxation, compulsory purchase (eminent domain), and the rights of others such as easements and covenants. Real property is typically the largest store of household wealth and the principal collateral in mortgage markets.
