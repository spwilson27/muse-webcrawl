Audiencia Nacional

The Audiencia Nacional (English: National Court) is a high court in Spain with jurisdiction over the entire national territory. Established in 1977 during Spain's transition to democracy, it is headquartered in Madrid and deals with the most serious and nationally significant cases, including terrorism, organised crime, drug trafficking, major financial crime, and crimes against the Spanish state, as well as extradition proceedings.

History

The court was created by royal decree-law on 4 January 1977, shortly before Spain's first democratic elections, partly to replace the Franco-era Tribunal de Orden Público (Public Order Court). Its creation was controversial: critics argued that a centralised court for political-type offences perpetuated the logic of the dictatorship's special tribunal, while supporters maintained that terrorism and organised crime required a specialised national forum.

Jurisdiction and structure

The Audiencia Nacional is organised into criminal, administrative-litigation and social chambers, supported by central investigating courts (juzgados centrales de instrucción) whose examining magistrates conduct judicial investigations. Its criminal jurisdiction includes terrorism, crimes against the Crown and government, offences committed by organised groups across several provinces, major drug trafficking, currency counterfeiting and certain crimes committed abroad by Spanish nationals or against Spaniards.

Notable cases

The court has handled proceedings arising from ETA and jihadist terrorism — including the trials following the 2004 Madrid train bombings — major corruption investigations such as the Gürtel case, and the investigation of former Chilean dictator Augusto Pinochet, whose 1998 arrest in London followed an extradition request by investigating judge Baltasar Garzón. The court's investigating judges, and Garzón in particular, became internationally prominent through the use of universal jurisdiction.
