Flag terminology

Flag terminology is the terminology used in vexillology, the study of flags, to describe precisely the parts, patterns, and other features of flags and their display.

Parts of a flag

  • Hoist — the half or part of the flag closest to the flagpole; also the vertical dimension of a flag.
  • Fly — the half farthest from the pole; also the horizontal length of a flag.
  • Field — the background of the flag.
  • Canton — a quarter or rectangular division, usually the upper hoist quarter, such as the blue quarter of the U.S. flag.
  • Charge — an emblem or device placed on the field.
  • Sleeve / hoist rope — the attachment by which the flag is fastened to the pole or halyard.

Basic patterns

Common patterns include horizontal and vertical stripes (tribands and tricolours), crosses (including the Nordic cross), diagonals (saltires and bends), pales, fesses, chevrons, and cantons. Heraldic terms such as "fess" (horizontal band) and "pale" (vertical band) are commonly borrowed.

Types of flags

  • Ensign — a flag flown at the stern of a vessel, indicating nationality.
  • Jack — a small flag flown at the bow of a ship.
  • Standard, banner, pennant, burgee, gonfalon, and vexilloid denote various forms and uses.
