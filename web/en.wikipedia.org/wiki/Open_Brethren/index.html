Open Brethren

The Open Brethren are one of two major branches of the Plymouth Brethren movement, an evangelical Christian movement that began in Dublin, Ireland, in the late 1820s. The "Open" branch is distinguished from the Exclusive Brethren by receiving to communion Christians from other churches who are judged sound in faith and conduct, and by the independence of each local assembly.

History

The movement split in 1848 over a dispute in Plymouth and Bristol involving John Nelson Darby and Benjamin Wills Newton, and George Müller's Bethesda chapel in Bristol, which declined to exclude members over the controversy. Congregations following the Bethesda position became the Open Brethren, while Darby's followers formed the Exclusive Brethren.

Beliefs and practice

Open Brethren assemblies are autonomous, led by a plurality of elders rather than ordained clergy, and typically hold a weekly Breaking of Bread service. They are known for missionary activity worldwide and for mission-support organisations. Notable figures include George Müller, founder of the Ashley Down orphanages, and missionary Jim Elliot.
