Wilberforce University is a private historically Black university in Wilberforce, Ohio, United States. Founded in 1856, it is the nation's oldest private historically Black university owned and operated by African Americans. It was named for the 18th-century English abolitionist William Wilberforce and is affiliated with the African Methodist Episcopal (AME) Church, which purchased the institution in 1863.

History

The university was established by members of the Cincinnati Conference of the Methodist Episcopal Church together with white and Black abolitionists, at a resort at Tawawa Springs that ironically had been popular with slaveholders. After enrolment collapsed with the Civil War, the AME Church bought the university in 1863 under Bishop Daniel Payne, who became its president — the first African American president of a university in the United States. W. E. B. Du Bois taught at Wilberforce in the 1890s.

Its sister institution, Central State University, began as a department of Wilberforce in 1887 before becoming a separate public university. Wilberforce's choir and its role in African American education have given the small liberal arts university an outsized place in Black American history, though it has faced periodic accreditation and financial difficulties in recent decades.
