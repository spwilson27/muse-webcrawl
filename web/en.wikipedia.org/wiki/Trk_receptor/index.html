Trk receptor

Trk receptors are a family of high-affinity receptor tyrosine kinases that bind neurotrophins and regulate the survival, growth and differentiation of neurons. The family comprises TrkA, TrkB and TrkC, encoded in humans by the NTRK1, NTRK2 and NTRK3 genes respectively. Their preferred ligands are nerve growth factor (NGF) for TrkA, brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and neurotrophin-4 for TrkB, and neurotrophin-3 for TrkC.

Family membersTrkA, TrkB, TrkC
GenesNTRK1, NTRK2, NTRK3
LigandsNGF, BDNF, NT-3, NT-4
TypeReceptor tyrosine kinase

Signalling

Neurotrophin binding induces receptor dimerisation and autophosphorylation of intracellular tyrosine residues, which recruit adaptor proteins and activate downstream pathways including Ras/MAPK, PI3K/Akt and PLCγ. These pathways promote neuronal survival and synaptic plasticity and are essential for the development of the peripheral and central nervous systems.

Clinical significance

Chromosomal rearrangements producing NTRK gene fusions create constitutively active Trk fusion proteins that drive a range of cancers. The TRK inhibitors larotrectinib and entrectinib were developed as tumour-agnostic therapies for fusion-positive cancers. Mutations in NTRK1 also cause congenital insensitivity to pain with anhidrosis.
