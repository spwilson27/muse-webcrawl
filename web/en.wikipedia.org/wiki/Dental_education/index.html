Dental education

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dental education is the professional training required to become a dentist. Around the world it typically involves several years of university study in a dental school, combining basic medical and dental sciences with supervised clinical practice, followed by licensing examinations and, in many countries, a period of vocational training. Dental specialists — orthodontists, oral surgeons, periodontists and others — complete additional postgraduate residency programmes after their initial qualification.

Structure of training

Dental curricula generally cover anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, pharmacology, pathology and microbiology together with dental-specific subjects: oral anatomy, dental materials, operative dentistry, prosthodontics, periodontics, endodontics, oral surgery, orthodontics, paediatric dentistry, oral medicine and radiology, and preventive and public-health dentistry. Students typically begin pre-clinical laboratory work on simulators and phantom heads before treating patients under supervision in university clinics.

Qualifications by country

In the United States and Canada, dentists qualify with the Doctor of Dental Surgery (DDS) or Doctor of Dental Medicine (DMD) after a four-year graduate programme (usually following a bachelor's degree), accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation. In the United Kingdom the Bachelor of Dental Surgery (BDS) is a five-year undergraduate degree regulated by the General Dental Council. In India the BDS is a five-year course including a compulsory internship, and similar bachelor-level degrees are used across much of Asia, Europe, Africa and Latin America, often under titles such as BDent, BDS or Odontología degrees.

History

The first dental school, the Baltimore College of Dental Surgery, was founded in 1840 and awarded the first DDS degrees. William Gies's 1926 report on dental education in the United States and Canada raised standards and tied dentistry more closely to university-based, science-led training.

Continuing education and specialties

Most jurisdictions require licensed dentists to complete continuing professional development. Recognised specialties commonly include orthodontics, oral and maxillofacial surgery, periodontics, endodontics, prosthodontics, paediatric dentistry, oral pathology, oral radiology and dental public health, each requiring two to six years of additional training.
