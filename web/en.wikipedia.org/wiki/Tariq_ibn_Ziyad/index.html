Tariq ibn Ziyad

Ṭāriq ibn Ziyād (Arabic: طارق بن زياد; died c. 720) was an Umayyad commander, probably of Berber origin, who led the Muslim conquest of Visigothic Hispania beginning in 711. Gibraltar is named after him: Jabal Ṭāriq, "Tariq's mountain".

The conquest

Serving Musa ibn Nusayr, governor of Ifriqiya, Tariq crossed from North Africa in the spring of 711 with an army composed largely of Berbers. In July 711 he decisively defeated King Roderic at the Battle of Guadalete, where Roderic was killed. Tariq then advanced rapidly, taking Córdoba, Málaga, and the Visigothic capital Toledo, before Musa arrived in 712 to complete the conquest of most of the Iberian Peninsula.

Later life and legend

In 714 both commanders were recalled to Damascus by Caliph al-Walid I; Tariq spent his last years in Syria in relative obscurity. Later legend credits him with burning his ships after landing to leave his men no retreat — a story not found in the earliest sources but enduring in popular memory.
