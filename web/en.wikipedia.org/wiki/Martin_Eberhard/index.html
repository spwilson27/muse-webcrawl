Martin Eberhard

Martin Eberhard (born May 15, 1960) is an American engineer and business executive. He co-founded Tesla Motors (now Tesla, Inc.) in 2003 with Marc Tarpenning and served as its first chief executive officer until 2007. Before Tesla he co-founded the e-book company NuvoMedia, maker of the Rocket eBook.

Early life and education

Eberhard grew up in Kensington, California, in the San Francisco Bay Area. He received a bachelor's degree in computer engineering in 1982 and a master's degree in electrical engineering in 1984, both from the University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign. He worked as an electrical engineer, including at Wyse Technology, where he was lead engineer on early network computing terminals.

NuvoMedia

In 1997 Eberhard and Marc Tarpenning co-founded NuvoMedia, which launched the Rocket eBook in 1998, one of the first handheld electronic book readers. The company was sold to Gemstar in 2000 for a reported $187 million.

Tesla

Interested in sports cars that did not depend on petroleum, Eberhard studied the economics of electric vehicles using lithium-ion cells and concluded a high-end electric sports car could be viable. After AC Propulsion's tzero prototype demonstrated the concept, Eberhard and Tarpenning incorporated Tesla Motors on July 1, 2003, in San Carlos, California. Elon Musk led the company's Series A financing in 2004 and became chairman. Eberhard led development of the Tesla Roadster.

In late 2007, amid Roadster production delays and cost overruns, Eberhard was replaced as CEO, first by interim CEO Michael Marks and then Ze'ev Drori, and he left the company in early 2008. In 2009 he sued Musk and Tesla alleging libel and breach of contract; the case was settled, and a subsequent agreement recognised Eberhard, Tarpenning, Musk, JB Straubel and Ian Wright as Tesla's five co-founders.

Later work

Eberhard later worked in Volkswagen's electronics research laboratory in Silicon Valley, and served as chief innovation officer and in other roles at the Chinese electric-vehicle company SF Motors (later Seres). He has also been involved with battery start-ups including Tiveni and Inevit.
