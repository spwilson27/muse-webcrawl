Partitive case

The partitive expresses a part of a whole, or an indefinite quantity.

The partitive case (abbreviated PTV or PART) is a grammatical case which denotes "partialness", "without result", or "without specific identity". It is found in Finnic languages such as Finnish and Estonian, and its characteristic meaning is that of a part of a larger whole — roughly corresponding to English phrases such as "some water" or "a bit of bread".

Finnish

In Finnish the partitive is one of the most frequently used cases. It marks, among other things, partial or indefinite direct objects (juon vettä, "I drink [some] water"), subjects expressing indefinite quantities, the objects of negated sentences, and complements of numerals greater than one (kolme taloa, "three houses"). The choice between the partitive and total object also expresses aspect: the partitive indicates an ongoing or unbounded action, while the total object indicates a completed, resultative one. It is formed with the endings -a/-ä, -ta/-tä or -tta/-ttä depending on the word.

Other languages

Estonian has a similarly central partitive. Related functions appear elsewhere: the French partitive article (du pain, "some bread") and the partitive genitive in languages such as Russian and Latin express comparable notions without constituting a separate morphological case of the Finnic type.
