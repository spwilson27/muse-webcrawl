Household income in the United States

Household income in the United States is measured by the Census Bureau in the Current Population Survey and the American Community Survey. Median household income — the level at which half of households earn more and half less — is a commonly cited gauge of middle-class living standards.

Levels and trends

The U.S. median household income was roughly 78,000 dollars in 2023. Real (inflation-adjusted) median income rose substantially in the late 1990s and again in the late 2010s, reaching a peak around 2019, and is strongly affected by household size, the number of earners, education, age, race and geography.

Distribution

Income varies widely by state: Maryland, Massachusetts and New Jersey have among the highest medians, while Mississippi, West Virginia and Arkansas are among the lowest. Income inequality, measured by the Gini coefficient, is higher in the United States than in most other developed economies.

Measures

Analysts distinguish household, family and personal income, and mean from median values; adjustments for household size produce "equivalised" income figures used for international comparison.
