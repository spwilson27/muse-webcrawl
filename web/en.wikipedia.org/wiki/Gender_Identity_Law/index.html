Gender Identity Law

CountryArgentina
Enacted9 May 2012
Signed23 May 2012
Effective4 June 2012
SubjectLegal gender recognition

The Gender Identity Law (Spanish: Ley de Identidad de Género) is Argentine legislation passed by the National Congress on 9 May 2012 and promulgated later that month. It was the first law in the world to recognise gender identity purely on the basis of self-determination, without requiring judicial approval, medical diagnosis, surgery, or hormone therapy as preconditions for legal recognition.

Provisions

The law allows any adult to change the name and gender marker on their national identity document and birth certificate by a simple administrative procedure based on a sworn statement of their self-perceived identity. It also guarantees access to gender-affirming medical treatments — including hormone therapy and surgeries — through the public health system and mandatory coverage by insurers, without requiring a diagnosis of mental illness. Minors may access the procedures through their legal representatives with regard to the child's evolving capacity.

Impact

The Argentine law became an international reference point for legal gender recognition reform and influenced later self-determination-based laws in countries such as Denmark, Malta, Ireland, Norway, Spain and others. Thousands of people amended their documents in the law's first years, and it has been praised by international human rights bodies as a model of best practice.
