Christmas cake

Christmas cake is a type of cake, often a fruitcake, served at Christmas in many countries. In the United Kingdom and Commonwealth countries it is traditionally a rich, dark fruit cake covered with marzipan and white royal icing, decorated with festive motifs.

History and preparation

The English Christmas cake evolved from plum porridge and Twelfth Night cakes eaten in earlier centuries. A traditional cake is made well in advance — ideally in November — and periodically "fed" with brandy or rum to mature the flavour. It is then covered with a layer of marzipan (almond paste) and finished with royal icing, which sets hard and white like snow.

Around the world

In Japan, Christmas cake is an entirely different confection: a light sponge layered with whipped cream and strawberries, sold commercially since the early twentieth century and strongly associated with Christmas Eve celebrations. Other festive cakes include Italy's panettone, Germany's stollen, and Scotland's Dundee cake and black bun.

Customs

Traditionally, a coin or small charm might be baked into festive cakes for luck. In Britain, making the Christmas cake was a family occasion, with each member stirring the mixture and making a wish on "Stir-up Sunday", the last Sunday before Advent.
