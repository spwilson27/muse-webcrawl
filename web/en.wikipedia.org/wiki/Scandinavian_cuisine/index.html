Scandinavian cuisine

Scandinavian cuisine comprises the traditional foods of Denmark, Norway, and Sweden, with closely related traditions in Finland and Iceland. Shaped by a cold climate and long winters, it historically relies on preserved fish and meat, root vegetables, rye bread, dairy products, and wild berries and mushrooms.

Common features

Seafood is central: herring (pickled, marinated, or fermented, as in Swedish surströmming), salmon (cured as gravlax), and cod, including dried stockfish and Norwegian lutefisk. Meats include pork in Denmark, lamb and mutton in Norway and Iceland, and reindeer and game in the north. Dense rye breads (Danish rugbrød, Swedish crispbread) accompany open-faced sandwiches such as Danish smørrebrød.

Traditions

The Swedish smørgåsbord is a festive buffet of hot and cold dishes. Meals often feature potatoes, dill, and cardamom-scented baking, including cinnamon buns. Seasonal feasts include Swedish midsummer and crayfish parties, and Christmas tables laden with ham, pickled herring, and rice pudding.

New Nordic Cuisine

Since the early 2000s the New Nordic Cuisine movement, launched with a 2004 manifesto and exemplified by Copenhagen's Noma restaurant, has reinterpreted local, seasonal ingredients — foraging, fermentation, and wild herbs — to international acclaim.
