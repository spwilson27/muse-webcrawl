Jay Norwood Darling

Jay Norwood Darling (October 21, 1876 – February 12, 1962), better known as Ding Darling, was an American cartoonist and conservationist. He won two Pulitzer Prizes for editorial cartooning and was an influential figure in the American conservation movement.

BornOctober 21, 1876, Norwood, Michigan
DiedFebruary 12, 1962, Des Moines, Iowa
OccupationCartoonist, conservationist

Career

Darling worked for the Des Moines Register for most of his career, signing his cartoons "Ding". His work was syndicated nationally and he won the Pulitzer Prize in 1924 and 1943.

Conservation

In 1934 President Franklin D. Roosevelt appointed Darling head of the U.S. Bureau of Biological Survey, forerunner of the Fish and Wildlife Service, where he promoted the Duck Stamp program. He helped found the National Wildlife Federation in 1936. The J. N. "Ding" Darling National Wildlife Refuge on Sanibel Island, Florida, is named after him.
