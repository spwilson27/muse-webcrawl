Xbox Game Pass

Xbox Game Pass is a subscription service from Microsoft that offers access to a rotating catalogue of video games from a range of publishers and Xbox Game Studios titles on release day, for a monthly fee. The service launched on Xbox One on 1 June 2017 and expanded to Windows PC (as PC Game Pass) in 2019. It is frequently described as a "Netflix for games" model for the Xbox ecosystem.

Service

Subscribers can download and play any game in the catalogue while it remains in the library and their subscription is active, and can buy games at a discount to keep them permanently. All new games published by Xbox Game Studios, and after Microsoft's acquisitions, by ZeniMax Media and Activision Blizzard studios, are added on their release date. The Ultimate tier bundles the console and PC libraries with EA Play and Xbox Cloud Gaming (xCloud), which streams games to consoles, PCs, mobile devices and smart TVs.

History and reception

The service grew rapidly: Microsoft reported 10 million subscribers in early 2020, 25 million by January 2022 and around 34 million by 2024 including the rebranded Core tier (which replaced Xbox Live Gold in 2023). Game Pass has been credited with shifting Xbox strategy from hardware sales toward recurring subscription revenue, and it has influenced rival offerings such as PlayStation Plus's revamped tiers. Publishers and analysts have debated its effect on traditional game sales, with Microsoft arguing it expands overall engagement and spending.
