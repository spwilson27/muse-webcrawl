Ciudad Madero

Ciudad Madero is a coastal city in the southeastern part of the Mexican state of Tamaulipas, on the Gulf of Mexico. It forms part of the Tampico metropolitan area together with the neighbouring cities of Tampico and Altamira. The city is named after Francisco I. Madero, the revolutionary leader and president of Mexico.

History

The settlement grew up around petroleum installations in the early twentieth century, at the southern edge of Tampico near the mouth of the Pánuco River. It was formally established as a municipality in the 1920s and developed rapidly with Mexico's oil industry.

Economy

Ciudad Madero's economy is dominated by the petroleum industry. The Francisco I. Madero refinery, operated by the state oil company Pemex, is one of the oldest and largest refineries in Mexico and a major source of employment. Petrochemical plants, port activities, fishing, and commerce also contribute.

Tourism

Playa Miramar, a long sandy beach on the Gulf coast, is the city's principal tourist attraction and one of the most visited beaches in northeastern Mexico. The beach is also notable for the raccoons that congregate along its breakwater, and it has been a site of sea turtle nesting protection efforts.

Geography and climate

The city lies on flat coastal terrain near sea level and has a tropical climate with hot, humid summers. It is vulnerable to tropical storms and hurricanes entering the western Gulf of Mexico, and to flooding from the Pánuco River and nearby lagoons.
