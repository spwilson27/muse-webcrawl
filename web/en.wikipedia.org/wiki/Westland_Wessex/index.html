Westland Wessex

Westland Wessex
Type: Anti-submarine / utility helicopter
Manufacturer: Westland Helicopters
First flight: 20 June 1958
Status: Retired
Produced: 1958–1971

The Westland Wessex was a British-built turbine-powered development of the Sikorsky H-34 (S-58), produced under licence by Westland Aircraft (later Westland Helicopters) at Yeovil, England. Where the American original used a piston engine, the Wessex used a Napier Gazelle turboshaft (and later twin Rolls-Royce Gnome engines), giving improved performance and hot-and-high capability.

Service

The Wessex entered service with the Royal Navy in 1961 as an anti-submarine helicopter, operating from aircraft carriers including HMS Ark Royal. Variants included the HAS.1 and HAS.3 for anti-submarine warfare, the HC.2 troop transport for the Royal Air Force, the HU.5 for Royal Marines support, and the HAS.31 for the Royal Australian Navy. It saw action in the Falklands War (1982), where Wessex helicopters performed transport, anti-submarine, and special-forces roles; one famously evacuated survivors and was itself lost with HMS Glamorgan's helicopter in the war's events.

Retirement

The type was exported to Australia, Brunei, Ghana, Iraq, and Uruguay. The Royal Navy retired its Wessex in 1988 and the RAF in 2003, after more than four decades of service; the last military operator was Uruguay.
