Wall of Sound

The Wall of Sound (also called the Spector Sound) is a music production formula developed by American record producer Phil Spector at Gold Star Studios, in the 1960s, with assistance from engineer Larry Levine, the session musicians later known as "the Wrecking Crew".

Technique

The intention was to exploit the possibilities of studio recording to create an unusually dense orchestral aesthetic. Spector layered many instruments—often multiple pianos, guitars, and percussion—playing the same parts in unison, and recorded them in an echo chamber environment so that the individual sounds fused into a single massive wash. He described his productions as "little symphonies for the kids".

Recordings were made in mono, and Spector frequently had instruments double parts conventionally assigned to others, building a texture in which no single instrument stood out. Arranger Jack Nitzsche helped translate Spector's ideas into scores for the large ensembles.

Notable recordings and influence

Classic Wall of Sound productions include the Ronettes' "Be My Baby" (1963), the Crystals' "Da Doo Ron Ron" (1963), the Righteous Brothers' "You've Lost That Lovin' Feelin'" (1964), and Ike & Tina Turner's "River Deep – Mountain High" (1966). The approach influenced Brian Wilson of the Beach Boys, Bruce Springsteen (notably on "Born to Run"), and many later producers, and the phrase "wall of sound" has since been applied more generally to any densely layered production.
