Landlord–tenant law

Landlord–tenant law is the body of law governing the rental of commercial and residential property: the creation, terms and termination of leases and tenancies, and the rights and obligations of landlords and tenants. In common-law systems it descends from English property law, in which a leasehold is an estate in land, but modern practice is extensively modified by statute, especially for residential premises.

Core concepts

A lease or rental agreement grants the tenant exclusive possession of premises for a term in exchange for rent. Landlord obligations typically include providing habitable premises (the implied warranty of habitability in most US states), maintaining structure and services, respecting the tenant's covenant of quiet enjoyment, and following statutory procedures — notice periods, court process and deposit protection — before eviction. Tenant obligations include paying rent, keeping the premises in good condition, and not causing nuisance. Remedies range from damages and injunctions to eviction (unlawful detainer or possession proceedings) and, for tenants, repair-and-deduct, rent withholding or constructive-eviction claims where the law allows.

Regulation

Many jurisdictions regulate residential rents (rent control or rent stabilisation), cap or escrow security deposits, prohibit discrimination in letting (for example under the US Fair Housing Act 1968 or the UK Equality Act 2010), and restrict "no-fault" evictions: England's Housing Act 1988 created assured and assured shorthold tenancies with section 21 no-fault possession, which reform legislation in the 2020s sought to abolish; Scotland replaced its regime with open-ended private residential tenancies in 2017. In the United States, landlord–tenant law is mainly state and municipal law, influenced by the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (1972); Germany's Mietrecht in the Civil Code gives tenants strong security of tenure and limits rent increases. Commercial leases are far less regulated and are largely governed by the parties' contract.
