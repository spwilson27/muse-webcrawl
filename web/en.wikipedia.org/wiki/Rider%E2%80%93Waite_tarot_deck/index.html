Rider–Waite tarot deck

The Rider–Waite tarot deck (also known as the Waite–Smith, Rider–Waite–Smith, or Rider deck) is a widely popular deck for tarot card reading, first published in 1909 by the Rider Company. It was created by the academic and mystic Arthur Edward Waite and illustrated by Pamela Colman Smith, both members of the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn.

Innovations

The deck was revolutionary for its fully illustrated Minor Arcana: previous decks generally showed only arrangements of suit symbols (pips) on the numbered cards, whereas Smith designed narrative scenes for every card, drawing partly on the late-15th-century Sola Busca tarot, the first fully illustrated deck. Waite also swapped the positions of Strength and Justice (making them VIII and XI respectively) and renamed some trumps.

Publication and influence

The deck was published in December 1909, accompanied by Waite's guide The Pictorial Key to the Tarot (1910). Although initially a modest success, it grew enormously in popularity in the later 20th century, especially in the English-speaking world, and shaped the design of innumerable later decks. Smith's striking art-deco-influenced artwork — much of it completed in a few months for a flat fee — receives increasing credit in the alternative name "Waite–Smith."

Composition

The deck contains 78 cards: 22 Major Arcana trumps from The Fool to The World, and 56 Minor Arcana in the suits of Wands, Cups, Swords and Pentacles, each with four court cards (Page, Knight, Queen, King).
