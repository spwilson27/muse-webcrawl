The 5.56×45mm NATO (official NATO nomenclature 5.56 NATO) is a rimless, bottlenecked intermediate cartridge family developed in the late 1970s in Belgium by FN Herstal. It consists of the SS109, L110, and SS111 cartridges. It was standardized on 28 October 1980 under STANAG 4172 and is the standard service rifle cartridge for NATO forces as well as many non-NATO countries.

History

The cartridge was derived from, but not identical to, the .223 Remington cartridge developed for the ArmaLite AR-15. The original M193 loading used a 55-grain bullet. In the 1970s NATO sought a second, smaller standard rifle caliber to eventually replace 7.62×51mm; trials led to adoption of the Belgian SS109 loading with a heavier 62-grain bullet incorporating a steel penetrator, designed for the FN Minimi light machine gun and effective at longer ranges.

Performance

The 5.56×45mm cartridge offers a flat trajectory, modest recoil, and light weight, allowing soldiers to carry more ammunition than with full-power rifle cartridges. Muzzle velocities for the SS109/M855 loading from a 20-inch barrel are roughly 940 m/s (about 3,100 ft/s). Its terminal effectiveness has been debated, prompting the development of improved loadings such as the M855A1 in United States service.

Weapons

Chamberings include the M16 rifle, M4 carbine, FN Minimi / M249, Heckler & Koch G36 and HK416, Steyr AUG, FAMAS, and many other service rifles and carbines worldwide.
