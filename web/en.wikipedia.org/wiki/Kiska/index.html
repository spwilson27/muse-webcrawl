Kiska (Aleut: Qisxa) is an island in the Rat Islands group of the Aleutian Islands of Alaska. It is about 22 miles long and varies from 2 to 6 miles wide. The island is dominated by Kiska Volcano, a stratovolcano at its northern end, and has no permanent population.

History

Kiska was sighted by Vitus Bering in 1741 and was long used seasonally by Aleut (Unangax̂) people and later by Russian and American traders. During the Second World War, Japanese forces occupied Kiska on 6 June 1942 — together with Attu, the only United States incorporated territory invaded in the war. A small US Navy weather station crew was captured. After months of American bombing, the Japanese secretly evacuated the garrison under cover of fog on 28 July 1943. When some 35,000 Allied (mostly American and Canadian) troops landed on 15 August 1943 they found the island empty, though more than 300 casualties resulted from friendly fire, booby traps and accidents during the operation.

Today

The former battlefield is preserved as part of the Aleutian Islands World War II National Historic Area and the Alaska Maritime National Wildlife Refuge. Relics of the occupation, including artillery and a Japanese midget submarine, remain on the island.
