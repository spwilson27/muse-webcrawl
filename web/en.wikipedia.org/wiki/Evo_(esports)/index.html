Evo (esports)

The Evolution Championship Series, commonly known as Evo, is an annual esports event that focuses exclusively on fighting games. Held in Las Vegas, Nevada, it is the largest and longest-running fighting game tournament in the world, and is considered the most prestigious event in the fighting game community (FGC). Evo is an open tournament: anyone may enter, and unknown players can in principle defeat world champions, a format that has produced many of the genre's most famous moments.

Evolution Championship Series

Genre: Fighting games

Venue: Las Vegas, Nevada (various; Mandalay Bay / Las Vegas Convention Center)

First held: 1996 (as Battle by the Bay)

Organiser: Evo LLC (Sony / RTS since 2021)

Frequency: Annual

History

Evo began in 1996 as "Battle by the Bay", a 40-player Super Street Fighter II Turbo and Street Fighter Alpha 2 tournament organised by Tom and Tony Cannon, Joey Cuellar and Seth Killian in Sunnyvale, California. It grew into the Evolution Championship Series, relocating to Las Vegas in 2005. The 2004 tournament produced "Evo Moment 37", in which Daigo Umehara, facing Justin Wong in Street Fighter III: 3rd Strike, parried all 15 hits of Chun-Li's super art with a sliver of health remaining — the most celebrated moment in competitive gaming history.

In March 2021, Evo was acquired by a joint venture of Sony Interactive Entertainment and RTS. The in-person event returned after the COVID-19 pandemic with record attendance; Evo 2022 and subsequent editions drew tens of thousands of attendees and over 10,000 unique competitors across game titles. International sister events include Evo Japan, launched in 2018.

Format and games

Competitions are run in double-elimination brackets, typically best-of-three or best-of-five sets, with finals staged in an arena. The game line-up changes yearly and has featured titles including Street Fighter 6, Tekken 8, Guilty Gear Strive, Mortal Kombat, The King of Fighters, Super Smash Bros. (in some years), Dragon Ball FighterZ and Granblue Fantasy Versus. Beyond the main tournaments, Evo hosts community side tournaments, arcade cabinets, artist alleys, panels and game announcements, making it part convention, part world championship.

Significance

Evo is the centrepiece of the fighting game calendar, anchoring tours such as the Capcom Pro Tour and Tekken World Tour. Winning an Evo title is regarded as the highest achievement in fighting games, and the event is credited with sustaining the genre through periods when its popularity waned elsewhere.
