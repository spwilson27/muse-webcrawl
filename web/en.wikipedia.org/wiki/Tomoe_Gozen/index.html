Tomoe Gozen

Tomoe Gozen (巴 御前, c. 1157 – c. 1247) was a female samurai, or onna-musha, of late Heian and early Kamakura Japan. She is best known for fighting alongside Minamoto no Yoshinaka in the Genpei War.

According to The Tale of the Heike, Tomoe was "especially beautiful, with white skin, long hair, and charming features", and also a fearsomely strong archer and swordswoman, "a warrior worth a thousand". She served as one of Yoshinaka's captains and is said to have fought at the Battle of Awazu in 1184, where Yoshinaka was killed.

Legend

Accounts of Tomoe's later life vary: some say she was ordered by Yoshinaka to flee the battlefield, others that she was captured, became a nun, or lived to old age. Because the sources are literary rather than documentary, historians debate how much of her story is historical. She remains one of Japan's most celebrated warrior women and a frequent subject of woodblock prints, theatre, and popular culture.
