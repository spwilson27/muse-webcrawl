Nitratine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Nitratine from the Atacama Desert

Nitratine is a mineral form of sodium nitrate (NaNO3). It is also known as nitratite, soda niter, or Chile saltpeter (distinguishing it from ordinary saltpeter, potassium nitrate). The mineral is colourless to white, with a vitreous lustre, and crystallises in the trigonal system, isostructural with calcite.

FormulaNaNO3
Crystal systemTrigonal
Hardness1.5–2 (Mohs)
Specific gravity2.24–2.29
SolubilityHighly soluble in water; deliquescent

Occurrence

Because it is highly soluble, nitratine occurs only in extremely arid environments where rainfall is essentially absent. The classic deposits lie in the Atacama Desert of northern Chile (Tarapacá and Antofagasta regions), where nitrate-bearing caliche deposits were mined on an enormous scale in the 19th and early 20th centuries, fueling an export boom, mining towns, and the War of the Pacific (1879–1884) over control of the nitrate fields. Smaller occurrences are known from Peru, Bolivia, Egypt, and the southwestern United States.

Uses

Sodium nitrate was historically vital as a fertilizer and as an oxidiser in explosives and gunpowder substitutes. The development of the Haber–Bosch process for synthetic ammonia in the early 20th century undercut the natural nitrate industry, and most Chilean oficinas closed. Sodium nitrate is still used in the food industry as a preservative (E251), in pyrotechnics, and in molten-salt heat storage.
