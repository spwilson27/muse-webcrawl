RuSHA

The RuSHA, or SS Race and Settlement Main Office (German: Rasse- und Siedlungshauptamt der SS, RuSHA), was one of the principal departments of Nazi Germany's SS. Founded by Heinrich Himmler in 1931, it was responsible for enforcing the racial purity of the SS, vetting the ancestry of SS members and their brides, and—during the Second World War—for the racial screening, resettlement and "Germanisation" of populations in the occupied territories.

Origins and racial vetting

The office originated in 1931 as the SS Race Office under Richard Walther Darré. Every SS applicant had to prove "Aryan" ancestry through extensive genealogical records, and permission to marry depended on the racial examination of the bride-to-be by RuSHA officials. The office maintained racial experts (Eignungsprüfer) who classified individuals according to Nazi racial categories based on appearance, ancestry and supposed hereditary value.

Wartime role

After 1939 the RuSHA became a central instrument of Nazi population policy in occupied Europe. Working alongside the Reich Commission for the Consolidation of German Nationhood (RKFDV) and the Volksdeutsche Mittelstelle (VoMi), its experts conducted racial examinations of ethnic Germans (Volksdeutsche) for resettlement into annexed Polish territories, screened the local population for "Germanisable" individuals, and participated in selecting children deemed racially valuable for removal from their families and upbringing in the Reich. The office was thus implicated in ethnic cleansing, forced resettlement and the kidnapping of children.

RuSHA trial

After the war, fourteen leading officials of the RuSHA, RKFDV, VoMi and the Lebensborn programme were prosecuted by the United States in the RuSHA trial (1947–48), one of the subsequent Nuremberg proceedings. Thirteen were convicted of crimes against humanity and war crimes; the head of the office, Otto Hofmann, received 25 years' imprisonment, and Ulrich Greifelt of the RKFDV received life imprisonment, though most sentences were later reduced under clemency.
