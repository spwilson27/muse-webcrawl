Richard Eugene Nisbett (born June 1, 1941) is an American social psychologist, Theodore M. Newcomb Distinguished Professor of Psychology at the University of Michigan. His research concerns how people reason, the systematic errors in everyday inference, and how culture shapes patterns of thought.

Career

Nisbett received his PhD from Columbia University under Stanley Schachter and taught at Yale before moving to Michigan in 1971. With Lee Ross he wrote Human Inference: Strategies and Shortcomings of Social Judgment (1980), a landmark account of biases in attribution and reasoning, including the fundamental attribution error. His later work with Dov Cohen on the "culture of honor" in the American South (Culture of Honor, 1996) linked regional violence to norms inherited from herding economies.

Culture and cognition

In The Geography of Thought: How Asians and Westerners Think Differently — and Why (2003), Nisbett argued that East Asian and Western populations differ systematically in perception and reasoning — broadly, holistic, context-sensitive attention versus analytic, object-focused attention — tracing the difference to contrasting social systems and philosophical traditions in ancient China and Greece. The book popularised the field of cultural psychology.

Popular works

Nisbett's books for general readers include Intelligence and How to Get It (2009), which argues that intelligence is substantially malleable and environmental, and Mindware: Tools for Smart Thinking (2015), on improving everyday reasoning with statistical and logical principles.
