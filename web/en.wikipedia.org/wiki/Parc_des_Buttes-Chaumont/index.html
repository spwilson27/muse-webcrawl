Parc des Buttes-Chaumont

Location19th arrondissement, Paris, France
Area24.7 hectares (61 acres)
Opened1867
DesignerJean-Charles Adolphe Alphand

The Parc des Buttes-Chaumont is a public park in the 19th arrondissement of Paris, France. Opened in 1867 during the reign of Napoleon III, it is one of the largest green spaces in Paris, famous for its dramatic artificial cliffs, grotto, waterfalls, and a lake surrounding the Île du Belvédère.

Features

The park was built on the site of former gypsum quarries. Its centrepiece is a rocky island crowned by the Temple de la Sibylle, modelled on the Temple of Vesta at Tivoli, reached by two bridges, one of them a suspension bridge designed by Gustave Eiffel's office. A 22-metre waterfall feeds from the grotto into the lake.

History

The site had an unsavoury past: it included gypsum mines and an animal rendering area near the former gibbet of Montfaucon. Baron Haussmann's transformation of the area into a picturesque English-style landscape garden was part of the Second Empire's programme of new Parisian parks.
