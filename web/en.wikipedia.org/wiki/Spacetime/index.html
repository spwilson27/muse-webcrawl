Spacetime

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Rubber-sheet style illustration of a mass curving a grid of spacetime, with a smaller object orbiting]
A mass curving spacetime (a two-dimensional analogy).

In physics, spacetime is a mathematical model that combines the three dimensions of space with the one dimension of time into a single four-dimensional continuum. The concept, introduced by Hermann Minkowski in 1908 ('Henceforth space by itself, and time by itself, are doomed to fade away into mere shadows, and only a kind of union of the two will preserve an independent reality'), is the arena of both special relativity — where spacetime is flat — and general relativity — where it is curved by mass and energy and itself acts on matter.

Events are points in spacetime; the interval between them, invariant under Lorentz transformations, classifies their separation as timelike, lightlike or spacelike, encoding causality: no influence travels outside the light cone. In general relativity the metric of spacetime obeys Einstein's field equations, making geometry dynamic — expanding in cosmology, rippling with gravitational waves, and collapsing into black holes. Whether spacetime is fundamental or emerges from quantum entanglement is a central question of Quantum gravity.

Minkowski space

The flat spacetime of special relativity is Minkowski space, with metric signature (−,+,+,+): the invariant interval Δs² = −c²Δt² + Δx² + Δy² + Δz². Observers disagree about distances and durations separately (length contraction, Time dilation) but agree on the interval — the geometrical fact from which the twin paradox and E = mc² follow.
