The Treaty of San Stefano was a treaty between the Russian Empire and the Ottoman Empire signed on 3 March 1878 at San Stefano (modern Yeşilköy), a village west of Constantinople. It ended the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–1878 on terms highly favorable to Russia and its Balkan allies.

Terms

• The Ottoman Empire recognized the full independence of Romania, Serbia, and Montenegro, each with territorial gains.
• A large, autonomous Principality of Bulgaria was created under nominal Ottoman suzerainty, stretching from the Danube to the Aegean Sea and including most of Macedonia—"Big Bulgaria".
• Bosnia and Herzegovina were to receive reforms.
• Russia gained territory in the Caucasus, including Kars, Ardahan, and Batum, and regained southern Bessarabia; Romania received Northern Dobruja in compensation for its own loss of southern Bessarabia.
• The Ottomans agreed to pay a large war indemnity.

Reaction and revision

The creation of a large Russian client state in the Balkans alarmed Britain and Austria-Hungary, which feared the collapse of the balance of power and Russian access to the Mediterranean. Under pressure—including the arrival of a British fleet at Constantinople—the treaty was substantially revised four months later by the Treaty of Berlin (1878) agreed at the Congress of Berlin. Bulgaria was divided into a smaller principality, the autonomous province of Eastern Rumelia, and a Macedonia returned to direct Ottoman rule.

Legacy

San Stefano remains a landmark date in Bulgarian history: 3 March is Bulgaria's national liberation day. The treaty's unfulfilled "Big Bulgaria" became a touchstone of Bulgarian national ambition, while its reversal at Berlin is remembered as a national grievance that shaped Balkan politics into the twentieth century.
