Paisley (Scots: Paisley; Scottish Gaelic: Pàislig) is a large town in the west central Lowlands of Scotland. Located north of the Gleniffer Braes and about 7 miles (11 km) west-southwest of Glasgow, it is the administrative centre of the Renfrewshire council area and historically the county town of Renfrewshire. Paisley is one of Scotland's largest settlements by population.

History

The town grew around Paisley Abbey, founded in the 12th century on a site associated with the 6th/7th-century Irish saint Mirin, whose chapel became a pilgrimage centre. William Wallace is traditionally said to have been educated by the abbey's monks. Paisley became famous in the 19th century for its weaving industry and the Paisley pattern — the teardrop motif (boteh) copied from Kashmiri shawls and reproduced on the town's Jacquard looms — which took the town's name around the world. Thread mills of J. & P. Coats made Paisley a global centre of cotton thread manufacture, and the town was a noted centre of Chartism and working-class radicalism, including the "Radical War" of 1820.

Today

The town is home to the University of the West of Scotland's Paisley campus, Paisley Museum, and Coats Observatory. St Mirren Football Club, nicknamed "the Buddies" (a Scots term for Paisley residents), plays at St Mirren Park. Paisley was shortlisted for UK City of Culture 2021.
