The 1994 Swedish European Union membership referendum was a consultative referendum held in Sweden on November 13, 1994. Voters were asked whether Sweden should join the European Union under the terms negotiated with the EU. A narrow majority, 52.3 percent, voted yes, against 46.8 percent voting no, and Sweden entered the European Union on January 1, 1995, together with Austria and Finland.

Campaign

Sweden had applied for membership in 1991 as the Cold War order dissolved and an economic crisis weakened confidence in an independent course. The yes side, backed by the governing parties, much of business and major newspapers, argued that membership would secure export markets and influence. The no side warned against loss of neutrality, democratic distance, threats to welfare policy and environmental standards; opposition was strongest in northern and rural districts.

Aftermath

The Riksdag ratified accession after the vote. Sweden remained outside the euro after rejecting adoption in a separate 2003 referendum, but participated in the single market, Schengen cooperation and most EU policies. The closeness of the 1994 result left a durable Eurosceptic current in Swedish politics.
