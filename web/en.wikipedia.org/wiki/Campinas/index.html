Campinas is a Brazilian municipality in the interior of the state of São Paulo, about 96 kilometres northwest of the state capital. With a population of roughly 1.2 million, it is one of the largest cities in Brazil and the centre of a major metropolitan region.

History

Campinas was founded in 1774 as a stop on the route to the gold mines of Goiás. During the nineteenth century it became a centre of coffee production, worked first by enslaved labour and later by European immigrants; the coffee wealth financed railways, theatres and the city's grand cathedral. In the twentieth century its economy diversified into industry and services.

Economy and education

Today Campinas is a significant technology and research hub, sometimes called Brazil's Silicon Valley. It hosts the University of Campinas (Unicamp), one of the country's leading research universities, and technology firms cluster around the university and research institutes. Viracopos International Airport, near the city, is a major cargo hub.

Culture

The city is associated with the composer Carlos Gomes, Brazil's most famous opera composer, and fields two historic football clubs, Ponte Preta and Guarani, whose rivalry is known as the Dérbi Campineiro.
