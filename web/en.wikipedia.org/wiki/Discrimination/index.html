Discrimination

Discrimination
DefinitionDifferential treatment based on group membership or perceived characteristics
Common groundsRace, ethnicity, sex, gender, age, disability, religion, sexual orientation
Key instrumentsUniversal Declaration of Human Rights; national anti-discrimination laws

Discrimination is the process of making unjustified distinctions between people based on the groups, classes, or other categories to which they belong or are perceived to belong, such as race, gender, age, class, religion, disability, or sexual orientation. Discriminatory behaviour takes many forms, from individual prejudice to institutional practices and laws, and it is prohibited by the anti-discrimination legislation of many countries and by international human rights law.

Types

Direct discrimination occurs when a person is treated less favourably than others in a comparable situation because of a protected characteristic. Indirect discrimination arises when an apparently neutral rule or practice disproportionately disadvantages a group. Institutional or structural discrimination refers to patterns embedded in the norms and procedures of organisations and societies. Discrimination in employment, housing, education, and access to services has been a major focus of civil-rights movements worldwide.

Legal frameworks

Article 7 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights affirms that all are equal before the law and entitled to equal protection against discrimination. Landmark national measures include the United States Civil Rights Act of 1964, which outlawed discrimination based on race, colour, religion, sex, or national origin in employment and public accommodations, and the United Kingdom's Equality Act 2010.
