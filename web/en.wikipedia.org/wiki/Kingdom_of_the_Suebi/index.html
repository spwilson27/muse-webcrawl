Kingdom of the Suebi

Kingdom of the Suebi Capital: Bracara Augusta (Braga)
Period: 409–585
People: Suebi (Germanic)
Religion: Arian Christianity, then Catholicism
Region: Gallaecia, north-west Iberian Peninsula

The Kingdom of the Suebi, also known as the Galician Kingdom, was a post-Roman Germanic kingdom established in the province of Gallaecia in the north-west of the Iberian Peninsula. Founded by the Suebi under King Hermeric in 409/411, it was one of the first kingdoms to break away from the Roman Empire, and it survived for roughly 170 years until it was conquered by the Visigothic king Leovigild in 585.

History

The Suebi crossed the Rhine in 406 together with Vandals and Alans, and reached Hispania in 409. By an agreement of 411 they settled in Gallaecia, with their capital at Bracara Augusta (modern Braga). Under King Rechiar (448–456) the Suebi expanded across much of the peninsula and became the first Germanic kings in the west to convert to Catholic Christianity, although the kingdom soon returned to Arianism.

After Rechiar's defeat and death at the hands of the Visigoths in 456 the kingdom was reduced to its Gallaecian heartland and passed through a shadowy period of competing kings. In the 550s King Chararic and his successors restored Catholicism, a conversion associated in chronicle tradition with the missionary Martin of Braga and the healing of the king's son.

Fall

The last kings, including Andeca, resisted the expanding Visigothic kingdom, but in 585 Leovigild invaded, deposed Andeca and annexed the Suebian realm, ending its independence. The territory thereafter formed part of the Visigothic kingdom, though Suebian identity persisted in Galicia for some time.

The principal narrative source for the kingdom is the chronicle of Hydatius, bishop of Aquae Flaviae, who recorded events in Gallaecia in the 5th century.
