Dial House is a large cottage in south-west Essex, England, located between Epping and North Weald Bassett. Since 1967 it has been home to an "open house" anarchist-pacifist intentional community, founded by the artist, writer and musician Penny Rimbaud. The house takes its name from a sundial on its gable. It is best known as the home of members of the anarcho-punk band Crass and as the base of the independent record label Crass Records.

History

Rimbaud (born Jeremy Ratter) moved into the 16th-century cottage in 1967 and established it as an open house, welcoming travellers, artists and drop-outs. In the 1970s the collective hosted the performance art group EXIT and the free-festival culture around the Stonehenge Free Festival. In 1977 residents including Rimbaud and Steve Ignorant formed Crass, one of the defining bands of anarcho-punk; the Dial House garden, studios and grounds became the band's headquarters and a hub of DIY culture and peace activism.

The community today

Dial House continues to function as a radical creative space, hosting events, exhibitions and residencies, and remains closely associated with anarchist pacifism, animal rights and anti-war activism. The surrounding land is managed with an emphasis on wildlife and permaculture.
