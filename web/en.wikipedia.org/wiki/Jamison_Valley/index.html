The Jamison Valley is a broad valley in the Blue Mountains of New South Wales, Australia, lying immediately south of the town of Katoomba. Its forested floor, ringed by sandstone cliffs, forms part of the Greater Blue Mountains Area UNESCO World Heritage Site, and it is one of the principal scenic landscapes of the Blue Mountains National Park.

Geography

The valley was carved by the Jamison Creek and Kedumba River system into the Triassic sandstone plateau of the Blue Mountains. From vantage points such as Echo Point at Katoomba, visitors look across the valley to the Three Sisters rock formation, Mount Solitary and the Ruined Castle. The valley floor lies several hundred metres below the clifftop towns, and much of it is covered in eucalypt forest, including tall blue gum forest in the sheltered gullies.

History and tourism

The valley carries the name of Sir John Jamison (1776–1844), a prominent early colonial landowner and physician who owned property in the area. Aboriginal people of the Gundungurra and Darug nations have long associations with the valley and its surrounding country.

Tourism developed from the late 19th century as the railway brought visitors to Katoomba. Attractions include the Scenic World complex near the clifftop, where the Scenic Railway — originally built for coal and shale mining — descends at a steep gradient into the valley, and the Scenic Skyway cable car crosses the gorge near Katoomba Falls. An extensive network of walking tracks descends into the valley, including the Giant Stairway beside the Three Sisters and the Federal Pass track, and longer routes cross the valley floor to Mount Solitary and the Ruined Castle.
