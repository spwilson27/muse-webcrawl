The 1988 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXIV Olympiad and commonly known as Seoul 1988, were an international multi-sport event held from 17 September to 2 October 1988 in Seoul, South Korea. They were the second Summer Olympic Games held in Asia (after Tokyo 1964) and the first held in a developing country. With 159 nations participating, the Games marked the return of both the Soviet Union and the United States to the same Olympics after successive boycotts in 1980 and 1984, although North Korea, Cuba, Ethiopia and Nicaragua boycotted.

Highlights

The Games are remembered for some of the most dramatic moments in Olympic athletics. Canadian sprinter Ben Johnson won the 100 metres in a world-record 9.79 seconds, but was disqualified three days later after testing positive for the anabolic steroid stanozolol; the gold medal passed to Carl Lewis. Florence Griffith Joyner of the United States won three gold medals and set 100 m and 200 m world records that still stand. In the pool, Matt Biondi won seven medals, Kristin Otto of East Germany won six golds, and Janet Evans emerged as a teenage star. Greg Louganis won both springboard and platform diving golds despite striking his head on the springboard during the preliminaries.

Medal table

The Soviet Union topped the medal table in its final Summer Olympics, with 55 gold medals and 132 medals overall, followed by East Germany (37 golds) in its own final Games, and the United States (36 golds). Host nation South Korea achieved its best-ever result, finishing fourth with 12 gold medals. Table tennis and tennis were introduced (tennis returning after 64 years), and demonstration sports included taekwondo, which later became a full Olympic sport.

Political context

The award of the Games to Seoul in 1981 was a milestone in South Korea's emergence as an economic power and helped accelerate its transition toward democracy in 1987–88. Extensive efforts to co-host events with North Korea failed, and Pyongyang boycotted. A dark prelude occurred in November 1987 when North Korean agents planted a bomb aboard Korean Air Flight 858, killing all 115 aboard, in an attempt widely seen as intended to disrupt the Games. Security held, however, and the Games proceeded peacefully, leaving Seoul with major new infrastructure including the Olympic Park and an expanded subway and airport.
