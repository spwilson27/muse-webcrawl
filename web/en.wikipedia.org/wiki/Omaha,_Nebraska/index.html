Omaha, Nebraska

Omaha is the largest city in the U.S. state of Nebraska and the county seat of Douglas County. Located on the Missouri River across from Council Bluffs, Iowa, it had a population of about 486,000 at the 2020 census, anchoring a metropolitan area of roughly one million. Founded in 1854, Omaha grew as a railroad and meatpacking hub and remains a significant transportation, finance, and insurance center.

History

The city was founded by speculators from Council Bluffs after the Kansas–Nebraska Act created Nebraska Territory, whose first capital Omaha became. The Union Pacific Railroad, chartered to build the first transcontinental railroad eastward from Omaha in 1863, and the vast Omaha Stockyards (founded 1883) drove growth; by the mid-20th century Omaha rivaled Chicago in meatpacking. The city hosted the Trans-Mississippi Exposition of 1898 and was the birthplace of Malcolm X and President Gerald R. Ford.

Economy

Omaha is headquarters to Berkshire Hathaway, led by investor Warren Buffett, as well as Union Pacific, Mutual of Omaha, and Kiewit. Offutt Air Force Base, home to U.S. Strategic Command, lies just south in Bellevue. Cultural landmarks include the Henry Doorly Zoo and Aquarium, the Joslyn Art Museum, and the College World Series, held annually in Omaha since 1950.
