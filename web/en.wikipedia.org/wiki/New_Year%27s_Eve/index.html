New Year's Eve is the last day of the year, 31 December in the Gregorian calendar. It is celebrated around the world with social gatherings, countdowns to midnight, fireworks and parties marking the transition to the New Year.

Celebrations

Major public celebrations include the ball drop in New York's Times Square (held since 1907), fireworks over Sydney Harbour, London's display along the Thames, and festivities at the Brandenburg Gate in Berlin. Television broadcasts of countdown events are a staple in many countries.

In Scotland, Hogmanay is a major festival with traditions such as first-footing — the first visitor after midnight bringing symbolic gifts of coal, shortbread or whisky. In Spain, revellers eat twelve grapes as the clock strikes midnight; in Denmark people leap from chairs at midnight.

Religious and cultural observances

Many Christians attend watchnight services on New Year's Eve, a tradition with roots in Methodism and in African American churches marking the Emancipation Proclamation's taking effect on 1 January 1863.

The evening also concludes the Christmas season in many cultures and is a peak night for restaurants, clubs and public transport services running through the night.
