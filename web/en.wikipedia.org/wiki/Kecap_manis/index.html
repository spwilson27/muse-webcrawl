Kecap manis (pronounced roughly "keh-chap MAH-nees"), also spelt ketjap manis, is a sweetened, aromatic soy sauce originating in Indonesia. Thick and syrupy, with the dark colour and slow pour of molasses, it is sweetened generously with palm sugar and flavoured with aromatics such as star anise, galangal, lemongrass, garlic and kaffir lime leaves.

Use

Kecap manis is a cornerstone of Indonesian cooking. It gives nasi goreng (fried rice) and mie goreng their characteristic sweetness and colour, glazes grilled satay and ikan bakar, and appears in dishes such as semur (braised stew), babi kecap (pork in soy sauce) and ayam kecap. Mixed with lime juice and chopped bird's eye chillies and shallots it becomes sambal kecap, a common dipping sauce. It is estimated that kecap manis accounts for the great majority of soy sauce consumed in Indonesia.

History

Soy sauce (kecap, from a Hokkien Chinese word related to "ketchup") was brought to the archipelago by Chinese traders and adapted to local tastes with abundant palm sugar. Production became a speciality of towns such as Majalengka and Kediri; well-known brands include Bango (founded 1928) and ABC. During the colonial era ketjap manis also entered Dutch cooking through rijsttafel cuisine, and it remains common in the Netherlands.
