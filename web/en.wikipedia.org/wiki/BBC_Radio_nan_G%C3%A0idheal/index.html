BBC Radio nan Gàidheal

BBC Radio nan Gàidheal is a Scottish radio station owned and operated by the BBC, broadcasting entirely in Scottish Gaelic. Based in Stornoway on the Isle of Lewis, it is the world's only full-time Gaelic radio service and a central institution of Gaelic-language media alongside the television channel BBC Alba.

History

Gaelic broadcasting on the BBC began in 1923, but a dedicated service emerged with Radio Highland and the programme strand Fàilte air an Dùthaich in the 1970s and 1980s. Radio nan Gàidheal was launched in 1985 and expanded to a full-day schedule, later becoming available across Scotland on FM, DAB, digital television, and online.

Programming

The station broadcasts news and current affairs (including Aithris na Maidne), traditional and contemporary Gaelic music, religious programming, sports coverage including shinty, and children's and educational content. Its flagship morning and drivetime programmes mix speech and music for a bilingual audience.

Audience

Surveys have repeatedly found that a large share of fluent Gaelic speakers listen regularly, giving the station an unusually high reach within its speech community, and it plays a recognised role in language maintenance and revitalization.
