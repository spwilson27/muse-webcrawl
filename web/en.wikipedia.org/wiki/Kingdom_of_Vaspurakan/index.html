Kingdom of Vaspurakan

Kingdom of Vaspurakan Capital: Van
Period: 908–1021
Ruling house: Artsruni
Religion: Armenian Apostolic Church
Region: South of Lake Van, historical Armenia

The Kingdom of Vaspurakan was a medieval Armenian kingdom centred on the region of Vaspurakan, south and east of Lake Van in the Armenian Highlands. Ruled by the Artsruni dynasty, it existed from 908, when Gagik Artsruni was crowned king, until 1021, when its last king, Senekerim-Hovhannes, ceded the kingdom to the Byzantine Empire in the face of invasions by Turkic raiders.

History

Vaspurakan had been a principality of the Artsruni family since late antiquity. In 908 Gagik I Artsruni, encouraged by the Sajid emir of Azerbaijan, had himself crowned king in rivalry with the Bagratuni king Smbat I of Ani. The two Armenian kingdoms were later reconciled, and Vaspurakan became a flourishing, semi-autonomous realm nominally subordinate to Ani.

Under Gagik and his successors the kingdom enjoyed a golden age of building and art. The Church of the Holy Cross (915–921) on the island of Akdamar in Lake Van, built by the architect Manuel, is famous for its elaborate exterior reliefs depicting biblical scenes, and remains the most celebrated monument of Vaspurakan.

End of the kingdom

In the early 11th century the region was raided by Turkic groups pressing in from the east. Unable to defend his realm, King Senekerim-Hovhannes negotiated the cession of Vaspurakan to Emperor Basil II in 1021, receiving in exchange estates around Sebasteia (Sivas) in Cappadocia, to which he migrated with some 14,000 families. Vaspurakan became the Byzantine theme of Basprakania, and a generation later passed to the Seljuk Turks after the Battle of Manzikert in 1071.
