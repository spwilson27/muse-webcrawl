Corruption

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Corruption is a form of dishonesty or a criminal offence undertaken by a person or organisation entrusted with a position of authority, in order to acquire illicit benefits or abuse power for one's own private gain. Transparency International's widely used definition is "the abuse of entrusted power for private gain". Corruption may involve activities like bribery, influence peddling, embezzlement, nepotism, cronyism and fraud, and it may facilitate further criminal enterprise such as drug trafficking, money laundering and human trafficking.

Forms and measurement

Analysts distinguish petty (administrative, everyday) from grand corruption (high-level policy capture and kleptocracy — rule by thieves — in which elites loot state assets at scale), and systemic corruption, where corrupt networks are the operating system rather than the exception. Measurement relies on perception indices (Transparency International's CPI), experience surveys, audits and forensic indicators; all are imperfect, and corruption estimates (often quoted as several percent of global GDP) are inherently uncertain.

Causes and control

Corruption flourishes with monopoly, discretion and low accountability (Klitgaard's formula: C = M + D − A), and is combated through transparency (open data, asset declarations, free media), independent anti-corruption agencies (Hong Kong's ICAC and Singapore's CPIB are the classic successes), judicial and electoral integrity, procurement reform and international instruments (OECD Anti-Bribery Convention 1997; UN Convention against Corruption 2003). Whether corruption "greases the wheels" of bureaucracy was debated in older development literature; the modern consensus finds it broadly corrosive of growth, trust and state legitimacy.
