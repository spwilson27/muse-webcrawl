Ethnic cleansing of Georgians in Abkhazia

The ethnic cleansing of Georgians in Abkhazia, also known in Georgia as the genocide of Georgians in Abkhazia, refers to the mass expulsion and killings of ethnic Georgians living in Abkhazia during and after the War in Abkhazia (1992–1993) and again in 1998. Between 200,000 and 250,000 Georgians were displaced from their homes, and thousands were killed, in events that the OSCE and other bodies have described as ethnic cleansing.

Background

Abkhazia, an autonomous republic within Soviet Georgia on the Black Sea coast, was ethnically mixed: the 1989 Soviet census recorded Georgians as about 45.7 percent of its population and ethnic Abkhaz as about 17.8 percent. As the Soviet Union collapsed, tensions between the Abkhaz separatist leadership, backed by volunteers from the North Caucasus and elements of the Russian military, and the Georgian government escalated into open war in August 1992.

Events of 1992–1993

During the war, both sides committed atrocities, but after Abkhaz forces and their allies captured Sukhumi on 27 September 1993, massacres of Georgian civilians and officials followed, including the killing of members of the pro-Georgian Abkhaz government. The fall of Sukhumi triggered the flight of nearly the entire Georgian population across the Inguri River, many crossing the Greater Caucasus on foot through the Svaneti mountains, where hundreds died of exposure. Pogroms and expulsions continued in the Gali, Ochamchire and Sukhumi districts.

1998 Gali events

Tens of thousands of Georgians who had spontaneously returned to the Gali district were driven out again in May 1998 when fighting between Georgian guerrillas and Abkhaz forces led to renewed burning of villages and killings.

Aftermath and recognition

The displaced population remains one of the largest unresolved refugee situations in the South Caucasus; as of the 2020s most internally displaced Georgians have not been able to return. The OSCE summit declarations of Budapest (1994) and Lisbon (1996) condemned the "ethnic cleansing" in Abkhazia. Georgia officially commemorates the victims, while the Abkhaz authorities dispute the framing and point to Georgian atrocities during the initial phase of the war.
