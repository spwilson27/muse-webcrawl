Carnegie Mellon University (CMU) is a private research university in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, United States. Founded in 1900 by industrialist Andrew Carnegie as the Carnegie Technical Schools, it became the Carnegie Institute of Technology in 1912 and merged with the Mellon Institute of Industrial Research in 1967 to form the present university.

Academics

CMU comprises seven colleges and independent schools, including the College of Engineering, the School of Computer Science, the Tepper School of Business, the College of Fine Arts, and the Heinz College. It is especially renowned for computer science, robotics (home to the first Robotics Institute, founded 1979), artificial intelligence, engineering, drama, and design.

Research and influence

Faculty and alumni include numerous Turing Award and Nobel laureates; pioneers such as Herbert A. Simon and Allen Newell founded the study of artificial intelligence there. The university is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity" and operates branch campuses including one in Qatar.

Campus life

The Tartans compete in NCAA Division III. Traditions include the Spring Carnival, buggy races (Sweepstakes), and "The Fence," painted by student groups since 1923.
