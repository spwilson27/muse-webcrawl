Charter for the Kingdom of the Netherlands

Signed28 October 1954
PartiesNetherlands, Aruba, Curaçao, Sint Maarten
TypeConstitutional document

The Charter for the Kingdom of the Netherlands (Dutch: Statuut voor het Koninkrijk der Nederlanden) is the constitutional framework of the Kingdom of the Netherlands, a federation of four constituent countries: the Netherlands, Aruba, Curaçao and Sint Maarten. It stands above the constitutions of the individual countries.

History

The Charter was signed in 1954, originally establishing a new constitutional order between the Netherlands, Suriname and the Netherlands Antilles. Suriname left the Kingdom on independence in 1975, Aruba gained separate status in 1986, and the Netherlands Antilles were dissolved in 2010, bringing Curaçao and Sint Maarten to country status.

Contents

The Charter regulates affairs of the Kingdom as a whole, including defence, foreign relations, and Dutch nationality, and the mutual relations between the countries.
