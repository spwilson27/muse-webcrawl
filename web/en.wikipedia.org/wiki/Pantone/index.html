Pantone LLC is an American company best known for the Pantone Matching System (PMS), a standardized color reproduction system used across graphic design, printing, fashion, product design, and manufacturing. By assigning each color a unique number, the system lets designers and printers ensure that colors match exactly regardless of the equipment or location used to produce them.

History

Pantone began as a commercial printing company in New Jersey in the 1950s. Lawrence Herbert, an employee who had studied chemistry, systematized the company's pigment stocks and ink production; he bought the business in 1962 and launched the first Pantone Matching System guide in 1963, with an initial set of standardized colors printed as fan books. The system reduced the chaos of color specification: instead of describing a color, a designer could simply cite a number such as "PMS 032."

In 2007 Pantone was acquired by X-Rite, a color measurement company, for $180 million; X-Rite itself was later acquired by Danaher Corporation.

Color of the Year

Since 2000, the Pantone Color Institute has announced an annual "Color of the Year," a selection intended to reflect and influence trends in fashion, design, and consumer products. Choices such as Cerulean (2000), Ultra Violet (2018), Living Coral (2019), and Viva Magenta (2023) have received wide media coverage.

Intellectual property

Pantone asserts trademark and proprietary rights over its color system and libraries. In 2022, a dispute with Adobe led to Pantone color libraries being removed from Adobe Creative Cloud applications unless users purchased a separate Pantone license, a change widely criticized in the design community.
