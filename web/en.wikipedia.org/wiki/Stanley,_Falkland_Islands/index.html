Stanley (also known as Port Stanley) is the capital city of the Falkland Islands, a British Overseas Territory in the South Atlantic Ocean. It is located on the island of East Falkland, on a north-facing slope in one of the wettest parts of the islands, around the sheltered harbour of Stanley Harbour. The great majority of the islands' population lives in Stanley.

History

The settlement was founded in 1843, when the capital was moved from Port Louis, and it was named after Lord Stanley, Secretary of State for War and the Colonies. Stanley developed as a port for ship repairs — many vessels rounding Cape Horn called in damaged — and later for the wool trade and fishing.

During the Falklands War of 1982, Stanley was occupied by Argentine forces for about ten weeks, from 2 April until the Argentine surrender on 14 June 1982. Argentina, which claims the islands as the Islas Malvinas, called the town Puerto Argentino. After the war, the town expanded with new housing, and minefields laid around it took decades to clear; the last were removed by 2020.

Features

Landmarks include Christ Church Cathedral, consecrated in 1892, with its whalebone arch outside; Government House; the Falkland Islands Museum; and the 1982 Liberation Memorial. The town's pubs, hotels, and shops serve residents, tourists — many arriving on cruise ships — and the British garrison at nearby Mount Pleasant.

Climate

Stanley has a subpolar oceanic climate, with cool summers, mild winters for its latitude, frequent wind, and precipitation spread throughout the year.
