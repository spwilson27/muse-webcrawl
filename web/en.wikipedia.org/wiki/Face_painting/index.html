Face painting

Face painting is the artistic application of cosmetic paint to a person's face for entertainment, ritual, theatrical, or cultural purposes. Unlike permanent body art, face paint is temporary and water-based, designed to be washed off.

Traditions

Painting the face is ancient and worldwide: Indigenous peoples have used natural pigments for ceremony, hunting, and war paint; Indian Kathakali performers wear elaborate painted masks of colour; Japanese kabuki actors use kumadori makeup; and corpse paint is a hallmark of black metal stage performance. Military camouflage face paint serves the practical purpose of breaking up the outline of the face.

Modern entertainment

At fairs, festivals, birthday parties, and sporting events, professional face painters decorate children's faces with animals, butterflies, superheroes, and tigers, while football fans paint team colours. Cosmetic-grade, FDA-compliant water-activated paints, sponges, and brushes are standard; acrylic craft paints should not be used on skin.

Techniques

Base coats are sponged on, details are added with round brushes, and one-stroke cakes combine several colours in a single brush load for petals and rainbows. Airbrush face painting is used for large events.
