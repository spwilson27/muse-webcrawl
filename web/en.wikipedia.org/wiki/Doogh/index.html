Doogh

Doogh (Persian: dugh) is a cold, savoury yogurt-based beverage popular across Iran, Afghanistan, Azerbaijan, Armenia, Turkey (as ayran), and the wider Middle East, Caucasus, and Central Asia. It is made by mixing yogurt with water (still or sparkling), salt, and often flavourings such as dried mint.

Preparation

Doogh is prepared by diluting strained or plain yogurt with water to a drinkable consistency, adding salt, and whisking or shaking until frothy. Commercial versions are sold both still and carbonated; the carbonated variety, long popular in Iran, has a sharp, tangy taste. Local variations may add cucumber, rose petals, or other herbs. Similar drinks include Turkish ayran, Armenian tan, and Indian salted lassi and chaas.

Cultural significance

In Iran, doogh is a traditional accompaniment to kebabs and other grilled meats, valued for its cooling effect in a hot climate. It is produced industrially in large volumes as well as made at home. Traditional doogh was stored and churned in goatskin bags, a method still associated with nomadic peoples of the region.
