Photograph

A photograph (also known as a photo, image, or picture) is an image created by light falling on a photosensitive surface, usually photographic film or an electronic image sensor such as a CCD or CMOS chip. Most photographs are now created using a smartphone or camera, which uses a lens to focus the scene's visible wavelengths of light into a reproduction on a surface of what the human eye would see. The process and practice of creating such images is called photography.

Etymology

The word "photography" was coined in 1839 by Sir John Herschel from the Greek phōs (light) and graphē (drawing), meaning "drawing with light."

History

The first permanent photograph, Nicéphore Niépce's View from the Window at Le Gras (c. 1826/27), was made by heliography using a bitumen-coated pewter plate. In 1839 Louis Daguerre's daguerreotype process—sharp images on silvered copper plates—was announced in France, while William Henry Fox Talbot developed the calotype, a paper negative from which many positive prints could be made. Wet collodion, gelatin dry plates, and roll film (Kodak, 1888) successively made photography faster, portable, and popular. Colour photography became widespread in the 20th century, and digital sensors displaced film for most uses in the 2000s.

Types

Photographs may be produced as negatives or positives, on paper, glass, metal, or screens. Genres include portrait, landscape, documentary, photojournalism, fine-art, scientific (such as X-ray and astronomical images), and aerial photography. Monochrome (black-and-white) and colour are the principal modes; other processes record infrared or ultraviolet light.

Preservation and law

Photographs are fragile records: prints fade through light, heat, and chemical instability, prompting archival standards for storage. Legally, the photographer generally holds copyright in a photograph, and photographs of people raise rights of privacy and publicity that vary by jurisdiction.

Cultural significance

Photographs shape memory, journalism, science, and art. Iconic images—from Civil War battlefields to the "Blue Marble" of Earth—have influenced public opinion and historical consciousness. The rise of smartphones has made photography ubiquitous, with trillions of photographs now taken each year.
