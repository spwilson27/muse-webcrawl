Fundamental theorem of Galois theory

Fundamental theorem of Galois theory
FieldAbstract algebra
StatementBijection between intermediate fields and subgroups of the Galois group
Named forÉvariste Galois

In mathematics, the fundamental theorem of Galois theory is a result that describes the structure of certain types of field extensions in terms of groups. It was proved by Évariste Galois in his work on the solvability of polynomial equations by radicals, and it is a central result of Galois theory.

The theorem establishes a one-to-one correspondence (the Galois correspondence) between the intermediate fields of a Galois field extension and the subgroups of its Galois group. This allows problems about fields to be translated into problems about groups, which are often easier to understand.

Statement

Let E/F be a finite Galois extension with Galois group G = Gal(E/F). Then there is a bijection between the set of intermediate fields K with F ⊆ K ⊆ E and the set of subgroups H of G, given by mapping K to the subgroup of automorphisms fixing K pointwise, and mapping H to its fixed field.

Properties of the correspondence

  • The correspondence is inclusion-reversing: larger subgroups correspond to smaller fields.
  • An intermediate field is a normal extension of F if and only if the corresponding subgroup is a normal subgroup of G; in that case the Galois group of the intermediate field over F is the quotient group.
  • The degree of the field extension equals the index of the corresponding subgroup.

Applications

The theorem is used to prove that there is no general formula using radicals for solving polynomial equations of degree five or higher (the Abel–Ruffini theorem), and to classify constructible polygons. It also underlies the modern treatment of field extensions in algebra.
