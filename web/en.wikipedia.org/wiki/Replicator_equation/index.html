Replicator equation

The replicator equation is a deterministic monotone nonlinear dynamical system used in evolutionary game theory. It describes how the frequencies of strategies in a population change over time according to their relative fitness (payoff).

Equation

For strategies i with frequency xi and fitness fi(x), the continuous-time replicator equation is ẋi = xi(fi(x) − φ(x)), where φ(x) = Σ xj fj(x) is the mean fitness of the population. Strategies performing above average grow; those below average shrink.

History and analysis

The equation was introduced by Taylor and Jonker in 1978. It is closely related to the Lotka–Volterra equations and to natural selection models in population genetics. Rest points include evolutionarily stable strategies (ESS), and replicator dynamics underlies much of the analysis of learning and cultural evolution. Discrete-time and stochastic variants are widely used in simulations.
