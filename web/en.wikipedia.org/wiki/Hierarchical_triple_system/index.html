Hierarchical triple system

A hierarchical triple system is a system of three gravitationally bound bodies — usually stars — arranged in a nested configuration: two bodies form a close inner binary, and that binary together with a third, more distant body forms a wider outer binary. The arrangement is described by an inner orbital period much shorter than the outer one, typically by a factor of at least five to ten, which makes the hierarchy dynamically stable over long timescales.

Dynamics

Because the inner and outer orbits are well separated, the system can be approximated as two nested Keplerian orbits that slowly perturb one another. Over long periods the outer body can drive oscillations in the eccentricity and inclination of the inner binary through the Kozai–Lidov mechanism, which can push the inner pair to very high eccentricities, triggering tidal interaction, mass transfer or mergers. Secular dynamics in triples are thought to play a role in forming close binaries, blue stragglers, and some types of supernovae and gravitational-wave sources.

Examples

Well-known hierarchical triples include Algol, the prototype eclipsing system; Alpha Centauri, in which Proxima Centauri orbits the inner Alpha Centauri A–B pair; and HD 188753. Surveys suggest that a substantial fraction of multiple-star systems are hierarchical, because random non-hierarchical triples are unstable and quickly eject one member.
