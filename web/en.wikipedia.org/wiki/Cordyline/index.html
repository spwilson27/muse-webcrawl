Cordyline

Cordyline is a genus of about 24 species of woody monocotyledonous flowering plants in the family Asparagaceae, subfamily Lomandroideae. The genus is native to the western Pacific Ocean region, from New Zealand, eastern Australia, Southeast Asia and Polynesia, with one species in western South America.

Scientific classification

Kingdom: Plantae

Order: Asparagales

Family: Asparagaceae

Genus: Cordyline Comm. ex R.Br.

Description

Cordylines are palm-like shrubs or small trees with clusters of long, narrow, strap-shaped leaves at the ends of branched stems. Several species are popular ornamental plants, including Cordyline australis, the cabbage tree of New Zealand, and Cordyline fruticosa, the ti plant of the Pacific islands, whose leaves are used in cooking, traditional dress and ceremony.

Cultivation

Many cultivars are grown for their colourful foliage in shades of green, red, purple and cream. Some species tolerate coastal conditions and light frost, while tropical species are widely grown as houseplants in cooler climates.
