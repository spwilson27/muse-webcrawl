In classical mythology, Cupid (Latin: Cupīdō, meaning "passionate desire") is the god of desire, erotic love, attraction, and affection. He is often portrayed as the son of the love goddess Venus and the war god Mars. He is also known in Latin as Amor ("Love"). His Greek counterpart is Eros.

Mythology

Although Eros appears in classical Greek art as a slender, winged youth, during the Hellenistic period he came to be depicted as a chubby boy — the image inherited by Roman Cupid. Cupid carries a bow and a quiver of arrows: those struck by his golden arrows fall in love, while those hit by leaden arrows are repelled by desire.

The most famous myth involving Cupid is the story of Cupid and Psyche, told in Apuleius's Metamorphoses (The Golden Ass). Venus, jealous of the mortal Psyche's beauty, orders Cupid to make her fall in love with a monster, but Cupid falls in love with her himself. After a series of trials imposed by Venus, the couple are united and Psyche is granted immortality.

Iconography and legacy

Cupid appears throughout Western art, from Roman frescoes to Renaissance paintings by Botticelli and Titian and Baroque sculpture. In the plural, winged putti or amoretti decorate countless works. In modern popular culture, Cupid is the emblem of Valentine's Day, depicted as a cherubic infant whose arrows cause people to fall in love.
