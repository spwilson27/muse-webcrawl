Early action

Early action (EA) is a type of early admission process offered by some colleges and universities in the United States. It allows students to apply earlier than the regular decision deadline — typically by November 1 or November 15 — and to receive an admission decision earlier, usually by mid-December. Unlike early decision, early action is non-binding: admitted students are not obliged to attend and may wait until the spring (commonly May 1) to respond, and they may apply early action to several institutions.

Variants

Some institutions offer restrictive early action (REA), also called single-choice early action, which is likewise non-binding but restricts the applicant from applying to other private colleges' early programmes, though early applications to public institutions and rolling-admission schools are usually permitted. Harvard, Yale, Princeton, Stanford and Notre Dame are among the universities using this variant.

Outcomes

An early action applicant generally receives one of three decisions: admission, rejection, or deferral to the regular decision pool. Because the applicant pool is often strong, some colleges defer a large share of early applicants.

Advantages and criticism

Supporters note that early action reduces student stress by providing an early answer without removing the freedom to compare financial aid offers. Critics argue that early admission plans, including early action, can favour applicants from wealthier backgrounds who are better advised about the process, and that they complicate colleges' yield management.
