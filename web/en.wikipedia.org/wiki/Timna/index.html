Timna

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Timna most commonly refers to the Timna Valley in the Negev desert of southern Israel, about 30 kilometres north of Eilat, famous for its ancient copper mines and its striking sandstone formations. The area is now the Timna Park, a geological and archaeological attraction.

Copper mining

Copper was mined in the Timna Valley intermittently from the Chalcolithic period (as early as the 5th–4th millennium BC) through the Bronze and Iron Ages, making it one of the oldest known copper-mining districts in the world. Egyptian expeditions of the New Kingdom period worked the mines intensively in the 14th–12th centuries BC, and a small temple dedicated to the goddess Hathor, protector of miners, was built at the foot of the sandstone cliffs known as Solomon's Pillars.

Excavations led by Beno Rothenberg revealed shafts, galleries, smelting camps and slag heaps. Later research suggested that at its peak in the 10th century BC the industry was operated by local Edomite or tribal groups using advanced, well-organised smelting technology — evidence popularly linked with the era of "King Solomon's mines".

Geology

The valley's mushroom-shaped rocks, arches and pillars were sculpted by wind and water erosion from multicoloured sandstone. Modern industrial copper mining was briefly revived in the 1950s before the area was developed as a park.
