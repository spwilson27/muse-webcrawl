Education in the United Kingdom

Education in the United Kingdom is a devolved matter, with each of the four countries — England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland — maintaining its own system under separate governments. Education is compulsory between roughly ages 5 and 16–18, and most children attend state-funded schools, though a small proportion attend fee-charging independent schools (historically called "public schools" in England).

Stages and qualifications

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, schooling is divided into primary and secondary phases, with pupils taking GCSEs around age 16 and A-levels at 18. Scotland instead follows its own system of Nationals, Highers and Advanced Highers. School inspection is carried out by Ofsted in England, Estyn in Wales, Education Scotland, and the ETI in Northern Ireland.

Higher education

The UK is home to some of the world's oldest and most highly ranked universities, including the Universities of Oxford and Cambridge, Imperial College London, the London School of Economics, and Scotland's ancient universities. Higher education expanded greatly after the Robbins Report of 1963 and the conversion of polytechnics into universities in 1992. Tuition fee regimes differ between the four nations.
