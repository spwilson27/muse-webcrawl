Multichannel television sound

Multichannel Television Sound (MTS) is the method of encoding three additional audio channels into an analog NTSC-format audio carrier. It was developed by the Broadcast Television Systems Committee, an industry group, and is sometimes known as BTSC after that organisation. The United States Federal Communications Commission adopted the MTS standard in 1984.

MTS allows a television broadcast to carry stereo audio (left and right channels encoded as a difference signal compatible with mono receivers) and a Separate Audio Program (SAP) channel, used for a second language, descriptive video for the visually impaired, or occasionally an unrelated audio service such as weather or campus radio. A fourth, low-quality "professional channel" (PRO) is used internally by broadcasters for cueing and telemetry.

The stereo difference channel uses dbx noise reduction companding to overcome noise in the narrow subcarrier bandwidth. With the transition to digital television (ATSC) in the United States, completed in 2009, MTS became obsolete for full-power broadcasters, though it persisted for a time on low-power and cable systems delivering analog NTSC feeds.
