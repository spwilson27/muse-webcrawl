Game Gear

The Game Gear is an 8-bit fourth-generation handheld game console released by Sega on 6 October 1990 in Japan, in April 1991 in North America and Europe, and in 1992 in Australia. It was Sega's answer to Nintendo's Game Boy, boasting a colour, backlit screen and hardware closely related to the Master System.

Hardware

The Game Gear used a Zilog Z80 CPU at 3.58 MHz, 8 KB of RAM and a 3.2-inch screen displaying 32 colours from a palette of 4,096 at a resolution of 160×144. Six AA batteries gave only three to five hours of play — the system's most criticised weakness against the Game Boy's far longer battery life. Because the hardware was based on the Master System, an adaptor, the Master Gear Converter, allowed it to play that console's cartridges.

Games and competition

More than 300 games were released for the system, including versions of Sonic the Hedgehog, Columns (packaged with the console in some regions), The GG Shinobi and Land of Illusion. Despite technically superior hardware, the Game Gear was outsold by the Game Boy roughly ten to one: its high price, bulk and battery consumption outweighed the appeal of a colour screen. Total sales were about 10.6 million units.

Legacy

Support ended in 1997, though Majesco briefly re-released the console in 2000. In 2020 Sega celebrated its 60th anniversary with the Game Gear Micro, a miniature version preloaded with four games per colour variant. The system's library is also represented on Nintendo's Virtual Console for the 3DS.
