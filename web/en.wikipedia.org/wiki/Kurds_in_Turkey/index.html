Kurds in Turkey

Kurds in Turkey are the largest ethnic minority in the country. Estimates of their number generally range from 15 to 20 percent of Turkey's population, concentrated primarily in the east and southeast of the country (often called Turkish Kurdistan) as well as large urban communities in cities such as Istanbul, Ankara and İzmir.

History

Following the establishment of the Republic of Turkey in 1923, Kurdish identity was officially denied for decades and Kurds were referred to as "Mountain Turks". Kurdish language, dress, names and culture were restricted, and a series of Kurdish revolts, including the Sheikh Said rebellion (1925), the Ararat rebellion (1930) and the Dersim rebellion (1937–38), were suppressed. The long-running conflict with the PKK, which began in 1984, deeply affected the region.

Language and culture

The most widely spoken Kurdish dialect in Turkey is Kurmanji (Northern Kurdish); Zazaki is also spoken by some communities. Restrictions on Kurdish language in education and broadcasting were gradually eased from the 1990s and especially during Turkey's EU accession reforms, including the launch of the state Kurdish-language channel TRT Kurdî in 2009.
