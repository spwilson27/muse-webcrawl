Discus (fish)

KingdomAnimalia
FamilyCichlidae
GenusSymphysodon
OriginAmazon River basin, South America

Discus are freshwater fish of the genus Symphysodon, native to the Amazon River basin in South America. Named for their distinctive laterally compressed, disc-shaped bodies, they are among the most popular and demanding aquarium fish, sometimes called the "king of the aquarium".

Species and habitat

Recognised species include Symphysodon discus (the Heckel discus), S. aequifasciatus and S. tarzoo. Wild discus inhabit slow-moving, warm, acidic blackwater tributaries, where they shelter among submerged roots and vegetation.

Biology

Discus are notable for their parental care: both parents secrete a nutritive mucus from their skin on which the fry feed for their first weeks of life, a form of care unique among cichlids in its extent. Adults reach about 12–15 cm in length and display vertical barring and colours ranging from brown and green to brilliant blue and red.

In the aquarium

Selective breeding has produced many cultivated colour forms — turquoise, pigeon blood, snakeskin and albino among them. Discus require warm water (around 28–30 °C), stable chemistry and high water quality, which gives them a reputation as challenging fish for experienced aquarists.
