IAM (band)

IAM
Origin: Marseille, France
Genres: French hip hop
Years active: 1989–present
Members: Akhenaton, Shurik'n, Khéops, Imhotep, Kephren, Freeman (former)

IAM is a French hip hop band from Marseille, formed in 1989 by Akhenaton (Philippe Fragione), Shurik'n (Geoffroy Mussard), Khéops (Éric Mazel), Imhotep (Pascal Perez), and Kephren (François Mendy); the rapper Freeman (Malek Brahimi) was a member until 2008. Taking their name and much of their imagery from ancient Egypt, IAM became one of the foundational and most commercially successful groups in French rap.

Career

IAM emerged from the Marseille scene with ...de la planète Mars (1991) and Ombre est lumière (1993). Their breakthrough came with L'école du micro d'argent (1997), recorded partly in New York, which sold well over a million copies and is widely considered the greatest French hip hop album ever made; it includes the hits "Petit frère" and "Nés sous la même étoile." The group popularized a thoughtful, historically and socially conscious style of rap, addressing immigration, racism, violence in the banlieues, and Mediterranean identity, often over lush samples drawing on film scores — notably Ennio Morricone — and North African music.

Style and influence

The members adopt Egyptian-themed personas: Akhenaton (after the pharaoh), Khéops (Cheops), Imhotep, and Kephren. Akhenaton and Shurik'n also released acclaimed solo albums, and members founded labels such as Côté Obscur. IAM's success helped establish Marseille as a rival rap capital to Paris and paved the way for later French hip hop generations. The group continued recording into the 2010s and 2020s with albums such as Arts martiens (2013) and Yasuke (2019).
