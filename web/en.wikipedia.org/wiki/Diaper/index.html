Diaper

A diaper (American English) or nappy (British English) is a type of underwear that allows the wearer to urinate or defecate without using a toilet, by absorbing or containing waste products. Diapers are primarily worn by infants and toddlers who are not yet toilet-trained, and by adults with incontinence or other medical needs.

Types

Cloth diapers, made of washable fabrics such as cotton, have been used for centuries in many forms. Disposable diapers, incorporating absorbent materials and later superabsorbent polymers, were commercialised in the mid-twentieth century — Marion Donovan's designs and Procter & Gamble's Pampers (1961) were milestones — and came to dominate markets in industrialised countries.

Environmental debate

The relative environmental impact of cloth versus disposable diapers is debated: disposables generate large volumes of landfill waste, while cloth diapers require water and energy for laundering. Adult diapers serve medical, occupational and spaceflight uses.
