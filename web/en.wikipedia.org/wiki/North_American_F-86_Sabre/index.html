North American F-86 Sabre

The North American F-86 Sabre, sometimes called the Sabrejet, is a transonic jet fighter aircraft developed by North American Aviation and first flown in 1947. It is best known as the United States' principal air-combat fighter of the Korean War, where it battled the Soviet-built MiG-15.

F-86 Sabre
Manufacturer: North American Aviation
First flight: 1 October 1947
Introduction: 1949
Number built: 9,860

Design

The Sabre used a 35-degree swept wing drawing on captured German wartime research, a single General Electric J47 turbojet, and (in the F-86F) leading-edge slat improvements and a more powerful engine. Armament was six .50-calibre machine guns in the nose, with underwing stores for bombs or rockets in fighter-bomber roles.

Service

In Korea, Sabres of the USAF claimed a favourable exchange ratio over MiG Alley. Canadair built the type under licence for the RCAF and export; versions served with dozens of air forces into the 1970s and beyond, and the design led to the F-100 Super Sabre. Over 9,800 were produced, making it the most numerous Western jet fighter of its era.
