Loyal opposition

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

His (or Her) Majesty's Loyal Opposition is the principal opposition party (or coalition) in a Westminster-style parliament — “loyal” because it opposes the government of the day while remaining loyal to the Crown and the constitutional order, distinguishing democratic opposition from sedition. The phrase “His Majesty's Opposition” is credited to the MP John Hobhouse in 1826 (offered half in jest, it stuck); the institution was constitutionalised in Britain by stages — the leader of the opposition receiving an official salary by the Ministers of the Crown Act 1937 — and exported across the Commonwealth.

The loyal opposition is a governing institution, not merely a critic: it forms a shadow cabinet, receives official briefings and state occasions, is consulted on appointments and emergencies, chairs certain committees (the Public Accounts Committee by convention), and stands as the government-in-waiting — a status that disciplines both sides (the opposition must look capable of office; the government faces an organised alternative). The concept's qualifier matters most in crises: opposition that questions the state's legitimacy itself (anti-system parties in Sartori's typology) or governments that brand opponents disloyal both break the compact on which parliamentary democracy's peaceful alternation depends.
