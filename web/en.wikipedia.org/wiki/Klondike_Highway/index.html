Klondike Highway

The Klondike Highway links Skagway, Alaska, with Dawson City, Yukon.

The Klondike Highway is a highway that runs from the Alaska Panhandle town of Skagway to Dawson City in the Yukon Territory of Canada. It roughly parallels the route taken by prospectors in the 1898 Klondike Gold Rush: the White Pass and the Yukon River corridor. The highway consists of Alaska Route 98 from Skagway to the international border and Yukon Highway 2 from the border through Whitehorse to Dawson City.

Route

From Skagway the road climbs steeply over White Pass (873 m / 2,865 ft), passing the abandoned gold-rush town of Bennett, British Columbia, before joining the shore of Tagish Lake and reaching Carcross. North of Carcross it joins the Alaska Highway just south of Whitehorse, the Yukon capital. The northern section, sometimes called the North Klondike Highway, continues from Whitehorse up the Yukon River valley through Carmacks, Pelly Crossing, and Stewart Crossing to Dawson City, at the confluence of the Yukon and Klondike rivers.

History

The White Pass section opened in 1978, initially for summer tourist traffic connecting with the Alaska Marine Highway ferry at Skagway, and began year-round operation in the 1980s, giving Whitehorse a tidewater port connection. The road to Dawson City developed from trails and the Overland Trail built in 1902 to bypass the difficult winter river route; it was progressively improved through the twentieth century and is now a paved, all-weather highway over most of its length, though the section north of Whitehorse has some gravel stretches.

Points of interest

Attractions along the route include the White Pass and Yukon Route railway, the Carcross Desert (one of the smallest deserts in the world), Emerald Lake, the Five Finger Rapids on the Yukon River, and the historic gold-rush townsite of Dawson City, a National Historic Site of Canada.
