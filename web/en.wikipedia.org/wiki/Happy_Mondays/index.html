Happy Mondays

OriginSalford, Greater Manchester, England
GenresMadchester, alternative dance, baggy
Years active1980–1993; 1999–2001; 2004–present (intermittently)

Key membersShaun Ryder, Bez, Paul Ryder, Mark Day, Gary Whelan, Paul Davis

Happy Mondays are an English rock band formed in Salford in 1980. Fronted by Shaun Ryder, with his brother Paul on bass and the dancer-percussionist Bez as an unlikely icon, they became, alongside the Stone Roses, the defining band of the "Madchester" scene, fusing indie rock with funk, house music, and rave culture on Tony Wilson's Factory Records.

History

The band developed through the mid-1980s with the albums Squirrel and G-Man Twenty Four Hour Party People Plastic Face Carnt Smile (White Out) (1987) and the Martin Hannett-produced Bummed (1988). Their breakthrough came with the Paul Oakenfold-produced singles "Step On" — a cover of John Kongos's "He's Gonna Step on You Again" — and "Kinky Afro", both top-five UK hits in 1990, and the platinum album Pills 'n' Thrills and Bellyaches (1990), produced by Oakenfold and Steve Osborne. They headlined festivals and embodied the baggy era's hedonism.

Collapse and reunions

The follow-up album Yes Please! (1992), recorded expensively in Barbados amid heavy drug use, sold poorly, and its costs contributed to the collapse of Factory Records. The band split in 1993; Shaun Ryder and Bez found further success with Black Grape. Happy Mondays have re-formed several times, releasing the album Uncle Dysfunktional in 2007 and touring with line-ups centred on Shaun Ryder, Bez, and Gary Whelan. Bassist Paul Ryder died in 2022.

Legacy

Happy Mondays' loose, groove-led sound and Ryder's surreal street-poet lyrics captured the ecstasy-era collision of football, fashion, and club culture in northern England. Their story features prominently in the film 24 Hour Party People (2002), about Factory Records.
