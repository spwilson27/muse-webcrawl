S-process

TypeNucleosynthesis process
SiteAsymptotic giant branch (AGB) stars; massive stars (weak s-process)
MechanismSlow neutron capture followed by beta decay

The s-process, or slow neutron-capture process, is a series of reactions in nuclear astrophysics by which atomic nuclei heavier than iron are synthesised in stars. In the s-process, a seed nucleus (typically iron-56) captures neutrons one at a time, at a rate slow enough that any unstable nucleus produced will usually undergo beta decay before capturing another neutron; the process therefore proceeds along the valley of beta stability.

Sites and products

The main s-process occurs in low- and intermediate-mass stars on the asymptotic giant branch, where neutrons are supplied chiefly by the reactions 13C(α,n)16O and 22Ne(α,n)25Mg during thermal pulses; convection then dredges the enriched material to the surface, from where stellar winds disperse it. A weaker s-process in massive stars builds nuclei up to about mass 90. The s-process accounts for roughly half of the abundances of heavy elements between iron and bismuth, with the r-process supplying most of the remainder.

Evidence

Its signature is visible in the abundance patterns of barium stars and carbon stars, in technetium lines in AGB spectra (proving synthesis is ongoing), and in isotopic anomalies in presolar grains extracted from meteorites, which match s-process predictions closely.
