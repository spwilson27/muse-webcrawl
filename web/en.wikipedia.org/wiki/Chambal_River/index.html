Chambal River

The Chambal River near Kota, Rajasthan

The Chambal River is a tributary of the Yamuna River in central India, flowing through the states of Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, and Uttar Pradesh. It is about 1,024 kilometres (636 miles) long and is one of the most pollution-free rivers in India.

The river rises in the Vindhya Range near Mhow in Madhya Pradesh and flows north and northeast before joining the Yamuna near Etawah in Uttar Pradesh. Its drainage basin is famous for the Chambal ravines, a badlands landscape of deep gullies that historically sheltered bandits known as dacoits.

Dams

Four major dams have been built on the Chambal for hydroelectric power and irrigation: the Gandhi Sagar Dam, the Rana Pratap Sagar Dam, the Jawahar Sagar Dam, and the Kota Barrage.

Wildlife

The National Chambal Sanctuary protects endangered species along a stretch of the river, including the gharial, the red-crowned roof turtle, and the Ganges river dolphin. The river is an important habitat for the mugger crocodile and several species of freshwater turtles.
