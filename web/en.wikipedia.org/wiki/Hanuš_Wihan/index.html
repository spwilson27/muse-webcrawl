Hanuš Wihan

Hanuš Wihan
Born: 5 June 1855, Police nad Metují, Bohemia
Died: 1 May 1920, Prague
Instrument: Cello
Associated acts: Bohemian Quartet

Hanuš Wihan (5 June 1855 – 1 May 1920) was a Czech cellist, considered one of the great cello virtuosos of his time. He was the founder of the Bohemian Quartet and a close associate of Antonín Dvořák.

Life and career

Wihan studied at the Prague Conservatory and in Munich. He taught at the Mozarteum in Salzburg and performed across Europe before returning to Prague, where he became professor of cello and chamber music at the Prague Conservatory.

Dvořák connection

Dvořák wrote his Cello Concerto in B minor (1895) with Wihan in mind, though the premiere was given by Leo Stern after a disagreement over a cadenza Wihan had composed. Dvořák's Rondo in G minor and Silent Woods were also written for Wihan.
