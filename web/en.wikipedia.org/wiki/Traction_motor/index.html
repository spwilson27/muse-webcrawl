Traction motor

A traction motor is an electric motor used to propel a vehicle — most commonly a railway locomotive, tram, or electric/hybrid road vehicle. Traction motors must deliver high torque at low speed, withstand vibration and contamination, and operate over a wide speed range.

Types

Historically, DC series-wound motors dominated because their torque-speed characteristic suits traction. Diesel-electric locomotives long used DC motors fed by the engine-driven generator. Modern practice favours AC induction motors and permanent-magnet synchronous motors driven by variable-frequency inverters (typically using IGBT power electronics), which offer better control, regenerative braking, and lower maintenance because they have no brushes or commutator.

Mounting and drive

In rail vehicles the motor may be axle-hung ("nose-suspended"), with part of its weight carried directly on the axle through a single-reduction gearbox, or fully sprung in the bogie frame with a flexible drive, as used on high-speed trains to reduce unsprung mass. Gear ratios trade starting tractive effort against maximum speed. In road EVs the motor is often integrated with the gearbox and inverter as a single "e-axle" unit.
