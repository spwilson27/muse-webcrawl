Child care

Child care
Also calledDay care, early childhood care
Typical agesInfancy to school age (0–12)
ProvidersParents, relatives, nannies, nurseries, preschools
RegulationLicensing varies by country/state

Child care, or day care, is the care and supervision of a child or children, usually from infancy through early adolescence, by someone other than the child's parents during part of the day. It ranges from informal care by relatives and nannies to regulated centre-based nurseries, kindergartens, and after-school programmes.

Types

Centre-based care serves groups of children with trained staff and structured activities; family child care operates from a provider's home with smaller groups; nannies and au pairs provide in-home care. Preschool and pre-kindergarten programmes blend care with early education.

Effects and debates

Research, including the long-running NICHD study in the United States, finds that high-quality care is associated with better language and cognitive outcomes, especially for disadvantaged children, while long hours of low-quality care may be linked to behavioural difficulties. Economists argue early-childhood investment yields high long-run returns, fuelling policy debates over subsidies and universal preschool.

Policy

Provision differs widely: Nordic countries offer heavily subsidized universal child care, while in countries such as the United States care is largely market-based and costly relative to income, with targeted subsidies (for example, Head Start) for low-income families. Licensing standards govern ratios, staff qualifications, and safety in most jurisdictions.
