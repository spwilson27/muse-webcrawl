Clarence Ray Allen

Clarence Ray Allen (January 16, 1930 – January 17, 2006) was an American criminal who was executed by lethal injection at San Quentin State Prison in California on the day after his 76th birthday. Blind, diabetic, and using a wheelchair, he was the oldest person ever executed in California and the second-oldest person executed in the United States in the modern era. Allen had been sentenced to death for ordering, from prison, the murders of a witness against him and of two other people who had testified.

Crimes

Allen ran a burglary and robbery ring in the Fresno area in the 1970s. In 1974 his 17-year-old accomplice Mary Sue Kitts was murdered after she told others about a burglary of Fran's Market in Fresno; Allen was convicted of that burglary and one count of murder and sentenced to life imprisonment. While at Folsom State Prison, Allen plotted to kill the witnesses who had testified against him, enlisting fellow inmate Billy Ray Hamilton, who was released on parole in 1980. In September 1980 Hamilton entered Fran's Market and murdered employees Bryon Schletewitz, Josephine Rocha, and Douglas White, and wounded two others. Hamilton was arrested shortly afterwards carrying a list of witnesses in Allen's case. Allen was convicted in 1982 of three counts of first-degree murder and conspiracy and was sentenced to death.

Execution

Allen's appeals lasted more than two decades. His attorneys argued that executing a 76-year-old, blind, and nearly deaf man who had spent 23 years on death row was cruel and unusual punishment, and the case drew international attention and protests. Governor Arnold Schwarzenegger denied clemency, stating that Allen's crimes "demonstrate the depths of his depravity" because he had ordered killings from prison to silence witnesses. The U.S. Supreme Court declined to intervene, and Allen was executed shortly after midnight on January 17, 2006. His case remains a frequent reference point in debates over the execution of elderly and infirm prisoners.
