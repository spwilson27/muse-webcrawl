Site C dam

The Site C dam is a hydroelectric dam built by BC Hydro on the Peace River in northeastern British Columbia, Canada, near Fort St. John. It is the third dam on the Peace River in British Columbia, downstream of the W. A. C. Bennett and Peace Canyon dams.

Project

The project comprises an earthfill dam about 1,050 metres long and 60 metres high, a powerhouse with six generating units totalling roughly 1,100 megawatts, and a reservoir about 83 kilometres long. Construction began in 2015; the reservoir was filled in 2024 and the generating units entered service in stages thereafter.

Controversy

Site C was among Canada's most debated infrastructure projects. Critics cited its rising cost (from an initial estimate near C$8 billion to more than C$16 billion), the flooding of agricultural land and Treaty 8 First Nations' rights and heritage sites, and whether cheaper alternatives existed. Supporters pointed to long-lived, low-carbon electricity for a growing province.
