Mount Merapi (Indonesian/Javanese: Gunung Merapi, "Fire Mountain") is an active stratovolcano on the border between Central Java and the Special Region of Yogyakarta, Indonesia. It is the most active volcano in Indonesia and has erupted regularly since 1548; its name reflects Javanese words for fire and mountain.

Activity

Merapi typically produces small eruptions every few years and larger ones each decade or so, characterised by lava dome growth, dome collapse and fast-moving pyroclastic flows (locally called wedhus gembel, "shaggy goat"). Deadly eruptions occurred in 1930 (about 1,300 deaths), 1994 and most severely in October–November 2010, when explosive eruptions killed 353 people and displaced some 350,000. Plumes from the 2010 event closed airspace across the region, and ash reached Yogyakarta, whose airport at Adisucipto lies in the volcano's shadow.

Cultural significance and monitoring

The volcano is sacred in Javanese tradition: the palace of Yogyakarta maintains a spiritual relationship with Merapi, the southern sea and the spirit realm, and a royal gatekeeper (juru kunci) traditionally tended the mountain; the gatekeeper Mbah Maridjan famously died in the 2010 eruption after refusing to evacuate. Merapi is monitored by Indonesia's volcanological agency (PVMBG) with observatories around its flanks, and its slopes above the farmland form part of Mount Merapi National Park.
