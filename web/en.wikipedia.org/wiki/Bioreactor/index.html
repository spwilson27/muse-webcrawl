Bioreactor

A bioreactor is any manufactured device or system that supports a biologically active environment. In one case, a bioreactor is a vessel in which a chemical process is carried out involving organisms or biochemically active substances derived from such organisms. This process can either be aerobic or anaerobic. These bioreactors are commonly cylindrical, ranging in size from litres to cubic metres, and are often made of stainless steel. The term may also refer to a device or system designed to grow cells or tissues in the context of cell culture, used in tissue engineering and in the production of biopharmaceuticals.

Design and operation

A bioreactor's environmental conditions — temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen, nutrient concentrations, and agitation — are closely monitored and controlled. Organisms growing in bioreactors may be submerged in liquid medium (submerged fermentation) or attached to the surface of a solid medium. Common operating modes include batch, fed-batch, and continuous (chemostat) culture.

Types

  • Stirred-tank bioreactor — the most common type, using a motor-driven impeller for mixing and gas dispersion.
  • Airlift bioreactor — mixing achieved by rising gas bubbles rather than mechanical agitation.
  • Photobioreactor — provides light for photosynthetic organisms such as algae and cyanobacteria.
  • Packed-bed and fluidised-bed reactors — cells immobilised on or in solid supports.
  • Single-use bioreactors — disposable plastic vessels increasingly used in pharmaceutical manufacturing.
  • Moss bioreactors and other plant-cell systems for producing recombinant proteins.

Applications

Bioreactors are used in the production of antibiotics, enzymes, vaccines, monoclonal antibodies, biofuels, and fermented foods and beverages, as well as in wastewater treatment, where activated sludge systems act as large-scale bioreactors. Small anaerobic digesters convert organic waste into biogas.
