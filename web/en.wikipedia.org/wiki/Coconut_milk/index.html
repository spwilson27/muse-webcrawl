Coconut milk

Coconut milk is an opaque, milky-white liquid extracted from the grated pulp of mature coconuts. The opacity and rich taste of coconut milk are due to its high oil content, most of which is saturated fat. Coconut milk is a traditional food ingredient used in Southeast Asia, Oceania, South Asia, and East Africa, and also in the Caribbean and tropical Latin America, where coconuts were introduced during the colonial era.

Production

Traditionally, coconut milk is made by pressing grated coconut flesh mixed with hot water through cheesecloth. The first pressing yields thick milk (sometimes called coconut cream when the fat content is highest), and a second pressing of the same pulp gives thin milk. Commercial products are mechanically extracted, homogenized, and often canned, with emulsifiers and stabilizers added. Powdered coconut milk is produced by spray-drying.

Culinary uses

Coconut milk is the base of many curries, including Thai green and massaman curries and Malaysian rendang (in which it is simmered until the oil separates). It appears in soups such as tom kha gai, in satay sauces, in rice dishes like nasi lemak, and in desserts including Filipino ginataan, Indonesian serabi, and the shaved-ice treats cendol and halo-halo. In the West, diluted sweetened versions are marketed as a plant-based milk substitute for drinks.

Nutrition

Coconut milk is energy-dense and high in medium-chain saturated fatty acids, principally lauric acid. It contains modest amounts of iron, magnesium, potassium, and manganese, but little protein compared with dairy milk.
