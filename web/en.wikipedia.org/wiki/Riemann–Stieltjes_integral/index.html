Riemann–Stieltjes integral

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, the Riemann–Stieltjes integral is a generalisation of the Riemann integral, named after Bernhard Riemann and Thomas Joannes Stieltjes. It integrates one function f with respect to another function g, written ∫ab f(x) dg(x), and reduces to the ordinary Riemann integral when g(x) = x.

Definition

Given a partition P of [a, b], one forms the Stieltjes sum Σ f(ξi) [g(xi) − g(xi−1)], weighting the values of f by the increments of g rather than by the lengths of the subintervals. If these sums approach a limit as the partition is refined, that limit is the Riemann–Stieltjes integral of f with respect to g on [a, b]. When g is differentiable with a Riemann-integrable derivative, the integral equals ∫ f(x) g′(x) dx; when g has a jump discontinuity, the integral picks up contributions f(x₀) times the size of the jump at x₀.

Properties and existence

The integral is linear in both f and g and obeys an integration-by-parts formula: ∫ f dg + ∫ g df = f(b)g(b) − f(a)g(a). It exists, for example, when f is continuous and g is of bounded variation. If f and g share a point of discontinuity, the integral may fail to exist.

Applications

The Riemann–Stieltjes integral is the classical tool for writing expectations uniformly for discrete and continuous random variables: E[X] = ∫ x dF(x), where F is the cumulative distribution function. It likewise expresses work against variable forces, charge distributions, and other quantities where the "weight" of an interval is not merely its length.
