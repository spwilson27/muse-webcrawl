The Battle of Mühldorf, fought on 28 September 1322 on the Ampfing Heath near Mühldorf am Inn, was the last great cavalry battle of the German Middle Ages fought without firearms. It decided the disputed royal election of 1314 in favour of Louis IV of Bavaria.

Background

The double election of 1314 had produced two rival kings: the Wittelsbach Louis of Bavaria and the Habsburg Frederick the Fair of Austria. Eight years of intermittent war culminated in Frederick's invasion of Bavaria.

The battle

Louis, reinforced minutes before the battle by Nuremberg burghers under Seyfried Schweppermann, defeated Frederick's army. Frederick himself was captured, along with over a thousand Austrian and Salzburg nobles.

Aftermath

Frederick was imprisoned at Trausnitz Castle until 1325, when the two rivals agreed to a co-rule (Treaty of Munich); Louis was later crowned Holy Roman Emperor.
