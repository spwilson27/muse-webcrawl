William Edward Parry

Sir William Edward Parry (1790–1855), Arctic explorer.

Sir William Edward Parry (19 December 1790 – 8 July 1855) was a British Royal Navy officer and Arctic explorer. He made some of the most successful early expeditions in search of the Northwest Passage and in 1827 attempted to reach the North Pole.

Northwest Passage expeditions

Parry served as second-in-command on John Ross's 1818 expedition, then led his own voyage of 1819–20 in HMS Hecla and Griper. Sailing through Lancaster Sound and the channel now named Parry Channel, he reached Melville Island and wintered in the ice — the first expedition to survive an Arctic winter deliberately — earning a £5,000 parliamentary reward for passing 110°W. Two further expeditions (1821–23 and 1824–25) explored Foxe Basin and Prince Regent Inlet; the second ended with the wreck of HMS Fury.

North Pole attempt

In 1827 Parry attempted to reach the North Pole from Spitsbergen using boats fitted with runners for hauling over ice. His party reached 82°45′N, a record for the highest latitude attained that stood for nearly fifty years, though drifting ice carried them south nearly as fast as they marched north.

Later life

Knighted in 1829, Parry served as Hydrographer of the Navy and in other administrative posts. He died in 1855 at Bad Ems in Germany. Parry Channel, Parry Island, and the lunar crater Parry are named after him.
