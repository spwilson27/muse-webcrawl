Great Lakes

The Great Lakes (French: Grands Lacs), also called the Great Lakes of North America, are a series of large interconnected freshwater lakes in the east-central interior of North America that connect to the Atlantic Ocean via the Saint Lawrence River. The five lakes are Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie, and Ontario. Together they form the largest group of freshwater lakes on Earth by total area and the second-largest by total volume, containing 21% of the world's surface fresh water by volume.

Geography

The lakes are divided among the jurisdictions of the Canadian province of Ontario and the U.S. states of Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan, Illinois, Indiana, Ohio, Pennsylvania, and New York. Lake Michigan is the only one located entirely within the United States. The lakes drain eastward through the St. Lawrence River to the Atlantic Ocean. Niagara Falls lies between lakes Erie and Ontario.

The lakes were formed at the end of the Last Glacial Period about 14,000 years ago, as retreating ice sheets carved basins that filled with meltwater. The region around the lakes is home to more than 30 million people, roughly a tenth of the population of the United States and a third of Canada's population.

Individual lakes

Lake Superior is the largest by surface area (82,100 km²) and volume, and the deepest, with a maximum depth of 406 m. Lake Michigan is the second largest by volume. Lake Huron contains Manitoulin Island, the largest island in a freshwater lake in the world, and Georgian Bay. Lake Erie is the shallowest and warmest. Lake Ontario is the smallest by surface area and the most easterly.

Economy and ecology

The lakes support major shipping via the Great Lakes Waterway and the Saint Lawrence Seaway, moving iron ore, coal, grain, and other bulk cargo. They are central to the region's manufacturing, agriculture, fishing, and tourism. Environmental concerns include invasive species such as the zebra mussel and sea lamprey, industrial pollution, and harmful algal blooms, particularly in Lake Erie. Binational agreements, notably the Great Lakes Water Quality Agreement, govern protection efforts.
