Premier League Hall of Fame

The Premier League Hall of Fame honours the leading players and managers who have graced the Premier League, the top level of the English football league system, since its creation in 1992. It was established in 2021 and is the highest individual honour awarded by the league.

History

The Hall of Fame was announced in February 2020, with the first inductees originally planned for that year, but the launch was postponed by the COVID-19 pandemic. Alan Shearer and Thierry Henry were announced as the inaugural inductees in April 2021. They were followed later that year by Eric Cantona, Roy Keane, Frank Lampard, Dennis Bergkamp, Steven Gerrard, and David Beckham, selected partly through a public vote from shortlists.

Eligibility

To be eligible, players must have retired, and only their Premier League career is considered. Managers were made eligible from 2023, when Sir Alex Ferguson and Arsène Wenger became the first managerial inductees. Inductees include Wayne Rooney, Patrick Vieira, Sergio Agüero, Vincent Kompany, Paul Scholes, Didier Drogba, Ian Wright, and Peter Schmeichel.

Selection

Candidates are shortlisted by the Premier League based on criteria including appearances, goals, assists, clean sheets, and titles won, with final selections made by a panel in combination, for some classes, with fan voting.
