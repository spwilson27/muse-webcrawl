Green–Kubo relations

The Green–Kubo relations (Melville S. Green 1954, Ryogo Kubo 1957) give the exact mathematical expression for transport coefficients γ in terms of integrals of time correlation functions:

γ = ∫0∞ ⟨Ȧ(t)Ȧ(0)⟩ dt

These relations are central results of statistical mechanics, connecting macroscopic transport properties such as viscosity, thermal conductivity, and electrical conductivity to the microscopic dynamics of a system at equilibrium, through the fluctuation-dissipation theorem.

Thermal and mechanical transport

In the 1950s, Green and Kubo showed that transport coefficients characterizing near-equilibrium dissipative processes could be calculated exactly from equilibrium time-correlation functions. For example, the shear viscosity η of a fluid is given by an integral over the autocorrelation of the off-diagonal components of the stress tensor:

η = (V / kBT) ∫0∞ ⟨σxy(t)σxy(0)⟩ dt

Similarly, thermal conductivity is related to the autocorrelation of the heat current, and electrical conductivity to the autocorrelation of the electric current.

Relation to Einstein relations

The Green–Kubo relations are closely related to the Einstein relations for diffusion. The diffusion coefficient D can be expressed either via the mean squared displacement of a particle at long times, or via the integral of the velocity autocorrelation function. Both approaches are widely used in molecular dynamics simulations to compute transport properties from atomistic trajectories.

Applications

Green–Kubo methods form the basis of equilibrium molecular dynamics calculations of transport coefficients in liquids, gases, and solids. They are applied in materials science, chemical engineering, and condensed matter physics, for example to compute the ionic conductivity of battery electrolytes, the viscosity of molten salts, and the thermal conductivity of semiconductors.
