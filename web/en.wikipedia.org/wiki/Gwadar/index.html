Gwadar (Balochi and Urdu: گوادر) is a port city on the southwestern coast of the Pakistani province of Balochistan. The city is located on the shores of the Arabian Sea opposite Oman, near the Strait of Hormuz. It sits on a natural hammerhead-shaped tombolo peninsula connected to the mainland by a narrow isthmus, with bays on either side.

History

Gwadar was a possession of the Sultanate of Oman for nearly two centuries, from about 1783, when the Khan of Kalat granted it to an Omani prince, until 1958, when Pakistan purchased the enclave from Oman for about US$3 million. The city remained a small fishing settlement for most of its history, with an economy based on artisanal fishing and trade.

Gwadar Port and CPEC

In 2001 Pakistan began developing a deep-water port at Gwadar with Chinese assistance. The port was inaugurated in 2007 and its operations were later transferred to a Chinese state-owned company. Gwadar is the flagship of the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) and is envisioned as a link in China's Belt and Road Initiative, potentially giving western China a shorter route to the Arabian Sea and energy supplies from the Gulf. Development plans include a free-trade zone, an international airport (New Gwadar International Airport), and an oil city, though progress has been slowed by security concerns and local grievances over water, electricity, and fishing rights.

Geography and climate

Gwadar has a hot desert climate with very little rainfall. The surrounding Makran coast is arid and rugged, with distinctive mud volcanoes and eroded rock formations nearby. The city is connected to Karachi by the Makran Coastal Highway.
