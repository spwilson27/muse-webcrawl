Ferdinand Emmanuel Edralin Marcos Sr. (11 September 1917 – 28 September 1989) was a Filipino politician, lawyer, and kleptocrat who served as the tenth president of the Philippines from 1965 to 1986. He ruled as a dictator under martial law from 1972 until 1981, and his 21 years in power were marked by massive corruption, extravagance, and brutality. Estimates of the wealth he and his family plundered run to between five and ten billion US dollars.

Early life and career

Marcos was born in Sarrat, Ilocos Norte, to a politically connected family. In 1939 he was convicted of murdering Julio Nalundasan, a political rival of his father, but the conviction was overturned by the Supreme Court in 1940, in a case argued by Marcos himself. He claimed an illustrious record as a guerrilla leader against the Japanese occupation, though US military records later cast serious doubt on much of that account. He entered politics after the war, serving in the House of Representatives and then the Senate, becoming Senate President before winning the presidency in 1965 and re-election in 1969 amid violence and vote-buying.

Martial law

On 21 September 1972, citing communist and Muslim insurgencies, Marcos proclaimed martial law. He dissolved Congress, arrested opposition leaders including Benigno "Ninoy" Aquino Jr., shut down media outlets, and ruled by decree under a new constitution that entrenched his power. The regime's security forces were responsible for thousands of extrajudicial killings, torture, and disappearances; the economy, after initial reformist technocracy, deteriorated into crony capitalism as Marcos allies monopolised industries. His wife Imelda became notorious for conspicuous consumption, including a collection of thousands of pairs of shoes left behind in the presidential palace.

Downfall

The assassination of Aquino at Manila airport in August 1983 galvanised opposition. Marcos called a snap election in February 1986 against Aquino's widow, Corazon Aquino; both sides claimed victory amid widespread fraud, and a military revolt led by Juan Ponce Enrile and Fidel Ramos triggered the People Power Revolution, in which millions of civilians filled EDSA highway to protect the rebels. On 25 February 1986 the Marcoses fled by US helicopter to exile in Hawaii, where Ferdinand died in 1989.

Legacy

Post-Marcos governments recovered only part of the family's hidden wealth, and the Marcoses returned to Philippine politics: Imelda and her children won elected office, and in 2022 Ferdinand "Bongbong" Marcos Jr. was elected president, a comeback that historians of memory link to decades of disinformation downplaying the dictatorship's abuses. Ferdinand Marcos's burial with military honours in the Heroes' Cemetery in 2016, authorised by President Rodrigo Duterte, was deeply controversial.
