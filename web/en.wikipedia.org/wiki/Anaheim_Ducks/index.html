The Anaheim Ducks are a professional ice hockey team based in Anaheim, California. They compete in the National Hockey League (NHL) as a member of the Pacific Division of the Western Conference. Founded in 1993 by the Walt Disney Company as the Mighty Ducks of Anaheim, the team took its name from the Disney film The Mighty Ducks.

The Ducks play their home games at Honda Center in Anaheim

History

The franchise began play in the 1993–94 season at the Arrowhead Pond of Anaheim. Early stars included Paul Kariya and Teemu Selänne. In the 2003 playoffs, goaltender Jean-Sébastien Giguère led the Mighty Ducks on a surprise run to the Stanley Cup Final, where they lost to the New Jersey Devils in seven games; Giguère won the Conn Smythe Trophy as playoff MVP, a rare honor for a player on the losing team.

Stanley Cup championship

Disney sold the team in 2005 to Henry and Susan Samueli, who shortened the name to Anaheim Ducks and changed the colors from purple and jade to black, gold, and orange. In 2006–07, captained by Scott Niedermayer and built around Chris Pronger, Ryan Getzlaf, and Corey Perry, the Ducks defeated the Ottawa Senators in five games to win the Stanley Cup — the first NHL championship for a California-based team.

Later years

The Ducks remained perennial playoff contenders through the 2010s, winning multiple Pacific Division titles, before entering a rebuilding period at the end of the decade. Notable players in franchise history include Getzlaf, the team's all-time scoring leader, Perry, Selänne, Kariya, and goaltender John Gibson.
