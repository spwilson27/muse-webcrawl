Magnet school

In the United States, a magnet school is a public school offering specialized courses or curricula intended to attract ("magnetize") students from across different school zones and racial backgrounds. Magnet schools are part of the public school system and are usually free of tuition.

History

Magnet schools grew rapidly in the 1970s and 1980s as a voluntary tool for school desegregation: by offering attractive special programs — in subjects such as science and technology, the performing arts, or international studies — districts hoped to draw a racially diverse enrolment without mandatory busing. They are now found in most large urban districts and are supported in part by the federal Magnet Schools Assistance Program.

Admissions

Admission may be by lottery, by audition or examination for competitive programs, or on a first-come basis. Magnet programs range from whole-school designs, in which every student follows the theme, to programs-within-a-school. The schools remain accountable to their districts and states like other public schools.
