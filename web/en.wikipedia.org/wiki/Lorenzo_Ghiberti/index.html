Lorenzo Ghiberti (1378 – 1 December 1455), born Lorenzo di Bartolo, was an Italian Renaissance sculptor and goldsmith from Florence, best known for the two sets of gilded bronze doors he created for the Florence Baptistery — the North Doors and the East Doors, the latter famously dubbed the "Gates of Paradise" by Michelangelo. His workshop was the leading sculpture workshop of Florence for half a century and trained a generation of artists including Donatello.

The 1401 competition

In 1401 the Arte di Calimala (cloth merchants' guild) held a competition for a new set of bronze doors for the Baptistery. Each competitor cast a trial panel of the Sacrifice of Isaac. Ghiberti's elegant, economical panel defeated the entries of six rivals, most notably Filippo Brunelleschi, whose more dramatic panel survives alongside Ghiberti's in the Bargello. The competition is traditionally treated as a founding moment of the Renaissance.

The Baptistery doors

The North Doors (1403–1424) present 28 panels, mostly scenes from the New Testament, in a Gothic quatrefoil format like Andrea Pisano's earlier South Doors. The East Doors (1425–1452), the "Gates of Paradise", present ten large rectangular panels of Old Testament scenes in which Ghiberti exploited linear perspective and graduated relief (rilievo schiacciato) to build deep pictorial space in bronze — a landmark of Renaissance art. The originals are now in the Museo dell'Opera del Duomo, with copies on the Baptistery.

Other work and writings

Ghiberti cast monumental bronze statues for the guild niches of Orsanmichele — St John the Baptist, St Matthew, and St Stephen — among the first life-size bronze statues of the Renaissance. His Commentarii, an unfinished three-book work, includes the earliest surviving autobiography by an artist and a history of art from antiquity that is a key source for the period.
