Pneumonia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pneumonia is an inflammatory condition of the lung primarily affecting the alveoli, typically caused by infection with Bacteria, viruses or fungi. Symptoms include cough, chest pain, fever and difficulty breathing; severity ranges from mild 'walking pneumonia' to life-threatening respiratory failure. Before Antibiotics, it was called by Osler 'the captain of the men of death'.

The commonest bacterial cause is Streptococcus pneumoniae (pneumococcus); viral causes include influenza, RSV and coronaviruses. Aspiration, hospital-acquired and immunocompromised-host pneumonias have their own typical organisms and severity. Diagnosis uses examination, chest X-ray and increasingly blood tests and ultrasound.

Burden and prevention

Pneumonia remains the single largest infectious killer of children under five worldwide (several hundred thousand deaths a year) and a major killer of the elderly. Pneumococcal, Hib and influenza vaccines, adequate nutrition, clean household air and prompt antibiotic access prevent much of this burden.

Treatment

Bacterial pneumonia is treated with antibiotics (choice by severity and local resistance), oxygen and fluids; severe cases need hospital or intensive care. Preventing influenza and COVID-19 prevents their pneumonias; smoking cessation and treating swallowing problems reduce risk.
