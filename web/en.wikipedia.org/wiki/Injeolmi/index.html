Injeolmi

Injeolmi (인절미) is a variety of tteok, or Korean rice cake, made by steaming and pounding glutinous rice flour, then shaping it into small pieces and coating it with steamed dried bean powder, typically from soybeans (kong-gomul). It is a representative type of pounded tteok (dojigae tteok) and is characterised by its chewy texture and nutty coating.

Preparation

Glutinous rice is washed, soaked and ground into flour, which is steamed in a siru earthenware steamer. The hot steamed dough is then pounded on a large wooden board or in a mortar with mallets until smooth and elastic. It is cut into bite-sized rectangular pieces and rolled in gomul — a powder made from steamed, dried and ground soybeans. Variations coat the cakes with sesame powder, mugwort powder, or use non-glutinous rice; a related form filled with sweetened red beans or honey is also common.

Cultural role

Injeolmi has traditionally been prepared for celebrations and rites, including weddings, first birthdays (dol), sixtieth birthdays (hwangap) and ancestral memorial services. Its sticky, clinging texture symbolises good fortune and lasting bonds, and the cake remains a popular everyday snack and street food in Korea, as well as an ingredient in modern fusion desserts such as injeolmi-flavoured shaved ice (bingsu) and toast.
