Drug discovery

Drug discovery is the process by which new candidate medications are discovered. Historically, drugs were found by identifying the active ingredient from traditional remedies or by serendipity, as with penicillin. Modern drug discovery involves identifying a biological target, screening chemical or biological libraries for "hits", and optimising those compounds into "leads".

Stages

Typical stages are target identification and validation; hit discovery by high-throughput screening, virtual screening, or fragment-based methods; hit-to-lead and lead optimisation, improving potency, selectivity, and ADMET properties; and preclinical studies before a candidate enters clinical trials. The process commonly takes over a decade, costs large sums, and suffers high attrition — only a small fraction of candidates reach approval.

Approaches

Approaches include rational drug design based on target structure, phenotypic screening, natural product screening, and repurposing of existing drugs.
