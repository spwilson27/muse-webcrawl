The Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (PCAOB) is a nonprofit corporation created by the Sarbanes–Oxley Act of 2002 to oversee the audits of public companies and SEC-registered brokers and dealers, in order to protect investors and the public interest in the preparation of informative, accurate and independent audit reports. It replaced the accounting profession's system of self-regulation after the Enron and WorldCom scandals destroyed the audit firm Arthur Andersen and public confidence in audited financial statements.

Functions

The PCAOB registers accounting firms that audit public companies (unregistered firms may not perform such audits), sets auditing, quality-control, ethics and independence standards, conducts inspections of registered firms — annually for firms auditing more than 100 issuers, at least triennially for others — and investigates and disciplines firms and auditors, with sanctions ranging from censure to fines and bars from practice. Its rules and budget are subject to approval by the Securities and Exchange Commission, whose commissioners appoint the Board's five members; by law only two members may be certified public accountants.

Funding and legal status

The Board is funded principally by an accounting support fee levied on public companies in proportion to their market capitalisation. Although created by federal statute and exercising regulatory power, the PCAOB is not formally a government agency — a structure tested in Free Enterprise Fund v. Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (2010), in which the U.S. Supreme Court upheld the Board's existence but held the dual layer of removal protection for its members unconstitutional, making members removable at will by the SEC.

International role

Foreign firms auditing U.S.-listed companies must also register, and the PCAOB conducts joint inspections with counterparts in many jurisdictions. Access disputes — most prominently with China, where inspectors were long barred from reviewing audit work papers — led to the Holding Foreign Companies Accountable Act (2020); in December 2022 the PCAOB announced it had secured complete inspection access in China and Hong Kong for the first time.
