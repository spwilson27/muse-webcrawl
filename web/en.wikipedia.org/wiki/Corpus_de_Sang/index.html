Corpus de Sang

The Corpus de Sang (Catalan for “Corpus of Blood”) was an uprising that took place in Barcelona and other parts of Catalonia on 7 June 1640, the feast of Corpus Christi, at the start of the Reapers' War (Guerra dels Segadors). During the riot, Catalan peasants and townspeople killed the viceroy of Catalonia, Dalmau de Queralt, Count of Santa Coloma, along with royal officials and Castilian soldiers quartered in the city.

Background

Catalonia had been strained by the Franco-Spanish War (1635–1659): the Count-Duke of Olivares sought to distribute the fiscal and military burdens of the Spanish monarchy more evenly through his Union of Arms scheme, and large numbers of Castilian and Italian troops were billeted in Catalan homes, provoking abuses and grievances. In the spring of 1640, armed clashes between peasants and soldiers multiplied, and insurgents burned the properties of royal officials and collaborators.

Events

Traditionally, large groups of reapers (segadors) entered Barcelona each June to be hired for the harvest. On Corpus Christi day 1640, tensions ignited into street fighting; royal judges and officials were attacked, prisoners were freed from the Drassanes, and the viceroy, fleeing toward the harbor, was killed on the beach near Montjuïc. The Pau Claris-led Generalitat subsequently repudiated Spanish rule, proclaimed a Catalan Republic in January 1641, and placed Catalonia under French protection, accepting Louis XIII as Count of Barcelona.

Legacy

The Corpus de Sang is remembered as the opening act of the Reapers' War, which lasted until 1652, when Barcelona surrendered to Spanish forces. The events are commemorated in the Catalan national anthem, Els Segadors (“The Reapers”), whose verses recall the revolt of 1640 and call Catalans to strike with the sickle.
