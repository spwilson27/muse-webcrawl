Daemon (computing)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In multitasking computer operating systems, a daemon (/ˈdiːmən/ or /ˈdeɪmən/) is a computer program that runs as a background process, rather than being under the direct control of an interactive user. Traditionally, the process names of a daemon end with the letter d: for example, syslogd implements the system logging facility, sshd handles incoming SSH connections, and httpd serves web requests.

Terminology

The term was coined by the programmers of MIT's Project MAC in the mid-1960s. They borrowed it from Maxwell's demon, an imaginary agent in a physics thought experiment, and from the Greek notion of a daemon as a helpful spirit working in the background. Daemons thus work tirelessly behind the scenes performing tasks without user intervention. The FreeBSD mascot, a cartoon demon named Beastie, plays on the similarity of pronunciation.

Behaviour

Daemons are usually started at boot time by init or a service manager such as systemd, and they typically run with no controlling terminal, detaching from the session that launched them (a procedure colloquially called "daemonising"). They perform services such as:

  • Network servers (web, mail, SSH, DNS)
  • Scheduled tasks (cron)
  • System logging and monitoring
  • Print spooling and device management

On Microsoft Windows, the equivalent background programs are called Windows services, and on macOS they are managed by launchd as launch daemons and agents. Daemons commonly respond to signals such as SIGHUP to reload their configuration.
