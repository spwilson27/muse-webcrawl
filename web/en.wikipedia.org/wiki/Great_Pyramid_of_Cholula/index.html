Great Pyramid of Cholula

LocationCholula, Puebla, Mexico
Heightabout 25 m (82 ft)
Baseabout 450 by 450 m (1,480 by 1,480 ft)
Volumeabout 4.45 million cubic metres

The Great Pyramid of Cholula, also known as Tlachihualtepetl (Nahuatl for "man-made mountain"), is a huge pyramid complex located in Cholula, Puebla, Mexico. It is the largest pyramid in the world by volume known to exist — larger than the Great Pyramid of Giza — and the largest monument ever constructed anywhere, with a total volume estimated at 4.45 million cubic metres.

Construction

The pyramid was built in at least six major stages over many centuries, beginning around the 3rd century BC and continuing into the early centuries AD, by successive cultures that inhabited Cholula, one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the Americas. The structure is made largely of adobe bricks and earth, and its final form measures roughly 450 metres (1,480 ft) square. Excavations have driven some 8 kilometres (5 mi) of tunnels through the pyramid, revealing superimposed platforms, stairways, and murals including the famous "Mural of the Drinkers."

Later history

By the Spanish conquest the pyramid was overgrown and resembled a natural hill. In 1594 the Spanish built the church of Nuestra Señora de los Remedios (Our Lady of Remedies) on its summit, which remains a major Catholic pilgrimage site. The pyramid lies within the Cholula archaeological zone and has been partially excavated and consolidated for visitors.
