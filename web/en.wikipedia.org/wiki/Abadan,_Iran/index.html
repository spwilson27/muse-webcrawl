Abadan (Persian: آبادان) is a city in Khuzestan province in southwestern Iran, situated on Abadan Island between the Karun and Arvand (Shatt al-Arab) rivers, close to the Iraqi border and the head of the Persian Gulf. The city grew from a small settlement into a major oil city after the Anglo-Persian Oil Company built its refinery there from 1909.

History

The Abadan Refinery, completed in 1912, became the largest oil refinery in the world and the centrepiece of Britain's petroleum interests, making Abadan a cosmopolitan company town with a large British expatriate community. The nationalisation of Iran's oil industry under Mohammad Mossadegh in 1951 triggered the Abadan Crisis, in which Britain organised a worldwide boycott of Iranian oil and the refinery was shut down until 1954. During the Iran–Iraq War the city was besieged and largely destroyed, its population evacuated; it was rebuilt after 1988.

Today

Abadan remains an important oil and petrochemical centre with a major port, known for its sweltering summers, date palms and a distinctive local culture shaped by the oil industry.
