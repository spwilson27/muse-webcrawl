El Zacatón

El Zacatón is a thermal-water-filled sinkhole (cenote) located in the Rancho La Azufrosa near Aldama, Tamaulipas, in northeastern Mexico. It is famous as one of the deepest known water-filled sinkholes in the world, with a total depth of about 339 metres (1,112 ft), and is part of a group of five interconnected sinkholes in the area.

Geology

The sinkhole formed in limestone through dissolution by mildly acidic, warm groundwater rising from a volcanic system. The water is around 30 °C and rich in sulfur, giving the cenote a distinctive smell. A layer of floating travertine and reeds covers part of the surface, from which the cenote takes its name: zacatón refers to the free-floating islands of zacate grass that drift across the surface.

Exploration

El Zacatón has been a focus of cave-diving exploration. In 1994, diver Sheck Exley died during an attempt to descend to the bottom. In 2007, the DEPTHX robotic probe, developed with NASA support, explored and mapped the sinkhole, reaching its bottom and confirming the depth of roughly 319–339 metres. The robot's mapping revealed that the cenote has no natural tunnels connecting it to other bodies of water at depth.

Microbial life

Studies of El Zacatón have found microbial mats growing on its walls below the sunlit zone, of interest to astrobiologists as analogues for life in extreme environments.
