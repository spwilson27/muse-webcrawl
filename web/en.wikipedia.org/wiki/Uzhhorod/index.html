Uzhhorod

Country: Ukraine

Oblast: Zakarpattia Oblast

Population: approx. 115,000

River: Uzh

Uzhhorod is a city in western Ukraine, on the Uzh River near the border with Slovakia. It is the administrative centre of Zakarpattia Oblast (Transcarpathia) and the historic centre of the region, with a population of about 115,000.

History

Uzhhorod, whose name means "Uzh castle", grew around a fortress of the Kingdom of Hungary and was known in Hungarian as Ungvár. After the First World War the city became part of Czechoslovakia as the capital of Subcarpathian Ruthenia; it was awarded to Hungary in 1938, taken by the Soviet Union in 1945 and incorporated into Soviet Ukraine, becoming part of independent Ukraine in 1991.

Sights

Landmarks include Uzhhorod Castle, housing the regional history museum; the former synagogue, now the philharmonic hall; the Greek Catholic cathedral; and an open-air museum of folk architecture. The city is also known for its avenues of cherry trees, which bloom spectacularly in spring, and for Uzhhorod National University, founded in 1945.
