Giant Lantern Festival

The Giant Lantern Festival (Filipino: Ligligan Parul) is an annual festival held in mid-December in the City of San Fernando in the Philippines. The festival features a competition of giant parol lanterns. Because of the popularity of the festival, the city has been nicknamed the "Christmas Capital of the Philippines".

The festival traces its roots to the lantern tradition brought to the Philippines during the Spanish colonial period. In the early 1900s, residents of San Fernando began crafting increasingly large and elaborate parols, and by 1908 an organized lantern competition had become part of the city's Christmas celebrations. Railway and electrical developments in the following decades allowed the lanterns to grow from modest star-shaped lamps into massive kaleidoscopic works of art.

The lanterns

The giant parols typically measure about 20 feet (6 metres) in diameter and are built from steel frames, fiberglass or plastic sheeting, and thousands of light bulbs — in modern lanterns, as many as 10,000 bulbs. A system of rotating cylinders and hairpins, controlled by skilled operators called kapitanes, makes the lights dance in time with music. Each lantern is produced by a barangay (village) of the city, and the competing entries are judged on craftsmanship, color harmony, and the complexity of their light choreography.

Significance

The festival has made lantern-making a defining industry of San Fernando and of the surrounding province of Pampanga. Parols from the region are exported around the world, and the festival itself draws tens of thousands of spectators each year. The event is staged in front of the city cathedral and, in recent decades, at shopping mall grounds in the city to accommodate the growing crowds.
