Rorschach (character)

Rorschach is a fictional character and one of the protagonists of Watchmen, the 1986–87 limited series published by DC Comics and created by writer Alan Moore and artist Dave Gibbons. His real name is Walter Joseph Kovacs. Named after the Rorschach inkblot test, he wears a white mask imprinted with a pattern of shifting black inkblots that never form a recognizable shape, reflecting his uncompromising black-and-white moral worldview.

Publication history

Moore and Gibbons based Rorschach in part on Steve Ditko's Charlton Comics character the Question, and on Ditko's earlier creation Mr. A, with elements drawn from Batman and film noir detectives. Rorschach narrates much of the series through excerpts from his journal, rendered in a clipped, telegraphic style that conveys his disturbed mental state.

Fictional character biography

Walter Kovacs was raised by an abusive, drug-addicted mother who worked as a prostitute. After a childhood incident in which he savagely attacked two older boys who taunted him, he was placed in a children's home, where he showed aptitude for study. Following his mother's murder, he took a job in a dress shop, where a garment ordered by a woman named Kitty Genovese — who never collected it — was made from a fabric with a heat-sensitive ink pattern. Kovacs kept the fabric and made it into his mask.

Genovese's subsequent brutal murder outside her apartment, witnessed by onlookers who did not intervene, convinced Kovacs that humanity was sunk in moral apathy. He began fighting crime as Rorschach, initially as a fairly conventional costumed vigilante. The turning point came in 1975, when he investigated the kidnapping of a young girl, Blair Roche, and discovered that her abductor had murdered her and fed her remains to his dogs. Kovacs killed the dogs and burned the man alive, later describing the episode as the moment "Kovacs" died and only Rorschach remained. From then on he spoke of himself in the third person as Rorschach and abandoned any mercy toward criminals.

In Watchmen

When the masked adventurer Edward Blake, the Comedian, is murdered in 1985, Rorschach begins investigating what he believes is a conspiracy to eliminate costumed heroes — his theory of a "mask killer". His investigation reunites him with his former partner Nite Owl (Dan Dreiberg) and draws in Silk Spectre, Doctor Manhattan and Adrian Veidt (Ozymandias). Rorschach is framed for murder by Veidt and imprisoned, where he defies inmates and his psychiatrist, Dr. Malcolm Long, whose optimism is gradually destroyed by Rorschach's bleak philosophy.

Freed from prison, Rorschach travels with Nite Owl to Antarctica, where they learn that Veidt has teleported a genetically engineered creature into New York City, killing millions and framing the attack as alien in origin in an attempt to unite humanity against a common threat. When the others agree to conceal the truth to preserve the fragile peace, Rorschach refuses to compromise — "Never compromise. Not even in the face of Armageddon." — and is killed by Doctor Manhattan rather than be allowed to expose Veidt. His journal, mailed earlier to the offices of the right-wing newspaper the New Frontiersman, ends the story ambiguous: whether it will be found and published is left unresolved.

Characterisation

Rorschach is an objectivist and moral absolutist who sees the world strictly in terms of good and evil. He is violent, socially withdrawn, fanatically devoted to his code, and indifferent to personal comfort, living in poverty on canned beans and stolen sugar cubes. Despite his brutality, critics have frequently ranked him among the greatest comic book characters ever created, and he is often cited as the breakout character of Watchmen.

In other media

Rorschach was portrayed by Jackie Earle Haley in Zack Snyder's 2009 film adaptation of Watchmen. A successor using the name and mask appears in the 2017–2019 sequel comic Doomsday Clock, and the masked Seventh Kavalry in HBO's 2019 Watchmen television series appropriate his mask and phrase "Watch over this boy".
