Genetic privacy

Genetic privacy concerns the collection, storage, use and disclosure of a person's genetic information — data derived from DNA testing that can reveal ancestry, health risks, biological relationships and identity. Because genetic data are inherently identifying and are shared in part with biological relatives, they raise privacy issues beyond those of ordinary medical records.

Issues

Concerns include genetic discrimination by employers or insurers, the use of consumer DNA databases by law enforcement (famously in identifying the Golden State Killer in 2018 through relatives' data), re-identification of "anonymous" research samples, and incidental findings such as misattributed parentage. Data breaches at testing companies can expose the information of millions of users at once.

Legal protections

In the United States, the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act of 2008 (GINA) prohibits genetic discrimination in health insurance and employment, but does not cover life, disability or long-term-care insurance. Other frameworks, including the European Union's GDPR, treat genetic data as a special category requiring heightened protection. Rules for forensic genealogy and direct-to-consumer testing continue to evolve.
