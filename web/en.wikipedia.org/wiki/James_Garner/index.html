James Garner (born James Scott Bumgarner; April 7, 1928 – July 19, 2014) was an American actor and producer. In a career spanning more than five decades, he starred in several television series, most notably as Bret Maverick in the Western series Maverick (1957–1962) and as Jim Rockford in the private-detective series The Rockford Files (1974–1980), for which he won an Emmy Award.

Early life and military service

Garner was born in Norman, Oklahoma. He dropped out of high school and served in the U.S. Army during the Korean War, where he was wounded twice and received two Purple Hearts. Returning to the United States, he drifted into acting after a friend in the business encouraged him to take a non-speaking role in a stage production of The Caine Mutiny Court-Martial.

Career

After signing with Warner Bros., Garner rose to fame as the wisecracking gambler Bret Maverick. He moved into films, appearing in Sayonara (1957), The Great Escape (1963), The Americanization of Emily (1964) — a personal favorite role — Grand Prix (1966), Support Your Local Sheriff! (1969), and Victor/Victoria (1982). He was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Actor for Murphy's Romance (1985).

As Jim Rockford, a wrongly convicted ex-con turned Los Angeles private investigator, Garner created one of television's defining roles, combining humor, vulnerability, and physical grit; he performed many of his own stunts and driving. Later work included the film Maverick (1994), Space Cowboys (2000), Divine Secrets of the Ya-Ya Sisterhood (2002), and The Notebook (2004).

Personal life and legacy

Garner married Lois Clarke in 1956, two weeks after they met, and they remained together until his death. An avid racing enthusiast, he drove the pace car at the Indianapolis 500 three times. He died of a heart attack in Los Angeles in 2014. His relaxed, anti-heroic screen persona influenced generations of television detectives and leading men.
