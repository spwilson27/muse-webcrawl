Demersal zone

The demersal zone is the part of the sea or ocean (or deep lake) that comprises the water column near the seabed and the benthos just above it. The word derives from the Latin demergere, "to sink". The demersal zone is typically occupied by fish and other organisms that feed on or near the bottom, known as demersal fish and benthic organisms.

Characteristics

The demersal zone lies just above the benthic zone and below the pelagic zone. Its depth can range from a few metres on the continental shelf to the margins of the deep ocean trenches. Because it is close to the bottom, it is influenced by the nature of the substrate — sand, mud, gravel, or rock — and by material sinking from above, including marine snow and the remains of organisms.

Life in the demersal zone

Demersal fish such as cod, haddock, flounder, and halibut live and feed in this zone and support major commercial fisheries, typically caught by bottom trawling. Many demersal species rest on the bottom or bury themselves in sediment, while others hover just above it. Invertebrates including crabs, shrimp, sea stars, and molluscs are also abundant. In deep water, demersal communities depend almost entirely on organic matter raining down from the sunlit surface layers.
