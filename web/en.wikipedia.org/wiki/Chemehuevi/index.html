Chemehuevi

The Chemehuevi are an Indigenous people of the Great Basin and Mojave Desert, the southernmost branch of the Southern Paiute. Their name for themselves is Nüwü ("the people"); "Chemehuevi" is generally said to be a Mojave word referring to those who "play with fish". Their traditional territory lay along the west bank of the Colorado River in present-day California, between the Mojave to the south and the Las Vegas Valley region, extending into the mountains and valleys of the eastern Mojave Desert.

History

Historically the Chemehuevi were a desert people living in small family groups, hunting game such as bighorn sheep, deer and jackrabbits, and gathering mesquite, pine nuts, and desert seeds. Those living near the Colorado River adopted floodplain agriculture — corn, beans, squash, melons and later wheat — from their Mojave neighbours, and Chemehuevi bands along the river became partly sedentary farmers.

Contact with Europeans came through Spanish explorers and, in the 19th century, through miners, settlers and the Colorado River steamboat trade. Conflict with the Mojave and pressure from settlers pushed many Chemehuevi from their valley lands. The Chemehuevi Reservation in San Bernardino County, California, along the Colorado River near Lake Havasu, was established in 1907, though much of it was later flooded by Parker Dam. Many Chemehuevi also live on the Colorado River Indian Tribes (CRIT) reservation in Arizona and California, alongside Mojave, Navajo and Hopi members, and on the Twenty-Nine Palms and other reservations.

Language

The Chemehuevi language is a Numic language of the Uto-Aztecan family, a dialect of the Colorado River Numic (Southern Paiute–Ute–Chemehuevi) language. It was documented extensively by linguists including John Peabody Harrington and, in the 20th century, by Margaret L. Press in her grammar and dictionary work with speaker Ralph Minthorn. The language is critically endangered, with a small number of fluent first-language speakers, and is the subject of revitalisation efforts.

Contemporary life

The federally recognised Chemehuevi Indian Tribe, headquartered at Havasu Lake, California, operates the Havasu Landing Resort and Casino on Lake Havasu. The tribe is known for fine basketry — Chemehuevi weavers, using willow, devil's claw and juncus, are regarded among the finest basket makers of the region.
