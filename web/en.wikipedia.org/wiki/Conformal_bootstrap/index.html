Conformal bootstrap

The conformal bootstrap is a non-perturbative mathematical method to constrain and solve conformal field theories (CFTs) using only fundamental principles such as symmetry, unitarity and the associativity of the operator product expansion — the bootstrap conditions.

Idea

In a CFT, all correlation functions are determined by the spectrum of primary operators — their scaling dimensions and spins — and the operator product expansion coefficients. Demanding that a four-point function computed in different channels agree (crossing symmetry) imposes powerful consistency constraints on this data.

Modern numerical bootstrap

Revived in 2008 by Rattazzi, Rychkov, Tonni and Vichi, numerical bootstrap methods use semidefinite programming to carve out the allowed space of CFT data. They have produced the world's most precise determinations of critical exponents for the 3D Ising and O(N) models, surpassing other methods, and are applied to gauge theories, condensed matter critical points and quantum gravity via holography.
