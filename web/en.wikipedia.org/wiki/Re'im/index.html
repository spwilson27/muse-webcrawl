Re'im (Hebrew: רעים, lit. "Friends") is a secular kibbutz in southern Israel. Located in the north-western Negev desert, close to the border with the Gaza Strip, it falls under the jurisdiction of Eshkol Regional Council. The kibbutz is situated near the confluence of the Besor and Gerar streams, west of the site of ancient Gerar.

History

Re'im was founded in 1949 by former Palmach members and members of the Israel Boy and Girl Scouts Federation. Its name commemorates Garin members — friends (re'im) — who fell in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. The kibbutz was designed by the architect Hanan Habaron, one of the founders of the kibbutz's architecture department. An abandoned structure from the Ottoman period, previously used by the founders as their first home, stands at the centre of the community.

October 2023 attack

On 7 October 2023, during the Hamas-led attack on Israel, the Supernova Sukkot Gathering, an open-air trance music festival held on grounds between Re'im and Kibbutz Be'eri, was attacked by militants. Around 364 people were killed at the festival site and others were taken hostage into Gaza, making it the deadliest incident of the day. Kibbutz Re'im itself was also attacked and its residents evacuated; a memorial was later established at the festival site. The nearby Re'im military base, headquarters of the IDF's Gaza Division, was overrun during the attack before being retaken.
