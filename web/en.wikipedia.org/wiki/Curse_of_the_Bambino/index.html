Curse of the Bambino


Image placeholder: Curse of the Bambino

The Curse of the Bambino was a superstitious explanation for the Boston Red Sox's failure to win the World Series from 1918 until 2004. According to legend, the drought began after the Red Sox sold star slugger Babe Ruth — nicknamed "the Bambino" — to the rival New York Yankees following the 1919 season, reportedly to finance the Broadway musical No, No, Nanette.

The drought

Before the sale, Boston had won five of the first fifteen World Series. Afterwards, the Yankees became the dominant franchise while the Red Sox suffered a series of agonising near-misses, including losses in 1946, 1967, 1975, and 1986, the last featuring Bill Buckner's famous error in Game 6 against the New York Mets.

Breaking the curse

The phrase was popularised by Boston Globe columnist Dan Shaughnessy's 1990 book The Curse of the Bambino. The "curse" was said to be broken in 2004, when the Red Sox came back from 3–0 down against the Yankees in the American League Championship Series and swept the St. Louis Cardinals in the World Series.
