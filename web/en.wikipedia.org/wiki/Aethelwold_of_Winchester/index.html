Æthelwold of Winchester

Æthelwold of Winchester (c. 904–984) was Bishop of Winchester from 963 to 984 and one of the leaders, with Dunstan and Oswald, of the English Benedictine Reform of the 10th century. He is venerated as a saint in the Catholic and Anglican churches, with his feast day on 1 August.

Life

Born at Winchester, Æthelwold was educated at the court of King Æthelstan and studied under Dunstan at Glastonbury Abbey, where he was ordained priest on the same day as Dunstan. He became abbot of Abingdon about 954 and restored it as a centre of monastic life. Appointed Bishop of Winchester in 963, he expelled the secular clerks from the Old and New Minsters and replaced them with monks, sometimes by force, and refounded or reformed monasteries throughout southern and eastern England, including Peterborough, Ely, and Thorney.

Works

Æthelwold translated the Rule of St Benedict into Old English and is associated with the Regularis Concordia, the customary that standardized monastic observance in England. His school at Winchester fostered a distinctive style of Latin prose and produced fine illuminated manuscripts, most famously the Benedictional of St Æthelwold. He was also a major political figure and a tutor of the young King Edgar.
