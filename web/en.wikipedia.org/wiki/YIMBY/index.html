YIMBY

YIMBY is an acronym for "yes, in my back yard," describing a pro-housing movement in contrast to NIMBY ("not in my back yard"). YIMBY advocates support building more housing — especially denser, multifamily, and transit-oriented housing — in high-demand urban areas, arguing that restrictive zoning and local veto points have caused housing shortages and high prices.

Origins and growth

The movement crystallized in the San Francisco Bay Area in the 2010s amid extreme housing costs, with groups such as the San Francisco Bay Area Renters' Federation and later YIMBY Action and California YIMBY organizing for upzoning and streamlined approvals. It drew an unusual coalition: market-oriented liberals and libertarians, environmentalists favoring infill over sprawl, and social justice advocates, alongside some developer support.

Policies and debates

Typical YIMBY-backed measures include legalizing duplexes and apartment buildings in single-family zones, reducing parking mandates, by-right approval for code-compliant projects, and state-level preemption of local restrictions — as in laws in Oregon, California, Minneapolis, and Auckland's Unitary Plan. Critics from the left argue market-rate construction alone does not guarantee affordability; critics from the right and neighborhood groups defend local control and neighborhood character. Economists generally find that increased supply moderates prices, though the scale and distribution of effects are debated.
