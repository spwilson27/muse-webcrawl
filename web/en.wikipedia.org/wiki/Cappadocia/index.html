Cappadocia (Turkish: Kapadokya) is a historical region in Central Anatolia, Turkey, largely in the provinces of Nevşehir, Kayseri, Aksaray, Kırşehir, and Niğde. It is famous for its unique geological formations, rock-cut churches and dwellings, and extensive underground cities.

Geology

Millions of years ago, eruptions of volcanoes including Mount Erciyes and Mount Hasan covered the plateau with soft volcanic tuff. Erosion sculpted the tuff into towers, cones, and "fairy chimneys" — tall, thin rock spires — capped by harder stone that protects the softer rock beneath.

History

Inhabited since ancient times and successively ruled by Hittites, Persians, Greeks, Romans, and Byzantines, Cappadocia became a refuge for early Christians, who carved churches, monasteries, and whole subterranean settlements such as Derinkuyu and Kaymaklı into the rock, decorated with Byzantine frescoes. The region was also home to the Cappadocian Fathers — Basil the Great, Gregory of Nyssa, and Gregory of Nazianzus — influential 4th-century theologians. In 1985 Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Tourism

Today Cappadocia is a major tourist destination, known for cave hotels and dawn hot-air balloon flights over the valleys.
