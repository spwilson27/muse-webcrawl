Nordic countries

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Nordic countries.

The Nordic countries are a geographical and cultural region in Northern Europe and the North Atlantic. The region comprises the sovereign states of Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden, together with the autonomous territories of the Faroe Islands and Greenland (within the Kingdom of Denmark) and Åland (within Finland). The five states and three autonomous territories share a population of about 28 million and have extensive cultural, historical and linguistic ties.

The term is often used interchangeably with Scandinavia, although in Nordic-language usage Scandinavia strictly denotes Denmark, Norway and Sweden (and sometimes their former territories), while Finland and Iceland complete the Nordic group.

Cooperation

The countries cooperate formally through the Nordic Council (founded 1952) and the Nordic Council of Ministers (1971). A common labour market dates from 1954 and the Nordic Passport Union abolished passport controls between member states decades before the Schengen Area, which all Nordic states joined. All except Norway and Iceland (which are in the EEA) are members of the European Union; Denmark, Finland, Norway, Iceland and Sweden are NATO members as of Finland's accession in 2023 and Sweden's in 2024.

Politics and society

The Nordic countries are consistently ranked among the world's most prosperous, egalitarian and least corrupt states. They share the "Nordic model": comprehensive welfare states, high levels of unionisation and collective bargaining, extensive public services funded by high taxation, and mixed-market economies. All five are constitutional monarchies (Denmark, Norway, Sweden) or republics (Finland, Iceland) with parliamentary systems and multi-party proportional representation.

Geography and climate

The region spans from temperate Denmark to the Arctic islands of Greenland and Svalbard. Landscapes include Norway's fjords, Sweden's forests and lakes, Finland's lakeland and boreal forest, volcanic Iceland, and the Greenland ice sheet, the world's second-largest body of ice. Climates range from oceanic to subarctic and polar, moderated along the Norwegian coast by the North Atlantic Current.

Languages

Danish, Norwegian, Swedish, Icelandic and Faroese are North Germanic languages, mutually intelligible to varying degrees in their mainland forms; Finnish, Sámi and Greenlandic belong to the Uralic and Eskimo–Aleut families respectively. English is widely spoken as a second language throughout the region.
