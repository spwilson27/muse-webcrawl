Oisín

Oisín (pronounced roughly "uh-SHEEN"), also spelled Ossian, is a legendary warrior and poet of Irish mythology, a member of the Fianna and the son of Fionn mac Cumhaill and Sadhbh. He is the narrator of much of the Fenian Cycle, recounting the deeds of the Fianna to Saint Patrick in his old age.

The voyage to Tír na nÓg

In the best-known tale, Oisín is visited by Niamh of the Golden Hair, a princess of Tír na nÓg, the Land of Youth. He rides with her across the sea and lives there in happiness, but after what seems three years he longs to see Ireland again. Niamh lends him her white horse and warns him not to dismount. On returning, Oisín finds three hundred years have passed; when he falls from the horse while helping men lift a stone, he instantly ages into an old man.

Legacy

James Macpherson's 18th-century "Ossian" poems, purported translations of ancient Scottish Gaelic verse, made the figure famous across Europe and influenced Romantic literature, despite controversy over their authenticity.
