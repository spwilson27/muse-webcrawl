Foreign object damage

Foreign object damage (FOD) is damage to an aircraft or other system caused by foreign objects — debris, loose items, wildlife or environmental material. In aviation the term also denotes the debris itself ("foreign object debris"). FOD is a major safety and cost concern, causing billions of dollars in damage annually, most notably to jet engines.

Types

Engine damage occurs when debris on runways and taxiways is ingested into engines, bending or fracturing fan and compressor blades; a famous example is the 2000 Concorde crash, caused when the aircraft struck a metal strip on the runway that burst a tyre and ruptured a fuel tank. Bird strikes are a closely related hazard, governed by similar prevention programmes. Internal FOD consists of objects left inside equipment during manufacture or maintenance — tools, fasteners or rags forgotten in closed assemblies.

Prevention

Airports minimise FOD with runway sweeping and inspection, FOD-detection radar systems, and "FOD walks" in which personnel line up to clear aprons by hand. Aerospace manufacturers and maintenance organisations use tool control (shadow boards, tool counts), clean-as-you-go discipline, FOD awareness training and designated FOD-critical areas, especially around military flight lines where standards are particularly strict.
