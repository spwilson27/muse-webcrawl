Oligospermia

Oligospermia, also called oligozoospermia, is a male fertility condition defined by an abnormally low concentration of sperm in the semen. Under the reference values of the World Health Organization, a sperm concentration below about 15 million sperm per millilitre of ejaculate is considered low. The condition is distinguished from azoospermia, the complete absence of sperm in the semen, and from asthenozoospermia (reduced motility) and teratozoospermia (abnormal morphology), though these frequently occur together.

Causes

Causes of oligospermia include pre-testicular factors such as hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and hormonal disorders; testicular factors such as varicocele, cryptorchidism, orchitis, genetic conditions including Klinefelter syndrome and Y-chromosome microdeletions, exposure to gonadotoxins, heat, radiation and certain drugs; and post-testicular factors such as obstruction of the ejaculatory ducts or retrograde ejaculation. Lifestyle factors including smoking, heavy alcohol use, obesity and anabolic steroid use are also associated with reduced sperm production. In a substantial fraction of cases no cause is found (idiopathic oligospermia).

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is by semen analysis, typically performed on at least two samples because sperm counts vary considerably over time and with abstinence interval, illness and fever. Physical examination, hormonal testing (FSH, LH, testosterone), scrotal ultrasound and genetic testing may be used to identify an underlying cause.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause: varicocele repair, hormonal therapy for hypogonadism, antibiotics for infection, or lifestyle modification. Where natural conception does not occur, assisted reproductive techniques are used, including intrauterine insemination for mild cases and in vitro fertilization with intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) for severe oligospermia.
