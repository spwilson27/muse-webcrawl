Maximilian Joseph Eugène Auguste Napoléon de Beauharnais, 3rd Duke of Leuchtenberg (2 October 1817 – 1 November 1852) was a Bavarian prince of the House of Beauharnais and, through his marriage to Grand Duchess Maria Nikolaevna, a member of the Russian imperial family, where he was granted the title Prince Romanovsky.

Life

Maximilian was the second son of Eugène de Beauharnais, stepson of Napoleon I, and Princess Augusta of Bavaria. He inherited the dukedom of Leuchtenberg from his elder brother Auguste in 1835. In 1839 he married Maria Nikolaevna, eldest daughter of Tsar Nicholas I of Russia, in the Winter Palace in St. Petersburg. Unusually for such a marriage, the couple remained in Russia, and Maximilian was given the style Imperial Highness and a commission in the Russian army.

Interests and patronage

A keen mineralogist and art collector, Maximilian served as president of the Imperial Academy of Arts from 1843 and was honorary president of the Russian Mineralogical Society. He founded an ironworks and took an active interest in mining and engineering in the Urals. His palace in St. Petersburg, the Mariinsky Palace built for Maria Nikolaevna, became a centre of the capital's cultural life.

Death and descendants

Maximilian died of tuberculosis in 1852, aged 35. His children, the princes and princesses Romanovsky, were raised at the Russian court, and his descendants form the Russian line of the Dukes of Leuchtenberg.
