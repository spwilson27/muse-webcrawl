The Rolling Stones

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Rolling Stones live in the 1970s: Jagger strutting at the mic, Richards slouched over a Telecaster]

The Rolling Stones are an English rock band formed in London in 1962. Active across eight decades, they are one of the most popular and enduring bands of the rock era. In their early years, the band pioneered the gritty, rhythm-and-blues-based sound that came to define hard rock, marketed as the dangerous counterpoint to the Beatles.

The core partnership of Mick Jagger and Keith Richards wrote most of the band's catalogue, including '(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction' (1965), 'Paint It Black', 'Sympathy for the Devil' and 'Gimme Shelter'. Their classic run of albums — Beggars Banquet (1968), Let It Bleed (1969), Sticky Fingers (1971) and Exile on Main St. (1972) — is considered one of the great sequences in rock. Founding member Brian Jones died in 1969; drummer Charlie Watts died in 2021, after 58 years with the band.
