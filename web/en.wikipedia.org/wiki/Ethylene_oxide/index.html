Ethylene oxide

FormulaC2H4O
Molar mass44.05 g/mol
Boiling point10.4 °C
AppearanceColourless gas
Other namesOxirane, epoxyethane

Ethylene oxide is an organic compound with the formula C2H4O. It is a cyclic ether and the simplest epoxide: a three-membered ring consisting of one oxygen atom and two carbon atoms. Ethylene oxide is a colourless and flammable gas with a faintly sweet odour. Because it is a strained ring, ethylene oxide easily participates in a number of addition reactions that result in ring-opening.

Production

Ethylene oxide is produced industrially by oxidation of ethylene in the presence of a silver catalyst at temperatures around 200–300 °C — the only practical direct oxidation of an alkene to an epoxide. World production exceeds 20 million tonnes annually, making it one of the largest-volume organic chemicals.

Uses

The great majority of ethylene oxide is converted to ethylene glycol for antifreeze and polyester production. The remainder goes into ethoxylated surfactants, ethanolamines, glycol ethers and other derivatives. Because it kills micro-organisms, ethylene oxide gas is widely used to sterilise medical equipment and supplies that cannot withstand steam sterilisation, and to fumigate some foodstuffs and cosmetics.

Hazards

Ethylene oxide is toxic, flammable and explosive. It is classified as a known human carcinogen: long-term occupational exposure is associated with increased risks of leukaemia and other cancers, which has led to progressively stricter emission and workplace exposure limits, particularly around commercial sterilisation facilities.
