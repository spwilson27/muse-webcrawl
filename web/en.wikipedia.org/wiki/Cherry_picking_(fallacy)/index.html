Cherry picking

Cherry picking, suppressing evidence, or the fallacy of incomplete evidence is the act of pointing to individual cases or data that seem to confirm a particular position while ignoring a significant portion of related and similar cases or data that may contradict that position. It is a kind of fallacy of selective attention, the most common example being the confirmation bias.

Etymology

The phrase is based on the perceived process of harvesting cherries: the picker would be expected to only select the ripest and healthiest fruits, giving an observer a misleading impression of the overall quality of the crop.

In science and debate

Cherry picking is a common criticism levelled in scientific and political debates, for instance when only favourable studies or time periods are cited. Related concepts include card stacking and misleading vividness.
