Colic

Colic is pain that starts and stops abruptly, caused by muscular contractions of a hollow organ such as the intestine or the ureter. The term is used for several conditions: infant colic, in which a healthy baby cries excessively; renal colic from kidney stones; biliary colic from gallstones; and equine colic, a major cause of death in horses.

Infant colic

Infant colic is commonly defined by the "rule of threes": crying for more than three hours a day, more than three days a week, for more than three weeks, in an otherwise healthy infant, typically starting in the first weeks of life and resolving by three to four months. Its cause is unknown; proposed explanations include gastrointestinal discomfort, immature gut flora and overstimulation.

Treatment

Most cases need only reassurance, soothing and ruling out serious illness. Changes to feeding, simethicone and some probiotic strains (notably Lactobacillus reuteri in breastfed infants) have been studied, with modest evidence for the latter.
