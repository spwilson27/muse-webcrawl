Computer security

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Computer security (also cybersecurity, digital security, or information technology security) is the protection of computer software, systems, and networks from threats that can lead to unauthorised information disclosure, theft or damage to hardware, software, or data, as well as disruption or misdirection of the services they provide.

The field is significant due to the expanded reliance on computer systems, the Internet, and wireless network standards such as Bluetooth and Wi-Fi. Growing use of "smart" devices, including smartphones, televisions, and the many devices constituting the Internet of things, has further increased the attack surface that security work must defend.

Core principles

Security is often described in terms of the CIA triad:

  • Confidentiality — information is accessible only to those authorised to have access.
  • Integrity — data is accurate and protected against unauthorised modification.
  • Availability — authorised users can access systems and data when needed.

Supporting mechanisms include authentication, authorization, encryption, and access control, governed by the principle of least privilege.

Threats

Threats include malware (viruses, worms, trojan horses, ransomware, and spyware), phishing and other social engineering, denial-of-service attacks, man-in-the-middle attacks, zero-day exploits against unpatched vulnerabilities, and insider threats. Attackers range from individual hackers to organised crime groups and state-sponsored advanced persistent threats.

Defences

Defensive practice combines technology and process: firewalls, antivirus and endpoint detection software, intrusion detection and prevention systems, timely software patching, secure software development, network segmentation, multi-factor authentication, backups, security monitoring, and user training. Organisations formalise these in security policies and standards such as ISO/IEC 27001, and employ specialists in roles from security analyst to chief information security officer.

Vulnerabilities and disclosure

A vulnerability is a weakness that can be exploited by a threat actor. Vendors and researchers coordinate disclosure so patches can be prepared before exploit details become public; catalogues such as the CVE list and databases maintained by national agencies track known vulnerabilities and their severity.
