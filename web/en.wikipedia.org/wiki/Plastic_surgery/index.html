Plastic surgery

Plastic surgery encompasses both reconstructive procedures and aesthetic (cosmetic) surgery.

Plastic surgery is a surgical specialty involving the restoration, reconstruction, or alteration of the human body. It can be divided into two main categories: reconstructive surgery and cosmetic (aesthetic) surgery. Reconstructive surgery includes craniofacial surgery, hand surgery, microsurgery, and the treatment of burns. The word "plastic" derives from the Greek plastikos, meaning to mould or shape; it has no connection with the synthetic material.

History

Reconstructive techniques were described in ancient India — the Sushruta Samhita (c. 600 BCE) describes forehead-flap rhinoplasty — and in Roman and medieval texts. Modern plastic surgery developed during the First World War, when surgeons such as Harold Gillies in Britain and, later, Archibald McIndoe treated servicemen with severe facial injuries; McIndoe's patients at East Grinstead formed the famous "Guinea Pig Club". The specialty expanded after the Second World War with advances in skin grafting, microsurgery and tissue expansion.

Reconstructive surgery

Reconstructive procedures correct defects from trauma, burns, cancer surgery (for example breast reconstruction after mastectomy), infection, and congenital conditions such as cleft lip and palate. Techniques include skin grafts, local and free tissue flaps, and microsurgical transfer of tissue with its blood supply.

Cosmetic surgery

Aesthetic procedures, usually elective and privately funded, include breast augmentation and reduction, liposuction, rhinoplasty, facelifts (rhytidectomy), abdominoplasty, and eyelid surgery (blepharoplasty). Non-surgical treatments such as botulinum toxin and dermal fillers are often grouped with the field. Cosmetic surgery is among the most commonly performed elective surgery worldwide, and its popularity has raised discussion of regulation, advertising standards, medical tourism, and the psychological screening of patients, including for body dysmorphic disorder.

Training

In most countries, plastic surgeons complete a dedicated residency or fellowship after medical school and general surgical training, with certification by national boards. Because the specialty is defined by techniques rather than one anatomical region, plastic surgeons operate on the whole body and often work in multidisciplinary teams.
