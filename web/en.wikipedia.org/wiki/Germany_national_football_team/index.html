Germany national football team

Nickname: Die Mannschaft ("The Team")
Association: Deutscher Fußball-Bund (DFB)
Confederation: UEFA (Europe)
Home stadium: Various (no single national stadium)
FIFA code: GER

The Germany national football team (German: Deutsche Fußballnationalmannschaft) represents Germany in men's international football and has played its matches since 1908. It is governed by the German Football Association (DFB), founded in 1900. Germany is one of the most successful national teams in international competition, having won four FIFA World Cups (1954, 1974, 1990, and 2014), three UEFA European Championships (1972, 1980, and 1996), and one FIFA Confederations Cup (2017).

History

Between 1949 and 1990, the DFB represented West Germany, while the Saarland and East Germany fielded separate teams. After German reunification in 1990, the East German players joined the unified team, which won its fourth World Cup in Brazil in 2014 under Joachim Löw, defeating Argentina 1–0 after extra time in the final. Famous earlier triumphs include the "Miracle of Bern" in 1954 and the home victory of 1974.

Team image and style

Germany traditionally plays in white shirts, black shorts, and white socks, colours derived from the flag of Prussia. The team has long been noted for its organisation, discipline, physical conditioning, and tournament consistency — for decades it was unusual for Germany to be eliminated before the semi-finals of a major tournament. A painful group-stage exit at the 2018 World Cup, the first since 1938, and a repeat in 2022 marked a downturn that led to generational change in the squad.

Notable players

Miroslav Klose is both Germany's record goalscorer (71 goals) and the World Cup's all-time top scorer. Other celebrated figures include Franz Beckenbauer — a World Cup winner as both captain (1974) and coach (1990) — Lothar Matthäus, the team's most-capped player, goalkeeper Manuel Neuer, and midfielders such as Toni Kroos and Bastian Schweinsteiger.
