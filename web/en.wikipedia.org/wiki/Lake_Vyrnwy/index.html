Lake Vyrnwy (Welsh: Llyn Efyrnwy) is a reservoir in Powys, Wales, built in the 1880s to supply the city of Liverpool with fresh water. It flooded the head of the Vyrnwy valley, submerging the village of Llanwddyn, whose inhabitants were rehoused in a new village below the dam.

The dam

The Vyrnwy dam, designed by Thomas Hawksley and George Deacon, was the first large masonry dam built in Britain: a stone gravity dam 44 m (144 ft) high and 357 m long, completed in 1888. Unusually, its spillway runs over the dam crest itself. Water travels to Liverpool through the Vyrnwy Aqueduct, about 110 km (68 mi) of tunnels and pipelines crossing the Welsh hills and the Cheshire plain.

Nature and recreation

The lake, some 7.6 km long, is surrounded by the Lake Vyrnwy Nature Reserve, managed with the RSPB; the surrounding estate is noted for its moorland and woodland birds. The famous Vyrnwy straining tower, a Gothic-style structure in the lake, draws off water for the aqueduct.
