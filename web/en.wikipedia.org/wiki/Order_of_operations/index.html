Order of operations

In mathematics and computer programming, the order of operations is a collection of rules that reflect conventions about which operations to perform first in order to evaluate a given mathematical expression. Without such rules, an expression like 1 + 2 × 3 would be ambiguous: read left to right it gives 9, but by convention multiplication takes precedence and the value is 7.

The rules

  1. Parentheses (and other grouping symbols) first, from innermost outward.
  2. Exponents and roots.
  3. Multiplication and division, from left to right (equal precedence).
  4. Addition and subtraction, from left to right (equal precedence).

Mnemonics

In the United States the acronym PEMDAS and the phrase "Please Excuse My Dear Aunt Sally" are taught; in other English-speaking countries BODMAS or BIDMAS (Brackets, Orders/Indices, Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction) are common. Mnemonics can mislead if taken to imply that multiplication strictly precedes division, or addition subtraction: those pairs have equal rank and are resolved left to right.

Special cases

Ambiguities arise with implied multiplication (juxtaposition, as in 1/2x), the unary minus with exponents (−32 = −9 by convention), and stacked exponentiation, which is evaluated right-to-left (top-down). Serial division expressed with the obelus (÷) or slashes is a frequent source of viral disputes; professional style guides recommend parentheses to remove doubt.

In computing

Programming languages define operator precedence tables in the same spirit, though the number of levels is much larger; compilers parse expressions accordingly. Reverse Polish notation, used by some calculators, eliminates the need for precedence rules entirely.
