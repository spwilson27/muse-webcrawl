Guilloché

Technique: Engine turning

Used on: Watches, jewellery, banknotes, passports

Guilloché is a decorative technique in which a very precise, intricate, repetitive pattern is mechanically engraved into a material with fine detail. The patterns are produced on a rose engine lathe by engine turning, in which a cutting head traces geometric curves guided by patterned cams ("rosettes").

Applications

Guilloché work is prized on fine watch dials and cases, gold and enamel jewellery and Fabergé objects. Printed guilloché patterns — fine interlaced curves generated mathematically — are used as a security feature on banknotes, passports and certificates because they are difficult to reproduce accurately with ordinary copying or printing equipment.
