Baylor Bears football

The Baylor Bears football team represents Baylor University in Waco, Texas, in American football. The Bears compete in the NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS) as a member of the Big 12 Conference and play their home games at McLane Stadium, which opened in 2014 on the banks of the Brazos River.

History

Baylor fielded its first football team in 1899. The programme spent much of the twentieth century in the Southwest Conference, winning conference titles in years including 1974 under coach Grant Teaff, whose "Miracle on the Brazos" team ended a long championship drought. After the Southwest Conference dissolved, Baylor joined the Big 12 in 1996 and endured a long period of losing seasons.

The programme's modern rise began under coach Art Briles, hired in 2008. Quarterback Robert Griffin III won the Heisman Trophy in 2011, the first in school history, and Baylor won back-to-back Big 12 championships in 2013 and 2014. Briles was dismissed in 2016 following a sexual assault scandal involving members of the team. After an interim season, Matt Rhule rebuilt the programme, reaching the Big 12 Championship Game in 2019 before departing for the NFL.

Recent years

Under coach Dave Aranda, hired in 2020, Baylor won the Big 12 championship in 2021 and defeated Ole Miss in the Sugar Bowl, finishing with a school-record 12 wins. The Bears' colours are green and gold, and their mascot is a live North American black bear, continuing a tradition dating to the early twentieth century.
