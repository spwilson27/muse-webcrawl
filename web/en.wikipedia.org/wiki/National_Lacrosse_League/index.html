The National Lacrosse League (NLL) is a men's professional box lacrosse league in North America. The league is headquartered in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, and its teams are divided into conferences. The NLL is the premier professional box lacrosse league in the world and has featured teams across Canada and the United States. Its season runs roughly from December to June, culminating in the NLL Finals for the NLL Cup.

History

The league was founded in 1986 as the Eagle Pro Box Lacrosse League by Chris Fritz and Russ Cline, playing its first season in 1987 with four teams. In 1988 it was renamed the Major Indoor Lacrosse League (MILL), and in 1997 the league restructured under its current name, the National Lacrosse League, with teams owned individually rather than by the league. The Philadelphia Wings, an original franchise, were among its most successful, and the Toronto Rock became a dominant club after joining in 1999.

Format and rules

Box lacrosse is played indoors on a hockey-rink-sized floor, five runners plus a goaltender per side, with a 30-second shot clock. NLL games consist of four 15-minute quarters. The fast pace, physical contact, and high scoring (commonly 10–15 goals per team) give the league its "fastest game on two feet" character.

Teams

Notable franchises have included the Buffalo Bandits, Toronto Rock (later based in Hamilton/Oakville), Calgary Roughnecks, Saskatchewan Rush, Halifax Thunderbirds, Colorado Mammoth, Georgia Swarm, San Diego Seals, and Vancouver Warriors. The Buffalo Bandits are noted for the league's largest crowds, regularly drawing over 15,000 fans to "Banditland" at KeyBank Center.

Players

Many NLL players come from Canadian box lacrosse and American field lacrosse backgrounds, and a significant number are members of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) nations, the originators of the game. Stars have included John Tavares — the league's all-time leading scorer — Gary and Paul Gait, Dhane Smith, and Josh Byrne. Most players historically held other full-time jobs, as average salaries remained modest compared with other major leagues.
