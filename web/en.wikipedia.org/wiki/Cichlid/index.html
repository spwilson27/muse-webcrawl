Cichlid

Cichlids are fish from the family Cichlidae in the order Cichliformes. They form one of the largest vertebrate families, with at least 1,650 scientifically described species and estimates of the total number ranging up to 3,000. Cichlids are found chiefly in fresh waters of Africa and the Americas, with smaller numbers in Asia and Madagascar.

Distribution and diversity

The great lakes of the East African Rift — Lake Malawi, Lake Tanganyika and Lake Victoria — each harbour hundreds of endemic cichlid species, the product of rapid adaptive radiations that make cichlids a classic example of explosive speciation. South and Central America host many well-known genera, including angelfish, discus and convict cichlids.

Behaviour

Cichlids are noted for advanced parental care: many species guard eggs and fry, and mouthbrooding — incubating the eggs in a parent's mouth — has evolved repeatedly. Their pharyngeal jaws, a second set of jaws in the throat, allow specialised feeding on snails, algae, plankton or other fish.

In aquaria and as food

Many cichlids are popular aquarium fish. Tilapias are among the world's most important farmed food fish, while introduced Nile perch and escaped tilapia have damaged native cichlid faunas, most notoriously in Lake Victoria.
