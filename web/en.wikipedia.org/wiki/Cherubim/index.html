Cherubim

Cherubim (singular cherub; Hebrew: כְּרוּבִים, kərūḇīm) are a class of celestial beings or angels mentioned in the Hebrew Bible. In Genesis, cherubim with a flaming sword guard the entrance to the Garden of Eden after Adam and Eve's expulsion. Golden cherubim spread their wings over the mercy seat of the Ark of the Covenant, and colossal carved cherubim stood in Solomon's Temple.

Ezekiel's vision

The Book of Ezekiel gives the most detailed description: each cherub has four faces — of a man, a lion, an ox (later called a cherub's face) and an eagle — four wings, human hands, and straight legs with feet like a calf's, moving amid wheels (the ophanim) beside the throne of God. These awesome hybrid creatures bear little resemblance to the plump childlike figures of later art.

In later tradition

In Christian angelology, following Pseudo-Dionysius, cherubim rank in the highest choir of angels, second only to the seraphim, and are associated with knowledge. Renaissance and Baroque art conflated cherubim with the putti — chubby winged infants derived from Greco-Roman erotes — an image now dominant in popular culture. The word is plausibly related to Akkadian karību, an intercessory spirit, and cherubim share features with winged hybrid guardians of ancient Near Eastern art such as the lamassu.
