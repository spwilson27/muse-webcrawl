Davao City (Cebuano: Dakbayan sa Dabaw) is a highly urbanized city in the Davao Region of the Philippines, located on the island of Mindanao. With a land area of about 2,444 square kilometres it is the largest city in the Philippines by area, and with roughly 1.78 million inhabitants at the 2020 census it is the third-most populous city in the country and the most populous in Mindanao. It serves as the centre of Metro Davao, the region's principal economic and commercial hub.

Davao City lies on the northwestern shore of Davao Gulf, in the shadow of Mount Apo.

Geography

The city sits along Davao Gulf at the mouth of the Davao River, with the island of Samal offshore. Mount Apo, the highest peak in the Philippines at 2,954 metres, rises to the west on the city's border. Davao City has a tropical rainforest climate with rainfall spread fairly evenly through the year and lies outside the main typhoon belt.

History

Indigenous groups including the Bagobo, Manobo and Ata peoples inhabited the area before Spanish forces under José Oyanguren established a settlement in 1848 after defeating the local ruler Datu Bago. The town grew rapidly in the early 20th century under American administration, when Japanese entrepreneurs developed extensive abacá plantations. Davao was chartered as a city in 1937. Rodrigo Duterte, later president of the Philippines, served as the city's mayor for over two decades and its politics remain closely associated with his family.

Economy

Davao is a major exporter of tropical fruit — bananas, durian, mangosteen and pomelo — earning it the nickname "Durian Capital of the Philippines". The port, Francisco Bangoy International Airport and a growing business-process outsourcing sector anchor its economy.
