Interracial marriage

Interracial marriage is a marriage involving spouses who belong to different "races" or racialized ethnicities. In the past, such marriages were outlawed in many jurisdictions, including the United States, Nazi Germany and apartheid South Africa; laws restricting marriage between different races were known as anti-miscegenation laws.

History and legal status

Relationships and marriages across ethnic groups have occurred throughout history, but colonial and segregationist regimes frequently prohibited or stigmatized them. In the United States, interracial marriage was banned in many states from the colonial era; the Supreme Court unanimously struck down all such bans in Loving v. Virginia (1967). Nazi Germany's Nuremberg Laws of 1935 prohibited marriages between Jews and those of "German or related blood", and South Africa's Prohibition of Mixed Marriages Act (1949) remained in force until 1985.

Prevalence and attitudes

Rates of interracial marriage have risen substantially in many countries since the late 20th century; in the United States, about one in six newlyweds in recent years has a spouse of a different race or ethnicity. Public approval has likewise increased dramatically, though social attitudes vary by country, community and the particular groups involved, and children of such marriages may be described as multiracial.
