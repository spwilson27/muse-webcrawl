Heart block

Heart block (HB) is a disorder of the heart's electrical conduction system in which the electrical impulses that coordinate the heartbeat are delayed or blocked as they travel from the atria to the ventricles. When the block occurs between the sinoatrial node and the atrioventricular node, or at the atrioventricular node itself, it is called atrioventricular block (AV block). Heart block can also occur within the bundle branches (bundle branch block).

Types

AV block is classified by severity. In first-degree AV block, impulses are slowed (a prolonged PR interval on the ECG) but all reach the ventricles. In second-degree AV block, some impulses fail to conduct; it is subdivided into Mobitz type I (Wenckebach), with progressive lengthening of the PR interval before a dropped beat, and Mobitz type II, in which beats are dropped without prior lengthening. In third-degree (complete) heart block, no atrial impulses reach the ventricles, which beat independently at a slow escape rhythm.

Causes and treatment

Causes include ageing and fibrosis of the conduction system, ischaemic heart disease and myocardial infarction, congenital defects, certain medications (such as beta blockers and digoxin), and infections such as Lyme disease. Mild block may cause no symptoms and need no treatment. Symptomatic or high-grade block can cause fatigue, dizziness, fainting (syncope), and heart failure, and is usually treated with an artificial pacemaker.
