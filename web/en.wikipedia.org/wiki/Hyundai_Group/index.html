Hyundai Group

Hyundai Group is a South Korean conglomerate founded by Chung Ju-yung in 1947 as a construction company. Hyundai — the name derives from the Korean word for "modernity" — grew into the largest chaebol in South Korea, with interests spanning shipbuilding, automobiles, construction, electronics, finance and heavy industry, before being broken up after the Asian financial crisis.

History

Hyundai Engineering and Construction was founded in 1947. The group expanded rapidly during South Korea's industrialisation, founding Hyundai Motor Company in 1967 and Hyundai Heavy Industries in 1972; its Ulsan shipyard became the largest in the world. Hyundai Electronics (later SK hynix) and other affiliates followed. Chung Ju-yung also ran for president in 1992 and later championed engagement with North Korea, symbolised by cattle drives across the DMZ in 1998 and the Mount Kumgang tourism project.

Break-up

After the 1997 Asian financial crisis and Chung's death in 2001, the group was dismantled: Hyundai Motor Group, Hyundai Heavy Industries Group and other units became independent groups, leaving the much-reduced Hyundai Group centred on Hyundai Engineering and Construction (later sold), Hyundai Elevator, Hyundai Asan and shipping interests.
