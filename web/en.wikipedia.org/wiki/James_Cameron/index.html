James Cameron

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: James Cameron aboard a research vessel during a deep-sea expedition, the explorer-director between films at the bottom of the ocean]

James Francis Cameron (born 16 August 1954) is a Canadian film director, producer, screenwriter and deep-sea explorer, the commercially most successful director in cinema history: he made three of the four highest-grossing films of all time — Avatar (2009), Avatar: The Way of Water (2022) and Titanic (1997) — each passing two billion dollars worldwide.

Born in Kapuskasing, Ontario, Cameron worked as a truck driver while teaching himself effects on Roger Corman's productions, broke through with The Terminator (1984, from his own fever dream of a metal skeleton), and defined modern action with Aliens (1986), The Abyss (1989), Terminator 2: Judgment Day (1991) and True Lies (1994). Titanic's eleven Academy Awards tied the all-time record; Avatar's 3D and performance-capture technology reset the industry's technical frontier.

Method and myth

Cameron's productions are legends of scale and will: Titanic's near-full-size ship set in a purpose-built Mexican tank, Avatar's decade of technology development before shooting, casts and crews driven to their limits and mostly forgiving him for the results. His perfectionism builds the tools the rest of cinema then rents.

The explorer

Cameron has made dozens of deep dives, co-designed the submersible Deepsea Challenger and in 2012 made the first solo descent to the Challenger Deep, the ocean's deepest point; his documentaries (Ghosts of the Abyss, Aliens of the Deep) and his Titanic wreck dives blur the line between his cinema and his oceanography. The Avatar sequels, planned as a five-film saga, continue into the late 2020s.
