Angelus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Angelus bell and prayer in a parish church

The Angelus is a Catholic devotion commemorating the Incarnation, traditionally recited three times a day — at 6 a.m., noon and 6 p.m. — announced by the ringing of the Angelus bell. It consists of three Hail Marys interspersed with versicles drawn from the Annunciation narrative, concluding with a collect prayer.

History and practice

The devotion developed in medieval Europe from the practice of evening prayer signalled by convent and curfew bells; the midday recitation was promoted in connection with calls for prayer during the Ottoman wars of the 15th century. The Angelus is still broadcast daily on Irish radio and television, and the Pope traditionally leads its recitation on Sundays from St Peter's Square (replaced by the Regina Caeli during Eastertide). Jean-François Millet's famous painting The Angelus (1857–59) depicts peasants pausing at dusk to recite it.
