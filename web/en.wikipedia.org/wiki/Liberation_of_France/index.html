Liberation of France

The Liberation of France in the Second World War was accomplished through diplomacy, resistance and Allied military campaigns in 1944 and 1945, ending the four-year German occupation following the Battle of France and the regime of Vichy France.

Liberation of France
Date1944–1945
Key eventsD-Day Normandy landings; Operation Dragoon; Liberation of Paris

Course

Allied forces landed in Normandy on 6 June 1944 (Operation Overlord) and in Provence on 15 August (Operation Dragoon). The French Resistance rose, Paris was liberated on 25 August 1944 by Leclerc's 2nd Armoured Division with Allied support, and Charles de Gaulle's Provisional Government took power. Remaining German pockets on the Atlantic coast held out until May 1945.

Aftermath

The liberation was followed by the épuration — purges of collaborators — purges, trials, and the restoration of the Republic, leading to the French Fourth Republic in 1946. See Normandy landings, Liberation of Paris and French Resistance.
