Eulerian number

In combinatorics, the Eulerian number A(n, m), often written ⟨n m⟩, is the number of permutations of the numbers 1 through n in which exactly m elements are greater than the previous element. Such elements are called ascents (or rises). The Eulerian numbers are named after Leonhard Euler, who studied them in his 1736 paper on the sums of powers and related counting problems.

Basic properties

For m > n the Eulerian number is zero, and A(n, 0) = 1 for all n, corresponding to the unique decreasing permutation. The Eulerian numbers obey the symmetry A(n, m) = A(n, n − 1 − m), and summing over all m gives the factorial: Σm A(n, m) = n!.

Recurrence relation

The Eulerian numbers satisfy the recurrence

A(n, m) = (n − m) A(n − 1, m − 1) + (m + 1) A(n − 1, m),

which is reminiscent of the recurrence for the binomial coefficients and Stirling numbers.

Worpitzky identity

The Eulerian numbers appear in the Worpitzky identity, which expresses powers in terms of binomial coefficients: xn = Σm A(n, m) C(x + m, n). This identity underlies Euler's formulas for sums of powers of integers.
