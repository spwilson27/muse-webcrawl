Battle of Kings Mountain

The Battle of Kings Mountain was fought on 7 October 1780 during the American Revolutionary War, near the border of North and South Carolina. Patriot militia from the backcountry — including the "Overmountain Men" from the Watauga settlements — surrounded and destroyed a Loyalist force under British Major Patrick Ferguson, the only British regular officer present in a battle fought almost entirely between Americans.

Background

After capturing Charleston and winning at Camden, Lord Cornwallis advanced into North Carolina in the autumn of 1780, with Ferguson's Loyalist militia covering his left flank. Ferguson's threats against the mountain settlements — that he would march over the mountains, hang their leaders and lay waste their country — provoked the Overmountain Men to muster. Some 900 picked riflemen rode after Ferguson, who took up a strong position on the flat-topped, wooded ridge of Kings Mountain.

The battle

The Patriots surrounded the ridge and attacked up its slopes from all sides, using the trees for cover. Repeated Loyalist bayonet charges drove the riflemen back downhill, but they rallied and returned each time. When Ferguson was shot down while trying to break through the encirclement, his second-in-command raised the white flag. Patriot losses were 28 killed and 62 wounded; the Loyalists lost around 290 killed, 163 wounded and 668 captured — effectively the entire force.

Aftermath

Kings Mountain destroyed Cornwallis's left wing and forced him to abandon his invasion of North Carolina, retreating into South Carolina. Thomas Jefferson called it "the turn of the tide of success" in the southern war; it revived Patriot morale after a string of disasters and began the chain of events leading to Cowpens, Guilford Courthouse and ultimately Yorktown. The battlefield is now preserved as Kings Mountain National Military Park.
