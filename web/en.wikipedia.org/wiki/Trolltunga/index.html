Trolltunga ("the troll's tongue") is a rock formation situated about 1,180 metres (3,870 ft) above sea level in Ullensvang Municipality in Vestland county, Norway. The cliff juts horizontally out from the mountain, hanging about 700 metres (2,300 ft) above the north side of the lake Ringedalsvatnet. Its distinctive tongue-like shape was formed by ice erosion during the Pleistocene: water froze in cracks in the rock and broke off large angular blocks, leaving the thin slab that projects over the valley today.

Tourism

Once known mainly to local hikers, Trolltunga became one of Norway's most famous natural attractions in the 2010s through social media. Visitor numbers rose from a few hundred per year before 2010 to tens of thousands per season. The hike from the main trailhead at Skjeggedal is a demanding round trip of roughly 27 kilometres (17 mi) with about 800–1,100 metres of ascent, typically taking 8–12 hours; a newer trailhead at P2 (Mågelitopp) shortens the walk. The hiking season runs roughly from June to September, and guided tours are recommended outside it.

Safety

The route is long, exposed to fast-changing mountain weather, and has no mobile coverage in places; Norwegian rescue services conduct several operations there each year, and authorities advise proper clothing, food, and starting early. A shuttle bus and parking facilities serve the trailheads, and a mountain rescue cabin (Trolltunga's emergency shelter) stands along the route. The popularity of the site has raised debate in Norway about overtourism and whether access should be regulated.
