Louis Charles Breguet (2 January 1880 – 4 May 1955) was a French aviation pioneer and aircraft manufacturer, founder of the Breguet aviation company (Société des Ateliers d'Aviation Louis Breguet). He was the grandson of Louis-François-Clement Breguet and great-great-grandson of Abraham-Louis Breguet, the famous watch and clock maker.

Early work and the helicopter

Trained as an electrical engineer at the École supérieure d'électricité (Supélec), Breguet first worked in the family electrical business. In 1907, with his brother Jacques and Professor Charles Richet, he built the Breguet-Richet Gyroplane No. 1, a four-rotor machine that lifted a man into the air — the first recorded vertical take-off by a piloted aircraft — though it was steadied by assistants on the ground and did not fly freely.

Aeroplanes

Breguet turned to fixed-wing aircraft and in 1911 founded his own company at Douai. His Breguet 14 reconnaissance bomber and light bomber was one of the most successful French aircraft of the First World War, produced in thousands. In the interwar years the Breguet 19 became famous for long-distance record flights, and in 1919 Breguet founded the Compagnie des Messageries Aériennes, an early airline that eventually became part of Air France.

During the Second World War, under the Vichy period and German occupation, Breguet largely withdrew from public life. After the war his company developed the Breguet Deux-Ponts transport and the Breguet Alizé anti-submarine aircraft. The firm later merged into Avions Marcel Dassault–Breguet Aviation in 1971.

Honours

Breguet was a member of the French Academy of Sciences and was elected to its chair in 1941. He won a silver medal in sailing at the 1924 Summer Olympics in Paris, in the 8 Metre class aboard the yacht Namoura.
