Parliamentary constituency boundaries are reviewed by four independent commissions.

In the United Kingdom, the boundary commissions are non-departmental public bodies responsible for determining the boundaries of parliamentary constituencies for the House of Commons. There are four separate commissions, one each for England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. The commissions for the devolved legislatures and for local government are separate bodies.

History

Ad hoc commissions reviewed boundaries in the 19th and early 20th centuries. Permanent boundary commissions were first established by the House of Commons (Redistribution of Seats) Act 1944, and periodic reviews have been held under successive legislation, principally the Parliamentary Constituencies Act 1986. Reviews were originally required every 8 to 12 years; the Parliamentary Voting System and Constituencies Act 2011 shortened the cycle to five years, but the Parliamentary Constituencies Act 2020 restored an eight-year cycle and fixed the size of the Commons at 650 seats.

Rules and procedure

By law, each constituency (with a few protected exceptions, such as island seats) must have an electorate within five per cent of the UK electoral quota, the average electorate per seat. Commissions publish initial proposals, consult the public through written representations and public hearings, issue revised proposals, and submit a final report to the Speaker. Since 2020 the reports are implemented automatically by Order in Council without a parliamentary vote, removing the possibility of the government of the day blocking changes it dislikes.

The 2023 review

The 2023 Periodic Review of Westminster constituencies, conducted under the 2020 Act, was the first to take effect automatically. Its recommendations applied at the 2024 general election, redrawing many boundaries to reflect population movement from the north and Wales toward London and the south-east of England.
