Paper recycling

Paper recycling is the process of turning waste paper into new paper products. Recycled paper is used for newsprint, packaging, tissue, and printing papers, saving trees, water, and energy compared with virgin pulp production.

Process

Collected paper is sorted by grade, cleaned, and pulped in water. Inks and adhesives are removed by washing or flotation deinking, after which the pulp may be bleached, blended with virgin fibre, and formed into new sheets. Fibres shorten each cycle, so paper can typically be recycled about five to seven times.

History and rates

Paper was recycled in Japan as early as the 11th century. Modern recovery rates exceed 70% in much of Europe, while global rates vary with collection systems and contamination, especially from food-soiled paper.
