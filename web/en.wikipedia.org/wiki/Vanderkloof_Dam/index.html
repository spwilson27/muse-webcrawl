The Vanderkloof Dam (originally the P. K. le Roux Dam) is an arch dam on the Orange River in South Africa, situated near the town of Petrusville and spanning the border between the Northern Cape and Free State provinces. Completed in 1977, it is the second-largest dam in South Africa by reservoir volume, after the Gariep Dam farther upstream, and forms a key part of the Orange River Project for irrigation, water supply, and hydroelectric power generation.

History

Construction of the dam began in 1971 as part of the broader Orange River development scheme. The dam was originally named after P. K. le Roux, a former Minister of Water Affairs, but was renamed Vanderkloof in 1981 after the nearby farm. Its reservoir, when full, covers approximately 300 square kilometres and holds on the order of 3.2 billion cubic metres of water.

Power generation

The dam's power station houses two generators of about 120 MW each, giving an installed hydroelectric capacity of roughly 240 MW, which is used primarily for peak-demand supply to the national grid operated by Eskom. Water released from the dam also supports extensive irrigation schemes downstream along the Orange River, including vineyards and lucerne farming in the region around Vanderkloof and Petrusville.
