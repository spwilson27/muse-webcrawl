Verapamil

Drug classCalcium channel blocker (non-dihydropyridine)
RoutesBy mouth, intravenous
Trade namesIsoptin, Calan, others

Verapamil, sold under various trade names including Isoptin and Calan, is a calcium channel blocker medication used for the treatment of high blood pressure, angina (chest pain from not enough blood flow to the heart), and supraventricular tachycardia. It may also be used for the prevention of migraines and cluster headaches. It is given by mouth or by injection into a vein.

Medical uses

Verapamil is used for hypertension, angina pectoris, and cardiac arrhythmia. It controls the heart rate in atrial fibrillation and is a treatment of choice for supraventricular tachycardia. Because it relaxes vascular smooth muscle it also lowers blood pressure. Off-label uses include migraine and cluster headache prophylaxis.

Mechanism of action

Verapamil is a non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker. It blocks L-type calcium channels in cardiac and vascular smooth muscle, reducing heart rate, slowing conduction through the atrioventricular node, weakening contraction, and dilating arteries. It is a class IV antiarrhythmic agent in the Vaughan Williams classification.

Side effects

Common side effects include headache, low blood pressure, nausea and constipation; constipation is particularly characteristic. Serious side effects include excessive slowing of the heart, worsening heart failure and allergic reactions. It interacts with many medicines as an inhibitor of the CYP3A4 enzyme and P-glycoprotein. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
