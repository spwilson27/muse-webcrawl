Giorgio Parisi

Giorgio Parisi (born 4 August 1948) is an Italian theoretical physicist whose research has centred on quantum field theory, statistical mechanics and complex systems. He shared the 2021 Nobel Prize in Physics "for the discovery of the interplay of disorder and fluctuations in physical systems from atomic to planetary scales" — work principally on the theory of spin glasses.

Career and research

A graduate of Sapienza University of Rome, where he spent most of his career as professor, Parisi made contributions across an unusually wide range of physics. With Guido Altarelli he derived the evolution equations for parton distribution functions in quantum chromodynamics (the Altarelli–Parisi, or DGLAP, equations, 1977). His most celebrated work is the replica-symmetry-breaking solution of the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model of spin glasses (1979 onwards), which introduced a deep mathematical structure — ultrametricity and a hierarchical organisation of states — later applied to neural networks, optimisation, structural glasses and other disordered systems, and underpinned rigorously by later mathematicians.

With collaborators he also developed the Kardar–Parisi–Zhang (KPZ) equation for growing interfaces (1986), studied turbulence and stochastic quantisation, and worked on collective animal behaviour, including large-scale field studies of starling flocks. Among his many honours are the Boltzmann Medal, the Dirac Medal, the Max Planck Medal and the Wolf Prize in Physics (2021).
