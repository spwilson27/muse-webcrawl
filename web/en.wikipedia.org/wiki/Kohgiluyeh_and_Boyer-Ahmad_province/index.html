Kohgiluyeh and Boyer-Ahmad province (Persian: استان کهگیلویه و بویراحمد, Ostân-e Kohgīlūye va Būyer-Aḥmad) is one of the 31 provinces of Iran, located in the south-west of the country in the Zagros Mountains. Its capital is the city of Yasuj. The province is largely mountainous, with oak forests, high pastures and rivers, and its inhabitants are predominantly Lurs who speak the Luri language.

Geography

The province covers about 15,504 square kilometres in the central Zagros. Mount Dena, in the Dena range in the north-east of the province, rises to about 4,409 metres and is the highest summit of the Zagros; the range is protected as the Dena Biosphere Reserve, noted for its biodiversity and populations of wild animals including the Persian fallow deer. Rivers such as the Beshar and the Marun drain the province, and the climate ranges from cold and snowy in the northern highlands around Yasuj and Sisakht to warm in the southern lowlands near Gachsaran (Dogonbadan). The province borders Khuzestan to the west, Fars to the south and east, Isfahan to the north-east and Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari to the north.

History

The region was part of the territory of Elam and later of the Persian empires; it became known as the homeland of Lur tribes whose chiefs enjoyed considerable autonomy under successive dynasties. Kohgiluyeh was historically a district attached variously to Fars and Khuzestan. The modern province was created in 1974 by joining the Kohgiluyeh region with the Boyer-Ahmad tribal territory, and Yasuj became its capital. The Boyer-Ahmad Lurs were among the tribal groups forcibly settled under Reza Shah in the 1930s, and the region remained largely pastoral and nomadic well into the twentieth century.

People and culture

The population was about 713,000 at the 2016 census. Most residents are Lurs speaking Southern Luri dialects, and Shia Islam predominates. Seasonal migration between winter quarters in the lowlands and summer pastures in the high mountains (the kooch) has historically structured Lur pastoral life, though settlement in towns such as Yasuj, Dehdasht, Gachsaran, Sisakht and Likak has grown rapidly since the 1970s.

Economy

Gachsaran is one of Iran's oldest oil-producing centres, and hydrocarbons remain important to the provincial economy, along with agriculture (wheat, apples — Sisakht is famous for its orchards), animal husbandry and carpet and kilim weaving. The province is among the less industrialised in Iran, and its mountains and forests attract domestic tourists for summer excursions and skiing on Dena's slopes.
