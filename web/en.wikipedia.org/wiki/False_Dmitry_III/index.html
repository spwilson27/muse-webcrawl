False Dmitry III (Russian: Лжедмитрий III), also known as the "Pskov thief", was a pretender to the Russian throne during the Time of Troubles. He claimed to be Tsarevich Dmitry Ivanovich, the youngest son of Ivan the Terrible, who had died at Uglich in 1591, and further claimed to be the same person as the earlier pretender False Dmitry II, who had been killed in 1610.

Career

Little is known of his background; he was reportedly a deacon or clergyman named Sidorka. He appeared in Ivangorod in March 1611, was driven off, reappeared, and was proclaimed "tsar" by Cossacks at Pskov in 1611 — hence his nickname, the Pskov thief. Pskov and several other towns acknowledged him, and Swedish forces in the region dealt with him during the Ingrian War.

In May 1612 his position collapsed; he fled Pskov, was captured, conveyed to Moscow in a cage, and executed in July 1612. He was the last of the major Dmitry pretenders of the Troubles, following False Dmitry I, who briefly reigned in 1605–1606, and False Dmitry II, the "thief of Tushino".
