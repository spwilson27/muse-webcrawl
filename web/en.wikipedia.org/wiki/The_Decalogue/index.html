The Decalogue

The Decalogue (Polish: Dekalog) is a 1989 Polish television drama series of ten one-hour films directed by Krzysztof Kieślowski, co-written with Krzysztof Piesiewicz and scored by Zbigniew Preisner. Each film is loosely inspired by one of the Ten Commandments and is set in and around a single Warsaw housing estate.

The films

The episodes treat the commandments not as literal subjects but as starting points for self-contained moral dramas: a father's faith in computer rationality is shattered by tragedy (I); a woman must choose whose life to preserve (II); adultery, murder, the death penalty, incestuous obsession, greed and bearing false witness figure among the themes. A silent figure played by Artur Barciś appears in nearly every episode as a mysterious observer — variously read as an angel or a witness. Two episodes were expanded into feature films: A Short Film About Killing (Decalogue V) and A Short Film About Love (Decalogue VI).

Reception

Dekalog is widely ranked among the greatest works of world cinema; critics including Roger Ebert praised its moral seriousness and formal economy, and Stanley Kubrick called the screenplay the only masterpiece he could name. The series helped establish Kieślowski's international reputation before his Three Colours trilogy.
