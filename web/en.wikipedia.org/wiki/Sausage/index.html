Sausage

A sausage is a meat product made of ground or minced meat — typically pork, beef or poultry — mixed with salt, spices and other flavourings, and usually stuffed into a casing, traditionally made from animal intestine and now often from collagen or cellulose. Sausage-making is one of the oldest food preservation techniques, allowing meat to be cured, smoked, fermented or dried for storage.

Sausages range from fresh bratwurst and breakfast links to dry-cured salami and chorizo.

Types

Sausages are classified by how they are prepared. Fresh sausages (such as British bangers, Italian sausage and bratwurst) must be cooked before eating. Cooked sausages include frankfurters and mortadella. Smoked and cured types include kielbasa and andouille, while fermented, air-dried sausages such as salami, chorizo and saucisson can be eaten uncooked because their acidity and low moisture inhibit spoilage. Blood sausages, liver sausages and vegetarian substitutes made from plant proteins, grains or mycoprotein form further large families. National varieties number in the thousands: Germany alone is often said to have more than a thousand types.

History

Sausage-like preparations are recorded in ancient Mesopotamia and in Homer's Odyssey, and the Romans developed numerous varieties. The word derives via Old French saussiche from Latin salsus, "salted". Sausages became staples of urban food culture in industrial Europe and America — as street food, in the hot dog, and in dishes such as cassoulet and sauerkraut-topped bratwurst.
