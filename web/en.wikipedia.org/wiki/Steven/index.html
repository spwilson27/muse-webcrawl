Steven

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Steven is a masculine given name in English. It is a variant spelling of Stephen, though the two are generally pronounced identically (STEE-vən). Both derive from the Greek name Stephanos (Στέφανος), meaning "crown," "wreath," or "that which surrounds" — the garland awarded to victors in ancient games.

History

The name entered Christian usage through Saint Stephen, the first Christian martyr (protomartyr), whose stoning is described in the Acts of the Apostles. It became widespread in medieval Europe in forms such as Latin Stephanus, French Étienne, German Stephan, and Spanish Esteban. In England it was borne by King Stephen (1135–1154). The spelling "Steven" grew common alongside "Stephen," particularly in North America; during the mid-20th century both spellings ranked among the most popular boys' names in the United States, with Steven at times the more frequent of the two. Familiar diminutives include Steve, Stevie, and Ste.

Notable people

People named Steven include filmmaker Steven Spielberg, physicist Steven Weinberg, Hong Kong martial-arts actor Steven Seagal, entrepreneur Steven Sinofsky, and media personality Steven Crowder. As a surname or in fiction, the name appears in Steven Universe, the animated series created by Rebecca Sugar, whose protagonist is Steven Universe. Variants are found in many languages: Steffen (Scandinavian/German), Stefan (German, Slavic), Stefano (Italian), Stepan (Russian, Ukrainian), and István (Hungarian).
