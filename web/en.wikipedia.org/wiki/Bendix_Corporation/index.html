Bendix Corporation

Founded1924, by Vincent Bendix
HeadquartersSouth Bend, Indiana (later Detroit area), United States
IndustryAutomotive, aviation, electronics, appliances
FateAcquired by Allied Corporation in 1983; brand survives in several businesses

The Bendix Corporation was a major American manufacturing and engineering company founded in 1924 by inventor Vincent Hugo Bendix, headquartered for much of its life in South Bend, Indiana. At various times it was a leading supplier of automotive brakes and starter drives, aircraft instruments and carburettors, radios and televisions, home appliances (the Bendix automatic washing machine), computers (the Bendix G-15 of 1956) and aerospace systems.

History

Vincent Bendix built his fortune on the Bendix drive, a starter mechanism licensed to automobile manufacturers, and on acquiring the brake company that became Bendix brakes. The Bendix Aviation Corporation, formed in 1929, sponsored the famous Bendix Trophy transcontinental air race, first awarded in 1931 and won by Jimmy Doolittle. During the Second World War Bendix supplied carburettors (the Bendix-Stromberg pressure carburettor), magnetos, landing gear and the secret Norden bombsight components, becoming one of the largest US war contractors. Postwar, the company diversified into computers, nuclear instrumentation (it operated the Kansas City Plant for the Atomic Energy Commission), hydraulic systems and the famous Bendix "washer" home laundry machines, which popularised the automatic washing machine and inspired the word "bendix" and the laundromat concept.

End and legacy

In 1982 Bendix, under chairman William Agee, made a celebrated hostile bid for Martin Marietta; the target counter-attacked in a "Pac-Man defence", and Bendix was ultimately acquired by Allied Corporation in 1983 in one of the era's most dramatic takeover battles. Allied (later AlliedSignal, then Honeywell) retained the Bendix name in aviation and braking businesses; "Bendix" trademarks survive today in commercial-vehicle air brakes (Bendix Commercial Vehicle Systems, part of Knorr-Bremse) and Honeywell avionics (BendixKing).
