Taishanese

Native to: Taishan, Guangdong, China; overseas Chinese communities

Language family: Sino-Tibetan → Sinitic → Yue → Siyi

Native speakers: approx. 750,000+ (plus diaspora)

Taishanese (Taishan dialect; Chinese: 台山話), also written Toishanese and known in Taishanese as Hoisan-wa, is a Yue Chinese dialect spoken in Taishan, in the Pearl River Delta region of Guangdong province, China. It belongs to the Siyi ("Four Counties") group of Yue dialects, together with the speech of Kaiping, Enping, and Xinhui.

History and diaspora

From the mid-19th century until the mid-20th century, Taishan was a major source of Chinese emigration to North America. As a result, Taishanese was the predominant variety of Chinese spoken in Chinatowns across the United States and Canada until the 1960s and 1970s, when it was gradually overtaken by standard Cantonese and, later, Mandarin as immigration patterns shifted.

Phonology

Taishanese differs notably from standard (Guangzhou) Cantonese in its phonology. Among its characteristic features are the merger or shift of certain initials — for example, the Cantonese /s/ corresponds to a voiceless lateral fricative-like sound (often transcribed /ɬ/) in many Taishanese words — and distinctive tone contours. Taishanese has its own set of tones, commonly analysed as having several level and contour tones on checked and unchecked syllables. Mutual intelligibility with Guangzhou Cantonese is partial.

Vocabulary

Taishanese preserves colloquial vocabulary and grammatical particles that differ from standard Cantonese, and its diaspora varieties absorbed borrowings from English. It is written with Chinese characters like other Yue varieties, though romanizations specific to Taishanese exist for linguistic documentation.
