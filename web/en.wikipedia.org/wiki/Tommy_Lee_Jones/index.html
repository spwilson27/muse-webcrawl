Tommy Lee Jones (born September 15, 1946) is an American actor. He has received various accolades including an Academy Award, a Golden Globe Award, a Primetime Emmy Award and two Screen Actors Guild Awards. Known for his gruff, authoritative screen persona, he rose to prominence with roles in Coal Miner's Daughter (1980), JFK (1991), and his Oscar-winning turn as U.S. Marshal Samuel Gerard in The Fugitive (1993).

Early life and education

Jones was born in San Saba, Texas, the son of a police officer and a school teacher. He attended Harvard University on a needs-based scholarship, rooming with Al Gore and graduating cum laude with a degree in English in 1969. He played guard on Harvard's undefeated 1968 football team, participating in the famous 29–29 tie with Yale. After graduation he moved to New York to pursue acting, making his Broadway debut in 1969 and his film debut in Love Story (1970).

Career

Television work in the 1970s and 1980s included an Emmy-winning portrayal of murderer Gary Gilmore in The Executioner's Song (1982) and Captain Woodrow Call in the acclaimed miniseries Lonesome Dove (1989). In film he earned Oscar nominations for JFK and later for In the Valley of Elah (2007) and Lincoln (2012, as Thaddeus Stevens). Popular successes include the Men in Black franchise (as Agent K), Batman Forever (Two-Face), Under Siege, Natural Born Killers, and the Coen brothers' No Country for Old Men (2007). He directed and starred in the western The Three Burials of Melquiades Estrada (2005), winning Best Actor at Cannes, and The Homesman (2014).

Personal life

Jones lives in Texas, where he ranches near San Antonio and raises cattle and polo ponies; he is fluent in Spanish and has campaigned for land conservation. He is also notable in Japan for a long-running series of advertisements for Suntory Boss coffee.
