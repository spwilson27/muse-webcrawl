South Pars / North Dome Gas-Condensate field

The South Pars / North Dome field is the world's largest natural gas field. It lies offshore in the Persian Gulf, shared between Iran — where the Iranian sector is called South Pars — and Qatar, where the Qatari sector is called North Dome (North Field). The field covers about 9,700 square kilometres, roughly 3,700 in Iranian waters and 6,000 in Qatari waters.

Reserves

The field is estimated to hold around 1,800 trillion cubic feet of gas in place, of which some 1,260 trillion cubic feet are recoverable, together with tens of billions of barrels of gas condensate. It accounts for a large share of both countries' proved gas reserves and of world reserves overall.

Development

Qatar developed the North Field from the late 1980s, building at Ras Laffan the world's largest liquefied natural gas export industry. Iran's South Pars, centred on Assaluyeh, has been developed in some two dozen phases of offshore platforms, pipelines and onshore processing, supplying most of Iran's domestic gas and petrochemical feedstock. International sanctions repeatedly complicated Iranian phases and foreign participation.

Expansion

Qatar's North Field East and North Field South expansions, announced in the 2020s, are set to raise Qatari LNG capacity from 77 million tonnes per year toward 142 million tonnes per year, reinforcing the field's central place in global gas markets.
