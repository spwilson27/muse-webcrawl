Zone melting

Zone melting (or zone refining, floating-zone process) is a group of similar methods of purifying crystals, in which a narrow region of a crystal is melted, and this molten zone is moved slowly along the crystal. The melt at the solid–liquid interface freezes progressively, and because impurities are generally more soluble in the melt than in the solid, they are swept along with the zone and concentrated at one end of the ingot, which can then be cut off and discarded.

History and principle

The method was developed in the early 1950s by William G. Pfann at Bell Telephone Laboratories as a means of producing ultra-pure germanium and silicon for transistors. Its effectiveness is described by the segregation (distribution) coefficient k, the ratio of an impurity's concentration in the freezing solid to that in the melt. For most impurities k is less than 1, so the solid that refreezes is purer than the liquid ahead of it. Repeated passes of the molten zone — sometimes dozens or hundreds — can reduce impurity levels to parts per billion or below.

Variants

  • Zone refining: the ingot rests in a boat or crucible while heaters traverse it.
  • Float-zone process: used especially for silicon, a vertical rod is held without a crucible; a molten zone is sustained by surface tension between the growing crystal and a feed rod, typically melted by a radio-frequency coil or focused lamps. Avoiding contact with a crucible yields extremely pure, low-oxygen silicon for high-power and detector applications.
  • Zone leveling: the same transport is used in reverse, to distribute a dopant uniformly along an ingot.
  • Floating zone in microgravity and optical floating-zone furnaces are used to grow large single crystals of oxides and intermetallic compounds for research.

Applications

Zone refining underpins the semiconductor industry, producing the ultra-pure silicon and germanium from which integrated circuits are made. It is also used to purify metals and organic compounds and to grow single crystals of materials that cannot be melted in crucibles.
