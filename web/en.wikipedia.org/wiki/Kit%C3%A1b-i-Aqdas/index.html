Kitáb-i-Aqdas

The Kitáb-i-Aqdas (Arabic: الكتاب الأقدس, "The Most Holy Book") is the central religious text of the Baháʼí Faith, written by Baháʼu'lláh, the founder of the religion, in 1873 while he was still imprisoned in the city of Acre, Ottoman Palestine. It is commonly referred to as "the Aqdas".

Content

The book sets out the laws and ordinances of the Baháʼí Faith, including obligatory prayer, the Nineteen Day Fast, inheritance laws, the institution of the Mashriqu'l-Adhkár (Baháʼí houses of worship), the prohibition of alcohol and other intoxicants, and laws concerning marriage, burial and personal conduct. It also establishes the Covenant of Baháʼu'lláh, appointing ʻAbdu'l-Bahá as his successor and interpreter, and foreshadows the Universal House of Justice.

Structure and style

Written in Arabic in a rhymed, elevated prose style reminiscent of the Quran, the Aqdas consists of 467 numbered paragraphs in the authorized English translation published in 1992, which was accompanied by questions and answers, notes, and a synopsis and codification of its laws first published in 1973.
