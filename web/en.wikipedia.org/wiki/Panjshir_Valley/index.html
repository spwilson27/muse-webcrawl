The Panjshir Valley (Dari: دره پنجشير, "valley of the five lions") is a valley in north-central Afghanistan, about 150 kilometres (93 mi) north of Kabul, carved by the Panjshir River through the Hindu Kush mountains. It forms the core of Panjshir Province, created in 2004, and is home to well over a hundred thousand people, predominantly Persian-speaking Tajiks.

Geography

The valley runs southwest from the Anjuman Pass toward the plains of Parwan. Its narrow floor, walled by steep mountains rising above 4,000 metres, makes it naturally defensible — a fact that has shaped its military fame. The Panjshir River supports irrigated orchards of mulberry, walnut, and apricot; the region is also known for emerald mining, and its emeralds were historically a source of revenue for local commanders.

History

Panjshir became legendary during the Soviet–Afghan War (1979–1989), when mujahideen commander Ahmad Shah Massoud — the "Lion of Panjshir" — repelled repeated Soviet offensives into the valley. It remained a stronghold of the Northern Alliance against the Taliban in the 1990s, never fully falling to them; Massoud was assassinated by al-Qaeda operatives on 9 September 2001, two days before the September 11 attacks, and is buried in the valley.

After the Taliban returned to power in August 2021, Panjshir was the last major centre of armed opposition, under the National Resistance Front associated with Ahmad Massoud, the commander's son, and Amrullah Saleh. Taliban forces reported taking the provincial capital Bazarak in September 2021 after fighting, though resistance activity was reported thereafter. The valley remains one of Afghanistan's most symbolically charged landscapes of resistance.
