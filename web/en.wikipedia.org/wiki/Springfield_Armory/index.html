Springfield Armory

The Springfield Armory, located in the city of Springfield, Massachusetts, was the primary center for the manufacture of United States military firearms from 1777 until its closing in 1968. It was the first federal armory and one of the first factories in the United States dedicated to the manufacture of weapons.

Famous first as the United States' primary arsenal during the American Revolutionary War, and then as the scene of a confrontation during Shays' Rebellion, the Springfield Armory in the 19th and 20th centuries became the site for numerous technological innovations of global importance, including interchangeable parts, the assembly line style of mass production, and modern business practices, such as hourly wages.

History

The site was selected in 1777 by George Washington's chief of artillery, Henry Knox, because of its location at the confluence of the Connecticut River and several major roads, and its distance from the coast, which protected it from naval attack. During the Revolutionary War, the armory stored muskets, cannons, and other weapons, and produced paper cartridges.

In 1794, President George Washington authorized the establishment of a national armory at Springfield, and production of the Model 1795 musket began the following year. The armory went on to produce many of the most famous American military small arms, including the Springfield Model 1861 rifled musket, the trapdoor Springfield, the Krag–Jørgensen rifle (under license), the M1903 Springfield rifle, the M1 Garand, and the M14 rifle.

Closure and historic site

The armory was controversially closed by Secretary of Defense Robert McNamara in 1968, ending nearly two centuries of continuous arms production. The site is now preserved as the Springfield Armory National Historic Site, administered by the National Park Service, and houses one of the world's largest collections of historic American firearms. Much of the grounds are occupied by Springfield Technical Community College.
