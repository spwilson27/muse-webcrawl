Tai languages

The Tai languages are a branch of the Kra–Dai language family, spoken by Tai peoples across mainland Southeast Asia and southern China. The best-known members are Thai (Siamese) of Thailand, Lao of Laos, Shan of Myanmar, and Zhuang of Guangxi, China — Zhuang being the Tai language with the most native speakers, though Thai has the greatest total number of speakers.

Classification

Scholars commonly divide Tai into three branches — Northern, Central, and Southwestern — based on shared sound changes, following the comparative work of Li Fang-Kuei. Southwestern Tai includes Thai and Lao, which are closely related and partially mutually intelligible. All Tai languages are tonal, analytic languages with subject–verb–object order and extensive use of classifiers.

History

The Tai peoples originated in what is now southern China and northern Vietnam and expanded southwestward over the past two millennia, establishing kingdoms such as Sukhothai and Lan Xang. Their languages absorbed vocabulary from Pali, Sanskrit, Khmer, and Chinese through religion, administration, and trade.
