Music of Turkmenistan

The music of Turkmenistan is centred on the art of the bagşy (bakhshi) — itinerant singer-poets who perform epic tales (dessan) and lyric songs while accompanying themselves on the two-stringed dutar. In 2021, the dutar-making craftsmanship and traditional music performance art of Turkmenistan were inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

A bagşy performing with the dutar.

The bagşy tradition

The bagşy is a central figure of Turkmen culture: part musician, part storyteller and healer, traditionally trained through a master-apprentice lineage and a ritual of initiation. Repertoire includes epic narratives from the Gorogly cycle — tales of the hero Görogly — and songs based on the poetry of the 18th-century national poet Magtymguly Pyragy, whose verses are sung throughout the country. A gyjak (spike fiddle) player often accompanies the dutar, heightening the drama of climactic passages.

Instruments

The dutar, carved from a single piece of mulberry wood with two silk or steel strings, is the national instrument. Other traditional instruments include the tüydük (end-blown flute), the gargy tüydük (reed instrument), the gyjak, and frame drums. Women traditionally perform distinctive throat-singing-like sounds (damak owazy) as a wedding entertainment.

Soviet and modern eras

In the Soviet period, folk ensembles were formalised into state troupes, and composers such as Nury Halmammedov incorporated Turkmen melodies into orchestral and film music; his score for the film The Decisive Step is a classic. After independence in 1991, traditional music was promoted as a national symbol, while popular music developed under tight state oversight; long-serving president Saparmurat Niyazov famously banned lip-syncing and recorded music at public events in 2005.
