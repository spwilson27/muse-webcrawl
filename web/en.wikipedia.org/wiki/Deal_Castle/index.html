Deal Castle

LocationDeal, Kent, England
Built1539–1540
Commissioned byHenry VIII
Managed byEnglish Heritage

Deal Castle is an artillery fort in Deal, Kent, England, built by order of Henry VIII between 1539 and 1540 as part of the King's Device programme to protect against invasion from France and the Holy Roman Empire. Together with Walmer and Sandown castles it guarded the Downs anchorage off the Kent coast.

Design

The castle has a distinctive plan resembling a Tudor rose, with a central circular keep surrounded by six semicircular bastions and an outer ring of six further bastions in a curtain wall, providing tiers of gun positions and over 140 openings for firearms. It was the largest of the Device forts on this stretch of coast.

History

Deal Castle saw action during the English Civil War, when it was held for Parliament, besieged by Royalist forces in 1648, and recaptured after hard fighting. It later served as the residence of the Captain of the Cinque Ports' deputy and, although its defences were updated during the Napoleonic Wars, it was damaged by bombing in the Second World War. It is now open to the public under English Heritage.
