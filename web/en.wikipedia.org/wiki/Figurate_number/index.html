Figurate number

The term figurate number is used by different writers for members of different sets of numbers, generalizing from triangular numbers to different shapes (polygonal numbers) and different dimensions (polyhedral numbers). In modern usage a figurate number is a number that can be represented by a regular geometrical arrangement of equally spaced points — pebbles in the Greek tradition — forming a regular polygon or polyhedron.

History

The study of figurate numbers is said to originate with Pythagoras, who associated numbers with geometrical figures. Hypsicles of Alexandria (c. 190–120 BC) gave the earliest surviving definition of polygonal numbers. Diophantus wrote a treatise On Polygonal Numbers, and the subject was later developed by Fermat, who claimed that every positive integer is a sum of at most n n-gonal numbers — a result proved for triangular numbers by Gauss and in general by Cauchy.

Polygonal numbers

The n-th s-gonal number is given by the formula P(s, n) = ((s−2)n2 − (s−4)n)/2. For s = 3 this gives the triangular numbers 1, 3, 6, 10, 15…; for s = 4 the square numbers; for s = 5 the pentagonal numbers 1, 5, 12, 22, 35…

Three-dimensional figurate numbers

Stacking polygonal arrangements gives polyhedral numbers: tetrahedral numbers (1, 4, 10, 20, 35…) count spheres in a triangular pyramid, while cubic numbers are ordinary cubes. Centered figurate numbers place a point in the middle surrounded by successive layers.
