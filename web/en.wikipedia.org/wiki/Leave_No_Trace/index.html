Leave No Trace

Leave No Trace is a set of outdoor ethics and the nonprofit organization that promotes them, originating in the United States. The Leave No Trace Center for Outdoor Ethics, based in Boulder, Colorado, teaches people how to enjoy the outdoors responsibly while minimizing impacts on nature, wildlife, and other visitors.

The seven principles

The framework consists of seven principles: (1) plan ahead and prepare; (2) travel and camp on durable surfaces; (3) dispose of waste properly; (4) leave what you find; (5) minimize campfire impacts; (6) respect wildlife; and (7) be considerate of other visitors. The principles are adapted for different ecosystems and activities from frontcountry parks to wilderness areas.

History

The concept developed through US Forest Service and National Park Service wilderness education programs in the 1960s–1980s, responding to rising backcountry use. The Leave No Trace program was formalized in the early 1990s with the founding of the nonprofit center in 1994, and has since spread internationally, with partner organizations in many countries.
