Dannebrog

The Dannebrog (Danish pronunciation: [ˈtænəˌpʁoˀ]) is the national flag of Denmark. It consists of a white Nordic cross on a red field, with the vertical part of the cross shifted to the hoist side. It is among the oldest national flags in continuous use in the world.

Legend

According to tradition, the flag fell from the sky during the Battle of Lyndanisse in present-day Tallinn, Estonia, on 15 June 1219, where King Valdemar II of Denmark was fighting in the crusade against the Estonians. As the Danes were losing, the flag descended from heaven and, taken up by the king, turned the battle in the Danes' favour. The name Dannebrog is generally interpreted as "the flag of the Danes" or "red flag".

History

The flag is documented in armorial sources from the 14th century, and a banner captured in 1500 was long preserved in Schleswig. The Dannebrog was adopted as the national flag for private citizens in the 19th century; its proportions and use are regulated by law and custom, and Danes celebrate Flag Day each year on 15 June, the anniversary of the legendary battle.

Design and influence

The Nordic cross design of the Dannebrog became the model for the flags of the other Nordic countries — Sweden, Norway, Finland, Iceland and their associated territories — which all bear variants of the off-centre cross.
