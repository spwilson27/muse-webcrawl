The 1900 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the II Olympiad and commonly known as Paris 1900, were an international multi-sport event held in Paris, France, from 14 May to 28 October 1900, as part of the 1900 World's Fair. No opening or closing ceremonies were held. The Games are notable for being the first in which women competed, and for a chaotic organisation that left some athletes unaware they had participated in the Olympics at all.

Organisation

Baron Pierre de Coubertin had intended the Games to be integral to the Exposition Universelle, but the exposition's organisers absorbed the sporting programme and rebranded events as "International Championships" and world's fair contests. Events sprawled across five months and venues around Paris, including the Bois de Boulogne for athletics in the Croix-Catelan stadium. Victors often received works of art or useful objects rather than medals, and the IOC later retroactively assigned medal standings.

Highlights

Women competed for the first time: Charlotte Cooper of Great Britain won the women's singles tennis title and became the first female Olympic champion. American Alvin Kraenzlein won four individual athletics gold medals, a feat unmatched in a single Games. Sports appearing only in 1900 included cricket, croquet, Basque pelota, and tug of war's debut; live pigeon shooting was held as an unofficial event. France, the host, fielded the most athletes and won the most medals overall. Marathon runners complained the course had been shortened and marked confusingly; American Margaret Abbott won the women's golf tournament without ever learning she was an Olympic champion.

Legacy

The disorganisation of Paris 1900 prompted reforms that gave the IOC greater control over subsequent Games. Despite their flaws, the Games expanded the Olympic programme to 19 sports and established the precedent of women's participation.
