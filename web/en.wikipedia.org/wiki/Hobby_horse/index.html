Hobby horse

A hobby horse is a toy consisting of a model horse's head on a stick, which a child "rides" by straddling the stick. The term also refers to the costumed horse figures used in folk customs and morris dancing, to a favourite personal preoccupation (in the idiom "to ride one's hobby horse"), and to competitive hobby horsing, in which participants simulate equestrian events.

Toy

Stick horses have been children's toys since antiquity. Traditional versions have a carved or stuffed head — often with yarn mane, button eyes, and reins — mounted on a wooden pole. In the 2010s, realistic hobby horses handmade from fabric became a popular craft, particularly in Finland.

Folk customs

In British folk tradition, hobby horses appear in May Day and mumming customs, most famously the 'Obby 'Oss festivals of Padstow and Minehead in Cornwall and Somerset. These consist of a performer inside a large framed costume with a horse's head, who dances through the town accompanied by musicians and crowds. Morris sides also perform with hobby horse characters.

Competitive hobby horsing

Hobby horsing as a sport, in which riders perform dressage and show jumping on hobby horses, gained wide attention in Finland in the 2010s, with national championships attracting hundreds of competitors. The activity has since spread internationally.

Idiom

Laurence Sterne's novel Tristram Shandy popularized the "hobby horse" as a metaphor for a person's ruling passion or pet obsession — the meaning that survives in the modern phrase.
