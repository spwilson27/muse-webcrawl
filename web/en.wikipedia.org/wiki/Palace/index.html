Palace

A palace is a grand residence, especially the home of a head of state, monarch, or high-ranking dignitary such as a bishop or archbishop. The word derives from the Palatine Hill in Rome, where Augustus Caesar established his imperial residence; the Latin palatium eventually came to mean the residence of the emperor and then any great royal house.

History

Palaces have existed since antiquity — in Mesopotamia, Egypt, Minoan Crete (Knossos), Persia (Persepolis), and China, where the Forbidden City in Beijing became the largest palace complex in the world. In Europe, medieval castles gradually gave way to comfortable palaces such as Versailles, whose splendour under Louis XIV set a model copied across the continent, from Schönbrunn to Peterhof.

Uses

Beyond royal residences, the term has been applied to grand public and commercial buildings: exhibition halls ("Crystal Palace"), cinemas ("picture palaces"), hotels, and municipal buildings. Many former palaces now serve as museums or seats of government and parliaments.

Largest palaces

The Palace of the Parliament in Bucharest is the heaviest building in the world, while the Forbidden City is the largest palace complex by enclosed area. Istanbul's Topkapı Palace and Bangkok's Grand Palace are among the most visited historic palaces.
