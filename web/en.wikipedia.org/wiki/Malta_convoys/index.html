Malta convoys

The Malta convoys were a series of Allied supply convoys of the Second World War that sustained the island of Malta, a British colony and naval base besieged by Axis forces from June 1940 to November 1942. Malta's position in the central Mediterranean, astride the supply routes to Axis armies in North Africa, made it both invaluable and desperately exposed to air attack from Sicily.

The siege

Convoys ran from Gibraltar in the west and Alexandria in the east, fighting through submarine, surface, and heavy air attack along the routes nicknamed the "bomb alley." Early operations such as the Club Runs flew off fighter aircraft — Hurricanes and later Spitfires — from carriers to reinforce Malta's air defence. The crisis deepened in early 1942 as the Luftwaffe and Regia Aeronautica bombed the island intensively; in April 1942 King George VI awarded Malta the George Cross "to bear witness to a heroism and devotion that will long be famous in history."

Operation Pedestal

The most famous convoy, Operation Pedestal in August 1942, sent fourteen merchant ships with a massive escort including three aircraft carriers. Only five merchantmen arrived, among them the crippled American tanker Ohio, lashed between two destroyers and towed into Grand Harbour with her cargo of aviation fuel — delivering just enough supplies to keep Malta fighting. Other major operations included Harpoon and Vigorous (June 1942) and the November 1942 Operation Stoneage, whose success effectively ended the siege as Axis forces retreated after El Alamein and the Torch landings.

Significance

Aircraft and submarines based at Malta — including the famous 10th Submarine Flotilla — sank a large share of Axis shipping to North Africa, materially contributing to the Allied victory in the desert war. The cost was heavy: numerous merchant ships, warships, and thousands of sailors and airmen were lost on the Malta runs.
