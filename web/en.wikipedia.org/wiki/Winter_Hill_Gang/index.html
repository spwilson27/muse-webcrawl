The Winter Hill Gang was an Irish-American organized crime syndicate based in the Winter Hill neighborhood of Somerville, Massachusetts. Emerging in the 1950s under James "Buddy" McLean, it became one of the most powerful criminal organizations in New England, controlling much of the Boston area's rackets in alliance — and rivalry — with the Italian-American Patriarca crime family.

History

After McLean was murdered in 1965 during the Irish Mob war with the Charlestown McLaughlin gang, leadership passed to Howie Winter. In the 1970s, James "Whitey" Bulger and Stephen Flemmi rose within the gang; Winter's imprisonment in 1979 left Bulger in control. It was later revealed that Bulger and Flemmi were long-time FBI informants, a scandal that exposed corrupt handler relationships and inspired books and films, including Black Mass and elements of The Departed.

Decline

Federal racketeering indictments in 1995 drove Bulger into hiding; he remained a fugitive for 16 years until his arrest in Santa Monica, California, in 2011. Convicted in 2013 of racketeering and involvement in 11 murders, Bulger was killed in prison in 2018.
