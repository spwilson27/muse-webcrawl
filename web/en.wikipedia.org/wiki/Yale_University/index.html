Yale University

Yale University is a private Ivy League research university in New Haven, Connecticut. Founded in 1701, it is the third-oldest institution of higher education in the United States and one of the nine Colonial Colleges chartered before the American Revolution.

Yale University
Motto: Lux et veritas (Light and truth)

Location: New Haven, Connecticut
Endowment: over $40 billion

History

Founded as the Collegiate School in Saybrook Colony, the school moved to New Haven in 1716 and was renamed Yale College in 1718 in honour of benefactor Elihu Yale. It expanded into a university with schools of medicine, divinity, law, and graduate studies in the 19th century.

Campus and colleges

Yale's central campus covers 260 acres with Collegiate Gothic architecture, including Harkness Tower and Sterling Memorial Library. Undergraduates belong to 14 residential colleges, modelled on Oxford and Cambridge, since the 1930s.

Academics and people

Yale comprises Yale College, the Graduate School of Arts and Sciences, and 12 professional schools including Yale Law School. Alumni and faculty include five US presidents, many heads of state, and dozens of Nobel laureates. Its library system holds over 15 million volumes.
