Technical support

Technical support, commonly known as tech support, is a user-facing set of services by which a technology company or third-party service vendor assists consumers and employees with technical problems concerning technical products such as computers, mobile phones or other electronic or mechanical goods. Technical support services are generally delivered via telephone, email, live support software, live chat or a website.

Tiers of support

Technical support is often subdivided into tiers, or levels, in order to better serve a business or customer base:

  • Level 0 (L0) – self-help, documentation and frequently asked questions that users consult themselves.
  • Level 1 (L1) – basic help desk support, handling straightforward issues using scripts and knowledge bases.
  • Level 2 (L2) – more in-depth troubleshooting by experienced technicians for problems L1 cannot solve.
  • Level 3 (L3) – expert-level support, often involving product engineers or developers.
  • Level 4 (L4) – support escalated to an outside vendor or the original manufacturer.

Remote support

With the spread of broadband internet access, remote desktop software became widely used, allowing support staff to access a user's computer directly to diagnose and repair problems. Remote support reduces cost and travel time but raises security considerations, which has led to industry standards and permission-based connection tools.

Outsourcing

Many organizations outsource technical support to call centres in lower-cost regions. Outsourced support is controversial because of perceived impacts on quality, language barriers and job losses in the customer's home country. Companies such as Dell and Microsoft have at times moved support operations back in-house in response to customer complaints.

Scams

Technical support scams involve fraudsters posing as support agents — often through cold calls or pop-up warnings claiming a virus infection — to trick victims into paying for unnecessary services or granting remote access. Microsoft and other companies warn users that legitimate firms do not make unsolicited support calls.
