In-game advertising

In-game advertising (IGA) is the placement of advertising within video games. Advertisements may be woven into the game world — as billboards in a virtual city, branded products used by characters, or sponsored stadiums in sports games — or delivered as interstitial ads, rewarded videos, and banners, especially in mobile games. The practice gives publishers an additional revenue stream and gives advertisers access to engaged audiences, particularly younger demographics that are hard to reach through traditional media.

Racing and sports games commonly feature realistic billboard advertising.

Types

Static in-game advertising is programmed into the game at release, like product placement in a film; early examples date to the 1980s, when games featured real brands for realism or under licence. Dynamic in-game advertising is inserted and updated over the internet, allowing advertisers to buy placements by time, region, and audience, much like web advertising. A related model is the advergame, a game created wholly to promote a product or brand. In free-to-play mobile games, rewarded video ads — which grant in-game currency or lives in exchange for watching — have become a dominant format.

Reception and regulation

Players generally accept advertising that fits the game's world, such as hoardings at a virtual football stadium, but react negatively to intrusive ads that interrupt gameplay — an issue that has drawn criticism in some full-price console and PC titles. Because many gamers are children, regulators in several countries scrutinise in-game advertising for disclosure and fairness, and rules on advertising to children, data collection, and gambling-like mechanics increasingly apply to games.
