Euronext Paris, formerly the Paris Bourse, is the French securities market, operated by Euronext and located in Paris. It is France's largest stock exchange and, by market capitalisation, among the largest in Europe, hosting the benchmark CAC 40 index of 40 leading French companies.

History

An official bourse in Paris was created by royal decree in 1724, following the chaos of John Law's Mississippi Bubble. Trading long took place in the Palais Brongniart, completed in 1826, where stockbrokers (agents de change) dealt by open outcry in the famous corbeille until the market's full computerisation in the late 1980s and 1990s under the CAC (Cotation Assistée en Continu) system. In September 2000 the Paris Bourse merged with the Amsterdam and Brussels exchanges to form Euronext, the first pan-European exchange; the merged group later combined with the NYSE (2007) before becoming independent again in 2014.

Operations

Euronext Paris lists equities, bonds, warrants, and exchange-traded products, and its derivatives arm offers futures and options, including the benchmark CAC 40 index futures. Trading is electronic via Euronext's Optiq platform, with clearing provided by LCH SA. Major listed companies have included LVMH, L'Oréal, TotalEnergies, Sanofi, and Airbus.
