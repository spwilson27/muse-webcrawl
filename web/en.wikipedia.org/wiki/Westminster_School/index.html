Westminster School is a public school (an independent boarding and day school) in Westminster, London, England, situated within the precincts of Westminster Abbey. It is one of the original nine schools named in the Public Schools Act 1868 and is renowned for its academic results, regularly sending a large proportion of leavers to Oxford and Cambridge.

History

The school traces its origins to a monastic school of the Benedictine abbey, documented from the 12th century. After the Dissolution of the Monasteries, Elizabeth I refounded the school in 1560, establishing forty King's (now King's) Scholars. Ben Jonson, Christopher Wren, John Locke and Jeremy Bentham were educated there. During the English Civil War the school stood next to the seat of Parliament; its pupils traditionally watch parliamentary proceedings and the school's scholars attend coronations in the Abbey.

Today

The school occupies buildings around Little Dean's Yard, including Church House and Ashburnham House. Girls were first admitted to the sixth form in 1973, and the school has expanded co-education downward in recent years. Its pupils, called Old Westminsters after leaving, include seven prime ministers.
