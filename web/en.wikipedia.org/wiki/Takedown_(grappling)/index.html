Takedown (grappling)

Takedown
FocusGrappling; bringing an opponent to the ground
Used inWrestling, judo, Brazilian jiu-jitsu, sambo, mixed martial arts

A takedown is a technique in grappling and martial arts that involves off-balancing an opponent and bringing them to the ground, typically with the attacker landing on top in a controlling position. Takedowns are fundamental to wrestling, judo, sambo, Brazilian jiu-jitsu, and mixed martial arts (MMA), where successfully taking the fight to the ground is often the prelude to dominant positions and submissions, and where takedown defense is a central skill.

Types

Leg attacks target the opponent's legs directly. The double-leg takedown drives through both legs while lifting and toppling the opponent; the single-leg takedown captures one leg and finishes by lifting, sweeping, or running the pipe. Trips and sweeps (such as the inside trip and judo's osoto gari) unbalance the opponent over a blocking leg. Throws, most elaborately developed in judo, use grips, leverage, and hip or shoulder rotation to project the opponent through the air; shoulder throws, hip throws, and sacrifice throws are major families. Body-lock and clinch takedowns arise from upper-body ties, and snap-downs pull a bent-over opponent directly to the mat.

Scoring and rules

In folkstyle and freestyle wrestling, a takedown earns points (three points in American folkstyle since 2023) and is the primary route to control and near-fall points. In judo, a clean throw onto the back scores ippon and ends the match. In Brazilian jiu-jitsu competitions, takedowns earn two points. In MMA, takedowns are not scored directly but contribute to judging criteria of control and effective grappling, and defending them — "sprawling," whizzers, and underhooks — is essential against wrestlers.

Defense

The universal first defense is the sprawl: throwing the legs back and driving the hips down onto the attacker's head and shoulders. Other defenses include crossfaces, front headlocks, sumi gaeshi-style counters, and grip fighting to prevent the attacker from establishing position in the first place.
