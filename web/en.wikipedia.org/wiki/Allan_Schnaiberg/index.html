Allan Schnaiberg (August 20, 1939 – June 6, 2009) was a Canadian-American sociologist at Northwestern University and a founder of environmental sociology. He is best known for the theory of the treadmill of production, set out in his book The Environment: From Surplus to Scarcity (1980).

Career

Born in Montreal, Schnaiberg studied at McGill University and took his PhD at the University of Michigan in 1968. He joined Northwestern University in 1969, chairing its sociology department and directing its programme in urban affairs, and taught there for four decades. His early work examined social stratification and organisations; from the 1970s he turned to the social causes of environmental degradation.

Treadmill of production

Schnaiberg argued that environmental harm is driven not primarily by consumer demand or population but by a political-economic system in which producers, labour, and the state are locked into pursuing continuous economic growth. Firms reinvest profits in ever more capital-intensive, resource-extracting production; workers depend on expansion for jobs; and governments depend on it for revenue and legitimacy — a self-reinforcing "treadmill." Environmental reforms, he held, tend to remain superficial as long as this dynamic dominates. The framework became one of the foundational theories of environmental sociology, developed further by his students and collaborators including Kenneth Gould and Adam Weinberg in Local Environmental Struggles (1996) and later works on the treadmill of consumption and destruction.

Honours

Schnaiberg chaired the American Sociological Association's section on environment and technology and received its distinguished contribution award. A graduate-student paper award in environmental sociology bears his name.
