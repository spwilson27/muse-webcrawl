Upfront (advertising)

In the television industry, an upfront is a gathering at the start of important advertising sales periods, held by television network executives and attended by major advertisers and the media. It is so named because of its main purpose: to allow marketers to buy television commercial airtime "up front", or several months before the television season begins.

United States

In the United States, the major broadcast networks' upfronts traditionally occur in New York City during the third week of May, the last full week of that month's sweeps period. The networks announce their fall primetime schedules, including tentative launch dates, and clip new series to sway advertising buys. The presentations are elaborate productions, often featuring musical numbers, comedy bits, and appearances by network stars.

The major networks traditionally spend significant sums presenting their fall lineups. The upfronts also feature the unveiling of program schedules for the upcoming season, which advertisers use to plan their spending across the year. Cable networks and streaming services have adopted similar events, and a "NewFronts" event focuses on digital video advertising.

History

The tradition dates back decades, growing as television became the dominant advertising medium. Over time the upfront market became a multibillion-dollar annual event, with a large share of prime-time advertising inventory sold during the upfront season. In recent years, the rise of streaming platforms and on-demand viewing has prompted debate about the future of the upfront model, though it remains a central institution of the American television business.
