The Spanish Steps (Italian: Scalinata di Trinità dei Monti) are a monumental stairway of 135 steps in Rome, Italy, climbing the steep slope between the Piazza di Spagna at the base and the church of Trinità dei Monti at the top, beside the Pincian Hill. They are among the most famous urban staircases in the world and a symbol of Rome.

History

The steps were financed by a bequest of the French diplomat Étienne Gueffier and built in 1723–1726 by Francesco de Sanctis and Alessandro Specchi, to a design reconciling French ambitions with the wishes of the papacy. Their Italian name refers to the church above, while the English name "Spanish Steps" derives from the Spanish Embassy to the Holy See, located in the piazza below since the seventeenth century. At the foot of the steps stands the Fontana della Barcaccia (1627–29), a fountain in the form of a sinking boat, attributed to Pietro Bernini and his son Gian Lorenzo Bernini. The obelisk before the church, the Obelisco Sallustiano, was erected in 1789.

Cultural significance

The steps have long been a gathering place for artists and travellers; the English poet John Keats died in 1821 in the house at the foot of the steps now known as the Keats–Shelley Memorial House. The staircase was famously featured in the 1953 film Roman Holiday. Since 2019, sitting on the steps has been prohibited under municipal rules intended to protect the monument, which underwent a major restoration funded by Bulgari in 2015–2016.
