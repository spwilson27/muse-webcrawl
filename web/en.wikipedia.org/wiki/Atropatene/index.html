Atropatene (Greek: Ἀτροπατηνή; Old Persian: Ātṛpātakāna) was an ancient Iranian kingdom established in the late 4th century BC in the region of modern-day Iranian Azerbaijan and Iranian Kurdistan. Ruled by the dynasty founded by the satrap Atropates, it preserved Iranian culture and Zoroastrian religion through the Hellenistic age, when much of the former Achaemenid Empire fell under Greek rule. The name "Azerbaijan" is generally derived from Atropatene.

History

Atropates was a Persian nobleman who served as satrap of Media under Darius III and then Alexander the Great. After Alexander's death in 323 BC, the satrapy of Media was divided: the smaller northern part, Media Atropatene, remained with Atropates and became effectively independent, while the south passed to other rulers. Atropates and his descendants intermarried with the Seleucid royal house but maintained their own kingdom, with its capital at Ganzak, a fortified city associated with an important Zoroastrian fire temple.

Atropatene remained a largely autonomous kingdom under Seleucid and then Parthian (Arsacid) overlordship, occasionally expanding at the expense of neighboring Armenia and Media. In the first century AD the kingdom passed to rulers connected with the Arsacid house. In AD 226 it was absorbed into the new Sasanian Empire of Ardashir I, becoming the province of Adurbadagan.

Religion and culture

Atropatene was a stronghold of Zoroastrianism; the sacred fire Ādur Gušnasp, one of the three great fires of ancient Iran, was later housed at Takht-e Soleyman in this region. The kingdom's endurance as an Iranian enclave within the Hellenistic world made it an important conduit of Achaemenid traditions into later Persian history.
