Gudbrandsdalslågen

Country: Norway

Length: about 204 km

Source: Lake Lesjaskogsvatnet

Mouth: Lake Mjøsa at Lillehammer

The Gudbrandsdalslågen is a river in Innlandet county, Norway, and one of the country's longest rivers. It flows from Lake Lesjaskogsvatnet through the Gudbrandsdal valley south-eastward to Lillehammer, where it empties into Lake Mjøsa, Norway's largest lake.

Course

Along its roughly 204 km course the river passes the municipalities of Lesja, Dovre, Sel, Nord-Fron, Sør-Fron, Ringebu, Øyer and Lillehammer. After Mjøsa its waters continue as the Vorma and Glomma to the Oslofjord. The river is famous for its stock of large trout (the Hunder trout strain) and for severe floods, notably in 1789 and 1995, which inundated farms and villages along the valley floor.
