In Cold Blood is a non-fiction novel by American author Truman Capote, first published in 1966. It tells the story of the 1959 murders of four members of the Clutter family in the small farming community of Holcomb, Kansas, and of the two killers, Richard Hickock and Perry Smith, who were captured, tried and executed in 1965.

Development

Capote spent six years researching and writing the book, assisted by his friend Harper Lee. He interviewed residents, investigators and the killers themselves, amassing thousands of pages of notes. Serialised in The New Yorker in 1965 before book publication, it became an international bestseller and is credited, along with Capote's claims for the form, with popularising the "nonfiction novel" and the modern true crime genre.

Reception and adaptations

The book was widely praised for its narrative power and detail, though questions were later raised about the accuracy of some scenes and dialogue. It has been adapted for film several times, including the 1967 film In Cold Blood and later works about Capote himself.
