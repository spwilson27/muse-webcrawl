Gardeners' World is a long-running British television gardening programme broadcast by the BBC, first aired in 1968. One of the BBC's longest-running series, it offers practical gardening advice, seasonal jobs, plant features and visits to notable gardens, and has been presented from the presenter's own garden in most eras.

Presenters and gardens

The programme was launched as Gardening Club in 1955 before becoming Gardeners' World in 1968 under Ken Burras. Percy Thrower presented from 1969 and made the show a national institution; later lead presenters included Geoff Hamilton, whose Barnsdale gardens in Rutland popularized organic and cottage gardening, Alan Titchmarsh at Barleywood, and Monty Don, who presented from Berryfields and then from his own garden, Longmeadow in Herefordshire, from 2011. The programme has aired on BBC Two, traditionally on Friday evenings.

Format and influence

Regular segments include "jobs for the weekend", viewer contributions and plant trials. A companion magazine, BBC Gardeners' World Magazine, launched in 1991, and the Gardeners' World Live show is held annually at the NEC in Birmingham.
