Ivano-Frankivsk

Ivano-Frankivsk is a city in western Ukraine, the administrative centre of Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast, located on the Bystrytsia River in the Carpathian foothills. Founded in 1662 as the Polish fortress town of Stanisławów, it was renamed in 1962 for the Ukrainian writer Ivan Franko.

History

Stanisławów was founded by the Polish magnate Andrzej Potocki and granted Magdeburg rights in 1662. It passed to Austria in the First Partition of Poland (1772), to Poland after the First World War, and to the Ukrainian SSR in 1939–45. The city suffered the destruction of its large Jewish community during the Holocaust.

Culture

Landmarks include the Armenian church, the former Jesuit church, and Rynok Square with its distinctive town hall, now a regional museum. The city is a centre of Ukrainian culture and the gateway to the Ukrainian Carpathians.
