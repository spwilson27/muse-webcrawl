Carcinoid syndrome

Carcinoid syndrome
SpecialtyEndocrinology, oncology
SymptomsFlushing, diarrhoea, wheezing, rapid heartbeat
CauseHormone-secreting neuroendocrine (carcinoid) tumours, usually metastatic to the liver
Key mediatorSerotonin

Carcinoid syndrome is a paraneoplastic syndrome comprising the constellation of symptoms mediated by the hormones secreted by neuroendocrine tumours (historically called carcinoid tumours), most importantly serotonin and its precursor 5-hydroxytryptophan. The syndrome typically occurs when vasoactive substances secreted by metastases in the liver escape hepatic inactivation and enter the systemic circulation.

Signs and symptoms

The classic features are episodic flushing of the face and upper body, watery diarrhoea, bronchospasm and wheezing, and palpitations or blood-pressure changes. Over time, serotonin-driven fibrosis can damage the right-sided heart valves (tricuspid and pulmonary), producing carcinoid heart disease, and can cause pellagra-like symptoms because tumour metabolism diverts dietary tryptophan away from niacin production. Attacks may be provoked by stress, alcohol, exercise or certain foods.

Cause and pathophysiology

Most cases arise from well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumours of the midgut (small intestine or appendix) that have metastasised to the liver. Tumours draining directly into the systemic circulation — such as bronchial or ovarian primaries — can cause the syndrome without liver metastases. Tumour cells secrete serotonin (metabolised to 5-HIAA), histamine, kallikrein and prostaglandins.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is usually confirmed by measuring urinary 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (5-HIAA), the main metabolite of serotonin, and the blood marker chromogranin A. Cross-sectional imaging, and somatostatin-receptor imaging such as gallium-68 DOTATATE PET/CT, localise the primary tumour and metastases.

Treatment

Somatostatin analogues (octreotide, lanreotide) are the mainstay for symptom control and slow tumour growth. Other options include the tryptophan hydroxylase inhibitor telotristat for refractory diarrhoea, surgical removal or debulking, liver-directed embolisation, and peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT) with lutetium-177 DOTATATE.
