Charadriiformes

Charadriiformes is a diverse order of small to medium-large birds. It includes about 390 species and has members in all parts of the world. Most charadriiform birds live near water and eat invertebrates or other small animals; however, some are pelagic (seabirds), some occupy deserts, and a few are found in thick forest. The order includes the gulls, terns, auks, skuas, plovers, sandpipers, snipes, jacanas, avocets, stilts, oystercatchers and pratincoles.

Suborders and families

The order is traditionally divided into three suborders. The Charadrii comprise the shorebirds or waders proper — plovers, oystercatchers, stilts, avocets, jacanas, sandpipers, snipes, curlews, coursers and pratincoles. The Lari include the gulls, terns, skimmers, skuas and auks. The Turnici comprise the buttonquails, small Old World birds once thought related to cranes or gamebirds. Molecular studies have generally supported these groupings while rearranging some families within them.

Evolution

The fossil record of charadriiforms reaches back to the Late Cretaceous, and molecular evidence suggests the group began diversifying before the end of the age of dinosaurs. The order's explosive radiation in the Paleogene produced the remarkable ecological variety seen today, from pelagic auks and skuas to desert coursers and the seedsnipes of the Andes.

Ecology

Many charadriiforms are long-distance migrants, breeding in the Arctic and wintering as far away as Patagonia, South Africa and Australasia. Their feeding methods are highly varied: probing bills for mudflat invertebrates, surface plunging by terns, pursuit diving by auks, and kleptoparasitism by skuas and frigatebird-like behaviour in some gulls.
