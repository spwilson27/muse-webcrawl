John Thomas Scopes (3 August 1900 – 21 October 1970) was an American teacher at the centre of the 1925 Scopes Trial in Dayton, Tennessee, one of the most famous court cases in American history.

The trial

The American Civil Liberties Union sought a test case against Tennessee's Butler Act, which prohibited teaching human evolution in state schools. Civic leaders in Dayton, hoping for publicity, recruited Scopes — a mathematics teacher and football coach who occasionally substituted in biology — to stand trial. Scopes was charged with teaching evolution from the state-approved textbook, which itself contained evolutionary material.

The trial pitted prosecutor William Jennings Bryan against defence lawyer Clarence Darrow and drew national attention. Scopes was convicted and fined $100, though the verdict was later overturned on a technicality. The spectacle was fictionalised in the play and film Inherit the Wind. Scopes maintained a low profile afterwards; he went on to work as a geologist in the oil industry, mostly in Venezuela and Louisiana, and published a memoir, Center of the Storm, in 1967.
