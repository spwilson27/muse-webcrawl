Herbert Saffir

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Herbert Seymour Saffir (March 29, 1917 – November 21, 2007) was an American civil engineer who co-created, with meteorologist Robert Simpson, the Saffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale, the standard classification for Atlantic and eastern Pacific hurricanes by sustained wind speed and potential damage.

Career

Born in Brooklyn, New York, Saffir earned a civil engineering degree from the Georgia Institute of Technology in 1940 and built a career in Florida, specialising in structural engineering and wind-resistant building design. Working in Coral Gables, he studied how hurricanes damaged buildings and helped develop South Florida's strict building codes after Hurricane Andrew in 1992.

The scale

In the early 1970s Saffir devised a five-category scale describing wind damage to structures; Simpson, then director of the National Hurricane Center, added storm surge and flooding effects. The Saffir–Simpson scale, from Category 1 (minimal) to Category 5 (catastrophic), became a fixture of hurricane reporting in the United States.

Death

Saffir died in Miami in 2007, aged 90.
