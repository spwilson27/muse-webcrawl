Type-II superconductor

A type-II superconductor is a superconductor characterised by two critical magnetic fields and the formation of magnetic vortices in an applied field. Unlike type-I superconductors, which expel magnetic flux completely up to a single critical field, type-II materials admit partial flux penetration between a lower critical field Hc1 and an upper critical field Hc2.

The mixed state

Between Hc1 and Hc2 the material is in the mixed (vortex) state: magnetic field passes through in quantised vortices, each carrying one flux quantum and surrounded by circulating supercurrents, as predicted by Alexei Abrikosov in 1957. Because Hc2 can be very large, type-II superconductors — including niobium–titanium, niobium–tin and the cuprate high-temperature superconductors — are used for high-field magnets in MRI scanners, particle accelerators and fusion reactors. Pinning of vortices to defects is essential to sustaining large lossless currents.
