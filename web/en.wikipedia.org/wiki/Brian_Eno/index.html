Brian Peter George Eno (born 15 May 1948) is an English musician, songwriter, record producer and visual artist. He is best known for coining and pioneering ambient music and for his production work with artists including David Bowie, Talking Heads, Devo, U2, Coldplay and James Blake. A self-described "non-musician", Eno has championed studio-based, process-oriented approaches to composition, including generative systems and the Oblique Strategies cards he created with Peter Schmidt.

Early career and Roxy Music

Eno studied at the Ipswich and Winchester schools of art, where he was influenced by minimalism, systems art and John Cage. In 1971 he joined the glam/art rock band Roxy Music as synthesizer and tape-effects player, bringing an eccentric visual presence; friction with frontman Bryan Ferry led him to leave in 1973 after two albums. His 1970s solo records — Here Come the Warm Jets (1974), Taking Tiger Mountain (By Strategy) (1974), Another Green World (1975) and Before and After Science (1977) — blended art rock with studio experimentation.

Ambient music and collaborations

After being injured in a car accident in 1975, Eno conceived music designed to reward but not demand attention, leading to Discreet Music (1975) and the explicitly titled Ambient 1: Music for Airports (1978), the first of his Ambient series. With Robert Fripp he developed tape-delay "Frippertronics" on (No Pussyfooting) (1973); with David Bowie he worked on the "Berlin Trilogy" (Low, "Heroes", Lodger); and with David Byrne he made the sample-based My Life in the Bush of Ghosts (1981). His production of Talking Heads' Remain in Light (1980) and U2's The Joshua Tree (1987) and Achtung Baby (1991, with Daniel Lanois) were commercially and critically landmark recordings.

Later work

Eno has continued to release solo and collaborative albums (including Another Day on Earth, Lux and, with Fred Again, Secret Life in 2023), create audiovisual installations, write on art and culture, and work as an activist on climate change. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Roxy Music in 2019.
