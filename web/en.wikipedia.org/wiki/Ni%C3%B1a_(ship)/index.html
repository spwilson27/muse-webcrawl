Niña (Spanish for "girl"), originally named Santa Clara, was one of the three ships of Christopher Columbus's first voyage in 1492 and, by several measures, his favourite. She was a caravel of about 50–60 tons, owned and captained by Juan Niño of Moguer; her nickname came from her owner's surname.

Career

On the outward voyage the Niña was re-rigged in the Canaries from lateen to square sails for the ocean crossing. After the Santa María was wrecked on Hispaniola, the Niña became Columbus's flagship for the return, surviving a violent storm that separated her from the Pinta. She sailed again on Columbus's second voyage (1493) and on a third expedition, and in 1496 made an exceptionally fast solo return crossing from the Caribbean to Cádiz.

Replicas

A sailing replica built for the 1992 quincentenary has since toured ports as a museum ship, the only one of the three replicas built using period techniques throughout.
