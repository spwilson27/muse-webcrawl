The American Legislative Exchange Council (ALEC) is an American nonprofit organisation of conservative state legislators and private-sector representatives that drafts and shares model legislation for adoption in state legislatures. Founded in 1973 in Chicago, it is one of the largest voluntary membership associations of state lawmakers in the United States.

Activities

ALEC's task forces — pairing legislators with corporate and foundation members — develop model bills on taxation, health care, education, energy, gun policy, criminal justice, and other issues. Hundreds of ALEC-modelled bills are introduced in state legislatures each year, and a fraction become law. The organisation holds annual meetings and policy summits and publishes policy reports and a state "economic competitiveness" ranking, Rich States, Poor States.

Criticism and controversy

Critics describe ALEC as a channel for corporate influence over legislation and have campaigned for companies to withdraw membership, which a number of major firms did after 2012 — when the organisation drew scrutiny over its support for "stand your ground" gun laws following the killing of Trayvon Martin — and again over its climate-change positions. ALEC disputes such characterisations and states that legislators control its agenda and votes.
