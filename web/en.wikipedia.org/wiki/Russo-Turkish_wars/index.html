The Russo-Turkish wars (or Russo-Ottoman wars) were a series of twelve wars fought between the Russian Empire (and its predecessor, the Tsardom of Russia) and the Ottoman Empire between the 16th and 20th centuries. They were among the longest-running series of conflicts in European history, driven by Russia's southward expansion, its desire for warm-water ports and access through the Turkish Straits, and the gradual decline of Ottoman power — the so-called "Eastern Question".

Causes

The conflicts arose from overlapping ambitions around the Black Sea. Russia sought secure southern borders against Crimean Tatar raids, control of the northern Black Sea coast, passage for its warships and trade through the Bosphorus and Dardanelles, and — increasingly in the 19th century — the role of protector of Orthodox Christians and Slavs within the Ottoman Empire. The Ottomans sought to preserve their European provinces and their dominance of the Black Sea, which they long treated as an "Ottoman lake".

Consequences

Over three and a half centuries the balance shifted decisively. Russia annexed Crimea in 1783, gained Bessarabia and Caucasian territories, and by 1878 had helped bring about the independence of Romania, Serbia, Montenegro, and an autonomous Bulgaria. Only in the Crimean War, when Britain and France intervened on the Ottoman side, was Russian expansion checked. The final clash, on the Caucasus front of the First World War, ended with the collapse of both empires in 1917–1918.
