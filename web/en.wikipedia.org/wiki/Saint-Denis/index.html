Saint-Denis is a commune in the northern suburbs of Paris, France, in the department of Seine-Saint-Denis. It is known above all for the Basilica of Saint-Denis, the burial place of nearly all French kings from Dagobert I to Louis XVIII, and for the Stade de France, the country's national stadium.

History

Legend holds that Saint Denis, a third-century bishop of Paris martyred at Montmartre, carried his severed head to the site of the present basilica and died there. A church rose over his tomb, and in the 12th century Abbot Suger rebuilt it in a revolutionary new style — the first Gothic building — making the abbey a symbol of the French monarchy. The royal necropolis was desecrated during the French Revolution, when royal tombs were emptied into mass graves; the restored basilica remains one of the monuments of medieval architecture.

Modern Saint-Denis

Industrialization turned Saint-Denis into a working-class "red" suburb, long governed by the Communist Party. The Stade de France, built for the 1998 FIFA World Cup — where France won its first title — anchors a redeveloped district near the Canal Saint-Denis. The town hosted events of the 2024 Paris Olympics, including the Olympic Village straddling Saint-Denis and Saint-Ouen. It remains one of France's most diverse and youngest communes, with significant immigration from North and West Africa and the overseas departments.
