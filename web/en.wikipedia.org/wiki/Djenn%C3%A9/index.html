Djenné

Djenné is a town and urban commune in the Inland Niger Delta region of central Mali. It is famous for its distinctive Sudano-Sahelian mud-brick architecture, above all the Great Mosque of Djenné, the largest mud-brick building in the world, rebuilt in 1907. Founded by merchants around 800 BC at nearby Jenné-Jeno and re-established around the 13th century, Djenné became a major centre of trans-Saharan trade and Islamic scholarship, rival to Timbuktu. The Old Towns of Djenné were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1988. The town's weekly Monday market is one of the largest in Mali.
