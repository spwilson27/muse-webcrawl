The Howard League for Penal Reform is a registered charity in the United Kingdom. It is the oldest penal reform charity in the world, founded in 1921 by the merger of the Howard Association (founded 1866) and the Penal Reform League (founded 1907). It takes its name from the 18th-century prison reformer John Howard. The charity campaigns for less crime, safer communities and fewer people in prison, and works with Parliament, the media, and the criminal justice professions.

History

The Howard Association, named after John Howard (1726–1790), author of The State of the Prisons, campaigned from 1866 for the improvement of prison conditions and the abolition of capital punishment. The Penal Reform League was founded in 1907 by figures including Margery Fry, who became a central figure in the merged organisation. In 1921 the two bodies united as the Howard League for Penal Reform, with Fry as its influential honorary secretary. The League campaigned successfully on many issues, including the abolition of the death penalty (achieved for murder in 1965), probation reform, and the treatment of young offenders.

Work

The League conducts research and campaigns on prison overcrowding, deaths in custody, children in prison, short-term sentences, and resettlement. It runs a legal service for young people in custody, publishes reports and briefings, and gives evidence to parliamentary committees. Its stated aim is to achieve "less crime, safer communities, fewer people in prison." Historic figures associated with the League include criminologist and peace campaigner Margery Fry, whose work helped establish state compensation for victims of violent crime.
