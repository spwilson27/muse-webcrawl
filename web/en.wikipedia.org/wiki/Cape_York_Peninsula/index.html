Cape York Peninsula is a large peninsula located in Far North Queensland, Australia. It is the largest unspoiled wilderness in northern Australia, covering about 288,804 square kilometres. Its tip, Cape York, is the northernmost point of the Australian mainland, separated from New Guinea by the Torres Strait.

Geography and environment

The peninsula's landscape is mostly flat tropical savanna, with eucalypt woodlands, rainforest pockets, wetlands, and wild rivers such as the Wenlock and Jardine. The Great Dividing Range runs along its eastern spine. Its undisturbed river systems and heathlands support exceptional biodiversity, and the region is home to about 18,000 people, the majority of them Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, who hold native title over much of the land.

History

Aboriginal peoples have lived on the peninsula for tens of thousands of years. Willem Janszoon's voyage on the Duyfken in 1606 made the first recorded European landing in Australia on the peninsula's western coast. Edmund Kennedy's ill-fated 1848 expedition attempted to explore the region. Mining of bauxite around Weipa began in the 1960s, and cattle grazing, tourism, and conservation are major land uses today.
