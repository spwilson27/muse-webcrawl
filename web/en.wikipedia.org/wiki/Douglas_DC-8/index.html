Douglas DC-8

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A Douglas DC-8 freighter with four engines loading cargo at night]
A DC-8 freighter.

The Douglas DC-8 is an early long-range narrow-body jet airliner designed and built by the Douglas Aircraft Company and later McDonnell Douglas. Announced in 1955 after Douglas\'s piston airliner dominance, it first flew in 1958 and entered service with United and Delta in 1959. Though outsold by the Boeing 707, the DC-8 became a durable freighter: re-engined Super 70s (converted by Cammacorp with CFM56 engines in the 1980s) flew cargo into the 2020s, and 386 were built in total.

Development and rivalry

Douglas, cautious after the DC-7, was beaten to market by Boeing; the DC-8 answered with a slightly wider cabin (six abreast) and, in later Series 60 \'Super\' stretches, much greater capacity. A DC-8 famously became the first airliner to exceed the speed of sound, in a dive during a 1961 test flight (Mach 1.012) before delivery to Canadian Pacific.

Service and freight afterlife

Passenger DC-8s served into the 1980s; hush-kitted and re-engined freighters served UPS, DHL partners and African and Latin American operators for decades more. NASA flew a DC-8 airborne science laboratory (N817NA) until its retirement in 2024, replaced by a 777.

Design

Four underwing turbojets (later turbofans), distinctive eyebrow-free cockpit (unlike the 707), and a structure noted for strength — factors in its longevity as a freighter and special-missions platform.
