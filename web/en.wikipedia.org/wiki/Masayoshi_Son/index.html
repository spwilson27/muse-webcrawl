Masayoshi Son (Japanese: 孫 正義; born 11 August 1957) is a Japanese entrepreneur, investor, and philanthropist of Korean descent. He is the founder and chief executive officer of the Japanese holding company SoftBank Group and of the SoftBank Vision Fund, the world's largest technology-focused investment fund.

Early life

Son was born in Tosu, Saga Prefecture, to ethnic Korean parents, in a poor household. At sixteen he moved to California, learned English, and entered the University of California, Berkeley, studying economics and computer science. As a student he sold an electronic translator to Sharp Corporation, an early success that funded his ventures.

SoftBank

Returning to Japan, Son founded SoftBank in 1981 as a software distributor; it expanded into publishing, internet services, and telecommunications, acquiring Vodafone Japan and later a controlling stake in Sprint in the United States. His 2000 investment of $20 million in the then-obscure Chinese start-up Alibaba grew into one of the most profitable venture investments ever made. SoftBank's $32 billion acquisition of ARM Holdings in 2016 and the launch of the roughly $100 billion Vision Fund in 2017 — backing Uber, WeWork, ByteDance, and many others — made Son one of the most powerful investors in technology. The collapse of WeWork's planned IPO in 2019 caused heavy losses and prompted a public apology, after which SoftBank adopted a more defensive strategy before returning to aggressive investment in artificial intelligence.
