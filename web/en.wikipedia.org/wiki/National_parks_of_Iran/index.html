National parks of Iran

Administered by: Department of Environment of Iran
First: Golestan National Park (1957 as protected area)
Notable fauna: Asiatic cheetah, Persian leopard, urial

Iran's system of protected areas, administered by the Department of Environment, includes around thirty national parks alongside wildlife refuges, protected areas and national natural monuments. Iran's varied geography — Caspian Hyrcanian forests, Zagros and Alborz mountains, central deserts and Persian Gulf coasts — gives it exceptional biodiversity, including the endangered Asiatic cheetah and the Persian leopard.

List of national parks

Major national parks include:

  • Golestan National Park — one of the oldest, in the northeast; Hyrcanian forest and steppe, part of a UNESCO biosphere reserve.
  • Kavir National Park — desert park southeast of Tehran.
  • Lar National Park — near Mount Damavand, known for the Lar Dam lake and brown trout.
  • Lake Urmia National Park — islands and wetlands of Lake Urmia, flamingo and pelican habitat.
  • Tandooreh National Park — rugged mountains in Khorasan, a stronghold of urial sheep and leopard.
  • Khojir and Sorkheh Hesar — parks east of Tehran in the Alborz foothills.
  • Bakhtegan, Bamu, Kolah Ghazi, Naybandan, Khar Turan, Paband, Salouk, Sarigol, Tandoureh and Turan (Khar Turan) — Turan National Park is a key refuge of the Asiatic cheetah and Persian onager.

Several parks overlap UNESCO biosphere reserves, and the Hyrcanian forests were inscribed as a World Heritage Site in 2019.
