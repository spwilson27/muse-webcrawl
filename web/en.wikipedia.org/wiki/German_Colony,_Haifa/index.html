German Colony, Haifa

Ben-Gurion Avenue and the Baháʼí Gardens
Founded1868
Founded byGerman Templers
LocationHaifa, Israel

The German Colony (Hebrew: המושבה הגרמנית, HaMoshava HaGermanit) is a neighbourhood in Haifa, Israel, established in 1868 by German members of the Templar Society (Temple Society), a pietistic movement that settled in the Holy Land in anticipation of the Second Coming. It was the first of several Templer colonies in Palestine.

History

The Templers laid out a broad main street — today's Ben-Gurion Avenue — running from the port toward Mount Carmel, lined with stone houses with red-tiled roofs, many bearing German biblical inscriptions over their doors. The settlers introduced modern farming, workshops, hotels, a hospital and horse-drawn transport services, helping to modernise Haifa and the region. During the Second World War the British Mandate authorities interned and deported many Templers as enemy aliens, ending the community.

Today

The restored colony is now one of Haifa's principal tourist and nightlife districts, with cafés, restaurants and boutiques along Ben-Gurion Avenue beneath the terraced Baháʼí Gardens on Mount Carmel. The Haifa City Museum occupies the former Templer community house and school, built in 1869.
