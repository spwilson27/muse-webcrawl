Women's Labour League

The Women's Labour League was a pressure group founded in London in 1906, affiliated to the British Labour Party, which campaigned to promote the interests of working-class women and to secure the election of women to Parliament and local government. Its first president was Margaret MacDonald, wife of Ramsay MacDonald, and among its leading figures was Mary Macarthur, the trade union organiser.

Activities

The League campaigned on issues including women's suffrage, maternity and infant welfare, housing, education, and the conditions of women workers. It organised lectures, published pamphlets, and lobbied Labour politicians to adopt policies benefiting women. The League sought women's representation within the labour movement at a time when the trade unions and the party were dominated by men.

Dissolution

In 1918, following the Labour Party's adoption of a new constitution that created women's sections within constituency parties, the Women's Labour League dissolved itself, its members transferring into the new party structure. Its work paved the way for the Labour women's organisation of the interwar period.
