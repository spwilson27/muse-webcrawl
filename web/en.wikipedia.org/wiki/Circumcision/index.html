Circumcision

Circumcision is a procedure that removes the foreskin from the human penis. In the most common form of the operation, the foreskin is extended with forceps, a circumcision device may be placed, and the foreskin is excised. It is one of the world's oldest and most widespread surgical procedures, performed for religious, cultural, social and medical reasons.

History and culture

Evidence of circumcision dates to ancient Egypt, where it is depicted in tomb reliefs of the Sixth Dynasty (around 2345–2182 BC), and it may be considerably older. In Judaism, circumcision (brit milah) is performed on the eighth day of life as a sign of the covenant described in Genesis. In Islam, though not mentioned in the Quran, it is widely practised as a sunnah and usually performed in childhood. The practice is also traditional among many African peoples as a rite of passage, among Aboriginal Australians, and in South Korea and the Philippines (where the rite is called tuli).

Globally, roughly 39% of males are circumcised, with high rates in the Middle East, North and West Africa, the United States and parts of Southeast Asia, and low rates in most of Europe and Latin America. Routine neonatal circumcision became common in English-speaking countries in the late 19th and 20th centuries, initially promoted on hygiene and moral grounds.

Medical aspects

Medically indicated circumcision treats conditions such as pathological phimosis, recurrent balanoposthitis and recurrent urinary tract infections. Evidence syntheses find that male circumcision reduces the risk of heterosexually acquired HIV infection (leading the WHO and UNAIDS to recommend voluntary medical male circumcision in high-prevalence settings), lowers risks of infant urinary tract infection, penile cancer and some sexually transmitted infections, and has a low rate of complications when performed by trained practitioners — most commonly bleeding and infection. The procedure also carries risks and remains ethically debated when performed on infants for non-therapeutic reasons, with medical bodies differing internationally on whether its health benefits justify routine use.

Procedure

In newborns, devices such as the Gomco clamp, Mogen clamp or Plastibell are commonly used, ideally with local anaesthesia (a dorsal penile nerve block or ring block). In older children and adults the foreskin is usually removed by freehand excision and suturing under local or general anaesthesia. Healing of an infant circumcision typically takes about a week to ten days.

Debate

Circumcision has been the subject of ethical, legal and human-rights debate, particularly regarding non-consensual circumcision of minors. A smaller number of men pursue foreskin restoration. Legislation and court rulings have varied by country, generally permitting the practice with parental consent while regulating ritual forms.
