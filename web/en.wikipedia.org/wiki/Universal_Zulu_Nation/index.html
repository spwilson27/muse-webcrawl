Universal Zulu Nation

The Universal Zulu Nation is an international hip hop awareness group founded by hip hop artist Afrika Bambaataa. Originally known simply as the Zulu Nation, it arose in the South Bronx, New York, in the 1970s, growing out of Bambaataa's efforts to redirect gang members toward music, dance, and art.

Origins

After a trip to Africa and inspired by the 1964 film Zulu, Bambaataa adopted the Zulu name for the loose organization of DJs, MCs, breakdancers, and graffiti artists forming around his Bronx parties. The date traditionally celebrated as the organization's anniversary is 12 November 1977.

Philosophy

The group's motto is "peace, unity, love and having fun". Afrika Bambaataa codified the elements of hip hop culture — DJing, MCing, breakdancing, and graffiti art, later with knowledge as a fifth element — and the Zulu Nation spread internationally, with chapters in Europe, Japan, Australia, and elsewhere promoting hip hop as a positive cultural force.

Later history

In 2016, allegations of sexual abuse were made against Bambaataa, who denied them; he stepped down from the organization, which subsequently restructured and distanced itself from him.
