Merlin (rocket engine)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Merlin engine under test.

Merlin is a family of rocket engines developed by SpaceX for use on its Falcon launch vehicles. Burning liquid oxygen and RP-1 kerosene in a gas-generator cycle, Merlin was deliberately designed for simplicity, low cost and mass production — and, unusually for an engine of its class, for reuse, since Falcon 9 first stages land and fly again with the same engines.

The current Merlin 1D produces about 845 kN of thrust at sea level; its vacuum variant, with a greatly enlarged nozzle, powers Falcon upper stages. A Falcon 9 flies ten Merlins per mission — nine on the first stage, one on the second — and Falcon Heavy flies 28.

Design

Merlin descends conceptually from the Apollo-era Fastrac work and uses a pintle injector of the kind used in the Apollo lunar module's descent engine, prized for stability and deep throttling. SpaceX manufactures the engines in-house at high rate; their turbopumps and chambers were progressively uprated from the 2006 Merlin 1A to the full-thrust 1D, and the engine's flight record runs to thousands of firings.
