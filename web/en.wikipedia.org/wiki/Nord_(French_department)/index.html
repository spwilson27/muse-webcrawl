Nord (French department)

[Image: Location of Nord in France]

Nord (French pronunciation: [nɔʁ] ⓘ; Dutch: Noorderdepartement, "Northern Department") is a department in the Hauts-de-France region of France bordering Belgium. It was created from the western halves of the historical counties of Hainaut and Flanders, as well as the Bishopric of Cambrai. It had a population of 2,608,234 in 2019, making it the country's most populous department. Its prefecture is Lille, and its largest city and capital is also Lille.

History

Nord was one of the original 83 departments created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790. The territory had been annexed from the Spanish Netherlands in stages during the 17th century under the treaties of the Pyrenees (1659), Aix-la-Chapelle (1668), and Nijmegen (1678). The department was historically an industrial heartland of coal mining, steel, and textiles, industries that declined sharply in the late 20th century.

Geography

Nord borders the departments of Pas-de-Calais and Aisne, the Belgian provinces of West Flanders and Hainaut, and the North Sea. It is part of the region of Hauts-de-France. The landscape is largely flat lowland, drained by rivers including the Scheldt (Escaut), Lys, and Sambre.

Demographics and culture

The department is densely populated, centred on the Lille metropolitan area, which includes Roubaix and Tourcoing. A West Flemish dialect was traditionally spoken in the far north-west around Dunkirk. Major towns include Valenciennes, Cambrai, Douai, and Maubeuge.

Politics

The department is administered by the Departmental Council of Nord, seated in Lille. Its arrondissements are Avesnes-sur-Helpe, Cambrai, Douai, Dunkirk, Lille, and Valenciennes.
