The Hajar Mountains (Arabic: جبال الحجر, "the Stone Mountains") are the highest mountain range in the eastern Arabian Peninsula, running about 700 kilometres through northern Oman and the eastern United Arab Emirates, from the Musandam Peninsula to the coast near Sur. They separate the low coastal plain of the Gulf of Oman from the high desert plateau of the interior.

Geology and geography

The range is famous in geology for its ophiolite sequence — slices of oceanic crust and mantle thrust onto the continent — among the largest and best exposed in the world. The highest peak is Jabal Shams (3,009 m), near the great canyon of Wadi Ghul. The Western Hajar include the Jebel Akhdar massif, renowned for terraced villages and rose gardens; the Eastern Hajar run toward the Arabian Sea.

Ecology and history

Wadis cutting through the range support distinctive flora and fauna, including the endangered Arabian tahr. Ancient copper mining in the mountains supplied Mesopotamia with the copper of "Magan". Historic forts and aflaj irrigation systems dot the foothills.
