Nabih Berri

Born: 28 January 1938, Bo, Sierra Leone

Office: Speaker of the Parliament of Lebanon (since 1992)

Party: Amal Movement

Nabih Berri (born 28 January 1938) is a Lebanese politician who has served as Speaker of the Parliament of Lebanon since 1992, making him one of the longest-serving parliamentary speakers in the world. He has led the Shia Muslim Amal Movement since 1980 and is a central figure in Lebanon's post-civil-war political order.

Early life and career

Berri was born in Bo, Sierra Leone, to Lebanese Shia immigrant parents from the village of Tibnine in southern Lebanon, and was raised in Lebanon. He studied law at the Lebanese University and in Paris, practised as a lawyer, and in the 1970s worked as an attorney for General Motors in Beirut and Detroit. He became involved with the movement founded by Imam Musa al-Sadr and took over Amal's leadership in 1980, two years after al-Sadr's disappearance in Libya.

Political role

Berri led Amal through the Lebanese Civil War, including its bitter "War of the Camps" against Palestinian factions, and was a key negotiator of Lebanon's internal settlements. Under the Taif Agreement's power-sharing arrangements, the speakership is reserved for a Shia Muslim; Berri has held it continuously since 1992, exercising great influence over legislation, government formation and the Shia community's politics alongside Hezbollah, Amal's ally since the 2000s. Critics associate him with Lebanon's entrenched confessional patronage system; supporters credit him as a stabilising broker.
