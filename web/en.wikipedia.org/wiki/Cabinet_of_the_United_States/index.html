Cabinet of the United States

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Cabinet of the United States is the principal advisory body to the President of the United States, composed of the vice president and the heads of the fifteen executive departments (State, Treasury, Defense, Justice — whose head is the Attorney General — Interior, Agriculture, Commerce, Labor, Health and Human Services, Housing and Urban Development, Transportation, Energy, Education, Veterans Affairs, Homeland Security). Department heads, titled Secretary (except the Attorney General), are nominated by the president and confirmed by the Senate. Additional officials — the White House chief of staff, the EPA administrator, the UN ambassador and others — may hold cabinet rank at the president's discretion.

The Cabinet is extra-constitutional (the Constitution mentions only that the president may require written opinions of department heads, Article II), a precedent from Washington's administration. Full cabinet meetings are largely ceremonial under modern presidents, who govern through the Executive Office and smaller councils; the Cabinet's constitutional moments come under the Twenty-fifth Amendment (disability determinations) and in the line of presidential succession. Its collective role contrasts with collective responsibility systems: US secretaries serve at the president's pleasure and answer to the president, not to a legislature.
