Cathay Pacific is the flag carrier of Hong Kong, with its head office and main hub at Hong Kong International Airport. Founded in 1946 by the American Roy Farrell and the Australian Sydney de Kantzow, the airline began with a single Douglas DC-3 — the celebrated "Betsy" — flying charter and cargo services around the post-war Pacific.

History

The airline's curious name reflects its founders' ambitions: "Cathay" is the ancient name for China, and "Pacific" their expectation of one day flying across the ocean to the United States. Cathay Pacific grew with Hong Kong's rise as a trading and financial centre, joining the Oneworld alliance as a founding member in 1999 and building one of the world's most profitable long-haul networks. Its sister operations include the low-cost carrier HK Express and the cargo airline Air Hong Kong; its former regional subsidiary Cathay Dragon was folded into the mainline brand in 2020.

Operations

The passenger fleet is built around wide-body Airbus A350s and Boeing 777s, serving destinations across Asia, Europe, North America, and Australasia. The airline has repeatedly been ranked among the world's best for long-haul service, and its "brushwing" livery, introduced in 1994 and refreshed in 2015, is a widely recognised emblem of Hong Kong.
