Papua is a province of Indonesia occupying the northern coast of Western New Guinea. It is bordered by the Pacific Ocean to the north, Papua New Guinea to the east, and the Indonesian provinces of Central Papua and Highland Papua to the south. Its capital and largest city is Jayapura.

History

The territory was part of Dutch New Guinea until 1962, was administered by Indonesia from 1963, and was incorporated following the controversial 1969 Act of Free Choice. It was formerly known as Irian Jaya until being renamed Papua in 2002 under a special autonomy law. In 2022 the province was reduced when Central Papua, Highland Papua, and South Papua were split off, leaving Papua with the northern coastal region and islands including Biak and Yapen.

Geography and people

The province includes part of the rugged New Guinea Highlands, the Lake Sentani basin, and extensive lowland rainforests of exceptionally high biodiversity, including Lorentz-adjacent ecosystems and the Mamberamo River basin. Indigenous Papuan peoples — speaking hundreds of distinct languages — form the majority in rural areas, alongside settlers from elsewhere in Indonesia concentrated in the cities.
