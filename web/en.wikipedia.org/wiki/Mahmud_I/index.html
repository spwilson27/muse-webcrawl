Mahmud I

Mahmud I (2 August 1696 – 13 December 1754), known as Mahmud the Hunchback, was the Sultan of the Ottoman Empire from 1730 to 1754, in succession to his uncle Ahmed III. He came to the throne in the aftermath of the Patrona Halil rebellion that ended the Tulip Period.

Reign

Born in Edirne to Sultan Mustafa II, Mahmud spent much of his early life confined in the palace Kafes. Elevated by the rebels of 1730, he eventually restored the authority of the throne, and Patrona Halil and his associates were eliminated in 1731. His reign saw the Russo-Austro-Turkish War of 1735–1739, in which Ottoman arms, aided by diplomacy, recovered Belgrade and northern Serbia by the Treaty of Belgrade (1739) — the empire's last major territorial gain in Europe. In the east, wars with Nader Shah's Persia ended with the Treaty of Kerden (1746), restoring earlier frontiers.

Domestic rule

Mahmud relied on able grand viziers, notably Hekimoğlu Ali Pasha, and began tentative military reforms, employing the French convert Comte de Bonneval (Humbaracı Ahmed Pasha) to reorganize the bombardier corps. He was also a notable patron of architecture, completing the Nuruosmaniye Mosque complex project begun under him (finished by his successor), and establishing libraries and fountains in Constantinople.

Death

Mahmud I died in 1754, reportedly of a heart complaint after Friday prayers, and was succeeded by his brother Osman III. He was buried in the tomb of his father in Istanbul.
