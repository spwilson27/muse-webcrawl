The Stadium of Light is an all-seater football stadium in Sunderland, England, and the home of Sunderland A.F.C. since 1997. With a capacity of 49,000, it is the ninth-largest football stadium in England. The stadium primarily hosts Sunderland matches and has also staged international fixtures and major concerts.

History

Sunderland moved to the Stadium of Light from Roker Park, their home since 1898, at the start of the 1997–98 season. The new stadium was built on the site of the former Monkwearmouth Colliery, and its name references the miners' lamps historically used in the Durham coalfield, as well as the club's working-class heritage. A miners' lamp monument stands outside the ground.

The stadium was officially opened by Prince Andrew, Duke of York, in July 1997, with Sunderland playing a friendly match against Ajax. The initial capacity was 42,000, and the north stand was extended in 2000, raising the capacity to its present 49,000.

Structure and facilities

The stadium is a four-sided bowl design with a continuous lower tier. The west stand contains the main reception, hospitality suites and the dressing rooms. The Davy lamp-inspired design and the "Stadium of Light" name were chosen partly in homage to Benfica's Estádio da Luz, which translates similarly.

Other events

The Stadium of Light has hosted England international matches, including a friendly against Turkey in 2003, England under-21 fixtures, and concerts by artists such as Oasis, Take That, Bruce Springsteen, Coldplay, Beyoncé and Ed Sheeran.
