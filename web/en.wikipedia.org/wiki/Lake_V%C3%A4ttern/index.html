Lake Vättern

Vättern is the second-largest lake in Sweden, after Vänern, and the sixth-largest lake in Europe. It lies in south-central Sweden, between the provinces of Östergötland and Västergötland.

Geography

The lake covers about 1,912 square kilometres and is long and narrow, roughly 135 km from north to south, with a maximum depth of 128 metres. Its water is famously clear and cold, with low nutrient levels; it serves as a drinking-water source for several municipalities. The island of Visingsö lies in its southern part, and the Göta Canal passes through its southern basin. The town of Jönköping sits at the lake's southern tip.

Geology

The basin of Vättern is a graben formed by tectonic activity more than 800 million years ago, partially following much older structures in the Scandinavian Shield.
