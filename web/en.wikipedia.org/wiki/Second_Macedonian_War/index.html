Second Macedonian War

Date200–197 BCE
LocationGreece and the Aegean
BelligerentsRoman Republic, Pergamum, Rhodes, Athens, Aetolian League vs. Kingdom of Macedon
OutcomeRoman victory; Treaty of Tempea

The Second Macedonian War (200–197 BCE) was fought between the Roman Republic, allied with Pergamum, Rhodes, Athens, and the Aetolian League, and the Kingdom of Macedon under Philip V. It ended Macedonian dominance of Greece and marked Rome's emergence as the arbiter of the eastern Mediterranean.

Origins

After the First Macedonian War and the Peace of Phoenice (205 BCE), Philip V expanded aggressively in the Aegean and Asia Minor, attacking Roman-protected states and seizing Cius and other cities; Pergamum and Rhodes appealed to Rome in 201 BCE. The Senate, despite war-weariness after the Second Punic War, obtained a declaration of war from the centuriate assembly — on the second vote — and issued an ultimatum that Philip rejected.

The war

Roman consul Publius Sulpicius Galba landed in Illyria in 200 BCE, and campaigns in 199 BCE under Publius Villius produced stalemate, including a checked assault at the Aous gorge. The energetic young consul Titus Quinctius Flamininus took command in 198 BCE, forced the pass of the Aous, and drove Philip out of Thessaly. The decisive Battle of Cynoscephalae in June 197 BCE — fought in fog on twin hills between Roman legions and the Macedonian phalanx — ended in a clear Roman victory, with some 8,000 Macedonians killed and 5,000 captured.

Settlement

By the Treaty of Tempea (196 BCE), Philip retained Macedon but surrendered his possessions in Greece, Asia, and the Aegean, paid an indemnity of 1,000 talents, gave up most of his fleet, and sent his son Demetrius as a hostage to Rome. At the Isthmian Games of 196 BCE, Flamininus proclaimed the "freedom of the Greeks," to enormous acclaim — a declaration that inaugurated decades of Roman involvement in Greek affairs and led ultimately to the Third and Fourth Macedonian Wars and the annexation of Macedon as a province in 148 BCE.
