Concours

Concours (French for "competition" or "contest") generally refers to a competitive examination system, particularly in France and other francophone countries, in which candidates are ranked against one another and only a fixed number of the highest-scoring candidates are admitted. It contrasts with an ordinary examination in which every candidate meeting a standard passes.

In France

Concours are the principal gateway to the French grandes écoles and to much of the civil service. After the classes préparatoires aux grandes écoles, students sit highly selective concours for schools such as the École polytechnique, the écoles normales supérieures, and business schools. Separate concours govern entry into public-service careers, teaching (including the agrégation, itself a concours), and the diplomatic and administrative corps.

Characteristics

Because the number of places is fixed in advance (numerus clausus), candidates are selected by rank order rather than by an absolute threshold. Concours typically combine written examinations with oral examinations before a jury. The system is prized as meritocratic but criticized for reinforcing social reproduction, since success depends heavily on access to preparatory classes and cultural capital.

Other uses

The word also appears in events such as the concours d'élégance for classic automobiles, music competitions, and agricultural shows in France.
