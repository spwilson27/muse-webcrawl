Zbigniew Antoni Preisner (born 20 May 1955) is a Polish composer best known for his film scores, in particular his long collaboration with director Krzysztof Kieślowski. His music — lyrical, melancholic and often built around solo voice, piano and orchestra — became one of the defining sounds of European art cinema in the late twentieth century.

Early life and career

Preisner was born in Bielsko-Biała and studied history and philosophy at the Jagiellonian University in Kraków; he never received formal conservatory training in composition, teaching himself music instead. He began composing for cabaret and television before writing his first film score in the late 1970s. His breakthrough came with Kieślowski's No End (1985), beginning a partnership that continued until the director's death in 1996.

Collaboration with Kieślowski

Preisner composed the music for Dekalog (1988), The Double Life of Véronique (1991) and the Three Colours trilogy (1993–94). A running conceit of the films is the fictional Dutch composer "Van den Budenmayer," whose music Preisner actually wrote. In Blue, the unfinished "Song for the Unification of Europe" structures the entire film; in Red, the music underscores the theme of fraternity. The scores sold in the millions worldwide, unusual for contemporary film music.

Other work

Beyond Kieślowski, Preisner has scored films by Agnieszka Holland (Europa Europa, Olivier, Olivier), Louis Malle (Damage), Hector Babenco, Thomas Vinterberg, and Fernando Trueba, among many others. His non-film works include Requiem for My Friend (1998), dedicated to Kieślowski's memory, the cantata Silence, Night and Dreams, and oratorios such as 2016 Dokad?. He has received numerous honours, including César Awards and a Golden Bear at Berlin for his music.

Style

Preisner's music is marked by economy of means: slow harmonic movement, modal melodies, spare orchestration, and prominent silence. He often writes the music before filming is complete, so that directors can shoot to the score — a method Kieślowski used extensively.
