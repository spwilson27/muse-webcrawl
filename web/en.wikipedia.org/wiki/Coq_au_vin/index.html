Coq au vin

Coq au vin
CourseMain
Place of originFrance
Main ingredientsChicken, wine, lardons, mushrooms, garlic

Coq au vin (French: "rooster/cock in wine") is a French dish of chicken braised with wine, lardons, mushrooms and optionally garlic. A red Burgundy wine is typically used, though many regions of France make variants using local wines, such as coq au vin jaune (Jura), coq au riesling (Alsace), and coq au Champagne.

History

Various legends trace the dish to ancient Gaul and Julius Caesar, but the recipe was not documented until the early 20th century. The dish made an old, tough rooster palatable through slow braising; modern versions generally use ordinary chicken. Julia Child's recipe in Mastering the Art of French Cooking (1961), and her preparation of it on the television programme The French Chef, helped make coq au vin famous in the United States.

Preparation

Standard recipes call for browning the chicken, then braising it slowly in red wine with pearl onions, mushrooms, carrots, garlic, thyme and bay leaf, together with salt pork or bacon lardons. The cooking liquid is often thickened with a beurre manié or, traditionally, with the bird's blood. The dish is commonly served with mashed potatoes, pasta or rice, and garnished with parsley.
