Batgirl is the name of several fictional superheroines appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics, depicted as female counterparts and allies of the superhero Batman. Although the character Betty Kane was introduced into publication in 1961 by Bill Finger and Sheldon Moldoff as Bat-Girl, she was replaced in 1967 by Barbara Gordon, who became the most iconic and enduring incarnation of the character.

The Barbara Gordon version of Batgirl debuted in Detective Comics #359 (January 1967), "The Million Dollar Debut of Batgirl!", by writer Gardner Fox and artist Carmine Infantino. Gordon is the daughter of Gotham City police commissioner James Gordon. After she was shot and paralyzed by the Joker in the 1988 graphic novel Batman: The Killing Joke, she reinvented herself as the information broker and hacker Oracle, a role she held for over two decades until DC's 2011 New 52 relaunch restored her mobility and returned her to the Batgirl mantle.

Publication history

Bette Kane

The original Bat-Girl, Bette Kane, was the niece and sidekick of Kathy Kane's Batwoman. She appeared in the early 1960s but fell out of use along with Batwoman when editor Julius Schwartz revamped the Batman line in 1964. The character was later reworked as the heroine Flamebird.

Barbara Gordon

Barbara Gordon was created at the request of the producers of the 1960s Batman television series, who wanted a new female character to appear simultaneously in print and on screen; Yvonne Craig portrayed her in the show's third season. In the comics, Barbara — a librarian with a doctorate and the Gotham police commissioner's daughter — secretly fights crime alongside Batman and Robin. Her tenure as Batgirl lasted until 1988, when writer Alan Moore's The Killing Joke depicted the Joker shooting her through the spine.

Editor Kim Yale and writer John Ostrander subsequently revived Gordon as Oracle, the wheelchair-using coordinator of the superhero community, who headlined the long-running Birds of Prey series with Black Canary and Huntress. Oracle became one of comics' most prominent disabled characters.

Cassandra Cain and Stephanie Brown

During the 1999 "No Man's Land" storyline, Cassandra Cain — a near-silent assassin's daughter trained to read body language instead of speech — became Batgirl, the first to headline her own Batgirl monthly series. Stephanie Brown, formerly the Spoiler and a Robin, took the mantle in 2009 in a well-received series written by Bryan Q. Miller. After the New 52 restored Barbara Gordon, Cain and Brown were reintroduced under the names Orphan and Spoiler.

In other media

Batgirl has appeared in numerous animated series, including The New Batman Adventures and The Batman, and in video games such as the Batman: Arkham series (as both Batgirl in DLC and Oracle) and Gotham Knights (2022), where she is one of four playable protagonists. A live-action Batgirl film starring Leslie Grace was filmed for HBO Max but was shelved by Warner Bros. Discovery in 2022 before release.
