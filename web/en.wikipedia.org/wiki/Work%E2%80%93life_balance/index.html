Work–life balance

Work–life balance is the equilibrium that an individual needs between time allocated for work and other aspects of life, including family, leisure, personal development and rest. The term gained prominence from the 1970s and 1980s onward, especially as women entered the workforce in larger numbers and working hours lengthened in many economies.

FieldOrganizational psychology; human resources
Related conceptsBurnout, flexible working, four-day week

Discussion

Poor work–life balance is associated with stress, burnout and health problems, while flexible scheduling, remote work and limits on working time are commonly proposed remedies. Legal frameworks such as the EU Working Time Directive cap weekly hours, and some countries have experimented with four-day weeks and "right to disconnect" laws. Critics note that the framing of a simple "balance" between two spheres oversimplifies how work and life interpenetrate.
