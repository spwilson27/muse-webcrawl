Whippet

Whippet
Origin: England
Type: Sighthound
Height: 47–56 cm (18.5–22 in)
Weight: 6.8–19 kg (15–42 lb)
Coat: Short, smooth, fine
Life span: 12–15 years

The Whippet is a British breed of medium-sized dog of the sighthound type. It is closely related to the Greyhound and resembles a smaller version of that breed. Whippets are among the fastest dogs of their size, able to reach speeds of up to about 35 mph (56 km/h), a result of their double suspension gallop.

History

The Whippet was developed in England, principally in the industrial north, during the nineteenth century. Working-class breeders crossed small Greyhounds with terriers and later Italian Greyhounds to produce a dog for hunting small game and for racing. Nicknamed the "poor man's racehorse," the Whippet was raced in alleyways and on improvised tracks — a sport known as "snap dog" racing, in which dogs snapped at a waved rag. The breed was recognised by the Kennel Club in 1891.

Characteristics

Whippets have a lean, elegant build with a deep chest, tucked-up waist, long neck, and fine short coat in a wide variety of colours. They are typically quiet, gentle, and undemanding indoors, despite their great speed outdoors, and are often described as spending much of the day asleep on soft furniture. The breed is generally healthy, though sensitive to cold owing to its lack of body fat.

Uses

Whippets compete in lure coursing, straight and oval track racing, agility, and conformation shows, and are popular companion animals. A mutation in the myostatin gene found in some Whippets produces heavily muscled "bully" individuals; dogs with two copies of the mutation are heavily over-muscled, while single-copy carriers may be faster racers.
