Kurdish music

Kurdish music refers to music performed in the Kurdish languages and zaza-gorani languages, and forms a central part of Kurdish culture. Traditionally, it was transmitted orally by bards known as dengbêj, who sang epic tales and laments, often accompanied by no instruments at all.

Traditions

The dengbêj tradition — exemplified by performers such as Şakiro, Karapetê Xaço and Evdalê Zeynikê — preserved Kurdish history, love stories and elegies across generations. Instrumental folk music commonly uses the tembûr (long-necked lute), duduk, zirne and daf (frame drum). Dance music for the govend (circle dance) is performed at weddings and Newroz celebrations.

Modern era

Kurdish music was restricted in Turkey for much of the twentieth century, and recordings circulated clandestinely. Pioneers of modern Kurdish recording included Mihemed Şêxo, Şivan Perwer — whose exile recordings became anthems of the Kurdish movement — and Nîzamuddin Arîç. Contemporary artists span folk, pop and rock, including Aynur Doğan, Ciwan Haco, Dilşa Demîr and the diaspora communities in Europe.
