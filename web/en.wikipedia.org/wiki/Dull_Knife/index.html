Dull Knife

Dull Knife (Cheyenne: Vóóhéhéve, rendered in Cheyenne as Morning Star; c. 1810–1883) was a chief of the Northern Cheyenne people, known for his resistance to the United States government and his efforts to lead his people back to their northern homeland in Montana. To the Cheyenne he is remembered by his Cheyenne name, Morning Star.

Life

Dull Knife fought alongside the Lakota and other Cheyenne bands during the years of conflict on the northern Plains, including the aftermath of the Battle of the Little Bighorn in 1876. In November 1876, Colonel Ranald Mackenzie's cavalry destroyed Dull Knife's village in the Bighorn Mountains — the Dull Knife Fight — a blow from which his band never fully recovered. He surrendered at Fort Robinson, Nebraska, in 1877.

In 1877 the Northern Cheyenne were removed to Indian Territory (present-day Oklahoma), where disease and malnutrition devastated them. In September 1878 Dull Knife and Little Wolf led some 350 Cheyenne northward in the Northern Cheyenne Exodus, fighting off pursuing troops across Kansas and Nebraska. Dull Knife's group was captured and imprisoned at Fort Robinson; when they refused an order to return south, they attempted a desperate escape in January 1879 (the Fort Robinson massacre), in which many were killed. Dull Knife survived and was eventually permitted to settle near the Tongue River in Montana, where the Northern Cheyenne Indian Reservation was established in 1884. He died in 1883 and is buried at Lame Deer, Montana.
