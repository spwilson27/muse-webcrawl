The zhonghu (Chinese: 中胡; pinyin: zhōnghú, "alto huqin") is a Chinese bowed string instrument in the huqin family. It is the alto counterpart of the better-known erhu: larger in body, with a deeper, mellower tone, and typically tuned a fifth or a fourth lower.

A zhonghu, the alto member of the huqin family.

Description

Like the erhu, the zhonghu has two strings stretched over a small sound box traditionally covered with snakeskin, a long fretless neck, and is played with a bow whose hair passes between the strings. Its resonator is noticeably bigger than the erhu's and its strings thicker, producing a rich middle register. Common tunings include G–D or F–C, a perfect fifth below the erhu's D–A.

Use

The instrument was developed in the early-to-mid 20th century as Chinese orchestras expanded by analogy with the Western string sections, requiring alto voices between the erhu (violin/viola register) and the cello-like gehu or cello. In the modern Chinese orchestra the zhonghu section fills the inner harmonies; it is also occasionally used as a solo instrument, valued for its warm, singing tone.
