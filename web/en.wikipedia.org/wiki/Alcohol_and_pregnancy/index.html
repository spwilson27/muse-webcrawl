Alcohol and pregnancy

Alcohol and pregnancy refers to the effects of consuming alcohol during pregnancy on the developing embryo and fetus. Because alcohol crosses the placenta, prenatal alcohol exposure can disrupt development at any stage of pregnancy. Health authorities including the World Health Organization, the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, and the U.S. Surgeon General recommend complete abstinence from alcohol during pregnancy, as no safe amount or safe time has been established.

Fetal alcohol spectrum disorders

The most serious consequence of prenatal alcohol exposure is fetal alcohol spectrum disorder (FASD), an umbrella term for a range of physical, behavioural and intellectual disabilities. Fetal alcohol syndrome (FAS), the most severe presentation, involves growth deficiency, characteristic facial features (such as a smooth philtrum, thin upper lip and small eye openings), and central nervous system damage that can cause lifelong cognitive and behavioural problems.

Other risks

Drinking during pregnancy is also associated with an increased risk of miscarriage, stillbirth, preterm birth and low birth weight.

Prevalence and prevention

Prevention efforts focus on public education, screening and brief intervention in prenatal care, and support for pregnant people with alcohol use disorder, who may require treatment rather than punishment to stop drinking safely.
