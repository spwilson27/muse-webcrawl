Penny dreadful
OriginUnited Kingdom, 1830s–1900s
FormatWeekly serial pamphlets, 8–16 pages
PriceOne penny
GenresGothic horror, crime, adventure

Penny dreadfuls were cheap British popular serial literature of the 19th century, costing one penny and aimed at working-class readers, especially young men. Published weekly in flimsy pamphlets, they delivered sensational tales of crime, horror and adventure, and were later reissued in the US as dime novels.

Content and titles

Famous titles include The String of Pearls (1846–47), which introduced the demon barber Sweeney Todd; Varney the Vampire (1845–47), which ran to over 200 episodes; and highwayman tales such as Dick Turpin and Spring-Heeled Jack. Gothic horror, executed criminals and daring outlaws were staples.

Reception

Middle-class commentators blamed penny dreadfuls for juvenile crime and moral corruption, much as later generations blamed comics and video games. Rising literacy, compulsory schooling and cheaper respectable magazines — and from the 1890s Alfred Harmsworth's halfpenny papers — drove them from the market by about 1900.
