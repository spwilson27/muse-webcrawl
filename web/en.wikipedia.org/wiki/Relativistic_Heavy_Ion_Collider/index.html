Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider

The Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) is the first and one of only two operating heavy-ion colliders, and the only spin-polarized proton collider ever built. Located at Brookhaven National Laboratory (BNL) in Upton, New York, it is used by an international team of researchers to study the primordial state of matter that existed shortly after the Big Bang.

History and design

RHIC began operation in 2000. The accelerator consists of two independent rings, 3.83 kilometres (2.38 miles) in circumference, in which heavy ions such as gold nuclei, or polarized protons, are accelerated to relativistic speeds and made to collide at energies of up to 200 GeV per nucleon pair for heavy ions and 500 GeV for proton–proton collisions.

Physics programme

By colliding heavy ions at extremely high energies, RHIC heats matter to trillions of degrees, recreating the quark–gluon plasma, a state of matter in which quarks and gluons are deconfined. Experiments at RHIC provided strong evidence that this plasma behaves like a nearly perfect liquid with very low viscosity. Its major detectors have included STAR, PHENIX (succeeded by sPHENIX), PHOBOS and BRAHMS.

Future

RHIC is scheduled to conclude operations in the mid-2020s so that its tunnel and infrastructure can be reused for the planned Electron–Ion Collider (EIC).
