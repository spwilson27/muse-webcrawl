Onsager reciprocal relations

The Onsager reciprocal relations express the equality of certain ratios between flows and forces in thermodynamic systems out of equilibrium, but close to it. They were derived by the Norwegian physical chemist Lars Onsager in 1931, work for which he received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1968.

Statement

For a system in which several irreversible processes occur simultaneously — for example, heat conduction and diffusion — the fluxes Ji are, near equilibrium, linear functions of the thermodynamic forces Xj: Ji = Σj Lij Xj. Onsager showed from the microscopic reversibility of molecular motion, together with the statistics of fluctuations, that the matrix of phenomenological coefficients is symmetric: Lij = Lji. Thus the effect of a temperature gradient on a matter flow (thermal diffusion, the Soret effect) is matched by the reciprocal effect of a concentration gradient on the heat flow (the Dufour effect), and the Seebeck and Peltier coefficients in thermoelectricity are similarly related.

Significance

The relations are sometimes called the "fourth law of thermodynamics" because of their generality: they constrain all coupled transport processes near equilibrium and underpin the thermodynamics of irreversible processes developed by Onsager, Ilya Prigogine and others. In the presence of magnetic fields or rotation, a generalised form (the Onsager–Casimir relations) relates coefficients with a change of sign of the field.
