Lijiang (Chinese: 丽江; pinyin: Lìjiāng) is a prefecture-level city in the northwest of Yunnan province, China. It is famous for its Old Town, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and for its setting beneath the Jade Dragon Snow Mountain. The city is a major centre of the Nakhi (Naxi) people, known for their Dongba pictographic script and distinctive matrilineal traditions among the related Mosuo.

Old Town

The Old Town of Lijiang (Dayan) has a history going back more than 800 years, when it was a staging point on the Ancient Tea Horse Road. Unlike most Chinese old towns it has no city wall — legend holds that the ruling Mu family avoided enclosing the town because the character for "Mu" inside a frame forms the character for "trapped". The town is laced with canals fed by the Black Dragon Pool, crossed by hundreds of small bridges, and lined with wooden shop houses along cobbled lanes. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1997. The nearby old towns of Shuhe and Baisha share the region's heritage; Baisha preserves celebrated Ming-dynasty murals.

History

The area came under Chinese administration in the Yuan dynasty, and the Mu chieftains governed as hereditary officials (tusi) for centuries, adopting much Han Chinese culture while retaining Nakhi identity. In February 1996 a major earthquake struck the region, killing several hundred people and damaging many old buildings; reconstruction preserved the traditional architecture and helped renew the town's fabric before its World Heritage listing.

Geography and tourism

Lijiang sits at about 2,400 metres elevation, giving it a mild highland climate. Sights include the Black Dragon Pool with its classic view of Jade Dragon Snow Mountain (Yulong Xueshan, 5,596 m), Tiger Leaping Gorge on the Jinsha River to the northeast, and Lugu Lake, home of the Mosuo people. Tourism is the dominant industry, and the crowds drawn by the Old Town's popularity have made it a prominent case study in both the benefits and the strains of heritage tourism in China.
