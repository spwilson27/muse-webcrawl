United Arab Emirates dirham

The United Arab Emirates dirham (Arabic: درهم إماراتي, sign: Dhs or د.إ; ISO code: AED) is the official currency of the United Arab Emirates. The dirham is subdivided into 100 fils (فلس). It is issued by the Central Bank of the United Arab Emirates.

History

The UAE dirham was introduced on 19 May 1973, replacing the Qatar and Dubai riyal at par and the Bahraini dinar (in Abu Dhabi) at a rate of 1 dinar = 10 dirhams. Before that, currencies used in the region included the Gulf rupee and the Saudi riyal. The name dirham derives from the Greek drachma and was used historically for silver coins across the Islamic world.

Exchange rate

The dirham has been pegged to the U.S. dollar since 1997 at a rate of 1 U.S. dollar = 3.6725 dirhams (approximately), a fixed rate that the UAE has maintained to provide monetary stability. Before the dollar peg, it was linked to the IMF's Special Drawing Rights.

Coins and banknotes

Coins are issued in denominations of 1, 5, 10, 25, and 50 fils and 1 dirham; the 1 dirham coin famously resembles in size and weight older coins and is sometimes confused with other currencies. Banknotes are issued in denominations of 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, and 1,000 dirhams. In recent years the central bank has introduced polymer banknotes with enhanced security features and tactile marks for the visually impaired.
