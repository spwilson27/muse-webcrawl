Robert K. Greenleaf

Robert K. Greenleaf
Born: 1904, Terre Haute, Indiana, United States
Died: 1990
Occupation: Management researcher, author, consultant
Employer: AT&T (1926–1964)
Known for: Originating the concept of servant leadership

Robert K. Greenleaf (1904–1990) was an American management thinker best known as the founder of the modern servant leadership movement. His 1970 essay "The Servant as Leader" introduced the idea that the most effective leaders are first servants of those they lead, and his ideas have influenced management education and practice worldwide.

Career

Greenleaf spent nearly four decades at AT&T, where he worked in management research and development and became director of management research, studying how large organisations function and how leaders within them develop. After retiring in 1964 he worked as a consultant to businesses, foundations, and universities, and in 1964 founded the Center for Applied Ethics, which later became the Greenleaf Center for Servant Leadership.

Servant leadership

Inspired in part by Hermann Hesse's novel Journey to the East, Greenleaf proposed that leadership begins with "the natural feeling that one wants to serve". The servant-leader, he wrote, focuses on the growth and well-being of people and communities, and the test of such leadership is whether those served "grow as persons" and become healthier, wiser, freer, and more autonomous. His major book, Servant Leadership: A Journey into the Nature of Legitimate Power and Greatness (1977), collected his essays. Ten characteristics of servant-leaders — including listening, empathy, stewardship, and commitment to the growth of people — were later distilled from his writings by Larry Spears.
