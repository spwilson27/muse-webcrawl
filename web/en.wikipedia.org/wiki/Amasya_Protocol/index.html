Amasya Protocol

The Amasya Protocol was an agreement reached in October 1919 between representatives of the Ottoman government in Constantinople and the Turkish national movement led by Mustafa Kemal. It followed the Amasya Circular and the congresses at Erzurum and Sivas, which had begun coordinating resistance after the First World War.

Terms and context

The protocol recognised important nationalist demands, including the integrity of Turkish-majority Ottoman territories, the need for a freely elected Chamber of Deputies and the rejection of foreign mandates or privileges that compromised independence. It also provided for elections and for the assembly to meet in a secure location, a point that soon became contentious.

Consequences

The agreement temporarily legitimised the national movement within Ottoman politics. Elections brought many nationalist deputies to the reopened parliament, which adopted the National Pact in 1920. Allied occupation of Constantinople and dissolution of the parliament then pushed the movement to establish the Grand National Assembly in Ankara.
