Vyākaraṇa

Vyākaraṇa (Sanskrit: व्याकरण, "analysis, explanation") refers to Sanskrit grammar and the study of language, one of the six Vedangas, the auxiliary disciplines associated with the study of the Vedas. Its most famous exponent was Pāṇini, whose work, the Aṣṭādhyāyī, is the oldest surviving complete grammar of Sanskrit and a landmark in the history of linguistics.

Purpose

Within the Vedic tradition, vyākaraṇa existed to preserve the exact form of the sacred texts: correct pronunciation and correct grammatical form were held to be essential for the efficacy of ritual recitation. Patañjali, in his Mahābhāṣya (the "great commentary" on Pāṇini), lists purposes of grammar including the preservation of the Vedas, the ability to adapt mantras to ritual needs, and the avoidance of incorrect speech.

Major figures

The tradition recognizes three great sages (munitraya): Pāṇini (c. 5th–4th century BCE), author of the Aṣṭādhyāyī with its roughly 4,000 sūtras; Kātyāyana, who wrote critical vārttikas (explanatory notes) on Pāṇini's rules; and Patañjali (2nd century BCE), whose Mahābhāṣya commented on both. Later grammarians such as Bhartṛhari, author of the Vākyapadīya, developed the philosophy of language within the tradition, including the doctrine of sphoṭa.
