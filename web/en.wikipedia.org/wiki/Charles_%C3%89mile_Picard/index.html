Charles Émile Picard (24 July 1856 – 11 December 1941) was a French mathematician whose work in complex analysis and differential equations made him one of the leading analysts of his generation.

Work

Picard is known for the Picard theorems of complex analysis — the "little" theorem that an entire non-constant function omits at most one complex value, and the "great" theorem on behaviour near an essential singularity. He also proved the Picard–Lindelöf theorem on the existence and uniqueness of solutions to ordinary differential equations, using the iterative method of successive approximations now called Picard iteration.

Honours

Picard was elected to the Académie des Sciences in 1889 and later to the Académie française. He served as president of the International Congress of Mathematicians and received numerous international honours.
