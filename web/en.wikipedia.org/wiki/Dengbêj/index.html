Dengbêj

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dengbêj is a Kurdish term for a singer or storyteller who performs dengbêjî, the art of singing epic tales, laments, and histories, traditionally unaccompanied or with minimal accompaniment. The word combines deng ("voice") and bêj ("to say" or "to sing"). Dengbêjs are regarded as living archives of Kurdish history, transmitting through memory and song the events, heroes, tragedies, and values of Kurdish society across centuries in which Kurdish was often excluded from written institutions.

The tradition

Dengbêj songs (kilam) recount epic love stories, battles, tribal feuds, laments for the dead (lawik), and the deeds of heroes and outlaws. Performances were traditionally held in the dîwanxane, the guest room of a village notable's house, where audiences gathered through the winter nights. A dengbêj required an exceptional memory — repertoires could include epics lasting hours — as well as a powerful, ornamented voice capable of long, melismatic lines and dramatic narration alternating with song.

Because the tradition carried Kurdish language and collective memory, it was suppressed at times when Kurdish cultural expression was banned, particularly in Turkey during much of the 20th century, when public use of Kurdish was prohibited. Performers and collectors nevertheless preserved the repertoire, sometimes in secret.

Notable dengbêjs

Among the most revered masters are Evdalê Zeynikê (19th century), considered the founder of the Serhat school; Reso; Karapetê Xaço, who recorded extensively for Radio Yerevan; Şakiro, famous for his virtuoso voice; and the pioneering female singer Meryem Xan. In the modern era, the Mala Dengbêjan ("House of Dengbêjs") in Diyarbakır, opened in 2007, supports elderly masters and documents their repertoires, and dengbêj recordings circulate widely in the Kurdish diaspora.
