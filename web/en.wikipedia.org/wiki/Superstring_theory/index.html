Superstring theory

Superstring theory is an attempt to explain all of the particles and fundamental forces of nature in one theory by modelling them as vibrations of tiny supersymmetric strings. It is a broad and varied subject within theoretical physics, incorporating supersymmetry into string theory and requiring extra spatial dimensions compactified at very small scales.

Overview

In superstring theory, the point-like particles of particle physics are replaced by one-dimensional objects called strings, whose different modes of vibration correspond to different particles, including a spin-2 mode identified with the graviton, making the theory a candidate for a quantum theory of gravity. Consistency requires ten spacetime dimensions.

Five theories and M-theory

There are five consistent supersymmetric string theories — Type I, Type IIA, Type IIB, and two heterotic theories — related by dualities. In 1995 Edward Witten proposed that they are limits of a single eleven-dimensional theory known as M-theory. The absence of experimental evidence for supersymmetry or extra dimensions, and the vast "landscape" of possible vacua, are prominent criticisms of the programme.
