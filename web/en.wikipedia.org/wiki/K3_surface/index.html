K3 surface

K3 surface
Dimension: 2 (complex)
Canonical bundle: trivial
Hodge numbers: h1,1=20, h2,0=1
Named after: Kummer, Kähler, Kodaira; also Mount K2

In mathematics, a K3 surface is a complex or algebraic surface that is a simply connected, compact complex surface with trivial canonical bundle and irregularity zero. Together with two-dimensional complex tori, K3 surfaces are the Calabi–Yau manifolds of complex dimension two, and they occupy a central place in algebraic geometry, differential geometry and string theory.

History

The name "K3" was coined by André Weil in honour of three algebraic geometers — Kummer, Kähler and Kodaira — and of the mountain K2 in Kashmir, in analogy with the mountain's beauty and difficulty.

Properties

All K3 surfaces are diffeomorphic to one another. Their second cohomology lattice is the unique even unimodular lattice of signature (3, 19). A K3 surface has Euler characteristic 24, trivial fundamental group, and a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic 2-form. By Yau's proof of the Calabi conjecture, every Kähler K3 surface admits a Ricci-flat metric.

Examples

Important examples include smooth quartic surfaces in projective 3-space, Kummer surfaces constructed from abelian surfaces with an involution, and elliptic K3 surfaces with a fibration by elliptic curves.

Applications

K3 surfaces appear in string theory as compactification spaces preserving supersymmetry, in mirror symmetry, and in the study of Mathieu moonshine.
