Nutan

Nutan Samarth-Bahl (born Nutan Samarth; 4 June 1936 – 21 February 1991), known mononymously as Nutan, was an Indian actress who worked primarily in Hindi cinema. In a career spanning four decades she appeared in over 70 films and is regarded as one of the finest actresses in the history of Indian cinema. She held the record of five Filmfare Awards for Best Actress for many years, and received the Padma Shri in 1974.

Early life and career

Nutan was born in Bombay into a Marathi family, the eldest daughter of filmmaker Kumarsen Samarth and actress Shobhna Samarth. After a brief stint as a child artist and an early debut in Hamari Beti (1950), she was sent to a finishing school in Switzerland. Her breakthrough came with Seema (1955), which won her the first of her Filmfare Best Actress awards. She went on to star in a string of acclaimed films, often playing strong-willed, naturalistic characters unusual for the era: Paying Guest (1957) opposite Dev Anand, Bimal Roy's Sujata (1959), in which she played an orphaned girl of an "untouchable" caste, and Roy's Bandini (1963), frequently cited among the greatest performances in Hindi cinema, as a woman imprisoned for murder.

Later career

After marrying naval officer Rajnish Bahl in 1959, Nutan continued to work, winning further Best Actress awards for Milan (1967) and Main Tulsi Tere Aangan Ki (1978), and later moving successfully into character roles, as in Meri Jung (1985). Her son Mohnish Bahl became an actor, and her younger sister Tanuja and niece Kajol are also prominent film actresses. Nutan died of cancer in Mumbai in 1991, aged 54.
