RPK

The RPK is a Soviet light machine gun developed from the AKM assault rifle family. Its name abbreviates a Russian phrase meaning Kalashnikov hand-held machine gun. It was intended to serve as a squad automatic weapon using the same 7.62×39mm ammunition as contemporary Kalashnikov rifles.

Design

Compared with the AKM, the RPK has a longer and heavier barrel, a bipod and a reinforced receiver. It feeds from box magazines, commonly 40 rounds, or from a 75-round drum. The fixed barrel limits sustained fire compared with belt-fed machine guns with quick-change barrels, but the design is simple and shares many parts and handling characteristics with the standard rifle.

Variants and service

The later RPK-74 adapted the concept to the 5.45×39mm cartridge alongside the AK-74. RPK-type weapons have been produced and used in many countries, and they remain common in conflicts and collections because of the global spread of Kalashnikov-pattern arms.
