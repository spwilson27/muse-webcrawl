Laskaris

The Laskaris family (Greek: Λάσκαρις, later Λάσκαρης) was a Byzantine Greek noble family whose members formed the ruling dynasty of the Empire of Nicaea from 1204 to 1261 and continued to rank among the senior aristocracy of the restored Byzantine Empire under the Palaiologoi until its fall in 1453.

Origins

The family is first attested in the 11th century as low-ranking officials. Its rise came with Theodore I Laskaris, son-in-law of Emperor Alexios III Angelos, who was proclaimed emperor at Nicaea after the Fourth Crusade captured Constantinople in 1204.

Emperors of Nicaea

  • Theodore I Laskaris (r. 1205–1221) — founder of the Empire of Nicaea.
  • John III Doukas Vatatzes (r. 1221–1254), married to Theodore's daughter Irene Laskarina.
  • Theodore II Laskaris (r. 1254–1258) — scholar-emperor.
  • John IV Laskaris (r. 1258–1261) — child emperor, blinded by Michael VIII Palaiologos, who recovered Constantinople in 1261 and founded his own dynasty.

Later history

Laskarids remained prominent generals and scholars in the Palaiologan period. Branches of the family later appear in southern France — the Lascaris counts of Tende and Ventimiglia — and in Italy. The scholar Constantine Lascaris (1434–1501) taught Greek in Italy after the fall of Constantinople.
