In mathematics, a differentiable manifold is a type of manifold on which it is possible to perform calculus. A smooth manifold is a differentiable manifold for which all the transition maps between coordinate charts are infinitely differentiable (class C∞). Smooth manifolds are the central objects of differential geometry: they generalise curves and surfaces to arbitrary dimension while supporting tangent vectors, differentiation and integration.

Definition

Let M be a second-countable Hausdorff topological space. A chart on M is a pair (U, φ) where U ⊆ M is open and φ : U → ℝn is a homeomorphism onto an open subset of ℝn. Two charts (U, φ) and (V, ψ) are smoothly compatible if the transition map

ψ ∘ φ−1 : φ(U ∩ V) → ψ(U ∩ V)

is infinitely differentiable (in the ordinary sense on ℝn). An atlas is a collection of pairwise compatible charts covering M; a maximal such atlas is a smooth structure, and M together with it is a smooth manifold of dimension n.

Structures on smooth manifolds

At each point p of a smooth manifold one defines the tangent space TpM, an n-dimensional vector space of derivations; collectively these form the tangent bundle. On this foundation rest smooth maps and their differentials, vector fields, differential forms and integration (Stokes' theorem), Riemannian metrics and curvature, and Lie groups — groups that are themselves smooth manifolds with smooth operations.

Examples and exotic structures

Spheres, tori, real and complex projective spaces, Grassmannians and every open subset of ℝn are smooth manifolds. Not every topological manifold admits a smooth structure, and some admit many inequivalent ones: John Milnor (1956) found "exotic" smooth structures on the 7-sphere (28 oriented classes in total), and ℝ4 admits uncountably many exotic smooth structures — a phenomenon unique to dimension 4. The Whitney embedding theorem guarantees that every smooth n-manifold embeds in ℝ2n, so smooth manifolds can always be realised as subsets of Euclidean space.
