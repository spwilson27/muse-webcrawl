Youssou N'Dour (born 1 October 1959) is a Senegalese singer, songwriter, musician, composer, and businessman, and one of the most celebrated African musicians in history. He helped develop mbalax, Senegal's popular music genre that blends traditional Wolof drumming and singing with Afro-Cuban and Western popular styles. Rolling Stone described him as "perhaps the most famous singer alive" in Senegal and much of Africa.

Early life and career

Born in Dakar to a Serer mother from a griot tradition, N'Dour began performing as a child and joined the Star Band, the house band of Dakar's Miami Club, in his teens. In 1979 he co-founded Étoile de Dakar, and later formed his own band, Super Étoile de Dakar, whose innovative arrangements — including the tama talking drum-driven rhythms of mbalax — made him a star across West Africa.

International success

N'Dour gained international attention through collaborations with Peter Gabriel, appearing on Gabriel's album So (1986) and touring with him. His 1994 duet with Neneh Cherry, "7 Seconds", became a worldwide hit. His album The Guide (Wommat) (1994) and the Grammy Award-winning Egypt (2004), an homage to his Muslim faith recorded with an Egyptian orchestra, further cemented his reputation. He has also composed film scores, including for the animated film Kirikou and the Sorceress.

Business and politics

In Senegal, N'Dour built a substantial media and entertainment business, including the newspaper L'Observateur and the television channel TFM. In 2012 he declared his candidacy for President of Senegal; although his candidacy was ruled invalid, he supported Macky Sall and subsequently served as Minister of Tourism and Culture (2012–2013) and later as a minister-adviser to the president.
