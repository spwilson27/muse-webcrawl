Supersport World Championship

The Supersport World Championship (WorldSSP) is a motorcycle racing championship for production-based middleweight sports bikes. It runs as a support class to the Superbike World Championship and is governed by the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM), with promotion by Dorna Sports.

History

The class began in 1997 as the Supersport World Series before gaining full world championship status in 1999. Traditionally the machinery consisted of 600 cc four-cylinder machines (with larger twins and triples permitted), and manufacturers such as Honda, Yamaha, Kawasaki, Ducati and Triumph contested the title. Turkey's Kenan Sofuoğlu became the championship's most successful rider, winning five titles between 2007 and 2016.

Next Generation regulations

In 2022 the championship adopted "Supersport Next Generation" rules, admitting larger and more diverse machinery — including the Ducati Panigale V2, MV Agusta F3 800 and Triumph Street Triple 765 — with engine performance balanced by rev limits and other restrictions so that different configurations can compete together. The class has served as a proving ground for riders who later advanced to Superbike or MotoGP competition.

Format

Riders score points in two races per round, held over the same weekends as the Superbike championship, with a Superpole qualifying session determining the grid. A secondary class, the Supersport 300 World Championship, was created in 2017 for smaller entry-level machines.
