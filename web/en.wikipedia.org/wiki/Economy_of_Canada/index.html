The economy of Canada is a highly developed mixed market economy, one of the ten largest in the world by nominal GDP, and a member of the G7. Although Canada possesses vast natural resources and a major energy and mining sector, its economy is dominated by services, which employ roughly three-quarters of Canadians and generate around 70 percent of GDP. Canada is one of the world's largest exporters of energy, minerals, and agricultural products, and its trade is deeply integrated with the United States under the Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement (CUSMA/USMCA).

Key sectors

Energy and natural resources

Canada holds the world's third-largest proven crude oil reserves, concentrated in Alberta's oil sands, and is a top producer of natural gas, uranium, potash, gold, and timber. Energy products are the single largest export category. Forestry, fishing, and mining remain significant, and Canada is a leading exporter of wheat, canola, and other farm goods from the Prairie provinces.

Manufacturing and services

Manufacturing is centred in Ontario and Quebec — automotive assembly and parts (integrated with the U.S. industry around the Great Lakes), aerospace (Bombardier, CAE), and food processing are prominent. Financial services, headquartered in Toronto, real estate, healthcare, technology, and tourism dominate employment. The Bank of Canada sets monetary policy with a 2 percent inflation target, and Canada's large banks weathered the 2008 financial crisis without failures, a stability often credited to conservative regulation.

Issues

Recurring concerns include heavy dependence on U.S. demand, low business investment and productivity growth relative to the United States, high household debt and housing costs in major cities, interprovincial trade barriers, and the economic transition pressures of decarbonisation on the oil and gas sector. Immigration has driven rapid population and labour-force growth in the 2020s.
