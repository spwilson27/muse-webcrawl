Spacewatch

OrganisationUniversity of Arizona, Lunar and Planetary Laboratory
LocationKitt Peak National Observatory, Arizona
Founded1980 (by Tom Gehrels and Robert S. McMillan)
FocusSurvey of asteroids and comets, especially near-Earth objects

Spacewatch is an astronomical survey at the University of Arizona dedicated to searching for and studying minor planets, especially near-Earth asteroids and comets that could pose an impact hazard. Based at Kitt Peak National Observatory, it was founded by Tom Gehrels and Robert S. McMillan and began operating in the early 1980s.

Methods and telescopes

Spacewatch pioneered the use of CCD detectors for asteroid surveys, using drift-scan techniques with a 0.9-metre telescope and, from 2001, a 1.8-metre telescope dedicated to follow-up and discovery of faint objects. Its deep limiting magnitude allowed it to find very small near-Earth asteroids and distant Centaurs and trans-Neptunian objects.

Discoveries

The project has discovered numerous asteroids, the comet 125P/Spacewatch, and contributed observations used to determine the orbits and impact risks of potentially hazardous objects. It recovered the long-lost asteroid 719 Albert in 2000. Spacewatch data feed the Minor Planet Center, and its follow-up observations remain important complements to wide-field surveys such as Pan-STARRS and the Catalina Sky Survey.
