Vapour pressure
DefinitionPressure exerted by a vapour in equilibrium with its condensed phase
SI unitPascal (Pa)
Depends onTemperature, substance
Governed byClausius–Clapeyron relation

Vapour pressure (or equilibrium vapour pressure) is the pressure exerted by a vapour in thermodynamic equilibrium with its condensed phases (solid or liquid) at a given temperature in a closed system. It is a measure of a substance's tendency to evaporate: a liquid with a high vapour pressure at a given temperature is described as volatile.

Explanation

At any temperature, some molecules at the surface of a liquid have enough kinetic energy to escape into the gas phase (evaporation), while vapour molecules continually return by condensation. In a closed container the two rates eventually balance, and the resulting equilibrium pressure is the vapour pressure. Because escape becomes easier as molecular kinetic energies rise, vapour pressure increases strongly — roughly exponentially — with temperature.

Temperature dependence

The variation of vapour pressure with temperature is described by the Clausius–Clapeyron relation, which relates the slope of the coexistence curve to the latent heat of vaporization. In practice, the empirical Antoine equation, log10 P = A − B/(C + T), is widely used to interpolate vapour pressures of pure substances over limited temperature ranges.

Boiling point

A liquid boils when its vapour pressure equals the ambient pressure: bubbles of vapour can then form throughout the liquid rather than only escaping from the surface. This is why water boils at lower temperatures at high altitude, where atmospheric pressure is reduced, and why pressure cookers raise the boiling point. The temperature at which vapour pressure equals one standard atmosphere is the normal boiling point.

Mixtures and solutions

For ideal mixtures, Raoult's law states that each component contributes to the total vapour pressure in proportion to its mole fraction in the liquid. Dissolving a non-volatile solute lowers the vapour pressure of a solvent — one of the colligative properties. In meteorology, the saturation vapour pressure of water controls humidity, dew point, and cloud formation.
