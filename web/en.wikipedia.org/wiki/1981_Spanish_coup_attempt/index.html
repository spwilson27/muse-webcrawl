1981 Spanish coup attempt

1981 Spanish coup attempt (23-F)
Date23–24 February 1981
LocationMadrid and Valencia, Spain
Key figuresAntonio Tejero; Jaime Milans del Bosch; Alfonso Armada; King Juan Carlos I
OutcomeCoup failed; conspirators imprisoned

The 1981 Spanish coup d'état attempt, known in Spain as the 23-F for the date of its occurrence (23 February), was an attempted coup d'état in Spain by military officers opposed to the country's democratization after Franco's death. It is most famous for the televised storming of the Congress of Deputies and for the decisive intervention of King Juan Carlos I, which defeated it.

Events

On the evening of 23 February 1981, during the investiture vote for prime minister Leopoldo Calvo-Sotelo, Lieutenant Colonel Antonio Tejero led some 200 Civil Guards into the Congress, firing into the ceiling and holding the deputies hostage — an assault captured live by television cameras. Simultaneously, Captain General Jaime Milans del Bosch declared a state of emergency in Valencia and sent tanks onto its streets. The conspirators expected a new "government of national salvation", possibly under General Alfonso Armada.

The coup unravelled when Juan Carlos, as commander-in-chief, refused to support it and, at around 1 a.m., appeared on television in uniform ordering the armed forces to uphold the democratic constitution. By midday on 24 February the hostages were released and Tejero arrested.

Aftermath

Tejero, Milans del Bosch, and Armada were sentenced to 30 years' imprisonment, though all were released earlier. The failed coup is widely credited with consolidating Spanish democracy: it discredited military intervention in politics and strengthened the legitimacy of the monarchy. The images of Tejero in his tricorn hat became iconic, and 23-F has been dramatized in the novel Anatomía de un instante by Javier Cercas.
