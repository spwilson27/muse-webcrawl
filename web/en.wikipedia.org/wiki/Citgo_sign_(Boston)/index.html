Citgo sign (Boston)

The Citgo sign is a large double-faced neon sign featuring the logo of the oil company Citgo, mounted on the roof of a building at 660 Beacon Street in Kenmore Square, Boston, Massachusetts. First installed in its present form in 1965, the 60-foot-square (18 m) sign has become one of Boston's best-known landmarks.

History

A sign has stood on the site since 1940, originally advertising Cities Service, Citgo's predecessor. The current sign, bearing Citgo's red "trimark" logo, was unveiled in 1965. After the 1970s energy crisis the sign was switched off; it was relit in 1983 after a public campaign, accompanied by a famous speech from the mayor. In 2010 the sign's neon tubes were replaced with energy-efficient LEDs.

Cultural significance

The sign is visible over the left-field wall of nearby Fenway Park, and generations of Boston Red Sox fans have used it as a target: home runs hit toward it are said to "see it go" — a play on the C-I-T-G-O letters. Runners in the Boston Marathon know it as the marker one mile from the finish line. The sign has repeatedly faced threats of removal when the building beneath it changed hands, and preservation campaigns have defended it as an unofficial historic landmark.
