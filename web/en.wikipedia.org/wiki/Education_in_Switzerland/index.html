Education in Switzerland

Education in Switzerland is highly decentralised: the 26 cantons are primarily responsible for compulsory education, while the Confederation and cantons share responsibility for post-compulsory and higher education. Switzerland is consistently ranked among the world's best-performing education systems, and its vocational training model is internationally admired.

Compulsory education

Compulsory schooling lasts 11 years, usually beginning at age 4 or 5 with two years of kindergarten, followed by primary school (six years in most cantons) and lower secondary level (Sekundarstufe I), where pupils are commonly grouped by ability levels. The HarmoS concordat has harmonised structures and objectives across cantons. Instruction is in the local national language (German, French, Italian or Romansh), with a second national language and English taught as foreign languages.

Upper secondary

After compulsory school, around two-thirds of young people enter vocational education and training (VET), typically a dual apprenticeship combining work in a company with part-time school attendance, leading to a Federal VET Diploma. Others attend baccalaureate schools (Gymnasium) leading to the Maturität, which grants entry to cantonal universities and the federal institutes of technology (ETH Zürich and EPFL).

Higher education

Switzerland has 10 cantonal universities, two federal institutes of technology, universities of applied sciences (Fachhochschulen), and universities of teacher education. A system of "permeability" allows movement between vocational and academic pathways, for example via the Federal Vocational Baccalaureate.
