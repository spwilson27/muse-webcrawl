Carmen Lombardo (16 July 1903 – 17 April 1971) was a Canadian musician, singer, and composer, best known as the featured saxophonist, flautist, and vocalist of Guy Lombardo and His Royal Canadians, the immensely popular dance orchestra led by his elder brother. His soft, vibrato-rich singing was a signature of the band's sound, billed as "the sweetest music this side of heaven."

Career

Born in London, Ontario, to Italian immigrant parents, Carmen formed a band with his brothers Guy, Lebert, and Victor in the early 1920s. The Royal Canadians became one of the most successful orchestras of the big-band era, famous for their annual New Year's Eve broadcasts and their rendition of "Auld Lang Syne," a tradition that continued on American radio and television for decades.

As the orchestra's principal vocalist and a distinctive alto saxophone soloist, Carmen was heard on hundreds of recordings. He was also a successful songwriter, composing popular standards including "Powder Your Face with Sunshine (Smile! Smile! Smile!)," written with Stanley Rochinski and recorded by Dean Martin and Doris Day, as well as "Sweethearts on Parade," "Coquette" (with Guy Lombardo and Gus Kahn), and "Boo-Hoo," the last a hit for the band in 1937 with Carmen on vocal.

Later life

Carmen Lombardo remained with the Royal Canadians until illness forced his retirement; he died in Miami in 1971. He was posthumously inducted into the Canadian Songwriters Hall of Fame along with his compositions.
