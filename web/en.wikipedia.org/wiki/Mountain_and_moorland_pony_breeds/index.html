Mountain and moorland pony breeds

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Exmoor ponies, one of the mountain and moorland breeds, on their native moor.

The mountain and moorland pony breeds, often abbreviated M&M, are a group of native pony breeds of the British Isles. They are hardy, sure-footed animals adapted over centuries to the uplands and moors from which they take their names, and they form specific classes ("M&M classes") at British horse shows.

The breeds

Nine breeds are traditionally recognised, often divided into small and large types:

  • Shetland — from the Shetland Islands; the smallest and among the strongest horses for its size.
  • Exmoor — an ancient breed from Exmoor, with a distinctive "mealy" muzzle and winter coat.
  • Dartmoor — from Dartmoor in Devon, a popular children's riding pony.
  • New Forest — from the New Forest in Hampshire, where ponies still run semi-feral.
  • Welsh Mountain (Section A), Welsh Pony (Section B), Welsh Pony of Cob Type (Section C), and Welsh Cob (Section D) — the four sections of the Welsh stud book.
  • Fell — from the northern Pennines; and the closely related Dales pony from the eastern Pennines.
  • Highland — from Scotland, a strong garron type in dun and grey shades.
  • Connemara — from western Ireland, prized as a sports pony.

Characteristics

Mountain and moorland ponies typically have thick winter coats, abundant mane and tail, hard hooves, good bone, and an ability to thrive on poor grazing ("good doers"). Many populations still live semi-ferally on commons and moors, where they play a conservation-grazing role maintaining heathland habitats. Several breeds appear on the Rare Breeds Survival Trust watchlist.
