Quadriceps femoris muscle

The quadriceps femoris muscle (Latin for "four-headed muscle of the femur"), often called the quads, is a large muscle group comprising the four muscles on the front of the thigh. It is the great extensor muscle of the knee, forming a large fleshy mass that covers the front and sides of the femur. In humans it is the strongest and leanest muscle group in the body.

NerveFemoral nerve (L2–L4)
ActionsKnee extension; hip flexion (rectus femoris)

Structure

The four muscles are the rectus femoris, which runs straight down the middle of the thigh from the pelvis; the vastus lateralis, on the outer side; the vastus medialis, on the inner side; and the vastus intermedius, lying between the other vasti beneath the rectus femoris. A fifth small muscle, the articularis genus, is sometimes counted with the group. All four heads converge on the quadriceps tendon, which envelops the patella (kneecap) and continues as the patellar ligament to the tibial tuberosity.

Function

All four heads extend the leg at the knee joint — the action used in kicking, rising from a chair and climbing stairs. Because the rectus femoris crosses the hip joint as well, it also assists in flexing the thigh. The vastus medialis, particularly its oblique fibres, stabilises the patella within its groove during movement.

Clinical significance

The quadriceps are commonly injured in sport: strains ("charley horses") and contusions from direct blows, and tears of the quadriceps tendon, especially in older athletes. Strengthening the quadriceps is a mainstay of rehabilitation after knee injuries and surgery, and weakness of the vastus medialis is implicated in patellofemoral pain syndrome.
