William of Poitiers (c. 1020 – c. 1090) was a Norman priest and chronicler, chaplain to William the Conqueror and archdeacon of Lisieux. His Latin Gesta Willelmi ducis Normannorum et regis Anglorum ("Deeds of William, Duke of the Normans and King of the English") is a principal narrative source for the Norman Conquest of England.

Life and work

Born near Préaux in Normandy into a family of some standing, William trained first as a soldier before taking holy orders and studying at Poitiers — from which his byname derives. He became a ducal chaplain at the court of William of Normandy and later archdeacon of Lisieux.

His Gesta Willelmi, composed in the 1070s, covers William's career with particular detail for the years 1066–1067: the preparations for invasion, the Battle of Hastings, and the consolidation of the conquest. Although openly panegyrical — comparing the duke to Caesar — the work preserves detail found nowhere else and William appears to have had access to participants' testimony. The text survives only incomplete, ending in 1067, through quotations and later manuscripts used by Orderic Vitalis and others.
