Jibbali, also known as Shehri or Śḥerɛ̄t, is a Modern South Arabian language spoken by a minority population in the Dhofar Governorate of Oman, particularly in the mountains and coastal towns around Salalah. The name "Jibbali" derives from Arabic jabal ("mountain"), referring to its speakers as mountain people, while "Shehri" refers to the Shahra tribe.

Classification

Jibbali belongs to the Modern South Arabian branch of the Semitic languages, alongside Mehri, Harsusi, Bathari, Hobyot, and Soqotri. These languages are not descended from Arabic but are sister languages that have been spoken in southern Arabia since antiquity, and they preserve features lost in other Semitic languages.

Phonology and grammar

Like other Modern South Arabian languages, Jibbali preserves a series of glottalized/ejective consonants and lateral fricatives believed to descend from Proto-Semitic sounds. Its verbal morphology includes distinctive patterns of internal vowel change and a set of derived verb stems. The language is primarily oral and has no widely used native writing system; it is generally written, when at all, in a modified Arabic script.

Status

Jibbali is considered endangered: Arabic dominates education, media, and government in Dhofar, and younger speakers increasingly shift to Arabic. Documentation efforts by linguists in recent decades have produced grammars, dictionaries, and text collections.
