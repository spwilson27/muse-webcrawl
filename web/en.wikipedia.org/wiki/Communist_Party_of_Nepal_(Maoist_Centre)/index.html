Communist Party of Nepal (Maoist Centre)

The Communist Party of Nepal (Maoist Centre) (Nepali: नेपाल कम्युनिष्ट पार्टी (माओवादी केन्द्र)), abbreviated CPN (Maoist Centre), is a major political party in Nepal. It traces its origins to the Maoist movement that launched the Nepalese Civil War in 1996, entered mainstream politics after the 2006 Comprehensive Peace Accord, and has repeatedly led or joined coalition governments. Its long-standing chairman is Pushpa Kamal Dahal, known by his nom de guerre "Prachanda".

History

The party descends from the Communist Party of Nepal (Maoist), formed in 1994 from a split in the communist movement. In February 1996 it launched a "People's War" against the monarchy, fighting a decade-long insurgency in which around 17,000 people died. The war ended with the Comprehensive Peace Accord of November 2006, after mass protests had forced King Gyanendra to restore parliament.

In government

The Maoists emerged as the largest party in the 2008 Constituent Assembly election, and Dahal became the first prime minister of the newly declared Federal Democratic Republic of Nepal. The party subsequently went through splits, mergers and re-formations: it merged in 2009 with smaller Maoist groups as the Unified CPN (Maoist), split in 2012, and in 2016 reconstituted itself under the "Maoist Centre" name. In 2018 it merged with the CPN (UML) to form the Nepal Communist Party, but the merger was annulled by the Supreme Court in 2021, reviving the Maoist Centre. Dahal served further terms as prime minister in 2016–17 and from December 2022 to 2024, leading shifting coalition governments.

Ideology

The party professes Marxism–Leninism and the "Prachanda Path", its name for the application of Maoist thought to Nepalese conditions. Since entering electoral politics it has combined revolutionary rhetoric with pragmatic coalition-building, and it remains a leading force among Nepal's several communist parties.
