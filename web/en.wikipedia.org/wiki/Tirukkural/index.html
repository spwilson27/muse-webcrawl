The Tirukkural (Tamil: திருக்குறள், lit. "sacred verses"), or shortly the Kural, is a classic Tamil language text consisting of 1,330 short couplets, or kurals, of seven words each. The text is divided into three books with aphoristic teachings on virtue (aram), wealth (porul) and love (inbam), respectively. It is considered one of the greatest works ever written on ethics and morality, and is known for its universality and secular nature. Its authorship is traditionally attributed to Valluvar, also known in full as Thiruvalluvar.

Structure

The Kural is structured into 133 chapters, each containing 10 couplets, for a total of 1,330 couplets. The chapters are grouped into three books or parts: Book I on virtue (Aram), Book II on government and society (Porul), and Book III on love (Inbam). Book I has 38 chapters, Book II has 70 chapters, and Book III has 25 chapters. The work avoids specifics and instead presents universal principles applicable to all people in all walks of life.

Contents

The first book, Aram, lays the foundation of virtue for a householder's life, covering topics such as gratitude, self-control, avoidance of envy, doing good deeds, and non-violence. The second book, Porul, deals with worldly affairs including statecraft, administration, friendship, agriculture, and the avoidance of vices such as gambling and intoxication. The third book, Inbam, treats the psychology of love, covering the emotions of lovers before and after marriage.

Commentaries and translations

The Tirukkural has attracted numerous commentaries over the centuries, the most celebrated being that of Parimelalhagar in the 13th century. It has been translated into many languages; the first Latin translation was made by Constantius Joseph Beschi in 1730, and the first English translation was by Nathaniel Edward Kindersley in 1794. G. U. Pope's complete English translation appeared in 1886. The text is held in such esteem that it is often called the "Tamil Veda" and "the universal code of conduct".

Legacy

The Kural's influence on Tamil culture is immense. Valluvar's image is ubiquitous in Tamil Nadu, and a 133-foot statue of Thiruvalluvar stands at Kanyakumari. The Tamil Nadu government has declared the day of Thiruvalluvar (mid-January, during the Pongal festival) a public holiday. Scholars compare its ethical universalism to works of Confucius and the maxims of other ancient traditions.
