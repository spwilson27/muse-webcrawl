Dodge

Dodge is an American brand of automobiles and a division of Stellantis, based in Auburn Hills, Michigan. Dodge vehicles have historically included performance cars, and for much of its existence Dodge was Chrysler's mid-priced brand above Plymouth.

The Dodge Charger, one of the brand's best-known nameplates.

History

Founded as the Dodge Brothers Company machine shop by brothers Horace Elgin Dodge and John Francis Dodge in the early 1900s, Dodge was originally a supplier of parts for Detroit-based automakers such as Ford. The company began building its own complete automobiles in 1914, and the brand was sold to Chrysler in 1928. The Dodge brothers both died in 1920 and the company was sold by their families to Dillon, Read & Co. in 1925 before going to Chrysler.

Vehicles

Notable models include the Charger, Challenger, Dart, Ram pickup line (spun off into the Ram Trucks brand in 2010), Caravan minivan, Durango SUV, and the Viper sports car. In recent years Dodge positioned itself as a performance "muscle" brand before beginning a transition toward electrified models such as the Charger Daytona.
