Robin Li

Robin Li Yanhong
Born: 17 November 1968, Yangquan, Shanxi, China
Occupation: Co-founder and CEO of Baidu

Robin Li Yanhong (Chinese: 李彦宏; born 17 November 1968) is a Chinese software engineer and billionaire entrepreneur. He is the co-founder of the search engine Baidu, the dominant internet search company in China, and has served as its chief executive since 2004. He is also the richest man in China as of certain rankings during the 2010s, and is a member of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference.

Early life and education

Li was born in Yangquan, Shanxi province, where his parents were factory workers. He studied information management at Peking University and then pursued a master's degree in computer science at the State University of New York at Buffalo in the United States, completing it in 1994 (though he had originally planned a PhD).

Career

In the United States, Li worked at Dow Jones & Company, where he helped develop software for the online edition of The Wall Street Journal, and at the search engine company Infoseek. In 1996 he developed the RankDex algorithm, which scored web pages by the quality and quantity of their inbound links — an approach that anticipated link-analysis search engines such as Google. He patented the related "Hyperlink Analysis" technology in 1997.

Li returned to China and, with Eric Xu, founded Baidu in January 2000 in a hotel room near Peking University. Baidu grew into China's leading search engine and listed on the NASDAQ in 2005 in one of the most successful foreign IPOs of the year. Under Li, the company expanded into artificial intelligence, autonomous driving (Apollo), cloud services and the chatbot ERNIE Bot, released in 2023.

Recognition

Li has been named among Time magazine's most influential people and has served as a delegate to national political consultative bodies in China. He has advocated national policy support for artificial intelligence.
