eBay Inc. is an American multinational e-commerce company headquartered in San Jose, California. Founded by Pierre Omidyar in 1995 as AuctionWeb, it operates ebay.com, one of the first and largest online auction and shopping websites, where individuals and businesses buy and sell a huge variety of goods — new, used, collectible and refurbished. A famous early success of the dot-com era, eBay helped define online marketplaces before the rise of Amazon-style retail, and remains a leading venue for collectibles, vehicle parts and pre-owned items.

History

Omidyar wrote the AuctionWeb code over a holiday weekend in September 1995; company lore holds that one of the first items sold was a broken laser pointer, for $14.83. The site was renamed eBay in 1997 (after Omidyar's consulting firm, Echo Bay) and went public in September 1998, in one of the most successful IPOs of the dot-com boom. Meg Whitman served as CEO from 1998 to 2008, overseeing expansion abroad and the 2002 acquisition of PayPal, which became eBay's main payment processor until the two companies split in 2015. eBay also owned Skype from 2005 until selling most of it in 2009.

Operations

Sellers list items by auction or at fixed "Buy It Now" prices, paying a final-value fee on sales. Buyer protection is provided by eBay Money Back Guarantee, and high-value categories such as sneakers, watches and trading cards route items through an Authenticity Guarantee inspection. In the 2020s, under CEO Jamie Iannone, eBay refocused on enthusiast categories — collectibles, refurbished electronics, luxury goods and auto parts — while shedding non-core businesses such as its classifieds and its stake in Adevinta.
