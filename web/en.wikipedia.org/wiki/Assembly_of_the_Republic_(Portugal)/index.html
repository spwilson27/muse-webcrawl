The Assembly of the Republic (Portuguese: Assembleia da República) is the unicameral parliament of Portugal. According to the Constitution of Portugal, "the Assembly of the Republic is the representative assembly of all Portuguese citizens". It is composed of 230 deputies elected for four-year terms and sits in the São Bento Palace in Lisbon, alongside the official residence of the prime minister.

Powers

The Assembly legislates on all matters except those reserved to the government, approves the state budget and international treaties, authorises declarations of war and peace, oversees the government through questions, inquiries and motions of confidence or censure, and can propose constitutional amendments (requiring a two-thirds majority). The government is responsible to both the Assembly and the President of the Republic.

Elections and composition

Deputies are elected by proportional representation using the D'Hondt method in 22 multi-member constituencies — the 18 mainland districts, the Azores, Madeira, and two emigrant constituencies (Europe and the rest of the world). The main parties in recent legislatures have been the Socialist Party (PS), the Social Democratic Party (PSD), Chega, the Liberal Initiative, and the Left Bloc and Portuguese Communist Party on the left. The President of the Assembly of the Republic, who presides over its work, is the second figure in the Portuguese state hierarchy, after the President of the Republic.

History

The current Assembly descends from the Constituent Assembly elected in April 1975, a year after the Carnation Revolution ended the Estado Novo dictatorship; the Constituent Assembly drafted the 1976 Constitution and then converted itself into the first Assembly of the Republic.
