Bonferroni inequalities

Bonferroni inequalities
Field: Probability theory
Named after: Carlo Emilio Bonferroni
Related result: Inclusion–exclusion principle
Simplest form: Boole's inequality (union bound)

In probability theory, the Bonferroni inequalities, named after Carlo Emilio Bonferroni, are a family of bounds on the probability of a union of events obtained by truncating the inclusion–exclusion expansion. Truncating after an odd number of terms gives an upper bound on the probability of the union, while truncating after an even number of terms gives a lower bound; the bounds alternate around the true value as more terms are included.

The inequalities

Writing S1 for the sum of the probabilities of the individual events, S2 for the sum over pairs of the probabilities of intersections, and so on, the probability of the union satisfies P ≤ S1, P ≥ S1 − S2, P ≤ S1 − S2 + S3, and so on, up to the exact value given by the full inclusion–exclusion formula. The first and simplest of these, P(A1 ∪ ... ∪ An) ≤ Σ P(Ai), is also known as Boole's inequality or the union bound.

Applications

The union bound underlies the Bonferroni correction in multiple hypothesis testing, in which a significance level is divided by the number of tests to control the family-wise error rate. The inequalities are also used in combinatorics and statistical mechanics, and in derandomization arguments in theoretical computer science.
