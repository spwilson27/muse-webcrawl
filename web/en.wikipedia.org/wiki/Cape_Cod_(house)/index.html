Cape Cod (house)

Cape Cod house
OriginColonial New England
Period17th century; revived 1930s–1950s
FeaturesSteep roof, central chimney, symmetrical facade

A Cape Cod house is a low, broad, single- or one-and-a-half-story frame building with a moderately steep pitched roof, a large central chimney, and very little ornamentation. Originating in New England in the 17th century, the style was adapted by English colonists from the half-timbered houses of their homeland to withstand the stormy, cold climate of the region, taking its name from Cape Cod, Massachusetts.

Characteristics

Traditional Cape Cods have a symmetrical front facade with the door in the centre, multi-paned double-hung windows with shutters, cedar-shingle siding left to weather grey, and a floor plan centred on a large chimney that provided heat for cooking and warmth. The steep roof with side gables minimised snow load, and the low ceiling height of the upper storey helped conserve heat.

Revival

The style was revived in the 1930s and became hugely popular after the Second World War, when compact, economical Cape Cod designs — often with dormers added to increase upstairs space — were built in suburbs across the United States, including developments such as Levittown.
