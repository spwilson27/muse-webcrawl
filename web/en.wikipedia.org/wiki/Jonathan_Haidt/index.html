Jonathan David Haidt (born October 19, 1963) is an American social psychologist and author. He is the Thomas Cooley Professor of Ethical Leadership at New York University's Stern School of Business. His research examines the intuitive foundations of morality, and he is known for moral foundations theory and for popular books on psychology, politics and technology.

Career

Haidt studied philosophy at Yale and earned his PhD in psychology from the University of Pennsylvania in 1992, working with Jonathan Baron and Alan Fiske. He taught at the University of Virginia from 1995 to 2011, where his research on moral judgment — including the phenomenon of "moral dumbfounding" — challenged purely rationalist accounts of moral reasoning. His 2001 "social intuitionist model" proposed that moral judgments are typically made intuitively and justified afterwards, a view he illustrated with the metaphor of the elephant (intuition) and its rider (reasoning).

Books

His books include The Happiness Hypothesis (2006), synthesising ancient wisdom with modern psychology; The Righteous Mind (2012), which sets out moral foundations theory and analyses political division; The Coddling of the American Mind (2018), co-written with Greg Lukianoff, on campus culture; and The Anxious Generation (2024), arguing that phone-based childhoods drove an epidemic of adolescent mental illness.

Haidt co-founded Heterodox Academy, an organisation advocating viewpoint diversity in universities.
