Solicitors Disciplinary Tribunal

The Solicitors Disciplinary Tribunal (SDT) is a statutory tribunal in England and Wales that hears and determines applications and complaints concerning solicitors. It is independent of both the legal profession's regulator, the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), and its representative body, the Law Society.

Jurisdiction and powers

The Tribunal is constituted under the Solicitors Act 1974. Its members are appointed by the Master of the Rolls and include both solicitor members and lay members. Cases are usually brought before it by the SRA following investigation of allegations of professional misconduct, though applications may also be brought by members of the public in certain circumstances.

The Tribunal's powers include striking a solicitor off the Roll, suspending a solicitor from practice (for a fixed period or indefinitely), imposing a fine, reprimanding a solicitor, and ordering the payment of costs. It also hears applications for restoration to the Roll and appeals concerning certain SRA decisions. Appeals from the Tribunal lie to the High Court.

Procedure

Allegations must be proved to the civil standard of proof — the balance of probabilities — following a change from the criminal standard that took effect in 2019. Hearings are ordinarily held in public at the Tribunal's hearing centre in London, and its judgments and findings are published.

Scottish equivalent

In Scotland the corresponding body is the Scottish Solicitors' Discipline Tribunal, established under the Solicitors (Scotland) Act 1980, which considers complaints of professional misconduct referred by the Law Society of Scotland.
