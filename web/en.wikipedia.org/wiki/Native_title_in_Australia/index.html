Native title in Australia

Native title

Key case: Mabo v Queensland (No 2) (1992)

Governing law: Native Title Act 1993 (Cth)

Tribunal: National Native Title Tribunal (est. 1994)

Native title is the set of rights and interests over land and waters in Australia held by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples under their traditional laws and customs, recognized by Australian common law. Its modern recognition dates from the High Court's decision in Mabo v Queensland (No 2) (1992), which rejected the doctrine of terra nullius and held that native title survived British sovereignty where it had not been extinguished.

Legislation

Parliament responded with the Native Title Act 1993, validating past grants and creating a claims process, and after the Wik Peoples v Queensland decision (1996) — which held native title could coexist with pastoral leases — the Howard government's 1998 amendments (the "10-point plan") restricted some rights.

Claims process

Claimants must prove a continuing connection to land under traditional law and custom. Determinations cover a large share of Australia's landmass, particularly in Western Australia, the Northern Territory, and Queensland. Rights vary from non-exclusive use rights to exclusive possession.
