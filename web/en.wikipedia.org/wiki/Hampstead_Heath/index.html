Hampstead Heath

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hampstead Heath is a large ancient London heath, covering 320 hectares (790 acres). The public park sits astride a sandy ridge, one of the highest points in London, running between Hampstead to the west and Highgate to the east. It is managed by the City of London Corporation and is one of the capital's most popular open spaces.

History

The Heath was recorded in the Domesday Book as part of the manor of Hampstead. For centuries it was common land used for grazing, sand digging and laundry. Attempts to enclose and build on parts of it in the nineteenth century provoked a public campaign, leading to the Hampstead Heath Act 1871, which preserved the Heath as open space for the public. The City of London Corporation took over management in 1989.

Features

The Heath is a mosaic of woodland, grassland, heathland and ponds. Its 25 or so ponds include three open-air bathing ponds: the Men's Pond, the Ladies' Pond and the mixed pond, used by swimmers all year round. Parliament Hill, at 98 metres, offers a protected view across central London and is a popular spot for kite flying.

On the southern edge stands Kenwood House, a neoclassical villa with a notable art collection, surrounded by landscaped grounds that host summer concerts. The Heath is a Site of Metropolitan Importance for Nature Conservation and a sanctuary for wildlife, including grass snakes, muntjac deer and several species of bat.

Access

The Heath is served by Gospel Oak and Hampstead Heath stations on the London Overground, and by Hampstead, Belsize Park, Golders Green, Tufnell Park and Archway tube stations nearby.
