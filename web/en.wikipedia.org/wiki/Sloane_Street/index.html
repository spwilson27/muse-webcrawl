Sloane Street

Sloane Street is a major London street in the Royal Borough of Kensington and Chelsea, running north to south from Knightsbridge to Sloane Square, crossing Pont Street midway. Along with the adjoining Brompton Road and King's Road area, it forms one of London's principal luxury shopping districts.

History

The street takes its name from Sir Hans Sloane (1660–1753), the physician and collector who bought the manor of Chelsea in 1712 and whose collections founded the British Museum. The street was laid out in the late 18th century as the area was developed; Henry Holland built on the east side in the 1770s. The surrounding "Hans Town" development, including Sloane Square and Cadogan Square, grew from this period.

Shopping

Sloane Street is lined with flagship stores of international luxury brands — among them Chanel, Dior, Gucci, Hermès, Louis Vuitton, Prada and Cartier — and is frequently compared with Bond Street and Paris's Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré. The term "Sloane Ranger", coined in the 1980s, describes the affluent young residents stereotypically associated with the area around Sloane Square.

Buildings

Notable buildings include Holy Trinity Church, Sloane Street, a major Arts and Crafts church; the Danish and Peruvian embassies; and the Jumeirah Carlton Tower hotel at the Knightsbridge end. The Cadogan Estate remains the principal landowner.
