Hattusa

Ḫattuša / Hattusa
Location: Near Boğazkale, Çorum Province, Turkey
Role: Capital of the Hittite Empire
Flourished: c. 1650–1180 BCE
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (1986)

Hattusa (Hittite: Ḫattuša) was the capital of the Hittite Empire in the late Bronze Age, located near modern Boğazkale in north-central Turkey.

History

Settled earlier by the Hattians, the city was made capital by Ḫattušili I around 1650 BCE. At its height it covered about 180 hectares, with massive fortifications, the Great Temple, royal archives of cuneiform tablets — including the Egyptian–Hittite peace treaty after Kadesh — and gates decorated with lions, sphinxes and warriors. It was abandoned around 1180 BCE amid the Bronze Age collapse.

Archaeology

Excavations since 1906, led for decades by German archaeologists, uncovered tens of thousands of clay tablets that enabled decipherment of Hittite. The nearby rock sanctuary of Yazılıkaya is part of the World Heritage site.
