Quraysh (surah)

Quraysh is the 106th chapter (surah) of the Quran. It is a short Meccan surah of four verses named for the Quraysh, the tribe associated with Mecca and with the Prophet Muhammad. The chapter is often read together with the preceding chapter, Al-Fil, because both concern divine protection and blessing connected with Mecca.

Content

The surah reminds the Quraysh of the familiarity and security granted to their winter and summer caravans. It calls them to worship the Lord of the House, the Kaaba, who fed them against hunger and made them safe from fear. The themes are gratitude, protection and the religious centrality of Mecca.

Context

In traditional interpretation, the caravan journeys refer to the trading expeditions that sustained Meccan commerce. The chapter&rsquo;s brevity and direct address make it one of the short surahs frequently memorized and recited. Commentators discuss its relationship to the elephant narrative in Al-Fil and to the sanctity of the Meccan sanctuary.
