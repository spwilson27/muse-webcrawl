Bernard of Menthon (French: Bernard de Menthon) was a medieval Christian saint and priest who founded the hospices on the Great St Bernard and Little St Bernard passes in the Alps. The passes, the hospices, and the famous rescue dogs — the St. Bernard breed — all bear his name.

Life

Accounts of Bernard's life were written centuries after his death and differ on dates; tradition places his birth at Menthon, near Lake Annecy in Savoy, to a noble family. According to legend, his parents arranged a marriage for him, but on the eve of the wedding he fled to devote himself to the Church, eventually becoming archdeacon of Aosta in Italy.

The hospices

The Alpine passes between Italy, France and Switzerland were vital but dangerous routes for pilgrims travelling to Rome; travellers faced cold, storms, avalanches and robbers. Around the middle of the eleventh century Bernard founded hospices staffed by Augustinian canons at the summits of the two passes that now carry his name. The hospices offered free food, shelter and aid to all travellers regardless of means, and their canons, aided by large mountain dogs, searched for travellers lost in the snow. The Great St Bernard Hospice still operates today, and the pass remains one of the classic crossings of the Alps.

Veneration

Bernard was canonised by Pope Innocent XI in 1681. In 1923 Pope Pius XI named him patron saint of the Alps, and he is also regarded as patron of mountaineers and skiers. His feast day is 15 June.
