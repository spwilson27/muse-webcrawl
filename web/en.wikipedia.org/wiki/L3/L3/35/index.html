L3/35

The L3/35, also known as the Carro Veloce CV-35, was a tankette that served with the Italian Army (Esercito Italiano) before and during World War II. It was developed from the earlier CV-33, itself derived from the British Carden Loyd tankette, and entered service in 1935.

Design

The L3/35 was a two-man, lightly armoured vehicle weighing about 3.2 tonnes. The standard version was armed with twin 8 mm FIAT-Revelli Modello 1935 machine guns in a fixed forward mount, with some vehicles fitted with a single 8 mm gun instead. Armour ranged from 6 to 13.5 mm, proof only against small-arms fire, and the open-topped fighting compartment and riveted construction further limited protection. A 43 hp petrol engine gave a top speed of about 42 km/h (26 mph) on roads.

Service

Around 2,000–2,500 L3 tankettes of all variants were built, making the type one of the most numerous Italian armoured vehicles. They were used in the Second Italo-Ethiopian War, the Spanish Civil War, and throughout World War II in North Africa, East Africa, the Balkans, and on the Eastern Front, where their thin armour and machine-gun armament proved inadequate against contemporary anti-tank weapons. Variants included a flamethrower version (L3 Lf) towing a fuel trailer, command vehicles with radios, and an anti-tank version experimentally fitted with a 20 mm Solothurn rifle.

Exports

The L3 series was widely exported, with users including Austria, Brazil, Bulgaria, China, Hungary, Iraq, Spain, and Venezuela.
