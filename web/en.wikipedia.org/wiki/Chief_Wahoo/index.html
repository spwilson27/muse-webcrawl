Chief Wahoo

Chief Wahoo was a logo used by the Cleveland Indians (now the Cleveland Guardians) of Major League Baseball from 1951 to 2018. The logo was a cartoon depiction of a Native American chief with red skin, a wide grin, a single feather and a triangular nose. It drew decades of criticism for being a racist caricature of Native Americans.

History

The franchise adopted a Native American caricature as early as 1932, and the version that evolved into Chief Wahoo first appeared in 1947, drawn by 17-year-old draughtsman Walter Goldbach and commissioned by team owner Bill Veeck. A redesigned version with red skin and a feather, introduced in 1951, became the team's primary logo for more than six decades.

Controversy and removal

Native American groups and their supporters protested the logo (along with the "Indians" name) for decades, particularly during the team's postseason appearances. In 2014 the team demoted Chief Wahoo to secondary status in favour of a block "C" logo. In January 2018, Major League Baseball and the club announced that Chief Wahoo would be removed from uniforms and stadium signage after the 2018 season, with Commissioner Rob Manfred citing the league's commitment to diversity and inclusion. In 2021 the team announced it would change its name to the Cleveland Guardians, completing the transition for the 2022 season.
