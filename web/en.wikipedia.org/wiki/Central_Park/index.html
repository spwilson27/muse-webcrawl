Location: Manhattan, New York City
Area: 843 acres (341 ha)
Created: 1857–1876
Designers: Frederick Law Olmsted, Calvert Vaux

Central Park is an urban park between the Upper West Side and Upper East Side neighbourhoods of Manhattan in New York City. It is the fifth-largest park in the city, covering 843 acres (341 ha). It is the most visited urban park in the United States, with an estimated 42 million visitors annually, and is one of the most filmed locations in the world.

History

The park was established in 1857 on land acquired by the city. In 1858, landscape architects Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux won a design competition for the park with their "Greensward Plan". Construction began the same year and continued during the American Civil War; the park was essentially complete by 1876. Its construction displaced some 1,600 residents, including the occupants of Seneca Village, a predominantly African-American community. Central Park was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1963 and a New York City scenic landmark in 1974.

Features

Although the park appears natural, it is almost entirely landscaped: its lakes, ponds and woodlands were created artificially, and its terrain was shaped with gunpowder blasts and millions of cartloads of soil. Major features include the Great Lawn, the Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis Reservoir, Sheep Meadow, the Ramble and Lake, Belvedere Castle, Bethesda Terrace and Fountain, the Central Park Zoo, and the Mall, a formal promenade. The park contains 58 miles (93 km) of pedestrian paths, 36 bridges and arches, and 9,000 benches.

Management

After a period of decline in the 1970s, the park was restored under the Central Park Conservancy, a private non-profit founded in 1980, which now manages the park under contract with the city and raises the majority of its operating budget. The restoration is widely regarded as a model of public–private park management.
