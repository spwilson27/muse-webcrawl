Prithvi (missile)

Prithvi (Sanskrit for "Earth") is a family of tactical surface-to-surface short-range ballistic missiles developed by India's Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) under the Integrated Guided Missile Development Program. It was India's first indigenously developed ballistic missile and was first test-fired in 1988 from Sriharikota.

Variants

Prithvi I is an army version with a range of about 150 km and a payload of up to 1,000 kg, using liquid propulsion. Prithvi II, developed for the Indian Air Force and later the army, extends the range to roughly 250–350 km with a reduced payload and an advanced inertial navigation system. Prithvi III is a two-stage, solid-fuel naval variant with a range around 350 km; its ship-launched derivative is known as Dhanush. The missiles can carry conventional warheads, and Prithvi II is regarded as part of India's strategic forces.

Development and service

Prithvi I entered service with the Indian Army in the mid-1990s. Successive user trials have tested extended ranges and improved accuracy, including manoeuvring re-entry features in later Prithvi II tests. The family paved the way for the Agni series of longer-range ballistic missiles and for India's missile defence programme, in which Prithvi-based interceptors (the Prithvi Air Defence and Prithvi Defence Vehicle) were developed.
