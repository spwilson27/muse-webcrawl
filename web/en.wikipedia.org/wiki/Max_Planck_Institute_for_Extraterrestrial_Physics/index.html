Max Planck Institute for Extraterrestrial Physics

The Max Planck Institute for Extraterrestrial Physics (German: Max-Planck-Institut für extraterrestrische Physik, MPE) is a research institute of the Max Planck Society, located in Garching near Munich, Germany. It specialises in space-based astronomy, studying the universe in X-ray, gamma-ray, infrared and submillimetre wavelengths, as well as plasma physics and the interstellar medium.

History

The institute grew out of a working group founded in 1961 by Reimar Lüst, initially devoted to ionospheric and magnetospheric research using sounding rockets and satellites. It became an independent Max Planck Institute in 1991. Its scientists have led or contributed instruments to major missions including ROSAT (the German X-ray satellite that performed the first all-sky survey with an imaging X-ray telescope), the XMM-Newton observatory, the Fermi Gamma-ray Space Telescope, Herschel, and the eROSITA instrument aboard Spectrum-Roentgen-Gamma.

Research

MPE research groups work on the physics of the interstellar and intergalactic medium, star formation, compact objects such as neutron stars and black holes, active galactic nuclei, galaxy clusters and cosmology. The institute is internationally known for long-term infrared observations of the Galactic Center, which tracked stars orbiting the supermassive black hole Sagittarius A* — work associated with Reinhard Genzel, a director at MPE, who shared the 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics for the discovery of the supermassive compact object at the Milky Way's centre.

Facilities

The institute builds flight hardware in its own laboratories and operates instrument teams and data centres. Together with neighbouring institutes (including the Max Planck Institute for Astrophysics and the headquarters of the European Southern Observatory) it forms part of the Garching research campus, one of Europe's largest concentrations of astronomy and physics institutes.
