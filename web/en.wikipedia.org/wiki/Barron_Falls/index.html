Barron Falls

LocationBarron Gorge National Park, Queensland, Australia
RiverBarron River
Total dropApproximately 125 metres (410 ft)
Nearest townKuranda

Barron Falls (Aboriginal: Din Din) is a steep tiered cascade in the Barron Gorge National Park in Far North Queensland, Australia. The falls are formed where the Barron River descends from the Atherton Tablelands toward the coastal plain near Cairns, dropping about 125 metres in a series of cascades through a rugged gorge.

Description

For much of the year, especially in the dry season, the falls are reduced to a trickle because most of the river's flow is diverted to the Barron Gorge Hydroelectric Power Station, built in 1963, and to irrigation. During the wet season, particularly after cyclones and heavy monsoon rain, the falls become a spectacular torrent. When the river is in flood, surplus water also spills from Lake Tinaroo over the falls.

Viewing and access

The falls are a popular stop on the Kuranda Scenic Railway, which halts at Barron Falls station, where passengers can view the cascades from a platform. The Skyrail Rainforest Cableway also passes over the gorge, with a mid-station offering boardwalks to lookouts. Walking tracks on the eastern side provide views from the gorge rim.

Indigenous significance

The falls and gorge are significant to the Djabugay people, the traditional owners of the area, and feature in traditional stories of the creation of the landscape.
