Hyoscyamine

Hyoscyamine (also known as daturine or duboisine) is a naturally occurring tropane alkaloid and plant toxin. It is a secondary metabolite found in certain plants of the family Solanaceae, including henbane (Hyoscyamus niger), mandrake, jimsonweed (Datura stramonium), tomato and deadly nightshade.

Pharmacology

Hyoscyamine is the levorotatory isomer of atropine and is an anticholinergic: it blocks the action of acetylcholine at muscarinic receptors in the parasympathetic nervous system. This reduces secretions, relaxes smooth muscle of the gastrointestinal and urinary tracts, and dilates the pupils.

Medical uses

Hyoscyamine is used to treat a variety of gastrointestinal disorders including spasms, irritable bowel syndrome and peptic ulcers, and to control symptoms of bladder spasm. It may also be used to reduce secretions before surgery. The sulfate salt is the common pharmaceutical form.

Toxicity

Overdose produces the classic anticholinergic toxidrome: dry mouth, dilated pupils, blurred vision, rapid heartbeat, urinary retention, confusion and delirium. Poisoning from the plants containing it has been known since antiquity; henbane was a traditional component of anaesthetic and psychoactive preparations.
