Konica

Konica Corporation
IndustryImaging, office equipment
Founded1873 (as Konishi-ya)
HeadquartersTokyo, Japan
FateMerged with Minolta (2003)

Konica Corporation was a Japanese manufacturer of photo film, photo processing chemicals, imaging equipment and office equipment, best known for its cameras and colour film. In 2003 the company merged with Minolta to form Konica Minolta Holdings.

History

The firm's roots go back to 1873, when Rokusaburo Sugiura began trading in photographic materials in Tokyo. As Konishi Honten and later Konishi Roku, the company produced Japan's first domestically made cameras and film. In 1928 it adopted the brand name Sakura for its film, and in 1948 the company was renamed Konishiroku (Konica).

Products

Konica was known for the Hexar rangefinder camera, the Autoreflex SLR system, the Hexanon lenses, and competitive colour negative films. The company also produced photocopiers and printing equipment under the Konica brand.

Merger

In March 2003 Konica merged with the Minolta Company, creating Konica Minolta. The combined company later withdrew from the photographic market, transferring its camera service business to Sony in 2006.
