Indian Railways

Indian Railways (IR) is the state-owned railway system of India, operated by the Ministry of Railways. It runs one of the largest railway networks in the world — over 68,000 km of route — carrying more than eight billion passengers a year in recent pre-pandemic years, and is among the world's largest employers, with around 1.2 million staff.

History

The first passenger train ran between Bombay (Mumbai) and Thane in 1853. The network grew rapidly under British rule through a mix of state and guaranteed private companies, and was nationalised after independence in 1947. The system uses several gauges, with broad gauge (1,676 mm) now dominant under the Project Unigauge policy; electrification has expanded to cover most broad-gauge routes.

Operations

Indian Railways is divided into zones such as Northern, Western, Central and Southern Railway, and runs services from suburban trains and long-distance expresses — including Rajdhani, Shatabdi and Vande Bharat trains — to extensive freight operations. Its production units build locomotives and coaches, and its catering and tourism arm is IRCTC.
