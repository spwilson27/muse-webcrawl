Mutual information

In probability theory and information theory, the mutual information (MI) of two random variables is a measure of the mutual dependence between them. It quantifies the amount of information obtained about one random variable by observing the other, expressed in units such as bits (shannons) or nats.

Definition

For discrete random variables X and Y, I(X;Y) = Σx,y p(x,y) log [ p(x,y) / (p(x)p(y)) ]. Mutual information is symmetric, non-negative, and equals the reduction in the entropy of one variable given knowledge of the other: I(X;Y) = H(X) − H(X|Y). It can also be expressed as the Kullback–Leibler divergence between the joint distribution and the product of the marginals.

Applications

Mutual information is used in machine learning for feature selection, in image registration (aligning images by maximising MI), in the analysis of neural coding, and in the theory of channel capacity, where capacity is the maximum mutual information between a channel's input and output.
