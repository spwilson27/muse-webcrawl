Điện Biên Phủ

Điện Biên Phủ is a city in the northwestern region of Vietnam, the capital of Điện Biên Province. It lies in a large valley (the Mường Thanh valley) near the border with Laos, surrounded by mountains. The city is chiefly known as the site of the Battle of Dien Bien Phu in 1954, in which Việt Minh forces under General Võ Nguyên Giáp defeated the French garrison, decisively ending the First Indochina War and leading to the Geneva Accords and the partition of Vietnam.

Geography

The city occupies the floor of the Mường Thanh valley, one of the largest and most fertile valleys in Vietnam's northwest, watered by the Nậm Rốm River. The surrounding population includes Tai peoples (White Tai and Black Tai), Hmong, and Kinh (ethnic Vietnamese). Rice cultivation dominates the valley floor.

History

The area was historically a Tai muang (principality) contested by Lao, Siamese, and Vietnamese powers before French colonial rule incorporated it into French Indochina. In November 1953 French forces parachuted into the valley in Operation Castor, building a fortified airhead to interdict Việt Minh supply lines into Laos. The Việt Minh hauled artillery through the mountains and besieged the garrison from March 1954; Điện Biên Phủ fell on 7 May 1954. After the war the town grew as an administrative centre; it was elevated to city status in 2003, and Điện Biên became a separate province in 2004. Remnants of the battlefield — including strongpoints such as A1 Hill, the command bunker of Colonel de Castries, and a war museum and cemetery — are major sites of commemoration and tourism.
