International economic law

International economic law
Branch ofPublic international law
Key institutionsWTO, IMF, World Bank, ICSID
Core areasTrade, investment, monetary law, development

International economic law is the branch of public international law governing economic relations between states and the regulation of cross-border economic activity. It encompasses the law of international trade, international investment law, international monetary and financial law, and the legal regimes of international development, together with adjacent fields such as international taxation and the law of international economic organisations.

History

The modern discipline emerged from the post-1945 Bretton Woods settlement, which created the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank, and from the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (1947), succeeded in 1995 by the World Trade Organization. In the 1960s and 1970s, decolonised states pressed for a New International Economic Order through the UN General Assembly, challenging the liberal premises of the system; the discipline has since expanded to cover regional trade agreements, bilateral investment treaties and global value chains.

Main fields

Trade law centres on the WTO agreements — GATT, GATS and TRIPS — their non-discrimination principles (most-favoured-nation and national treatment) and the WTO dispute settlement system. Investment law rests on a network of some three thousand treaties protecting foreign investors, enforced largely through investor–state arbitration. Monetary law concerns the IMF's Articles of Agreement, exchange arrangements and sovereign debt. Increasingly the field engages sanctions law, digital trade, climate measures and the regulation of state-owned enterprises.

Criticism

Scholars debate whether international economic law is a coherent discipline or a loose aggregate, and critics argue that its rules constrain the policy space of developing states; defenders point to its role in promoting trade, investment and predictable dispute resolution.
