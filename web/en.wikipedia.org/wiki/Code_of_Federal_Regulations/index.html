The Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) is the codification of the general and permanent rules and regulations — sometimes called administrative law — published in the Federal Register by the executive departments and agencies of the federal government of the United States. The CFR is divided into 50 titles that represent broad areas subject to federal regulation.

The CFR is published by the Office of the Federal Register, an agency within the National Archives and Records Administration (NARA). It is updated once each calendar year on a staggered basis: titles 1–16 are revised as of January 1, titles 17–27 as of April 1, titles 28–41 as of July 1, and titles 42–50 as of October 1.

Structure

Each of the CFR's 50 titles is divided into chapters, which are usually assigned to a particular agency or bureau, and further into parts and sections. Citations take the form of title, part, and section — for example, 21 CFR 101.62 refers to Title 21 (Food and Drugs), Part 101 (Food Labeling), Section 62. The code covers subject areas including agriculture (Title 7), energy (Title 10), commerce and foreign trade (Title 15), labor (Title 29), transportation (Title 49), and wildlife and fisheries (Title 50).

History

The Federal Register Act of 1935 created the Federal Register, the daily journal of federal agency rules, and authorized the codification of existing regulations. The first edition of the Code of Federal Regulations appeared in 1938, compiling regulations in force as of June 1, 1938. A pocket-part and revised-volume system has since kept the code current.

Electronic version

The eCFR is a continuously updated, unofficial editorial compilation of the CFR maintained by the Office of the Federal Register and the Government Publishing Office. It integrates amendments from the daily Federal Register, generally within two business days, and is freely available online. The official annual edition remains the legal authority, supplemented by the Federal Register and the List of CFR Sections Affected (LSA).

Rulemaking process

Under the Administrative Procedure Act of 1946, agencies typically publish a notice of proposed rulemaking in the Federal Register, receive public comments, and then publish a final rule, which is incorporated into the CFR. Rules codified in the CFR have the force and effect of law.
