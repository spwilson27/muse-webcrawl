Diptera is the order of insects commonly called the true flies, including house flies, mosquitoes, midges, crane flies, hoverflies, blow flies and fruit flies. More than 150,000 species have been described, making it one of the largest orders of insects, and the true total is thought to be far higher.

Characteristics

The name Diptera, "two wings", refers to the order's defining feature: only the forewings are developed for flight, while the hindwings are reduced to small knobbed organs called halteres, which act as gyroscopes sensing the fly's rotation in flight. This gives flies exceptional manoeuvrability. Mouthparts are adapted for sucking and often for piercing skin or sponging up liquids.

Life cycle and ecology

Dipterans undergo complete metamorphosis: egg, larva (often called a maggot), pupa and adult. Larvae occupy an enormous range of habitats, especially moist and decaying matter. Ecologically, flies are vital as pollinators, decomposers and food for other animals. A minority are of great medical and economic importance: mosquitoes transmit malaria, dengue and other diseases, while tsetse flies transmit sleeping sickness and many species are crop and livestock pests. The fruit fly Drosophila melanogaster is a cornerstone model organism of genetics.
