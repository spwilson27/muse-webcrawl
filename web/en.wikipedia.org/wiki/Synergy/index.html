Synergy

Synergy is an interaction or cooperation giving rise to a whole that is greater than the simple sum of its parts — often summarised by the phrase "1 + 1 > 2". The term comes from the Greek synergia, "working together". The complementary negative phenomenon, in which combined elements produce less than the sum of their separate effects, is sometimes called dysergy or negative synergy.

Uses of the concept

In pharmacology and toxicology, synergy describes drugs or chemicals whose combined effect exceeds the sum of their individual effects — for example, combinations of antibiotics, or the heightened harm of alcohol combined with sedatives. In biology and systems theory, synergistic effects appear in cooperative behaviour, symbiosis, and the emergent properties of complex systems; R. Buckminster Fuller popularised the term in his writings on synergy and synergetics.

In business and management, synergy is the benefit expected when companies merge or business units cooperate — cost savings from shared functions, increased revenue from cross-selling, or combined market power. Projected synergies are a standard justification for mergers and acquisitions, though critics note they are frequently overestimated, and "synergy" became a byword for corporate jargon in the 1990s.

Related ideas

Synergy is closely related to the concept of emergence in systems science, to holism ("the whole is more than the sum of its parts", a phrase traceable to Aristotle), and to team synergy in organisational psychology.
