BASE jumping

BASE jumping is the recreational sport of jumping from fixed objects and using a parachute to descend safely to the ground. "BASE" is an acronym that stands for four categories of fixed objects from which one can jump: buildings, antennae (referring to radio masts), spans (bridges), and earth (cliffs).

Wingsuit BASE jumping from a cliff

History

The acronym was coined by filmmaker Carl Boenish, the sport's widely recognised founder, who in 1978 filmed jumps from El Capitan in Yosemite National Park. Boenish's numbering system awards a "BASE number" to jumpers who have completed jumps from all four object categories; the first, BASE 1, was awarded to Phil Smith in 1981. Boenish himself died in a cliff-jumping accident in Norway in 1984.

Technique and equipment

BASE jumps are performed from far lower altitudes than skydives from aircraft, leaving little time to react to malfunctions; jumpers therefore use a single, specially packed parachute with no reserve. Wingsuit BASE jumping, in which the jumper glides away from a cliff before deploying, developed from the late 1990s and includes the high-risk discipline of proximity flying.

Legality and risk

Because access to objects often requires trespass, BASE jumping is frequently illegal or requires permits; an exception is the annual Bridge Day event at the New River Gorge Bridge in West Virginia. The sport is among the most dangerous recreational activities, with studies estimating a fatality risk many times that of skydiving.
