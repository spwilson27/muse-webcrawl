Liubartas or Lubart (Ukrainian: Любарт; baptized Demetrius, c. 1300 – c. 1384) was the youngest son of Gediminas, Grand Duke of Lithuania. He became prince of Lutsk and ruler in Volhynia (in modern Ukraine) and, through a long career of war and diplomacy, a central figure in the Lithuanian expansion into the lands of the former Kievan Rus'.

Life

Liubartas married a daughter of Andrew of Galicia, heir of the Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia, and after Andrew's death asserted a claim to Volhynia. In the Galicia–Volhynia Wars he competed with Casimir III of Poland and Louis I of Hungary for the succession. By the treaties of the 1350s–1360s the contested lands were divided: Poland took Galicia, while Liubartas retained Volhynia with its seat at Lutsk, which he fortified and made a flourishing capital.

Rule in Volhynia

In Lutsk, Liubartas raised the great masonry castle that still bears his name, supported the Orthodox Church, and encouraged trade. Although a vassal of his brothers and nephews in Vilnius, he governed Volhynia substantially on his own, fighting the Teutonic Knights on Lithuania's behalf and negotiating with Poland and Hungary. After his death around 1384, his lands passed to his son Fiodor and were gradually absorbed into the centralizing Grand Duchy under Vytautas.

Legacy

Liubartas is remembered in Ukraine as the Lithuanian prince who became a Volhynian ruler in his own right; Lutsk Castle (Lubart's Castle) is one of Ukraine's best-preserved medieval fortresses and appears on the 200-hryvnia banknote.
