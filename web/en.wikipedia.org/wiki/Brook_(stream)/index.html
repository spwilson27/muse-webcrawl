Brook (stream)

A brook is a small stream of fresh water, typically shallow, narrow and easily fordable. The term is used especially in the United Kingdom and the north-eastern United States for a natural watercourse smaller than a river and often smaller than a creek, though the words overlap and usage is largely a matter of local custom and place-naming tradition.

A woodland brook.

Terminology

English has many regional words for small watercourses: beck in northern England, burn in Scotland and north-east England, bourne in the chalk country of the south, gill in upland districts, and rivulet, rill and run elsewhere. In hydrology, brooks are generally first- or second-order streams in the Strahler stream order — headwater channels with no significant tributaries of their own.

Ecology

Because they are shaded, cool and well oxygenated, brooks support distinctive communities of invertebrates, amphibians and fish such as the bullhead and juvenile trout, and their leaf-litter inputs fuel the food webs of larger rivers downstream. Brooks are also vulnerable to culverting, pollution and abstraction, and many urban brooks were buried during Victorian development, a few of which are now being "daylighted" again.
