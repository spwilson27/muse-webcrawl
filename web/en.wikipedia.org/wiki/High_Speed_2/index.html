High Speed 2

High Speed 2 (HS2) is a high-speed railway under construction in England. Originally planned as a Y-shaped network linking London with Birmingham, Manchester, and Leeds, the project has been repeatedly scaled back; following government decisions in 2023, only the London–Birmingham section (Phase 1) is being built. HS2 is being delivered by HS2 Ltd, a company wholly owned by the UK Department for Transport.

History

The project was announced in 2009 by the Labour government and confirmed by the coalition government in 2012. Phase 1, from London to the West Midlands, received royal assent in 2017, and main construction began in 2020. Costs rose substantially from early estimates, and in October 2023 Prime Minister Rishi Sunak announced the cancellation of all sections north of Birmingham, with funds redirected to other transport schemes under the "Network North" programme.

Route and stations

Phase 1 runs from a rebuilt London Euston station via Old Oak Common in west London to Birmingham Curzon Street, with an interchange at Birmingham Interchange near the airport. The line is designed for speeds of up to 360 km/h and will free capacity on the existing West Coast Main Line.

Criticism

HS2 has been controversial for its escalating cost, its environmental impact on ancient woodlands, and questions over its economic benefit; supporters argue it is essential for rail capacity and regional connectivity.
