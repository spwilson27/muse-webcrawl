Televisa

Grupo Televisa, S.A.B. de C.V. is a Mexican telecommunications and media company, headquartered in the Santa Fe district of Mexico City. Founded in 1973 by the merger of Telesistema Mexicano and Televisión Independiente de México, it grew — under the Azcárraga family — into the largest producer of Spanish-language content in the world, famous above all for its telenovelas, news (Noticieros Televisa), and sports coverage through channels such as Las Estrellas, Canal 5, and N+ Foro.

History

Televisa's roots lie in the radio and early television ventures of Emilio Azcárraga Vidaurreta, founder of XEW radio (1930) and of Mexico's first television station, XHTV (1950). The 1973 merger created a near-monopoly broadcaster closely aligned for decades with the governing PRI — a relationship that drew persistent criticism. Emilio Azcárraga Jean took over in 1997 and led expansion into cable and satellite. Competition from TV Azteca (from 1993) and streaming eroded its audience dominance in the 2010s.

TelevisaUnivision

In 2022 Televisa combined its content business with the U.S. Spanish-language network Univision to form TelevisaUnivision, headquartered in Mexico City and New York, in which Televisa retained the largest stake; the group operates the ViX streaming service. Televisa itself retained its cable (Izzi Telecom), publishing, gaming (PlayCity), and football interests, including Club América until a 2024 spin-off of those businesses into a separate listed vehicle.
