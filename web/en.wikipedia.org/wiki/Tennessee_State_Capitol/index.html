Tennessee State Capitol

Tennessee State Capitol
Location: Capitol Hill, Nashville, Tennessee
Architect: William Strickland
Built: 1845–1859
Architectural style: Greek Revival
Designated: National Historic Landmark (1971)

The Tennessee State Capitol, located in Nashville on top of Capitol Hill, is the seat of government for the U.S. state of Tennessee. It houses the Tennessee General Assembly and the office of the governor. Designed by architect William Strickland, it is one of the oldest working state capitols in the United States and a prominent example of Greek Revival architecture.

History

The cornerstone was laid on July 4, 1845, and construction continued until 1859. Strickland, a leading American architect, died in 1854 during construction and is entombed in the building's northeast wall; his son Francis supervised completion. The capitol was built of locally quarried limestone, partly by convict and enslaved labour. During the Civil War the building was occupied by Union forces and fortified as "Fort Johnson".

Unlike most state capitols, the Tennessee capitol has a tower rather than a dome, modelled on the Choragic Monument of Lysicrates in Athens. The grounds contain the tombs of President James K. Polk and his wife Sarah, as well as statues and memorials including equestrian statues of Andrew Jackson and memorials to Tennessee soldiers.

Design and preservation

The building was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1971. It has been repeatedly renovated while largely retaining its original appearance; a major restoration of the exterior and grounds was completed in the 2010s. Interior features include the legislative chambers, the Old Supreme Court Chamber, and the governor's office on the first floor.
