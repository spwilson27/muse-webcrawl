Humility


Allegorical representation of Humility.

Humility is the quality of being humble. In its theological sense, humility is the acceptance of one's role in the universe and before God; dictionary definitions also describe it as a low self-regard and sense of unworthiness. In a religious context, humility can mean a recognition of self in relation to a deity (i.e. God) or deities, and subsequent submission to that deity as a member of that religion.

In philosophy and religion

In Christianity, humility is considered a virtue central to spiritual life; Jesus is presented as the model of humility, and Saint Benedict made humility the foundation of his monastic Rule. Aristotle, by contrast, treated the corresponding virtue as proper pride or "greatness of soul" (megalopsychia), with humility closer to a vice of undue self-deprecation. In Buddhism, humility is cultivated through the recognition of non-self (anattā) and interdependence; in Islam, submission to God (tawadu) is a central virtue.

Psychological research

Contemporary psychology studies humility as a trait associated with accurate self-assessment, openness to new ideas, and low levels of self-focus; "intellectual humility" — recognizing that one's beliefs might be wrong — is linked to better learning and reduced polarization.
