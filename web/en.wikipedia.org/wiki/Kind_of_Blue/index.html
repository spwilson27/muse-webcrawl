Kind of Blue is a studio album by the American jazz trumpeter and composer Miles Davis, released on August 17, 1959, by Columbia Records. It was recorded in two sessions at Columbia's 30th Street Studio in New York City with a sextet featuring saxophonists John Coltrane and Julian "Cannonball" Adderley, pianists Bill Evans and Wynton Kelly, bassist Paul Chambers, and drummer Jimmy Cobb.

Background and recording

The album marked Davis's full embrace of modal jazz: rather than the dense chord changes of bebop and hard bop, the compositions are built on scales (modes), giving the soloists greater melodic freedom. Davis brought only brief sketches of scales and melody lines to the sessions, and the musicians had little rehearsal, producing the spontaneous, spacious performances heard on the record. Pianist Bill Evans, who had recently left the group but returned for these sessions, contributed composition ideas and wrote the original liner notes; he plays on all tracks except "Freddie Freeloader", which features Wynton Kelly.

Legacy

Kind of Blue is widely regarded as one of the greatest albums of all time and the best-selling jazz record ever released; it was certified quintuple platinum in the United States. In 2002 it was inducted into the National Recording Registry of the Library of Congress. Its modal approach influenced generations of jazz, rock, and classical musicians, and tracks such as "So What" and "All Blues" have become jazz standards.
