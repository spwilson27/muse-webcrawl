The Daily Show

The Daily Show is an American late-night talk and news satire television program airing on Comedy Central. Premiered on 22 July 1996, it describes itself as a "fake news" program: a host seated at a news desk delivers comic monologues on current events, supported by a team of "correspondents" who report — in character, with exaggerated, self-important personas — from the studio or in the field. It has won numerous Primetime Emmy Awards and two Peabody Awards, and is one of the most influential satire programs in American television.

The Daily Show's news-desk format spoofs cable news conventions.

History

The show debuted under Craig Kilborn with a lighter, pop-culture-focused tone. Jon Stewart took over in 1999 and refocused it on politics and media criticism, especially after the disputed 2000 election ("Indecision 2000") and during the Iraq War era; under Stewart it became a cultural and political force, credited with shaping how many younger Americans followed the news, and famous for moments such as Stewart's 2004 Crossfire appearance. Its correspondents became stars in their own right — Stephen Colbert, Steve Carell, John Oliver, Samantha Bee, Ed Helms, Rob Corddry, and others — and the show spawned spin-offs including The Colbert Report and Last Week Tonight.

Trevor Noah succeeded Stewart in 2015, bringing a younger, more international perspective, and left in December 2022. After a year of rotating guest hosts drawn from the show's correspondents and alumni, Jon Stewart returned in 2024 as a part-time host (principally Monday nights) and executive producer, with the correspondent team hosting the remaining nights.

Format and influence

A typical episode combines a headline segment, correspondent pieces, a celebrity or expert interview, and a closing "Moment of Zen" clip. Polling has repeatedly found its audience unusually well informed about public affairs, fuelling debate about satire's role in civic life; the program's election coverage brands ("Indecision", later "Democalypse") became institutions of American campaign seasons. It has won the Emmy for Outstanding Variety Talk Series ten consecutive times (2003–2012), among many honours.
