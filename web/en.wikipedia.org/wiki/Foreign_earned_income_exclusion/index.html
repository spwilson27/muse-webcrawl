Foreign earned income exclusion

Statute: Internal Revenue Code § 911
Eligibility: US citizens and resident aliens living abroad
Amount: Inflation-adjusted annually (over $120,000 in recent years)

The foreign earned income exclusion is a provision of United States tax law (26 U.S.C. § 911) that allows qualifying US citizens and resident aliens who live and work abroad to exclude a limited amount of their foreign-earned income from US federal income tax. It exists because the United States taxes its citizens on worldwide income regardless of where they live.

Qualification

A taxpayer must have a tax home in a foreign country and satisfy either the bona fide residence test (residence in a foreign country for an uninterrupted period including an entire calendar year) or the physical presence test (presence in foreign countries for at least 330 full days in a 12-month period). Only earned income — wages and self-employment income from services performed abroad — qualifies; pensions, investment income and US-source income do not.

Related provisions

Section 911 also provides a housing cost amount exclusion or deduction for qualifying housing expenses above a base amount. Taxpayers may instead, or in addition, claim the foreign tax credit for income taxes paid to another country; the same income cannot benefit from both. The exclusion is claimed on Form 2555.
