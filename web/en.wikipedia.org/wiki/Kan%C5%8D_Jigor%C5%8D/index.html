Kanō Jigorō (嘉納 治五郎, 10 December 1860 – 4 May 1938) was a Japanese educator, athlete, and martial artist, the founder of judo and founder of the Kodokan institute in Tokyo in 1882. He reformed the older jujutsu schools into a modern discipline organised around the principles of maximum efficiency (seiryoku zen'yō) and mutual welfare and benefit (jita kyōei).

Education and the Olympics

Kanō was an influential educational administrator, principal of the Tokyo Higher Normal School, and is considered a father of physical education in Japan. In 1909 he became the first Asian member of the International Olympic Committee and worked to promote the Olympic movement in Asia.

Death and legacy

Kanō died of pneumonia on the voyage home from an IOC meeting in Cairo in 1938. Judo became an Olympic sport in 1964, and 28 October, his birthday, is observed as World Judo Day.
