Caledonian Airways

Caledonian Airways was a British independent airline, founded in the 1960s, which became one of the United Kingdom's leading charter airlines before being absorbed into larger aviation groups. A later airline of the same name operated in the 1990s as a charter carrier.

Founded1961

History

The original Caledonian Airways was founded in 1961 by Adam Thomson and John de la Haye. Based initially at Prestwick Airport in Scotland, the airline began operations with a single Douglas DC-7, flying charter services for tour operators and the British military. It grew rapidly during the 1960s, acquiring jet aircraft including the Boeing 707, and in 1970 merged with British United Airways to form British Caledonian, which became the UK's second-largest airline until its takeover by British Airways in 1988.

A new company revived the name in 1995, operating charter flights from London Gatwick and other UK airports using Lockheed L-1011 TriStar and Boeing 757 aircraft. This second Caledonian Airways was later sold and eventually rebranded under the JMC Air identity following its acquisition by the Thomas Cook group.

Fleet

Over its history the airline operated types including the Douglas DC-6 and DC-7, Bristol Britannia, Boeing 707, Lockheed L-1011 TriStar, and Boeing 757.
