Moro conflict

The Moro conflict was a long-running insurgency in the Mindanao region of the Philippines, fought between the Philippine government and Moro Muslim separatist groups seeking autonomy or independence for the Bangsamoro homeland. Its modern phase is generally dated from the late 1960s, though Moro resistance to outside rule stretches back through the Spanish and American colonial periods.

Background

The Moro peoples — Muslim ethnolinguistic groups including the Tausug, Maguindanao, Maranao, and Yakan — had maintained sultanates such as Sulu and Maguindanao for centuries before Spanish colonization. They resisted Spanish, then American, rule, and were incorporated into the independent Philippine republic in 1946. In the twentieth century, government-sponsored migration of Christian settlers from Luzon and the Visayas, together with land laws that disregarded customary tenure, left many Moros dispossessed in their own homeland and fuelled grievances against Manila.

The modern insurgency

The 1968 Jabidah massacre — the alleged killing of Moro army recruits on Corregidor — is widely seen as the spark of the modern movement. Nur Misuari founded the Moro National Liberation Front (MNLF) in the early 1970s, and full-scale war under the Marcos administration killed tens of thousands. The 1976 Tripoli Agreement promised autonomy but was never fully implemented. Hashim Salamat later broke away to form the Moro Islamic Liberation Front (MILF), which pursued a more explicitly Islamic programme and became the largest rebel group.

Splinter and extremist groups complicated the picture. Abu Sayyaf, founded in the early 1990s, became notorious for kidnappings and bombings and later aligned with transnational jihadist networks; the Bangsamoro Islamic Freedom Fighters split from the MILF in 2008.

Peace process

The MNLF signed a Final Peace Agreement with the government in 1996, and Misuari became governor of the Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (ARMM), though the settlement was widely judged a failure. After years of negotiation, the MILF and the government of President Benigno Aquino III signed the Comprehensive Agreement on the Bangsamoro in 2014. Under President Rodrigo Duterte, Congress passed the Bangsamoro Organic Law in 2018, ratified by plebiscite in 2019, creating the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) with the MILF leading its transition government — widely regarded as ending the main conflict, though smaller armed groups remain active.
