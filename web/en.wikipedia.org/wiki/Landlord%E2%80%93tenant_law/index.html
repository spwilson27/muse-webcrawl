Landlord–tenant law

Landlord–tenant law is the body of law governing the rental of commercial and residential property. It is composed primarily of state statutes and common law, and it regulates the rights and duties of landlords and tenants under a lease, including rent, repairs, security deposits, privacy and eviction.

History

The landlord–tenant relationship originated in English feudal land law, where the tenant held an estate in land and the landlord's main remedy was distress for rent. Modern statutes, especially in the twentieth century, shifted the relationship toward contract law and added consumer protections for residential tenants.

Key doctrines

Important doctrines include the implied warranty of habitability, requiring landlords to maintain premises fit for human habitation; the covenant of quiet enjoyment, protecting tenants against substantial interference; restrictions on security deposits; anti-retaliation rules; and formal eviction procedures that prohibit self-help measures such as lockouts or utility shutoffs.

By jurisdiction

In the United States, landlord–tenant law is mostly state law, supplemented by local ordinances such as rent control in some cities. England and Wales regulate assured shorthold tenancies under the Housing Act 1988, while many European countries provide strong security of tenure and rent regulation.
