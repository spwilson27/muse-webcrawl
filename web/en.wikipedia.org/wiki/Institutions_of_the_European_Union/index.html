Institutions of the European Union

The institutions of the European Union are the seven principal decision-making bodies of the European Union, listed in Article 13 of the Treaty on European Union. They are distinct from the EU's advisory bodies and agencies, which are established for specific tasks.

The seven institutions

European Parliament — the directly elected legislature, sitting in Strasbourg and Brussels, which shares legislative and budgetary power with the Council. Council of the European Union — representing member state governments, meeting in configurations of ministers. European Council — the heads of state or government, which sets overall political direction. European Commission — the executive, holding the sole right to propose legislation and ensuring treaties are upheld. Court of Justice of the European Union — the judiciary based in Luxembourg. European Central Bank — in Frankfurt, responsible for monetary policy of the euro area. European Court of Auditors — which audits EU finances.

Interinstitutional balance

Most legislation is adopted by the ordinary legislative procedure, in which Parliament and Council act as co-legislators on a proposal from the Commission. The institutions are served by a common civil service and work in all 24 official languages of the Union.
