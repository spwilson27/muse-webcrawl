Greenhouse gas emissions from agriculture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Greenhouse gas emissions from agriculture are a substantial part of humanity's contribution to climate change. The agriculture and food system accounts for roughly a quarter to a third of global anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions when land use, supply chains, and consumption are included; emissions from crop and livestock production alone are about 10–14 percent of the total.

Sources

  • Methane (CH₄) from enteric fermentation in ruminant livestock and from rice paddies and manure.
  • Nitrous oxide (N₂O) from nitrogen fertilisers, manure, and agricultural soils — a gas with a global warming potential roughly 270 times that of CO₂ over 100 years.
  • Carbon dioxide from deforestation and land-use change for farming, plus energy use in machinery, fertiliser manufacture, and transport.

Mitigation

Options include feed additives that suppress methane in cattle, alternate wetting and drying in rice cultivation, precision fertiliser application, restoring degraded soils and agroforestry, reducing food waste, and shifting diets away from the most emissions-intensive products such as beef and lamb.
