The Who are an English rock band formed in London in 1964. Their classic lineup — lead singer Roger Daltrey, guitarist and songwriter Pete Townshend, bassist John Entwistle, and drummer Keith Moon — is regarded as one of the most influential rock acts of the 20th century, known for explosive live performances, the rock operas Tommy (1969) and Quadrophenia (1973), and pioneering contributions to the power chord, the Marshall stack, and synthesizer use in rock.

History

The band emerged from Shepherd's Bush and Acton, first as the Detours and briefly as the High Numbers, adopting mod imagery and the slogan "Maximum R&B." Singles such as "I Can't Explain" and "My Generation" (1965) made them stars of British pop. Townshend's guitar-smashing and Moon's frenzied drumming defined their stage act. The album The Who Sell Out (1967) parodied pirate radio; Tommy (1969), a rock opera about a deaf, dumb, and blind pinball player, brought international fame, including a celebrated performance at Woodstock. Who's Next (1971), salvaged from the abandoned Lifehouse project, included "Baba O'Riley" and "Won't Get Fooled Again" and is often ranked among the greatest rock albums.

Keith Moon died of a drug overdose in 1978 and was replaced by Kenney Jones; the band wound down in 1983. Entwistle died in 2002, on the eve of a tour. Daltrey and Townshend have continued to tour and record as the Who, releasing Endless Wire (2006) and Who (2019).

Legacy

The Who have sold over 100 million records worldwide, were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1990, and are credited with shaping hard rock, punk, and power pop.
