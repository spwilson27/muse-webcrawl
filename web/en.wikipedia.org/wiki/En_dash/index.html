En dash

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

–

The en dash, or en rule, is a punctuation mark used primarily to express ranges of values and connections or contrasts between two terms. It is traditionally half the width of an em dash and, in modern digital typography, is conventionally slightly longer than a hyphen. Its name derives from the fact that in traditional typesetting it was the width of the letter "n" in the font being used, just as the em dash is the width of the letter "m". In Unicode, the en dash is U+2013 (–), and in HTML it may be entered as &ndash;.

Usage

Ranges

The most common use of the en dash is to express an inclusive range of numbers, dates or pages, where it stands for "to" or "through": pages 112–114, the years 1939–1945, Monday–Friday. Style guides differ on whether a range introduced by "from" or "between" should use the en dash ("from 1939 to 1945", not "from 1939–1945").

Connections and compounds

An en dash joins two terms that stand in a relationship of connection, direction, opposition or collaboration: the London–Edinburgh train, the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, a 4–3 victory. It is also used in place of a hyphen in compound modifiers when one of the elements is itself an open compound or contains a space: pre–Civil War, New York–based, post–World War II. In chemistry and mathematics it appears in names and theorems joining two people, such as the Erdős–Szekeres theorem or the Dodd–Frank Act.

Other uses

In some European conventions the en dash, spaced, serves the function that the em dash serves in English, as a parenthetical break. It is also used as a minus sign in informal typography, though a true minus sign (−) is preferred in formal mathematical typesetting.

Distinguishing the dashes

  • Hyphen (-): joins words in compounds (well-known) and splits words at line breaks.
  • En dash (–): roughly the width of an "n"; used for ranges and relationships.
  • Em dash (—): roughly the width of an "m"; used for parenthetical breaks and interruptions.
  • Minus sign (−): used in mathematics, aligned with the horizontal bar of the plus sign.
  • Figure dash (‒): the width of a digit, used in phone numbers and tabular material.

Typing the en dash

On Windows, the en dash can be typed with Alt+0150 on the numeric keypad; on macOS, with Option+Hyphen. In Microsoft Word, a hyphen typed between two words with spaces is often autocorrected to an en dash. In LaTeX it is entered as --. Many publishers' house styles, including those of Wikipedia, specify the en dash for ranges and for the names of paired awards, theorems and conflicts.
