Lexical functional grammar

Lexical functional grammar (LFG) is a constraint-based grammar framework in theoretical linguistics, developed by Joan Bresnan and Ronald Kaplan. This article presents the framework under its lowercase-styled headword form; the theory is the same as that described under Lexical Functional Grammar.

Principles

The main idea of LFG is that syntactic structure is divided into constituent structure and functional structure, connected by a correspondence function. Lexical entries carry functional equations that must be satisfied by the f-structure, subject to principles of completeness, coherence and uniqueness. Unlike transformational approaches, LFG assumes a single level of syntactic representation with rich lexical information.

History

The framework emerged in the late 1970s and was presented in Bresnan's 1982 edited volume The Mental Representation of Grammatical Relations. It has since been applied to many languages and implemented computationally.
