Lord George Paulet CB (12 August 1803 – 22 November 1879) was an officer of the British Royal Navy, best known for the Paulet affair of 1843, in which, as captain of HMS Carysfort, he provisionally seized the Hawaiian Islands for the United Kingdom — an annexation that his superior officer repudiated five months later, restoring Hawaiian sovereignty.

Career

Paulet was a younger son of Charles Paulet, 13th Marquess of Winchester, and entered the navy as a boy. He served in the First Anglo-Burmese War era and sailed extensively in the Pacific, a station where British naval captains exercised wide discretionary authority in disputes involving British subjects. He was promoted captain in 1833. Later in his career he commanded other ships and rose by seniority to the rank of admiral on the retired list.

The Paulet affair

In February 1843 Paulet arrived at Honolulu in Carysfort to investigate complaints by the British consul, Richard Charlton, and other British subjects about land disputes and alleged discrimination under the government of King Kamehameha III. Paulet issued a list of demands and, when the king's response did not satisfy him, landed forces and on 25 February 1843 compelled a provisional cession of the islands to Britain, subject to the decision of the British government. A commission under Paulet governed for about five months, during which land and legal disputes festered. On 31 July 1843, Rear-Admiral Richard Darton Thomas, Paulet's commander-in-chief, arrived, disavowed the seizure, and formally restored the sovereignty of the Hawaiian Kingdom — an event still commemorated in Hawaii as Sovereignty Restoration Day (Lā Hoʻihoʻi Ea). Paulet's conduct was not formally punished, though the unauthorised annexation was disowned by London.

Later life

Paulet continued in naval service and was appointed a Companion of the Order of the Bath. He lived to the age of 76, dying in 1879. The affair that bears his name remains a standard episode in Hawaiian history and in the study of 19th-century "gunboat diplomacy".
