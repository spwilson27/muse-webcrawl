Harry Fox

Harry Fox (born Arthur Carringford; May 25, 1882 – July 20, 1959) was an American vaudeville dancer, singer, and comedian. He is credited with popularising the ballroom dance known as the foxtrot, which took its name from him.

Career

Born in Pomona, California, Fox performed in circuses and vaudeville from his teens. In 1914 he appeared at the New York Roof garden theatre with a trotting step danced with the Dolly Sisters, whom he accompanied as part of the act "Harry Fox and the Millinery Girls" / his company. His "Fox's trot" — a smooth walking step danced to ragtime music — was taken up by dance instructors Vernon and Irene Castle and codified as the foxtrot, becoming the most popular ballroom dance of the 1910s and 1920s.

Personal life and later years

Fox briefly married the vaudeville star Eva Tanguay; he was later married to Jenny Dolly of the Dolly Sisters, and subsequently to actress Beatrice Curtis. He appeared in a handful of silent films and the sound serial The Beasts of the City era productions, but his career declined with vaudeville itself. He died in Woodland Hills, California, in 1959.
