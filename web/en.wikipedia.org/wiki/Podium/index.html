Podium

A podium (plural podia or podiums) is a platform used to raise something or someone a short distance above its surroundings. The word derives from the Greek podion, a small foot or base, via Latin podium. Podiums are familiar in sports award ceremonies, orchestral conducting, public speaking, and architecture.

Sport

In sport, a podium is a stepped platform on which the top three competitors stand to receive medals or trophies, with the winner on the highest, central step, the second-place competitor on their right, and the third-place competitor on the left. Victory podiums were introduced at the 1932 Winter Olympics in Lake Placid and used at the 1932 Summer Olympics in Los Angeles, at the instigation of Lord Burghley, and have since become universal at the Olympic Games and most major championships; in motorsport the podium ceremony is accompanied by spraying champagne, a tradition popularized after the 1967 24 Hours of Le Mans. Finishing among the first three in an event is commonly described as "a podium" or "making the podium."

Speaking and performance

Conductors stand on a low podium to be visible above the orchestra. Speakers often stand behind a lectern that is frequently (though incorrectly) called a podium; strictly, a podium is the raised platform one stands on, while a lectern is the stand one speaks from. Fashion shows sometimes use an extended podium as a catwalk or runway.

Architecture

In architecture, a podium is the high base or plinth on which classical temples were raised (as in Roman temples on their podia), the projecting base of a wall or building, or—in modern usage—the low, wide block of a high-rise development from which a tower rises, often containing parking, retail, or amenities while the tower above is set back. In the architecture of ancient Rome, the podium also designated the wall enclosing the arena of an amphitheatre and the platform above it for distinguished spectators.
