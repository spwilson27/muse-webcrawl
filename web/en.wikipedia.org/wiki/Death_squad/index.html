Death squad

[Image: Memorial to victims of death squad violence in El Salvador]

A death squad is an armed group whose primary activity is carrying out extrajudicial killings or enforced disappearances as part of political repression, genocide, ethnic cleansing, or revolutionary terror. Death squads are often, though not formally, associated with the state: they may be composed of off-duty police or soldiers, sponsored by officials, or tolerated by authorities who deny responsibility, giving governments plausible deniability.

Characteristics

Victims are typically targeted for their real or suspected political views, activism, union membership, journalism, or identity. Killings are frequently carried out anonymously, with bodies displayed publicly as a warning, and are intended to terrorise a wider population into submission. Because the perpetrators act outside the law while often enjoying state protection, prosecutions are rare without political change.

Historical examples

Notorious death squads operated in Latin America during the Cold War, notably in El Salvador—where squads linked to the security forces murdered Archbishop Óscar Romero in 1980 and tens of thousands of civilians during the civil war—and in Guatemala, Argentina (during the "Dirty War"), and Chile after the 1973 coup. In Europe, the Nazi Einsatzgruppen carried out mass shootings of Jews, Roma, and others during the Second World War, murdering over a million people. Death squads also operated in the Philippines during the Duterte presidency's drug war, and in numerous other conflicts in Africa, Asia, and the Middle East.
