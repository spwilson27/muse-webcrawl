Dracula is a 1931 American pre-Code horror film directed and co-produced by Tod Browning for Universal Pictures. It is based on the 1924 stage play Dracula by Hamilton Deane and John L. Balderston, itself adapted from Bram Stoker's 1897 novel Dracula. The film stars Bela Lugosi as Count Dracula, a vampire who emigrates from Transylvania to England and preys upon the blood of living victims, including a young woman whose fiancé and a vampire hunter, Professor Van Helsing, strive to destroy him.

Production

Lugosi had played Dracula on Broadway in 1927 and campaigned for the film role, accepting a modest salary. Cinematographer Karl Freund's fluid camera work and the atmospheric sets of Dracula's castle and Carfax Abbey established much of the visual grammar of the Universal horror cycle. A Spanish-language version, directed by George Melford, was filmed simultaneously at night on the same sets with a Spanish-speaking cast led by Carlos Villarías; long considered lost, it was rediscovered in the 1970s and is now often judged the more visually dynamic of the two.

Reception and legacy

Dracula was a commercial success that effectively launched the Universal Monsters series and the American horror boom of the 1930s, and it made Lugosi a star indelibly identified with the role — his evening dress, cape and Hungarian-accented delivery defined the popular image of the Count for decades. In 2000 the film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically and aesthetically significant.
