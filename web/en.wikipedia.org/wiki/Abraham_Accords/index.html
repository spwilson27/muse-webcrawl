Abraham Accords

The Abraham Accords are a set of agreements normalising relations between Israel and several Arab states, brokered by the United States and first signed in 2020. The initial accords were concluded with the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain, followed by normalisation agreements with Sudan and Morocco. They take their name from Abraham, patriarch of the Abrahamic religions, symbolising the claimed fraternity of the signatories.

Contents

The Israel–UAE agreement, announced on 13 August 2020, was the first normalisation between Israel and a Gulf state; in return, Israel suspended its planned annexation of parts of the West Bank. Bahrain joined the framework days later, and representatives of Israel, the UAE and Bahrain signed the Abraham Accords Declaration and bilateral agreements at the White House on 15 September 2020. Sudan agreed to normalise relations in October 2020 (announced alongside its removal from the US list of state sponsors of terrorism) and signed the declaration in January 2021, though full normalisation stalled amid Sudan's internal conflicts. Morocco normalised relations in December 2020, in parallel with US recognition of Moroccan sovereignty over Western Sahara.

Reactions and significance

Supporters describe the accords as a breakthrough realigning the region around shared concern over Iran, opening trade, direct flights, tourism and technology links; the subsequent Negev Forum institutionalised cooperation. Palestinian leaders condemned the agreements as a betrayal that abandoned the Arab Peace Initiative's condition of Palestinian statehood first. The accords survived the tensions of the Gaza war that began in 2023, though expansion — notably to Saudi Arabia — was put on hold.
