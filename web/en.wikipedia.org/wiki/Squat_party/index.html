Squat party

Revellers at an unlicensed squat party in a disused industrial building.

A squat party is a party held in an abandoned or unused building that has been occupied without the permission of its owner, a practice known as squatting. Squat parties are typically unlicensed, free or cheap to enter, and are publicised by word of mouth, flyers, or phone lines giving the location only on the night. They are closely associated with the free party and rave scenes, sound system culture, and the teknival movement in Europe.

History

Unlicensed parties in occupied buildings grew out of the squatting movements of the 1970s and 1980s in cities such as London, Amsterdam, and Berlin, where vacant property was plentiful and housing activism overlapped with punk and later acid house culture. In the United Kingdom the acid house era of the late 1980s and the free festival scene brought squat parties to wide public attention, and legislation including the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 was aimed in part at suppressing them. Similar scenes developed across continental Europe, especially in France, the Netherlands, Germany, and the Czech Republic, where large multiday teknivals drew tens of thousands of people.

Organisation

Squat parties are usually organised by collectives running sound systems who locate a suitable empty warehouse, office block, or industrial unit, gain entry — sometimes by forcing a lock, sometimes through an already open access point — and set up generators, lighting, and sound equipment. Parties may last a single night or continue for days. Because they are unlicensed, organisers contend with noise complaints, police intervention, and safety risks in derelict buildings, while attendees and supporters argue that the events reclaim wasted urban space and provide free culture outside commercial nightlife.
