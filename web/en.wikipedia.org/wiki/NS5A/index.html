Nonstructural protein 5A (NS5A) is a zinc-binding and proline-rich phosphoprotein produced by the hepatitis C virus (HCV). It is cleaved from the viral polyprotein by the viral NS3/4A protease and is essential for viral RNA replication and for the assembly of new virus particles, although it has no known enzymatic activity of its own. NS5A is a major target of direct-acting antiviral drugs used to cure hepatitis C.

NS5A comprises an N-terminal amphipathic helix and three domains (I–III) separated by low-complexity sequences.

Structure

NS5A is anchored to membranes of the endoplasmic reticulum by an N-terminal amphipathic alpha-helix. The remainder of the protein consists of three domains separated by low-complexity sequences. Domain I is a structured region that coordinates a zinc ion through conserved cysteine residues and can dimerize; domains II and III are largely intrinsically disordered. NS5A exists in two major phosphorylation states, a basally phosphorylated form (p56) and a hyperphosphorylated form (p58), whose balance regulates the switch between viral replication and assembly.

Function

Within infected cells, NS5A is a component of the viral replication complex located on an induced membrane structure called the membranous web. It interacts with the viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase NS5B, other nonstructural proteins, and numerous host factors, including cyclophilin A and phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase III alpha (PI4KA). NS5A also modulates host signalling pathways and was historically associated with resistance of the virus to interferon-based therapy; an "interferon sensitivity determining region" (ISDR) in NS5A was reported to correlate with treatment response in some studies.

Role in assembly

Besides replication, NS5A helps transfer viral RNA to core protein on lipid droplets, a key step in packaging the genome into new virions. This dual role makes NS5A indispensable at two distinct stages of the viral life cycle.

NS5A inhibitors

NS5A is the target of a class of highly potent direct-acting antivirals whose precise mechanism is thought to involve blocking formation of the replication complex and virion assembly. Members of the class include daclatasvir (the first approved, in 2014), ledipasvir, velpatasvir, ombitasvir, elbasvir, and pibrentasvir. These drugs are combined with NS5B polymerase inhibitors such as sofosbuvir in regimens that cure most hepatitis C infections. Resistance-associated substitutions in NS5A, such as changes at positions 28, 30, 31, and 93, can reduce drug susceptibility.
