The Wreck of the Deutschland

"The Wreck of the Deutschland" is a long ode by English poet Gerard Manley Hopkins, written in 1875–76 and regarded as his masterpiece. Composed after a seven-year poetic silence following his conversion to Catholicism and entry into the Jesuit order, it introduced Hopkins's distinctive "sprung rhythm" and dense, alliterative style.

Background

The poem commemorates the wreck of the German steamship Deutschland in a snowstorm at the mouth of the Thames on December 6–7, 1875, in which 157 passengers and crew died. Among the dead were five Franciscan nuns exiled from Germany by the anti-Catholic Falk Laws; reports described one tall nun calling out "O Christ, Christ, come quickly." Hopkins's Jesuit superior suggested someone ought to write a poem about the disaster, which Hopkins took as permission to resume writing.

Structure and themes

The ode consists of 35 eight-line stanzas in two parts: the first ten stanzas are a personal meditation on God's mastery and Hopkins's own spiritual crisis and conversion; the remaining twenty-five retell the shipwreck, centring on the "tall nun" as a heroic witness of faith, and close with prayers for her and for England's conversion. Its language — coining words like "instress" and "inscape" in his wider work — and its sprung rhythm were radically innovative.

Publication

Rejected by the Jesuit journal The Month, the poem remained unpublished until 1918, nearly thirty years after Hopkins's death, when Robert Bridges included it in the first edition of his poems. It is now considered one of the greatest religious poems in English.
