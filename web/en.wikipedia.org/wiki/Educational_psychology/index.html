Educational psychology

Educational psychology is the branch of psychology concerned with the scientific study of human learning. The study of learning processes takes place from both cognitive and behavioural perspectives, and allows researchers to understand individual differences in intelligence, cognitive development, affect, motivation, self-regulation and self-concept, as well as their role in learning.

Scope

Educational psychologists work in schools, universities and research settings. In the United Kingdom, the term "educational psychologist" refers to a practitioner who assesses and supports children and young people experiencing barriers to learning. In North America, the field is closely related to school psychology and to research on instructional design, classroom management, testing and assessment.

History and theories

Key figures include William James, whose Talks to Teachers (1899) is an early text, Edward Thorndike, a founder of the field and of the connectionist laws of learning, and B. F. Skinner, whose behaviourism shaped programmed instruction. Jean Piaget's stages of cognitive development, Lev Vygotsky's sociocultural theory and zone of proximal development, and later cognitive and constructivist theories have all deeply influenced the discipline.
