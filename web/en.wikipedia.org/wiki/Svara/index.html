Svara

Svara (Sanskrit: स्वर, also spelled swara) is a concept in Indian classical music denoting a musical note or tone — more precisely, a pitch position within the octave, defined relative to a tonic. The seven basic svaras form the scale from which ragas are built, and the system underlies both Hindustani (North Indian) and Carnatic (South Indian) music, as well as the chant traditions from which it grew.

The seven svaras

The seven notes, sung with the solfa syllables sa re ga ma pa dha ni, are:

  • Shadja (Sa) — the tonic, immovable
  • Rishabha (Re/Ri)
  • Gandhara (Ga)
  • Madhyama (Ma)
  • Panchama (Pa) — the perfect fifth, immovable
  • Dhaivata (Dha)
  • Nishada (Ni)

Full names derive traditionally from the sounds of animals and birds: the peacock (shadja), bull (rishabha), goat (gandhara), heron or krauncha (madhyama), cuckoo (panchama), frog or horse (dhaivata), and elephant (nishada).

Variants

Besides the natural (shuddha) forms, Re, Ga, Dha, and Ni each have lowered (komal) variants, and Ma has a raised (tivra) variant, giving twelve pitch positions per octave, roughly analogous to the Western chromatic scale. Carnatic music names further variant forms (sixteen svara names in total). Unlike equal temperament, svaras are tuned by ear to the tonic and drone, often with just intonation, and classical theory describes 22 microtonal divisions (shrutis) within the octave.

Role in performance

Students learn svaras through exercises such as sarali varisai and alankaras, and sung solfa (sargam) is itself a performance element. A raga selects a subset of svaras with prescribed ascending (aroha) and descending (avaroha) patterns. Svara should be distinguished from sruti in its other sense (the drone/tonic pitch) and from nada (musical sound) generally. The word svara in Sanskrit also means a vowel or accent in language, reflecting its origin in Vedic chant, where three accents (udātta, anudātta, svarita) organised recitation.
