Burgundian Netherlands

The Burgundian Netherlands were the collection of fiefdoms in the Low Countries held by the Dukes of Burgundy of the House of Valois, and later their Habsburg heirs, between 1384 and 1482. Through marriage, inheritance, purchase, and conquest, the dukes united most of the region — Flanders, Brabant, Holland, Zeeland, Hainaut, Namur, Limburg, and Luxembourg — into a single political complex.

History

Philip the Bold acquired Flanders in 1384 through his wife Margaret III. Philip the Good greatly expanded the territories and centralised their government, summoning the first States General in 1464. Charles the Bold's ambitions ended with his death at Nancy in 1477; his daughter Mary issued the Great Privilege and married Maximilian of Habsburg, passing the lands to the Habsburgs. The period saw flourishing cities such as Bruges, Ghent, and Antwerp, and the art of the Early Netherlandish painters.

Legacy

The Burgundian union laid the groundwork for the later Seventeen Provinces and, ultimately, the modern states of Belgium, the Netherlands, and Luxembourg.
