River Parrett

River Parrett
The Parrett at Bridgwater
LocationDorset and Somerset, England
SourceThorney Mills, Chedington, Dorset
MouthBridgwater Bay, Bristol Channel
Length59 km (37 mi)

The River Parrett flows through the counties of Dorset and Somerset in South West England, from its source in the Thorney Mills springs in the hills around Chedington in Dorset. It flows north through the Somerset Levels to Bridgwater and empties into Bridgwater Bay on the Bristol Channel.

Course

From Chedington the Parrett flows north past South Perrott, Merriott and Martock to Muchelney, where it crosses the Somerset Levels. It is joined by the Isle, the Yeo and the Tone — by the River Tone at Burrowbridge — before passing Langport and Bridgwater. Below Bridgwater the river is tidal, with one of the largest tidal ranges in the world in the Bristol Channel beyond.

History and flooding

The Parrett was navigable to Langport and beyond, and Bridgwater was a significant port. The low-lying Levels around the river have flooded throughout recorded history; the winter floods of 2013–14 inundated large areas, isolating villages such as Muchelney and Thorney, and prompted extensive dredging of the Parrett and Tone and plans for a tidal barrier at Bridgwater. The river also features in the Battle of Sedgemoor (1685), fought on its floodplain near Westonzoyland.

Wildlife

The Parrett estuary and the surrounding Levels form part of the Somerset Levels and Moors Special Protection Area, important for wintering wildfowl and wading birds. The river and its rhynes (drainage channels) support eels — traditionally trapped in the Parrett — and the estuary mudflats at Bridgwater Bay are a National Nature Reserve.
