In string theory and related areas of theoretical physics, a brane is a physical object that generalises the notion of a point particle to higher dimensions. The word derives from "membrane": a 2-brane is a two-dimensional membrane sweeping out a three-dimensional world-volume, a 1-brane is a string, and a 0-brane is a point. Branes are dynamical objects that propagate through spacetime and carry energy, charge and tension.

D-branes

D-branes (Dirichlet branes), identified by Joseph Polchinski in 1995, are branes on which open strings can end. Their discovery was pivotal to the second superstring revolution: they are the carriers of Ramond–Ramond charges, their dynamics underlie gauge theories in holographic dualities such as AdS/CFT, and in M-theory they unify with M2- and M5-branes.

Braneworld cosmology

In braneworld scenarios, our observable universe is a 3-brane embedded in a higher-dimensional "bulk". Standard Model particles, being open strings, are confined to the brane, while gravity — a closed-string mode — can leak into the bulk. Models such as ADD (large extra dimensions) and the Randall–Sundrum models use this geometry to address the hierarchy problem, and "ekpyrotic" cosmologies attribute the Big Bang to a collision between branes.
