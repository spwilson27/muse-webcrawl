Church grim

The church grim most often appears as a spectral black dog

The church grim is a guardian spirit in English and Scandinavian folklore, believed to oversee a particular church and protect the churchyard from sacrilege and the Devil. In England it typically takes the form of a large black dog; in Scandinavia, where it is called the kyrkogrim (Swedish) or kirkegrim (Danish), it may also appear as a lamb, a pig, or another animal.

Origins

Folklore held that the first creature buried in a new churchyard became its guardian, bound to watch over the dead. To spare a human soul this duty, animals — often a black dog or a lamb — were said to be buried alive in the churchyard, in some accounts beneath the church's foundation or at its north side. The spirit of the buried creature then patrolled the grounds.

Beliefs

The grim was said to foretell death and disaster, tolling the church bell at midnight before a death in the parish, and to appear during storms. Seeing the church grim could be an omen, and gravediggers might encounter it while digging. Beliefs about church grims were recorded by folklorists in various parts of England and Scandinavia into the nineteenth century.

In popular culture

The church grim is an ancestor of the "Grim", the black-dog death omen in J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban. Black dog apparitions such as Black Shuck belong to the same broad tradition of British canine folklore.
