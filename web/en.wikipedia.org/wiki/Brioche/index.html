Brioche

[Image: A brioche à tête — the classic fluted loaf with a small top knot]

Overview

Brioche is a bread of French origin whose high egg and butter content gives it a rich, tender crumb and golden crust. It is classified as a viennoiserie — a yeasted baked good bridging bread and pastry. Common forms include brioche à tête (a fluted round loaf with a small crown), brioche Nanterre (a loaf-pan loaf) and braided brioche.

History and uses

Brioche is documented from the 15th century and associated especially with Normandy butter. The phrase "let them eat cake", often attributed to Marie Antoinette, was in Rousseau's telling about "brioche" and an unnamed princess — a frequently corrected misattribution. Today brioche buns are prized for burgers, and enriched doughs like panettone and challah are its cousins in other traditions.
