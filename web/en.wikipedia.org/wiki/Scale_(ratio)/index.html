Scale (ratio)

The scale of a map, model, or drawing is the ratio of a distance on the representation to the corresponding distance in reality. It is usually written as a representative fraction such as 1:50,000, meaning that one unit on the map corresponds to 50,000 of the same units on the ground.

Types of scale

A scale may be expressed as a ratio or fraction, as a verbal statement ("one inch to the mile"), or graphically as a scale bar divided into units of real distance. Large-scale representations show a small area in great detail (the ratio is comparatively large, e.g. 1:1,250), while small-scale maps cover large areas with less detail (e.g. 1:1,000,000).

Scale in models

Scale models reproduce objects at standardised ratios, such as 1:72 for aircraft kits or 1:87 (HO scale) for model railways. Because area scales as the square and volume as the cube of linear scale, scale effects matter in engineering testing, for example in wind-tunnel and towing-tank experiments.

Distortion

On maps of large regions, the nominal scale is exact only along certain lines or points because projection inevitably stretches or compresses distances elsewhere.
