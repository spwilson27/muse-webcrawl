A backing vocalist or background singer is a singer who provides vocal harmony with the lead vocalist or other backing vocalists. A backing vocalist may also sing alone as a lead vocalist for a section of a song, or provide a counter-melody. Backing vocalists are used in a wide variety of popular music, traditional music, jazz, musical theatre and opera contexts, both in the studio and on stage.

Roles and techniques

Backing parts range from holding long notes behind a chorus, to echoing phrases in call-and-response, to singing full thirds and fifths of harmony, doubling the lead at the unison or octave, and performing wordless "ooh" and "aah" textures. Lead singers sometimes pre-record their own backing vocals through multitracking, famously in the layered harmonies of Queen or the solo work of Prince, while other productions employ specialist groups. In live performance, band members — guitarists, keyboardists, drummers — frequently double as backing vocalists, a role exemplified by the harmonies of the Beatles and the Eagles.

Notable backing vocalists and culture

Many successful solo artists began as backing singers, including Whitney Houston, Mariah Carey, Sheryl Crow, Phil Collins and Luther Vandross, who arranged and sang backgrounds for acts such as David Bowie and Diana Ross. Professional session vocalists such as Darlene Love, Merry Clayton — whose duet with Mick Jagger on "Gimme Shelter" (1969) is among the most celebrated backing performances in rock — and the Andraé Crouch choir became stars within the trade. The 2013 documentary 20 Feet from Stardom, which won the Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature, profiled backing singers including Love, Clayton, Lisa Fischer and Táta Vega, and drew attention to their skill and frequent lack of recognition.
