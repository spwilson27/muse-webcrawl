Board of Deputies of British Jews

Board of Deputies of British Jews
Founded: 1760
Headquarters: London, England
Purpose: Representative body of British Jewry

The Board of Deputies of British Jews, commonly referred to simply as the Board of Deputies, is the largest and second oldest Jewish communal organisation in the United Kingdom, having been established in 1760. It is the principal representative body of British Jews, comprising deputies elected by synagogues and communal organisations.

History

The Board was founded in 1760 when Sephardi elders presented a loyal address to George III on his accession, leading to a standing committee of deputies. Ashkenazi deputies joined later, and the body was formally united in the 19th century. It played a central role in the campaign for Jewish emancipation in Britain.

Role

The Board advocates on behalf of the Jewish community with government, parliament and the media, defends religious freedoms such as circumcision and ritual slaughter, combats antisemitism, and supports Jewish education. See also British Jews and Chief Rabbi.
