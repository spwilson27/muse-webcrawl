Bus transport in the United Kingdom

Bus transport in the United Kingdom provides extensive local and long-distance services. Buses are the most used form of public transport in the country. Local bus services were deregulated under the Transport Act 1985 in Great Britain outside London, where contracts are instead franchised by Transport for London; Northern Ireland's services remain publicly operated by Translink.

History

Horse-drawn omnibuses appeared in British cities in the 1820s–30s, followed by steam buses, trolleybuses and motor buses. Municipal fleets and large territorial companies were nationalised in part after 1947 into the National Bus Company and Scottish Transport Group, then privatised in the 1980s. Major present-day operators include Stagecoach, FirstGroup, Arriva, Go-Ahead Group and National Express, which also runs long-distance coach services.

Use

Bus journeys in Great Britain number in the billions each year, though patronage outside London declined over the long term. Concessionary free travel is available to older and disabled people across the UK. Recent policy has moved toward franchising in combined-authority areas such as Greater Manchester's Bee Network.
