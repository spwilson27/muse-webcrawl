Accipiter

Accipiter is a genus of birds of prey in the family Accipitridae, comprising the goshawks and sparrowhawks. Members of the genus are found on all continents except Antarctica and are characterised by short, rounded wings and long tails adapted for fast, manoeuvrable flight through woodland, where they hunt mainly birds and small mammals.

Description

Accipiters range in size from the tiny little sparrowhawk of Africa, among the smallest of all raptors, to the northern goshawk, which can exceed 60 cm in length. Females are typically substantially larger than males — a sexual dimorphism especially marked in bird-eating species. Plumage is usually grey or brown above and barred below, and adults often have red or orange eyes. Long legs and toes with sharp talons are used to seize and kill prey.

Species

The genus traditionally contained around 50 species, including the Eurasian sparrowhawk (A. nisus), sharp-shinned hawk (A. striatus), Cooper's hawk and northern goshawk (A. gentilis). Molecular studies in the 2020s have led several checklists to split the genus, moving many species to resurrected genera such as Astur and Tachyspiza, and retaining Accipiter for a smaller core group of sparrowhawks.

Hunting and behaviour

Accipiters are typically ambush hunters, watching from a perch before a short, dashing pursuit through trees. Their diet consists mainly of small to medium-sized birds, supplemented by mammals such as squirrels and bats. They build stick nests in trees and are generally solitary outside the breeding season.
