City of Birmingham Symphony Orchestra (CBSO)

Founded: 1920
Principal hall: Symphony Hall, Birmingham
Notable chief conductors: Adrian Boult, Simon Rattle, Sakari Oramo, Andris Nelsons, Mirga Gražinytė-Tyla

The City of Birmingham Symphony Orchestra (CBSO) is a British orchestra based in Birmingham, England. It is one of the five major symphony orchestras in London and the provinces commonly grouped among the UK's leading ensembles. Its principal concert venue is Symphony Hall, Birmingham, widely regarded as one of the finest concert halls in the world for its acoustics. Since 1998 the orchestra's rehearsal and administrative home has been the CBSO Centre in Birmingham's Jewellery Quarter area.

History

The orchestra was founded in 1920 as the City of Birmingham Orchestra, giving its first concert in September 1920 under conductor Appleby Matthews, with Edward Elgar conducting a concert in November of that year. Adrian Boult served as chief conductor from 1924 to 1930, laying foundations of professional discipline. Later chief conductors included George Weldon, Rudolf Schwarz, Hugo Rignold, and Louis Frémaux.

The CBSO rose to international prominence under Simon Rattle, chief conductor from 1980 to 1998, whose tenure brought major recordings, tours, and the move to the newly opened Symphony Hall in 1991. Rattle was succeeded by Sakari Oramo (1998–2008), Andris Nelsons (2008–2015), and Mirga Gražinytė-Tyla (2016–2022). Kazuki Yamada became chief conductor and artistic advisor in 2023.

Associated ensembles

The orchestra supports the CBSO Chorus, CBSO Youth Orchestra, and CBSO Youth Chorus, and runs extensive education and community programmes across the West Midlands.
