Toyota Corolla (E80)

The Toyota Corolla (E80) is the fifth generation of the Corolla compact car, produced by Toyota from 1983 to 1987 (with some variants built until 1990). It was a major departure for the range: most models switched to front-wheel drive, while the sporty coupé versions retained rear-wheel drive. Those rear-drive coupés — the Corolla Levin and Sprinter Trueno, known by their AE86 chassis code — have become cult classics.

Design and engineering

The E80 adopted a transverse-engine, front-wheel-drive layout for the sedan, hatchback and wagon models, improving interior space and fuel economy. Engines ranged from 1.3- and 1.6-litre petrol units to a 1.8-litre diesel. A fuel-injected 1.6-litre twin-cam "4A-GE" powered the performance models.

AE86

The rear-wheel-drive AE86 Levin and Trueno coupés and hatchbacks, fitted with the 4A-GE engine, became famous in drifting and in Japanese popular culture, notably as the hero car of the manga and anime series Initial D. Their light weight, balance and limited-slip differential made them enduring favourites in grassroots motorsport.

Markets

In North America the E80 was sold as both the Corolla and, in a joint venture with General Motors, as the Chevrolet Nova, built at the NUMMI plant in Fremont, California. In Australia it was also assembled locally and sold with Holden-badged counterparts in later generations.
