Queen Elizabeth Islands

Location: Northern Canada
Area: c. 419,000 km²
Territories: Nunavut, Northwest Territories

The Queen Elizabeth Islands are the northernmost cluster of the Canadian Arctic Archipelago, lying north of Lancaster Sound and Viscount Melville Sound in Nunavut and the Northwest Territories. They cover about 419,000 km² and include Ellesmere Island, Axel Heiberg Island, and Devon Island, the largest uninhabited island on Earth.

Geography

The islands are largely covered by ice caps and glaciers and have a polar desert climate. They were formerly known as the Parry Islands. The group was renamed for Queen Elizabeth II on her coronation in 1953. Channels between the islands form part of the Northwest Passage.

Population

The population is very small, concentrated in hamlets such as Grise Fiord and Resolute on the southern fringes; most of the islands are uninhabited. Research stations and the Canadian Armed Forces station at Alert on Ellesmere Island — the northernmost permanently inhabited place in the world — are located in the group.
