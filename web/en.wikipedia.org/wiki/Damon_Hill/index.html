Damon Hill

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Damon Graham Devereux Hill, OBE (born 17 September 1960), is an English former racing driver and broadcaster who won the Formula One World Championship in 1996. He is the son of two-time world champion Graham Hill, and, with Nico Rosberg, one of only two sons of a Formula One world champion to win the title himself. Across his career from 1992 to 1999, Hill won 22 Grands Prix for Williams and Jordan.

Early career

Hill was born in Hampstead, London. After his father's death in a 1975 plane crash, the family lost most of its fortune, and Hill worked as a motorcycle courier to fund his racing. He began in motorcycle racing before switching to cars in 1984, progressing through Formula Ford, British Formula Three and International Formula 3000. In 1991 he became test driver for Williams, while also racing sports cars.

Formula One

Hill made his Grand Prix debut with the struggling Brabham team in 1992. For 1993 he was promoted to the Williams race seat alongside Alain Prost and won three races. Following Ayrton Senna's death at Imola in 1994, Hill became team leader and took the championship fight with Michael Schumacher to the final round in Adelaide, losing by one point after a controversial collision.

After another runner-up finish in 1995, Hill won eight races in 1996 to secure the World Championship—but Williams declined to renew his contract. He joined the uncompetitive Arrows team for 1997, nearly winning in Hungary, then moved to Jordan, where he took the team's first victory at the 1998 Belgian Grand Prix. He retired at the end of 1999.

Later life

Hill served as president of the British Racing Drivers' Club from 2006 to 2011, overseeing the redevelopment of Silverstone, and worked as a Formula One pundit for Sky Sports from 2012. His son Josh also raced in junior single-seater categories.
