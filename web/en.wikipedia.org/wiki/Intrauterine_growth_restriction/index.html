Intrauterine growth restriction

Intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR), also called fetal growth restriction (FGR), refers to poor growth of a fetus while in the mother's womb during pregnancy. It is commonly defined as an estimated fetal weight below the 10th percentile for gestational age, with pathological restriction distinguished from constitutionally small but healthy fetuses (small for gestational age).

Causes

IUGR may result from maternal factors (hypertension and pre-eclampsia, malnutrition, smoking, alcohol, infections such as cytomegalovirus and rubella), placental factors (placental insufficiency, abruption, abnormal cord insertion), and fetal factors (chromosomal abnormalities such as trisomy 18, congenital anomalies, multiple gestation). Growth restriction is described as symmetric when all measurements are reduced proportionally — usually reflecting an early insult — and asymmetric when head growth is relatively spared, typically from later placental insufficiency diverting blood flow to the brain.

Diagnosis and management

Screening uses fundal height measurement, with ultrasound biometry and Doppler studies of the umbilical and middle cerebral arteries to assess severity and fetal well-being. There is no effective in-utero treatment; management centres on surveillance and timing of delivery, balancing the risks of prematurity against stillbirth. Affected newborns face higher risks of hypoglycaemia, hypothermia and, in later life, metabolic and cardiovascular disease — an association central to the developmental origins of health and disease hypothesis.
