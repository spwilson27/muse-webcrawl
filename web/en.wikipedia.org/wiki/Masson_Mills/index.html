Masson Mills

Masson Mills is a cotton mill complex at Matlock Bath, Derbyshire, England, built in 1783 by Richard Arkwright as part of his pioneering factory-based system of textile production. Powered by the River Derwent, it is one of the mills of the Derwent Valley Mills World Heritage Site and is regarded as one of the best-preserved of Arkwright's mills, having worked continuously for over 200 years.

History

Arkwright built Masson Mill in 1783, close to his earlier Cromford Mill, taking advantage of the strong and reliable flow of the Derwent below Matlock Bath. The mill used his water frame spinning machinery and was powered by a single waterwheel; in 1801 the sluice and weir arrangements were improved. Masson was unusual in having a relatively small fall of water and used a breastshot wheel. The mill complex grew during the 19th century with additional buildings and later steam power supplementing water power.

Present day

Textile production at the site continued into the modern era under English Sewing Cotton. The mill now houses a shopping village, a restaurant, and the Masson Mills Working Textile Museum, which displays historic textile machinery, including looms and spinning equipment, and a collection of bobbins. The site forms part of the Derwent Valley Mills UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 2001.
