Aaron

Aaron (Hebrew: אַהֲרֹן, ʾAhărōn) is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, the elder brother of Moses and Miriam, and the first high priest of the Israelites. According to the Book of Exodus, Aaron served as Moses's spokesman before Pharaoh and was consecrated, with his sons, as the founder of the hereditary priesthood (the Aaronic priesthood, or Kohanim). He appears prominently in the Torah and is also mentioned in the New Testament and the Quran (as Harun).

Biblical narrative

Aaron, a Levite three years older than Moses, was appointed his "prophet" or spokesman (Exodus 7:1) because Moses protested that he was slow of speech. Together they confronted Pharaoh before the Exodus from Egypt. In the wilderness, Aaron fashioned the golden calf at the people's demand while Moses was on Mount Sinai — an act for which he was rebuked but forgiven. He was consecrated high priest with elaborate vestments, including a breastplate bearing the Urim and Thummim, and his sons Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar were ordained priests; Nadab and Abihu died after offering "strange fire".

Aaron's authority was confirmed by the miracle of his rod budding with almonds (Numbers 17). With Moses, he was barred from entering the Promised Land after striking the rock at Meribah. Aaron died on Mount Hor at the age of 123, and his son Eleazar succeeded him (Numbers 20).

In later tradition

In Judaism, Aaron is remembered as a peacemaker, "a lover of peace and pursuer of peace" (Pirkei Avot). In Christianity he is a type of Christ's priesthood, and the "order of Aaron" is contrasted with the order of Melchizedek. In Islam, Harun is a prophet and Moses's assistant and brother.
