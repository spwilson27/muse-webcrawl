Gideon Gono

Gideon Gono (born 29 November 1959) is a Zimbabwean banker and businessman who served as Governor of the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe from 2003 to 2013, under President Robert Mugabe. His tenure coincided with one of the worst episodes of hyperinflation in history, during which the Zimbabwean dollar collapsed and was abandoned in 2009. Gono was a controversial figure, praised by the government for keeping the state financed amid sanctions and economic crisis, and criticised by economists and the opposition for quasi-fiscal operations and money-printing that fuelled inflation.

Early career

Gono began his career as a tea boy and clerk, rising through banking and finance. He worked at the Zimbabwe Development Bank and became managing director of the Commercial Bank of Zimbabwe (CBZ, "Jewel Bank"), building a reputation as an energetic deal-maker close to the ruling ZANU–PF establishment. He also served in university administration, as chairman of the University of Zimbabwe council. In November 2003 Mugabe appointed him central bank governor with a public mandate to turn the economy around.

Governorship and hyperinflation

Gono's Reserve Bank undertook extensive quasi-fiscal activities — funding farm mechanisation, fuel and food imports, parastatals and subsidised credit schemes — financed substantially by printing money. Zimbabwe's inflation spiralled: official figures reached 231 million percent in July 2008, while independent estimates put peak monthly inflation in November 2008 in the billions of percent, among the highest ever recorded. The bank issued ever-larger denominations, culminating in the 100 trillion dollar note, and repeatedly redenominated the currency. Gono blamed sanctions, drought and speculation, and defended his policies in his 2008 book Zimbabwe's Casino Economy. After the 2009 unity government adopted a multi-currency regime, the Zimbabwean dollar was abandoned and inflation collapsed; Gono remained governor until his term ended in 2013.

Later life

Gono was placed on United States and European Union sanctions lists during his governorship (the EU removed him in 2012). In 2014 he was controversially disqualified from taking a Senate seat for Manicaland over residency requirements. He has since pursued farming and business interests and has occasionally commented on economic policy.
