Zhegalkin normal form

The Zhegalkin normal form, also called the algebraic normal form (ANF) or Reed–Muller expansion, is a way of representing a Boolean function as a polynomial over the two-element field, using XOR (addition modulo 2) as addition and AND as multiplication. It is named after the Russian mathematician Ivan Zhegalkin, who introduced the representation in 1927.

Definition

Any Boolean function f(x1, …, xn) can be written uniquely as a sum (mod 2) of products of subsets of its variables, plus possibly the constant 1. For example, the function f = x ∨ y (OR) has the Zhegalkin form f = x ⊕ y ⊕ xy, and implication x → y has the form 1 ⊕ x ⊕ xy.

Properties

The representation is unique for each Boolean function, which makes it useful for checking functional equivalence. A function is affine (linear plus constant) exactly when its Zhegalkin form contains no product of two or more variables. The form is widely used in cryptography, where the degree of the polynomial (the "nonlinearity" of the function) is a measure of resistance to linear cryptanalysis, and in coding theory through the related Reed–Muller codes.
