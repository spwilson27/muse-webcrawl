Chunyun (Chinese: 春运, "Spring transport") is the annual period of exceptionally heavy travel in China around the Chinese New Year (Spring Festival). It is often described as the largest periodic human migration on Earth: hundreds of millions of migrant workers, students and families travel from the cities where they work back to their hometowns, then return, over a roughly 40-day season.

Scale and causes

The phenomenon grew with China's internal labour migration: workers employed in coastal manufacturing and construction centres traditionally return home for the New Year reunion dinner, the most important family occasion of the year. Total journeys across all modes of transport have reached roughly three billion in peak years. Rail travel dominates media attention, with tickets historically hard to obtain and stations and trains severely crowded.

Management

The Chinese government treats chunyun as a major logistical operation: extra trains and flights are scheduled, real-name ticketing and online booking (via the 12306 platform) were introduced to combat scalping, and the expanding high-speed rail network has substantially relieved pressure since the 2010s. Road travel, including long-distance buses and private cars, now accounts for the majority of journeys. The 2020 chunyun was disrupted by the COVID-19 outbreak, and subsequent years saw official encouragement of staggered or local celebrations before travel rebounded.
