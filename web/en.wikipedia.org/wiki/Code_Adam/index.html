Code Adam

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Code Adam is a missing-child safety programme used by retail stores, theme parks, and other public venues in North America. When a child is reported missing, staff announce "Code Adam" with a description of the child; employees monitor and temporarily hold the exits, search the premises, and, if the child is not found within about ten minutes, call law enforcement.

Origins

The programme was created by Walmart in 1994 and named in memory of Adam Walsh, the six-year-old son of John Walsh who was abducted from a Florida department store in 1981 and murdered; the case led to the creation of the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children. The procedure was widely adopted across the retail industry and is now a common element of store safety training.

Procedure

Staff obtain the child's description, broadcast the alert, position employees at every exit to observe departing customers, and search areas where a child might hide or wander. Children found are reunited with the reporting adult only after verification. Variants of the code are used in hospitals, libraries, and government buildings.
