Brevibacterium linens

Brevibacterium linens
Domain: Bacteria; Phylum: Actinomycetota
Found on: human skin; washed-rind cheeses
Notable trait: orange-red pigment; methanethiol production

Brevibacterium linens is a bacterium found on human skin, where it contributes to body odour, and on the surface of many cheeses, where it is responsible for the characteristic orange colour and pungent aroma of washed-rind varieties.

Cheese ripening

Cheesemakers smear young cheeses such as Limburger, Époisses, Munster, and Taleggio with brine containing B. linens. The bacterium tolerates salt, breaks down proteins, and produces sulfur compounds including methanethiol that give these cheeses their strong smell.

Biology

It is a Gram-positive, rod-shaped actinobacterium that produces carotenoid pigments. On skin it metabolises sweat components; it is rarely pathogenic, though infections have been reported in immunocompromised patients.

Similar organisms

Modern sequencing shows cheese rinds host a community of related brevibacteria and corynebacteria, and some strains once called B. linens have been reclassified.
