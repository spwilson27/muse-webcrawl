Boris Yeltsin

Boris Nikolayevich Yeltsin
Born: 1 February 1931, Butka, USSR
Died: 23 April 2007, Moscow
President of Russia: 1991–1999

Boris Yeltsin (1931–2007) was a Soviet and Russian politician who served as the first President of the Russian Federation from 1991 to 1999.

Rise

A construction engineer from Sverdlovsk, he rose in the Communist Party, became Moscow party chief under Mikhail Gorbachev, then broke with him as a reformer. Elected president of the Russian SFSR in June 1991, he famously defied the August 1991 hardline coup from atop a tank outside the White House.

Presidency

Yeltsin oversaw the dissolution of the Soviet Union (Belovezha Accords, December 1991), launched "shock therapy" market reforms and mass privatisation amid economic collapse and the rise of oligarchs, fought the 1993 constitutional crisis (shelling parliament), and waged the First Chechen War. Re-elected in 1996 despite ill health and alcohol problems, he resigned on 31 December 1999, naming Vladimir Putin his successor. See Russia, Vladimir Putin.
