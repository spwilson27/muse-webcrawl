Windsor County is a county located in the U.S. state of Vermont. As of the 2020 census, the population was approximately 57,753. The county seat is the town of Woodstock. The county is named after Windsor, Connecticut, the home of one of its first settlers.

Windsor County is the largest county in Vermont by land area, covering the east-central part of the state along the Connecticut River, which forms its eastern border with New Hampshire.

The Green Mountains in Windsor County

History

Windsor County was created on February 22, 1781, from land that had been part of Cumberland County in the independent Vermont Republic. The town of Windsor, founded in 1761, was where the constitution of Vermont was signed in 1777, making the county central to the state's early history. The county's economy historically rested on agriculture, timber, and small-scale manufacturing, including the machine-tool industry along the Connecticut River.

Geography

According to the U.S. Census Bureau, the county has a total area of about 977 square miles, of which roughly 969 square miles are land. The Green Mountains run through the western portion of the county, and the Connecticut River defines the eastern edge. Prominent peaks include Killington Peak, just over the county line, and Okemo Mountain near Ludlow.

Communities

Towns in Windsor County include Woodstock, Hartford, Springfield, Windsor, Hartland, Ludlow, Chester, Norwich, Royalton, Bethel, Randolph, and Weathersfield. The town of Hartford contains the village of White River Junction, a historic railroad hub and the county's largest population center.
