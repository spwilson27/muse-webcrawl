Paris (Greek: Πάρις), also known as Alexander, was a prince of Troy in Greek mythology, son of King Priam and Queen Hecuba. His abduction of Helen, wife of Menelaus of Sparta, provoked the Trojan War. In the Judgement of Paris he awarded the golden apple to Aphrodite, earning her patronage and Helen's love, but the enmity of Hera and Athena.

Life

Before his birth Hecuba dreamed she bore a firebrand that burned Troy; the infant Paris was therefore exposed on Mount Ida, but survived, suckled by a bear and raised by the herdsman Agelaus. Recognised at funeral games for his prowess, he was restored to the royal house. Paris then judged the beauty contest of the goddesses, sailed to Sparta, and carried off Helen (and Spartan treasure) in violation of guest-friendship.

The Trojan War

In the Iliad Paris is depicted as a handsome, skilled archer but a reluctant and somewhat effeminate duellist; Hector repeatedly reproaches him. He fights Menelaus in single combat in Book 3 and is rescued by Aphrodite. Later tradition credits Paris with killing Achilles by an arrow to the heel, guided by Apollo. Paris himself was mortally wounded by Philoctetes; taken to Mount Ida, he begged the healing of his first wife, the nymph Oenone, who refused; she repented too late and killed herself on his funeral pyre.
