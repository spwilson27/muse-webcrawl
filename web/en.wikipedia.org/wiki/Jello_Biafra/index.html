Jello Biafra (born Eric Reed Boucher; June 17, 1958) is an American singer, spoken-word performer and political activist, best known as the lead singer and songwriter of the San Francisco punk rock band Dead Kennedys. He founded the independent label Alternative Tentacles in 1979, ran for mayor of San Francisco that same year, and later became a prominent spoken-word artist and Green Party figure. His stage name combines the gelatin dessert brand Jell-O with Biafra, the short-lived secessionist state in Nigeria whose name became associated with famine.

Dead Kennedys

Biafra formed Dead Kennedys in 1978 with guitarist East Bay Ray. The band's debut single "California Über Alles" (1979) satirised Governor Jerry Brown, and the album Fresh Fruit for Rotting Vegetables (1980) included "Holiday in Cambodia" and "Kill the Poor". Biafra's lyrics combined sardonic humour with attacks on the religious right, consumer culture and Cold War politics; "Nazi Punks Fuck Off" (1981) rebuked violence in the punk scene. His run for mayor of San Francisco in 1979, promising such things as requiring businessmen to wear clown suits, won over 6,000 votes and fourth place. In 1979 he co-founded Alternative Tentacles, which released the band's records in the United States and became an influential independent label.

Obscenity trial and activism

In 1986 Los Angeles prosecutors charged Biafra with distributing "harmful matter to minors" over a poster by H. R. Giger included in the album Frankenchrist — the first such prosecution of a musician over album artwork in the United States. The jury deadlocked in 1987 and the charge was dismissed; the trial bankrupted the label's finances but made Biafra a leading free-speech campaigner, and he testified against music censorship associated with the PMRC. Dead Kennedys had split in 1986 amid disputes that later produced litigation: in 2000 a court awarded control of the band's catalogue to his former bandmates, and the revived band has toured without him.

Later career

Biafra has released a long series of spoken-word albums of political commentary, collaborated with bands including Ministry (in Lard), the Melvins, Nomeansno and Mojo Nixon, and fronted Jello Biafra and the Guantanamo School of Medicine. In 2000 he contested the Green Party's presidential nomination, finishing second to Ralph Nader; he remains a prominent voice of the American punk-informed left.
