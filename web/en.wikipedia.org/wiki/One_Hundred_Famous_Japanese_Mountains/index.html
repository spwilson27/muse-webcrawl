One Hundred Famous Japanese Mountains

The One Hundred Famous Japanese Mountains (日本百名山, Nihon Hyaku-meizan) is a list of celebrated peaks compiled by the mountaineer and writer Kyūya Fukada in his 1964 book of the same name. Fukada selected mountains he had personally climbed, weighing their grace, history, and individuality, and generally restricting the list to peaks of 1,500 metres or higher, with a few exceptions such as Mount Tsukuba.

The list

The hundred mountains are distributed across Japan from Hokkaido (including Mount Rishiri and Mount Meakan) to Yakushima in the south (Mount Miyanoura). The tallest and most revered is Mount Fuji (3,776 m); others include Mount Kita, Mount Hotaka, Mount Yari, Mount Tate, Mount Haku, Mount Asama, Mount Zaō, and Mount Daisetsu. Climbing all one hundred — "hyaku-meizan" completion — became a popular lifetime goal in Japan, inspiring television documentaries, guidebooks, and a lasting hiking boom. Later lists of 200 and 300 famous mountains extended the concept.
