Australian rules football, also called Australian football or Aussie rules, or more simply footy, is a contact sport played between two teams of 18 players on an oval field, often a modified cricket ground. Points are scored by kicking the oval ball between the central goal posts (worth six points) or between a central and outer post (worth one point, called a behind).

Players may position themselves anywhere on the field and use any part of their bodies to move the ball. The primary methods are kicking, handballing and running with the ball. There are rules on how the ball can be handled: for example, a player running with the ball must intermittently bounce or touch it on the ground, throwing the ball is not allowed, and the ball must not be held for too long. If a player takes possession of the ball after kicking it more than 15 metres, a "mark" is awarded and the player may take an unimpeded kick. Tackling is permitted above the knees and below the shoulders.

History

The sport was invented in Melbourne, Victoria, in 1859 by Tom Wills, who sought a game to keep cricketers fit during winter. The Melbourne Football Club published the first laws of the game in May 1859, predating the codification of association football in England. The game spread throughout Australasia and the world, becoming especially dominant in the southern Australian states of Victoria, South Australia, Western Australia and Tasmania.

The Victorian Football League (VFL), founded in 1896, broke away from the Victorian Football Association and eventually became the national Australian Football League (AFL). The AFL Grand Final, held annually at the Melbourne Cricket Ground, regularly draws crowds approaching 100,000 spectators.

Laws of the game

The game is played on an oval field generally 135 to 185 metres long and 110 to 155 metres wide. Four goal posts stand at each end: two taller goal posts in the middle, flanked by two shorter behind posts. A goal, worth six points, is scored when the ball is kicked through the goal posts by the attacking team without touching a player or the post. A behind, worth one point, results when the ball passes between a goal post and a behind post, touches a post, or is carried or forced through.

Matches consist of four quarters of 20 minutes of playing time, with the clock stopped for stoppages. A game begins with a centre bounce, in which the umpire bounces the ball in the centre circle and the opposing ruckmen contest it.

Skills and positions

Players are named in traditional positions such as full-forward, centre half-back, wing, rover and ruck, though modern tactics involve fluid movement across zones. Distinctive skills include the drop punt, the torpedo punt, the banana kick (checkside), handballing by punching the ball with a clenched fist, and the high-flying spectacular mark.

Popularity

Australian rules football has the highest spectator attendance and television viewership of any sport in Australia. It is also played at amateur level in many countries, including Nauru, where it is the national sport, and Papua New Guinea. A women's national league, AFL Women's (AFLW), was launched in 2017.
