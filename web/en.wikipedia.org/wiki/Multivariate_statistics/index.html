Multivariate statistics


A scatterplot matrix, a common multivariate visualisation.

Multivariate statistics is a subdivision of statistics encompassing the simultaneous observation and analysis of more than one outcome variable — that is, multivariate random variables. Multivariate statistics concerns understanding the different aims and background of each of the different forms of multivariate analysis, and how they relate to each other.

Principal methods

  • Principal component analysis (PCA) — dimension reduction via orthogonal components of maximum variance.
  • Factor analysis — modelling observed variables in terms of latent factors.
  • Multivariate regression and MANOVA — multiple responses modelled jointly.
  • Discriminant analysis and cluster analysis for classification and grouping.
  • Canonical correlation analysis and multidimensional scaling.

Theory

Much of classical multivariate theory is built on the multivariate normal distribution and the Wishart distribution for sample covariance matrices; Hotelling's T² generalises Student's t-test to several dimensions. Modern applications range from genomics and finance to psychometrics and machine learning, where high-dimensional data are routine.
