Georges Desvallières (14 May 1861 – 5 October 1950) was a French painter and a central figure in the revival of modern sacred art in France. He co-founded the Salon d'Automne in 1903 and, with Maurice Denis, founded the Ateliers d'art sacré in 1919 to train artists in the service of the Church.

Early career

Descended from a family of artists, Desvallières studied at the Académie Julian and the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris, where his teachers included Tony Robert-Fleury and William Bouguereau, and he formed an important friendship with Gustave Moreau. His early work explored mythological, literary and darkly sensual themes in a Symbolist vein, and he became a prominent figure in Parisian exhibiting societies, helping to organise the first Salon d'Automne as a freer alternative to the official Salon.

War and religious art

Although in his fifties, Desvallières served as an infantry officer in the First World War in the Vosges; his son Daniel was killed at the front in 1915. The experience deepened the religious conversion of his maturity, and his later painting was devoted almost entirely to sacred subjects, war memorials and the commemoration of the dead, including the stained glass of the ossuary chapel at Douaumont. In 1919 he and Maurice Denis established the Ateliers d'art sacré (Ateliers of Sacred Art), which sought to renew Christian art with the language of modern painting and trained a generation of church artists and decorators.

Legacy

Desvallières was elected to the Académie des Beaux-Arts in 1930. His work, long overshadowed by the avant-gardes he helped to exhibit, has been reassessed in modern exhibitions as a distinctive bridge between Symbolism, modernism and devotional art.
