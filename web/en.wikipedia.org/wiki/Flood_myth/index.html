Flood myth

A flood myth or deluge myth is a narrative in which a great flood, usually sent by a deity, destroys civilisation, often in an act of divine retribution. Survivors in a vessel repopulate the world. Flood myths appear in many cultures worldwide and are among the most widespread of all mythological themes.

Flood myth
Examples: Noah, Gilgamesh, Atrahasis, Deucalion, Manu
Motifs: Divine wrath, ark, survivor, renewal

Mesopotamian and biblical accounts

The Sumerian flood story, the Akkadian Atrahasis and the flood episode in the Epic of Gilgamesh (hero Utnapishtim) predate and closely parallel the Genesis story of Noah’s Ark, in which God floods the earth and Noah preserves his family and pairs of animals. Scholars link these traditions and debate memories of real Mesopotamian river floods.

Other traditions

Greek myth tells of Deucalion; Hindu tradition of Manu saved by the fish Matsya; Chinese myth of Gun and Yu controlling the waters; and flood tales occur in Mesoamerican, African, Australian Aboriginal and Pacific traditions.

Interpretations

Explanations range from memories of post-glacial sea-level rise and local river or coastal floods to symbolic readings about chaos, punishment and rebirth. Comparative mythology studies their diffusion and independent invention.
