United States v. Windsor

United States v. Windsor, 570 U.S. 744 (2013), was a landmark decision of the U.S. Supreme Court holding that Section 3 of the Defense of Marriage Act (DOMA), which defined marriage for federal purposes as between a man and a woman, was unconstitutional under the Fifth Amendment.

United States v. Windsor
Decided: June 26, 2013
Citation: 570 U.S. 744
Decision: 5–4
Majority: Kennedy, joined by Ginsburg, Breyer, Sotomayor, Kagan

Background

Edith Windsor and Thea Spyer, a New York couple, married in Canada in 2007. When Spyer died in 2009, Windsor owed $363,053 in federal estate taxes because DOMA prevented recognition of their marriage and thus the spousal exemption. She sued for a refund.

Decision

Justice Anthony Kennedy's majority opinion held DOMA deprived same-sex couples legally married under state law of liberty protected by the Fifth Amendment's Due Process Clause, noting its purpose and effect were to disparage and injure those the state sought to protect.

Impact

The ruling required federal recognition of lawful same-sex marriages and paved the way for Obergefell v. Hodges (2015), which legalized same-sex marriage nationwide.
