The ATF gunwalking scandal concerned a series of operations run by the United States Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives between 2006 and 2011 in which agents allowed licensed firearms dealers to sell weapons to illegal straw buyers, hoping to trace the guns to Mexican drug cartels. In the largest operation, Operation Fast and Furious, run by the ATF's Phoenix Field Division, roughly 2,000 firearms were sold; many were lost, and hundreds were recovered at crime scenes in Mexico and the United States.

Exposure

The scandal broke after US Border Patrol agent Brian Terry was killed in December 2010 in a shootout near Rio Rico, Arizona; two rifles traced to Fast and Furious were recovered at the scene. ATF whistleblowers, notably agent John Dodson, disclosed the tactic to Congress and the media. Attorney General Eric Holder was held in contempt of Congress in 2012 — the first sitting attorney general so cited — over the Justice Department's refusal to release documents, which the administration shielded under executive privilege.

Findings

A 2012 report by the DOJ Inspector General found serious failures by ATF and DOJ officials and recommended discipline for 14 individuals; ATF's acting director resigned amid the fallout. Mexico's government protested that it had not been informed, and the episode remains a touchstone in debates over firearms policy and federal law-enforcement oversight.
