William Holabird (11 September 1854 – 19 July 1923) was an American architect noted for his work with the Chicago School of architecture. A graduate of West Point, he worked in the office of William Le Baron Jenney before founding, with Martin Roche, the firm Holabird & Roche in 1881 (the partnership began as Holabird & Simonds, then Holabird, Simonds & Roche).

Work

Holabird & Roche became one of the most prolific firms in Chicago's rebuilding and commercial expansion. The Tacoma Building (1889, demolished 1929) was an early skyscraper using riveted steel framing with terra-cotta cladding. Other major works include the Marquette Building (1895), the Republic Building, numerous Chicago hotels and office blocks, and, after Holabird's death under successor leadership (Holabird & Root), the Chicago Board of Trade Building (1930). The firm's large, regularly spaced "Chicago windows" became a hallmark of the commercial style.
