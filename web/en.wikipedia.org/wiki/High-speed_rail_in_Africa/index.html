High-speed rail in Africa

High-speed rail in Africa refers to railway services in Africa operating at speeds of 200 km/h or more. Africa's first true high-speed line opened in Morocco in 2018, making Morocco the first African country with high-speed rail. Several other countries including Egypt, Algeria, South Africa and Nigeria have planned or are constructing high-speed lines.

Morocco – Al Boraq

The Tangier–Kenitra high-speed line, branded Al Boraq, opened on 15 November 2018. It is 186 km long, part of a planned 350 km Tangier–Casablanca route, and trains reach 320 km/h, reducing Tangier–Casablanca journey time from 4h45 to about 2h10. It was built with French technical assistance and uses Alstom Euroduplex-derived trainsets. Plans exist to extend south to Marrakech and Agadir.

Egypt

Egypt is constructing a network of about 2,000 km of electrified high-speed lines built by Siemens Mobility and partners, sometimes called a "Suez Canal on rails". Lines will link Ain Sokhna on the Red Sea to Alexandria and Marsa Matruh on the Mediterranean, Cairo to Abu Simbel, and Luxor to Hurghada, with Velaro trainsets designed for desert conditions.

Other projects

Algeria has upgraded lines and studied high-speed corridors. South Africa has long debated a Johannesburg–Durban high-speed line and Gautrain is a rapid transit link but not true high-speed. Nigeria, Kenya, Ethiopia and Libya have proposed lines, often with Chinese financing, though most remain at feasibility stage due to cost and gauge issues.

Challenges

Obstacles include high construction costs, differing track gauges, electricity supply, maintenance capacity, and competition from road and air transport.
