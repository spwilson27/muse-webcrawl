Boeing AH-64 Apache

AH-64 Apache

Role: Attack helicopter

Manufacturer: Hughes Helicopters / McDonnell Douglas / Boeing

First flight: 30 September 1975

Introduced: April 1986

Status: In service

Primary user: United States Army

Armament: 30 mm M230 chain gun, AGM-114 Hellfire missiles, Hydra 70 rockets

The Boeing AH-64 Apache is an American twin-turboshaft attack helicopter with a tailwheel-type landing gear arrangement and a tandem cockpit for a crew of two. It features a nose-mounted sensor suite for target acquisition and night vision systems, and is armed with a 30 mm (1.18 in) M230 chain gun carried between the main landing gear, under the aircraft's forward fuselage, and four hardpoints mounted on stub-wing pylons for carrying armament and stores, typically AGM-114 Hellfire missiles and Hydra 70 rocket pods.

Development

The Apache began as the Model 77 developed by Hughes Helicopters for the United States Army's Advanced Attack Helicopter program to replace the AH-1 Cobra. The prototype YAH-64 first flew in 1975 and was selected over Bell's YAH-63 in 1976. Production began in 1982, and the type entered service in 1986. The AH-64D Apache Longbow, equipped with a distinctive mast-mounted AN/APG-78 fire-control radar, added the ability to detect, classify, and engage targets from behind cover. The latest AH-64E Apache Guardian version, with more powerful engines and improved avionics, began deliveries in 2011.

Operational history

Apaches first saw combat during the 1989 invasion of Panama, then famously opened the air campaign of the 1991 Gulf War by destroying Iraqi early-warning radar sites. They have since served extensively in the Balkans, Afghanistan, and Iraq, and are operated by numerous allied air forces including those of the United Kingdom (as the AgustaWestland Apache), the Netherlands, Israel, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, South Korea, and India.
