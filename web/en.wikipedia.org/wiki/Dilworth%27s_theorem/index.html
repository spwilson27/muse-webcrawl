Dilworth's theorem

Dilworth's theorem is a result in order theory and combinatorics, named after mathematician Robert P. Dilworth, who published it in 1950. It states that in any finite partially ordered set, the size of a minimum chain decomposition equals the size of a maximum antichain.

Statement

A chain is a subset of a poset in which every two elements are comparable; an antichain is a subset in which no two elements are comparable. Dilworth's theorem asserts that if the largest antichain has size k, then the poset can be partitioned into k chains, and no fewer. It is closely related to Mirsky's theorem and to the König–Egerváry theorem in graph theory, and underlies results such as the Erdős–Szekeres theorem on monotonic subsequences.
