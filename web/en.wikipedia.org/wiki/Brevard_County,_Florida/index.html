Brevard County is a county in the U.S. state of Florida, located on the Atlantic coast in the east-central part of the state. Its county seat is Titusville and its largest city is Palm Bay. The county is widely known as Florida's Space Coast, because it is home to the Kennedy Space Center and Cape Canaveral Space Force Station.

History

The area was inhabited for thousands of years by Indigenous peoples, including the Ais and Timucua. Brevard County was established in 1854 and named for Theodore Washington Brevard, a Florida state comptroller. The arrival of the space program in the 1950s and 1960s transformed the county, bringing rapid population growth around Cape Canaveral; all crewed NASA launches to the Moon departed from the county.

Geography

The county stretches about 72 miles (116 km) north to south along the Atlantic coast but is only about 26 miles wide. It includes barrier islands, the Indian River Lagoon — one of the most biologically diverse estuaries in North America — and the St. Johns River on its western boundary. Merritt Island separates the lagoon from the ocean and hosts the space center.

Economy

Aerospace and defence are major employers, with companies supporting launches by NASA, SpaceX and United Launch Alliance. Tourism is also significant, centred on beaches such as Cocoa Beach, the cruise port of Port Canaveral, and visitors drawn by rocket launches.
