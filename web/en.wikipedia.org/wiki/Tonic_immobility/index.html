Tonic immobility

Tonic immobility is a natural state of paralysis that animals enter, often called animal hypnosis or feigning death. It may occur when an animal faces a predator it cannot escape, and is thought to reduce the chance of being eaten, since many predators lose interest in prey that appears dead.

Tonic immobility has been observed in many species, including chickens, rabbits, lizards, sharks, and insects. It can be induced by restraint — for example, gently holding a chicken on its back — after which the animal remains still, unresponsive but conscious, for seconds to minutes. In sharks, some species enter the state when inverted, a fact used by researchers and by orcas, which flip rays and sharks to immobilise them.

In humans

A similar involuntary paralysis and inability to speak or move has been reported in humans during extreme trauma, and researchers have studied tonic immobility as a response in survivors of assault. It is distinct from voluntary freezing or playing dead, being an automatic defensive reaction of the nervous system.
