Abortion debate

The abortion debate is a longstanding controversy over the moral, legal and religious status of induced abortion. Its central questions include the moral status of the embryo and fetus, the rights of the pregnant person, the proper role of the state, and the consequences of restricting or permitting abortion. Positions are commonly grouped under the labels pro-life (favouring restrictions or prohibition) and pro-choice (favouring legal access), though most public opinion falls between the poles, typically supporting legality in early pregnancy and restrictions later.

Arguments

Opponents of abortion generally argue that human life, and with it a right to life, begins at conception or at some developmental milestone such as implantation, viability or the onset of a heartbeat, and that abortion is therefore a form of killing that the state may prohibit. Supporters of legal abortion emphasise bodily autonomy — the principle that no person can be compelled to sustain another with their body — and argue that personhood begins later, that criminalisation endangers women's health without eliminating abortion, and that access is a matter of equality and socioeconomic freedom. Religious traditions differ: the Roman Catholic Church and many evangelical churches oppose abortion in nearly all cases, while many mainline Protestant, Jewish and other traditions permit it in at least some circumstances.

Legal history

Abortion law varies widely. The United States Supreme Court constitutionalised a right to abortion in Roe v Wade (1973), modified it in Planned Parenthood v Casey (1992), and overturned it in Dobbs v Jackson Women's Health Organization (2022), returning regulation to the states. Most European countries permit abortion on request in the first trimester and regulate it thereafter; Ireland liberalised its law by referendum in 2018, while Poland sharply restricted access following a 2020 Constitutional Tribunal ruling. Debates continue over gestational limits, exceptions, medication abortion, and public funding.
