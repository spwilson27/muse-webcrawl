Customer lifetime value

Placeholder image: Customer lifetime value

In marketing, customer lifetime value (CLV or often CLTV), lifetime customer value (LCV), or life-time value (LTV) is a prediction of the net profit attributed to the entire future relationship with a customer. The prediction model can have varying levels of sophistication and accuracy, ranging from a crude heuristic to the use of complex predictive analytics techniques.

Overview

CLV is used to guide decisions about customer acquisition, retention, and segmentation. Because acquiring a new customer typically costs more than retaining an existing one, businesses compare a segment's CLV with its customer acquisition cost (CAC) to judge profitable growth. Customer lifetime value historically places a longer-term value on customers, encouraging companies to shift from quarterly profits towards the long-term health of customer relationships.

Calculation

A simple model multiplies average revenue per customer by gross margin and by the expected duration of the relationship. A common formulation for a retention-based model is:

CLV = (margin × retention rate) / (1 + discount rate − retention rate)

where margin is per-period profit per customer, the retention rate is the probability a customer remains active in the next period, and the discount rate converts future cash flows to present value. More advanced approaches use probabilistic models of individual purchase behavior, such as Pareto/NBD and BG/NBD models.

Uses and limitations

Firms use CLV for budgeting marketing spend, prioritizing service, identifying high-value segments, and valuing the firm itself. Limitations include sensitivity to assumptions about retention and discount rates, difficulty estimating values for new customers, and the risk that focusing on current high-value customers neglects future potential. CLV estimates are generally model-based projections and should not be treated as certain cash flows.
