Vair

Vair (from Latin varius, "variegated") is a fur tincture in heraldry, originally representing the pelts of squirrels — typically the red squirrel, or the grey-and-white winter pelts of the Eurasian red squirrel — sewn together into a patterned material used to line cloaks.

Appearance

In heraldic depiction, vair consists of rows of bell- or shield-shaped panes, conventionally argent (white/silver) and azure (blue), alternating in horizontal rows. Variants include vairy, in which other tinctures are used, and forms such as vair-en-pointe, in which the panes are arranged point-to-point.

History

Vair was a luxury material in the Middle Ages; in French the squirrel fur was called petit-gris. In the Cinderella tale as told by Charles Perrault, the heroine's slippers were described in French as pantoufles de vair ("slippers of fur"), which is often said to have been mistranslated into English as "glass slippers" (verre), though this explanation is disputed.
