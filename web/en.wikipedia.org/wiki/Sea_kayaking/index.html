Sea kayaking

Sea kayaking is the sport and recreational activity of paddling a sea kayak on open waters — seas, bays, estuaries and large lakes. It developed from the skin-on-frame hunting kayaks of Inuit, Aleut and Yup'ik peoples of the Arctic, adapted in the 20th century into fibreglass, plastic and composite touring boats.

Equipment

Sea kayaks are typically 4.5–5.5 metres long, with sealed bulkheads for buoyancy and storage, a spraydeck to keep water out of the cockpit, and often a skeg or rudder to aid tracking in wind. Paddlers carry safety equipment including a paddle float, bilge pump, whistle, flares and a VHF radio, and dress for immersion in cold water.

Activity

The pursuit ranges from sheltered day trips to multi-week expeditions and open crossings; notable achievements include solo circumnavigations of Britain, Australia and South America. Skills taught by coaching schemes (such as those of British Canoeing and the American Canoe Association) include edging, rolling, rescues, navigation and tidal planning.
