Hyphen

The hyphen (‐) is a punctuation mark used to join words and to separate syllables of a single word. The use of hyphens is called hyphenation. The hyphen should not be confused with the various dashes (figure dash ‒, en dash –, em dash —, horizontal bar ―), which are longer and have different uses, or with the minus sign (−).

Uses

Hyphens join compound modifiers before a noun ("a well-known author"), form many compound words ("mother-in-law", "editor-in-chief" in some styles), link prefixes in certain cases ("re-enter", "anti-inflammatory"), join the parts of written-out compound numbers ("twenty-one"), and connect double-barrelled names. A hanging or suspensive hyphen appears in constructions such as "nineteenth- and twentieth-century literature". At line breaks, a hyphen marks the division of a word across lines; a "soft hyphen" in computing indicates a permissible break point that appears only when used.

History and conventions

The word derives from Greek hyph' hen, "into one", because the mark was written beneath two letters to join them. Hyphenation practice varies by language, publisher and style guide, and English usage has trended over centuries from hyphenated compounds towards closed (solid) forms — "to-day" became "today", and "e-mail" became "email".

In computing

The ASCII hyphen-minus (-) serves as hyphen, minus sign and dash in plain text, while Unicode provides a dedicated hyphen character (U+2010) and a non-breaking hyphen (U+2011) that prevents a line break at that point.
