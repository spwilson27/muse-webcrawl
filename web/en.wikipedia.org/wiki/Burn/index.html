Burn

A burn is an injury to skin, or other tissues, caused by heat, cold, electricity, chemicals, friction, or ultraviolet radiation (such as sunburn). Most burns are due to heat from hot liquids (scalds), solids, or fire. Burns are classified by depth: first-degree burns affect only the epidermis; second-degree (partial-thickness) burns extend into the dermis; third-degree (full-thickness) burns destroy the full thickness of skin; and fourth-degree burns involve deeper tissues such as muscle or bone.

Signs and management

Severity is assessed by depth and the percentage of total body surface area affected. Large burns can cause shock from fluid loss, infection and hypothermia. First aid consists of cooling the burn with running water and covering it with a sterile dressing. Hospital treatment may include intravenous fluids, wound care, antibiotics, pain control and skin grafting; major burns are managed in specialist burn centres.

Epidemiology

Burns are a major global cause of injury and death, disproportionately affecting people in low- and middle-income countries, and children. Many are preventable through fire safety, safe cooking practices and regulation of hot water temperatures.
