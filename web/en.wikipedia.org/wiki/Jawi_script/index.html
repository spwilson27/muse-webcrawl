Jawi script

Jawi (جاوي) is an Arabic-derived alphabet used for writing the Malay language and several other languages of Southeast Asia, including Acehnese and Minangkabau. One of the two official scripts of Brunei, it remains in religious and cultural use in Malaysia, Indonesia and southern Thailand.

History

Jawi developed with the arrival of Islam in the Malay Archipelago; its earliest known example is the Terengganu Inscription Stone (1303). It adds letters to the Arabic alphabet — such as ca چ, nga ڠ, pa ڤ, ga ݢ and nya ڽ — for sounds absent from Arabic.

Modern use

The Latin-based Rumi script became dominant for everyday Malay in the 20th century, but Jawi persists on Malaysian banknotes, state coats of arms, signage and in Islamic education, and has seen official promotion drives in Malaysia in recent years.
