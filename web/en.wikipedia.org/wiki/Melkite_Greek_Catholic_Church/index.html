Melkite Greek Catholic Church

The Melkite Greek Catholic Church is an Eastern Catholic church in full communion with the Holy See, following the Byzantine Rite. It traces its origins to the ancient Patriarchate of Antioch and to the Christians of Syria, Lebanon, Palestine, Jordan, and Egypt. The term "Melkite" derives from the Syriac and Arabic word for "king" (malka), originally applied to those Christians in the Middle East who accepted the decrees of the Council of Chalcedon (451) supported by the Byzantine emperor.

History

After the East–West Schism, the Melkite church remained part of Eastern Orthodoxy for centuries, though contacts with Rome continued. In 1724, a pro-union party in the Patriarchate of Antioch elected Cyril VI Tanas as patriarch, and he entered into communion with the Roman Catholic Church, creating the modern Melkite Greek Catholic Church. The patriarchate was formally recognized by Rome in the following years. In the 19th century, Patriarch Maximos III Mazloum secured recognition of the patriarch's authority from the Ottoman Empire and consolidated the church's position.

Structure and membership

The church is headed by the Patriarch of Antioch, who resides in Damascus, Syria; its cathedral is Our Lady of the Dormition Cathedral in Damascus. Since 2017 the patriarch has been Youssef Absi. The Melkite church has roughly 1.5 million members worldwide, concentrated in Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Israel, and Palestine, with large diaspora communities in Brazil, the United States, Canada, Australia, and Argentina. Its liturgy is celebrated in Arabic, Greek, and the languages of the diaspora, and married men may be ordained to the priesthood, following Eastern tradition.
