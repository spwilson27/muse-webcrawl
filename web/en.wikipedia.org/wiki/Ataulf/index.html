Ataulf (also Athaulf or Adolphus; c. 370 – 415) was king of the Visigoths from 410 to 415, succeeding his brother-in-law Alaric I, who had sacked Rome in 410. Rather than destroying the Roman Empire, Ataulf came to envision preserving it with Gothic military strength — a remark reported by the historian Orosius, who quotes him as first wishing to replace "Romania" with "Gothia" but concluding that the Goths needed Roman law and order.

Reign

Ataulf led the Goths out of Italy into southern Gaul in 412, negotiating with the imperial court at Ravenna and with the usurper Jovinus. In January 414, at Narbonne, he married the emperor Honorius's half-sister Galla Placidia, captured at the sack of Rome, in a lavish Roman ceremony — a union meant to bind Goths and Romans. The imperial general Constantius blockaded the Goths by sea, forcing Ataulf into Spain in 415, where he made Barcelona his seat.

Death and succession

In the summer of 415 Ataulf was assassinated in his stables at Barcelona by a retainer, possibly avenging a former master. After the brief, violent reign of Sigeric, Wallia succeeded and reached the settlement with Rome that Ataulf had sought, establishing the Visigothic kingdom in Aquitaine. His infant son by Placidia, Theodosius, died in 415; Placidia was returned to Ravenna in 416.
