Dolly Sisters

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Dolly Sisters were identical twin dancers and entertainers, Roszika ("Rosie") Deutsch (1892–1970) and Janszieka ("Jenny") Deutsch (1892–1941), born in Balassagyarmat, Hungary, and emigrating to the United States as children. Performing in matching costumes with elaborate feathered headdresses, they became stars of vaudeville, the Ziegfeld Follies, and the great revues of Paris and London during the 1910s and 1920s.

Career

The twins began dancing professionally in New York in 1907 and adopted the stage name Dolly. They appeared in Florenz Ziegfeld's Midnight Frolic and toured internationally, becoming synonymous with the glamour of the era. Their identical appearance was central to their act, and they were famous for extravagant salaries, jewels, and gambling at the casinos of the French Riviera, where they reportedly won and lost fortunes.

Later life

The act ended after Jenny was seriously injured in a car accident in France in 1933. Jenny Dolly struggled with depression and died by suicide in Hollywood in 1941. Rosie lived quietly in the United States and died in New York in 1970. Their life story inspired the 1945 film The Dolly Sisters, starring Betty Grable and June Haver.
