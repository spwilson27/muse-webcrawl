Call and response

Call and response is a form of interaction between a speaker and one or more listeners in which statements ("calls") are punctuated by expressions ("responses") from the listeners. In music, it is a technique in which one musician offers a phrase and a second player or group answers with a direct commentary or response.

In music

The form is prominent in African and African-diasporic traditions: work songs, spirituals, gospel, blues, jazz and soul all use leader–chorus exchange inherited in part from West African musical practice and reinforced in church worship, where a preacher's call draws amens and other responses from the congregation. In blues and jazz, a vocal line may be answered by an instrument; in big-band arrangements, sections trade phrases.

Wider uses

Call and response also occurs in military cadence calls, protest chants, children's songs and audience-participation performance. In communication theory it models dialogue in which feedback from listeners shapes the speaker's delivery.
