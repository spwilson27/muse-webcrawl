Japan Standard Time

Japan Standard Time (JST; Japanese: 日本標準時, Nihon Hyōjunji) is the standard time zone in Japan, 9 hours ahead of UTC (UTC+09:00). The entire country observes a single time zone, and daylight saving time is not used.

History

Before the Meiji era, each feudal domain kept local solar time. In 1886 the government fixed standard time on the 135° east meridian, which passes through Akashi, Hyōgo, and it took effect in 1888. A western standard time for Taiwan and the Yaeyama Islands (UTC+8) existed until 1937.

Present day

Japan does not observe daylight saving time, although it was imposed briefly (1948–1951) during the Allied occupation. Sharing UTC+9 with South Korea and eastern Indonesia, JST means winter sunsets come notably early in eastern Japan, a recurring point of public debate.
