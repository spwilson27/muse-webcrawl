Monstrance

A monstrance, also known as an ostensorium (or in Latin ostensorium or monstrantia, from ostendere, "to show"), is a vessel used in Roman Catholic, Old Catholic, High Church Lutheran and Anglican churches for the more convenient adoration of the Blessed Sacrament, such as during Eucharistic adoration or Benediction of the Blessed Sacrament. It is also used in the Eastern Catholic Churches and in some parishes of the Lutheran Church for the service of Benediction.

Design

Monstrances are typically crafted in the shape of a sunburst on a stand, often surmounted by a cross. The centre contains a circular window, called a lunette, which holds the consecrated Eucharistic host visible to the faithful. The design flourished in the late medieval period following the institution of the Feast of Corpus Christi in 1264, and elaborate examples were produced in gold and silver by goldsmiths across Europe.

Use

During Eucharistic adoration, the monstrance is placed on the altar so the congregation may pray before the exposed Sacrament. In Benediction, the priest, wearing a humeral veil, uses the monstrance to bless the congregation. Monstrances are also carried in solemn processions, most notably on Corpus Christi. The Council of Trent affirmed the practice of exposing the Sacrament for veneration.
