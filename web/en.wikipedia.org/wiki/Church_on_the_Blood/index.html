Church on the Blood

The Church on the Blood in Honour of All Saints Resplendent in the Russian Land.

The Church on the Blood in Honour of All Saints Resplendent in the Russian Land (Russian: Храм-на-Крови во имя Всех святых, в земле Российской просиявших) is a Russian Orthodox church in Yekaterinburg, Russia. Completed in 2003, it stands on the site of the Ipatiev House, in whose basement the former Emperor Nicholas II, his family, and four retainers were executed by the Bolsheviks on the night of 16–17 July 1918. The church commemorates the canonisation of the Romanov family as passion bearers by the Russian Orthodox Church in 2000.

History

The Ipatiev House, requisitioned in 1918 and dubbed the "House of Special Purpose", was demolished in 1977 on the orders of the Politburo — carried out by local party chief Boris Yeltsin — to prevent it becoming a focus of pilgrimage. After the fall of the Soviet Union the site became a place of Orthodox veneration. A wooden chapel and crosses marked the spot before the regional government and church agreed on constructing a permanent church; the foundation stone was laid in 1992 and construction, to a design in a Russo-Byzantine revival style, lasted from 2000 to 2003. The completed church was consecrated on 16 June 2003, a month before the 85th anniversary of the murders.

Architecture

The five-domed church rises about 60 metres and contains an upper church for services and a lower church incorporating the stylised execution room at basement level, where a museum of the Romanovs is also housed. The ensemble includes the separate Church of St Nicholas, a patriarchal residence, and a monument to the Imperial family. Each July the church is the destination of an all-night vigil and a procession of tens of thousands of pilgrims to the Monastery of the Holy Royal Passion-Bearers at Ganina Yama, where the bodies were disposed of.
