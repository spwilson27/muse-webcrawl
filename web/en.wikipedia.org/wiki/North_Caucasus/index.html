North Caucasus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The North Caucasus (Russian: Северный Кавказ, Severny Kavkaz) is a region of Russia in Eastern Europe, lying on the northern slopes and foothills of the Greater Caucasus mountain range between the Black Sea and the Caspian Sea. It is sometimes considered the natural northern boundary of the Caucasus region as a whole.

Geography

The region includes Mount Elbrus, at 5,642 m the highest peak in Europe. South of the watershed lie the independent states of the South Caucasus (Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan); to the north stretch the steppe lowlands of the Kuban and Terek rivers.

Political divisions

Within the Russian Federation, the North Caucasus comprises the republics of Adygea, Karachay-Cherkessia, Kabardino-Balkaria, North Ossetia–Alania, Ingushetia, Chechnya, and Dagestan, together with Krasnodar Krai and Stavropol Krai. Most of the area belongs to the North Caucasian Federal District, created in 2010 with its capital at Pyatigorsk.

Peoples and history

The region is extraordinarily diverse linguistically and ethnically, home to Circassians, Chechens, Ingush, Avars, Dargins, Kumyks, Ossetians, and dozens of other groups speaking Northeast and Northwest Caucasian, Turkic, and Iranian languages. The Russian Empire conquered the region in the Caucasian War of 1817–1864, which ended with the mass expulsion of Circassians. The late Soviet and post-Soviet periods saw the First and Second Chechen Wars (1994–96 and 1999–2009) and a long-running Islamist insurgency, followed by the proclamation of the Caucasus Emirate in 2007. The 2014 Winter Olympics were held in Sochi, on the region's Black Sea coast.
