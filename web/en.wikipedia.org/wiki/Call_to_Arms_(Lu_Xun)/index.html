Call to Arms (Chinese: 呐喊; pinyin: Nàhǎn) is a collection of short stories by Lu Xun, the founder of modern Chinese literature. First published in 1923, it collects fourteen stories written between 1918 and 1922, together with the author's famous preface, in which he describes the "iron house" parable that motivated his turn to literature as a means of awakening his countrymen.

Contents

The collection opens with "Diary of a Madman" (1918), generally regarded as the first major work of modern Chinese fiction written in the vernacular (baihua); its narrator becomes convinced that the Confucian society around him is literally cannibalistic. Other well-known stories include "Kong Yiji", about a failed scholar; "Medicine", in which a bun soaked in an executed revolutionary's blood is bought as a cure; "The True Story of Ah Q", whose deluded protagonist became a byword for self-deceiving rationalisation of defeat (the "Ah Q spirit"); and "My Old Home", a melancholy account of returning to one's native village.

Significance

Together with its successor collection, Wandering (1926), Call to Arms established the modern Chinese short story and remains central to the Chinese literary canon, widely taught in schools. Its stories blend satire, symbolism and social criticism aimed at the ills of late-imperial and early-republican China.
