Battle of the Nile

The Battle of the Nile (also known as the Battle of Aboukir Bay) was fought from 1 to 3 August 1798 in Aboukir Bay, Egypt, between a British fleet under Rear-Admiral Sir Horatio Nelson and a French fleet under Vice-Admiral François-Paul Brueys. Nelson's victory destroyed French naval power in the Mediterranean, stranded Napoleon Bonaparte's army in Egypt, and was one of the most complete naval victories of the age of sail.

Background

The French fleet had convoyed Napoleon's expedition to Egypt and anchored in a line in Aboukir Bay, close inshore, believing the shoals protected its landward side. Nelson, after weeks of searching the Mediterranean, found the French late on 1 August and attacked at once, though evening was falling.

The battle

Nelson's leading ships, led by HMS Goliath, rounded the head of the French line and passed between it and the shore — the French had left room enough — so that the French van was attacked from both sides. Ship by ship the British doubled on the French line. At about 10 p.m. the French flagship L'Orient, a 118-gun first rate, caught fire and blew up with an explosion heard miles away; Brueys was already dead. By morning, of thirteen French ships of the line, nine were captured and two destroyed; only two ships of the line and two frigates, under Rear-Admiral Villeneuve, escaped.

Aftermath

British casualties were about 900; French losses, including prisoners, were several thousand. Napoleon, cut off from France, marched into Syria and eventually slipped back to France in 1799, abandoning his army. The victory encouraged the formation of the Second Coalition against France, and Nelson — wounded in the head during the battle — became a national hero and was made Baron Nelson of the Nile.
