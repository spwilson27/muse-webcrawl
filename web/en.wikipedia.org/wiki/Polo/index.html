Polo is a ball game played on horseback, a traditional field sport and one of the world's oldest known team sports. The game is played by two opposing teams with the objective of scoring goals by using a long-handled wooden mallet to hit a small hard ball through the opposing team's goal. Each team has four mounted riders, and the game usually lasts one to two hours, divided into periods called chukkas or chukkers.

History

Polo originated in ancient Persia (modern Iran) around the 6th century BC or earlier, where it was played by noblemen and cavalry units as training for war. From Persia the game spread across Asia, becoming popular in India, China, and Tibet; the name "polo" derives from the Balti word pulu, meaning ball. In the 19th century, British tea planters and cavalry officers encountered the game in Manipur, India, founded clubs, and codified rules. The Hurlingham Polo Association in England became the sport's governing body, and polo spread to Argentina, the United States, and the British Empire. Polo was an Olympic sport from 1900 to 1936.

Rules and play

Outdoor polo is played on a field 300 yards (274 m) long and 160 yards (146 m) wide, the largest field in organised sport. Players are rated on a handicap scale from −2 to 10 goals; the best players, mostly found in Argentina, hold 10-goal handicaps. A match is divided into chukkas of seven minutes each — typically four to six chukkas in club matches and up to eight in high-goal tournaments. Players change horses between or even during chukkas because of the intense exertion.

A central safety rule is the "line of the ball", an imaginary line created by the ball's trajectory, which establishes right of way and governs when players may cross or challenge, reducing dangerous collisions.

Horses

Polo mounts, traditionally called "polo ponies" regardless of size, are typically thoroughbreds or thoroughbred crosses selected for speed, agility and temperament. They are trained to respond to leg and rein cues while tolerating the mallet, the ball, and close contact with other horses.

Modern game

Argentina is the dominant polo nation, home to the Argentine Open Championship at Palermo in Buenos Aires, one of the three tournaments — with Hurlingham and Tortugas — making up the Argentine Triple Crown. Major events elsewhere include the Queen's Cup and Gold Cup in England and the U.S. Open Polo Championship in Florida. Variants include arena polo (three players per side on a smaller enclosed field), beach polo, snow polo, and elephant polo.
