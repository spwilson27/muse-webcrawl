Responsibility to protect

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The responsibility to protect (R2P or RtoP) is a global political commitment endorsed by all United Nations member states at the 2005 World Summit to address the international community's repeated failures to prevent mass atrocities. It holds that sovereignty entails a state's responsibility to protect its population from genocide, war crimes, ethnic cleansing and crimes against humanity; that the international community should assist states in doing so; and that, where a state manifestly fails, the international community should take collective action, through the Security Council, in accordance with the UN Charter.

Origins and application

R2P emerged from the ICISS commission (2001), convened by Canada after NATO's 1999 Kosovo intervention and the Rwanda and Srebrenica failures, reframing "humanitarian intervention" as a responsibility continuum (prevent, react, rebuild). The Security Council invoked R2P language authorising the 2011 Libya intervention — whose transformation into regime change provoked backlash from Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa (including Brazil's "responsibility while protecting" proposal) and Council paralysis over Syria. Later practice (Central African Republic, The Gambia 2017 diplomacy, atrocity-prevention agendas) kept the doctrine alive chiefly as prevention and accountability rather than military intervention.
