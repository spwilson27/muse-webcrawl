Alaska Railroad

Alaska Railroad (reporting mark ARR)
Locale: Alaska, United States
Main line: Seward to Fairbanks, 470 miles (760 km)
Completed: 1923
Owner: State of Alaska (Alaska Railroad Corporation)

The Alaska Railroad is a Class II railroad that operates freight and passenger trains in the U.S. state of Alaska. Its main line runs 470 miles from Seward on the Kenai Peninsula through Anchorage to Fairbanks in the interior, with branches to Whittier and Palmer. It is owned by the state of Alaska and operated by the Alaska Railroad Corporation, a state-owned corporation.

History

Construction began under the federal Alaska Engineering Commission in 1914, absorbing the struggling Alaska Northern Railway at Seward. President Warren G. Harding drove the golden spike at Nenana on 15 July 1923, completing the line. The federal government operated the railroad until 1985, when it was transferred to the state of Alaska for $22.3 million.

Services

The railroad carries freight — including coal, gravel, petroleum products and containers arriving by railbarge at Whittier — and extensive passenger services popular with tourists, including the Denali Star between Anchorage and Fairbanks via Denali National Park, the Coastal Classic to Seward, and the Glacier Discovery train. Unusually, the railroad still offers flag-stop service on the Hurricane Turn train for residents of roadless backcountry areas.
