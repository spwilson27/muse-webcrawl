Pen name

A pen name, also called a nom de plume, is a pseudonym adopted by an author instead of their real name. Famous examples include Mark Twain (Samuel Clemens), George Orwell (Eric Blair), George Eliot (Mary Ann Evans), and Dr. Seuss (Theodor Geisel).

Reasons for use

Authors adopt pen names for many reasons: to conceal gender (as the Brontë sisters did with the Bell brothers' names, and J.K. Rowling was advised to do with initials); to separate works in different genres (Iain Banks / Iain M. Banks); to avoid confusion with another author; to protect privacy or safety; to create a marketable persona; or because publishers believe a different name will sell better. Some authors, such as Stephen King writing as Richard Bachman, used a pen name to publish more books than the market norm allowed.

Collective and shared names

Some pen names are shared by multiple writers, such as Carolyn Keene for the Nancy Drew series or Ellery Queen. Pen names differ from stage names and from allonyms, which are the real names of other people used as bylines.
