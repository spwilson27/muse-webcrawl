Working capital

Working capital (WC) is a financial metric which represents operating liquidity available to a business, organisation, or other entity, including governmental entities. Along with fixed assets such as plant and equipment, working capital is considered a part of operating capital. Gross working capital is equal to current assets. Working capital is calculated as current assets minus current liabilities.

Working capital
FormulaCurrent assets − Current liabilities
Main componentsCash, inventories, accounts receivable, accounts payable
Related measureCash conversion cycle

Interpretation

If a company's current assets do not exceed its current liabilities, it may run into trouble paying back creditors or go bankrupt. A company can be endowed with assets and profitability but may fall short of liquidity if its assets cannot readily be converted into cash. Positive working capital is required to ensure that a firm is able to continue its operations and has sufficient funds to satisfy both maturing short-term debt and upcoming operational expenses.

Working capital management

Working capital management involves managing inventories, accounts receivable and payable, and cash so as to keep operations running efficiently without tying up excessive funds. The cash conversion cycle measures the time between paying suppliers for inventory and collecting cash from customers; shortening it releases cash for other uses. Decisions on working capital involve trade-offs between profitability and risk: holding large inventories and offering generous credit can support sales but increase financing costs, while stretching payments to suppliers can strain relationships.

Changes in working capital

In cash flow statements, the change in working capital from one period to the next is reported within operating activities: increases in current assets (such as building up receivables) consume cash, while increases in current liabilities (such as payables) provide it.
