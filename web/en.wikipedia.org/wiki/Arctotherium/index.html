Arctotherium

Arctotherium
Temporal range:Early Pleistocene–Late Pleistocene (~1.2 million – 11,000 years ago)
Family:Ursidae
Tribe:Tremarctini (short-faced bears)
Species:A. angustidens, A. bonariense, A. tarijense, A. vetustum, A. wingei

Arctotherium is an extinct genus of short-faced bears endemic to Central and South America during the Pleistocene epoch. Its members arrived from North America during the Great American Interchange after the formation of the Isthmus of Panama. The earliest and largest species, Arctotherium angustidens, is considered the largest bear ever discovered.

Description

A remarkably complete specimen of A. angustidens from near La Plata, Argentina, described in 2011, was estimated to weigh between 983 and 2,042 kg (2,167–4,500 lb), with the most plausible figure around 1,600 kg — making it the largest known bear and among the largest terrestrial mammalian predators of all time. Later species of the genus were progressively smaller, probably reflecting shrinking prey and habitat, with A. wingei the size of a modern spectacled bear.

Diet and extinction

Like its northern cousin Arctodus, Arctotherium combined predation and scavenging with omnivory; dental wear and healed injuries in the La Plata giant suggest a life of aggressive confrontations, probably over carcasses. The genus disappeared around 11,000 years ago in the end-Pleistocene extinctions. Its only living relative is the spectacled bear (Tremarctos ornatus), the last survivor of the short-faced bear lineage.
