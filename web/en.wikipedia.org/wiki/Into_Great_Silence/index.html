Into Great Silence


The Grande Chartreuse, the mother house of the Carthusian order.

Into Great Silence (German: Die große Stille) is a 2005 documentary film directed by Philip Gröning. It is an austere, meditative portrait of the everyday lives of Carthusian monks of the Grande Chartreuse, the head monastery of the Carthusian order, located in the Chartreuse Mountains of the French Alps.

Production

Gröning first proposed the film to the monks in 1984; they replied that he should ask again in some years, and sixteen years later invited him to proceed. Gröning lived in the monastery for months and filmed alone over four seasons, using natural light and ambient sound. The film contains almost no dialogue, no commentary, no music beyond the monks' chanting, and no artificial lighting.

Subject matter

The monks live under a rule of near-perpetual silence, devoting themselves to prayer, the chanting of the Divine Office in a chapel rebuilt after avalanche damage, manual work, study and solitary meals in their individual cells, gathering communally chiefly for liturgy and a weekly walk and conversation.

Reception

The nearly three-hour film became a surprise arthouse success, particularly in Germany, and won awards including the Special Jury Prize at the 2006 Sundance Film Festival and the European Film Award for Best Documentary. Critics praised its immersive depiction of contemplative life.
