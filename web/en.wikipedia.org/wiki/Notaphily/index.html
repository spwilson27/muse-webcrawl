Notaphily

Notaphily is the study and collecting of paper money and banknotes. A person who studies or collects paper money is called a notaphilist. The term comes from the Latin nota (a mark or note) combined with the Greek -philia (love of). Notaphily is considered a branch of numismatics, though it is often treated as a distinct discipline because of the different production techniques, artistry and historical context of paper currency.

History

Paper money originated in China, becoming widespread by the Song dynasty, and was introduced to Europe much later; early European notes date from the 17th century. Collecting banknotes as a hobby grew significantly in the mid-20th century, and organised notaphily took off from the 1970s with the founding of societies such as the International Bank Note Society (IBNS, founded 1961) and the publication of standard catalogues, notably the Standard Catalog of World Paper Money.

Collecting

Collectors value notes for their rarity, historical significance, printing errors, low or unusual serial numbers, signatures, replacement notes and artistic design. Condition is critical, and banknotes are graded on scales ranging from Poor to Uncirculated (Crisp Uncirculated), sometimes by professional grading services that encapsulate the notes. Particularly prized areas include early Chinese notes, large-size United States notes, "specimen" notes, and emergency and occupation issues.
