Amsterdam Stock Exchange

The Amsterdam Stock Exchange (Dutch: Amsterdamse effectenbeurs), now known as Euronext Amsterdam, is a stock exchange located in Amsterdam, Netherlands. Founded in 1602 by the Dutch East India Company, it is generally considered the world's oldest modern stock exchange.

History

In 1602 the Dutch East India Company (VOC) offered transferable shares to the public, traded at the Amsterdam exchange — the first formal stock market for securities. Options, futures and short selling developed there in the 17th century, along with the tulip mania of 1636–37. The exchange occupied Hendrik de Keyser's building from 1611 and later the Beurs van Berlage (1903).

Euronext era

In 2000 Amsterdam merged with the Brussels and Paris exchanges to form Euronext. Its benchmark is the AEX index of leading Dutch listed companies. Trading is electronic, based at Beursplein 5.
