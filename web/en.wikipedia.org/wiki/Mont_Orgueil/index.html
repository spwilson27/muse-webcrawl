Mont Orgueil is a castle in Jersey that overlooks the harbour of Gorey, in the parish of Saint Martin. It is also called Gorey Castle (French: Château Mont Orgueil, Norman: lé Vièr Châté).

History

Construction of the castle began shortly after the division of the Duchy of Normandy in 1204, when Jersey remained loyal to the English Crown while mainland Normandy went to Philip II of France. The castle was first mentioned in documents in 1212. It was the primary defence of Jersey until the development of gunpowder meant that the nearby headland became vulnerable to cannon fire, and Elizabeth Castle off Saint Helier was built in the 1590s.

Later use

Mont Orgueil served as a prison until the construction of a prison in St Helier in the mid-17th century, and held notable prisoners including the regicide William Phipos. During the English Civil War the island's Royalist supporters held out at the castle for a time. In the Second World War the German occupying forces fortified the site as part of the Atlantic Wall.

Today

The castle is now a museum administered by the Jersey Heritage Trust. It is open to the public and hosts exhibitions on Jersey's history, including artworks depicting the island's past.
