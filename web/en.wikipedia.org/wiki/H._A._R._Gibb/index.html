Sir Hamilton Alexander Rosskeen Gibb (2 January 1895 – 22 October 1971), known as H. A. R. Gibb, was a Scottish historian and orientalist, one of the leading British scholars of Islam and Arabic literature of the twentieth century.

Life and career

Born in Alexandria, Egypt, Gibb was educated at the Royal High School, Edinburgh, and the University of Edinburgh, where his studies were interrupted by service in the Royal Field Artillery during the First World War. He completed his degree at the School of Oriental Studies in London, where he later became professor of Arabic. In 1937 he moved to the University of Oxford as Laudian Professor of Arabic, and in 1955 he became director of the Center for Middle Eastern Studies at Harvard University, where he remained until his retirement.

Work

Gibb's Arabic Literature: An Introduction (1926, revised 1963) remained a standard survey for decades. With E. Levi-Provençal and others he was an editor of the second edition of the Encyclopaedia of Islam. His translation of Ibn Battuta's Travels for the Hakluyt Society made the great Moroccan traveller's account widely accessible in English, and his studies of Islamic political thought, including essays collected in Studies on the Civilization of Islam (1962), shaped the field. His influential 1949 Whidden Lectures, published as Mohammedanism: An Historical Survey, introduced generations of students to the history of Islam.
