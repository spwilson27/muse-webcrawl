The foreign relations of Italy are the Italian Republic's external relations with the rest of the world. Since the end of the Second World War, Italian foreign policy has rested on two traditional pillars: European integration ("Europeanism") and the Atlantic alliance with the United States ("Atlanticism"), complemented by an active Mediterranean policy reflecting Italy's geographic position.

European Union

Italy was a founding member of the European Coal and Steel Community and signed the Treaty of Rome in 1957, which established the European Economic Community. It remains one of the largest member states of the European Union, a member of the eurozone and of the Schengen Area, and hosts several EU agencies.

Transatlantic relations

Italy is a founding member of NATO (1949) and hosts significant alliance infrastructure, including the Allied Joint Force Command Naples and the US Naval Air Station Sigonella in Sicily. Italy contributes troops to alliance and international missions and is a member of the G7, which it has chaired on several occasions.

Mediterranean and beyond

Italy's foreign policy gives particular attention to the Mediterranean basin, North Africa and the Balkans — including migration management, energy security and stability in Libya — and to its historically large emigrant communities in the Americas and Australia. Italy maintains a special relationship with the Holy See, whose territory is entirely surrounded by the city of Rome, and is home to the headquarters of the Food and Agriculture Organization and other UN agencies.
