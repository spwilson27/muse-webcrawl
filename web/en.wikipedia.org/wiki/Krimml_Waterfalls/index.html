The Krimml Waterfalls (German: Krimmler Wasserfälle), with a total height of 380 metres, are the highest waterfalls in Austria and among the tallest in Europe. They lie on the Krimmler Ache river near the village of Krimml in the state of Salzburg, within the Hohe Tauern National Park.

Description

The falls descend in three great stages — the Upper, Middle and Lower Krimml Falls — as the Krimmler Ache plunges from the hanging Krimml valley down to the Salzach valley floor. Flow is strongest in early summer, when glacial meltwater swells the stream; about 5.6 m³/s passes on average over the year. A well-maintained path built by the Austrian Alpine Club from 1879 onwards climbs beside the falls with a series of viewing platforms.

History and conservation

In the 1960s the falls were threatened by a hydroelectric scheme that would have diverted much of their water; protests led by conservationists and the Alpine Club defeated the plan and helped galvanise the movement that created Austria's national parks. The falls received the European Diploma of Protected Areas from the Council of Europe in 1967.

Health tourism

Since the 19th century the fine mist at the falls' base has been promoted as beneficial for asthma and allergies; modern studies commissioned locally attribute measurable relief of allergic asthma symptoms to the aerosol of negative air ions, and Krimml markets itself as a health-resort village centred on the falls.
