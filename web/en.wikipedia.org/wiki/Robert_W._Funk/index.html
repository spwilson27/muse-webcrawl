Robert Walter Funk (July 18, 1926 – September 3, 2005) was an American biblical scholar, founder of the scholarly society the Westar Institute and its offshoot, the Jesus Seminar. Funk was a leading voice in the mid-to-late twentieth-century "historical Jesus" research movement and a pioneer in the field of biblical parable scholarship.

Life and career

Funk was born in Evansville, Indiana, and earned bachelor's and master's degrees from Butler University and its affiliated Christian Theological Seminary, followed by a doctorate at Vanderbilt University. He held teaching posts at several institutions, including Harvard Divinity School, Drew University, and the University of Montana, where he began organizing the scholarly work that would lead to the founding of Westar in 1985.

Through the Jesus Seminar, founded in 1985, Funk convened scores of scholars to vote, using colored beads, on the authenticity of the sayings and deeds attributed to Jesus in the Gospels. The Seminar's publications, including The Five Gospels (1993), attracted wide public attention and controversy.

Scholarship

Funk's own academic work focused on the parables of Jesus, the Gospel of Mark, and Hellenistic rhetoric. His book Language, Hermeneutic, and Word of God (1966) applied the "new hermeneutic" to biblical interpretation, and Honest to Jesus (1996) laid out his vision for a renewed Christianity, arguing that the church should move from dogma toward the authentic voiceprint of Jesus and the early community.
