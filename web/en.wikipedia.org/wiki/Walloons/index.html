Walloons

The Walloons (French: Wallons) are a Romance ethnic group native to Wallonia in southern Belgium, distinguished by their French-related culture and language. They form one of Belgium's principal communities alongside the Dutch-speaking Flemings of Flanders. Walloons number roughly 3.5–4 million in Belgium, with a diaspora in France and North America.

History

The word derives from a Germanic term for "foreigner/Romance-speaker" (the same root as "Welsh"). The Walloon lands, romanised early, passed through the Frankish kingdoms, the prince-bishopric of Liège and the Spanish and Austrian Netherlands before joining Belgium in 1830, when the francophone elite dominated the new state. The region industrialised early around coal and steel (the sillon industriel), then declined in the later 20th century as Flanders prospered.

Language and identity

Historically Walloons spoke Walloon and other langues d'oïl (Picard, Lorrain, Champenois); today French is near-universal, and Walloon is an endangered regional language recognised by the French Community of Belgium. Belgium's federal structure gives Wallonia its own region and the francophones a French Community. The red rooster of Wallonia was adopted as a symbol in 1913 following the Walloon movement's campaign for recognition.
