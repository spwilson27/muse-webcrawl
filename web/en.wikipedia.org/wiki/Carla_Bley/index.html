Carla Bley

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Carla Bley (born Lovella May Borg; May 11, 1936 – October 17, 2023) was an American jazz composer, pianist, organist and bandleader. A leading figure in the free jazz movement of the 1960s, she is best known for the jazz opera Escalator over the Hill (1971) and for compositions such as "Ida Lupino" and "Lawns" that entered the standard repertoire. She was a 2015 NEA Jazz Master.

Early life

Bley was born in Oakland, California, to a church organist father, from whom she learned piano and hymn singing. She left home as a teenager and moved to New York City, where she worked as a cigarette girl at the Birdland jazz club — listening nightly to the era's great pianists. She married pianist Paul Bley in 1957 and began writing compositions for him and for musicians including Jimmy Giuffre, George Russell and Art Farmer; her tune "Ida Lupino" dates from this period.

Career

With trumpeter Michael Mantler she co-founded the Jazz Composer's Orchestra in 1964, and founded the independent labels JCOA and WATT Records to release ambitious large-scale work outside the commercial system. Escalator over the Hill (1971), a sprawling "chronotransduction" with libretto by Paul Haines and dozens of performers, is regarded as a landmark of jazz composition. Her 1970s–80s Carla Bley Band albums, including Dinner Music (1977) and Sextet (1987), mixed jazz with rock, tango and deadpan humour. She also wrote Charlie Haden's Liberation Music Orchestra arrangements (beginning with its 1969 debut) and appeared on Nick Mason's Fictitious Sports (1981). From the 1990s she toured in a long-running trio with saxophonist Andy Sheppard and her partner, bassist Steve Swallow, recording for ECM albums such as Trios (2013) and Life Goes On (2020).

Style and legacy

Bley's writing is noted for its singable melodies, quirky harmonic turns and irony — music at once sophisticated and deliberately plain-spoken. Dozens of her compositions have become standards recorded across the jazz world. She died at her home in Willow, New York, in 2023, aged 87, from complications of brain cancer.
