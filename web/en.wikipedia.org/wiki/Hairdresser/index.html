Hairdresser

A hairdresser is a person whose occupation is cutting or styling hair in order to change or maintain a person's appearance. This is achieved using a combination of hair colouring, haircutting, and hair texture techniques. Hairdressers typically work in beauty salons or barbershops, or are self-employed; many specialise in areas such as colouring, extensions, or styling for weddings and events.

History

Hair care and styling are ancient practices: razors and hair ornaments survive from the Bronze Age, and in ancient Egypt and Rome hairdressing was performed by servants and slaves for wealthy clients. In 17th- and 18th-century France, hairdressers such as the celebrated Legros de Rumigny and, later, Léonard Autié, hairdresser to Marie Antoinette, raised the trade's status with elaborate court coiffures. The modern salon developed in the late 19th and 20th centuries with figures such as Martha Matilda Harper, who pioneered the salon franchise, and Vidal Sassoon, who popularised geometric cuts in the 1960s.

Training

In most countries hairdressers must complete vocational training or an apprenticeship and obtain a licence or certification in cosmetology or hairdressing, covering cutting, chemical treatments, hygiene, and safety. Training hours and licensing requirements vary widely by jurisdiction.
