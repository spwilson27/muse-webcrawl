A DJ mixer is a type of audio mixing console used by disc jockeys (DJs) to control and manipulate multiple audio signals, typically from two or more turntables, CD players or digital media players. Its defining feature is the crossfader, which lets the DJ blend smoothly — or cut sharply — between sources.

Features

Unlike a studio mixing console, a DJ mixer emphasises real-time performance controls: per-channel gain, three-band equalisation for blending tracks tonally, channel faders, a replaceable crossfader, headphone cueing so the DJ can preview the next track, and often built-in effects. Phono preamplifiers accommodate turntables, and microphone inputs allow announcements.

Battle mixers and club mixers

"Battle" mixers, developed for hip hop turntablism, are compact two-channel designs with a smooth, adjustable-curve crossfader suited to scratching, as in the classic Vestax and Rane models. Club mixers, such as the Pioneer DJM series, offer four channels, extensive effects and digital connectivity for house and techno DJs who mix long transitions.

History

Early disco DJs in the 1970s used simple mixers adapted from broadcast and studio equipment; purpose-built DJ mixers emerged as hip hop and club culture grew in the 1980s and 1990s. Modern mixers increasingly integrate USB audio interfaces and control of DJ software, though the basic layout of channels, EQ and crossfader remains standard.
