p–n junction

Field: Semiconductor physics
Basis of: Diodes, transistors, solar cells
Key feature: Rectifying current flow

A p–n junction is the interface between p-type and n-type semiconductor materials within a single crystal. It allows electric current to pass in one direction but not the other, and it forms the fundamental building block of most semiconductor devices, including diodes, transistors, LEDs, and solar cells.

Formation and depletion region

When p-type and n-type materials are joined, electrons and holes diffuse across the interface and recombine, creating a depletion region containing immobile ionized dopants and a built-in electric field, typically with a built-in potential of about 0.7 V in silicon.

Biasing

Forward bias lowers the barrier and permits large current flow; reverse bias widens the depletion region and blocks current except for a small leakage, until breakdown. This asymmetric behavior is called rectification.
