Languages of Spain

The languages of Spain are dominated by Spanish (Castilian), the sole official language at the national level and the mother tongue of the great majority of the population. The 1978 Constitution declares Castilian the official language of the State, while allowing other Spanish languages to be co-official in their autonomous communities. The principal co-official languages are Catalan/Valencian, Galician, and Basque; Aranese (a variety of Occitan) is co-official in Catalonia. Several further languages — Asturian, Leonese, and Aragonese — enjoy lesser legal recognition, and Spain is also home to Caló, Spanish Sign Language, and large immigrant-language communities.

Co-official languages

Catalan is co-official in Catalonia and the Balearic Islands; the closely related Valencian has the same status in the Valencian Community. It is spoken by roughly nine million people across all territories where it is used. Galician, closely related to Portuguese, is co-official in Galicia and spoken by most of that community's population, though everyday use is declining among the young. Basque (Euskara), a language isolate unrelated to any other known language, is co-official in the Basque Autonomous Community and the Basque-speaking zone of Navarre, with roughly 750,000 speakers. Aranese, the Gascon variety of Occitan spoken in the Val d'Aran, is co-official throughout Catalonia since 2006 and taught in Aran's schools.

History and policy

Spain's languages descend mostly from Vulgar Latin, layered over pre-Roman languages of which only Basque survives. Under Franco (1939–75) regional languages were suppressed in public life. The democratic transition brought the 1978 constitutional settlement and statutes of autonomy, followed by "linguistic normalisation" laws promoting Catalan, Galician, and Basque in education and media — policies that revived the languages but remain politically contentious, particularly over the language of schooling in Catalonia. Spain signed the European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages in 1992 and ratified it in 2001.
