Arthur Hiller

Arthur Hiller
Born: November 22, 1923, Edmonton, Alberta, Canada
Died: August 17, 2016 (aged 92), Los Angeles, California, U.S.
Occupation: Film director
Years active: 1955–2006

Arthur Hiller, OC (November 22, 1923 – August 17, 2016) was a Canadian-American film director. His filmography includes more than 33 films during a 50-year career, and he is best remembered for directing Love Story (1970), one of the most commercially successful films of its era. He was president of the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences from 1989 to 1993 and of the Directors Guild of America from 1986 to 1989.

Early life

Hiller was born in Edmonton, Alberta, to a Jewish family that had emigrated from Poland. During the Second World War he served as a navigator in the Royal Canadian Air Force. He studied at the University of Toronto and began his career in Canadian radio and television before moving to the United States to direct episodes of American television series such as Alfred Hitchcock Presents, Gunsmoke and The Rifleman.

Career

Hiller's feature film career spanned comedy, drama and romance. His notable films include The Americanization of Emily (1964), Promise Her Anything (1965), The Hospital (1971), The Man in the Glass Booth (1975), Silver Streak (1976), The In-Laws (1979), Making Love (1982), Outrageous Fortune (1987) and Taking Care of Business (1990). Love Story, starring Ali MacGraw and Ryan O'Neal, was nominated for seven Academy Awards including Best Director.

Honours

Hiller received the Academy's Jean Hersholt Humanitarian Award in 2002. He was made an Officer of the Order of Canada and received a star on Canada's Walk of Fame. He died in Los Angeles in 2016 at the age of 92.
