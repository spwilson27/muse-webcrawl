Data provenance

Data provenance is the documentation of where data came from and the processes and methodology by which it was produced. It provides a historical record of the data and its origins, including the people, institutions, entities and processes involved in generating, recording, curating and otherwise influencing the data. Provenance is a key concept in data management, reproducible science, and the assessment of data trustworthiness.

Concepts

The term derives from the art-world notion of provenance — the documented history of an artwork's ownership. In computing, provenance metadata records derivation: which datasets were inputs, which programs and parameters produced an output, and when. Such records let users judge quality and fitness for use, debug errors, and reproduce results.

Standards

The W3C PROV family of specifications (PROV-DM, PROV-O, PROV-N), published in 2013, provides a general model of entities, activities and agents for exchanging provenance on the web. In workflow systems and scientific databases, provenance is often captured automatically as data flows through pipelines; this is sometimes called data lineage in commercial products.

Applications

  • Scientific workflows and reproducibility
  • Data quality and trust assessment
  • Regulatory audit and accountability
  • Digital forensics and archival description
