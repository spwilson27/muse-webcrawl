Carl Barks

Carl Barks (March 27, 1901 – August 25, 2000) was an American cartoonist, author and painter. He is best known for his work in the Disney comic books, where he created Scrooge McDuck in 1947 and the fictional city of Duckburg, and for writing and drawing some 500 Donald Duck stories that are widely regarded as classics of the comics medium. Because his work was published anonymously under Walt Disney's name, fans long knew him only as "the Good Duck Artist".

Career

Born in Merrill, Oregon, Barks taught himself to draw by copying his favourite newspaper cartoonists. He joined the Walt Disney studio in 1935 as an in-betweener and story man, contributing gags to Donald Duck cartoons including Modern Inventions (1937). Leaving the studio in 1942 for health reasons, he began drawing Donald Duck comic books for Western Publishing that same year; his first long adventure, "Donald Duck Finds Pirate Gold" (1942), was followed by a quarter-century of monthly stories in Walt Disney's Comics and Stories and the Uncle Scrooge title launched in 1952.

Legacy

Barks created or developed much of Duckburg's cast, including Gladstone Gander, the Beagle Boys, Gyro Gearloose, Magica De Spell, Flintheart Glomgold and John D. Rockerduck. His adventure stories — treasure hunts to lost cities and mythical lands, drawn with meticulous researched detail — inspired later creators such as Don Rosa and George Lucas and Steven Spielberg, who credited a Barks story with the booby-trapped temple opening of Raiders of the Lost Ark. After retiring in 1966 he painted popular oil paintings of the ducks. He was inducted into the Will Eisner Award Hall of Fame in 1987.
