Privacy

Privacy concerns the ability of individuals to control information about themselves.

Privacy is the ability of an individual or group to seclude themselves or information about themselves, and thereby express themselves selectively. Privacy is commonly understood as a fundamental human right, though its scope and meaning vary across cultures and legal systems. The domain of privacy partially overlaps with security, and includes related concepts such as anonymity, secrecy, and the right to be let alone.

Key formulations"The right to be let alone" (Warren & Brandeis, 1890)
Legal instrumentsUDHR Art. 12; ICCPR Art. 17; GDPR (EU); national constitutions
DimensionsBodily, informational, territorial, communications

History of the concept

Samuel Warren and Louis Brandeis's 1890 Harvard Law Review article "The Right to Privacy" — prompted by intrusive press photography and gossip columns — argued for a legal right protecting an "inviolate personality". Alan Westin's Privacy and Freedom (1967) defined privacy as individuals' claim "to determine for themselves when, how, and to what extent information about them is communicated to others".

Types

  • Informational privacy / data protection: control over personal data — now governed by regimes such as the EU General Data Protection Regulation (2016, effective 2018).
  • Bodily privacy: protection against intrusive procedures such as searches, testing, and medical interventions.
  • Territorial privacy: inviolability of the home and personal spaces.
  • Communications privacy: secrecy of correspondence, extended to telephone and electronic communications.

Privacy and technology

Computing, the internet, and smartphones have made large-scale collection and analysis of personal data routine, in commercial surveillance, advertising, and state intelligence programmes. Technologies for protection include encryption, anonymity networks, and "privacy by design". Debates centre on trade-offs with security and convenience, the concept of consent, and asymmetries of power between individuals and platforms or governments.
