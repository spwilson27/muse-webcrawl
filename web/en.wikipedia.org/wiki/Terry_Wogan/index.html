Terry Wogan

Born: August 3, 1938, Limerick, Ireland
Died: January 31, 2016, Taplow, England
Occupation: Broadcaster, radio and television presenter

Sir Michael Terence Wogan (August 3, 1938 – January 31, 2016) was an Irish radio and television broadcaster who worked for the BBC in the United Kingdom for most of his career. Between 1993 and his semi-retirement in 2009, his weekday morning programme Wake Up to Wogan on BBC Radio 2 regularly drew an audience of around eight million, making him the most listened-to radio broadcaster in Europe.

Career

Wogan began his career with Raidió Teilifís Éireann in Ireland before joining the BBC in the 1960s, presenting on the Light Programme and then BBC Radio 1 and Radio 2. His television work included the chat show Wogan (1982–1992), the game show Blankety Blank, and Come Dancing. He was the BBC's television commentator for the Eurovision Song Contest from 1971 to 2008, famed for his gently sardonic commentary, and a central figure in the annual Children in Need telethon from its launch in 1980. After leaving the breakfast show in 2009 he presented Weekend Wogan on Radio 2 until shortly before his death from cancer in 2016.

Honours

Wogan was appointed an honorary Knight Commander of the Order of the British Empire (KBE) in 2005; as he held dual Irish and British citizenship, he was entitled to use the title "Sir".
