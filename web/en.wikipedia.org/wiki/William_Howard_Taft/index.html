William Howard Taft

William Howard Taft (September 15, 1857 – March 8, 1930) was the 27th president of the United States (1909–1913) and the 10th chief justice of the United States (1921–1930), the only person to have held both offices.

Born: September 15, 1857, Cincinnati, Ohio
Died: March 8, 1930, Washington, D.C.
President: 1909–1913
Chief Justice: 1921–1930
Party: Republican

Early life

Born in Cincinnati into the prominent Taft family, son of Alphonso Taft, he graduated from Yale and Cincinnati Law School. He became solicitor general, a federal judge, Governor-General of the Philippines, and Secretary of War under Theodore Roosevelt.

Presidency

Elected in 1908, Taft pursued trust-busting, tariff reform (Payne–Aldrich Tariff), and Dollar Diplomacy. His split with Roosevelt led the Republicans to divide in 1912, allowing Woodrow Wilson to win.

Chief Justice

Appointed by Warren G. Harding in 1921, Taft led the Supreme Court, promoted judicial reform, and oversaw construction of the Supreme Court building. He died in 1930 and is buried at Arlington National Cemetery.
