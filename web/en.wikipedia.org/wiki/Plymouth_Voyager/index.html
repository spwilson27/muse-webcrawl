Plymouth Voyager

Plymouth Voyager
Manufacturer: Plymouth (Chrysler)
Production: 1974–2000 (nameplate)
Class: Full-size van; minivan

The Plymouth Voyager is a nameplate used by the Plymouth division of Chrysler on two distinct vehicle lines. From 1974 to 1983 it designated a full-size passenger van, a rebadged variant of the Dodge Sportsman. From 1984 to 2000 it was used for the Chrysler minivans sold by Plymouth, alongside the Dodge Caravan, which together effectively created the modern minivan segment.

Minivan (1984–2000)

Introduced for the 1984 model year on Chrysler's front-wheel-drive S platform, the Voyager was offered in standard and extended Grand Voyager forms. It featured sliding side doors, removable seats, and car-like handling that distinguished it from truck-based vans. Generations followed in 1991, 1996, and a final run for 2000; after the Plymouth brand was discontinued, the Voyager name briefly continued under Chrysler in some markets.

Legacy

The Voyager and its Dodge and Chrysler siblings are credited with inventing the minivan market in North America, selling in the millions and reshaping family transportation. The name was revived by Chrysler for a value-oriented minivan in 2020.
