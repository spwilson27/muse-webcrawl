Sound effects library

A sound effects library is a collection of recorded sound effects made available, usually under licence, for use in film, television, radio, video games, theatre and other media productions. Libraries range from general collections covering thousands of everyday sounds to specialist sets devoted to particular subjects such as animals, vehicles, weapons or historical environments.

History

Broadcasters such as the BBC began assembling sound archives in the early days of radio, when effects were needed for drama productions. Commercial libraries grew with the record and compact disc era: the BBC Sound Effects library, the Hollywood Edge, and Sound Ideas (whose Series 4000 and General HD collections became industry standards) supplied editors with catalogued recordings on disc. The internet transformed distribution, and libraries are now sold or subscribed to as downloadable digital files, organised by metadata keywords and auditioned through asset-management software.

Licensing and use

Most commercial libraries are supplied royalty-free for synchronisation in productions after an initial purchase or subscription, though the sounds themselves may not be resold or redistributed as standalone files. Sounds from libraries are frequently edited, layered and processed by sound designers rather than used unmodified. Some individual library sounds have become famous through repeated use: the Wilhelm scream, first recorded for the 1951 film Distant Drums and archived in studio libraries, has been used in hundreds of films.

Notable libraries

Prominent sound effects libraries and archives include the BBC Sound Effects collection (tens of thousands of recordings, many released for public reuse), Sound Ideas, The Hollywood Edge (Premiere Edition), Pro Sound Effects, and the free and community-contributed Freesound database, whose recordings are shared under Creative Commons licences.
