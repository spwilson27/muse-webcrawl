Kavirajamarga

Kavirajamarga (Kannada: ಕವಿರಾಜಮಾರ್ಗ, "The Royal Road of the Poets", c. 850 CE) is the earliest surviving work on rhetoric, poetics and grammar in the Kannada language, written during the reign of the Rashtrakuta king Amoghavarsha I (Nripatunga). It is traditionally attributed to Amoghavarsha himself or to his court poet Srivijaya, and is regarded as the founding text of Kannada literature.

Contents

Modelled in part on Sanskrit works of poetics such as Dandin's Kavyadarsa, the text instructs aspiring poets on metres, figures of speech, the virtues and faults of composition, and the styles of earlier Kannada writers, several of whom are named and praised — evidence of a literary tradition that predates the surviving text.

Cultural significance

Kavirajamarga contains one of the most quoted geographic descriptions in Kannada literature, defining the Kannada-speaking land as extending "from the Kaveri to the Godavari". Its proud assertion of the Kannada language and region makes it an enduring symbol of Kannada identity, and its first publication in print in the 19th century helped spur the modern Kannada literary renaissance.
