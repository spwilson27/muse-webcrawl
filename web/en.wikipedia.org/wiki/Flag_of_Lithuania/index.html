Flag of Lithuania

Use: National flag and civil ensign
Proportion: 3:5
Adopted: 25 April 1918 (first); 20 March 1989 (re-adopted)
Design: A horizontal tricolour of yellow, green and red

The national flag of Lithuania consists of a horizontal tricolour of yellow, green, and red. It was adopted on 25 April 1918 during Lithuania's period of interwar independence (1918–1940). During the long post-WWII Soviet occupation, during which Lithuania was known as the Lithuanian Soviet Socialist Republic, the flag was changed to a red flag with a gold hammer and sickle. The tricolour was restored in 1988 as the national movement gathered strength, and was officially re-adopted on 20 March 1989, before full independence was declared in March 1990.

History

The three colours were chosen from folk textiles and the Lithuanian national awakening of the 19th and early 20th centuries. A commission including the artist Antanas Žmuidzinavičius and the historian Jonas Basanavičius settled the design in 1917–1918, after earlier proposals that featured the white knight Vytis on a red field were considered impractical for a civil flag. The state flag (historical flag), showing the mounted knight, was separately adopted in 2004 for use over state institutions.

Symbolism

Yellow symbolises the golden wheat fields of Lithuania and the sun, green represents the forests and countryside and the hope of the nation, and red stands for the blood shed in struggles for freedom and the courage of the Lithuanian people.

Protocol

Lithuanian law requires the flag to be flown at state institutions. National flag days include 16 February (State Restoration Day), 11 March (Restoration of Independence Day) and 6 July (Statehood Day). The official ratio of the flag was changed from 1:2 to 3:5 in 2004.
