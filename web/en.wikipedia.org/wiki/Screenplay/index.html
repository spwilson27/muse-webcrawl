Screenplay

A screenplay, or script, is a written work by screenwriters for a film, television show or video game. Screenplays can be original works or adaptations of existing pieces of writing, and unlike a stage play they are primarily a blueprint for a visual medium: they specify dialogue and action, along with scene headings, transitions and other technical cues used by the production.

Format

The standard industry format uses a monospaced font (usually 12-point Courier) with prescribed margins so that one page corresponds roughly to one minute of screen time. Elements include scene headings ("sluglines") such as INT. or EXT. followed by location and time of day, action lines, character names, dialogue, and parentheticals. A feature screenplay is typically 90–120 pages.

Types

A spec script is written on speculation, without commission, in the hope of being sold or optioned. A shooting script is the version used during production, revised and colour-coded as changes are made, and including numbered scenes. Writers may be commissioned to write an original screenplay, adapt a novel or other source, or rewrite another writer's work.

Structure

Mainstream screenwriting commonly follows a three-act structure, with a setup, confrontation and resolution, and influential manuals such as Syd Field's Screenplay (1979) codified turning points and page benchmarks. Writers are recognised in awards categories for best original screenplay and best adapted screenplay.
