Rubidium–strontium dating
Type: Radiometric dating method
Parent isotope: Rubidium-87
Daughter isotope: Strontium-87
Half-life of ⁸⁷Rb: approx. 49.2 billion years

Rubidium–strontium dating is a radiometric dating technique used to determine the age of rocks and minerals from the quantities of rubidium-87 (⁸⁷Rb) and strontium-87 (⁸⁷Sr) they contain. The radioactive isotope ⁸⁷Rb decays to stable ⁸⁷Sr by beta decay with a half-life of about 49.2 billion years.

Method

Rubidium substitutes readily for potassium in minerals such as micas and feldspars, while strontium substitutes for calcium. When a rock crystallises, its minerals take up different Rb/Sr ratios but the same initial ⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr ratio. Over time, ⁸⁷Sr accumulates in proportion to each mineral's Rb content. Plotting ⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr against ⁸⁷Rb/⁸⁶Sr for several minerals from the same rock yields a straight line — an isochron — whose slope gives the age and whose intercept gives the initial strontium isotope ratio.

Applications

The method is especially useful for dating old igneous and metamorphic rocks, some billions of years old, and for tracing the sources of magmas and crustal materials through their strontium isotope signatures. Difficulties arise because both elements are mobile during metamorphism and weathering, which can disturb the system; the isochron approach helps detect such disturbance. Rb–Sr dating of meteorites helped establish the age of the solar system at about 4.56 billion years.
