Political risk

Political risk is a type of risk faced by investors, corporations, and governments that political decisions, events, or conditions will significantly affect the profitability of a business actor or the expected value of a given economic action. Political risk can be understood as the likelihood that political forces will cause drastic changes in a country's business environment that adversely affect the profit and other goals of a particular business enterprise.

Types

Macro-level political risk

Macro-level risks affect all foreign actors in a given location: expropriation or nationalization of assets, currency inconvertibility, war, terrorism, civil unrest, sanctions, and sweeping changes to tax or regulatory regimes.

Micro-level political risk

Micro-level risks target particular firms, industries, or projects — for example selective regulatory enforcement, breach or repudiation of contracts by state entities, corrupt demands, or sector-specific windfall taxes.

Risk analysis

Political risk analysis seeks to forecast instability and policy change. Analysts combine qualitative expert judgment with quantitative indicators — measures of government stability, corruption, rule of law, socioeconomic conditions, ethnic tension, and democratic accountability — compiled into country-risk ratings by insurers, consultancies, and credit rating agencies. Corporations and lenders use such assessments when pricing investments, purchasing political risk insurance, or designing joint ventures and stabilization clauses to limit exposure.

Political risk insurance

Export credit agencies and multilateral insurers, such as the World Bank's Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency (MIGA), as well as private insurers, sell coverage against losses from expropriation, political violence, and currency transfer restrictions.
