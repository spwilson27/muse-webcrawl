Shiraoi, Hokkaido

Shiraoi (白老町, Shiraoi-chō) is a town located in Iburi Subprefecture, Hokkaido, Japan. Situated on the Pacific coast between Tomakomai and Noboribetsu, it had a population of roughly 16,000–17,000 in the 2020s. The town's name derives from the Ainu language, from shirauoi, meaning "place of many horse flies" or, in another interpretation, "rainbow".

Ainu heritage

Shiraoi is one of the best-known centres of Ainu culture in Japan. The former Ainu Museum (Porotokotan) stood on the shore of Lake Poroto, with reconstructed traditional dwellings (cise). In July 2020 the site was transformed into Upopoy (ウポポイ), the National Ainu Museum and Park, established as a national centre for the revival and development of Ainu culture. Its name means "singing together in a large group" in Ainu.

Economy

Fishing, agriculture and tourism anchor the local economy. Shiraoi is celebrated for Shiraoi beef, a brand of Japanese Black wagyu raised locally, and for seafood landed at its port. The town is accessible by rail on the JR Muroran Main Line and lies close to the hot-spring resort of Noboribetsu.

Geography

The town stretches from the Pacific shoreline to forested hills inland, near Lake Kuttara and Mount Horohoro. Rivers such as the Shiraoi River flow from the mountains to the sea, supporting salmon runs.
