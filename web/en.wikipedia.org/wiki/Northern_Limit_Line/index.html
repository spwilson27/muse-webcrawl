Northern Limit Line

The Northern Limit Line (NLL) is a disputed maritime demarcation line in the Yellow Sea (West Sea) between North Korea and South Korea. It was drawn unilaterally by the United Nations Command in August 1953, at the end of the Korean War, as the seaward extension of the Military Demarcation Line, running between the mainland and five South Korean–held islands off the North Korean coast: Baengnyeong, Daecheong, Socheong, Yeonpyeong, and Udo.

Status

South Korea treats the NLL as the practical maritime border and patrols up to it. North Korea has never accepted it, arguing that it was proclaimed without its agreement and lies too far north; in 1999 Pyongyang announced its own maritime demarcation line farther south, enclosing much of the area between the NLL and the five islands. The waters around the line are rich fishing grounds, especially for blue crab, and the crabbing seasons regularly bring the two navies into close proximity.

Clashes

The line has been the scene of repeated armed incidents. Naval battles near Yeonpyeong Island in 1999 and 2002 killed sailors on both sides. In March 2010 the South Korean corvette Cheonan sank near Baengnyeong with the loss of 46 sailors; an international investigation attributed the sinking to a North Korean torpedo, which Pyongyang denied. In November 2010 North Korean artillery shelled Yeonpyeong Island, killing South Korean marines and civilians. A further artillery exchange occurred near Baengnyeong in 2014, and tensions around the line remain a persistent feature of inter-Korean relations.
