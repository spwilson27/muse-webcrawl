Glossitis

Glossitis is inflammation of the tongue. It may occur on its own or as a manifestation of a wide range of local and systemic conditions. Glossitis typically causes the tongue to become swollen, red and sore; in many cases the dorsal surface loses its normal papillae and appears smooth and glossy — a change known as atrophic glossitis.

Causes

Causes of glossitis include:

  • Nutritional deficiencies — especially of iron, vitamin B12, folate, riboflavin and niacin; the classic glossitis of pernicious anaemia (Hunter's glossitis) is associated with the beefy-red, painful tongue of B12 deficiency.
  • Infections — bacterial, viral (e.g., herpes simplex) and fungal (oral candidiasis).
  • Allergic reactions — to toothpaste, mouthwash, foods or medications.
  • Irritation and injury — burns, rough teeth, dental appliances, alcohol, tobacco and spicy foods.
  • Systemic diseases and conditions — including coeliac disease, Sjögren syndrome (through dry mouth), amyloidosis and hormonal changes.

Types

Named varieties include geographic tongue (benign migratory glossitis), in which map-like smooth patches migrate across the tongue; median rhomboid glossitis, a smooth red patch in the midline of the posterior dorsum often associated with candidal infection; and atrophic glossitis, the smooth depapillated tongue of nutritional deficiency.

Treatment

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause — correcting deficiencies, treating infection, or removing irritants — together with symptomatic measures such as good oral hygiene, a soft diet and pain relief. Prognosis is generally good once the cause is addressed.
