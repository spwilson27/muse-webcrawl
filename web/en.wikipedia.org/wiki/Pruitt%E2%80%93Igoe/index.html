Pruitt–Igoe was a large public housing project in St. Louis, Missouri. Comprising 33 eleven-storey apartment buildings on a 57-acre site, it was designed by architect Minoru Yamasaki and opened in 1954 as housing for low-income residents.

Decline and demolition

Within a decade the complex suffered from severe maintenance failures, vacancy, and crime. Its demolition, begun in 1972, was televised and became an emblem of the failures of post-war public housing policy and high-rise modernist design; architecture critic Charles Jencks famously declared that modern architecture "died" with its demolition.

Interpretations

Later scholarship has emphasised funding formulas, racial segregation, and St. Louis's economic decline — rather than design alone — as causes of the project's failure. The 2011 documentary The Pruitt-Igoe Myth revisited the story through residents' accounts.
