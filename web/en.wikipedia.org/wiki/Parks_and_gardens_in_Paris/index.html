Parks and gardens in Paris

The Jardin du Luxembourg, one of the most famous gardens in Paris.

Paris today has more than 421 municipal parks and gardens, covering more than 2,000 hectares (about 4,900 acres). The city is ringed by two large woodland parks, the Bois de Boulogne to the west and the Bois de Vincennes to the east, each more than twice the size of New York's Central Park.

History

The oldest gardens include the Tuileries Garden, created by Catherine de' Medici in 1564 and later redesigned by André Le Nôtre, and the Luxembourg Garden, created for Marie de' Medici in 1612. The great expansion of Parisian green space came under Napoleon III and his prefect Baron Haussmann in the 1850s and 1860s, when the Bois de Boulogne and Bois de Vincennes were created, along with parks such as the Parc des Buttes-Chaumont and Parc Montsouris, designed by Jean-Charles Alphand in the English landscape style.

Modern parks

Twentieth- and twenty-first-century additions include the Parc de la Villette, designed by Bernard Tschumi on the site of former slaughterhouses; the Parc André-Citroën; the Parc de Bercy; and the Coulée verte René-Dumont (Promenade plantée), an elevated linear park on a disused railway viaduct that inspired New York's High Line.
