The Silmarillion is a collection of mythopoeic stories by the English writer J. R. R. Tolkien, edited and published posthumously by his son Christopher Tolkien in 1977. It recounts the history of Eä, the universe containing Middle-earth, from its creation to the end of the Third Age.

Contents

The book comprises five parts: the Ainulindalë, the creation myth; the Valaquenta, describing the Valar and Maiar; the Quenta Silmarillion, the main body, telling of the Silmarils, the wars of the Elves against the Dark Lord Morgoth, and tales such as Beren and Lúthien, Túrin Turambar and the Fall of Gondolin; the Akallabêth, the downfall of Númenor; and Of the Rings of Power and the Third Age.

Development

Tolkien worked on the stories from 1914 until his death in 1973; publishers rejected an early version in 1937 as too obscure, and The Lord of the Rings was published instead. The 1977 text drew on decades of drafts later published in The History of Middle-earth.
