Treaty of Riga

Peace of Riga / Treaty of Riga
Signed: 18 March 1921, Riga, Latvia
Parties: Poland vs Soviet Russia and Soviet Ukraine
Context: End of Polish–Soviet War

The Peace of Riga, signed on 18 March 1921 between Poland on one side and Soviet Russia and Soviet Ukraine on the other, ended the Polish–Soviet War and fixed the eastern border of the Second Polish Republic.

Terms

The border was drawn east of the Curzon Line, giving Poland large territories inhabited mainly by Ukrainians and Belarusians; Soviet Russia recognised Polish possession and agreed to pay 30 million gold roubles (never fully paid) and return looted cultural property. Both sides renounced further claims and pledged non-interference, and the Soviets recognised Ukrainian SSR sovereignty in form, while Józef Piłsudski's federation plans and Symon Petliura's Ukrainian People's Republic were abandoned — Petliura's Ukraine was partitioned.

Aftermath

The settlement held until 1939, when the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact and the Soviet invasion of eastern Poland annulled it in practice; post-1945 borders shifted Poland westward instead. See Polish–Soviet War and Curzon Line.
