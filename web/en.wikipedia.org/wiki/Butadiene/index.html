Butadiene

1,3-Butadiene (often simply butadiene) is the organic compound with the formula CH2=CH−CH=CH2. It is a colourless gas that is easily condensed to a liquid. It is important industrially as a monomer in the production of synthetic rubber. The molecule can be viewed as the union of two vinyl groups. It is the simplest conjugated diene.

Although butadiene breaks down quickly in the atmosphere, it is nevertheless found in ambient air in urban and suburban areas as a consequence of its constant emission from motor vehicles. The name butadiene can also refer to the isomer 1,2-butadiene, which is a cumulated diene (an allene) of comparatively little industrial importance.

Production

Most butadiene is produced as a by-product of steam cracking of hydrocarbons to produce ethylene, from the C4 fraction of the cracker output. Other routes include catalytic dehydrogenation of n-butane (the Houdry process) and oxidative dehydrogenation of butenes. During the Second World War, butadiene was produced from ethanol in the Soviet Union and the United States, and from coal-derived acetylene in Germany.

Uses

Most butadiene (about 60%) is polymerised to produce synthetic rubbers: polybutadiene rubber, styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR), and nitrile rubber. It is also used to make ABS resins, adiponitrile (a nylon precursor, via hydrocyanation), chloroprene, and sulfolane. The Diels–Alder reaction of butadiene is a classic method for forming six-membered rings in organic synthesis.

Safety

Butadiene is flammable and forms explosive peroxide compounds on exposure to air. Long-term occupational exposure is associated with leukaemia, and the International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies 1,3-butadiene as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1). Storage typically requires inhibitors such as tert-butylcatechol to prevent polymerisation and peroxide formation.
