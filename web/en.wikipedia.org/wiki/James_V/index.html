Minority

James V (10 April 1512 – 14 December 1542) was King of Scotland from 1513 until his death. He succeeded his father James IV, killed at Flodden, at seventeen months old. His long minority was dominated by the rivalry of his mother Margaret Tudor and the pro-French regent John Stewart, Duke of Albany, and then by the domination of the Douglas earls of Angus, who kept the young king a virtual prisoner until he escaped in 1528 and exiled the Douglases.

Personal rule

James V ruled energetically and ruthlessly: he taxed the Church heavily with papal consent, founded the College of Justice (1532), made progresses through the Highlands and Isles to assert royal authority, and amassed a fortune through feudal casualties and ecclesiastical revenues. Legend remembers him as the "King of the Commons," wandering his kingdom in disguise as the "Gudeman of Ballengeich" to hear his subjects' grievances.

French marriages

To bind Scotland to France against Henry VIII, James married first Madeleine of Valois (1537), who died within months of arriving in Scotland, then Mary of Guise (1538), mother of his successor. The marriages brought large dowries and a brilliant French Renaissance court to Scotland, visible in the palaces he rebuilt at Stirling, Falkland, and Linlithgow.

Solway Moss and death

Refusing to follow Henry VIII into schism from Rome, James faced English invasion in 1542; his army was routed at Solway Moss (24 November 1542). Broken in health and spirit, he died at Falkland on 14 December, six days after the birth of his daughter Mary, Queen of Scots — on hearing the news he is said to have lamented of the Stewart dynasty, "It came with a lass, and it will pass with a lass."
