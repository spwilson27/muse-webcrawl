Pole dance

Pole dance combines dance and acrobatics centred on a vertical pole. Performers climb, spin, swing, and invert on the pole, holding poses that require significant strength, flexibility, and coordination. The form has roots in traditional practices such as Chinese pole acrobatics and the Indian sport of mallakhamb, in the hoochie-coochie dances of early twentieth-century American travelling shows, and in the striptease performances of mid-century burlesque and, later, strip clubs.

Styles

Modern pole dance spans a spectrum of styles. Pole fitness (or pole sport) emphasises athletic and gymnastic technique on static and spinning poles and is taught in mainstream studios; the International Pole Sports Federation has campaigned for recognition as a sport, and the discipline was granted observer status by the Global Association of International Sports Federations in 2017. Artistic pole blends dance movement, floorwork, and storytelling; exotic pole retains the sensual style, high heels, and floorwork of its club origins. Major competitions include the World Pole Sport & Fitness Championships and national events such as Pole Sport Organization competitions in the United States.

Technique

Moves are categorised as spins, climbs, sits, inversions, and holds (such as the flag and the ayesha). Skin contact provides much of the grip, so practitioners typically wear shorts and sleeveless tops; grip aids such as chalk or resin may be used. Poles are usually chrome, stainless steel, brass, or powder-coated, in diameters of 40–50 mm, and may be static or free-spinning. As with gymnastics, training progresses through conditioning and graded tricks to limit falls and overuse injuries.
