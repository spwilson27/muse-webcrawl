The Stirling Prize is an annual prize for excellence in architecture, presented by the Royal Institute of British Architects (RIBA) since 1996. Named after the architect James Stirling (1926–1992), it is awarded to the architects of the building judged to have made the greatest contribution to British architecture that year; eligible buildings are in the United Kingdom or by RIBA members elsewhere in the European Union. It is considered the most prestigious architecture award in the UK and is often described as the equivalent of the Booker Prize or Turner Prize for architecture.

Notable winners

Early winners included the Centenary Building, University of Salford (Stephen Hodder, 1996) and Lord's Media Centre (Future Systems, 1999). Later winners include 30 St Mary Axe ("the Gherkin", Foster + Partners, 2004), the Scottish Parliament Building (2005), the Everyman Theatre, Liverpool (2014), Goldsmith Street council housing in Norwich (2019) — the first social housing scheme to win — the Elizabeth line (2024), and Appleby Blue Almshouse (2025). Zaha Hadid won twice consecutively (2010, 2011), and Foster + Partners has won three times.

Selection

The winner is chosen from the winners of the RIBA National Awards via a shortlist, traditionally of six buildings, judged by a jury chaired by the RIBA president. Winners receive a cash prize (currently £20,000).
