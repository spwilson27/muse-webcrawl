I Am Prepared to Die

"I Am Prepared to Die" is the name given to the three-hour speech given by Nelson Mandela on 20 April 1964 from the dock of the defendant's stand at the Rivonia Trial in Pretoria, South Africa. The speech is so named because it ends with the words "I am prepared to die". Despite what was widely expected, Mandela and his co-accused were not sentenced to death but to life imprisonment.

Mandela began the speech by saying he had been among the founders of Umkhonto we Sizwe, the armed wing of the African National Congress (ANC), and that he had helped plan acts of sabotage against the South African government. He defended the ANC's decision to move beyond purely non-violent protest after the Sharpeville massacre of 1960 and the banning of the ANC, arguing that all lawful channels of protest had been closed to the African people.

Background

The Rivonia Trial took place in South Africa between 9 October 1963 and 12 June 1964. Ten leaders of the African National Congress were tried for 221 acts of sabotage designed to "ferment violent revolution". Mandela had already been sentenced to five years' imprisonment in 1962 for incitement and for leaving the country illegally. The trial took its name from Rivonia, the suburb of Johannesburg where many of the accused had been arrested at Liliesleaf Farm in July 1963.

The speech

Rather than testifying and being cross-examined, Mandela chose to make a statement from the dock. The speech traced his life and political development, the history of the ANC's struggle, the systematic discrimination faced by Black South Africans in education, employment, land ownership and political rights, and the reasoning behind the turn to sabotage. Mandela was careful to deny that the ANC had acted under the direction of the Communist Party or any foreign power.

The speech concluded with words that have since become famous:

During my lifetime I have dedicated myself to this struggle of the African people. I have fought against white domination, and I have fought against black domination. I have cherished the ideal of a democratic and free society in which all persons live together in harmony and with equal opportunities. It is an ideal which I hope to live for and to achieve. But if needs be, it is an ideal for which I am prepared to die.

Outcome and legacy

On 11 June 1964 the accused were found guilty. The next day Mandela and seven co-accused were sentenced to life imprisonment rather than the death penalty, which the speech may have helped to avert by making the trial an international cause célèbre. Mandela spent 27 years in prison, most of them on Robben Island. The speech is considered one of the great speeches of the twentieth century and has been widely quoted; a recording of parts of it has been preserved, and the phrase "I am prepared to die" has become a symbol of resistance to apartheid.
