Hoshana Rabbah

Hoshana Rabbah (Aramaic: הושענא רבה, "Great Hoshana/Supplication") is the seventh day of the Jewish holiday of Sukkot, falling on the 21st day of the month of Tishrei. It is marked by a synagogue service in which seven circuits are made around the bimah with the lulav and etrog, accompanied by the recitation of the Hoshanot prayers, and by the ritual beating of willow branches.

Rituals

Following the custom of the Temple in Jerusalem, where willow branches were placed around the altar, worshippers on Hoshana Rabbah carry a bundle of five willow branches (aravot) and beat them against the ground or a surface, typically five times, so that some leaves fall off. Extra psalms and supplications, including Psalm 27 recitations and kabbalistic prayers, are added, and the shofar may be sounded. Many have the custom of staying awake the preceding night studying Torah, reading Deuteronomy, the Psalms, and, in Sephardic and kabbalistic tradition, the Zohar.

Significance

According to tradition, while the judgment for the year is sealed on Yom Kippur, it is not delivered until Hoshana Rabbah, giving a final opportunity for repentance; the day is thus treated as a kind of final Day of Judgment, and the greeting "a good final sealing" is exchanged. The Midrash describes God's judgment regarding water for the coming year being completed on this day. The following day is Shemini Atzeret, and the evening terminating Hoshana Rabbah begins the holiday of Simchat Torah in the diaspora sequence after Shemini Atzeret.
