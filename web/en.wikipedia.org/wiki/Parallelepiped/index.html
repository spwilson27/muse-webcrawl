Parallelepiped

A parallelepiped is a three-dimensional figure formed by six parallelograms. It is the three-dimensional analogue of the parallelogram, just as the cube is a special case. Any parallelepiped can be described as the image of a unit cube under an affine transformation, or equivalently as the set of points spanned by three vectors from a common origin.

Properties

A parallelepiped has 6 faces, 12 edges, and 8 vertices. Opposite faces are parallel and congruent. Its volume equals the absolute value of the scalar triple product of the three edge vectors meeting at one vertex. Special cases include the cube (all edges equal, all angles right), the rectangular cuboid, and the rhombohedron (all edges equal).

Applications

Parallelepipeds arise in crystallography, where the unit cell of a crystal lattice is a parallelepiped, and in physics in the computation of flux and in finite-element meshes.
