Prince consort

A prince consort is the husband of a ruling queen regnant who is himself a prince but not a king. Because most monarchies historically reserved the title of king for a male sovereign, the husband of a female sovereign usually receives a princely title instead, even though he shares his wife's station socially.

Examples

The best-known British example is Prince Albert, husband of Queen Victoria, who was formally granted the title Prince Consort in 1857 — the only British holder of that exact title as a formal style. Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, husband of Elizabeth II, was a prince consort in function though styled by his dukedom and as a prince of the United Kingdom.

Other examples include Prince Henrik of Denmark, husband of Margrethe II; Prince Claus of the Netherlands, husband of Queen Beatrix; and Prince George of Denmark, husband of Queen Anne.

Related titles

The female equivalent for the husband of a king is a queen consort. In a few cases a queen's husband has been made king jure uxoris, notably in Spain and Portugal, where Ferdinand VII-era and Portuguese practice allowed the husband to be styled king once the couple had an heir.
