National Council (Switzerland)

National Council
Type: Lower house of the Federal Assembly
Seats: 200
Meeting place: Federal Palace, Bern
Election system: Proportional representation

The National Council (German: Nationalrat; French: Conseil national; Italian: Consiglio nazionale; Romansh: Cussegl naziunal) is the larger chamber of the Federal Assembly of Switzerland, representing the Swiss people. It has 200 seats, apportioned among the cantons in proportion to their population.

Composition and elections

Members of the National Council are elected for four-year terms by proportional representation in the cantons, which serve as constituencies. Every canton is entitled to at least one seat. Zurich, the most populous canton, elects the most members. Swiss citizens aged 18 and over are eligible to vote and to stand for election.

Role

Together with the 46-member Council of States, which represents the cantons, the National Council exercises federal legislative power. The two chambers have equal powers; draft legislation must pass both. Sitting together as the United Federal Assembly, the two councils elect the members of the Federal Council, the federal chancellor and, in wartime, a general.
