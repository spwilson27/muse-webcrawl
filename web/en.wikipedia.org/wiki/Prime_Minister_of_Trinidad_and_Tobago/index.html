The Prime Minister of Trinidad and Tobago is the head of government of the Republic of Trinidad and Tobago. Appointed by the president from among the members of the House of Representatives — by convention the leader of the party or coalition commanding a majority — the prime minister leads the cabinet, which is collectively responsible to parliament. The role was titled Chief Minister before independence and Premier between 1959 and 1962.

History

Eric Williams, historian and founder of the People's National Movement (PNM), became the country's first prime minister at independence from Britain on 31 August 1962 and held the post until his death in 1981. He was succeeded by George Chambers. The opposition National Alliance for Reconstruction under A. N. R. Robinson won a landslide in 1986; Robinson's government survived an attempted coup by the Jamaat al Muslimeen in 1990, during which the prime minister was held hostage in parliament. Basdeo Panday became the first Indo-Trinidadian prime minister in 1995, followed by Patrick Manning (2001–10), Kamla Persad-Bissessar — the country's first female prime minister (2010–15) — and Keith Rowley of the PNM, who took office on 9 September 2015 and won re-election in 2020.

Residence and office

The prime minister's official residence is in St Ann's, Port of Spain, alongside the President's House; the Office of the Prime Minister is also located there. Trinidad and Tobago became a republic in 1976, replacing the governor-general with a president as head of state, but the prime minister's position and powers continued essentially unchanged.
