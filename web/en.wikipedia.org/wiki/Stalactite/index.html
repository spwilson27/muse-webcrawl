Stalactite

A stalactite is a mineral formation that hangs from the ceiling of caves, hot springs, or man-made structures, produced by the precipitation of minerals from water dripping through the ceiling. The corresponding formation rising from the cave floor is a stalagmite; the two may join to form a column.

Limestone stalactites in a show cave

Formation

Rainwater enriched with carbon dioxide dissolves calcium carbonate from limestone as calcium bicarbonate; when the solution seeps into a cave and degasses CO2, calcite is redeposited. Each new drip leaves a minute ring of calcite, first forming a hollow "soda straw" that thickens into the familiar icicle shape. Growth is slow — commonly around a millimetre per decade to a few millimetres per year.

Types

Stalactites also form in lava tubes, from ice (seasonal icicles), and in concrete structures, where calcium hydroxide leaching produces fast-growing calthemites. The word derives from the Greek stalaktites, "dripping".
