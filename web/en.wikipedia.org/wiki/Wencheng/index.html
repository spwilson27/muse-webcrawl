Wencheng

Princess Wencheng (Tibetan: Mun-chang Kung-co; c. 623–680) was a member of the imperial clan of Tang dynasty China who was married to Songtsen Gampo, the 33rd ruler of the Tibetan Empire, in 641. Her marriage formed part of a diplomatic alliance between Tang China and Tibet, and she is revered in Tibetan tradition as an incarnation of the goddess Tara.

Background

After military clashes between Tang forces and Tibet in the late 630s, Emperor Taizong agreed to a marriage alliance. Wencheng, a daughter of a minor prince of the Li imperial family, travelled to Lhasa in 641 with a large retinue. According to Tibetan histories, she brought with her seeds, farming and weaving techniques, books, artisans, and — most famously — a revered statue of the Buddha as a twelve-year-old prince, the Jowo, which remains enshrined in the Jokhang Temple in Lhasa.

Legacy

Wencheng is credited in tradition with spreading Buddhism, Chinese calendrical science and agricultural methods in Tibet, although modern historians distinguish the legendary accretions from the historical record. Together with the Nepalese princess Bhrikuti, she is one of the two foreign queens of Songtsen Gampo celebrated in Tibetan Buddhism. Her story has been retold in Tibetan opera, Chinese drama and modern pageants, and she remains a potent symbol of Han–Tibetan relations.
