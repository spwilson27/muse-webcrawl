Higher Education Funding Council for England

Formed1993
Dissolved2018
HeadquartersBristol, England
SuccessorsOffice for Students; Research England

The Higher Education Funding Council for England (HEFCE) was a non-departmental public body of the UK Government responsible for distributing public funds for teaching and research to universities and colleges in England. It operated from 1993, when it succeeded the Universities Funding Council and the Polytechnics and Colleges Funding Council under the Further and Higher Education Act 1992, until its abolition on 31 March 2018.

Functions

HEFCE distributed several billion pounds of grant funding annually, allocated teaching funds largely by student numbers and subject costs, and funded research selectively through quality-related (QR) funding driven by the Research Assessment Exercise and later the Research Excellence Framework (REF), which it administered jointly with the funding bodies of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. It also monitored the financial health and accountability of the institutions it funded.

Abolition

Under the Higher Education and Research Act 2017, HEFCE's functions were divided between a new regulator, the Office for Students (OfS), which took over teaching funding and regulation, and Research England, within UK Research and Innovation, which took over research and knowledge-exchange funding from April 2018.
