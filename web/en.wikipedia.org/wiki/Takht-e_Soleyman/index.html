TypeArchaeological site / fire temple complex
PeriodsSasanian, Ilkhanid

Takht-e Soleyman (Persian: تخت سلیمان, "Throne of Solomon") is an archaeological site in West Azerbaijan, Iran, centred on a fortified Zoroastrian sanctuary built around a natural artesian lake on a volcanic hill. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2003.

History

The site's sacred fire temple, Adur Gushnasp, was one of the three most revered fires of Zoroastrianism during the Sasanian Empire (224–651 CE), associated with the warrior class. Sasanian kings were crowned at the sanctuary. The complex was sacked during the Byzantine–Sasanian wars and again during the Mongol invasions, but the Ilkhanid ruler Abaqa Khan rebuilt it in the 13th century as a summer palace.

Description

The oval fortified enclosure contains the remains of the fire temple, an Anahita temple, audience halls, and the deep crater lake whose mineral-rich waters were regarded as sacred. Nearby stands the hollow volcanic cone known as Zendan-e Soleyman ("Solomon's Prison"), an earlier sanctuary site.
