Life (video games)

Life in video games is a measure of how long a player character can survive before being removed from play, or in many designs the number of attempts a player has before the game ends. The concept is closely related to health, hit points, and the extra life or "1-up".

History

The lives system originated in arcade games of the late 1970s and early 1980s, where players typically began with three lives and could earn additional ones at score thresholds. Because arcade machines were designed to encourage repeated coin insertion, losing all lives usually meant a game over unless the player paid to continue.

Mechanics

A life is commonly lost when a character's health reaches zero, falls into a hazard, or is defeated by an enemy. In platform games, lives may also represent the number of respawns available at checkpoints. Many modern games have abandoned limited lives in favor of unlimited retries, quick respawning, or difficulty settings that preserve tension without forcing a full restart.

Extra lives

An extra life, often called a 1-up, is a power-up or reward that increments the player's life counter. Iconic examples include the green 1-up mushroom in the Super Mario series. Extra lives may be hidden in levels, awarded for collecting a set number of items, or granted for reaching score milestones.
