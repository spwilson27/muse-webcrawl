Rías Baixas

LocationGalicia, northwestern Spain
Principal ríasMuñoz/Muros y Noia, Arousa, Pontevedra, Vigo
OceanAtlantic Ocean

The Rías Baixas (Galician for "Lower Rías") are a series of four estuarine inlets on the southwestern coast of Galicia, Spain, in the province of Pontevedra and the southern part of A Coruña province. From north to south they are the Ría de Muros y Noia, the Ría de Arousa, the Ría de Pontevedra, and the Ría de Vigo. They are rías — drowned river valleys flooded by the sea as sea levels rose after the last Ice Age.

Geography and economy

The rías are sheltered, relatively shallow estuaries with a mild, rainy Atlantic climate, greener than most of Spain. Their nutrient-rich waters make them one of the world's most important areas for shellfish aquaculture: mussels, clams, cockles, and scallops are grown on floating rafts (bateas), and the ports of the rías support major fishing and canning industries. Vigo is the largest city and one of Europe's principal fishing ports.

The Cíes Islands, at the mouth of the Ría de Vigo, form part of the Atlantic Islands of Galicia National Park and are noted for their beaches and seabird colonies. The coastline is also a significant tourist destination, and the region gives its name to the Rías Baixas wine appellation, famous for Albariño white wines.
