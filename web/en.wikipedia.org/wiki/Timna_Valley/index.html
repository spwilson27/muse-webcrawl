Timna Valley


Solomon's Pillars in Timna Park

The Timna Valley is located in southern Israel in the southwestern Arava, approximately 30 kilometres (19 mi) north of the Gulf of Aqaba and the city of Eilat. The valley is celebrated for its striking desert scenery and for some of the oldest copper mines in the world, worked intermittently from the Chalcolithic period onward.

Copper mining

Copper ore has been mined in Timna since the 5th millennium BCE. Systematic excavation by Beno Rothenberg from 1964 onward revealed extensive Egyptian mining expeditions of the New Kingdom period (14th–12th centuries BCE), including a sanctuary of the goddess Hathor. Rothenberg's findings challenged the popular attribution of the mines to King Solomon, though later radiocarbon research indicated renewed production in the 10th century BCE in the era associated with the early Israelite and Edomite kingdoms. Mining continued under the Nabataeans and Romans, and briefly in the modern state of Israel from 1959 to 1976.

Geology and tourism

The valley's multicoloured sandstone has been sculpted into formations such as Solomon's Pillars, the Mushroom, and natural arches. Timna Park, one of Israel's major tourist attractions of the Negev desert, presents the mining history with reconstructed smelting camps, a life-size model of the biblical Tabernacle, and marked hiking trails.
