Operator: Soviet space program
Launch: 14 February 1972

Luna 20

Luna 20 was the second of three successful Soviet lunar sample return missions, part of the Luna programme. The robotic probe was launched from Earth orbit on 14 February 1972 and soft-landed on the Moon on 21 February 1972 in the Apollonius Highlands near Mare Fecunditatis (the Sea of Fertility), just north of the crater Apollonius, some 120 km from the site where Luna 16 had landed.

Mission

A drill on the lander collected samples of lunar regolith, obtaining about 55 grams of material. The ascent stage lifted off the lunar surface on 22 February 1972 carrying the sample capsule, and the sealed container was recovered on Earth on 25 February 1972, landing in Kazakhstan near the city of Dzhezkazgan. The returned samples came from a highland region of the Moon and complemented the mare samples returned by Luna 16 and Apollo missions.

Significance

The Luna 20 samples allowed scientists to compare highland and mare regolith composition. The mission demonstrated the reliability of the Soviet Ye-8-5 sample return design, which was flown again successfully as Luna 24 in 1976.
