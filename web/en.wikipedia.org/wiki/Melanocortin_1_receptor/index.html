Melanocortin 1 receptor

MC1R (melanocortin 1 receptor)

Also known as: MSHR (melanocyte-stimulating hormone receptor)

Gene: MC1R, chromosome 16q24.3 (human)

Family: G protein-coupled receptors (rhodopsin-like)

The melanocortin 1 receptor (MC1R), also known as the melanocyte-stimulating hormone receptor (MSHR), is a G protein-coupled receptor that binds melanocortin peptides including α-MSH and ACTH. It is expressed mainly on melanocytes and is the principal regulator of melanin production in skin and hair.

Function

Activation of MC1R raises intracellular cAMP, triggering melanocytes to switch production from the red/yellow pigment pheomelanin to the brown/black eumelanin, thereby darkening skin and hair and increasing protection against ultraviolet radiation. MC1R signalling also promotes DNA repair in melanocytes and modulates inflammation.

Genetics and pigmentation

Loss-of-function variants of MC1R are strongly associated with fair skin, freckling, and red hair — the "red hair colour" (RHC) phenotype, including the Arg151Cys, Arg160Trp, and Asp294His alleles. Because carriers tan poorly and burn easily, MC1R variants raise the risk of melanoma and other skin cancers; the gene is a classic example of human pigmentation genetics and of balancing selection across populations. In many mammals, homologous variation at this locus (the extension locus) controls coat colour.

Agonists

Afamelanotide, a synthetic α-MSH analogue that activates MC1R, is approved for the treatment of erythropoietic protoporphyria to increase pain-free light exposure.
