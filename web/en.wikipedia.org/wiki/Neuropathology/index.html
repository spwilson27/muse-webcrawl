Neuropathology

Neuropathology is the study of disease of nervous system tissue, usually in the form of either small surgical biopsies or whole brains obtained at autopsy. Neurosurgeons rely on the diagnoses provided by neuropathologists, who examine tissue samples to determine the nature of neurological disorders. Neuropathology is a subspecialty within the practice of pathology, and is closely related to the fields of neurology, neurosurgery, and neuroradiology.

Brain tumors

A major component of neuropathology is the clinical, radiographic, and histopathological diagnosis of brain tumors. Neuropathologists classify tumors of the central nervous system according to schemes developed by the World Health Organization (WHO), using microscopic examination of tumors together with immunohistochemistry and molecular genetic analysis. Important tumor groups include gliomas such as astrocytomas and glioblastomas, meningiomas, medulloblastomas, and metastatic carcinomas from systemic cancers.

Other areas

Neuropathologists also evaluate tissue in cases of neurodegenerative disease, such as Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, as well as inflammatory and demyelinating diseases such as multiple sclerosis, infectious diseases of the nervous system, and muscle and peripheral nerve biopsies used to diagnose neuromuscular disorders. Molecular testing, including analysis of IDH mutations and 1p/19q codeletion status, has become central to the modern practice of the discipline.
