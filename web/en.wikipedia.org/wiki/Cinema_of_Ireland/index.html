Cinema of Ireland

Filming on location in Ireland

The cinema of Ireland comprises films produced in Ireland, in both the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland, as well as the broader Irish film industry. Ireland has a long history as a filming location and, since the late twentieth century, a growing indigenous production sector supported by the Irish Film Board (now Screen Ireland), tax incentives, and studios such as Ardmore Studios in County Wicklow, founded in 1958.

Early history

Film production in Ireland began in the silent era; the Kalem Company of the United States shot a series of Irish-themed pictures on location around Killarney in the 1910s, including The Lad from Old Ireland (1910). Domestic production remained small for decades, constrained by limited finance, strict censorship under the Censorship of Films Act 1923, and competition from British and American imports. The state-supported Ardmore Studios and the short-lived Irish Film Board of the early 1980s attempted to build capacity.

Growth from the 1990s

The re-establishment of the Irish Film Board in 1993, together with the Section 481 tax incentive, fostered a new generation of filmmakers. Internationally noticed Irish films of the period include My Left Foot (1989), which won Academy Awards for Daniel Day-Lewis and Brenda Fricker; The Commitments (1991); In the Name of the Father (1993); and The Crying Game (1992), directed by Neil Jordan, who became one of Ireland's most prominent directors alongside Jim Sheridan and Lenny Abrahamson.

Contemporary cinema

Twenty-first-century Irish cinema has combined local stories with international co-productions. Notable works include The Wind That Shakes the Barley (2006), winner of the Palme d'Or at Cannes; the animated films of Cartoon Saloon, such as The Secret of Kells (2009), Song of the Sea (2014), and Wolfwalkers (2020); Room (2015); Brooklyn (2015); Sing Street (2016); and the Irish-language film The Quiet Girl (An Cailín Ciúin, 2022), the first Irish-language feature nominated for an Academy Award. Ireland also hosts major inward productions attracted by its landscapes, crews, and incentives, and the Irish Film & Television Awards (IFTAs) honour domestic achievement.
