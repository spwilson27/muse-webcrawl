The General Certificate of Education Advanced Level, usually abbreviated to A level or GCE A level, is a school leaving qualification offered in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, and by examination boards internationally. A levels are typically studied over two years after GCSEs and are the principal qualification used for admission to universities in the United Kingdom.

History

A levels were introduced in 1951, replacing the Higher School Certificate. For decades they were taken at the end of a two-year sixth-form course, with students usually specialising in three or four subjects. The Curriculum 2000 reforms split the qualification into AS (Advanced Subsidiary) and A2 units, a structure later partly unwound in England from 2015, when A levels were re-linearised: all examinations are again taken at the end of the course, and the AS became a standalone qualification.

Structure and grading

Subjects range across the sciences, mathematics, humanities, languages and the arts. Assessment is mainly by written examination, though some subjects include coursework or practical endorsements. Results are graded from A* (introduced in 2010 for exceptional performance) down to E, the minimum pass; work below that standard is graded U (unclassified). Universities make offers expressed in grades or in UCAS tariff points.

International use

British examination boards such as Cambridge International and Pearson Edexcel offer International A levels in many countries, and the qualification is widely recognised for university entry around the world. Variants of the A level are also used in a number of Commonwealth education systems.
