Herbert Charles Onslow Plumer, 1st Viscount Plumer (13 March 1857 – 16 July 1932) was a senior British Army officer who commanded the British Second Army during the First World War. He is remembered as one of the most successful British generals of the war, particularly for his meticulously planned victory at the Battle of Messines in June 1917. After the war he served as commander of the British Army of the Rhine and as High Commissioner of the British Mandate for Palestine.

Early career

Plumer was educated at Eton and the Royal Military College, Sandhurst, and was commissioned into the 65th Regiment of Foot in 1876. He served in Sudan in 1884, in Southern Africa, and commanded a mounted infantry column in the Second Boer War, taking part in the relief of Mafeking. By 1914 he was one of the most experienced corps commanders in the army.

First World War

Plumer took command of the Second Army in Flanders in May 1915 and held the Ypres Salient for most of the war. His methodical "bite and hold" approach — limited objectives, overwhelming artillery preparation, and consolidation against counter-attacks — contrasted with more ambitious breakthrough plans. At Messines in June 1917 the detonation of nineteen huge mines under the German positions, followed by a carefully rehearsed infantry assault, captured the ridge south of Ypres with comparatively limited losses. During the Third Battle of Ypres (Passchendaele) his Second Army took over the main effort in September 1917 and won a series of set-piece victories at Menin Road, Polygon Wood and Broodseinde before the offensive bogged down in mud. In the German spring offensive of 1918 Plumer was recalled from command of British forces in Italy to defend the salient.

Later life

Promoted field marshal in 1919 and raised to the peerage as Baron Plumer of Messines, he became Viscount Plumer in 1929. He served as High Commissioner for Palestine from 1925 to 1928. Plumer was noted for his white moustache, monocle and bluff manner, and for the unusual degree of trust he inspired in the soldiers he commanded. He died in London in 1932 and was buried in Westminster Abbey.
