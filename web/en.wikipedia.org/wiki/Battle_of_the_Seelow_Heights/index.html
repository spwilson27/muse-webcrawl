The Battle of the Seelow Heights (German: Schlacht um die Seelower Höhen) was part of the Seelow–Berlin Offensive Operation (16 April – 2 May 1945). A pitched battle lasting three days, it saw some 1 million Soviet soldiers of Marshal Georgy Zhukov's 1st Belorussian Front attack the position known as the "Gates of Berlin" defended by about 100,000 German soldiers of Army Group Vistula under General Gotthard Heinrici.

Soviet artillery during the assault on the Seelow Heights.

Background

The Seelow Heights, rising some 90 metres above the Oderbruch floodplain east of Berlin, were the last major natural barrier before the capital. The Red Army had held bridgeheads over the Oder at Küstrin since February 1945. Stalin, anxious to take Berlin before the Western Allies, set Zhukov in competition with Marshal Ivan Konev's 1st Ukrainian Front approaching from the south.

The battle

Zhukov opened the assault before dawn on 16 April with one of the largest artillery bombardments of the war — some 9,000 guns — and 140 anti-aircraft searchlights meant to blind the defenders. But Heinrici had pulled his troops back to the second and third lines on the heights the night before, so the bombardment largely fell on empty positions. Soviet infantry and armour, advancing over the waterlogged plain and congested in the narrow breakthrough sector, suffered heavy casualties. Furious at the delay, Stalin authorised Konev to turn north toward Berlin. Only on 19 April, after committing his tank armies into the battle, did Zhukov break through the last German line; German losses effectively destroyed the 9th Army's ability to defend Berlin itself.

Aftermath

Soviet casualties in the operation were at least 30,000 killed; German losses were comparable. The heights taken, the road to Berlin lay open, and the Battle of Berlin began within days; the capital fell on 2 May 1945.
