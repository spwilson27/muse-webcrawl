Bian lian

A bian lian performer changes masks in a fraction of a second.

Bian lian (simplified Chinese: 变脸; traditional Chinese: 變臉; pinyin: biàn liǎn, "face-changing") is an ancient Chinese dramatic art that forms part of Sichuan opera. Performers wear vividly coloured silk masks and, in a split second, switch from one mask to another with a movement of a fan, a breath, or a flick of the head, revealing a new expression while the audience barely perceives the change.

Technique

The methods of bian lian are a closely guarded family secret, traditionally passed down only to male heirs within acting families. The masks are painted on layers of silk or similar material worn over the face, attached by fine threads or mechanisms hidden in the costume; techniques include blowing powder from the palm, dragging masks away with concealed lines, and drawing painted masks across the face. A skilled performer may change masks a dozen or more times in a single routine, each change reflecting a shift in the character's emotions.

History and protection

Face-changing is thought to have developed in Sichuan opera during the late Qing dynasty, drawing on older folk practices. In 2006 the secret of bian lian was listed among China's national intangible cultural heritage items, and China has sought to protect the technique from disclosure abroad, even as celebrated performers — such as the actor Hu Bingqing and the "King of Face-Changing" Peng Denghuai — have performed it internationally.
