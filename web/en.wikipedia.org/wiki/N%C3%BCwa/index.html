Nüwa (Chinese: 女媧), also spelled Nügua, is a mother goddess in Chinese mythology, credited with creating humanity and repairing the pillar of heaven. She is traditionally depicted with a human head and the body of a serpent, and is often shown intertwined with her brother and husband Fuxi, the two holding a compass and set square symbolising cosmic order.

Myths

According to the myths, after Pangu separated heaven and earth, Nüwa moulded figures from yellow clay and gave them life, creating human beings; later figures were made more quickly by dragging a rope through mud, explaining the distinction in some tellings between nobles and commoners. In another famous tale, the water god Gonggong shattered Mount Buzhou, one of the pillars holding up the sky, causing floods and chaos. Nüwa smelted five coloured stones to patch the heavens, cut the legs from a giant tortoise to serve as new pillars, and stemmed the floodwaters with reed ashes.

Worship

Nüwa is honoured as an ancestress of humankind and a protectress of marriage. Major temples dedicated to her exist in She County, Hebei, and she remains a prominent figure in Chinese folk religion, literature, and art.
