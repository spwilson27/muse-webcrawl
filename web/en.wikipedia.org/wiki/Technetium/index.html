Technetium

SymbolTc
Atomic number43
Group7 (transition metal)
AppearanceShiny grey metal

Technetium is a chemical element; it has symbol Tc and atomic number 43. It is the lightest element whose isotopes are all radioactive. Technetium is a silvery-grey transition metal resembling platinum and manganese. Nearly all of the technetium used is produced synthetically; only minute traces occur naturally in uranium ores as a spontaneous fission product.

History

The element filled the gap below manganese predicted by Mendeleev ("eka-manganese"). Several early claims—"davyum", "nipponium" and the 1925 claim of "masurium"—were not confirmed. In 1937 Carlo Perrier and Emilio Segrè at Palermo isolated the element from a molybdenum foil irradiated in the Berkeley cyclotron, making technetium the first element produced artificially; its name derives from the Greek technetos, "artificial". Minute natural quantities were later found in uranium ores and in red giant stars, whose spectra confirmed ongoing nucleosynthesis.

Properties and isotopes

Technetium is a hexagonal close-packed metal melting at 2,157 °C, chemically similar to rhenium. No isotope is stable; the longest-lived, 97Tc and 98Tc, have half-lives of about 4 million years, while 99Tc (half-life 211,000 years) is a major fission product in nuclear waste. The metastable isomer 99mTc, with a 6-hour half-life and a clean 140 keV gamma ray, is the most widely used radioisotope in diagnostic nuclear medicine, employed in tens of millions of scans annually; it is obtained from 99Mo generators at hospitals.

Applications

Beyond medical imaging, pertechnetate is an effective corrosion inhibitor for steel in closed water systems, though radioactivity limits its use. Technetium compounds also serve as catalysts and as superconductors at low temperature (Tc metal superconducts below 7.46 K).
