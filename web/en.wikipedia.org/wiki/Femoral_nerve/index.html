Femoral nerve

The femoral nerve is the largest branch of the lumbar plexus, arising from the dorsal divisions of the ventral rami of the second, third, and fourth lumbar nerves (L2–L4). It supplies the muscles of the anterior compartment of the thigh and provides sensory innervation to the skin of the anterior thigh and the medial side of the leg and foot through its terminal branch, the saphenous nerve.

Structure

Course

The femoral nerve emerges from the lateral border of the psoas major muscle, descends in the groove between the psoas and iliacus muscles, and passes beneath the inguinal ligament just lateral to the femoral artery (remembered by the lateral-to-medial order: nerve, artery, vein — "NAVY"). About 4 cm below the inguinal ligament it divides into anterior and posterior divisions.

Branches

  • Anterior division — muscular branches to pectineus and sartorius; cutaneous branches including the intermediate and medial cutaneous nerves of the thigh.
  • Posterior division — muscular branches to the four heads of the quadriceps femoris (rectus femoris, vastus lateralis, vastus medialis, vastus intermedius); articular branches to the hip and knee joints; and the saphenous nerve, the nerve's terminal sensory branch.

The saphenous nerve descends with the femoral vessels through the adductor canal, becomes superficial on the medial side of the knee, and supplies skin on the medial side of the leg and foot as far as the ball of the big toe.

Function

Motor fibres of the femoral nerve supply the knee extensors (quadriceps femoris), the hip flexors (iliacus, pectineus, and — via its branch from the plexus — psoas), and sartorius. Sensory fibres supply the skin of the front of the thigh and, via the saphenous nerve, the medial leg and foot. The nerve also mediates the patellar (knee-jerk) reflex, tested clinically at the L3–L4 level.

Clinical significance

Injury to the femoral nerve — from pelvic fracture, hip surgery, retroperitoneal haematoma, diabetes, or entrapment — causes weakness of knee extension and hip flexion, wasting of the quadriceps, loss of the knee reflex, and sensory loss over the anterior thigh and medial leg. Femoral nerve block is a common regional anaesthetic technique for surgery on the front of the thigh and knee. Stretching the nerve by extending the hip with the knee flexed (the femoral nerve stretch or "reverse straight-leg raise" test) reproduces pain in upper lumbar radiculopathy.
