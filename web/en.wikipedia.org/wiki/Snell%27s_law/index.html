Snell's law

Snell's law (also known as the Snell–Descartes law or the law of refraction) is a formula used to describe the relationship between the angles of incidence and refraction when light or other waves pass through a boundary between two different isotropic media, such as water, glass or air.

Formulan₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂
Named afterWillebrord Snellius (1621)
FieldOptics

Statement

The law states that the ratio of the sines of the angles of incidence (θ₁) and refraction (θ₂) equals the ratio of the refractive indices of the second medium to the first: sin θ₂ / sin θ₁ = n₁ / n₂. It had been described earlier by Ibn Sahl (984) and was published by Snell in 1621; Descartes gave it wide circulation. Snell's law explains phenomena such as the apparent bending of a straw in water and predicts total internal reflection beyond the critical angle.
