The Diocese of London is a Church of England diocese covering much of Greater London north of the River Thames and a portion of Surrey. Its cathedral is St Paul's, and its bishop, the Bishop of London, is the third most senior bishop in the Church of England after the archbishops of Canterbury and York. The diocese administers its work through five episcopal areas — Two Cities (central London), Edmonton, Willesden, Stepney and Kensington — each with an area (suffragan) bishop.

History

A Bishop of London (Restitutus) is recorded at the Council of Arles in 314, but the diocese in its historic form dates from the Gregorian mission: Mellitus was consecrated its bishop in 604 and founded the first St Paul's. The diocese originally covered the East Saxon kingdom (London, Middlesex and Essex). Over the centuries its territory was reduced by the creation of new dioceses — Rochester, and in the 19th century St Albans (1877) and Chelmsford (1914) — leaving it with its modern, compact but intensely populous area. Historically the diocese also had jurisdiction over British congregations in northern and central Europe, now the Diocese in Europe (founded 1980), and in colonial America, where London exercised oversight of Anglican parishes until the creation of American dioceses after independence. Under its "Capital Vision 2020" and subsequent strategies the diocese reported church growth while national attendance declined, and in the 21st century it generated the "church planting" and resource-church movement led by Holy Trinity Brompton and others.

Organisation

The diocese comprises around 500 parishes grouped into deaneries and two archdeaconries. Its offices are at Causton Street, Pimlico. It also covers the Peculiars of Westminster Abbey and St James's Palace only in limited respects, those being Royal Peculiars.
