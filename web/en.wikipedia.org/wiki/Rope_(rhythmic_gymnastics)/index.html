The rope is one of the apparatus used in rhythmic gymnastics. Made of hemp or synthetic material, it may be knotted at the ends and is cut to a length proportional to the height of the gymnast. Rope routines emphasise leaps and jumps through the turning rope, together with swings, circles, throws, catches and figures of eight, all performed while the rope remains taut and in constant motion.

Technique

The fundamental skills of rope work include skipping (single and double unders), releasing one end of the rope and catching it, wrapping the rope around parts of the body, and performing echappé movements in which the rope is briefly let go and regained. Because the rope offers little visual "body" compared with other apparatus, routines foreground the gymnast's jumping ability and coordination; the apparatus must be handled without knots forming (other than the permitted end knots) and without the rope wrapping unintentionally.

History in competition

Rope was among the original apparatus of modern rhythmic gymnastics and featured in individual competition at World Championships from the 1960s. Under the rotation system used by the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG), four of the five apparatus — hoop, ball, clubs, ribbon and rope — are used in any Olympic cycle. Rope was dropped from the senior individual programme after the 2009–2012 cycle (its last senior World Championships appearance was 2010, and it was not contested at the London 2012 Olympics), but it remains in use in junior competition and in some national programmes.
