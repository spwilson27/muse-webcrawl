Astrid Lindgren Memorial Award

Astrid Lindgren Memorial Award (ALMA)
Country: Sweden
Presented by: Swedish Arts Council
First awarded: 2003
Reward: SEK 5 million

The Astrid Lindgren Memorial Award (Swedish: Litteraturpriset till Astrid Lindgrens minne) is an international children's literary award administered by the Swedish Arts Council. The world's largest award for children's and young adult literature, it is presented annually to authors, illustrators, oral storytellers, and reading promoters whose work is of the highest artistic quality and marked by the humanistic spirit of Astrid Lindgren.

History

The award was established by the Swedish government in 2002, the year of Lindgren's death, to honour the author of the Pippi Longstocking books and to promote interest in children's literature worldwide. It was first presented in 2003, when it was shared by the American author Maurice Sendak and the Austrian author Christine Nöstlinger. The prize sum of five million Swedish kronor makes it one of the richest literary prizes in the world.

Laureates

Laureates have included the authors Philip Pullman, Katherine Paterson, Shaun Tan (also an illustrator), Meg Rosoff, Jacqueline Woodson, and Jean-Claude Mourlevat, as well as organisations promoting reading, such as the South African project PRAESA and the Australian Indigenous Literacy Foundation. In 2023 the award went to the American author Laurie Halse Anderson, and in 2024 to the Indigenous Literacy Foundation of Australia.

Selection

Candidates are nominated by organisations and institutions around the world. The laureate is chosen by a jury of authors, illustrators, literary scholars, and librarians appointed by the Swedish Arts Council, and the award is traditionally announced in Stockholm and Bologna in the spring.
