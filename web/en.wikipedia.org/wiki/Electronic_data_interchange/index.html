Electronic data interchange

Electronic data interchange (EDI) is the computer-to-computer exchange of business documents — such as purchase orders, invoices and shipping notices — in a standard electronic format between business partners. EDI replaces paper-based document exchange, reducing cost, errors and processing time in supply chains.

Standards

Two standards dominate: UN/EDIFACT, developed under the United Nations and used internationally, and ANSI X12, used mainly in North America. Industry-specific variants include HL7 for health care and RosettaNet for electronics. Documents are transmitted over value-added networks or directly via internet protocols such as AS2. Modern implementations may use XML- or JSON-based formats, but the classical standards remain deeply embedded in retail, automotive, logistics and health-care systems.

History

EDI grew out of cargo documentation standardisation in the 1960s and work by the US Transportation Data Coordinating Committee. It spread in the 1970s and 1980s as large companies required suppliers to adopt it. Though web-based commerce and APIs have supplemented it, EDI remains a backbone technology of global trade, processing trillions of dollars of transactions annually.
