Sean Parker

Parker in 2011.

Sean Parker (born 3 December 1979) is an American entrepreneur and philanthropist. He co-founded the file-sharing service Napster in 1999, was the first president of Facebook, and co-founded the online engagement platform Causes and the enterprise communications company Airtime. Through the Parker Foundation and the Parker Institute for Cancer Immunotherapy, he has become a prominent funder of life-science research and public policy.

Born: 3 December 1979, Herndon, Virginia, U.S.

Occupation: Entrepreneur, philanthropist

Known for: Co-founding Napster; first president of Facebook; Parker Institute for Cancer Immunotherapy

Organisations: Napster, Plaxo, Facebook, Causes, Airtime, Parker Foundation

Career

Parker taught himself programming as a teenager and, at 19, co-founded Napster with Shawn Fanning; the service revolutionised music distribution before being shut down amid copyright litigation in 2001. He then co-founded the contact-management service Plaxo in 2002. In 2004 he joined Mark Zuckerberg's fledgling "Thefacebook" as its founding president, helping secure early investment from Peter Thiel and guiding the company's early growth before departing in 2005. As a partner at Founders Fund he backed Spotify, in which he took a board role and helped negotiate its licensing deals.

Philanthropy

In 2015 Parker launched the Parker Foundation with a $600 million commitment, and in 2016 announced the Parker Institute for Cancer Immunotherapy, a $250 million collaboration uniting major cancer centres. He has also funded research into allergies, autoimmune disease and malaria, and campaigned for criminal-justice and drug-policy reform.

In popular culture

Parker was portrayed by Justin Timberlake in the 2010 film The Social Network, a depiction he criticised as largely fictional.
