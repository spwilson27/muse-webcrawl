Indian pangolin

The Indian pangolin (Manis crassicaudata), also called the thick-tailed pangolin, is a pangolin native to the Indian subcontinent, found in India, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Pakistan and Bangladesh. Like other pangolins, it is covered in large overlapping scales made of keratin and feeds almost entirely on ants and termites, which it laps up with a very long, sticky tongue.

Description and behaviour

Adults measure roughly 84–122 cm including the thick tail and weigh 10–16 kg. The Indian pangolin is nocturnal and solitary, sheltering by day in burrows it digs with powerful claws. When threatened, it rolls into a tight ball. Females usually give birth to a single pup, which rides on the mother's tail.

Conservation

The species is listed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List and is included in CITES Appendix I. It is hunted for its meat and scales, which are used in traditional medicine, and is heavily trafficked both locally and internationally.
