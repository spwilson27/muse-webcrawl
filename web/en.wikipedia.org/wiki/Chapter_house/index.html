Chapter house

Chapter house
TypeMeeting room of a cathedral or monastery chapter
Typical locationEast range of the cloister
Notable examplesYork Minster, Wells Cathedral, Westminster Abbey

A chapter house or chapterhouse is a building or room in a cathedral or collegiate church in which meetings of the chapter (the body of clergy who administer the institution) are held. The term also applies to the corresponding room in a monastery, where the monks gathered daily to hear a chapter of the monastic rule read aloud — the origin of the name.

Architecture

Chapter houses were usually located on the east side of the cloister, near the church. In England, many were built to distinctive polygonal plans — octagonal at York Minster, Wells, Lichfield, and Westminster Abbey, decagonal at Lincoln — with a central pier (absent at York, whose wooden vault spans unsupported) supporting the vaulted roof. Stone benches ran around the walls, with a raised seat for the presiding officer.

Functions

Beyond business meetings, chapter houses hosted elections, legal proceedings, and burials of dignitaries. The chapter house of Westminster Abbey served as a meeting place of the House of Commons in the 14th century. In monasteries it was also the place where faults were confessed and discipline administered ("holding chapter").
