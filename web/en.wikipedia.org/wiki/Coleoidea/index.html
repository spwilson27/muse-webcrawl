Coleoidea

Coleoidea, or Dibranchiata, is one of the two subclasses of the class Cephalopoda, containing the octopuses, squid, cuttlefish and their relatives. Unlike the other subclass, the nautiloids (Nautiloidea), coleoids have an internal or absent shell, a mantle that envelops the body, and typically eight or ten appendages surrounding the mouth. They are characterised by complex eyes, chromatophore-based colour change, and advanced nervous systems; many are fast, active predators.

Evolution

The earliest certain coleoids date from the Devonian or Carboniferous periods. The extinct belemnoids, abundant in Jurassic and Cretaceous seas, were coleoids with internal, bullet-shaped shells. Modern coleoids are divided into two superorders: the Decapodiformes (squid and cuttlefish, with eight arms and two tentacles) and the Octopodiformes (octopuses and the vampire squid, with eight arms).

Anatomy

Coleoids swim principally by jet propulsion, expelling water from the mantle cavity through a funnel. Their shells are reduced: a chitinous pen in squid, a porous cuttlebone in cuttlefish, and almost wholly absent in octopuses. Most can eject ink from an ink sac to confuse predators, and many deep-sea forms are bioluminescent.

Intelligence and behaviour

Coleoids, especially octopuses, are noted for problem-solving, learning and camouflage abilities. Their genomes and capacity for RNA editing have attracted considerable scientific interest. Most species are short-lived, commonly one to two years, and are semelparous, breeding once before death.
