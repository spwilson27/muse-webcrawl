The Grand Chessboard: American Primacy and Its Geostrategic Imperatives is a 1997 book by the Polish-American political scientist Zbigniew Brzezinski, former National Security Advisor to President Jimmy Carter. It sets out a geostrategy for the United States in the post–Cold War world.

Argument

Brzezinski argues that Eurasia is the "grand chessboard" on which global primacy will be contested, and that American primacy depends on preventing any rival power or hostile coalition from dominating that landmass. He identifies four key regions — the West (Europe), the Middle (Russia and Central Eurasia), the South (the Middle East and South Asia) and the East (China and East Asia) — and distinguishes "geostrategic players" (France, Germany, Russia, China, India) from "geopolitical pivots" (Ukraine, Azerbaijan, South Korea, Turkey, Iran). His most quoted claim is that "without Ukraine, Russia ceases to be a Eurasian empire".

Recommendations and reception

The book recommends enlarging NATO and the European Union eastward, engaging China, and managing the "Eurasian Balkans" of Central Asia. It was praised as a lucid statement of American grand strategy and criticised as a blueprint for hegemony; it has been widely cited in debates over NATO enlargement and U.S.–Russia relations.
