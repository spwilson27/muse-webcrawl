Funds from operations

Funds from operations (FFO) is a measure used by real estate investment trusts (REITs) and other real estate companies to define the cash flow generated from their operations. Because real estate assets historically appreciate rather than depreciate, and because accounting rules require depreciation charges against earnings, net income is considered a poor measure of a REIT's operating performance. FFO adjusts net income to correct for this.

Definition

The definition most widely used was developed by the National Association of Real Estate Investment Trusts (Nareit). Under the Nareit definition, FFO is computed as net income (computed in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles), excluding gains or losses from sales of property, plus depreciation and amortization of real estate assets, and after adjustments for unconsolidated partnerships and joint ventures.

Adjusted funds from operations

Adjusted funds from operations (AFFO) refines FFO by deducting recurring capital expenditures needed to maintain the earning power of a property portfolio, such as tenant improvements, leasing commissions, and straight-line rent adjustments. Analysts often regard AFFO as a measure closer to distributable cash flow.

Use

Investors commonly compare REITs using the ratio of share price to FFO per share, analogous to the price-to-earnings ratio used for other companies, because FFO is viewed as a more faithful indicator of a property company's recurring operating results than GAAP net income.
