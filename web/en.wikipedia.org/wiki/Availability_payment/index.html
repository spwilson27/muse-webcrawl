Availability payment

An availability payment is a payment made by a public authority to a private concessionaire under a public–private partnership (PPP) contract, in return for making an infrastructure asset — such as a road, bridge, school, hospital or courthouse — available for use at an agreed standard of performance. Unlike a toll or user-fee concession, an availability-payment concession does not depend on demand or usage: the private partner is paid for the availability and condition of the facility, not for how much it is used.

Structure

Typically, a special-purpose company designs, builds, finances, operates and maintains the asset under a long-term contract (often 25–35 years). Once construction is complete and the facility passes acceptance tests, the public authority begins paying a periodic — usually monthly or quarterly — "unitary" or availability payment. The payment covers debt service, return on equity, and operating and maintenance costs. Payments are subject to deductions ("abatements") if the asset is unavailable or services fall below the specified performance standards; persistent failure can lead to default and termination.

Risk allocation

Availability-payment structures transfer construction, financing, lifecycle maintenance and availability risk to the private partner, while the public sector retains demand (usage) risk. This distinguishes them from "real toll" concessions, where the concessionaire's revenue depends on traffic, and from "shadow tolls", where the authority pays per user but bears volume bands. Lenders generally regard availability payments as relatively secure revenue, because they are backed by government appropriations or contractual commitments, though they remain subject to annual budget appropriation in some jurisdictions.

Use

The model is widely used in the United Kingdom's Private Finance Initiative and in social infrastructure and transport PPPs in Canada, Australia, and increasingly the United States, where projects such as Florida's I-595 corridor and the Port Miami Tunnel were procured on availability-payment terms.
