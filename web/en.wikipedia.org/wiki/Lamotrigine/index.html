Lamotrigine

Trade namesLamictal, others
ClassAnticonvulsant; mood stabilizer
RoutesBy mouth

Lamotrigine, sold under the brand name Lamictal among others, is a medication used to treat epilepsy and stabilize mood in bipolar disorder. For epilepsy it is used to treat focal seizures, primary and secondary tonic-clonic seizures, and seizures associated with Lennox–Gastaut syndrome. In bipolar disorder it is used mainly to treat and prevent depressive episodes.

Medical uses

Lamotrigine is approved for maintenance treatment of bipolar I disorder and for several seizure types. It is considered a first-line treatment for bipolar depression. Off-label uses have included post-traumatic stress disorder, borderline personality disorder, and neuropathic pain.

Side effects

Common side effects include headache, dizziness, drowsiness, nausea, and rash. Serious side effects include potentially life-threatening skin reactions such as Stevens–Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis, which is why the dose is increased slowly during initiation. Lamotrigine works by blocking voltage-sensitive sodium channels and inhibiting the release of glutamate.
