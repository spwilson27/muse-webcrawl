Vermiculite

Vermiculite is a hydrous phyllosilicate mineral that undergoes significant expansion when heated. It forms by the weathering or hydrothermal alteration of biotite or phlogopite mica.

Properties and expansion

When heated to around 300 °C, vermiculite exfoliates into worm-like strands — the name comes from the Latin vermiculare, "to breed worms". Commercially, crude ore is heated in furnaces to 900–1100 °C, expanding the flakes to 8–30 times their original volume, producing a lightweight, fire-resistant, absorbent material.

Uses

Expanded vermiculite is used as loose-fill insulation, in lightweight concrete and plaster, as a soil conditioner and hydroponic growing medium that retains water and nutrients, in packaging, brake linings, and as a carrier for fertilisers and pesticides.

Health concerns

The vermiculite mine at Libby, Montana, operated until 1990, was contaminated with tremolite asbestos, causing widespread asbestos-related disease among miners and residents and leading to a major environmental cleanup. Pure vermiculite itself is not asbestos, but deposits may be contaminated.
