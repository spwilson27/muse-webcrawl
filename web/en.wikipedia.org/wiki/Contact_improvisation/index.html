Contact improvisation

Contact improvisation is a postmodern dance form based on the physical principles of touch, momentum, shared weight and improvisation. Dancers maintain a moving point of contact between their bodies and explore falling, rolling, counterbalance, lifting and being lifted.

Origins

The form was developed in 1972 by American choreographer Steve Paxton, building on his work with Judson Dance Theater and studies of aikido and gymnastics. Performances titled Magnesium at Oberlin College are often cited as the first contact improvisation.

Practice

Practice happens in open sessions called jams, taught through exercises emphasizing reflexes, spherical space, "the small dance" of standing still, and safe giving and taking of weight. It is deliberately non-hierarchical — central to the postmodern dance ethos.

Spread

Disseminated by Paxton, Nancy Stark Smith and others, and documented in the journal Contact Quarterly (founded 1975), contact improvisation spread worldwide and influenced contemporary dance partnering technique.
