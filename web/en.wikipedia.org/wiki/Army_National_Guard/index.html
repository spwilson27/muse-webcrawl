Army National Guard

The Army National Guard (ARNG) is the land component of the United States National Guard and one of the reserve components of the United States Army. It is organised in all 50 states, three territories and the District of Columbia, and traces its origins to colonial militias formed in 1636.

Dual status

Guard units serve under their state governor for domestic duties such as disaster relief, but can be federalised by the President for overseas combat and support missions. The Army National Guard makes up roughly a third of the US Army's total force structure.

Organisation

The ARNG is administered by the National Guard Bureau, whose chief is a member of the Joint Chiefs of Staff. Its combat formations include eight infantry divisions and numerous brigade combat teams, alongside aviation, engineer and support units.
