Faber-Jackson relation

Named afterSandra Faber and Robert Jackson
Published1976
Applies toElliptical galaxies

The Faber-Jackson relation (often typeset Faber–Jackson relation) is an empirical power-law correlation between the luminosity of an elliptical galaxy and the velocity dispersion of its stars, discovered by Sandra Faber and Robert Earl Jackson in 1976.

Relation

A galaxy's luminosity L scales roughly as the fourth power of its central stellar velocity dispersion σ (L ∝ σ⁴). Brighter elliptical galaxies therefore show broader stellar absorption lines, indicating faster stellar motions within a deeper gravitational potential.

Applications

The relation provides a distance indicator for elliptical galaxies and, combined with galaxy radii, forms part of the fundamental plane used to study galaxy formation and evolution. Its spiral-galaxy analogue is the Tully–Fisher relation.
