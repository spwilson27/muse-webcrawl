Pic du Midi Observatory

LocationPic du Midi de Bigorre, Hautes-Pyrénées, France
Altitude2,877 m (9,439 ft)
Established1878
Telescopes2 m Bernard Lyot Telescope; coronagraphs

The Pic du Midi Observatory (French: Observatoire du Pic du Midi) is an astronomical observatory located at 2,877 metres on top of the Pic du Midi de Bigorre in the French Pyrenees. Construction began in 1878, and the observatory is part of the Midi-Pyrénées Observatory.

History

The Société Ramond began building a meteorological station on the summit in 1878, and the site gradually developed into an observatory for astronomy and atmospheric science. The harsh mountain conditions made early work heroic: observers wintered on the summit, and supplies arrived by mule and later by cable car. A television transmitter and radar station were added in the mid-20th century.

Instruments and research

The observatory's principal instrument is the 2-metre Bernard Lyot Telescope, installed in 1980 and still among the largest telescopes in France; it is equipped with the NARVAL spectropolarimeter, used to study stellar magnetic fields. The Pic du Midi is also famous for solar astronomy: Bernard Lyot perfected the coronagraph there in the 1930s, allowing observation of the Sun's corona without an eclipse, and the observatory's solar telescopes and the CLIMSO coronagraphs continue this tradition. Lunar observers at the Pic du Midi produced high-quality photographs and maps that were used in preparing for the Apollo missions.

Tourism

Reached by cable car from La Mongie, the summit is a major tourist attraction, famous for its panoramic views, its "night at the summit" visitor programme, and exceptionally clear skies; the surrounding area was designated an International Dark Sky Reserve in 2013, the first in France.
