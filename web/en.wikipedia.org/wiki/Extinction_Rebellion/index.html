Extinction Rebellion (abbreviated XR) is a global environmental movement that uses nonviolent civil disobedience to press governments to act on the climate crisis and biodiversity loss. Founded in the United Kingdom in 2018 by activists including Roger Hallam and Gail Bradbrook, it spread rapidly to dozens of countries through autonomous local groups united by shared demands and principles.

Demands

The movement's three core demands are that governments "tell the truth" by declaring a climate and ecological emergency; that they "act now" to halt biodiversity loss and reduce greenhouse-gas emissions to net zero by 2025 (in the UK formulation); and that they create and be led by citizens' assemblies on climate and ecological justice. Its symbol, the extinction symbol — an hourglass within a circle, designed by the artist Esp — represents time running out for the planet's species.

Actions

XR launched with a declaration of rebellion at Parliament Square, London, in October 2018. In April 2019 its protesters occupied five central London sites, including Waterloo Bridge and Oxford Circus, for eleven days; more than a thousand people were arrested, and the action is credited with helping prompt the UK Parliament to declare a climate emergency and legislate a net-zero target. Further "International Rebellion" waves followed in October 2019 and subsequently, alongside targeted actions against banks, insurers, media organisations and fossil-fuel infrastructure. Offshoots and successor campaigns, such as Insulate Britain and Just Stop Oil, emerged from the same activist networks.

Reception

The movement has been praised for rapidly raising the public salience of climate change and criticised for disruptive tactics, the burden of arrests on participants and aspects of its internal organisation. Police and lawmakers in the UK responded with expanded public-order powers in the Police, Crime, Sentencing and Courts Act 2022 and the Public Order Act 2023, legislation widely understood to target XR-style protest.
