Mandative

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The term mandative describes clauses and verb forms that express a demand, requirement, recommendation or wish that something be done. In English grammar, the mandative subjunctive is the use of the base form of the verb in a subordinate clause following verbs and expressions such as demand, insist, recommend, essential and important: for example, "They demanded that he leave at once" or "It is essential that she be informed."

Usage

The mandative subjunctive is the usual construction in American English. British English also uses it, though should + infinitive ("that he should leave") is a common alternative, and indicative forms are also found. The construction is recognisable by the bare infinitive form (especially be) and by the absence of third-person singular -s.

Related concepts

The mandative subjunctive is a survival of the Old English subjunctive mood. It is distinguished from the formulaic (irrealis) subjunctive in expressions like "God save the King" and from past-tense irrealis constructions such as "if I were you".
