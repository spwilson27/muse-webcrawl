Charnwood Forest is an upland tract of rocky hills, heath and ancient woodland in north-western Leicestershire, England, lying between Leicester, Loughborough, Coalville and Ashby-de-la-Zouch. Rising to 278 metres at Bardon Hill, it is a landscape of unusual character within the English Midlands, and is internationally famous for its Precambrian fossils — among the oldest evidence of complex life on Earth. Part of the area lies within the modern National Forest.

Geology and fossils

The hills of Charnwood are formed of some of the oldest rocks in England — volcanic and sedimentary rocks of the Precambrian (Ediacaran) period, about 600 million years old, poking through the surrounding Triassic lowlands. In 1957 schoolboy Roger Mason found in a quarry the frond-like fossil now called Charnia masoni, one of the first recognised fossils of a large, complex Precambrian organism; a similar fossil had been noticed the year before by Tina Negus. The discovery helped transform understanding of the origins of animal life, and Charnwood rocks contain a celebrated assemblage of Ediacaran fossils.

Landscape and history

The forest's rugged outcrops, bracken heaths and oak woods contrast with the surrounding farmland. Notable sites include Bradgate Park, the childhood home of Lady Jane Grey, the ruins of Ulverscroft Priory, and Beacon Hill, topped by an Iron Age hillfort and one of the highest points in the East Midlands. Quarrying of the hard Charnwood stone (including granite at Mountsorrel and Bardon) has been a major industry since the nineteenth century, and several large quarries remain active. Much of the countryside is protected, popular for walking, and rich in wildlife habitats.
