Michael I (Romanian: Mihai I; October 25, 1921 – December 5, 2017) was the last King of Romania, reigning from July 20, 1927 to June 8, 1930 and again from September 6, 1940 until his forced abdication on December 30, 1947.

Two reigns

Michael first came to the throne as a five-year-old when his father, Carol, was passed over after renouncing his rights; a regency governed in his name until Carol returned in 1930 and reigned as Carol II. When Carol abdicated in 1940 amid territorial losses, Michael, now 18, became king again, but real power lay with the dictator Ion Antonescu, who aligned Romania with Nazi Germany.

The coup of 1944 and abdication

On August 23, 1944, as Soviet forces approached, Michael carried out a royal coup: he had Antonescu arrested and declared Romania's switch to the Allied side, an act credited with shortening the war in southeastern Europe. After the war he resisted communist takeover, including a "royal strike" in 1945 refusing to sign government decrees, but in December 1947 the communist government forced him to abdicate — he later said, at the threat of violence — and the Romanian People's Republic was proclaimed.

Exile and return

Michael lived in exile mainly in Switzerland, working at various times as a farmer, stockbroker and commercial pilot, and married Princess Anne of Bourbon-Parma in 1948. After the fall of communism in 1989 he was at first barred from Romania, but in 1992 huge crowds greeted his visit; in 1997 his citizenship was restored and some royal properties were returned. He remained a respected public figure, addressing parliament on his 90th birthday in 2011, and died in Switzerland in 2017. His state funeral in Bucharest drew large crowds; he was buried at Curtea de Argeș beside other Romanian monarchs.
