Latvian Song and Dance Festival

The Latvian Song and Dance Festival (Latvian: Vispārējie latviešu Dziesmu un Deju svētki) is one of the largest amateur choral and dance events in the world, held in Riga, Latvia, roughly every five years since 1873. Tens of thousands of singers and dancers, many in folk costume, perform together, culminating in a massed-choir concert at the Mežaparks open-air stage. Together with its Estonian and Lithuanian counterparts, it is inscribed on UNESCO's list of Intangible Cultural Heritage.

History

The first festival in 1873, during the Latvian National Awakening, gathered 1,003 singers. It grew into a central institution of Latvian national identity, continuing through independence, the Soviet occupation — when repertoire was censored but the tradition sustained national feeling — and the "Singing Revolution" of the late 1980s, when mass singing helped drive the restoration of independence in 1991.

The festival today

The modern festival involves some 40,000 participants, including diaspora choirs, with a week of concerts, a dancers' grand performance at Daugava Stadium, a participants' parade through Riga, and the closing massed concert attended by audiences in the tens of thousands. The repertoire centres on Latvian folk songs and choral classics such as "Gaismas pils" ("Castle of Light"). A separate Latvian School Youth Song and Dance Festival is held in the intervening cycle.
