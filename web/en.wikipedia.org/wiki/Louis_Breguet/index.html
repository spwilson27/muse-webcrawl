Louis Charles Breguet (2 January 1880 – 4 May 1955) was a French aviation pioneer and aircraft designer, founder of the Breguet Aviation company, and one of the founding figures of French civil and military aviation. Descended from the famous watchmaking family of Abraham-Louis Breguet, he combined electrical engineering with flight, made the first successful helicopter-type flight in 1907, and went on to build some of the most widely used French aircraft of both world wars and the first generation of French airliners.

Early experiments and the gyroplane

Trained as an electrical engineer at Supélec, Breguet took over the family electrical works at Douai. Fascinated by vertical flight, he built with his brother Jacques and Professor Charles Richet the Breguet-Richet Gyroplane No. 1, which on 29 September 1907 lifted a man off the ground — the first time a rotary-wing craft had raised a pilot, though it was steadied by helpers and not in free flight. He turned to fixed-wing aircraft and in 1909 founded his aircraft works; his Breguet Type III and subsequent machines set speed and distance records, and in 1911 he carried eleven passengers on one flight.

Commercial aviation and major designs

The Breguet XIV of 1916 became one of the finest French reconnaissance-bombers of the First World War, built in the thousands and used postwar to open pioneer airmail routes for the Lignes Aériennes Latécoère. In 1919 Breguet founded the Compagnie des Messageries Aériennes, one of the airlines that later merged into Air France. His name attaches to the classic "Breguet range equation" used worldwide to calculate aircraft range. Later designs included the Breguet XIX record-breaker, the Wibault-built airliners, the Deux-Ponts ("Provence") double-deck airliner of 1949, and postwar maritime patrol designs continued by his company — eventually absorbed into Dassault as Breguet Aviation/Dassault-Breguet, producing the Atlantic patrol aircraft and the Alpha Jet.

Breguet was also a champion sailor, winning a bronze medal in sailing at the 1924 Paris Olympics. He died in 1955 and is commemorated by Louis Breguet Square in Paris and the Breguet school of aeronautics he helped found.
