Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute

The Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute (STRI, Spanish: Instituto Smithsonian de Investigaciones Tropicales) is a research institute of the Smithsonian Institution based in Panama. It is the only Smithsonian bureau based outside the United States, and is dedicated to understanding tropical ecosystems and their relevance to human welfare. STRI's facilities host some 1,400 scientists, students and visitors each year, and its best-known station is on Barro Colorado Island in Gatun Lake, in the Panama Canal watershed.

History

The institute's roots lie in a 1910 agreement under which the Smithsonian began biological surveys of the Canal Zone during construction of the Panama Canal. In 1923 Barro Colorado Island, isolated when the Chagres River was dammed to form Gatun Lake, was set aside as a nature reserve for scientific study — one of the earliest protected areas in the Neotropics. The station there was administered by a consortium of universities and agencies before passing to the Smithsonian in 1946. The institute was formally renamed the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute in 1966, and a 1976 agreement with the Republic of Panama, followed by the Torrijos–Carter Treaties, established its modern status.

Facilities and research

STRI operates a network of field stations and laboratories across Panama, including:

  • Barro Colorado Island and the surrounding Barro Colorado Nature Monument — a site of long-term forest monitoring, including a 50-hectare Forest Dynamics Plot established in 1980 in which every tree stem is mapped and measured.
  • Marine stations on both coasts — the Galeta Point laboratory near Colón on the Caribbean side and facilities at Naos Island and the former Bocas del Toro Research Station on the Pacific/Caribbean — enabling comparative study of two oceans separated by the Isthmus of Panama.
  • The Gamboa laboratories, Agua Salud project on forest hydrology and reforestation, the canopy crane network, and the Punta Culebra Nature Center for public education.

Research spans forest ecology, evolution, animal behaviour, marine biology, archaeology of pre-Columbian Panama, palaeoecology and climate change. STRI coordinates the Panama portion of the global ForestGEO network of large forest plots, and trains Latin American scientists through fellowships and courses.

Directors

Directors have included Martin H. Moynihan (first director, 1960s–1973), Ira Rubinoff (1973–2004), Eldredge Bermingham (2008–2018) and Joshua Tewksbury (from 2022).
