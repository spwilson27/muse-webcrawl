Maquis

Maquis may refer to several related concepts:

  • Maquis shrubland — a dense Mediterranean biome of evergreen shrubs (see below).
  • Maquis (World War II) — rural guerrilla bands of the French Resistance during the Second World War; see Maquis (World War II).
  • Spanish Maquis — guerrillas who resisted Francoist Spain after the Spanish Civil War.
  • Maquis in Star Trek fiction — a resistance movement of Federation colonists in the Demilitarized Zone.

Maquis shrubland

Maquis (French: maquis, Italian: macchia) is a shrubland biome in the Mediterranean region, typically consisting of densely growing evergreen shrubs such as kermes oak, strawberry tree, wild olive, mastic and rockroses, often intermixed with aromatic herbs. It develops on dry, rocky soils and in areas degraded by fire, grazing or the clearance of former oak woodland. The word comes from the Corsican macchia, meaning thicket; the rugged Corsican maquis was a traditional refuge for bandits, which is how the term came to be applied to guerrilla fighters who "took to the maquis".
