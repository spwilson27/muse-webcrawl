William Lamb, 2nd Viscount Melbourne


Born 15 March 1779, London, England
Died 24 November 1848 (aged 69), Brocket Hall, Hertfordshire
Office Prime Minister of the United Kingdom (1834; 1835–1841)

William Lamb, 2nd Viscount Melbourne (15 March 1779 – 24 November 1848) was a British Whig statesman who served twice as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, in 1834 and from 1835 to 1841. He is best known as the mentor of the young Queen Victoria.

Early life and marriage

Lamb was educated at Eton and Trinity College, Cambridge. In 1805 he married Lady Caroline Ponsonby, whose passionate affair with Lord Byron and her novel Glenarvon caused a public scandal; the couple separated in 1825. Lamb entered Parliament in 1805 and held junior office under Canning and others, serving as Chief Secretary for Ireland and later, under Earl Grey, as Home Secretary (1830–1834), where he dealt sternly with the Swing Riots and the Tolpuddle Martyrs.

Premierships

Melbourne succeeded Grey as Prime Minister in July 1834 but was dismissed by William IV in November — the last time a British monarch dismissed a government. He returned to office after the 1835 election and remained until 1841. His second ministry passed the Municipal Corporations Act 1835 and dealt with rebellions in Canada.

Mentor to Victoria

When Queen Victoria came to the throne in 1837 aged 18, Melbourne became her close adviser and political tutor, meeting her almost daily. Their relationship survived the Bedchamber Crisis of 1839. After leaving office in 1841 he continued to advise her by letter until a stroke weakened him; he died at Brocket Hall in 1848. The city of Melbourne, Australia, was named after him.
