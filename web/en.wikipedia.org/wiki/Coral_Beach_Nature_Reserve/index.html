Coral Beach Nature Reserve

The Coral Beach Nature Reserve is a protected marine nature reserve in the Red Sea near Eilat, Israel. It protects a fringing coral reef that is among the northernmost coral reefs in the world, stretching about 1.2 kilometres along the coast of the Gulf of Aqaba south of the city.

Description

The reserve encompasses the reef flat, the reef crest, and the fore-reef slope down to depths of several tens of metres. The reef supports a rich community of stony corals, soft corals, reef fish, sea anemones with their clownfish, moray eels, sea turtles and many invertebrates, reflecting the high biodiversity of the Red Sea.

Conservation

The reef at Eilat has been under pressure from tourism, port activity, fish farming (removed from the gulf in 2008), and periodic mass mortality events. The reserve, administered by the Israel Nature and Parks Authority, restricts access to marked entry points, with piers allowing swimmers and snorkellers to enter the water without walking on the reef. Diving and snorkelling along the reef wall are popular, and the adjacent Coral World Underwater Observatory lies just to the south.

Access

The reserve has a beach with visitor facilities, shaded areas, and snorkelling equipment rental. Entry is for a fee, and fishing, collecting, and feeding fish are prohibited within the reserve.
