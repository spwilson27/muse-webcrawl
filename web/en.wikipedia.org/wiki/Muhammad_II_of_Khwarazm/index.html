Ala al-Din Muhammad II (Persian: علاءالدین محمد خوارزمشاه; 1169 – December 1220) was the Shah of the Khwarazmian Empire from 1200 to 1220. His empire stretched from the borders of Iraq to the Indus, but his execution of Mongol envoys provoked the Mongol conquest that destroyed it — and him.

Expansion

Succeeding his father Tekish in 1200, Muhammad first fought a long war with the Ghurids for control of Khurasan; after the assassination of Mu'izz al-Din Muhammad Ghuri in 1206 he absorbed most of the Ghurid lands. He then broke the Qara Khitai, annexing Transoxiana with the help of the Naiman prince Küchlük, took Samarkand and Bukhara, and by 1215–1217 had subdued much of Persia, proclaimed himself Shah and contemplated marching on Baghdad against the Abbasid caliph al-Nasir, whose hostility he blamed for his troubles. A winter campaign toward Iraq in 1217–18 was abandoned after snowstorms in the Zagros.

Clash with the Mongols

In 1218 a Mongol caravan of several hundred merchants was seized at Otrar by Muhammad's governor Inalchuq, who accused them of espionage; Muhammad approved their execution and the confiscation of their goods. When Genghis Khan sent ambassadors demanding the governor's punishment, Muhammad killed the chief envoy and humiliated the others. The khan responded with the invasion of 1219.

Collapse and death

Rather than concentrate his larger army, Muhammad dispersed it among his garrison cities, which the Mongols annihilated one by one — Bukhara and Samarkand fell in early 1220. Muhammad fled west across Persia, pursued by the generals Jebe and Subutai, lost his treasure and camp, and in December 1220 died of illness, destitute, on an island in the Caspian Sea near Abaskun, having designated his son Jalal al-Din as heir. His empire effectively perished with him, though Jalal al-Din resisted the Mongols for another decade.
