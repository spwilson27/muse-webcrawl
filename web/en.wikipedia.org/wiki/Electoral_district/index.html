Electoral district

An electoral district, also known as an election district, legislative district, voting district, riding (in Canada) or constituency (in the United Kingdom), is a territorial subdivision for electing members to a legislative body. Generally, only voters (constituents) who reside within the district are permitted to vote in an election held there.

District magnitude

District magnitude is the number of representatives elected from a given district. Single-member districts elect one representative, typically under a plurality or majoritarian system such as first-past-the-post. Multi-member districts elect two or more representatives, usually under proportional representation or the single transferable vote.

Apportionment and redistricting

Districts are usually required to be roughly equal in population so that each vote carries similar weight, a principle summarised as "one person, one vote". The periodic redrawing of district boundaries is called redistricting (in the United States) or redistribution. When boundaries are manipulated for partisan advantage, the practice is known as gerrymandering.

Types

Districts may coincide with administrative divisions such as counties or municipalities, or be drawn independently by boundary commissions. Some systems use at-large districts covering an entire jurisdiction, while others reserve special districts for particular groups, such as the Māori electorates in New Zealand.
