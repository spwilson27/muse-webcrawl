My Struggle (Knausgård novels)

My Struggle

Author: Karl Ove Knausgård

Original title: Min kamp

Volumes: 6 (2009–2011)

Language: Norwegian

The six volumes of Min kamp.

My Struggle (Norwegian: Min kamp) is a six-volume autobiographical novel series by the Norwegian writer Karl Ove Knausgård, published between 2009 and 2011. Running to some 3,600 pages, the books recount the author's life — childhood in Kristiansand, adolescence, his father's death, his marriages and children, and the writing life itself — in extreme, often mundane detail, and are a landmark of the "autofiction" and Scandinavian literary-realism wave.

Volumes and content

The first volume centres on Knausgård's upbringing under a stern father and the father's death and squalid funeral, a set piece widely praised for its intensity. Later volumes deal with love and fatherhood in Stockholm, his childhood in depth, his years as a student and would-be writer in Bergen, and the writing of the series itself. The sixth volume includes a long essay on Hitler and the title's deliberate echo of Mein Kampf, exploring the boundary between "I" and "we" and the road to mass violence.

Reception and controversy

The books were a sensation in Norway, where it was reported that roughly one in ten Norwegians bought a copy; they sparked public debate and private conflict, as family members objected to their portrayal — Knausgård's uncle contested the account of the father's death, and the first wife's and second wife's lives are exposed in unflattering candour. Translated into dozens of languages (in English by Don Bartlett, with Martin Aitken for the final volume), the series won international acclaim and made Knausgård one of the most discussed novelists of the 2010s, frequently compared with Proust.
