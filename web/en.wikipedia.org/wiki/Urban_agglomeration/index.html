Urban agglomeration

An agglomeration extends beyond the administrative city boundary

An urban agglomeration is an extended city or town area comprising the built-up area of a central place and any suburbs or continuously developed territory linked to it by proximity and infrastructure, regardless of administrative boundaries. The term is used by the United Nations and many national statistical offices to compare the populations of cities on a consistent, physical basis — the continuously urbanized footprint — rather than by municipal limits, which vary enormously in meaning between countries.

Definitions

In India, the Census defines an urban agglomeration as a continuous urban spread constituting a town and its adjoining outgrowths, or two or more physically contiguous towns, with a total population of at least 20,000; it must consist of at least one statutory town. In France, the corresponding concept is the unité urbaine, a commune or group of communes with a continuous built-up zone (gaps of no more than 200 metres) and at least 2,000 inhabitants. The UN's World Urbanization Prospects uses "urban agglomeration" for population concentrations of 300,000 or more, drawing on each country's preferred definition, which complicates cross-national comparison.

Related concepts

The urban agglomeration differs from a metropolitan area, which adds economically integrated but not necessarily contiguous territory (commuter belts), and from a conurbation, formed when several originally separate cities merge into a single continuous urban region. A megalopolis chains multiple metropolitan areas together. Ranking the world's largest urban agglomerations typically places Tokyo, Delhi, and Shanghai at the top, each exceeding 30 million inhabitants by UN estimates.
