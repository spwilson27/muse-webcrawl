The Army Air Corps (AAC) is the aviation component of the British Army, first formed in 1942 and re-formed in its present shape in 1957. It provides battlefield helicopter support — attack, reconnaissance, liaison and utility — as well as fixed-wing surveillance.

History

The original Army Air Corps was formed in the Second World War, in 1942, as an umbrella for the Glider Pilot Regiment and the Parachute Regiment; it was disbanded in 1949. The modern corps was created on 1 September 1957 from the Glider Pilot Regiment and the Air Observation Post squadrons of the Royal Artillery. It expanded with helicopters such as the Scout, Sioux, Gazelle and Lynx, serving in Malaya, Northern Ireland, the Falklands, the Gulf, the Balkans, Iraq and Afghanistan.

Modern organisation

The corps' principal combat power is the Apache AH-64E attack helicopter, flown by regiments of 1 Aviation Brigade Combat Team (formed in 2020 from Joint Helicopter Command elements). It also operates the Wildcat AH1 reconnaissance helicopter, Gazelle in training and residual roles, and the fixed-wing Islander/Defender and DA42 surveillance aircraft in support roles. Training is centred on the Army Aviation Centre at Middle Wallop, Hampshire.
