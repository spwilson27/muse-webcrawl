Hundred Family Surnames


The opening lines of the Baijiaxing.

The Hundred Family Surnames (Chinese: 百家姓; pinyin: Bǎijiāxìng) is a classic Chinese text composed of surnames. The book was created in the early Song dynasty. It originally listed 411 surnames, and was later expanded to 504, of which 60 are compound (two-character) surnames.

Content

The surnames are arranged in rhyming groups of four characters for easy memorization by children, who traditionally learned the text together with the Three Character Classic and the Thousand Character Classic. The first four surnames are Zhào (趙), Qián (錢), Sūn (孫), and Lǐ (李): Zhao was the surname of the Song emperors, Qian that of the king of Wuyue, Sun that of his queen, and Li that of the king of Southern Tang. The order of surnames does not reflect their actual frequency of use.

Influence

Because of its ubiquity, "the Hundred Family Surnames" became a byword in Chinese for ordinary people collectively, and editions continue to be published, sometimes updated with modern surname rankings.
