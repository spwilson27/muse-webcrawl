Georges Auguste Escoffier (French: [ʒɔʁʒ oɡyst ɛskɔfje]; 28 October 1846 – 12 February 1935) was a French chef, restaurateur, and culinary writer who popularized and updated traditional French cooking methods. Much of Escoffier's technique was based on that of Marie-Antoine Carême, one of the codifiers of French haute cuisine; Escoffier's great achievement was to simplify and modernize Carême's elaborate and ornate style. He has been called "the king of chefs and the chef of kings".

Career

Escoffier began his apprenticeship at age 13 in his uncle's restaurant in Nice. After army service as a chef during the Franco-Prussian War, he worked in Paris restaurants before joining César Ritz in 1884. Together they opened the Savoy Hotel in London (1889), where Escoffier headed the kitchens and created dishes for celebrity guests, and later the Ritz Hotel in Paris (1898) and the Carlton in London (1899). His partnership with Ritz defined the modern luxury hotel restaurant.

Contributions to cuisine

Escoffier codified his system in Le Guide Culinaire (1903), which contains more than 5,000 recipes and remains a reference work for French classical cooking. He systematized the five mother sauces (béchamel, velouté, espagnole, hollandaise, and tomate), reorganized the professional kitchen into the brigade de cuisine — a hierarchy of specialized stations that is still the model for fine-dining kitchens — and championed simpler presentation, lighter sauces, and seasonal ingredients over Carême's grand architectural displays.

He created many famous dishes, often named for celebrated patrons: Peach Melba for the Australian soprano Nellie Melba, Melba toast, Fraises à la Sarah Bernhardt, and Tournedos Rossini (attributed to his era's codification). Escoffier also professionalized kitchen discipline, insisting on cleanliness, sobriety, and quiet in place of the shouting and drinking customary in 19th-century kitchens.

Legacy

Escoffier was the first chef appointed to the Legion of Honour (1919, officer 1928). His books, including A Guide to Modern Cookery (the English abridgement of Le Guide Culinaire) and Ma Cuisine (1934), shaped generations of chefs, and culinary schools and foundations worldwide bear his name.
