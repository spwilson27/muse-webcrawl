Ibrahim Pasha of Egypt

Ibrahim Pasha (1789 – 10 November 1848) was an Egyptian general and statesman, the eldest son of Muhammad Ali, the Ottoman governor and de facto ruler of Egypt. One of the most capable military commanders of his era, Ibrahim led Egyptian armies in Arabia, Greece, and Syria, and briefly succeeded his father as ruler of Egypt in 1848, shortly before his own death.

Early career

Born in Kavala in Ottoman Macedonia, Ibrahim came to Egypt as a youth. He first won distinction commanding the Egyptian expedition against the Wahhabi movement in Arabia (1816–1819), capturing Diriyah and ending the first Saudi state — a campaign that earned him the title of Pasha and governorships in the Hijaz.

Greek War of Independence

In 1825 Ibrahim landed in the Peloponnese at the head of a large Egyptian army sent to aid the Ottomans against the Greek revolutionaries. His forces devastated much of the Morea and nearly crushed the revolt, but the intervention of Britain, France, and Russia — culminating in the destruction of the Ottoman-Egyptian fleet at the Battle of Navarino in 1827 — forced his withdrawal.

Conquest of Syria

In 1831–1833 Ibrahim conquered Syria and southeastern Anatolia from the Ottoman Empire, defeating Ottoman armies at Homs, Beilan, and Konya, and advancing to within reach of Istanbul before European diplomacy halted him. He governed Syria for nearly a decade, introducing administrative reforms while imposing conscription and taxes that provoked widespread revolts. A second war in 1839 saw another Egyptian victory at Nezib, but British and Austrian intervention compelled Egypt to evacuate Syria in 1840–41.

Ruler of Egypt

As Muhammad Ali's mental faculties declined, Ibrahim became the effective ruler, and he was formally invested as governor in mid-1848. He died in Cairo on 10 November 1848, only a few months later, predeceasing his father. He was succeeded by his nephew Abbas.
