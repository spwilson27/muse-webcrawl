Arita ware

Arita ware (Japanese: 有田焼, Arita-yaki) is a broad term for Japanese porcelain made in the area around the town of Arita, in the former Hizen Province of northwestern Kyushu, since the early 17th century. It was the first porcelain produced in Japan.

Origins

According to tradition, the Korean potter Yi Sam-pyeong (known in Japan as Kanagae Sanbee) discovered porcelain stone at Izumiyama in Arita around 1616, after Korean craftsmen were brought to Japan in the wake of the Imjin War. Kilns multiplied and Arita became the centre of a major industry.

Exports and styles

Exported through the port of Imari by the Dutch East India Company from the 1650s, Arita porcelain — called Imari ware in the West — strongly influenced European ceramics. Styles range from underglaze blue (sometsuke) to overglaze enamelled Kakiemon and the brocade-like Kinrande. Production continues today in Arita and the neighbouring town of Hasami.
