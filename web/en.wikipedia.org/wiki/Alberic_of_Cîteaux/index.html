Alberic of Cîteaux (c. 1050 – 26 January 1109), sometimes known as Aubrey, was a French monk and saint, one of the founders of the Cistercian Order. He served as the second abbot of Cîteaux Abbey from 1099 until his death.

Life

Alberic was a hermit in the forest of Collan near Tonnerre before joining Robert of Molesme at the new monastery of Molesme in Burgundy. When Robert and his companions resolved to found a stricter house, Alberic was among the twenty-one monks who left Molesme in 1098 to establish the New Monastery at Cîteaux, in a marshy wilderness south of Dijon.

Alberic became prior under Robert, and when Robert was recalled to Molesme by order of the pope in 1099, Alberic was elected the second abbot of Cîteaux. His abbacy was a period of hardship and consolidation: the community suffered poverty and few recruits, but Alberic secured papal protection for the young foundation, placing it under the guardianship of the Holy See in 1100.

The white habit

Tradition credits Alberic with adopting the undyed white (or grey-white) woolen habit that became the distinguishing dress of the Cistercians, in contrast to the black habit of the Benedictines of Cluny. According to legend, the Virgin Mary appeared to Alberic and presented him with the white habit. He is also associated with the drawing up of early Cistercian customs, the Instituta, which governed the order's daily life.

Death and veneration

Alberic died on 26 January 1109 and was succeeded by Stephen Harding, under whom the order entered its great period of expansion. Alberic is venerated as a saint, and his feast day, 26 January, is shared with the other Cistercian founders, Robert of Molesme and Stephen Harding.
