Edward "Ned" Maddrell (1877 – 27 December 1974) was a fisherman from the Isle of Man and the last surviving native speaker of the Manx language. With his death in 1974, Manx was widely reported to have become extinct as a native community language, although it has since been revived by second-language speakers and is now taught in Manx schools.

Life

Maddrell was born in Cregneash, a village in the south of the island noted for preserving traditional Manx customs. Although his parents were English speakers, he was raised by a great-aunt who spoke Manx, and the language was still common among the older generation in Cregneash when he was a boy. He went to sea at thirteen and spent his working life as a fisherman, sailing as far as Ireland and Scotland.

Recordings and legacy

As the number of native Manx speakers dwindled during the twentieth century, Maddrell became an important source for linguists and revivalists. In 1948 the Irish Folklore Commission, at the instigation of Taoiseach Éamon de Valera, sent a team to record him speaking Manx. He also worked with younger Manx enthusiasts, including Brian Stowell, teaching them the language and helping to transmit its traditional pronunciation. Recordings of Maddrell's speech are held by Manx National Heritage and are used by students of the revived language.

Though sometimes described more precisely as the last native speaker — a few elderly people survived who had learned Manx later in life — Maddrell is commemorated as the end of an unbroken line of Manx speech stretching back centuries. The subsequent growth of Manx-medium education means the language is no longer classified as extinct.
