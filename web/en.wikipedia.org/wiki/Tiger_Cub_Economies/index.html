Tiger Cub Economies

The Tiger Cub Economies are the economies of the developing countries of Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Thailand, and Vietnam, the five major countries of Southeast Asia. The term was coined by analogy with the original Four Asian Tigers — Hong Kong, Singapore, South Korea, and Taiwan — whose export-led industrialization the Tiger Cubs sought to emulate.

Overview

Following the model of the Asian Tigers, the Tiger Cub economies pursued export-oriented policies, attracting foreign direct investment with educated, relatively low-cost workforces and improving infrastructure. Their growth was interrupted by the 1997 Asian financial crisis, in which Indonesia and Thailand were among the hardest hit and required IMF programmes.

Development

Since the 2000s the five economies have been among the faster-growing emerging markets, members or partners of ASEAN, and major recipients of manufacturing investment diversifying out of China. Their combined population exceeds 450 million, and analysts sometimes group them in investment categories such as the CIVETS or MINT successor groupings.
