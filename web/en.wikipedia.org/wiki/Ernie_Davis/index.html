Ernest Davis (14 December 1939 – 18 May 1963) was an American football player who was a halfback at Syracuse University. In 1961 he became the first African American to win the Heisman Trophy, college football's most prestigious individual award.

College career

Davis followed Jim Brown at Syracuse, wearing the same number 44, and led the Orangemen to the 1959 national championship, being named Most Valuable Player of the 1960 Cotton Bowl. He overcame racial discrimination on and off the field, including being barred from the Cotton Bowl awards banquet in segregated Dallas. Nicknamed the "Elmira Express" after the New York city where he grew up, he finished his college career in 1961 as the Heisman Trophy winner.

Professional prospects and death

Davis was the first overall pick of the 1962 NFL draft by Washington and was traded to the Cleveland Browns, where he was to form a backfield with Jim Brown. Before he could play a professional game, he was diagnosed with leukaemia in the summer of 1962 and died the following May at the age of 23. His story was dramatised in the 2008 film The Express.
