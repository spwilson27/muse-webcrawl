Cypselus
Ruled: 7th century BC
City: Corinth
Title: Tyrant

Cypselus (Greek: Κύψελος) was the first tyrant of Corinth, ruling in the 7th century BC, conventionally from about 657 to 627 BC. He overthrew the ruling Bacchiad oligarchy and founded a dynasty continued by his son Periander.

Legend and rise

According to Herodotus, the Bacchiads heard an oracle that the child of Eetion and Labda would overthrow them, and sent men to kill the infant; the baby smiled at them and survived, being hidden in a chest (kypsele), from which he took his name. As an adult, Cypselus seized power and exiled or killed many Bacchiads.

Reign

Ancient sources portray Cypselus as a comparatively mild ruler who governed without a bodyguard. Under his rule Corinth prospered, founded colonies, and produced the famous Chest of Cypselus, an ornately decorated votive offering dedicated at Olympia and described by Pausanias.
