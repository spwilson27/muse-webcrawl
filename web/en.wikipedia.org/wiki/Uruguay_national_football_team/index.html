Uruguay national football team


La Celeste — "the Sky Blue"

The Uruguay national football team, nicknamed La Celeste ("the Sky Blue"), represents Uruguay in international football and is governed by the Uruguayan Football Association (AUF), founded in 1900. Despite a population of only about 3.5 million, Uruguay is one of the most successful national teams in history, famous for the fighting spirit known as garra charrúa.

Golden era

Uruguay dominated early world football: Olympic gold medals in 1924 (Paris) and 1928 (Amsterdam) — tournaments that served as world championships — were followed by victory in the first FIFA World Cup, hosted and won in Montevideo in 1930, beating Argentina 4–2 in the final at the Estadio Centenario. In 1950 Uruguay stunned Brazil 2–1 at the Maracanã in the deciding match of the World Cup, the famous Maracanazo, to win a second world title.

Other honours

Uruguay has won the Copa América a joint-record 15 times, most recently in 2011, and finished fourth at the 2010 World Cup, where Diego Forlán won the Golden Ball. Great players include José Nasazzi, Obdulio Varela, Alcides Ghiggia, Enzo Francescoli, Diego Godín, Luis Suárez — the team's record scorer — and Edinson Cavani. The team plays most home matches at the Estadio Centenario, a UNESCO-recognised monument to world football.
