5G NR

5G NR (New Radio) is the global standard for the air interface of 5G networks, developed by the 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP). It is the successor to LTE and is designed to deliver significantly higher data rates, lower latency, and support for a much larger number of connected devices than previous generations of mobile networks.

Overview

5G NR was first specified in 3GPP Release 15, published in 2018, with the first commercial networks launching in 2019. The standard operates in two frequency ranges: Frequency Range 1 (FR1), covering sub-6 GHz bands, and Frequency Range 2 (FR2), covering millimeter-wave bands between roughly 24 and 52 GHz. Later releases extended the range up to 71 GHz.

Technical features

NR uses orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) with a flexible numerology: subcarrier spacing can scale from 15 kHz to 480 kHz, allowing the air interface to adapt to different frequency bands and service requirements. Channels up to 100 MHz wide in FR1 and 400 MHz wide in FR2 can be aggregated to increase throughput.

Key technologies include massive MIMO and beamforming, particularly important in millimeter-wave bands where signals attenuate rapidly; support for both standalone (SA) and non-standalone (NSA) deployments; and network slicing, which allows operators to create multiple virtual networks over a shared physical infrastructure.

Latency and use cases

NR targets user-plane latency as low as 1 millisecond for ultra-reliable low-latency communications (URLLC), alongside enhanced mobile broadband (eMBB) and massive machine-type communications (mMTC). These capabilities underpin applications such as industrial automation, autonomous vehicles, augmented reality, and the Internet of Things.

Evolution

Release 16 (2020) added features for industrial IoT and vehicle-to-everything communication. Release 17 extended NR to unlicensed spectrum and non-terrestrial networks. Release 18, branded "5G Advanced", introduced further energy-efficiency and AI/ML-based network optimisations.
