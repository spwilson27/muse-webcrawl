Nickel allergy

Nickel allergy is a common form of allergic contact dermatitis caused by direct and prolonged contact with items that release nickel ions onto the skin. It is among the most frequent causes of contact allergy worldwide, affecting roughly 8–19% of adults in Europe and North America, with a higher prevalence in women, largely attributed to exposure through ear piercing and jewellery.

Nickel allergy

Type: Type IV hypersensitivity

Symptoms: Itching, redness, eczema at contact site

Diagnosis: Patch testing

Treatment: Avoidance; topical corticosteroids

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms typically appear 12 to 72 hours after exposure and include itching, erythema (redness), papules, vesicles and scaling confined initially to the area of contact, such as the earlobes (earrings), wrist (watch straps), neck (necklaces) or abdomen (belt buckles and jean studs). In sensitized individuals, dietary nickel may also provoke systemic flares.

Causes and mechanism

Nickel ions released by corrosion of nickel-containing alloys penetrate the skin and act as haptens, binding to skin proteins and triggering a delayed, T-cell–mediated (type IV) hypersensitivity reaction. Once sensitization occurs, it generally persists for life.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is confirmed by patch testing with nickel sulfate. Management centres on avoidance of nickel-releasing items; the dimethylglyoxime spot test can identify such objects. Regulation such as the European Union's Nickel Directive limits nickel release from consumer products and has reduced sensitization rates in younger cohorts.
