Rio de Janeiro

Rio de Janeiro: Sugarloaf and Guanabara Bay

Rio de Janeiro, commonly known as Rio, is the capital of the state of Rio de Janeiro and Brazil's second-most populous city, with about 6.7 million residents in the city and 13.6 million in the metropolitan region. It is set in a spectacular landscape where steep granite mountains — Corcovado, crowned by Christ the Redeemer (1931), and Sugarloaf — plunge to Guanabara Bay and Atlantic beaches such as Copacabana and Ipanema.

Portuguese explorers, mistaking the bay for a river mouth, named it 'January River' in 1502. Rio was Brazil's capital from 1763 to 1960. It hosted the 2014 FIFA World Cup final and the 2016 Olympic Games, the first in South America. Rio is the home of Carnival — the world's largest — samba and bossa nova, and of vast hillside favelas such as Rocinha alongside wealthy districts. Its cultural landscape is a UNESCO World Heritage site.
