Urartian language

Urartian or Vannic is an extinct language spoken by the inhabitants of the ancient kingdom of Urartu, located in the region of Lake Van in the Armenian Highlands of eastern Anatolia. It was probably spoken by most of the population around Lake Van and in the areas along the upper Zab valley.

Classification

Urartian is an ergative, agglutinative language that belongs, together with Hurrian, to the Hurro-Urartian language family. It survives in many inscriptions found in the territory of the Urartian kingdom, written in a borrowed Assyrian cuneiform script. Urartian is not related to Armenian, which later replaced it in the region, though there were loanwords in both directions.

Attestation

The language is attested from royal inscriptions dating from the 9th to the 6th century BC, recording building projects, military campaigns, and religious dedications of the Urartian kings. A small number of bilingual inscriptions with Assyrian aided its decipherment. After the fall of Urartu, the language disappeared from the written record.

Features

Urartian used suffixes extensively to mark grammatical functions, and its ergative alignment meant transitive subjects were marked differently from intransitive subjects and objects. Much of its vocabulary concerns kingship, warfare, irrigation works, and temple building, reflecting the contents of the surviving inscriptions.
