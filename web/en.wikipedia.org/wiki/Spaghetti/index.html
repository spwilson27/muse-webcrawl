Spaghetti

Uncooked spaghetti (left) and a served dish

Spaghetti (Italian: [spaˈɡetti]) is a long, thin, solid, cylindrical pasta. It is a staple food of traditional Italian cuisine. Like other pasta, spaghetti is made of milled wheat, water, and sometimes enriched with vitamins and minerals. Italian spaghetti is typically made from durum-wheat semolina. The word spaghetti is the plural form of the Italian word spaghetto, which is a diminutive of spago, meaning "thin string" or "twine".

History

While some accounts credit the Chinese with inventing noodles (and legend attributed their introduction to Italy to Marco Polo), the production of pasta in Italy — especially in Sicily — developed independently and is documented from the 12th century. The geographer Muhammad al-Idrisi, writing for Roger II of Sicily in 1154, described a town in Sicily where strings of dough called triyah were manufactured and exported widely. By the 19th century, spaghetti factories in Naples industrialised production, and the pairing of spaghetti with tomato sauce became canonical.

Preparation

Spaghetti is cooked in a large pot of salted boiling water, then drained and dressed. In Italy it is served on a plate or in a shallow bowl, classically twirled around a fork. Well-known preparations include spaghetti aglio e olio (garlic and olive oil), amatriciana, carbonara, puttanesca, alle vongole (with clams), and with ragù alla bolognese. Outside Italy, spaghetti is frequently served with meatballs and tomato sauce, a dish developed in Italian-American cuisine.

Records and culture

On 1 April 1957 the BBC current-affairs programme Panorama broadcast its famous "spaghetti-tree hoax", showing Swiss farmers harvesting spaghetti from trees; many credulous viewers phoned in asking how to grow their own. The dish also features in the celebrated candlelit scene of Disney's Lady and the Tramp (1955).
