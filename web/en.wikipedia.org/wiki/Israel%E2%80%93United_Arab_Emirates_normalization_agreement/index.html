Israel–United Arab Emirates normalization agreement

Israel–UAE normalization agreement

Signed: September 15, 2020, Washington, D.C.

Announced: August 13, 2020

Part of: Abraham Accords

Signatories: Israel, United Arab Emirates, United States (witness)

The Israel–United Arab Emirates normalization agreement, officially the Abraham Accords Peace Agreement: Treaty of Peace, Diplomatic Relations and Full Normalization Between the United Arab Emirates and the State of Israel, established full diplomatic relations between the two countries. It was announced on August 13, 2020, and signed at the White House on September 15, 2020.

Terms

Under the agreement, the UAE became the third Arab country, after Egypt (1979) and Jordan (1994), to formally normalize relations with Israel. The two states agreed to establish embassies, launch direct flights, and cooperate in fields including tourism, trade, technology, energy, and security. Israel agreed to suspend its plans to annex parts of the West Bank.

Aftermath

The agreement opened the Abraham Accords process, soon joined by Bahrain, Sudan, and Morocco. Trade between Israel and the UAE grew rapidly, and the first direct commercial flights and embassy openings followed within months. Palestinian leaders criticized the agreement as abandoning the Arab Peace Initiative's linkage between normalization and a Palestinian state.
