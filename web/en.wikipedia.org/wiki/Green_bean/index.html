Green bean

SpeciesPhaseolus vulgaris
Also calledFrench beans, string beans, snap beans
OriginCentral and South America
Energy31 kcal per 100 g

Green beans are young, unripe fruits of various cultivars of the common bean (Phaseolus vulgaris). They are known by many common names, including French beans, string beans, and snap beans. Immature or young pods of the runner bean (Phaseolus coccineus), yardlong bean (Vigna unguiculata subsp. sesquipedalis), and hyacinth bean (Lablab purpureus) are used in a similar way.

History and etymology

Green beans were first cultivated in the Andes thousands of years ago and were introduced to Europe by Spanish explorers returning from the Americas in the 16th century. String beans were so called because early varieties had a fibrous "string" along the seam of the pod; most modern cultivars have been bred stringless.

Culinary use

Green beans are eaten around the world and are sold fresh, canned, and frozen. They can be raw, steamed, stir-fried, or stewed. In the United States they are the base of green bean casserole, a Thanksgiving staple created in 1955. In French cuisine, slender haricots verts are prized.

Production

The world's largest producers of green beans include China, Indonesia, India, and Turkey. In 2020, world production of green beans was about 26 million tonnes.
