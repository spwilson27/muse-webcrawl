The RS-25, also known as the Space Shuttle Main Engine (SSME), is a cryogenic liquid-fuel rocket engine burning liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen. Built by Rocketdyne (now part of Aerojet Rocketdyne), three RS-25s powered each Space Shuttle orbiter during ascent, providing thrust alongside the solid rocket boosters.

Design

The RS-25 uses a staged-combustion cycle with dual preburners and operates at some of the highest chamber pressures of any operational engine, achieving a vacuum specific impulse of about 452 seconds. On the Shuttle the engines were reusable and gimballed for steering; they were throttled between roughly 65% and 109% of rated power during flight.

Space Launch System

After the Shuttle's retirement in 2011, remaining RS-25s were adapted for NASA's Space Launch System, whose core stage uses four engines. For SLS the engines are expended with the core stage; new-production RS-25s with simplified, additively manufactured components are being built for later Artemis missions. The first SLS flight, Artemis I, launched in November 2022.
