Sid Abel

Born: 22 February 1918, Melville, Saskatchewan, Canada
Died: 8 February 2000, Farmington Hills, Michigan, US
Position: Centre / left wing
Teams: Detroit Red Wings, Chicago Black Hawks

Sidney Gerald Abel (February 22, 1918 – February 8, 2000) was a Canadian professional ice hockey player and coach. A centre and left winger best known as the captain of the Detroit Red Wings, Abel centred the famed "Production Line" with Gordie Howe and Ted Lindsay, winning three Stanley Cups with Detroit (1943, 1950, and 1952). He was inducted into the Hockey Hall of Fame in 1969.

Playing career

Born in Melville, Saskatchewan, Abel joined Detroit in 1938 and became a regular during the Second World War era, winning his first Stanley Cup in 1943 before leaving for service with the Royal Canadian Air Force. Returning after the war, he formed the Production Line — named for Detroit's automobile industry — with Lindsay and the young Howe, which became one of the most famous forward lines in hockey history. Abel won the Hart Memorial Trophy as the NHL's most valuable player in 1949, after leading the league's playoffs in earlier years and finishing near the top in scoring. Detroit won back-to-back Cups in 1950 and 1952, and Abel served as team captain from 1943.

In 1952 Abel was sold to the Chicago Black Hawks, where he served as player-coach for two seasons before retiring as a player in 1954. He later returned to Detroit as head coach (1958–1968) and general manager, taking the Red Wings to four Stanley Cup Finals in the 1960s, though without winning. His jersey number 12 was retired by the Red Wings in 1995.

Legacy

Abel's son Gerry also played in the NHL, and his grandson Brent Johnson became an NHL goaltender. The Production Line endures in Red Wings lore, commemorated by statues at Little Caesars Arena in Detroit.
