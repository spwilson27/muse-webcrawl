Sean-nós singing


Origin: Ireland (Gaeltacht regions)
Style: Unaccompanied, highly ornamented solo singing
Meaning: "Old style" in Irish

Sean-nós singing (from Irish sean nós, "old style") is a traditional form of Irish unaccompanied solo singing, usually in the Irish language. It is characterised by elaborate melodic ornamentation, free rhythm following the natural phrasing of the words, and a restrained, intense delivery.

Characteristics

Singers ornament the melody with melisma, grace notes and subtle variation from verse to verse. Songs often tell stories of love, emigration, historical events and loss, and performances are traditionally met with attentive silence, the audience sometimes holding the singer's hand or offering quiet encouragement.

Regions and competitions

Distinct regional styles survive in Connemara, Donegal and Munster. Sean-nós singing is a centrepiece of the Oireachtas na Gaeilge festival, whose Corn Uí Riada competition is the most prestigious honour in the genre. Noted singers include Joe Heaney and Darach Ó Catháin.
