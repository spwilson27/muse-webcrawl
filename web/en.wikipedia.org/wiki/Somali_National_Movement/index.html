Somali National Movement

The Somali National Movement (SNM) was a rebel group founded in 1981 in London by Somali exiles, drawn mainly from the Isaaq clan-family of northern Somalia. It waged an armed insurgency against the regime of Siad Barre, and after the collapse of the Somali state in 1991 it spearheaded the declaration of independence of Somaliland, which remains internationally unrecognised but has functioned as a de facto state ever since.

The SNM operated principally in the northern regions of Somalia, the former British Somaliland.

Founding and insurgency

The SNM was established in London in April 1981 by members of the Somali diaspora angered by the Barre regime's marginalisation and repression of the north following the Ogaden War. After relocating to Ethiopia, it launched cross-border guerrilla operations from 1982. The regime's response included severe repression of Isaaq civilians; in 1988, after the SNM attacked the cities of Burao and Hargeisa, government forces bombed and shelled Hargeisa into near-total destruction, killing tens of thousands in what is often called the Isaaq genocide.

Fall of Barre and independence

As the regime disintegrated in early 1991, SNM forces took control of the north. At a conference in Burao in May 1991, clan elders and the SNM leadership rescinded the 1960 union with the rest of Somalia and declared the independence of the Republic of Somaliland within the borders of the former British protectorate. The SNM itself evolved into a political administration, handing over power to a civilian government under Mohamed Haji Ibrahim Egal after the Borama conference of 1993.

Legacy

The SNM is remembered in Somaliland as a liberation movement that ended in a locally negotiated peace process involving traditional elders, a model credited with Somaliland's relative stability compared with south-central Somalia. Its veterans and symbols remain influential in Somaliland politics.
