Stochastic partial differential equation

A stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE) is a partial differential equation in which one or more terms is a stochastic process, so that the solution is itself a random field. SPDEs are the natural infinite-dimensional generalisation of stochastic differential equations and are used to model systems in which noise acts continuously in space and time, such as turbulent flows, fluctuating interfaces, and neuronal activity across a spatial domain.

Formulation

A prototypical SPDE is the stochastic heat equation

∂u/∂t = Δu + σ(u) ξ,

where ξ is space-time white noise — a random "function" so rough that the equation must be interpreted in a distributional sense. In finite dimensions the noise term is a Brownian motion; in space-time the driving noise is typically a cylindrical Wiener process on a Hilbert space, and solutions are defined as mild solutions using the semigroup generated by the linear part of the operator, with the stochastic integral understood in the Itô sense.

Regularity difficulties

Because white noise in dimension two or more is extremely singular, products of the solution with the noise can be ill-defined, and classical solution theories break down. This difficulty, long a barrier for equations such as the Kardar–Parisi–Zhang (KPZ) equation of interface growth, was dramatically advanced by Martin Hairer's theory of regularity structures (introduced in 2013), which systematised renormalisation for a broad class of singular SPDEs and earned him the Fields Medal in 2014. Related approaches include paracontrolled distributions and rough paths theory.

Examples and applications

Important examples include the stochastic Navier–Stokes equations (modelling turbulence with random forcing), the stochastic Allen–Cahn equation (phase separation with thermal fluctuations), the KPZ equation and its continuum limit, stochastic quantisation in quantum field theory (the Parisi–Wu programme), and neurobiological models such as stochastic versions of the FitzHugh–Nagumo equations on a spatial domain. SPDEs also arise as scaling limits of interacting particle systems in statistical mechanics, where microscopic randomness produces macroscopic fluctuating hydrodynamics.

Solution concepts

Depending on the regularity of the noise and coefficients, one speaks of strong, weak (martingale), mild, and renormalised solutions. When the noise is spatially smooth (for example a finite sum of Fourier modes with random amplitudes), classical PDE theory often applies pathwise. The difficult and active frontier of the field concerns singular noises, invariant measures of SPDEs, and their scaling limits.
