Lingua franca

Lingua franca
DefinitionA language used for communication between speakers of different native languages
Original senseMediterranean Lingua Franca (Sabir)
Modern examplesEnglish, Swahili, Standard Arabic, Mandarin Chinese

A lingua franca is a language that is adopted as a common language between speakers whose native languages are different. Historically, lingua francas have developed around trade, administration, religion and diplomacy. A lingua franca may itself be a pidgin developed for the purpose, a language of one influential group, or an artificial standard.

Origin of the term

The term originally denoted the Mediterranean Lingua Franca, or Sabir, a pidgin based largely on Italian and Provençal with borrowings from Spanish, Catalan, Arabic, Turkish, Greek and other languages, used for trade in the Mediterranean basin from the medieval period into the 19th century. "Lingua franca" is Italian for "Frankish language", Frank being the term used in the Levant for Western Europeans generally. By extension the term came to mean any vehicular language used between communities.

Examples

Many languages have served the role across history: Aramaic in the ancient Near East; Koine Greek in the Hellenistic world; Latin in medieval and early modern European scholarship and religion; Persian in Central and South Asia; Arabic following the spread of Islam; and French in European diplomacy in the 18th and 19th centuries. Today English functions as the principal global lingua franca of science, aviation, business and the internet, while regional lingua francas such as Swahili in East Africa, Hausa in West Africa, Hindi–Urdu in South Asia and Bazaar Malay in Southeast Asia remain vital. A lingua franca need not displace local languages; typically it occupies specific domains while home languages persist, though prolonged use can contribute to language shift.
