National Geographic Society
Founded: January 1888
Type: Nonprofit scientific and educational organization
Headquarters: Washington, D.C., United States
Key people: Alexander Graham Bell (early president); Gilbert H. Grosvenor

The National Geographic Society is one of the largest nonprofit scientific and educational organizations in the world. Founded in Washington, D.C., in January 1888 by a group of 33 explorers and scientists, its stated purpose is to increase and diffuse geographic knowledge and to illuminate and protect the wonder of the world.

Activities

The Society publishes National Geographic magazine and supports scientific field research, exploration, conservation, and education. Over its history it has sponsored thousands of expeditions and projects, including support associated with explorers such as Robert Peary, Hiram Bingham, Jacques Cousteau, Jane Goodall, and Robert Ballard. Its cartographic division long produced maps and atlases widely used by the public and by the United States government.

Media

In 2015 the Society's media assets were placed in National Geographic Partners, a joint venture in which the Walt Disney Company now holds the majority stake; proceeds fund the Society's grant-making and mission programs.
