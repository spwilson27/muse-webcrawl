Roxas Boulevard

Roxas Boulevard is a prominent seafront promenade and major arterial road in Metro Manila, Philippines. It runs along the eastern shore of Manila Bay from Intramuros in the City of Manila southward through Ermita, Malate and Pasay to Parañaque, a distance of about 7.6 kilometres (4.7 mi). Formerly known as the Manila Bay Boulevard and, during the American colonial period, Dewey Boulevard, it was renamed in honour of President Manuel Roxas. The boulevard is famous for its sunsets over Manila Bay, its palm-lined baywalk, and as the address of institutions including the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, the Philippine Senate (at the GSIS complex), the Cultural Center of the Philippines, and numerous hotels, casinos and embassies.

History

The boulevard originated in the Burnham Plan for Manila of 1905, in which architect Daniel Burnham envisioned a grand waterfront drive along Manila Bay. Built on reclaimed land in the early American period, it was named Dewey Boulevard after Admiral George Dewey, victor of the Battle of Manila Bay in 1898. Lined with palm trees and elite residences, it became one of Manila's most fashionable addresses. After Philippine independence it was renamed for Manuel Roxas, the first president of the Third Republic.

During the Marcos era the bayfront was greatly expanded with reclamation for the Cultural Center of the Philippines complex, Folk Arts Theater, Philippine International Convention Center and film center projects associated with First Lady Imelda Marcos. The seawall promenade, known as the Baywalk, was redeveloped in the 2000s with open-air cafés and viewing decks, though portions have since been altered by rehabilitation projects of Manila Bay, including the controversial dolomite beach opened in 2020–2021 near the boulevard's northern end.

Route

Roxas Boulevard begins at Rizal Park in Manila, at the junction with Padre Burgos Avenue and Katigbak Parkway, and proceeds south as a wide multi-lane divided highway. It passes the United States Embassy, the Manila Yacht Club, the Bangko Sentral complex, the Cultural Center of the Philippines, the Philippine Senate in Pasay, and the SM Mall of Asia area, continuing south toward the airport road and Coastal Road in Parañaque. It forms part of the R-1 radial road and Asian Highway 26.
