Resident Commissioner of Puerto Rico

The Resident Commissioner of Puerto Rico (Spanish: Comisionado Residente de Puerto Rico) is a non-voting member of the United States House of Representatives elected by the voters of Puerto Rico every four years, the only member of the House with a four-year term. The resident commissioner represents the commonwealth — the most populous of the non-voting delegations — and may serve on committees, introduce legislation and speak on the floor, but cannot vote on the final passage of bills in the full House. The commissioner can, however, vote in committee, including in the Committee of the Whole under rules upheld by the House subject to a re-vote if their votes are decisive.

History

The position was created by the Foraker Act of 1900, which established a civil government for Puerto Rico after its cession by Spain to the United States following the Spanish–American War of 1898. The first resident commissioner, Federico Degetau, took his seat in 1901. The Jones–Shafroth Act of 1917, which granted US citizenship to Puerto Ricans, retained the office. In 2004, an amendment (approved by Congress in 2000) extended the term from two to four years beginning with the 2004 election, making it unique among House positions.

Role and powers

The resident commissioner is assigned to committees like other representatives, receives the same salary, staff allowances and office space, and sponsors legislation on behalf of Puerto Rico. Because Puerto Rico has a population larger than several US states, the office represents more constituents than any voting member of the House. Commissioners have historically affiliated with either the Democratic or Republican Party, often while belonging to a Puerto Rican political party such as the Popular Democratic Party or the New Progressive Party.

Notable officeholders

Past resident commissioners include Luis Muñoz Rivera, a leading figure in Puerto Rican politics; Santiago Iglesias Pantín, founder of the Socialist Party of Puerto Rico; Luis Fortuño, who later became governor; Pedro Pierluisi, also a later governor; and Jenniffer González-Colón, elected in 2016 and 2020, who became governor of Puerto Rico in 2025. Pablo José Hernández Rivera, of the Popular Democratic Party, won the 2024 election and took office in January 2025.
