Bundelkhand is a geographical and cultural region of central India, spanning parts of the present-day states of Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh. The region takes its name from the Bundela Rajputs, who ruled much of it from the 16th century onward. It is bounded roughly by the Yamuna River to the north, the Vindhyan ranges to the south, and is drained by tributaries such as the Betwa, Dhasan, and Ken rivers.

History

In the early medieval period the region was ruled by the Chandela dynasty, whose kings built the celebrated temples of Khajuraho (now a UNESCO World Heritage Site) between about 950 and 1050 CE. After Chandela decline, the Bundela Rajputs rose to prominence; their ruler Bir Singh Deo built the palace-fort at Orchha in the early 17th century, and the Bundela chief Chhatrasal led a celebrated resistance against Mughal authority in the later 17th century, aided by the Maratha leader Shivaji's example and Bajirao I's support. Under British rule the region was a patchwork of princely states and directly administered districts. Jhansi, in Bundelkhand, became famous in the Indian Rebellion of 1857 through the resistance of Rani Lakshmibai.

Geography

Bundelkhand is a plateau region of rocky uplands, ravines, and low hills on the northern edge of the Deccan, with generally poor soils and erratic rainfall. The area is prone to drought, and its reservoirs and medieval tanks — many built by Chandela and Bundela rulers — remain important sources of water. The Ken-Betwa river-linking project, intended to transfer water within the region, has been a major and contested development proposal.

Culture

The regional language is Bundeli, closely related to Hindi. Bundelkhand has a rich tradition of folk poetry and music, including the Alha-Khand epic ballads celebrating the heroes Alha and Udal, and the works of poets associated with its courts. Cuisine, dress, and festivals share features with both the Gangetic plain and central India.

Economy and issues

Agriculture is the main livelihood, but frequent droughts, water scarcity, and limited industry have made Bundelkhand one of India's more economically distressed regions, with significant out-migration. Demands for a separate Bundelkhand state, carved from Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh, have been raised periodically by regional parties and activists, citing neglect by both state governments.
