Pebble (watch)

Developer: Pebble Technology
Type: Smartwatch
Release date: 2013
Discontinued: 2016

Pebble was a line of smartwatches developed by Pebble Technology Corporation and widely credited with popularising the modern smartwatch. The original Pebble was funded through Kickstarter in 2012, raising more than $10 million — then a platform record — and began shipping in January 2013. The watches paired with Android and iOS smartphones over Bluetooth, displayed notifications, and ran downloadable watchfaces and apps written in C and JavaScript.

Design

Pebble watches used a low-power black-and-white e-paper (memory LCD) display that was always on and readable in sunlight, giving a battery life of about a week — far longer than contemporary LCD smartwatches. The original model had four buttons, a vibrating motor, a magnetometer and an ambient light sensor, and was water-resistant. Later models included the aluminium Pebble Steel (2014), the colour-screen Pebble Time (2015) with a microphone and the Timeline interface, the round Pebble Time Round, and the Pebble 2 with a heart-rate sensor.

Kickstarter records

The 2012 campaign for the original Pebble raised $10.27 million from nearly 69,000 backers. The Pebble Time campaign in 2015 surpassed it, raising over $20 million and briefly becoming the most funded Kickstarter project ever. The company's app store hosted thousands of third-party watchfaces and applications.

Closure and legacy

Despite strong sales — more than two million watches — Pebble struggled financially. In December 2016 the company announced it was shutting down and that Fitbit had acquired its key assets, including software and intellectual property; hardware warranties were voided and the servers were later switched off, though community projects such as Rebble revived web services for remaining users. Fitbit itself kept Pebble's software talent at the core of its own smartwatch efforts. In 2025, founder Eric Migicovsky announced new watches reviving the Pebble name and open-sourcing the PebbleOS software, with Google having released the original source code.
