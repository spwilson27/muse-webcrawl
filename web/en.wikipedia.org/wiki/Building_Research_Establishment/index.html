Building Research Establishment

The Building Research Establishment (BRE) is a centre of building science in the United Kingdom, owned by the charitable BRE Trust. Based at Garston near Watford, Hertfordshire, it provides research, consultancy, testing, certification and training for the built environment. BRE is best known internationally for creating BREEAM (the BRE Environmental Assessment Method), first launched in 1990 and one of the world's leading sustainability assessment methods for buildings and communities.

History

BRE traces its origins to the Building Research Station, established by the government in 1921 to investigate building materials and methods, initially to support the post-First World War housing programme. It later absorbed the Fire Research Station and the Princes Risborough Laboratory, becoming the Building Research Establishment in 1972. It was part of the Department of the Environment until 1997, when it was privatised and bought by the BRE Trust, a registered charity whose profits from BRE's commercial work support research and education in the built environment.

Activities

BRE operates large-scale testing facilities, including one of Europe's largest structural test halls and a burn hall for fire testing. It maintains the Innovation Park at its Watford campus, showcasing full-scale demonstration buildings with innovative low-carbon designs. BRE also manages the Code for Sustainable Homes (historically), SABRE security assessment, and the LPCB (Loss Prevention Certification Board) certification of fire and security products. Its research has informed UK building regulations on energy efficiency, fire safety, flood resilience and indoor air quality.
