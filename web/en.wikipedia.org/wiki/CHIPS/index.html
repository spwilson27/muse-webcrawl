CHIPS

CHIPS most commonly refers to the Clearing House Interbank Payments System, a United States private clearing house for large-value transactions. By 2024 it settled well over one trillion US dollars daily, making it the principal private-sector system for clearing and settling large-value domestic and international US dollar payments, complementing the Federal Reserve's Fedwire service.

Operation

CHIPS was established in 1970 by the New York Clearing House Association. It originally used end-of-day net settlement, but since 2001 it has operated as a real-time final settlement system in which payments are released against prefunded balances held at the Federal Reserve, allowing very large gross values to be settled with comparatively small amounts of liquidity through continuous multilateral netting and queuing algorithms.

Participants and use

Direct participants are major US and foreign banks; the system is the main channel for the dollar leg of international foreign-exchange and Eurodollar transactions. CHIPS uses the Universal Payment Identification Code (UPIC) to allow corporate payments without revealing account details.

Other meanings

"Chips" may also refer informally to semiconductor chips, and "CHIPS" appears in the CHIPS and Science Act of 2022 (Creating Helpful Incentives to Produce Semiconductors), which subsidizes US semiconductor manufacturing.
