Seal of the Prophets

Seal of the Prophets (Arabic: خاتم النبيين, khātam an-nabiyyīn) is a title used in the Qur'an and in Islamic tradition to designate the prophet Muhammad as the last of the prophets sent by God. The phrase derives from Qur'an 33:40, which describes Muhammad as the "Messenger of Allah and the Seal of the Prophets".

Interpretation

Mainstream Muslim belief in the finality of prophethood (khatm an-nubuwwa) holds that Muhammad is the last prophet, after whom no new prophet will come, and that his revelation completes and supersedes earlier scriptures. Jurists and theologians have historically treated denial of the doctrine as heresy. The interpretation is contested by the Ahmadiyya movement, which holds that subordinate, non-law-bearing prophethood remains possible — a position that has led to Ahmadis being declared non-Muslims in Pakistan and persecuted in several countries. The Bahá'í Faith, while honouring Muhammad as a Manifestation of God, believes revelation continues progressively after him.

Related concepts

Similar ideas appear in other traditions: Manichaeism described Mani as the seal of the prophets, and the title is echoed in later Sufi doctrines of the "seal of sainthood" (khatm al-wilāya).
