Alcea is a genus of flowering plants in the mallow family, Malvaceae. The genus comprises roughly 60 to 80 species native to Europe and Asia, and its members are commonly known as hollyhocks. The best-known species is the common hollyhock, Alcea rosea, a widely grown ornamental garden plant.

Description

Species of Alcea are biennial or short-lived perennial herbaceous plants. They typically produce tall, unbranched or sparsely branched stems bearing large, showy flowers in shades of pink, red, white, yellow and purple. The leaves are lobed and rough to the touch. Flowers have five petals and a prominent central column of fused stamens surrounding the pistil, characteristic of the mallow family.

Distribution and habitat

The genus is native from southwestern and central Asia eastward into China, with some species extending into southeastern Europe. Hollyhocks thrive in sunny, well-drained sites and are frequently grown against walls and fences in cottage gardens.

Cultivation and uses

Hollyhocks have been cultivated in Europe since at least the 15th century and are believed to have been introduced from China. They are popular as background plants in borders. Various parts of the plant have been used in traditional medicine, and the flowers have been used to produce dyes. The genus is susceptible to hollyhock rust, a fungal disease caused by Puccinia malvacearum.
