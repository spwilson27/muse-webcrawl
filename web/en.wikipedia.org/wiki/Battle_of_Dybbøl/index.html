Battle of Dybbøl

The Battle of Dybbøl (Danish: Slaget ved Dybbøl; German: Erstürmung der Düppeler Schanzen) was fought on 18 April 1864 during the Second Schleswig War. Prussian forces stormed the Danish fortifications at Dybbøl, near Sønderborg in southern Jutland. The Prussian victory was a decisive moment in the war and in the unification of Germany.

Background

After Denmark incorporated Schleswig in the November Constitution of 1863, Prussia and Austria invaded in February 1864. Danish forces withdrew to the entrenched position at Dybbøl, covering the crossing to the island of Als. Prussian forces under Prince Friedrich Karl besieged the position and subjected it to weeks of bombardment with rifled artillery, for which the Danish earthworks and the armoured ship Rolf Krake could only partly compensate.

The assault

On 18 April, after a six-hour bombardment by some 102 guns, Prussian storm columns attacked the shattered redoubts at 10 a.m. and carried them in fierce hand-to-hand fighting. The Danish 8th Brigade launched a brave but costly counter-attack to cover the retreat across the pontoon bridges to Als. Among the Danish dead were many of the defenders of the redoubts; the famous Dybbøl Mill was damaged in the fighting.

Aftermath

The defeat broke Danish resistance on the mainland. Prussian troops later crossed to Als (29 June), and by the Treaty of Vienna (October 1864) Denmark ceded Schleswig, Holstein and Lauenburg — roughly 40 percent of its territory. Dybbøl became a lasting symbol of Danish national memory, and the battlefield and mill remain major memorial sites.
