Kangchenjunga

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Kangchenjunga's broad five-peaked snowy massif glowing pink at sunrise from Darjeeling]
Kangchenjunga from Darjeeling.

Kangchenjunga, at 8,586 metres (28,169 ft), is the third-highest mountain in the world. It lies in the eastern Himalayas on the border between Nepal and the Indian state of Sikkim. Its name is often translated as 'the Five Treasures of the Snow', for its five peaks. It was thought to be the world's highest mountain until 1852, when calculations for Mount Everest were published.

Climbing

The first ascent was by Joe Brown and George Band on 25 May 1955, on a British expedition. Out of respect for the beliefs of the people of Sikkim, who regard the summit as sacred, climbers traditionally stop a few feet short of the very top — a custom begun by the first ascenders.

Conservation

The surrounding region is protected as Kangchenjunga National Park in India (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) and the Kanchenjunga Conservation Area in Nepal, home to red pandas, snow leopards and rich rhododendron forests.
