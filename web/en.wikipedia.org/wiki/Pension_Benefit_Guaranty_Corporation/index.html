The Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation (PBGC) is a United States federal government agency created by the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 (ERISA) to protect pension benefits in private-sector defined benefit plans. The PBGC pays, or pays through a third party, monthly retirement benefits, up to a guaranteed maximum, to participants of insured plans that are insolvent or terminated without sufficient funds. It is not funded by general tax revenues; instead, its operations are financed by insurance premiums set by Congress and paid by sponsors of defined benefit plans, investment income, assets from pension plans it trusts, and recoveries from the companies formerly responsible for the plans.

History

The PBGC was created on September 2, 1974, when President Gerald Ford signed ERISA into law. Its creation was prompted in part by high-profile pension plan failures, such as the 1963 shutdown of the Studebaker automobile plant, which left many workers without the full pensions they had been promised. Before ERISA, there was no federal insurance program for private pension plans, and workers could lose most or all of their retirement benefits if their employer went bankrupt.

Over the following decades the PBGC took over a number of large failed plans, including those of steelmakers such as Bethlehem Steel and LTV, airlines such as Pan Am, Eastern Air Lines, TWA, and United Airlines, and automakers and parts suppliers. Its largest single takeover at the time was the United Airlines plan in 2005. The agency's financial condition has periodically been a matter of concern, particularly in its multiemployer insurance program, which was projected to become insolvent before Congress enacted the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021, providing special financial assistance to troubled multiemployer plans.

Insurance programs

The PBGC runs two distinct insurance programs:

  • Single-employer program, covering plans sponsored by one employer or members of a controlled group, which insures the majority of covered participants.
  • Multiemployer program, covering collectively bargained plans maintained by more than one employer, usually within the same or related industries.

The programs are legally separate and their funds may not be commingled. The PBGC insures defined benefit plans only; it does not insure defined contribution plans such as 401(k) plans, whose benefits depend on account balances rather than a promised formula.

Guarantee limits

The PBGC guarantee is subject to a statutory maximum monthly amount, adjusted annually, and to other limits: benefits earned shortly before plan termination, recent benefit increases, and certain early-retirement supplements may not be fully guaranteed. The maximum guarantee for a participant retiring at age 65 in a single-employer plan that terminates in a given year is set by formula in ERISA; for plans terminating in 2024, the monthly maximum was $7,107.95 (for a life annuity with no survivor benefit). Multiemployer guarantees are calculated under a different, lower formula.

Governance and funding

The PBGC is governed by a board of directors consisting of the Secretaries of Labor, Commerce, and Treasury, with the Secretary of Labor as chair. A director, appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate, manages day-to-day operations. Premiums include a flat-rate per-participant premium and, for single-employer plans, a variable-rate premium based on a plan's unfunded vested benefits.
