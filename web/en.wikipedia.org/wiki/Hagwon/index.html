Hagwon

A hagwon (Korean: 학원; Hanja: 學院) is a for-profit private educational institution in South Korea — commonly known as a cram school — that supplements regular schooling, typically outside normal school hours. Hagwons teach subjects ranging from mathematics, science and Korean to English language, art and music, and prepare students for school exams and university entrance examinations.

Role in South Korean education

Private tutoring is deeply embedded in South Korean education culture, driven by intense competition for admission to prestigious universities. A large majority of schoolchildren attend at least one hagwon, and the sector employs a vast number of teachers, including well-known "star" instructors. Evening hagwon districts such as Daechi-dong in Seoul are famous for their density of academies.

Regulation and debate

Governments have repeatedly attempted to curb private education spending with measures such as evening curfews for hagwons, citing student stress and household costs; in 1980 private tutoring was banned outright, a prohibition gradually dismantled and finally ruled unconstitutional in 2000. The industry remains a subject of social debate over educational inequality.
