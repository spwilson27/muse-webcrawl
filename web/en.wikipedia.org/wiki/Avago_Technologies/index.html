Avago Technologies was a Singaporean multinational semiconductor company that designed, developed, and supplied analog and mixed-signal integrated circuits, optoelectronic components, and radio-frequency (RF) devices. The company's technology originated in the semiconductor products division of Hewlett-Packard, and it became an independent company through a series of spinoffs before acquiring Broadcom Corporation in 2016 and adopting the Broadcom name.

History

Avago's roots trace to 1961, when Hewlett-Packard established a semiconductor products group that produced early light-emitting diodes (LEDs) and other components. In 1999, HP spun off its test-and-measurement, chemical analysis, and components businesses as Agilent Technologies, which included the semiconductor products group. In 2005, Agilent sold that group to private equity firms Kohlberg Kravis Roberts and Silver Lake Partners, and the business was reconstituted as Avago Technologies.

Avago went public on the NASDAQ stock exchange in 2009. Under chief executive Hock Tan, the company pursued aggressive growth through acquisitions, including the storage semiconductor maker LSI Corporation in 2014, PLX Technology, and Emulex. In May 2015, Avago announced a $37 billion agreement to acquire Broadcom Corporation, then the largest acquisition in the semiconductor industry. The deal closed in February 2016, and the combined company took the name Broadcom Limited, with Avago's ticker symbol AVGO retained.

Products

Avago was known for optocouplers, fiber-optic transceivers, motion encoders, infrared transceivers, and RF power amplifiers used in mobile phones, as well as surface acoustic wave and film bulk acoustic resonator (FBAR) filters used in wireless handsets. Its products served the wireless communications, enterprise storage, wired infrastructure, and industrial markets.
