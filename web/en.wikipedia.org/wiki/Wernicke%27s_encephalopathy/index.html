Wernicke's encephalopathy

CauseThiamine (vitamin B1) deficiency

Wernicke's encephalopathy is an acute neurological syndrome caused by severe deficiency of thiamine (vitamin B1). It is most often seen in people with chronic heavy alcohol use and malnutrition, but also occurs after bariatric surgery, hyperemesis gravidarum, and other states of nutritional depletion. It was described by Carl Wernicke in 1881.

Signs and symptoms

The classic triad consists of confusion, gait ataxia, and eye-movement abnormalities such as nystagmus and ophthalmoplegia — though the full triad is present in only a minority of patients, and the condition is frequently underdiagnosed.

Treatment and prognosis

Wernicke's encephalopathy is a medical emergency treated with high-dose parenteral thiamine, given before glucose to avoid precipitating or worsening the syndrome. Without prompt treatment it may progress to the chronic amnestic Korsakoff syndrome or death; together the two are often termed Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome.
