Parliament was an American funk band formed in the late 1960s by George Clinton as part of his Parliament-Funkadelic collective. Where its sister band Funkadelic fused funk with psychedelic rock, Parliament pursued a horn-driven, dance-oriented funk built around an elaborate science-fiction mythology of "Aqua Boogie", Dr. Funkenstein and the descent of the Mothership. Their albums Mothership Connection (1975), The Clones of Dr. Funkenstein (1976) and Funkentelechy vs. the Placebo Syndrome (1977) produced hit singles including "P. Funk (Wants to Get Funked Up)", "Tear the Roof off the Sucker (Give Up the Funk)", "Aqua Boogie" and "Flash Light". Parliament was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997, together with Funkadelic.

History

Clinton had led the doo-wop group The Parliaments in the 1950s–60s, scoring a hit with "(I Wanna) Testify" (1967). A contractual dispute over the name led him to relaunch the vocal group's backing band as Funkadelic in 1970; once the naming issue was resolved, he revived the name as Parliament, recording Osmium (1970) for Invictus Records. Signing to Casablanca Records in 1974, Parliament — with keyboardist Bernie Worrell, bassist Bootsy Collins, guitarist Garry Shider and the Horny Horns (Maceo Parker and Fred Wesley, ex-James Brown) — became one of the defining acts of 1970s funk. The P-Funk Earth Tour of 1976–77 featured the theatrical landing of the Mothership, a full-size stage prop now held by the Smithsonian's National Museum of African American History and Culture.

Legacy

Parliament's records became foundational sample sources for hip hop — especially G-funk — and for electronic dance music. "Flash Light" and the Mothership mythology remain staples of the funk canon; Clinton continued to tour with Parliament-Funkadelic line-ups into the 2020s.
