Prince Paolo Petrovich Troubetzkoy (Russian: Па́вел Петро́вич Трубецко́й; 15 February 1866 – 12 February 1938), also spelled Trubetskoy, was an Italian sculptor of Russian descent, regarded as the leading "impressionist" sculptor of his era. Bernard Shaw called him "the most astonishing sculptor of modern times."

Life and career

Troubetzkoy was born at Intra on Lake Maggiore, the son of the Russian diplomat Prince Pyotr Trubetskoy and the American opera singer Ada Winans. Largely self-taught, he worked in Milan in the 1880s–90s, modelling lively, sketch-like bronzes of fashionable society. From 1897 to 1906 he taught in Moscow and became closely associated with Leo Tolstoy, of whom he made celebrated portrait busts and statuettes — Tolstoy on horseback among them — at Yasnaya Polyana.

Major works

His most famous commission was the colossal equestrian monument to Tsar Alexander III in St Petersburg (unveiled 1909), whose massive, ponderous horse and rider provoked both admiration and satire. Troubetzkoy also portrayed many leading figures of his day, including George Bernard Shaw, Puccini, Rodin and Segantini, and produced numerous animal sculptures. A lifelong vegetarian and animal lover, he later worked in Paris, England, and Hollywood, where he sculpted film stars, before settling again at Pallanza on Lake Maggiore.

Style

Troubetzkoy's bronzes are characterised by rapid, textured modelling that captures a fleeting likeness and a sense of movement, applying impressionist principles to sculpture. His plasters and bronzes are held by the Museo del Paesaggio at Verbania-Pallanza, the Tretyakov Gallery, and many other collections.
