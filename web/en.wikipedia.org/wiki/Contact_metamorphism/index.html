Contact metamorphism

Contact metamorphism is the alteration of rock by heat from a nearby magma intrusion. It occurs at relatively low pressure and high temperature close to igneous intrusions, producing a baked zone called a metamorphic aureole.

Aureoles and zoning

Temperature decreases away from the intrusion, so the aureole is zoned: innermost rocks are highest grade (e.g. hornfels, or skarn where fluids react with carbonates), grading outward into spotted slates and unaltered country rock. Aureole widths range from centimeters around thin dikes to kilometers around large plutons.

Products

  • Hornfels — hard, fine-grained rock from baked mudstone.
  • Marble — recrystallized limestone.
  • Skarn — calc-silicate rocks hosting ore minerals such as garnet, pyroxene and magnetite.
  • Quartzite — recrystallized sandstone.

Comparison with regional metamorphism

Unlike regional metamorphism, contact metamorphism is local, static (non-deforming), and driven by heat rather than by directed pressure; index minerals such as andalusite and cordierite reflect low-pressure, high-temperature conditions.
