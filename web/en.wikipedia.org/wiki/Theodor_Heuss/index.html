Overview

Theodor Heuss (31 January 1884 - 12 December 1963) was a German liberal politician, journalist, and writer who served as the first President of the Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany) from 1949 to 1959. A respected figure across party lines, he came to personify the young republic's democratic respectability after the Nazi era.

Life and career

Heuss studied economics and political science, worked as a journalist and editor, and was associated with Friedrich Naumann's liberal movement. He served in the Reichstag during the Weimar Republic for liberal parties. Under the Nazi regime he was barred from political office and largely from publishing, and withdrew into writing biographies.

After 1945 Heuss helped found the Free Democratic Party (FDP), becoming its first chairman, and served on the Parliamentary Council that drafted the Basic Law in 1948-49. Elected president by the Federal Convention in September 1949, he was re-elected in 1954 and served two terms, declining a constitutional amendment that would have permitted a third. As president he emphasized reconciliation, civic education, and Germany's moral reckoning, becoming affectionately known to the public as 'Papa Heuss'. He died in Stuttgart in 1963.
