This is a list of horse breeds — domesticated varieties of the horse (Equus ferus caballus) developed for specific purposes such as racing, riding, driving, and draught work. Hundreds of breeds exist worldwide.

Light and riding horse breeds

  • Arabian — one of the oldest and most influential breeds; endurance riding.
  • Thoroughbred — developed in England for flat racing.
  • American Quarter Horse — the most popular breed in the United States; sprinting and ranch work.
  • Andalusian — Spanish breed prized for dressage.
  • Lipizzan — famous for the Spanish Riding School in Vienna.
  • Hanoverian — German warmblood, leading sport horse breed.
  • Dutch Warmblood (KWPN) — top-ranked in dressage and show jumping.
  • Morgan — one of the earliest American breeds.
  • American Saddlebred — a gaited show breed.
  • Tennessee Walking Horse — known for its smooth running walk gait.
  • Appaloosa — spotted coat patterns; developed by the Nez Perce.
  • Paint Horse — pinto-spotted American breed.
  • Akhal-Teke — Turkmen breed with a distinctive metallic sheen.
  • Mustang — free-roaming horse of the American West.
  • Friesian — black Dutch breed with long feathering.
  • Icelandic horse — small, hardy, five-gaited breed.
  • Standardbred — used for harness racing.
  • Marwari — Indian breed with inward-curving ears.
  • Connemara pony — Irish riding pony.
  • Welsh Pony and Cob — versatile British pony breed.

Draught (draft) breeds

  • Clydesdale — Scottish heavy horse with heavy feathering.
  • Shire — English breed; among the largest horses in the world.
  • Percheron — French draught breed, widely exported.
  • Belgian Draught — one of the strongest heavy breeds.
  • Suffolk Punch — English breed, always chestnut in colour.
