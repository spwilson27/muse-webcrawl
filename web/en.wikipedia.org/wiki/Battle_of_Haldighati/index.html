Battle of Haldighati
Date18 June 1576
LocationHaldighati pass, near Gogunda, Rajasthan, India
ResultMughal victory
BelligerentsMughal Empire vs. Kingdom of Mewar
CommandersMan Singh I of Amber (Mughal); Maharana Pratap (Mewar)

The Battle of Haldighati was fought on 18 June 1576 between the armies of the Mughal Empire, led by Man Singh I of Amber, and the forces of Maharana Pratap, the ruler of Mewar, in a narrow mountain pass at Haldighati near Gogunda, in present-day Rajasthan. The pass takes its name from its turmeric-coloured (haldi) soil. The battle was part of the Mughal emperor Akbar's campaign to subdue Mewar, whose ruler refused to submit personally to Mughal authority.

The battle

Maharana Pratap's smaller force — a few thousand cavalry supported by Bhil archers — met the much larger Mughal army in the confined terrain, which partly negated the Mughal advantage in numbers and artillery. The fighting was exceptionally fierce and lasted only a few hours. Pratap, mounted on his celebrated horse Chetak, is said to have charged directly at Man Singh. Though the Mughals held the field, Pratap escaped into the hills; Chetak is legendarily said to have carried him to safety despite mortal wounds before dying. Casualties were heavy on both sides.

Aftermath

The Mughals occupied Gogunda and Udaipur but could not capture Pratap, who continued a guerrilla resistance from the Aravalli hills and eventually recovered much of Mewar. The battle became an enduring symbol of Rajput resistance and valour in Indian historical memory, and Haldighati is today a site of memorials, including a cenotaph for Chetak.
