The River Corrib (Irish: Abhainn na Gaillimhe, "river of Galway") flows from Lough Corrib through the city of Galway to Galway Bay in the west of Ireland. At roughly six kilometres it is one of the shortest rivers in Ireland, yet its mean discharge — around 100 cubic metres per second, fed by the second-largest lake in Ireland — makes it one of the largest Irish rivers by volume.

Course

The river leaves Lough Corrib near Menlo and runs south through Galway city, passing under the Salmon Weir Bridge and the university (NUI Galway) grounds, before dividing around Nun's Island and recombining to enter the Claddagh basin and Galway Bay. A canal system, developed in the 19th century (the Eglinton Canal), links the river with the sea and once supplied water power to mills and distilleries.

Uses

The Salmon Weir regulates lake levels and is fitted with a fish pass; the Corrib fishery is noted for salmon and brown trout angling. A hydroelectric station at the weir, built in the 1950s, supplies a small amount of power. The river passes several historic sites, including Galway Cathedral and the Fisheries Watchtower.
