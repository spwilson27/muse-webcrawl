Pamukkale, meaning "cotton castle" in Turkish, is a natural site in Denizli Province in southwestern Turkey. The area is famous for a carbonate mineral left by the flowing of thermal spring water, which has formed dazzling white travertine terraces, terraced basins and petrified waterfalls cascading down a hillside some 200 metres high.

Geology

The terraces are formed of travertine, a sedimentary rock deposited by water from hot springs with a very high mineral content. Water emerging at about 35 °C is supersaturated with calcium carbonate; as it flows over the cliff edge and degasses, the carbonate precipitates, first as soft gels and eventually hardening into the white terraces. There are 17 hot springs in the ancient city of Hierapolis above the terraces.

Hierapolis

The attraction of the springs led to the founding of the Greco-Roman spa city of Hierapolis at the top of the cliff in the early 2nd century BC. Its ruins — including a large necropolis, theatre, baths and the Plutonium — together with Pamukkale were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1988.

Tourism and protection

Pamukkale became a major tourist destination in the late 20th century. Hotels built on the terraces in the 1960s caused significant damage and were demolished after World Heritage listing; visitors now walk barefoot on designated routes, and thermal water is channelled on a managed rotation to allow the terraces to regenerate their white colour.
