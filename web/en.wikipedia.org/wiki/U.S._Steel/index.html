U.S. Steel

United States Steel Corporation, more commonly known as U.S. Steel, is an American integrated steel producer headquartered in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. Founded in 1901 by J. P. Morgan and attorney Elbert H. Gary, it was formed through the merger of Andrew Carnegie's Carnegie Steel Company with Gary's Federal Steel Company and William Henry Moore's National Steel Company. At its founding it was the largest business enterprise ever launched, with a capitalization of $1.4 billion, and it produced about two-thirds of the steel made in the United States.

For much of the 20th century U.S. Steel was the emblem of American industrial might. It was the first company to reach a billion dollars in market capitalization, and its ticker symbol "X" became one of the most recognized on the New York Stock Exchange. Its mills around Pittsburgh and at Gary, Indiana, supplied steel for bridges, skyscrapers, railroads, automobiles, and warships.

History

The company was incorporated in New Jersey on February 25, 1901. Judge Elbert Gary, after whom the city of Gary, Indiana, is named, served as its chairman. The new corporation absorbed Carnegie's vast holdings, including the Homestead Steel Works, site of the bitter 1892 Homestead Strike. In 1907 the company acquired the Tennessee Coal, Iron and Railroad Company during the Panic of 1907, a deal approved by President Theodore Roosevelt that later became the subject of an antitrust suit the government ultimately lost in 1920.

During both world wars U.S. Steel was a major supplier to the military. After World War II it faced growing competition from foreign producers and domestic mini-mills. In 1982, under chairman David Roderick, the company bought Marathon Oil and in 1986 renamed its parent holding company USX Corporation, reflecting its diversification into energy; the steel unit kept the U.S. Steel name. In 2001 the energy business was spun off and the company reverted to the United States Steel Corporation name.

Operations

U.S. Steel operates major facilities including Gary Works in Indiana, its largest mill; the Mon Valley Works near Pittsburgh, including the Edgar Thomson Plant and the Clairton coke works; Granite City Works in Illinois; and Great Lakes Works in Michigan. In 2021 it completed the full acquisition of Big River Steel in Arkansas, a modern electric-arc-furnace mini-mill, as part of a strategy combining integrated and mini-mill production. Its European operations are centered on U.S. Steel Košice in Slovakia.

Nippon Steel acquisition

In December 2023 Japan's Nippon Steel agreed to acquire U.S. Steel for $14.9 billion, a deal that drew political opposition in the United States over national security and labor concerns. The transaction was blocked by President Joe Biden in January 2025 but was subsequently approved under the Trump administration with a "golden share" arrangement giving the U.S. government certain governance rights, and the acquisition closed in June 2025, making U.S. Steel a subsidiary of Nippon Steel North America while retaining its name and Pittsburgh headquarters.
