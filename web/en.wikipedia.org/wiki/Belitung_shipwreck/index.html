Belitung shipwreck

Discovered: 1998, off Belitung Island, Indonesia

Date of wreck: c. 826 CE

Vessel type: Arab dhow (stitched-plank)

Cargo: over 60,000 Tang dynasty artefacts

The Belitung shipwreck (also known as the Tang shipwreck) is the wreck of an Arab dhow that sank around 826 CE in the Gaspar Strait off Belitung Island, Indonesia, while sailing from Africa or Arabia back toward China. Discovered in 1998 by sea-cucumber divers, the wreck contained the largest single collection of Tang dynasty artefacts ever found, providing striking evidence of the maritime trade routes — the Maritime Silk Road — linking Tang China with the Abbasid world.

The ship

The vessel was about 18 metres long and built in the western Indian Ocean tradition: its planks were stitched together with cord rather than nailed, and made of African and West Asian timbers. It is one of the earliest Arab ships found in Southeast Asian waters and is comparable to the 9th-century Jewel of Muscat, a replica vessel built in Oman and sailed to Singapore in 2010.

The cargo

The cargo comprised more than 60,000 items, mostly mass-produced Changsha ware bowls, along with fine Yue ware, white wares, cobalt-blue-and-white ceramics (among the earliest examples of blue-and-white porcelain), gold and silver vessels including an octagonal gold cup, mirrors, and spices. A bowl dated by inscription to 826 CE fixed the date of the voyage. The collection was purchased by Singapore and is displayed at the Asian Civilisations Museum as the Tang Shipwreck collection.
