Clarivate

Clarivate is a provider of data, analytics and workflow services to academia, government, life sciences and intellectual property customers.

Clarivate Plc is an American publicly traded analytics company that operates a collection of subscription-based services, largely in the field of bibliometrics and scientometrics, patents, trademark research, pharmaceutical and biotech intelligence, and intellectual property management. Clarivate has been described as an important actor in the scholarly publishing analytics market, and its products include the Web of Science, EndNote, ProQuest, Ex Libris, and the Derwent World Patents Index.

History

The company traces its origins to the Institute for Scientific Information (ISI), founded by Eugene Garfield in 1960, and to the Intellectual Property and Science division of Thomson Reuters. In 2016, Thomson Reuters sold that division to private equity firms Onex Corporation and Baring Private Equity Asia for $3.55 billion; the business was relaunched under the name Clarivate Analytics.

Clarivate became a publicly traded company in 2019 through a reverse merger with Churchill Capital Corp. It expanded aggressively by acquisition: in 2020 it acquired CPA Global, an intellectual property services company, and in December 2021 it completed the acquisition of ProQuest, a major provider of library content and software, for about $5.3 billion. The acquisitions made Clarivate one of the largest vendors to academic libraries worldwide and drew scrutiny from library organisations over market concentration.

Products and services

Clarivate's best-known product is the Web of Science, a citation index platform descended from Garfield's Science Citation Index. It also publishes the Journal Citation Reports, which computes the journal impact factor, and compiles annual lists such as the Highly Cited Researchers and the Citation Laureates. Other products include Cortellis (pharmaceutical intelligence), CompuMark (trademark research), MarkMonitor (brand protection), and the library systems Alma and Primo through Ex Libris.

Criticism

Clarivate has been criticised for the opacity and influence of its journal selection processes for the Web of Science and for the annual delisting of journals from its indexes, which can have significant consequences for the researchers who publish in them. Its role in measuring academic performance has also been questioned by advocates of open research metrics.
