IAU definition of planet

The IAU definition of planet is the definition of the term planet adopted by the International Astronomical Union (IAU) at its General Assembly in Prague on 24 August 2006. Under the definition, a planet is a celestial body that (a) is in orbit around the Sun, (b) has sufficient mass for its self-gravity to overcome rigid body forces so that it assumes a hydrostatic equilibrium (nearly round) shape, and (c) has cleared the neighbourhood around its orbit.

Bodies meeting the first two criteria but not the third — such as Pluto, Eris, and Ceres — were classified as dwarf planets. The definition thus reduced the Solar System's planets from nine to eight and reclassified Pluto, a change that proved publicly controversial.

Background

The definition was prompted by the discovery of numerous large trans-Neptunian objects, above all Eris (announced in 2005), which is more massive than Pluto; astronomers faced either admitting dozens of new planets or defining the term restrictively. A draft proposal that would have kept Pluto and added Charon, Ceres, and Eris was replaced in committee by the final text; the "clearing the neighbourhood" clause, criticised as vague, refers to a planet's gravitational dominance of its orbital zone, quantified by measures such as the planetary discriminant.

Criticism and later debate

Critics, including Alan Stern, principal investigator of the New Horizons mission to Pluto, argue the definition is imprecise — no planet fully clears its orbit — and excludes exoplanets by referring specifically to the Sun; some planetary scientists advocate a geophysical definition based on intrinsic properties alone. The cultural reaction to Pluto's demotion was considerable, including legislative gestures in some U.S. states, and "pluto" was named 2006 word of the year by the American Dialect Society as a verb meaning to demote or devalue.
