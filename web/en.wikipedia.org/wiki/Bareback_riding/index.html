Bareback riding

Bareback riding is a rodeo event in which a rider attempts to stay on a bucking horse (a "bareback bronc") for eight seconds using only a leather-and-rawhide rigging cinched around the horse's barrel, with a built-in handle gripped in one hand. Despite the name, the horse wears this rigging and a flank strap, but no saddle, and the rider may not touch the horse or equipment with the free hand.

Rules and scoring

The ride is scored out of 100 points, half for the horse's bucking performance and half for the rider, who is judged on control, timing and the "spur out" — starting with spurs set above the horse's shoulders at the first jump — and on spurring in rhythm with the bucking. Riders wear a protective vest and a glove rosin-treated for grip; the event is considered among the most physically punishing in rodeo, imposing severe strain on the rider's arm, back and neck. Bareback riding is one of the standard events of the Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association and the National Finals Rodeo, alongside bull riding, saddle bronc riding, steer wrestling, team roping, tie-down roping and barrel racing.

General usage

Outside rodeo, "bareback" simply means riding a horse without a saddle, a practice used for training, swimming horses, and in informal riding, sometimes with only a bareback pad for comfort.
