Shinichi Suzuki

Died26 January 1998, Matsumoto, Japan
OccupationViolinist, music educator

Shinichi Suzuki (鈴木 鎮一, 1898–1998) was a Japanese violinist and music educator, and the founder of the Suzuki method of music education, one of the most influential approaches to teaching music to young children in the 20th century.

Life

Suzuki was born in Nagoya, one of twelve children; his father owned a violin factory. He taught himself violin as a teenager after hearing a recording of Mischa Elman, then studied in Tokyo and, from 1921 to 1928, in Berlin with Karl Klingler. On returning to Japan he formed a string quartet with his brothers and began teaching. After the Second World War he developed his teaching philosophy in Matsumoto, where he founded the Talent Education Research Institute. His students' performances abroad in the 1950s and 1960s attracted international attention, and the method spread widely in the United States and elsewhere.

Philosophy

Suzuki's central insight — which he called the "mother-tongue method" — was that children learn their native language effortlessly by listening, imitation, repetition, and encouragement, and that musical ability could be developed the same way. Key elements include beginning lessons as early as age three or four, extensive listening to recordings of the repertoire, parental involvement in practice, delaying music reading until playing is established, and group lessons alongside individual instruction. He held that "every child can" learn, and that talent education aimed at character as much as at skill. His ideas are set out in his book Nurtured by Love (1969).
