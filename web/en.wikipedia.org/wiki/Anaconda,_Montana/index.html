Anaconda is a city in and the county seat of Deer Lodge County, Montana, United States. Located in southwestern Montana at the foot of the Anaconda Range, the city was founded in 1883 by copper magnate Marcus Daly as the site of a smelter to process copper ore from nearby Butte. It forms a consolidated city-county government with Deer Lodge County.

History

Daly chose the site along Warm Springs Creek for the Washoe smelter works of the Anaconda Copper Mining Company. The town was first platted under the name "Copperopolis"; Daly is said to have taken the name Anaconda from a newspaper reference to Horace Greeley's description of Ulysses S. Grant's armies encircling Robert E. Lee's like an anaconda, as well as from the existing Anaconda mine in Butte. Anaconda became one of the largest copper-smelting centres in the world, and contended with Helena to become Montana's state capital in the 1890s, losing the vote of 1894.

The Smelter Stack

The Anaconda Smelter Stack, completed in 1918, is one of the tallest freestanding brick structures in the world at 178.3 m (585 ft). The smelter closed in 1980, but the stack was preserved as a state park monument and landmark after public campaigning to save it from demolition.

Environment

Decades of smelting left heavy-metal contamination across the area; the Anaconda Co. Smelter site was placed on the federal Superfund list in 1983, and remediation, including the Old Works Golf Course designed by Jack Nicklaus on reclaimed smelter land, has continued since.

Geography and recreation

Anaconda sits along the Pintler Scenic Route near Georgetown Lake and the Anaconda-Pintler Wilderness, offering fishing, skiing at Discovery Ski Area, and hunting.
