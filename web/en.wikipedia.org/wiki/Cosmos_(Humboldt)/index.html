AuthorAlexander von Humboldt
LanguageGerman
Published1845–1862 (5 volumes)
SubjectNatural science

Cosmos: A Sketch of a Physical Description of the Universe (German: Kosmos: Entwurf einer physischen Weltbeschreibung) is an influential treatise on science and nature written by the German scientist and explorer Alexander von Humboldt. Its first volume appeared in 1845, when Humboldt was in his seventies; five volumes were published, the last posthumously in 1862.

Cosmos began as a series of lectures Humboldt gave at the University of Berlin in 1827–28. The work attempts to unify diverse branches of scientific knowledge and culture into a single physical description of the universe, treating the heavens and the Earth, and the interplay between the physical world and human thought, art and feeling.

Contents and influence

The volumes survey astronomy, the Earth's form and magnetism, climate, geology, volcanoes, plant and animal geography, and finally the human response to nature in art and literature. Cosmos was a bestseller, quickly translated into many languages; its English translation sold widely in Britain and America. It helped popularise science as an interconnected, empirical enterprise, popularised the word Kosmos in its modern sense, and influenced writers and naturalists including Charles Darwin (who kept Humboldt's earlier narratives aboard HMS Beagle), Henry David Thoreau, and Walt Whitman. The 1980 television series and book Cosmos by Carl Sagan consciously echoed Humboldt's title and ambition.
