Murchison meteorite

The Murchison meteorite is a carbonaceous chondrite that fell near Murchison, Victoria, Australia, on 28 September 1969. More than 100 kilograms of material were recovered, making it one of the most studied meteorites in the world.

Organic compounds

Murchison is famous for containing a rich diversity of organic compounds, including more than 70 amino acids, many not found in terrestrial biology, as well as nucleobases such as uracil and xanthine. These findings support the idea that key building blocks of life could have been delivered to the early Earth by meteorites.

Presolar grains

The meteorite also contains presolar grains, including silicon carbide grains older than the Solar System itself; some have been dated to around 7 billion years, the oldest material dated on Earth.
