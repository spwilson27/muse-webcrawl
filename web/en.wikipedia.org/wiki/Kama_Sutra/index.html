Kama Sutra

The Kama Sutra (Sanskrit: कामसूत्र, Kāmasūtra) is an ancient Indian Hindu text widely considered to be the standard work on human sexual behaviour in Sanskrit literature. It was written by Vātsyāyana, probably between the 2nd and 4th centuries CE. Despite its modern popular reputation, only a portion of the work deals with sexual positions; most of it is a guide to virtuous living, love, courtship, marriage and relationships.

Contents

The text consists of seven parts (adhikaraṇas) divided into 36 chapters. It situates kāma (pleasure and desire) as one of the four aims of human life (puruṣārtha), alongside dharma, artha and moksha. Chapters cover general conduct and accomplishments of a cultivated citizen, the stages of courtship and marriage, relations with spouses and courtesans, and the arts of love, including the famous discussion of embraces, kisses and 64 sexual positions.

Transmission and translations

The work was transmitted through numerous commentaries, notably the Jayamaṅgalā of Yashodhara. It became famous in the West through the 1883 English translation produced under the direction of Richard Burton and F. F. Arbuthnot, published privately because of Victorian obscenity laws. Modern scholarly translations, such as that of Wendy Doniger and Sudhir Kakar (2002), have restored attention to the text's literary and social dimensions.
