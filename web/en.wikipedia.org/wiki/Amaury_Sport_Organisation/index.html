Amaury Sport Organisation

The Amaury Sport Organisation (A.S.O.) is a French company that organizes major international sporting events, best known as the owner and organizer of the Tour de France cycle race. It is part of the Amaury media group, which also publishes the sports newspaper L'Équipe. From its headquarters in Issy-les-Moulineaux near Paris, A.S.O. runs some 90 events a year across about 30 countries, spanning cycling, motorsport, mass-participation running, sailing and golf.

A.S.O.'s cycling portfolio includes the Tour de France and Tour de France Femmes, the Vuelta a España (through a stake in organizer Unipublic), Paris–Nice, Paris–Roubaix, Liège–Bastogne–Liège, the Critérium du Dauphiné, and mass-participation events such as L'Étape du Tour. In motorsport it organizes the Dakar Rally, which it took over from founder Thierry Sabine's organization and moved from Africa to South America (2009–2019) and then to Saudi Arabia (from 2020).

History

The Tour de France was created in 1903 by the newspaper L'Auto, ancestor of L'Équipe, and the organization of the Amaury family's races was consolidated into A.S.O. in 1992. Under long-serving Tour director Jean-Marie Leblanc and his successor Christian Prudhomme (from 2007), A.S.O. expanded from a race organizer into a global sports-events business.

Events

Beyond cycling and the Dakar, A.S.O. organizes the Paris Marathon and other running events, the Lacoste Ladies Open de France and other golf tournaments, and sailing races. Its mass-participation division markets amateur versions of its flagship properties, such as L'Étape series rides on closed Tour de France routes in many countries.

Relations with cycling's governing bodies

As owner of the sport's most valuable race, A.S.O. has periodically clashed with the UCI over race calendars, revenue sharing and anti-doping control, notably withdrawing its races from the UCI ProTour in 2008. Its commercial weight makes it one of the most powerful private organizers in world sport.
