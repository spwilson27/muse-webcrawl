Proto-Uralic is the reconstructed proto-language of the Uralic language family, the ancestor of languages including Finnish, Estonian, Hungarian, the Sámi languages, Mordvin, Mari, Komi, Udmurt and the Samoyedic languages such as Nenets. It is reconstructed exclusively by the comparative method, as no written records exist.

Homeland and chronology

The location of the Proto-Uralic homeland (Urheimat) is contested: older scholarship favoured the region between the Volga and the Urals, while many recent proposals place it further east, in western Siberia near the Ob River. Estimates of its date range from about 6000 to 3000 BCE. Reconstructed vocabulary for the boreal forest zone — names for spruce, Siberian pine, skiing, sledges and fishing — is used to argue for a northern forest homeland.

Phonology

Proto-Uralic is reconstructed with a modest consonant inventory (including *p, *t, *k, *s, *ś, *m, *n, *ń, *ŋ, *l, *r, *j, *w and a disputed fricative *x), eight vowels in first syllables (*i, *e, *ä, *a, *u, *o, *ü, *ö in some reconstructions), vowel harmony, and a rule that stress fell on the first syllable. A hallmark of the family is consonant gradation-like alternations, developed differently in the branches, and the absence of initial consonant clusters.

Grammar

The language was agglutinative and exclusively suffixing. Nouns inflected for number and a rich case system that included a genitive in *-n, accusative in *-m, and local cases *-na (essive/locative), *-ta (ablative/partitive) and *-ŋ (lative); possessive suffixes marked the person of the possessor. Verbs had subjective and objective conjugations — agreement with a definite object, preserved in Hungarian and several other branches — and negation was expressed with a special negative verb, a feature retained in Finnish (en, et, ei…). Basic word order was subject–object–verb.

Legacy

The reconstruction of Proto-Uralic underpins study of Uralic prehistory and debates over contacts with neighbouring families, including early Indo-European loanwords into Uralic. A core of several hundred reconstructed roots is widely accepted.
