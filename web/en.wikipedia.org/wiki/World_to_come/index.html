World to come

Hebrewעולם הבא, Olam Ha-Ba
ReligionJudaism
Related conceptsMessianic Age, resurrection of the dead, Gan Eden, Gehenna

The World to Come (Hebrew: עולם הבא, Olam Ha-Ba) is a central concept in Jewish eschatology, referring to the era or state that follows the present world (Olam Ha-Zeh, "this world"). It is associated with the Messianic Age, the resurrection of the dead, and the reward of the righteous in the afterlife.

Biblical and rabbinic views

The Hebrew Bible speaks only vaguely of an afterlife, centered on Sheol, the shadowy abode of the dead. By the Second Temple period, ideas of resurrection and a future age of divine judgment had developed, appearing explicitly in the Book of Daniel. Rabbinic literature elaborated these themes: the Mishnah teaches that "all Israel has a share in the World to Come," while also listing those said to forfeit their share.

Nature of the World to Come

Rabbinic sources debate its character. Some describe it as a perfected earthly existence in the Messianic Age; others, notably Maimonides, describe a purely spiritual existence of disembodied souls contemplating God, in which there is "no eating, drinking, or procreation." Medieval and later Jewish thought commonly distinguishes the days of the Messiah on earth from the World to Come proper that follows the resurrection.

Gan Eden and Gehenna

Tradition associates the World to Come with Gan Eden (the Garden of Eden), the paradisiacal reward of the righteous, while Gehenna is a place of purification for the wicked, usually limited to twelve months. The righteous are said to "sit with crowns on their heads and delight in the radiance of the Divine Presence."
