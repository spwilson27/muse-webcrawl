Clava carin is a species of entomopathogenic fungus in the family Clavicipitaceae. Like its better-known relatives the caterpillar fungi, it parasitizes insect larvae and produces a club-shaped fruiting body (stroma) that emerges from the host.

Description

The fungus infects lepidopteran larvae in soil and leaf litter. After killing the host, it produces a slender, club-like stroma — the feature to which the genus name Clava (Latin for "club") refers. Species in this group are of interest for their secondary metabolites, several of which show immunosuppressive and insecticidal activity.

Distribution and ecology

Members of the genus are found in tropical and subtropical forest habitats, typically in humid microclimates where host insects are abundant. Along with other clavicipitaceous fungi, they are studied as potential agents of biological pest control.
