Bid–ask spread

Bid–ask spread
Also calledBid–offer spread
DefinitionAsk price minus bid price
Relates toLiquidity, transaction costs

The bid–ask spread (also bid–offer spread) is the difference between the price a buyer is willing to pay for an asset (the bid) and the price a seller is willing to accept (the ask, or offer). It is a key measure of market liquidity and an implicit cost of trading.

Basics

If the best bid for a share is $9.95 and the best ask is $10.00, the spread is $0.05. A trader who buys at the ask and immediately sells at the bid loses the spread, so it functions as a round-trip transaction cost. Market makers earn the spread in exchange for providing immediacy and bearing inventory risk.

Determinants

Spreads widen with low trading volume, high volatility, information asymmetry (adverse selection) and high order-processing or inventory costs. Highly liquid instruments such as major currency pairs and large-cap stocks have very tight spreads; thinly traded assets have wide ones. Spreads are often quoted in percentage terms as the spread divided by the midpoint price.

Types of spread

The quoted spread is the difference between posted bid and ask prices. The effective spread compares the actual execution price with the midpoint at the time of the trade. The realised spread measures the market maker's revenue after accounting for subsequent price movements.
