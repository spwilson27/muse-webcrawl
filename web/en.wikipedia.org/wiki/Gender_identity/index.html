Gender identity

[Image placeholder: Gender identity spectrum]

Gender identity is the personal sense of one's own gender. A person's gender identity can correlate with their sex assigned at birth, in which case they are described as cisgender, or may differ from it, as with transgender people. Some people identify outside the male/female binary and use terms such as non-binary or genderqueer. Gender identity is distinct from sexual orientation and from gender expression.

Formation and development

Research suggests gender identity generally becomes established in early childhood, by around age three or four. Its origins are debated: studies of twins, intersex conditions, and prenatal hormone exposure suggest biological contributions, while socialisation and culture shape how identity is understood and expressed. Most major medical and psychological associations recognise gender identity as a real and generally stable aspect of a person.

Recognition and society

A distress arising from incongruence between identity and assigned sex is termed gender dysphoria, classified in the DSM-5 and reclassified in the ICD-11 under sexual health rather than mental disorders. Legal recognition of gender identity varies widely: many jurisdictions allow changes to legal gender markers, sometimes requiring medical steps and sometimes based on self-declaration (Argentina's 2012 Gender Identity Law was an early self-declaration model). The topic is the subject of active political and social debate.
