Penguin Books is a British publishing house founded in 1935 by Allen Lane. It revolutionised publishing by selling high-quality paperback books at low prices through outlets such as Woolworths, making serious literature widely affordable.

History

Lane's first ten titles, issued in 1935 at sixpence each — the price of a packet of cigarettes — used the famous colour-coded three-band covers designed by Edward Young: orange for fiction, green for crime and blue for biography. Penguin expanded with Pelican Books for non-fiction (1937) and Puffin Books for children (1940). In 1960 Penguin's unexpurgated edition of D. H. Lawrence's Lady Chatterley's Lover led to a landmark obscenity trial, which Penguin won, transforming British publishing freedom.

Penguin today

Penguin Classics, launched under E. V. Rieu with his translation of the Odyssey (1946), became the world's largest classics list. Penguin merged with Random House in 2013 to form Penguin Random House, the largest trade publisher in the English-speaking world, while retaining the penguin colophon originally drawn at London Zoo.
