Battle of Bapheus

The Battle of Bapheus occurred on 27 July 1302 between an Ottoman army under Osman I and a Byzantine army under George Mouzalon, near the site of the present-day Turkish town of Yenişehir in Bithynia. The battle ended in an Ottoman victory and is traditionally regarded as the first major battle of the Ottoman Beylik — a milestone in the emergence of the Ottoman state and the decline of Byzantine power in Anatolia.

Background

By 1300, Osman I's small frontier principality was raiding deep into Byzantine Bithynia, threatening the fertile plains around Nicomedia and Nicaea. Emperor Andronikos II, preoccupied with the Balkans and short of money, dispatched a modest force under the megas hetaireiarches George Mouzalon — about 2,000 men, half of them Alan mercenaries — to relieve the pressure. The main contemporary source is the Byzantine historian George Pachymeres.

Battle

Osman, joined by neighboring Turkish lords, met the Byzantines at Bapheus, near the plain below Nicomedia, on 27 July 1302. The Byzantine force — its Alan contingent reluctant and poorly coordinated — was routed by the more numerous and motivated Turkish cavalry. Mouzalon escaped to Nicomedia under the protection of the Alan troops.

Significance

The victory at Bapheus gave Osman control of the countryside of Bithynia and left the great Byzantine cities of the region isolated. Ottoman tradition and later historiography treat the battle as the effective founding triumph of the Ottoman state, preceding the sieges that eventually took Bursa (1326), Nicaea (1331) and Nicomedia (1337).
