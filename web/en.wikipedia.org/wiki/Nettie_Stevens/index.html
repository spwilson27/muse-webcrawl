Nettie Stevens

Nettie Maria Stevens
Born: 7 July 1861, Cavendish, Vermont
Died: 4 May 1912, Baltimore, Maryland
Fields: Genetics, embryology
Known for: XY sex-determination system

Nettie Maria Stevens (1861–1912) was an American geneticist who discovered sex chromosomes. In 1905, working independently of Edmund Beecher Wilson, she showed that male mealworms produce sperm with either X or Y chromosomes, and that this determines the sex of offspring — the XY sex-determination system.

Life and education

Born in Vermont, Stevens worked as a teacher and librarian before earning degrees from Stanford University and a PhD from Bryn Mawr College (1903), where she worked with Thomas Hunt Morgan. She did research at Bryn Mawr and at the Naples zoological station and in Germany.

Work

Stevens studied mealworms (Tenebrio molitor) and other insects, carefully counting chromosomes in germ cells. Her 1905 paper Studies in Spermatogenesis established chromosomal sex determination, overturning ideas that sex was determined by environment or by the mother alone.

Legacy

Stevens died of breast cancer at 50, before her contribution was fully recognised; credit long went disproportionately to Wilson and Morgan. She is now celebrated as a pioneer of genetics and of women in science.
