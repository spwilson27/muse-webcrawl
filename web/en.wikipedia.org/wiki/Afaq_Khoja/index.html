Afaq Khoja (1626–1694), born Hidayat Allah, was a Naqshbandi Sufi master and political leader who came to rule Kashgaria (the western Tarim Basin) in the late 17th century. A sayyid claiming descent from the Prophet Muhammad through the Central Asian saint Ahmad Kasani (Makhdum-i Azam), he led the Aq Taghliq ("White Mountain") faction of khojas in their long struggle against the rival Qara Taghliq ("Black Mountain") faction.

Rise to power

Afaq Khoja preached across East Turkestan and Tibet — tradition credits him with contact with the 5th Dalai Lama — building a devoted following. Expelled from Kashgar by his rivals around 1670, he returned in 1678–1680 with the support of the Zunghar (Dzungar) Mongol ruler Galdan Boshugtu Khan, whose army conquered the Yarkent Khanate and installed Afaq as its client ruler. His theocratic government from Kashgar combined Sufi religious authority with political rule, a pattern that his descendants (the Afaqi khojas) maintained, contested bitterly with the Qara Taghliq line, for generations.

Legacy

The khoja factions' feuding and the resulting Zunghar and later Qing involvement shaped Xinjiang's history into the 19th century, when Afaqi descendants repeatedly invaded from Kokand. Afaq Khoja's mausoleum at Kashgar — a large tiled complex containing the tombs of some seventy members of his family across five generations — is one of the finest surviving monuments of Islamic architecture in Xinjiang, and is also popularly associated with Iparhan, the "Fragrant Concubine" of the Qianlong Emperor.
