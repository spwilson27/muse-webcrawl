Bem Sex-Role Inventory

AuthorSandra Bem
Published1974
Items60 (20 masculine, 20 feminine, 20 neutral)
Response formatSelf-rating, 7-point scale
MeasuresMasculinity, femininity, androgyny

The Bem Sex-Role Inventory (BSRI) is a psychological test developed by the American psychologist Sandra Bem in 1974 to measure the extent to which a person identifies with culturally defined masculine and feminine personality traits. It was a landmark instrument in the psychology of gender because it treated masculinity and femininity as two independent dimensions rather than opposite ends of a single scale, making it possible to classify people as sex-typed, cross-sex-typed, undifferentiated, or androgynous — high on both dimensions.

Structure and scoring

Respondents rate themselves on 60 adjectives and traits on a scale from 1 ("never or almost never true") to 7 ("always or almost always true"). Twenty items are culturally masculine (e.g. assertive, independent, athletic, forceful), 20 are culturally feminine (e.g. affectionate, gentle, sympathetic, yielding), and 20 are neutral fillers (e.g. friendly, truthful, conscientious). Originally, androgyny was scored by the difference between the two scales; later practice used median splits on the masculinity and femininity scores. A short form with 30 items was published in 1981.

Significance and criticism

The BSRI underpinned Bem's gender schema theory and the argument that androgynous individuals might be more behaviourally flexible and psychologically healthy. It became one of the most cited measures in gender research. Critics have noted that its item content reflects 1970s American stereotypes, that the scales arguably measure the narrower traits of instrumentality and expressiveness rather than global masculinity and femininity, and that the desirability of some items has shifted with culture. Bem herself later distanced gender research from the trait approach, and modern researchers often use the inventory cautiously or alongside other measures.
