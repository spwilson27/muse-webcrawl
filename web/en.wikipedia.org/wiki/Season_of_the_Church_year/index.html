Season of the Church Year

A season of the Church year, in Western Christian liturgical practice, is a portion of the liturgical year marked by a distinctive theological theme, a characteristic liturgical colour, and a prescribed cycle of scripture readings and prayers. Together the seasons form the annual rhythm by which churches commemorate the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ and the history of salvation. The principal seasons are Advent, Christmas, Ordinary Time, Lent, the Paschal Triduum, and Easter.

Structure of the year

The liturgical year begins on the First Sunday of Advent, four Sundays before Christmas Day. After the Christmas season, which extends at least to the feast of the Baptism of the Lord, the first period of Ordinary Time runs until Ash Wednesday, which opens Lent. Lent lasts forty days of fasting (excluding Sundays) and ends at the Mass of the Lord's Supper on Holy Thursday evening, when the Triduum begins. The Triduum — Holy Thursday, Good Friday, Holy Saturday, and the Easter Vigil — is reckoned by many authorities as a single liturgical observance, the summit of the year. The Easter season then continues for fifty days, ending at Pentecost.

Readings and propers

Each season carries its own "propers": prayers, antiphons, and lectionary readings appointed to it. In the Roman Rite the Sunday readings follow a three-year cycle (Years A, B, and C), while weekday readings follow a two-year cycle; many Protestant lectionaries, including the Revised Common Lectionary, derive from this scheme. The character of the readings changes with the season: Advent readings stress prophecy and expectation, Lent turns to repentance and the journey to Jerusalem, and Eastertide is dominated by the Acts of the Apostles and the Gospel of John.

Variation among traditions

Not all churches observe all the seasons. Many Reformed and evangelical traditions historically rejected the liturgical year beyond Christmas and Easter, though a renewed interest in Advent and Lent has spread widely since the twentieth century. The Eastern Orthodox Church follows a related but distinct cycle, with four major fasting periods (Great Lent, the Nativity Fast, the Apostles' Fast, and the Dormition Fast) and a year that begins on 1 September.
