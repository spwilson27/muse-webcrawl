New South Wales Court of Appeal


Queen's Square, Sydney

The New South Wales Court of Appeal is the highest court for civil and criminal matters in the Australian State of New South Wales. Established pursuant to the New South Wales Acts of Parliament in 1965, it was the first court of its kind to be established in Australia following the establishment of courts of appeal in other states and territories. Its process for dealing with applications for leave in civil cases was effectively abolished upon the commencement of the Uniform Civil Procedure Rules in 2005.

The Court of Appeal operates pursuant to the Supreme Court Act 1970 (NSW). The Court hears appeals from a variety of courts and tribunals and also deals with cases referred for the opinion of the Court. Its decisions may only be appealed to the High Court of Australia by special leave.

History

Before 1966, appeals from the Supreme Court of New South Wales were heard by the Full Court, consisting of a bench of three (or sometimes more) judges of the Court. The Court of Appeal was established under the Supreme Court and Circuit Courts (Amendment) Act 1965, commencing operation on 1 January 1966, with Sir Gordon Wallace as its first President.

Jurisdiction

The Court has appellate jurisdiction over civil and criminal matters arising in New South Wales, including appeals from the Supreme Court sitting in its common law and equity divisions, the District Court, and a range of tribunals. Most civil appeals require leave, granted where the appeal raises a question of principle or public importance, or where the administration of justice requires it.

Composition

The Court is constituted by the Chief Justice of New South Wales, the President of the Court of Appeal, and the Judges of Appeal. Matters are ordinarily heard by a bench of three judges, though important questions of law may be heard by five.
