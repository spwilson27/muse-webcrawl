The 1st Airborne Division was an airborne infantry division of the British Army during the Second World War. Raised in 1941 under the command of Major-General Frederick "Boy" Browning, it became one of the most famous British formations of the war, principally for its role in Operation Market Garden and the Battle of Arnhem in September 1944.

Formation

Following the success of German airborne operations in 1940, Prime Minister Winston Churchill ordered the creation of a British parachute force. The division was formed in November 1941 from the 1st Parachute Brigade and the 1st Airlanding Brigade, later joined by a second parachute brigade and Polish parachute troops under its command at Arnhem.

Operations

Elements of the division took part in the Bruneval Raid (Operation Biting) in February 1942 and the disastrous Operation Freshman glider raid in Norway. The division fought in North Africa, Sicily (Operation Fustian, the capture of the Primosole Bridge) and Italy, where it landed at Taranto in September 1943.

Arnhem

On 17 September 1944 the division, now commanded by Major-General Roy Urquhart, dropped around the Dutch town of Arnhem as part of Operation Market Garden, tasked with seizing the bridge over the Lower Rhine. Only one battalion, under Lieutenant-Colonel John Frost, reached the bridge and held its northern end for four days. Cut off and outnumbered by SS panzer divisions, the division was virtually destroyed; of some 10,000 men who landed, roughly 2,000 were evacuated across the river on the night of 25/26 September. The division never fought again and was disbanded in 1945.

Legacy

The stand at Arnhem made the division legendary. Its veterans formed the core of the post-war Parachute Regiment, whose maroon beret and Pegasus insignia derive from the wartime airborne forces.
