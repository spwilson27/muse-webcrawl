Wall Street

[Image: the Wall Street street sign mounted on a post at the corner of Broad Street, New York]

Wall Street is a street in the Financial District of Lower Manhattan, New York City, running from Broadway to the East River, and by metonymy the name for the United States financial industry as a whole — its banks, exchanges, funds and regulators' counterparties. The name derives from a defensive wall built by Dutch settlers in 1653 along the northern edge of New Amsterdam.

History and institutions

Securities trading under a buttonwood tree on the street in 1792 founded what became the New York Stock Exchange, still housed at 11 Wall Street. The street and surrounding district host the headquarters or offices of major investment banks, the Federal Reserve Bank of New York a few blocks away, and the Charging Bull statue at Bowling Green. Wall Street stands in political rhetoric both for financial expertise and for speculation and excess — in the 1929 crash that began the Great Depression, the 2008 financial crisis and the Occupy Wall Street protest movement of 2011. Much of the industry's trading is now electronic and located elsewhere (in New Jersey data centres, or on the Nasdaq), but the name endures for the US stock market and high finance generally.
