tf–idf

tf–idf (term frequency–inverse document frequency) is a numerical statistic intended to reflect how important a word is to a document in a collection or corpus. It is widely used in information retrieval and text mining.

Definition

The tf–idf value increases proportionally with the number of times a word appears in the document (term frequency) and is offset by the logarithm of the ratio of the number of documents in the corpus to the number of documents containing the word (inverse document frequency). Thus terms common to many documents, like "the", receive low weights, while rarer, document-specific terms score highly.

History and applications

The inverse document frequency idea was introduced by Karen Spärck Jones in 1972, building on Hans Peter Luhn's work. tf–idf underlies classic search engines, document classification, and recommender systems, and remains a baseline for text representation in natural language processing.
