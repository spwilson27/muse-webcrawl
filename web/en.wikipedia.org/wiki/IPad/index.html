iPad

The iPad is a line of tablet computers designed, developed, and marketed by Apple Inc., running Apple's iPadOS (originally iOS) mobile operating system. The first-generation iPad was announced by Steve Jobs on 27 January 2010 and released on 3 April 2010. Its success is widely credited with establishing the modern tablet category.

History

Apple had explored tablet computing as early as the Newton MessagePad (1993) and prototype "K48" devices of the mid-2000s; the iPhone project took precedence, and the tablet was shelved until after the iPhone's launch. The original iPad featured a 9.7-inch multitouch display, the Apple A4 chip, and Wi-Fi or optional 3G connectivity. Successive generations added Retina displays, the iPad mini (2012), the productivity-oriented iPad Pro (2015, with Apple Pencil and keyboard support), and the lower-cost iPad Air line. By the mid-2020s Apple had sold well over 500 million iPads in total.

Software

iPads ship with apps such as Safari, Mail, Photos, and the App Store catalogue of millions of applications. In 2019 Apple split the tablet's operating system into iPadOS, adding features such as improved multitasking, external drive support, and trackpad/mouse support, positioning larger models as laptop alternatives.
