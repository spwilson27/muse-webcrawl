Fort Vaux (French: Fort de Vaux) was a polygonal fort that formed part of the ring of fortifications around the city of Verdun in north-eastern France. Built in the 1880s as part of the Séré de Rivières system, it became famous for its heroic defence during the Battle of Verdun in the First World War.

Battle of Verdun

After the fall of nearby Fort Douaumont in February 1916, Fort Vaux became a key objective of the German offensive. From 2 June 1916 the garrison under Commandant Sylvain-Eugène Raynal resisted assaults and a bombardment of tens of thousands of shells. Fighting raged in the fort's corridors with grenades, flamethrowers and gas, while the defenders' water supply ran out; Raynal's last carrier pigeon, Le Vaillant, famously carried a final plea for relief before dying. Forced by thirst, the garrison surrendered on 7 June 1916 and was treated with military honours by the German Crown Prince's troops.

Recapture and legacy

French forces retook the ruined fort on 2 November 1916. Today Fort Vaux is preserved as a memorial and museum, its cratered surface and shattered turrets testimony to the intensity of the battle; the story of the garrison and its pigeon remains one of the best-known episodes of Verdun.
