Drag (entertainment)

Drag is a performance art form, heavily associated with LGBTQ culture, in which a person dresses in clothing and adopts a persona conventionally associated with a different gender, usually for entertainment. Drag shows feature drag queens (performers, usually men, presenting as female personas) and drag kings (usually women presenting as male personas), involving singing, lip-syncing, comedy, dancing, and exaggerated fashion and makeup. The word may derive from the 19th-century theatre slang for long skirts trailing ("dragging") on the floor, though its exact origin is debated.

History

Female impersonation has deep roots on stage, from the boy actors of Shakespeare's era to 19th-century minstrel and vaudeville performers such as Julian Eltinge. In the 20th century drag flourished in underground gay bars and in Harlem's drag balls — the ballroom culture documented in the film Paris Is Burning (1990) — which developed "houses," competitions, and voguing. Performers such as Divine brought drag into cult cinema, and RuPaul's Drag Race (from 2009) carried it to global mainstream popularity in the 2010s and 2020s.

Elements and terminology

Drag involves wigs, makeup, padding, and costuming, overseen by "drag mothers" who mentor newcomers within drag families. Slang from ballroom culture — "reading," "shade," "werk" — has entered wider popular usage. Drag story hours and brunches have become popular family and fundraising events, while also drawing political controversy and proposed legislation in parts of the United States.
