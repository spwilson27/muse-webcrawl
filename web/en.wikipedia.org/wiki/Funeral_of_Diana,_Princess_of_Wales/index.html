Funeral of Diana, Princess of Wales

The funeral of Diana, Princess of Wales, took place on Saturday, 6 September 1997, at Westminster Abbey in London, one week after her death in a car crash in Paris on 31 August 1997. The ceremonial funeral, modelled on a state funeral but not officially one, was watched by an estimated 2.5 billion people worldwide, making it one of the most-watched television broadcasts in history.

The week of mourning

Diana's death provoked an unprecedented outpouring of public grief in Britain. Hundreds of thousands of mourners laid flowers outside Kensington Palace and Buckingham Palace, and signed books of condolence. Criticism of the Royal Family's initial seclusion at Balmoral led Queen Elizabeth II to return to London and broadcast live to the nation on 5 September, paying tribute to Diana as an "exceptional and gifted human being".

The service

On the morning of the funeral, the coffin was carried on a gun carriage from Kensington Palace to Westminster Abbey, escorted by members of the Welsh Guards. Behind it walked Prince Philip, Prince Charles, Diana's brother Earl Spencer, and her sons Prince William (aged 15) and Prince Harry (aged 12). Around one million people lined the route, and huge crowds watched on screens in Hyde Park.

The service, conducted by the Dean of Westminster, included readings by Diana's sisters and a memorable address by Earl Spencer, who criticised the treatment of his sister by the press and, indirectly, the Royal Family. Elton John performed a rewritten version of "Candle in the Wind" (subtitled "Goodbye England's Rose"), which became the best-selling single of all time. Among the 2,000 guests were world leaders, celebrities, and representatives of the charities Diana had supported.

Burial

After the service, the coffin was driven to Althorp, the Spencer family estate in Northamptonshire, where Diana was buried in a private ceremony on an island in the estate's Oval Lake. Her grave remains closed to general public access, though visitors can view a memorial temple on the shore.
