First Air Flight 6560 was a domestic charter flight in Canada from Yellowknife Airport, Northwest Territories, to Resolute Bay Airport, Nunavut. On August 20, 2011, the Boeing 737-210C operating the flight crashed on a hill east of the runway at Resolute Bay while attempting to land in fog. Of the 15 people on board — four crew and eleven passengers — twelve were killed and three survived.

Accident

The aircraft, a combi-configured 737-200 registered C-GNWN, was carrying passengers and freight to the remote Arctic community of Resolute. The crew flew an instrument landing system (ILS) approach in reduced visibility. During the descent the aircraft drifted off the localiser course; a late go-around was initiated only seconds before the aircraft struck a hill about 1 kilometre (0.6 mi) east of the runway, at about 11:42 a.m. local time. Canadian Forces personnel participating in the nearby Operation Nanook exercise were among the first responders and assisted in rescuing the three survivors.

Investigation

The Transportation Safety Board of Canada (TSB) issued its final report in 2014. It found that the aircraft's compass system had been incorrectly set at the start of the flight, causing a heading error that the crew's instruments displayed ambiguously; during the approach the aircraft deviated from the localiser, the crew did not recognise the severity of the deviation, and crew resource management broke down as the captain dismissed the first officer's concerns and a late warning from the ground proximity warning system came only moments before impact. The TSB also identified latent calibration errors in the Resolute Bay ILS arising from deflection by nearby terrain and structures, and issued recommendations on compass-setting procedures, approach monitoring and CRM training.

Aftermath

The crash was one of the deadliest aviation accidents in the Canadian Arctic. It prompted changes at First Air (later part of Canadian North) in flight-data monitoring and approach procedures, and renewed attention to instrument approach reliability at remote northern airports.
