Camera stabilizer

A camera stabilizer is a device designed to hold a camera in a manner that prevents or compensates for unwanted camera movement, such as shake, while filming.

TypesMechanical gimbal, Steadicam, electronic 3-axis gimbal, in-body / lens stabilisation
Inventor (Steadicam)Garrett Brown, 1975

Types

Steadicam

Invented by Garrett Brown and introduced in films such as Bound, Rocky (1976) and The Shining (1980), the Steadicam combines a sled, articulated spring arm and vest to isolate the camera from the operator's movements, enabling smooth tracking shots.

Electronic gimbals

Modern three-axis motorised gimbals (e.g. DJI Ronin, Zhiyun) use brushless motors, accelerometers and gyroscopes to keep a camera level. Handheld versions serve phones and mirrorless cameras.

Other methods

Tripods, monopods, dollies, camera cranes, image stabilisation in lenses and bodies, and software stabilisation also serve stabilising roles. Body-mounted and vehicle-mounted rigs are used in professional production.

Use

Stabilizers are ubiquitous in film, television, sports broadcasting and online video, balancing smooth motion against the expressive use of handheld shake.
