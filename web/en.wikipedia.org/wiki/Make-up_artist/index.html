Make-up artist

A make-up artist, also known as a makeup artist or MUA, is an artist whose medium is the human body, applying makeup and prosthetics on others for theatre, television, film, fashion, magazines, weddings, and other productions, including all aspects of the modeling industry.

Techniques and fields

Make-up artists work across several specialities: beauty and fashion makeup for photography and runway; editorial and bridal work; theatrical makeup designed to read under stage lighting; and special effects makeup, which uses prosthetics, latex appliances, fake blood, and sculpting to create injuries, creatures, ageing, and other character transformations. Airbrush makeup, applied with a fine mist spray, is common in high-definition film and television work.

Recognition

Awards in the field include the Academy Award for Best Makeup and Hairstyling, established in 1981, as well as Emmy, BAFTA, and guild awards. Notable pioneering artists include Max Factor, who popularized the term "make-up" and developed products for early Hollywood, and Rick Baker and Dick Smith, celebrated for their creature and character effects work.
