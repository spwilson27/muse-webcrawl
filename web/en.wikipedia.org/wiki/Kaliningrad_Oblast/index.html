Kaliningrad Oblast

Kaliningrad Oblast (Russian: Калининградская область) is a federal subject (oblast) of the Russian Federation, situated on the Baltic Sea coast. It is an exclave, separated from the rest of Russia by Lithuania and Poland. Its administrative centre and largest city is Kaliningrad, formerly known as Königsberg. The oblast covers about 15,100 km² and has a population of roughly one million.

History

The territory corresponds to the northern part of the former German province of East Prussia. Following the Second World War, the Potsdam Agreement transferred it to the Soviet Union, and the German population was expelled and replaced by settlers from across the USSR. The oblast was established in 1946 and named after Soviet leader Mikhail Kalinin. Until 1991 it was connected to the rest of the Soviet Union through the Lithuanian SSR; after the dissolution of the USSR it became an exclave of Russia.

Geography and economy

The oblast includes the Curonian Spit and Vistula Spit lagoons' shores, sandy Baltic beaches and resorts such as Svetlogorsk and Zelenogradsk. Its ice-free ports of Kaliningrad and Baltiysk host the Russian Navy's Baltic Fleet. The region is known for amber: the Kaliningrad Amber Combine near Yantarny works one of the world's largest amber deposits. A special economic zone offers tax privileges to investors.
