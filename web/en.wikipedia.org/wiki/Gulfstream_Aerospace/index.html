Gulfstream Aerospace Corporation is an American aircraft manufacturer and a wholly owned subsidiary of General Dynamics. It designs, develops, manufactures, markets and services business jets, and has produced more than 2,000 aircraft since 1958. Gulfstream's principal facility is in Savannah, Georgia, where its large-cabin jets are built and completed.

History

The company grew out of Grumman Aircraft's development of the turboprop Grumman Gulfstream I, first flown in 1958, one of the first purpose-built business aircraft. The jet-powered Gulfstream II followed in 1966, establishing the formula of a stand-up cabin, transcontinental range and the oval windows that became a brand signature. The business passed through American Jet Industries and Gulfstream American before being acquired by Chrysler in 1985 and sold on to an investor group; General Dynamics bought Gulfstream in 1999 for US$5.3 billion.

Under General Dynamics, Gulfstream launched a succession of ultra-long-range flagships: the G550 (2003), the G650 (2012), whose ER variant set city-pair speed records and flew around 13,000 km nonstop, and the G700, announced in 2019 and certified in 2024, with the still larger G800 following. The company also acquired Israel Aircraft Industries' business-jet line in 2001, origin of its super-midsize G280.

Products

The current range comprises the G280, G400, G500, G600, G650ER, G700 and G800, spanning super-midsize to ultra-large-cabin categories. Gulfstream aircraft serve corporate, head-of-state and military users; US military designations include the C-20 and C-37. The company is also known for its global service network and for the supersonic business-jet research it has conducted, including sonic-boom shaping studies with NASA.
