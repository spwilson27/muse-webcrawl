Shrinkage estimator

A shrinkage estimator is an estimator that, either explicitly or implicitly, incorporates the effects of shrinkage: estimates are pulled toward a central value such as the overall mean. Although the procedure introduces bias, the resulting estimators can have lower mean squared error than standard unbiased estimators, particularly when many parameters are estimated simultaneously.

Background

The modern theory of shrinkage began with Charles Stein's discovery in 1956 that the usual estimator of the mean of a multivariate normal distribution is inadmissible when the dimension is three or more. The James–Stein estimator shrinks individual sample means toward a common value and dominates the maximum likelihood estimator under squared-error loss.

Methods

Shrinkage ideas underlie ridge regression, the LASSO, empirical Bayes methods, and the Ledoit–Wolf covariance estimator. Related ideas appear in actuarial credibility theory and in machine learning as regularization.
