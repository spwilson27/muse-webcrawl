Kahan Commission

The Kahan Commission (officially the Commission of Inquiry into the Events at the Refugee Camps in Beirut) was an Israeli commission of inquiry established on 28 September 1982 to investigate the Sabra and Shatila massacre, in which Christian Phalangist militiamen killed hundreds of Palestinians and Lebanese Shias in refugee camps in Beirut during the 1982 Lebanon War.

Findings

Chaired by Supreme Court President Yitzhak Kahan, the commission published its report in February 1983. It found that the Phalangists bore direct responsibility for the massacre, but that Israeli leaders bore indirect responsibility: Defence Minister Ariel Sharon was found personally responsible for ignoring the danger of bloodshed and revenge when he allowed the Phalangists to enter the camps, and the commission recommended his removal as Defence Minister.

Aftermath

Sharon initially refused to resign but stepped down as Defence Minister after mass protests, remaining in the cabinet as a minister without portfolio. The commission is frequently cited as a landmark in Israeli public accountability and the law of command responsibility.
