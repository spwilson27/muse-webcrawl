Plato (crater)

Coordinates: 51.6° N, 9.4° W

Diameter: about 101 km

Depth: about 1 km

Plato is a lava-filled lunar impact crater on the Moon's northern near side, at the western end of the Montes Alpes, on the shore of the Mare Imbrium. It is named after the ancient Greek philosopher Plato. Its dark, smooth floor of solidified basalt makes it one of the most recognizable features on the Moon, sometimes nicknamed the "Greater Black Lake".

Description

Plato is about 101 km in diameter, with a rim rising more than 2 km above the surrounding terrain but only about 1 km above its flooded floor. The floor is unusually free of large craters, containing only a few small craterlets, and it darkens as the lunar day progresses. At its eastern edge a large triangular block of the rim has slipped inward.

Transient phenomena

Plato is one of the craters most often associated with reports of lunar transient phenomena — temporary mists, glows, or obscurations claimed by observers since the 19th century — though such reports remain scientifically unconfirmed.
