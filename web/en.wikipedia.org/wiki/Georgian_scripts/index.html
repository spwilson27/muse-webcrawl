Georgian scripts

The Georgian scripts are the three writing systems used to write the Georgian language and other Kartvelian languages: Asomtavruli, Nuskhuri, and Mkhedruli. Unusually, all three survive in use — Mkhedruli as the everyday alphabet, and the two historical scripts in ecclesiastical, epigraphic, and decorative contexts. In 2016 UNESCO inscribed the "living culture of three writing systems of the Georgian alphabet" on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

History

Georgian tradition attributes the invention of the alphabet to King Pharnavaz I of Iberia in the 3rd century BC, while Armenian sources credit Mesrop Mashtots in the 5th century AD. The earliest surviving inscriptions, in Asomtavruli — a majuscule, monumental script — date from the 5th century AD, such as the inscriptions at Bolnisi Sioni (AD 494) and in the Judaean desert. Nuskhuri, a minuscule book hand, developed from Asomtavruli by the 9th century; together the two church scripts are known as Khutsuri ("priestly"). Mkhedruli, the "secular/cavalry" script, emerged by the 10th–11th centuries and gradually became the standard script for secular writing.

The scripts today

Modern Mkhedruli has 33 letters and is written without case distinctions, though a capital form, Mtavruli, has been used for emphasis since the 19th century and was encoded in Unicode in 2018. The alphabet is phonemic, with one letter per phoneme, and is distinctive in appearance, with rounded, flowing letterforms and no ascender/descender system of the Latin type. The Georgian Orthodox Church continues to use Asomtavruli and Nuskhuri in icons, manuscripts, and inscriptions.
