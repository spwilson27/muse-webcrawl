Kapap

Kapap (Hebrew: קפא"פ, קפ"פ; an acronym for Krav Panim el Panim, "face-to-face combat") is an Israeli military hand-to-hand combat system. It originated in the 1930s and 1940s within the Jewish paramilitary forces of Mandatory Palestine and the early Israel Defense Forces, and is regarded as a precursor and close relative of Krav Maga.

History

Kapap training was developed for units such as the Palmach and Haganah, drawing on boxing, wrestling, judo, stick and knife fighting, and British military combatives taught by instructors such as Gershon Kopler and Yehuda Marcus. After the founding of the IDF, elements of the system were absorbed into official close-combat training, while Krav Maga became the standardised system. Kapap continued to be taught in specialised units and was later revived and systematised for military, law-enforcement and civilian audiences, notably by instructors in Israel and internationally from the 1990s onward.

Training

Modern Kapap emphasises principles over fixed techniques: relative positioning, economy of motion, and pushing/pulling dynamics, applied to unarmed defence, weapons disarms, ground fighting and multiple-attacker scenarios. Training is scenario-based and stress-tested, and assessment focuses on performing under fatigue and pressure.
