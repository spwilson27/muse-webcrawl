The Audi Q7 is a full-size luxury crossover SUV made by the German manufacturer Audi, unveiled in September 2005 at the Frankfurt Motor Show. Production of this seven-seater SUV began in autumn of 2005 at the Volkswagen Bratislava Plant in Bratislava, Slovakia.

It was the first SUV from Audi and went on sale in 2006. Later, Audi's second SUV, the Q5, was unveiled as a 2009 model. Audi has since unveiled a third SUV model, the Q3, which went on sale in the third quarter of 2011, and a fourth model, the Q2, which went on sale in November 2016. The Q7 shares a Volkswagen Group MLB platform and chassis with the Bentley Bentayga, Lamborghini Urus, Porsche Cayenne and the Volkswagen Touareg.

First generation (Typ 4L; 2005–2015)

The first-generation Q7 was previewed by the Pikes Peak quattro concept of 2003. It offered up to seven seats, petrol and diesel V6 and V8 engines, and briefly the 6.0-litre V12 TDI — the most powerful diesel engine fitted to a production car at the time. A facelift followed in 2009. The Q7 was one of the vehicles implicated in the Volkswagen emissions scandal because of its 3.0 TDI engines.

Second generation (Typ 4M; 2015–present)

The second generation, unveiled at the 2015 North American International Auto Show in Detroit, is up to 325 kg lighter thanks to extensive use of aluminium. Engines include turbocharged petrol V6s and TDI diesels, the performance SQ7 with a 4.0-litre V8 (diesel, later petrol), and plug-in hybrid e-tron versions. A facelift arrived in 2020 and a second refresh for 2024.
