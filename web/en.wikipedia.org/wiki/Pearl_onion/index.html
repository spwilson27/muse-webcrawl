Pearl onion

Pearl onion
SpeciesAllium cepa (and closely related Allium varieties)
SizeTypically under 2.5 cm (1 in) in diameter
UsesPickling; creamed onions; stews; cocktail garnish (Gibson)

The pearl onion (also known as a button onion or baby onion) is a small, round onion, traditionally a variety of the common onion (Allium cepa) harvested young or grown in dense plantings and poor soil so that the bulbs remain small — usually less than about 2.5 cm across. They are typically white, though yellow and red types exist.

Cultivation

Pearl onions are produced from short-day or specially bred varieties sown densely so the bulbs crowd one another and stay small; harvesting occurs when the bulbs reach marble size. Their small size makes peeling laborious, so commercial pearl onions are often sold frozen and already peeled, or pickled in jars.

Culinary use

Because of their size, pearl onions are cooked whole. They are classic in creamed onions (a traditional accompaniment to roast turkey at Thanksgiving in the United States), in stews such as coq au vin and boeuf bourguignon, and in British pickled onions served with cheese. Pickled cocktail onions are the defining garnish of the Gibson cocktail. Their flavour is milder and sweeter than that of mature onions.

Related onions

Pearl onions should not be confused with the smaller silverskin onions used chiefly for pickling, or with cipollini, the flat Italian Borettana onions sometimes marketed similarly.
