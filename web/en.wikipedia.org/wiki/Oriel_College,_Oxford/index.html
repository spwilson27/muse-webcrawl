Oriel College is a constituent college of the University of Oxford in England. Located in Oriel Square, it was founded in 1324 by Adam de Brome in the name of the King, Edward II, and was the last Oxford college to be founded by a monarch — it maintains the title of "the King's College", and the reigning monarch is its official visitor. Its full name is the House of the Blessed Mary the Virgin in Oxford.

History

The college takes its name from a property, "La Oriole", that stood on the site. Oriel was prominent in 19th-century Oxford: its fellows included John Henry Newman, Edward Bouverie Pusey, and John Keble, central figures of the Oxford Movement, and the college was known for open, merit-based fellowships and the "Noetics". Cecil Rhodes, who studied at Oriel, left the college money in his will and his name attaches to Rhodes House; a statue of Rhodes on the High Street building became the focus of the "Rhodes Must Fall" protests from 2015, and the college's governing body signalled in 2021 its wish to remove it, subject to planning processes. Notable Oriel features include the 17th-century First Quad and Library, and Tackley's Inn, one of Oxford's oldest surviving academic halls.

People

Notable alumni and fellows include the theologian Richard Hooker-era scholars, the poet Matthew Arnold, explorer and founder figures such as Cecil Rhodes, and Nobel laureates including economist James Meade. The college admitted women for the first time in 1985, the last Oxford college to do so.
