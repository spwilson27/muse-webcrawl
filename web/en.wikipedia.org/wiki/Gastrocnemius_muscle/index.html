Gastrocnemius muscle

Gastrocnemius
OriginProximal to articular surfaces of lateral and medial condyles of femur
InsertionMiddle part of posterior surface of calcaneus (via Achilles tendon)
ArterySural arteries
NerveTibial nerve (S1, S2)
ActionsPlantar flexion of foot; flexion of knee; supination of foot

The gastrocnemius muscle (plural gastrocnemii) is a superficial two-headed muscle that is in the back part of the lower leg of humans. It runs from its two heads just above the knee to the heel, a two-joint muscle; together with the underlying soleus it forms the bulk of the calf, and the two muscles (with the small plantaris) are sometimes grouped as the triceps surae. Its name derives from the Greek gaster (belly) and knēmē (leg) — the "belly of the leg".

Structure

The medial head arises from behind the medial condyle of the femur and the lateral head from the lateral condyle. The two heads unite, contributing to the lower boundaries of the popliteal fossa, and converge into a broad aponeurosis that joins the tendon of soleus to form the calcaneal (Achilles) tendon, inserted on the posterior surface of the calcaneus. Because it crosses both the knee and ankle joints, the gastrocnemius is most effective as a plantar flexor when the knee is extended. A small sesamoid bone, the fabella, is sometimes present in the lateral head.

Function

The gastrocnemius is a powerful plantar flexor, essential for walking, running and jumping: it provides the main propulsive force in gait. It also assists in flexing the knee and in supinating the foot. Its fast-twitch fibres make it important for explosive movements, while the soleus contributes more to posture.

Clinical significance

A tear of the medial head of gastrocnemius — "tennis leg" — is a common sports injury, typically occurring during sudden push-off with the knee extended. Gastrocnemius is also prone to cramps and to shortening in habitual high-heel wearers. The muscle's sural nerve supply makes it relevant in compartment syndrome assessments, and its heads form flaps used in reconstructive surgery around the knee.
