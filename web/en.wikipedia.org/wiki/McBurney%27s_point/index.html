McBurney's point

McBurney's point is a point on the surface of the right lower abdomen that corresponds to the most common location of the base of the appendix. It lies one-third of the distance from the anterior superior iliac spine to the umbilicus, along the line connecting those two landmarks.

Clinical significance

Tenderness at McBurney's point is a classic sign of acute appendicitis, reflecting localised parietal peritoneal inflammation overlying the inflamed appendix. Deep tenderness there is sometimes called McBurney's sign.

History

The point is named after the American surgeon Charles McBurney, who described it in 1889 in a paper on early operative intervention in appendicitis. McBurney also described the muscle-splitting gridiron incision used in open appendectomy.
