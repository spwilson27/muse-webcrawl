Achromatopsia

Achromatopsia, also known as rod monochromacy, is a rare inherited disorder of the retina in which cone photoreceptors do not function, causing complete colour blindness, reduced visual acuity, photophobia and nystagmus.


Symptoms: total colour blindness, light sensitivity, nystagmus, low acuity
Cause: mutations in cone phototransduction genes (CNGA3, CNGB3, GNAT2, PDE6C, etc.)
Frequency: ~1 in 30,000–50,000

Signs and genetics

Affected infants show involuntary eye movements and aversion to bright light; vision relies on rods, so it is poor in daylight and somewhat better in dim conditions. The condition is autosomal recessive. CNGB3 mutations account for most cases in people of European ancestry and nearly all cases on Pingelap atoll in Micronesia, where prevalence is unusually high (~5%) — the population made famous by Oliver Sacks's The Island of the Colorblind.

Management

There is no cure; dark or red-tinted lenses, low-vision aids and tinted contacts help with glare. Gene-therapy trials targeting CNGA3 and CNGB3 are under investigation.
