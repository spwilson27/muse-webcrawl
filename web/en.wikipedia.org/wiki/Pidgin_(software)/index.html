Pidgin (formerly named Gaim) is a free and open-source multi-protocol instant messaging client. It allows users to log in to accounts on multiple messaging networks simultaneously within a single application.

History

The program began in 1998 as a Linux clone of AOL Instant Messenger written by Mark Spencer. Renamed Gaim as it added protocols, it was renamed Pidgin in 2007 as part of a legal settlement with AOL. Its core library, libpurple, also powers other clients.

Features

Pidgin natively supported protocols such as XMPP, IRC, and the now-defunct AIM, ICQ, and Yahoo! Messenger networks, with many more available through plugins. It supports tabbed conversations, file transfer, and — through plugins — end-to-end encryption such as Off-the-Record Messaging.
