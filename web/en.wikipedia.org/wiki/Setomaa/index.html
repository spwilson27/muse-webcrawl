Setomaa (Seto: Setomaa; Russian: Сетумаа) is a historical region and cultural area in the south-east of Estonia and the north-west of Russia, the traditional homeland of the Seto people, an indigenous Finnic ethnic group related to the Estonians but distinguished by their Eastern Orthodox faith and distinctive language and culture.

History

The Setos lived for centuries at the borderland between the Latin West and the Orthodox East, under the influence of the nearby Pskov and the Pechory Monastery (Pskovo-Pechersky Monastery), founded in the 15th century. Under the 1920 Treaty of Tartu, all of Setomaa was assigned to Estonia. In 1945, during the Soviet occupation, the eastern part of the region — including the town of Pechory (Petseri) — was transferred to the Russian SFSR, dividing the Seto community with a border that, after Estonia restored independence in 1991, became the external frontier of the European Union and NATO.

Culture

Setomaa is renowned for the Seto leelo polyphonic singing tradition, inscribed by UNESCO on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2009. Seto culture also features elaborate silver jewellery and colourful traditional costumes, Orthodox chapels known as tsässons, and festivals including the annual Seto Kingdom Day, at which a representative of the Seto king (ülemsootska) is elected.

On the Estonian side, the region is administered largely by Setomaa Parish in Võru County, formed in 2017, which promotes Seto language and culture. The Seto language, a variety of South Estonian, is spoken by several thousand people.
