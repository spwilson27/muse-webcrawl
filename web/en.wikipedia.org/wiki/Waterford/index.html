Waterford (Irish: Port Láirge) is a city in County Waterford in the south-east of Ireland. It is located within the province of Munster. The city is built at the confluence of the "Three Sisters" — the Barrow, Nore and Suir rivers — and is Ireland's oldest city, founded by Vikings in 914 AD. Waterford is known internationally for Waterford Crystal, the glassmaking company founded in the city in 1783.

History

A Viking settlement was established at Waterford in 853, and the fortified town founded in 914 by the Norse became one of Ireland's principal ports. Reginald's Tower, part of the surviving medieval defences, is the oldest civic building in Ireland and the first to use mortar. In 1170 the Anglo-Normans under Strongbow (Richard de Clare) captured the city, and the marriage of Strongbow and Aoife MacMurrough took place here after the siege. Waterford received its first charter in 1205 and remained a loyal royal port through the medieval period; it withstood sieges by Perkin Warbeck in 1495, earning the motto Urbs Intacta Manet Waterfordia ("Waterford remains the untaken city"). The city was besieged twice during the Cromwellian conquest (1649–50) before capitulating.

Economy and culture

Waterford was historically a major port trading with Newfoundland and continental Europe, and its glass industry, re-founded in 1947 after the original Penrose works had closed in 1851, made "Waterford" synonymous with fine lead crystal. The city is home to the South East Technological University (formerly Waterford Institute of Technology) and hosts festivals including Spraoi, the Winterval Christmas festival, and the Waterford Walls street-art project. The Viking Triangle district contains the Medieval Museum and Bishop's Palace museum.

Geography

The city stands on the south bank of the River Suir, with the suburb of Ferrybank on the north (County Kilkenny) side. Waterford Harbour, formed by the estuary of the Three Sisters, opens to the Celtic Sea some 15 km downstream near Dunmore East and Passage East.
