Non-resident Indian and Overseas Citizen of India

A Non-Resident Indian (NRI) is an Indian citizen who resides outside India, typically for employment, business, or education. An Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) is a foreign national who was previously eligible for, or held, Indian citizenship, or whose ancestors were Indian citizens, and who has registered for a lifelong visa-like status under the Citizenship Act, 1955. Together with Persons of Indian Origin (PIO), these categories define India's legal relationship with its global diaspora, one of the largest in the world.

NRI status

Under Indian tax law, a citizen is treated as non-resident if they spend fewer than a prescribed number of days in India during a financial year. NRIs retain Indian citizenship and passports, may vote (though historically with practical restrictions requiring presence in their constituency), and hold special bank accounts (NRE and NRO) for foreign earnings and Indian income. They may not purchase agricultural land in India, though they may own other immovable property.

OCI status

The OCI scheme, introduced in 2005, grants registered holders a lifelong, multiple-entry visa to India, exemption from police reporting, and parity with NRIs in many economic and financial matters. OCI is not dual citizenship—India does not permit it—and holders cannot vote, hold constitutional posts, or buy agricultural land. In 2015 the separate PIO card scheme was merged into OCI.

Diaspora

The Indian diaspora is estimated at over 30 million people, concentrated in the United Arab Emirates, the United States, Saudi Arabia, and many other countries. Remittances from overseas Indians are the largest of any country, exceeding $100 billion annually in recent years, and the government celebrates the diaspora each January at the Pravasi Bharatiya Divas convention.
