Overview

The Organisation of African Unity (OAU) was an intergovernmental organisation established on 25 May 1963 in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, with 32 signatory governments. Its purposes were to promote the unity and solidarity of African states, coordinate the eradication of colonialism and apartheid (through its Liberation Committee, which backed movements in Angola, Mozambique, Rhodesia, Namibia and South Africa), and defend members' sovereignty and territorial integrity.

Record and succession

The OAU entrenched the inviolability of colonial borders (uti possidetis, Cairo Resolution 1964), which limited interstate wars but froze contested frontiers. Its strict non-interference doctrine drew criticism during atrocities (Amin's Uganda, Bokassa, the Rwandan genocide era). Through the Sirte Declaration (1999) the OAU was transformed into the African Union, launched in 2002 with stronger institutions and a right of intervention in grave circumstances (Article 4(h)). Africa Day, 25 May, marks the OAU's founding.
