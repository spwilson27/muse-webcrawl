Dungan Revolt (1862–1877)

The Dungan Revolt was a major uprising of Hui Muslims and other Muslim ethnic groups in north-western China, principally in Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, and Xinjiang, against the Qing dynasty. Beginning in 1862 amid the wider chaos of the Taiping and Nian rebellions, it lasted until 1877 and caused enormous loss of life through warfare, massacre, famine, and displacement.

Course of the revolt

The revolt began with violence between Hui and Han communities in Shaanxi in 1862 and rapidly spread. In Gansu the Sufi leader Ma Hualong built a powerful base at Jinjipu. In Xinjiang, rebellions from 1864 expelled Qing authority, and the Kashgarian adventurer Yaqub Beg founded the state of Yettishar ("Seven Cities"), which won recognition from the Ottoman Empire, Britain, and Russia.

Suppression and aftermath

The Qing general Zuo Zongtang methodically reconquered the north-west: Shaanxi was pacified by 1869, Gansu by 1873 (Ma Hualong surrendered and was executed in 1871), and Xinjiang was retaken in 1876–1878 after Yaqub Beg's death. The demographic impact was catastrophic — population losses in Shaanxi and Gansu are estimated in the millions, much of the Hui population of Shaanxi was killed or expelled, and many survivors resettled in Gansu or fled to the Russian Empire, where their descendants form the Dungan people of Central Asia. Xinjiang was reorganised as a province of China in 1884.
