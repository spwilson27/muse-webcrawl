House of the Vestals

The House of the Vestals (Latin: Atrium Vestae) was the residence of the Vestal Virgins, the priestesses of the goddess Vesta, in ancient Rome. It stood in the Roman Forum, immediately behind the circular Temple of Vesta, at the foot of the Palatine Hill, and formed part of the complex of the Regia and the temple precinct.

History

The residence was repeatedly rebuilt over the centuries, most substantially after the Great Fire of Rome in AD 64 and again under Septimius Severus in the early 3rd century AD, whose rebuilding produced the surviving complex. The Vestals occupied the house for the thirty years of their service, tending the sacred fire of Vesta in the adjacent temple and guarding sacred objects including, according to tradition, the Palladium of Troy.

Architecture

The imperial-period building was a large complex of some fifty rooms arranged on three floors around a long rectangular courtyard. The courtyard was decorated with pools and lined with statues of the chief Vestals (Vestales Maximae), many of whose inscribed bases survive. The complex included baths, kitchens, reception rooms and shrines, reflecting the Vestals' high status and wealth.

Excavation

The ruins visible today were excavated in the late 19th century. The house and the Temple of Vesta remain among the most evocative remains of the Forum, and the site is included within the Archaeological Park of the Colosseum.
