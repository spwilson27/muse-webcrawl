Chinese water torture cell

The Chinese water torture cell is a famous stage magic escape act created and first performed by Harry Houdini in 1912. Houdini was suspended upside down by his ankles in a locked, glass-and-steel cell filled with water, from which he escaped while concealed behind a curtain. The act is properly called the "Upside Down Water Torture Cell" (U.S.D.), and Houdini guarded its method so closely that its secret was not fully revealed until long after his death.

The performance

Houdini's ankles were clamped in the top frame of the cell, which was then hoisted and lowered head-first into the water-filled tank; the top was locked and the tank enclosed by a steel cage, after which a curtain hid it from view. Assistants stood by with axes, ready to smash the glass in case of emergency, and a "breath counter" allowed Houdini to signal. After some two to three minutes of suspense, Houdini emerged from behind the curtain, free and dripping, while the cell was shown still locked.

The secret

Houdini obtained a patent in 1913 that described the cell only partially, without disclosing the true escape method. The cell's construction exploited the manner in which the top stock rotated once submerged: with air trapped at the top of the inverted tank, Houdini could turn himself upright, take a breath, and release the ankle stocks, exiting through an undetected opening when the curtain concealed him. The original cell, damaged after Houdini's death in 1926, was eventually restored and is held in collections devoted to Houdini memorabilia.

Legacy

The escape remained in Houdini's repertoire for the rest of his career and is regarded as his most technically elaborate signature act, distinct from both the unrelated "Chinese water torture" of folklore and from his milk can escape, which it superficially resembled in presentation.
