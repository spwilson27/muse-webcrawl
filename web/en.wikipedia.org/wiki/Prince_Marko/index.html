Prince Marko

Reign: 1371 – 1395

Predecessor: Vukašin Mrnjavčević

Born: c. 1335

Died: 17 May 1395

House: Mrnjavčević

Marko Mrnjavčević (c. 1335 – 17 May 1395) was the de jure Serbian king from 1371 to 1395, while he was the de facto ruler of a territory in western Macedonia centered on the town of Prilep. He is known as Prince Marko (Serbian: Краљевић Марко, Kraljević Marko) and King Marko in the oral traditions of the South Slavs, in which he became a major character of epic poetry.

Life

Marko's father, King Vukašin, shared power with the last Serbian emperor of the Nemanjić dynasty, Stefan Uroš V. Vukašin was killed at the Battle of Maritsa in 1371, and Marko succeeded him as king and co-ruler; but after Uroš's death later that year the Serbian Empire fragmented, and Marko's authority was recognised by few. He became an Ottoman vassal, compelled to provide troops for the sultan's campaigns, and died fighting for the Ottomans against the Wallachians at the Battle of Rovine in 1395.

Folklore

In epic poetry Marko is transformed into a mighty hero of superhuman strength, defender of the weak and enemy of injustice — a portrayal that contrasts sharply with the historical vassal. His inseparable companion is his talking, wine-drinking horse Šarac. The Marko cycle is shared across Serbian, Bulgarian, and Macedonian traditions, where he is celebrated as a national hero. His fortress above Prilep, known as Markovi Kuli ("Marko's Towers"), still dominates the town.
