Amory Lovins

Amory Bloch Lovins (born 13 November 1947) is an American physicist and energy-policy analyst, regarded for four decades as one of the world's leading advocates of energy efficiency and renewable energy. In a celebrated 1976 Foreign Affairs article and the book Soft Energy Paths (1977), Lovins contrasted a "hard path" of ever-larger centralised power stations with a "soft path" combining aggressive efficiency with decentralised renewables — a forecast of demand-side savings that proved far closer to actual US energy trends than official projections of his era.

Career

Lovins studied physics at Harvard and Oxford, leaving formal academia without completing a doctorate to work on energy issues, initially as a representative of Friends of the Earth in London. In 1982 he and his then-wife Hunter Lovins founded the Rocky Mountain Institute (RMI) in Snowmass, Colorado, a think tank and consultancy promoting resource efficiency. His family home there, built as an efficiency showcase, famously grows banana crops indoors in the Colorado mountains while using a small fraction of normal energy.

Ideas

Lovins coined the term negawatt for a unit of saved energy, arguing that efficiency is the cheapest and largest "source" of energy services. His concept of integrative design — optimising whole systems rather than components — underlies the Hypercar project for ultralight, ultra-efficient vehicles, and his books Natural Capitalism (1999, with Paul Hawken and Hunter Lovins), Winning the Oil Endgame (2004) and Reinventing Fire (2011), the last mapping a path for the US economy to run largely without fossil fuels by 2050. He has advised governments, utilities and major corporations in dozens of countries and has received honours including the Right Livelihood Award and a MacArthur Fellowship.

Criticism

Critics have challenged some of Lovins's technology forecasts and cost assumptions, particularly regarding nuclear power economics and the speed of renewable deployment, though his central early claim — that US energy demand growth could be radically curtailed by efficiency — was borne out.
