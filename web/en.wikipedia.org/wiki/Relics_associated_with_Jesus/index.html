Relics associated with Jesus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A number of relics associated with Jesus have been claimed and venerated throughout Christian history, particularly during the Middle Ages. They include objects said to have touched or been used by Jesus — most often items connected with his Passion — and the Roman Catholic Church classifies such "contact relics" and instruments of the Passion among its most revered treasures, while stopping short of requiring belief in the authenticity of any particular object.

Notable relics

The most studied is the Shroud of Turin, a linen cloth bearing the faint image of a man, preserved in Turin Cathedral; radiocarbon tests in 1988 dated its flax to the medieval period, though the result remains disputed. Other prominent claims include the Veil of Veronica and the Image of Edessa (Mandylion), the Sudarium of Oviedo, fragments of the True Cross distributed across Europe, the Crown of Thorns kept at Notre-Dame de Paris, the Holy Lance said to have pierced Christ's side (versions are held in Vienna, Rome and Armenia), the Holy Nails, the Seamless Robe of Trier, and the Holy Chalice identified with the cup of the Last Supper, a candidate for which is preserved in Valencia Cathedral.

Assessment

Many relics surfaced in the medieval period amid a flourishing trade in sacred objects, and the existence of multiple rival examples of the same item — several heads of John the Baptist, for instance — was already mocked by contemporaries and by Reformation critics such as John Calvin. Modern scholarship treats most as products of medieval piety, while the objects continue to draw pilgrims and devotion.
