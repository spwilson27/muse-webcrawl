Personal selling

Personal selling is a form of person-to-person communication in which a seller attempts to persuade prospective buyers to purchase a product or service. It is one of the elements of the promotional mix in marketing, alongside advertising, sales promotion, and public relations. Unlike advertising, personal selling involves direct, two-way interaction, allowing the salesperson to tailor the message to the individual customer, answer questions, and handle objections in real time.

The selling process

Textbooks commonly describe personal selling as a sequence of stages: prospecting for potential customers; pre-approach research; the approach; a presentation or demonstration; handling objections; closing the sale; and follow-up to ensure satisfaction and encourage repeat business. Modern practice emphasises consultative and relationship selling — diagnosing customer needs and building long-term relationships — over high-pressure techniques.

Roles and settings

Personal selling is especially important for complex, expensive, or customised products, such as industrial equipment, enterprise software, and financial services. It takes place in field (outside) sales, retail (inside) sales, and increasingly through video calls and other digital channels. Salespeople are typically compensated through salary, commission, or a combination of both.
