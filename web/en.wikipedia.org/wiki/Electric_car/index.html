An electric car or battery electric vehicle (BEV) is an automobile propelled by one or more electric motors using energy stored in rechargeable batteries. Electric cars were popular in the late 19th and early 20th centuries before gasoline cars prevailed, then revived from the 2000s with lithium-ion battery advances.

History

Early electric vehicles appeared in the 1830s–1880s. By 1900 they held a significant market share, but cheap mass-produced internal combustion cars displaced them. Modern revival began with the GM EV1 (1996), the Tesla Roadster (2008), Nissan Leaf (2010), and rapid expansion led by China, Europe, and the United States.

Technology

BEVs offer instant torque, regenerative braking, high drivetrain efficiency, and lower running costs, but face challenges of range, charging time, battery cost and degradation, and charging infrastructure. Fast-charging networks and falling battery prices have narrowed gaps.

Environmental impact

Electric cars produce no tailpipe emissions; lifecycle emissions depend on electricity mix and battery manufacturing, generally falling below comparable gasoline cars in most grids and improving as grids decarbonize.

Market

China is the largest market and manufacturer, with BYD and Tesla leading global sales. Governments have used subsidies, mandates, and announced phase-outs of new combustion car sales.
