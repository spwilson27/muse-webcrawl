ebXML

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

ebXML (Electronic Business using eXtensible Markup Language) is a family of XML-based standards sponsored by OASIS and UN/CEFACT whose mission is to provide an open, XML-based infrastructure that enables the global use of electronic business information in an interoperable, secure, and consistent manner by all trading partners.

ebXML arose as an alternative to traditional Electronic Data Interchange (EDI), aiming to make electronic business messaging usable by organizations of any size. The initiative was launched in 1999 and the first specifications were approved in 2001.

Components

The ebXML architecture consists of several cooperating specifications:

  • ebXML Messaging Service (ebMS) — a secure and reliable way to exchange business documents, built on SOAP with attachments.
  • Registry and Repository (ebRIM/ebRS) — a registry for storing and discovering business artifacts such as schemas, process definitions and partner profiles.
  • Collaboration Protocol Profile and Agreement (CPP/CPA) — machine-readable descriptions of a party's messaging capabilities and the agreement between two parties.
  • Business Process Specification Schema (BPSS) — a declarative language for defining business collaborations.
  • Core Components (CCTS) — a methodology for building reusable, context-aware data components.

Adoption

ebXML messaging (ebMS version 3.0, also profiled as AS4) has been adopted in government and industry data-exchange networks, particularly in Europe, where AS4 underpins several public-sector e-delivery infrastructures. The core components work influenced later UN/CEFACT and ISO data-modelling standards.
