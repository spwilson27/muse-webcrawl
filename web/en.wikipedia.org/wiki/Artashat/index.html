Artashat

The Ararat plain near Artashat, with Mount Ararat in the distance

Artashat (Armenian: Արտաշատ) is a town in Armenia, the administrative centre of Ararat Province, located on the Araks River in the Ararat plain about 30 km southeast of Yerevan. Near it lie the ruins of the ancient Armenian capital of the same name.

Ancient Artaxata

The ancient city, known in Greek sources as Artaxata, was founded by King Artaxias I in the 2nd century BC, reportedly on the advice of Hannibal, who had taken refuge at the Armenian court. It served as the capital of the Kingdom of Armenia for centuries, grew into a major Hellenistic trade centre — dubbed the "Armenian Carthage" — and was famously attacked by the Roman general Corbulo in AD 58.

Modern town

The modern town grew in the Soviet period around a major wine and canning industry based on the orchards and vineyards of the Ararat plain. The monastery of Khor Virap, where Gregory the Illuminator was imprisoned, stands on a hill nearby.
