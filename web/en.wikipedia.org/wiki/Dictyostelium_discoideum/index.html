Dictyostelium discoideum is a species of soil-dwelling amoeba in the phylum Amoebozoa, a cellular slime mould. Often given the nickname Dicty by researchers, it lives mainly on decaying forest litter, feeding on bacteria. It is a major model organism in cell and developmental biology because it can live both as independent single cells and as a coordinated multicellular organism.

Life cycle

When food is plentiful, Dicty amoebae live and divide as solitary cells. When starved, up to a hundred thousand cells stream together, guided by pulses of cyclic AMP that they secrete and relay, forming a migrating slug (pseudoplasmodium) that moves toward light and heat. The slug then culminates into a fruiting body: about a fifth of the cells sacrifice themselves to form a dead cellulose stalk, lifting the rest, which become dormant spores, ready to disperse and germinate when conditions improve.

Research

Dicty's genome, about 34 megabases on six chromosomes, was sequenced in 2005. Scientists use it to study chemotaxis, cell signalling, phagocytosis, self-recognition and the evolution of multicellularity and altruism. "Cheater" strains that avoid becoming stalk raise questions about the stability of cooperation. The organism also serves in pharmacology, including studies of how drugs such as lithium and valproate act.
