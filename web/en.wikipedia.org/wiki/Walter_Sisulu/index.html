Walter Max Ulyate Sisulu (18 May 1912 – 5 May 2003) was a South African anti-apartheid activist and member of the African National Congress (ANC). Mentor to Nelson Mandela and a central figure in the liberation movement, he served 25 years (1964–1989) in prison on Robben Island alongside Mandela.

Early political career

Sisulu was born in Engcobo in the Transkei to a Xhosa mother and a white foreman father. Raised by his mother's family, he moved to Johannesburg as a young man and worked in mines, factories and a dairy while educating himself. He joined the ANC in 1940 and in 1944, together with Mandela, Oliver Tambo and Anton Lembede, co-founded the ANC Youth League, which pushed the organisation toward mass action. As ANC secretary-general from 1949, he was a principal organiser of the 1952 Defiance Campaign and the 1955 Congress of the People.

Imprisonment

Banned and repeatedly arrested through the 1950s, Sisulu went underground after the ANC was outlawed in 1960 and helped found its armed wing, Umkhonto we Sizwe. Arrested at Liliesleaf Farm in Rivonia in July 1963, he was convicted of sabotage in the Rivonia Trial and sentenced to life imprisonment, which he served mostly on Robben Island. He was released on 15 October 1989, months before Mandela.

Later life

After the ANC's unbanning in 1990, Sisulu served as the organisation's deputy president (1991–1994) during the negotiations that ended apartheid and was elected to Parliament in South Africa's first democratic elections in 1994, retiring in 1999. He died in Johannesburg in 2003 and was given an official state funeral. His wife, Albertina Sisulu, was herself a leading activist, known as the "Mother of the Nation".
