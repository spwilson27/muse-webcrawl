Jersey (cycling)

Classification jerseys of the Tour de France: yellow, green, polka dot, and white.

In road bicycle racing, a jersey is a distinctive shirt worn by the leader of a classification during a stage race, allowing spectators and commentators to identify riders' standing at a glance. The best known is the yellow jersey (maillot jaune) of the Tour de France, awarded to the overall leader on cumulative time. Each major race has its own colors and classifications, and the rainbow jersey is awarded to the reigning world champion in each discipline.

Jerseys are presented on the podium after each stage and worn during the following stage. A rider leading several classifications wears the most prestigious and the next-placed riders wear the others. The colors often reflect race sponsors or history: the Tour's yellow matched the yellow newsprint of the organizing newspaper L'Auto (first awarded in 1919, to Eugène Christophe), and the Giro's pink mirrors the pink paper of La Gazzetta dello Sport.

Other notable jerseys

  • Rainbow jersey — worn for a year by the UCI world champion in each road, track, cyclo-cross, and mountain-bike discipline, white with bands of blue, red, black, yellow, and green.
  • National champion's jersey — incorporates the colors of the rider's national flag.
  • Continental champion's jerseys — e.g. the European champion's white jersey with blue bands and gold stars.
  • Combativity and team classifications — some races mark the most aggressive rider with a special number or jersey (at the Tour, a red race number, formerly a red jersey).

The garment itself

Beyond classification markers, "jersey" also denotes the standard cycling top: a close-fitting shirt, traditionally wool and now made of synthetic moisture-wicking fabrics, with a front zipper and three pockets across the lower back for food and spares. Team jerseys carry sponsors' logos and are registered with the UCI. Winners of classification jerseys keep them permanently, and career collections — Eddy Merckx and others amassed dozens of yellow jerseys — are treasured memorabilia.
