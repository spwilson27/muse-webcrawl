Personal locator beacon (PLB)
TypeEmergency distress beacon
Frequency406 MHz (Cospas-Sarsat); 121.5 MHz homing
SystemCospas-Sarsat
UsersHikers, sailors, pilots, outdoor workers

A personal locator beacon (PLB) is a portable emergency beacon designed to be carried by an individual to alert search and rescue services and allow them to locate the person in distress. When activated, a PLB transmits a coded distress signal on 406 MHz to the international Cospas-Sarsat satellite system, which relays the alert, with the beacon's unique identifier and (on GPS-equipped models) its position, to rescue coordination centres.

Operation

PLBs transmit a digital 406 MHz burst roughly every 50 seconds with a unique hexadecimal ID registered to the owner, whose contact details help rescuers verify emergencies and plan a response. Most modern units include a GNSS receiver that encodes position accurate to about 100 metres; without GPS, satellites can still locate the beacon by Doppler shift to within a few kilometres. A low-power 121.5 MHz homing signal and a strobe light assist rescuers in the final stage of the search.

Comparison with related devices

PLBs differ from maritime EPIRBs (registered to a vessel, float free and activate automatically) and aviation ELTs (fixed to aircraft). Satellite messenger devices such as inReach or SPOT offer two-way messaging but depend on commercial networks and subscriptions, whereas a PLB's distress function works through the government-operated Cospas-Sarsat system without a subscription. PLBs are typically rated for at least 24 hours of transmission once activated.

Regulation

PLBs must be type-approved and registered with the relevant national authority (such as NOAA in the United States). Land use was historically restricted in some countries but has been progressively liberalised.
