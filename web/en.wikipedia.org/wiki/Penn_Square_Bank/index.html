Penn Square Bank

Founded: 1960, Oklahoma City, Oklahoma
Failed: 5 July 1982
Industry: Banking

Penn Square Bank was a small bank in Oklahoma City, Oklahoma, based in the Penn Square shopping mall, whose collapse on 5 July 1982 became one of the most consequential bank failures in modern American history.

The failure

Under energy banker Bill Patterson, the bank made over $2 billion in high-risk loans to oil and gas drillers during the late-1970s energy boom, selling participations in the loans to larger banks across the country. When energy prices fell and drilling collapsed, the loans soured. Regulators closed the bank over the Independence Day weekend; unusually, the FDIC chose a payoff rather than a purchase-and-assumption, leaving uninsured depositors and creditors with losses.

Consequences

The failure spread losses to participant banks, most severely Continental Illinois, which suffered a run in 1984 and required the largest federal rescue then seen — giving rise to wide use of the phrase "too big to fail." Penn Square became a case study in loan-participation risk, energy lending, and supervisory failure, recounted in books such as Mark Singer's Funny Money.
