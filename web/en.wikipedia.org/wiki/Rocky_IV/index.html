Rocky IV

Rocky IV
Directed by: Sylvester Stallone
Release date: November 27, 1985
Box office: about $300 million

Rocky IV is a 1985 American sports drama film written by, directed by and starring Sylvester Stallone. In the fourth instalment of the Rocky series, Rocky Balboa travels to the Soviet Union to avenge the death of his friend Apollo Creed, who is killed in the ring by the Soviet boxer Ivan Drago (Dolph Lundgren in his breakout role). The film also stars Talia Shire, Burt Young, Carl Weathers and Brigitte Nielsen.

Plot

Soviet champion Ivan Drago arrives in America with his wife Ludmilla and a team boasting of computerised training. Apollo Creed, eager to return to the spotlight, challenges Drago to an exhibition bout in Las Vegas; ignoring Rocky's warnings, Creed is brutally beaten and dies in Rocky's arms in the second round. Blaming himself, Rocky agrees to fight Drago in Moscow on Christmas Day, without sanction or purse. Training in primitive conditions in Siberia while Drago uses high-tech equipment and steroids, Rocky wins over the hostile Soviet crowd and knocks out Drago in the fifteenth round, delivering a speech about change that moves even the Soviet leadership.

Reception and legacy

The most financially successful film in the series, Rocky IV grossed about $300 million worldwide. Critics faulted its jingoism and heavy use of montage and music video sequences — the soundtrack produced hits including Survivor's "Burning Heart", Robert Tepper's "No Easy Way Out" and James Brown's "Living in America" — but the film became an emblematic pop-culture artefact of the late Cold War. Dolph Lundgren's character Drago and his line "If he dies, he dies" became iconic; the character returned in Creed II (2018). A director's cut, Rocky IV: Rocky vs. Drago, was released in 2021, reducing the film's lighter subplots (including Paulie's robot) and adding previously unseen footage.
