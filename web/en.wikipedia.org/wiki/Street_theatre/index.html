Street theatre

Street theatre is a form of theatrical performance and presentation in outdoor public spaces without a specific paying audience. These spaces can be anywhere, including shopping centres, car parks, recreational reserves, college campuses and street corners. Performers range from buskers to organised theatre companies, and the content ranges from slapstick comedy to serious political protest.

Unlike other forms of theatre, street theatre relies on the physical space and the accidental audience that gathers. Actors frequently use their natural voices and bodies without amplification, and portable props and costumes are common because performances must be easy to transport and set up.

History

Performance in public spaces is as old as theatre itself: medieval mystery plays, mummers' plays, the Italian commedia dell'arte and travelling minstrels all performed outdoors for whoever gathered. In the twentieth century, street theatre became a favoured tool of political movements; groups such as the San Francisco Mime Troupe, Bread and Puppet Theater and the Living Theatre used free outdoor performance to reach audiences beyond conventional theatre-goers.

In Britain, companies such as Welfare State International and IOU Theatre developed large-scale outdoor spectacle from the 1970s onwards. In India, street theatre (nukkad natak) became an important medium of social commentary, notably through the work of Safdar Hashmi and the Jana Natya Manch company.

Motivations

Performers choose the street for a variety of reasons:

  • Accessibility – reaching audiences who would not enter a theatre.
  • Economics – no venue hire; performers may pass a hat for donations.
  • Politics – public space makes performance a form of demonstration or intervention.
  • Experiment – the unpredictability of the street encourages improvisation.

Festivals

Major festivals celebrating street theatre and outdoor arts include the Edinburgh Festival Fringe, the Aurillac International Street Theatre Festival in France, the Stockton International Riverside Festival, and the Greenwich+Docklands International Festival in London.
