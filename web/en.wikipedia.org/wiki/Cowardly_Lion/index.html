Cowardly Lion

The Cowardly Lion is a fictional character created by American author L. Frank Baum. He first appears in Baum's children's novel The Wonderful Wizard of Oz (1900) as one of the companions who accompany Dorothy Gale on her journey along the yellow brick road to the Emerald City. Although lions are proverbially the kings of beasts and symbols of courage, this Lion believes himself to be a coward and hopes the Wizard of Oz will give him courage.

Character

Dorothy meets the Lion in the forest, where he leaps out at the party and attacks the Tin Woodman and the Scarecrow; when Dorothy slaps him for threatening Toto, he confesses his cowardice and shame. He joins the journey to the Emerald City, yet throughout the book he repeatedly performs brave deeds — leaping a wide ditch with his friends on his back, fighting the Kalidahs, and carrying the party across the poppy field — showing that he already possesses the courage he thinks he lacks. When the Wizard's fraud is exposed, the Lion receives a drink (a placebo) from the Wizard, which he believes confers courage. In later Oz books he becomes king of the beasts and a counsellor at the court of Princess Ozma, serving with Dorothy's other companions.

Portrayals

The Cowardly Lion is best known from the 1939 MGM film The Wizard of Oz, where he was played by comedian Bert Lahr in a heavy lion costume. Lahr's performance, including the song "If I Only Had the Nerve" and his mock-operatic number "If I Were King of the Forest", is among the most celebrated in the film. The character has since appeared in numerous adaptations and reinterpretations of the Oz stories.
