Lester Dent

Lester Bernard Dent (October 12, 1904 – March 11, 1959) was an American pulp-fiction author best known as the creator and principal writer of Doc Savage, the superhuman adventurer-scientist whose magazine defined the hero pulps of the 1930s and 1940s. Writing under the house name "Kenneth Robeson," Dent produced, at a punishing monthly pace, 159 of the 181 Doc Savage novels published by Street & Smith.

Career

Born in La Plata, Missouri, Dent worked as a telegrapher before selling his first pulp story in 1929. In 1933 publisher Street & Smith launched Doc Savage Magazine, and Dent — guided by editor John L. Nanovic and outlines drawn up with publisher Henry Ralston — invented Clark "Doc" Savage Jr., the bronze-skinned, polymath hero, together with his five aides, the Fabulous Five. Dent's famously fast, formula-driven prose, later codified as his "Master Fiction Plot," became a textbook example for aspiring genre writers.

Later life

Beyond Doc Savage, Dent wrote westerns, mysteries, and adventure novels under his own name and pseudonyms, travelled widely, treasure-hunted in the Caribbean from his schooner Albatross, and prospected for oil. He died of a heart attack in 1959 after a final stay at his Florida home. Doc Savage's enduring popularity — through 1960s Bantam paperback reprints and beyond — has kept Dent's work in print and made him one of the most studied of the pulp authors.
