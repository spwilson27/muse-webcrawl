Business intelligence

Business intelligence (BI) comprises the strategies and technologies used by enterprises for the data analysis and management of business information. BI technologies provide historical, current, and predictive views of business operations, supporting better decision making. Common functions include reporting, online analytical processing (OLAP), analytics, dashboard development, data mining, process mining, complex event processing, business performance management, benchmarking, text mining, predictive analytics, and prescriptive analytics.

History

The term "business intelligence" is often credited to IBM researcher Hans Peter Luhn, who used it in a 1958 article to mean "the ability to apprehend the interrelationships of presented facts in such a way as to guide action towards a desired goal." It was revived in 1989 by analyst Howard Dresner of Gartner, who defined BI as an umbrella term for concepts and methods to improve business decision making using fact-based support systems. The field developed from earlier executive information systems and decision support systems, was transformed by the data warehouse and extract–transform–load (ETL) tooling of the 1990s, and later by self-service visualisation tools such as Tableau, Microsoft Power BI, and Qlik.

Components

  • Data warehousing — central repositories (and later data lakes and lakehouses) integrating data from operational systems.
  • Reporting and querying — scheduled reports and ad hoc query tools for business users.
  • OLAP — multidimensional analysis enabling fast slicing and drilling into aggregated data.
  • Dashboards and scorecards — visual displays of key performance indicators.
  • Data mining and advanced analytics — statistical and machine-learning methods applied to business data.

BI and business analytics

Business intelligence is often contrasted with business analytics: BI is commonly described as descriptive and diagnostic — answering what happened and why — whereas analytics extends to predictive and prescriptive questions. In practice the terms overlap heavily and are sometimes used interchangeably.
