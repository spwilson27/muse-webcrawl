Lars Peter Hansen (born 26 October 1952) is an American economist, the David Rockefeller Distinguished Service Professor at the University of Chicago. He shared the 2013 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Eugene Fama and Robert Shiller "for their empirical analysis of asset prices".

Contributions

Hansen's most influential work is the generalized method of moments (GMM), introduced in his 1982 Econometrica paper "Large Sample Properties of Generalized Method of Moments Estimators". GMM allows researchers to test economic models — especially rational-expectations and asset-pricing models — using moment conditions implied by theory without fully specifying the whole statistical distribution of the data. It became one of the standard tools of modern econometrics and empirical finance.

With Ravi Jagannathan he derived the Hansen–Jagannathan bound, a widely used diagnostic relating the volatility of the stochastic discount factor to asset returns, central to debates over the equity premium puzzle. Later work with Thomas Sargent developed "robustness" methods for decision-making under model uncertainty, and Hansen has written extensively on uncertainty, risk, and long-run macroeconomic consequences.

Career

Hansen studied mathematics and economics at Utah State University and earned his PhD in economics from the University of Minnesota in 1978, advised by Christopher Sims. After positions at Carnegie Mellon, he joined the University of Chicago in 1981. He has directed the Becker Friedman Institute and is a member of the National Academy of Sciences and a fellow of the Econometric Society, which awarded him the Frisch Medal.
