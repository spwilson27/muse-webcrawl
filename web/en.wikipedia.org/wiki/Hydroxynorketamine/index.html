Hydroxynorketamine

Hydroxynorketamine (HNK) is a minor metabolite of the anaesthetic and antidepressant drug ketamine. It is formed by the hydroxylation of norketamine, itself produced by N-demethylation of ketamine, mainly by liver enzymes of the cytochrome P450 family.

Pharmacology

Unlike ketamine, hydroxynorketamine is not an appreciable antagonist of the NMDA receptor and does not produce dissociative or anaesthetic effects at relevant doses. Research published in 2016 suggested that the (2R,6R)-enantiomer of hydroxynorketamine may be responsible for much of ketamine's rapid antidepressant action, apparently acting through activation of AMPA receptors and other downstream pathways rather than NMDA blockade. This finding attracted interest because a metabolite lacking dissociative side effects could in principle be developed as an antidepressant in its own right.

Research

(2R,6R)-Hydroxynorketamine has entered early clinical development for the treatment of major depressive disorder and related conditions. The extent to which it accounts for ketamine's therapeutic effects in humans remains debated, and later studies have produced mixed results regarding its potency and mechanism. Its stereoisomer, (2S,6S)-hydroxynorketamine, has also been studied.
