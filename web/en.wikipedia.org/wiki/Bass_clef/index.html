Bass clef

The bass clef, also called the F clef, is a musical clef used for instruments and voices with low ranges. Its symbol is derived from a stylised letter F: the two dots of the clef straddle the fourth line of the staff, marking it as the F below middle C. The lines of the bass staff, from bottom to top, represent the notes G–B–D–F–A, and the spaces A–C–E–G.

Use

The bass clef is the standard lower clef of the grand staff in piano music, played by the left hand. Orchestral instruments that use it as their primary clef include the cello, double bass (sounding an octave lower), bassoon, trombone, bass tuba, and bass guitar, as well as bass and baritone singers. Some instruments, such as the cello and trombone, switch to tenor or treble clef for high passages to avoid excessive ledger lines.

History

The F clef originated in medieval notation, where a letter F placed at the start of the staff fixed the position of that note; over centuries the letterform evolved into the modern symbol, a curved shape whose two dots surround the F line. Together with the treble (G) clef and the C clefs, it forms the standard modern clef system.
