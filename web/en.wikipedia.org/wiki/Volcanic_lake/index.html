Volcanic lake


Crater Lake in Oregon, a volcanic caldera lake

A volcanic lake is a lake in a crater or caldera that has been formed during a volcanic eruption, or in a depression created by volcanic processes such as a lava flow damming a valley. Crater lakes form when precipitation and groundwater fill a volcanic crater above an impermeable layer of hardened lava or ash.

Characteristics

Volcanic lakes often have distinctive chemistry: some are highly acidic, such as the lake at Kawah Ijen in Indonesia, while others accumulate dissolved carbon dioxide. Sudden release of that gas, a limnic eruption, caused the disasters at Lake Monoun (1984) and Lake Nyos (1986) in Cameroon.

Examples

Well-known volcanic lakes include Crater Lake in the United States, Lake Toba in Indonesia—the largest volcanic lake in the world—Lake Taupo in New Zealand, and the crater lakes of Kelimutu, famous for their changing colours.

Hazards

Continued volcanic activity can heat lake water, alter its chemistry, or destabilise crater rims, so many volcanic lakes are monitored for signs of unrest.
