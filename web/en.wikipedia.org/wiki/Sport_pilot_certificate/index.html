Sport pilot certificate

In the United States, a sport pilot certificate is a pilot certificate issued by the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) that allows the holder to fly light-sport aircraft. Created by the FAA's sport pilot/light-sport aircraft rule of 2004, the certificate is intended to make recreational flying simpler and less expensive to enter than the traditional private pilot certificate.

Requirements

Applicants must be at least 17 years old (16 for gliders and balloons), be able to read, speak, write, and understand English, and hold a valid U.S. driver's licence as evidence of medical fitness in most cases, in place of an aviation medical certificate — provided they have never had a medical certificate denied or revoked. Training requirements are lower than for a private pilot: a minimum of 20 hours of flight time for aeroplanes, including dual instruction and solo flight, together with a written knowledge test and a practical test (checkride) with an examiner.

Privileges and limitations

Sport pilots may fly light-sport aircraft — limited by maximum take-off weight (1,320 lb for landplanes, 1,430 lb for seaplanes), a maximum of two seats, and a maximum speed — in day visual flight rules conditions, below 10,000 feet, carrying at most one passenger, and not for compensation or hire. They may not fly at night, in controlled airspace without additional endorsements and training, or outside the United States without permission from the destination country. A multi-year FAA rulemaking known as MOSAIC has proposed expanding the aircraft and privileges available to sport pilots.
