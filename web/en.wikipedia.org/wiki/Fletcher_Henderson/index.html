James Fletcher Hamilton Henderson (December 18, 1897 – December 29, 1952) was an American pianist, bandleader, arranger and composer, important in the development of big band jazz and swing music. His orchestra was one of the most successful African-American bands of the 1920s and a training ground for leading soloists.

Career

Henderson moved to New York in 1920 intending to study chemistry, but instead found work as a song demonstrator and accompanist, recording with blues singers such as Bessie Smith. His orchestra, formed in 1923 and resident at the Roseland Ballroom, featured Louis Armstrong in 1924–25 and later Coleman Hawkins, Benny Carter and Chu Berry. Arranger Don Redman helped create the band's influential sectional style.

Later work

After his own band's commercial decline in the 1930s, Henderson became a key arranger for Benny Goodman, supplying charts such as "King Porter Stomp" that helped launch the swing era. He died in 1952 following a stroke.
