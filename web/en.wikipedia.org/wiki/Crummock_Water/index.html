Crummock Water is a lake in the Lake District in Cumbria, North West England, situated between Buttermere to the south and Loweswater to the north. The lake is 2.5 miles (4.0 km) long, 0.6 miles (0.97 km) wide, covers an area of 0.97 square miles (2.5 km2), and reaches a depth of 144 feet (44 m). The River Cocker leaves the lake at its northern end and flows to Cockermouth, where it joins the River Derwent.

Crummock is owned by the National Trust and used by United Utilities as a reservoir. Its steep western shore is dominated by the screes of Mellbreak, while the craggy hills of the Buttermere valley — Fleetwith Pike at the head of the valley, and the High Stile ridge — make it among the most dramatically set of the Lakeland waters.

Geography

Crummock Water lies in the Vale of Lorton at the north-west of the Lake District National Park. It is separated from its neighbour Buttermere by a flat alluvial plain at the village of Buttermere — the two lakes were once a single post-glacial lake, divided by the sediment fan of Sour Milk Gill and other streams. Inflows include Rannerdale Beck, which passes through the Rannerdale valley famous for its spring bluebells. Around the shore stand the fells of Grasmoor (the highest hill of the north-western fells), Whiteless Pike, Rannerdale Knotts, and Mellbreak, whose sheer scree slopes plunge to the western shore along a narrow path known as the "Mellbreak traverse."

History and use

The name likely derives from Old English or Brittonic elements meaning "crooked lake," a reference to its narrowing shape. Crummock became a reservoir supplying water to the Workington and Cockermouth areas; its level was raised slightly by a small dam, and in recent decades United Utilities has planned to cease abstraction and remove infrastructure, returning the lake toward natural levels as part of the Thirlmere aqueduct supply strategy. The Rannerdale valley beside the lake is associated in local legend with a Norman ambush defeated by Britons after the Conquest — the story behind the "secret valley" and its bluebells, said to grow where the battle was fought.

Recreation

A quiet road on the eastern shore (B5289) serves the lake, which is popular for wild swimming, kayaking, sailing, and fishing (the lake holds charr, trout, pike, and perch). The lakeshore path offers a moderate circuit walk; the ascent of Mellbreak from Kirkstile and the climb of Rannerdale Knotts are classic half-day walks. There is no motor-boat traffic of consequence, and Crummock has a quieter character than Windermere or Derwentwater.
