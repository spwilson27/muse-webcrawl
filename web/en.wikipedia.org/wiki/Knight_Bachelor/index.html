Knight Bachelor

A Knight Bachelor is a man who has been knighted by the British monarch but not as a member of one of the organised orders of chivalry. It is the oldest and lowest-ranking form of knighthood in the British honours system.

Honour

Knights Bachelor are entitled to the title Sir before their forename. The honour is usually awarded for public service or achievement in fields such as the arts, science, business and the judiciary; many senior judges of the High Court are appointed Knights Bachelor on appointment. Unlike knights of an order, Knights Bachelor have no post-nominal letters, though they may use an insignia introduced in 1926.

History and equivalents

The rank dates to medieval times, when a knight bachelor was a knight who fought under another lord's banner rather than his own. Women receiving the equivalent honour are appointed Dames Commander of the Order of the British Empire (DBE), since there is no female equivalent rank of Knight Bachelor.
