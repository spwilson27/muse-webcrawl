Cromarty

Scottish Gaelic: Cromba

Population: approx. 700

Council area: Highland

Country: Scotland

Location: Tip of the Black Isle, at the mouth of the Cromarty Firth

Cromarty (Scottish Gaelic: Cromba) is a town and former royal burgh in Ross and Cromarty, in the Highland area of Scotland. Situated at the tip of the Black Isle, on the southern shore of the mouth of the Cromarty Firth, it is a remarkably well-preserved example of an 18th–19th-century Scottish fishing and trading port.

History

Cromarty grew around its castle and church in medieval times and was made a royal burgh in the 13th century. Its early-19th-century prosperity owed much to George Ross, who developed the harbour, a brewery, and factories producing hemp rope and canvas for export. The harbour, built of local stone in the 1780s, served trade across the North Sea, while the town also prospered from herring fishing and the export of cured pork.

The town's most famous son is Hugh Miller (1802–1856), the stonemason, geologist and writer whose fossil discoveries in the local Old Red Sandstone and whose bestselling book The Old Red Sandstone made him one of Victorian Britain's best-known men of science. His cottage and a museum are maintained by the National Trust for Scotland.

Geography

The entrance to the Cromarty Firth is guarded by two steep headlands, the Sutors of Cromarty, and the deep, sheltered firth beyond became an important naval anchorage, notably at Invergordon during both world wars. Dolphins, including bottlenose dolphins of the Moray Firth population, are regularly seen from the shore. A small ferry crosses from Cromarty to Nigg in summer, one of the smallest car ferries in Britain.
