Law officers of the Crown

The law officers of the Crown are the chief legal advisers to the Crown and to the governments of the United Kingdom and its constituent parts, as well as to certain Crown Dependencies and overseas territories. They are appointed by the monarch on ministerial advice, are usually members of Parliament, and combine legal duties with political office.

England and Wales

  • Attorney General for England and Wales — chief legal adviser to the Crown and government; oversees the Crown Prosecution Service and the Serious Fraud Office.
  • Solicitor General for England and Wales — the Attorney General's deputy.

Scotland

  • Lord Advocate — chief legal adviser to the Scottish Government and head of the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service.
  • Solicitor General for Scotland — the Lord Advocate's deputy.
  • Advocate General for Scotland — legal adviser to the UK Government on Scots law.

Northern Ireland and Wales

The Attorney General for Northern Ireland advises the Northern Ireland Executive, while the Counsel General for Wales is the chief legal adviser to the Welsh Government. Historically the Attorney General for England and Wales also held the Northern Ireland role until devolution of policing and justice in 2010.

Duties

Law officers advise ministers on the legality of government action, represent the Crown in important litigation, exercise superintendence over prosecutions, and in some cases hold powers such as entering a nolle prosequi or referring unduly lenient sentences to the Court of Appeal.
