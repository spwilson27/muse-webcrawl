Tamil Nadu ("Tamil Country") is a state in southern India, lying in the southernmost part of the Indian peninsula, bordered by Kerala, Karnataka, and Andhra Pradesh, the union territory of Puducherry, and the Bay of Bengal and Indian Ocean coasts. Its capital and largest city is Chennai. With roughly 72 million inhabitants at the 2011 census, it is one of India's most populous and most urbanised states, and one of its largest state economies.

History

The region has been inhabited continuously for over 15,000 years and was home to the classical Tamil kingdoms of the Chera, Chola, and Pandya dynasties, whose Sangam literature (c. 300 BCE–300 CE) is among the oldest in India. The Cholas built an extensive maritime empire in the 10th–12th centuries, leaving monumental temples at Thanjavur, Gangaikonda Cholapuram, and Darasuram, now UNESCO World Heritage Sites. From the 17th century the coast became a theatre of European colonial rivalry; the British East India Company established Madras (now Chennai) and the region became the core of the Madras Presidency. After independence it became Madras State, was reorganised on linguistic lines in 1956, and renamed Tamil Nadu in 1969.

Geography

The state is bounded by the Eastern and Western Ghats, between which lies a fertile plateau drained by rivers including the Kaveri, Vaigai, and Tamiraparani. The Coromandel Coast along the Bay of Bengal receives much of its rain from the northeast monsoon. The southern tip at Kanyakumari is the meeting point of the Bay of Bengal, the Arabian Sea, and the Indian Ocean.

Economy and culture

Tamil Nadu is a leading centre of automobile manufacturing, textiles, leather goods, information technology, and film — the Tamil cinema industry ("Kollywood") is based in Chennai's Kodambakkam district. The state is famed for its Dravidian temple architecture with towering gopurams, the classical dance form Bharatanatyam, Carnatic music, and Pongal, its January harvest festival. Tamil, its official language, is one of the world's longest-surviving classical languages.
