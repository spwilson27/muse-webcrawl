Winkel tripel projection
ClassificationPseudo-azimuthal / compromise
CreatorOswald Winkel
Year1921

The Winkel tripel projection (German: "triple") is a modified azimuthal map projection proposed by Oswald Winkel in 1921. It is constructed as the arithmetic mean of the equirectangular projection and the Aitoff projection, and it aims to minimize the three kinds of distortion — area, direction, and distance — from which it takes its name.

Properties

The projection is neither conformal nor equal-area, but its overall distortion is low, which makes it suitable for general world maps. The central meridian is straight, other meridians are curved, and the poles are shown as lines about half the length of the equator. The standard parallels of the underlying equirectangular projection are usually set at about 50°28′.

Use

The National Geographic Society adopted the Winkel tripel as its standard reference world map projection in 1998, replacing the Robinson projection, and it remained in use by the society until 2022, when it switched to the Equal Earth projection for its atlas maps.
