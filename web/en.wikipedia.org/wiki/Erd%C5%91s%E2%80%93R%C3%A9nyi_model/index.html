Erdős–Rényi model

In the mathematical field of graph theory, the Erdős–Rényi model refers to either of two closely related models for generating random graphs, named after Paul Erdős and Alfréd Rényi, who studied them in papers published from 1959 onwards.

The two models

In the G(n, M) model, a graph is chosen uniformly at random from all graphs on n labelled vertices having exactly M edges. In the G(n, p) model, a graph is constructed by connecting each pair of the n vertices independently with probability p. The two models have similar properties when M and p are matched so that the expected number of edges, p·n(n−1)/2, equals M.

Properties

The model is famous for its sharp threshold phenomena. For example, when p passes the threshold 1/n, a giant connected component emerges containing a positive fraction of all vertices; below this threshold all components are small. When p exceeds (ln n)/n, the graph becomes connected with high probability. Degree distributions in G(n, p) follow a binomial distribution, approximated by a Poisson distribution in sparse regimes.

Applications

The Erdős–Rényi model provides the standard null model in network science against which real-world networks are compared, although real networks typically deviate from it through clustering and heavy-tailed degree distributions.
