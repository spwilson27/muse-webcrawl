Theme system

FieldLinguistics (morphology, typology)
Known forAnalysis of ergative and split case/agreement systems
Proposed byJonathan Bobaljik and collaborators

In linguistics, a theme system is an approach to describing case and verbal morphology, developed in work by Jonathan Bobaljik and others, in which the shape of a verb and its arguments depends on the verb's "theme" — its transitivity class. The notion is used especially in analysing languages with ergative–absolutive alignment and the splits found within them.

Background

Many ergative languages do not mark all transitive subjects alike: morphology can depend on whether the clause is perfective or imperfective, on the person of the arguments, or on lexical properties of the verb. The theme-system proposal argues that a set of small, recurring morphological patterns — in which the same theme vowel or agreement pattern signals a coherent class of predicates — can unify apparently scattered facts about case marking, auxiliary selection and agreement in languages such as Hindi-Urdu, Georgian and various Mayan and Indo-Aryan languages.

Relation to other approaches

Theme systems sit within the broader study of argument structure and case theory, alongside analyses based on unaccusativity, aspect-driven splits and dependent case. The term should not be confused with the "theme" of theme–rheme (topic–comment) structure in information packaging, nor with musical or architectural themes; and it is unrelated to Los Angeles's Theme Building.
