Final Four

The Final Four is the last four teams remaining in a tournament, most famously the NCAA Division I men's and women's basketball tournaments in the United States. In the NCAA context, the term refers both to the four regional champions that advance to the national semifinals and to the semifinal round itself, played on the Saturday before the national championship game on Monday night. The phrase is a registered trademark of the NCAA.

NCAA basketball

The NCAA men's tournament, first held in 1939 with eight teams, expanded over decades to the modern field of 68 teams, from which four regional winners emerge after the rounds known as the Sweet Sixteen and Elite Eight. Since 2003 the men's Final Four has been held exclusively in large domed stadiums, drawing crowds above 70,000. The women's tournament adopted the same structure, and its Final Four is staged in a separate city.

Reaching the Final Four is considered a landmark achievement for a college basketball program, celebrated with the cutting down of the nets. UCLA holds the record for men's appearances and titles under coach John Wooden, while UConn's women's program has been the dominant force in the women's game. Cinderella runs by low seeds — such as George Mason (2006), VCU (2011), and Loyola Chicago (2018) on the men's side — are among the tournament's most famous stories.

Other uses

The term is also used generically for the semifinal stage of other knockout competitions. In European basketball, the EuroLeague concludes with a Final Four weekend at a predetermined venue. College sports including volleyball, lacrosse, ice hockey (the "Frozen Four"), and soccer stage analogous championship weekends, and reality television and game shows have adopted the phrase for their last four contestants.

Cultural significance

The Final Four weekend is one of the largest events in American sport, combining the semifinals, championship game, concerts, and fan festivals in the host city, and generating substantial television audiences and economic impact. "March Madness", the broader tournament, is a fixture of American sports culture.
