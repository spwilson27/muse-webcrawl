Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society

Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society is a scientific journal published by the Royal Society. Founded in 1665 by Henry Oldenburg, it is regarded as the world's first and longest-running journal devoted exclusively to science.

Early issues published work by Isaac Newton, Antonie van Leeuwenhoek, Robert Hooke and many others. In 1887 the journal split into two series: Philosophical Transactions A, covering mathematics, physics and engineering sciences, and Philosophical Transactions B, covering biological sciences — both still published today, often as theme issues.
