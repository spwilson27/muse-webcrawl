Monsanto (Idanha-a-Nova)

Monsanto is a village and civil parish in the municipality of Idanha-a-Nova, in the Castelo Branco District of eastern Portugal, near the Spanish border. It is famous for its houses built among, beneath, and into enormous granite boulders: walls, roofs, and even whole dwellings are formed from the rocks of the hillside, giving the village the appearance of growing out of the stone itself. In 1938 Monsanto was voted the "most Portuguese village in Portugal" in a national contest promoted by the Estado Novo regime.

The village is crowned by the ruins of a medieval castle, associated with the Knights Templar, who received Monsanto from King Afonso Henriques in the 12th century. Among its monuments are the Romanesque chapel of São Pedro de Vir-a-Corça nearby, the parish church, and the Lucano tower (Torre de Lucano) with its silver rooster weather vane, a replica of the trophy awarded in 1938. The village is one of the twelve designated Historical Villages of Portugal (Aldeias Históricas). It later gained international visibility as a filming location for the television series House of the Dragon.
