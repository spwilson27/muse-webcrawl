Ouigo is a low-cost, high-speed train service operated by France's national railway SNCF, launched on 2 April 2013. Modelled on low-cost airlines, it offers no-frills TGV services at fares from €10, using high-density trains, out-of-centre stations, and online-only booking. The brand has since expanded to conventional-speed services in France (Ouigo Train Classique, 2022) and to Spain, where Ouigo España competes on the liberalized high-speed market.

Model

Ouigo cuts costs by running refurbished TGV Duplex and Réseau trainsets in a single class with more seats (no first class, no buffet car), minimizing station time, using peripheral stations such as Marne-la-Vallée–Chessy, Tourcoing, and Nantes rather than city-centre termini, and charging for extras including larger luggage, seat selection, and power outlets. Production costs are estimated around 30–40 percent below classic TGV inOui services, in exchange for high load factors. The trains are painted in an unmistakable pink and light-blue livery.

Development in France

The initial network linked the Paris region with Lyon, Marseille, and Montpellier; service later spread to the Atlantic coast, the north, and the east, carrying tens of millions of passengers annually and materially growing the French high-speed market rather than merely cannibalizing inOui. In April 2022 SNCF added Ouigo Train Classique: refurbished Corail coaches hauled at conventional speeds on routes such as Paris–Nantes and Paris–Lyon at flat low fares from €5.

Spain

Under Spain's rail liberalization, Ouigo España — a subsidiary of SNCF — began Madrid–Barcelona services on 10 May 2021, the first private operator to compete with Renfe's AVE and low-cost Avlo, using TGV Euroduplex double-deck sets. It subsequently extended to Valencia (2022), Alicante, and toward Tarragona and Andalusia as infrastructure and framework capacity allowed. Fares start at €9, and the entrant is credited with sharply lowering prices and raising ridership on Spain's busiest corridors.
