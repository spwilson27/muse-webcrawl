Peer instruction

Peer instruction is an interactive teaching method developed by Harvard physics professor Eric Mazur in the early 1990s to engage students during lectures and expose misconceptions. It replaces part of traditional lecturing with conceptual questions ("ConcepTests") that students answer individually, then discuss with neighbors, then answer again.

Method

A typical cycle: the instructor poses a multiple-choice conceptual question; students commit to an answer using flashcards, clickers, or apps; if responses are split, students persuade one another in small groups; a second vote follows, usually converging toward the correct answer; the instructor then explains the reasoning. Research by Mazur and others showed roughly doubled gains on concept inventories such as the Force Concept Inventory compared with traditional lectures.

Adoption

Peer instruction has spread from physics to many disciplines and is a foundational technique of active learning and the flipped classroom.
