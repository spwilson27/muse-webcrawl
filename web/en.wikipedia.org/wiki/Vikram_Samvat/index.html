Vikram Samvat

Vikram Samvat (IAST: Vikrama Saṃvata; abbreviated VS), also known as the Vikrami calendar, is a Hindu calendar historically used in the Indian subcontinent and still used in several Indian states and as the official calendar of Nepal. It is a lunisolar calendar beginning in 57 BCE, traditionally said to commemorate King Vikramaditya of Ujjain's victory over the Sakas.

Structure

The Vikram Samvat year is roughly 57 years ahead of the Gregorian year: for example, the year 2081 VS began in 2024 CE. Like other Hindu calendars it uses lunar months adjusted to the solar year by an intercalary month (adhika masa) added about every 32–33 months. Two conventions exist for the start of the year: in north India and Nepal the year begins with the month of Chaitra (Chaitradi), while in Gujarat and Maharashtra it begins with Kartika (Kartikadi), the day after Diwali.

Usage

In Nepal, Vikram Samvat (called Bikram Sambat) is the official calendar, used in government, newspapers, and daily life, with the new year beginning in mid-April (1 Baisakh). In India it is used for religious festivals and traditional almanacs (panchangas), alongside the Gregorian and Indian national calendars.

Historically, the era was known in inscriptions simply as Samvat or the Krita/Malava era, and its association with a king named Vikramaditya appears in later literature.
