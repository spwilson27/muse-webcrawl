The Choir of King's College, Cambridge is an English choir of international renown, based in King's College at the University of Cambridge. It is one of the world's most famous collegiate choirs, known above all for its annual Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols, broadcast worldwide from King's College Chapel on Christmas Eve.

History

The choir was established by King Henry VI when he founded King's College in 1441. The statutes provided for a choir of sixteen choristers — boys drawn from poor backgrounds and educated at what became King's College School — together with choral scholars (undergraduates of the college) and lay clerks. In the modern choir the lower parts are sung by fourteen undergraduate choral scholars.

The Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols was introduced in 1918 by the Dean, Eric Milner-White, and first broadcast by the BBC in 1928. The service always opens with a solo chorister singing the first verse of "Once in Royal David's City".

Recordings and tours

The choir has made a large number of recordings, notably for the Decca and EMI labels and more recently on its own King's College record label, and tours internationally. It also sings choral evensong in chapel on most days during term.
