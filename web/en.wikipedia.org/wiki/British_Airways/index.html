British Airways (BA) is the flag carrier airline of the United Kingdom. Headquartered at Waterside near its main hub at London Heathrow Airport, it is the largest UK airline by fleet size and international destinations. British Airways is a founding member of the Oneworld airline alliance and, since 2011, has been owned by the International Airlines Group (IAG), which also owns Iberia, Aer Lingus and Vueling.

History

British Airways was created in 1974 following the merger of the two state-owned carriers, the British Overseas Airways Corporation (BOAC) and British European Airways (BEA), under the British Airways Board. The airline was privatised in 1987 under the government of Margaret Thatcher, in one of the decade's flagship privatisations. In the 1980s and 1990s BA marketed itself as "The World's Favourite Airline" and operated the supersonic Concorde in partnership with Air France until the type's retirement in 2003.

In January 2011 British Airways merged with Spain's Iberia to form International Airlines Group, while both airlines continued to operate under their own brands. BA subsequently absorbed BMI in 2012, acquiring valuable slots at Heathrow.

Destinations and fleet

BA serves more than 200 destinations worldwide. Its long-haul fleet is centred on the Boeing 777, Boeing 787 Dreamliner and Airbus A350-1000, while short-haul services from Heathrow and Gatwick use the Airbus A320 family. The airline was the launch customer-era operator of the Airbus A380, which it flies on flagship routes to North America, Asia and South Africa.

Services

Long-haul cabins are branded World Traveller (economy), World Traveller Plus (premium economy), Club World (business) and First. Short-haul services offer Euro Traveller and Club Europe. BA's frequent-flyer programme is the Executive Club, whose currency is Avios.
