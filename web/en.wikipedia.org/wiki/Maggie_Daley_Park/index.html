Maggie Daley Park is a 20-acre (81,000 m2) public park in the Loop community area of Chicago, Illinois, United States. It is part of the larger Grant Park and is bounded by Columbus Drive to the west, Monroe Street to the north, Lake Shore Drive to the east, and Randolph Street to the south. The park opened in stages beginning in late 2014 and was formally dedicated in 2015. It is named for Margaret C. "Maggie" Daley (1943–2011), the former first lady of Chicago and wife of Mayor Richard M. Daley, who was known for her advocacy of children and the arts.

History

The park occupies the site of the former Daley Bicentennial Plaza, built in the 1970s over parking garages at the northeast corner of Grant Park. In 2012 the plaza was demolished and the site redeveloped as part of a redesign led by landscape architect Michael Van Valkenburgh Associates. The redesign created dramatic topography with hills and valleys above the existing parking structures, and a curving pedestrian connection, the BP Pedestrian Bridge designed by Frank Gehry, to Millennium Park to the west.

Features

The park is known for its large children's Play Garden, which includes themed play spaces such as the Enchanted Forest, the Harbor, the Sea, and a water play area, along with slides and climbing structures. The park's Climbing Park features custom rock-climbing and bouldering walls. In winter, the park's quarter-mile Skating Ribbon provides a winding ice-skating course through pine trees, replacing the traditional oval rink; in warmer months the ribbon is used for rollerblading and scooters. Other amenities include picnic groves, tennis courts, a miniature golf course, and a field house.

Cancer Survivors' Garden

At the eastern edge of the park lies the Cancer Survivors' Garden, a contemplative garden that predates the redesign and was preserved within the new park.
