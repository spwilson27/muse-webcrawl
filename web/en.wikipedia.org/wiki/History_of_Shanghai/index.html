The history of Shanghai traces a Yangtze Delta fishing and textile town that became China's largest city and a global financial centre after its opening as a treaty port in 1842.

Early history

Shanghai became a market town in 1074 and a county seat in 1292. Surrounded by a city wall in 1554 against wokou pirates, it grew on the cotton and tea trades but remained secondary to Suzhou and Hangzhou.

Treaty port era

The Treaty of Nanking (1842) opened Shanghai to British trade; the British, French and American concessions grew into the International Settlement and French Concession. By the 1930s Shanghai was Asia's great cosmopolitan port, famed for the Bund, commerce, nightlife — and crime and inequality.

War and revolution

Japanese forces bombed the city in 1932 and captured it in the 1937 Battle of Shanghai. Communist forces took Shanghai in May 1949; under the People's Republic its industries were socialised and heavily taxed to fund national development.

Reform era

After 1990 the development of Pudong, across the Huangpu, made Shanghai the emblem of China's economic rise, crowned by the 2010 World Expo and the opening of the Shanghai Free-Trade Zone in 2013.
