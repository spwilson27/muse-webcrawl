Vulcan salute

The Vulcan salute is a hand gesture popularised by the 1960s television series Star Trek. It consists of a raised hand with the palm forward and the fingers parted between the middle and ring finger, with the thumb extended. It is typically accompanied by the phrase "Live long and prosper".

Origin

Actor Leonard Nimoy, who played the Vulcan character Spock, devised the gesture for the 1967 episode "Amok Time". Nimoy based it on the Priestly Blessing performed by Jewish kohanim, in which both hands form the shape of the Hebrew letter shin (ש), standing for Shaddai (Almighty). Nimoy had seen the blessing as a child in an Orthodox synagogue and recalled the striking hand position. In the series, the salute is the traditional greeting of Vulcans, answered by "Peace and long life".

Cultural influence

The salute became one of the most recognisable symbols of Star Trek and of science-fiction fandom. It has been used by astronauts aboard the International Space Station, appeared in countless parodies and tributes, and was given an emoji — 🖖 "vulcan salute" — in Unicode 7.0 (2014). After Nimoy's death in 2015, the gesture was widely used in public memorials, and then–Prime Minister of Canada and other public figures marked his passing with it.
