Laki (لەکی Lakī) is a language spoken by the Lak people of western Iran, principally in Lorestan, Ilam, Kermanshah, and Hamadan provinces. It is written in the Perso-Arabic script and has a notable tradition of oral and written poetry.

Classification

The classification of Laki has long been debated. Some scholars treat it as a dialect of Southern Kurdish, others as a dialect of Luri, and still others — including the Encyclopaedia Iranica tradition and Ethnologue, which lists it as a separate language — as a distinct Northwestern Iranian language closely related to Kurdish, sharing many features with both Kurdish and Luri. Laki grammar features a split-ergative system in past-tense transitive clauses and a rich set of clitic pronouns.

Literature

Laki has a respected poetic tradition, including the works of poets such as Mela Perishan (14th century, whose divan mixes Laki, Kurdish, and Persian) and later figures who composed religious verse for the Ahl-e Haqq (Yarsan) community, among whom Laki is widely spoken.
