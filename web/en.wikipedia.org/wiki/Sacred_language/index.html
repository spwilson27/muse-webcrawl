Sacred language

A sacred language, holy language or liturgical language is a language that is cultivated and used primarily for religious reasons by people who may speak another, primary language in their daily lives. Sacred languages often preserve archaic forms long after the spoken language has changed.

Examples

Well-known sacred languages include Sanskrit and Pali in Hinduism and Buddhism; Classical Arabic for the Qur'an; Biblical Hebrew in Judaism; Latin, Church Slavonic, Koine Greek and Ge'ez in various Christian traditions; Avestan in Zoroastrianism; and Classical Chinese in Confucian and Taoist texts. In some traditions the language is believed to be intrinsically holy, so that scripture cannot be fully translated; in others its use is historical and practical.

Characteristics

Sacred languages are typically learned through formal education rather than natively, are written in distinctive scripts, and are reserved for liturgy, scripture and scholarship. Their prestige can strongly influence vernacular literary languages, as Latin did in medieval Europe and Sanskrit did across South and Southeast Asia.
