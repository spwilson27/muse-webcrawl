Eric Eustace Williams (25 September 1911 – 29 March 1981) was a Trinidadian historian and statesman who served as the first Prime Minister of Trinidad and Tobago. He led the country to independence from Britain in 1962 and governed until his death in 1981. A noted scholar, he is best known for his book Capitalism and Slavery (1944), which argued that the profits of the transatlantic slave trade financed the Industrial Revolution and that abolition followed economic rather than purely humanitarian motives.

Early life and scholarship

Williams was educated at Queen's Royal College in Port of Spain and won an island scholarship to St Catherine's College, Oxford, where he took a first in history. He completed his doctorate in 1938; his dissertation became Capitalism and Slavery, published in 1944 while he taught at Howard University in Washington, D.C. The "Williams thesis" reshaped debate about slavery and industrial capitalism and remains widely discussed.

Political career

Returning to Trinidad, Williams founded the People's National Movement (PNM) in 1956 and won the general election that year, becoming Chief Minister, then Premier, and at independence in 1962 the first Prime Minister. His government expanded education, developed the petroleum industry, and pursued regional integration through the Caribbean Free Trade Association. Williams's famous 1961 "Massa Day Done" speech signalled a break with colonial deference. He remained in office through challenges including the 1970 Black Power protests, dying in office in 1981.
