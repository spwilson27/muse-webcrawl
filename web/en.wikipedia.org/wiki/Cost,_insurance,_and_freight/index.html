Cost, insurance, and freight (CIF) is an international shipping agreement (Incoterm) used in the transportation of goods by sea and inland waterways. Under CIF, the seller delivers the goods on board the vessel (or procures goods already so delivered) at the port of shipment, and additionally pays the costs of carriage to the named destination port and the cost of marine insurance against the buyer's risk of loss of or damage to the goods during carriage.

CIF applies only to goods carried by sea or inland waterway.

Summary of obligations

Under CIF, the seller must:

  • Supply the goods and the commercial invoice;
  • Obtain any export licence and complete export formalities;
  • Contract for carriage to the named port of destination;
  • Take out insurance cover for the buyer's benefit (under Incoterms 2020, the default minimum is Institute Cargo Clauses (C), though parties often agree to Clauses (A) cover);
  • Deliver the goods on board the vessel at the port of shipment on the agreed date or within the agreed period.

The buyer bears the risk from the moment the goods are on board the vessel, even though the seller arranges and pays for the carriage and insurance. The buyer is responsible for import clearance and duties, and for costs arising after delivery other than those included in the seller's contract of carriage.

Transfer of risk vs. transfer of cost

CIF is frequently misunderstood: the seller pays freight and insurance to the destination port, but risk of loss or damage passes to the buyer at the port of shipment, once the goods are loaded on board. This division reflects the term's origins in documentary sales, in which the buyer pays against documents (bill of lading, insurance policy and invoice) rather than against physical arrival of the goods.

Comparison with related terms

CIF is the maritime counterpart of CIP (carriage and insurance paid to), which can be used for any mode of transport. It is closely related to CFR (cost and freight), which is identical except that the seller has no obligation to procure insurance. CIF should not be used for containerised cargo delivered to a terminal before loading; the ICC recommends CIP in that case.

Legal treatment

The CIF contract is a classic form of documentary sale in English commercial law: the buyer must pay the price on tender of conforming documents even if the goods have been lost, relying on the insurance policy for recovery. In the United States, CIF terms are also addressed in the Uniform Commercial Code (§2-320), though parties commonly incorporate the ICC Incoterms rules instead.
