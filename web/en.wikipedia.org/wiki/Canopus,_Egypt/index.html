Canopus, Egypt

Canopus (Greek: Κάνωβος, Kanobos) was an ancient Egyptian coastal town in the Nile Delta, located near the modern city of Alexandria at Abu Qir Bay. It stood at the mouth of the westernmost (Canopic) branch of the Nile.

History

Canopus was a major port and religious centre long before the founding of Alexandria, famous for a great temple (Serapeum) of the god Serapis, which drew pilgrims seeking healing. The Canopic branch of the Nile and the canal to Alexandria made it a hub of trade. The Decree of Canopus (238 BC), a bilingual inscription issued under Ptolemy III, is a key source for Egyptian calendrical history.

Decline and rediscovery

The city declined with silting, earthquakes and subsidence, and much of it is now submerged under Abu Qir Bay. Underwater archaeology, notably by Franck Goddio's team from the 1990s, has recovered statues, coins, jewellery and temple remains from Canopus and neighbouring Heracleion.

The bright star Canopus is named after the city or its legendary pilot in Greek myth.
