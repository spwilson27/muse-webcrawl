The 2018 Sulawesi earthquake and tsunami struck Central Sulawesi, Indonesia, on 28 September 2018. A shallow magnitude 7.5 earthquake, preceded by foreshocks, triggered a destructive tsunami that struck the city of Palu and surrounding coastal areas of Donggala. The combined effects of the earthquake, tsunami and widespread soil liquefaction killed at least 4,340 people, making it the deadliest earthquake of 2018.

Earthquake

The mainshock occurred at 18:02 local time at a depth of about 20 km, on the Palu-Koro strike-slip fault. A magnitude 6.1 foreshock had struck earlier that afternoon. Strong shaking caused extensive building collapse in Palu, Donggala and Sigi Regency.

Tsunami

Waves reached heights of around 6 metres in Palu, sweeping through beachfront areas where a festival was being prepared. A tsunami warning was issued and then lifted before the largest waves arrived, a sequence that was later criticised. Submarine landslides generated by the earthquake are thought to have contributed to the wave heights in the narrow Palu bay.

Liquefaction

Large-scale soil liquefaction in neighbourhoods such as Balaroa and Petobo caused entire districts to subside or be carried away by flowing mud, accounting for many of the deaths and missing persons.

Response

Indonesian authorities coordinated search and rescue, and international aid was accepted from numerous countries. Mass burials were conducted to prevent disease, and a two-week emergency response period was declared.
