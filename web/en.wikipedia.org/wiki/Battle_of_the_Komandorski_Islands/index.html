The Battle of the Komandorski Islands was a naval battle between American and Japanese forces which took place on 27 March 1943 in the northern Pacific, south of the Soviet Komandorski Islands. The battle was a daylight surface gunnery duel, unusual for the Pacific War in that it was fought without air or submarine support on either side.

The heavy cruiser USS Salt Lake City in action off the Komandorski Islands.

Background

American intelligence learned that a Japanese convoy was sailing to reinforce the garrisons on Attu and Kiska in the Aleutian Islands. Rear Admiral Charles H. McMorris, with the heavy cruiser Salt Lake City, light cruiser Richmond, and four destroyers, was sent to intercept. He encountered a force under Vice Admiral Boshirō Hosogaya that was twice his strength: the heavy cruisers Nachi and Maya, light cruisers Tama and Abukuma, four destroyers, and two transports.

The battle

Fighting at long range for over three hours, the American ships were gradually overwhelmed. Salt Lake City was hit repeatedly, lost steering, and finally stopped dead in the water — but the destroyers' smoke screen and a desperate torpedo attack, led by Lieutenant Commander W. J. Whitehead's ships and notably USS Bailey, broke the Japanese concentration. Low on ammunition and fearing (wrongly) that American aircraft were approaching, Hosogaya broke off the action and retired.

Aftermath

The Japanese convoy never reached the Aleutian garrisons, making the battle a strategic American success despite tactical damage. Hosogaya was relieved of command for his failure. Thereafter Japan supplied its Aleutian outposts only by submarine, contributing to their abandonment later in 1943.
