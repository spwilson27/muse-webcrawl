Checkmate is a fictional covert operations and intelligence agency appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. A spinoff of the Suicide Squad milieu, the organization was created in 1988 amid DC's line of late-1980s espionage titles and is structured after the pieces of a chess game: field agents are its Knights and Pawns, supervised by Bishops, with operations directed by a ruling pair of Kings and Queens (Black and White) intended to keep the agency's two halves in check.

Publication history

Checkmate first appeared in Action Comics #598 (1988) and received its own ongoing series later that year, written by Paul Kupperberg, which ran for 33 issues. In this incarnation the agency was a U.S. government body run by Harry Stein, with Knight agents wearing distinctive visored armor; its recurring operatives included Gary Washington (Knight I) and the liaison with other agencies. The title folded in 1991 as DC's spy line contracted.

The organization was reinvented in the 2005 OMAC Project storyline, where Maxwell Lord seized control of a restructured Checkmate and used it against Earth's metahumans, murdering Blue Beetle (Ted Kord) and unleashing the OMAC cyborgs. After Lord's death, a new series by Greg Rucka and Nunzio DeFilippis (2006–2008) recast Checkmate as a United Nations-affiliated agency enforcing the metahuman balance of power, co-led by Amanda Waller's White Queen and Taleb Beni Khalid's Black King, with Sasha Bordeaux as Black Queen. The series was praised for its realistic, political-thriller treatment of superhuman geopolitics.

In other media

Checkmate has appeared in animated form in Justice League Unlimited and Young Justice, and its chess-piece codename system and rivalry with Task Force X recur across DC media. Sasha Bordeaux's tenure as Black Queen made her one of the organization's signature characters in the modern era.
