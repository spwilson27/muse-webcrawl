Von Mises yield criterion

The von Mises yield criterion (also known as the maximum distortion energy criterion) is a formula for calculating whether a material will fail under a given stress state. It states that yielding of a ductile material begins when the second deviatoric stress invariant reaches a critical value. It is a part of plasticity theory that mostly applies to ductile materials, such as some metals.

Prior to yielding, material response can be assumed to be of a linear elastic, nonlinear elastic, or viscoelastic behavior depending on its formulation. This criterion is named after the German-American applied mathematician Richard von Mises, who proposed it in 1913.

Formulation

In terms of the principal stresses σ1, σ2, σ3, the von Mises equivalent stress is defined as:

σv = √[ ½ ((σ1 − σ2)2 + (σ2 − σ3)2 + (σ3 − σ1)2) ]

Yielding is predicted when the equivalent stress σv reaches the material's yield strength in uniaxial tension, σy. In the space of principal stresses, the yield surface is a circular cylinder whose axis is the hydrostatic line σ1 = σ2 = σ3, reflecting the fact that hydrostatic pressure alone does not cause yielding in the criterion.

Interpretation

Physically, the criterion says that yielding begins when the elastic energy of distortion reaches the same value as in uniaxial tension at yield. Because only deviatoric (shape-changing) stresses contribute, the von Mises criterion ignores the hydrostatic component of stress. Experimental results for ductile metals generally agree better with the von Mises criterion than with the older Tresca (maximum shear stress) criterion, although the two give identical predictions for uniaxial and equal biaxial loading, and Tresca is more conservative in other states.
