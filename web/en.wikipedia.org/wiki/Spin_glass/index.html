Spin glass

A spin glass is a magnetic state of matter characterised by magnetic moments ("spins") that freeze into random, disordered orientations below a characteristic temperature, without the regular long-range order of a ferromagnet. Spin glasses arise when disorder and frustration — competing interactions that cannot all be satisfied simultaneously — prevent the system from settling into a single simple ground state.

Behaviour

In a typical spin glass, a small concentration of magnetic atoms (such as iron or manganese) is dissolved in a non-magnetic metal host (such as copper or gold). The indirect RKKY interaction between moments oscillates in sign with distance, so randomly positioned moments experience a mixture of ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic couplings. On cooling, the magnetic susceptibility shows a sharp cusp at the freezing temperature, yet no conventional magnetic Bragg peaks appear: the spins freeze cooperatively but point in random directions.

Key phenomena

  • Frustration – on a triangle of antiferromagnetically coupled spins, the third spin cannot be antiparallel to both neighbours.
  • Aging – the response of the system depends on how long it has waited below the freezing temperature.
  • Memory and rejuvenation – temperature cycling can temporarily erase (rejuvenate) aging, yet the system recovers its previous state (memory) on returning to the earlier temperature.
  • Many metastable states – the energy landscape contains an enormous number of local minima separated by barriers of many sizes.

Theoretical models

The Edwards–Anderson model represents spins on a lattice with random nearest-neighbour couplings. The Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model, in which every spin interacts with every other through random couplings, was solved by Giorgio Parisi using the replica method and replica symmetry breaking, revealing an ultrametric organisation of states. Parisi's work, which also found applications in neural networks, optimisation, and computer science, contributed to his 2021 Nobel Prize in Physics. The concept of spin-glass order is described by the Edwards–Anderson order parameter, the frozen local magnetisation averaged over disorder.

Applications and analogies

Spin-glass mathematics underlies the Hopfield model of associative memory, simulated annealing, and the theory of combinatorial optimisation problems such as the travelling salesman problem. Analogies have been drawn with protein folding, neural computation, and even social and economic systems.
