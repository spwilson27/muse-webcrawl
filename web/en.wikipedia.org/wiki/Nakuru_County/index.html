Nakuru County is a county in the former Rift Valley Province of Kenya. Its capital and largest town is Nakuru, which was granted city status in 2021, becoming Kenya's fourth city. The county is one of the 47 counties established under Kenya's 2010 constitution and is among the most populous and economically significant in the country.

Geography

Nakuru County lies in the Great Rift Valley and contains several notable lakes, including Lake Nakuru, Lake Naivasha and Lake Elmenteita. Lake Nakuru National Park, famous for its large flocks of flamingos and populations of black and white rhinoceros, is a major tourist attraction and a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Kenya Lake System. The county also includes part of the Mau Forest complex and the Menengai Crater, one of the largest volcanic calderas in the world.

Economy

Agriculture is the backbone of the county economy, including large-scale flower farming around Lake Naivasha, dairy and cereal production, and horticulture. The county hosts the Olkaria geothermal fields near Naivasha, which supply a large share of Kenya's electricity. Industry, transport and tourism also contribute significantly.

Administration

The county is divided into sub-counties including Nakuru Town East, Nakuru Town West, Naivasha, Njoro, Molo, Gilgil, Kuresoi North, Kuresoi South, Rongai, Subukia and Bahati. County government is headed by an elected governor.
