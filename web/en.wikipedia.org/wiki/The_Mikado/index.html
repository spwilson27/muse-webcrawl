The Mikado

The Mikado; or, The Town of Titipu is a comic opera in two acts, with music by Arthur Sullivan and libretto by W. S. Gilbert, their ninth of fourteen operatic collaborations. It opened at the Savoy Theatre in London on 14 March 1885 and ran for 672 performances.

Plot

In the fictional Japanese town of Titipu, flirting has been made a capital crime by the Mikado. Ko-Ko, a cheap tailor, has been appointed Lord High Executioner but cannot behead anyone before himself. His ward Yum-Yum is in love with the wandering minstrel Nanki-Poo, secretly the Mikado's son, who has fled court to escape marriage to the formidable Katisha. A tangled series of mock executions, false affidavits and revelations ends with every law satisfied in form and none in fact, and the couples united.

Satire

Although dressed in Japanese costume — inspired by a Japanese exhibition in Knightsbridge and a falling sword in Gilbert's study — the opera's real target is Victorian British bureaucracy, politics and institutions. Famous numbers include "A Wand'ring Minstrel I," "Three Little Maids from School," "I've Got a Little List," the title song "A More Humane Mikado," and the madrigal "Brightly Dawns Our Wedding Day."

Legacy

The Mikado is the most internationally successful of the Savoy operas and has been translated into many languages; for over a century it has been in near-continuous production somewhere in the world. Its "little list" song is traditionally updated with topical references. In recent decades productions have debated and revised elements of yellowface performance practice.
