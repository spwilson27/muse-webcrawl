Brown–Forman

Brown–Forman, based in Louisville, Kentucky, owns the Jack Daniel's brand.

The Brown–Forman Corporation is an American-based company, one of the largest in the spirits and wine business. Founded in 1870 by George Garvin Brown in Louisville, Kentucky, it is headquartered there and publicly traded on the New York Stock Exchange. The Brown family retains voting control through a dual-class share structure.

History

George Garvin Brown, a young pharmaceuticals salesman, founded the company with the then-novel idea of selling whiskey in sealed glass bottles to guarantee quality. Its Old Forester brand, launched in 1870, is regarded as the first bourbon marketed exclusively in bottles. The company survived Prohibition by obtaining one of the few licenses to sell whiskey for medicinal purposes, and expanded after repeal through acquisitions.

Brands

Brown–Forman's best-known product is Jack Daniel's Tennessee whiskey, the best-selling American whiskey brand in the world, produced in Lynchburg, Tennessee. Other brands include Woodford Reserve and Old Forester bourbons, Herradura and El Jimador tequilas, Finlandia vodka (sold to Coca-Cola HBC in 2023), Chambord liqueur, Sonoma-Cutrer wines and Fords Gin.
