Anthony Colin Bruce Chapman CBE (19 May 1928 – 16 December 1982) was an English design engineer, inventor and builder in the automotive industry, and founder of Lotus Cars. One of the most influential figures in motor racing history, he revolutionised Formula One car design through his obsessive pursuit of light weight and a series of innovations including the monocoque chassis, the use of the engine as a stressed structural member, and pioneering aerodynamics including ground-effect cars.

Career

Chapman built his first special, the Lotus Mark 1 trials car, in 1948 from an Austin 7, and founded Lotus in 1952. His sports cars progressed through the Eleven to the Elite and Elan road cars. Team Lotus entered Formula One in 1958 and, with drivers Jim Clark, Graham Hill, Jochen Rindt, Emerson Fittipaldi and Mario Andretti, won seven Constructors' Championships and six Drivers' Championships between 1963 and 1978, plus the Indianapolis 500 in 1965.

His design philosophy — "Simplify, then add lightness" — produced the monocoque Lotus 25, the Ford-Cosworth-powered Lotus 49, the wedge-shaped Lotus 72, and the ground-effect Lotus 78 and 79. Chapman died suddenly of a heart attack in December 1982, shortly after becoming involved in the DeLorean Motor Company affair, in which Lotus had engineered the DeLorean sports car; at his death he faced the prospect of investigation over missing development funds.
