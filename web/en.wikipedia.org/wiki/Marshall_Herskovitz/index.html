Marshall Herskovitz

Marshall Schreiber Herskovitz (born February 23, 1952) is an American film director, writer, and producer. He is known for his work in television and film, frequently in collaboration with Edward Zwick, with whom he co-created the television drama series thirtysomething (1987–1991) and My So-Called Life (1994).

Early life and education

Herskovitz was born in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. He attended Brandeis University and later the AFI Conservatory in Los Angeles, where he met Edward Zwick. The two formed a creative partnership, the Bedford Falls Company, that lasted for decades.

Career

Herskovitz and Zwick first gained prominence as writer-producers on the television series Family. Their series thirtysomething won multiple Emmy Awards and was acclaimed for its realistic depiction of baby-boomer professionals. They followed with My So-Called Life, starring Claire Danes, which became a cult classic despite a single season, and Once and Again.

In film, Herskovitz co-wrote and produced Legends of the Fall (1994) and produced Traffic (2000), I Am Sam (2001), Blood Diamond (2006), and The Last Samurai (2003). He directed the films Jack the Bear (1993) and Dangerous Beauty (1998).

Awards

Herskovitz has won Emmy Awards, a Directors Guild of America Award, and the Humanitas Prize. He has also served as president of the Producers Guild of America.
