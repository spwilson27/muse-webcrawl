Median strip

A median strip, or central reservation in British English, is the reserved area that separates opposing lanes of traffic on divided roads, including dual carriageways, motorways and divided streets. It may be a raised kerb, a paved area, a landscaped strip or a safety barrier.

Functions

The median separates opposing traffic, reducing head-on collisions; provides recovery and stopping space; offers room for turning lanes, street lighting, signage, landscaping and future widening; and gives pedestrians a refuge when crossing wide roads.

Design

Widths range from narrow painted or kerbed strips on urban streets to tens of metres on rural motorways. High-speed roads often use steel or concrete barriers in the median, while suburban arterials may use planted medians that must be balanced against maintenance and visibility needs.
