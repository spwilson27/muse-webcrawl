Brushless DC electric motor

A brushless DC motor: permanent magnets on the rotor, windings on the stator.

A brushless DC electric motor (BLDC), also known as an electronically commutated motor, is a synchronous electric motor powered by direct-current electricity through an inverter or switching power supply that produces alternating current in the windings. Unlike a brushed motor, it has no brushes or mechanical commutator; an electronic controller switches current to the stator windings in sequence, and the rotor — carrying permanent magnets — follows the rotating magnetic field.

Construction and operation

The controller determines rotor position from Hall-effect sensors or from sensorless back-EMF detection, and energizes the (usually three) phases in a trapezoidal or sinusoidal pattern. Motors may be "inrunners", with the rotor inside the stator, or "outrunners", common in drones and model aircraft, where the outer casing rotates.

Advantages and applications

Eliminating brushes removes a major source of wear, sparking and electrical noise, giving BLDC motors long service life, high efficiency and high power density. They are ubiquitous in computer fans and hard drives, drones, electric vehicles, cordless tools, washing machines, HVAC systems and industrial servo drives.
