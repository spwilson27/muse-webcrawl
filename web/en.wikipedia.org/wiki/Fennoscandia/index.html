Fennoscandia

Fennoscandia is the geographical peninsula in north-western Europe comprising the Scandinavian Peninsula, Finland, Karelia and the Kola Peninsula. In political and cultural terms it generally refers to Norway, Sweden, Finland and parts of north-western Russia.

Geology

The region is underlain by the Fennoscandian Shield, one of the oldest exposed pieces of continental crust, made up largely of Archaean and Proterozoic rocks. The weight of the ice sheets during the last glacial period depressed the land, and post-glacial rebound continues today, reaching up to about a centimetre per year around the Gulf of Bothnia.

Geography and ecology

Fennoscandia spans the Scandinavian Mountains in the west and low-lying lake districts in Finland, and its biomes range from temperate forest in the south to boreal taiga and Arctic tundra in the north. The term was coined by the Finnish geologist Wilhelm Ramsay in 1898.
