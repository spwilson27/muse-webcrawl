Garryales

Garrya elliptica
KingdomPlantae
CladeAngiosperms; Eudicots; Asterids
OrderGarryales
FamiliesGarryaceae; Eucommiaceae

The Garryales are a small order of dichlamydeous flowering plants classified in the asterid clade of the eudicots. In the APG IV system, the order comprises two families: the Garryaceae and the Eucommiaceae.

Families and genera

The Garryaceae include the genus Garrya, a group of dioecious shrubs and small trees native to western North America, Mexico, and Central America, known for their long, hanging catkins, and the genus Aucuba, evergreen shrubs native to eastern Asia, including the popular garden plant Aucuba japonica (spotted laurel). Roughly 18 species of Garrya and about 10 species of Aucuba are recognized.

The Eucommiaceae contain a single living species, Eucommia ulmoides, a deciduous tree native to China. It is notable as the only temperate-zone plant that produces rubber, and its bark is used in traditional Chinese medicine under the name du zhong.

Classification

Older systems, such as the Cronquist system, placed these groups in different orders, but molecular phylogenetic studies placed them together as the Garryales, within the lamiid (euasterid I) clade of the asterids. The order is named for the genus Garrya, itself named by David Douglas in honor of Nicholas Garry of the Hudson's Bay Company.

Characteristics

Members of the Garryales are woody plants, often dioecious (with separate male and female individuals in Garryaceae), bearing small, wind-pollinated flowers arranged in catkin-like inflorescences and producing berry-like fruits or samaras. Eucommia is distinguished by leaves and bark that exude latex threads when torn.
