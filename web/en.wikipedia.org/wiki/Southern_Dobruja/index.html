Southern Dobruja

Southern Dobruja (Bulgarian: Южна Добруджа; Romanian: Dobrogea de Sud) is the southern part of the Dobruja region on the Black Sea, lying within northeastern Bulgaria. It corresponds roughly to the modern Bulgarian provinces of Dobrich and Silistra, and is separated from Romanian Northern Dobruja by the present-day border between the two countries.

History

Dobruja was contested for centuries between Bulgaria, Romania and the Ottoman Empire. Southern Dobruja was part of Bulgaria from 1878 until the Second Balkan War, when Romania annexed it in 1913. It remained Romanian until the Treaty of Craiova in September 1940, concluded under Axis pressure, returned it to Bulgaria — a settlement confirmed after the Second World War and unusual among Axis-era border changes in surviving to the present day.

Population exchange and economy

The 1940 treaty involved a compulsory population exchange: Bulgarian inhabitants of Northern Dobruja moved south, and Romanian settlers and Aromanians in the south moved north, reshaping the region's ethnic composition. Southern Dobruja is a fertile steppe of chernozem soils and is a major grain-producing area, often called Bulgaria's granary.
