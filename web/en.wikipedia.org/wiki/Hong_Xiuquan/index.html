Hong Xiuquan

Hong Xiuquan (1 January 1814 – 1 June 1864), born Hong Huoxiu, was a Chinese revolutionary who led the Taiping Rebellion (1850–1864) against the Qing dynasty. A Hakka schoolteacher who repeatedly failed the imperial examinations, Hong came to believe he was the younger brother of Jesus Christ, commissioned to rid China of "demons" — the ruling Manchus. He founded the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom, with its capital at Nanjing, and ruled it as Heavenly King until its fall.

Visions and the God Worshipping Society

After failing the provincial examinations in 1837, Hong suffered a breakdown and experienced visions of ascending to heaven, where a bearded elder — whom he later understood as God the Father — gave him a sword and seal to destroy demons. In 1843, after a final examination failure, Hong read Christian tracts by Liang Fa and concluded that he was Christ's younger brother. With his cousin Feng Yunshan he built the God Worshipping Society among the impoverished Hakka of Guangxi.

The Taiping Rebellion

In 1850 Hong's followers clashed with local militia, and in January 1851 he proclaimed the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom at Jintian. Taiping armies swept north, capturing Nanjing in 1853 and making it their capital, Tianjing ("Heavenly Capital"). The kingdom proclaimed radical reforms: land redistribution, the equality of the sexes in principle, bans on opium, footbinding and prostitution, and a new calendar. Internal purges — notably the Tianjing Incident of 1856, in which the East King Yang Xiuqing was slaughtered — fatally weakened the movement.

Death and legacy

Besieged by Zeng Guofan's Xiang Army, Hong died in Nanjing in June 1864, weeks before the city fell; the cause is debated, with suicide by poison or illness both proposed. The rebellion was among the deadliest conflicts in history, with deaths commonly estimated at 20–30 million. Hong remains a controversial figure: honoured by later Chinese revolutionaries as a peasant rebel, criticised for his theocratic rule.
