John Henry Patterson (December 13, 1844 – May 7, 1922) was an American businessman who founded the National Cash Register Company, building it into one of the dominant American corporations of its era. He is regarded as a pioneer of modern salesmanship, employee welfare programmes and commercial training.

Career

After serving as a Union officer in the Civil War and running a coal business, Patterson bought into a Dayton cash-register venture in 1884 and reorganised it as the National Cash Register Company. He patented improvements, drove out competitors and built a legendary sales force, introducing the sales quota, guaranteed territories, formal sales training at "Sugar Camp", and the idea that the cash register prevented theft and error rather than merely recording sales.

Welfare capitalism and the 1913 flood

Patterson instituted factory welfare measures — clean, light workplaces, baths, lunchrooms, medical care and parks — that made NCR a model of welfare capitalism. After the Great Dayton Flood of 1913, he turned his factories into relief headquarters and led the city's recovery effort. Among his protégés were Thomas J. Watson, later of IBM, and Charles Kettering.
