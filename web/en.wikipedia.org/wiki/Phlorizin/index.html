Phlorizin

Phlorizin (also phloridzin) is a glucoside of the dihydrochalcone phloretin. It occurs naturally in the bark, roots and fruit of apple trees and other members of the rose family.

Phlorizin inhibits the sodium-glucose cotransporters SGLT1 and SGLT2 in the kidney and intestine, causing glucose to be excreted in the urine. This effect was historically used to create experimental models of diabetes and later inspired the development of gliflozin drugs (SGLT2 inhibitors) used to treat type 2 diabetes. Phlorizin itself is not used clinically because of poor selectivity and gastrointestinal side effects.
