Zurich (German: Zürich) is the largest city in Switzerland and the capital of the canton of Zurich. It lies at the northwestern end of Lake Zurich where the Limmat river flows out of the lake. Zurich is a global centre for banking and finance and consistently ranks among the world's highest cities for quality of life.

History

The area has been settled since Neolithic times; the Roman customs post Turicum gave the city its name. In the Middle Ages Zurich joined the Swiss Confederacy (1351), and under Huldrych Zwingli it became a centre of the Reformation in the 1520s. Industrialisation in the 19th century — machine-building and textiles — laid the foundations of its modern prosperity.

Economy and culture

Zurich hosts the headquarters of major banks and insurers, the Swiss stock exchange, and a growing technology sector. ETH Zurich and the University of Zurich rank among Europe's leading universities. Cultural landmarks include the Grossmünster and Fraumünster churches, the Kunsthaus art museum, the old town (Altstadt), and the luxury shopping street Bahnhofstrasse. Events such as the Street Parade draw hundreds of thousands of visitors.
