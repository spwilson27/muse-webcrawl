Hemispatial neglect

Hemispatial neglect is a neuropsychological condition in which, after damage to one hemisphere of the brain (most commonly the right parietal lobe, e.g. from a stroke), a deficit in attention and awareness towards the side of space opposite to the damaged hemisphere (contralesional space) is observed. It is characterised by a failure to report, respond, or orient to stimuli presented to the contralesional side, which cannot be attributed to primary sensory or motor deficits.

A clock drawn by a patient with left-sided neglect

Signs and symptoms

Patients may eat only from one side of a plate, shave or dress only one side of the body, bump into objects on the neglected side, and when asked to copy a drawing or write, crowd all details onto the attended side or omit the neglected half. On line-bisection tasks they mark the midpoint far to the right of centre, and on cancellation tests they fail to cross out targets on the left. Severe cases involve anosognosia, denial of the deficit itself. Neglect differs from hemianopia: it is a disorder of attention and representation rather than of vision, and patients may even neglect the left side of imagined scenes (as shown in Bisiach's famous Piazza del Duomo study).

Causes and treatment

Neglect typically follows right-hemisphere stroke involving the inferior parietal lobule and temporoparietal junction, though frontal and subcortical lesions also produce it; left neglect is more common and more severe than right neglect. Management includes scanning and visual-search training, limb activation, prism adaptation therapy (in which prism glasses shift the visual field), optokinetic stimulation and, experimentally, non-invasive brain stimulation.
