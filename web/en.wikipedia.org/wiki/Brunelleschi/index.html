Filippo Brunelleschi

Filippo di ser Brunellesco di Lippo Lapi (1377 – 15 April 1446), known as Filippo Brunelleschi, was an Italian architect, designer and sculptor, recognised as a founding father of Renaissance architecture. His masterwork is the dome of Florence Cathedral, the largest masonry dome ever built, and he is credited with developing the mathematical technique of linear perspective in art.

Early life and the Baptistery competition

Trained as a goldsmith and sculptor, Brunelleschi entered the famous 1401 competition for the bronze doors of the Florence Baptistery; his trial panel of the Sacrifice of Isaac survives, though the commission went to Lorenzo Ghiberti. Around 1415 he demonstrated linear perspective in two lost panel paintings of Florentine scenes, a discovery that transformed Western art.

The dome

In 1418 the cathedral works advertised a competition for the dome over the vast octagonal crossing, which had stood open for decades. Brunelleschi's proposal — a double-shelled dome built without full wooden centring, its bricks laid in a herringbone pattern, raised with machines of his own invention such as the reversible ox-hoist — won. Construction ran from 1420 to 1436; the lantern, to his design, was finished after his death. The dome spans about 45 metres and remains the largest brick dome in the world.

Other works

His buildings established the classical language of the Renaissance: the Ospedale degli Innocenti (foundling hospital) with its graceful loggia, the churches of San Lorenzo and Santo Spirito, the Old Sacristy, and the Pazzi Chapel. He also designed fortifications, stage machinery and clocks.

Legacy

Brunelleschi was buried in Florence Cathedral, an honour reflecting his civic stature. His rational, measured architecture — described by his biographer Antonio Manetti and by Vasari — set the pattern for Renaissance building for two centuries.
