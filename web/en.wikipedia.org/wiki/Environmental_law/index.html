Environmental law is the collection of laws, regulations, agreements, and common law that governs how humans interact with their environment. Its purpose is to protect the environment, human health, and natural resources, and to manage the impacts of human activity. Environmental law operates at local, national, and international levels.

Areas

Major areas include pollution control laws governing air and water quality and waste management; chemical safety and cleanup of contaminated land; natural-resources law covering water rights, mining, forestry, and fisheries; wildlife and biodiversity conservation; land-use planning and environmental impact assessment; and, increasingly, climate change law addressing greenhouse-gas emissions. Principles frequently invoked include the polluter-pays principle, the precautionary principle, prevention, and public participation.

Development

Modern environmental law crystallised in the late 1960s and 1970s, with milestones such as the US National Environmental Policy Act (1970), the creation of environmental agencies like the EPA, and the 1972 Stockholm Conference, which launched international environmental diplomacy. International environmental law has since produced major treaties on ozone depletion (Montreal Protocol), biodiversity, hazardous waste, and climate change (the UNFCCC, Kyoto Protocol, and Paris Agreement).
