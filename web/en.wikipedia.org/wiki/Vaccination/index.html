Vaccination

Vaccination
OriginJenner's smallpox vaccine, 1796
PrincipleTrain the immune system with a harmless form of a pathogen
SuccessesSmallpox eradicated; polio near-eradicated
[Image: A nurse administering a vaccine injection into a patient's upper arm]

Vaccination is the administration of a vaccine to stimulate the immune system to develop protection against a disease. Vaccines contain weakened or inactivated pathogens, their parts, or instructions (such as mRNA) for making them, so the body learns to respond without suffering the illness.

Impact

Vaccination eradicated Smallpox, has nearly eradicated polio, and has dramatically reduced measles, diphtheria, tetanus and many other diseases; during the COVID-19 pandemic billions of doses were given within a year of the virus's identification. Population-level 'herd immunity' protects those who cannot be vaccinated.
