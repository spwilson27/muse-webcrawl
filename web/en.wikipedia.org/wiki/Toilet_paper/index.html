Toilet paper

Toilet paper is a tissue paper product primarily used to clean the anus and surrounding region after defecation, and to clean the perineal area after urination. It is typically supplied as a long strip of perforated paper wrapped around a cardboard core and dispensed from a holder near a toilet.

History

Paper for hygiene was used in China by the 6th century AD; by the 14th century, mass-produced toilet paper was recorded in Zhejiang province. In the West, people long relied on materials such as leaves, moss, corncobs, and later old newspapers and catalogues. Joseph Gayetty introduced the first packaged commercial toilet paper in the United States in 1857 as "medicated paper." The perforated roll format was popularized in the 1880s and 1890s by the Scott Paper Company and the Albany Perforated Wrapping Paper Company, whose patent disputes helped shape the industry.

Production and properties

Modern toilet paper is usually made from virgin wood pulp or recycled paper, engineered to be soft and strong when dry yet to disintegrate quickly in water so as not to block plumbing. It is sold in one- to four-ply grades, sometimes embossed, scented, or lotioned. Average consumption varies widely by country, with the United States among the highest per-capita users.

Cultural notes

The proper orientation of a roll — paper hanging "over" or "under" — is a famously trivial but persistent debate, with polls consistently favouring "over." Shortages triggered by panic buying, notably during the 2020 COVID-19 pandemic, made toilet paper a symbol of supply-chain anxiety.
