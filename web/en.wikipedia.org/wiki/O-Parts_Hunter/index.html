O-Parts Hunter, known in Japan as 666 Satan (Japanese: 666～サタン～), is a Japanese manga series written and illustrated by Seishi Kishimoto, the younger twin brother of Naruto creator Masashi Kishimoto. It was serialised in Square Enix's Monthly Shōnen Gangan from 2001 to 2007 and collected in nineteen volumes. In North America it was licensed by Viz Media under the O-Parts Hunter name, avoiding the religiously charged Japanese title.

Plot

The story is set in a world scattered with the ruins of an ancient civilisation and its artifacts, the O-Parts — objects of immense, varied power that can only be wielded by rare individuals called O.P.T.s (O-Parts Tacticians). The protagonist, Jio Freed, is an ambitious boy who dreams of world domination to escape a childhood of rejection; unknown to him, his body houses the spirit of Satan, whose power makes him a target of both the government-like Zenom Syndicate and the Stea government, who are racing to gather the legendary O-Parts needed to unlock the Kabbalah and its reverse — a contest that would grant control over ultimate power. Jio travels with the treasure hunter Ruby Crescent, who hires him as a bodyguard, and a growing band of companions.

Reception

The series drew frequent comparisons to Naruto because of the twin brothers' similar art styles and thematic interests, though Seishi Kishimoto's story leans more heavily on kabbalistic and apocalyptic imagery, with demons and angels embodied in characters marked by numbers and names from demonology.
