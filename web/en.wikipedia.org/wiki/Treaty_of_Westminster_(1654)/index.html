Treaty of Westminster (1654)

The Treaty of Westminster, concluded on 5/15 April 1654, was the peace treaty ending the First Anglo-Dutch War between the Commonwealth of England and the Dutch Republic.

Terms

The Dutch agreed to strike their flag to English warships in the "British seas," to pay compensation for the Amboyna massacre of 1623 and other East Indies grievances, and to submit outstanding disputes to arbitration by the Swiss cantons—arbitration that awarded damages and assigned the island of Run in the East Indies to England in principle (though the Dutch retained it in practice until 1667). A secret annex, the Act of Seclusion, drawn up by Johan de Witt, barred the young Prince William of Orange from the stadtholderate, satisfying Oliver Cromwell's wish to prevent a monarchical restoration linked to the Stuarts. The Act provoked outrage among Orangists when it leaked.

Context

The war had arisen from commercial rivalry and England's Navigation Act of 1651. The treaty failed to resolve the underlying competition, and further Anglo-Dutch wars followed in 1665–67 and 1672–74.
