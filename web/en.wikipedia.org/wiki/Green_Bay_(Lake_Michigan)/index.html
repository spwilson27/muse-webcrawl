Green Bay is an arm of Lake Michigan, located along the south coast of Michigan's Upper Peninsula and the east coast of Wisconsin. It is separated from the rest of the lake by the Door Peninsula in Wisconsin, the Garden Peninsula in Michigan, and the chain of islands between them, all formed by the Niagara Escarpment.

Geography

The bay is about 120 miles (193 km) long, with a width ranging from 10 to 20 miles (16 to 32 km). Its total surface area is about 1,626 square miles (4,211 km²). At its southern end, the city of Green Bay, Wisconsin, stands where the Fox River flows into the bay.

History

French explorer Jean Nicolet reached Green Bay in 1634 and established one of the oldest European settlements in the United States at La Baye. The bay was historically called La Baye des Puants ("the bay of the Stinkers") by the French, a reference to the Ho-Chunk people who lived around it.

Ecology

The bay supports commercial and sport fisheries, but has suffered from agricultural runoff and hypoxia ("dead zones") in its southern waters. The Fox River, which drains into the bay, was the site of a major PCB contamination cleanup.
