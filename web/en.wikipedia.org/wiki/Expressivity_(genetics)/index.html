Expressivity (genetics)

Expressivity is the degree to which a phenotype is expressed by individuals having a particular genotype. The term is usually contrasted with penetrance: penetrance is the proportion of individuals with a genotype who show the phenotype at all, whereas expressivity describes the range or severity of the phenotype among those who do show it.

A trait shows variable expressivity when the same genotype produces phenotypes of differing severity — for example, in neurofibromatosis type I, where affected members of one family may range from having only café-au-lait spots to numerous tumours. Variation can arise from modifier genes, environmental influences, and chance developmental events.
