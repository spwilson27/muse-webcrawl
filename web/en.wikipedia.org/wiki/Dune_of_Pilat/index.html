The Dune of Pilat (French: Dune du Pilat), also called the Grande Dune du Pilat, is the tallest sand dune in Europe. It is located in La Teste-de-Buch in the Arcachon Bay area of southwestern France, about 60 kilometres southwest of Bordeaux, on the coast of the Bay of Biscay.

Geography

The dune measures roughly 500 to 600 metres from east to west and about 2.7 to 3 kilometres from north to south. Its height fluctuates, generally between about 100 and 115 metres above sea level. The dune runs parallel to the shoreline and is backed by the extensive Landes forest, which it slowly encroaches upon as wind drives the sand inland.

Formation

The dune formed from sand deposited by wind, tides and ocean currents. A large offshore sandbank, the Banc d'Arguin, lies at the entrance to Arcachon Bay and supplies much of the sand. Studies of the dune's internal layers suggest it has existed in some form for thousands of years, with its growth accelerating in recent centuries, burying earlier vegetation and, historically, threatening nearby properties and roads.

Tourism

The dune is one of France's most visited natural sites, attracting well over a million visitors a year. A seasonal staircase is installed to help visitors climb the steep eastern face, and the summit offers views over the bay, the Banc d'Arguin nature reserve and the pine forest. The site is popular for paragliding.
