Rotterdam Convention

The Rotterdam Convention (formally, the Rotterdam Convention on the Prior Informed Consent Procedure for Certain Hazardous Chemicals and Pesticides in International Trade) is a multilateral environmental treaty adopted on 10 September 1998 in Rotterdam, Netherlands, and in force since 24 February 2004.

Provisions

The Convention establishes a legally binding Prior Informed Consent (PIC) procedure: exporting countries must notify and obtain the consent of importing countries before shipping chemicals listed in Annex III, which include severely hazardous pesticides and industrial chemicals. It also requires export notifications for chemicals banned or severely restricted domestically, and promotes information exchange about hazardous chemicals. The treaty does not ban trade; it empowers importers, especially developing countries, to decide which chemicals they can safely manage.

Administration

The Convention is administered jointly by UNEP and the FAO, and its secretariat conferences are held jointly with the Basel and Stockholm conventions, the other global chemicals and wastes treaties. Disputes over listing substances such as chrysotile asbestos have been prominent at its Conferences of the Parties.
