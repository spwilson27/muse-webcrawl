Regent

A regent (from the Latin word regens, "[one] ruling or governing") is a person appointed to govern a state pro tempore (Latin for "for the time being") because the actual monarch is a minor, is absent, is incapacitated, or because the throne is vacant.

Regency

The period during which a regent rules is called a regency. A regency may be established by a constitution, a statute, or by the decision of a council of regency. In some monarchies, the regent is the queen consort or another close relative of the monarch; in others, the office may be elected or appointed by parliament.

Famous regents

Well-known regents include Philippe II, Duke of Orléans, who governed France during the minority of Louis XV; the Prince Regent of the United Kingdom, later George IV, who gave his name to the Regency era; and Dorgon of the early Qing dynasty in China.
