Plücker coordinates

In geometry, Plücker coordinates, introduced by Julius Plücker in the 19th century, assign six homogeneous coordinates to each line in projective 3-space, P3. Because they satisfy a single quadratic relation (the Plücker relation), lines can be identified with points on a quadric in P5, known as the Klein quadric. Plücker coordinates are a fundamental tool of line geometry.

Definition

Given two distinct points on a line with homogeneous coordinates p = (p0, p1, p2, p3) and q = (q0, q1, q2, q3), the Plücker coordinates of the line are the six 2×2 determinants of the 4×2 matrix formed by p and q:

Pij = piqj − pjqi.

These six numbers are defined up to a common scale factor and satisfy the Plücker relation:

P01P23 − P02P13 + P03P12 = 0.

Conversely, any six-tuple satisfying this relation corresponds to exactly one line in P3.

Direction–moment form

For a line in Euclidean 3-space, the coordinates can be organised as a pair (d, m), where d is the direction of the line and m = p × d is its moment about the origin. This form is widely used in computer vision, robotics and computer graphics: for example, to test intersections of lines with geometry and to represent rays.

Applications

Plücker coordinates are used in ray tracing and visibility computation, in screw theory and kinematics, and in the mathematical treatment of line complexes and congruences founded in the 19th century.
