El Mariachi

El Mariachi
Directed by: Robert Rodriguez
Starring: Carlos Gallardo, Consuelo Gómez
Released: 1992 (premiered); 1993 (US)
Budget: approx. US$7,000

El Mariachi is a 1992 Spanish-language American independent action film written, directed, produced, photographed, and edited by Robert Rodriguez. It is the first instalment of his Mexico Trilogy, followed by Desperado (1995) and Once Upon a Time in Mexico (2003).

Plot

A travelling mariachi (Carlos Gallardo) arrives in a small Mexican border town looking for work, carrying his guitar in a black case. At the same time, a hitman named Azul — recently escaped from prison and hunting the local drug lord Moco — arrives carrying an identical case full of weapons. Mistaken for the killer, the mariachi is drawn into a spiral of violence as Moco's men hunt him down, and he finds refuge with the bar owner Dominó.

Production

Rodriguez famously made the film for about US$7,000, money partly raised by participating in clinical drug trials, intending it as a calling card for the Spanish-language home video market. It was shot in Ciudad Acuña, Mexico, with non-professional actors, a single camera, and extensive improvisation. Acquired by Columbia Pictures, which spent far more on prints, transfers, and marketing than the production cost, it became a landmark of independent and guerrilla filmmaking. Rodriguez chronicled the experience in his book Rebel Without a Crew.

Recognition

The film won the Audience Award at the 1993 Sundance Film Festival and was inducted into the United States National Film Registry in 2011 for its cultural and historical significance.
