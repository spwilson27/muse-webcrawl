Tyrone Guthrie

Sir William Tyrone Guthrie (2 July 1900 – 15 May 1971) was an English theatrical director and producer who founded the Stratford Festival in Canada and the Guthrie Theater in Minneapolis, Minnesota.

Early life

Guthrie was born in Tunbridge Wells, Kent, the son of Dr Thomas Guthrie and Norah Power. He studied history at New College, Oxford, where he became involved in student theatre.

Career

Guthrie began his career in radio drama at the BBC in Belfast, then directed at the Cambridge Festival Theatre and the Old Vic in London. He directed the Scottish National Players and worked at Sadler's Wells. After the Second World War he directed Benjamin Britten's Peter Grimes and helped found the Edinburgh International Festival, directing its first production, The Thrie Estaites, in 1948.

In 1953 Guthrie was invited to help create a Shakespeare festival in Stratford, Ontario, Canada; the Stratford Festival opened under a tent with Richard III and All's Well That Ends Well, starring Alec Guinness. In 1963 he founded the Tyrone Guthrie Theater in Minneapolis, one of the leading resident theatres in the United States, opening with Hamlet.

Legacy

Guthrie was knighted in 1961. He is remembered as a pioneer of the thrust stage and of regional theatre in North America.
