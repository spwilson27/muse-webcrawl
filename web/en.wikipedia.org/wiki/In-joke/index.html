In-joke

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An in-joke, also known as an inside joke or a private joke, is a type of humour whose significance is understood only by members of a particular group — people who share some common knowledge, experience, or context that outsiders lack. To those outside the group, an in-joke may appear meaningless, nonsensical, or even surreal, which is often part of its appeal to insiders.

Characteristics

In-jokes arise naturally within families, circles of friends, workplaces, professional communities, and online groups. They typically grow out of shared experiences: a memorable event, a mistake, a catchphrase, or a moment of embarrassment can become a lasting reference point. Because they depend on shared history, in-jokes reinforce social bonds and group identity, functioning as a kind of shorthand that signals belonging.

In media

Writers and filmmakers often plant in-jokes for attentive audiences: references to earlier works, the creators' own lives, or a fandom's lore. Fan communities develop elaborate vocabularies of such references, and works such as long-running television series accumulate them over time. In software, hidden Easter eggs serve a similar function for programmers and users in the know.
