Central line (London Underground)

Central line
Colour: Red

The Central line is a London Underground line that runs from Epping in Essex through central London to Ealing and West Ruislip in the west. Coloured red on the Tube map, it is the longest Underground line, at 46 miles (74 km), and one of the busiest, carrying over 260 million passengers a year before the COVID-19 pandemic.

History

The line opened in 1900 as the Central London Railway, the "Twopenny Tube", running from Bank to Shepherd's Bush. It was extended over the following decades, absorbing the Epping branch of the former Great Eastern Railway after the Second World War under the New Works Programme. The branch to Ongar closed in 1994.

Route

In the west the line splits into the Ealing Broadway and West Ruislip branches. In central London it serves Liverpool Street, Bank, Oxford Circus, Bond Street and Notting Hill Gate. East of Stratford the line surfaces and runs to Epping, with a shuttle branch (the Hainault loop) between Woodford and Hainault.

Rolling stock

Services are operated by 1992 Stock trains, which are scheduled for replacement under the New Tube for London programme. The line is noted for the heat of its deep-level tunnels in summer.
