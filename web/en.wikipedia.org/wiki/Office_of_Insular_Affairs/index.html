The Office of Insular Affairs (OIA) is a unit of the United States Department of the Interior responsible for coordinating federal policy for the US territories and administering US financial assistance to the freely associated states. It is headed by the Assistant Secretary of the Interior for Insular and International Affairs.

Responsibilities

The office coordinates relations with the territories of Guam, the US Virgin Islands, American Samoa and the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands, and administers federal funding and technical assistance. (Puerto Rico, as the largest territory, deals with Washington mainly through other channels, though it has historically been within the office's remit in some respects.) The OIA also manages the Compacts of Free Association with the Marshall Islands, the Federated States of Micronesia and Palau, including the disbursement of US assistance under those agreements.

History

The office traces its origins to the Division of Territories and Island Possessions, created in 1934 to take over territorial administration from the War Department's Bureau of Insular Affairs, which had managed affairs in the Philippines and Puerto Rico after the Spanish–American War. It later became the Office of Territories (1950) and then the Office of Territorial Affairs, before being reorganised as the Office of Insular Affairs in 1995.

Functions

OIA provides grants for infrastructure, energy, climate resilience and public financial management in the insular areas, promotes private-sector development, and works to improve the canneries' and governments' accountability for federal funds. It also coordinates the Interagency Group on Insular Areas, chaired by the Secretary of the Interior.
