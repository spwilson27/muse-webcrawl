Florida Keys

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Florida Keys are a coral cay archipelago off the southern coast of Florida, forming the southernmost part of the continental United States. They begin at the southeastern coast of the Florida peninsula, about 15 miles (24 km) south of Miami, and extend in a gentle arc south-southwest and then westward to Key West, the westernmost of the inhabited islands, and on to the uninhabited Dry Tortugas. The islands lie along the Florida Straits, dividing the Atlantic Ocean to the east from the Gulf of Mexico to the northwest, and marking the boundary between the two bodies of water.

Geography

The Keys are the exposed portion of an ancient coral reef, the Key Limestone, capped in places by oolitic Miami Limestone. At the nearest point, Key West is about 95 miles (153 km) by air from Cuba. Major islands include Key Largo, Islamorada's Plantation Key, Marathon's Key Vaca, Big Pine Key and Key West. The total land area is about 137.3 square miles (356 km²), and the permanent population is roughly 73,000, concentrated in Monroe County.

History and transport

The islands were inhabited by Calusa and Tequesta peoples before Spanish exploration; Juan Ponce de León charted them in 1513. For centuries they were isolated, reached only by boat, and known for wrecking, fishing, sponging and smuggling. Henry Flagler's Florida East Coast Railway completed the Overseas Railroad to Key West in 1912; after the 1935 Labor Day hurricane destroyed much of the line, its bridges were converted to the Overseas Highway (U.S. Route 1), which today links the islands with 42 bridges, including the Seven Mile Bridge. In 1982, residents of Key West jokingly declared the mock secession of the "Conch Republic" in protest at a Border Patrol roadblock.

Environment

The Keys have a tropical savanna climate and are surrounded by the Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary and the only living coral barrier reef in the continental United States. The islands are vulnerable to hurricanes and to sea-level rise, and host unique species such as the Key deer.
