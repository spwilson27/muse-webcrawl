Postpartum psychosis

Postpartum psychosis (or puerperal psychosis) is a rare but severe mental illness that occurs in roughly 1 to 2 per 1,000 women after childbirth. It typically begins suddenly in the first days to two weeks after delivery and is considered a psychiatric emergency because of risks of suicide and, rarely, harm to the infant.

Symptoms

Symptoms often begin with insomnia, restlessness, and rapidly fluctuating mood, progressing to confusion, paranoia, grandiose or bizarre delusions (frequently centred on the baby), hallucinations, and disorganized behaviour. The presentation commonly has manic and psychotic features and may resemble delirium.

Causes and risk factors

The strongest risk factor is a personal or family history of bipolar disorder or a previous postpartum psychosis; women with bipolar disorder have a markedly elevated risk after delivery. A first episode of postpartum psychosis is often the first presentation of bipolar disorder. Precipitants are thought to include the abrupt hormonal changes of childbirth, sleep deprivation, and immune system changes.

Treatment

Treatment usually requires urgent hospital admission, ideally to a specialist mother-and-baby unit so the mother can remain with her infant. Management includes antipsychotic medication, mood stabilizers such as lithium, treatment of insomnia, and in severe or refractory cases electroconvulsive therapy, which is highly effective. Most women recover fully from the episode, but there is a high risk of recurrence after subsequent pregnancies, so preventative planning is important.
