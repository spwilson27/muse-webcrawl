Serenity Prayer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Serenity Prayer is a prayer attributed to the American theologian Reinhold Niebuhr (1892–1971). Its best-known form is:

God, grant me the serenity to accept the things I cannot change,
the courage to change the things I can,
and the wisdom to know the difference.

A longer version continues with petitions about living one day at a time, accepting hardship as a pathway to peace, and taking the world as it is. Niebuhr used versions of the prayer in sermons from the 1930s onward, notably at the Heath Evangelical Union Church in Massachusetts, and it was printed in a 1944 Book of Prayers and Services for the Armed Forces.

Adoption by Alcoholics Anonymous

Alcoholics Anonymous adopted the prayer in the early 1940s after a member spotted it in a newspaper obituary notice in 1941; AA's New York office reproduced it on cards, and it became closely associated with the fellowship and with twelve-step programmes generally, commonly recited at meetings. The prayer passed from AA into wide popular culture, appearing on plaques, posters, and in music.

Authorship research

Niebuhr's authorship was occasionally questioned, with similar formulations traced to earlier philosophical and religious traditions. Research by Yale librarian Stephen Goranson and others documented 1930s attestations crediting Niebuhr, and Niebuhr himself acknowledged that while the thought might have been "spooking around for years", he composed the prayer. His daughter Elisabeth Sifton's book The Serenity Prayer (2003) discusses its composition around 1943.
