Maxwell construction

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In statistical mechanics, the Maxwell construction (or equal area rule) is the method of constructing a flat phase transition region on a pressure–volume (P–V) diagram, in place of the unphysical oscillatory behaviour predicted by an equation of state such as the van der Waals equation below the critical temperature.

The loop problem

The van der Waals isotherms below the critical temperature contain a "wiggle" in which part of the isotherm has a positive slope (∂P/∂V > 0), implying a negative compressibility — a physical impossibility for a stable homogeneous phase. In reality, the system separates into coexisting liquid and vapour phases at a constant pressure.

Equal area rule

James Clerk Maxwell (1875) showed that the correct tie-line pressure P0 is fixed by requiring that the areas of the two lobes between the isotherm and the horizontal line be equal. Physically, this equality is the condition that the Gibbs free energy (chemical potential) of the liquid and gas phases be equal at coexistence: the work done around the loop vanishes, so the system can pass from one phase to the other without net work.

Application

The construction is applied to any mean-field equation of state that produces a loop, including the van der Waals and Dieterici equations, and conceptually to analogous first-order transitions described by order-parameter free energies. It determines the coexistence (saturation) pressure as a function of temperature and hence the boundaries of the coexistence region in the phase diagram.
