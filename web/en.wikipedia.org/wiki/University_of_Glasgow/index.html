University of Glasgow

The University of Glasgow (Scottish Gaelic: Oilthigh Ghlaschu) is a public research university in Glasgow, Scotland. Founded by papal bull in 1451, it is the fourth-oldest university in the English-speaking world and one of Scotland's four ancient universities. It enrolls around 35,000 students and is a member of the Russell Group.

History

The university was founded in 1451 at the suggestion of King James II by Bishop William Turnbull, with a bull from Pope Nicholas V. It moved in 1870 from the High Street to its present site at Gilmorehill in the West End, where the landmark Gothic revival Gilbert Scott Building was erected. Glasgow was at the heart of the Scottish Enlightenment: Adam Smith studied and later taught there, and alumni include James Watt, Lord Kelvin, and Joseph Lister.

Today

The university is organized into four colleges — Arts, Medical, Veterinary and Life Sciences, Science and Engineering, and Social Sciences. It consistently ranks among the top universities in the United Kingdom, and eight Nobel laureates are associated with it. Its Hunterian Museum, founded in 1807, is Scotland's oldest public museum.
