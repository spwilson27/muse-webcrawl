Benediction of the Blessed Sacrament

[Image placeholder: A priest in a cope, holding a monstrance veiled by a humeral veil, giving Benediction to a kneeling congregation]

Benediction of the Blessed Sacrament, also called Benediction with the Blessed Sacrament or the Exposition of the Blessed Sacrament when followed by adoration, is a Roman Catholic devotional ceremony in which a priest or deacon blesses the congregation with the Eucharist displayed in a monstrance. It is also practised in some Anglican (Anglo-Catholic) and Lutheran churches.

The rite

The ceremony ordinarily begins with the exposition: the consecrated host is placed in a monstrance (ostensorium) on the altar amid candles, and the hymn O salutaris hostia is sung while the Sacrament is incensed. A period of adoration, prayer, readings or litanies may follow. Before the blessing itself, the hymn Tantum ergo — the last two verses of Thomas Aquinas's Pange lingua — is sung, with the versicle Panem de caelo praestitisti eis ("You gave them bread from heaven") and a collect. The celebrant then dons a humeral veil, takes up the monstrance, and makes the sign of the cross over the kneeling congregation in silence, bells being rung. The Divine Praises are commonly recited, and the Sacrament is returned to the tabernacle (reposition), often concluding with Psalm 117 and antiphon.

History

The devotion grew out of the medieval elevation of the host at Mass and the feast of Corpus Christi (instituted 1264), and out of evening services in which a blessing with the Sacrament concluded vespers or compline. It became one of the most familiar Catholic devotions of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, frequently held on Sunday evenings in parish churches. Traditional rules forbade Benediction during a certain period of Holy Week and regulated the rite in detail; the post-conciliar Holy Communion and Worship of the Eucharist Outside Mass (1973) gives the current norms.
