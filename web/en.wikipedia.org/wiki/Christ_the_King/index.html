Christ the King

Christ the King is a title of Jesus in Christianity referring to the idea of the Kingdom of God where Christ is described as being seated at the right hand of God. Many Christian denominations consider the kingship of Christ to be one of the names of Jesus, expressing the belief that Christ rules over all creation.

Origins

The title is derived from several passages of Scripture, including the prophecy in the Book of Daniel, the Magnificat in the Gospel of Luke, and passages in Matthew, John, and Revelation in which Christ is described as king. In Luke 1:32–33, the angel Gabriel announces that God will give Jesus "the throne of his father David" and that "of his kingdom there shall be no end".

Feast of Christ the King

The Solemnity of Our Lord Jesus Christ, King of the Universe, commonly known as the Feast of Christ the King, was instituted by Pope Pius XI in 1925 with the encyclical Quas primas, in response to growing secularism and nationalism after the First World War. In the Roman Catholic Church it is celebrated on the last Sunday of the liturgical year, the Sunday before Advent. The feast is also observed by many mainline Protestant churches, including Anglicans, Lutherans, Methodists, and Presbyterians, and by some Eastern Orthodox communities.

Devotion and monuments

The theme has inspired numerous monumental statues of Christ with outstretched arms, including Cristo Rei in Portugal, Christ the King in Świebodzin, Poland (completed 2010, among the tallest statues of Jesus), and Cristo Rey in Cali, Colombia and in Mexico. Devotion to Christ the King was a rallying cry of the Cristeros during the Cristero War in Mexico in the 1920s, whose battle cry was "¡Viva Cristo Rey!" ("Long live Christ the King!").
