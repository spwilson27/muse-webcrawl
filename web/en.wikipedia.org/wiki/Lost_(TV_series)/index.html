Lost (TV series)

Lost is an American science fiction drama television series created by Jeffrey Lieber, J. J. Abrams, and Damon Lindelof that aired on ABC from September 22, 2004, to May 23, 2010, with a total of 121 episodes over six seasons. It contains elements of supernatural fiction and follows the survivors of Oceanic Flight 815, a commercial passenger jet flying between Sydney and Los Angeles, which crashes on a mysterious tropical island somewhere in the South Pacific Ocean. Episodes typically feature a primary storyline set on the island concurrent with flashback- or flashforward-enhanced sequences providing additional insight into the characters involved.

Overview

The survivors encounter the island's mysterious phenomena — a "Smoke Monster", polar bears, the Dharma Initiative's abandoned stations, and the hostile "Others" — while later seasons introduce time travel and, in the final season, a "flash-sideways" narrative. The pilot episode, among the most expensive ever made at over $14 million, attracted 18.6 million viewers.

Reception and legacy

Lost was consistently praised by critics, won the Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Drama Series in 2005 and the Golden Globe for Best Television Series – Drama in 2006, and is widely cited among the greatest television series of all time, despite continuing debate over its finale, "The End". Its mythology-driven storytelling, large ensemble, and expansive transmedia marketing (alternate reality games, mobisodes, the Lost Experience) influenced subsequent network television and online fan culture.
