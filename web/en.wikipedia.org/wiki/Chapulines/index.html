Chapulines

Chapulines, plural for chapulín (from Nahuatl chapolin), are grasshoppers of the genus Sphenarium that are widely eaten in parts of Mexico, especially in the state of Oaxaca. They are a pre-Hispanic food tradition that continues today.

Preparation

The grasshoppers are collected in alfalfa fields, washed, and toasted on a comal with garlic, lime juice and salt, sometimes with chili, giving a sour, salty and spicy flavour. They are sold in markets, eaten as a snack, and used as a filling for tacos and tlayudas.

Health and safety

Chapulines are rich in protein. Health concerns have been raised about lead contamination in some market samples, attributed to traditional production methods, prompting studies and regulatory attention.
