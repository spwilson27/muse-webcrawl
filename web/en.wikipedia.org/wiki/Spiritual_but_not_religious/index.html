Spiritual but not religious

"Spiritual but not religious" (SBNR), also known as "spiritual but not affiliated" (SBNA), is a popular phrase and initialism used to describe people who identify as having a spirituality or spiritual life without belonging to an organised religion.

Characteristics

SBNR individuals typically value personal experience, meditation, nature, and eclectic practices drawn from various traditions over doctrine, ritual obligation, and institutional membership. The orientation is associated with the rise of the "nones" — the religiously unaffiliated — in Western countries, and with "believing without belonging". Survey research finds SBNR identification especially common among younger adults in the United States and Europe.

Criticism

Religious commentators sometimes criticise SBNR as consumerist or self-indulgent, while sociologists note the diversity of the category: it ranges from committed practitioners of alternative spiritualities to people who simply reject organised religion while retaining belief in God or a higher power.
