Huddersfield Town Association Football Club is a professional association football club based in Huddersfield, West Yorkshire, England. Founded in 1908, the club is nicknamed "the Terriers" and plays its home games at the John Smith's Stadium.

History

Golden years

Under legendary manager Herbert Chapman, Huddersfield won the FA Cup in 1922 and became the first English club to win three successive league championships (1923–24, 1924–25, 1925–26)—a feat matched only by Arsenal, Liverpool, Manchester United, and Manchester City. Chapman left for Arsenal in 1925, but the team remained a power through the decade, finishing runners-up three more times and reaching two further FA Cup finals.

Decline and recovery

After 1956 the club slid down the divisions, bottoming out in the Fourth Division in the 1970s. Following decades in the lower leagues, Huddersfield rose under manager David Wagner: in 2017 they won the Championship play-off final on penalties against Reading to reach the Premier League for the first time, surviving one season before relegation in 2019. They reached the Championship play-off final again in 2022, losing to Nottingham Forest, and were relegated to League One in 2024.

Honours

  • First Division champions: 1923–24, 1924–25, 1925–26
  • FA Cup winners: 1921–22; runners-up: 1920, 1928, 1930, 1938
  • Second Division champions: 1969–70
  • Fourth Division champions: 1979–80
