The former Russian imperial family — the House of Romanov — was executed by Bolshevik revolutionaries under Yakov Yurovsky on the orders of the Ural Regional Soviet in Yekaterinburg on the night of 16–17 July 1918. The victims were the former emperor Nicholas II, his wife Alexandra, their children Olga, Tatiana, Maria, Anastasia, and Alexei, and four loyal retainers: physician Eugene Botkin, maid Anna Demidova, footman Alexei Trupp, and cook Ivan Kharitonov.

Background

After Nicholas II abdicated in March 1917 during the February Revolution, the family was placed under house arrest, first at Tsarskoye Selo, then at Tobolsk in Siberia, and finally, in April–May 1918, at the Ipatiev House in Yekaterinburg, dubbed the "House of Special Purpose". As White forces approached the city during the Russian Civil War, the Ural Soviet resolved to kill the family rather than let them be rescued.

The execution

Around midnight the family was woken and taken to a basement room under the pretext of a move for their safety. Yurovsky read the death sentence, and the firing squad opened fire. The shooting was chaotic; bullets ricocheted off jewels sewn into the children's clothing, and several victims were finished off with bayonets and further shots. The bodies were stripped, mutilated, doused with acid, and buried in the Koptyaki forest; two bodies (later identified as Alexei and one of his sisters) were burned and buried separately.

Aftermath and identification

The main grave was located in 1979 by Alexander Avdonin and Geli Ryabov and excavated officially in 1991; DNA analysis confirmed the identities of Nicholas, Alexandra, and three daughters. The second grave, found in 2007, yielded the remains of Alexei and Maria, confirmed by DNA testing in 2008–2009. The family was interred in St Catherine's Chapel of the Peter and Paul Cathedral in St Petersburg on 17 July 1998. Nicholas, Alexandra, and their children were canonised as passion bearers by the Russian Orthodox Church in 2000. In 2015 the Russian Investigative Committee reopened the case, and the remains were exhumed for further study.
