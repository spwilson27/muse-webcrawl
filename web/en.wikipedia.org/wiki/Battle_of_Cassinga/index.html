Battle of Cassinga

The Battle of Cassinga, also known as the Cassinga raid or Cassinga massacre, was a controversial South African airborne assault on 4 May 1978 on a camp at Cassinga in southern Angola, carried out as part of Operation Reindeer during the South African Border War. South Africa maintained that Cassinga was a military headquarters and transit camp of SWAPO's armed wing, PLAN; SWAPO and its supporters maintained it was principally a refugee camp. Hundreds of people were killed, and the event remains bitterly disputed.

Paratroops descending on Cassinga, 4 May 1978.

The operation

Operation Reindeer struck several targets inside Angola simultaneously. At Cassinga, some 370 South African paratroopers were dropped after an air strike by Canberra and Buccaneer aircraft on a camp roughly 250 kilometres inside Angola. The paratroopers fought a day-long battle, also engaging Angolan and Cuban reinforcements, including tanks, that approached from a nearby base, before being extracted by helicopter. South African losses were light (four killed); Angolan, Cuban and Namibian deaths at Cassinga are estimated at around 600 or more.

Controversy

The status of the camp is the central dispute. The South African Defence Force presented captured documents and photographs of trenches, weapons and military infrastructure, and later South African investigations described Cassinga as a military base. SWAPO described a refugee transit camp, and survivors testified to mass killings of civilians; mass graves were shown to visiting journalists. The UN Security Council condemned the raid in Resolution 428. In Namibia, 4 May is commemorated as Cassinga Day, a public holiday honouring those killed.
