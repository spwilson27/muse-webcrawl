Sparkasse (Germany)

Sparkassen are public savings banks in Germany, operated by local authorities (cities, districts, or municipal associations) as institutions under public law. Together with the Landesbanken and associated institutions they form the Sparkassen-Finanzgruppe, one of the largest financial groups in Europe, coordinated by the Deutscher Sparkassen- und Giroverband (DSGV).

Characteristics

There are roughly 350 Sparkassen. Under the regional principle (Regionalprinzip), each confines its business largely to its owner's territory. Sparkassen have a public mandate to provide banking services to the whole population, promote savings, and lend to local small and medium-sized enterprises. They are not profit-maximizing: surpluses are often used for reserves or community purposes.

History

The first savings banks appeared in the late 18th century (Hamburg 1778, Göttingen 1801); the first municipal Sparkasse opened in 1818 in Berlin. Their deposit guarantee and institutional protection scheme, jointly operated with the Landesbanken, historically rested on guarantor liability by their public owners, which was phased out by 2005 following European Commission pressure.
