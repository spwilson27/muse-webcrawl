Monooxygenase

Monooxygenases are enzymes that incorporate one hydroxyl group into substrates in many metabolic pathways. In this reaction, the two atoms of dioxygen are reduced to one hydroxyl group and one H2O molecule by the concomitant oxidation of NAD(P)H. Monooxygenases are classified as EC 1.14 in the Enzyme Commission numbering scheme and are also called mixed-function oxidases or hydroxylases.

Families

Important families of monooxygenases include the cytochrome P450 enzymes, a large superfamily of haem-containing proteins involved in the metabolism of drugs, steroids, fatty acids and xenobiotics; the flavin-containing monooxygenases (FMOs), which oxidize soft nucleophiles such as amines and sulfides; and copper- and iron-dependent enzymes such as dopamine β-monooxygenase and tyrosinase.

Function

Monooxygenases participate in catabolism, biosynthesis of hormones and signalling molecules, and detoxification. In humans, cytochrome P450 monooxygenases in the liver are responsible for the oxidative metabolism of most clinically used drugs, making them central to pharmacology and toxicology. Defects in monooxygenase genes can cause metabolic diseases, such as congenital adrenal hyperplasia from steroid 21-hydroxylase deficiency.
