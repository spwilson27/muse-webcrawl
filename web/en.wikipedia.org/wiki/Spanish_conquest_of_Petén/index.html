Spanish conquest of Petén

The Spanish conquest of Petén was the campaign by Spanish conquistadores to conquer the independent Maya polities of the Petén Basin, in modern Guatemala — the last part of the Maya region to fall, with the Itza capital Nojpetén captured only in 1697.

Background

Hernán Cortés crossed Petén in 1525 and met the Itza king Kan Ek', but dense jungle and distance frustrated conquest for over 150 years. Franciscan missions and military entradas repeatedly failed against the Itza, Kowoj and other groups, who maintained trade and ritual networks.

Final campaign

In the 1690s Spain built a road from Yucatán and a fort near Lake Petén Itzá. Martín de Ursúa launched an amphibious assault with a galeota (armed boat) and took Nojpetén on 13 March 1697 after fierce fighting. Kowoj resistance continued for some years.

Aftermath

The fall of Nojpetén ended the last independent Native American kingdom in Mesoamerica and opened Petén to colonial settlement, though the region remained sparsely populated.
