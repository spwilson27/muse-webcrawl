Cosmos: Possible Worlds

Cosmos: Possible Worlds is a 2020 American science documentary television series, a follow-up to the 2014 series Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey and to the original 1980 series Cosmos: A Personal Voyage. Hosted by astrophysicist Neil deGrasse Tyson and written, directed and executive-produced by Ann Druyan, the thirteen-episode series premiered in March 2020 on the National Geographic channel, and aired on Fox later that year.

Content

Carrying forward the "Spaceship of the Imagination" device, the series explores possible future worlds for humanity — space colonisation, terraforming, and the search for extraterrestrial life — along with the history of scientific ideas, from the deep past of Earth to the fate of the universe. Episodes include "Ladder to the Stars", "The Fleeting Grace of the Habitable Zone", "Lost City of Life", "The Cosmic Connectome" and "A Tale of Two Atoms". Druyan's companion book, Cosmos: Possible Worlds, accompanied the series.

Reception

The series received generally positive reviews for its visual effects and optimism, and was nominated for a Primetime Emmy Award.
