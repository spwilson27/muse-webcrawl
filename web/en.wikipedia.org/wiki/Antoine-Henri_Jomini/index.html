Antoine-Henri Jomini

Born: 6 March 1779, Payerne, Switzerland
Died: 22 March 1869, Paris, France
Known for: Military theory; The Art of War (1838)

Antoine-Henri, Baron Jomini (6 March 1779 – 22 March 1869) was a Swiss military officer who served as a general in the French and later in the Russian service, and one of the most celebrated writers on the Napoleonic art of war. Jomini's works, most notably his Précis de l'Art de la Guerre (The Art of War, 1838), shaped military education in the nineteenth century and influenced generations of officers, particularly during the American Civil War.

Life and career

Born in Payerne in the canton of Vaud, Jomini entered military service during the French Revolutionary Wars and rose rapidly under the patronage of Michel Ney, whose chief of staff he became. He served in the campaigns of 1805–1807 and wrote his early Traité des grandes opérations militaires while on campaign. Falling out with Napoleon's chief of staff Berthier, he transferred to Russian service in 1813, eventually becoming a full general and a close adviser to Tsars Alexander I and Nicholas I. He helped found the Russian military academy in St Petersburg.

Theory

Jomini sought to reduce strategy to a set of enduring principles derived from the study of Frederick the Great and Napoleon. His central concept was the decisive concentration of superior force against a fraction of the enemy's force at the decisive point, achieved by manoeuvring along interior lines. He emphasised lines of operation, strategic points, and the geometrical structure of theatres of war. His work is often contrasted with that of Carl von Clausewitz: where Clausewitz stressed the philosophical and political nature of war, Jomini offered a prescriptive, systematic doctrine, and for much of the nineteenth century Jomini's influence on staff colleges exceeded Clausewitz's.
