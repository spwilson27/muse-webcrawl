Green–Tao theorem

FieldNumber theory, combinatorics
Proved byBen Green, Terence Tao
Year2004
StatementThe primes contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions

The Green–Tao theorem, proved by Ben Green and Terence Tao in 2004, states that the sequence of prime numbers contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. In other words, for any natural number k, there exist infinitely many arithmetic progressions of primes with k terms. The proof is an extension of Szemerédi's theorem to the set of primes.

Background

In 1770, Lagrange and Waring investigated how many primes are needed to represent every integer as a sum of squares; interest in additive questions about primes goes back much further. Van der Waerden's theorem (1927) and Szemerédi's theorem (1975) showed that any sufficiently dense set of integers contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions, but the primes have density zero, so those results do not apply directly. Erdős and Turán conjectured in 1936 that any set of integers of positive upper density contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions; the prime case was a major open problem.

Proof outline

Green and Tao showed that the primes can be embedded in a "pseudorandom" larger set that has positive relative density, and proved a relative version of Szemerédi's theorem. They later strengthened the result, with Tamar Ziegler, to handle general polynomial progressions.

Extensions

In 2006 Green and Tao extended the method to show that if a subset of the primes has positive relative upper density, it too contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. Work on the Gaussian primes and on polynomial patterns followed, culminating in the Green–Tao–Ziegler theorem on linear equations in primes.
