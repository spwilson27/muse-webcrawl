Progressive trance is a subgenre of trance music that emerged in the early 1990s, particularly in the United Kingdom. It is characterised by long tracks that build gradually, adding and removing layers over several minutes, with restrained melodies, atmospheric pads, and a deeper, less euphoric feel than mainstream or uplifting trance. Tracks typically run at around 130–135 beats per minute.

History

Early progressive trance drew on progressive house and European techno. Labels such as Guerilla Records and artists including Leftfield, Spooky, and John Digweed and Sasha helped define the sound in the early-to-mid 1990s; Sasha and Digweed's Northern Exposure mixes (1996) are widely cited landmarks. In Germany, producers such as Oliver Lieb also shaped the genre's hypnotic, minimal style.

Characteristics

Compared to uplifting trance, progressive trance uses subtler breakdowns, fewer anthemic supersaw leads, and longer, DJ-friendly arrangements designed for mixing. In the 2000s and 2010s the term was also applied commercially to a big-room-influenced style by some artists, leading to debate among fans about the genre's definition.
