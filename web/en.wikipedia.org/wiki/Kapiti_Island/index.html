Kapiti Island

Kapiti Island is an island about 5 kilometres (3 miles) off the west coast of the lower North Island of New Zealand, in the Tasman Sea. About 10 kilometres long and 2 kilometres wide, it rises to 521 metres at Tuteremoana. The island is one of New Zealand's most important nature reserves, administered by the Department of Conservation.

History

The island has a long Māori history and was a stronghold of the Ngāti Toa chief Te Rauparaha in the 1820s and 1830s, from which he directed campaigns across Cook Strait. Whaling stations operated there in the 1830s and 1840s. Recognising its conservation potential, the government acquired most of the island as a bird sanctuary in 1897, making it one of the world's earliest fully protected nature reserves.

Conservation

Possums, rats and other introduced pests were progressively eradicated, culminating in rat eradication in the 1990s, allowing the forest to regenerate. The island is now a sanctuary for rare birds including little spotted kiwi, kōkako, stitchbird (hihi), takahē and saddleback, many translocated there as insurance populations. Public access is strictly limited by permit, and the surrounding waters form the Kapiti Marine Reserve.
