Khuddaka Nikaya

The Khuddaka Nikāya (Pāli for "minor collection") is the fifth and last of the nikāyas, or collections, in the Sutta Piṭaka of the Pāli Canon, the scriptures of Theravāda Buddhism. Unlike the other four nikāyas, it is a heterogeneous miscellany of prose and verse works in varied styles, attributed both to the Buddha and to his disciples.

Contents

The collection contains some of the best-known Buddhist texts, including the Dhammapada (verses on the Dhamma), the Udāna, the Itivuttaka, the Sutta Nipāta (among the earliest Buddhist poetry), the Theragāthā and Therīgāthā (verses of elder monks and nuns), the Jātaka tales of the Buddha's past lives, and the Paṭisambhidāmagga. The Burmese edition of the canon includes additional books such as the Milindapañha.

Significance

Because of its variety, scholars regard parts of the Khuddaka Nikāya — especially the Sutta Nipāta and the verse collections of elders — as preserving some of the earliest strata of Buddhist literature.
