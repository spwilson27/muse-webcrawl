E. F. Benson

Born: Edward Frederic Benson, 24 July 1867, Wellington College, Berkshire
Died: 29 February 1940 (aged 72), London
Occupation: Novelist
Notable works: Mapp and Lucia series; Dodo

Edward Frederic Benson (24 July 1867 – 29 February 1940) was an English novelist, biographer, memoirist, historian and short story writer, known professionally as E. F. Benson. He is best remembered for the Mapp and Lucia novels, a series of sharp social satires set in the fictional town of Tilling, based on Rye, East Sussex, where Benson lived for many years and served as mayor.

Life

Benson was born at Wellington College, where his father, Edward White Benson, was headmaster; his father later became Archbishop of Canterbury. His mother Mary (Sidgwick) Benson was a noted figure in her own right, and Benson's siblings included the novelist A. C. Benson, the Egyptologist Margaret Benson, and the Catholic priest and novelist Robert Hugh Benson — the family was the subject of his memoir Our Family Affairs and later biographies. Educated at Marlborough College and King's College, Cambridge, Benson worked briefly in archaeology in Greece before the success of his first novel, Dodo (1893), a succès de scandale based partly on Margot Asquith, launched his literary career.

The Mapp and Lucia novels

The six novels centre on two formidable social rivals: Emmeline "Lucia" Lucas, a culture-climbing widow, and Elizabeth Mapp, a sharp-elbowed spinster. The series comprises Queen Lucia (1920), Miss Mapp (1922), Lucia in London (1927), Mapp and Lucia (1931), Lucia's Progress (1935), and Trouble for Lucia (1939). The books have been repeatedly adapted for television, notably by Channel 4 in 1985 (starring Geraldine McEwan and Prunella Scales) and by the BBC in 2014.

Other work

Benson was extraordinarily prolific, publishing some 93 books: society novels, volumes on his family, biographies (including works on Queen Victoria's daughters and Charlotte Brontë), histories, and a celebrated series of ghost stories, among them "The Room in the Tower" and "Mrs Amworth", much admired by M. R. James and H. P. Lovecraft. He was a keen figure skater and wrote a book on the sport. Benson, who never married, died of throat cancer in London in 1940.
