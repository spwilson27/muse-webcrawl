Planning permission in the United Kingdom

A site notice for a planning application

Planning permission in the United Kingdom is the planning permission required in the United Kingdom in order to be allowed to build on land, or change the use of land or buildings. Within the UK, an owner of land or a building will need planning permission before carrying out most forms of "development", as defined in the Town and Country Planning Act 1990.

History

The modern system originates in the Town and Country Planning Act 1947, which effectively nationalised development rights: since 1948, the right to develop land has been separated from ownership of the land itself, and most development requires express consent from the local planning authority. Scotland and Northern Ireland operate under their own, similar legislation.

The process

Applications are normally decided by the local planning authority – the district, borough, or unitary council – in accordance with the development plan for the area, unless material considerations indicate otherwise. Certain minor developments benefit from permitted development rights granted nationally and do not need an application. Larger or controversial applications may be decided by a planning committee of elected councillors rather than by officers under delegated powers.

Decisions can be appealed to the Planning Inspectorate in England (and equivalent bodies elsewhere in the UK), and an inspector may allow or dismiss the appeal after written representations, a hearing, or a public inquiry. Enforcement action may be taken against development carried out without permission.

Types of permission

Outline planning permission establishes the principle of development, with details ("reserved matters") approved later. Full planning permission grants consent for a fully specified scheme. Permission is also required for works affecting listed buildings (listed building consent) and for advertisements, trees protected by preservation orders, and development in conservation areas under additional controls.
