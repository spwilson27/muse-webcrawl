The Texas State Capitol is the seat of government of the US state of Texas, located in downtown Austin. Completed in 1888 to the design of architect Elijah E. Myers (who also designed the Michigan and Colorado capitols), it is built of distinctive "sunset red" granite quarried at Granite Mountain near Marble Falls. At 302.64 feet (92.2 m) tall, it stands about 14.6 feet (4.5 m) taller than the United States Capitol in Washington.

History

The present building is the third capitol on or near the site. Construction was financed through the famous XIT Ranch land deal: the state traded three million acres of Panhandle public land to a Chicago syndicate (the Capitol Freehold Land and Investment Company) in return for building the capitol. Convict labour and imported Scottish stonemasons were used in construction. The building was dedicated in May 1888, with the zinc statue of the Goddess of Liberty placed atop the dome. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1986.

The building

The capitol houses the chambers of the Texas Legislature (the Senate and House of Representatives) and the office of the Governor of Texas. The rotunda displays portraits of former governors and presidents of the Republic of Texas; the floor seals commemorate the "six flags" of Texas. A fire in 1983, which killed one person, prompted a major restoration. To preserve the historic building and grounds, a vast four-level underground extension was completed in 1993, adding offices, committee rooms and parking while leaving the surface appearance largely unchanged; more recent projects have included the Texas Capitol Mall.

Grounds

The 22-acre grounds, landscaped from designs by William Munro Johnson, contain monuments including the Heroes of the Alamo Monument, the Terry's Texas Rangers Monument, and the controversial Confederate monuments whose retention or removal has been debated in recent decades.
