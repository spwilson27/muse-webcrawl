Casemate

A casemate is a fortified gun emplacement or armoured structure from which guns are fired, protected by walls and a roof. The term is used both for vaulted chambers within land fortifications and for fixed gun positions in warships and armoured vehicles.

Fortifications

In fortress design, casemates were bomb-proof vaulted rooms built into ramparts, housing artillery that fired through embrasures, or serving as barracks and stores protected from shellfire. They became central to the polygonal and detached-fort systems of the 19th century, and to 20th-century works such as the Maginot Line, whose artillery blocks fought from steel-clad casemates.

Naval use

On warships, casemates were armoured enclosures in the ship's side for medium-calibre guns, common in pre-dreadnought and early dreadnought battleships. They offered protection but shipped water in heavy seas and limited arcs of fire, and were largely superseded by turrets. In armoured fighting vehicles, a casemate denotes a fixed superstructure mounting a gun with limited traverse, as in many Second World War tank destroyers and assault guns such as the StuG III.
