Goddess movement

The Goddess movement is a spiritual movement that emerged in the 1970s, primarily in North America and Western Europe, venerating a female deity or goddess and drawing on feminist, Neopagan and earth-centred spirituality.

Goddess movement
Emergence: 1970s, second-wave feminism
Key figures: Zsuzsanna Budapest, Starhawk, Carol P. Christ
Related: Dianic Wicca, feminist spirituality

Beliefs and history

Drawing on ideas of ancient matriarchal goddess-worship popularised by archaeologist Marija Gimbutas, practitioners celebrate the divine feminine, seasonal cycles and nature. Zsuzsanna Budapest founded Dianic Wicca in 1971; Starhawk's The Spiral Dance (1979) became a central text. Thealogian Carol P. Christ argued for the Goddess as a symbol of female empowerment.

Criticism

Scholars debate the historical evidence for a universal prehistoric goddess religion, and the movement has been discussed alongside barriers like the glass ceiling that shaped its feminist context.
