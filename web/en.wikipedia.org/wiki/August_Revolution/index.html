August Revolution

The August Revolution (Vietnamese: Cách mạng tháng Tám), also known as the August General Uprising, was a revolution led by the Việt Minh against French colonial rule and the Japanese-backed Empire of Vietnam in August 1945. Within about two weeks, Việt Minh committees seized power in most of Vietnam's cities and towns, culminating in the abdication of Emperor Bảo Đại and Hồ Chí Minh's proclamation of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in Hanoi on 2 September 1945.

Background

France had ruled Vietnam as part of French Indochina since the late 19th century. In 1940 Japan stationed forces in Indochina while leaving the French administration in place; on 9 March 1945 Japan overthrew the French and proclaimed the Empire of Vietnam under Bảo Đại. The Việt Minh, the independence front founded in 1941 and dominated by the Indochinese Communist Party, built up guerrilla forces and village-level organisation, especially during the famine of 1944–45 in the north. Japan's surrender in mid-August 1945 created a power vacuum.

The uprising

On 13–15 August the Việt Minh held a national congress at Tân Trào and ordered a general uprising. Hanoi rose on 19 August, Hue on 23 August and Saigon on 25 August; local committees seized public buildings across the country, in many places with little resistance. Bảo Đại abdicated on 30 August, handing the seal and sword to representatives of the provisional government. On 2 September 1945 Hồ Chí Minh read the Declaration of Independence in Ba Đình Square, Hanoi, proclaiming the Democratic Republic of Vietnam.

Aftermath

The revolution did not settle Vietnam's future: British and Chinese forces occupied the south and north respectively to disarm the Japanese, France returned to reassert colonial control, and in December 1946 the conflict became the First Indochina War.
