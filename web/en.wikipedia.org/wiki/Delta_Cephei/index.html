Delta Cephei

Delta Cephei (δ Cephei, abbreviated Delta Cep, δ Cep) is a multiple star system about 887 light-years away in the northern constellation of Cepheus. Its primary is a yellow supergiant that is the prototype of the Cepheid variable stars — pulsating stars whose period–luminosity relation, discovered by Henrietta Swan Leavitt, makes them fundamental "standard candles" for measuring cosmic distances.

Variability

The variability of Delta Cephei was discovered by John Goodricke in 1784. The star's apparent magnitude varies between about 3.5 and 4.4 over a period of 5.366 days, rising rapidly to maximum and declining more slowly. The pulsation is driven by the kappa mechanism: changes in the opacity of ionised helium in the star's outer layers alternately trap and release heat, causing the star to expand and contract and its spectral type to shift between about F5 and G3.

System

Delta Cephei is accompanied by a blue main-sequence companion (Delta Cephei B) and is part of a wider multiple system. Its well-determined distance, measured precisely by the Hubble Space Telescope's Fine Guidance Sensors and by Gaia, anchors the Cepheid distance scale used to calibrate the distances of galaxies.
