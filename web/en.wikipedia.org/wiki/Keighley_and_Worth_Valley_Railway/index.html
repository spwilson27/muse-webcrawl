Keighley and Worth Valley Railway

Keighley & Worth Valley Railway
Location: West Yorkshire, England
Length: 5 miles (8 km)
Termini: Keighley – Oxenhope
Opened: 1867; preserved from 1968
Stations: Keighley, Ingrow West, Damems, Oakworth, Haworth, Oxenhope

The Keighley and Worth Valley Railway (KWVR) is a heritage railway in West Yorkshire, England, running from Keighley up the Worth Valley to Oxenhope. Opened in 1867 as a branch of the Midland Railway to serve the valley's textile mills, it closed under British Railways in 1962 but was bought and reopened by a preservation society in 1968 — one of the earliest preserved railways in Britain.

History

The line was built to serve the woollen mills of the Worth Valley and carried both freight and passengers. Threatened with closure, local campaigners formed a preservation society; after years of negotiation they purchased the line from British Railways and reopened it in June 1968. The railway has since been operated largely by volunteers.

The railway today

The KWVR runs steam and diesel trains through six stations, including Oakworth — famous as the station in the 1970 film The Railway Children — and Haworth, associated with the Brontë family. Ingrow West hosts the Vintage Carriages Trust's Museum of Rail Travel and the Bahamas Locomotive Society's Ingrow Loco centre. The line also connects with national rail services at Keighley.
