Law review

A law review (or law journal) is a scholarly journal devoted to legal scholarship, typically published by law students at a law school in the United States, Canada and some other countries. Law reviews publish long-form articles by law professors, judges and practitioners, as well as shorter notes and comments written by student editors.

Unlike most academic fields, US law reviews are student-edited rather than peer-reviewed; membership is a prestigious credential, usually earned through grades and a writing competition. Prominent examples include the Harvard Law Review (founded 1887), the Yale Law Journal and the Columbia Law Review.

Law review articles follow The Bluebook citation style and are frequently cited by courts, including the US Supreme Court.
