Mother (stylised as MOTHER, and released outside Japan as EarthBound Beginnings) is a role-playing video game developed by Ape Inc. and published by Nintendo for the Family Computer. It was written and designed by Japanese copywriter Shigesato Itoi and released in Japan on 27 July 1989. It is the first entry in the Mother series, followed by Mother 2 (1994, released in the West as EarthBound) and Mother 3 (2006).

Gameplay and story

Unlike most role-playing games of its era, which used fantasy settings, Mother is set in a contemporary (1980s) United States. The player controls a young boy, Ninten, who develops psychic powers (PSI) and sets out from his home town of Podunk to investigate strange occurrences — poltergeists, rampaging animals, and a sinister alien presence. He is joined by Lloyd, a bullied boy genius; Ana, a young psychic from Snowman; and Teddy, the leader of a street gang. The story is tied to Ninten's great-grandparents, George and Maria, who were abducted by aliens decades earlier; the final confrontation is with the alien Giygas (Giegue), whose enmity is bound up with Maria's memory of raising him. Its final battle can be won by having Ana's lullaby — assembled from eight melodies scattered through the game — sung to Giygas.

Development and release

Itoi pitched the idea of a modern-day RPG to Nintendo president Hiroshi Yamauchi, and Shigeru Miyamoto championed the project. The score, by Keiichi Suzuki and Hirokazu Tanaka, was also released as a studio album featuring vocal arrangements. A complete English localisation under the name Earth Bound was finished in 1990 but shelved because the NES market was ending; a prototype cartridge later changed hands among collectors, and its ROM was preserved. The game was finally released in the West in 2015, as EarthBound Beginnings for the Wii U Virtual Console, 26 years after its Japanese debut.

Reception and legacy

Though its high random-encounter rate and difficulty curve were criticised, Mother sold about 400,000 copies in Japan and is remembered for its emotional writing, humour, and unconventional setting. It founded one of Nintendo's most cult-followed series and introduced motifs — ordinary children against cosmic horror, melodies as plot devices — that its sequels expanded.
