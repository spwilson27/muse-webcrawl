Indigenous peoples in Brazil

Total population: about 1.7 million (2022 census)

Languages: some 270 indigenous languages

Agency: FUNAI (National Indigenous People Foundation)

The Indigenous peoples in Brazil are the original inhabitants of the territory of modern Brazil, comprising hundreds of distinct peoples speaking languages from many families, including Tupian, Macro-Jê, Arawakan, and Cariban.

History

Before Portuguese colonization began in 1500, the indigenous population is estimated in the millions. Colonization brought disease, enslavement, and displacement that devastated many peoples, though hundreds of groups survived, including some that remain in voluntary isolation in the Amazon.

Rights and lands

The 1988 Constitution of Brazil recognizes indigenous peoples' original rights to their traditional lands. Demarcated Indigenous Territories cover roughly 13 percent of Brazil's land area, mostly in the Amazon, and play a major role in forest conservation.

Contemporary issues

Indigenous communities continue to face pressure from illegal mining, logging, land grabbing, and debates over the "time frame" thesis (marco temporal) limiting land claims.
