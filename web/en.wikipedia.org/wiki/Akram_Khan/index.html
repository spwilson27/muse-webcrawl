Akram Khan

Akram Khan MBE (born 29 July 1974) is an English dancer and choreographer of Bangladeshi descent. Trained in the classical Indian dance form of kathak as well as in contemporary dance, he has been one of the most prominent figures in British dance since the early 2000s, known for collaborations across disciplines and cultures and for works blending kathak with modern dance. He founded the Akram Khan Company in 2000.

Early life and training

Khan was born in London to a Bangladeshi family who ran a restaurant in Wimbledon. He began learning kathak at the age of seven under the guru Pratap Pawar, and as a teenager appeared in Peter Brook's celebrated stage production of the Mahabharata (1987–89), touring the world with the company. He later studied contemporary dance at De Montfort University and the Northern School of Contemporary Dance.

Career

In the 1990s Khan established himself as a kathak soloist of exceptional speed and precision, then began fusing the form with contemporary dance. With producer Farooq Chaudhry he founded his own company in 2000; early works such as Kaash (2002, with artist Anish Kapoor and composer Nitin Sawhney) brought international attention. His career is marked by high-profile collaborations: Sacred Monsters (2006) with ballerina Sylvie Guillem, zero degrees (2005) with Sidi Larbi Cherkaoui, Antony Gormley, and Nitin Sawhney, In-i (2008) with actress Juliette Binoche, and Vertical Road (2010). His full-length solo DESH (2011), about Bangladesh and his father's homeland, won the Olivier Award for Best New Dance Production. In 2012 he choreographed and performed a section of the London Olympic Games opening ceremony.

Later work

Khan created a radical reimagining of Giselle for English National Ballet in 2016, relocating the story among migrant garment-factory workers; it was widely acclaimed and filmed for cinema release. Subsequent works include Until the Lions (2016), XENOS (2018) — his final solo performance piece, about Indian soldiers in the First World War — and Outwitting the Devil (2019). He announced his retirement from full-length solo performance after XENOS but continues to choreograph for his company and others. He was appointed MBE in 2005 for services to dance.
