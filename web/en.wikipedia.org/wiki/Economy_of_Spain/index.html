Economy of Spain

The economy of Spain is a highly developed social market economy. It is the world's fifteenth-largest economy by nominal GDP and one of the largest in the European Union, where it ranks fourth behind Germany, France and Italy. Spain joined the European Economic Community in 1986 and was a founding member of the eurozone in 1999.

History

Under the autarkic policies of the Franco era, Spain remained largely isolated until the Stabilization Plan of 1959 opened the economy to foreign trade and investment. The result was the so-called "Spanish miracle" of the 1960s, a period of rapid industrialisation and urbanisation. Following the transition to democracy and accession to the European Community, Spain modernised its infrastructure with the help of EU structural funds and became a major recipient of foreign investment.

The adoption of the euro and low interest rates fuelled a housing and construction boom in the 2000s. The bust that followed the global financial crisis of 2008 pushed unemployment above 26 percent in 2013 and forced a bank bailout in 2012. A painful internal devaluation, labour-market reform and export growth brought the economy back to growth from 2014, though public debt remained above 100 percent of GDP. The COVID-19 pandemic hit Spain especially hard because of its dependence on tourism; GDP fell by roughly 11 percent in 2020 before recovering strongly.

Sectors

Services and tourism

Services account for roughly two-thirds of GDP. Spain is consistently among the world's most visited countries, receiving over 80 million international tourists annually in strong years. Tourism contributes around 12 percent of GDP and a similar share of employment, concentrated in Catalonia, the Balearic Islands, the Canary Islands, Andalusia and the Valencian Community.

Industry

Spain is one of Europe's largest automobile producers, hosting plants of SEAT (Volkswagen Group), Stellantis, Renault, Ford and Mercedes-Benz. Other significant industries include chemicals, pharmaceuticals, shipbuilding, steel and renewable-energy equipment; Spain has been a pioneer in wind power, with companies such as Iberdrola and Acciona prominent internationally.

Agriculture

Spain is the world's largest producer of olive oil and one of the largest exporters of wine, citrus fruit and vegetables. The greenhouses of Almería supply much of Europe's winter produce. Agricultural water use, especially in the drought-prone south-east, is a recurring political issue.

Regional economies

Income per capita varies widely. Madrid, the Basque Country, Navarre and Catalonia are the most prosperous regions, while Extremadura, Andalusia and the Canary Islands lag behind. Catalonia and Madrid together account for well over a third of national GDP.
