Powering Past Coal Alliance

The Powering Past Coal Alliance (PPCA) is a coalition of national and sub-national governments, businesses and organisations working to advance the transition from unabated coal power generation to clean energy. It was launched by the governments of Canada and the United Kingdom at the COP23 climate summit in Bonn, Germany, in November 2017.

Declaration

Members of the alliance sign a declaration committing them to phasing out existing traditional coal power, placing a moratorium on new traditional coal stations without carbon capture and storage, investing in clean energy, and restricting financing for traditional coal power without CCS. The founding declaration set the goal of phasing out unabated coal in OECD and EU countries by no later than 2030, and in the rest of the world by no later than 2050.

Membership

The alliance began with a small group of countries including Canada, the United Kingdom, France, Finland, the Netherlands, Mexico and New Zealand, together with provinces, states and cities and a number of companies and non-governmental organisations. Membership subsequently grew to include more than one hundred governments and organisations. At COP26 in Glasgow in 2021, further countries joined and the alliance announced new finance commitments supporting coal phase-out in developing economies.

Activities

The alliance works with partners such as Bloomberg Philanthropies and energy-transition research groups to share policy experience, support just-transition planning for coal-dependent communities, and promote the economic case that solar and wind are now cheaper than coal generation in most markets.
