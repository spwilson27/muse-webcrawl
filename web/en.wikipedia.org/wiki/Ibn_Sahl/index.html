Ibn Sahl (c. 940–1000) was a Persian mathematician and physicist of the Islamic Golden Age, associated with the Buyid court in Baghdad. He is known for his treatise On Burning Mirrors and Lenses (984), in which he set out the earliest known formulation of the law of refraction — the relationship later known in Europe as Snell's law — more than six centuries before Willebrord Snellius.

In his treatise Ibn Sahl analysed how curved mirrors and lenses focus light, deriving the shapes of plano-convex and biconvex lenses (anaclastic lenses) that refract rays to a focus without spherical aberration. His geometric construction of a refracted ray implies the constant ratio of sines that defines the refractive index.

Legacy

Ibn Sahl's manuscript survived only in fragments and was reconstructed by the historian Roshdi Rashed in the 1990s. His work influenced Ibn al-Haytham, whose Book of Optics cites him, and through that tradition the development of geometrical optics.
