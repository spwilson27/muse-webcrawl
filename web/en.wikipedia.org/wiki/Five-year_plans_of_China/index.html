Five-year plans of China

The Five-Year Plans of China are a series of social and economic development plans initiated by the Chinese Communist Party since 1953. Modelled on Soviet planning, the plans set targets and priorities for industry, agriculture, infrastructure and (increasingly) social and environmental goals. They are drafted by the National Development and Reform Commission and approved by the National People's Congress.

History

The First Five-Year Plan (1953–1957) concentrated on heavy industry, with Soviet assistance funding 156 major industrial projects; steel, coal, machine-building and defence industries were established in the northeast and interior. The Second Plan (1958–1962) coincided with the Great Leap Forward, which disrupted planning and contributed to a severe famine. Later plans restored more balanced growth.

After the reform and opening up begun in 1978, the plans became more indicative than mandatory in a marketising economy. The Seventh Plan (1986–1990) was the first to include social development in a comprehensive way. Reflecting the changed role of planning, the Eleventh programme (2006–2010) was officially called a "guideline" (guihua) rather than a "plan" (jihua) in Chinese.

Recent plans

The Twelfth Five-Year Plan (2011–2015) emphasised rebalancing toward domestic consumption, services and "inclusive growth". The Thirteenth (2016–2020) targeted moderate growth, innovation and the elimination of extreme poverty. The Fourteenth Five-Year Plan (2021–2025) introduced the strategy of "dual circulation", stressing technological self-reliance, green development and common prosperity, alongside long-range objectives for 2035.
