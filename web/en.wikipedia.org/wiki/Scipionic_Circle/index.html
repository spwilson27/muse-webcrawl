The Scipionic Circle was a group of philosophers, poets, and politicians in the Roman Republic around Scipio Aemilianus, the conqueror of Carthage in 146 BC. The group is credited with fostering Hellenistic culture in Rome and developing Roman humanism.

Members

Associated figures included the Greek historian Polybius, the Stoic philosopher Panaetius of Rhodes, the satirist Lucilius, and the playwright Terence. Scipio's close friend Gaius Laelius was also central. Earlier tradition linked Scipio Africanus, victor of the Second Punic War, with a similar circle, though modern scholars debate the distinction.

Historical debate

The term derives largely from Cicero's dialogues De re publica and De amicitia, which are set among Scipio's friends. Modern historians question whether a formal, self-conscious circle existed, or whether Cicero idealized a looser network of patronage and friendship for philosophical purposes.

Influence

Through Panaetius, Stoicism was adapted to Roman values. The circle promoted Greek literature, rhetoric, and philosophy while defending Roman tradition, shaping the cultural outlook of the late Republic.
