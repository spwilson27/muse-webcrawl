Béarnaise sauce

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A silver boat of glossy béarnaise sauce flecked with tarragon beside a sliced steak]
A silver boat of glossy béarnaise sauce flecked with tarragon beside a sliced steak

Béarnaise sauce is a sauce made of clarified butter emulsified in egg yolks and white wine vinegar, flavoured with Tarragon and shallots. It is a 'daughter' of Hollandaise — one of the five Mother sauces derivatives of classical French cuisine — differing by its reduction of vinegar, shallot, chervil and tarragon. Created in the 1830s at the restaurant Le Pavillon Henri IV in Saint-Germain-en-Laye, it is named, by tradition, for the Béarn, birthplace of Henry IV of France.

Preparation

A reduction of vinegar, wine, shallots, tarragon stems and peppercorns is whisked into yolks over heat, then mounted with clarified butter and finished with chopped tarragon and chervil. The emulsion breaks if overheated and is classically served warm with grilled steak (the steak frites–béarnaise is a Parisian bistro institution), eggs and asparagus.

Derivatives

Substituting the vinegar reduction gives sister sauces: Choron (tomato), Foyot (meat glaze), Maltaise (blood orange, from hollandaise) and Sauce Rachel. Béarnaise's tarragon tang distinguishes it in the family of butter emulsions.
