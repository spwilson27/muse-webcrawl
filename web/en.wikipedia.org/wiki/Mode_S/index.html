Mode S

Mode S transponders reply selectively with a unique 24-bit aircraft address

Mode S (Mode Select) is a secondary surveillance radar (SSR) technique used in aviation that allows air traffic control to interrogate individual aircraft selectively rather than receiving replies from every transponder in the radar beam. Each aircraft carries a unique 24-bit ICAO address, and replies can include altitude, identity and other downlinked data.

Background

Conventional SSR (Modes A and C) suffers from "garbling" when replies from several aircraft overlap, and from excessive interrogations. Mode S, developed from the 1970s onwards and standardised by ICAO, addresses each aircraft individually, reducing channel congestion and enabling data-link functions. It remains interoperable with older Mode A/C equipment.

Variants

Mode S Elementary Surveillance (ELS) provides identity and altitude reporting; Enhanced Surveillance (EHS) downlinks parameters such as selected altitude, ground speed and magnetic heading. Mode S also provides the air-to-air data link used by airborne collision avoidance systems (ACAS II / TCAS), and its extended squitter forms the basis of ADS-B position broadcasting.

Mandates

Mode S carriage has been progressively mandated for commercial and much general aviation traffic in Europe, North America and elsewhere, replacing Mode A/C in controlled airspace.
