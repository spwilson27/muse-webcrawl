The Santa María (Spanish for Saint Mary), originally named La Gallega, was the largest of the three ships used by Christopher Columbus in his first voyage across the Atlantic Ocean in 1492, serving as his flagship.

Construction and voyage

Built in Pontevedra, Galicia, and owned by Juan de la Cosa, the Santa María was a slow but sturdy carrack of about 100 tons. Columbus sailed her to the Bahamas and the northern coasts of Cuba and Hispaniola.

Wreck

On Christmas Day 1492 the ship ran aground off Cap-Haïtien, Haiti, and could not be saved. Her timbers were used to build the settlement of La Navidad, the first European outpost in the Americas, whose garrison of 39 men was found dead when Columbus returned.

Replicas

Several full-scale replicas have been built, the most notable for the 1992 quincentenary, which moor at the Wharf of the Caravels in Palos de la Frontera and elsewhere.
