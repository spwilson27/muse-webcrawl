Hussein bin Ali al-Hashimi (Arabic: الحسين بن علي الهاشمي; c. 1853/1854 – 4 June 1931) was an Arab leader from the Hashemite family who was the Sharif and Emir of Mecca from 1908, and, after proclaiming the Arab Revolt against the Ottoman Empire, King of the Hejaz from 1916 to 1924. In 1924 he proclaimed himself Caliph, a claim recognised by few, and abdicated after the Saudi conquest of the Hejaz.

Early life

Hussein was born in Istanbul, then the Ottoman capital, as the eldest son of Sharif Ali bin Muhammad. He belonged to the Dhawu Awn clan of the Hashemites, descendants of Hasan ibn Ali, grandson of the Prophet Muhammad, who had traditionally held the Sharifate of Mecca. He spent much of his early life in the Hejaz before being summoned to Istanbul, where he lived under the watch of Sultan Abdul Hamid II.

Sharif of Mecca

Following the Young Turk Revolution of 1908, Hussein was appointed Sharif of Mecca. He sought to balance Ottoman authority against his own ambitions for the Hashemite emirate, and grew increasingly suspicious of the Committee of Union and Progress and its centralising, Turkifying policies.

The Arab Revolt

In 1915–1916 Hussein exchanged a famous series of letters with Sir Henry McMahon, the British High Commissioner in Egypt — the McMahon–Hussein Correspondence — in which Britain appeared to promise support for Arab independence in return for an Arab revolt against the Ottomans. On 10 June 1916 Hussein proclaimed the Arab Revolt. His sons Faisal and Abdullah led Arab forces, advised by officers including T. E. Lawrence, which captured Aqaba and Damascus and helped end Ottoman rule in the Arab lands.

King of the Hejaz and later life

After the war Hussein was recognised by Britain as King of the Hejaz, though not as ruler of the wider Arab territories he claimed; the conflicting promises of the Sykes–Picot Agreement and the Balfour Declaration frustrated his ambitions. His sons became kings of Iraq and Transjordan. In March 1924, following the Turkish abolition of the caliphate, Hussein proclaimed himself Caliph. Later that year the forces of Abdulaziz Ibn Saud conquered Mecca and Taif; Hussein abdicated in favour of his son Ali and went into exile in Cyprus. He died in Amman in 1931 and was buried in Jerusalem.
