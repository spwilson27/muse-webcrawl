Conservation area

A conservation area is a tract of land, or an area of a town or city, that has been awarded protected status in order to protect its natural environment, wildlife, or historic built character. The term is used in several countries with differing legal meanings. In the United Kingdom, conservation areas designated under planning law protect areas of "special architectural or historic interest the character or appearance of which it is desirable to preserve or enhance." In other jurisdictions, including parts of North America and parts of Canada, conservation areas are protected natural lands administered by conservation authorities.

United Kingdom

Conservation areas in the UK were introduced by the Civic Amenities Act 1967; the first designations included Stamford, Lincolnshire. There are now more than 10,000 conservation areas in England alone, covering historic city centres, villages, suburbs (such as the "garden suburbs" of Hampstead and Letchworth), industrial settlements and canal-side areas. Designation is made by local planning authorities under the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990.

Within a conservation area, additional planning controls apply: demolition of most buildings requires consent, permitted development rights are restricted (for example on extensions and cladding), and six weeks' notice must be given before works to trees. Local authorities must prepare appraisals and management plans and, in their planning decisions, pay special attention to preserving or enhancing the area's character.

Canada and the United States

In Ontario, Canada, conservation areas are natural areas owned and managed by local conservation authorities under the Conservation Authorities Act; examples include wetlands, forests and valley lands open to the public for hiking, birdwatching and education, such as those administered by the Toronto and Region Conservation Authority. Comparable terms elsewhere include "nature reserve," "heritage conservation district," and "protected area." In Hong Kong, conservation areas may also be zoned under town planning controls to protect the natural environment and rural character.
