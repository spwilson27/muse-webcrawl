Misdemeanor

A misdemeanor (American English) or misdemeanour (British English, historical) is any "lesser" criminal act, generally punished less severely than a felony and more severely than an infraction. In the United States, a misdemeanor is typically punishable by a fine and/or imprisonment of less than one year, usually in a local jail rather than a state prison.

Classification

Many jurisdictions divide misdemeanors into classes — for example class A, B and C misdemeanors in United States federal and many state systems — with maximum penalties scaled accordingly. Examples of common misdemeanors include petty theft, simple assault, disorderly conduct, trespassing, vandalism, and first-offense driving under the influence.

History

The term derives from the common-law distinction between felonies, which originally carried the penalty of death or forfeiture of property, and lesser offences. England and Wales abolished the felony/misdemeanour distinction in 1967, though the term survives in other common-law systems.

Consequences

Although less serious than felonies, misdemeanor convictions can carry collateral consequences including a criminal record, loss of some licences, and immigration consequences for non-citizens.
