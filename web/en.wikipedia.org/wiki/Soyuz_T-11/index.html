Soyuz T-11

Soyuz T-11 was a crewed spaceflight of the Soviet Union to the Salyut 7 space station in 1984. Launched on 3 April 1984 from Baikonur Cosmodrome, it carried commander Yuri Malyshev, flight engineer Gennady Strekalov, and Indian research cosmonaut Rakesh Sharma, who became the first Indian citizen in space.

Mission

The crew docked with Salyut 7, joining the resident crew of Leonid Kizim, Vladimir Solovyov and Oleg Atkov. During about eight days aboard, Sharma conducted Earth observation photography of India and a series of experiments, including sessions of yoga in microgravity and materials and life-science work under the Soviet–Indian joint programme. The three visitors returned to Earth on 11 April 1984 aboard Soyuz T-10, leaving their fresher spacecraft for the long-duration resident crew, a standard lifeboat-swap practice.

Significance

Sharma's flight made India the fourteenth nation with a citizen in space. His exchange with Prime Minister Indira Gandhi — replying "Saare Jahan Se Achha" when asked how India looked from space — became a celebrated moment in India. He was awarded the Ashoka Chakra and the title Hero of the Soviet Union.
