Thessaly (Greek: Θεσσαλία, Thessalía) is a traditional geographic and modern administrative region of Greece, comprising most of the fertile Thessalian plain in the centre of the Greek mainland. Surrounded by mountain ranges — Pindus to the west, Olympus to the northeast, Ossa and Pelion to the east — the plain is drained by the Pineios River and is one of Greece's principal agricultural areas.

History

Thessaly was inhabited from the Palaeolithic and was one of the earliest centres of Neolithic farming in Europe, with sites such as Sesklo and Dimini. In classical antiquity the region was a loose federation of cities ruled by aristocratic families famous for their cavalry; its horsemen were considered the finest in Greece. Thessaly came under Macedonian dominance under Philip II, then passed to Rome, Byzantium, and — after periods of Serbian and Catalan rule — the Ottoman Empire, under which it remained until 1881, when most of Thessaly was ceded to Greece following the Congress of Berlin.

In myth, Thessaly was the home of the centaurs of Mount Pelion, of Achilles and the Myrmidons of Phthia, and of Jason, who sailed from Iolcos in the Argo.

Geography and economy

The modern region has its capital at Larissa and includes the port of Volos and the rock monasteries of Meteora, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, near Kalambaka. The plain produces cereals, cotton, fruit and livestock. In September 2023, Storm Daniel caused catastrophic flooding across the Thessalian plain, inundating farmland and settlements around Karditsa and Larissa.
