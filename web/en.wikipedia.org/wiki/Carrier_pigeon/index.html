A carrier pigeon is a domestic pigeon bred and trained to carry messages, exploiting the homing pigeon's innate ability to find its way home over long distances. For centuries pigeons were the fastest means of long-distance communication, and they were used extensively in war into the 20th century.

History

Pigeon posts were used in the ancient world — by Egyptians, Persians and Romans — and Julius Caesar employed them in Gaul. In the 19th century, Paul Julius Reuter founded his news agency on pigeon links bridging gaps in the telegraph network, and during the Siege of Paris (1870–71) pigeons carried microfilmed dispatches into and out of the besieged city.

Military use

Both world wars saw large-scale military pigeon services. Birds such as Cher Ami, who delivered a message that saved the US "Lost Battalion" in 1918 despite severe wounds, and G.I. Joe and Paddy in the Second World War, were awarded the Dickin Medal for gallantry. Switzerland maintained a military pigeon service until 1994, and pigeons were valued because they could not be jammed or intercepted as easily as radio.

Homing ability

Carrier pigeons navigate using the sun, visual landmarks and sensitivity to the Earth's magnetic field, and can average 60–80 km/h over long flights. Racing homing pigeons remains a popular sport, with birds released up to 1,000 km from home.
