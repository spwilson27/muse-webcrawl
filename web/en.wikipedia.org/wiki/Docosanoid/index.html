Docosanoid

Docosanoids are signaling molecules made by the metabolism of the twenty-two-carbon omega-3 fatty acid docosahexaenoic acid (DHA). They form a subclass of the specialized pro-resolving mediators (SPMs), alongside resolvins, protectins, and maresins derived from DHA and other polyunsaturated fatty acids, and the eicosanoids derived from twenty-carbon arachidonic acid.

Docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), the parent compound of the docosanoids.

Members

Well-characterized docosanoids include resolvin D series compounds, protectin D1 (also called neuroprotectin D1 when formed in neural tissue), and the maresins. They are produced enzymatically through lipoxygenase and cyclooxygenase pathways, often via transcellular biosynthesis involving platelets and leukocytes.

Function

Docosanoids act as potent local mediators that actively promote the resolution of inflammation, reduce pain, protect neural and retinal tissue, and support tissue regeneration, distinguishing them from classical anti-inflammatory agents that merely suppress inflammation.
