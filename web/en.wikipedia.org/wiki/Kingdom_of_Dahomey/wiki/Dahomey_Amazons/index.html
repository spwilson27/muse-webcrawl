Dahomey Amazons

The Dahomey Amazons, known in Fon as the Mino ("our mothers"), were an all-female military regiment of the Kingdom of Dahomey in present-day Benin. European observers, struck by their ferocity in battle, compared them to the Amazons of Greek legend. They are among the few documented female front-line combat units in modern history.

History

Female guards and hunting parties existed at the Dahomean court from the seventeenth century, but the Mino were expanded into a formal army corps under King Ghezo in the nineteenth century, eventually numbering several thousand. Recruited as royal wives, slaves, and volunteers, they trained rigorously, swore to celibacy and to victory or death, and fought with muskets, machetes and clubs in Dahomey's wars. They played a prominent role in resisting French conquest during the Franco-Dahomean Wars of 1890–1894, after which the corps was disbanded; the last veterans died in the twentieth century.

Legacy

The Mino have inspired wide cultural interest, including the 2022 film The Woman King.
