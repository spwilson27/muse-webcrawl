Abomey

Abomey is a city in southern Benin that was the capital of the historic Kingdom of Dahomey from the seventeenth century until the kingdom's conquest by France in the 1890s. It is famed for the Royal Palaces of Abomey, a complex of earthen palaces built by successive Dahomean kings, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1985.

History

Founded, according to tradition, in the early 1600s, Abomey grew as the centre of the Fon kingdom of Dahomey. Each king built his own palace within the royal complex, decorating its walls with bas-relief panels narrating the deeds of his reign. The palaces now house the Historical Museum of Abomey, displaying thrones, weapons, textiles and ritual objects including the celebrated appliqué banners of the kingdom.

Heritage

The site bears witness to a vanished kingdom's architecture and court culture, though many structures were damaged by fire and looting during the French conquest; restoration has been ongoing with international support.
