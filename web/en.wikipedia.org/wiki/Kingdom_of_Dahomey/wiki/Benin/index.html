Benin (French: Bénin), officially the Republic of Benin, is a country in West Africa bordered by Togo, Nigeria, Burkina Faso, Niger and the Gulf of Guinea. Its territory encompasses the lands of the former Kingdom of Dahomey, from which the country took its name between independence in 1960 and 1975, when it was renamed Benin after the Bight of Benin.

History

The region was home to the powerful Fon kingdom of Dahomey, conquered by France in the 1890s and incorporated into French West Africa. Independence came in 1960, followed by political instability and a Marxist-Leninist period under Mathieu Kérékou; Benin then became a notable model of democratic transition in Africa in the early 1990s.

Geography and economy

The country stretches from the Niger River in the north to a short Atlantic coastline in the south. Its economy is based largely on agriculture, especially cotton and palm oil, and on trade through the port of Cotonou.
