Fon people

The Fon people, also called the Fon nu or Agadja, are a Gbe ethnic group native mainly to southern Benin, with communities in neighbouring Togo and Nigeria. They are the largest ethnic group in Benin, numbering several million, and speak the Fon language, part of the Gbe language cluster.

History

The Fon founded the Kingdom of Dahomey in the seventeenth century, centred on Abomey, which became one of West Africa's most powerful states through trade and warfare. Through the Atlantic slave trade, Fon culture and religion were carried to the Americas, where Fon Vodun strongly influenced Afro-Caribbean religions such as Haitian Vodou and Louisiana Voodoo.

Culture and religion

Fon society traditionally centres on the Vodun religion, with a pantheon headed by the dual creator Mawu-Lisa and spirits such as Legba, Sakpata and Xevioso; Benin officially recognises Vodun Day as a national holiday. Fon art includes the appliqué cloth banners and carved royal stools of the Abomey court.
