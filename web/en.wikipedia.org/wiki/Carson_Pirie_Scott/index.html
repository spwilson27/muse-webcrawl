Carson Pirie Scott & Co., trading as Carson's, was an American department store chain founded in 1854 and centered in the Midwestern United States. It grew from a dry-goods store in Peru, Illinois, into a Chicago institution.

Flagship building

The downtown Chicago flagship at State and Madison streets occupies the Carson Pirie Scott Building (1899–1904), a landmark of the Chicago School designed by Louis Sullivan, famed for its steel-frame construction and ornate cast-iron ornament at the entrances. The store closed in 2007; the building is now known as the Sullivan Center.

Later history

The chain passed through owners including P.A. Bergner & Co. (1989) and The Bon-Ton Stores (2006), which operated Carson's alongside its other nameplates. When Bon-Ton liquidated in 2018, all Carson's stores closed; the brand was briefly revived online by another retailer.
