Moses Austin (October 4, 1761 – June 10, 1821) was an American businessman and pioneer who played a central role in the development of the American lead industry and in the early settlement of Texas. He is best known for obtaining permission from Spanish authorities to settle 300 American families in Texas — a plan carried out after his death by his son, Stephen F. Austin, the "Father of Texas".

Early life and business

Born in Durham, Connecticut, Austin moved to Philadelphia and then Virginia, where he entered the dry goods and lead mining businesses. He founded the town of Austinville, Virginia, around his lead mines on the New River. After financial reverses, he moved west to Spanish Louisiana (present-day Missouri), where his mine at Potosi made him wealthy and influential.

The Texas venture

Ruined again by the failure of the Bank of St. Louis, Austin traveled to San Antonio de Béxar in 1820 and, with the help of the Baron de Bastrop, persuaded Governor Antonio María Martínez to approve his colonization plan. The Spanish grant, issued in January 1821, allowed Austin to bring 300 families to Texas.

Death and legacy

On the journey home Austin contracted pneumonia and died in June 1821, urging his son Stephen to carry on the enterprise. Stephen F. Austin's colony became the foundation of Anglo-American Texas.
