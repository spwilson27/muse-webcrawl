Wizz Air Holdings plc, styled as WIZZ, is a Hungarian ultra-low-cost airline headquartered in Budapest. Founded in September 2003 by József Váradi, a former CEO of Malév Hungarian Airlines, the airline made its first flight on 19 May 2004 from Katowice to London Luton, shortly after Hungary and Poland joined the European Union. Wizz Air grew to become the largest airline in Central and Eastern Europe and one of Europe's biggest low-cost carriers, carrying over 60 million passengers a year by the mid-2020s.

Business model and network

Wizz Air follows the ultra-low-cost model: a single-class cabin, high aircraft utilisation, secondary airports where possible, and extensive ancillary fees. Its network centres on Central and Eastern Europe — Poland, Romania, Hungary, Bulgaria, Serbia, North Macedonia, Albania, and Ukraine (before 2022) — with major operations also in Western Europe, the United Kingdom (through subsidiary Wizz Air UK), and, from 2020, Abu Dhabi through Wizz Air Abu Dhabi. Subsidiaries have also included Wizz Air Malta (2022) and the former Wizz Air Ukraine and Wizz Air Bulgaria.

Fleet

The airline operates an all-Airbus A320 family fleet, increasingly centred on the high-capacity A321neo, which Wizz Air configures with up to 239 seats and promotes as the most fuel-efficient narrowbody in service. The group has held one of the largest A321neo order books in the world, with several hundred aircraft on order from Airbus.

Criticism

Wizz Air has faced recurring criticism over labour relations, flight-delay compensation disputes, and environmental rankings — the UK Civil Aviation Authority at one point named it the worst major airline for flight delays — alongside scrutiny of its rapid growth and network changes.
