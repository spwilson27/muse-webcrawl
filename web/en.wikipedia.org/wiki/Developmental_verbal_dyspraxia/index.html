Developmental verbal dyspraxia

Developmental verbal dyspraxia, also known as childhood apraxia of speech (CAS), is a motor speech disorder in which children have difficulty planning and coordinating the movements needed for speech, in the absence of weakness or paralysis of the speech muscles. It is a neurological childhood speech sound disorder in which the precision and consistency of speech movements are impaired.

Characteristic features include inconsistent consonant and vowel errors on repeated productions of syllables or words, lengthened and disrupted coarticulatory transitions between sounds, and inappropriate prosody, especially in the realisation of word and phrase stress. Children with CAS often know what they want to say but cannot reliably produce it, and their speech may be markedly less intelligible than their language comprehension would suggest.

Diagnosis is made by a speech-language pathologist. Treatment involves intensive, frequent therapy focused on motor learning principles and repeated practice of speech movements. A small number of cases have been linked to mutations in the FOXP2 gene.
