Falls of Clyde (waterfalls)

Corra Linn, the most powerful of the falls

The Falls of Clyde are a series of four waterfalls (linns) on the River Clyde near New Lanark in South Lanarkshire, Scotland. From upstream they are Bonnington Linn, Corra Linn, Dundaff Linn and Stonebyres Linn. Together they drop about 60 metres (200 ft), with Corra Linn — at roughly 26 metres (86 ft) — the largest and most celebrated.

History and culture

The falls became a famous attraction of the Picturesque and Romantic movements: visitors included William Wordsworth, who wrote of Corra Linn, and the painter J. M. W. Turner, who sketched and painted them. The ruined Corra Castle overlooks Corra Linn. Much of the river's water is now diverted to the Bonnington hydroelectric power station, opened in 1927 as one of Scotland's first public hydro schemes, though the falls are regularly restored to full flow on "falls days" and after heavy rain.

Nature reserve

The surrounding gorge woodland forms the Falls of Clyde Wildlife Reserve, managed by the Scottish Wildlife Trust, within the Clyde Valley Woodlands National Nature Reserve. The ancient semi-natural woodland supports badgers, otters and roe deer, and peregrine falcons have nested at the falls. The reserve lies beside the model village of New Lanark, a UNESCO World Heritage Site founded by David Dale and developed by Robert Owen around the water power of the Clyde.
