The Australian Defence Force (ADF) is the military organisation responsible for the defence of Australia and its national interests. It consists of three branches: the Royal Australian Navy (RAN), the Australian Army, and the Royal Australian Air Force (RAAF), operating under a unified command structure established by the Defence Force Reorganisation Act 1975.

History

Australian forces originated with Federation in 1901, when the colonies' separate military forces were amalgamated. Australians served in large numbers in both world wars — at Gallipoli and the Western Front in the First World War, and in the Mediterranean, Pacific, and air war over Europe in the Second. Since 1947 the ADF has emphasised a joint, self-reliant defence of the continent and the surrounding maritime approaches, while contributing to coalition operations in Korea, Vietnam, Iraq, and Afghanistan, and to peacekeeping worldwide.

Structure and command

The Governor-General is Commander-in-Chief. Operational command runs through the Chief of the Defence Force, supported by the Vice Chief and the three service chiefs; administrative policy is handled jointly with the civilian Department of Defence under the "diarchy" arrangement. Joint Operations Command, headquartered near Bungendore, NSW, directs deployed operations.

Role and deployments

The ADF's primary tasks are deterring and defeating armed attacks on Australia, contributing to stability in the Indo-Pacific, and supporting civil authorities in emergencies such as bushfires and floods (as under Operation Bushfire Assist 2019–20). Australia maintains close alliance ties with the United States under the ANZUS Treaty and, since 2021, the AUKUS partnership with the UK and US, centred on nuclear-powered submarines.
