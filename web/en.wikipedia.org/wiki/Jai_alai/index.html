Jai alai

OriginBasque Country (Spain and France)
EquipmentPelota (ball), cesta (wicker basket-glove)
VenueFronton (three-walled court)
Ball speedUp to about 300 km/h

Jai alai (Basque: jai alai, "merry festival") is a Basque sport involving a ball bounced off a walled space by accelerating it to high speeds with a hand-held wicker implement called a cesta. It is a variation of Basque pelota and is often described as one of the fastest ball sports in the world.

Play

The game is played on a three-walled court (fronton), generally in rotating doubles or singles matches under a pari-mutuel format where spectators bet on the outcomes. The ball, or pelota, is made of goatskin over a hard core and can reach speeds near 300 km/h; players catch and throw it in one fluid motion using the long curved cesta strapped to the wrist.

History and spread

Jai alai was taken by Basque emigrants to the Americas and the Philippines, and in the twentieth century frontons in Florida made it a prominent betting sport in the United States. Its popularity declined from the 1980s with labour disputes and competition from lotteries and casinos, but it continues in the Basque Country and in a reduced professional circuit abroad.
