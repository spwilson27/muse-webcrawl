Prajnaparamita

Prajñāpāramitā (Sanskrit: प्रज्ञापारमिता, "the Perfection of Wisdom") refers to a central concept of Mahayana Buddhism and to the large body of sutras devoted to it. Prajñā is the wisdom that directly realises emptiness (śūnyatā) — the absence of intrinsic existence in all phenomena — and as one of the six (or ten) pāramitās, or perfections, cultivated by a bodhisattva, it is regarded as the perfection that guides and completes all the others.

The literature

The Prajñāpāramitā corpus grew over centuries and exists in many recensions, from the vast Perfection of Wisdom in 100,000 Lines down to the famously brief Heart Sutra and the Diamond Sutra. Many are framed as teachings of the Buddha, often addressed to Subhuti, guarded by the bodhisattva Avalokiteshvara or the goddess who personifies the perfection herself. The texts were among the earliest Mahayana scriptures translated into Chinese, and a printed copy of the Diamond Sutra dated 868 CE is the world's oldest dated printed book.

Doctrine

The sutras teach that all dharmas (phenomena) are empty of svabhāva, or own-being; even the categories of Buddhist doctrine — the aggregates, the path, and nirvana itself — are empty. This wisdom is not nihilism: emptiness is itself the middle way between eternalism and annihilationism, later systematised by Nagarjuna's Madhyamaka philosophy, which grew directly out of the Prajñāpāramitā tradition.

Personification and worship

Prajñāpāramitā is also venerated as a goddess, the "Mother of all Buddhas", depicted holding a lotus and the book of the sutra. Her mantra and the copying, recitation, and worship of the texts themselves were considered powerful sources of merit and protection throughout Buddhist Asia.
