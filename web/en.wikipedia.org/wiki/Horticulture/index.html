Horticulture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A plant nursery

Horticulture is the art, science, and business of cultivating plants, including fruits, vegetables, flowers, and ornamental plants. The word derives from the Latin hortus (garden) and cultura (cultivation). Whereas agronomy deals with broad-acre field crops, horticulture typically concerns intensive cultivation in smaller areas with high-value crops, and it includes the design, construction, and maintenance of gardens and landscapes.

Branches

The main branches of horticulture include pomology (fruit and nut crops), olericulture (vegetables), floriculture (flowers and bedding plants), arboriculture (trees and shrubs), and landscape horticulture. Horticultural science draws on plant physiology, genetics, soil science, entomology, and plant pathology, and modern practice ranges from home gardens and allotments to glasshouse complexes, vertical farms, and hydroponic production.

History

Gardening is as old as agriculture itself, with notable traditions including the hanging gardens of Babylon, Roman villa gardens, Islamic paradise gardens, Chinese scholar gardens, and Japanese gardens. Scientific horticulture developed in the seventeenth to nineteenth centuries with botanical gardens, plant collecting expeditions, and the breeding work that produced most modern fruit and vegetable cultivars. Professional bodies such as the Royal Horticultural Society, founded in 1804, helped standardise education and practice, and horticultural therapy and community gardening are recognised modern applications.
