Carl Ferdinand Cori

Carl Ferdinand Cori (5 December 1896 – 20 October 1984) was a Czech-American biochemist and pharmacologist. Together with his wife Gerty Cori and Argentine physiologist Bernardo Houssay, he received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1947 for the discovery of how glycogen is converted into glucose.

Life and career

Born in Prague, Cori studied at the German University in Prague and served as a medical officer in the Austro-Hungarian army during the First World War. Frustrated by postwar conditions in Europe, he emigrated to the United States in 1922, joining the State Institute for the Study of Malignant Diseases in Buffalo, New York. In 1931 he became professor of pharmacology at Washington University in St. Louis, later chairing its biochemistry department.

Work

The Coris characterised the Cori cycle, showing how lactate produced by muscle is converted back to glucose in the liver. They isolated glucose 1-phosphate (the "Cori ester") and discovered the enzyme phosphorylase, establishing the pathway of glycogen breakdown. Their laboratory trained a generation of biochemists, including several future Nobel laureates.
