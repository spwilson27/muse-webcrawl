Neil Jordan

Neil Patrick Jordan (born 25 February 1950) is an Irish film director, screenwriter and novelist. He won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay for The Crying Game (1992) and is known for films blending literary storytelling with themes of Irish history, sexuality and violence.

Career

Jordan began as a writer of short stories and the novel Night in Tunisia (1976), and worked as a script consultant on John Boorman's Excalibur before making his directorial debut with Angel (1982). His breakthrough came with Mona Lisa (1986), followed by the gothic fantasy The Company of Wolves (1984), the comedy High Spirits (1988), and We're No Angels (1989).

The Crying Game became an international sensation and earned six Academy Award nominations. Jordan later directed the big-budget Interview with the Vampire (1994), the historical epic Michael Collins (1996), which won the Golden Lion at Venice, The Butcher Boy (1997), The End of the Affair (1999), Breakfast on Pluto (2005), Ondine (2009), Byzantium (2012) and Greta (2018). On television he created the historical drama The Borgias (2011–2013).
