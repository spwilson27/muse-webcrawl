Edward Flanders Robb Ricketts (May 14, 1897 – May 11, 1948) was an American marine biologist, ecologist, and philosopher. Renowned as the author of the classic handbook Between Pacific Tides (1939), he is best known to general readers as the friend and collaborator of writer John Steinbeck and as the model for the character "Doc" in Steinbeck's novels Cannery Row (1945) and Sweet Thursday (1954), and for "Doc Burton" in In Dubious Battle.

Life and work

Ricketts was born in Chicago and attended the University of Chicago without completing a degree, studying zoology under Warder Clyde Allee, whose work on animal aggregations and social organisation influenced Ricketts's ecological thinking. In 1923 he moved to California and co-founded Pacific Biological Laboratories, a marine specimen supply company, first in Pacific Grove and later at 800 Cannery Row in Monterey. The laboratory supplied preserved specimens to schools and universities and became a gathering place for scientists, writers and artists.

Ricketts organised marine life not by taxonomy alone but by habitat and shore zone; Between Pacific Tides, published by Stanford University Press, arranged the invertebrates of the Pacific coast by their intertidal habitats and became a standard reference, still in print in revised editions. In 1940 he and Steinbeck chartered the purse seiner Western Flyer for a collecting expedition to the Gulf of California (Sea of Cortez), described in Sea of Cortez: A Leisurely Journal of Travel and Research (1941), whose narrative portion Steinbeck later reissued as The Log from the Sea of Cortez.

Ricketts also wrote essays in ecological philosophy, including the essay "Breaking Through," on non-teleological thinking — a holistic way of understanding events "as they are" rather than in terms of causes and purposes — which influenced Steinbeck's writing. On May 8, 1948, Ricketts's car was struck by a train at a crossing in Monterey; he died three days later, shortly before his fifty-first birthday.

Legacy

The Monterey Bay Aquarium Research Institute named a remotely operated vehicle Doc Ricketts in his honour. His laboratory on Cannery Row is preserved, and the voyage of the Western Flyer has been commemorated by the Western Flyer Foundation, which restored the vessel.
