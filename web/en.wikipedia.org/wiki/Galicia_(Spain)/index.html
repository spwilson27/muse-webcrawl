Galicia (Spain)

Galicia is an autonomous community in northwestern Spain, comprising the provinces of A Coruña, Lugo, Ourense and Pontevedra. Its capital is Santiago de Compostela, famed as the destination of the Camino de Santiago pilgrimage. The Galician language, closely related to Portuguese, is co-official with Spanish.


Capital: Santiago de Compostela
Largest city: Vigo
Languages: Galician, Spanish

Geography

Galicia's Atlantic coast is cut by deep inlets called rías; the interior is green and hilly. The climate is oceanic and wetter than most of Spain, hence "Green Spain".

History and culture

Settled by Celtic peoples and Romanised as Gallaecia, Galicia later became a medieval kingdom. Galician culture is marked by the bagpipe (gaita), seafood cuisine, Celtic heritage festivals, and a strong emigration tradition to the Americas.
