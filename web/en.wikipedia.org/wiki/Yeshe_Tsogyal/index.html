Yeshe Tsogyal

Born: c. 757, Tibet
Died: c. 817
Tradition: Nyingma school of Tibetan Buddhism
Teacher: Padmasambhava

Yeshe Tsogyal (Tibetan: ཡེ་ཤེས་མཚོ་རྒྱལ, "Victorious Ocean of Knowledge", c. 757–817) was the principal consort and foremost female disciple of the Indian tantric master Padmasambhava, who is credited with establishing Buddhism in Tibet. Revered as a female Buddha and an emanation of the goddess Sarasvatī, she is regarded as the "mother of Tibetan Buddhism" and one of the most important women in the tradition's history.

Life

According to her traditional biography, Yeshe Tsogyal was born into the Kharchen clan and was married to the Tibetan emperor Trisong Detsen, who later offered her to Padmasambhava as a mandala offering. She became his closest student, receiving the full range of his teachings, and accompanied him to meditation retreats throughout Tibet and Nepal. She is said to have attained enlightenment in her lifetime, reaching the rainbow body.

Terma tradition

Yeshe Tsogyal is remembered as the scribe and concealer of countless terma (treasure teachings) dictated by Padmasambhava and hidden in rocks, lakes, and even in the mind-streams of future disciples, to be revealed by later tertöns (treasure revealers) at prophesied times. Her memoirs, known as the Life and Liberation of Princess Mandarava-adjacent corpus and her own autobiography, were themselves recovered as terma centuries later.

Legacy

Within the Nyingma school she embodies the union of wisdom and method and is invoked as a dakini and long-life deity. Later masters, including Machig Labdrön and modern teachers, have been regarded as her emanations. Her biography remains a central inspirational text for women practitioners in Tibetan Buddhism.
