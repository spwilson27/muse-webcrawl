Flat-twelve engine

ConfigurationTwelve cylinders in two flat banks of six
Notable usersFerrari, Porsche (racing)
ApplicationsFormula One, sports prototypes, grand tourers

A flat-twelve engine is a twelve-cylinder piston engine with two banks of six cylinders lying flat on either side of a common crankshaft. It combines the inherent balance and smoothness of a twelve-cylinder layout with a very low centre of gravity, making it attractive for mid-engined racing and sports cars. Most flat-twelves are effectively 180° V12 engines rather than true boxer engines: opposed pistons share crankpins and move in the same direction, unlike a boxer, in which opposed pistons move towards and away from each other.

Racing use

Porsche's Types 912 and 917 engines powered the dominant 917 sports-prototype of 1970–71. In Formula One, Ferrari's 3-litre flat-twelve family (introduced with the 312B of 1970 and developed through the 312T series) won championships with Niki Lauda in 1975 and 1977 and powered the constructors' titles of 1975–1977 and 1979. The low, wide engine suited ground-effect aerodynamics poorly in its later years, and Ferrari switched to a V6 turbo for 1981.

Road cars

Ferrari used flat-twelves in the 365 GT4 BB (1973), the 512 BB, and the Testarossa line (1984–1996), whose 4.9-litre engine was among the largest and most powerful production flat-twelves. The layout's width, complex exhaust routing, and difficulty meeting emissions and packaging demands ended its production use in the 1990s.
