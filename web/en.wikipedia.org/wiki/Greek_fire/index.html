Greek fire

Greek fire was an incendiary weapon used by the Byzantine Empire from the 7th century AD. Projected through siphons from ships, it burned even on water and was decisive in repelling Arab sieges of Constantinople. Its formula was a closely guarded state secret and remains uncertain.

Use

Tradition credits Kallinikos, a refugee architect from Heliopolis, around 672. Greek fire helped break the Arab sieges of Constantinople in 674–678 and 717–718, and was used against Rus' and Venetian fleets. It was delivered by ship-mounted siphons, hand-held cheirosiphons, and grenades.

Mystery

The secret was so compartmentalised it was eventually lost by the 12th–13th centuries. Modern reconstructions suggest a petroleum-based mixture thickened with resins, ignited on projection. See Byzantine Empire and Gunpowder.
