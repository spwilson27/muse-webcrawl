The West Midlands is a metropolitan and ceremonial county in the larger West Midlands region of England. Created by the Local Government Act 1972, it came into existence on 1 April 1974 and covers an area of 902 square kilometres (348 sq mi). The county is a heavily urbanised conurbation centred on Birmingham, historically the world's first industrial city, and takes in the cities of Coventry and Wolverhampton along with the boroughs of Dudley, Sandwell, Solihull and Walsall.

History

Although the West Midlands is a new county in name, its constituent parts have long histories. Birmingham grew from a small market town into the "workshop of the world" during the Industrial Revolution, powered by the Lunar Society, the engineering works of Boulton and Watt, and a dense network of canals. Coventry was a major centre of the medieval woollen trade and later of watchmaking, bicycle manufacture and the motor industry; it was heavily bombed in the Coventry Blitz of November 1940. Wolverhampton and the Black Country towns to the west became synonymous with coal mining, iron founding and metal manufacturing.

The county was governed by the West Midlands County Council from 1974 until its abolition in 1986, after which powers passed to the seven metropolitan district councils. In 2016 the districts combined to form the West Midlands Combined Authority, headed since 2017 by a directly elected Mayor of the West Midlands.

Geography

The county is landlocked and lies at the heart of England. It borders Staffordshire to the north and west, Warwickshire to the east and south, and Worcestershire to the south and west. Much of the land is built up, but there are green spaces including Sutton Park in Birmingham, one of the largest urban parks in Europe, and the countryside of the Meriden Gap separating Birmingham from Coventry.

Economy and transport

Manufacturing remains important, with Jaguar Land Rover plants at Solihull and Castle Bromwich, but the economy is increasingly service-based. Birmingham is a major financial and professional services centre, and the county is served by Birmingham Airport, the M5, M6, M40 and M42 motorways, and Birmingham New Street, the busiest railway station in the United Kingdom outside London.
