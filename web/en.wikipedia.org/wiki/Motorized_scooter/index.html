Motorized scooter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A modern standing electric scooter.

A motorized scooter is a powered two-wheeled vehicle with a step-through frame and a platform for the rider's feet, propelled by an electric motor or a small internal combustion engine. The term covers classic seated scooters such as the Vespa, stand-up kick scooters fitted with motors, and the lightweight e-scooters used in dockless sharing systems.

History

The motorized scooter appeared in 1915 with the American Autoped, a standing scooter with a small gasoline engine mounted over the front wheel, used by delivery services and famously by suffragettes and socialites of the era. After the Second World War, Italy's Piaggio Vespa (1946) and Innocenti's Lambretta (1947) popularised the enclosed-body, seated scooter as cheap urban transport, and the type spread worldwide.

Electric scooters and sharing

From 2017, companies such as Bird and Lime launched dockless, app-unlocked electric kick-scooter fleets in cities across North America and Europe. The systems proved popular for short "last mile" trips but also drew criticism over sidewalk clutter, rider injuries, and uncertain legality; many jurisdictions responded with speed limits, parking rules, and licensing schemes. Privately owned e-scooters and seated mopeds remain widely used, particularly in Asia, where manufacturers such as Honda, Yamaha, and NIU sell them in large numbers.

Legal status

Classification varies: depending on power and top speed, a motorized scooter may be regulated as a bicycle, a moped requiring registration and a driving licence, or a full motorcycle. Seated scooters with engines above 50 cc are generally treated as motorcycles in most jurisdictions.
