Heat recovery ventilation

Heat recovery ventilation (HRV), also known as mechanical ventilation heat recovery (MVHR), is a ventilation system that provides fresh air to a building while recovering heat from the stale air being exhausted. By transferring heat between the outgoing and incoming air streams, the system maintains indoor air quality in airtight, energy-efficient buildings without the large energy penalty of simply opening windows or exhausting heated air.

How it works

Two air streams — exhaust air leaving the building and supply air entering it — pass through a heat exchanger, most often a counter-flow plate exchanger, so that the streams transfer heat without mixing. Modern units recover 70–95 per cent of the heat in the outgoing air. Fans draw air from wet rooms (kitchens and bathrooms) and supply fresh air to living rooms and bedrooms. Related energy recovery ventilators (ERVs) also transfer moisture between the streams, which can be advantageous in very dry or very humid climates.

Applications

Heat recovery ventilation is a core technology of low-energy building standards such as Passivhaus, where near-airtight construction makes controlled ventilation essential. Systems include filters for pollen and particulates, frost protection strategies for cold climates, and sometimes a small heater or connection to a ground heat exchanger. Regular filter replacement and balancing of airflows are the main maintenance requirements.
