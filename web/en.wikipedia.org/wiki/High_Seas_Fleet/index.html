High Seas Fleet

The High Seas Fleet (German: Hochseeflotte) was the battle fleet of the German Imperial Navy and saw action during the First World War. Created in 1907 as a result of the Fleet Acts championed by Admiral Alfred von Tirpitz, it was centred on modern dreadnought battleships based at Wilhelmshaven and Kiel.

Origins

Tirpitz's "risk theory" held that a German battle fleet strong enough to inflict unacceptable losses on the Royal Navy would deter Britain from war. The resulting Anglo-German naval arms race saw Germany complete thirteen dreadnought battleships and five battlecruisers by 1914, against a still larger British force.

First World War

The fleet fought the Battle of Jutland on 31 May – 1 June 1916, its only full-scale fleet action, under Admiral Reinhard Scheer. Although German ships inflicted heavier losses, the British Grand Fleet retained control of the North Sea and the blockade of Germany continued. Thereafter the fleet largely remained in port, its inactivity contributing to declining morale.

Mutiny and scuttling

In October 1918 a planned final sortie provoked the Wilhelmshaven and Kiel mutinies, which sparked the German Revolution. After the Armistice, the fleet was interned at Scapa Flow in Scotland. On 21 June 1919, Rear Admiral Ludwig von Reuter ordered the ships scuttled to prevent their seizure; 52 of the 74 interned vessels sank.
