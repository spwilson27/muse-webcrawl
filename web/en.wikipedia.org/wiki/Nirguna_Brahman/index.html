Nirguna Brahman

Nirguna Brahman (Sanskrit: निर्गुण ब्रह्मन्) is a concept in Hindu philosophy referring to the Absolute Reality, Brahman, "without attributes" or "without qualities" (from nir-, "without", and guṇa, "quality"). It is contrasted with Saguna Brahman, Brahman "with attributes", the personal God who can be named, worshipped, and described.

Concept

In the Upanishads, Brahman is frequently indicated by negation—neti neti, "not this, not this"—because it is held to lie beyond all empirical qualities and conceptual categories. The Brihadaranyaka Upanishad describes the imperishable as unseen but seeing, unheard but hearing, unthinkable but thinking.

In Vedanta schools

In Adi Shankara's Advaita Vedanta, Nirguna Brahman is the ultimate truth: pure consciousness without limiting adjuncts, while Saguna Brahman (Ishvara, the Lord) is Brahman as conceived through maya for the purposes of worship and creation. Other schools interpret the distinction differently: in Ramanuja's Vishishtadvaita, Brahman is understood as qualified by auspicious attributes and by sentient and non-sentient beings as its modes, so "nirguna" is read as "free of inauspicious qualities". Madhva's Dvaita likewise rejects an attributeless absolute. The distinction also underlies the nirguna and saguna traditions of bhakti poetry, represented respectively by saints such as Kabir and Dadu, and by devotees of personal deities such as Tulsidas.
