Letter of credence

A letter of credence (French: Lettre de créance) is a formal diplomatic letter that designates a diplomat as ambassador to another sovereign state. The letter is addressed from one head of state to another, asking the recipient to give credence — that is, to believe — to whatever the ambassador says on behalf of the sending government.

Ceremony

The presentation of credentials is one of the oldest rituals of diplomacy. On arrival in the host country, a newly appointed ambassador presents the letter of credence at a formal ceremony with the host head of state, often accompanied by an honour guard and a private audience. Until the credentials are presented, the ambassador cannot officially begin work.

Content and form

The letter is usually signed by the sending head of state and countersigned by the foreign minister. It requests that the envoy be received kindly and that full faith be given to their representations. Copies, known as working copies or copies d'usage, are typically presented to the foreign ministry on arrival so protocol arrangements can begin.

Recall

When an ambassador's mission ends, a corresponding letter of recall (lettre de rappel) is issued, formally withdrawing the envoy's accreditation. See also diplomacy and ambassador.
