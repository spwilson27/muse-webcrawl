Cree

The Cree are among the largest Indigenous peoples of North America

The Cree (Cree: Nêhiyawak) are a North American Indigenous people who live primarily in Canada, where they form one of the country's largest First Nations groups, and in northern parts of the United States. Their traditional territory extends from the Rocky Mountains in the west to the Atlantic coast in the east — a vast area stretching across what are now Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Ontario, and Quebec.

Groups and languages

The Cree are divided into a number of subgroups distinguished by dialect and geography, including the Plains Cree, Woods Cree, Swampy Cree, Moose Cree, and the East Cree (Eeyou) of the James Bay region of Quebec. Their language, Cree, is an Algonquian language with a dialect continuum and is one of the most widely spoken Indigenous languages in Canada; it is traditionally written in Canadian Aboriginal syllabics as well as in Latin script.

History

The Cree were early and important partners in the North American fur trade, supplying furs to the Hudson's Bay Company and the North West Company and acting as middlemen with peoples further west. Contact brought trade goods and firearms but also devastating epidemics of smallpox. In 1885, some Cree bands took part in the North-West Rebellion alongside Louis Riel's Métis forces. In the twentieth century, Cree communities were profoundly affected by residential schools, hydroelectric development — especially the James Bay Project, which led to the landmark James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement of 1975 — and ongoing negotiations over land and self-government.

Culture

Traditionally the Cree of the forests were hunter-gatherers following moose, caribou, fish, and waterfowl, while the Plains Cree adopted equestrian buffalo hunting. Extended family bands, seasonal gatherings, storytelling, and ceremonies such as the Sun Dance (among Plains Cree) and the shaking tent rite structured social and spiritual life. Today Cree governments, language revitalisation programs, and artists sustain a vigorous cultural presence.
