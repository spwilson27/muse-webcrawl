Saint-Emilion

Saint-Émilion (Gascon: Sent Milion) is a commune in the Gironde department in southwestern France, some 35 km northeast of Bordeaux. It is one of the principal red wine areas of the Bordeaux wine region, and its medieval old town and vineyard landscape are listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The town is named after Émilion, an eighth-century Breton hermit who settled in a cave in the limestone plateau and around whom a religious community grew. The Romans had already planted vines in the area, and the poet Ausonius praised its wines. In the Middle Ages Saint-Émilion flourished as a stop on the pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela, and its great monolithic church — hewn entirely from a single block of limestone rock between the ninth and twelfth centuries — was carved in this period.

Wine

Saint-Émilion is one of the four great appellations of Bordeaux's Right Bank, on the northern bank of the Dordogne. Its red wines are based principally on Merlot and Cabernet Franc, grown on a varied mosaic of limestone, clay and gravel soils. The appellation operates its own classification, revised roughly every ten years, with Château Cheval Blanc, Château Ausone, Château Angélus and Château Pavie historically among its top-ranked premiers grands crus classés A.

Sights

Besides the monolithic church and its 68-metre bell tower, visitors come for the underground catacombs, the collegiate church, the ruined ramparts and the Tour du Roy keep. In 1999 the jurisdiction of Saint-Émilion became the first wine-growing landscape inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List.
