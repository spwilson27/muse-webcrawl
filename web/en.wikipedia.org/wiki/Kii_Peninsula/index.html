The Kii Peninsula (紀伊半島, Kii-hantō) is the largest peninsula on the island of Honshu in Japan, projecting southward into the Pacific Ocean. It spans Wakayama, Mie and southern Nara prefectures.

Geography

The peninsula is mountainous and heavily forested, with deep valleys, high rainfall — Owase is among Japan's wettest places — and a rugged ria coastline. Rivers such as the Kumano and Yoshino drain its interior.

Sacred sites

The Kii Mountains are home to three sacred sites — Yoshino and Ōmine, Kumano Sanzan, and Mount Kōya — linked by the Kumano Kodō pilgrimage routes. In 2004 they were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range." Shugendō mountain asceticism has been practised here for over a millennium.

Economy

Forestry, fisheries, mandarin oranges and tourism sustain the region, though many rural communities face depopulation and ageing.
