Treaty of Cahuenga

The Treaty of Cahuenga, also called the Capitulation of Cahuenga, was signed on 13 January 1847 at Campo de Cahuenga in the San Fernando Valley of Alta California. It ended organized fighting between United States and Californio forces in California during the Mexican–American War.

Terms

The agreement was negotiated between Lieutenant Colonel John C. Frémont for the United States and representatives of the Californio leader Andrés Pico. Its generous terms allowed the Californios to return to their homes unmolested, surrender their artillery, take no further part in the war, and enjoy the rights and privileges of United States citizens, with no one required to swear allegiance until a formal peace treaty was signed.

Significance

Although not an official treaty between governments — the war itself was formally ended by the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo in 1848 — the capitulation secured California for the United States with relatively little further bloodshed and is commemorated at the Campo de Cahuenga historic site near present-day Universal City, Los Angeles.
