Blondie (band)

Blondie is an American rock band formed in New York City in 1974 by singer Debbie Harry and guitarist Chris Stein. Emerging from the CBGB punk and new wave scene, Blondie achieved mainstream success by fusing rock with disco, pop, reggae, and early hip hop, scoring hits including "Heart of Glass," "Call Me," "Atomic," "The Tide Is High," and "Rapture." The band has sold over 40 million records worldwide.

Rise to fame

After two albums, Parallel Lines (1978) broke through internationally, driven by the disco-rock crossover "Heart of Glass." "Call Me," from the film American Gigolo, was the biggest US single of 1980, and "Rapture" (1981) became the first number-one US single to feature rapping. The reggae cover "The Tide Is High" also topped charts on both sides of the Atlantic.

Breakup and reunion

Financial mismanagement, Stein's serious illness, and internal tensions led to the band's breakup in 1982. Harry pursued a solo career. Blondie re-formed in 1997, scoring a UK number one with "Maria" (1999), and has continued recording and touring, with albums including Pollinator (2017). Drummer Clem Burke, a member throughout, died in 2025.

Legacy

Blondie's genre-hopping and Harry's iconic image influenced generations of pop and alternative artists, from Madonna to No Doubt and Lady Gaga.
