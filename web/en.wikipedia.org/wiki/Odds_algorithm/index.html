Odds algorithm

The odds algorithm gives an optimal stopping rule for selecting the last success in a sequence of independent trials.

The odds algorithm is a mathematical method for solving a class of optimal stopping problems. It was introduced by F. Thomas Bruss in 2000. The problem it solves is: given a sequence of independent events with known success probabilities, observed one after another, select the last success, stopping only once and never being able to return to a previous event.

The algorithm

For each event i with success probability pi, define its odds ri = pi / (1 − pi). Working backwards from the end of the sequence, sum the odds until the sum first reaches or exceeds 1; the index at which this happens is the threshold s. The optimal strategy is to skip all events before s and then stop at the first success from s onward. The probability of winning under this strategy equals the product of the failure probabilities from s onward, multiplied by the sum of the odds from s onward.

Applications

The odds algorithm unifies and generalises several classical problems, including the secretary problem, in which the strategy and the winning probability (approaching 1/e) emerge as special cases. Applications discussed in the literature include selling an asset at the last favourable opportunity, clinical trial stopping rules, and search problems.
