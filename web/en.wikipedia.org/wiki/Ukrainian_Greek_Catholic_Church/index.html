The Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church (UGCC) is a Byzantine Rite Eastern Catholic church in full communion with the pope and the worldwide Catholic Church. It is the second-largest particular church in the Catholic communion after the Latin Church, with roughly five million faithful in Ukraine and the Ukrainian diaspora.

The Patriarchal Cathedral of the Resurrection of Christ in Kyiv, completed in 2013.

History

The church traces its origin to the Union of Brest (1596), by which several bishops of the Kyivan metropolitanate in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth entered communion with Rome while retaining the Byzantine liturgy, Church Slavonic and Ukrainian-language worship, a married clergy, and their own canon law. Its heartland became Galicia, where under Metropolitan Andrey Sheptytsky (1901–1944) it became closely identified with Ukrainian national life.

In 1946 the Soviet authorities staged the pseudo-synod of Lviv, which purported to dissolve the union and forcibly transferred the church's property to the Russian Orthodox Church. The UGCC was driven underground — becoming known as the "Church of the Catacombs" — and its leader, Josyf Slipyj, spent 18 years in the Gulag. It was the largest banned religious organisation in the USSR.

Modern church

The church was legalised again in 1989 during perestroika, re-emerged into public life, and in 2005 moved its major archiepiscopal see from Lviv to Kyiv. Since 2011 it has been led by Major Archbishop Sviatoslav Shevchuk. During the Revolution of Dignity and the full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine beginning in 2022, the church played a prominent public and charitable role.

Structure

The church is organised into archeparchies and eparchies in Ukraine, Poland, Germany, France, the United Kingdom, Canada, the United States, Brazil, Argentina, and Australia, serving the worldwide Ukrainian diaspora.
