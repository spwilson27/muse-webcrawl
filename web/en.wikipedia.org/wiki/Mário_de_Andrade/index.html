Mário Raul de Morais Andrade (9 October 1893 – 25 February 1945) was a Brazilian poet, novelist, musicologist, art historian and critic, and photographer. A central figure of Brazilian modernism, he was one of the organisers of the Semana de Arte Moderna (Week of Modern Art) held in São Paulo in February 1922, the founding event of modernism in Brazil. His rhapsodic novel Macunaíma (1928) is considered one of the masterpieces of Brazilian literature.

Life and work

Born in São Paulo, Andrade trained as a concert pianist and taught music history at the Conservatório Dramático e Musical. His poetry collection Paulicéia Desvairada (Hallucinated City, 1922), influenced by European avant-gardes yet fiercely local in diction, announced the modernist project of forging a distinctly Brazilian art. Through essays, letters, and mentorship — he was called "the Pope of modernism" — he shaped a generation of writers including Oswald de Andrade (no relation) and Manuel Bandeira.

Macunaíma

Macunaíma: o herói sem nenhum caráter ("Macunaíma: the hero with no character at all") weaves Indigenous Amazonian mythology, Afro-Brazilian folklore, and urban São Paulo slang into the picaresque journey of its shape-shifting hero from the jungle to the city and back. Written in six days in December 1926, the novel is celebrated for its linguistic inventiveness and remains a foundational text of Brazilian national identity; it was adapted into a celebrated 1969 film by Joaquim Pedro de Andrade.

Folklore and public service

Andrade pioneered the study of Brazilian folk music and culture, undertaking a research expedition to the Northeast and Amazon in 1928–29 and later directing São Paulo's Department of Culture (1935–1938), where he founded public libraries, the city's discotheque of Brazilian music, and the Missão de Pesquisas Folclóricas (Folklore Research Mission) of 1938. Under the Estado Novo he fell out of favour and moved to Rio de Janeiro, returning to São Paulo shortly before his death from a heart attack in 1945.
