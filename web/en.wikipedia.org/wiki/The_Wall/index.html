The Wall

[Hero image placeholder: Pink Floyd performing The Wall live, with the giant white-brick wall across the stage.]

The Wall is the eleventh studio album by the English rock band Pink Floyd, released on 30 November 1979 by Harvest Records in the United Kingdom and Columbia Records in the United States. It is a rock opera about Pink, a jaded rock star whose self-imposed isolation from society is symbolised by a wall. The album was a commercial success, topping charts in several countries, and features the band's only number-one single, "Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2".

Concept

Bassist and principal songwriter Roger Waters conceived the album after a hostile concert experience in Montreal in 1977, in which he spat at a disruptive fan; disgusted with himself and with the alienation of stadium rock, he imagined building a wall between the band and its audience. The narrative follows Pink from the death of his father in the Second World War, through an overprotective upbringing, a failed marriage and rock-star excess, to a fascist demagogue fantasy and a self-imposed trial that orders the wall torn down.

Legacy

The Wall was adapted into the 1982 film Pink Floyd – The Wall, directed by Alan Parker and starring Bob Geldof. It is among the best-selling albums of all time, has been certified 23× platinum in the United States, and its stage productions — by Pink Floyd in 1980–81 and by Waters in 2010–13 — are landmarks of rock spectacle.
