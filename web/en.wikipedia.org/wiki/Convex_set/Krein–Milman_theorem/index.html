Krein–Milman theorem

The Krein–Milman theorem is a theorem in functional analysis, proved by Mark Krein and David Milman in 1940. It states that if S is a compact convex subset of a locally convex topological vector space, then S is equal to the closed convex hull of its extreme points.

Statement and converse

In particular, a compact convex set has at least one extreme point, and every point of the set is a limit of convex combinations of extreme points. The converse — that the closed convex hull of the extreme points of a bounded set with the Radon–Nikodým property is the set itself — is part of a circle of results characterizing such spaces.

Proof idea

Standard proofs use the Hahn–Banach separation theorem to expose faces of the set, Zorn's lemma to obtain minimal faces, and show that minimal faces are singletons, which are then extreme points; density follows by a separation argument.

Applications

The theorem is used throughout functional analysis and its applications: classifying states of C*-algebras (pure states are extreme points), in ergodic theory (ergodic measures are the extreme points of the convex set of invariant measures), and in the theory of capacities and Choquet theory, which generalizes the theorem by representing points as integrals over extreme points.
