Convex optimization

Convex optimization studies the minimization of convex functions over convex sets. Its defining advantage is that every local minimum is a global minimum, so local search methods reliably find the optimum when a problem is recognized — or reformulated — as convex.

Problem classes

Linear programs, least squares problems, quadratic programs, geometric programs, second-order cone programs and semidefinite programs are all convex, and are solved in practice by specialized solvers. Sums of convex functions subject to convex inequality constraints and affine equality constraints preserve convexity.

Theory

Lagrangian duality associates a dual problem whose optimum lower-bounds the primal optimum; under constraint qualifications such as Slater's condition, strong duality holds. The Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions are necessary — and for convex problems also sufficient — for optimality.

Methods

Interior point methods follow a central path defined by barrier functions and solve moderate-size problems to high accuracy in polynomial time. First-order methods (subgradient, proximal, and accelerated gradient methods) scale to very large problems with modest accuracy requirements. Software systems such as CVX, CVXPY and MOSEK make convex modelling routine.
