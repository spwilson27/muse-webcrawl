Krein–Milman theorem

In the mathematical discipline of functional analysis, the Krein–Milman theorem is a proposition about convex sets in topological vector spaces. It states that a compact convex set in a locally convex topological vector space is equal to the closed convex hull of its extreme points.

Krein–Milman theorem
FieldFunctional analysis
Named afterMark Krein, David Milman
Key notionExtreme point

Statement

Let K be a nonempty compact convex subset of a locally convex Hausdorff topological vector space. Then K is the closed convex hull of its extreme points — points of K that cannot be written as a proper convex combination of two distinct points of K. In particular, K has at least one extreme point.

History

Hermann Minkowski had proved the finite-dimensional version: a compact convex set in finite dimensions is the convex hull of its extreme points. Mark Krein and David Milman extended the result to infinite-dimensional spaces in 1940, showing that the closure of the convex hull is needed in general.

Applications

The theorem is used throughout functional analysis and its applications: in the theory of Choquet integrals and ergodic theory, in showing that the state space of a C*-algebra is generated by its pure states, and in the existence arguments for equilibria in game theory and optimal control. A related result, Milman's partial converse, says that extreme points of the convex hull of a compact set lie in the closure of the set itself.
