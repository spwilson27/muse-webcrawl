Extreme point

In mathematics, an extreme point of a convex set S in a real vector space is a point in S that does not lie in any open line segment joining two distinct points of S. Equivalently, a point x is extreme if it cannot be written as a convex combination x = ty + (1−t)z with y, z distinct points of S and 0 < t < 1.

Examples

The extreme points of a closed disk are exactly the points of its boundary circle; the extreme points of a polygon are its vertices; the extreme points of a line segment are its endpoints. The unit ball of many function spaces has rich sets of extreme points — for example, the extreme points of the unit ball of L1 are (suitably normalized) indicator-type functions, while the unit ball of a strictly convex normed space has every boundary point extreme.

Role in theorems

The Krein–Milman theorem asserts that a compact convex set in a locally convex space equals the closed convex hull of its extreme points. In linear programming, the fundamental theorem states that if an optimum exists, it is attained at an extreme point (vertex) of the feasible polytope — the fact exploited by the simplex method. Choquet theory refines this representation by expressing points as barycenters of probability measures supported on the extreme points.
