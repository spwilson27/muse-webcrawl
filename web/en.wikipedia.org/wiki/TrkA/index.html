TrkA

TrkA (NTRK1)
Also known asHigh affinity nerve growth factor receptor
GeneNTRK1 (chromosome 1q23.1)
LigandNerve growth factor (NGF)
TypeReceptor tyrosine kinase

TrkA, also known as the high affinity nerve growth factor receptor, is a protein in humans encoded by the NTRK1 gene. It is the catalytic receptor for nerve growth factor (NGF), a neurotrophin essential for the development and survival of sympathetic and sensory neurons. TrkA is the first member of the tropomyosin receptor kinase (Trk) family, which also includes TrkB and TrkC.

Function

Binding of NGF causes TrkA to dimerise and autophosphorylate on tyrosine residues in its intracellular kinase domain. The phosphorylated receptor activates downstream signalling pathways including Ras/MAPK, PI3K/Akt, and PLC-γ, which drive neuronal differentiation, neurite outgrowth, and cell survival. In the adult nervous system, TrkA is expressed strongly on nociceptive (pain-sensing) neurons, and NGF–TrkA signalling sensitises these neurons, a mechanism relevant to chronic pain.

Clinical significance

Congenital insensitivity to pain

Loss-of-function mutations in NTRK1 cause congenital insensitivity to pain with anhidrosis (CIPA, also called HSAN type IV), a rare autosomal recessive disorder in which patients cannot feel pain or temperature and cannot sweat, leading to repeated injuries, self-mutilation and recurrent fevers.

Cancer

Chromosomal rearrangements that fuse the kinase domain of NTRK1 to other genes produce constitutively active oncoproteins. NTRK fusions occur at low frequency in many tumour types, including colorectal, lung, thyroid and pancreatic cancers, and at high frequency in rare tumours such as secretory breast carcinoma and infantile fibrosarcoma. Fusion-positive cancers can be treated with TRK inhibitor drugs such as larotrectinib and entrectinib, among the first "tumour-agnostic" cancer therapies approved.

The trk oncogene itself was originally discovered as a transforming gene in a human colon carcinoma, from which the receptor family takes its name (tropomyosin receptor kinase, after the tropomyosin fusion partner in the original oncogene).
