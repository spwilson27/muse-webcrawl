Khoisan languages

Khoisan languages is a name for the non-Bantu languages of southern and eastern Africa characterised by their use of click consonants. Once treated as a single language family, the grouping is now generally considered a convenient label for several unrelated families — principally Khoe-Kwadi, Kx'a and Tuu — together with the Hadza and Sandawe isolates of Tanzania.

Click consonants

Khoisan languages are famous for click consonants, which function as ordinary speech sounds. Some, such as Taa (!Xóõ), have among the largest consonant inventories of any language, with dozens of distinct clicks combined with various phonation types and tones.

Status

Most Khoisan languages are endangered. Historically spoken by hunter-gatherer and pastoralist peoples across a wide area, they have been displaced by Bantu languages and, more recently, by Afrikaans and English. The best-known surviving languages include Khoekhoegowab (Nama/Damara) in Namibia, with well over 100,000 speakers, and Juǀʼhoan in Namibia and Botswana.
