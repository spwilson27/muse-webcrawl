OpenCourseWare

OpenCourseWare (OCW) is course material — syllabi, lecture notes, videos, assignments and exams — created by universities and shared freely on the web for use by students and self-learners worldwide. The movement began with MIT OpenCourseWare, announced in 2001 and launched in 2002, which eventually published materials from essentially all MIT courses.

History and spread

MIT's initiative, supported by foundations including the William and Flora Hewlett Foundation, inspired a global consortium (later Open Education Consortium, now Open Education Global). Hundreds of institutions published OCW, typically under Creative Commons licences. OCW is credited with helping seed the wider open educational resources (OER) movement and, later, massive open online courses (MOOCs), though OCW itself generally offers no enrolment, credit or instructor interaction.
