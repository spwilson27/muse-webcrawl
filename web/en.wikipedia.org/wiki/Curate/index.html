Curate


Image placeholder: Curate

A curate is a person who is invested with the cure (care) of souls of a parish. In the Church of England, the term historically referred to the priest in charge of a parish (the perpetual curate), but in modern usage it most commonly denotes an assistant priest, often a newly ordained deacon or priest, who serves under the incumbent (vicar or rector) while completing training.

History

Originally, the curate was the priest who actually exercised the cure of souls where the rector was non-resident. The "perpetual curate" was a distinct benefice holder in parishes where tithes had been impropriated. The role changed over the nineteenth and twentieth centuries as stipendiary assistant curates became standard in larger parishes.

Other uses

In the Roman Catholic Church, a curate (French: curé) can be the parish priest himself. The verb "to curate" and the noun "curator" derive from the same Latin root, curare, meaning to care for.
