Lucha libre (Spanish for "freestyle wrestling") is the term for professional wrestling in Mexico and other Spanish-speaking countries. It is characterised by colourful masks, rapid sequences of holds and manoeuvres, and high-flying aerial techniques. Wrestlers, known as luchadores, traditionally divide into técnicos (the "good guys") and rudos (the "bad guys"), and matches commonly feature trios (tríos) of wrestlers.

The mask

The mask (máscara) is central to lucha libre: it embodies a luchador's identity and persona, and losing it in a wager match (lucha de apuestas) is a profound disgrace marking the end of a masked career. A wrestler who is unmasked may not legally remask under the same identity in sanctioned competition. Masks trace their introduction to the 1930s, when promoter Salvador Lutteroth founded Empresa Mexicana de Lucha Libre (now CMLL) in 1933, often called the world's oldest wrestling promotion.

Style and rules

Lucha libre emphasises acrobatics: dives to the floor (topes), springboard attacks and intricate submission chains. Tag matches are often contested under rules where a legal tag is made simply by a partner entering as the other leaves the ring. The most celebrated luchadores—El Santo, Blue Demon and Mil Máscaras—became folk heroes who crossed into film and popular culture.

Promotions

The two dominant Mexican promotions are Consejo Mundial de Lucha Libre (CMLL), which runs the historic Arena México in Mexico City, and Lucha Libre AAA Worldwide (AAA). Lucha libre's masked aesthetic has influenced wrestling worldwide, particularly through stars such as Rey Mysterio.
