Chamber of commerce

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A chamber of commerce is a form of business network, for example, a local organization of businesses whose goal is to further the interests of businesses. Business owners in towns and cities form these local societies to advocate on behalf of the business community. Local businesses are members, and they elect a board of directors or executive council to set policy for the chamber. The board or council then hires a president, CEO or executive director, plus staff appropriate to the size, to run the organization.

The first chamber of commerce was founded in 1599 in Marseille, France. The first in the English-speaking world was founded in Jersey in 1768. The world's largest chamber of commerce is generally considered to be the United States Chamber of Commerce, founded in 1912.

History

The term "chamber" derives from the Latin camera, meaning a room or vault. The Chamber of Commerce of Marseille, recognized as one of the oldest, was created in 1599 by the city council to represent merchants and protect trade. In the 18th century, chambers spread through Europe and North America; the New York Chamber of Commerce was founded in 1768, the same year as the Jersey chamber, among the earliest in the English-speaking world. The British Chambers of Commerce was founded in 1860, and the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC), headquartered in Paris, was founded in 1919 to promote international trade and investment.

Characteristics

Chambers of commerce are typically classified by their scope: local, regional, state, national, and international, as well as bilateral chambers that promote trade between two countries (such as an American chamber in a foreign country). They may be compulsory-membership bodies under public law, as in much of continental Europe (for example Germany's Industrie- und Handelskammern), or purely voluntary associations, as in the United States and the United Kingdom.

Activities

  • Advocacy and lobbying – representing business interests before local, regional and national government.
  • Networking – events, mixers and committees that connect member businesses.
  • Services – issuing certificates of origin, providing trade documentation, arbitration, and business advice.
  • Promotion – tourism promotion, trade fairs and economic development marketing for their region.
