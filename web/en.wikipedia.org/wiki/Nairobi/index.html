Nairobi is the capital and largest city of Kenya. Its name comes from the Maasai phrase Enkare Nyorobi, "the place of cool waters", referring to the Nairobi River. Founded in 1899 as a rail depot on the Uganda Railway, it became the capital of British East Africa in 1907 and of independent Kenya in 1963. At about 1,700 m above sea level, Nairobi has a mild highland climate unusual for the tropics.

City and sights

Nairobi is famous for the Nairobi National Park (established 1946), where lions, rhinos and giraffes roam against a backdrop of the city skyline — often called the world's only wildlife capital. Other landmarks include the Kenyatta International Convention Centre, the Nairobi National Museum, the Karen Blixen Museum (the Out of Africa author's home), and the Sheldrick Wildlife Trust's elephant nursery. The city hosts the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) and UN-Habitat headquarters in Gigiri, making it a diplomatic hub for Africa comparable to Tanzania's regional role further south.

Economy and challenges

Nairobi is East Africa's financial and technology hub ("Silicon Savannah"), home to the Nairobi Securities Exchange, regional banks, and the M-PESA mobile-money ecosystem pioneered by Safaricom. It is also a city of stark contrasts: wealthy suburbs and gleaming towers stand near Kibera, one of Africa's largest informal settlements. Congestion, water supply, and inequality are persistent challenges as the metropolitan population keeps growing.
