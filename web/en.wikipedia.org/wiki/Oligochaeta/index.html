Oligochaeta

Oligochaeta is a subclass of animals in the phylum Annelida, consisting of many types of aquatic and terrestrial worms, including all of the various earthworms. Specifically, oligochaetes are distinguished by having relatively few bristles (chaetae) on each segment, unlike the many-bristled polychaetes, and by the absence of parapodia.

Characteristics

Oligochaetes range from a fraction of a millimetre to over 3 metres in length (the giant Gippsland earthworm). They possess a clitellum, a thickened glandular section of the body wall used in reproduction, which is present year-round in mature individuals. All are hermaphrodites; reproduction is typically sexual, with cocoons produced by the clitellum, though some species reproduce asexually by fragmentation.

Ecology

Terrestrial oligochaetes — the earthworms — are major soil engineers, aerating and mixing soil and recycling organic matter. Aquatic oligochaetes such as Tubifex worms live in sediments of lakes and rivers, often in polluted waters with low oxygen, and are widely used as fish food and as indicators of water quality.

Classification

The group includes more than 8,000 described species in families such as Lumbricidae (the common earthworms), Tubificidae, Enchytraeidae (pot worms), and Megascolecidae.
