Metropolitan area

A metropolitan area or metro is a region consisting of a densely populated urban agglomeration and its surrounding territories that share industries, commercial areas, transport networks, infrastructures and housing. A metropolitan area usually comprises multiple cities, large towns, administrative divisions and adjacent rural areas, all socially and economically integrated with the core city. As social, economic and political institutions have changed, metropolitan areas have become key economic and political regions in developed countries.

Definitions

There is no universal definition of a metropolitan area; statistical agencies define it for their own purposes, usually on the basis of commuting flows and population density. In the United States, the Office of Management and Budget defines a Metropolitan Statistical Area (MSA) as a core urban area of at least 50,000 inhabitants plus adjacent counties with a high degree of social and economic integration as measured by commuting; smaller cores (10,000–50,000) form Micropolitan Statistical Areas, and neighbouring MSAs may be grouped into Combined Statistical Areas. The European Union and OECD use "functional urban areas" based on commuting patterns, while Japan defines metropolitan areas (toshi-ken) around central cities of 50,000 or more.

Global cities and megacities

The largest metropolitan areas — such as Tokyo (the world's largest, with roughly 37 million people in its greater area), Delhi, Shanghai, São Paulo, Cairo, Mexico City and New York — function as command centres of the global economy. A metropolitan area exceeding ten million inhabitants is often termed a megacity. Contiguous metropolitan areas can merge into a megalopolis, such as the Boston–Washington corridor in the north-eastern United States or the Taiheiyō Belt in Japan.

Governance

Metropolitan areas rarely coincide with a single local government. Fragmentation among municipalities complicates transport planning, housing and taxation, leading some countries to create metropolitan governments or authorities — for example Transport for London, the Verband Region Stuttgart, or special-purpose transit districts in the United States.
