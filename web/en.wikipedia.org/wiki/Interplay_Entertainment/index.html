Interplay Entertainment Corp. is an American video game developer and publisher based in Beverly Hills, California. Founded in 1983 by Brian Fargo as Interplay Productions, the company became one of the most influential publishers of computer role-playing games in the 1980s and 1990s.

History

Interplay began by developing text adventures and early role-playing games such as The Bard's Tale series and Wasteland (1988). In the 1990s it published landmark titles including Fallout (1997) and Fallout 2, Planescape: Torment, and — through its Black Isle Studios division and its relationship with BioWare — the Baldur's Gate series. Its slogan, "By gamers. For gamers.", reflected its developer-focused culture.

Financial difficulties in the early 2000s led to the sale of assets and the closure of Black Isle Studios in 2003. Interplay sold the Fallout intellectual property to Bethesda Softworks in 2007 while retaining a licence to develop a Fallout massively multiplayer game, a project that was later cancelled amid litigation. In later years the company largely operated as a licensor of its back catalogue, which also includes Earthworm Jim, Descent, MDK and Battle Chess.

Notable games

Interplay's catalogue includes The Bard's Tale (1985), Wasteland (1988), Fallout (1997), Fallout 2 (1998), Baldur's Gate (1998, publisher), Planescape: Torment (1999), and Icewind Dale (2000).
