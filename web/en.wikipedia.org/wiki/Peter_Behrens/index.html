Peter Behrens

BornApril 14, 1868, Hamburg, German Empire
DiedFebruary 27, 1940, Berlin, Germany
OccupationArchitect, designer, painter

Peter Behrens (April 14, 1868 – February 27, 1940) was a German architect, graphic designer and painter, and a pioneer of modernism and industrial design. He is best known for his work for the electrical company AEG, for whom he designed products, graphics, advertising and factory buildings in a unified corporate identity — one of the earliest comprehensive examples of corporate design.

Career

Behrens began as a painter and graphic artist associated with the Jugendstil movement and the Darmstadt Artists' Colony. In 1907 he was appointed artistic adviser to AEG in Berlin. His AEG Turbine Hall (1909) in Moabit, Berlin, with its steel frame and vast glazed gable, became a landmark of modern industrial architecture. He designed AEG products such as electric kettles, fans and lamps, applying systematic, standardised forms suited to mass production.

Influence

Behrens's Berlin office employed three architects who would define twentieth-century modernism: Walter Gropius, Ludwig Mies van der Rohe and Le Corbusier. He was a founding member of the Deutscher Werkbund and later designed buildings including the Hoechst Administration Building in Frankfurt and the German embassy in Saint Petersburg. His integration of architecture, product design and typography makes him a founding figure of industrial design.
