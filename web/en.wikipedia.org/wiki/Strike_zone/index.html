Strike zone

In baseball, the strike zone is the volume of space through which a pitch must pass in order to be called a strike, assuming the batter does not swing. If such a pitch is not swung at and is not called a strike, it is called a ball. The strike zone is defined in the Official Baseball Rules as the area over home plate whose upper limit is a horizontal line at the midpoint between the top of the shoulders and the top of the uniform pants, and whose lower limit is a line at the hollow beneath the kneecap; the zone is determined from the batter's stance as the batter is prepared to swing.

Enforcement

In practice, the called strike zone varies among umpires and has historically differed from the rulebook definition; studies using pitch-tracking systems (such as PITCHf/x and Statcast) have shown the effective zone tends to be wider than the plate and lower at the bottom than the written rules suggest, and that it has changed measurably over time. The introduction of automated ball-strike challenge systems in the minor leagues and their testing at higher levels has renewed debate about the zone's exact boundaries.

History

The vertical limits of the zone have been redefined several times: changes in 1950, 1963 (expanding the zone, contributing to the "second deadball era"), 1969, and 1988 adjusted the top and bottom boundaries in attempts to balance pitching and hitting.
