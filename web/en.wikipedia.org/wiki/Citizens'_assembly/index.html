Citizens' assembly

A citizens' assembly is a group of people selected by lottery from the general population to deliberate on issues of public policy and make recommendations to political decision-makers. It is a form of deliberative democracy and an example of sortition in politics. Members are usually chosen to be broadly representative of the population by demographics such as age, gender, region, and socio-economic status.

Process

A typical assembly runs through learning, consultation, and deliberation phases: members hear evidence from experts and stakeholders, question them, discuss options in facilitated small groups, and finally vote on recommendations. Assemblies are advisory in most cases, though governments sometimes commit in advance to act on or vote upon the recommendations.

Notable examples

The Citizens' Assembly of British Columbia (2004) recommended a new electoral system, later put to referendum. Ireland's Constitutional Convention (2012) and Citizens' Assembly (2016–2018) addressed issues including marriage equality and abortion, leading to successful referendums. France's Citizens' Convention on Climate (2019–2020) produced 149 proposals on reducing greenhouse gas emissions. The United Kingdom has used citizens' assemblies on topics such as social care and climate change.
