Yakitori

Yakitori (Japanese: 焼き鳥, lit. "grilled bird") is a Japanese type of skewered chicken. Its preparation involves skewering the meat with kushi (串), a type of skewer typically made of steel, bamboo, or similar materials. Afterwards, they are grilled over a charcoal fire. During or after cooking, the meat is typically seasoned with tare sauce or salt.

Preparation

Yakitori is traditionally cooked over binchōtan charcoal, which burns at a high temperature and produces little smoke. The chicken is cut into small, uniform pieces and threaded onto skewers. Cuts used include thigh (momo), breast (mune), skin (kawa), cartilage (nankotsu), heart (hatsu), liver (rebā), and meatballs formed from minced chicken (tsukune).

Seasoning

Two principal styles of seasoning are used: shio (salt), which brings out the flavour of the meat itself, and tare, a sweetened soy-based sauce made with mirin, sake, soy sauce, and sugar, in which the skewer is dipped during and after grilling. Tare pots in long-established shops are continually replenished, developing a deep, accumulated flavour.

Yakitori restaurants

Yakitori is sold in specialist yakitori-ya and in izakaya, where it is commonly eaten as an accompaniment to beer or other drinks. In Japan, districts of small yakitori stalls under railway arches — such as Yūrakuchō in Tokyo — are well known. In a broader sense, skewered grilled foods of other kinds (pork, vegetables such as shiitake mushrooms and negi scallions) are also served at yakitori shops, even though strictly they are kushiyaki.
