Howth gun-running


The harbour at Howth, County Dublin.

The Howth gun-running was the transport of arms and ammunition by yacht from Germany to Ireland on 26 July 1914 by the Irish Volunteers. Erskine Childers, helped by his wife Molly Childers, the English writer Mary Spring Rice, and a crew of Irish nationalist volunteers, sailed from Milford Haven to Hamburg aboard his yacht the Asgard, where they loaded a cargo of 1,500 Mauser Model 1871 rifles and 45,000 rounds of ammunition bought from a dealer in Hamburg.

Landing

The Asgard sailed into Howth harbour in broad daylight on Sunday, 26 July 1914. The landing was carried out openly and without secrecy: within a few hours, hundreds of metropolitan police and soldiers were ordered to Howth, but failed to seize the weapons. The guns were openly landed and distributed to members of the Irish Volunteers summoned for the occasion.

Bachelor's Walk shootings

On the return to Dublin, King's Own Scottish Borderers encountered a column of Volunteers at Bachelor's Walk, and after an exchange with civilians, opened fire on the crowd, killing three people and wounding some 38. Another man died later in hospital. The incident, known as the Bachelor's Walk shootings, was a significant moment in the build-up to the Easter Rising of 1916, since the weapons landed at Howth played a key role in the events of Easter Week.

Background

The gun-running was a response to the formation of the Ulster Volunteer Force, which in April 1914 had landed around 25,000 rifles at Larne, County Antrim, without any interference from the authorities. The Irish National Volunteers, led by John Redmond and later Eoin MacNeill, resolved to obtain arms themselves.
