Edenton Tea Party

The Edenton Tea Party was a political protest organised by women in Edenton, North Carolina, in October 1774. Fifty-one women, led by Penelope Barker, signed a statement resolving to boycott British tea and other goods in support of the colonial resistance to British taxation policies.

Background

Following the Boston Tea Party of December 1773 and the passage of the Coercive Acts, colonial protests spread. On 25 October 1774, the women of Edenton met at the home of Elizabeth King and signed a formal resolution, an unusually public political act for women of the period.

Reaction

The protest was reported in London newspapers and satirised in a famous 1775 cartoon mocking the women, "A Society of Patriotic Ladies, at Edenton in North Carolina". The event is remembered as one of the earliest organised political actions by women in the American colonies.
