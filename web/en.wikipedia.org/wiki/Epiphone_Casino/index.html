Epiphone Casino

The Epiphone Casino is a thinline hollow-bodied electric guitar manufactured by Epiphone since 1961. It is fitted with two single-coil P-90 pickups and a fully hollow archtop body, giving it a warm, resonant tone distinct from its close relative, the semi-hollow Gibson ES-330, to which it is otherwise very similar.

History

The Casino was introduced in 1961, shortly after Gibson acquired Epiphone, as part of a line of Epiphone versions of Gibson designs produced in Kalamazoo, Michigan. Production of the original US-made model ended around 1970; the model was later reissued with manufacture in Japan and then Korea and China, and US production resumed in the 2020s.

Notable players

The Casino is strongly associated with the Beatles: Paul McCartney bought one in 1964 and used it for solos on songs such as "Taxman" and "Ticket to Ride"; John Lennon and George Harrison acquired Casinos in 1966, and Lennon famously stripped his to a natural finish and played it at the Beatles' 1969 rooftop concert and throughout his solo career. Other users include Keith Richards, Dave Davies, Noel Gallagher, and Paul Weller.
