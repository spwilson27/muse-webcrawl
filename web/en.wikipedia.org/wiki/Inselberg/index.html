Inselberg

An inselberg (from German "island mountain") is an isolated rock hill, knob or small mountain rising abruptly from a gently sloping or level surrounding plain, especially in arid and semi-arid regions.

Formation

Inselbergs are residual landforms: resistant rock (often granite, gneiss or quartzite) survives while surrounding material is eroded away, through processes including pediplanation and deep weathering followed by stripping. Famous examples include Uluru in Australia, Sugarloaf Mountain in Rio de Janeiro, and the granite domes of Zimbabwe.

Types and ecology

Forms range from bornhardts (dome-shaped) to castle koppies (blocky tors). Their isolated, harsh surfaces host distinctive flora and fauna, acting as ecological islands and refuges.
