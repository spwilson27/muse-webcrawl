Melanocortin 4 receptor

MC4R (melanocortin 4 receptor)

Gene: MC4R, chromosome 18q21.32 (human)

Family: G protein-coupled receptors

Ligands: α-MSH (agonist); AgRP (antagonist/inverse agonist)

The melanocortin 4 receptor (MC4R) is a G protein-coupled receptor expressed predominantly in the central nervous system, including the paraventricular nucleus of the hypothalamus. It binds the melanocortin peptide α-MSH and is a central node in the leptin–melanocortin pathway controlling appetite and energy expenditure.

Function

When leptin signals adequate fat stores, POMC neurons release α-MSH, activating MC4R to suppress food intake and increase energy expenditure. The hunger hormone pathway opposes this via AgRP, an endogenous antagonist of MC4R released from AgRP/NPY neurons during fasting.

Genetics and obesity

Loss-of-function mutations in MC4R are the commonest known cause of monogenic severe obesity, found in a few percent of people with early-onset obesity; inheritance is typically autosomal dominant with hyperphagia beginning in childhood. Conversely, gain-of-function variants are protective against obesity and type 2 diabetes. Genome-wide association studies also implicate common variants near MC4R in population variation in body-mass index.

Therapeutics

The MC4R agonist setmelanotide is approved for chronic weight management in certain rare genetic obesities (POMC, PCSK1, or leptin-receptor deficiency, and Bardet–Biedl syndrome), demonstrating that bypassing upstream defects to activate MC4R restores satiety signalling.
