Alma Gluck

Alma Gluck (1884–1938), born Reba Fiersohn in Iași, Romania, was an American soprano celebrated on operatic stages and as one of the best-selling recording artists of the early phonograph era. Her family emigrated to New York when she was a child; she worked as a stenographer before serious vocal study with Arturo Buzzi-Peccia.

Career

Gluck made her debut at the Metropolitan Opera in 1909 in Massenet's Werther and sang leading lyric roles there and in Europe. She became even more famous in recital, and her Victor Red Seal records sold in the millions; her 1914 recording of "Carry Me Back to Old Virginny" was among the first classical recordings to sell a million copies. She helped popularise song recitals across small-town America.

Personal life

She was married first to insurance executive Bernard Glick, then to the violinist Efrem Zimbalist, with whom she gave joint recitals. Her daughter from her first marriage was the author Marcia Davenport. Gluck retired in the late 1920s and died in New York in 1938.
