The Cigar Lake Mine is a large underground uranium mine in northern Saskatchewan, Canada, on the southwestern shore of Cigar Lake in the Athabasca Basin. It is one of the world's largest producing uranium mines and holds the world's largest undeveloped high-grade uranium reserves known when it was discovered.

Geology and development

The orebody, discovered in 1981, lies about 450 metres underground at the unconformity between the Athabasca sandstone and the basement rock, with grades far higher than typical uranium deposits. The weak, water-bearing ground made conventional mining impractical: development proceeded by freezing the surrounding rock and mining the ore remotely with high-pressure water jets (jet boring), a technique pioneered at commercial scale here.

Flooding and production

Major water inflows flooded the workings in 2006 and again in 2008, delaying production for years while the mine was dewatered and the ground-freezing programme extended. Production finally began in 2014, with ore trucked to the McClean Lake mill for processing. At full capacity the mine produces about 18 million pounds of U₃O₈ per year and has repeatedly been the world's largest uranium producer.
