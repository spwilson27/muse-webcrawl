Strata title

Strata title is a form of ownership devised for multi-level apartment blocks and horizontal subdivisions with shared areas. The concept was pioneered in the Australian state of New South Wales in 1961, with the Conveyancing (Strata Titles) Act 1961, and has since spread to many jurisdictions including New Zealand, Singapore, Malaysia, British Columbia (Canada), South Africa, and parts of the Middle East.

How it works

A strata plan divides a building or land into "lots" (individually owned units, apartments, or townhouses) and "common property" (foyers, stairways, driveways, gardens, and the building structure itself). Owners receive a registered title to their lot and an automatic share in the owners corporation (also called a body corporate or strata corporation), which owns and manages the common property. Owners pay levies to fund maintenance, insurance, and administration, and are bound by by-laws regulating matters such as noise, pets, parking, and alterations.

Governance and disputes

The owners corporation elects a committee, holds annual general meetings, and may engage a strata manager. Legislation in most jurisdictions sets out rules for meetings, financial reporting, defect rectification, and dispute resolution through tribunals. Strata title differs from the North American condominium model mainly in legal origin, though the practical structures are similar; it differs from company title, an older Australian system in which a company owns the building and residents hold shares.
