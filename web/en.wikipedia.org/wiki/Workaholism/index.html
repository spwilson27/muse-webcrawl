Workaholism

Workaholism is a condition characterised by an uncontrollable need to work incessantly. The term, formed by combining "work" and "alcoholism", was popularised by psychologist Wayne Oates in 1971. Workaholism is associated with long working hours, perfectionism, impaired health and strained family relationships.

Definition and study

Researchers distinguish enthusiastic, compulsive workaholics from engaged workers who enjoy their jobs. Scales such as the Bergen Work Addiction Scale measure symptoms including preoccupation with work, withdrawal-like restlessness when not working, and continuing to work despite harm. Workaholism correlates with burnout, sleep problems and cardiovascular risk, and is debated as to whether it constitutes an addiction like other behavioural addictions.
