The United States Air Force Air Demonstration Squadron, commonly known as the Thunderbirds, is the air demonstration squadron of the United States Air Force. The squadron, based at Nellis Air Force Base in Nevada, tours the United States and much of the world performing aerobatic formation and solo flying in specially marked aircraft, and serves as a public-relations and recruiting asset for the Air Force.

History

The team was activated on 25 May 1953 at Luke Air Force Base, Arizona, flying the Republic F-84G Thunderjet. Its name was chosen in reference to the Thunderbird of Native American legend, prominent in the folklore of the American Southwest. Over the decades the Thunderbirds have flown the F-84F, the F-100 Super Sabre (the first supersonic demonstration aircraft), the F-105, the F-4 Phantom II, and the T-38 Talon, before converting to the F-16 Fighting Falcon in 1983.

The show

A typical performance features six F-16s: four flying tight formations — including the signature diamond, with wingtips only a few feet apart — and two solo aircraft performing opposing and high-speed manoeuvres, all accompanied by coloured smoke.

Incidents

The team's history includes the 1982 "Diamond Crash" at Indian Springs, in which four pilots were killed during practice, leading to a fleet-wide review and the transition to the F-16.
