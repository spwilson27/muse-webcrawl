Armet

Italian armet, c. 1450

TypeHelmet
Period of use15th – early 16th century
OriginItaly

The armet is a type of helmet which was developed in the 15th century. It was extensively used in Italy, France, England, the Low Countries and Spain. It was distinguished by being the first helmet of its era to completely enclose the head while being compact and light enough to move with the wearer. Its use was essentially restricted to the fully armoured man-at-arms.

Description

The armet consists of a close-fitting skull bowl, two large cheekpieces hinged at the sides which meet at the chin, and a pivoted visor. The cheekpieces, locked at the front, protect the jaw and throat, and a disc (rondel) at the back often guarded the strap securing the visor. Unlike the sallet, which sometimes terminated in a long tail, or the great bascinet, the armet fitted snugly without restricting head movement.

Use and decline

Armets formed part of fine Milanese and Gothic plate harnesses. By the early 16th century they were being superseded by the close helm, which offered similar protection with a simpler construction, and by the burgonet for lighter troops.
