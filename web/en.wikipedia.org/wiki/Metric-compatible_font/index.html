Metric-compatible font

Two fonts with identical metrics produce identical line breaks

A metric-compatible font (or metric-equivalent font) is a typeface that has the same character advance widths, line heights, and other spacing metrics as another font, so that substituting one for the other does not change the layout of a document—text reflows identically, with the same line breaks and page breaks, even though the visual letterforms differ.

Purpose

Metric compatibility is especially important in free and open-source software, where widely used proprietary fonts cannot always be distributed. Metric-compatible free fonts allow documents created with the proprietary fonts to be rendered and printed with the same pagination on systems that use the free substitutes. Well-known examples include the Liberation fonts (Liberation Serif, Sans, and Mono), metrically compatible with Times New Roman, Arial, and Courier New respectively; Croscore fonts such as Carlito (compatible with Calibri) and Caladea (compatible with Cambria); Gelasio, compatible with Georgia; and Liberation Sans Narrow, compatible with Arial Narrow. Google's Noto and other projects have also produced metric-compatible designs.

Limitations

Metric compatibility guarantees only identical spacing, not identical appearance: the substitute font's glyphs are drawn in a different style, and kerning pairs, hinting, and glyph coverage may differ, leading to small visual or rendering differences even when the layout metrics agree.
