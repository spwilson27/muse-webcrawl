Pecan

Scientific nameCarya illinoinensis
FamilyJuglandaceae
Native rangeSouth-central North America
UsesFood (nuts), timber

The pecan (Carya illinoinensis) is a species of hickory native to the south-central United States and Mexico, growing naturally along river valleys from Illinois and Indiana south to Texas and northern Mexico. Its edible seed is a drupe nut widely used in cooking.

Description

The pecan tree is a large deciduous tree, growing to 20–40 metres tall, with pinnate leaves and a spreading crown. The nut is enclosed in a husk that splits into four sections when ripe, releasing the thin-shelled, elongated brown nut with its rich, buttery kernel.

Cultivation and use

Pecans are a major commercial nut crop in the United States and Mexico; Georgia, New Mexico and Texas are leading producers. The word "pecan" derives from an Algonquian word meaning a nut requiring a stone to crack. Pecans are eaten raw, roasted and salted, and are the key ingredient of pecan pie, a classic of Southern American cuisine. The pecan is the state tree and state nut of Texas.
