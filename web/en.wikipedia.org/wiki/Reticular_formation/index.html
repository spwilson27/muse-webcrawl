Reticular formation

The reticular formation is a set of interconnected nuclei and nerve fibres located throughout the brainstem — the medulla, pons and midbrain. Rather than a single structure, it is a diffuse network ('reticular' means net-like) involved in many fundamental functions.

Functions

The reticular activating system (RAS) projects to the thalamus and cortex and regulates arousal, consciousness and the sleep–wake cycle; damage to it can cause coma. Reticular nuclei also contribute to autonomic control of breathing and cardiovascular function, motor tone and posture via the reticulospinal tracts, pain modulation, and the coordination of reflexes such as swallowing and vomiting.

Neurochemistry

The network contains the major monoamine systems, including the serotonergic raphe nuclei, the noradrenergic locus coeruleus and dopaminergic groups, which broadly modulate brain state and are targets of many psychoactive drugs, including anaesthetics.
