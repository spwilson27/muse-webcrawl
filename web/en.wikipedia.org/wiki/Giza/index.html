Giza is a city in northern Egypt, on the west bank of the Nile River opposite Cairo, with which it forms part of the Greater Cairo metropolis. It is world-famous for the nearby Giza plateau, the necropolis of ancient Memphis, where stand the three great pyramids of the Fourth Dynasty and the Great Sphinx — among the oldest and most iconic monuments of ancient Egypt.

The Giza plateau

The Giza pyramid complex comprises the Great Pyramid of Khufu (c. 2580–2560 BCE), the only surviving wonder of the ancient world, the slightly smaller Pyramid of Khafre behind its guardian, the Great Sphinx, and the Pyramid of Menkaure, together with subsidiary pyramids, mastaba cemeteries and the remains of the workers' town. The complex is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the focus of Egyptology since the nineteenth century; the Grand Egyptian Museum, built near the plateau, is designed to display the full Tutankhamun collection.

The modern city

Modern Giza grew with Cairo's expansion across the Nile and is one of Egypt's largest cities, home to Cairo University's main campus, the Giza Zoo and dense residential districts stretching toward the desert edge. Tourism centred on the plateau remains central to its economy.
