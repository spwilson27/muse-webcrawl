Overview

North Ossetia–Alania, officially the Republic of North Ossetia–Alania, is a republic of Russia situated in the North Caucasus of Eastern Europe. Its capital is Vladikavkaz. The Ossetians are an Iranian-speaking people traditionally regarded as descendants of the medieval Alans, a connection reflected in the republic's name.

History

The region came under Russian control in the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries as the empire expanded into the Caucasus; Vladikavkaz was founded as a fortress in 1784. Under the Soviet Union the territory became the North Ossetian Autonomous Oblast and later an autonomous republic within Russia.

After the Soviet collapse, North Ossetia received refugees from conflicts in neighbouring South Ossetia and was the scene of the Beslan school siege of 2004, in which more than 330 hostages, many of them children, were killed. An ethnic conflict with neighbouring Ingushetia over the Prigorodny District erupted in 1992.

Geography and economy

The republic lies on the northern slopes of the Greater Caucasus, with the Terek River flowing through its lowlands. Agriculture, food processing, and nonferrous metallurgy are important to the economy, and the Georgian Military Road through the mountains links Russia with Georgia.
