Pomacentridae

Pomacentridae is a family of ray-finned fishes, commonly known as damselfishes and clownfishes. They are found primarily in tropical marine waters, especially on coral reefs of the Indo-Pacific and Atlantic. The family contains around 400 species in about 30 genera.

Description

Most pomacentrids are small, brightly coloured, deep-bodied fishes, usually less than 15 cm in length. Many species are highly territorial, defending feeding patches of algae. Males typically guard and aerate the eggs, which are laid on a prepared surface.

Clownfish and anemones

The clownfishes (subfamily Amphiprioninae) are famous for their mutualistic relationship with sea anemones: protected by a mucus layer from the anemone's sting, the fish gains shelter while defending and feeding its host.

Genera

Well-known genera include Amphiprion (clownfishes), Chromis, Dascyllus, Pomacentrus, Stegastes, and Abudefduf (sergeant majors). Damselfishes are popular in the marine aquarium trade.
