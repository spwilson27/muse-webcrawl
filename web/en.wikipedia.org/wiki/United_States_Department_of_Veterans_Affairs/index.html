United States Department of Veterans Affairs

VA
Formed: 21 July 1930 (Veterans Administration); Cabinet status 15 March 1989
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Motto: "To care for him who shall have borne the battle"

The United States Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) is an executive department of the federal government charged with providing lifelong healthcare services to eligible military veterans at its medical centers and outpatient clinics, providing benefits such as disability compensation, pensions, education, home loans, and managing the national cemeteries.

History

The Veterans Administration was created by executive order in 1930, consolidating the Veterans' Bureau, the Bureau of Pensions, and the National Homes for Disabled Volunteer Soldiers. In 1988 President Ronald Reagan signed legislation elevating it to a cabinet department, effective March 15, 1989.

Organization

The department comprises three main administrations: the Veterans Health Administration, which runs the largest integrated health care system in the United States; the Veterans Benefits Administration; and the National Cemetery Administration. The VA is the second-largest federal department by employees after the Department of Defense.

Benefits

Under laws such as the G.I. Bill, the VA provides education assistance, vocational rehabilitation, guaranteed home loans, and burial benefits to veterans, service members, and their survivors.
