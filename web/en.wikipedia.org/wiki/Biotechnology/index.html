Biotechnology

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Biotechnology is the use of living organisms, cells or biological molecules to make products and technologies. Traditional biotechnology — selective breeding and fermentation for bread, beer and cheese — is ancient; modern biotechnology dates from recombinant DNA methods of the 1970s and now spans medicine, agriculture and industry.

Methods and products

Core techniques include genetic engineering, PCR, DNA Sequencing, cell culture and Cloning. Landmark products include recombinant human Insulin (first marketed in 1982), monoclonal antibody drugs, genetically modified crops, industrial enzymes and mRNA vaccines. Gene therapy and genome editing with CRISPR extend biotechnology into direct treatment of genetic disease.

Issues

Biotechnology raises debates over the safety and labelling of genetically modified food, patenting of genes and organisms, access to expensive biologic medicines, and the ethics of editing human embryos. Biosecurity — preventing misuse of powerful techniques — is a growing concern.
