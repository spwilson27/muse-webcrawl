Animal Welfare Act of 1966

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Animal Welfare Act of 1966 (AWA) is a United States federal law that regulates the treatment of animals in research, teaching, testing, exhibition, transport, and by dealers. It is the principal federal statute governing the care and use of animals in laboratories in the United States and is administered by the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) through its Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS).

History

Public concern about stolen pets being sold to laboratories, highlighted by magazine reporting on dog dealers and the case of Pepper, a Dalmatian that disappeared and was traced to a research hospital, galvanised Congress in 1966. The original act focused on licensing dealers and preventing the sale of stolen dogs and cats to research facilities. Major amendments followed in 1970, 1976, 1985, and later years: the 1970 amendments broadened coverage to more species and to exhibitors such as zoos and circuses; the 1985 Improved Standards for Laboratory Animals Act required measures to minimise pain and distress, exercise for dogs, and attention to the psychological well-being of primates, and mandated institutional animal care and use committees (IACUCs).

Scope and standards

The act sets minimum standards for housing, feeding, sanitation, veterinary care, and handling of covered warm-blooded animals, including dogs, cats, nonhuman primates, guinea pigs, hamsters, and rabbits. Research facilities, dealers, breeders, and exhibitors must be licensed or registered and are inspected by APHIS. Notably, the statute excludes purpose-bred rats, mice, and birds — which constitute the large majority of animals used in research — as well as farm animals used for food and fibre production.

Criticism and debate

Animal welfare advocates argue that the exclusions and limited enforcement weaken the law, while research institutions contend that the standards and IACUC review provide substantial oversight. The act remains the legal foundation for federal laboratory-animal oversight and is periodically amended, for example to extend standards to animal fighting ventures and pet protection during disasters.
