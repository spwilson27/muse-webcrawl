Koleda

Koleda (also Koliada, Koljada and related forms) is a traditional Slavic winter festival and the associated custom of carol singing, originally a pre-Christian celebration of the winter solstice that became incorporated into the Christmas and New Year season.

Customs

Groups of carolers — often young men or children, sometimes in costume or masks, and in some traditions led by a figure carrying a star or a goat effigy — go from house to house singing kolyadki (koleda songs) that wish the household health and abundance, receiving food, drink or money in return. The term also denotes the songs themselves and, in South Slavic traditions, festivities around Christmas Eve.

Origins

The name is commonly linked to the Roman calends. Scholars associate the festival's roots with pagan Slavic rites honouring the rebirth of the sun; after Christianisation the customs were adapted to celebrate the Nativity.
