Mayflower Compact

The Mayflower Compact, originally titled the "Agreement Between the Settlers of New Plymouth", was the first governing document of Plymouth Colony. It was signed aboard the ship Mayflower on 11 November 1620 (Old Style) by 41 of the ship's 101 passengers, while anchored at Cape Cod.

Background

The Mayflower had been bound for the Colony of Virginia, where the colonists held a patent, but storms drove the ship north to Cape Cod — outside the jurisdiction of their charter. Some of the "Strangers" (non-Separatist passengers) argued that this freed them from any obligation to the group. To prevent disorder, the Pilgrim leaders drafted a compact establishing a "civil Body Politick" that would enact "just and equal Laws" for the general good of the colony, to which all signers promised obedience.

Text and significance

The brief document, written in the style of a church covenant, combines loyalty to King James with the formation of a self-governing community. The original manuscript is lost; the text survives in William Bradford's Of Plymouth Plantation and in Mourt's Relation (1622). The Compact governed Plymouth until the colony was absorbed into the Province of Massachusetts Bay in 1691, and it is regarded as an early example of self-government and a precursor of later American constitutional ideas.
