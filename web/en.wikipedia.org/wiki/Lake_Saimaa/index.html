Lake Saimaa (Finnish: Saimaa) is the largest lake in Finland and the fourth-largest natural freshwater lake in Europe. It is a labyrinthine system of interconnected basins and channels in the southeast of the country, dotted with roughly 13,000 islands and fringed by an enormously long, indented shoreline. The cities of Lappeenranta, Imatra, Savonlinna, Mikkeli, Varkaus, and Joensuu lie on or near its shores.

Geography and navigation

Saimaa was formed by glacial retreat after the last ice age, and continuing post-glacial uplift gradually isolated it from the Baltic Sea. It drains eastward through the Vuoksi River to Lake Ladoga in Russia. The Saimaa Canal, opened in 1856 and rebuilt in the twentieth century, connects the lake from Lappeenranta to the Gulf of Finland near Vyborg, giving inland Finland a navigable route to the sea; canals and locks also link the lake's basins for commercial shipping, log floating historically, and pleasure boating.

Nature

The lake is home to the Saimaa ringed seal (Pusa hispida saimensis), one of the world's rarest and most endangered seals, a landlocked freshwater subspecies numbering only a few hundred individuals and protected by fishing restrictions and snow-den conservation. The lake also supports the endangered landlocked Saimaa salmon. Linnansaari and Kolovesi national parks protect representative stretches of the lake's island scenery.
