2011–2013 Russian protests

The 2011–2013 Russian protests, sometimes called the Snow Revolution, were a series of mass political demonstrations in Russia that began after the legislative election of 4 December 2011, amid widespread allegations of electoral fraud in favour of the ruling United Russia party.

Background

In September 2011, President Dmitry Medvedev announced that Prime Minister Vladimir Putin would run for president again in 2012, with Medvedev taking the post of prime minister — a "castling" move that angered many Russians. The December 2011 Duma elections were marred by reports of ballot stuffing and manipulation.

Course of the protests

Protests began on 5 December 2011 in Moscow. A rally on Bolotnaya Square on 10 December drew tens of thousands, and a larger demonstration on Sakharov Avenue on 24 December was estimated at up to 100,000 people — among the largest in Russia since the 1990s. The white ribbon became a symbol of the movement. Opposition figures including Alexei Navalny, Boris Nemtsov and Sergei Udaltsov played prominent roles.

Protests continued through the presidential election of March 2012, which Putin won. A demonstration on 6 May 2012, the eve of Putin's inauguration, ended in clashes with police and led to the "Bolotnaya Square case" prosecutions. New laws restricting protests and NGOs followed, and the movement gradually subsided during 2013.

Demands

Protesters demanded annulment of the election results, new fair elections, registration of opposition parties, release of political prisoners, and investigation of election officials.
