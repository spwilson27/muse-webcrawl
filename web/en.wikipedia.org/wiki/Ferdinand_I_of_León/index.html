Ferdinand I of León

Ferdinand I (c. 1015 – 27 December 1065), called the Great (el Magno), was the first king of León to also hold the kingdoms of Castile and Galicia together, ruling León and Galicia from 1037 and Castile from 1035. He is often regarded as the founder of the unified Christian monarchy of northern Spain.

Reign

The younger son of Sancho III of Navarre, Ferdinand received Castile as a kingdom on his father's death. In 1037 he defeated and killed his brother-in-law Bermudo III of León at the Battle of Tamarón, acquiring León and Galicia through his wife Sancha, Bermudo's sister. He subsequently campaigned against the Muslim taifa kingdoms, extracting tribute payments (parias) and capturing Coimbra in 1064.

Death and succession

On his death Ferdinand divided his realms among his three sons: Sancho received Castile, Alfonso received León, and García received Galicia — a partition that led to civil war among the brothers before Alfonso VI reunited the kingdoms.
