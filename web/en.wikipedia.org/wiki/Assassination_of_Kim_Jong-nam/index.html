Assassination of Kim Jong-nam

Kim Jong-nam, the eldest son of North Korean leader Kim Jong-il and half-brother of Kim Jong-un, was assassinated on 13 February 2017 at Kuala Lumpur International Airport in Malaysia. He was attacked with VX nerve agent by two women who smeared the chemical on his face; he died within about 20 minutes while being taken to hospital.

Background

Kim Jong-nam had once been considered the favourite to succeed his father, but fell from favour after a 2001 incident in which he was caught attempting to enter Japan on a false passport to visit Tokyo Disneyland. He lived largely in exile, mainly in Macau, and had occasionally criticised his family's regime.

The attack

On the morning of 13 February 2017, while Kim waited at a check-in area of the airport's budget terminal for a flight to Macau, two women — Siti Aisyah of Indonesia and Đoàn Thị Hương of Vietnam — approached him in sequence and applied a liquid to his face. Malaysian police later identified the substance as VX, a chemical weapon banned under the Chemical Weapons Convention. Kim sought help from airport staff, suffered seizures, and died in an ambulance en route to Putrajaya Hospital.

Investigation and trial

Both women claimed they believed they were participating in a television prank. They were charged with murder, but in March 2019 the charge against Aisyah was dropped, and Hương pleaded guilty to a lesser charge of causing injury and was released in May 2019. Malaysian police identified several North Korean suspects, including embassy staff, who left the country; North Korea denied involvement. The killing caused a diplomatic crisis between Malaysia and North Korea, including reciprocal travel bans and the expulsion of ambassadors.
