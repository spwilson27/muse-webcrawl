Barnett formula
Named afterJoel Barnett, Chief Secretary to the Treasury
Devised1978
PurposeAdjusting devolved administration budgets in line with changes in comparable English spending

The Barnett formula is a mechanism used by the Treasury in the United Kingdom to automatically adjust the amounts of public expenditure allocated to Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales to reflect changes in spending levels allocated to public services in England. It was devised in 1978 by Joel Barnett, then Chief Secretary to the Treasury, as a temporary measure ahead of planned devolution.

Operation

Under the formula, when spending on a devolved service in England changes, the corresponding block grant changes by a population-based proportion of the English change, multiplied by a "comparability percentage" reflecting how fully the service is devolved. Because the formula changes only the increment and not the historic baseline, it tends over time to equalize per-head spending across the UK — the so-called "Barnett squeeze".

Criticism

The formula has no statutory basis and has been criticized for not assessing relative need; Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have generally had higher identifiable spending per head than England. Successive reviews, including the Holtham Commission for Wales, have recommended needs-based alternatives, and reforms such as the Scottish fiscal framework have modified its application.
