Long Day's Journey into Night

Long Day's Journey into Night is a tragedy play in four acts by American playwright Eugene O'Neill, written 1939–1941 and published posthumously in 1956. It won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama and is widely considered his masterpiece and one of the finest American plays.

Long Day's Journey into Night
Written by: Eugene O'Neill
Characters: James, Mary, Jamie and Edmund Tyrone
Premiere: 1956 (Stockholm); Broadway 1956
Setting: Monte Cristo Cottage, August 1912

Synopsis

Over a single day and night, the Tyrone family confronts Mary Tyrone's morphine addiction, Edmund's tuberculosis, Jamie's alcoholism and James's miserliness and regrets. The semi-autobiographical play lays bare addiction, resentment and love.

Legacy

The 1962 film directed by Sidney Lumet starred Katharine Hepburn. Revivals remain staples of major theatres worldwide.
