Advanced Audio Coding

Advanced Audio Coding (AAC) is an audio coding standard for lossy digital audio compression. Designed to be the successor of the MP3 format, AAC generally achieves higher sound quality than MP3 encoders at the same bit rate. It was standardised by ISO and IEC as part of the MPEG-2 and MPEG-4 specifications and is the default or standard audio format for a wide range of platforms, including iTunes and the iPhone, Nintendo and PlayStation consoles, YouTube and digital radio (DAB+).

Technology

AAC uses a modified discrete cosine transform (MDCT) with larger block sizes than MP3, perceptual noise shaping, and improved joint-stereo and entropy coding, sampling at rates from 8 to 96 kHz and supporting up to 48 channels. Its profiles tailor the codec to different uses: Low Complexity (AAC-LC) for general use; High Efficiency AAC (HE-AAC), which adds spectral band replication, for low bitrates; and low-delay variants for communication. The extended HE-AAC with MPEG-D DRC (xHE-AAC) is used for streaming services requiring seamless loudness and bitrate adaptation.

Adoption and licensing

Apple's adoption of AAC as the iTunes Store and iPod format in 2003 drove mainstream uptake, and the format is now natively supported by virtually all operating systems, browsers and mobile devices. AAC is a patented format administered through a licensing pool, although the key patents have progressively expired; no licence is required for streaming or distributing content in AAC.
