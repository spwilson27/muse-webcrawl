Leonard Bernstein

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Leonard Bernstein conducting with his whole body in the 1960s: hair flying, baton a blur, in the ecstatic style of his podium years]

Leonard Bernstein (25 August 1918 – 14 October 1990) was an American conductor, composer, pianist and educator, one of the most famous musicians of the twentieth century. As music director of the New York Philharmonic (1958–69) and through his televised Young People's Concerts he became classical music's great populariser in America; as a composer he crossed every line between concert hall and Broadway: the musical West Side Story (1957), the film score On the Waterfront (1954), three symphonies, the ballets Fancy Free (1944) and the operetta Candide (1956).

Born in Lawrence, Massachusetts, to Ukrainian-Jewish immigrants, Bernstein studied at Harvard and Curtis, assisted Koussevitzky at Tanglewood, and shot to fame on 14 November 1943, substituting at a few hours' notice for the ill Bruno Walter with the New York Philharmonic on national radio — the most famous conducting debut in American history.

Conductor and communicator

Bernstein's podium manner — leaping, dancing, conducting with his face — divided critics and entranced publics; his Mahler recordings were central to that composer's post-war revival, and his late cycles with the Vienna Philharmonic (Beethoven, Brahms) and his 1989 Berlin performances of Beethoven's Ninth celebrating the Wall's fall ('Freiheit' substituted for 'Freude', in his legendary gesture) made him classical music's world citizen. The Charles Eliot Norton lectures at Harvard (1973, The Unanswered Question) remain a monument of music talk.

Composer and legacy

West Side Story, with Sondheim's lyrics and Robbins's choreography, is the Broadway canon's summit; his serious works — the 'Age of Anxiety' symphony, Chichester Psalms (1965), the theatre piece Mass (1971) — argue faith in a fractured century. Marrying Felicia Montealegre, raising three children, funding Black Panthers (the 'radical chic' party of legend) and chaining himself to causes, he lived at full voltage. His centenary in 2018 and Bradley Cooper's film Maestro (2023) renewed his public.
