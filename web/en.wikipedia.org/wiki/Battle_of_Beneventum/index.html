Battle of Beneventum

The Battle of Beneventum (275 BC) was the final battle of the Pyrrhic War, fought near the city of Beneventum in southern Italy between the Roman Republic, under the consul Manius Curius Dentatus, and the combined forces of King Pyrrhus of Epirus and his Samnite and Tarentine allies. The Roman victory ended Pyrrhus's five-year campaign in Italy and confirmed Rome's dominance over the Italian peninsula.

Pyrrhus's war elephants were thrown into confusion by Roman tactics at Beneventum.

Background

Pyrrhus had come to Italy in 280 BC at the request of Tarentum, winning costly victories over Rome at Heraclea (280 BC) and Asculum (279 BC) — the origin of the term "Pyrrhic victory". After an expedition to Sicily against Carthage and further fighting in Italy, he returned to face the Romans in Samnium in 275 BC.

The battle

According to the ancient sources, Pyrrhus attempted a night march to surprise the Roman camp near Beneventum, but his guides lost their way and the attack came at dawn, forfeiting surprise. In the ensuing fighting the Romans drove Pyrrhus's elephants back into his own lines — one account describes a wounded young elephant panicking and stampeding the others — and the Roman legions prevailed. Pyrrhus withdrew to Tarentum and soon sailed back to Epirus with the remnant of his army, reportedly remarking on leaving Italy that he left "a field of battle for Romans and Carthaginians".

Aftermath

Tarentum surrendered to Rome in 272 BC, completing Roman control of Magna Graecia and uniting Italy south of the Po under Roman hegemony. Pyrrhus was killed campaigning at Argos in Greece in 272 BC. The battle demonstrated that the manipular legion could defeat the Hellenistic phalanx-and-elephant system that had earlier prevailed at such cost, and it set the stage for Rome's confrontation with Carthage in the Punic Wars.
