Dhammayazika Pagoda

LocationPwasaw, near Bagan, Myanmar
ReligionTheravada Buddhism
BuilderKing Narapatisithu
Completed1196–1198

The Dhammayazika Pagoda is a Buddhist temple at Pwasaw, east of Bagan, Myanmar. It was built in 1196–1198 by King Narapatisithu of the Pagan Kingdom. Its name means "pertaining to the King of the Dhamma (Law)".

Architecture

Unlike most Bagan stupas, which stand on square or octagonal bases, the Dhammayazika is built on a pentagonal plan. Five small shrines at the corners of the pentagon each enshrine an image of one of the five Buddhas of the present aeon (Kalpa): Kakusandha, Koṇāgamana, Kassapa, Gautama, and the future Buddha Maitreya — Bagan temples usually honoured only four. Terraces of the stupa are decorated with glazed Jataka plaques, and the bell-shaped dome is richly gilded.

History

Legend relates that Narapatisithu received a sacred relic from the king of Sri Lanka and built the pagoda to enshrine it. Restored after the 1975 Bagan earthquake, the pagoda remains an active place of pilgrimage and devotion among the thousands of monuments of the Bagan Archaeological Zone, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2019.
