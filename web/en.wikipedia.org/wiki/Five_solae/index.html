Five solae

TraditionProtestantism
PeriodProtestant Reformation

The five solae are five Latin phrases that summarize the core theological convictions of the Protestant Reformation. The word sola means "alone", and each phrase asserts that salvation or authority rests on one thing alone, in contrast to the teaching of the Roman Catholic Church of the time.

The five

  • Sola scriptura — "by Scripture alone": the Bible is the sole infallible rule of faith and practice.
  • Sola fide — "by faith alone": sinners are justified by faith, not by works.
  • Sola gratia — "by grace alone": salvation is entirely a gift of God's grace.
  • Solus Christus — "Christ alone": Christ is the only mediator between God and humanity.
  • Soli Deo gloria — "glory to God alone": all glory is due to God alone, not to saints or other intermediaries.

Although the individual phrases were used by the Reformers, grouping them as a canonical set of five is a later, largely 20th-century, summary of Reformation doctrine. They remain a popular shorthand for Protestant distinctives in Lutheran and Reformed traditions.
