Southwark Cathedral, formally the Cathedral and Collegiate Church of St Saviour and St Mary Overie, is a Church of England cathedral in Southwark, London, on the south bank of the River Thames close to London Bridge. It is the mother church of the Diocese of Southwark, which was created in 1905; before that the building served as a parish church. A church has stood on the site since at least the early 12th century, and some traditions trace Christian worship there back much earlier.

History

The present building was begun in the early 12th century as the priory church of St Mary Overie ("over the river"), an Augustinian house. After a fire in 1212, the church was rebuilt in the Gothic style; its choir, completed around 1220, is among the earliest Gothic architecture in London. The church survived the Dissolution of the Monasteries by becoming the parish church of St Saviour in 1540.

The building has strong literary associations. John Gower, the medieval poet and friend of Geoffrey Chaucer, is buried in the church, where his effigy — with its head resting on three books — can still be seen. John Harvard, benefactor of Harvard University, was baptised at St Saviour's in 1607; the Harvard Chapel in the cathedral commemorates him. Charles Dickens, who knew the area well, set scenes of Oliver Twist nearby, and the cathedral's bells feature in local tradition.

Architecture

The cathedral is built of stone in an early Gothic style. The nave was rebuilt in the 1890s by Sir Arthur Blomfield, replacing a decayed early 19th-century structure. The crossing tower carries a set of twelve bells. Notable furnishings include a 15th-century screen, a monument to William Shakespeare (whose brother Edmund was buried in the church in 1607), and modern stained glass including a window depicting Shakespeare and characters from his plays.

Today

Southwark Cathedral stands at the heart of a busy district, next to Borough Market. As well as daily worship, it hosts concerts, exhibitions, and community events, and it serves as the venue for the Diocese of Southwark's major services.
