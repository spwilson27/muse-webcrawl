Demonstration (political)

A demonstration is action by a mass group or collection of groups of people in favour of a political or other cause or against a policy, usually consisting of walking together in a march and a rally with speeches. Demonstrations are a form of nonviolent activism and of freedom of assembly and expression.

[Image: images/political-demonstration-march.jpg — Crowd marching with placards in a political demonstration]

Forms

Forms include marches, rallies, picketing, sit-ins, vigils and die-ins, as well as counter-demonstrations by opponents. Organizers seek media coverage, to show breadth of support, persuade bystanders and pressure decision-makers. Demonstrations range from small local pickets to gatherings of millions, such as anti-war marches in 2003 and women's marches in 2017.

Legal status and policing

Peaceful assembly is protected by Article 21 of the ICCPR, the First Amendment in the United States, and Article 11 of the European Convention, though permits, time-place-manner rules and police tactics such as kettling are contested. Demonstrations sometimes escalate into riots or are met with repression, which itself becomes a political issue.
