The Myth of the Twentieth Century (German: Der Mythus des zwanzigsten Jahrhunderts) is a 1930 book by Alfred Rosenberg, a leading ideologue of the Nazi Party. It sets out a pseudoscientific racial history of the world, presenting a supposed "Nordic" or "Aryan" race as the creator of all higher culture and arguing for a new racially based religion ("the myth of the blood") to replace the existing churches.

Content and influence

Drawing on the racial theories of Houston Stewart Chamberlain and Arthur de Gobineau, Rosenberg attributed the rise and fall of civilisations to racial purity and "contamination", denigrated Judaism and Christianity, and called for a German national church. The book sold over a million copies by 1944 and was treated as one of the canonical texts of Nazism alongside Hitler's Mein Kampf, although Hitler privately kept some distance from Rosenberg's religious ideas, and the Catholic Church placed the book on its Index Librorum Prohibitorum in 1934.

Assessment

Historians regard the book as turgid and incoherent as well as pernicious; its historical and scientific claims are without foundation. Rosenberg was convicted of crimes against humanity at the Nuremberg trials and executed in 1946.
