Santa Rosa, California

State: California
County: Sonoma (county seat)
Population: about 178,000 (2020)

Santa Rosa is a city in and the county seat of Sonoma County, in the North Bay region of California. Its estimated 2020 population was 178,127. It is the largest city in California's Wine Country and the Redwood Empire, and the fourth-most populous city in the San Francisco Bay Area after San Jose, San Francisco, and Oakland.

History

Founded in the mid-19th century on lands of the Carrillo family rancho, Santa Rosa grew as an agricultural and rail centre. Much of the downtown was destroyed by the 1906 San Francisco earthquake, which per capita caused severe damage in Santa Rosa. The city was rebuilt and expanded after the Second World War.

Notable connections

Santa Rosa was the home of horticulturist Luther Burbank, whose gardens and home are preserved as a historic site, and of cartoonist Charles M. Schulz, creator of Peanuts; the Charles M. Schulz Museum stands in the city, and the local airport is named for him. Alfred Hitchcock filmed Shadow of a Doubt (1943) in Santa Rosa.

Wildfires

In October 2017 the Tubbs Fire, part of the North Bay fires, devastated parts of the city, destroying the Coffey Park neighbourhood and thousands of structures; it was at the time the most destructive wildfire in California history. Recovery and rebuilding followed in subsequent years.

Economy

The economy rests on health care (Kaiser Permanente, Sutter), wine and agriculture, tourism, technology, and education; Santa Rosa Junior College is a major institution.
