Colorado River

The Colorado River is a major river of the Southwestern United States and northwestern Mexico, about 2,330 kilometres long. Rising in the Rocky Mountains of Colorado, it flows southwest through the Colorado Plateau, carving the Grand Canyon in Arizona, then forms much of the Arizona-Nevada and Arizona-California borders before entering Mexico and, historically, reaching the Gulf of California.

The river and its tributaries drain about 640,000 square kilometres and supply water to some 40 million people and over two million hectares of farmland. Hoover Dam, completed in 1935, and Glen Canyon Dam create its two great reservoirs, Lake Mead and Lake Powell.

Water use and decline

The Colorado's flow was divided among seven US states and Mexico by agreements beginning with the Colorado River Compact of 1922, which allocated more water on paper than the river reliably carries. Long-term drought and overallocation have meant the river has rarely reached the sea in recent decades, and its reservoirs fell to record lows in the early 2020s, prompting unprecedented water-use cuts.
