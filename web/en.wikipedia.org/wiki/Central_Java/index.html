Central Java

Central Java (Indonesian: Jawa Tengah) is a province of Indonesia located in the middle portion of the island of Java. Its capital and largest city is Semarang, on the north coast. The province covers about 32,800 square kilometres and has a population of roughly 36 million, making it one of the most densely populated provinces in the country. It is bordered by West Java to the west, the Indian Ocean and the Yogyakarta Special Region to the south, East Java to the east, and the Java Sea to the north.

History

Central Java was the heartland of powerful early kingdoms. In the 8th and 9th centuries the Buddhist Sailendra dynasty raised the great stupa of Borobudur, the largest Buddhist monument in the world, while the Hindu Sanjaya dynasty built the temple complexes of Prambanan. The region later came under the Mataram Sultanate, whose court culture shaped classical Javanese arts, and subsequently under Dutch colonial rule. The modern province was established after Indonesian independence; the city of Surakarta (Solo) and Yogyakarta retained special court traditions, with Yogyakarta becoming a separate special region.

Geography

The province features a chain of volcanoes running along its spine, including Mount Merapi — one of Indonesia's most active volcanoes, on the border with Yogyakarta — as well as Slamet, Sumbing, Sindoro, and the Dieng Plateau. Fertile volcanic plains support intensive rice cultivation, and the northern coastal plain hosts the major ports and industrial cities of Semarang, Tegal, and Pekalongan.

Culture

Central Java is regarded as the centre of refined Javanese culture: the court cities of Surakarta and (in the neighbouring special region) Yogyakarta preserve gamelan music, wayang shadow-puppet theatre, classical dance, and batik textile production, with Surakarta and Pekalongan famed for their batik industries. The predominant language is Javanese, spoken alongside Indonesian.
