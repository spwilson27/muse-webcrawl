Nick Fury

Colonel Nicholas Joseph "Nick" Fury Sr. is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer/artist Jack Kirby and writer Stan Lee, he first appeared in Sgt. Fury and his Howling Commandos #1 (May 1963). A World War II army hero, the character was initially a cigar-chomping leader of an elite US Army unit.

Nick Fury

First appearance: Sgt. Fury and his Howling Commandos #1 (1963)

Created by: Stan Lee, Jack Kirby

Affiliation: S.H.I.E.L.D., Howling Commandos, Avengers (ally)

Abilities: Expert tactician and marksman; retarded aging via the Infinity Formula

Publication history

Fury first appeared as a World War II sergeant leading the Howling Commandos, a diverse unit fighting in the European theatre. In 1965, Stan Lee and Jack Kirby reimagined the character as a modern-day spy in Strange Tales, introducing him as the director of S.H.I.E.L.D. (Supreme Headquarters, International Espionage and Law-Enforcement Division), a fictional espionage agency. This version, distinguished by his eyepatch and graying temples, became the definitive portrayal.

Fictional character biography

Born in New York City's Hell's Kitchen in the early 20th century, Fury enlisted in the army and served with distinction during World War II. During the war he was treated with the Infinity Formula, a serum that dramatically slowed his aging. After the war he joined the CIA and later became director of S.H.I.E.L.D., overseeing operations against organizations such as HYDRA and A.I.M. Fury is known for his pragmatism, willingness to operate in moral gray areas, and his role in assembling intelligence on superhuman threats.

In other media

Samuel L. Jackson portrays Nick Fury in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, beginning with a post-credits appearance in Iron Man (2008). The character has also appeared in numerous animated series and video games.
