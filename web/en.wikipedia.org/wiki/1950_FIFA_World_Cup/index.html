The 1950 FIFA World Cup was the fourth edition of the FIFA World Cup and was held in Brazil from June 24 to July 16, 1950. It was the first World Cup held since 1938, the intervening tournaments of 1942 and 1946 having been cancelled because of the Second World War. Uruguay won the tournament, defeating hosts Brazil 2–1 in the decisive match of the final round — a game known in Brazil as the Maracanazo ("the Maracanã blow").

Format and participants

Only 13 teams participated after several withdrawals, including India, Scotland, and Turkey. England took part for the first time. Uniquely, there was no single final match: the tournament used a final round-robin group of four teams (Brazil, Uruguay, Spain, Sweden), and the championship came down to the last game, in which Brazil needed only a draw to win the title.

The tournament also produced one of the greatest upsets in football history: the United States, a team of part-timers, defeated England 1–0 in Belo Horizonte, with the winning goal scored by Joe Gaetjens.

The Maracanazo

On July 16, 1950, before an officially recorded crowd of 173,850 at the newly built Maracanã Stadium in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil took the lead through Friaça early in the second half, but Juan Alberto Schiaffino equalised for Uruguay and Alcides Ghiggia scored the winner in the 79th minute, silencing the stadium. The defeat became a defining trauma in Brazilian football culture and led Brazil to abandon its white kit in favour of the now-famous yellow and green shirt.
