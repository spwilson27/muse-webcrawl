Merle Travis

Merle Robert Travis (November 29, 1917 – October 20, 1983) was an American country and western singer, songwriter and guitarist born in Rosewood, Kentucky. He is celebrated for his syncopated fingerpicking guitar style — known as "Travis picking" — and for writing enduring songs such as "Sixteen Tons" and "Dark as a Dungeon".

Career

Raised in Muhlenberg County, Kentucky, Travis learned guitar from Mose Rager and Ike Everly, developing a style in which the thumb plays alternating bass notes while the fingers pick melody. After service in the U.S. Marine Corps he settled in California, recording for Capitol Records from 1946. His 1947 album Folk Songs of the Hills introduced several coal-mining songs he wrote, including "Sixteen Tons", later a number-one hit for Tennessee Ernie Ford in 1955.

Influence

Travis picking profoundly influenced Chet Atkins, Doc Watson and generations of fingerstyle guitarists. Travis also collaborated with luthier Paul Bigsby on one of the first solid-body electric guitars, and designed headstock concepts later adopted widely. He was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 1977.
