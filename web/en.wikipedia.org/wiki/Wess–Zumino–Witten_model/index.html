Wess–Zumino–Witten model

In theoretical physics and mathematics, the Wess–Zumino–Witten (WZW) model, also called the Wess–Zumino–Novikov–Witten model, is a type of two-dimensional conformal field theory named after Julius Wess, Bruno Zumino, Sergei Novikov and Edward Witten. A WZW model is associated to a Lie group, and its symmetry algebra is an affine Lie algebra built from the corresponding Lie algebra.

Action

The field in a WZW model is a map from a two-dimensional spacetime to a Lie group. The action combines a nonlinear sigma-model kinetic term with the Wess–Zumino term, a topological term written as an integral over a three-dimensional manifold whose boundary is spacetime. The coefficient of the Wess–Zumino term must be an integer, called the level, for the quantum theory to be well defined.

Properties

WZW models are exactly soluble: their equations of motion factorise into left- and right-moving currents satisfying a Kac–Moody algebra, and correlators obey the Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations. The SU(2) model at level 1 is equivalent to a free boson at the self-dual radius. WZW models arise naturally in string theory, in the study of Chern–Simons theory, and in condensed-matter applications such as the Kondo effect.
