Dordrecht

Dordrecht, historically known in English as Dort, is a city and municipality in the province of South Holland, Netherlands. It is the oldest city in the Holland region, granted city rights in 1220.

History

Situated where the Merwede forks into the Noord and Oude Maas, Dordrecht became a major trading port for wine, timber and grain, with staple rights granted in 1299. The St Elizabeth's flood of 1421 drowned the surrounding polder and turned Dordrecht into an island. In 1572 the first assembly of the free Dutch States met here, and in 1618–19 the Synod of Dort settled the Arminian controversy within the Reformed Church, producing the Canons of Dort.

Geography and today

The municipality occupies the Island of Dordrecht, bordered by major rivers and close to the Biesbosch National Park. Its historic port quarter, the Grote Kerk with its leaning tower, and the Dordrechts Museum draw visitors; the city remains a hub for inland shipping and maritime industry near Rotterdam.
