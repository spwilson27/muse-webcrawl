Vinaya

The Vinaya (Pali and Sanskrit: विनय) is the regulatory framework for the Buddhist monastic community (sangha), based on a set of texts known as the Vinaya Piṭaka. It contains the rules and procedures that govern the daily lives of monks (bhikkhus) and nuns (bhikkhunis) and the communal life of the order.

Overview

According to tradition, the Buddha laid down rules one by one in response to specific incidents, explaining the origin and rationale of each rule. The rules are collected in the Prātimokṣa (Pali: Pātimokkha), which is recited communally twice a month at the uposatha observance. The Theravāda code lists 227 rules for monks and 311 for nuns; other schools preserve somewhat different counts.

Textual traditions

Six Vinaya recensions survive in whole or part. The Pali Vinaya of the Theravāda school is preserved in Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia; the Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya is followed in Tibetan Buddhism; and the Dharmaguptaka Vinaya is standard in East Asian Buddhism (China, Korea, Japan, Vietnam). Despite differences, the codes are closely parallel in structure and content, reflecting a common pre-sectarian core.

Categories of rules

Rules are graded by seriousness, from the four pārājika offences — sexual intercourse, theft, killing, and false claims of spiritual attainment — which entail expulsion, through offences requiring a formal meeting of the sangha, expiation, and mere confession, down to rules of training and etiquette covering robes, alms bowls, dwellings, and deportment.

Role in Buddhist practice

The Vinaya defines the identity of the monastic order and has traditionally been regarded as the foundation of the Buddha's dispensation: "When the Vinaya endures, the Dhamma endures." Ordination lineages are traced through unbroken Vinaya transmission, which is why the question of reviving the bhikkhuni order has turned largely on Vinaya interpretation.
