UseChromogenic substrate for β-galactosidase

X-gal (also abbreviated BCIG for 5-bromo-4-chloro-3-indolyl β-D-galactopyranoside) is an organic compound consisting of galactose linked to a substituted indole. It is widely used in molecular biology as a chromogenic substrate for the enzyme β-galactosidase, most famously in blue–white screening of bacterial colonies.

Use in blue–white screening

When β-galactosidase (the product of the lacZ gene) cleaves X-gal, the galactose is released and the indole portion oxidises and dimerises to form 5,5′-dibromo-4,4′-dichloro-indigo, an intensely blue, insoluble pigment. In blue–white screening, plasmid vectors carry a fragment of lacZ (lacZα) whose multiple cloning site, when occupied by an inserted DNA fragment, disrupts α-complementation. Colonies with an empty vector therefore turn blue on X-gal plates (usually supplemented with the inducer IPTG), while colonies carrying a recombinant plasmid remain white or cream-coloured, allowing easy visual selection.

Other applications

X-gal is also used in histochemistry and in reporter-gene assays in transgenic organisms, where lacZ expression marks cells or tissues in which a promoter of interest is active; the blue precipitate marks the sites of expression. Analogous substrates such as Bluo-gal and Magenta-gal produce blue and magenta products respectively and are sometimes used in combination staining.
