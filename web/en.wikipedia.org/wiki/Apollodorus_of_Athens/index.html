Apollodorus of Athens (Greek: Ἀπολλόδωρος ὁ Ἀθηναῖος; c. 180 – c. 120 BC) was a Greek scholar, grammarian and historian of Athens. A pupil of the great Alexandrian critic Aristarchus of Samothrace and of the Stoic philosopher Diogenes of Babylon, he spent much of his career in Alexandria before returning to Athens.

Works

Apollodorus's most influential work was the Chronicle (Chronika), a universal chronology in iambic verse extending from the fall of Troy (which he dated to 1184/3 BC, following Eratosthenes) down to his own time. Arranged by Athenian archon years, it became the standard framework for dating Greek literary and political history and was used by later chronographers including Diodorus Siculus. His scholarly output also included a commentary on Homer's Catalogue of Ships, a treatise On the Gods in 24 books treating Greek cult and theology, and works on etymology and geography.

The Bibliotheca

The mythological handbook known as the Bibliotheca ("Library"), a systematic summary of Greek myth from the origins of the gods to the Trojan War, was traditionally attributed to Apollodorus and still circulates under the name "Apollodorus." Modern scholarship judges it the work of a later author — conventionally "Pseudo-Apollodorus" — probably of the 1st or 2nd century AD. Despite the misattribution, the Bibliotheca remains one of the most valuable surviving sources for Greek mythology.
