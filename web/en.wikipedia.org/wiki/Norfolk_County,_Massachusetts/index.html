Norfolk County is a county located in the U.S. state of Massachusetts. At the 2020 census, the population was 725,981. Its county seat is Dedham. The county was named after the English county of Norfolk.

Norfolk County is unusual in form: it consists of two disconnected pieces, the towns of Cohasset being separated from the rest of the county by Hingham Bay, Plymouth County, and Suffolk County. Norfolk County is included in the Boston–Cambridge–Newton metropolitan statistical area.

History

Massachusetts's Norfolk County was created on 26 March 1793 by legislation signed by Governor John Hancock, carved out of Suffolk County. Most of its towns were formerly part of Suffolk County; Boston and several other communities remained in Suffolk. An earlier Norfolk County existed in colonial Massachusetts from 1679 to 1680 but covered territory in what is now coastal New Hampshire.

Demographics and economy

Norfolk County is one of the wealthiest counties in the United States, with a high median household income. Notable communities include Quincy, Brookline, Needham, Wellesley, Franklin, and Foxborough, home of Gillette Stadium and the New England Patriots.

Unlike most Massachusetts counties, Norfolk County retains a county government, including a registry of deeds, county commissioners, and a sheriff, though many traditional county functions elsewhere in the state were transferred to the Commonwealth in the late 1990s.
