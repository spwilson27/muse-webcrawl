The Silence of the Lambs (novel)

The Silence of the Lambs is a psychological horror novel by the American author Thomas Harris, published in 1988 by St. Martin's Press. It is the second novel in Harris's Hannibal Lecter series, following Red Dragon (1981), and introduces the FBI trainee Clarice Starling, who seeks the help of the imprisoned cannibalistic serial killer Dr. Hannibal Lecter to catch another murderer, "Buffalo Bill".

Plot

Jack Crawford of the FBI's Behavioral Science Unit sends trainee Clarice Starling to interview Hannibal Lecter at the Baltimore State Hospital for the Criminally Insane, ostensibly for a survey but really hoping Lecter will help profile the killer dubbed "Buffalo Bill", who skins his female victims. Lecter trades insights for personal revelations — a quid pro quo — guiding Starling toward the insight that Bill knew his first victim, while Starling's memories of screaming lambs on a Montana ranch, which she tried and failed to save, explain the title. In the climax she confronts Bill alone in his basement labyrinth.

Reception and adaptation

The novel won the Bram Stoker Award for best novel and the Anthony Award, and was praised for its taut plotting and the creation of Lecter, one of fiction's most enduring villains. Jonathan Demme's 1991 film adaptation, starring Jodie Foster and Anthony Hopkins, won the Academy Awards for Best Picture, Director, Actor, Actress and Adapted Screenplay — the "Big Five" — and is frequently ranked among the greatest thrillers ever made.
