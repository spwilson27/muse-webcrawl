Motorola

Motorola, Inc. was an American multinational telecommunications company based in Schaumburg, Illinois. Founded in Chicago in 1928 by brothers Paul and Joseph Galvin as the Galvin Manufacturing Corporation, it adopted the name Motorola in 1947. The company was a pioneer in radio communications, mobile telephony, and semiconductors.

History

The company's name derived from its popular car radios ("motor" + "Victrola"). Motorola supplied military radio equipment in the Second World War, including the SCR-536 "Handie-Talkie," and later produced the first commercial portable cellular telephone (the DynaTAC 8000X, 1983), the first flip phone (StarTAC, 1996), and the best-selling Razr V3 (2004). Its semiconductor division made the 6800 and 68000 microprocessor families used in early personal computers. Motorola equipment also carried the first words from the Moon in 1969.

Decline and split

After losses in the 2000s, Motorola split on January 4, 2011, into two independent companies: Motorola Mobility (consumer handsets and home devices) and Motorola Solutions (public safety and enterprise equipment). Google acquired Motorola Mobility in 2012 for $12.5 billion and sold it to Lenovo in 2014 for about $2.9 billion; Motorola Solutions continues as a separate company.

Legacy

Motorola is credited with inventing the first handheld mobile phone and pioneering quality-management practices such as Six Sigma, developed there in 1986. The "batwing" logo remains in use by both successor companies.
