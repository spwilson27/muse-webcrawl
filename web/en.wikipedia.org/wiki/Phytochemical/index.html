Phytochemical

Phytochemicals are chemical compounds produced by plants, generally to help them resist fungi, bacteria and plant virus infections, and also consumption by insects and other animals. The name comes from the Greek word phyton, meaning "plant". Some phytochemicals have been used as poisons and others as traditional medicines.

As a term, phytochemicals is generally used to describe plant compounds that are under research with unestablished effects on health and are not scientifically defined as essential nutrients. Regulatory agencies governing food labeling in Europe and the United States have provided guidance for industry to limit or prevent health claims about phytochemicals on food product labels.

Definition

Phytochemicals are chemicals of plant origin. They are non-nutritive plant chemicals that have protective or disease preventive properties. They are non-essential nutrients, meaning that they are not required by the human body for sustaining life. It is estimated that more than 5,000 different phytochemicals have been identified, but a large percentage still remain unknown.

Categories

Major classes of phytochemicals include carotenoids (such as beta-carotene, lycopene and lutein), flavonoids (including anthocyanins and quercetin), phenolic acids, organosulfur compounds found in garlic and onions, glucosinolates found in cruciferous vegetables, and alkaloids such as caffeine and capsaicin. See also flavonoid and carotenoid.

Health claims

Although some phytochemicals show antioxidant or hormone-like activity in laboratory and animal studies, clinical evidence that they prevent or treat disease in humans remains limited, and health claims are restricted by food-labeling regulators.
