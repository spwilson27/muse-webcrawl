Premarital sex

Premarital sex is sexual activity practiced by people before they are married. Historically, it was considered a moral issue and was taboo in many cultures; in some countries it remains illegal or socially prohibited. Attitudes toward premarital sex have changed substantially since the mid-twentieth century, particularly in Western countries, where it has become widely accepted.

History and cultural attitudes

Many religious traditions, including Christianity, Islam, and Judaism, have historically discouraged or condemned sex outside marriage. Social norms in much of Europe and North America began to shift during the sexual revolution of the 1960s and 1970s, aided by the availability of reliable contraception. Surveys in the United States have repeatedly found that the large majority of Americans have engaged in premarital sex, including cohorts who came of age in the 1950s.

Prevalence

Research by the Guttmacher Institute and others indicates that by age 44, roughly 99 percent of Americans who have ever had sex did so before marriage. Similar patterns are reported across most Western nations, while rates and acceptance vary widely in parts of Africa, the Middle East, and Asia, where religious and legal restrictions may apply.

Health and social issues

Public-health discussions of premarital sex commonly address contraception, sexually transmitted infections, and unintended pregnancy. Educational approaches range from comprehensive sex education to abstinence-only programs, whose effectiveness has been widely studied.
