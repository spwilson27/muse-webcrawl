Almost surely

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In probability theory, an event is said to happen almost surely (often abbreviated a.s.) if it happens with probability 1. Equivalently, a property holds almost surely if the set of outcomes where it fails has probability zero — even though that exceptional set need not be empty.

The concept is the probabilistic counterpart of "almost everywhere" in measure theory, and the phrase "with probability 1" (w.p. 1) is used interchangeably. Almost sure convergence is one of the fundamental modes of convergence of random variables.

Definition

Let (Ω, F, P) be a probability space. An event E ∈ F occurs almost surely if P(E) = 1. A random variable X has a property almost surely if P({ω : X(ω) has the property}) = 1. The complement of an almost sure event is called a null event; its probability is zero, but it may contain possible outcomes.

Examples

Dart throwing

Throw a dart uniformly at a square dartboard and consider the event that the dart hits a point whose coordinates are both rational numbers, or lies exactly on a diagonal. The set of such points is nonempty — indeed dense — but has area (probability) zero. Hence the dart almost surely misses every rational point and every diagonal, even though hitting them is geometrically possible.

Coin flipping

Flip a fair coin infinitely often. The outcome "all heads forever" is a possible sequence, but its probability is 0. By the strong law of large numbers, the proportion of heads converges to 1/2 almost surely: with probability 1, any single infinite sequence drawn at random displays the limiting frequency, even though exceptional sequences (all heads, or any prescribed pattern) exist.

Almost sure convergence

A sequence of random variables Xn converges almost surely to X if P({ω : Xn(ω) → X(ω)}) = 1. Almost sure convergence implies convergence in probability and in distribution, and is implied by summable-tail conditions via the Borel–Cantelli lemma. It underlies the strong law of large numbers.

"Almost never"

The negation phrasing is also common: an event happens almost never if its probability is 0. "Almost never" does not mean impossible — a dart can land on a specified point — only that it does so with probability zero.
