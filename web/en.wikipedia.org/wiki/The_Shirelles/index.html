Overview

The Shirelles were an American girl group formed in 1957 in Passaic, New Jersey, by Shirley Owens, Doris Coley, Addie 'Micki' Harris, and Beverly Lee. Widely regarded as the first major girl group of the rock and roll era, they achieved a string of hits in the early 1960s that helped define the genre and open the way for Motown and later groups.

Career

After early singles on Tiara and Decca records, the group signed with Scepter Records, where producer Luther Dixon shaped their sound. Their 1960 recording of 'Will You Love Me Tomorrow', written by Gerry Goffin and Carole King, became the first number-one hit on the Billboard Hot 100 by an African-American girl group. Other major hits included 'Dedicated to the One I Love', 'Mama Said', 'Soldier Boy' (a number one in 1962), 'Baby It's You', and 'Foolish Little Girl'.

The group's popularity faded in the later 1960s amid legal disputes with Scepter over royalties and trust funds. The Shirelles were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1996, and their recordings remain foundational to the girl-group sound.
