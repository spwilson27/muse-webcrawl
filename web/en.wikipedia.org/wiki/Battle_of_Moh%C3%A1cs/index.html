The Battle of Mohács, fought on 29 August 1526 near Mohács in southern Hungary, was a crushing defeat of the Kingdom of Hungary by the Ottoman Empire under Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent. King Louis II of Hungary died in the battle, and medieval Hungary effectively ceased to exist as a unified kingdom.

Background

The Ottomans advanced up the Danube after taking Belgrade in 1521. The Hungarian army, about 25,000–30,000 strong with many of its magnates and the king, faced perhaps 50,000–60,000 Ottoman troops with superior artillery and Janissary infantry.

The battle

The Hungarian heavy cavalry charged the Ottoman centre and was shattered by Janissary fire and cannon. The battle lasted barely two hours; Hungarian losses were enormous, and Louis II drowned fleeing the field.

Consequences

Hungary was partitioned: the centre came under direct Ottoman rule for 150 years, the east became Transylvania, and the west and north passed to the Habsburgs. "More was lost at Mohács" remains a Hungarian saying for catastrophe.
