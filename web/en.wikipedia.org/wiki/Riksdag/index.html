Riksdag

Type: Unicameral legislature
Seats: 349
Meeting place: Riksdagshuset, Helgeandsholmen, Stockholm
Elections: Every four years, proportional representation

The Riksdag (Swedish: riksdagen) is the national legislature and supreme decision-making body of Sweden. Since 1971 it has been a unicameral assembly with 349 members (riksdagsledamöter), elected by proportional representation for four-year terms.

History

The Riksdag traces its origins to the assembly at Arboga in 1435 and the Riksdag of the Estates, in which the four estates — nobility, clergy, burghers and peasants — met separately. A bicameral Riksdag replaced it in 1866, and universal suffrage and parliamentarism developed in the early 20th century. The 1974 Instrument of Government made the Riksdag formally the foremost organ of state.

Functions

The Riksdag enacts legislation, determines taxes and expenditure, scrutinises the government, and elects the prime minister through a process of negative parliamentarism: a candidate is approved unless an absolute majority votes against.
