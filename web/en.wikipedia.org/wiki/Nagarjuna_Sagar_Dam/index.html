Nagarjuna Sagar Dam

LocationKrishna River, Telangana/Andhra Pradesh border, India
Construction1955–1969 (powerhouse completed 1978)
Height / length124 m / approx. 1.45 km (masonry dam)
Installed capacity816 MW

Nagarjuna Sagar Dam is a masonry dam across the Krishna River between Nalgonda district in Telangana and Palnadu district in Andhra Pradesh, India. When completed it was among the largest masonry dams in the world, and its reservoir — one of India's biggest artificial lakes — supplies irrigation and hydroelectric power to the surrounding states.

History

The project was inaugurated by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru on 10 December 1955, in the spirit of his description of such dams as "temples of modern India". Construction employed tens of thousands of workers and used stone masonry rather than concrete for much of the structure. The dam was completed in 1969, and its power station — eight reversible and conventional units totalling 816 MW — was commissioned through the 1970s. The reservoir is named for the Buddhist philosopher Nagarjuna; the ancient site of Nagarjunakonda, with its third-century CE Buddhist remains, was excavated and its monuments relocated to an island museum in the reservoir before the valley was submerged.

Features

The main dam is about 124 metres high with 26 crest gates, flanked by long earthen dykes. Two great canals leave the reservoir: the right-bank Jawahar Canal and the left-bank Lal Bahadur Canal, together irrigating well over a million hectares in Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, principally for paddy, cotton and chillies. The dam also supplies drinking water, including to Hyderabad via downstream arrangements.

Significance and issues

Nagarjuna Sagar transformed the dry Deccan districts downstream into a rice bowl, but its waters are governed by Krishna basin allocations disputed among Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Maharashtra and adjudicated by tribunals. Since the bifurcation of Andhra Pradesh in 2014, operation of the dam — which straddles the new state border — has been a recurring point of contention between the two Telugu states.
