Labanotation


A Labanotation score

Labanotation (also called Laban script or Kinetography Laban) is a comprehensive system for recording and analysing human movement, published by the Hungarian choreographer and movement theorist Rudolf Laban in his 1928 work Schrifttanz ("written dance"). It is the most widely used of the major dance notation systems and is employed by choreographers, ethnologists, physical therapists and movement researchers to document dances and other forms of motion so that they can be restaged, studied and preserved.

History

Laban developed the system over two decades of teaching and experimenting with movement in Germany, France and Austria. After the First World War he refined his notation at his movement schools, and in 1928 the system was formally introduced as Kinetographie Laban. Following Laban's emigration to England in 1937, the notation spread to the English-speaking world. In the United States, Ann Hutchinson Guest founded the Dance Notation Bureau in New York in 1940, where the name "Labanotation" became standard; in Europe the system continued as Kinetography Laban under scholars such as Albrecht Knust.

Structure

The score is written on a vertical three-line staff read from bottom to top, representing the passage of time. Columns flanking the centre line correspond to parts of the body: the columns nearest the staff represent the supporting legs, then the legs in gesture, the body, the arms and finally the head and hands at the outer edges. Direction symbols, shaped like pins or arrows, indicate the direction of a movement; their shading distinguishes levels (high, middle and low), and their length shows duration.

Uses

Labanotation scores form the archive from which companies such as ballet and modern dance troupes reconstruct historic choreography. The system also underpins broader movement analysis in ergonomics, anthropology and robotics, where precise descriptions of gesture are required.
