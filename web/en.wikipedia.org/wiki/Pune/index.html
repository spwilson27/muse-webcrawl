Pune (Marathi: Puṇē), formerly spelled Poona, is a city in the Indian state of Maharashtra, situated on the Deccan Plateau at the confluence of the Mula and Mutha rivers, about 150 km southeast of Mumbai. It is the second-largest city in Maharashtra and one of India's largest metropolitan areas. Historically the seat of the Peshwas, the prime ministers of the Maratha Empire, Pune is today a major centre of education, information technology, manufacturing and research, often called the "Oxford of the East" for its many universities and institutes.

History

Settlement at the site dates back over a thousand years; copper-plate grants from the Rashtrakuta period mention a village called Punnaka. In the 17th century the town came under the Bhonsle family: Shivaji, founder of the Maratha polity, spent much of his youth at the Lal Mahal palace in Pune. After 1713 the city became the seat of the Peshwas, who effectively ruled the expanding Maratha Empire from Shaniwar Wada, the fortified palace built by Bajirao I in 1732. The Peshwa era ended with the Third Anglo-Maratha War in 1818, after which Pune became a British cantonment town and seasonal seat of the Bombay Presidency government.

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries Pune was a centre of India's freedom movement and social reform, associated with figures such as Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Mahadev Govind Ranade and Jyotiba Phule. Mahatma Gandhi was imprisoned at the Aga Khan Palace during the Quit India movement, and his wife Kasturba died there in 1944.

Economy and education

Pune hosts Savitribai Phule Pune University (founded 1949), the Film and Television Institute of India, the National Defence Academy at Khadakwasla, and numerous research institutes including the National Chemical Laboratory and the Inter-University Centre for Astronomy and Astrophysics. The Hinjawadi Rajiv Gandhi Infotech Park anchors a large IT industry, while the Pimpri-Chinchwad and Chakan belts host automobile and engineering plants of companies such as Bajaj Auto, Tata Motors and Mercedes-Benz India. The city is also known for its pleasant climate relative to coastal Mumbai, its Marathi theatre and culture, and landmarks such as Shaniwar Wada, the Aga Khan Palace, Sinhagad Fort and the Dagdusheth Ganpati temple.

Geography and climate

Pune lies at roughly 560 m above sea level at the western edge of the Deccan, in the rain shadow of the Western Ghats. It has a tropical wet-and-dry climate with a monsoon season from June to September and mild winters. Hills such as Vetal and Parvati rise within the city, and reservoirs on the Khadakwasla, Panshet and Mulshi dams supply its water.
