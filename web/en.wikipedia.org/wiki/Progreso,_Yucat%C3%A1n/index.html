Progreso is a port city in the Mexican state of Yucatán, on the Gulf of Mexico coast about 30 kilometres north of the state capital, Mérida. It is the chief seaport of Yucatán and a popular beach destination and cruise-ship stop.

History

Progreso was founded in 1871 to give Mérida and the henequen-producing interior a nearer port than Sisal. It grew with the henequen boom and retained its importance for fishing, container shipping and tourism after that industry declined. Its pier, extended far into the shallow gulf, is among the longest in the world at about 6.5 kilometres, built to reach water deep enough for ocean-going ships.

Tourism and economy

The city's malecón (seafront promenade), beaches and seafood restaurants attract visitors from Mérida and international cruise passengers. Fishing for octopus, grouper and other species, along with port operations and salt production in the surrounding area, anchor the local economy. Nearby attractions include the Chicxulub crater area and the flamingo reserves of Celestún to the west.
