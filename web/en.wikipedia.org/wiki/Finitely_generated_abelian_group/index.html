Finitely generated abelian group

In abstract algebra, an abelian group (G, +) is called finitely generated if there exist finitely many elements x1, ..., xs in G such that every element of G can be written in the form

x = n1x1 + n2x2 + ... + nsxs

for some integers n1, ..., ns. In this case, the set {x1, ..., xs} is called a generating set of the group G. Clearly, every finite abelian group is finitely generated. The finitely generated abelian groups can be completely classified.

Examples

  • The integers, (ℤ, +), form a finitely generated abelian group, generated by 1.
  • The integers modulo n, ℤ/nℤ, for any positive integer n, are a finite (hence finitely generated) abelian group.
  • Any direct sum of finitely many finitely generated abelian groups is again finitely generated abelian.
  • Every lattice forms a finitely generated free abelian group.

The rationals, (ℚ, +), are an example of an abelian group that is not finitely generated.

Classification

The fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups (which is a special case of the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain) states that every finitely generated abelian group G is isomorphic to a direct sum of primary cyclic groups and infinite cyclic groups:

G ≈ ℤr ⊕ ℤ/q1ℤ ⊕ ... ⊕ ℤ/qtℤ,

where the rank r ≥ 0, and the numbers q1, ..., qt are powers of distinct primes, uniquely determined by G (up to order). Equivalently, G can be expressed as a direct sum of cyclic groups of orders k1, ..., ku where ki divides ki+1; these are the invariant factors of G.

Subgroups

Every subgroup of a finitely generated abelian group is itself finitely generated abelian. Moreover, every subgroup of a free abelian group ℤn is free abelian of rank at most n.
