Heptarchy

The Heptarchy is a collective name for the seven traditional kingdoms of Anglo-Saxon England: Northumbria, Mercia, East Anglia, Essex, Kent, Sussex, and Wessex.

History

From the end of Roman Britain in the 5th century, Germanic kingdoms formed and competed, with overlordship (the Bretwalda) passing among rulers such as Offa of Mercia. The term heptarchy, popularised by later historians, oversimplifies a fluid landscape that included many smaller peoples and kingdoms.

Unification

Viking invasions in the 9th century destroyed or weakened several kingdoms; Wessex under Alfred the Great survived, and his descendants — Edward the Elder, Æthelstan and their successors — united England by the 10th century.

Historiography

Modern historians use the term cautiously, noting kingdoms rose and merged continuously and the neat sevenfold scheme derives partly from Bede and later chroniclers.
