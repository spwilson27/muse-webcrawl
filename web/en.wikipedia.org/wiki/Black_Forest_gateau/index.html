Black Forest gateau (German: Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte, literally "Black Forest cherry torte") is a chocolate sponge cake with a rich cherry filling, originating in Germany. It typically consists of several layers of chocolate sponge cake sandwiched with whipped cream and cherries, decorated with additional whipped cream, maraschino cherries, and chocolate shavings. Traditionally, Kirschwasser, a clear spirit made from sour cherries, is added to the cake.

History

The cake is named not directly after the Black Forest mountain range, but rather after the specialty liquor of that region, Schwarzwälder Kirsch(wasser). One account credits confectioner Josef Keller with inventing the cake in Bad Godesberg in 1915; another attributes its modern form to Erwin Hildenbrand in Tübingen in the 1930s. It became widely popular in Germany after the Second World War and spread internationally, becoming a standard offering in bakeries and restaurants.

Regulation

In Germany, a cake may be sold as Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte only if it contains genuine Black Forest cherry Kirschwasser. Since 2013 the name has enjoyed protected geographical indication status in the European Union.

World record

In 2012, a team of chefs in Singapore made the world's largest Black Forest cake, weighing nearly 7,000 pounds (about 3,200 kg).
