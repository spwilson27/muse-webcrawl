Isonzo

Isonzo

Slovenian name: Soča

Length: 138 km (86 mi)

Source: Julian Alps, Slovenia

Mouth: Adriatic Sea, near Monfalcone, Italy

The Isonzo (Italian) or Soča (Slovene) is a 138-kilometre river flowing through western Slovenia and northeastern Italy. Renowned for its striking emerald-green water, it rises in the Julian Alps and empties into the Gulf of Trieste in the Adriatic Sea near Monfalcone.

World War I

The Isonzo valley was the scene of the Battles of the Isonzo, twelve major engagements fought between Italy and Austria-Hungary from 1915 to 1917 along the river line, including the breakthrough at Caporetto (Kobarid) in October–November 1917. The fighting caused enormous casualties and is commemorated by museums and memorials at Kobarid and Redipuglia.

Tourism and nature

The upper Soča valley in Slovenia is a center for whitewater rafting, kayaking, and fly fishing, and forms part of the setting of Triglav National Park. The river's distinctive color comes from its limestone bedrock and suspended mineral particles.
