Strathspey (dance)

A strathspey is a type of dance tune in 4/4 time, named after the Strathspey region of Scotland, in Moray and Badenoch and Strathspey, where the River Spey flows. It is similar to a hornpipe but slower and more stately, and contains many dot-cut rhythms (a Scotch snap followed by a longer note), which give it a distinctive character.

In Scottish country dancing and Highland dancing, the strathspey forms one of the standard tempos, alongside the reel and the jig. Strathspey steps are slower and more elegant than reel steps, with a characteristic travelling step and setting step, performed to music at roughly 112–124 beats per minute in some traditions, though tempos vary by context.

Music

Strathspey tunes are written in 4/4 time and commonly feature the "Scotch snap", a short accented sixteenth-note followed by a dotted eighth-note. Famous strathspey tunes include "Tullochgorum" and "The Marquis of Huntly's Highland Fling". The fiddling style of the Scottish Highlands is particularly associated with the strathspey, with composers such as William Marshall and James Scott Skinner ("The Strathspey King") producing many examples.

Cape Breton fiddling in Nova Scotia, Canada, preserved the strathspey tradition, where it is often played faster than in modern Scotland.
