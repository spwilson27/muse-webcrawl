The Slavs or Slavic peoples are an ethnolinguistic group who speak the various Slavic languages of the larger Balto-Slavic branch of the Indo-European language family. They are native to Eurasia, stretching from Central, Southeast and Eastern Europe, across northern Asia to the Pacific Ocean, and constitute the largest ethnolinguistic group in Europe. Slavs are customarily grouped into East Slavs (Russians, Ukrainians, Belarusians), West Slavs (Poles, Czechs, Slovaks, Sorbs and others) and South Slavs (Serbs, Croats, Bosniaks, Bulgarians, Macedonians, Montenegrins and Slovenes).

History

The Slavs emerged as a distinct group in the early centuries CE, with their presumed homeland in the forest-steppe of present-day Ukraine and Belarus. From the 6th century they expanded rapidly across Eastern and Central Europe and the Balkans during the Migration Period, absorbing or displacing other peoples. Slavic states formed in the Middle Ages, including Kievan Rus', Great Moravia, the Bulgarian Empire and the Polish kingdom. The missionary brothers Cyril and Methodius created the Glagolitic alphabet and translated liturgy into Old Church Slavonic in the 9th century.

Culture

Slavic cultures share linguistic heritage and many customs, but differ in religion and historical experience, split broadly between Orthodox East and South and Catholic West, with Muslim Slavic communities in the Balkans. The 19th-century Pan-Slavism movement sought political unity of Slavic peoples.
