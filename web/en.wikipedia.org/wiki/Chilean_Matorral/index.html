Chilean Matorral

The Chilean Matorral is a terrestrial ecoregion of central Chile, located on the west coast of South America. It is in the Mediterranean forests, woodlands, and scrub biome, and is one of five Mediterranean-climate regions in the world.

Geography

The Matorral occupies central Chile between roughly 32° and 37° south latitude, lying between the Pacific Ocean to the west and the Andes to the east, bordered by the Atacama Desert to the north and Valdivian temperate forests to the south. Its climate features wet winters and long, dry summers.

Flora

Characteristic vegetation is evergreen sclerophyllous shrubland and woodland, including species such as litre (Lithraea caustica), quillay (Quillaja saponaria), peumo (Cryptocarya alba), boldo (Peumus boldus), and the Chilean wine palm (Jubaea chilensis). The region has high plant endemism.

Conservation

The ecoregion is heavily populated — it includes Santiago and Valparaíso — and much of its original vegetation has been converted to agriculture, plantations, and urban land. Protected areas include La Campana and Rio Clarillo national parks.
