Claudia gens

Claudia gens
OriginSabine (Attus Clausus, traditionally 504 BC)
BranchesPatrician Claudii Pulchri, Crassi, Nerones; plebeian Claudii Marcelli
Famous membersAppius Claudius Caecus; emperors Tiberius and Claudius

The gens Claudia was one of the most illustrious patrician families at ancient Rome, producing numerous consuls, censors, and dictators across the Republic and giving its name to the Julio-Claudian dynasty of emperors. According to tradition, the gens was founded by the Sabine Attus Clausus (in Latin, Appius Claudius), who migrated to Rome with his followers in 504 BC and was admitted to the patriciate.

Character and history

The Claudii acquired a reputation in Roman historiography for pride and stubborn defense of aristocratic privilege; Livy and Suetonius both comment on the family's haughtiness across generations. Notable early members include Appius Claudius Sabinus, the decemvir Appius Claudius Crassus, and the censor Appius Claudius Caecus, builder of the Appian Way and the Aqua Appia. The patrician branches used the cognomina Pulcher, Crassus, Nero, and Centho, while the famous plebeian branch bore the name Marcellus, including Marcus Claudius Marcellus, the conqueror of Syracuse.

Imperial connections

The Claudian name entered the imperial house through Livia Drusilla, wife of Augustus, and her sons Tiberius and Drusus (Claudii Nerones). Tiberius Claudius Nero Caesar became the emperor Tiberius, and his nephew Claudius reigned from AD 41 to 54; Nero, the last Julio-Claudian emperor, was connected to the gens by adoption and descent. The Via Claudia Augusta, the Aqua Claudia aqueduct, and the Roman name of many towns preserve the family's imprint.
