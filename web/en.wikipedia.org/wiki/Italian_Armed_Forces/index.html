Italian Armed Forces

The Italian Armed Forces (Italian: Forze Armate Italiane) are the military forces of the Italian Republic. They consist of the Italian Army (Esercito Italiano), the Italian Navy (Marina Militare), the Italian Air Force (Aeronautica Militare), and the Carabinieri, a gendarmerie corps with military and police functions. A fifth force, the Guardia di Finanza, is a separate military corps under the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

Command

The President of the Republic is the commander-in-chief, chairing the Supreme Defence Council. Operational command rests with the Chief of the Defence Staff (Capo di Stato Maggiore della Difesa), subordinate to the Minister of Defence. The armed forces have roughly 165,000 active personnel, making them among the largest in the European Union.

History

The armed forces descend from the Royal Army of the Kingdom of Italy, created in 1861 on national unification. Italy fought in both world wars; after the armistice of 8 September 1943 the forces split between the royal co-belligerent forces in the south and the armed forces of the Italian Social Republic in the north. The republican armed forces were rebuilt after 1946, and Italy became a founding member of NATO in 1949. Conscription was suspended in 2005, making the forces all-volunteer.

Role

Article 52 of the Italian Constitution describes the defence of the homeland as a "sacred duty". Italian forces regularly deploy on international peacekeeping and stabilisation missions under UN, NATO, and EU mandates, and assist civil authorities at home in emergencies.
