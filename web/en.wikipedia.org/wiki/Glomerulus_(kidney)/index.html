Glomerulus (kidney)

The glomerulus (plural glomeruli) is a tuft of capillaries at the beginning of each nephron in the kidney, where blood is filtered to form urine. Together with the surrounding Bowman's capsule it forms the renal corpuscle, the site of the first step of urine formation: the production of an essentially protein-free filtrate of plasma.

Structure

Blood enters the glomerulus through the afferent arteriole and leaves through the efferent arteriole, whose narrower diameter keeps glomerular pressure high. The filtration barrier has three layers: the fenestrated endothelium of the capillaries, the glomerular basement membrane, and the foot processes (pedicels) of podocytes, specialized cells whose interdigitating processes leave filtration slits covered by a thin diaphragm. Mesangial cells occupy the central stalk of the tuft, providing structural support, contracting in response to signals, and clearing trapped material. Bowman's capsule, a double-walled cup, collects the filtrate and funnels it into the proximal convoluted tubule.

Function

The glomerular filtration rate (GFR) — about 180 litres per day in a healthy adult human — is governed by the balance of hydrostatic and oncotic pressures across the barrier and by the surface area available for filtration. The barrier is both size-selective and charge-selective, retaining cells and most plasma protein (notably albumin) while passing water, ions, glucose, and small solutes. Damage to any layer produces proteinuria and haematuria, as in the diseases grouped under glomerulonephritis. The juxtaglomerular apparatus, where the tubule passes between the afferent and efferent arterioles, senses sodium chloride delivery and helps regulate GFR and renin release.
