Lawrence is a small town in Otago, in the South Island of New Zealand. It is located 35 kilometres northwest of Milton, on State Highway 8, the main route to the Central Otago town of Alexandra. Lawrence was the first town in New Zealand where gold was discovered in payable quantities, and it grew rapidly during the Otago gold rush of the 1860s.

History

In May 1861 Gabriel Read discovered gold in a creek bed at what became known as Gabriel's Gully, a few kilometres northeast of the present town. The find triggered the Otago gold rush: within months thousands of miners arrived, and the settlement at Tuapeka — renamed Lawrence after the British general Sir Henry Lawrence — boomed. At its height the town's population rivalled Dunedin's, with estimates of up to 11,500 residents in 1862.

As the easily won alluvial gold was exhausted, the population fell sharply, and Lawrence settled into a role as a service town for surrounding farmland. The town retains many buildings from its gold-rush era, and Gabriel's Gully is preserved as a historic reserve. The "New Zealand" national anthem's tune was composed by John Joseph Woods, a Lawrence schoolteacher, in 1876.

Geography

Lawrence sits in the Tuapeka valley amid rolling farmland and forestry blocks, west of the Clutha River. State Highway 8 passes through the town, linking the Southern Motorway near Milton with Central Otago and the Lindis Pass.

Notable people

John Joseph Woods, composer of the music for "God Defend New Zealand", lived in Lawrence. The town was also home to the writer Ella May Miller and, briefly, goldfields administrator and politician James Macassey.
