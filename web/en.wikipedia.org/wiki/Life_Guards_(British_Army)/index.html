The Life Guards (LG) is the most senior regiment of the British Army and part of the Household Cavalry. Together with the Blues and Royals it forms the Household Cavalry, the sovereign's mounted bodyguard and an operational armoured reconnaissance formation.

History

The regiment traces its origins to troops of horse raised in 1659–1661 around Charles II in exile and in England at the Restoration. The Life Guards served in the great battles of the 18th and 19th centuries, including Dettingen, Waterloo — where they charged as part of the Household Brigade — and in the Crimean and Boer Wars. In 1922 the 1st and 2nd Life Guards were amalgamated into a single regiment.

Modern role

Operationally the regiment provides armoured reconnaissance within the Household Cavalry Regiment at Bulford, while its mounted squadron in London carries out ceremonial duties including the King's Life Guard at Horse Guards and state occasions. Life Guards wear a red tunic with a white helmet plume, distinguishing them from the Blues and Royals' blue tunic.

Battle honours

Honours include Dettingen, Peninsula, Waterloo, Tel el Kebir, South Africa 1899–1900, the First and Second World Wars, and Iraq 2003.
