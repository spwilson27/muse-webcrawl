Type: Ofuda (Shinto talisman)
Issued by: Ise Grand Shrine (Jingū)
Dedicated to: Amaterasu

Jingū taima

Jingū taima (神宮大麻) is a type of ofuda, a talisman issued by the Ise Grand Shrine in Japan. It is the most widely distributed of all Shinto ofuda and is enshrined in household altars (kamidana) throughout the country.

Description

The taima is a wooden tablet wrapped in washi paper, bearing the name of the Ise shrine and the goddess Amaterasu, the ancestral deity of the Imperial House. It functions as a yorishiro — an object capable of attracting and housing a kami — allowing the deity of Ise to be worshipped in the home.

History and distribution

During the Edo period, travelling shrine representatives known as onshi distributed Ise talismans (then called Ise-kō or o-harai taima) to households across Japan, helping to spread devotion to Ise nationwide. After the Meiji Restoration the distribution was reorganized under the shrine administration; today Jingū taima are distributed through local Shinto shrines under the Association of Shinto Shrines, and are traditionally renewed each year around the New Year.
