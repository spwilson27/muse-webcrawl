Clam chowder

Clam chowder is any of several chowder soups in American cuisine containing clams. In addition to clams, common ingredients include diced potatoes, salt pork, and onions, and sometimes celery. Other vegetables are not typically used, although some recipes add carrots or other garnishes. Contrary to popular belief, it is believed that clams were only added to chowder in the mid-1800s because of their abundance along the New England coast.

The most famous varieties are New England clam chowder, made with milk or cream, and Manhattan clam chowder, made with a tomato-based broth. The rivalry between them is a longstanding feature of American food culture: in 1939 a Maine legislator introduced a bill — only partly in jest — that would have made it illegal to put tomatoes in clam chowder.

History

Chowder derives from the fish stews of coastal France and Britain; the word may come from the French chaudière, a cauldron used by Breton fishermen, who are credited with bringing the dish to Nova Scotia and New England. Early American chowders were layered stews of fish, ship's biscuit, and salt pork thickened with flour. By the nineteenth century, clams had become a standard ingredient in coastal New England, and potatoes replaced the biscuit. Clam chowder was served at picnics, church suppers, and political events, and was popularised nationally through restaurants and, later, canned versions.

Varieties

  • New England clam chowder — a milk- or cream-based chowder traditionally made with quahogs or other hard clams, salt pork, onions, and potatoes, thickened slightly and served with oyster crackers.
  • Manhattan clam chowder — a clear, tomato-based broth with vegetables such as celery, peppers, and carrots; the name, originally a slur by Mainers implying New Yorkers would put tomatoes in anything, became standard in the early twentieth century.
  • Rhode Island clam chowder — a clear-broth version using clam juice and water without dairy or tomato, common in Rhode Island and southeastern Connecticut.
  • Long Island clam chowder — a hybrid combining cream and tomato.
  • Minorcan clam chowder — a tomato-based chowder from St. Augustine, Florida, spiced with datil peppers.
