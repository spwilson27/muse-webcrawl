Amasya Circular

Amasya Circular
Date22 June 1919
PlaceAmasya, Ottoman Empire
SignatoriesMustafa Kemal Pasha, Rauf Bey, Refet Bey (Ali Fuat Pasha by telegram)
SignificanceFirst written declaration of the Turkish National Movement; call for the Sivas Congress

The Amasya Circular (Turkish: Amasya Genelgesi or Amasya Tamimi) was a joint communiqué issued on 22 June 1919 by Mustafa Kemal Pasha and his associates in the Anatolian city of Amasya. It is regarded as the founding declaration of the Turkish War of Independence, announcing that the Ottoman government in Istanbul could no longer protect the country and that the nation's independence would be saved by the nation's own determination.


Amasya, where the circular was issued in June 1919

Background

After the Ottoman Empire's defeat in the First World War and the Armistice of Mudros (October 1918), Allied forces occupied parts of Anatolia, and Greek troops landed at İzmir on 15 May 1919. Mustafa Kemal, sent to Samsun as inspector of the Ninth Army Troops Inspectorate, arrived on 19 May 1919 and began organising resistance; from Samsun he moved to Amasya, where he conferred with Rauf (Orbay) and Refet (Bele), securing the adherence of Ali Fuat (Cebesoy) by telegram and the approval of Kâzım Karabekir in Erzurum.

Contents

The circular declared, in its principal points:

  1. The integrity of the homeland and the independence of the nation are in danger.
  2. The Istanbul government is unable to discharge its responsibilities.
  3. The independence of the nation will be saved by the determination and resolve of the nation itself.
  4. A national congress, free from all pressure and control, should convene at Sivas; delegates from every province should be sent.
  5. A national committee should be formed to represent the movement pending the congress.

Consequences

The circular was telegraphed to military and civil authorities across Anatolia, effectively proclaiming a rival source of legitimacy to the sultan's government. Istanbul responded by recalling and then dismissing Mustafa Kemal, who resigned his commission and continued as a civilian leader. The Erzurum Congress (July–August 1919) and Sivas Congress (September 1919) followed the circular's programme, adopting its principles and forming the Representative Committee that became the nucleus of the Ankara government and, eventually, the Republic of Turkey.
