Confluent hypergeometric function

In mathematics, a confluent hypergeometric function is a solution of a confluent hypergeometric equation, which is a degenerate form of a hypergeometric differential equation where two of the three regular singularities merge into an irregular singularity. The term confluent refers to the merging of singular points of families of differential equations.

Kummer's equation

The standard form is Kummer's equation:

z w′′ + (b − z) w′ − a w = 0.

Its solutions include Kummer's function M(a, b, z), also written 1F1(a; b; z), defined by a generalized hypergeometric series, and Tricomi's function U(a, b, z), which provides a second independent solution. Ernst Kummer published the first systematic study in 1836.

Special cases

Many familiar special functions are special or limiting cases, including the exponential and error functions, Bessel functions, Laguerre polynomials, Hermite polynomials, incomplete gamma functions, and the Whittaker and parabolic cylinder functions that arise in physics.

Applications

Confluent hypergeometric functions appear in the solutions of the Schrödinger equation for the hydrogen atom and Coulomb problems, in statistics (for example the noncentral chi-squared distribution), and in wave propagation and heat conduction problems.
