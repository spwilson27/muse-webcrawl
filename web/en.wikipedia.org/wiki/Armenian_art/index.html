Armenian art

Armenian art is the distinctive art of the Armenian people, developed over some three millennia in the Armenian Highlands and the Armenian diaspora. It is particularly noted for its church architecture, illuminated manuscripts and carved stone crosses known as khachkars.

Architecture

After Armenia adopted Christianity in 301 AD, a characteristic style of domed stone church architecture emerged, seen in monuments such as Etchmiadzin Cathedral and the churches of Ani. Armenian builders pioneered techniques of dome construction that influenced later Byzantine and Islamic architecture.

Manuscripts and stone carving

Medieval Armenian scriptoria produced richly illuminated Gospels, notably in Cilician Armenia under artists such as Toros Roslin. Khachkars — intricately carved memorial stelae bearing a cross within a rosette — were inscribed by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2010.
