Annie Jump Cannon

Annie Jump Cannon
Born: 11 December 1863, Dover, Delaware
Died: 13 April 1941, Cambridge, Massachusetts
Fields: Astronomy
Institutions: Harvard College Observatory
Known for: Harvard spectral classification (O B A F G K M); Henry Draper Catalogue

Annie Jump Cannon (1863–1941) was an American astronomer whose catalogue work was central to the development of modern stellar classification. She created the Harvard Classification Scheme — the spectral sequence O, B, A, F, G, K, M — still in use today.

Early life

Cannon studied physics and astronomy at Wellesley College, graduating in 1884. Scarlet fever left her largely deaf. After her mother's death she returned to study at Radcliffe and joined Edward Pickering's group of women "computers" at Harvard College Observatory in 1896.

Spectral classification

Building on work by Williamina Fleming and Antonia Maury, Cannon simplified spectral classes into a single temperature-ordered sequence and could classify stars at extraordinary speed — reportedly up to three per minute. She classified some 225,300 stars for the Henry Draper Catalogue (published 1918–1924) and extended the work in later catalogues, classifying more stars in her lifetime than anyone before her.

Honours

Cannon was the first woman elected an officer of the American Astronomical Society, received the first honorary doctorate Oxford awarded to a woman (1925), and won the Henry Draper Medal in 1931. The lunar crater Cannon and asteroid 1120 Cannonia are named for her.
