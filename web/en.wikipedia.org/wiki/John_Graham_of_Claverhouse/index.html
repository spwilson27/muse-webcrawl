John Graham of Claverhouse

John Graham of Claverhouse, 1st Viscount Dundee (c. 21 July 1648 – 27 July 1689) was a Scottish soldier and nobleman. Remembered by Covenanters as "Bluidy Clavers" for his suppression of their conventicles, he is celebrated by Jacobites as "Bonnie Dundee" for his leadership of the Jacobite rising of 1689, in which he died at the moment of victory at Killiecrankie.

Early career

Educated at St Andrews, Graham served in French and Dutch armies before returning to Scotland, where as captain of the King's Life Guard of Horse he pursued Presbyterian Covenanters in the south-west during the "Killing Time." He defeated a Covenanter force at Drumclog in 1679 but was himself defeated at Bothwell Brig shortly afterwards.

Jacobite rising

Created Viscount Dundee by James VII in 1688, Graham refused to accept William of Orange and raised the Highlands for James after the Convention of Estates declared the throne vacant. On 27 July 1689 his Highland charge routed a larger government army at the Pass of Killiecrankie, but Dundee was shot down leading the attack, and the rising soon collapsed without him.

Walter Scott popularised the "Bonnie Dundee" legend in song and in Old Mortality, where Claverhouse appears in heroic (and historically softened) form.
