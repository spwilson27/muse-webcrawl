Pirate Parties International

Pirate Parties International, founded 2010 in Brussels

Pirate Parties International (PPI) is an international non-governmental, non-profit organisation founded in April 2010 in Brussels, Belgium. It serves as the umbrella association of the world's Pirate Parties — political parties advocating copyright and patent reform, government transparency, freedom of information and expression, privacy, and direct democracy.

History

The pirate party movement began in Sweden in 2006 with the founding of the first Pirate Party by Rick Falkvinge, campaigning against the enforcement regime of the file-sharing era. Parties on the model spread to dozens of countries; before PPI, they coordinated informally through the Pirate Party International collective founded in 2006. PPI formalised the network in 2010 with 22 national parties among its founding members. In 2015 internal disputes led several founding parties, including the Swedish and Icelandic parties, to resign or suspend their membership.

Aims and structure

PPI exists to help establish and support pirate parties, coordinate campaigns and share information among members, and promote the pirate movement internationally. It is governed by an annual general assembly of member parties and an elected board, and holds consultative and observer relationships with international bodies; it is an observer member of the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) and holds consultative status with ECOSOC. A separate European Pirate Party, founded in 2014, groups the European members for EU-level politics.

Notable members' successes

Member parties have won seats in national parliaments in Iceland, the Czech Republic and Luxembourg, and Pirate candidates entered the European Parliament beginning in 2009, when the Swedish party won two seats following the Pirate Bay trial verdict.
