Belt of Venus

The Belt of Venus (also called Venus's Girdle, the antitwilight arch, or antitwilight) is an atmospheric phenomenon visible shortly before sunrise and after sunset, during civil twilight. It is a pinkish or rosy band seen in the sky opposite the Sun, above the bluish-grey segment of the Earth's shadow.


The pink Belt of Venus above the blue-grey Earth shadow

Explanation

After sunset, looking east, an observer sees the Earth's shadow rising as a dark blue-grey band at the horizon. Above it, fine dust and aerosols in the upper atmosphere scatter the reddened light of the already-set Sun back toward the observer by backscattering, producing a diffuse pink glow — the antitwilight arch. The same display appears in the west before sunrise. The phenomenon is best seen under clear, clean skies and from high vantage points, and lasts roughly 10–20 minutes. Its name associates the rosy band with Venus, goddess of beauty, though it has no connection with the planet.
