Minolta

Minolta Co., Ltd. was a Japanese manufacturer of cameras, photographic lenses, photocopiers, fax machines, laser printers and other optical equipment, founded in Osaka in 1928 by Kazuo Tashima as Nichi-Doku Shashinki Shōten (the Japanese-German Camera Shop). It became one of the world's leading camera makers before merging with Konica in 2003.

History

The company adopted the name Minolta — said to derive from a phrase meaning "ripening rice fields" and also styled as an acronym for "Machinery and Instruments Optical by Tashima" — in the 1930s. Early products included the Nifcalette (1929) and the Minolta Vest (1934). After the Second World War, Minolta produced the first Japanese camera used in space-related work and built a reputation for innovation.

Photographic innovations

Minolta's landmarks include the SR-2 (1958), its first single-lens reflex; the SR-T series; the X-700 (1981); and most famously the Maxxum 7000 (1985), the first commercially successful autofocus SLR system with the focusing motor in the camera body, which transformed camera design industry-wide. The company also produced the popular Hi-Matic rangefinders, the CLE rangefinder for the Leica M mount, and light meters widely used in photography and cinematography. Minolta's autofocus technology led to a major patent dispute with Honeywell, settled in 1991 for a large sum that weakened the company financially.

Decline and merger

Struggling in the digital transition, Minolta merged with Konica in 2003 to form Konica Minolta. In 2006 Konica Minolta withdrew from the camera business entirely, transferring its digital SLR assets and the Maxxum/Dynax lens mount system to Sony, whose Alpha cameras continue the mount's lineage.
