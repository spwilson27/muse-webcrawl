Walter Brown Gibson (September 12, 1897 – December 6, 1985) was an American author and professional magician, best known for creating the pulp-fiction hero the Shadow. Under the house pen name "Maxwell Grant", he wrote 282 of the 325 Shadow novels published by Street & Smith between 1931 and 1949, an output often estimated at more than 15 million words.

Gibson at his typewriter, where he produced Shadow novels at extraordinary speed

Career

Gibson began as a journalist and writer on magic, ghost-writing books for famous magicians including Harry Houdini and Howard Thurston. In 1931 Street & Smith hired him to develop a magazine around the Shadow, a mysterious narrator of its radio detective programme. Gibson invented the character's identity as wealthy Lamont Cranston — later supplemented by the "real" identity of aviator Kent Allard — and produced novels at a pace of up to two per month, sometimes completing a 60,000-word novel in a matter of days.

Magic writing

Beyond the Shadow, Gibson wrote hundreds of books and articles on stage magic, conjuring tricks, and the occult, edited magic periodicals, and helped found the tradition of popular instructional magic books. He also created newspaper features and wrote for radio and comics.

Legacy

Gibson's productivity made him one of the most prolific pulp authors in history. The Shadow's catchphrase — "Who knows what evil lurks in the hearts of men? The Shadow knows!" — became a staple of American popular culture, and Gibson's novels were widely reprinted in paperback from the 1960s onward.
