Remote past

The remote past (abbreviated REM) is a grammatical tense that refers to a time that is relatively far in the past compared with some other reference point. Many languages distinguish degrees of remoteness in the past: an event that happened earlier today (hodiernal past), yesterday (hesternal past), or before yesterday (remote or pre-hodiernal past) may each require a different verb form.

Remoteness distinctions

Languages with graded past tenses are especially common among Bantu languages and in some languages of the Americas, Papua New Guinea and Aboriginal Australia. A well-known example is Yagua (Peru), which distinguishes events of earlier the same day, a day or so ago, a week or so ago, a month or so ago, and the distant past. Some languages have four or more degrees of past remoteness; the exact boundaries are relative and depend on the language's system rather than on absolute time.

Contrast with simple past

English lacks a dedicated remote past tense, conveying remoteness lexically ("a long time ago") or through the pluperfect ("had gone") for events before another past event. In linguistic glossing, the remote past contrasts with the recent past, immediate past, hodiernal past and hesternal past, and similar remoteness distinctions occur in future tenses in some languages.
