Universal Decimal Classification

The Universal Decimal Classification (UDC) is a bibliographic classification system used by libraries in many countries. It was developed by Paul Otlet and Henri La Fontaine, expanding the Dewey Decimal Classification, and first published in French between 1904 and 1907.

Structure

UDC covers all knowledge in ten main classes, from 0 Generalities and computer science to 9 Geography, biography and history. Its faceted, synthetic notation uses auxiliary tables for place, time, language and form, and symbols such as + and : to combine subjects.

Use

The system is maintained by the UDC Consortium and is used in national bibliographies and libraries across Europe and beyond, comprising tens of thousands of classes.
