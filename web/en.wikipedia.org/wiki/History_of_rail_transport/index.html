History of rail transport

FieldTransport
MilestonesStockton & Darlington 1825; transcontinental railroad 1869; Shinkansen 1964

The history of rail transport runs from ancient wagonways and early steam locomotives through the railway mania, dieselisation and electrification to high-speed trains.

Origins

Grooved wagonways guided carts in mines from the 16th century; iron rails and steam locomotives followed — Trevithick's locomotive (1804) and the Stockton and Darlington Railway (1825), the first public steam railway. The Liverpool and Manchester Railway (1830) proved intercity rail travel; networks spread across Europe and North America, and the first U.S. transcontinental railroad was completed in 1869.

Expansion and competition

Standard gauges, steel rails, air brakes and block signalling made railways the dominant land transport of the industrial age. Subways and electric traction transformed cities. After 1945, cars and aircraft eroded passenger traffic in many countries, and steam gave way to diesel and electric power.

High-speed era

Japan's Shinkansen (1964) launched modern high-speed rail, followed by France's TGV (1981) and China's vast network from 2008, the world's largest. Freight rail remains vital for bulk goods, and urban transit is expanding worldwide.
