Patience (game)

Patience (Europe), or solitaire (US and Canada), is a genre of card games played by a single player. The goal of most patience games is to arrange the cards of a shuffled deck into a specified order, usually by suit and rank on foundation piles, within rules governing how cards may be moved in a tableau layout.

History

Patience games are first documented in northern Europe in the late 18th and early 19th centuries, with the first collections published in Russia, Sweden, France and England. The game spread widely in the 19th century; Klondike, the best-known variant, became famous during the Klondike Gold Rush and later as the solitaire bundled with Microsoft Windows from 1990, which introduced the game to hundreds of millions of computer users.

Types

Well-known patience games include Klondike, FreeCell, Spider, Pyramid, Canfield, Yukon and Forty Thieves. Games are classified by whether all cards are visible from the start (open games such as FreeCell, which are almost always winnable with perfect play) or partly hidden (closed games such as Klondike, where luck plays a larger role).

Terminology

Common terms include the stock (undealt cards), waste, tableau (the main layout) and foundations (piles built up by suit from ace to king).
