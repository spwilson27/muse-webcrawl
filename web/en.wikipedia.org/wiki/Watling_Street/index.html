Watling Street

TypeRoman road
RouteLondon to Wroxeter, with branches to Chester and York
Modern roadsA2, A5

Watling Street is a historic route in England that originally crossed the Thames at London and ran northwest through St Albans (Verulamium), Dunstable, Towcester, and on to Wroxeter (Viroconium) near modern Shrewsbury. The Romans paved the route, which began at the Kentish ports of Richborough and Dover, shortly after their invasion of Britain in AD 43.

History

The name derives from the Old English Wæcelinga Stræt, the road of the people of St Albans. Watling Street formed the boundary between the Danelaw and English Mercia under the treaty of Alfred and Guthrum in the late 9th century. It remained a major highway through the coaching era and is now largely followed by the A2 and A5 roads. The Battle of Watling Street, in which Suetonius Paulinus defeated Boudica in AD 60 or 61, took place somewhere along the road, though its exact site is unknown.
