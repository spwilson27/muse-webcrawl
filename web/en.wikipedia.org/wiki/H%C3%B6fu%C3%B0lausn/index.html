Höfuðlausn

Höfuðlausn (Old Norse for "head-ransom") is a skaldic poem attributed to Egill Skallagrímsson, hero of Egils saga. According to the saga, Egill composed the poem in a single night in York and recited it the next morning before King Eric Bloodaxe, who had condemned him to death; the king was so pleased with the praise poem that he granted Egill his life — his "head" — in exchange.

Content and form

The poem is a drápa of about twenty stanzas praising Eric Bloodaxe's generosity and prowess in battle. Unusually for skaldic verse of its period, it is composed in the runhent ("end-rhymed") metre, with end-rhymes throughout — a feature that has led some scholars to question its date and authenticity, suggesting influence from English or later verse forms.

The saga account

In Egils saga (chapter 62), Egill, shipwrecked on the Northumbrian coast, falls into the hands of his enemy Eric Bloodaxe at York. Urged by his friend Arinbjörn, Egill composes the poem overnight; at dawn he sits with a swallow fluttering at the window (which Arinbjörn attributes to a sorceress), learns the poem by heart, and recites it before the king, who spares his life.
