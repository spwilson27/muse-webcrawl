Saccule

Part ofVestibular system, inner ear
LocationVestibule of the inner ear
FunctionSenses vertical linear acceleration and head tilt relative to gravity
NerveVestibular nerve (inferior division)

The saccule is a bed of sensory cells in the inner ear. It is one of the two otolith organs (the other being the utricle) of the vestibular system. The saccule converts head movements and the pull of gravity into neural signals that the brain uses for balance and the sense of spatial orientation. Specifically, it is sensitive primarily to vertical linear acceleration.

Anatomy

The saccule is a small, roughly spherical sac located in the vestibule of the bony labyrinth, in a spherical recess on the medial wall. It is connected to the cochlear duct by the ductus reuniens and to the endolymphatic duct by the utriculosaccular duct. The sensory epithelium of the saccule is called the macula sacculi, oriented approximately vertically when the head is upright.

Mechanism

The macula is covered by a gelatinous layer containing tiny calcium carbonate crystals called otoconia (or otoliths). When the head tilts or accelerates vertically, gravity and inertia shift the otoconial mass relative to the sensory hair cells beneath it, bending their hair bundles. This opens ion channels and generates receptor potentials, causing the hair cells to signal through the vestibular nerve. Signals from the saccule contribute to postural reflexes, including the vestibulo-ocular reflex and the vestibular-evoked myogenic potential (VEMP) used in clinical testing.

Clinical significance

Disorders affecting the saccule and the otolith organs include benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV), in which displaced otoconia enter the semicircular canals, and Ménière's disease, in which endolymphatic hydrops may distend the saccule. Saccular function can be assessed clinically with cervical VEMP testing.
