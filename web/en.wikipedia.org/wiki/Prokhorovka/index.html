Prokhorovka

Prokhorovka (Russian: Прохоровка) is an urban locality (a settlement) and the administrative centre of Prokhorovsky District of Belgorod Oblast, Russia, located on the Psyol River south-east of Kursk. It is chiefly famous as the site of the Battle of Prokhorovka on 12 July 1943, one of the largest tank engagements in military history, fought during the Battle of Kursk in the Second World War.

The battle

During Operation Citadel, the German offensive against the Kursk salient, the II SS-Panzer Corps advanced on Prokhorovka as it drove north toward Kursk. On 12 July 1943 the Soviet 5th Guards Tank Army under Pavel Rotmistrov counter-attacked, and hundreds of tanks clashed at close range around the settlement and the Oktyabrsky state farm. Soviet accounts long inflated the scale — claiming up to 1,500 tanks and decisive German losses — while modern research based on archival records puts the numbers lower (roughly 600–900 armoured vehicles engaged on the day in the immediate sector) and records heavy Soviet losses; nevertheless the German advance in the southern sector stalled, and combined with the Allied landing in Sicily and the Soviet counteroffensive at Orel, Hitler cancelled Citadel days later, permanently surrendering the strategic initiative on the Eastern Front.

Memorial

The battlefield is preserved as part of the state military-historical museum reserve "Prokhorovka Field", one of Russia's "three battlefields" alongside Kulikovo and Borodino. A 59-metre white bell tower topped by a golden dome and the figure of the Mother of God (the "Zvonnitsa"), designed by Vyacheslav Klykov, was inaugurated in 1995 for the 50th anniversary of the victory, together with the museum "Third Battlefield of Russia".
