Bedriacum (also spelled Betriacum) was a small town or posting station on the Via Postumia in northern Italy, near the modern town of Calvatone in the province of Cremona. It is famous as the site of two decisive battles fought in AD 69, the Year of the Four Emperors.

The battles of 69 AD

In the First Battle of Bedriacum (April 14, 69), the forces of the emperor Otho were defeated by the legions supporting Vitellius under his generals Caecina and Valens; Otho took his own life two days later. In the Second Battle of Bedriacum (October 24, 69), the Flavian forces of Vespasian, commanded by Antonius Primus, crushed the Vitellians and went on to sack Cremona, paving the way for Vespasian's accession and the founding of the Flavian dynasty.

Site

The exact site of the ancient settlement was long debated; archaeological work near Calvatone has identified a Roman vicus consistent with the ancient sources, including the histories of Tacitus, who gives the most detailed account of both battles.
