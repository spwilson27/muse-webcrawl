Rodong Sinmun (Korean: 로동신문; Hanja: 勞動新聞; "Workers' Newspaper") is a North Korean newspaper that serves as the official organ of the Central Committee of the Workers' Party of Korea. It is the country's most widely read newspaper and the authoritative voice of the North Korean state and party, publishing leadership statements, editorials setting the official line, and authorized accounts of domestic and foreign affairs.

History

The paper was first published on 1 November 1945 under the name Chongro ("Right Path") as the organ of the North Korean branch bureau of the Communist Party of Korea; it was renamed Rodong Sinmun in September 1946 as the Workers' Party was formed. Its editorials and New Year joint editorials (traditionally published together with the military and youth papers) historically signalled major policy directions, including economic campaigns and diplomacy.

Content and role

A typical issue runs six pages: front-page coverage of the Supreme Leader's activities and guidance visits, party editorials, domestic economic reporting, cultural and social items, and a final page of international news and commentary hostile to the United States, Japan, and South Korean authorities. Joint editorials on special occasions articulate doctrine. Since 2012 the paper has maintained a website, including an English-language edition from 2012, making its content a primary source for foreign analysts monitoring Pyongyang's signalling.

Influence

Because North Korea has no independent press, Rodong Sinmun's phrasing is closely watched by diplomats and scholars for shifts in policy or rhetoric. Foreign correspondents in Pyongyang and open-source analysts regularly cite its editorials as evidence of regime priorities, from nuclear policy statements to the elevation of new slogans under Kim Jong Un.
