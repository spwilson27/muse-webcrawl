Golden Age of Piracy

The Golden Age of Piracy is a common designation for the period between the 1650s and the 1730s, when maritime piracy was a significant factor in the histories of the North Atlantic and Indian Oceans.

Histories of piracy often subdivide the Golden Age into three periods: the buccaneering period (approximately 1650 to 1680), characterised by Anglo-French seamen based in Jamaica and Tortuga attacking Spanish colonies and shipping in the Caribbean; Pirate Round (1690s), associated with long-distance voyages from the Americas to rob East India Company targets in the Indian Ocean; and the post-Spanish Succession period (1715 to 1726), when Anglo-American sailors left unemployed by the end of the War of the Spanish Succession turned en masse to piracy in the Caribbean, the American eastern seaboard, the West African coast, and the Indian Ocean.

Notable pirates

Famous pirates of the era include Blackbeard (Edward Teach), Calico Jack (John Rackham), Bartholomew Roberts, Henry Morgan, Anne Bonny, Mary Read, and Captain William Kidd. The pirate haven of Nassau in the Bahamas served as the capital of an informal "Pirates' republic" until Governor Woodes Rogers suppressed it in 1718.

Decline

By the late 1720s the Golden Age was over. European navies, greatly expanded after the wars of the period, began systematic anti-piracy patrols; colonial governors hanged captured pirates in public spectacles; and the British Acts of Grace of 1717 and 1718 offered pardons that many pirates accepted.

In popular culture

The era has shaped the modern popular image of the pirate, from Robert Louis Stevenson's Treasure Island to the Pirates of the Caribbean film series.
