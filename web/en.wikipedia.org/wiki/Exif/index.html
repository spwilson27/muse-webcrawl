Exchangeable image file format (Exif, often incorrectly called "EXIF") is a standard that specifies formats for images, sound, and ancillary tags used by digital cameras (including smartphones), scanners and other systems handling image and sound files recorded by digital cameras. The specification uses existing JPEG and TIFF formats with the addition of specific metadata tags.

Contents of Exif data

Exif tags store information such as camera make and model, date and time of capture, exposure time, aperture (f-number), ISO speed, focal length, flash status, image orientation, and a thumbnail of the image. GPS-enabled devices may also store geolocation coordinates, altitude and direction. Audio files and video have related metadata standards.

Privacy concerns

Because Exif data can include precise location and device identifiers, publishing unprocessed photographs can unintentionally reveal where a picture was taken and where the photographer lives or works. Many social media platforms strip Exif data on upload for this reason.

Related standards

Exif metadata coexists with other metadata schemes such as IPTC Information Interchange Model data and Adobe's XMP. The "MakerNote" area of Exif allows manufacturers to store proprietary settings, which has complicated interoperability between software tools.
