Emperor Huizong of Song (2 November 1082 – 4 June 1135), personal name Zhao Ji, was the eighth emperor of the Song dynasty of China. One of the most cultured monarchs in Chinese history — a gifted painter, calligrapher, poet and patron of the arts — he is also remembered as a disastrous ruler whose neglect of government and military affairs led to the fall of the Northern Song.

Reign

Huizong came to the throne in 1100 after the death of his brother, Emperor Zhezong. Guided by the powerful minister Cai Jing, he lavished resources on art, Daoist religion and extravagant projects such as the Genyue imperial garden in Kaifeng, stocked with fantastic rocks and plants gathered from across the empire by the notorious "flower and rock" convoys. He created the "slender gold" style of calligraphy and his paintings of birds and flowers remain celebrated masterpieces.

Fall and captivity

Seeking to recover territory from the Liao dynasty, Huizong allied with the Jurchen Jin dynasty — which destroyed the Liao and then turned on the Song. When Jin armies besieged Kaifeng, Huizong abdicated in January 1126 in favour of his son Qinzong. The capital fell in 1127 in the Jingkang Incident; both emperors were captured and carried north into captivity, where Huizong died in 1135. The Song court re-established itself in the south as the Southern Song.
