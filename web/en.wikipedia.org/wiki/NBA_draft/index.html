NBA draft

The NBA draft is an annual event in which the teams of the National Basketball Association (NBA) select eligible players, primarily from American colleges and international leagues, to join their rosters. It has been held since 1947 and currently consists of two rounds of 30 picks each, for a total of 60 selections.

Draft order and lottery

Since 1985, the order of the first selections among non-playoff teams has been determined by a draft lottery, introduced to discourage "tanking" (deliberately losing to obtain a better pick). The lottery was reformed in 2019 so that the three worst teams each have a 14% chance of winning the first overall pick. Playoff teams pick in reverse order of their regular-season records. The first overall pick is one of the most coveted prizes in American sport; celebrated examples include LeBron James (2003), Tim Duncan (1997) and Victor Wembanyama (2023).

Eligibility

Players must be at least 19 years old during the calendar year of the draft and, for American players, at least one year removed from high school. College players may declare early or remain eligible until their college eligibility expires; international players follow related rules. Undrafted players may still sign as free agents.

Format and history

The draft historically ran many rounds; it was shortened to two rounds in 1989. It is now a major televised spectacle, typically held in late June. Famous draft classes include 1984 (Michael Jordan, Hakeem Olajuwon, Charles Barkley, John Stockton), 1996 (Kobe Bryant, Allen Iverson, Steve Nash) and 2003 (James, Dwyane Wade, Carmelo Anthony, Chris Bosh).
