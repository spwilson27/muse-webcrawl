Gouda cheese

[Image: Wheels of Gouda cheese in a Dutch market, coated in wax]

Overview

Gouda is a mild, yellow cheese made from cow's milk, named after the city of Gouda in South Holland, Netherlands. It is one of the world's most popular cheeses, accounting for a large share of Dutch cheese production. Gouda is a washed-curd cheese: part of the whey is replaced with warm water, reducing lactose and giving a sweeter, smoother paste.

Ageing

Young Gouda is mild and elastic; aged Gouda (oude kaas) becomes firm, darker and crystalline with butterscotch and nutty flavours. Traditional farmhouse Gouda is boerenkaas. The name Gouda is not protected by EU PDO status in general use (unlike "Gouda Holland"), so Gouda-style cheese is made worldwide. Wax coatings — red, yellow or black for aged wheels — protect the rind.
