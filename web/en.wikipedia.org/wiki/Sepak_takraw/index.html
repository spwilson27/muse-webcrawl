Sepak takraw


A player executing an acrobatic kick (placeholder image)

Sepak takraw, or kick volleyball, is a team sport native to Southeast Asia. Played with a rattan (or synthetic) ball on a court resembling a badminton court, it combines elements of football and volleyball: players may use their feet, legs, chest and head — but not their hands — to send the ball over a net. The name joins the Malay word sepak ("kick") with the Thai word takraw ("woven ball").

Rules

In the standard regu event, two teams of three players face each other across a net 1.52 m high at the centre. Teams have up to three touches to return the ball, and the same player may take consecutive touches, enabling the spectacular bicycle-style "roll spike". Matches are played in sets, conventionally to 21 points with rally scoring under current international rules. Other formats include doubles and the hoop and circle variants.

History and organisation

Ball-kicking games of this kind have been played in the Malay world and Thailand for centuries; the sport was standardised in the twentieth century and was demonstrated at the 1965 Southeast Asian Peninsular Games. It has been a regular Asian Games sport since 1990, where Thailand has been the dominant nation. The international governing body is the International Sepak Takraw Federation (ISTAF), founded in 1988, which organises the King's Cup and ISTAF World Cup competitions.
