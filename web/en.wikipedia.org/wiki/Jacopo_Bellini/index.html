Life

Jacopo Bellini (c. 1400 – c. 1470) was a Venetian painter and draughtsman, founder of the Bellini family workshop that dominated Venetian painting for a century. A pupil of Gentile da Fabriano, with whom he worked in Florence in the 1420s, Jacopo absorbed the innovations of the Florentine Renaissance — linear perspective, classical antiquity, and naturalistic narrative — and carried them back to Venice. His sons Gentile and Giovanni Bellini became the pre-eminent painters of their generation, and his daughter Nicolosa married Andrea Mantegna, forging the decisive alliance between Venetian and Paduan art.

The sketchbooks

Jacopo's fame rests chiefly on two sketchbooks — one in the Louvre, Paris (c. 1430–1450) and one in the British Museum — containing some 290 drawings in pen, chalk, and leadpoint. They are a unique window into a Renaissance artist's workshop imagination: Madonnas and saints, classical ruins, exotic animals, perspective studies of architecture, scenes of the Passion, and even giraffes and lions. The books circulated within the family workshop as pattern sources, shaping compositions by Gentile, Giovanni, and Mantegna.

Surviving works

Few paintings survive securely attributed to Jacopo: a Madonna and Child in the Uffizi, a Crucifixion in Verona, and panels in the Accademia, Venice. His historical importance lies less in individual pictures than in his role as the transmitter of Florentine perspective to Venice and as patriarch of the city's greatest artistic dynasty.
