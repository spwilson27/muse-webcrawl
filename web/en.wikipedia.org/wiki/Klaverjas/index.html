Klaverjas

OriginNetherlands
Players4 (in partnerships of 2)
Cards32 (piquet pack)

Klaverjas (Dutch: klaverjassen) is a traditional Dutch four-player trick-taking card game played with a 32-card piquet pack. It is the national card game of the Netherlands and belongs to the Jack–Nine family of games, which also includes the French game Belote.

Overview

The game is played by four players in two fixed partnerships, with partners sitting opposite each other. Cards rank unusually: in the trump suit the jack and nine are the highest cards, while in plain suits the ranking follows a modified order. Points are scored for winning tricks containing valuable cards and for combinations (roem) such as sequences and four of a kind declared during play.

Variants

Two principal traditions exist: the Rotterdam variant (Rotterdams klaverjassen), in which players are obliged to trump in when unable to follow suit, and the Amsterdam variant (Amsterdams klaverjassen), in which a player may undertrump a partner's winning card under certain conditions; the variants also differ in how bonus combinations are counted.

History

The name derives from klaver (clubs) and jas, an old term for the jack. The game developed in the Netherlands in the early 18th century from earlier Jack–Nine games and remains a staple of Dutch social life, played in cafés, clubs and family gatherings.
