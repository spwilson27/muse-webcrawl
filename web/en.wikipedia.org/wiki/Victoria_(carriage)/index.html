Victoria (carriage)

Victoria
TypeHorse-drawn carriage
Introducedc. 1869 (England)
PassengersTwo, plus driver

The victoria is a type of elegant, four-wheeled horse-drawn carriage with a low, open body, seating for two passengers, and a raised driver's box in front. It was named after Queen Victoria, who popularised the style in Britain in the late 1860s.

Description

The victoria's body is mounted low on elliptical springs, making it easy for passengers to step in and out — a quality that made it a favourite ladies' carriage. It is typically drawn by one or two horses. A folding leather hood (calash top) can be raised over the passengers in bad weather. The driver sits on an elevated box seat at the front, and some examples include a small rumble seat behind for a footman.

History

The design was adapted from French carriage styles and was introduced to England around 1869, where it quickly became fashionable after Queen Victoria purchased one. Victorias became the standard fashionable carriage for afternoon drives in parks such as London's Hyde Park and Paris's Bois de Boulogne. In several Indian cities, victorias long remained in use as tourist carriages, notably in Mumbai, where horse-drawn victorias operated around landmarks such as the Gateway of India well into the twenty-first century before being phased out and replaced in part by electric versions.
