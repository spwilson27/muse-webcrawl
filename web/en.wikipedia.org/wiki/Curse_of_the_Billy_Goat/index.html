Curse of the Billy Goat

Wrigley Field, where the curse was pronounced in 1945.

The Curse of the Billy Goat was a sports-related superstition said to have afflicted the Chicago Cubs baseball team. According to legend, tavern owner William "Billy Goat" Sianis cursed the franchise during the 1945 World Series after he and his pet goat were turned away from Wrigley Field, declaring that the Cubs would never win the World Series again.

The drought

The Cubs lost the 1945 Series to the Detroit Tigers and did not reach another World Series for 71 years. The drought became studded with agonizing near-misses: the "Black Cat" game during the 1969 pennant race collapse, Leon Durham's error in the 1984 NLCS, and the 2003 NLCS, when the Cubs stood five outs from the Series before a collapse against the Florida Marlins in the infamous "Steve Bartman incident" game.

Breaking the curse

The curse was said to be broken on November 2, 2016, when the Cubs defeated the Cleveland Indians in Game 7 of the World Series in extra innings, winning their first championship since 1908 — a 108-year drought, the longest in North American major professional sports at the time.

Legacy

The curse remains one of the most famous in American sports folklore, frequently compared with Boston's Curse of the Bambino, and the Sianis family's Billy Goat Tavern became a Chicago institution.
