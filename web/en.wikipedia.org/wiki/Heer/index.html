Heer

Heer (German for "army") is the German word for a land army. The term may refer to several German land forces in different eras, most prominently the army of Nazi Germany's Wehrmacht (1935–1945) and the land component of the modern Bundeswehr of the Federal Republic of Germany (1955–present). This article concerns the modern German Army, with the historical forces treated in their own articles.

The Heer is the land component of the Bundeswehr.

History

Wehrmacht Heer (1935–1945)

After Adolf Hitler repudiated the Treaty of Versailles in 1935, the Reichswehr's land forces were renamed the Heer as part of the Wehrmacht, alongside the Kriegsmarine (navy) and Luftwaffe (air force). The Wehrmacht Heer grew into a force of millions, spearheaded the invasions of Poland, France, the Balkans and the Soviet Union, and was implicated in extensive war crimes on the Eastern Front before Germany's defeat in 1945, when it was dissolved by the Allies.

Bundeswehr Heer (1955–present)

The Federal Republic of Germany founded the Bundeswehr on 12 November 1955, with the Heer as its army. Built for Cold War defence within NATO, it relied on conscription and reached a strength of around 340,000 with the Leopard 1 and Leopard 2 main battle tanks as its centrepiece. After reunification in 1990 it absorbed elements of the East German Nationale Volksarmee's land forces, then shrank steadily; conscription was suspended in 2011. The modern Heer is an all-volunteer force organised around armoured, mechanised, airmobile and special forces (KSK) formations, and has deployed to Kosovo, Afghanistan and Mali.

Structure and equipment

The Army Command (Kommando Heer) in Strausberg directs divisions including the 1st and 10th Panzer Divisions, the Rapid Forces Division (airmobile and special forces) and multinational corps headquarters elements. Principal equipment includes the Leopard 2 tank, Puma and Boxer armoured vehicles, PzH 2000 self-propelled howitzers, and Tiger and NH90 helicopters (the latter under Army aviation).
