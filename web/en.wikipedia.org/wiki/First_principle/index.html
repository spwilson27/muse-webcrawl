First principle

A first principle is a basic proposition or assumption that cannot be deduced from any other proposition or assumption within a system. In philosophy, first principles are drawn from first cause attitudes, and in science and mathematics they correspond to axioms and postulates from which other statements are derived.

In philosophy

Aristotle was the first to systematically discuss first principles (archai), holding that every science rests on indemonstrable starting points grasped by reason. In Cartesian philosophy, Descartes sought a first principle immune to doubt, arriving at cogito ergo sum ("I think, therefore I am").

In science

In physics, calculations performed from first principles — ab initio — begin directly at the level of established laws such as Newton's laws of motion or quantum mechanics, without empirical parameters fitted to experiment. In modern usage, "first principles thinking" refers to reducing a problem to its fundamental truths and reasoning upward from them rather than by analogy.
