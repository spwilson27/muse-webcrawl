The 2001 World Series was the championship series of Major League Baseball's 2001 season, contested between the National League champion Arizona Diamondbacks and the three-time defending champion New York Yankees. The Diamondbacks won the best-of-seven series four games to three, becoming the fastest expansion franchise in MLB history to win a championship, in only their fourth season.

Background

The 2001 season had been interrupted by the September 11 attacks, pushing the Series into November for the first time. The Yankees, seeking a fourth consecutive title, rallied the city's emotions, and the middle games at Yankee Stadium produced two famous consecutive comebacks: in Games 4 and 5, the Yankees tied the score on two-out, two-run home runs in the ninth inning (by Tino Martinez and Scott Brosius) off closer Byung-Hyun Kim, then won both games in extra innings.

Game 7

In the deciding game in Phoenix, the Yankees led 2–1 in the bottom of the ninth against Mariano Rivera, but the Diamondbacks rallied: Mark Grace singled, Tony Womack doubled home the tying run, and Luis Gonzalez looped a single over a drawn-in infield to score Jay Bell and win the championship. Starting pitchers Randy Johnson and Curt Schilling, who combined for four wins, were named co-MVPs.
