William Rosecrans

William Starke Rosecrans
Born: 6 September 1819, Kingston Township, Ohio
Died: 11 March 1898 (aged 78), Redondo Beach, California
Allegiance: United States (Union)
Commands: Army of the Cumberland

William Starke Rosecrans (September 6, 1819 – March 11, 1898) was an American inventor, coal-oil company executive, diplomat, politician, and U.S. Army officer. He gained fame for his role as a Union general during the American Civil War, achieving early victories in western Virginia and at Iuka and Corinth, and commanding the Army of the Cumberland in the Stones River campaign and the brilliant Tullahoma Campaign.

Civil War career

A graduate of West Point, Rosecrans served as an engineer and professor before resigning in 1854 for business ventures. Returning to service in 1861, he won the Battle of Rich Mountain in western Virginia. In the West he succeeded Don Carlos Buell in command of the Army of the Cumberland. His hard-fought victory at Stones River (Murfreesboro) at the turn of 1863 boosted Northern morale, and his nearly bloodless Tullahoma Campaign maneuvered the Confederates out of middle Tennessee. However, his defeat at the Battle of Chickamauga in September 1863—one of the worst Union defeats in the Western Theater—led to his removal from command. He later commanded the Department of Missouri, defeating Sterling Price's raid in 1864.

Later life

After the war Rosecrans served as U.S. Minister to Mexico and was elected to Congress from California (1881–1885), later serving as Register of the Treasury. He died in 1898 and was eventually reinterred at Arlington National Cemetery.
