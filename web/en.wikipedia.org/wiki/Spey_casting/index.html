Spey casting

Spey casting is a fly casting technique used in fly fishing. It is a form of roll cast performed with a long, two-handed rod, allowing the angler to make long casts when there is little or no room behind for a conventional back cast — for example when standing against trees, banks, or cliffs on a river.

Origins

The technique developed in the mid-19th century on the River Spey in Scotland, where anglers fishing for Atlantic salmon with long rods of greenheart wood needed to cast heavy flies across wide pools. Traditional Spey casts include the single Spey and double Spey, in which the line is repositioned on the water before a dynamic roll cast propels it forward.

Modern practice

Modern Spey casting uses graphite rods of 12–16 feet and specialized line systems — long-belly Spey lines, Scandinavian "shooting head" styles, and compact Skagit heads designed to turn over heavy sink tips and large flies. The technique has spread from Scottish salmon rivers to steelhead fishing in the Pacific Northwest and to two-handed casting generally. The related underhand style was developed in Scandinavia by Göran Andersson.
