Music of Georgia (country)

The music of Georgia (the country in the Caucasus) is dominated by its ancient polyphonic singing tradition, considered one of the oldest polyphonic traditions in the world. In 2001, UNESCO recognised Georgian polyphonic singing as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.

Georgian singers performing polyphonic songs at a supra (feast).

Traditional polyphony

Georgian traditional music is mostly vocal and divided into East Georgian and West Georgian styles. Songs are typically sung in three voice parts: a top solo part, a second solo part, and a bass part often sung by the whole group as a sustained drone or moving line. The harmony uses dissonances — including seconds and sevenths — that would be unusual in Western music, resolved in distinctive cadential figures.

Regional styles vary widely: the table songs of Kakheti in the east feature a long, ornamented top voice over a slow drone; Gurian songs in the west include the virtuosic yodelling-like krimanchuli top voice; Svaneti in the high Caucasus preserves some of the most archaic harmonies. The famous song "Mravalzhamieri", sung at feasts, and the haunting healing song "Batonebo" are among the best-known examples.

A famous Georgian song, "Tsintskaro", was recorded by the Rustavi Choir and featured in Werner Herzog's film Nosferatu. The song "Chakrulo", performed by the ensemble Erisioni, was included on the Voyager Golden Record sent into space in 1977.

Instruments

Traditional instruments include the panduri and chonguri (lutes), the salamuri (flute), the doli (drum), the garmoni (accordion), and the duduki. Dance music, accompanying the vigorous Georgian national dances, is an important part of the tradition.

Classical and modern music

Georgia has produced classical composers such as Zakaria Paliashvili, whose opera Abesalom da Eteri is a national symbol, and Giya Kancheli, renowned for his symphonies. In the contemporary era, the British-Georgian singer Katie Melua achieved international fame, and the capital Tbilisi supports jazz, rock, and electronic scenes.
