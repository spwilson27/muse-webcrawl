Salto Grande Dam

Location: Uruguay River, between Concordia (Argentina) and Salto (Uruguay)
Construction began: 1974
Opening: 1979
Installed capacity: 1,890 MW
Turbines: 14 × 135 MW Kaplan

The Salto Grande Dam (Spanish: Represa de Salto Grande) is a large hydroelectric dam on the Uruguay River, located between Concordia in Argentina and Salto in Uruguay. It is a joint project of the two countries, administered by the Comisión Técnica Mixta de Salto Grande (CTM), and was the first great binational hydroelectric undertaking on the continent.

History

Plans to harness the rapids of Salto Grande date to the early 20th century; Argentina and Uruguay signed the governing convention in 1946, but construction only began in April 1974. The first turbine entered service in June 1979 and the plant was completed in the early 1980s. The project also created the Salto Grande Bridge on the dam crest, an international road and rail link between the two countries.

Description

The dam is a composite structure of concrete gravity sections and earth embankments with a total length of about 3,000 metres. Its powerhouse contains 14 Kaplan turbines of 135 MW each, giving an installed capacity of 1,890 MW. The reservoir covers some 783 km². Electricity is shared between Argentina and Uruguay, and the reservoir is used for recreation and fishing. Navigation is provided for by a lock, and fish passages were incorporated to mitigate the barrier the dam creates for migratory fish.
