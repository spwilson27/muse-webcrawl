Premiers of the Australian states

The premier is the head of government of each of the six Australian states. A premier performs the same role at the state level as the Prime Minister of Australia does at the federal level. The premier is appointed by the state governor, who in normal circumstances follows convention by appointing the leader of the party or coalition holding the majority in the lower house of the state parliament.

Role

Premiers chair cabinet, lead the state public service, and represent their states at intergovernmental forums such as National Cabinet alongside the prime minister and territory chief ministers. Because Australia is a federation, state governments hold primary responsibility for areas such as health, education, policing, and transport, making premiers among the country's most powerful political figures.

Long-serving premiers include Sir Henry Bolte of Victoria, Joh Bjelke-Petersen of Queensland, and Bob Carr of New South Wales. The two mainland territories (the ACT and Northern Territory) are instead led by chief ministers.
