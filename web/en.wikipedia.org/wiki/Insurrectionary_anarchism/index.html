Insurrectionary anarchism

Insurrectionary anarchism is a revolutionary theory and tendency within the anarchist movement that emphasizes insurrection and informal organization as the means of overthrowing the state and capitalism, rather than building mass formal organizations such as unions or parties.

Drawing on earlier traditions of "propaganda of the deed", modern insurrectionary anarchism developed notably in Italy in the 1970s, associated with figures such as Alfredo Bonanno and groups like the Informal Anarchist Federation (FAI/FRI). It stresses affinity groups, direct action, and permanent conflictuality with authority.

Insurrectionary anarchists are often critical of anarcho-syndicalism and platformism, viewing formal organizations as liable to become bureaucratic and reformist.
