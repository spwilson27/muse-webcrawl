The Egyptian–Ottoman War of 1831–1833, also called the First Egyptian–Ottoman War or First Syrian War, was a military conflict between the Ottoman Empire and Egypt under Muhammad Ali Pasha. It arose from Muhammad Ali's demand for control of Greater Syria as a reward for his assistance to the Sultan during the Greek War of Independence.

Course of the war

Egyptian forces under Ibrahim Pasha, Muhammad Ali's son, invaded Syria in late 1831, capturing Acre in May 1832 after a long siege and then taking Damascus and Aleppo. Egyptian armies defeated Ottoman forces at Homs and at the Battle of Belen Pass, and in December 1832 crushed the main Ottoman army at the Battle of Konya in Anatolia, opening the road to Constantinople.

Settlement

Alarmed, the European powers intervened. Russia sent forces to protect the Ottoman capital, while Britain and France pressed for a settlement. Under the Convention of Kütahya (May 1833), Muhammad Ali retained Egypt and gained Syria and Adana, though he remained nominally an Ottoman vassal. The uneasy peace collapsed into a second war in 1839.
