YCbCr

YCbCr, Y′CbCr, or Y Pb/Cb Pr/Cr is a family of color spaces used as part of the color image pipeline in video and digital photography systems. Y′ is the luma component (brightness derived from gamma-corrected RGB), and Cb and Cr are the blue-difference and red-difference chroma components. YCbCr is the basis of digital video standards such as JPEG and MPEG compression, and of component digital video interfaces defined in ITU-R BT.601 and its successors.

Principle

Human vision is far more sensitive to brightness than to colour detail, so YCbCr encodings exploit chroma subsampling — recording chroma at lower spatial resolution than luma — to reduce bandwidth with little perceived loss. Common subsampling schemes are written 4:4:4 (no subsampling), 4:2:2, 4:2:0 (the chroma is sampled at half resolution in both directions, as in JPEG, MPEG-2, H.264 and most consumer video), and 4:1:1.

Relation to other spaces

YCbCr is frequently confused with YUV, the analogue colour encoding used in PAL and NTSC television; the two are related but YUV properly refers to analogue signals, while YCbCr is its scaled and offset digital counterpart. YCbCr values are computed from RGB by a standardised linear matrix (with offsets of 16 for luma and 128 for chroma in "studio swing" encoding), and the choice of matrix depends on the standard: BT.601, BT.709, and BT.2020 define different conversion coefficients.
