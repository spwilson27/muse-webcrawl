Norrmalm

Norrmalm is a city district in central Stockholm, Sweden, taking its name from the borough (stadsdelsområde) that also includes Skeppsholmen and parts of Östermalm. Historically the northern suburb beyond the old town, it developed into Stockholm's commercial city centre, containing the main railway station, the business district around Sergels torg, and the shopping street Drottninggatan.

History

Norrmalm was laid out with a grid plan in the 17th century. In the 1950s–1970s its southern part (Lower Norrmalm) was controversially redeveloped — hundreds of older buildings were demolished to make way for modernist offices, the Kulturhuset cultural centre, the Sergels torg plaza with its glass obelisk, and the Hötorgsskraporna towers — a clearance still debated in Sweden as a loss of heritage. Landmarks include Stockholm Concert Hall (home of the Nobel Prize ceremony), the Nationalmuseum on Blasieholmen, and St James's Church.
