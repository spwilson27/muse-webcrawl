"A Night in Tunisia" is a musical composition written by American trumpeter Dizzy Gillespie in 1942, while he was a member of Benny Carter's band. It has become one of the best-known jazz standards and a signature tune of the bebop era.

Composition

The piece is famous for its Afro-Cuban-flavoured bass line and its unusual structure: a Latin-tinged A section in a minor key with a distinctive ostinato, followed by a swinging bridge. Its interlude — a dramatic break built on a descending bass line — is among the most recognisable passages in jazz; Charlie Parker's celebrated alto saxophone break on the 1946 Dial recording is a landmark of bebop improvisation.

Recordings

Gillespie recorded the tune many times, and a vocal version with lyrics by Jon Hendricks appeared under the title "Interlude". Notable recordings include those by Charlie Parker (1946), Bud Powell, Sarah Vaughan, the Modern Jazz Quartet, Art Blakey and the Jazz Messengers — whose 1957 Blue Note album took the song as its title — and Ella Fitzgerald. The composition is a fixture of jam sessions and jazz education.
