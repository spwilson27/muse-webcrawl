The dutar (Persian: دوتار, literally "two strings") is a traditional long-necked two-stringed lute played across Central and South Asia, including Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, the Uyghur region of Xinjiang, and Khorasan in Iran. It is the signature instrument of Turkmen music and of the bakhshi epic singers of the region.

Description

The dutar has a pear-shaped body carved from a single block of mulberry wood, a thin soundboard often of mulberry or apricot wood, and a long neck with tied or inlaid frets. Its two strings—traditionally silk, now usually nylon or steel—are tuned a fourth or fifth apart. The player strums and plucks with the right hand while the left hand stops the strings; the sound is soft and intimate, well suited to accompanying sung poetry.

Cultural role

In Turkmenistan the dutar accompanies the bakhshi, who sing episodes from epics such as Gorogly and romances from the destan tradition. Among Uyghurs the dutar is central to the muqam tradition and everyday folk music, typically played alongside the rawap and frame drum. The craft of making and playing the Turkmen dutar was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2021.
