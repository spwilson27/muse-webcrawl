County Galway (Irish: Contae na Gaillimhe) is a county in the west of Ireland, in the province of Connacht. It is the second-largest of Ireland's 32 counties by area. The county town, and the largest settlement, is the city of Galway, a major centre for culture, education and medical-technology industry. County Galway is known for the wild landscape of Connemara, the limestone plateau of the Burren (shared with County Clare), Lough Corrib, and the Aran Islands.

Geography

The county is divided by Lough Corrib, Ireland's second-largest lake. West of the lough lies Connemara, a rugged district of bog, quartzite mountains (the Twelve Bens and Maumturks) and an indented Atlantic coastline; it contains one of Ireland's largest Gaeltacht (Irish-speaking) regions. East of the lough the land is flatter farmland drained by the River Shannon and its tributaries, including the Suck and the Clare. Offshore lie the Aran Islands (Inishmore, Inishmaan and Inisheer), the island of Inishbofin, and Omey Island.

History

The territory corresponds broadly to the medieval kingdom of Uí Maine and the lordships of the O'Flaherty and Burke families. Galway city grew around a fort built in 1124 at the mouth of the River Corrib and was dominated for centuries by fourteen merchant families known as the "Tribes of Galway". The county in its present form was created by the Tudor administration in the 16th century. Like other western counties, Galway suffered severely during the Great Famine (1845–1852). In the 20th century the county became a stronghold of Irish cultural revival, and its Gaeltacht, university (University of Galway, founded 1845) and festivals — including the Galway International Arts Festival and the Galway Races — give it a prominent cultural profile.

Towns

Principal towns besides Galway city include Tuam, Ballinasloe, Loughrea, Athenry, Clifden, Oranmore and Gort. Athenry is notable for its largely intact medieval town walls; Clifden is the chief town of Connemara.

Economy and culture

Galway is a European hub for the medical-device industry, and also has significant tourism, fishing and agriculture sectors. The county's traditional sailing boat, the Galway hooker, and the Claddagh ring both originate here. Galway city served as a European Capital of Culture in 2020 (programme extended into 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic).
