Dog sled

A dog sled or dog sleigh is a sled pulled by one or more sled dogs, used to travel over ice and through snow. Numerous types of sleds are used, depending on their function. They can be used for dog sled racing. In Greenland the dogs pull in a fan formation; in other regions, such as Alaska and Canada, they pull in a tandem formation.

History

Dog power has been used for hunting and travel for at least 8,000 to 9,000 years. Archaeological evidence from Zhokhov Island, Siberia, suggests specialised sled dogs were bred over 9,000 years ago. Dog sleds were vital for Arctic and sub-Arctic peoples including the Inuit, Yupik, Chukchi and Sámi, and were used by European and American explorers, including Roald Amundsen's expedition to the South Pole in 1911.

In Alaska, dog teams delivered mail, supplies and medicine, most famously the 1925 serum run to Nome, commemorated by the Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race.

Construction

Traditional sleds were made of wood with runners of bone, ivory or steel. Modern racing sleds use aluminium, carbon fibre and plastic runners. The team is attached by a gangline, with the lead dog at the front responding to voice commands — "gee" for right and "haw" for left — from the musher standing on the runners at the rear.
