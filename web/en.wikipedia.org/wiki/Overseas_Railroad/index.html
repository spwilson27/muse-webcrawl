Overseas Railroad

OwnerFlorida East Coast Railway
RouteMiami to Key West, Florida, USA
Length128 miles (206 km) extension
Opened22 January 1912

The Overseas Railroad was the Key West Extension of Henry Flagler's Florida East Coast Railway, carrying trains from Miami across the Florida Keys to Key West on a chain of bridges and causeways over open ocean. Completed in 1912, it was hailed as an engineering marvel — sometimes called "the Eighth Wonder of the World" — and operated until the catastrophic Labor Day hurricane of 1935 destroyed much of the line.

Construction

Flagler, the Standard Oil co-founder who had built hotels and railways down Florida's east coast, began the extension in 1905. Thousands of workers laboured in extreme heat and isolation, building 42 bridges, including the seven-mile Long Key viaduct and the Bahia Honda bridge, the longest span. Hurricanes in 1906, 1909 and 1910 killed hundreds of workers and repeatedly wrecked the works, but construction continued. On 22 January 1912 the first train carried the 82-year-old Flagler into Key West, linking the island city to the mainland United States by rail for the first time.

Destruction and legacy

On 2 September 1935 the Labor Day hurricane, one of the most intense ever to strike the United States, tore up some 40 miles of track in the Middle Keys; a rescue train sent from Miami was swept off the rails at Islamorada, and more than 400 people, many of them World War I veterans working in federal camps, were killed. The bankrupt railway sold the roadbed to the state, which converted the surviving bridges into the Overseas Highway (U.S. Route 1), opened in 1938. Many original bridge spans still stand beside the modern highway, and several are listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
