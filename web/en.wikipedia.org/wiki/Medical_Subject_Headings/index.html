Medical Subject Headings

MeSH descriptors are arranged in hierarchical trees

Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) is a comprehensive controlled vocabulary for the purpose of indexing journal articles and books in the life sciences. Created and maintained by the United States National Library of Medicine (NLM), MeSH is used to index the MEDLINE/PubMed database and the NLM catalog, enabling precise subject searching of the biomedical literature.

Structure

MeSH consists of descriptors — preferred terms for concepts — arranged in hierarchical tree structures across 16 main categories, including anatomy, organisms, diseases, chemicals and drugs, and analytical and therapeutic techniques. A descriptor may appear in several trees, reflecting the many ways a concept can be classified. Each descriptor record includes synonyms ("entry terms") that map variant wording to the preferred term, a scope note defining the concept, and links to earlier indexing.

Descriptors can be qualified by subheadings (qualifiers) that specify an aspect of the topic, such as diagnosis, therapy, or adverse effects. Additional vocabularies cover supplementary chemical concepts, publication types, and geographic locations.

Use

Trained indexers at the NLM assign MeSH descriptors to each article indexed in MEDLINE, increasingly assisted by automated indexing software. Searchers of PubMed benefit from automatic term mapping, and searching with a MeSH descriptor automatically "explodes" the term to include narrower terms beneath it in the tree, improving recall. MeSH is updated annually, is translated into many languages, and forms part of the NLM's Unified Medical Language System (UMLS).
