Twenty-One Demands

The Twenty-One Demands (Japanese: 対華21ヶ条要求) were a set of demands presented by the Empire of Japan to the Republic of China on 18 January 1915, during the First World War. Accepting them would have greatly extended Japanese control over Shandong, Manchuria and Inner Mongolia and — under the notorious "Group Five" demands — reduced China to a virtual Japanese protectorate. Most of the demands were accepted by Yuan Shikai's government in May 1915 after a Japanese ultimatum, provoking lasting outrage in China.

Contents

The demands were arranged in five groups. Group One confirmed Japan's takeover of Germany's rights in Shandong. Group Two extended Japan's leaseholds and railway concessions in southern Manchuria and eastern Inner Mongolia to 99 years. Group Three gave Japan joint control of the Hanyeping iron and steel complex. Group Four barred China from ceding coastal territory to any other power. Group Five — initially kept secret — required Japanese advisers in China's government, police and military, arms purchases from Japan, and other measures amounting to a protectorate.

Negotiation and outcome

Chinese negotiators stalled and leaked Group Five to foreign journalists and diplomats, mobilising international criticism; Britain and the United States expressed reservations, and the US issued the doctrine of non-recognition of agreements impairing China's integrity. Faced with a Japanese ultimatum on 7 May 1915, Yuan's government accepted a revised set of demands (excluding Group Five, which was deferred) on 9 May — a date commemorated in China as National Humiliation Day. Treaties were signed on 25 May 1915.

Legacy

The episode deepened Chinese nationalism, contributed to Yuan Shikai's declining legitimacy, and fed grievances that erupted in the May Fourth Movement of 1919 when the Paris Peace Conference awarded Shandong to Japan. Shandong was returned to China in 1922 under the Washington Conference treaties, but the Twenty-One Demands remained an enduring symbol of Japanese imperialism in Asia.
