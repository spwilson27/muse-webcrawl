Khedive (Ottoman Turkish: خدیو, khidīv; from Persian khidīw, "lord") was a vice-regal title used by the rulers of the Khedivate of Egypt from 1867 to 1914. The title, ranking above pasha and roughly equivalent to viceroy or sovereign prince, was formally granted by the Ottoman Sultan to Isma'il Pasha in 1867, replacing the simpler title of wāli (governor) borne by his predecessors of the Muhammad Ali dynasty.

History

Muhammad Ali, an Ottoman Albanian commander, seized power in Egypt after the French occupation and was recognised as hereditary wāli in 1841. His grandson Isma'il, seeking recognition of Egypt's de facto autonomy, obtained the title Khedive from Sultan Abdülaziz by firman in 1867, in exchange for increased tribute. Isma'il's extravagant borrowing for projects such as the Suez Canal led to bankruptcy, his deposition in 1879, and ultimately the British occupation of Egypt in 1882.

When the Ottoman Empire entered the First World War, Britain declared a protectorate over Egypt, deposed Abbas II, and installed Hussein Kamel with the title Sultan of Egypt, ending the khedivate.
