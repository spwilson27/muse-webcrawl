The Livonian Brothers of the Sword (Latin: Fratres militiæ Christi Livoniae) were a Catholic military order founded in 1202 by Bishop Albert of Riga to spearhead the conquest and conversion of the pagan peoples of Livonia (modern Latvia and Estonia) during the Livonian Crusade. They wore white mantles marked with a red sword and cross.

History

Unlike the great orders, the Sword Brothers were recruited mainly from ministeriales and lower nobility of northern Germany, and their rule was modelled on the Templars'. Under their first master, Wenno von Rohrbach, and his successor Volkwin, the brothers built castles along the Daugava and Gauja rivers and campaigned against the Livs, Letts, Estonians, Semigallians and Curonian peoples, often in tension with the Bishop of Riga over the division of conquered lands (the order claimed one third).

The order's expansion brought it into conflict with Denmark (over northern Estonia), the Republic of Novgorod, and the Lithuanians. On 22 September 1236, returning from a raid into Samogitia, Volkwin and the flower of the order were annihilated by Samogitian and Semigallian forces at the Battle of Saule. The survivors were absorbed in 1237 into the Teutonic Order, whose Livonian branch thereafter ruled the region.

Organisation

The order was headed by a Master (Herrmeister) elected for life, advised by a chapter of senior brothers, and governed a network of castle districts administered by commanders and advocates (Vögte). Settled German colonists and converted native auxiliaries supported its small core of knight-brothers, sergeants and priests.
