Terminal degree

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A terminal degree is the highest achievable academic degree in a given field of study. The term is used mainly in the United States. In most academic disciplines the terminal degree is a research doctorate such as the Doctor of Philosophy (PhD). In some professional fields, the terminal degree is a professional doctorate, such as the Juris Doctor (JD) for law, the Doctor of Medicine (MD), the Doctor of Education (EdD), the Doctor of Business Administration (DBA) or the Doctor of Psychology (PsyD).

Usage

The concept matters in academia because tenure-track faculty positions typically require a terminal degree in the relevant field. In fields such as fine arts, creative writing and some design disciplines, a master's degree — for example the Master of Fine Arts (MFA) — is recognised as the terminal degree, since no higher degree in the practice of the field is ordinarily offered. Similarly, the Master of Architecture (M.Arch) is a professional terminal degree in architecture, and in library science the master's degree (MLS/MLIS) is terminal.

Research and professional doctorates

Research doctorates (PhD and equivalents) require original research culminating in a dissertation. Professional (or "practice") doctorates emphasise advanced professional practice. Whether a professional doctorate counts as terminal depends on the field: the JD is the terminal professional degree for law practice and legal academia in the US, while the rarer Doctor of Juridical Science (SJD) is a research degree above it.

Outside the United States

The term is less common internationally, where the doctorate (or, in some European systems, the habilitation as a further qualification beyond the doctorate) simply marks the highest qualification. Professional fields may be governed by licensing qualifications rather than degrees.
