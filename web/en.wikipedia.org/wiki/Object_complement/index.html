Object complement

Object complement (placeholder image)

In grammar, an object complement is a predicative expression that follows a direct object of a verb and completes its meaning by renaming it or describing its state. Object complements occur mainly after verbs of making, naming, electing, finding, and considering.

They elected him president. (noun complement)

We painted the house red. (adjective complement)

The news made her angry. (adjective complement)

The object complement stands in a predicative relationship with the object, similar to the relationship between a subject and a subject complement: in "They elected him president", the complement asserts of the object (him) that he became president.

Forms

An object complement may be a noun phrase, an adjective phrase, or occasionally a prepositional phrase or infinitive phrase, depending on the verb: "They consider him a genius", "I found the book interesting", "She put the vase on the table" (debated in some analyses).
