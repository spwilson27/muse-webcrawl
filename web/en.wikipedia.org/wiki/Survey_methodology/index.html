Survey methodology

A face-to-face survey interview

Survey methodology is the study of sampling individual units from a population and the associated techniques of survey data collection, such as questionnaire construction and methods for improving the number and accuracy of responses. It is a field within applied statistics and social research used in opinion polling, health research, market research, and official statistics.

Total survey error

The organising framework of the field is total survey error: the difference between a survey estimate and the true population value arises from both sampling error (because only a sample is observed) and nonsampling errors, including coverage error (the sampling frame misses parts of the population), nonresponse error (selected respondents decline to participate), measurement error (questions are misunderstood or answered inaccurately), and processing error.

Modes of data collection

Surveys are administered face-to-face, by telephone (including random-digit dialling), by mail, and increasingly online and by mobile app. Modes differ in cost, coverage, response rates, and measurement effects: respondents give more socially desirable answers to a human interviewer than to a self-administered web form, for example. Mixed-mode designs combine modes to balance these trade-offs.

Questionnaire design

Methodologists study how question wording, order, and response options shape answers. Cognitive interviewing and pretesting are used to detect comprehension problems before fieldwork. Weighting and imputation adjust the collected data for nonresponse and missing values.
