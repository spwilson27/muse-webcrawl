Lacrosse (satellite)

Lacrosse is a series of territorial radar imaging reconnaissance satellites used by the United States National Reconnaissance Office (NRO). It is also known as the Onyx program. The satellites use synthetic aperture radar (SAR) to image the Earth's surface through cloud cover and at night, producing high-resolution images regardless of weather conditions.

Overview

The first Lacrosse satellite was launched in December 1988 aboard the Space Shuttle Atlantis on mission STS-27. Subsequent satellites were launched on Titan IV rockets from Vandenberg Air Force Base. The program was officially declassified in 2008, although the satellites themselves had long been tracked by amateur observers.

Design

Lacrosse satellites are distinguished by a very large radar antenna, reported to be roughly 18 metres in diameter, which allows them to form detailed radar images of ground targets. The satellites operate in low Earth orbits at inclinations of about 57 or 68 degrees and at altitudes of several hundred kilometres.

Operations

At least five Lacrosse satellites were launched between 1988 and 2005. The later satellites are sometimes referred to as Lacrosse 2 through 5 or as Onyx Block II. They have since been succeeded by the Topaz series of radar imaging satellites under the Future Imagery Architecture program.
