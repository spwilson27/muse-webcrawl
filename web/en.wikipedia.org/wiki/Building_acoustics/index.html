Building acoustics

Building acoustics shapes how sound behaves within and between rooms.

Building acoustics is the branch of architectural acoustics concerned with controlling sound in buildings: the quality of sound within a space (room acoustics) and the insulation of spaces against airborne and structure-borne noise from inside and outside the building.

Room acoustics

Within a room, acoustic quality depends on volume, shape and surface materials. Key measures include reverberation time — classically estimated by Sabine's formula — speech intelligibility indices, and the control of echoes, focusing and flutter echoes using absorptive and diffusive treatments. Design goals differ sharply between a concert hall, a classroom, an open-plan office and a recording studio.

Sound insulation

Insulation against airborne sound (voices, music, traffic) is rated by quantities such as the sound reduction index and the Sound Transmission Class (STC). Impact sound from footsteps is controlled with resilient floor layers and floating floors; flanking transmission through junctions often limits real-world performance. Building regulations in many countries prescribe minimum insulation between dwellings, and noise from building services — fans, ducts and plumbing — is controlled by silencers, isolation mounts and careful layout.
