Birmingham Political Union

The Birmingham Political Union was a campaigning organisation in Birmingham, England, founded in 1829–30 by the banker Thomas Attwood to press for parliamentary reform. It became the model for political unions across Britain and played a major role in the crisis that led to the passing of the Reform Act 1832.

Origins

Attwood, a Birmingham banker concerned with currency policy and industrial distress, founded the Union to unite middle- and working-class reformers behind demands for a wider franchise, shorter parliaments, and the ballot. Its disciplined organisation — a council, local classes, and mass meetings — made it a formidable pressure group outside Parliament.

Role in the Reform crisis

During the "Days of May" 1832, when the Duke of Wellington attempted to form a government after the Lords rejected the Reform Bill, the Union mobilised huge meetings on Newhall Hill — reportedly attended by some 200,000 people — and Attwood threatened a run on gold and resistance to taxes. Faced with the threat of mass civil unrest and a plan to create new peers, the Lords passed the bill. The Reform Act gave Birmingham its first Members of Parliament.

Later years

After 1832 the Union declined, but it was revived in 1837 and its leaders and methods fed into the Chartist movement; Attwood himself presented the first Chartist petition to Parliament in 1839 as MP for Birmingham.
