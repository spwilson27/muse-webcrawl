Kevin A. Carson (born 1963) is an American political writer and blogger. A self-described anarchist and mutualist, Carson is the author of several books on political economy, including Studies in Mutualist Political Economy (2007), Organization Theory: A Libertarian Perspective (2008), and The Homebrew Industrial Revolution: A Low-Overhead Manifesto (2010).

Carson is a senior fellow and the Karl Hess Chair in Social Theory at the Center for a Stateless Society (C4SS), a left-libertarian think tank. His work focuses on the critique of corporate power, which he argues rests on government privilege, and on the prospects for decentralized, small-scale production technologies to undermine large hierarchical organizations.

Thought

Carson describes his political position as mutualism, drawing on the 19th-century tradition of Pierre-Joseph Proudhon, Benjamin Tucker, and the American individualist anarchists, as well as on classical liberal political economy and Austrian economics. He argues that state intervention systematically benefits large corporations at the expense of workers and small producers, through subsidies, intellectual property monopolies, and regulatory barriers to entry.

In Studies in Mutualist Political Economy, Carson attempted to revive the labor theory of value and integrate it with marginalist insights, arguing that in a genuinely free market, competition would drive prices toward the cost of production and eliminate most profit, rent, and interest as forms of unearned income.
