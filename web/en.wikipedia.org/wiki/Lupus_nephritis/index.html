Lupus nephritis

Lupus nephritis

Complication ofSystemic lupus erythematosus
DiagnosisKidney biopsy
TreatmentCorticosteroids, mycophenolate, cyclophosphamide, belimumab, voclosporin

Lupus nephritis is inflammation of the kidneys caused by systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), in which immune complexes deposit in the glomeruli. It is one of the most serious manifestations of lupus, developing in roughly 30–50% of adults with SLE (and a larger share of children), and it is a major determinant of morbidity and mortality in the disease.

Signs and symptoms

Many patients are initially asymptomatic, with disease detected by rising creatinine, proteinuria, haematuria, or hypertension on routine monitoring. More overt presentations include nephrotic syndrome (heavy proteinuria, oedema, hypoalbuminaemia) or a rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis.

Classification

Kidney biopsy is classified by the International Society of Nephrology/Renal Pathology Society (ISN/RPS) system into six classes: I (minimal mesangial), II (mesangial proliferative), III (focal proliferative, <50% of glomeruli), IV (diffuse proliferative, ≥50%), V (membranous), and VI (advanced sclerosing). The proliferative classes III and IV, alone or combined with class V, generally require aggressive immunosuppression.

Treatment

All patients receive hydroxychloroquine and blockade of the renin–angiotensin system. Induction therapy for proliferative disease uses glucocorticoids plus mycophenolate mofetil or cyclophosphamide; the calcineurin inhibitor voclosporin and the biologic belimumab are approved add-on therapies that improve renal response rates. A substantial minority of patients progress to end-stage kidney disease requiring dialysis or transplantation, although outcomes have improved markedly over recent decades.
