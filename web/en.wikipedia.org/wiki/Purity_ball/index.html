Purity ball


A father–daughter dance at a purity ball in the United States.

A purity ball is a formal dance event typically held in the United States in which fathers and their daughters—often adolescents—participate in a ceremony at which the daughters make a pledge of sexual abstinence until marriage ("purity pledge"), and the fathers pledge to protect their daughters' purity of mind, body, and soul. The events are associated with evangelical Christian abstinence culture.

History

The first purity ball is generally credited to Randy and Lisa Wilson, who held one in Colorado Springs, Colorado, in 1998 for their five daughters; the Wilsons founded a ministry promoting the concept. The events spread during the 2000s alongside abstinence-only education and purity-ring ceremonies. Ceremonies typically involve a formal dinner, ballroom dancing, a pledge signed by fathers and daughters, and sometimes the giving of a ring or other token symbolizing the father's role as guardian until the daughter marries.

Criticism

Purity balls have been criticized by feminists, sex educators, and some religious commentators, who argue that they tie a girl's worth to her virginity, place responsibility for sexual morality disproportionately on girls, and promote an abstinence model that research finds ineffective at delaying sexual activity. Defenders describe them as celebrations of the father–daughter relationship and of fathers' involvement in their daughters' lives. The phenomenon received wide media attention, including a 2008 documentary and extensive coverage in the U.S. and British press.
