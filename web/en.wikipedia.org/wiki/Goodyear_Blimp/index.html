Goodyear Blimp

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The Goodyear blimp airship with its illuminated sign flying over a stadium at dusk]
The Goodyear Blimp over a stadium.

The Goodyear Blimp is a fleet of advertising airships operated by the Goodyear Tire and Rubber Company since 1925 — among the most recognisable corporate icons in the United States. The current fleet comprises three semi-rigid Zeppelin NT airships (Wingfoot One, Two and Three, introduced 2014–2018), replacing the classic non-rigid blimps (such as Spirit of Akron and the GZ-20 fleet) that defined the type for decades at sporting events.

History

Goodyear built airships for the US Navy in both world wars and adapted the type to advertising from 1925 (Pilgrim). Blimps became fixtures of American television sports coverage from the 1960s, providing aerial camera platforms for the Rose Bowl, the World Series and the Olympics; the phrase \'reading \'Goodyear\' on the blimp\' became a cultural shorthand for big-event spectacle.

The Zeppelin NT era

Since 2014 Goodyear\'s blimps are actually semi-rigid Zeppelin NTs built with Luftschiffbau Zeppelin: 75 m long, helium-filled, with three swivelling engines and a 14-seat gondola — larger, faster and quieter than the blimps they replaced, though the company retains the \'blimp\' name for tradition.

Operations

The three ships are based in California (Carson), Ohio (Akron area) and Florida (Pompano Beach), touring events nationwide and giving rare passenger rides (usually by invitation or charity auction). A European Zeppelin NT has flown in Goodyear colours since 2020 for events such as Le Mans.
