The island of Ireland lies in the North Atlantic Ocean, west of Great Britain, and is the second-largest island of the British Isles and the third-largest in Europe. Politically it is divided between the Republic of Ireland, which covers about five-sixths of the island, and Northern Ireland, part of the United Kingdom, in the northeast. The island is nicknamed the "Emerald Isle" for its lush green landscape, a product of its mild, wet oceanic climate.

Physical geography

Ireland consists of a broad central limestone lowland, containing extensive bogs and lakes, ringed by discontinuous coastal mountain ranges including the MacGillycuddy's Reeks in County Kerry, home to Carrauntoohil (1,038.6 m), the island's highest summit, and the Wicklow Mountains south of Dublin. The River Shannon, at 360.5 km the longest river in Ireland or Britain, drains much of the midlands to the Atlantic. Lough Neagh in Northern Ireland is the largest freshwater lake in the British Isles by surface area. The western coastline is rugged and indented, with notable features including the Cliffs of Moher and the basalt columns of the Giant's Causeway in County Antrim.

Climate

The climate is temperate maritime, moderated by the North Atlantic Drift: winters are mild and summers cool, with frequent rain and cloud and rare extremes of temperature. Rainfall is heaviest in the west and in the mountains; the east around Dublin is driest.

Provinces and settlement

Traditionally the island is divided into four provinces — Leinster, Munster, Connacht, and Ulster — subdivided into 32 counties. The largest cities are Dublin, the capital, on the east coast, and Belfast in the northeast, followed by Cork, Limerick, Galway, and Derry.
