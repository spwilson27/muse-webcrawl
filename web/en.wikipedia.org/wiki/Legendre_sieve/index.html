Legendre sieve

In mathematics, the Legendre sieve is the simplest method in modern sieve theory. It is a generalisation of the sieve of Eratosthenes and is named after Adrien-Marie Legendre, who used it in his work on the distribution of prime numbers. The sieve counts numbers up to x that are not divisible by any of the first a primes, a count denoted φ(x, a).

The φ function

The function satisfies the recurrence φ(x, a) = φ(x, a − 1) − φ(x/pa, a − 1), where pa is the a-th prime. Taking a to be the number of primes up to √x gives a formula for the prime-counting function π(x) — the Meissel–Lehmer prime-counting algorithm is a practical descendant of this idea.

Limitations

The Legendre sieve itself is too weak to prove strong results about primes: its inclusion–exclusion error terms grow too quickly, a difficulty known in sieve theory as the parity problem. Nevertheless it provided the prototype for later, more powerful sieves such as Brun's sieve and the Selberg sieve.
