Sewer gas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sewer gas is a complex mixture of toxic and non-toxic gases produced and collected in sewage systems by the decomposition of organic household and industrial wastes. Its principal components typically include hydrogen sulfide, methane, ammonia, carbon dioxide, nitrogen and water vapour. The characteristic "rotten egg" odour of sewer gas is caused mainly by hydrogen sulfide.

Sources

Sewer gas enters buildings through dry or defective plumbing traps, cracked pipes, blocked vents and unused drains. The water held in a trap (such as the U-bend under a sink) normally forms a seal that prevents gas from escaping into occupied spaces; if the water evaporates or is siphoned out, the seal fails.

Health effects

Exposure to low levels of sewer gas can cause irritation of the eyes and respiratory tract, headaches, dizziness, nausea and fatigue. Hydrogen sulfide is the component of greatest concern: at high concentrations it rapidly deadens the sense of smell and can cause unconsciousness and death, a hazard well known to sewer and confined-space workers. Methane is not toxic at typical levels but is flammable and can create an explosion risk in enclosed spaces, and both methane and carbon dioxide can displace oxygen.

Prevention

Keeping traps filled with water, maintaining plumbing vents, repairing damaged pipes and sealing unused drains are the standard means of excluding sewer gas from buildings. In the nineteenth century, fear of disease carried by sewer gas ("miasma") was a major motivation for the sanitary reform and modern plumbing codes adopted in cities such as London and Paris.
