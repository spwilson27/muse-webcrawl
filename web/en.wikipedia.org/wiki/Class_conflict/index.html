Class conflict

Class conflict is the struggle between different classes in society that results from conflicting economic interests. The concept is central to Marxism, where Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels argued that the history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles. In a capitalist society, the principal conflict is between the bourgeoisie, who own the means of production, and the proletariat, who sell their labor power.

Marxist theory

According to Marx, class conflict arises because the interests of the owning class and the working class are fundamentally opposed: the bourgeoisie seeks to maximize profit by minimizing wages, while the proletariat seeks higher wages and better working conditions. Marx predicted that this conflict would eventually lead to revolution and the overthrow of capitalism, followed by the establishment of a classless, communist society.

Historical examples

Examples of class conflict throughout history include slave revolts in ancient Rome such as the uprising led by Spartacus, peasant revolts in feudal Europe, and the labor movements of the industrial era. The French Revolution and the Russian Revolution are often cited as major episodes of class conflict.

Criticism

Critics of the Marxist view argue that class is only one of many sources of social conflict, alongside race, gender, religion, and nationality. Others note that the growth of a large middle class in developed countries has complicated the simple two-class model.
