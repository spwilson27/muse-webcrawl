Place of worship

Places of worship take many forms across religions, from churches and mosques to temples and synagogues.

A place of worship is a specially designed structure or space where individuals or a group of people such as a congregation come to perform acts of devotion, veneration, or religious study. A building constructed or used for this purpose is sometimes called a house of worship. Temples, churches, mosques, and synagogues are examples of structures created for worship.

Places of worship are often among the most architecturally significant buildings in a community and have historically been centres of social as well as religious life. Many jurisdictions give them special legal treatment, such as tax exemptions, and planning law may treat their construction or conversion as a distinct land use.

By religion

Christianity

Christian places of worship are usually called churches; larger and more architecturally significant ones, especially seats of bishops, are cathedrals, and smaller ones may be chapels. Some traditions use "meeting house" (Quakers, Mennonites) or "kingdom hall" (Jehovah's Witnesses). Church buildings typically include an altar or communion table, seating for the congregation, and often stained glass, an organ, and a baptistery.

Islam

A Muslim place of worship is a mosque (masjid), characterised by a prayer hall oriented toward Mecca (the qibla wall with its mihrab niche), a minbar (pulpit), and often one or more minarets from which the call to prayer is announced. Ablution facilities are provided for ritual washing before prayer.

Judaism

A Jewish place of worship is a synagogue, which serves for prayer, study, and communal gathering. Its central features include the ark (aron ha-kodesh) containing Torah scrolls, oriented toward Jerusalem, and the bimah, a raised platform from which the Torah is read.

Hinduism, Buddhism and others

Hindu worship takes place in a mandir (temple), housing images (murtis) of deities in an inner sanctum (garbhagriha). Buddhist temples include pagodas, wats, and gompas, often centred on a stupa or image hall. Sikh worship centres on the gurdwara, which houses the Guru Granth Sahib and a communal kitchen (langar). Baháʼí houses of worship are nine-sided structures open to all faiths.
