Armagh (from Irish Ard Mhacha) is a city and the county town of County Armagh in Northern Ireland, famous as the ecclesiastical capital of Ireland: both the Roman Catholic Archbishop of Armagh and the Church of Ireland Archbishop of Armagh — each styled Primate of All Ireland — have their seats there, in two cathedrals dedicated to St Patrick on opposite hills.

History

Nearby Navan Fort (Emain Macha) was the legendary royal seat of the kings of Ulster. Tradition holds that St Patrick founded his church at Armagh in 445 AD, and it grew into one of Ireland's most important monastic and scholarly centres; the Book of Armagh (c. 807) is its best-known manuscript. Brian Boru, the high king killed at Clontarf in 1014, is buried in the Church of Ireland cathedral grounds. The city suffered heavily during the Nine Years' War and later conflicts. Granted city status in 1994, Armagh also hosts the Armagh Observatory (founded 1790) and Planetarium.

The city today

Armagh is known for its Georgian architecture, the Mall (a former racecourse turned public green ringed by notable buildings), its two St Patrick's Cathedrals, and the surrounding apple-growing countryside — County Armagh is called the "Orchard County". The city gives its name to the Armagh, Banbridge and Craigavon council area.
