Mozaffar ad-Din Shah Qajar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mozaffar ad-Din Shah Qajar (1853–1907), Shah of Persia from 1896 to 1907.

Mozaffar ad-Din Shah Qajar (Persian: مظفرالدین شاه قاجار; 25 March 1853 – 7 January 1907) was the fifth shah of Qajar Persia, reigning from 1896 until his death in 1907. He is remembered chiefly for signing the Persian Constitution of 1906 during the Persian Constitutional Revolution, which established a parliament (Majlis) and limited the absolute power of the monarchy, though he remained largely a weak and vacillating ruler who mortgaged Persia's finances to foreign loans.

Early life and accession

Mozaffar ad-Din was the fourth son of Naser al-Din Shah. Named crown prince, he spent some three decades as governor of Azerbaijan, resident in Tabriz, where he acquired a reputation for mildness and indecision rather than statecraft. He acceded to the throne after his father was assassinated on 1 May 1896 by Mirza Reza Kermani. His coronation in Tehran was delayed by empty coffers; money for the ceremonies had to be raised through the Russian-sponsored Discount and Loan Bank.

Reign

Plagued by ill health — he suffered from kidney disease and gout — and by a chronic shortage of funds, Mozaffar ad-Din financed his court and three expensive journeys to Europe (in 1900, 1902, and 1905) through loans from Russia and concessions to foreign interests. On his first European tour an anarchist attempted his life in Paris. The loans and concessions, together with the appointment of Belgian officials to run the customs administration, provoked growing resentment among merchants, clergy, and intellectuals.

Constitutional Revolution

In late 1905 protests over high prices, foreign economic dominance, and the flogging of sugar merchants escalated into a mass movement demanding a "house of justice" and a constitution. After months of strikes and sanctuary (bast) protests, including a mass sit-in at the British legation in the summer of 1906, Mozaffar ad-Din capitulated. He signed the royal decree convening an elected assembly in August 1906, the first Majlis opened in October, and on 30 December 1906 he signed the new constitution. Gravely ill, he died eight days later, on 7 January 1907, and was succeeded by his son Mohammad Ali Shah, who attempted to destroy the constitutional order.

Legacy

Mozaffar ad-Din is credited with bringing cinema to Iran: his court photographer Mirza Ebrahim Akkas Bashi filmed the shah's 1900 European trip and public ceremonies in Tehran, and a cinema hall was established at the palace. His reign marks the end of unchallenged Qajar absolutism.
