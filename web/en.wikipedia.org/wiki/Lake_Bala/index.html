Bala Lake (Llyn Tegid)
Location: Gwynedd, Wales
Coordinates: 52°53′N 3°38′W

Bala Lake, known in Welsh as Llyn Tegid, is a large freshwater glacial lake in Gwynedd, Wales. At nearly 6 km long it is the largest natural lake in Wales. The River Dee rises in the surrounding hills and flows through the lake; the town of Bala sits at its northern end.

Geology and management

The lake occupies a glacial trough along the Bala fault. Its level is artificially controlled as part of the River Dee regulation scheme, using sluice gates near Bala and the associated river works built after serious flooding of the Dee valley in the 1950s; this also helps secure water supplies abstracted downstream for north-west England.

Wildlife

Llyn Tegid is a designated Ramsar site and Site of Special Scientific Interest. It is famous for the gwyniad (Coregonus pennantii), a rare freshwater whitefish found naturally only in this lake, left behind after the last Ice Age. The lake also supports a population of the glutinous snail and otters, and a unique fish locally called the "gwyniad's" companion species community. Between the 16th and 17th centuries, a legendary monster, sometimes jokingly called "Teggie", was said to inhabit the lake.

Recreation

The narrow-gauge Bala Lake Railway (Rheilffordd Llyn Tegid) runs along the south-eastern shore from Llanuwchllyn to Bala. The lake is a popular centre for sailing, canoeing, windsurfing and wild swimming, and the surrounding area lies within Snowdonia (Eryri) National Park.
