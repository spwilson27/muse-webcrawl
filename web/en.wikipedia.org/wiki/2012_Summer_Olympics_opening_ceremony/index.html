The 2012 Summer Olympics opening ceremony, officially titled Isles of Wonder, took place on the evening of 27 July 2012 in the Olympic Stadium, London. Directed by filmmaker Danny Boyle, with musical direction by Underworld and writing by Frank Cottrell-Boyce, the ceremony traced a celebratory history of Britain — from an agrarian "green and pleasant land" through the Industrial Revolution, the creation of the National Health Service and British popular culture — and was widely acclaimed as one of the finest Olympic ceremonies ever staged.

Programme

The ceremony opened with a filmed sequence following the River Thames and a rural scene in the stadium, complete with maypole, cricket and livestock. The Industrial Revolution segment ("Pandemonium") saw the countryside stripped away as chimneys rose from the ground and molten metal was forged into five glowing Olympic rings that ascended above the stadium. Kenneth Branagh, dressed as engineer Isambard Kingdom Brunel, delivered Caliban's "Be not afeard" speech from Shakespeare's The Tempest, from which the ceremony took its title.

Subsequent sections celebrated the NHS and children's literature — with nurses and children on hospital beds joined by characters including Mary Poppins and a giant Lord Voldemort — and British music and technology. A comic sequence featured Rowan Atkinson as Mr Bean with the London Symphony Orchestra, and a pre-recorded film showed Daniel Craig as James Bond escorting Queen Elizabeth II to the stadium, apparently parachuting in; the Queen's appearance alongside the fictional spy was one of the night's most talked-about moments.

Parade and lighting of the cauldron

More than 200 national teams paraded into the stadium, led as customary by Greece and concluding with Great Britain to David Bowie's "Heroes". After speeches, the Olympic flame — carried down the Thames on a speedboat by footballer David Beckham — was brought into the stadium and passed to seven young athletes, each nominated by a British Olympic champion, who together lit Thomas Heatherwick's cauldron of 204 copper petals, one for each competing nation. Paul McCartney closed the show with "Hey Jude".

Reception

The ceremony drew a UK television audience peaking at nearly 27 million and an estimated global audience in the hundreds of millions. Critics praised its humanity, humour and inventiveness, contrasting its warm, narrative approach with the monumental scale of the 2008 Beijing ceremony.
