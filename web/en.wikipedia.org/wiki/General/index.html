General

A general officer is an officer of high rank in the armies, and in some nations' air and space forces, marines or naval infantry. In some usages, the term general refers specifically to a four-star commissioned officer rank; in others it is the generic name for all grades of general officer, which typically include brigadier general (or brigadier), major general, lieutenant general, and general, in ascending order.

History

The word general derives from the Latin generalis ("general, of a whole class"), used in titles such as capitaine général in 16th-century France, where an officer was appointed to command an army as a whole rather than a single company or regiment. The rank spread through European armies during the early modern period as armies grew larger and required a professional senior command structure.

Rank structure

Most modern armies organize general-officer grades according to the NATO rank scale (OF-6 to OF-9, and OF-10 in wartime grades such as field marshal or general of the army):

  • Brigadier general / brigadier — typically commands a brigade.
  • Major general — typically commands a division.
  • Lieutenant general — typically commands a corps.
  • General — typically commands an army or the entire service.
  • Field marshal / general of the army — five-star grades, usually honorary or wartime.

Generals are appointed by the head of state or government in most countries and serve as senior operational commanders and advisers on military policy. The corresponding naval ranks are the grades of admiral.

Other uses

"General" also appears in non-army titles, including general of the air force, general of police in some countries, the superior general of a religious order (such as the Jesuits), the attorney general, surgeon general, and the secretary-general of an organization.
