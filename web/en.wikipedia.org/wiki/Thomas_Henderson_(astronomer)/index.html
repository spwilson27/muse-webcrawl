Thomas James Alan Henderson (28 December 1798 – 23 November 1844) was a Scottish astronomer and mathematician best known for measuring the parallax of Alpha Centauri — the first published measurement of the distance to a fixed star. He was the first Astronomer Royal for Scotland, from 1834 until his death.

Early life

Henderson was born in Dundee and trained as a lawyer, working as a clerk and assistant to powerful patrons including the Earl of Eldon. Astronomy was his passionate hobby; his skill with observations and calculations brought him to the attention of the astronomical community, and in 1831 he was appointed to work at the Royal Observatory at the Cape of Good Hope.

The Alpha Centauri measurement

At the Cape, Henderson and his assistant William Meadows made intensive observations of Alpha Centauri in 1832–33, suspecting its large proper motion meant it was nearby. Back in Edinburgh he reduced the data and found a parallax of about one arcsecond, implying a distance of roughly three and a quarter light-years (the modern value is 4.37 light-years). Cautious about instrumental error, he delayed publication; Friedrich Wilhelm Bessel announced his parallax of 61 Cygni first, in 1838, and Henderson published in January 1839. Priority histories credit Henderson with the first measurement, Bessel with the first publication.

Astronomer Royal for Scotland

In 1834 Henderson became the first holder of the chair of astronomy at Edinburgh and Astronomer Royal for Scotland, based at the Royal Observatory on Calton Hill. Ill health plagued his later years, and he died in Edinburgh in 1844, aged 45.
