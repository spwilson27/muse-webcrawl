Prognosis

Prognosis (Greek: πρόγνωσις, "fore-knowing") is a medical term for predicting the likely or expected development of a disease, including whether the signs and symptoms will improve or worsen (and how quickly) or remain stable over time; expectations of quality of life, such as the ability to carry out daily activities; the potential for complications and associated health issues; and the likelihood of survival (including life expectancy). A prognosis is made on the basis of the normal course of the diagnosed disease, the individual's physical and mental condition, and additional factors.

Methodology

Prognostic estimates are commonly derived from clinical studies of groups of similar patients, expressed as statistics such as survival rates, median survival, and five-year survival. Individual prognosis also depends on prognostic factors such as disease stage, biomarkers, comorbidities, and response to treatment. Prediction models and scoring systems are used in fields such as oncology and cardiology.

Related concepts

Prognosis contrasts with diagnosis, which identifies the disease that is present. Prognostic information guides treatment decisions, advance care planning, and counselling of patients and families. The term is also used outside medicine, for example in meteorology and economics, for a forecast of future conditions.
