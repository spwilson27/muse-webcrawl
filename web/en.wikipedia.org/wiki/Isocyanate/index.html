Isocyanate

In organic chemistry, an isocyanate is a compound containing the functional group –N=C=O, consisting of a nitrogen atom double-bonded to carbon and to oxygen. Isocyanates react with compounds containing alcohols (hydroxyl groups) to form polyurethanes, which makes them a cornerstone of the polymer industry.

Production and uses

The most important commercial isocyanates are methylene diphenyl diisocyanate (MDI) and toluene diisocyanate (TDI), produced industrially by treating amines with phosgene. They are used to manufacture flexible and rigid foams, coatings, adhesives, sealants and elastomers.

Safety

Isocyanates are potent respiratory sensitizers and a leading cause of occupational asthma; exposure is strictly regulated. The most notorious incident involving an isocyanate was the 1984 Bhopal disaster, in which a leak of methyl isocyanate gas killed thousands of people in India.
