The Grand National Assembly of Turkey (Turkish: Türkiye Büyük Millet Meclisi, TBMM) is the unicameral Turkish legislature and the supreme legislative body of the Republic of Turkey. Founded in Ankara on 23 April 1920 during the Turkish War of Independence, it originally convened as a rival government to the Ottoman administration in occupied Istanbul, and its opening date is commemorated as National Sovereignty and Children's Day.

History

The Assembly was established under the leadership of Mustafa Kemal (later Atatürk) as the seat of the nationalist movement. It governed the country through the War of Independence, abolished the Sultanate in 1922, and proclaimed the Republic of Turkey on 29 October 1923. The Assembly has operated under the constitutions of 1921, 1924, 1961, and 1982, and was briefly bicameral under the 1961 constitution, which added a Senate of the Republic until 1980.

Composition and elections

Since a 2017 constitutional referendum that transformed Turkey into a presidential system, the Assembly has 600 members elected for five-year terms by proportional representation in multi-member districts using the D'Hondt method, with a national electoral threshold reduced from 10% to 7% in 2022. Members must be at least 18 years old. General elections are normally held on the same day as presidential elections.

Powers

The Assembly enacts, amends, and repeals laws; approves the state budget; ratifies international treaties; decides on the declaration of war and the dispatch of armed forces abroad; and elects members of high judicial bodies. Under the presidential system its oversight of the executive is limited, though it retains the power to refer the president to the Supreme Criminal Tribunal by a two-thirds vote.
