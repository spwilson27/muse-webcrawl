Conversos

Conversos (Spanish and Portuguese for "converts") were Jews in Spain and Portugal who converted to Catholicism, and their descendants, particularly during the 14th and 15th centuries. Following waves of anti-Jewish violence — notably the pogroms of 1391 that began in Seville — and mounting legal pressure, tens of thousands of Iberian Jews accepted baptism, many under coercion. Those who secretly continued to practise Judaism were called judaizantes (Judaizers) by the authorities and are known in Hebrew tradition as anusim ("the forced ones"); the pejorative term marranos was also applied to them.

History

After the 1391 massacres, further pressure followed: the preaching campaigns of Vincent Ferrer, the Laws of Ayllón (1412), and the Disputation of Tortosa (1413–14) produced further conversions. By the mid-15th century conversos formed a substantial and often prosperous urban class in Castile and Aragon, prominent in trade, tax farming, medicine, and royal administration — which itself bred resentment among "Old Christians".

Anti-converso violence erupted in Toledo in 1449, accompanied by the first limpieza de sangre ("purity of blood") statute excluding conversos from office. Suspicions that many converts secretly practised Judaism led Ferdinand and Isabella to establish the Spanish Inquisition in 1478. The Alhambra Decree of 1492 expelled practising Jews from Spain, and Portugal followed with forced mass conversion in 1497, creating a large Portuguese converso (New Christian) population.

Emigration and legacy

Many conversos emigrated to Italy, North Africa, the Ottoman Empire, the Low Countries, and the Spanish and Portuguese colonies in the Americas, where some openly returned to Judaism — forming notable communities in Amsterdam and elsewhere. Inquisition tribunals in Mexico City, Lima, and Cartagena prosecuted alleged Judaizers into the 18th century. Converso ancestry remained socially salient in Iberia for centuries; on Mallorca, descendants known as Chuetas faced discrimination into modern times.
