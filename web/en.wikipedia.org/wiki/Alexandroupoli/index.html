Alexandroupoli (Greek: Αλεξανδρούπολη) is a city in northeastern Greece and the capital of the Evros regional unit. It lies on the Aegean Sea at the eastern edge of Greek Thrace, near the border with Turkey and the Evros river delta. The port city is the largest urban centre of Greek Thrace.

History

The modern city was founded in the 1870s as a fishing village called Dedeağaç during Ottoman rule, and grew after the arrival of the railway linking it to Constantinople and Thessaloniki. It changed hands repeatedly during the Balkan Wars and First World War, passing between the Ottoman Empire, Bulgaria and Greece, and was renamed Alexandroupoli in 1920 in honour of King Alexander of Greece. The Treaty of Lausanne (1923) confirmed it as Greek territory.

Economy and transport

Alexandroupoli is a commercial port and transport hub where the Egnatia Odos motorway and the railway from Thessaloniki meet the routes toward Turkey. In recent years it has gained strategic importance as an energy gateway, hosting a floating liquefied natural gas (LNG) terminal and serving as a logistics point for NATO. Ferries connect it with the island of Samothrace. The nearby Evros Delta National Park is an important wetland for migratory birds.
