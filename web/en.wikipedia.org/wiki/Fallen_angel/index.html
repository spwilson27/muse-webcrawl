Fallen angel
ReligionsChristianity, Judaism, Islam
Principal sourcesBook of Enoch, Revelation, Isaiah

A fallen angel is an angel who, rebelling against God, is exiled or cast out of Heaven. The term is most developed in Christian theology, where fallen angels are identified with demons led by Satan, but similar beings appear in Judaism and Islam.

Origins

The idea draws on several scriptural passages: the "sons of God" who descend to earth in Genesis 6, elaborated in the apocryphal Book of Enoch into the story of the Watchers, angels who corrupted humanity and were imprisoned; the taunt against the king of Babylon in Isaiah 14, later read as the fall of Lucifer; and the war in heaven in Revelation 12, in which Michael casts down the dragon and his angels.

In theology and literature

Augustine taught that angels were created good and that some fell by their own free will through pride; Aquinas held their choice to be irrevocable. The most famous literary treatment is John Milton's Paradise Lost (1667), which dramatises Satan's revolt and expulsion. In Islam, Iblis is usually classed not as an angel but as a jinn who refused to bow before Adam.

Other uses

In finance, a "fallen angel" is a bond downgraded from investment grade to junk status; the phrase is also used generally for a once-favoured person or institution that has fallen from grace.
