Mark Antony

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Marble bust of Mark Antony, from a Roman portrait]

Mark Antony (83–30 BC; Marcus Antonius) was a Roman general and politician, Julius Caesar's most trusted lieutenant and, after Caesar's assassination, a member of the Second Triumvirate with Octavian and Lepidus that ruled the Roman world. He governed the eastern provinces, took Cleopatra VII of Egypt as ally and lover, married her and made the Donations of Alexandria to their children — gifts Octavian's propaganda turned into proof of his oriental corruption. Defeated at Actium (31 BC) and pursued to Alexandria, he died by suicide in August 30 BC as Octavian took Egypt; Cleopatra followed days later. Shakespeare's Antony and Cleopatra fixed his image as the great soldier undone by love.

Caesar's lieutenant, triumvir

Antony distinguished himself in Gaul, defended Caesar's interests as tribune in 49 BC (his expulsion was Caesar's stated cause of war), fought at Pharsalus and governed Italy turbulently during Caesar's absences. His funeral oration for Caesar (44 BC) — as dramatised from the sources by Shakespeare — turned the crowd against the assassins. In the ensuing wars he joined Octavian and Lepidus in the Triumvirate (43 BC), whose proscriptions killed thousands, Cicero among them; then with Octavian he destroyed Brutus and Cassius at Philippi (42 BC) and took the rich east as his sphere.

Cleopatra and downfall

Summoning Cleopatra to Tarsus (41 BC), Antony entered the alliance — political, financial and personal — that defined his life: twins, then a third child, a marriage (while married to Octavian's sister Octavia) that Roman opinion read as treason. His Parthian invasion (36 BC) failed expensively; the Donations of Alexandria (34 BC) crowned Cleopatra 'Queen of Kings' and enraged Rome. War was declared (on Cleopatra, pointedly) in 32 BC; after Actium and a last stand in Egypt, Antony fell on his sword on hearing a false report of Cleopatra's death, dying in her arms in her mausoleum — the scene Plutarch made immortal.
