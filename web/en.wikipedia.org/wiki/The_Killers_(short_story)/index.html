"The Killers" is a short story by Ernest Hemingway, first published in Scribner's Magazine in 1927 and included the same year in the collection Men Without Women. It is one of Hemingway's most famous and most anthologised stories, and an early appearance of his recurring character Nick Adams.

Plot

Two hitmen, Al and Max, enter a lunchroom in a small town outside Chicago, tie up Nick Adams and the cook, and announce that they have come to kill Ole Andreson, a former prizefighter who "got mixed up in something in Chicago" and now eats at the diner each evening. Andreson never arrives, and the killers leave. Nick goes to warn Andreson, who lies fully dressed on his bed, staring at the wall; he refuses to run and says he has decided he can do nothing about it. Nick returns to the diner shaken, declaring that he is going to leave town because he cannot stand thinking about Andreson waiting to be killed.

Themes and style

The story is a classic example of Hemingway's "iceberg theory", conveying most of its meaning beneath terse, dialogue-driven surface action. It is commonly read as an initiation story in which Nick confronts violence, death, and futility for the first time, while Andreson's passive acceptance of death embodies a grim fatalism.

Adaptations

The story has been adapted for film several times, most notably Robert Siodmak's film noir The Killers (1946), starring Burt Lancaster and Ava Gardner, which expands the story's premise into a full investigation, and the 1964 version starring Lee Marvin and Ronald Reagan.
