Switch

[Image placeholder: electrical switch]

In electrical engineering, a switch is an electrical component that can disconnect or connect the conducting path in an electrical circuit, interrupting the electric current or diverting it from one conductor to another. The most common type of switch is an electromechanical device consisting of one or more sets of movable electrical contacts connected to external circuits.

Operation

When a pair of contacts is touching, current can pass; when separated by an insulating gap, no current flows. Switches can be operated manually — such as a light switch or a keyboard button — or function as sensing elements for position, level, or temperature (as in a thermostat), or as high-speed semiconductor devices such as transistors.

Terminology

Switches are described by their poles and throws: an SPST (single-pole, single-throw) switch makes or breaks one circuit, while changeover switches (SPDT) connect a common terminal to one of two paths. Momentary switches, such as doorbell buttons, close only while actuated, unlike maintained (latching) switches.

Types

Common types include toggle, rocker, rotary, slide, push-button, mercury tilt, reed, and knife switches. In electronics and computing, the term also extends to switching circuits and network switches. Arcing at contacts when interrupting current is a key design concern, addressed by contact materials and snap-action mechanisms.
