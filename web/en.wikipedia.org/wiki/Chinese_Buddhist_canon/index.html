Chinese Buddhist canon

The Chinese Buddhist canon refers to the total body of Buddhist literature deemed canonical in Chinese Mahayana Buddhism, traditionally called the Dàzàngjīng (大藏經, "Great Treasury of Scriptures"). It includes not only translations of Indian Āgamas, Vinaya and Abhidharma texts and Mahayana sutras, but also commentaries, histories, encyclopaedias, biographies and original compositions by Chinese, Korean and Japanese masters. Successive imperial editions were printed from the Song dynasty onward, and the canon is preserved today in standard modern editions such as the Japanese Taishō Tripiṭaka.

History

Buddhist texts were translated into Chinese from the 2nd century CE onward, by translators such as Kumārajīva and Xuanzang, and cataloguers organised the growing corpus into a "three baskets" (Tripiṭaka) structure. The first complete printed canon, the Kaibao Canon, was carved onto woodblocks in Sichuan between 971 and 983 under the Song dynasty. Subsequent editions included the Khitan (Liao) Canon, the Jin-dynasty Zhaocheng Canon, and the Korean Goryeo Canon — the second carving of which survives as the Tripitaka Koreana at Haeinsa, comprising over 80,000 woodblocks and designated a UNESCO Memory of the World.

Contents

The canon is typically divided into sections for sutras (scriptures attributed to the Buddha), vinaya (monastic discipline), śāstras (treatises and commentaries), and miscellaneous works. Distinctively, the Chinese canon preserves many Indian texts whose Sanskrit originals are lost, as well as major East Asian compositions such as the Platform Sutra, the treatises of Zhiyi and Fazang, Chan records and Pure Land commentaries.

Modern editions

The Taishō Shinshū Daizōkyō (Taishō Tripiṭaka), published in Japan between 1924 and 1932, is the most widely used scholarly edition, and its digitised version maintained by CBETA (Chinese Buddhist Electronic Text Association) has made the canon freely searchable online. The Zhonghua Dazangjing, published in the People's Republic of China, collates multiple historical editions.
