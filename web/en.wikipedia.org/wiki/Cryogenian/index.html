Cryogenian

Cryogenian Period
720 – 635 million years ago
Era: Neoproterozoic
Glaciations: Sturtian, Marinoan

The Cryogenian is a geologic period in the Neoproterozoic Era, lasting from about 720 to 635 million years ago. It was marked by the most severe ice ages in Earth's history, the Sturtian and Marinoan glaciations, during which ice may have reached the equator — the Snowball Earth hypothesis.

Climate

Glacial deposits at low palaeolatitudes, cap carbonates and banded iron formations record extreme cold followed by rapid warming as volcanic carbon dioxide accumulated under the ice. The breakup of the supercontinent Rodinia may have triggered cooling through enhanced weathering.

Life

Despite the cold, microbial life persisted in refugia; sponge-grade animals and diverse acritarch microfossils appear, and the following Ediacaran Period saw the first large multicellular organisms.

Name

The name derives from Greek kryos (cold) and genesis (birth). The period was ratified in 1990, the first defined by a Global Boundary Stratotype precursor based on global events rather than a single section.
