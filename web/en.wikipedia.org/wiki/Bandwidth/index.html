Bandwidth

Bandwidth is a term used in several technical fields to describe a range of frequencies or a rate of data transfer. In signal processing and communications, bandwidth is the width of a frequency band, measured in hertz. In computer networking, it commonly refers to the maximum rate at which data can be transmitted over a connection, measured in bits per second.

Signal bandwidth unitHertz (Hz)
Network bandwidth unitBits per second (bit/s)

Signal processing

For an analogue signal, bandwidth is the difference between the upper and lower frequencies of a continuous band. A telephone voice channel, for example, traditionally occupies roughly 300 Hz to 3,400 Hz, a bandwidth of about 3.1 kHz. The Shannon–Hartley theorem relates the maximum data rate of a channel to its bandwidth and signal-to-noise ratio.

Networking

In networking, bandwidth describes the capacity of a link: a 100 Mbit/s Ethernet connection can in theory transfer 100 million bits each second. Actual throughput is usually lower because of protocol overhead, congestion, and latency. Bandwidth is often confused with latency, which measures delay rather than capacity.

Other uses

The term is also used metaphorically for a person's capacity to deal with work or information, and in optics for the spectral width of light sources.
