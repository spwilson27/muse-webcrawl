Katechon

The katechon (from Greek: τὸ κατέχον, "that which withholds", or ὁ κατέχων, "the one who withholds" or "the restrainer") is a biblical concept that appears in the Second Epistle to the Thessalonians, where an unnamed entity or person is said to hold back the revelation of the "man of lawlessness" until the appointed time. It has generated a long tradition of speculation in theology and political thought about what — or who — restrains evil and the Antichrist.

Biblical text

"And you know what is restraining him now so that he may be revealed in his time. For the mystery of lawlessness is already at work. Only he who now restrains it will do so until he is out of the way." — 2 Thessalonians 2:6–7 (ESV)

Paul writes in both the neuter ("what restrains", τὸ κατέχον) and the masculine ("he who restrains", ὁ κατέχων), a grammatical ambiguity that has fuelled competing identifications throughout church history.

Interpretations

Early church fathers such as Tertullian and John Chrysostom commonly identified the restrainer with the Roman Empire and its emperor: so long as the empire stood, the end could not come — a belief that also led Christians to pray for the empire despite persecution. Other interpretations, ancient and modern, have proposed the preaching of the gospel, the Holy Spirit, the archangel Michael, the Jewish state, the church itself, or a future individual. Many modern scholars regard the katechon as a rhetorical device through which Paul shared with his first readers an understanding he does not spell out for later ones.

In political thought

The German jurist Carl Schmitt seized on the katechon as a central concept of political theology: in The Nomos of the Earth (1950) he presents the belief in a restrainer of the Antichrist as the force that made the Christian empire and its historical eschatology possible, bridging the delay of the end times with concrete political order. The concept has since been taken up, in different registers, by thinkers such as Jacob Taubes, Giorgio Agamben and Massimo Cacciari (author of The Withholding Power), and figures in contemporary debates about empire, eschatology and the restraint of chaos.
