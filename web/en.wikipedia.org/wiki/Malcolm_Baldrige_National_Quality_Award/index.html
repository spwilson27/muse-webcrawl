Malcolm Baldrige National Quality Award

The Malcolm Baldrige National Quality Award is the highest presidential honour for performance excellence in the United States. Established by Congress in 1987 and named for Malcolm Baldrige Jr., Secretary of Commerce from 1981 until his death in a rodeo accident in 1987, the award is presented annually by the President of the United States and administered by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST).

The Baldrige framework

Organisations are assessed against the Baldrige Excellence Framework, whose criteria address seven categories: leadership; strategy; customers; measurement, analysis, and knowledge management; workforce; operations; and results. The framework is sector-specific, with versions for business/nonprofit, education, and health care organisations, and it is widely used for self-assessment even by organisations that never apply for the award.

History and recipients

The award was created amid concern about American competitiveness in the 1980s, particularly against Japanese manufacturers practising total quality management. The first awards were given in 1988; Motorola was among the earliest recipients, recognised the same year it developed Six Sigma. Other notable recipients include Xerox Business Services, Ritz-Carlton (twice), FedEx, and numerous hospitals and school districts after eligibility was extended to health care and education in 1999 and to nonprofits in 2007. Up to eighteen awards may be given annually across six categories, though in some years no award is made in a category.

Many US states run Baldrige-based quality award programmes, and the criteria influenced quality frameworks abroad, complementing models such as the Deming Prize and the EFQM Excellence Model.
