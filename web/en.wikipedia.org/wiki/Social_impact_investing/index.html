Social impact investing

Impact investing refers to investments made with the intention to generate positive, measurable social and environmental impact alongside a financial return. The term was coined in 2007 at a Rockefeller Foundation convening at Bellagio, Italy. Impact investments can be made in both emerging and developed markets, and target a range of returns from below-market (concessionary) to market-rate, depending on investors' strategic goals.

Characteristics

Practitioners generally identify four defining characteristics of impact investing:

  1. Intentionality — the investor intends the investment to have a positive social or environmental effect.
  2. Investment with return expectations — the investment is expected to generate a financial return on capital or at least return the capital.
  3. Range of return expectations and asset classes — investments span cash, fixed income, private equity, and venture capital.
  4. Impact measurement — investors commit to measuring and reporting social and environmental performance, using standardized metrics such as the IRIS+ system.

History and market

Impact investing grew out of older traditions of socially responsible investing, community development finance, and microfinance, but differs in proactively seeking measurable benefit rather than merely screening out harmful activities. The Global Impact Investing Network (GIIN), founded in 2009, estimates the market at over US$1 trillion in assets under management worldwide (estimated at $1.164 trillion in 2022). Major participants include pension funds, foundations, development finance institutions such as the IFC, fund managers, and family offices.

Related instruments

Social impact bonds (also "pay-for-success" contracts), first launched in the UK in 2010 at Peterborough Prison, link investor returns to verified social outcomes paid for by government. Green bonds and social bonds earmark proceeds for environmental and social projects. Critics of impact investing point to risks of "impact washing" — overstating outcomes — and to the difficulty of measuring and attributing social change, which has driven efforts toward standard frameworks such as the Operating Principles for Impact Management (2019).
