Statistical hypothesis testing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Statistical hypothesis testing is a method of statistical inference used to decide whether sample data are consistent with a specified hypothesis. A statistical hypothesis test typically pits a null hypothesis (H0), usually representing "no effect", against an alternative hypothesis (H1), and evaluates the probability of the observed data — summarised by a p-value — under the assumption that the null hypothesis is true.

Process

The standard procedure is to formulate the hypotheses, choose a significance level α (often 0.05), compute a test statistic from the data, and reject the null hypothesis if the statistic falls in the critical region. Rejecting a true null hypothesis is a Type I error; failing to reject a false null is a Type II error, with 1 − β being the statistical power of the test.

History and criticism

The modern framework blends the significance testing of R. A. Fisher with the decision-theoretic approach of Jerzy Neyman and Egon Pearson. Hypothesis testing has been criticised for the misinterpretation of p-values and its role in publication bias and the replication crisis, prompting calls for greater use of confidence intervals, effect sizes and Bayesian methods.
