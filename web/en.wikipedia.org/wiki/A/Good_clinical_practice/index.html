Good clinical practice

Good clinical practice (GCP)
FieldClinical research regulation
Key standardICH E6 guideline (first adopted 1996)
Applies toDesign, conduct, monitoring, recording and reporting of clinical trials

Good clinical practice (GCP) is an international ethical and scientific quality standard for designing, conducting, recording and reporting trials that involve the participation of human subjects. Compliance provides public assurance that the rights, safety and well-being of trial subjects are protected — consistent with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki — and that the clinical trial data are credible.

The ICH guideline

The most widely adopted GCP standard is the ICH E6 guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation, finalised in 1996 and revised as E6(R2) in 2016; a comprehensive modernisation, E6(R3), was adopted in 2025. It sets out thirteen core principles and detailed responsibilities for institutional review boards / independent ethics committees, investigators and sponsors, covering informed consent, protocol compliance, source documentation, adverse-event reporting, monitoring and audit.

Implementation

GCP has legal force through medicines regulation: the EU Clinical Trials Regulation, US FDA regulations (21 CFR Parts 50, 56, 312) and their equivalents require trials supporting marketing applications to follow it. Investigators and sponsors document compliance through essential documents, standard operating procedures and inspection by regulators.
