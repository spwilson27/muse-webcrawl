Randomized controlled trial

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A randomized controlled trial (RCT) is a type of scientific experiment, most commonly used in clinical research, in which participants are randomly allocated to receive one of several interventions, typically an experimental treatment or a control such as standard care or a placebo. Randomisation minimises selection bias and balances known and unknown confounders between groups, making RCTs the gold standard for evaluating the efficacy of new treatments.

Design

Key design elements include random allocation, concealment of the allocation sequence, blinding of participants, clinicians and outcome assessors, pre-specified outcome measures, and analysis by intention to treat. Trials are usually registered in advance and reported following the CONSORT statement. The 1948 Medical Research Council trial of streptomycin for tuberculosis is widely regarded as the first modern RCT.

Limitations

RCTs can be expensive and slow, may exclude patients unrepresentative of real-world practice, and can be unethical when withholding a known effective treatment. Alternatives and complements include observational studies, pragmatic trials and meta-analyses of multiple RCTs.
