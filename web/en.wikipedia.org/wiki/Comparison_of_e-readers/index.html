Comparison of e-readers

[Image: E Ink e-readers compared]

An e-reader is a portable electronic device designed primarily for reading digital e-books and periodicals, typically using an electrophoretic (E Ink) display that is readable in bright sunlight, consumes very little power, and can run for weeks on a charge. This article compares e-reader devices by manufacturer, display, supported formats and connectivity.

Characteristics compared

Devices are compared on screen size (commonly 6–7 inches for book readers and 10.3 inches or more for note-taking models), resolution (typically 300 ppi for text models), front-lighting with adjustable colour temperature, water resistance, storage (8–64 GB), weight, physical page-turn buttons, stylus support and battery life. Colour E Ink (Kaleido and Gallery panels) has appeared in models from Kobo, PocketBook and Onyx since 2020.

History

Early devices included the Rocket eBook (1998) and Sony's Librie (2004), the first with an E Ink display. The Amazon Kindle (2007) popularised the category with integrated wireless book purchasing; competition from Kobo (2010), Barnes & Noble's Nook (2009) and others followed, while tablet computers and smartphones limited the market to dedicated reading devices.
