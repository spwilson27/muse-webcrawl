Banská Bystrica

Banská Bystrica (German: Neusohl; Hungarian: Besztercebánya) is a city in central Slovakia, located on the Hron River in a long valley encircled by the mountain chains of the Low Tatras, the Veľká Fatra and the Kremnica Mountains. With a population of roughly 76,000, it is the sixth most populous municipality in Slovakia and the capital of the Banská Bystrica Region. The name means "mining creek" — banská referring to mining, Bystrica being a Slavic name for a fast-flowing stream.

History

The site has been mined since the Bronze Age. German settlers were invited in the 13th century, and King Béla IV granted extensive royal privileges in 1255. In the late Middle Ages the town grew wealthy from copper mining, dominated by the Thurzo–Fugger company, one of early modern Europe's great mining enterprises, whose copper was exported across the continent.

In August 1944 Banská Bystrica became the centre of the Slovak National Uprising against Nazi Germany and the collaborationist Slovak state; the uprising is commemorated by the striking SNP Memorial and museum. After 1945 the city expanded as an administrative and industrial centre of central Slovakia, and is home to Matej Bel University, founded in 1992.

Sights

  • The historic SNP Square with the Clock Tower (1552) and Marian column
  • The Town Castle (Mestský hrad) with the Church of the Assumption of the Virgin Mary
  • The Museum and Memorial of the Slovak National Uprising
  • The Thurzo House and other burgher houses of the old town
