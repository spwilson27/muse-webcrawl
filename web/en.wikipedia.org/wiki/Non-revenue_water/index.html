Non-revenue water

Non-revenue water (NRW) is drinking water that has been produced and is "lost" before it reaches the customer, or used by customers who are not billed. It is the difference between the volume of water put into a distribution system and the volume billed to customers, usually expressed as a percentage.

Components

The International Water Association's standard water balance divides NRW into: unbilled authorized consumption (such as water for firefighting or municipal use); apparent losses from metering inaccuracies, billing errors, and unauthorized consumption (theft); and real losses — physical leakage from pipes, joints, reservoirs, and overflows.

Scale of the problem

Globally, average NRW is estimated at around 30 percent of production, but levels vary enormously: well-managed systems in countries such as Singapore and the Netherlands achieve below 5–10 percent, while utilities in some developing cities lose more than half of their treated water. The World Bank has estimated that the worldwide cost of NRW runs to tens of billions of US dollars per year.

Reduction strategies

Utilities reduce NRW through active leakage control, district metered areas, pressure management, pipeline renewal, accurate customer metering, and anti-theft enforcement. Performance-based contracts with private operators have been used in cities such as Phnom Penh, where NRW fell from about 72 percent in 1993 to around 6 percent in 2010.
