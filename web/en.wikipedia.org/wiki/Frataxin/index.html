Frataxin

Frataxin
GeneFXN (chromosome 9q21.11)
LocationMitochondrion
FunctionIron–sulfur cluster biogenesis; iron metabolism
DiseaseFriedreich's ataxia (when deficient)

Frataxin is a protein encoded by the FXN gene in humans. It is located in the mitochondrion and is involved in the biogenesis of iron–sulfur clusters, which are essential cofactors for many enzymes of energy metabolism, as well as in the regulation of mitochondrial iron. Deficiency of frataxin causes Friedreich's ataxia, the most common hereditary ataxia.

Structure and function

Human frataxin is synthesised as a precursor of 210 amino acids and imported into the mitochondrial matrix, where it is processed to its mature form. The protein has a compact fold of alpha helices and beta sheets, with an acidic surface patch that can bind iron. Frataxin acts as an allosteric activator of the core iron–sulfur cluster assembly complex — containing the scaffold protein ISCU and the cysteine desulfurase NFS1 — accelerating the transfer of sulfur needed to build clusters. It thereby supports the function of aconitase, the respiratory chain complexes, and other iron–sulfur enzymes, and helps protect the cell from iron-mediated oxidative damage.

Friedreich's ataxia

In most patients with Friedreich's ataxia, both copies of the FXN gene carry an expanded GAA trinucleotide repeat in the first intron, which suppresses gene expression and reduces frataxin to a fraction of normal levels. The resulting mitochondrial iron accumulation and energy failure progressively damage the spinal cord, peripheral nerves, heart, and pancreas, producing gait ataxia, cardiomyopathy, and frequently diabetes. Longer repeat expansions are associated with earlier onset and more severe disease.

Therapeutic research

Strategies under investigation include boosting FXN expression (for example with histone deacetylase inhibitors or erythropoietin), gene therapy to deliver a functional copy of the gene, protein replacement, and antioxidants. The Nrf2 activator omaveloxolone became the first drug approved for Friedreich's ataxia, receiving FDA approval in 2023 for patients aged 16 and older.
