Alasdair mac Mhaighstir Alasdair

Alasdair mac Mhaighstir Alasdair (c. 1698 – 1770), also known in English as Alexander MacDonald, was a Scottish Gaelic poet, writer, and Jacobite. He is regarded as one of the greatest poets in Scottish Gaelic literature and played a foundational role in preserving and developing the language's literary tradition during the 18th century.

Life

Alasdair was born at Dalilea in Moidart, in the Scottish Highlands, the son of a Church of Scotland (later Scottish Episcopal) minister, the Reverend Alexander MacDonald (Maighstir Alasdair), from whom he took his patronymic "mac Mhaighstir Alasdair" ("son of Master Alexander"). He was educated at the University of Glasgow, though he may not have completed a degree. He worked as a teacher and catechist for the Society in Scotland for Propagating Christian Knowledge (SSPCK) in Ardnamurchan.

In 1745 he joined the Jacobite rising and served as a captain in the Clanranald regiment and as Gaelic tutor to Prince Charles Edward Stuart, teaching the prince some Gaelic. After the defeat at Culloden in 1746, he went into hiding for a time before eventually settling at Arisaig and later Eilean Shona, where he died in 1770.

Works

In 1741 he published the first Scottish Gaelic–English dictionary, Leabhar a Theagasc Ainminnin ("A Galick and English Vocabulary"), commissioned by the SSPCK. In 1751 he published Ais-Eiridh na Sean Chánoin Albannaich ("The Resurrection of the Ancient Scottish Language"), the first printed book of original secular poetry in Scottish Gaelic. Many of his poems were preserved through oral tradition and later collections.

His poetry includes nature poems describing the seasons and the sea, political songs urging Jacobite resistance, and vivid descriptions of Highland life. Poems such as "Birlinn Chlann Raghnaill" ("The Birlinn of Clanranald"), a celebrated maritime poem, demonstrate his command of rhythm, imagery, and the classical Gaelic syllabic and stressed metres.

Legacy

Alasdair mac Mhaighstir Alasdair is considered second only to Sorley MacLean in his importance to Scottish Gaelic poetry, and his work influenced generations of Gaelic writers. His grave is at Kilmory, Arisaig.
