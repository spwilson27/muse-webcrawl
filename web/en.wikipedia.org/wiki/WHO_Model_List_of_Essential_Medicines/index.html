WHO Model List of Essential Medicines

The WHO Model List of Essential Medicines (EML) is a list published by the World Health Organization containing the medications considered to be most effective, safe and cost-effective for priority health needs. First published in 1977 with 208 medicines, it is updated every two years.

Structure

The list is divided into a core list — minimum medicine needs for a basic health-care system — and a complementary list for priority diseases requiring specialized facilities or monitoring. Since 2007 a separate list has been published for children.

Influence

Most countries maintain national essential medicines lists based on the WHO model, which guide public procurement, reimbursement, and donations, and underpin the concept of access to medicines as part of the right to health.

Contents

Sections cover anaesthetics, analgesics, antiallergics, antidotes, anticonvulsants, anti-infectives (antibacterials, antiretrovirals, antituberculosis medicines), cardiovascular medicines, vaccines, and many others. Recent editions added expensive cancer medicines and direct-acting antivirals for hepatitis C, sparking debate about affordability.
