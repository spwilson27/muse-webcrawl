The Kingdom of the Two Sicilies was the largest of the Italian states before unification, comprising the island of Sicily and the southern part of the Italian peninsula (the former Kingdom of Naples). It was created in 1816 when Ferdinand IV of Naples and III of Sicily merged his two crowns after the Napoleonic Wars, ruling as Ferdinand I of the Two Sicilies.

History

The odd name reflects the medieval division of the old Norman-Swabian Kingdom of Sicily into an island kingdom ("Sicily beyond the Strait") and a mainland one ("Sicily on this side of the Strait"). The Bourbon kingdom was conservative and agrarian, though it achieved Italian firsts such as the Naples–Portici railway (1839) and the first Italian steamship. Constitutional revolts erupted in 1820 and 1848; in the latter, Sicily briefly declared independence before Ferdinand II — "King Bomba" for his bombardment of Messina — restored absolutism.

Fall

In 1860 Giuseppe Garibaldi landed in Sicily with his Thousand volunteers; Bourbon resistance collapsed within months, and Francis II surrendered the fortress of Gaeta in February 1861. The kingdom was annexed to the new Kingdom of Italy under Victor Emmanuel II. A harsh guerrilla conflict, labelled "brigandage", followed in the south through the 1860s, and the economic gap between Italy's north and south (the questione meridionale) has roots partly in this period.
