Maverick (TV series)

Maverick is an American Western television series created by Roy Huggins for Warner Bros. Television, broadcast on ABC from 1957 to 1962 for five seasons and 124 episodes. It starred James Garner as Bret Maverick, a roguish, wisecracking poker player travelling the frontier, forever seeking a big game and reluctantly drawn into adventure. The show's ironic, comic tone set it apart from the earnest westerns of the era and helped make Garner a star.

Format and cast

To meet production schedules, the series soon alternated episodes between Bret and his brother Bart Maverick, played by Jack Kelly; later seasons introduced further family members, including Brent Maverick (Robert Colbert) and the English cousin Beauregard "Pappy" Maverick — also played by Garner in flashback-origin episodes. Bret and Bart relied on wit, bluff, and the advice of their father, quoted as aphorisms ("As my old pappy used to say..."). Garner left after a contractual dispute following the third season, and Roger Moore played cousin Beau Maverick for one season before also departing.

Legacy and adaptations

The series was revived in the films The New Maverick (1978) and the short-lived Young Maverick (1979) and Bret Maverick (1981–82). A feature film, Maverick (1994), directed by Richard Donner and starring Mel Gibson as Bret, Jodie Foster, and Garner in a new role, was a major box-office success. The series is often cited as a precursor of the anti-hero western and of Garner's later persona in The Rockford Files.
