Hwanin

Hwanin (Korean: 환인; Hanja: 桓因), also known as Haneunim ("Lord of Heaven"), is the supreme god of heaven in Korean mythology and in the Dangun creation myth. He is the father of Hwanung and the grandfather of Dangun Wanggeom, the legendary founder of Gojoseon, the first Korean kingdom. The myth is recorded principally in the Samguk Yusa (13th century) and the Jewang Un-gi.

In the Dangun myth

According to the myth, Hwanin was the Lord of Heaven (identified in some readings with the Buddhist god Indra, as the name Hwanin renders Śakra). His son Hwanung repeatedly begged to descend to the human world to govern it; Hwanin, perceiving that his son's rule would benefit humankind, granted him 3,000 followers and the three heavenly seals or treasures, and Hwanung descended to Mount Taebaek beneath a sacred sandalwood tree, founding the "Divine City" (Sinsi). Hwanung's son by the bear-woman Ungnyeo was Dangun, who founded Gojoseon in 2333 BCE by traditional reckoning — a date still commemorated by the Korean national holiday Gaecheonjeol.

Interpretation

Hwanin functions as a sky father and ultimate source of legitimacy in the myth, sanctioning the descent of divine rule to earth. Korea's indigenous Cheondoism and other Korean new religions venerate the heavenly god under names such as Haneullim; in modern Korean Christian usage, Hananim is the usual word for God. Historians read the myth as an amalgam of a sky-god cult, totemic bear and tiger motifs, and foundations legends recorded long after the events they purport to describe.
