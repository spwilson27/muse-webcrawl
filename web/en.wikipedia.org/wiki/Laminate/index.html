Laminate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Laminate
Type:Composite material
Process:Lamination
Common uses:Flooring, countertops, printed circuit boards, structural composites

A laminate is a material made by bonding together two or more layers of material, usually with heat, pressure, welding or adhesives. The process of creating a laminate is lamination. Laminated materials combine the properties of their constituent layers, and are often stronger, more stable or more attractive than any single layer alone. Laminates range from paper and fabric products to advanced fibre-reinforced composites used in aerospace engineering.

Types

Decorative laminates, such as those sold under names like Formica, consist of layers of kraft paper impregnated with phenolic resin beneath a decorative melamine surface, pressed into rigid sheets used for countertops, furniture and wall panels. Laminate flooring layers a photographic wear surface over a fibreboard core. Electrical laminates, such as FR-4 glass-epoxy, form the insulating substrate of printed circuit boards. Structural composite laminates stack plies of fibre reinforcement — glass, carbon or aramid — in resin matrices, with the orientation of each ply tailored to the loads the part must carry.

Properties

Because properties can be varied through the thickness of a laminate, designers can produce materials that are stiff in one direction and flexible in another, resistant to moisture and wear on a surface while remaining light in the core. Delamination — separation of the layers under stress, impact or moisture ingress — is the characteristic failure mode of laminated materials and a central concern in their design and inspection.

History

Layered materials are ancient: laminated bows and plywood-like constructions were known in antiquity. Modern industrial laminates date from the early 20th century, when phenolic resin-impregnated paper and fabric laminates (such as Bakelite-based products) were developed for electrical insulation and gears, and high-pressure decorative laminates were patented in the 1910s in the United States.
