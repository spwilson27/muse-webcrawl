ITV (TV network)

ITV, legally known as Channel 3, is a British free-to-air public broadcast television network. It was launched in 1955 as Independent Television to provide competition to BBC Television, and was the first commercial television network in the United Kingdom funded by advertising. It is a network of regional franchises that share programming, traditionally coordinated by the largest franchise holders and, since 2004, largely by ITV plc.

History

ITV was created by the Television Act 1954, with the Independent Television Authority awarding franchises region by region; the first broadcasts, from London's Associated-Rediffusion, began on 22 September 1955. Over the following decades the network became home to many of British television's most popular programmes, including the soap opera Coronation Street (from 1960) and long-running drama and entertainment formats.

Structure

The network comprises fifteen regional franchises. ITV plc owns thirteen of them in England, Wales, and southern Scotland; STV Group owns the two Scottish franchises, and UTV in Northern Ireland has been owned by ITV plc since 2016. National programming on ITV is complemented by regional news. Sister channels include ITV2, ITV3, ITV4, and ITVX, the network's streaming service, launched in 2022 as the successor to ITV Hub.

Programming

Well-known ITV programmes include Coronation Street, Emmerdale, Downton Abbey, Broadchurch, I'm a Celebrity...Get Me Out of Here!, The X Factor, and Britain's Got Talent, along with ITV News bulletins.
