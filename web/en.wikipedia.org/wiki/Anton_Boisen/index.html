Anton Theophilus Boisen (29 October 1876 – 23 October 1965) was an American chaplain and theologian who founded the clinical pastoral education (CPE) movement, which trains clergy and theological students in supervised ministry to people in crisis, especially in hospitals and psychiatric institutions.

Life

Boisen was born in Indiana to a Danish immigrant professor father. After studying forestry and then theology at Union Theological Seminary, he suffered several episodes of acute mental illness, including hospitalisation in 1920 that he later interpreted as both a pathological breakdown and a profound religious experience — a "disturbance" that could be a problem-solving, creative process.

Clinical pastoral education

In 1925 Boisen began supervising theological students at Worcester State Hospital in Massachusetts, insisting they study "living human documents" — real patients — rather than only books. With physician Richard Cabot's support, his method of case study, verbatim reports and group supervision spread through the Council for Clinical Training (founded 1930) and became the standard model of CPE, now required by many denominations and chaplaincy boards.

Works

His books include Exploration of the Inner World (1936), a landmark study of religion and mental illness, and Out of the Depths (1960), an autobiographical account of his own breakdowns and recovery.
