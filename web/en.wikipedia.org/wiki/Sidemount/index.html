Sidemount

Sidemount is a scuba diving equipment configuration in which the breathing-gas cylinders are carried clipped to the diver's harness along the sides of the torso, roughly under the armpits, rather than on the back as in conventional backmount. The cylinders are typically attached at the hip and chest and can be manipulated, removed, or staged by the diver while underwater.

History

Sidemount originated in British cave diving (sump diving) in the 1960s and 1970s, where divers needed to pass through tight restrictions and transport cylinders separately through dry cave sections. It was later adopted in North American cave diving and popularised for open-water and technical diving in the 2000s.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages include a streamlined, low profile for penetrating wrecks and caves; easy access to cylinder valves; the ability to don cylinders in the water, which can help divers with back problems; and redundancy with two independent gas supplies. Drawbacks include more complex gas management and procedure differences from backmount training.

Equipment

A sidemount setup uses a harness with a wing-style buoyancy compensator designed for the configuration, two regulators with appropriate hose routing, and cylinders rigged with cam bands and clips. Training agencies including PADI, SSI, and TDI offer sidemount courses.
