The Sultanate of Hobyo (Somali: Saldanadda Hobyo) was a 19th- and early 20th-century Somali kingdom founded by Sultan Yusuf Ali Kenadid in the 1870s. Centred on the port of Hobyo on the Indian Ocean coast of what is now the Mudug region of Somalia, it governed a swathe of the north-central Somali coast and hinterland until it was conquered by Italy during the Campaign of the Sultanates in the mid-1920s.

Founding

Yusuf Ali Kenadid was a nephew of the Majeerteen Sultan and a former subordinate of Sultan Osman Mahamuud. After failing in a bid for the Majeerteen throne, he went into exile in Yemen, where he acquired military training and Arab support, and returned with a band of Hadhrami musketeers to carve out his own state among the Hawiye clan territories of the Hobyo region. From his new capital he built a fortified administration, concluded trade treaties, and extended his authority inland.

Relations with Italy

In December 1888 Kenadid signed a treaty of protection with Italy, seeking Italian backing against his Majeerteen rivals and against the expansionist Ethiopian Empire of Menelik II — with whom he also, at times, intrigued against the Italians. The sultanate exported ivory, livestock, and ostrich feathers through Hobyo and maintained armed forces with Yemeni musketeers at their core. Kenadid's ambition to conquer the Majeerteen Sultanate led to his downfall: after his 1903 invasion failed, the Italians exiled him to Eritrea.

Decline and conquest

His son, Ali Yusuf Kenadid, succeeded as sultan but ruled a weakening state. When Italy under Fascist governor Cesare Maria De Vecchi resolved to end the protectorate system and occupy the sultanates outright, Hobyo resisted; the Campaign of the Sultanates (1924–1927) overran the territory with colonial troops, Oman of Hobyo fell in 1925, and Ali Yusuf was deposed and exiled. The sultanate's lands were incorporated into Italian Somaliland.
