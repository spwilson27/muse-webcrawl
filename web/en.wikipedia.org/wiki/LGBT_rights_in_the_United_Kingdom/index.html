LGBT rights in the United Kingdom

LGBT rights in the UK
Decriminalisation: 1967 (England & Wales), 1980 (Scotland), 1982 (Northern Ireland)
Same-sex marriage: 2014 (E&W, Scotland), 2020 (NI)
Anti-discrimination: Equality Act 2010

LGBT rights in the United Kingdom have advanced dramatically since the mid-20th century; the UK is generally ranked among the more legally protective countries for lesbian, gay, bisexual and transgender people, though debates continue, especially over transgender healthcare and recognition.

History

Male homosexual acts, criminalised since the Buggery Act 1533 and prosecuted under the 1885 Labouchère Amendment (used against Oscar Wilde and, in 1952, Alan Turing), were partially decriminalised by the Sexual Offences Act 1967 following the Wolfenden report. The age of consent was equalised in 2001.

Modern law

Section 28 (1988), which banned the "promotion of homosexuality" by local authorities, was repealed in 2003. Civil partnerships arrived in 2005, adoption rights were extended, the Gender Recognition Act passed in 2004, and same-sex marriage was legislated in 2013–14 in England, Wales and Scotland, and imposed for Northern Ireland in 2020. The Equality Act 2010 protects sexual orientation and gender reassignment. See Stonewall.
