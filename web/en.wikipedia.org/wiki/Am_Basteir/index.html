Am Basteir

Am Basteir (Scottish Gaelic: Am Baisteir, "the executioner") is a mountain in the Cuillin range on the Isle of Skye, Scotland. It rises to 934 metres (3,064 ft) and is classified as a Munro. Its Gaelic name is traditionally explained by the shape of its summit ridge, said to resemble an executioner's axe, or from its position near the "Bhasteir" tooth.

Features

Am Basteir lies at the northern end of the main Black Cuillin ridge, above Sligachan. On its western side it is linked to the neighbouring Munro Bruach na Frìthe via Bealach a' Bhasteir, while to the south the ridge continues toward Sgùrr nan Gillean. A notable feature near the summit is the Basteir Tooth, a dramatic pinnacle separated from the main peak by a narrow gap; the Tooth is usually climbed (by the "King's Cave Chimney" or abseil and scramble routes) as part of a traverse of the ridge.

Climbing

The normal ascent follows the path from Sligachan up the Allt Dearg Mòr into Coire a' Bhasteir, then climbs scree slopes to the bealach and along the short summit ridge, which involves some exposed scrambling. Like the rest of the Cuillin, the rock is gabbro, prized for its grip, though wet conditions and the inaccuracy of magnetic compasses (due to local magnetic rock) demand care. Am Basteir is frequently combined with Bruach na Frìthe and Sgùrr nan Gillean in a single outing.
