Four-acceleration

In the special theory of relativity, four-acceleration is a four-vector (a vector in four-dimensional spacetime) analogous to classical acceleration. It is defined as the derivative of the four-velocity with respect to the particle's proper time:

A = dU/dτ = (γu γa c, γu² a + γu γa u)

Definition

where U is the four-velocity, τ is proper time, γ is the Lorentz factor, u is the ordinary three-velocity and a is the ordinary three-acceleration. In an instantaneously co-moving inertial frame, the four-acceleration reduces to (0, a), so its spatial components equal the proper acceleration felt by the object.

Properties

The four-acceleration is always orthogonal to the four-velocity: A · U = 0, because the magnitude of the four-velocity is constant (equal to c). Its Minkowski magnitude is constant and equals the negative square of the proper acceleration divided by c²: A · A = −α²/c², where α is the proper acceleration. For uniform (constant proper) acceleration, the resulting motion is hyperbolic motion, in which the particle approaches but never reaches a Rindler horizon.
