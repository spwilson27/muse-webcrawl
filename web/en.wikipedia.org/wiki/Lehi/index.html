Lehi

In the Book of Mormon, Lehi (Hebrew: לחי) is a prophet who lived in Jerusalem during the reign of King Zedekiah (around 600 BC). Lehi is the father of Nephi — the primary narrator of the first part of the book — and of Laman, Lemuel, Sam, Jacob and Joseph. According to the narrative, Lehi was commanded in a vision to leave Jerusalem before its destruction by the Babylonians, journeyed with his family through the Arabian wilderness for eight years, and then sailed to a promised land in the Americas, where his descendants divided into the Nephite and Lamanite peoples who dominate the rest of the Book of Mormon story.

Narrative

The First Book of Nephi opens with Lehi praying for his people and receiving visions — including a vision of God on his throne and a warning that Jerusalem would be destroyed for its wickedness. After preaching repentance and being threatened by the city's inhabitants, Lehi was commanded in a dream to take his family into the wilderness. They camped by the Red Sea; Lehi sent his sons back to Jerusalem twice — once to obtain the brass plates (a scriptural record kept by Laban, whom Nephi eventually killed) and once to persuade the family of Ishmael to join them.

In the valley of Lemuel and later at a place they called Nahom, the family was guided by a curious brass ball called the Liahona, which pointed the way according to their faith. Lehi's famous dream of the tree of life — a tree bearing white fruit, an iron rod leading to it, and a great and spacious building mocking those who held to the rod — is one of the most studied passages of the Book of Mormon and is recounted in 1 Nephi 8. After eight years of travel, hunger and family conflict, the party reached a coastal land they called Bountiful, where Nephi built a ship under divine direction and the company sailed to the promised land. Lehi blessed his children before his death (recorded in 2 Nephi 1–4, sometimes called "Lehi's final blessing"), after which his sons' families split into rival peoples.

Significance in Latter-day Saint tradition

Members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints regard Lehi as a genuine historical prophet and his family's record as scripture. The Book of Lehi — a manuscript said to have formed the first part of Joseph Smith's translation — was lost in 1828 with the "116 pages" stolen from Martin Harris, and its content is believed to be partly summarised in the small plates of Nephi. Non-Latter-day Saint scholars generally treat Lehi as a literary figure of the 19th-century text; proposed identifications of Book of Mormon sites such as Nahom with the Yemeni tribal name NHM remain debated.
