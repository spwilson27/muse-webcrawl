Cauchy integral theorem

The Cauchy integral theorem (also known as the Cauchy–Goursat theorem) in complex analysis, named after Augustin-Louis Cauchy (and Édouard Goursat), is an important statement about line integrals for holomorphic functions in the complex plane. Essentially, it says that if two different paths connect the same two points, and a function is holomorphic everywhere "in between" the two paths, then the integrals of that function along the two paths are the same.

Statement

Let U be a simply connected open subset of the complex plane ℂ, let f : U → ℂ be a holomorphic function, and let γ be a closed rectifiable curve in U. Then:

∮γ f(z) dz = 0

Goursat's proof removed Cauchy's original assumption that the derivative f′ be continuous. Versions of the theorem also hold for domains with holes, provided the curve winds appropriately, leading to the general form used with the residue theorem.

Significance

The theorem is the foundation of complex analysis: from it Cauchy derived the Cauchy integral formula, and hence the analyticity of holomorphic functions. It is also closely related to Green's theorem in the plane, applied to the real and imaginary parts of a holomorphic function.
