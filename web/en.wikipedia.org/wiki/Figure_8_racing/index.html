Figure 8 racing

Figure 8 racing is a form of stock car racing in which automobiles race on a track that purposely intersects itself, increasing the risk of collisions. The course is shaped like a figure 8, and racers must negotiate the centre crossing — where traffic from both loops passes through the same point — on every lap. It is a staple of American short-track and county-fair motorsport, closely related to demolition derbies in spirit and venue.

History

The format is generally traced to the years just after World War II, with races held at venues such as the Indianapolis Speedrome, which became the best-known figure 8 track in the United States and hosts long-distance events including the annual World Figure 8 Championship, a gruelling three-hour endurance race run since the 1970s. Figure 8 events spread to short tracks across the Midwest and beyond; school bus figure 8 races became a popular fairground novelty.

Format and safety

Cars are usually inexpensive stock cars or purpose-built late models with full roll cages. The crossing, sometimes called the "X", is the heart of the spectacle: drivers judge gaps in perpendicular traffic at speed. Despite frequent contact, serious injuries are relatively uncommon at typical speeds, and events are usually run under yellow or red flags when the crossing becomes blocked. Championship racing uses scoring and safety crews positioned around the crossing.

In popular culture

Figure 8 racing has been featured on television coverage of grassroots motorsport and inspired mini-game events in racing video games.
