Nordic skiing

Nordic skiing encompasses the various types of skiing in which the toe of the ski boot is fixed to the binding in a manner that allows the heel to rise off the ski, unlike alpine skiing, where the boot is attached to the ski at both toe and heel. The name refers to Norway and the Scandinavian countries where these forms of skiing originated as a means of winter travel. Recreational disciplines include cross-country skiing and telemark skiing; competitive disciplines include cross-country skiing, ski jumping, and Nordic combined.

History

Skiing began in Scandinavia several thousand years ago as a practical way of travelling over snow; rock carvings and preserved skis from bog deposits in Norway and Sweden date the practice to at least 3000 BCE. In the nineteenth century, Sondre Norheim of Telemark, Norway, pioneered techniques including the telemark and Christiania turns and binding designs with stiff cable bindings that held the heel laterally. Norwegian explorer Fridtjof Nansen's 1888 crossing of Greenland on skis brought wide attention to the sport. Nordic events were included in the first Winter Olympic Games at Chamonix in 1924.

Disciplines

Cross-country skiing is contested in classical style, in which the skis move parallel in groomed tracks, and freestyle (skating) technique. Ski jumping is performed from normal and large hills, judged on distance and style. Nordic combined joins ski jumping with a cross-country race, with jumping points converted to start intervals under the Gunder method. Telemark skiing, named after the Norwegian region, uses a lunge-like free-heel turn and remains popular as a recreational discipline.

Equipment

Nordic skis are long, narrow, and light compared with alpine skis, with a camber that distributes the skier's weight so that a grip zone — treated with grip wax or a patterned fishscale base in classical skiing — engages the snow during the kick phase. Boots are flexible, resembling sturdy shoes, and bindings such as the New Nordic Norm (NNN) and Salomon Nordic System (SNS) clamp only the toe bar of the boot.
