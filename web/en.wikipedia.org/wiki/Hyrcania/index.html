Hyrcania

Region: South-eastern shore of the Caspian Sea (modern Gorgan, Iran)
Historical period: Antiquity
Satrapy of: Achaemenid, Seleucid, and Parthian empires

Hyrcania (Greek: Ὑρκανία, Hyrkania; Old Persian: Varkāna) is a historical region in northern Iran, on the south-east corner of the Caspian Sea — which itself was known in antiquity as the Hyrcanian Sea. The name derives from the Old Persian word for "wolf" (varka), hence "Wolf-land". The region corresponds roughly to the modern Iranian province of Golestan and the plain of Gorgan.

History

Hyrcania was a satrapy of the Achaemenid Empire, valued for its fertility — classical authors marvelled at its vines, figs, and grain — and for its soldiers. Alexander the Great passed through in pursuit of Bessus after the death of Darius III and received the submission of its satrap Phrataphernes. Under the Seleucids the region saw Greek colonisation; it later became part of the Parthian Empire, and its capital in various periods included Zadracarta and Syrinx. Hyrcanian tigers (Caspian tigers) and the region's wildness passed into classical literature; Virgil and other poets used "Hyrcanian" as an epithet for savagery.

Geography

Bracketed by the Elburz mountains to the south and the Caspian to the north, Hyrcania enjoys a humid, subtropical climate unusual in Iran, supporting dense forests (the Hyrcanian forests, now a UNESCO World Heritage listing) and intensive agriculture. The Great Wall of Gorgan, a Sasanian-era defensive fortification, ran across its northern frontier against steppe nomads.
