St Peter Port

St Peter Port (French: Saint-Pierre-Port) is a town and parish on the east coast of Guernsey in the Channel Islands. It is the capital of the Bailiwick of Guernsey and its principal port, with a population of roughly 18,000. The town climbs steeply from its harbour in a series of narrow streets and terraces.

St Peter Port harbour and the town

Geography and harbour

The harbour at St Peter Port is one of the oldest in the Channel Islands and has been in use since Roman times. Castle Cornet, a fortress guarding the harbour entrance, was built from the 13th century onwards and now houses several museums. Ferries connect the town with England, France and the other Channel Islands.

History

The town grew as Guernsey's trade expanded in the 17th and 18th centuries, when merchants profited from privateering, smuggling and trade in wine and brandy. Fine Georgian and Regency houses were built along the waterfront and on the hills above. During the Second World War, Guernsey was occupied by German forces from 1940 to 1945, and St Peter Port remained the administrative centre under occupation.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the Town Church, Candie Gardens and the Guernsey Museum, Hauteville House — the home of the exiled French writer Victor Hugo from 1856 to 1870 — and the States of Guernsey parliament building. The town is the centre of the island's finance industry and its main shopping district.
