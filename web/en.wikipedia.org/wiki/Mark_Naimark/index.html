Mark Naimark

Mark Aronovich Naimark
BornDecember 5, 1909, Odessa, Russian Empire
DiedDecember 30, 1978, Moscow, USSR
FieldsFunctional analysis, representation theory

Mark Aronovich Naimark (1909–1978) was a Soviet mathematician who made important contributions to functional analysis and representation theory.

Work

Naimark is best known for the Gelfand–Naimark theorem (1943), proved with Israel Gelfand, which shows that every C*-algebra can be represented as an algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space — a foundation of noncommutative geometry and quantum theory.

He also proved Naimark's dilation theorem, which states that any positive operator-valued measure can be dilated to a projection-valued measure, and developed the theory of representations of the Lorentz and other semisimple Lie groups with Gelfand.

Legacy

His monographs, including Normed Rings and Linear Differential Operators, were standard references in Soviet and international mathematics. A minor planet, 3668 Naimark, is named in his honour.
