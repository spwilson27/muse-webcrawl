Tanngrisnir and Tanngnjóstr

Tanngrisnir and Tanngnjóstr (Old Norse "teeth-barer, snarler" and "teeth-grinder") are the goats who pull the chariot of the Norse god Thor in Norse mythology. They are attested in the Poetic Edda and the Prose Edda of Snorri Sturluson.

The resurrection myth

According to the Prose Edda, Thor may slaughter and eat the goats each evening; so long as their bones are gathered unbroken in their skins, he can restore them to life the next morning by hallowing them with his hammer Mjölnir. In the famous tale, the boy Þjálfi cracks one bone for its marrow, and the resurrected goat rises lame — as compensation, Þjálfi and his sister Röskva become Thor's servants.

Significance

The episode illustrates Thor's role as a god of sustenance and the peasantry, and the goats remain among the most recognisable animals of Norse myth, reflected in the Yule goat traditions of Scandinavia.
