Engineering fit

Engineering fits are the planned relationships between mating mechanical parts, describing the difference between the sizes of a hole and a shaft before assembly. Fits are specified so that parts assemble correctly and function as intended — rotating, sliding, or remaining rigidly fixed — without excessive looseness or damaging tightness. The modern system is standardised by ISO 286 and the equivalent ANSI/ASME B4.1 standards.

Classes of fit

ClassDescriptionExample
Clearance fitThe shaft is always smaller than the hole; a gap (clearance) always exists.Shaft in a freely rotating bearing
Interference fitThe shaft is always larger than the hole; parts must be pressed, shrunk or expanded to assemble.Press-fitted gear on a shaft
Transition fitDepending on actual sizes within tolerance, the result may be clearance or interference.Locating dowel in a machine housing

The ISO system

The ISO system defines fits using a combination of a letter (denoting the position of the tolerance zone relative to the nominal size) and a number (the International Tolerance grade, IT, denoting its width). Uppercase letters denote holes and lowercase denote shafts — for example, a hole designated H7 has its tolerance zone beginning at the nominal size, while a shaft g6 lies slightly below it. Two basis systems are used: the hole basis system, in which the hole is held at the basic H size and the shaft varied, and the less common shaft basis system.

Common fits

Widely used combinations include H7/g6 (close running fit), H7/f7 (free running fit), H7/k6 (transition, light keying fit) and H7/p6 (interference, press fit). Selection depends on load, speed, lubrication, temperature differences between parts, and the need for disassembly.
