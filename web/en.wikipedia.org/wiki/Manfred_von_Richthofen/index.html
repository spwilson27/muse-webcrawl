Manfred Albrecht Freiherr von Richthofen (2 May 1892 – 21 April 1918), known in English as Baron von Richthofen or the Red Baron, was a fighter pilot with the German Air Force during World War I. He is considered the ace-of-aces of the war, being officially credited with 80 air combat victories.

Career

Originally a cavalryman, Richthofen transferred to the air service in 1915 and was mentored by Oswald Boelcke. He scored his first victory in September 1916 and rose rapidly, taking command of the fighter squadron Jasta 11 in January 1917 and later of the first German fighter wing, Jagdgeschwader I — the unit of brightly painted aircraft known to the Allies as "the Flying Circus". His own aircraft, notably an Albatros and later a Fokker Dr.I triplane, were painted bright red, giving rise to his famous nickname.

Death

Richthofen was fatally wounded on 21 April 1918 while pursuing a Canadian Sopwith Camel at low altitude over the Somme. Credit for the fatal shot is disputed between Canadian pilot Roy Brown and Australian anti-aircraft gunners on the ground; post-mortem evidence favours a shot fired from below. He was buried with full military honours by the Australian Flying Corps. His younger brother Lothar was also a notable ace.

Legacy

The Red Baron remains one of the most famous fighter pilots of all time, a staple of popular culture ranging from the Peanuts comic strip's Snoopy fantasies to films and the 1960s rock song "Snoopy vs. the Red Baron".
