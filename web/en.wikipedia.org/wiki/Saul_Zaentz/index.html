Saul Zaentz

Saul Zaentz (February 28, 1921 – January 3, 2014) was an American film producer and record executive. As head of Fantasy Records he oversaw the career of Creedence Clearwater Revival, and as an independent producer he won three Academy Awards for Best Picture: One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest (1975), Amadeus (1984), and The English Patient (1996).

Career

Born in Passaic, New Jersey, Zaentz joined Fantasy Records in the 1950s and became its owner, building it into a major jazz label before Creedence Clearwater Revival's success — followed by bitter litigation with the band's John Fogerty. Turning to film, he also produced The Unbearable Lightness of Being (1988) and Goya's Ghosts (2006). He famously owned film rights to J. R. R. Tolkien's The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings, producing the 1978 animated adaptation and licensing the rights used for Peter Jackson's trilogies through Middle-earth Enterprises.

Honours

In 1996 Zaentz received the Academy's Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award. He died in San Francisco in 2014, aged 92.
