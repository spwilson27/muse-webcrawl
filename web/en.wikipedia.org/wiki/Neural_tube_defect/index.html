Closure of the neural tube normally completes by the fourth week of development.

Neural tube defects (NTDs) are a group of birth defects in which an opening in the brain or spinal cord remains from early in human development. In the third week of pregnancy, called gastrulation, specialised cells on the dorsal side of the embryo form the neural plate; its edges fold and fuse to form the neural tube, the precursor of the brain and spinal cord. This process, neurulation, is normally complete by the fourth week. When closure fails, serious malformations result.

Types

  • Spina bifida — incomplete closure of the spinal cord and vertebral column; the most severe form, myelomeningocele, involves protrusion of the cord and meninges.
  • Anencephaly — failure of closure at the head end, leaving the brain largely absent; incompatible with prolonged life.
  • Encephalocele — a sac-like protrusion of brain tissue through an opening in the skull.
  • Iniencephaly — a rare defect involving the occiput and spine with severe retroflexion of the head.

Causes and prevention

NTDs are multifactorial: both genetic predisposition and environmental factors contribute. The best-established modifiable risk factor is insufficient folate (vitamin B9) around the time of conception. The landmark MRC Vitamin Study (1991) showed that folic acid supplementation reduced recurrence of NTDs by about 72%. Many countries have introduced mandatory fortification of flour or grain products with folic acid, followed by substantial falls in NTD rates. Other risk factors include certain anti-epileptic drugs (such as valproate), poorly controlled maternal diabetes, obesity and hyperthermia. Women of childbearing age are commonly advised to take 400 micrograms of folic acid daily before conception and through the first 12 weeks of pregnancy, with higher doses for those at increased risk.

Diagnosis

Many NTDs can be detected before birth by ultrasound and by raised alpha-fetoprotein in maternal blood or amniotic fluid. Screening is a routine part of prenatal care in many health systems.
