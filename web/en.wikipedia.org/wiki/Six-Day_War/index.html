The Six-Day War, also called the June War or the 1967 Arab–Israeli War, was fought from 5 to 10 June 1967 between Israel and a coalition of Arab states led by Egypt (then the United Arab Republic), Jordan and Syria.

Prelude

In May 1967 Egypt's President Gamal Abdel Nasser expelled UN peacekeepers from Sinai, massed troops there, and closed the Straits of Tiran to Israeli shipping, which Israel declared a casus belli. Jordan signed a defence pact with Egypt; Syria had clashed with Israel over the Golan Heights and water diversion.

Course

On 5 June Israel launched Operation Focus, a surprise air strike that destroyed most of the Egyptian air force on the ground within hours, then those of Jordan and Syria. Ground forces under generals including Yeshayahu Gavish, Ariel Sharon and Israel Tal overran Sinai to the Suez Canal; Israel captured East Jerusalem and the West Bank from Jordan after Jordanian shelling began, and on 9–10 June seized the Golan Heights from Syria despite heavy fortifications.

Consequences

Israel roughly tripled the territory it controlled. UN Security Council Resolution 242 (November 1967) called for Israeli withdrawal from occupied territories and recognition of every state's right to live in peace — the "land for peace" formula. The war displaced hundreds of thousands of Palestinians, began the Israeli settlement project in the occupied territories, and reshaped Middle East politics, leading toward the Yom Kippur War of 1973.
