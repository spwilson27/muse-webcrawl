Music of Bolivia

The music of Bolivia is shaped by the country's Indigenous Andean heritage—Quechua and Aymara traditions—blended with Spanish colonial influences and, in the lowlands, Afro-Bolivian culture. It is best known internationally for Andean folk instruments such as the charango, zampoña (panpipes), quena (notched flute), and bombo drum.

Andean folk music

Bolivian folk genres include the cueca, huayño, morenada, tinku, caporales, diablada, and saya. The great urban festivals—notably the Carnival of Oruro, recognized by UNESCO in 2001—feature massive brass bands and costumed dance fraternities performing diablada and morenada. Influential folk groups include Los Kjarkas, whose song "Llorando se fue" was adapted internationally (as "Lambada"), Savia Andina, and Kalamarka.

Instruments

The charango is a small ten-stringed lute traditionally made from armadillo shell. Other characteristic instruments include the ronroco, guitar, violin, tarka and pinquillo flutes, siku panpipes of many sizes, and the wankara drum.

Regional traditions

In the Chaco region, música chaqueña is tied to gaucho culture. Tarija is known for the cueca tarijeña and lively Easter celebrations (Pascua Florida). The Afro-Bolivian community of the Yungas preserves the saya, central to their cultural revival since the 1980s.

Classical and contemporary music

Composers such as Simeón Roncal and Eduardo Caba incorporated folk themes into art music. Modern Bolivian artists span rock, jazz, cumbia, and electronic music.
