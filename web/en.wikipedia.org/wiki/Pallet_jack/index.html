Pallet jack

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A pallet jack, also known as a pallet truck, pump truck, or jigger, is a tool used to lift and move pallets. It is the most basic form of a forklift and is intended to move pallets within a warehouse or the cargo area of a vehicle.

Design and operation

The pallet jack has two low forks that slide into the openings of a pallet. A hydraulic pump, operated by pumping the tow handle (tiller), raises the forks just enough to lift the load clear of the floor; a release lever lowers it again. The front wheels are mounted inside the ends of the forks and deploy as the jack lifts, while larger steer wheels at the handle end allow the operator to pull or push the load across smooth floors. Typical manual pallet jacks are rated for loads of roughly 2,000–2,500 kg (4,400–5,500 lb).

Types

Manual pallet jacks are powered entirely by the operator's pumping action. Powered pallet jacks, also called electric pallet trucks or walkie jacks, use an electric motor for lifting and travel and are suited to longer distances and heavier workloads. Variants include high-lift (scissor) trucks, weigh-scale trucks with built-in scales, low-profile jacks for shallow pallets, and stainless-steel models for food and pharmaceutical environments.

History

Simple two-wheeled hand trucks for moving crated goods date to the nineteenth century, but the modern hydraulic pallet jack emerged alongside the standardized wooden pallet and the forklift in the early twentieth century, as palletized shipping transformed freight handling during and after the Second World War.
