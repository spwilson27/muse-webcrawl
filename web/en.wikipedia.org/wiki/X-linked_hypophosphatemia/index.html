X-linked hypophosphatemia

X-linked hypophosphatemia (XLH) is a rare inherited disorder characterised by low levels of phosphate in the blood (hypophosphatemia), defective bone mineralisation, and impaired growth. It is the most common form of hereditary hypophosphatemic rickets, affecting roughly 1 in 20,000 people.

Cause

XLH is caused by loss-of-function mutations in the PHEX gene on the X chromosome, inherited in an X-linked dominant pattern. PHEX deficiency leads to excess circulating fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23), which suppresses phosphate reabsorption in the kidneys and reduces production of active vitamin D, so phosphate is wasted in the urine and bones are under-mineralised.

Signs and symptoms

Children present with rickets: bowed legs, short stature, bone pain and dental abnormalities such as spontaneous tooth abscesses. Adults develop osteomalacia, fractures and pseudofractures, enthesopathy, osteoarthritis and hearing loss.

Treatment

Conventional therapy combines oral phosphate salts with active vitamin D analogues (calcitriol or alfacalcidol), requiring frequent dosing and monitoring for complications such as nephrocalcinosis and hyperparathyroidism. Burosumab, a monoclonal antibody against FGF23, was approved in 2018 for children and later for adults with XLH and directly addresses the underlying pathophysiology.
