Little Men

Little Men: Life at Plumfield with Jo's Boys is a novel by the American author Louisa May Alcott, published in 1871. It is the sequel to Little Women (1868) and Good Wives (1869), and is followed by Jo's Boys (1886). The novel draws on Alcott's experience of her father Bronson Alcott's educational experiments and of the transcendentalist community in Concord, Massachusetts.

Plot summary

Josephine "Jo" March, now Mrs Bhaer, runs with her husband, Professor Friedrich Bhaer, a boarding school for boys at Plumfield, the estate inherited from Aunt March. The story begins with the arrival of Nat Blake, a shy orphaned street musician, and proceeds through the school year in a series of episodes about the boys: the wild, good-hearted newcomer Dan; Demi and Daisy, the children of Meg March Brooke; Jo's own sons Rob and Teddy; the sturdy Tommy Bangs; the vain Nan, a girl admitted as an experiment in coeducation; and others. The Bhaers govern less by punishment than by trust, self-reporting of faults, and affection — an idealised portrait of humane, progressive education.

Themes

The book advocates moral education through kindness rather than fear, the cultivation of each child's individual gifts, and (unusually for its time) the equal education of girls, embodied in Nan, who grows up to become a doctor. Alcott also reflects on poverty, disability and the rehabilitation of "bad" boys — Dan's story continues into Jo's Boys.

Characters

  • Jo Bhaer (née March) — founder and heart of Plumfield
  • Professor Friedrich Bhaer — her German husband
  • Nat Blake — the orphan violinist whose arrival opens the book
  • Dan Kean — the rough boy from the streets
  • Demi and Daisy Brooke — Meg's twins
  • Annie "Nan" Harding — the spirited girl pupil
  • Tommy Bangs, Emil, Franz, Rob and Teddy — the other boys of Plumfield

Adaptations

The novel has been filmed (1934, 1940 and 1998) and adapted for television, including a 1970s anime series and a 1998–99 Canadian TV series.
