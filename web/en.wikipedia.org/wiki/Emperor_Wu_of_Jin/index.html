Emperor Wu of Jin (236 – 17 May 290), personal name Sima Yan, courtesy name Anshi, was the founding emperor of the Jin dynasty of China. The grandson of Sima Yi and son of Sima Zhao, he forced the last Cao Wei emperor to abdicate in 266 and in 280 conquered the state of Eastern Wu, reunifying China after nearly a century of division in the Three Kingdoms period.

Rise to power

The Sima family had effectively controlled the Cao Wei regime since Sima Yi's coup of 249. Sima Yan succeeded his father Sima Zhao as regent and King of Jin in 265, and in February 266 compelled Emperor Cao Huan to abdicate, establishing the Jin dynasty with Luoyang as capital. To secure his rule he enfeoffed his numerous sons and relatives as princes with their own territories and troops — a policy intended to protect the dynasty that instead armed the factions of the later War of the Eight Princes.

Conquest of Wu

In late 279 Jin launched a massive multi-pronged invasion of Eastern Wu, with fleets descending the Yangtze from Sichuan — commander Wang Jun famously melted down the iron chains Wu had stretched across the river — and in 280 the last Wu emperor, Sun Hao, surrendered at Jianye (Nanjing). China was reunified for the first time since 220.

Later reign

After reunification Emperor Wu's court became noted for extravagance: he reportedly maintained a harem of some 10,000 women, drawn into the palace after the conquest, and toured it in a goat-drawn cart. His decision to retain his developmentally disabled son Sima Zhong as heir — despite warnings — set the stage for the chaos that followed his death in 290, when he was succeeded by Emperor Hui.
