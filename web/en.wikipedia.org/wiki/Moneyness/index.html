Moneyness

In finance, moneyness is the relative position of the current price (or future price) of an underlying asset, such as a stock, with respect to the strike price of a derivative contract based on that asset, most commonly a call or put option. It describes how far an option is from having intrinsic value and is a key concept in options trading and pricing.

Categories

  • In the money (ITM): An option is in the money if it would have positive value if exercised immediately. A call is ITM when the underlying price is above the strike; a put is ITM when the underlying price is below the strike.
  • At the money (ATM): The underlying price equals (or is very near) the strike price.
  • Out of the money (OTM): The option has no intrinsic value; exercising it would not be profitable. Its price consists entirely of time value.

The terms "deep in the money" and "deep out of the money" describe options far from the strike in either direction.

Definitions

Moneyness is sometimes quantified as the ratio of the underlying price to the strike (S/K), or as the standardised quantity d2 or the delta of the option in the Black–Scholes model. In the volatility-surface literature, strikes are commonly quoted by their moneyness or delta rather than by absolute price.

Significance

Moneyness determines an option's intrinsic value, influences its sensitivity (the Greeks) to movements in the underlying, and explains patterns such as the volatility smile, in which implied volatility varies systematically with moneyness.
