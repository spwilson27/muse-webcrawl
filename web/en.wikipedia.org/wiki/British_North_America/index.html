British North America comprised the colonial territories of the British Empire in North America from 1783 onwards. The term was first used informally in 1783, following the Treaty of Paris that ended the American Revolutionary War and recognized the independence of the United States. The colonies remaining to Britain lay to the north and west: the future Canada, together with — at various times — Bermuda and British claims in the Pacific Northwest. Politically, British North America consisted of separate colonies administered individually until several united in the Canadian Confederation of 1867.

Formation

Britain's North American empire expanded dramatically in 1763, when the Treaty of Paris ending the Seven Years' War ceded Canada (New France) and French Louisiana east of the Mississippi to Britain. After American independence in 1783, some 40,000–50,000 United Empire Loyalists migrated north, prompting the creation of New Brunswick (1784) and the division of Quebec into Upper Canada (Ontario) and Lower Canada (Quebec) by the Constitutional Act of 1791.

The colonies

  • Upper and Lower Canada, united as the Province of Canada in 1841 after the Rebellions of 1837–38 and Lord Durham's Report.
  • Nova Scotia and New Brunswick, the Maritime colonies, alongside Prince Edward Island.
  • Newfoundland, a separate colony and later dominion.
  • Rupert's Land and the North-Western Territory, administered by the Hudson's Bay Company.
  • British Columbia, formed from the Vancouver Island and mainland colonies in 1866.

Confederation

The Charlottetown and Quebec Conferences of 1864 produced the Seventy-Two Resolutions, and the British North America Act, 1867 (now the Constitution Act, 1867) united Canada, Nova Scotia, and New Brunswick into the Dominion of Canada on 1 July 1867. Manitoba, British Columbia, and Prince Edward Island joined by 1873, Rupert's Land was transferred in 1870, and Alberta and Saskatchewan were created in 1905. Newfoundland, which had become a dominion in 1907, joined Canada only in 1949. The phrase "British North America" survives chiefly in the former name of Canada's constitution and in historical usage.

Administration

Each colony had a governor (later lieutenant governor), an appointed legislative council, and an elected assembly. Responsible government — executives accountable to the assemblies — was conceded first in Nova Scotia in 1848 and spread through the colonies, marking the transition from crown colonies to self-governing societies within the Empire.
