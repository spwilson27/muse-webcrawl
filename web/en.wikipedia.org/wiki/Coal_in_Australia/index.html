Coal in Australia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Coal in Australia is a major part of the Australian economy and its energy system. Australia is one of the world's largest producers and exporters of coal, with large reserves of both thermal (steaming) coal, used for electricity generation, and metallurgical (coking) coal, used in steel production. The principal coalfields are in Queensland, New South Wales and Victoria.

Reserves and production

Australia's black coal resources are concentrated in the Bowen Basin of Queensland and the Sydney Basin of New South Wales, while Victoria's Latrobe Valley holds extensive deposits of brown coal (lignite) used almost entirely for domestic electricity generation. Major export ports include Newcastle in New South Wales — one of the largest coal export terminals in the world — and Hay Point and Gladstone in Queensland. Japan, China, South Korea and India have historically been among the largest buyers of Australian coal.

Electricity generation

Coal-fired power stations long supplied the majority of Australia's electricity, although their share has declined as wind, solar and hydroelectric generation have grown and older plants have retired. Notable stations have included Loy Yang and Hazelwood in Victoria (Hazelwood closed in 2017), Eraring, Bayswater and Liddell in New South Wales, and Tarong and Stanwell in Queensland.

History

Coal was discovered near Newcastle in the 1790s, soon after European settlement, and mining expanded through the nineteenth century to supply steamships, railways and industry. The post-war period saw rapid growth in exports, particularly to Japan's steel industry, and coal became one of Australia's most valuable export commodities.

Environmental and political debate

Coal mining and coal-fired generation are central to debates over climate change in Australia. Issues include greenhouse gas emissions, proposals for new mines such as the Carmichael (Adani) project in the Galilee Basin, rehabilitation of mine sites, and effects on water resources and communities. The sector is also a significant employer in regional areas, and its future is closely tied to national energy and climate policy.
