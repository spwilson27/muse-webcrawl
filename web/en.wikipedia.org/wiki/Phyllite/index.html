Phyllite


Phyllite is a foliated metamorphic rock intermediate in grade between slate and schist, composed mainly of quartz, sericite mica and chlorite.

Phyllite is a type of foliated metamorphic rock formed from the further metamorphism of slate. It is composed mainly of quartz, sericite mica, and chlorite. The name derives from the Greek phyllon ("leaf"), referring to its foliated, flaky character.

Formation and properties

Phyllite represents a grade of regional metamorphism between slate and schist, typically derived from shale or mudstone. Continued heat and pressure recrystallise the microscopic clay minerals of slate into slightly larger flakes of mica, which are aligned by directed pressure. Unlike slate's dull surface, phyllite's foliation planes show a characteristic silky or satiny lustre; grains remain too fine to identify with the naked eye, distinguishing it from coarser schist. Its foliation (phyllitic cleavage) is often wavy or crenulated.

Occurrence and use

Phyllites are common in low-grade regional metamorphic terrains such as the Dalradian of Scotland, the Appalachians, and parts of the Alps and Himalayas. Historically some phyllites have been split for roofing and flooring, though they are less durable than true slate.
