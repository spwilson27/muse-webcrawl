R. B. Bennett

R. B. Bennett
Born: Richard Bedford Bennett, 3 July 1870, Hopewell Hill, New Brunswick
Died: 26 June 1947, Mickleham, Surrey, England
Office: 11th Prime Minister of Canada (1930–1935)
Party: Conservative Party of Canada

Richard Bedford Bennett, 1st Viscount Bennett (3 July 1870 – 26 June 1947) was a Canadian lawyer, businessman, and politician who served as the 11th prime minister of Canada from 1930 to 1935, through the worst of the Great Depression.

Early life and career

Born in Hopewell Hill, New Brunswick, Bennett trained as a teacher, then studied law at Dalhousie University and was called to the bar in 1893. He moved to Calgary in 1897, built one of the most successful law practices in the young province of Alberta, and amassed a fortune through law, ranching, and investment. He served in the legislatures of the Northwest Territories and Alberta and entered federal politics as a Conservative MP in 1911, later serving as Minister of Justice and Minister of Finance in the government of Arthur Meighen. He became leader of the Conservative Party in 1927.

Prime Minister

Bennett won the 1930 election promising aggressive action against the Depression — famously pledging to use tariffs to "blast a way" into world markets. His government raised tariffs, created the Bank of Canada, the Canadian Radio Broadcasting Commission (forerunner of the CBC), and the Canadian Wheat Board, and passed the Relief Act. Yet mass unemployment persisted; in 1935, thousands of unemployed men in federal relief camps launched the On-to-Ottawa Trek, which Bennett's government stopped at Regina in a riot on 1 July 1935. Late in his term, Bennett delivered radio speeches proposing a Canadian "New Deal" of regulation and social reform, but many measures were later struck down by the courts. His government was defeated by Mackenzie King's Liberals in October 1935.

Later life

In 1939 Bennett retired to England, where he was created Viscount Bennett in 1941 — the only Canadian prime minister to receive a British peerage. He died at Mickleham, Surrey, in 1947 and is buried there, the only Canadian prime minister interred outside Canada.
