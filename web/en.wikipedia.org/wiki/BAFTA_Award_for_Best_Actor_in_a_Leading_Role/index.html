BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Leading Role

The BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Leading Role is a British Academy Film Award presented annually by the British Academy of Film and Television Arts (BAFTA) to recognise an actor who has delivered an outstanding leading performance in a film. It is one of the most prestigious acting honours in British cinema.

History

From 1952 to 1967, the award was split into two categories: Best British Actor and Best Foreign Actor. The two categories were merged into a single Best Actor award beginning with the 1968 ceremony. In 1985, the name was changed to Best Actor in a Leading Role to distinguish it from the award for Best Actor in a Supporting Role.

Notable winners

Peter Finch holds the record for most wins in the category, with five victories. Other multiple winners include Daniel Day-Lewis, who won four times, and Marlon Brando, Alec Guinness and Dustin Hoffman. The first recipient of the combined award was Spencer Tracy for Guess Who's Coming to Dinner. Recent winners have included Austin Butler for Elvis, Will Smith for King Richard, Anthony Hopkins for The Father, Joaquin Phoenix for Joker, and Cillian Murphy for Oppenheimer.
