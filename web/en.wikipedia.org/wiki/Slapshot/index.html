Slapshot

A slapshot (also spelled slap shot) is a powerful shot in ice hockey. The player raises the stick in a large backswing, strikes the ice just behind the puck so the flexing shaft stores energy like a spring, and follows through, launching the puck at the highest speeds of any hockey shot — elite slapshots exceed 160 km/h (100 mph).

Technique

The shooter positions the puck near the front foot, winds the stick up to waist or shoulder height, and drives the blade into the ice a few centimetres behind the puck. The blade's contact with the ice bends the shaft; as the blade reaches the puck, the stored energy releases, whipping the puck forward. Weight transfer from the back leg to the front leg and a full follow-through complete the motion. Because of its long windup, the slapshot is most often used by defencemen from the point, especially on power plays.

History

The shot is often credited to Eddie Dara of the Ottawa area and was popularized in the 1950s and 1960s by players such as Bernie "Boom Boom" Geoffrion of the Montreal Canadiens, whose nickname came from the sound of his shots, and Bobby Hull, whose powerful slapshot helped drive the adoption of goalie masks. Zdeno Chára recorded the fastest shots at NHL All-Star skills competitions, reaching 108.8 mph (175.1 km/h) in 2012.

Variants and related shots

The one-timer is a slapshot taken directly off a pass without stopping the puck. The wrist shot and snap shot sacrifice power for a quicker release, while the backhand and slapshot's smaller cousin, the "half-slap," are used in tight spaces.
