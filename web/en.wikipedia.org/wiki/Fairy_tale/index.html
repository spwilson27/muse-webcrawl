Fairy tale

A fairy tale (alternative names include fairy story, fairy fiction, magic tale, or wonder tale) is a short story that belongs to the folklore genre. Such stories typically feature magic, enchantments, and mythical or fanciful beings. In most cultures, there is no clear line separating myth from folk or fairy tales; all these together form the literature of preliterate societies.

Fairy tales may be distinguished from other folk narratives such as legends (which generally involve belief in the veracity of the events described) and explicit moral tales, including beast fables. The term is mainly used for tales with origins in European tradition and, at least in recent centuries, mostly relates to children's literature.

History

The oral tradition of the fairy tale came long before the written page. Tales were told or enacted dramatically, rather than written down, and handed down from generation to generation. Literary fairy tales appeared in 17th-century France, notably the Histoires ou contes du temps passé (1697) of Charles Perrault, which introduced "Cinderella", "Sleeping Beauty" and "Little Red Riding Hood". The Brothers Grimm published their Children's and Household Tales in 1812, and Hans Christian Andersen wrote original literary fairy tales such as "The Little Mermaid" and "The Ugly Duckling".

Definitions and classification

Folklorists have classified fairy tales under the Aarne–Thompson–Uther Index (ATU 300–749, "Tales of Magic"). Vladimir Propp's Morphology of the Folktale (1928) analysed Russian fairy tales into a sequence of recurring narrative functions performed by stock characters such as the hero, the villain, the donor and the helper.

Motifs

Common motifs include enchanted forests, talking animals, wishes, magical objects, transformations, forbidden chambers, and the rule of three. Many tales open with the formula "Once upon a time" and end with the protagonists living "happily ever after".

Criticism and interpretation

Psychological interpretations, such as Bruno Bettelheim's The Uses of Enchantment (1976), argue that fairy tales help children confront existential anxieties. Feminist scholars have criticised the passivity of many traditional heroines, prompting numerous modern retellings with active protagonists.
