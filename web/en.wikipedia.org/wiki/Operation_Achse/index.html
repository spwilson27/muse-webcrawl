Operation Achse (German: Fall Achse, "Case Axis") was the codename of the German operation to forcibly disarm the Italian armed forces following the announcement of the Armistice of Cassibile between Italy and the Allies on 8 September 1943.

German planners had prepared for an Italian defection since the fall of Benito Mussolini in July 1943. Under Field Marshal Erwin Rommel in the north and Field Marshal Albert Kesselring in the south, German forces moved swiftly to seize Rome, disarm Italian divisions across Italy, the Balkans and southern France, and secure key ports and airfields.

Course of the operation

Within days, German troops had disarmed the bulk of the Italian army. Roughly 600,000 Italian soldiers were taken prisoner and deported to Germany as "Italian military internees", many being used as forced labour. Some Italian units resisted, notably on the Greek island of Cephalonia, where thousands of Italian prisoners were massacred by German troops after surrendering. The Italian fleet largely escaped to Malta, though the battleship Roma was sunk by a German Fritz X guided bomb en route.

Aftermath

Germany established the puppet Italian Social Republic under Mussolini in northern Italy, while the royal government in the south declared war on Germany in October 1943 and fought alongside the Allies as a co-belligerent. The operation secured Germany's southern flank but tied down substantial forces in Italy for the remainder of the war.
