Operation Olympic Games

Operation Olympic Games was a covert cyber-warfare campaign conducted by the United States, in collaboration with Israel, against Iran's nuclear programme. The operation is best known for producing the Stuxnet computer worm, which sabotaged uranium enrichment centrifuges at the Natanz enrichment plant. Details of the programme were first reported publicly in 2012, principally by journalist David E. Sanger.

Background

Concerned at Iran's enrichment activities and seeking an alternative to airstrikes, the administration of President George W. Bush began developing a cyber weapon around 2006 that could physically damage the IR-1 centrifuges at Natanz. The programme continued and was accelerated under President Barack Obama. The worm, developed with Israel's Unit 8200, targeted Siemens Step7 programmable logic controllers configuring the variable-frequency drives that spun the centrifuges.

Stuxnet

Stuxnet spread via infected USB drives and Windows exploits, eventually escaping Natanz onto the wider internet, where it was discovered by security researchers in 2010. Within the target system it intermittently altered rotor speeds — driving centrifuges to overspeed and then slowing them — while replaying recorded normal sensor data to operators, causing roughly a thousand centrifuges to fail and setting back the Iranian programme. The operation is widely regarded as the first demonstrated use of a cyber weapon to cause physical destruction.

Significance

The disclosure of Olympic Games opened public debate about state-sponsored cyber operations and the norms governing them; subsequent reporting described related tools such as Flame and Duqu. The operation is a standard case study in cyber warfare and in the strategy of delaying nuclear proliferation by covert means.
