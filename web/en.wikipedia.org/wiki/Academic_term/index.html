Academic term

An academic term (or simply term) is a portion of an academic year, the time during which an educational institution holds classes. The schedules adopted vary widely between and within countries. Common systems divide the year into two semesters, three trimesters, or three terms (as in the United Kingdom, where terms are traditionally named Michaelmas, Hilary or Lent, and Trinity at Oxford and Cambridge).

In North America, the semester system predominates: a fall semester running roughly from late August or September to December, and a spring semester from January to May, often supplemented by optional summer sessions. Universities on the quarter system, such as several in the University of California system and Stanford, divide the year into autumn, winter, and spring quarters of about ten weeks.

Regional variations

In the Southern Hemisphere, the academic year typically aligns with the calendar year, beginning in February or March and ending in November or December, with a long summer break over Christmas. In Japan, the school and university year traditionally begins in April. Many European universities run two semesters with examination periods in January/February and May/June.

Related terms

A "term" may also refer to the full period of enrolment for a course. Other related concepts include the reading week or mid-term break, the examination period at a term's end, and sabbatical terms for faculty research leave.
