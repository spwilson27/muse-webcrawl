Hardy–Littlewood circle method

The Hardy–Littlewood circle method is a technique in analytic number theory developed by G. H. Hardy and J. E. Littlewood in a series of papers beginning in 1920. It counts the number of representations of an integer by a polynomial or additive form by expressing the counting problem as the coefficient of a generating function, extracted by a contour integral.

Method

The integral over the unit circle is split into major arcs, neighbourhoods of rationals with small denominators, where the generating function can be approximated closely and yields the main term, and minor arcs, where cancellation in exponential sums shows the contribution is negligible. The Farey dissection organises this splitting.

Applications

The method gave the first asymptotic formulas for the partition function (building on work with Ramanujan), progress on Waring's problem — including I. M. Vinogradov's proof that every large odd integer is a sum of three primes — and results toward the weak Goldbach conjecture, completed by Harald Helfgott in 2013 using these ideas.
