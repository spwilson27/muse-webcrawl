Barley

Barley
Scientific nameHordeum vulgare
FamilyPoaceae (grasses)
OriginFertile Crescent, domesticated c. 10,000 years ago
UsesLivestock feed, malt for beer and whisky, food

Barley (Hordeum vulgare) is a cereal grain in the grass family and one of the oldest domesticated crops in the world, first cultivated in the Fertile Crescent more than ten thousand years ago. It ranks among the top five cereal crops globally by production.

Cultivation

Barley is a hardy, short-season crop tolerant of cool climates and marginal soils, grown from the Arctic Circle to the tropics. Cultivars are classified as two-row or six-row according to the arrangement of grains on the ear, and as hulled or hulless (naked). Major producers include Russia, Australia, France, Germany, and Ukraine.

Uses

Most barley is used as animal feed and for malting. Malted barley is essential to beer and whisky production, its enzymes converting starches to fermentable sugars. As a human food it appears in soups (notably Scotch broth), pearl barley, barley flour, and the roasted-grain drink mugicha. Barley is rich in soluble beta-glucan fibre, though like wheat it contains gluten and is unsuitable for people with coeliac disease.
