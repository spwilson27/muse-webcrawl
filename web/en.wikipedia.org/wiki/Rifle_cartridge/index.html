Rifle cartridge

A rifle cartridge is a type of ammunition designed for use in rifles. Like all modern cartridges it packages a projectile (bullet), propellant and primer in a single case, but rifle cartridges are generally longer and more powerful than handgun cartridges, with bottle-necked cases common for high-velocity flat-shooting rounds.

Characteristics

Rifle cartridges operate at high chamber pressures (often 50,000–65,000 psi) and drive relatively light bullets at velocities of 600–1,000 m/s, giving flat trajectories and long effective ranges. Cases may be rimmed, rimless, belted or rebated-rim designs. Well-known examples include the .22 Long Rifle (a rimfire round), .223 Remington / 5.56×45mm NATO, 7.62×51mm NATO, .30-06 Springfield, 7.62×39mm, and the .308 Winchester.

Categories

They include varmint cartridges, intermediate cartridges for assault rifles, full-power battle-rifle and machine-gun cartridges, big-game cartridges, and rimfire training and small-game rounds.
