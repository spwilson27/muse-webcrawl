The Presidential Citizens Medal is an award bestowed by the President of the United States. It is the second-highest civilian award in the United States, second only to the Presidential Medal of Freedom. Established by Executive Order 11494 on 13 November 1969 by President Richard Nixon, the medal recognizes U.S. citizens who have performed exemplary deeds of service for their country or their fellow citizens.

Unlike the Medal of Freedom, which may be granted to anyone (including non-citizens and the military), the Citizens Medal is restricted to United States citizens. The award is granted solely by the president, who may present it at any time. Recipients are selected by the White House, often following public nominations; in 2011 the Obama administration crowdsourced nominations for the first time, inviting the public to submit names through the White House website.

Description

The medal is a gold disc bearing the Seal of the President of the United States encircled by a laurel wreath. It is suspended from a ribbon of dark blue with a central light-blue stripe and white edges. Recipients receive the medal together with a certificate.

History and notable recipients

The first medals were presented in 1973. Recipients have included civil rights figures, public servants, educators, athletes, and private citizens recognized for acts of heroism or long service. Among well-known recipients are Henry "Hank" Aaron (2001), Muhammad Ali (2001), Ruby Bridges (2001), and the families and responders honored after major national events. In January 2023, President Joe Biden awarded the medal to twelve people, including Capitol Police officers and election officials, for their roles during and after the January 6, 2021 attack on the U.S. Capitol. In January 2025, Biden awarded the medal to twenty recipients, including former Representative Liz Cheney and Representative Bennie Thompson, who led the House January 6 committee.
