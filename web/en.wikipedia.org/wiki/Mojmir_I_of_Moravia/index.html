Mojmir I of Moravia

Mojmir I (Czech and Slovak: Mojmír I; c. 795 – 846) was the first historically attested duke of Moravia and the eponymous founder of the Mojmirid dynasty. Ruling from the 830s until 846, he united the Slavic principalities of Moravia and Nitra into the realm known to later historians as Great Moravia, the first major West Slavic state.

Background

Slavic settlement of the middle Danube followed the collapse of Avar power under Frankish attack at the end of the eighth century. By the 820s two neighbouring principalities had formed: Moravia, along the Morava river, and Nitra in modern Slovakia, ruled by Prince Pribina. Mojmir, duke of the Moravians, took advantage of the Frankish civil wars to expand.

Unification of Moravia and Nitra

In 833 Mojmir attacked and expelled Pribina, annexing Nitra and thereby creating a single large principality north of the Danube — the nucleus of Great Moravia. The Frankish sources, notably the Annals of Fulda and the Salzburg Conversio, treat Mojmir as both a partner and a threat. The first Moravian churches, including the rotunda at Mikulčice, were built in his time, and Mojmir himself is generally considered to have accepted baptism along with his retinue.

Fall and succession

In August 846 Louis the German marched into Moravia. Mojmir died — whether in battle, of natural causes, or by deposition the sources do not clearly say — and the Frankish king installed Mojmir's nephew Rastislav, who would go on to invite the Byzantine mission of Cyril and Methodius.
