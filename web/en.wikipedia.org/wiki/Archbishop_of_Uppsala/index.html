Archbishop of Uppsala

Uppsala Cathedral, the seat of the Archbishop of Uppsala.

The Archbishop of Uppsala (Swedish: Ärkebiskopen i Uppsala) is the primate of the Church of Sweden and the metropolitan archbishop of the Archdiocese of Uppsala. The office is the senior episcopal position in the Swedish church, though since the Reformation the archbishop has been primus inter pares (first among equals) rather than exercising jurisdiction over the other diocesan bishops.

History

Uppsala became an archbishopric in 1164, when Stefan, a monk from Alvastra Abbey, was consecrated as the first Archbishop of Uppsala by the Archbishop of Lund with papal approval. The archdiocese covered the Swedish realm and the see was originally located at Gamla Uppsala (Old Uppsala) before moving to Östra Aros, modern Uppsala, in the 1270s, where Uppsala Cathedral was built.

During the Swedish Reformation of the 1520s–1530s under Gustav Vasa, the church broke with Rome. Laurentius Petri, consecrated archbishop in 1531, is regarded as the first Lutheran Archbishop of Uppsala; remarkably, his consecration preserved the historic episcopate through Archbishop Petrus Magni of Västerås, who had himself been consecrated in Rome — a point central to the Church of Sweden's claim of apostolic succession.

Role today

The archbishop leads the Church of Sweden nationally, represents it internationally and ecumenically, chairs certain national church bodies, and serves as diocesan bishop of the Diocese of Uppsala. Archbishops are elected by an electoral body drawn from the whole church. Notable modern holders include Nathan Söderblom (1914–1931), Nobel Peace Prize laureate and pioneer of the ecumenical movement; Anders Wejryd (2006–2014); Antje Jackelén (2014–2022), the first woman to hold the office; and Martin Modéus, who succeeded her in 2022.
