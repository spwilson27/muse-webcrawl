Flat racing

Flat racing is a form of horse racing in which horses gallop directly between two points on a level track, without jumping obstacles. It contrasts with jump racing (over hurdles or fences) and harness racing. Flat races are run on turf or on synthetic ("all-weather") surfaces over distances typically from five furlongs (about 1,000 metres) to more than two miles.

Racing

Flat races are usually run by Thoroughbreds, though breeds such as Arabians and Quarter Horses also have flat racing. Two-year-olds race over the shortest distances; stamina tests for older horses extend to staying races such as the Ascot Gold Cup at two and a half miles. Races are categorized as conditions races, handicaps (in which horses carry weights calculated to equalize their chances), maidens for horses that have not yet won, and claimers and sellers in North America.

The most prestigious flat races are the Group 1 (or Grade I) events. In Britain, the five Classics — the 2000 Guineas, 1000 Guineas, Epsom Derby, Epsom Oaks, and St Leger — anchor the season, which traditionally runs on turf from spring to autumn, while in North America the highlights include the Kentucky Derby and the Breeders' Cup. The richest flat races include the Saudi Cup, the Dubai World Cup, and the Prix de l'Arc de Triomphe.

Weight and handicapping

In weight-for-age races, younger horses carry less weight than older ones according to a published scale, and fillies receive an allowance from colts. In handicaps, an official handicapper assigns each horse a weight in proportion to its assessed ability, aiming for a dead heat.

The flat season

In Britain and Ireland, the turf flat season runs from March or April to November, complemented by all-weather racing year-round. Flat racing is also a major sport in France, Japan, Australia, the United States, Hong Kong, and the Middle East.
