Japan Sumo Association

The Japan Sumo Association (日本相撲協会, Nihon Sumō Kyōkai), officially the Japan Sumo Association public interest incorporated foundation, is the body that operates and controls professional sumo wrestling in Japan under the jurisdiction of the Japanese Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology.

Organisation

The association is owned and governed by the oyakata (elders), former wrestlers who hold one of 105 elder shares (toshiyori kabu) and run the training stables (heya) in which wrestlers live and train. It is led by a chairman (rijichō) elected by the board of directors. Its headquarters are at the Ryōgoku Kokugikan arena in Tokyo.

Activities

The association organises the six annual grand tournaments (honbasho) — three in Tokyo and one each in Osaka, Nagoya and Fukuoka — as well as regional exhibition tours. It maintains sumo's traditional rules, rankings (banzuke), rituals and customs, trains gyōji referees and yobidashi announcers, and runs the Sumo Museum. Membership of the association is restricted to former professional wrestlers, making professional sumo a closed, self-governing world.
