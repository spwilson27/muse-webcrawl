Samantabhadra (Sanskrit: समन्तभद्र, literally "Universal Worthy" or "All-Encompassing Good") is a bodhisattva in Mahayana Buddhism associated with practice and meditation. Along with the Buddha and the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī, he forms the Shakyamuni Triad in some Mahayana traditions. He is the patron of the Lotus Sutra and, according to East Asian tradition, made ten great vows that are the basis of his practice.

Role in Mahayana Buddhism

In the Avataṃsaka Sūtra (Flower Garland Sutra), Samantabhadra is famed for his ten great vows, which include paying homage to all Buddhas, making offerings, repenting transgressions, rejoicing in the merits of others, requesting the Buddhas to teach, and dedicating all merit to the liberation of all beings. The final chapter of the sutra, the Gaṇḍavyūha, describes the pilgrim Sudhana receiving instruction from Samantabhadra, and the text "Entry into the Realm of Reality" is often recited as a standalone work.

Samantabhadra embodies the aspect of practice and conduct, complementing Mañjuśrī, who embodies wisdom. Where Mañjuśrī rides a lion, Samantabhadra is depicted riding a white elephant with six tusks, symbolizing the strength of practice and the six perfections (pāramitās).

East Asian Buddhism

In China, Samantabhadra (Puxian) is one of the four great bodhisattvas of Chinese Buddhism, and his sacred mountain is Mount Emei in Sichuan province. In Japanese Buddhism, as Fugen Bosatsu, he is particularly venerated in the Tendai and Shingon schools, often depicted in esoteric mandalas. In Tibetan Buddhism, Samantabhadra (Kuntu Zangpo) also appears as a primordial Buddha (Adi-Buddha) in the Nyingma tradition, a distinct emanation of the same name.

Iconography

Samantabhadra is typically shown seated on his elephant, holding a lotus stem, a wish-fulfilling jewel, or with hands in meditation posture. In esoteric forms he may appear with multiple arms. Statues at Mount Emei, including the massive golden statue at the summit, depict him with ten faces atop an elephant, representing his ten vows.
