Protected health information

Protected health information (PHI) under the US Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (HIPAA) is any information about health status, provision of health care, or payment for health care that is created or collected by a covered entity and can be linked to a specific individual. PHI is often interpreted loosely to include any part of a patient's medical record or payment history.

Identifiers

HIPAA lists 18 identifiers that, combined with health information, make it PHI, including names, geographic subdivisions smaller than a state, dates (other than year), telephone and fax numbers, email addresses, Social Security numbers, medical record numbers, health plan beneficiary numbers, account numbers, biometric identifiers, and full-face photographs.

Rules

The HIPAA Privacy Rule governs uses and disclosures of PHI by covered entities (health plans, health care clearinghouses, and most health care providers) and their business associates. The Security Rule sets administrative, physical and technical safeguards for electronic PHI (ePHI). Individuals generally have the right to access and obtain copies of their own PHI.

De-identification

Data from which the 18 identifiers have been removed, so that the risk of identifying an individual is very small, is no longer PHI and may be used or disclosed without restriction under HIPAA.
