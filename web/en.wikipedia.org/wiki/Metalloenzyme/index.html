Metalloenzyme

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Metalloproteins are proteins that contain a metal ion cofactor; those metalloproteins that function as enzymes are termed metalloenzymes. A large fraction — by some estimates up to half — of all proteins require metals, and metalloenzymes catalyse many of the most demanding reactions in biology, including nitrogen fixation, photosynthesis, respiration, and DNA synthesis.

Conventionally the field distinguishes proteins that bind their metal tightly and permanently (metalloenzymes proper, such as carbonic anhydrase with its Zn2+) from metal-activated enzymes in which a loose, transient metal ion (often Mg2+ or Mn2+) is required for activity.

Roles of the metal

Metal ions in active sites serve several functions: they act as Lewis acids that polarise substrates and stabilise negative charge; they undergo redox cycling (Fe, Cu, Mn, Mo, Co, Ni) to transfer electrons or activate small molecules such as O2, N2, CO2, and H2; and they serve as structural templates organising the active site. Iron–sulfur clusters, haem groups, and binuclear centres are common architectures.

Examples

  • Carbonic anhydrase (Zn) — hydrates CO2 at near diffusion-limited rates.
  • Nitrogenase (FeMo cofactor) — reduces atmospheric N2 to ammonia.
  • Cytochrome c oxidase (Fe, Cu) — terminal oxidase of respiration.
  • Superoxide dismutases (Cu/Zn, Mn, Fe, Ni) — detoxify superoxide radicals.
  • Ribonucleotide reductase (Fe or Mn, or cobalamin-dependent forms) — makes deoxyribonucleotides for DNA synthesis.
  • Alcohol dehydrogenase and many proteases (Zn).
  • Urease (Ni) — the first enzyme ever crystallised (1926).

Medicine and metal homeostasis

Because pathogens and hosts compete for transition metals, metalloenzymes are important drug targets: HIV protease inhibitors, carbonic anhydrase inhibitor diuretics and glaucoma drugs, and metallo-β-lactamase inhibitors all exploit metal chemistry. Disorders of metal handling (for instance Wilson's and Menkes diseases of copper transport) show the toxicity of metal imbalance. The study of metalloenzymes lies at the core of bioinorganic chemistry.
