Rayalaseema

Rayalaseema (Telugu: రాయలసీమ) is a geographic region in the Indian state of Andhra Pradesh. It comprises the districts of Anantapur, Annamayya, Chittoor, Kurnool, Nandyal, Sri Sathya Sai, Tirupati and YSR Kadapa in the southern part of the state. The region is bordered by Tamil Nadu to the south, Karnataka to the west, and Telangana to the north.

History

The name is associated with the Vijayanagara Empire's ruling dynasty (the "Rayas"); the region formed part of the Madras Presidency under British rule and, along with Coastal Andhra, formed Andhra State in 1953 and then Andhra Pradesh in 1956. The historic city of Tirupati, with the famous Venkateswara temple at Tirumala, is its best-known pilgrimage centre.

Geography and economy

Rayalaseema is a semi-arid plateau region, among the driest parts of peninsular India, and historically prone to drought and famine. Agriculture depends on groundwater and tanks, with groundnuts, sunflower and horticulture prominent; the region also has mining and cement industries around Kadapa and Kurnool.
