First Fleet

The First Fleet was a fleet of 11 British ships that took the first British colonists and convicts to Australia. It comprised two Royal Navy vessels, three store ships and six convict transports. On 13 May 1787 the fleet under the command of Captain Arthur Phillip, with over 1,400 people (convicts, marines, sailors, civil officers and free settlers), left Portsmouth, England and arrived at Botany Bay in New South Wales between 18 and 20 January 1788, before moving to Sydney Cove in Port Jackson on 26 January, where the settlement of Sydney was founded.

The voyage

The fleet sailed via Tenerife, Rio de Janeiro, and Cape Town, where it took on supplies. The voyage of more than 24,000 kilometres (15,000 miles) took about 250 days. Remarkably few lives were lost — around 40–50 people of the roughly 1,400 aboard — a credit to Phillip's planning and the fleet's surgeon-general, John White.

Ships

  • Naval escorts: HMS Sirius (flagship) and HMS Supply
  • Convict transports: Alexander, Charlotte, Friendship, Lady Penrhyn, Prince of Wales, Scarborough
  • Store ships: Borrowdale, Fishburn, Golden Grove

People

The fleet carried roughly 750 convicts (about 550 men and 200 women), some 250 Royal Marines and officers with families, and ships' crews. Arthur Phillip became the first Governor of New South Wales. The arrival of the First Fleet began the European colonization of Australia, with profound and devastating consequences for the Aboriginal peoples of the Sydney region.

Legacy

26 January is commemorated as Australia Day, marking the raising of the British flag at Sydney Cove; it is also observed by many Indigenous Australians as Invasion Day or a day of mourning.
