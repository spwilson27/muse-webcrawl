Electric motorcycle

Electric motorcycles and scooters are two- or three-wheeled plug-in electric vehicles powered by rechargeable battery packs driving an electric motor. Unlike electric bicycles, they generally have no pedals and are licensed and ridden like conventional motorcycles.

Characteristics

Electric motors deliver maximum torque from a standstill, giving electric motorcycles rapid acceleration with a single-speed or direct-drive transmission and no clutch. Range depends on battery capacity and riding style: urban models typically offer 70–150 km per charge, while large touring and sport models exceed 200 km. Running costs and routine maintenance are lower than for petrol machines, as there are fewer moving parts and no oil changes, but purchase prices remain higher and charging takes longer than refuelling.

Manufacturers and history

Experiments with electric two-wheelers date to the earliest years of motorcycling, but modern machines became practical with lithium-ion batteries in the 2000s. Specialist manufacturers such as Zero Motorcycles, Energica and LiveWire (Harley's electric brand) produce full-size motorcycles, while in Asia — particularly China, India and Taiwan — electric scooters and mopeds are produced in the tens of millions and dominate urban sales. Battery-swapping networks, notably Gogoro in Taiwan, address charging time for light machines. Major manufacturers including Honda, Yamaha, BMW and Triumph have introduced or announced electric models.

Racing

Electric motorcycles compete in events such as the former TT Zero at the Isle of Man and the MotoE World Cup, which ran alongside MotoGP from 2019.
