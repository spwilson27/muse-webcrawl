Valdemar I (14 January 1131 – 12 May 1182), also known as Valdemar the Great (Danish: Valdemar den Store), was King of Denmark from 1154 until his death in 1182. His reign, shared with the churchman Absalon, marked the revival of Denmark as a Baltic power after decades of civil war.

Early life and civil war

Valdemar was the posthumous son of Canute Lavard, a popular duke murdered in 1131. Raised at the court of the magnate Asser Rig alongside Absalon, he became a claimant in the Danish civil war that followed the death of Eric III. The war ended after the "Bloodfeast of Roskilde" in 1157, at which his rival Sweyn III attempted to murder Valdemar and Canute V; Valdemar escaped, defeated Sweyn at the Battle of Grathe Heath, and became sole king.

Reign

With Absalon, Bishop of Roskilde and later Archbishop of Lund, Valdemar campaigned against the Wendish pirates who raided the Danish coasts, conquering Rügen in 1168 and toppling the great temple fortress of Arkona. He fortified the Danevirke frontier, built castles around the kingdom, and began the transformation of Copenhagen, which Absalon founded as a fortified harbour. Valdemar also issued laws and strengthened royal administration.

Death and legacy

Valdemar died at Vordingborg in 1182 and was buried at St. Bendt's Church, Ringsted. He was succeeded by his son Canute VI. His younger son Valdemar II ("the Victorious") later extended Danish power further around the Baltic.
