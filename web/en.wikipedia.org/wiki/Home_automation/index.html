Home automation

Also known asDomotics, smart home technology
ApplicationsLighting, climate control, security, appliances, entertainment
Key protocolsZigbee, Z-Wave, Thread, Matter, KNX, X10

Home automation or domotics is building automation for a home, called a smart home or smart house. A home automation system monitors and controls home attributes such as lighting, climate, entertainment systems, and appliances. It may also include home security such as access control and alarm systems. When connected with the Internet, home devices are an important constituent of the Internet of Things (IoT).

History

Early home automation began with labour-saving machines: electrified appliances spread through the early 20th century, and in 1966 the ECHO IV computer project demonstrated centralized control of home devices. The X10 protocol, released in 1975, allowed compatible devices to communicate over household power wiring. Dedicated systems such as KNX became standard in European commercial and high-end residential installations. The spread of smartphones, Wi-Fi, and voice assistants in the 2010s brought home automation to the mass market.

Applications

  • Lighting control — remote and scheduled switching and dimming, occupancy-based automation.
  • Heating, ventilation and air conditioning — smart thermostats that learn occupancy patterns and optimize energy use.
  • Security and surveillance — smart locks, video doorbells, cameras, and intrusion and leak sensors.
  • Entertainment — multi-room audio and video distribution and voice-controlled playback.
  • Accessibility — voice control and automation that assist elderly and disabled residents to live independently.

Standards and interoperability

The market has long been fragmented among competing wireless standards such as Zigbee and Z-Wave and proprietary ecosystems from major technology companies. The Matter standard, launched in 2022 by the Connectivity Standards Alliance with backing from Amazon, Apple, Google, and Samsung, aims to unify smart-home interoperability over IP-based networking including Thread and Wi-Fi.

Criticism

Concerns include privacy, as connected devices collect detailed data about household behaviour; security vulnerabilities in poorly maintained IoT devices; and dependence on cloud services and vendors, which can leave devices inoperable if a service is discontinued.
