Henry V (German: Heinrich V; 11 August 1081 or 1086 – 23 May 1125) was king of Germany (from 1099 to 1125) and Holy Roman Emperor (from 1111 to 1125), the fourth and last ruler of the Salian dynasty. He was the son of Henry IV and Bertha of Savoy.

Accession

Henry was made co-king in 1098, ostensibly to secure the succession. In 1104 he rebelled against his father, winning the support of the church and princes by promising to end the Investiture Controversy, and in 1105 he forced Henry IV to abdicate. As king, however, Henry V pursued his father's policies of royal control over bishoprics.

Coronation and the Concordat of Worms

In 1111 Henry marched to Rome, seized Pope Paschal II, and extracted a coronation and concessions on investiture before withdrawing; the concessions were promptly repudiated. Civil strife in Germany followed. The long conflict finally ended with the Concordat of Worms (1122), negotiated with Pope Callixtus II: the emperor renounced investiture with ring and staff and granted free canonical elections, while retaining a role in elections in Germany and the right to invest bishops with their temporal regalia by the sceptre. The concordat is a landmark in the development of church–state relations in medieval Europe.

Death

Henry married Matilda, daughter of Henry I of England, in 1114, but the marriage produced no children. He died at Utrecht in 1125, and with him the Salian dynasty ended; he was buried in Speyer Cathedral beside his father. Lothair of Supplinburg was elected his successor.
