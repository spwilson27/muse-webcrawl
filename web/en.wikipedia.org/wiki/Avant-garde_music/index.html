Avant-garde music

Avant-garde music is music considered ahead of its time, pushing or breaking boundaries of convention in composition, performance, or listening. The term overlaps with experimental music, though avant-garde often implies a stronger aesthetic or political challenge to established norms.

History

Early landmarks include Erik Satie's furniture music, Luigi Russolo's Futurist noise manifesto The Art of Noises (1913), Arnold Schoenberg's atonality and twelve-tone technique, and Edgard Varèse's experiments with sound masses. After 1945, John Cage's chance music and 4′33″, Karlheinz Stockhausen's electronic and serial works, La Monte Young's minimal drones, and free jazz by Ornette Coleman and Cecil Taylor expanded the field. Later movements include musique concrète, fluxus, noise music, and sound art.

Characteristics

Common traits: extended techniques, unconventional instruments or prepared piano, electronics and tape, indeterminacy, graphic scores, microtonality, and rejection of traditional harmony or form.
