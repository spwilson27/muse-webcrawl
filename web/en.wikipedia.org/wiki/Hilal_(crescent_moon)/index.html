Hilal (crescent moon)

Hilal (Arabic: هلال, hilāl) is the Arabic term for the crescent moon as it appears in the first days after the astronomical new moon. The sighting of the hilal marks the beginning of each month in the Islamic lunar calendar and is of particular religious significance in determining the start of Ramadan, the festival of Eid al-Fitr, and the date of Eid al-Adha.

Moon sighting

Traditionally, the new month begins when qualified witnesses sight the young crescent shortly after sunset on the 29th day of the current month; if the crescent is not seen, the month is completed to 30 days. Because visibility depends on the moon's age, elongation from the sun, latitude, and weather, the hilal is typically first visible between about 15 and 40 hours after conjunction.

Debates and methods

Differences in sighting criteria — local naked-eye sighting versus astronomical calculation or sightings reported from other countries — regularly lead neighbouring Muslim communities to begin Ramadan or celebrate Eid on different days. Organisations such as moonsighting committees attempt to reconcile testimony with astronomical predictions, and some countries, including Saudi Arabia with its Umm al-Qura calendar, rely partly on calculated criteria.
