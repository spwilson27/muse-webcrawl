Unification of Moldova and Romania

The unification of Moldova and Romania is a popular concept in the two countries that began in the late 1980s, during the Revolutions of 1989. The Romanian Revolution in 1989 and the independence of Moldova in 1991 further contributed to the development of a movement for the unification of the two Romanian-speaking countries. The question of reunification is recurrent in both societies.

Historical background

Bessarabia, roughly corresponding to the modern Republic of Moldova, was part of the Principality of Moldavia before being annexed by the Russian Empire in 1812. In 1918, amid the collapse of the Russian Empire, Bessarabia united with the Kingdom of Romania. The Soviet Union occupied the territory in 1940, and the Moldavian SSR was created. Moldova declared independence from the USSR on 27 August 1991.

Support and opposition

Supporters point to the shared language, history and culture of the majority populations. Opponents raise concerns about the region of Transnistria, the autonomous region of Gagauzia, economic disparities, and Moldova's constitutional neutrality. Public opinion polls in Moldova have generally shown minority support for unification, though support has fluctuated, while in Romania support has traditionally been higher. Various political parties and civic movements in both countries advocate or oppose the idea.
