Encephalitis

Encephalitis is inflammation of the brain. It is most often caused by viral infections — including herpes simplex virus, arboviruses such as West Nile virus and Japanese encephalitis virus, and enteroviruses — though autoimmune processes, in which the immune system attacks brain tissue, are an increasingly recognised cause. Severity ranges from mild illness to life-threatening disease.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms typically include fever, headache, confusion, personality change, seizures, and altered consciousness. Infants may show poor feeding, irritability, and a bulging fontanelle. Encephalitis differs from meningitis, which is inflammation of the membranes surrounding the brain, though the two can co-occur (meningoencephalitis).

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis relies on lumbar puncture with cerebrospinal fluid analysis and viral PCR testing, MRI imaging, and EEG. Because herpes simplex encephalitis is devastating if untreated, empirical intravenous aciclovir is usually started immediately when viral encephalitis is suspected. Autoimmune encephalitis, such as anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis, is treated with immunotherapy. Supportive care in hospital, sometimes in intensive care, is essential.
