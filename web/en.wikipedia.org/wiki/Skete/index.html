Skete

A skete is a community of Christian hermits living under the oversight of a monastery, combining solitary contemplative life with elements of communal worship. The term derives from Sketis (Scetis), the desert region west of the Nile Delta in Egypt where some of the earliest Christian monastic communities — those of the Desert Fathers — were established in the 3rd and 4th centuries.

Structure

In a skete, monks or nuns live in individual cells or small houses grouped around a central church. They spend most of the week in solitude, working and praying alone, and gather on Saturdays, Sundays, and feast days for common liturgy and meals. Sketes are typically dependent on a parent monastery, which provides material support and spiritual direction through an elder.

Traditions

Sketes are characteristic of Eastern Orthodox monasticism, especially on Mount Athos in Greece, where many sketes are attached to the twenty ruling monasteries, and in Russia, where the skete model influenced the tradition of hermitages and the spiritual practice of the startsi (elders). In the Russian tradition, sketes were often centres of the Old Believers as well. The term is also used, loosely, for small dependent monastic settlements in other traditions.
