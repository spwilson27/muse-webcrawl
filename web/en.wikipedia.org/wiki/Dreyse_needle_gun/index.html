Dreyse needle gun

Type Bolt-action rifle
Place of origin Kingdom of Prussia
In service 1841–1871
Designer Johann Nicolaus von Dreyse
Calibre 15.4 mm
Action Bolt action, needle-fired paper cartridge

The Dreyse needle gun (German: Zündnadelgewehr) was a breech-loading bolt-action rifle adopted by the Prussian army in 1841. Designed by Johann Nicolaus von Dreyse, it fired a paper cartridge in which the primer sat at the base of the bullet; a long, thin firing pin — the "needle" — pierced the cartridge to strike it.

Impact

The needle gun gave Prussian infantry a rate of fire several times that of muzzle-loading muskets and allowed soldiers to reload lying prone. It proved decisive in the Second Schleswig War (1864) and especially the Austro-Prussian War of 1866, where at Königgrätz Prussian firepower inflicted heavy casualties on Austrian troops still using muzzle-loaders. By the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71) it was outranged by the French Chassepot, and it was replaced by the Mauser Model 1871.

Characteristics

The rifle's weaknesses were gas leakage at the breech, breakage and corrosion of the needle, and modest range; but its bolt-action breech-loading concept shaped all subsequent military rifles.
