Lockheed bribery scandals

The Lockheed bribery scandals were a series of revelations in the mid-1970s that the American aerospace company Lockheed had paid bribes and commissions to foreign politicians and officials to secure sales of its military aircraft, including the F-104 Starfighter, the L-1011 TriStar and the C-130 Hercules. The disclosures, aired in United States Senate hearings in 1975–76, caused political crises in several countries.

Countries affected

  • Netherlands — Prince Bernhard, husband of Queen Juliana, was accused of accepting over $1 million; he resigned his military and business posts after an inquiry.
  • Japan — former prime minister Kakuei Tanaka was arrested in 1976 and later convicted of taking ¥500 million in connection with TriStar sales.
  • West Germany and Italy — defence officials and politicians were investigated over Starfighter purchases; former German defence minister Franz Josef Strauss was accused but not charged.
  • Saudi Arabia — vast commissions routed through arms dealer Adnan Khashoggi came to light.

Consequences

Lockheed's chairman and president resigned, and the affair was a principal spur to the passage of the United States Foreign Corrupt Practices Act of 1977, which made it unlawful for American companies to bribe foreign officials.
