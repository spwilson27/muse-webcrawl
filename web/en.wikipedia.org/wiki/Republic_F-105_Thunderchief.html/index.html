Republic F-105 Thunderchief

The Republic F-105 Thunderchief was an American supersonic fighter-bomber used by the United States Air Force. Capable of Mach 2, it conducted the majority of strike bombing missions during the early years of the Vietnam War; it was the only American aircraft to have been pulled from combat due to high loss rates. First flown in 1955, the F-105 entered service in 1958 and was withdrawn in 1984.

Design and development

The F-105 was designed by Republic Aviation as a replacement for the F-84F Thunderstreak, intended to deliver a nuclear weapon at supersonic speed. It was powered by a single Pratt & Whitney J75 turbojet and featured distinctive forward-swept wing-root intakes. The aircraft carried its main bomb load internally in a bomb bay, and was the largest single-seat, single-engine combat aircraft in history.

Operational history

In Vietnam, the F-105 flew over 20,000 sorties in Operation Rolling Thunder and other campaigns, striking targets in North Vietnam. Specialised two-seat versions (EF-105F, F-105F and F-105G) performed the dangerous "Wild Weasel" suppression of enemy air defences mission, hunting surface-to-air missile sites. Of 382 F-105s lost in Southeast Asia, most fell to anti-aircraft fire, SAMs and MiG fighters. The type was replaced by the F-4 Phantom II and later the F-111.
