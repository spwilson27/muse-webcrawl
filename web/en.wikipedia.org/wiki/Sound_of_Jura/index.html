Sound of Jura

Sound of Jura
Scottish Gaelic: An Linne Dhura
Location: Argyll and Bute, Scotland
Coordinates: approx. 56.05° N, 5.7° W

The Sound of Jura (Scottish Gaelic: An Linne Dhura) is a strait on the west coast of Scotland, in Argyll and Bute. It separates the island of Jura, to the west, from the Kintyre peninsula and the mainland of Argyll to the east. The sound runs roughly north–south for about 27 miles (43 km), extending from Loch Linnhe and the Firth of Lorn in the north down toward the North Channel.

Geography

At its northern end the sound is joined by the Sound of Luing and opens toward the island of Scarba and the Gulf of Corryvreckan, famous for its tidal whirlpool, which lies between Jura and Scarba. The island of Shuna and a number of smaller islets lie within the sound. On the Jura shore stand the villages of Craighouse and the distillery settlements; the Paps of Jura dominate views across the water. Settlements on the mainland side include Crinan, where the Crinan Canal meets the sound, and Ardfern on Loch Craignish.

Navigation and ferries

The Sound of Jura forms part of the sheltered inshore route used by yachts and small vessels travelling the west coast. A passenger ferry crosses from Port Askaig on Islay to Feolin on Jura near its southern end, and vehicle ferries connect the mainland with Jura via Islay. The waters are used by fishing boats and by aquaculture, particularly salmon farming in the sea lochs opening off the sound.
