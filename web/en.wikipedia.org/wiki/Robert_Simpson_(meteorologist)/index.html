Robert Simpson (meteorologist)

BornNovember 19, 1912, Corpus Christi, Texas, U.S.
DiedDecember 18, 2014 (aged 102), Washington, D.C., U.S.
FieldsMeteorology, tropical cyclones
Known forSaffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale; directing the National Hurricane Center

Robert H. Simpson (November 19, 1912 – December 18, 2014) was an American meteorologist, hurricane specialist, and first director of the National Hurricane Research Project. He served as director of the National Hurricane Center (NHC) from 1968 to 1973 and, with engineer Herbert Saffir, developed the Saffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale used to classify Atlantic hurricanes by sustained wind speed.

Early life and education

Simpson was born in Corpus Christi, Texas. He earned his bachelor's degree from Southwestern University in 1933 and a master's degree in meteorology from Emory University in 1935; he later received a PhD from the University of Chicago in 1962.

Career

Simpson joined the U.S. Weather Bureau in 1940 and was posted to Panama, where he taught meteorology to airline pilots during World War II and observed tropical weather. After the war he served in Hawaii, where in 1949 he flew into a typhoon in the Pacific with the Air Force — an experience that shaped his research interest in the structure of tropical cyclones, particularly the eye and eyewall.

In 1955 he became the first director of the National Hurricane Research Project, established after devastating hurricanes struck the U.S. East Coast in 1954. He participated in Project Stormfury, the government's hurricane modification experiment, and in 1968 became director of the National Hurricane Center in Miami, a post he held until 1973. It was during that period that he and Herbert Saffir created the five-category scale linking hurricane winds to expected damage.

Later life

After leaving the NHC, Simpson co-founded Simpson Weather Associates in Charlottesville, Virginia, with his wife, Joanne Simpson (née Malkus), herself a pioneering meteorologist. He remained active in atmospheric science well into old age and died in Washington, D.C., in December 2014, aged 102.
