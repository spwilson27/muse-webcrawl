Radius of convergence

The disc of convergence of a power series in the complex plane.

In mathematics, the radius of convergence of a power series is the radius of the largest disk in which the series converges. It is either a non-negative real number or infinity. Inside the disk the series converges absolutely; outside it, it diverges; on the boundary, convergence may or may not occur.

Definition

For a power series Σ an(z − c)n, the radius of convergence R is given by the Cauchy–Hadamard theorem:

1/R = lim sup |an|1/n

When the limit of the ratio |an+1/an| exists, R is its reciprocal, a consequence of the ratio test.

Examples

The geometric series Σ zn has radius of convergence 1. The exponential series Σ zn/n! converges everywhere, so its radius of convergence is infinite. The series Σ n! zn converges only at its centre, giving radius 0.

Complex analysis

For a function analytic at a point, the radius of convergence of its Taylor series equals the distance from the centre to the nearest singularity in the complex plane. See also power series and Taylor series.
