Battle of Solway Moss

The Battle of Solway Moss was fought on 24 November 1542 near the Solway Firth on the Anglo-Scottish border, between English border forces and a Scottish invasion army. The much larger Scottish force was routed with little fighting; many Scottish nobles were captured. The defeat contributed to the death of James V of Scotland a few weeks later.

Background

After James V refused to meet Henry VIII at York and declined to break with the Catholic Church, English raids into Scotland were answered by a large Scottish incursion into Cumberland in November 1542.

The battle

Rivalries among the Scottish nobility fatally undermined command: James V had appointed his favourite Oliver Sinclair to lead, and many lords refused to obey him. Attacked by English cavalry under Thomas Wharton near Longtown, the Scots panicked in the boggy ground of Solway Moss, and many drowned in the Esk or surrendered. About twenty Scottish nobles and some 1,200 men were taken prisoner.

Aftermath

James V, already ill and distraught, died at Falkland Palace on 14 December 1542, days after the birth of his daughter Mary. The captured lords were released after agreeing to support Henry's plan for her marriage to Prince Edward — setting the stage for the Rough Wooing and the Battle of Pinkie.
