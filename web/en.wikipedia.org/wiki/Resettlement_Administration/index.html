Resettlement Administration

The Resettlement Administration (RA) was a United States federal agency created by President Franklin D. Roosevelt in 1935, during the New Deal, to relocate struggling urban and rural families to communities planned by the federal government. It was directed by Rexford Tugwell, a member of Roosevelt's "Brain Trust".

Programmes

The RA combined functions transferred from several agencies and pursued three main lines of work: resettling farm families from worn-out land onto better farms or into new planned towns; land-utilisation projects that retired submarginal farmland and converted it to parks, forests and wildlife refuges; and rehabilitation loans and debt adjustment for farmers. Its best-known projects were the three "Greenbelt" towns — Greenbelt, Maryland; Greenhills, Ohio; and Greendale, Wisconsin — and more than a hundred other resettlement communities.

Photography section

The RA's Information Division, under Roy Stryker, employed photographers including Dorothea Lange, Walker Evans, Gordon Parks and Arthur Rothstein to document rural poverty and the agency's work. Their photographs — among them Lange's Migrant Mother, taken under a related programme — became iconic images of the Great Depression.

End

Criticised by conservatives as collectivist and by some farmers' organisations as impractical, the RA was absorbed into the new Farm Security Administration in 1937, which continued many of its programmes under less controversial management.
