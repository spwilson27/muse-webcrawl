Inclusion (education)

Inclusion in education is the practice of educating children with disabilities and special educational needs in general education classrooms alongside their peers, with appropriate supports, rather than in separate special schools or classes. It is grounded in the principle that all children belong in the mainstream of school and community life.

Principles and practice

Inclusive education involves adapting curricula, teaching methods and school organisation so that diverse learners can participate meaningfully. Supports may include teaching assistants, differentiated instruction, assistive technology and individualised education plans. Full inclusion advocates place every child in a general classroom full-time; others support a continuum of placements depending on individual needs.

Policy and debate

International frameworks such as the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (2006) affirm a right to inclusive education, and laws in many countries require education in the least restrictive environment. Debate continues over resources, teacher training and whether inclusion benefits all students equally, but research generally supports positive social outcomes and academic outcomes comparable to segregated settings when support is adequate.
