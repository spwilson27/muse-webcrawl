Carpathian Ruthenia

Carpathian Ruthenia (also called Subcarpathian Ruthenia or Transcarpathia) is a historical region on the southwestern slopes of the Carpathian Mountains, in present-day western Ukraine (Zakarpattia Oblast). Its principal city is Uzhhorod.

History

For centuries the region formed part of the Kingdom of Hungary. After the First World War it was assigned to Czechoslovakia, where it constituted the province of Subcarpathian Rus'. In 1938–1939 it briefly declared independence as Carpatho-Ukraine before being annexed by Hungary. After the Second World War it was transferred to the Soviet Union and incorporated into the Ukrainian SSR.

People

The region is home to Ukrainians, Rusyns, Hungarians, Romanians, Slovaks and other groups, and is known for its wooden churches and mountain folk culture.
