Hugo Gernsback
Born: 16 August 1884, Luxembourg City, Luxembourg
Died: 19 August 1967 (aged 83), New York City, US
Occupation: Inventor, writer, magazine publisher
Known for: Founding Amazing Stories; coining "science fiction"; Hugo Awards named for him

Hugo Gernsback (born Hugo Gernsbacher; 16 August 1884 – 19 August 1967) was a Luxembourgish–American inventor, writer, editor, and magazine publisher, best known for publications including the first science fiction magazine, Amazing Stories, founded in 1926. He is widely considered the "Father of Science Fiction."

Career

Emigrating to the United States in 1904, Gernsback published popular electronics magazines such as Modern Electrics and Science and Invention, which mixed technical articles with speculative fiction. Amazing Stories gave the genre a dedicated market; Gernsback coined the term "science fiction" (after an earlier "scientifiction"). His own novel Ralph 124C 41+ (1911–12) anticipated technologies including radar and television.

Legacy

Though criticised for low author payments, Gernsback's magazines nurtured science fiction fandom and early careers of many writers. The annual Hugo Awards for science fiction achievement, first presented in 1953, are named in his honour.
