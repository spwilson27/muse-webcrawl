Saudi-led intervention in Yemen


Date: 26 March 2015 – present
Location: Yemen and southwestern Saudi Arabia
Lead belligerent: Saudi Arabia

The Saudi-led intervention in Yemen began in March 2015 when Saudi Arabia, leading a coalition of nine countries from West Asia and North Africa, launched a military intervention in Yemen following the Houthi takeover of the capital Sanaa and the flight of President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi. The intervention, codenamed Operation Decisive Storm (and later Operation Restoring Hope), sought to restore Hadi's internationally recognized government and counter what Saudi Arabia viewed as Iranian influence through the Houthi movement.

Course of the conflict

The coalition conducted an extensive bombing campaign and imposed a naval and air blockade. In July 2015, coalition and pro-government forces retook Aden, the southern port city, but the Houthis and allied forces retained control of Sanaa and much of the north. The war settled into a prolonged stalemate punctuated by major episodes such as the battle for the port of Hudaydah in 2018, which ended with the UN-brokered Stockholm Agreement.

Humanitarian crisis

The conflict has produced what the United Nations has repeatedly described as one of the world's worst humanitarian crises, with mass displacement, cholera outbreaks, and millions facing food insecurity; the UN estimated that the war had caused about 377,000 deaths by the end of 2021, the majority from indirect causes such as hunger and disease. A UN-mediated truce in 2022 significantly reduced fighting, and subsequent negotiations have continued amid the wider regional tensions.
