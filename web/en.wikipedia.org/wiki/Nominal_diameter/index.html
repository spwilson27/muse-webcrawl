Nominal diameter

The nominal diameter is a conventional designation of the size of a part — such as a pipe, tube, bolt or drill — that serves as a reference label rather than an exact measurement. The actual measured diameter may differ from the nominal value because of tolerances and the design of fittings.

Pipes

In piping, the metric system uses DN (diamètre nominal, e.g. DN50) and the imperial system uses NPS (nominal pipe size, e.g. 2-inch). Neither number equals the inside or outside diameter exactly for most sizes; for example, a 2-inch NPS pipe has an outside diameter of 2.375 inches.

Fasteners and other parts

Bolts are designated by nominal diameter (e.g. M10 for a metric screw whose major diameter is about 10 mm). Similar conventions apply to lumber, drills, bearings and tire sizes, where the nominal figure identifies the standard size class.
