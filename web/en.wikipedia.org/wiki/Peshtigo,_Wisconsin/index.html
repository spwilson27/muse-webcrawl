Peshtigo is a city in Marinette County, Wisconsin, United States. The population was 3,420 at the 2020 census. The city is located at the mouth of the Peshtigo River on Green Bay, an arm of Lake Michigan. Its name derives from an Ojibwe word for the river, often interpreted as "river of wild geese" or similar.

The Great Peshtigo Fire

Peshtigo is best known for the Great Peshtigo Fire of 8 October 1871, the deadliest wildfire in recorded history. On the same night as the Great Chicago Fire — which overshadowed it in the national press — a firestorm swept through the town and surrounding forests, destroying the entire community and burning roughly 1.2 million acres across northeastern Wisconsin and Michigan's Upper Peninsula. Estimates of the death toll range from 1,500 to 2,500 people, with the city of Peshtigo itself losing several hundred residents, a large share of its population of about 1,700.

Survivors famously escaped by immersing themselves in the Peshtigo River and in wells. The town was rebuilt, and today the Peshtigo Fire Museum preserves artifacts of the disaster, while a mass grave and memorial in the local cemetery commemorate unidentified victims.

Geography and economy

Peshtigo covers about 3.2 square miles. Historically a lumber town, its economy later relied on paper and wood products, including a large paper mill, as well as manufacturing and tourism tied to the nearby river and bay. The Peshtigo River is noted for fishing and whitewater rafting upstream.
