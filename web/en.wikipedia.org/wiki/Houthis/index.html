Overview

The Houthis, officially Ansar Allah, are a Zaydi Shia Islamist political and armed movement that emerged in northern Yemen in the 1990s, named for the al-Houthi family of religious scholars. Led first by Hussein Badreddin al-Houthi — killed in the first of six wars with the Yemeni government (2004–2010) — and since by his brother Abdul-Malik, the movement seized Sanaa in 2014, precipitating the Yemeni Civil War and a Saudi-led military intervention from 2015.

Conflict and the Red Sea

The Houthis control Yemen's northwest, including the capital and the Red Sea port of Hodeidah, and built a missile and drone arsenal with Iranian support (per UN and Western assessments; Iran acknowledges political support). From November 2023, declaring solidarity with Gaza, they attacked Red Sea shipping, sinking or damaging vessels and driving global carriers around the Cape of Good Hope, and drew US/UK airstrikes (2024–25). A 2022 UN truce largely froze the front lines inside Yemen without a final settlement; the movement's governance, repression of critics and the humanitarian catastrophe in territory it rules are documented by UN experts.
