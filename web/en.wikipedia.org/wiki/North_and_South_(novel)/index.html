North and South (novel)

Margaret Hale in Milton
AuthorElizabeth Gaskell
Published1854–1855 (serial); 1855 (book)
GenreSocial novel

North and South is a social novel by the English writer Elizabeth Gaskell, first published in serial form in Charles Dickens's magazine Household Words in 1854–55. It is regarded as one of the classic "Condition of England" novels exploring the conflicts of the Industrial Revolution.

Plot

Margaret Hale, the daughter of a clergyman, is uprooted from the gentle rural south of England when her father, troubled by religious doubt, leaves the Church and moves the family to the fictional northern manufacturing town of Milton (modelled on Manchester). There Margaret encounters the harsh realities of mill life and clashes repeatedly with John Thornton, a self-made cotton manufacturer and her father's pupil. Through the suffering of her friend Bessy Higgins, a mill worker dying of byssinosis, and a strike by the mill hands, Margaret comes to understand both the workers' grievances and the pressures on the masters; mutual prejudice gradually gives way to respect and love.

Themes

The novel contrasts the values of the agricultural South and industrial North, examines the ethics of capital and labour, trade unions and strikes, and gives its heroine an unusually independent voice in religious and economic debate. Gaskell drew on her own experience of Manchester, where her husband was a Unitarian minister.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted for television in 1975 and again by the BBC in 2004, the latter starring Daniela Denby-Ashe and Richard Armitage, which greatly increased the book's popularity.
