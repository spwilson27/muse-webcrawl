César Award for Best Actress

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The César Award for Best Actress (French: César de la meilleure actrice) is one of the César Awards, presented annually by the Académie des Arts et Techniques du Cinéma to recognise the outstanding performance by a French film actress in a leading role. The award has been presented since the first César ceremony in 1976.

History

The first recipient was Romy Schneider for L'important c'est d'aimer (1975). Isabelle Adjani holds the record with five wins in the category. Other multiple winners include Romy Schneider, Sabine Azéma, Nathalie Baye, Isabelle Huppert, and Catherine Deneuve, who has received the most nominations in the category.

Selection

Nominees are chosen by the members of the Academy from eligible French films released in the preceding year, with the winner chosen by a second round of voting. The ceremony is France's most prestigious film awards event, often compared to the Academy Awards in the United States.
