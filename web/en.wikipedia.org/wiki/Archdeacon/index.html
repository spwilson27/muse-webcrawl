An archdeacon is a senior Christian cleric who assists a diocesan bishop in administration and oversight of a subdivision of the diocese called an archdeaconry. Historically described as the "bishop's eye" (oculus episcopi), the archdeacon supervised clergy discipline, church property and visitations.

History

The office arose in the 4th century from the head deacon of a cathedral; by the Middle Ages archdeacons held substantial jurisdictional power, which later councils and reforms progressively curtailed in the Roman Catholic Church, where the office largely gave way to vicars general.

Modern roles

In the Church of England and other Anglican churches, archdeacons remain key administrators, addressed as "The Venerable". In Eastern Orthodoxy an archdeacon is a senior deacon, often attached to a bishop or patriarch. The title also survives in fiction, most famously the Venerable Bede's era and Anthony Trollope's Archdeacon Grantley.
