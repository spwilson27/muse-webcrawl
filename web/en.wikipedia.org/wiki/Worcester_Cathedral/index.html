Worcester Cathedral

Worcester Cathedral, formally the Cathedral Church of Christ and the Blessed Mary the Virgin, is an Anglican cathedral in Worcester, England, overlooking the River Severn. Built between 1084 and 1504, it contains the tomb of King John.

Location: Worcester, Worcestershire
Founded: 680; current building 1084–1504
Style: Norman to Perpendicular Gothic
Burials: King John, Prince Arthur

History and architecture

Wulfstan began the Norman crypt in 1084. The cathedral mixes every English Gothic style, with a famous chapter house and cloister. King John was buried before the high altar in 1216, as he wished; Arthur, Prince of Wales, also lies here. Edward Elgar, from Worcester, is closely associated with its musical tradition and the Three Choirs Festival.
