First Dynasty of Egypt

The First Dynasty of ancient Egypt (Dynasty I) covers the first sequence of Egyptian kings to rule over a unified Egypt. It immediately follows the unification of Upper and Lower Egypt, possibly by Narmer, and marks the beginning of the Early Dynastic Period (c. 3100–2686 BC). The capital during this period was Thinis, and later Memphis; the kings were buried in great mastaba tombs at Abydos and Saqqara.

History

Radiocarbon studies suggest the dynasty began around 3100 BC. Tradition credited Menes with unifying the Two Lands; modern scholarship generally equates him with Narmer or his successor Hor-Aha. The reign of Den, in the middle of the dynasty, is particularly well attested: the first use of the title "King of Upper and Lower Egypt", the first depiction of the king wearing the double crown, and elaborate royal tombs. Under the First Dynasty the institutions of the Egyptian state — administration, taxation, writing, royal iconography — took lasting form.

Burials and human sacrifice

The royal tombs at Umm el-Qa'ab in Abydos were surrounded by subsidiary burials of retainers, apparently sacrificed to serve the king in the afterlife — a practice that peaked under Djer, whose tomb had over 300 subsidiary burials, and was abandoned by the end of the dynasty.
