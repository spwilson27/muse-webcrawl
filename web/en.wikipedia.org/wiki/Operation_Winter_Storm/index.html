Operation Winter Storm

Operation Winter Storm
Part of the Battle of Stalingrad
Date: 12–23 December 1942
Location: Southwest of Stalingrad, USSR
Result: Soviet victory

Operation Winter Storm (German: Unternehmen Wintergewitter) was a German offensive in December 1942 during the Battle of Stalingrad of the Second World War. It was an attempt by the German Army Group Don, commanded by Field Marshal Erich von Manstein, to break through the Soviet encirclement and relieve the German Sixth Army trapped inside Stalingrad.

Background

On 23 November 1942, Soviet forces completed Operation Uranus, encircling some 250,000–300,000 Axis troops in the Stalingrad pocket. Hitler ordered the Sixth Army to hold its positions and be supplied by air, while Manstein assembled a relief force built around General Hermann Hoth's Fourth Panzer Army and the LVII Panzer Corps under Friedrich Kirchner.

The offensive

The attack began on 12 December 1942 from the Kotelnikovo area, roughly 120 kilometres southwest of Stalingrad. The panzer divisions initially made good progress against the Soviet 51st Army, driving toward the Aksay River and then the Myshkova River. By 19 December the spearheads stood within about 48 kilometres of the pocket's perimeter.

The relief effort was fatally weakened by the diversion of the 6th Panzer Division and by the launch of the Soviet Operation Little Saturn on 16 December, which threatened the Italian Eighth Army on the Don and forced Manstein to divert forces to protect his flank and rear.

Failure

Soviet counterattacks, including the commitment of the fresh 2nd Guards Army to the Myshkova line, halted the advance. On 23 December Hoth's forces were driven back, and the relief attempt was abandoned. The Sixth Army's commander, Friedrich Paulus, had not been authorised to attempt a breakout to meet the relief column, and the army surrendered at the end of January 1943.
