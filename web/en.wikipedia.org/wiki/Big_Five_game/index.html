Big Five game

The Big Five game animals are the five African species traditionally considered the most difficult and dangerous to hunt on foot: the lion, the African leopard, the African bush elephant, the rhinoceros (black and white), and the African buffalo. The term was coined by big-game hunters and is now widely used in safari tourism.

The five animals

Lion (Panthera leo), leopard (Panthera pardus), African bush elephant (Loxodonta africana), rhinoceros (Ceratotherium simum and Diceros bicornis), and African buffalo (Syncerus caffer). The buffalo is sometimes said to be the most dangerous of the five because of its size, aggression when wounded, and tendency to charge.

Tourism and conservation

"Big Five" safaris are a mainstay of wildlife tourism in southern and East Africa, and the animals feature on South African rand banknotes. All five face conservation pressures from habitat loss and poaching, particularly elephants and rhinos for ivory and horn. Photographic tourism has largely replaced hunting in most protected areas.
