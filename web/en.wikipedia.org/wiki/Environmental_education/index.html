Environmental education

Environmental education (EE) refers to organised efforts to teach how natural environments function, and particularly how human beings can manage behaviour and ecosystems to live sustainably. It is a multidisciplinary field integrating disciplines such as biology, chemistry, physics, ecology, earth science, atmospheric science, mathematics, and geography.

History

The roots of environmental education lie in the nature study movement of the late 19th and early 20th centuries and in conservation education of the Dust Bowl era. The modern field took shape in the 1960s and 1970s alongside the environmental movement: the 1975 Belgrade Charter, adopted at a UNESCO–UNEP workshop, set out a global framework for environmental education, and the 1977 Tbilisi Declaration formulated its goals and guiding principles. In the United States, the Environmental Education Act of 1970 and Earth Day (first celebrated in 1970) were milestones.

Goals and components

Following the Tbilisi Declaration, environmental education aims to develop in learners:

  • Awareness and sensitivity to the environment and environmental challenges
  • Knowledge and understanding of the environment and its problems
  • Attitudes of concern for the environment and motivation to improve or maintain environmental quality
  • Skills to identify and help resolve environmental challenges
  • Participation in activities that lead to the resolution of environmental challenges

EE is taught in formal settings (schools and universities), non-formal settings (museums, zoos, nature centres, and programmes such as Outward Bound), and through informal media. Related fields include outdoor education, experiential education, and education for sustainable development.
