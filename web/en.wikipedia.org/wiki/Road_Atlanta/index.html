Michelin Raceway Road Atlanta is a 2.540 mi (4.088 km) road course located just north of Braselton, Georgia, United States. The facility is used for a wide variety of racing events, including the Petit Le Mans, the AMA Superbike Championship, World Challenge, and sports car endurance racing, as well as comedy, corporate and driving-school events.

History

The track opened in 1970, carved out of the red-clay hills of Hall County, and quickly became a home of the SCCA's Can-Am and Formula 5000 series. In 1978 the track was sold, and it passed through several owners — and a period of decline — before entrepreneur Don Panoz purchased it in 1996. Panoz invested heavily, re-profiling the dangerous "Dip" at the end of the back straight and adding the Turn 10a/10b complex, making the circuit safer while preserving its fast, flowing character.

In 1998 Panoz inaugurated the Petit Le Mans, a 1,000-mile (or 10-hour) endurance race modelled on the 24 Hours of Le Mans, which became the flagship event of the American Le Mans Series and remains the season finale of the IMSA SportsCar Championship. In 2012 the facility was sold to NASCAR Holdings (via IMSA), and in 2019 it was renamed Michelin Raceway Road Atlanta under a naming-rights deal with Michelin.

Circuit

The 12-turn course is famed for its elevation changes and blind corners: the sweeping downhill Esses (Turns 2–5), the fast Turn 1 climb, and the plunge under the bridge at Turn 11 into the downhill Turn 12 onto the front straight. A shorter 1.77-mile configuration and a karting circuit also exist within the 750-acre facility.
