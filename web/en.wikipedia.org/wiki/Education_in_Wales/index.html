Education in Wales

Education in Wales is devolved to the Welsh Government and differs in several respects from education in England, most notably in the role of the Welsh language. Schooling is compulsory between the ages of 5 and 16, and the majority of pupils attend local authority maintained comprehensive schools.

Curriculum and language

The Curriculum for Wales, rolled out from 2022, replaced the previous national curriculum with six areas of learning and experience. Welsh is taught to all pupils until age 16, either as a first or second language, and a substantial minority of schools are Welsh-medium, teaching mainly through Welsh. Pupils take GCSEs and A-levels regulated in Wales by Qualifications Wales, with awarding largely through the WJEC.

History and higher education

The nineteenth century saw the spread of elementary schooling and the influential 1847 reports on education in Wales. The University of Wales was founded in 1893 as a federal university; today Wales's universities include Cardiff, Swansea, Bangor and Aberystwyth. Inspections of schools are carried out by Estyn.
