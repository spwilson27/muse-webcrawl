Unsolved mysteries

An unsolved mystery is an event, disappearance, crime or phenomenon for which no explanation has been established to general satisfaction, despite investigation. Such mysteries attract enduring public interest and have been a staple of journalism, books, documentaries and television, notably the long-running American series Unsolved Mysteries (1987–2010, revived 2020), which appealed to viewers for tips and is credited with helping to solve hundreds of cases it profiled.

Categories

Disappearances

Famous unsolved disappearances include the vanishing of aviator Amelia Earhart in 1937, the fate of the crew of the Mary Celeste in 1872, the disappearance of the Roanoke Colony in the 1580s, the missing crew of Malaysia Airlines Flight 370 in 2014, and the 1971 skyjacker known as D. B. Cooper, the only unsolved case of air piracy in United States history.

Crimes

Unsolved crimes that continue to fascinate include the Jack the Ripper murders of 1888, the Zodiac Killer case in California in the late 1960s, the Black Dahlia murder of 1947, and the 1996 murder of JonBenét Ramsey. Some have been solved decades later through DNA evidence, as with the identification of the Golden State Killer in 2018.

Phenomena and artifacts

Other mysteries concern unexplained phenomena or objects: the Voynich manuscript, an undeciphered illustrated codex; the Wow! signal of 1977; ball lightning; the disappearance of the crew of the Carroll A. Deering; and the "Bloop" sound recorded in the Pacific in 1997, later attributed to icequakes.

Study and skepticism

Investigators caution that "unsolved" often reflects missing evidence rather than genuinely anomalous causes; many celebrated mysteries have mundane explanations that evidence can no longer confirm. Cold case units and advances in forensic genetics and archival research periodically resolve cases long considered permanently unsolved.
