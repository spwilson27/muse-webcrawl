Port of Genoa

The Port of Genoa (Italian: Porto di Genova) is the largest seaport in Italy by cargo volume and one of the busiest ports on the Mediterranean Sea. Stretching for roughly 22 kilometres along the Ligurian coast west of the city centre of Genoa, it handles containers, bulk cargo, passengers, and shipbuilding and repair, and forms part of the Ports of Genoa authority together with Savona and Vado Ligure.

History

Genoa's maritime importance dates to the medieval Republic of Genoa, whose fleets dominated western Mediterranean trade. The old harbour, the Porto Antico, was redesigned for the 1992 Columbian celebrations by architect Renzo Piano, gaining the Aquarium of Genoa, one of Europe's largest aquariums, and the Bigo lift. Modern container operations are concentrated at terminals such as PSA Genova Pra' and the Sampierdarena basin.

Activity

The port handles over 60 million tonnes of cargo and more than two million TEUs in a typical year, alongside busy ferry and cruise traffic to Sardinia, Corsica, Sicily, and North Africa. It is the southern maritime gateway of the Rhine–Alpine European transport corridor serving industrial northern Italy and southern Europe.
