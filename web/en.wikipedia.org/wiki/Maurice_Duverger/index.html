Maurice Duverger

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Maurice Duverger (5 June 1917 – 16 December 2014) was a French jurist, political scientist and politician, a founder of modern political science in France. His Les Partis politiques (1951; English Political Parties, 1954) systematised the study of party organisation — cadre parties versus mass parties, the role of dues-paying membership and branches — and stated the proposition now called Duverger's law: that single-member plurality elections tend to produce two-party systems, while proportional representation encourages multipartyism.

Duverger founded the political science institute at Bordeaux, advised on the institutions of the Fifth Republic, and helped shape the semi-presidential model he himself theorised (a directly elected president with considerable powers alongside a prime minister responsible to parliament). He sat in the European Parliament (1989–94) as an independent on the Italian Communist lists. His long career bridged the jurist's and the sociologist's approaches to politics, and Political Parties remains a founding text of comparative politics.
