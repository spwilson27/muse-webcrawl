Preventive healthcare

Preventive healthcare, or prophylaxis, consists of measures taken for the purposes of disease prevention, as opposed to treating disease after it occurs. It includes vaccination, screening for cancers and other conditions, counselling on diet and tobacco use, and population-level interventions such as fluoridation of water and food fortification.

Levels of prevention

Primary prevention aims to stop disease before it starts (for example, immunisation and seat-belt use). Secondary prevention detects disease early through screening so treatment can begin sooner. Tertiary prevention reduces the impact of established disease, and quaternary prevention avoids unnecessary medical intervention itself.

Effectiveness

Many preventive services are cost-effective; some, such as childhood immunisation, save more than they cost. Others involve trade-offs between early detection and overdiagnosis, making evidence-based recommendations by bodies such as the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force important for practice.
