Captain Robert Laurence Nairac GC (31 August 1948 – 15 May 1977) was a British Army officer who was abducted and killed by the Provisional Irish Republican Army while on an undercover operation in South Armagh during the Troubles. He was posthumously awarded the George Cross for his conduct in captivity, in which he gave no information to his captors.

Life and career

Nairac was born in Mauritius, educated at Ampleforth College and Lincoln College, Oxford, where he read modern history and boxed for the university. Commissioned into the Grenadier Guards, he undertook tours of Northern Ireland, increasingly in plain-clothes intelligence roles connected with the Special Air Service and 14 Intelligence Company, operating in the republican "bandit country" of South Armagh.

Death

On the night of 14–15 May 1977 Nairac, posing as an IRA sympathiser named "Danny McErlean", visited the Three Steps Inn at Drumintee. He was recognised, abducted from the pub car park, taken across the border, interrogated, tortured and shot. His body was never recovered and he is counted among "the Disappeared". Several men were convicted in connection with the killing; the George Cross citation praised his "exceptional courage and devotion to duty in the face of a brutal interrogation".

Controversy

Nairac's name has featured in allegations of collusion surrounding other killings of the period, including the Miami Showband massacre and the Dublin and Monaghan bombings; official inquiries have examined but not substantiated several such claims, and his role remains a subject of dispute.
