The National Panhellenic Conference (NPC) is an umbrella organization for 26 international women's fraternities and sororities in North America. Founded in 1902, it is one of the largest organizations advocating for women's fraternal organizations, representing several million women worldwide across hundreds of college campuses in the United States and Canada.

History

The first inter-sorority conference met in Boston in 1902, convened by Alpha Phi to address problems of competitive rushing and pledging. The organization adopted the name National Panhellenic Conference the following year. Over the twentieth century the NPC developed the system of "unanimous agreements" binding on all member groups, governing recruitment practices, total membership limits, and relations between chapters on shared campuses.

Structure and activities

On each campus with NPC chapters, a College Panhellenic Association coordinates formal recruitment — the structured process, often called "rush", by which new members join. The NPC publishes the Manual of Information, supports leadership and educational programming, awards scholarships through member foundations, and advocates on issues affecting collegiate women and Greek life generally.

Members

Member organizations include Alpha Chi Omega, Alpha Delta Pi, Alpha Phi, Chi Omega, Delta Delta Delta, Delta Gamma, Gamma Phi Beta, Kappa Alpha Theta, Kappa Delta, Kappa Kappa Gamma, Pi Beta Phi, Sigma Kappa, and Zeta Tau Alpha, among others. The NPC is distinct from the National Pan-Hellenic Council (NPHC), which coordinates nine historically Black Greek-letter organizations.
