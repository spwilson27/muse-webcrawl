Firearm death rates in the United States by state

Firearm death rates in the United States vary substantially by state. This article presents statistics compiled from data published by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), including total firearm deaths as well as deaths by suicide and homicide, expressed per 100,000 population.

Historically, states in the South and Mountain West, such as Alaska, Mississippi, Louisiana, Alabama, and Wyoming, have recorded among the highest total firearm mortality rates, while states in the Northeast, such as Massachusetts, Rhode Island, New York, and New Jersey, along with Hawaii, have recorded among the lowest. The majority of firearm deaths in the United States are suicides, a pattern that holds in most states, though in some jurisdictions homicides form a larger share.

Methodology

Rates are typically age-adjusted and drawn from the CDC's WONDER database of death certificates, using ICD-10 codes for firearm-related underlying causes of death, including suicide (X72–X74), homicide (X93–X95), unintentional discharge (W32–W34), and legal intervention.
