The Arctic warbler (Phylloscopus borealis) is a widespread leaf warbler that breeds across northern Europe and the Palearctic into Alaska, and winters in Southeast Asia. It is a strongly migratory insectivorous songbird of birch and willow scrub in the taiga and tundra edge, and undertakes one of the longest migrations, relative to body size, of any Old World warbler — birds breeding in Alaska travel to winter quarters in Southeast Asia.

Description

Like most leaf warblers, it is greenish above and off-white below, with a prominent long whitish supercilium (eyebrow stripe), a single wing bar, and a relatively stout bill for the genus. It is best distinguished from similar species — the greenish warbler and Kamchatka leaf warbler, formerly treated as part of the same complex — by voice: the male sings a fast, high-pitched trill, and the call is a distinctive buzzy "dzrit". Molecular and vocal studies have split the former Arctic warbler complex into three species.

Distribution and behaviour

The breeding range spans from northern Scandinavia eastwards across Siberia to Alaska, in damp birch and conifer forest with dense undergrowth. The nest is built on the ground in a low shrub, and a clutch of five or six eggs is laid. The species feeds mainly on small insects and other invertebrates gleaned from foliage. Vagrants regularly reach western Europe in autumn and occasionally the western Aleutians and beyond.
