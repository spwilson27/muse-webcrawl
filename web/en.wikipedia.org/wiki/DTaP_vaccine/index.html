DTaP vaccine

The DTaP vaccine is a combination vaccine whose full name is diphtheria and tetanus toxoids and acellular pertussis vaccine. It protects children against three bacterial diseases: diphtheria, tetanus (lockjaw), and pertussis (whooping cough).

Use

In the United States, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) recommends that children receive five doses of DTaP, generally at ages 2 months, 4 months, 6 months, 15–18 months, and 4–6 years. A related vaccine, Tdap, with reduced amounts of diphtheria and pertussis antigens, is given to adolescents and adults as a booster.

History

DTaP replaced the older whole-cell DTP vaccine in many countries during the 1990s. The acellular pertussis component, containing purified antigens of Bordetella pertussis rather than whole killed cells, causes fewer local and febrile reactions, though immunity may wane sooner, prompting booster recommendations.

Side effects

Common reactions include soreness at the injection site, mild fever, and fussiness. Serious allergic reactions are very rare.
