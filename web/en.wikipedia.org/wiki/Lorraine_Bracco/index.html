Lorraine Bracco

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lorraine Bracco Born: 2 October 1954, Brooklyn, New York, U.S.
Occupation: Actress
Years active: 1979–present

Lorraine Bracco (born October 2, 1954) is an American actress known for her distinctively husky voice and her portrayals of strong-willed women. She received an Academy Award nomination for playing Karen Hill in Martin Scorsese's crime film Goodfellas (1990), and won widespread acclaim as psychiatrist Dr. Jennifer Melfi on the HBO drama series The Sopranos (1999–2007).

Early life

Bracco was born in the Bay Ridge neighbourhood of Brooklyn, New York, to a French mother and an Italian-American father, and grew up on Long Island. As a teenager she moved to France, where she worked as a model and as a radio disc jockey before beginning an acting career in French films.

Career

Her English-language breakthrough came with Ridley Scott's thriller Someone to Watch Over Me (1987). In Goodfellas (1990), her performance as the wife of mob associate Henry Hill earned her nominations for the Academy Award and the Golden Globe for Best Supporting Actress. During the 1990s she appeared in films including Medicine Man (1992), The Basketball Diaries (1995) and Hackers (1995).

From 1999 she played Dr. Jennifer Melfi, the psychiatrist treating mob boss Tony Soprano, throughout the six seasons of The Sopranos, receiving four Emmy Award nominations. Later television work included a regular role as Angela Rizzoli on the crime series Rizzoli & Isles (2010–2016). Bracco also owns a line of wines and has written a memoir, To the Fullest (2015), about her health and weight loss.
