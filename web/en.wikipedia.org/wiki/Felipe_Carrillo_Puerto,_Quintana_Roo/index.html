Felipe Carrillo Puerto is a municipality and its municipal seat (a city of the same name) in the Mexican state of Quintana Roo, in the central part of the Yucatán Peninsula. The municipality covers roughly 13,800 km², one of the largest in the state, and its population is predominantly Maya; Yucatec Maya is widely spoken.

History

The town was founded in 1850 during the Caste War of Yucatán as Chan Santa Cruz, capital of the independent Maya state of Chan Santa Cruz, whose Cruzob inhabitants worshipped the "Speaking Cross". It remained the religious and military centre of Maya resistance until Mexican federal troops occupied it in 1901. Under President Lázaro Cárdenas in the 1930s the settlement was renamed Felipe Carrillo Puerto, honouring the assassinated socialist governor of Yucatán, Felipe Carrillo Puerto.

Geography and economy

The municipality lies inland along federal highway 307 and the road to Mérida, between the Caribbean coast and the state of Yucatán. To the east it adjoins the Sian Ka'an Biosphere Reserve. Agriculture, forestry, beekeeping and chicle production have historically driven the economy, with tourism growing as Maya cultural sites and the nearby coast develop.
