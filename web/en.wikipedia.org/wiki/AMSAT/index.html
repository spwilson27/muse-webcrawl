AMSAT is a name for amateur radio satellite organizations worldwide, headed by the Radio Amateur Satellite Corporation (AMSAT-NA), founded in 1969 in the United States to foster amateur radio participation in space research and communication. AMSAT groups design, build, and arrange launches for satellites carrying amateur transponders, which are designated in the OSCAR (Orbiting Satellite Carrying Amateur Radio) series once in orbit.

History

The first amateur satellite, OSCAR 1, was built by California radio amateurs and launched in 1961, only four years after Sputnik. AMSAT was formed in 1969 to continue the work on a coordinated basis. Its satellites have ranged from simple beacons and "bent pipe" transponders to sophisticated spacecraft such as AMSAT-OSCAR 10 and OSCAR 13 in high elliptical orbits, the Phase 3 program, and later the Fox series of CubeSats (AO-85/Fox-1A and successors) launched from 2015 onward.

Activities

AMSAT supports communications for licensed amateurs worldwide, educational outreach, and experiments including digital store-and-forward messaging, APRS, and telemetry. National AMSAT organizations exist in the United Kingdom (AMSAT-UK), Germany (AMSAT-DL), and many other countries. AMSAT also coordinates with ARISS, which operates the amateur station aboard the International Space Station.
