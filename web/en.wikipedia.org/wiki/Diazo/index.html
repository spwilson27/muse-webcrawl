Diazo

Diazo compounds are a class of organic compounds characterised by a diazo group (=N2+) bonded to a carbon atom, with the general formula R2C=N2. The best-known example is diazomethane, CH2N2; ethyl diazoacetate is a widely used, more manageable member of the family.

Reactivity

Diazo compounds lose nitrogen readily — thermally, photochemically or under metal catalysis — to generate carbenes or metal carbenoids, enabling cyclopropanation, X–H insertion and ylide chemistry. α-Diazocarbonyl compounds undergo the Wolff rearrangement. Diazonium salts, a related class, underpin the azo dye industry through azo coupling.

Safety

Many diazo compounds are toxic and potentially explosive, particularly low-molecular-weight examples such as diazomethane, which is handled in dilute ethereal solution with specialised glassware.
