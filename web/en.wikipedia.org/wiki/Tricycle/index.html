Tricycle

A tricycle, or trike, is a three-wheeled vehicle. Human-powered tricycles come in two main layouts: delta, with two wheels at the back, and tadpole, with two wheels at the front. Tricycles are used by children learning to pedal, by adults who need stability, by cargo and passenger carriers in many countries, and in recumbent designs for touring and racing.

History

Three-wheelers appeared in the late 17th century with hand-cranked invalid carriages such as Stephan Farffler's vehicle of 1655. Pedal tricycles flourished in the 1870s–1880s, before the safety bicycle, because they were stable and suited the clothing and roads of the day; James Starley's Coventry Rotary and Salvo models were influential. In the 20th century the tricycle survived mainly as a children's toy and as motorised three-wheelers and utility vehicles.

Modern uses

Motorised tricycles — auto rickshaws such as the tuk-tuk — are essential transport across Asia. Cycle rickshaws, cargo trikes, vending trikes, and ice-cream carts serve cities worldwide. Recumbent tadpole trikes are popular for comfort and aerodynamics, and three-wheeled landing gear arrangements take their name from the tricycle layout. Advantages over bicycles include stability at rest and greater load capacity; disadvantages include width, weight, and a tendency to lift a wheel in hard cornering.
