Fort Zeelandia (Chinese: 熱蘭遮城) was a fortress built over ten years from 1624 to 1634 by the Dutch East India Company (VOC) on a sandy islet off the coast of Tainan, in present-day Anping District, Taiwan. It was the headquarters and principal stronghold of Dutch Formosa, anchoring the Dutch trade entrepôt that linked China, Japan and Southeast Asia.

History

After failing to secure a base at Macau and occupying the Penghu islands, the Dutch settled at Tayouan (modern Anping) in 1624 and built Zeelandia — a substantial brick fortress with walls made using mortar of glutinous rice, sugar, sand and shell lime — alongside the town of Tayouan and, across the bay, Fort Provintia. From here the VOC governed western Taiwan, taxing the deer-hide and sugar trades and conducting missionary work among the Siraya and other indigenous peoples.

Siege and aftermath

In April 1661 the Ming loyalist Koxinga landed with some 25,000 men and besieged the fort. After a nine-month siege, Governor Frederick Coyett surrendered Zeelandia on 1 February 1662, ending 38 years of Dutch colonial rule; the episode is dramatised in the only surviving first-hand Dutch account, Coyett's 't Verwaerloosde Formosa. Koxinga renamed the fort Anping. Under Qing and Japanese rule most of the fortress was demolished or rebuilt — bricks were reused for coastal defences — and the site today preserves one original Dutch wall, a later customs residence and the Fort Zeelandia Museum.
