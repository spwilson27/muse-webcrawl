Justin Kan (born July 16, 1983) is an American internet entrepreneur and investor. He is the co-founder of the live video platforms Justin.tv and Twitch, as well as the mobile social video application Socialcam. He was formerly a partner at venture capital firm Y Combinator. His website Justin.tv launched in 2007 and pioneered live streaming with Kan broadcasting his own life continuously via a wearable camera, an experiment that coined the term "lifecasting".

Early life and education

Kan grew up in Seattle and graduated from Yale University in 2005 with degrees in physics and philosophy. At Yale he met Emmett Shear, with whom he later co-founded Twitch. Kan's first startup, the online calendar application Kiko, was backed by Y Combinator and later sold on eBay after Google Calendar's launch undercut it.

Career

Justin.tv and Twitch

In 2007 Kan, Shear, Michael Seibel and Kyle Vogt launched Justin.tv, initially a 24/7 live broadcast of Kan's life, which then opened as a platform for anyone to stream video channels. Its gaming section was spun out as Twitch in 2011 and grew rapidly; Amazon acquired Twitch in 2014 for approximately $970 million.

Later ventures

Kan's subsequent ventures include the legal services company Atrium, which he co-founded in 2017 and shut down in 2020, and the gaming NFT marketplace Fractal, launched in 2021. He has been an active angel investor and startup adviser, and has also co-founded the venture firm Goat Capital.
