Saarbrücken

Saarbrücken (German pronunciation: [zaːɐ̯ˈbʁʏkn̩]; French: Sarrebruck) is the capital and largest city of the German state of Saarland. It has a population of around 180,000 and lies on the river Saar, directly on the border with France, about 70 kilometres south of Trier and west of Kaiserslautern.

The city is the administrative, commercial, and cultural centre of the Saarland and home to Saarland University, known internationally for computer science research institutes including the Max Planck Institute for Informatics and the German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence (DFKI).

History

Saarbrücken developed around a castle first documented in 999. The town's history was shaped by coal mining and steelmaking in the Saar basin and by its contested position between France and Germany: the Saar Territory was administered by the League of Nations after the First World War, returned to Germany by plebiscite in 1935, and after the Second World War became the French-protected Saar Protectorate until a 1955 referendum led to its accession to the Federal Republic of Germany in 1957. The old town (Alt-Saarbrücken) on the left bank and St. Johann on the right bank were united with Burbach in 1909.

Sights

Landmarks include the baroque Ludwigskirche (Ludwig's Church), among Germany's finest Protestant baroque churches, the Saarbrücken Castle, the Old Bridge (Alte Brücke) over the Saar, and the Basilica of St. John. The city hosts the Max Ophüls Prize film festival for young German-language cinema.
