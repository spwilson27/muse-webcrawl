Acrylic acid

Acrylic acid (IUPAC: propenoic acid) is an organic compound with the formula CH2=CHCOOH. It is the simplest unsaturated carboxylic acid, consisting of a vinyl group attached directly to a carboxylic acid terminus. It is a colourless, corrosive liquid with a distinctive acrid odour, miscible with water, alcohols and ethers.

Production

Acrylic acid is produced industrially mainly by the two-step oxidation of propylene: propylene is oxidised to acrolein, which is then further oxidised to acrylic acid over mixed-metal oxide catalysts. Global production runs to several million tonnes per year.

Uses

Most acrylic acid is converted to its esters — methyl, ethyl, butyl and 2-ethylhexyl acrylates — used in paints, adhesives, textiles and plastics. The acid itself is polymerised to polyacrylic acid and cross-linked superabsorbent polymers used in hygiene products, and to carbomers used as thickeners in pharmaceuticals and cosmetics.
