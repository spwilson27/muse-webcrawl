Astrophotography

Astrophotography, also known as astronomical imaging, is the photography or imaging of astronomical objects, celestial events, or areas of the night sky. The first photograph of an astronomical object (the Moon) was taken in 1840, but it was not until the late 19th century that advances in photographic technology allowed for detailed stellar photography.

Besides being able to record the details of extended objects such as the Moon, Sun, and planets, modern astrophotography has the ability to image objects invisible to the human eye such as dim stars, nebulae, and galaxies. This is done by long time-exposure since both film and digital cameras can accumulate and sum photons over long periods of time or using specialized astronomical cameras.

History

John William Draper took the first known photograph of the Moon in 1840. The first photograph of a star, Vega, was made at Harvard College Observatory in 1850, and Henry Draper photographed the Orion Nebula in 1880. Astrophotography transformed professional astronomy in the 20th century, and photographic plates were eventually supplanted by CCD sensors in the 1980s and 1990s.

Methods

Amateur astrophotography ranges from night-scape photography with a fixed camera and wide-angle lens, through lunar and planetary imaging with webcams and high-frame-rate cameras, to deep-sky imaging with cooled CCD or CMOS cameras on equatorial mounts that track the rotation of the Earth. Images are commonly improved by stacking many exposures and by calibration with dark, bias, and flat frames.
