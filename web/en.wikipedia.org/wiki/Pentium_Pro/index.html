Pentium Pro

Pentium Pro

Launched: November 1, 1995

Manufacturer: Intel

Clock rates: 150–200 MHz

Architecture: P6 microarchitecture, x86

Successor line: Pentium II

The Pentium Pro is a sixth-generation x86 microprocessor developed and manufactured by Intel and introduced on November 1, 1995. It implemented the P6 microarchitecture, the foundation of Intel's mainstream processors for the following decade and a half.

Design

The Pentium Pro was a major departure from the Pentium (P5). It introduced out-of-order execution in an x86 consumer part: internally it translated x86 instructions into micro-operations and executed them dynamically, using register renaming and speculative execution. Its on-package L2 cache — the cache die sat beside the CPU die in the same ceramic package — ran at full processor speed, a costly but fast arrangement. Clock speeds ranged from 150 to 200 MHz, with L2 cache sizes of 256 KB, 512 KB or 1 MB.

Market and legacy

Optimised for 32-bit code, the Pentium Pro excelled in servers and workstations running Windows NT or Unix, but performed comparatively poorly on the 16-bit-heavy Windows 95 consumer software of the day, and its packaging made it expensive. It was therefore positioned mainly for high-end systems. Its microarchitecture evolved into the Pentium II, Pentium III and, through the Pentium M, the Core line of processors.
