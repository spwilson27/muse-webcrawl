Attic orators

The Attic orators were the ten great Athenian speechwriters and public speakers of the 5th and 4th centuries BCE selected by Hellenistic scholars of Alexandria for the "Canon of the Ten": Antiphon, Andocides, Lysias, Isocrates, Isaeus, Demosthenes, Aeschines, Lycurgus, Hyperides, and Dinarchus.

Significance

Their speeches — forensic (courtroom), deliberative (assembly) and epideictic (ceremonial) — are central sources for Athenian law, politics and society, and defined the Attic Greek prose style studied for centuries. Many speeches were written by logographers for clients to deliver. Demosthenes is generally ranked greatest; Lysias is prized for plain, characterful style; Isocrates ran an influential school of rhetoric and wrote long pamphlet-speeches.
