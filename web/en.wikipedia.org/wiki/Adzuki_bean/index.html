Adzuki bean

Scientific nameVigna angularis
OriginEast Asia
Other namesAzuki bean, aduki bean, red mung bean
UsesRed bean paste, soups, sweets

The adzuki bean (Vigna angularis), also called azuki or aduki bean, is an annual vine widely cultivated throughout East Asia for its small (approximately 5 mm) beans, which are most often red but also occur in white, black, grey, and mottled varieties. It is one of the most important legume crops in Japan, China, and Korea.

Cultivation and history

The adzuki bean was domesticated in East Asia thousands of years ago, with evidence of cultivation in China and Japan dating to the Neolithic and Jōmon periods. Japan is a major centre of varietal development, and the bean is deeply embedded in its food culture; major production also occurs in China, Taiwan, and South Korea.

Culinary use

Adzuki beans are most famous as the basis of sweet red bean paste (Japanese anko, Chinese hóngdòushā, Korean pat), used to fill mochi, dorayaki, buns, and many other confections. They are also eaten as whole beans in soups, cooked with rice (Japanese sekihan, served on celebratory occasions), sprouted, or made into drinks and ice cream. Like other legumes, they are a good source of protein, fiber, iron, and folate.
