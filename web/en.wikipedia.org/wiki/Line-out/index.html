Line-out

A line-out or lineout is a means by which play is restarted in rugby union after the ball has gone into touch (the area beyond the touch line at the side of the field). When the ball goes into touch, the side that did not touch it last is usually awarded the throw-in, though this is reversed in some situations, such as when a team kicks the ball directly into touch from outside its own 22-metre line under the "50:22" and related laws.

Procedure

The two sets of forwards form two parallel lines, perpendicular to the touch line, between the 5-metre and 15-metre lines and at least one metre apart. A player from the team awarded the throw stands in touch and throws the ball straight down the middle of the gap between the lines. Players from both sides may be lifted by teammates to compete for the ball in the air.

The number of players in a line-out is determined by the team throwing in, and the defending team may not field more players than the attacking team. At least two players from each side must take part. The ball must travel at least five metres before it can be played.

Strategy

The line-out is a crucial set piece: winning clean possession provides a platform for attack, often via a driving maul formed immediately after the catch. Teams use coded calls to signal the intended target jumper, and lifting pods of two players raise the jumper to maximise height. Defending teams may contest the throw or, if they cannot win the ball, organise to stop the ensuing maul.

Offences

Common offences include throwing the ball in crookedly (not straight), jumping early, barging or obstructing opponents, and closing the gap between the lines before the throw. These are penalised by a free kick or, for repeated or deliberate offences, a penalty kick.
