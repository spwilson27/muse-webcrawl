Hemimorphite

CategorySorosilicate mineral
FormulaZn4(Si2O7)(OH)2·H2O
Crystal systemOrthorhombic
ColourColourless, white, blue, green, grey, brown
Mohs hardness4.5–5
Specific gravity3.4–3.5

Hemimorphite is the chemical compound Zn4(Si2O7)(OH)2·H2O, a component of calamine. It is a sorosilicate mineral which has been historically mined from the upper parts of zinc and lead ores, chiefly associated with smithsonite, ZnCO3. They were assumed to be the same mineral and both were classed under the same name of calamine. In the second half of the 18th century it was discovered that these two different minerals were both present in calamine. They closely resemble each other.

The silicate was the rarer of the two, and was named hemimorphite, because of the hemimorphic development of its crystals. This unusual form, which is typical of only a few minerals, means that the crystals are terminated by dissimilar faces. Hemimorphite most commonly forms crystalline crusts and layers, also massive, granular, rounded and reniform aggregates, concentrically striated, or finely needle-shaped, fibrous or stalactitic, and rarely fan-shaped clusters of crystals.

Occurrence

Some occurrences are in zinc ore beds where the mineral is intimately associated with smithsonite and other secondary zinc minerals in the oxidized zone of ore deposits. Notable localities include the zinc-lead mines of Mapimí and Santa Eulalia in Mexico, which produce striking sky-blue botryoidal specimens; Vieille Montagne in Belgium; Tarnowskie Góry in Silesia, Poland; and localities in the United States including Missouri, Montana, and Arizona.

In China, blue hemimorphite from Wenshan in Yunnan Province has been sold as a gemstone and ornamental material. Hemimorphite is strongly pyroelectric: it develops electric charges when heated or cooled, a property linked to its polar, hemimorphic crystal structure.

Associated minerals

Hemimorphite occurs in association with other zinc minerals in the oxidized zones of sphalerite-bearing ore bodies. Common associated minerals include smithsonite, cerussite, anglesite, hydrozincite, aurichalcite, and willemite, as well as iron and manganese oxides.

Uses

Hemimorphite is a minor ore of zinc when found in sufficient quantity. Attractively coloured specimens, especially the blue and green botryoidal crusts from Mexico and China, are cut as cabochons and used in jewellery and carvings, though its moderate hardness (4.5–5) limits its durability.
