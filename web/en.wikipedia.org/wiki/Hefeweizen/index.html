Hefeweizen

Hefeweizen (German: "yeast wheat") is a traditional Bavarian style of wheat beer (Weißbier) in which a significant proportion of the grain bill is malted wheat, and which is served unfiltered, with the yeast kept in suspension. The suspended yeast gives the beer its characteristic cloudy appearance and contributes flavours commonly described as banana and clove, produced by the special top-fermenting yeast strains used. A filtered version, in which the yeast is removed, is known as Kristallweizen.

Characteristics

By German tradition, a wheat beer must contain at least 50% malted wheat, the remainder typically being pilsner or Vienna barley malt. Hefeweizen is usually pale straw to deep gold in colour, with alcohol content around 4.9–5.6% ABV, low hop bitterness, and high carbonation. The ester isoamyl acetate gives the banana note, while the phenol 4-vinyl guaiacol gives the clove-like aroma; brewers adjust fermentation temperature and yeast pitch rate to balance the two. The beer is traditionally served in a tall, curving vase-shaped glass that accommodates its large, long-lasting foam head.

Serving

Hefeweizen is poured with the bottle swirled near the end to rouse the yeast sediment. Serving the beer with a slice of lemon is common in some regions but is considered by many brewers in Bavaria to detract from the foam and flavour, and is often regarded as a North American custom.
