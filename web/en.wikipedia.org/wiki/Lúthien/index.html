Lúthien Tinúviel is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, appearing principally in The Silmarillion and in the standalone volume Beren and Lúthien (2017). An Elven princess of Doriath, daughter of King Thingol and the Maia Melian, she is described as the most beautiful of all the Children of Ilúvatar.

The tale of Beren and Lúthien

Lúthien fell in love with the mortal Man Beren. Her father, unwilling to permit the union, demanded as bride-price one of the Silmarils from the crown of Morgoth. On the quest, Lúthien's enchantments rescued Beren repeatedly: she escaped from Doriath, overcame Sauron at Tol-in-Gaurhoth with the hound Huan, and in Angband sang Morgoth and his court to sleep so Beren could cut a Silmaril from the crown. After Beren's death she pleaded before Mandos, whose pity moved him: Lúthien chose mortality, and the two were restored to life together, becoming the ancestors of the Half-elven line including Elrond and Aragorn.

Significance

Tolkien regarded the tale — among the earliest he wrote (first version 1917) — as central to his mythology, and identified Lúthien with his wife Edith; "Lúthien" is inscribed on her gravestone, and "Beren" on his.
