Joan Beverly Kroc (née Mansfield; August 27, 1928 – October 12, 2003) was an American philanthropist and the third wife of Ray Kroc, founder of the McDonald's restaurant empire. Inheriting the bulk of Kroc's fortune on his death in 1984, she became one of the most generous — and famously spontaneous — philanthropists in the United States.

Philanthropy

Joan Kroc's giving ranged from disaster relief to peace advocacy. She founded Operation Cork, an alcohol-awareness program, after her own experience with alcoholism, and funded the Betty Ford Center's early work. She gave $7 million to flood relief in Grand Forks, North Dakota in 1997, largely anonymously. She endowed institutes for international peace studies at the University of Notre Dame (the Kroc Institute, 1986) and the University of San Diego (the Joan B. Kroc Institute for Peace and Justice).

Her largest gifts came at her death in 2003 from a brain tumor: approximately $1.6 billion to the Salvation Army — the largest single charitable gift in American history at the time — to build Kroc Centers for recreation and education in underserved communities, and $225 million to National Public Radio.

Baseball

From her husband she also inherited the San Diego Padres baseball team, owning the club from 1984 until selling it in 1990. She attempted to donate the team to the city of San Diego, but Major League Baseball rules prevented it.
