Per capita income

Per capita income (PCI) is a measure of the average income earned per person in a given area — such as a country, region, or city — in a specified year. It is calculated by dividing the total income of the area by its total population.

Per capita income is widely used as an indicator of the standard of living and economic well-being of a population, and figures such as GDP per capita are published by institutions including the World Bank and the International Monetary Fund. Comparisons between countries are often made using purchasing power parity (PPP) adjustments to account for differences in the cost of living.

Critics note that as a simple average, per capita income says nothing about the distribution of income: a country with a few very wealthy individuals and widespread poverty can show the same figure as a more equal society. For this reason it is often considered alongside measures such as median income, poverty rates, and inequality indices like the Gini coefficient.
