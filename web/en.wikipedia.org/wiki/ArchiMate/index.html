ArchiMate is an open and independent enterprise architecture modelling language maintained by The Open Group. It provides a graphical notation for describing, analysing, and visualising the relationships among business domains, processes, applications, and technology in an unambiguous way, and is designed to complement the TOGAF framework.

History

ArchiMate originated in the Netherlands in 2002–2004, developed by a consortium led by the Telematica Instituut (now Novay) together with Dutch government and industry partners. Version 1.0 was published by The Open Group in 2009 after the language was transferred to it. Version 2.0 (2012) added motivation and implementation-and-migration extensions; version 3.0 (2016) restructured the metamodel, added physical elements and a strategy layer; version 3.1 followed in 2019 and version 3.2 in 2022.

Language structure

The core framework classifies elements by layer and aspect. The principal layers are Business, Application, and Technology, later extended with Strategy, Physical, and Implementation and Migration elements, plus a Motivation aspect. Aspects distinguish active structure (who or what performs behaviour), behaviour, passive structure (information and physical objects), and motivation (stakeholders, goals, principles, and requirements).

Elements are connected by a small set of relationships — including composition, aggregation, assignment, realisation, serving, access, influence, triggering, and flow — each with a defined notation, giving ArchiMate models a uniform, layered "service-oriented" style.

Tooling and exchange

ArchiMate models can be exchanged between tools using the ArchiMate Model Exchange File Format, an XML-based standard. The best-known open-source editor is Archi; commercial enterprise architecture tools widely support the language as well.
