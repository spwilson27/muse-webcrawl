Kiyomizu-dera

Kiyomizu-dera Japanese: 清水寺 ("Pure Water Temple")
Location: Higashiyama, Kyoto, Japan
Founded: 778 (traditionally)
Present main hall: 1633
School: Kita-Hossō Buddhism
Designation: UNESCO World Heritage Site (Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto)

Kiyomizu-dera (清水寺, "Pure Water Monastery") is a Buddhist temple in the Higashiyama district of eastern Kyoto, Japan. Founded in the early Heian period — tradition dates it to 778 — it takes its name from the pure water of the Otowa Falls on the site, and is dedicated to Kannon, the bodhisattva of compassion. It is one of the Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto UNESCO World Heritage Sites and one of Japan's most visited temples.

Main hall and the stage

The temple's main hall, rebuilt in 1633 under Tokugawa Iemitsu, is famed for its vast wooden stage, built onto the steep hillside and supported by 168 massive zelkova pillars — assembled with traditional joinery without a single nail. The stage, 13 metres above the slope below, gave rise to the Japanese expression "to jump off the stage at Kiyomizu," meaning to take a decisive plunge. In the Edo period hundreds of pilgrims survived jumping from the stage in the belief that a surviving wish would be granted; the practice has been prohibited since 1872.

Site

The grounds include the three-storey Koyasu pagoda, the Zuigudō hall, the Jishu Shrine to the god of love and matchmaking with its two "love stones," and the Otowa Falls, whose three streams are drunk for health, longevity and success in studies. The temple draws especially large crowds during the cherry-blossom season and in autumn, when the maples below the stage turn red and are illuminated at night.
