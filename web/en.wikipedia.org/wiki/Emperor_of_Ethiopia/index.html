Emperor of Ethiopia

The Emperor of Ethiopia (Ge'ez: ንጉሠ ነገሥት, nəgusä nägäst, "King of Kings") was the hereditary ruler of the Ethiopian Empire, from the restoration of the Solomonic dynasty around 1270 until the abolition of the monarchy in 1975. At times, around the era of the Zemene Mesafint (Era of the Princes), the emperor was a figurehead, while real power was held by regional warlords.

History

The Solomonic dynasty claimed descent from King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba through their son Menelik I, a claim elaborated in the medieval epic Kebra Nagast. Yekuno Amlak founded the restored Solomonic line about 1270, overthrowing the Zagwe dynasty. Under emperors such as Amda Seyon I and Zara Yaqob, the empire expanded and consolidated across the Ethiopian highlands.

In the 19th century, Tewodros II reunified the empire after the Zemene Mesafint. His successors — Yohannes IV, Menelik II, and Haile Selassie — presided over Ethiopia's survival as one of the few African states to resist European colonisation, most famously at the Battle of Adwa in 1896. Ethiopia was occupied by Fascist Italy from 1936 to 1941, during which Haile Selassie lived in exile before being restored.

Abolition

Haile Selassie, the last reigning emperor, was deposed by the Derg military committee in September 1974, and the monarchy was formally abolished in 1975. The imperial family and its supporters continue to regard the head of the house as emperor in pretence.
