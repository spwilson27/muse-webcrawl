The Coma Cluster (Abell 1656) is a large cluster of galaxies that contains over 1,000 identified galaxies. Along with the Leo Cluster (Abell 1367), it is one of the two major clusters comprising the Coma Supercluster. It is located in and takes its name from the constellation Coma Berenices.

Properties

The cluster's mean distance from Earth is about 99 megaparsecs (321 million light-years). Its ten brightest spiral galaxies lie in a region roughly 6 million light-years across, while the whole cluster spans considerably more. The central region is dominated by two supergiant elliptical galaxies, NGC 4874 and NGC 4889. Most of the galaxies are ellipticals and dwarf spheroidals, and the space between them is filled with hot X-ray-emitting plasma, the intracluster medium.

Dark matter

In 1933 the Swiss astronomer Fritz Zwicky studied the velocities of galaxies in the Coma Cluster and found they moved far too fast to be held together by the gravity of the visible matter alone. This was among the first evidence for what is now called dark matter, though the result was not widely accepted for decades.
