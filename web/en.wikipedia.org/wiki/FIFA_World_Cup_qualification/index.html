FIFA World Cup qualification

FIFA World Cup qualification is the process by which national teams affiliated to FIFA earn places at the FIFA World Cup finals. Qualification tournaments are organised by each of the six continental confederations — AFC (Asia), CAF (Africa), Concacaf (North and Central America), CONMEBOL (South America), OFC (Oceania) and UEFA (Europe) — with intercontinental play-offs deciding the final berths.

History

The inaugural 1930 World Cup was by invitation only. For 1934, teams had to qualify for the first time, and every subsequent tournament has featured a qualifying competition. The host nation qualifies automatically. As the finals expanded — to 16 teams in 1982, 24 in 1986, 32 in 1998 and 48 from 2026 — the number of qualifying places grew accordingly.

Format

Formats differ by confederation, ranging from CONMEBOL's single round-robin league of ten teams to UEFA's group stage followed by play-offs, and multi-round systems with preliminary rounds for lower-ranked teams in Asia, Africa, Oceania and Concacaf. Around 200 national teams enter each cycle, making qualification the largest sporting qualifying competition in the world.

Allocation of slots

For the expanded 48-team 2026 tournament, slots were allocated as follows: UEFA 16, CAF 9, AFC 8, CONMEBOL 6, Concacaf 3 (plus three hosts), OFC 1, and two places via intercontinental play-offs.
