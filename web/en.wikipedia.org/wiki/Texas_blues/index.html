Texas blues
Stylistic originsBlues, country blues, jazz, swing
Cultural origins1920s, Texas, United States
Typical instrumentsElectric guitar, bass, drums, harmonica

Texas blues is a style of blues music that developed in Texas in the early 20th century. Compared with Delta blues, Texas blues tends to be more swing- and jazz-influenced, with a strong emphasis on guitar — first acoustic, and later emphatic, amplified electric lead guitar.

Electric guitar is central to the Texas blues sound

History

The style's recorded history begins with Blind Lemon Jefferson of Dallas, the first great male country-blues recording star of the 1920s. In the 1930s and 1940s T-Bone Walker pioneered the electric blues guitar style in Dallas and Houston, while Lightnin' Hopkins developed a looser solo country-blues idiom. Freddie King, "the Texas Cannonball", Albert Collins, "the Iceman", and Johnny Winter carried the tradition into the rock era.

Blues-rock revival

In the 1980s Stevie Ray Vaughan and his band Double Trouble brought Texas blues to worldwide fame with a fiery, virtuoso guitar style, sparking a broad blues revival. The Fabulous Thunderbirds, led by Vaughan's elder brother Jimmie Vaughan, and later players such as Kenny Wayne Shepherd continued the Austin-centred tradition.
