Trabzon (historically Trebizond, from Greek Trapezous) is a city on the Black Sea coast of northeastern Turkey and the capital of Trabzon Province. Founded by Greek colonists from Sinope, traditionally dated to 756 BC, the city became a major terminus of trade routes linking the Black Sea to Persia and Central Asia.

History

Trapezus was mentioned by Xenophon, whose Ten Thousand reached the sea there after their march from Mesopotamia, as recounted in the Anabasis. The city later came under Roman and then Byzantine rule. After the sack of Constantinople in 1204, members of the Komnenos dynasty founded the Empire of Trebizond, a Byzantine successor state that survived as an independent polity until its conquest by the Ottoman sultan Mehmed II in 1461 — eight years after Constantinople itself fell. The thirteenth-century church of Hagia Sophia in the city, famed for its frescoes, is a legacy of that era.

Geography and economy

Trabzon lies in a humid, rainy part of the Eastern Black Sea region, backed by the Pontic Mountains. The surrounding province is a leading producer of hazelnuts and tea. The port remains important for regional trade, and the city is home to Trabzonspor, one of Turkey's most successful football clubs, and to the Sumela Monastery, clinging to a cliff face in the Altındere valley south of the city.
