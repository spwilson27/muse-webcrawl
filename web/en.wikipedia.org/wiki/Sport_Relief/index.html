Sport Relief

Sport Relief was a British charity event organised by Comic Relief, in partnership with BBC Sport, which ran biennially from 2002 to 2020. It combined entertainment and sporting challenges to raise money to tackle poverty and social injustice in the United Kingdom and in some of the world's poorest countries.

Format

The campaign alternated years with Red Nose Day, Comic Relief's other major fundraiser. Televised appeals on the BBC featured comedy sketches, celebrity sporting challenges and films showing how donations were used. Members of the public took part in the Sport Relief Mile (later the Sport Relief Games and similar mass-participation runs, swims and cycles) held at venues across the country.

Challenges and records

Celebrity endurance challenges became a hallmark of the event. David Walliams swam the English Channel and later the length of the Thames for the charity; Eddie Izzard ran a series of marathon challenges in South Africa and the UK; and John Bishop, Davina McCall, Zoe Ball and Greg James completed multi-day triathlon-style challenges. Schools and workplaces also organised their own fundraising events.

Legacy

Across its campaigns Sport Relief raised hundreds of millions of pounds. The standalone event was retired after 2020, with sport-themed fundraising folded into Comic Relief's broader appeals.
