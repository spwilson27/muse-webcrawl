Revierderby

Revierderby
Location: Ruhr, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany
Teams: Borussia Dortmund, FC Schalke 04
First meeting: 3 May 1925

The Revierderby is the name given to football matches between Borussia Dortmund and FC Schalke 04, two clubs from Germany's Ruhr region (Ruhrgebiet, locally nicknamed das Revier). It is one of the most fiercely contested derbies in German football, rooted in the working-class, coal-mining and steel-making identity of the region; Dortmund and Gelsenkirchen, home of Schalke, lie roughly 30 kilometres apart.

History

The first meeting took place on 3 May 1925, a 4–2 victory for Schalke during their dominant "Schalker Kreisel" era. Schalke dominated the fixture through the 1930s and 1940s, while Dortmund's Oberliga triumphs in the 1950s and 1960s evened the rivalry. The fixture has produced celebrated results in the Bundesliga era, including Dortmund's 3–2 win in 1997 on the way to their European Cup triumph, and Schalke's 4–4 draw at Dortmund in November 2017, when they recovered from 4–0 down at half-time.

Other Revier clubs

The term is sometimes extended to matches involving other Ruhr clubs such as VfL Bochum, MSV Duisburg, Rot-Weiss Essen and SG Wattenscheid 09, whose meetings are occasionally called the "kleines Revierderby" (small Revier derby). Historically the Dortmund–Schalke fixture is by far the most prominent.

Fan culture

Both clubs draw some of the largest crowds in world football. Dortmund's Westfalenstadion (Signal Iduna Park) is famous for the Südtribüne, the "Yellow Wall", while Schalke's home is the Veltins-Arena in Gelsenkirchen. The rivalry, though intense, is traditionally considered less hostile than some other German derbies, reflecting the shared regional identity of the Ruhr.
