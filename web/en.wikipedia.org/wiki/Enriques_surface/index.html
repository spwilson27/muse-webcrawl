Enriques surface

FieldAlgebraic geometry
Kodaira dimension0
Named forFederigo Enriques

In mathematics, an Enriques surface is an algebraic surface with vanishing irregularity and trivial canonical divisor up to torsion — precisely, a smooth compact complex surface whose second canonical divisor is trivial but whose first is not, with Euler characteristic 12. They were first studied by the Italian mathematician Federigo Enriques in 1896.

Properties

Enriques surfaces are quotients of K3 surfaces by a fixed-point-free involution, so a K3 surface is their universal double cover. They have Kodaira dimension 0 and fundamental group Z/2. Over fields of characteristic 2, additional singular and supersingular types occur, giving a richer classification.

Classification

In the Enriques–Kodaira classification of compact complex surfaces, Enriques surfaces form one of the five classes of surfaces of Kodaira dimension 0, alongside tori, K3 surfaces, hyperelliptic surfaces, and Kodaira surfaces.
