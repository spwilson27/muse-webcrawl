Libertarian paternalism

Libertarian paternalism is the idea that it is both possible and desirable for private and public institutions to affect behaviour while respecting freedom of choice. It was coined by behavioural economist Richard Thaler and legal scholar Cass Sunstein in 2003 and popularised in their 2008 book Nudge.

Concept

Because choices are always presented in some context, "choice architects" inevitably influence decisions. Libertarian paternalism steers people toward better outcomes — via defaults, framing and feedback — without forbidding options or significantly changing economic incentives, e.g. automatic enrolment in pension savings with an opt-out.

Criticism

Critics question who decides what is better for choosers, warn of manipulation and a slippery slope to coercion, and doubt decision-makers' knowledge. Supporters reply that some choice architecture is unavoidable. See Nudge theory and Behavioural economics.
