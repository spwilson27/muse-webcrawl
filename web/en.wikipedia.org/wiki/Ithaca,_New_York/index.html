Ithaca, New York

Ithaca is a city in and the county seat of Tompkins County, New York, United States. It lies on the southern shore of Cayuga Lake in Central New York, in the Finger Lakes region. The city's population was about 32,000 at the 2020 census, with a much larger student population during the academic year.

Education

Ithaca is best known as the home of Cornell University, an Ivy League institution founded in 1865 whose campus occupies East Hill above the city, and of Ithaca College, founded as a music conservatory in 1892. The presence of the two schools dominates the city's economy and culture.

Geography and culture

The city sits in a valley carved by glacial action, surrounded by hills and by gorges with waterfalls — including Ithaca Falls and the falls at Buttermilk Falls and Robert H. Treman state parks — celebrated in the local slogan "Ithaca is Gorges". In the early 20th century Ithaca was a significant centre of the silent film industry. The area is also known for the Ithaca Farmers Market, the Moosewood Restaurant and its vegetarian cookbooks, and a politically progressive culture.

History

The region was the homeland of the Cayuga people of the Haudenosaunee. The settlement took its name from the Greek island of Ithaca, home of Odysseus. Ithaca was incorporated as a village in 1821 and as a city in 1888.
