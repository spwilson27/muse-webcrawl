Bark (botany)

Bark is the outermost layer of stems and roots of woody plants. Plants with bark include trees, woody vines, and shrubs. Bark refers to all the tissues outside the vascular cambium and is a nontechnical term. It overlays the wood and consists of the inner bark and the outer bark. The inner bark, which in older stems is living tissue, includes the innermost layer of the periderm. The outer bark on older stems includes the dead tissue on the surface of the stems, along with parts of the outermost periderm and all the tissues on the outer side of the periderm.

Botanical description

In woody plants, bark consists of the secondary phloem produced by the vascular cambium, together with the periderm (itself comprising the cork cambium or phellogen, the cork or phellem, and the phelloderm) and older, dead phloem layers. As the stem grows in diameter, the outer bark cracks, peels, or forms fissures in patterns characteristic of each species, which are often used in tree identification.

Functions

Bark protects the plant against physical damage, herbivores, pathogens, and desiccation, and it insulates against temperature extremes and fire in some species, such as the thick bark of giant sequoias and cork oaks. The living inner bark transports sugars produced in photosynthesis from the leaves to the rest of the plant.

Uses

Bark has many human uses: cork from the cork oak, cinnamon from the inner bark of Cinnamomum trees, quinine from cinchona bark, tannins for leather tanning, latex and resins, mulch, and traditional canoe and basket materials such as birch bark. Removing a complete ring of bark (girdling) severs the phloem and kills the tree.
