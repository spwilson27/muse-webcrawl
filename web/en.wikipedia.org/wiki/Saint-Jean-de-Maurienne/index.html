Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne is a commune in the Savoie department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region in south-eastern France. It is the chief town of the Maurienne valley, on the river Arc, and a subprefecture of the department. The town lies on the road and railway routes between Lyon and Turin via the Mont Cenis passes, and is a gateway to the ski resorts and high Alpine cols (Galibier, Croix de Fer, Madeleine) popular with cyclists.

History

The town grew around the cult of Saint John the Baptist; its cathedral preserves relics said to be three fingers of John the Baptist, brought from Alexandria in the 6th century by Saint Thecla. Saint-Jean was the capital of the Maurienne, a valley controlled historically by the House of Savoy, whose founder Humbert I is associated with the town. It was an important medieval diocese; the Diocese of Maurienne dates to the 6th century.

Sights

The Cathedral of Saint John the Baptist, built from the 11th century in Romanesque style with a Gothic cloister, dominates the old town. The crypt is among the oldest in France. The town also houses the Opinel museum, devoted to the famous Savoyard folding knife, whose factory is nearby.
