Green Tara

Green Tara (Sanskrit: Śyāmatārā; Tibetan: Sgrol ljang) is a female Buddha and bodhisattva in Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism, regarded as the principal form of the goddess Tara — the embodiment of compassionate activity and the swift rescuer of beings from fear and danger.

Iconography

Green Tara is depicted as a youthful goddess with green skin, seated in lalitasana with her right leg extended, foot resting on a lotus, signifying her readiness to step down and aid sentient beings. Her hands form the varada (giving) and vitarka (teaching) mudras and hold the stems of blue utpala lotuses. The extended leg and green colour — the colour of the Buddha family of Amoghasiddhi — symbolise enlightened activity.

Origins and practice

Tara devotion arose in India by about the 6th century CE and became central in Tibetan Buddhism, where Tara is said to protect against the "eight great fears" (lions, elephants, fire, snakes, robbers, imprisonment, water, and demons). Her mantra, Oṃ tāre tuttāre ture svāhā, is among the most widely recited in the Buddhist world. The famous Praise to the Twenty-One Taras is recited daily in Tibetan monasteries; Green Tara is the chief of the twenty-one emanations, of which White Tara, associated with longevity, is the other best known.

In Tibetan tradition

Tibetan tradition identifies the seventh-century Nepalese princess Bhrikuti, wife of emperor Songtsen Gampo, as an emanation of Green Tara, and the Dalai Lamas are likewise regarded as manifestations of Avalokiteshvara closely linked to her cult. Tara meditation (sadhana) is practised across all schools of Tibetan Buddhism.
