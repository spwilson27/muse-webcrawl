Pydna (Greek: Πύδνα) was an ancient Greek city in Pieria, on the western shore of the Thermaic Gulf in Macedonia. One of the oldest Macedonian coastal cities, it is best known as the site of the Battle of Pydna in 168 BC, in which the Roman army of Lucius Aemilius Paullus decisively defeated King Perseus of Macedon, ending the Antigonid kingdom and establishing Roman supremacy in the Greek world. The modern village of Makrygialos stands near the site, within the present municipality of Pydna-Kolindros.

History

Pydna was already inhabited in prehistoric times and appears in tradition as a Macedonian stronghold contested by Athens: the Athenians briefly held it in the 5th century BC, and it was besieged and taken by the Athenian general Timotheus. Under Philip II the city was firmly Macedonian. In 317–316 BC Cassander besieged Pydna, where Olympias, mother of Alexander the Great, had taken refuge; after a winter siege, starvation forced surrender and Olympias was put to death. The city remained an important port and road station on the route later formalised as the Via Egnatia.

The Battle of Pydna, fought on 22 June 168 BC on the plain near the city during the Third Macedonian War, pitted the Macedonian phalanx against the more flexible Roman manipular legion. When the phalanx advanced over uneven ground and gaps opened in its formation, Roman troops infiltrated the gaps and slaughtered the phalangites at close quarters; some 20,000 Macedonians were killed in about an hour. Perseus fled and was captured the following year, and Macedonia was divided into four republics before becoming a Roman province. Pydna continued as a Roman and Byzantine town — it was an episcopal see — before declining in the medieval period, when the population moved inland to the site later called Kitros.

Archaeology

Excavations at Makrygialos have uncovered extensive cemeteries with rich Macedonian tombs, workshops, and parts of the ancient city and its harbour installations. Finds include gold wreaths, weapons and fine pottery, displayed in regional museums. The bishop's palace complex of Byzantine Pydna (Louloudies) has also been excavated nearby.
