Extrajudicial punishment

Punishment imposed without trial or legal process.

Extrajudicial punishment is the punishment of a person by a government authority or other party without the sanction of any judicial proceeding or legal process. It ranges from fines, property seizure, imprisonment, and banishment imposed without trial to its most extreme form, extrajudicial killing. Such punishments violate the right to due process guaranteed by instruments including the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights.

Forms

Historically, rulers imposed punishments directly by decree — bills of attainder, letters de cachet, and banishment among them. In modern states, extrajudicial punishment may occur through administrative detention, punitive demolitions, forced disappearances, or the actions of death squads and paramilitary proxies acting with official tolerance. Vigilantism and lynching are forms of extrajudicial punishment carried out by non-state actors.

Legal status

Extrajudicial punishment is prohibited under international human rights law: no one may be subjected to arbitrary arrest, everyone is entitled to a fair and public hearing by an independent tribunal, and the death penalty, where retained, may be imposed only pursuant to a final judgement by a competent court. Even in states of emergency, the core guarantees against arbitrary deprivation of life and liberty are non-derogable. The UN Human Rights Committee and special rapporteurs on extrajudicial, summary, or arbitrary executions monitor and report on such practices worldwide.
