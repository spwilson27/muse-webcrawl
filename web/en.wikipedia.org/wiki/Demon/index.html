Demon

A demon is a malevolent supernatural entity prominent in religion, mythology, folklore and fiction. Demons are usually depicted as harmful beings that may be summoned, controlled, or exorcised, and as agents of temptation, disease or misfortune. The word derives from the Greek daimōn (δαίμων), which in early Greek religion referred more neutrally to a divine power, spirit, or tutelary deity; figures such as Socrates' daimonion were not evil.

In religions

In Christianity, demons are generally understood as fallen angels who rebelled against God under Satan and who afflict humans through temptation and possession; the New Testament describes Jesus performing exorcisms. In Islam, harmful beings include the shayatin (devils) and some jinn. In Hinduism, the asuras oppose the devas, while Zoroastrianism speaks of daevas. Mesopotamian, Jewish and other traditions also developed elaborate demonologies, lists and hierarchies of named demons, codified in grimoires such as the Lesser Key of Solomon.

In culture

Demons remain staples of literature, film and games, from Paradise Lost and Goethe's Faust to modern horror. Figuratively, "demon" is used for inner struggles, as in addiction, and in science Maxwell's demon is a famous thought experiment in thermodynamics.
