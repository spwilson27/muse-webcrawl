Treaties of Velasco

Signed: 14 May 1836

Location: Velasco, Texas

Parties: Republic of Texas; Antonio López de Santa Anna

Context: Texas Revolution

The Treaties of Velasco were two documents, one public and one secret, signed in Velasco, Texas, on 14 May 1836 between the interim government of the Republic of Texas and the captured Mexican president and general Antonio López de Santa Anna. They were concluded three weeks after Santa Anna's defeat and capture at the Battle of San Jacinto, and purported to end the Texas Revolution.

Terms

In the public treaty, Santa Anna agreed that Mexican troops would withdraw south of the Rio Grande, that hostilities would cease, that prisoners would be exchanged, and that property seized during the campaign would be restored. In the secret treaty, Santa Anna promised, in exchange for his release and safe passage to Veracruz, to work for Mexican recognition of Texan independence with the Rio Grande as the boundary, and to refrain from taking up arms against Texas again.

Repudiation and consequences

The Mexican government refused to ratify the treaties, arguing that Santa Anna, as a prisoner, had no authority to bind the nation, and that he had signed under duress. Santa Anna himself repudiated them once free. Mexico therefore never recognised Texan independence, and the boundary dispute — Texas claiming the Rio Grande, Mexico the Nueces River — festered until the United States annexed Texas in 1845 and the Mexican–American War settled the matter by force.
