Brown–Peterson cohomology

Brown–Peterson cohomology
Field: algebraic topology
Introduced by: Edgar H. Brown Jr. and Franklin P. Peterson (1966)
Spectrum: BP
Coefficient ring: polynomial algebra over Z(p) on generators v1, v2, …
Related theories: Morava K-theories, Morava E-theories, complex cobordism MU

Brown–Peterson cohomology is a generalized cohomology theory used in algebraic topology, introduced by Edgar H. Brown Jr. and Franklin P. Peterson in 1966. Associated to each prime p, it is represented by a spectrum BP which is a summand of p-localized complex cobordism, split off by a Quillen idempotent.

Structure

The coefficient ring BP* is a polynomial algebra over the localized integers Z(p) on generators vi of degree 2(pi − 1). The Adams–Novikov spectral sequence, based on BP-cohomology, is a principal tool for computing stable homotopy groups of spheres. Daniel Quillen showed that the formal group law of BP is the universal p-typical formal group law, tying the theory to the arithmetic of formal groups.

Role in chromatic homotopy theory

In chromatic homotopy theory, BP serves as the hub from which the Morava K-theories K(n) and Morava E-theories are obtained as quotients and localizations, stratifying stable homotopy theory by "chromatic level" or height. The quotient BP⟨n⟩ and the related theory Johnson–Wilson E(n) also descend from BP.
