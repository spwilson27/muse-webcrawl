Polish United Workers' Party

Polish United Workers' Party (PZPR)
Founded: December 1948
Dissolved: January 1990
Ideology: Marxism–Leninism

The Polish United Workers' Party (Polish: Polska Zjednoczona Partia Robotnicza, PZPR) was the communist party that governed the Polish People's Republic from its founding in 1948 until 1989. It was formed by the merger of the Polish Workers' Party and the Polish Socialist Party.

History

The party exercised a monopoly on political power, though it coexisted with small satellite parties. Its dominance was challenged by recurring crises: the Poznań protests of 1956, the March 1968 events, the 1970 coastal protests, and the rise of the Solidarity trade union in 1980, which led to martial law in 1981.

End of the party

Following the Round Table Talks and the partially free elections of June 1989, the party lost power. In January 1990 the PZPR dissolved itself; its main successor was the Social Democracy of the Republic of Poland.
