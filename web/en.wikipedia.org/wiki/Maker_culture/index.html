Maker culture
Key publicationMake magazine (founded 2005)
Key eventMaker Faire (first held 2006)
Related termsDIY, hackerspaces, fab labs

Maker culture is a contemporary technology-based extension of DIY culture that emphasises hands-on creation, learning by doing, and the sharing of designs and techniques. The "maker movement" grew rapidly in the 2000s around affordable tools such as 3D printers, laser cutters, and open-source microcontroller platforms like Arduino and Raspberry Pi.

History

The movement is commonly dated to the launch of Make magazine by Dale Dougherty in 2005 and the first Maker Faire in San Mateo, California, in 2006. Maker Faires subsequently spread worldwide, attracting hundreds of thousands of visitors. Community workshops — hackerspaces, makerspaces, and MIT-style fab labs — provide shared access to tools and instruction.

Themes

Maker culture values open-source hardware, repair and reuse (in reaction to sealed consumer electronics), interdisciplinary skills combining craft, electronics and software, and the democratisation of manufacturing. Commentators such as Chris Anderson (in his 2012 book Makers) predicted a "new industrial revolution" of desktop fabrication; critics have questioned whether the movement has delivered on that promise and noted its demographic narrowness.
