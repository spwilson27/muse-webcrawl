The House of Habsburg (German: Haus Habsburg), also known as the House of Austria, is one of the most prominent and important dynasties in European history. The house takes its name from Habsburg Castle, a fortress built in the 1020s in present-day Switzerland by Radbot of Klettgau.

The dynasty rose to power in Austria in 1282 and, through a celebrated policy of dynastic marriage, came to rule Spain, the Netherlands, Bohemia, Hungary, and vast overseas empires, while providing most Holy Roman Emperors from 1440 until the empire's dissolution in 1806.

Rise and expansion

Rudolf I became King of the Romans in 1273 and seized Austria and Styria from Ottokar II of Bohemia. The motto attributed to the house—"Let others wage war; you, fortunate Austria, marry"—reflects its strategy: marriages brought Burgundy, Spain, Bohemia, and Hungary into the Habsburg orbit under Maximilian I and his descendants.

Spanish and Austrian branches

After the abdication of Charles V in 1556, the dynasty split into the Spanish Habsburgs, who ruled Spain and its empire until 1700, and the Austrian Habsburgs. The Spanish line ended with the childless Charles II, triggering the War of the Spanish Succession. The Austrian line continued, becoming the House of Habsburg-Lorraine after 1745 through Maria Theresa's marriage to Francis of Lorraine.

End of the monarchy

The Habsburgs ruled the Austrian Empire and later Austria-Hungary until 1918, when Emperor Charles I renounced participation in state affairs following defeat in the First World War. The family remains prominent in European public life; Karl von Habsburg is the current head of the house.
