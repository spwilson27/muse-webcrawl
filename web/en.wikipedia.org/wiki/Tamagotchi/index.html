Tamagotchi

TypeVirtual pet, handheld digital toy
InventorAki Maita (Bandai)
ManufacturerBandai
ReleasedNovember 23, 1996 (Japan)

The Tamagotchi (Japanese: たまごっち) is a handheld digital pet created by Aki Maita of the Japanese toy company Bandai and first released in Japan on November 23, 1996, followed by a worldwide launch in 1997. The name is a portmanteau of tamago ("egg") and uotchi ("watch"). Housed in a small egg-shaped computer with an LCD screen and three buttons, the Tamagotchi simulates a creature that hatches and grows through life stages according to the care it receives.

Gameplay

The owner must feed the pet, play games with it, clean up after it, give it medicine when sick, and discipline and praise it. Neglect results in poor growth, illness, or the pet's departure — in early international versions the creature "dies", displayed as an angel or ghost — prompting owners to check on it constantly through beeping attention calls. The device's real-time demands made it so distracting that many schools banned Tamagotchis in the late 1990s.

History and impact

The Tamagotchi became one of the biggest toy fads of the late 1990s, selling tens of millions of units and triggering shortages, imitators and moral debate about children's attachment to virtual creatures. Maita and Bandai executive Akihiro Yokoi received the 1997 Ig Nobel Prize in Economics for the invention. Sales declined after the initial mania, but Bandai revived the brand repeatedly: infrared-connecting Tamagotchi Connection (2004), colour-screen Tamagotchi iD (2009), the Tamagotchi Pix (2021) and the smartwatch-like Tamagotchi Smart. Cumulative shipments surpassed 91 million units by 2023, and the franchise spans anime, films, games and merchandise.
