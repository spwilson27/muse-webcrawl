Cambridge University Press

Cambridge University Press is the university press of the University of Cambridge. Granted letters patent by King Henry VIII in 1534, it is the oldest university press in the world. It is also the King's Printer. In 2021 it merged with Cambridge Assessment to form Cambridge University Press and Assessment.

History

Cambridge University Press has been printing books since 1584, making it the oldest continuously operating press in the world. It has published works by authors including John Milton, Isaac Newton, and Ernest Rutherford, and prints the King James Version of the Bible under its royal privilege. In the 19th and 20th centuries it grew into a major international academic publisher.

Publishing today

The Press publishes more than 50,000 titles and over 400 academic journals, alongside English-language teaching and educational materials for schools. It operates offices in more than 40 countries and sells to nearly every country in the world. Its academic publishing covers science, technology, medicine, humanities, and social sciences.
