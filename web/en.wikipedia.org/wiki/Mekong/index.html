Mekong

Floating market on the Mekong Delta in Vietnam

The Mekong River is a trans-boundary river in East and Southeast Asia. It is the world's twelfth-longest river and the third-longest in Asia, flowing about 4,500 to 4,900 kilometres from the Tibetan Plateau through China, Myanmar, Laos, Thailand, Cambodia and Vietnam, where it drains into the South China Sea through the Mekong Delta.

In China the river is known as the Lancang Jiang. It forms much of the border between Laos and Thailand and feeds Tonlé Sap, Cambodia's great lake, whose flow reverses seasonally with the Mekong's flood pulse.

Ecology and economy

The Mekong basin is one of the most biodiverse river systems on Earth and supports an inland fishery of enormous importance, as well as rice farming for tens of millions of people. It is home to species such as the Irrawaddy dolphin and the giant Mekong catfish. Extensive dam building in China and the lower basin countries has raised concerns about sediment, fish migration and the delta's vulnerability to sea-level rise.
