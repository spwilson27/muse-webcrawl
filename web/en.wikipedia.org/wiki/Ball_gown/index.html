Ball gown

A ball gown, or ballgown, is a formal evening gown worn to a ball or other formal event. The classic silhouette consists of a fitted bodice, a defined natural waist, and a very full skirt reaching the floor, often supported by crinolines, hoops, or multiple layers of net and tulle. Ball gowns are the most formal style of women's dress in modern Western dress codes, corresponding to white tie occasions.

History

Formal court dress of the 18th century, with wide panniered skirts, evolved into the empire-line gowns of the Regency era and then the vast crinoline skirts of the mid-Victorian period. In the 1950s Christian Dior's "New Look" revived the hourglass ball gown with nipped-in waists and voluminous skirts, a shape popularised by designers such as Charles Frederick Worth in the nineteenth century and, in the twentieth, by Norman Hartnell's dresses for Queen Elizabeth II. Modern red-carpet dressing continues the tradition, with designers creating elaborate gowns for events such as the Met Gala.

Usage

Ball gowns are worn at debutante balls, state dinners, charity galas, opera opening nights, weddings (as bridal or bridesmaid dresses), and proms. Etiquette traditionally pairs them with long gloves, evening jewellery, and an upswept hairstyle. Fabrics include silk satin, taffeta, organza, chiffon, and tulle, often embellished with beading, sequins, or lace.

Variations

Related silhouettes include the A-line gown, the mermaid gown, and the princess-line gown; the ball gown remains distinguished by the fullness of its skirt from the waist.
