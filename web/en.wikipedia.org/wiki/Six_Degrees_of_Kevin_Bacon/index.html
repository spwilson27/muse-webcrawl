Six Degrees of Kevin Bacon

Six Degrees of Kevin Bacon or Bacon's Law is a parlour game in which players challenge each other to find the shortest path that connects an arbitrary actor to prolific American actor Kevin Bacon, assuming that any two people who have appeared in a film together are "linked". It rests on the "six degrees of separation" concept, which posits that any two people on Earth are six or fewer acquaintance links apart.

Origin

The game originated in 1994 among three students at Albright College in Pennsylvania — Craig Fass, Brian Turtle, and Mike Ginelli — after watching Bacon's films Footloose and The Air Up There. It spread through a letter to talk-show host Jon Stewart, appearances on television, and a 1996 book; a story often repeated is that Bacon himself initially disliked the idea but later embraced it, founding the charitable network SixDegrees.org in 2007.

Bacon numbers

An actor's Bacon number is the number of links in the shortest chain to Kevin Bacon: Bacon himself is 0, an actor who appeared in a film with him is 1, and so on. The vast majority of film actors have a Bacon number of 2 or 3; the centre of the Hollywood collaboration graph is in fact not Bacon — analyses of the Internet Movie Database (such as the University of Virginia's "Oracle of Bacon") show actors such as Harvey Keitel have lower average separation — but Bacon's unusually broad filmography made him a fitting mascot. The concept parallels the Erdős number in mathematics and has inspired combined Erdős–Bacon numbers.
