Law of truly large numbers

The law of truly large numbers, attributed to statisticians Persi Diaconis and Frederick Mosteller, states that with a large enough number of independent samples, any outrageously unlikely event is likely to occur. It explains apparent coincidences and "one-in-a-million" events: given enough opportunities — people, days, trials — such events will happen to somebody, somewhere.

The law is related to the law of large numbers and to Littlewood's law, which observes that a person experiencing events at a certain rate can expect a "miracle" (a one-in-a-million event) roughly once a month.

It is a standard caution against interpreting improbable occurrences as evidence of design, paranormal phenomena or fraud without accounting for the number of chances.
