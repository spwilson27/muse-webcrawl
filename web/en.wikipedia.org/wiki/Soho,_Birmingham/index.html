Soho, Birmingham

Soho House, Matthew Boulton's home, now a museum

Soho is an area of south Handsworth and Smethwick, on the north-western edge of Birmingham, England. It is historically significant as the site of the Soho Manufactory of Matthew Boulton and as one of the birthplaces of the Industrial Revolution's steam power.

History

In 1761 the entrepreneur Matthew Boulton leased land at Soho and by 1766 had built the Soho Manufactory, one of the largest factories of its age, producing metal goods, "toys" (buttons, buckles, and boxes), silverware, and ormolu for world markets. In 1775 Boulton went into partnership with James Watt; their firm of Boulton and Watt produced steam engines at the Soho Foundry (opened 1795–96 in adjacent Smethwick), supplying the engines that powered factories, mines, and mills across Britain and abroad. Boulton's Soho Mint struck coins for the British government and foreign states.

Landmarks

Boulton's home, Soho House, survives as a museum of Birmingham Museums Trust, displaying the Lunar Society circle's interests; Boulton, Watt, and their partner William Murdoch are commemorated by the gilded Boulton, Watt and Murdoch statue in the city centre. Soho Road is now a busy commercial street and the heart of Birmingham's South Asian community, known for its shops, restaurants, and the annual Vaisakhi celebrations.

Transport

The area is served by the West Midlands Metro (Midland Metro) tram stops at Soho Benson Road and by rail services at Jewellery Quarter and Smethwick stations nearby; the Soho Loop of the Birmingham Canal Navigations served the manufactories.
