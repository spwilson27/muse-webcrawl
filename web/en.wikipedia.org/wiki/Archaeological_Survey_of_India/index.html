Archaeological Survey of India

An ASI excavation at a protected archaeological site.

The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) is an Indian government agency, attached to the Ministry of Culture, that is responsible for archaeological research and the conservation and preservation of cultural historical monuments in the country. It was founded in 1861 by Alexander Cunningham, who became its first director-general, with the support of Lord Canning, the Governor-General of India.

Functions

The ASI administers more than 3,600 ancient monuments, archaeological sites, and remains of national importance under the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act, 1958. Its work includes maintaining centrally protected monuments such as the Taj Mahal, conducting excavations (famously including the Indus Valley sites of Harappa and Mohenjo-daro under John Marshall in the 1920s), running site museums, publishing journals and reports, and regulating archaeological activity in India.

Organization

The survey is headquartered in New Delhi and operates through regional circles across India, together with specialist branches for epigraphy, science, horticulture, and underwater archaeology. Notable director-generals have included Alexander Cunningham, John Marshall, Mortimer Wheeler, and B. B. Lal.
