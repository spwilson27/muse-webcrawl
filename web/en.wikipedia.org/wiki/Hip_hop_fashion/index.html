Hip hop fashion

Hip hop fashion comprises the distinctive styles of dress that developed within hip hop culture from its origins in the Bronx, New York, in the 1970s and 1980s, and which grew into one of the dominant influences on global street style and the modern streetwear industry.

1980s: the old school look

Early hip hop dress drew on sportswear and b-boy practicality: tracksuits, Kangol hats, fat-laced Adidas and Puma trainers, Cazal glasses, leather bombers and heavy gold "dookie" chains. Run-DMC's laceless Adidas Superstars — celebrated in their 1986 hit "My Adidas" — won the first major endorsement deal between a rap group and a sportswear brand. In Harlem, tailor Dapper Dan famously remixed luxury-house logos onto custom garments for rappers and hustlers, anticipating later collaborations between street culture and high fashion.

1990s and 2000s: baggy clothes and hip hop brands

The 1990s brought oversized silhouettes — baggy jeans, work boots such as Timberlands, hockey and basketball jerseys, and bright colours — alongside Afrocentric prints and Pan-African colours. Black-owned labels including Cross Colours, Karl Kani, FUBU, Sean John, Rocawear and Baby Phat built large businesses, while artists increasingly launched their own lines. By the 2000s hip hop style merged with luxury branding, a shift symbolised by Pharrell Williams's collaborations and later by Virgil Abloh's appointment at Louis Vuitton. Sneaker culture, centred on releases such as Nike's Air Force 1 and Air Jordan lines, became a major market in its own right.
