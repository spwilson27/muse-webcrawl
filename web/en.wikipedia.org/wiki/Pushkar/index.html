Pushkar

Pushkar
Country: India
State: Rajasthan
District: Ajmer
Known for: Brahma Temple, Pushkar Lake, Camel Fair

Pushkar is a town in the Ajmer district of the Indian state of Rajasthan, situated about 10 km northwest of Ajmer. It is one of the five sacred dhams (pilgrimage sites) for devout Hindus and is often called Tirtha-Raj, "the king of pilgrimage sites". The town lies on the shore of Pushkar Lake, a sacred lake surrounded by 52 bathing ghats.

Sacred sites

Pushkar is famous for its temple dedicated to Brahma, the Hindu god of creation — one of the very few temples in the world dedicated to him. According to legend, Brahma performed a yajna (fire sacrifice) at the lake, and the town grew around the ritual. Other notable temples include the Savitri Temple, perched on a hill overlooking the lake, and the Varaha and Rangji temples.

Pushkar Fair

The annual Pushkar Camel Fair, held around the Hindu month of Kartik (October–November), is one of the world's largest livestock fairs, drawing hundreds of thousands of visitors, traders, and pilgrims. Camels, cattle, and horses are traded, and the fair features races, competitions, and cultural performances.

Geography

The town is encircled by the Aravalli hills, with the Nag Pahar ("Snake Mountain") separating Pushkar Lake from Ajmer. Its setting in an arid landscape makes the lake and its ghats the focal point of the town's religious and social life.
