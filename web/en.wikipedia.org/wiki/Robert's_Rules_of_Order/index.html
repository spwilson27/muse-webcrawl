Robert's Rules of Order
Author: Henry Martyn Robert
First published: 1876
Current edition: Robert's Rules of Order Newly Revised, 12th edition (2020)
Subject: Parliamentary procedure

Robert's Rules of Order is a book of parliamentary procedure first published in 1876 by U.S. Army officer Henry Martyn Robert. It provides a standard set of rules for conducting meetings and making decisions in deliberative assemblies, and it is the most commonly adopted parliamentary authority in the United States, used by legislatures' subordinate bodies, clubs, churches, school boards, homeowners' associations, and countless other organisations.

Background

Robert devised the rules after an embarrassing experience chairing a church meeting in 1863, which convinced him of the need for a standard, portable manual based on the practice of the U.S. House of Representatives but adapted to ordinary societies. The first edition, the Pocket Manual of Rules of Order for Deliberative Assemblies, appeared in February 1876.

Contents

The rules govern motions and their precedence, debate, voting, quorum, committees, elections, and the duties of officers such as the chair and secretary. Key concepts include the main motion, subsidiary and privileged motions, points of order, appeals, and the requirement that business be conducted fairly, protecting the rights of the majority, the minority, individual members, and absentees.

Editions

The work has been repeatedly revised by Robert's descendants and associates. Robert's Rules of Order Newly Revised (RONR), first published in 1970, is the current authorised edition; the 12th edition appeared in 2020. A shorter guide, Robert's Rules of Order Newly Revised In Brief, is published for newcomers.
