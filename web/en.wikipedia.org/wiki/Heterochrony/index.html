Heterochrony

Heterochrony, in evolutionary developmental biology, is a change in the timing or rate of developmental events relative to an ancestor, producing altered size and shape — a major mechanism of evolutionary change.

Concepts

The term was introduced by Ernst Haeckel. Later frameworks, notably Stephen Jay Gould's Ontogeny and Phylogeny (1977), distinguish paedomorphosis — adults retaining juvenile traits, via neoteny, progenesis or postdisplacement — from peramorphosis, the extension of development via acceleration, hypermorphosis or predisplacement.

Examples

The axolotl salamander is a classic neotenic example, breeding while retaining larval gills. Human evolution has often been described, controversially, as neotenous relative to other primates. Heterochrony in gene expression timing, measurable in transcriptomic 'hourglass' models, underlies many morphological shifts.
