The Age of Innocence

The Age of Innocence is a 1920 novel by American author Edith Wharton. Set in upper-class New York City during the 1870s, it won the 1921 Pulitzer Prize for Fiction, making Wharton the first woman to receive the award.

AuthorEdith Wharton
LanguageEnglish
GenreNovel, romance
Published1920
PublisherD. Appleton & Company

Plot

Newland Archer, a lawyer from one of New York's most respected families, is engaged to the conventional May Welland. His life is disrupted by the arrival of May's cousin, Countess Ellen Olenska, who has separated from her Polish husband and seeks a divorce. Archer falls in love with Ellen but is bound by the rigid codes of their society; he marries May, and Ellen eventually returns to Europe. Decades later, widowed, Archer stands outside Ellen's Paris apartment and chooses to leave without seeing her, preserving his memory of their love.

Themes

The novel examines the conflict between individual desire and social convention, portraying old New York society as a gilded but stifling world of ritual, reputation and quiet sacrifice.

Adaptations

Martin Scorsese directed a celebrated film adaptation in 1993 starring Daniel Day-Lewis, Michelle Pfeiffer and Winona Ryder. Earlier film versions appeared in 1924 and 1934, and the novel has been adapted for stage and radio.
