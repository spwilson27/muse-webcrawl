Jubilee

A jubilee is a special anniversary of an event, usually denoting the 25th, 40th, 50th, 60th, or 70th anniversary of a significant occasion, such as a marriage, the reign of a monarch, or the founding of an institution. The word derives from the Hebrew yovel, the "ram's horn" (shofar) blown to proclaim the biblical Jubilee year.

Biblical origins

The Book of Leviticus prescribes that after seven cycles of seven years — in the fiftieth year — a Jubilee (yovel) be proclaimed throughout the land: fields lie fallow, ancestral property returns to its original owners, debts are released, and Hebrew slaves are set free. The Jubilee year stands at the center of later Jewish and Christian ideas of periodic social and economic restoration, and its command to "proclaim liberty throughout the land" is inscribed on the Liberty Bell in Philadelphia.

Royal jubilees

Jubilees of monarchs are marked with names derived from precious materials:

  • Silver jubilee — 25 years
  • Ruby jubilee — 40 years
  • Golden jubilee — 50 years
  • Diamond jubilee — 60 years (in British tradition, 75th in some others)
  • Sapphire jubilee — 65 years
  • Platinum jubilee — 70 years

Notable examples include the Golden Jubilee of Queen Victoria (1887), her Diamond Jubilee (1897), and the Platinum Jubilee of Elizabeth II (2022), marking seventy years on the British throne. Wedding anniversaries follow a similar scale of gifts and metals.

Catholic Holy Years

In the Roman Catholic Church, a jubilee or Holy Year is a year of universal pardon and remission of sins. Boniface VIII proclaimed the first Christian jubilee in 1300; they came to be held ordinarily every 25 years, with extraordinary jubilees proclaimed for special occasions, such as the Great Jubilee of 2000 and the Extraordinary Jubilee of Mercy in 2015–2016. Pilgrims traditionally pass through the Holy Doors of Rome's major basilicas.

Other uses

The term is also used for anniversaries of institutions, ordinations (e.g., a priest's silver or golden jubilee), businesses, and civic events, and appears in names of celebrations such as Juneteenth, historically called Jubilee Day in some communities.
