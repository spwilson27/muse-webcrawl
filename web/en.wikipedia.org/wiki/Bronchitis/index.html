Bronchitis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Specialty: Pulmonology
Symptoms: Cough, mucus production, wheezing, shortness of breath
Types: Acute, chronic

Bronchitis is inflammation of the bronchi (large and medium-sized airways) in the lungs that causes coughing. Bronchitis usually begins as an infection of the nose, ears, throat, or sinuses and then spreads to the bronchi. Symptoms include coughing up mucus, wheezing, shortness of breath, and chest pain.

Acute bronchitis

Acute bronchitis is a lower respiratory tract infection characterized by a cough lasting less than three weeks. In more than 90 percent of cases the cause is a viral infection, commonly the same viruses that cause the common cold. Antibiotics are therefore usually not recommended; treatment is supportive, with rest, fluids and symptomatic relief. The cough typically resolves within two to three weeks.

Chronic bronchitis

Chronic bronchitis is defined as a productive cough lasting at least three months in each of two successive years. It is one of the conditions included in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). The most important cause is long-term exposure to irritants that damage the lungs and airways, above all cigarette smoke; occupational dusts, chemical fumes and air pollution also contribute. Management focuses on smoking cessation, bronchodilators, pulmonary rehabilitation and vaccination against influenza and pneumococcus.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is usually based on signs and symptoms. A chest X-ray may be used to exclude pneumonia when the heart rate, respiratory rate or temperature is abnormal. Sputum culture is rarely helpful in acute cases.
