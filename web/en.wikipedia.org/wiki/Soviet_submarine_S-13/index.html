Soviet submarine S-13

S-13

ClassS-class (Srednyaya) submarine
Commissioned31 July 1941
FateStruck 1956; broken up 1958
Notable actionsSinking of Wilhelm Gustloff and General von Steuben, January–February 1945

S-13 was a Soviet S-class submarine of the Baltic Fleet during the Second World War. Commanded by Captain Alexander Marinesko, she is best known for sinking the German military transport ships Wilhelm Gustloff and General von Steuben in early 1945 — two of the deadliest sinkings in maritime history.

Patrol of January–February 1945

On the night of 30 January 1945, S-13 torpedoed the Wilhelm Gustloff, which was evacuating German military personnel and civilians from East Prussia ahead of the Red Army. The ship sank within the hour; estimates of the dead range from about 6,000 to more than 9,000, making it the worst single-ship loss of life in history. On 10 February, S-13 sank the hospital and transport ship General von Steuben, with the loss of roughly 3,000–4,000 lives.

Marinesko's fate

Despite the tonnage sunk — the highest by any Soviet submarine commander — Marinesko was not made a Hero of the Soviet Union, partly because of disciplinary problems, and he was discharged from the navy in 1945. He died in 1963; the title was awarded posthumously in 1990.

Legacy

S-13 was decommissioned in 1954 and scrapped in 1958. The Wilhelm Gustloff sinking is central to Günter Grass's novel Crabwalk (2002), and Marinesko is commemorated by monuments in Kaliningrad and Kronstadt.
