Band plan

A band plan is a plan for using a particular band of radio frequencies within a radio spectrum allocation. Each plan designates the types of emissions, transmission modes, and usage to be accommodated in specific sections of the band — for example, reserving one segment for CW (Morse code), another for single-sideband voice, another for digital modes, satellites or beacons.

Role in amateur radio

Band plans are most familiar in amateur radio, where national allocations made by regulators (such as the FCC in the United States) are subdivided voluntarily by the amateur community — through the IARU's regional organisations (Region 1: Europe/Africa, Region 2: the Americas, Region 3: Asia-Pacific) and national societies such as the ARRL. Because they are gentlemen's agreements rather than law (except where licence conditions incorporate them), band plans rely on community observance to keep incompatible modes apart and reduce interference.

Typical structure

On the HF bands, plans generally place narrowband modes (CW, data) at the low end of each band, SSB voice above them, and reserve small segments for propagation beacons, emergency traffic and satellite uplinks. On VHF and UHF the plans additionally specify repeater input/output offsets, simplex calling frequencies and satellite sub-bands.
