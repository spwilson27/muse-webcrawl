Local Government Act 1972

Territorial extentEngland and Wales

Commencement1 April 1974

The Local Government Act 1972 (c. 70) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that reformed local government in England and Wales with effect from 1 April 1974. It created a broadly two-tier system of counties and districts, replaced many historic counties with new administrative counties, and created the six metropolitan counties covering the major conurbations outside London.

Background

The Act followed the Redcliffe-Maud Report of 1969, which had recommended unitary authorities, and the proposals of the Conservative government of Edward Heath, which preferred a two-tier structure. Separate legislation (the Local Government (Scotland) Act 1973) covered Scotland, and London had already been reorganised in 1965.

The new structure

The Act established 45 counties in England and 8 in Wales, subdivided into districts. Six metropolitan counties — Greater Manchester, Merseyside, South Yorkshire, Tyne and Wear, West Midlands, and West Yorkshire — had metropolitan boroughs as the lower tier. Civil parishes were retained and new ones could be created.

Later changes

The metropolitan county councils were abolished in 1986. Further reorganisations in the 1990s created unitary authorities in many areas, and Welsh local government was restructured in 1996. Nevertheless, the 1972 Act remains a foundational statute of English and Welsh local government law.
