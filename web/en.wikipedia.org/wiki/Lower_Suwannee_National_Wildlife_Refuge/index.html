The Lower Suwannee National Wildlife Refuge is a National Wildlife Refuge in Florida, protecting habitat along the final 26 miles of the Suwannee River before it empties into the Gulf of Mexico. Established in 1979, it covers roughly 53,000 acres of riverine swamp, bottomland hardwood forest, tidal marsh, and coastal islands.

Wildlife

The refuge supports West Indian manatees in its tidal creeks, Gulf sturgeon spawning runs in the Suwannee River, bald eagles, osprey, swallow-tailed kites, and large wading-bird rookeries. Its uplands host gopher tortoises, white-tailed deer, and wild turkey, while salt marshes shelter clapper rails and marsh rabbits.

Recreation

Visitors use the refuge for fishing, hunting, wildlife observation, and paddling. The River Trail near the refuge headquarters at Fowler's Bluff provides boardwalk access through hardwood hammock, and the Shell Mound unit features an ancient Native American shell midden and a fishing pier near Cedar Key.

Management

The refuge is administered together with Cedar Keys National Wildlife Refuge. Prescribed burning, hydrological restoration, and invasive-species control are used to maintain the floodplain ecosystem, one of the largest undeveloped river delta systems in the United States.
