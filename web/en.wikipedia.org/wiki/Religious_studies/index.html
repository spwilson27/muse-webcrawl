Religious studies

Religious studies, also known as the study of religion, is the academic field devoted to research into religious beliefs, behaviours and institutions. Unlike theology, it approaches religion from a (putatively) neutral, interdisciplinary standpoint, drawing on history, anthropology, sociology, psychology, philosophy and philology.

History

The discipline developed in 19th-century Europe, with scholars such as Max Müller founding the comparative study of religion. In Germany it was known as Religionswissenschaft, and the phenomenological approach of Gerardus van der Leeuw and Mircea Eliade influenced 20th-century curricula, especially in North America, where departments expanded after the 1960s.

Approaches

Scholars use historical, comparative, ethnographic and textual methods. Debates in the field concern the definition of 'religion' itself, insider–outsider problems, and the legacy of colonial-era classifications such as the 'world religions' paradigm.
