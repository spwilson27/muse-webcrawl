Stirling's approximation

In mathematics, Stirling's approximation (or Stirling's formula) is an asymptotic approximation for factorials. It is a good approximation, leading to accurate results even for small values of n. It is named after James Stirling, though a related result was first announced by Abraham de Moivre.

n! ∼ √(2πn) (n/e)n

More precisely, the approximation states that

limn→∞ n! / (√(2πn) (n/e)n) = 1.

Equivalently, taking logarithms gives ln(n!) ≈ n ln n − n + ½ ln(2πn), a form often more convenient in applications such as statistical mechanics, where factorials of enormous numbers arise in counting microstates.

Derivation

Roughly speaking, the approximation can be derived by comparing the sum ln(n!) = Σ ln k with the integral of ln x, using the trapezoidal rule or the Euler–Maclaurin formula. The constant √(2π) can be obtained from the Wallis product or from the central limit theorem's normalisation of the normal distribution.

Stirling series

A refinable asymptotic expansion, the Stirling series, gives ln(n!) = n ln n − n + ½ ln(2πn) + 1/(12n) − 1/(360n3) + ..., allowing the approximation to be made as accurate as desired for large n.

Applications

Stirling's approximation is used throughout combinatorics, probability (for example in approximations to the binomial distribution), statistical mechanics, and the analysis of algorithms involving factorials and binomial coefficients.
