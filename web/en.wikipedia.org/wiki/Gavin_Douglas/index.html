Gavin Douglas (c. 1474 – September 1522) was a Scottish bishop, makar and translator. He was a prominent figure in the politics of early sixteenth-century Scotland and, as a poet, is counted among the great Scottish Chaucerians alongside William Dunbar and Robert Henryson.

The third son of Archibald Douglas, 5th Earl of Angus ("Bell the Cat"), Gavin Douglas studied at St Andrews and became provost of St Giles in Edinburgh in 1501. After James IV's death at Flodden in 1513 he became embroiled in the struggles of the minority of James V; he was made bishop of Dunkeld in 1515, but his family's feuds and his English connections eventually drove him into exile in London, where he died of the plague in 1522.

Works

Douglas's greatest achievement is the Eneados, completed in 1513: the first complete translation of Virgil's Aeneid into any Anglic language (Middle Scots), including the thirteenth book by Mapheus Vegius, each book with an original prologue. His best-known original poem is The Palice of Honour (c. 1501), a dream allegory. Ezra Pound praised the Eneados highly, considering Douglas's version in some respects superior to later translations.
