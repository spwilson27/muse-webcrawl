Child abuse

Child abuse is physical, sexual, or psychological maltreatment or neglect of a child or children, especially by a parent or a caregiver. Child abuse may include any act or failure to act by a parent or caregiver that results in actual or potential harm to a child and can occur in a child's home, or in organisations, schools, or communities the child interacts with. The terms child abuse and child maltreatment are often used interchangeably, although some researchers distinguish between them, treating child maltreatment as an umbrella term covering neglect, exploitation, and trafficking.

Types

Four main categories are generally recognised. Physical abuse involves deliberate injury, such as hitting, shaking or burning. Sexual abuse is any sexual act with a child, including exploitation. Emotional or psychological abuse includes constant criticism, threats, rejection and failure to provide a supportive environment. Neglect is the failure to provide for a child's basic needs, including food, shelter, supervision, education and medical care, and is the most common form of maltreatment reported in many jurisdictions.

Prevalence and effects

The World Health Organization estimates that a large share of the world's children aged 2–17 experience violence each year, and that child maltreatment is a leading contributor to mental disorders, chronic disease and premature death. Long-term consequences documented in research, including the Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE) studies, include depression, post-traumatic stress disorder, substance misuse and increased risk of physical illnesses in adulthood.

Prevention and law

Nearly all countries have laws against child abuse, mandatory reporting requirements for professionals, and child protective services empowered to investigate and intervene. The United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989) obliges states to protect children from all forms of violence. Prevention strategies include home-visiting programmes, parenting support, public education and poverty reduction.
