Goodwill (accounting)

In accounting, goodwill is an intangible asset recognised when a firm is purchased as a going concern. It reflects the excess of the purchase price over the fair value of the identifiable net assets acquired, and is attributed to attributes such as brand reputation, a loyal customer base, good employee relations, and proprietary technology that cannot be separately identified and sold.

Recognition and measurement

Goodwill arises only in a business combination: it is calculated as the consideration transferred, plus any non-controlling interest, minus the fair value of the identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed. Internally generated goodwill — the value a business builds in its own name — is never recognised on the balance sheet under IFRS or US GAAP.

Impairment

Under modern standards (IAS 36 and ASC 350), goodwill is not amortised but must be tested for impairment at least annually at the level of the cash-generating unit to which it belongs. If the unit's carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount, the difference is written off as an impairment loss through profit or loss, and such write-downs cannot later be reversed. Major write-downs — such as those following the acquisitions of AOL by Time Warner or of Autonomy by Hewlett-Packard — frequently attract litigation and regulatory scrutiny.
