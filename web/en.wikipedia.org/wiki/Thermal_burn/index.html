Thermal burn
Specialty: Emergency medicine, dermatology
Causes: Heat, flame, steam, hot liquids or surfaces

A thermal burn is a type of burn resulting from making contact with heated objects, such as boiling water, steam, hot cooking oil, fire, or other hot liquids and surfaces. Thermal burns are the most common type of burn injury and occur when the skin or other tissues are damaged by heat.

Classification

Burns are classically classified by depth. First-degree burns are superficial, affecting only the epidermis, and typically heal without scarring. Second-degree (partial-thickness) burns extend into the dermis and often blister. Third-degree (full-thickness) burns destroy both layers of skin and may appear white, charred, or leathery. Fourth-degree burns extend into underlying fat, muscle, or bone.

Causes

Common causes include scalds from hot liquids and steam, flame burns from fires, contact burns from hot objects, and flash burns from explosions. Scalds are especially common in young children and older adults.

Treatment

Immediate first aid involves stopping the burning process and cooling the burn with cool running water. Severe burns require emergency medical care, fluid resuscitation, wound care, infection prevention, and sometimes skin grafting. Complications can include hypovolemic shock, infection, and scarring.
