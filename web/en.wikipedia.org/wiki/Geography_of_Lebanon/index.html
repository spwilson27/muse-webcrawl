The geography of Lebanon describes a small country on the eastern shore of the Mediterranean Sea, bordered by Syria to the north and east and Israel to the south. Lebanon is roughly 217 km long from north to south and between 32 and 56 km wide, giving it a total area of about 10,452 square kilometres.

Physical geography

The country is dominated by two parallel mountain ranges running north to south: the Lebanon Mountains (Mount Lebanon) along the coast and the Anti-Lebanon range along the eastern border. Between them lies the fertile Beqaa Valley, an agricultural heartland drained by the Litani River, the longest river entirely within Lebanon. The highest peak is Qurnat as Sawda', at 3,088 metres, in the northern Lebanon range.

Climate

Lebanon has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and cool, rainy winters. The mountains receive heavy precipitation, including snow, which supports cedar forests and ski resorts. The coastal plain is narrow and densely populated, containing the major cities of Beirut, Tripoli, Sidon, and Tyre.
