Pasiphaë (Greek: Πασιφάη, "wide-shining") was a queen of Crete and a figure of Greek mythology. The daughter of the sun god Helios and the Oceanid Perseis, and sister of the witch Circe, she married King Minos of Crete. She is best known as the mother of the Minotaur.

Mythology

According to the myth, Minos prayed to Poseidon for a sign of divine favour and the god sent a magnificent white bull from the sea. Minos was supposed to sacrifice the bull to Poseidon but, captivated by its beauty, kept it and sacrificed another in its place. In revenge, Poseidon (in some versions acting through Aphrodite) caused Pasiphaë to fall in love with the bull. The craftsman Daedalus built her a hollow wooden cow covered in hide, in which she concealed herself to mate with the animal. From this union she gave birth to the Minotaur — a creature with the head of a bull and the body of a man — whom Minos confined in the Labyrinth.

Family

By Minos, Pasiphaë was the mother of Acacallis, Androgeus, Ariadne, Deucalion, Phaedra, Glaucus, Catreus, and Xenodice. Her daughters Ariadne and Phaedra became central figures in their own myths: Ariadne helped Theseus escape the Labyrinth, and Phaedra's disastrous love for her stepson Hippolytus is the subject of Euripides' tragedy.

Cult and interpretation

Pasiphaë was worshipped as an oracular goddess at Thalamae in Laconia, where she was identified with Ino and sometimes with Selene, the moon. Her name, "wide-shining" or "all-bright", has been taken to suggest an origin as a moon goddess, fitting her descent from Helios, the sun.
