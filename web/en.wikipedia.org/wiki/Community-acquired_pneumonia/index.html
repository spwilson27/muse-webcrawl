Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) is an acute infection of the lung parenchyma acquired outside a hospital or healthcare setting — conventionally, pneumonia that develops in a person who has not been hospitalised within the preceding 48 hours and is not resident in a long-term care facility. It is one of the most common infectious diseases worldwide and a leading cause of death, particularly in young children and older adults.

Signs and symptoms

Typical presentations include fever, productive cough, breathlessness, pleuritic chest pain, and crackles or bronchial breathing on auscultation; older patients may present mainly with confusion or functional decline. A new infiltrate on chest radiography distinguishes pneumonia from acute bronchitis.

Causes

The commonest bacterial cause is Streptococcus pneumoniae, followed by Haemophilus influenzae, Moraxella catarrhalis, and Staphylococcus aureus. "Atypical" organisms include Mycoplasma pneumoniae, Chlamydophila pneumoniae, and Legionella pneumophila. Respiratory viruses — influenza, respiratory syncytial virus, and SARS-CoV-2 among them — account for a substantial share, alone or in co-infection. In many cases no pathogen is identified.

Severity assessment and treatment

Severity scores such as CURB-65 (Confusion, Urea, Respiratory rate, Blood pressure, age ≥65) and the Pneumonia Severity Index guide decisions on outpatient versus hospital or intensive-care treatment. Management is with empiric antibiotics chosen by severity and local guidance (for example a beta-lactam, macrolide, or respiratory fluoroquinolone), supportive care, and review at 48 hours. Prevention includes pneumococcal, influenza, and COVID-19 vaccination and smoking cessation.
