Grímsvötn is an active volcano with a fissure system located in Vatnajökull National Park in Iceland. It lies beneath the northwestern side of the Vatnajökull ice cap and covers a caldera lake of the same name. It is one of Iceland's most active volcanoes, erupting frequently, and most of its eruptions are subglacial.

Activity

Grímsvötn's eruptions can melt large volumes of ice and trigger jökulhlaups — glacial outburst floods — on the Skeiðarársandur plain to the south. A major eruption and flood in 1996, associated with the Gjálp fissure, produced one of the largest floods of the 20th century and damaged bridges on Iceland's ring road. The volcano's most recent eruption, in May 2011, was its largest in a century, sending an ash plume about 20 km high and briefly disrupting air travel in northwestern Europe.

The subglacial lake

Geothermal heat continuously melts ice in the caldera, maintaining a lake sealed beneath an ice shelf. Water accumulates until it lifts the ice dam and drains in a jökulhlaup. The caldera lake is also notable for the discovery of bacteria living in its extreme, isolated environment.
