Ignatian spirituality

Ignatian spirituality, also known as Jesuit spirituality, is a Catholic spirituality founded on the experiences of Ignatius of Loyola (1491–1556), founder of the Society of Jesus. Its central text is Ignatius's Spiritual Exercises, a manual of meditations, prayers and contemplative practices.

Principles

Key themes include "finding God in all things," the daily Examen (a prayerful review of the day), discernment of spirits, and the ideal of being "contemplatives in action." Practitioners are encouraged to notice how God is present in ordinary work and relationships, not only in formal prayer.

The Spiritual Exercises

The Exercises are typically made as a thirty-day retreat or in daily life over several months, structured in four "weeks" moving through sin and mercy, the life of Christ, his Passion, and the Resurrection.
