Human performance modeling

Human performance modeling (HPM) is a method of quantifying human behaviour, cognition and performance, typically within computer models, in order to predict how people will interact with systems and environments. It is used in human factors engineering, ergonomics and the design of safety-critical systems.

Approaches

Models range from simple predictive laws, such as Fitts's law for the time required to move to a target, to cognitive architectures such as ACT-R and GOMS, which simulate perception, memory and decision-making. Task network models break work into sequences of tasks to estimate workload and completion times.

Applications

Human performance models inform the design of aircraft cockpits, nuclear power plant control rooms, medical devices and military systems, allowing designers to detect overload, bottlenecks and error-prone procedures before systems are built.
