Paul the Deacon

Paul the Deacon (c. 720 – 13 April 799), born Paulus Diaconus, was a Lombard Benedictine monk, scribe and historian best known for his History of the Lombards (Historia Langobardorum).

Bornc. 720, Cividale del Friuli
Died13 April 799, Monte Cassino
OccupationMonk, historian

Life

Paul served at the court of the Lombard king Desiderius and later spent time at the court of Charlemagne in Francia, where he was a valued scholar, before retiring to the monastery of Monte Cassino.

Works

His Historia Langobardorum, written in the 790s, is the principal narrative source for the Lombards from their legendary origins to 744. He also compiled a history of the bishops of Metz, wrote homilies and poetry, and is credited with the hymn Ut queant laxis, whose opening syllables later gave their names to the musical notes ut (do), re, mi, fa, sol and la.
