The Codex Maya of Mexico (Spanish: Códice Maya de México), formerly known as the Grolier Codex, is a Maya screenfold codex that surfaced in the 1970s and was long suspected of being a forgery. Extensive analysis announced in 2018 authenticated it as the oldest surviving book of the Americas, dating to the early 13th century.

Content

The surviving eleven pages form part of a Venus almanac: a calendar tracking the movements of the planet Venus, with deities and day signs accompanying each station of the planet's 584-day synodic cycle. The full codex is estimated to have had around twenty pages.

Authenticity

The codex was displayed at the Grolier Club in New York in 1971 after reportedly being found in a cave in Chiapas. Its unusual provenance led scholars to doubt it for decades. Radiocarbon dating of the paper, analysis of pigments (including Maya blue), and iconographic study eventually confirmed its age, and Mexico's Instituto Nacional de Antropología e Historia officially declared it authentic in 2018.
