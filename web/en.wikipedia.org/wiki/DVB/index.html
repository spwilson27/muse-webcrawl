DVB

Digital Video Broadcasting (DVB) is a set of international open standards for digital television maintained by the DVB Project, an industry consortium founded in 1993 and based in Geneva, Switzerland. DVB standards are published by ETSI and are used for terrestrial, satellite, cable and IPTV distribution in most of the world outside North America, China and Japan.

Standards

  • DVB-S / DVB-S2 — satellite broadcasting
  • DVB-T / DVB-T2 — terrestrial broadcasting
  • DVB-C / DVB-C2 — cable
  • DVB-H — handheld/mobile (now largely obsolete)
  • DVB-IPTV — delivery over IP networks

All DVB systems use the MPEG transport stream to carry audio, video and data, with conditional access provided through the DVB Common Scrambling Algorithm and Simulcrypt/CA systems.

Adoption

DVB standards were first deployed in the mid-1990s and became the foundation of digital television transitions in Europe, Australia, much of Asia, Africa and Latin America. By the 2010s, second-generation standards (DVB-S2, DVB-T2, DVB-C2) offered roughly 30–50% more capacity using LDPC/BCH forward error correction and advanced modulation.
