Multiplayer video game

A multiplayer video game is a video game in which more than one person can play in the same game environment at the same time, either locally (on the same device or through a local network) or online via the internet. Multiplayer games contrast with single-player games, in which the player competes against computer-controlled opponents or a narrative challenge.

History

Some of the earliest video games, such as Tennis for Two (1958) and Spacewar! (1962), were multiplayer. Multiuser dungeons (MUDs) on university networks in the late 1970s and 1980s pioneered persistent online play, and networked first-person shooters such as Doom (1993) and Quake (1996) popularized deathmatch play over local networks and the internet. The growth of broadband internet enabled massively multiplayer online games (MMOs) such as Ultima Online (1997), EverQuest (1999), and World of Warcraft (2004), supporting thousands of simultaneous players in persistent worlds.

Modes of play

  • Competitive — players oppose one another individually or in teams (deathmatch, battle royale, capture the flag, versus fighting games).
  • Cooperative — players work together against computer-controlled challenges.
  • Split screen / shared screen — multiple players on one display, common on consoles.
  • Hotseat and asynchronous — players take turns on the same device, or play at different times, as in play-by-mail and mobile word games.
  • Massively multiplayer — large persistent online worlds with hundreds to thousands of players.

Social and cultural aspects

Multiplayer games underpin esports, streaming culture, and online communities, and have given rise to guilds, clans, virtual economies, and in-game social spaces. They also present design and moderation challenges, including cheating, griefing, latency management, and matchmaking balance.
