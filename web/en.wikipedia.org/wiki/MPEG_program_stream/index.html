MPEG program stream (PS) is a container format for multiplexing digital audio, video and other data, defined in the MPEG-1 and MPEG-2 standards (ISO/IEC 11172-1 and ISO/IEC 13818-1 Part 1, Systems). It is designed for reasonably reliable media such as discs, and is the format used by DVD-Video and HD DVD.

Structure

A program stream consists of a sequence of packs, each beginning with a pack header containing timing information (the System Clock Reference) and followed by packetised elementary stream (PES) packets belonging to individual video, audio or private streams. Unlike the fixed 188-byte packets of the MPEG transport stream, program stream packets are variable in length, which makes the format efficient for storage but less suitable for error-prone broadcast channels.

Coding details

Streams are identified by stream IDs, and PES packets carry presentation and decoding timestamps that keep audio and video synchronised. DVD .VOB files and HD DVD .EVO files are enhanced forms of the program stream with additional private streams for subtitles, menus and copy protection.
