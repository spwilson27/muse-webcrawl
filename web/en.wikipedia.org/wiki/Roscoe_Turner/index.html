Roscoe Turner

Born: 29 September 1895, Corinth, Mississippi
Died: 23 June 1970

Roscoe Turner (1895–1970) was an American aviator, one of the most flamboyant and successful air racers of the 1930s. Recognisable by his waxed moustache, military-style uniform and pet lion cub "Gilmore", he was a three-time winner of the Thompson Trophy and won the Bendix Trophy in 1933.

Career

Turner learned to fly after serving in the First World War era Army, then barnstormed and worked in film aviation in the 1920s. In the 1930s he dominated closed-course and cross-country racing, often flying the Turner-Laird and Wedell-Williams racers. In 1934 he and his crew flew a Boeing 247 to third place in the MacRobertson Air Race from England to Australia. He set several transcontinental speed records.

Later life

Turner founded an aviation company in Indianapolis, ran a flight school and charter operation, and remained a public figure in American aviation. Indianapolis International Airport's former terminal area and the Roscoe Turner Airport in Corinth, Mississippi, commemorate him.
