Ann Druyan

Born: June 13, 1949, Queens, New York, US
Occupation: Writer, producer
Spouse: Carl Sagan (m. 1981; died 1996)

Ann Druyan (born June 13, 1949) is an American documentary producer and director specializing in the communication of science. She co-wrote the 1980 PBS documentary series Cosmos: A Personal Voyage with Carl Sagan, whom she later married, and was the creator and producer of its successors Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey (2014) and Cosmos: Possible Worlds (2020).

Career

Druyan served as creative director of NASA's Voyager Interstellar Message Project, selecting the music and images included on the Voyager Golden Record carried aboard the Voyager spacecraft launched in 1977. With Sagan she co-wrote the books Comet (1985) and Shadows of Forgotten Ancestors (1992), and co-wrote the story for the 1997 film Contact, based on Sagan's novel. She is also the author of Cosmos: Possible Worlds (2020), companion to the television series.

Awards and honours

Druyan won a Primetime Emmy Award and a Peabody Award for her work on Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey, and received the National Geographic Society's Hubbard Medal. Cosmos: Possible Worlds won a Peabody Award in 2021. She co-founded Cosmos Studios to produce science-based entertainment, and has been an advocate for science education, secular humanism and nuclear disarmament. The asteroid 4970 Druyan is named in her honour.

Personal life

Druyan married Carl Sagan in 1981; they had two children, including the writer Sasha Sagan. Their collaboration, and the famous story of Druyan's recorded brainwaves on the Golden Record made shortly after their engagement, has become a well-known part of the Voyager story.
