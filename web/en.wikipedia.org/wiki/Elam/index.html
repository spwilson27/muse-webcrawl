Elam

Elam (Elamite: Haltamti) was an ancient civilization centred in the far west and southwest of modern-day Iran, stretching from the lowlands of Khuzestan and Ilam provinces into the Zagros highlands. One of the oldest civilizations of the ancient Near East, Elam existed from roughly 2700 BC until its conquest by the Achaemenid Empire in the sixth century BC.

History

Elamite history is conventionally divided into the Old Elamite (c. 2700–1500 BC), Middle Elamite (c. 1500–1100 BC) and Neo-Elamite (c. 1100–539 BC) periods. The Elamites were in near-constant contact — commercial, cultural and military — with the peoples of Mesopotamia, including Sumer, Akkad, Babylon and Assyria. Elamite kings such as Shutruk-Nakhunte carried off Babylonian monuments, including the Code of Hammurabi stele, to Susa. In 647 BC the Assyrian king Ashurbanipal sacked Susa; although Elam recovered in reduced form, it was absorbed into the Persian Achaemenid Empire under Cyrus the Great.

Language and writing

The Elamite language is a language isolate unrelated to the Semitic or Indo-European languages around it. It was written first in a proto-Elamite script, later in a linear script and then in cuneiform adapted from Mesopotamia. Elamite remained an official language of the Achaemenid Empire, and thousands of Elamite administrative tablets survive from Persepolis.

Culture

Elamite religion centred on gods such as Inshushinak, patron of Susa, and the goddess Kiririsha. The great ziggurat of Chogha Zanbil, built by Untash-Napirisha around 1250 BC, is among the best-preserved monuments of the Elamite world.
