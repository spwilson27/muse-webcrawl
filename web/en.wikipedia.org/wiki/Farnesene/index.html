Farnesene

Farnesene refers to a set of six sesquiterpene isomers with the molecular formula C15H24, classified as α-farnesene and β-farnesene according to the position of a double bond. The farnesenes occur naturally in the essential oils of many plants and are notable as insect pheromones and as a platform chemical for renewable fuels and materials.

Chemical formula:C15H24
Class:Sesquiterpenes
Precursor:Farnesyl pyrophosphate
Related:Farnesol, farnesane

Occurrence and roles

α-Farnesene is a major volatile of apple peel, where its oxidation products are implicated in the storage disorder superficial scald. Many insects use farnesenes in communication: (E)-β-farnesene is the principal component of the alarm pheromone released by aphids when attacked by predators, warning nearby aphids to disperse. Plants such as wild potato release (E)-β-farnesene to repel aphids, and transgenic plants engineered to emit it have been tested as a pest-management strategy.

Industrial use

Because farnesene can be produced by fermenting sugars with engineered yeast, it became an early product of industrial synthetic biology. Hydrogenation yields farnesane, a renewable diesel and jet-fuel blendstock; farnesene is also polymerised and derivatised into lubricants, cosmetics ingredients (via squalane production chains) and speciality chemicals. The biotechnology company Amyris pioneered large-scale farnesene fermentation in the 2000s–2010s.
