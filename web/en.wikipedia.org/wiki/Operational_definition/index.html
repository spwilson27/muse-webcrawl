Operational definition

An operational definition specifies a concept in terms of the concrete, replicable procedures used to measure it. Rather than defining a term by its essence, an operational definition states how the variable is observed, measured, or manipulated in a particular study. For example, "hunger" might be operationally defined as the number of hours since a subject last ate.

Origins

The idea is associated with the physicist Percy Williams Bridgman, whose 1927 book The Logic of Modern Physics introduced operationalism: the meaning of a scientific concept is synonymous with the set of operations used to measure it. Bridgman's approach influenced behaviourist psychology and the philosophy of science more broadly.

Use in research

Operational definitions are standard in experimental psychology, education research and the social sciences, where abstract constructs such as intelligence, anxiety or socioeconomic status must be rendered measurable. A test score, a questionnaire scale, or a behavioural count can each serve as the operational definition of a construct.

Criticism

Critics note that a single construct can be operationalised in many incompatible ways, and that reducing a concept to one measurement risks confusing the measure with the thing measured. Nonetheless, explicit operational definitions remain essential for replication and for comparing results across studies.
