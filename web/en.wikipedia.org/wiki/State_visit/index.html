State visit

A state visit is a formal visit by a head of state to a foreign country, at the invitation of the head of state of that foreign country, who is also the official host for the duration of the state visit. Speaking for the host, it is generally called a state reception. State visits are considered the highest expression of friendly bilateral relations between two sovereign states, and are generally characterised by an emphasis on official public ceremonies.

Less formal visits than a state visit to another country with a lesser emphasis on ceremonial events, by either a head of state or a head of government, are termed official visits, working visits, or guest-of-government visits. In parliamentary democracies, heads of state may formally issue and accept invitations; however, they are often at the direction of the government.

Components of a state visit

State visits typically involve some or all of the following components, which vary by host country:

  • A flag-raising or arrival ceremony, often including a 21-gun salute and the playing of both national anthems
  • A review of an honour guard or guard of honour
  • A state banquet or state dinner hosted by the head of state, where both leaders deliver speeches
  • An exchange of gifts and decorations
  • A visit to the legislature of the host country, sometimes including an address to a joint session
  • Visits to national landmarks, war memorials and wreath-laying ceremonies
  • Cultural events celebrating the links between the two nations

By country

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, a state visit is a royal event involving the King and other members of the royal family. An arrival ceremony usually takes place on Horse Guards Parade, or at Windsor Castle, with an inspection of the guard of honour, followed by a carriage procession and a state banquet at Buckingham Palace. Generally only two state visits are hosted per year; incoming visits by commonwealth realms' governors-general are not classed as state visits.

United States

In the United States, state visits are the preserve of the president as head of state. They include a South Lawn arrival ceremony at the White House with military honours, a 21-gun salute, a state dinner, and often an address to a joint meeting of Congress. Visits by heads of government, such as prime ministers, are termed official visits instead.

France

In France, state visits (visites d'État) are hosted by the President of the Republic and include a ceremony at the Arc de Triomphe, a procession down the Champs-Élysées, and a state dinner at the Élysée Palace.

History

The practice developed from the personal meetings of monarchs in earlier centuries, such as the Field of the Cloth of Gold in 1520. The modern institution of a programme of reciprocal state visits between heads of state crystallised in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries as diplomacy became more public and ceremonial protocol was standardised.
