Valentino Mazzola


Born: 26 January 1919, Cassano d'Adda, Italy
Died: 4 May 1949 (aged 30), Superga, Italy
Position: Attacking midfielder / forward

Valentino Mazzola (26 January 1919 – 4 May 1949) was an Italian footballer who played as an attacking midfielder or forward. He was the captain and symbol of the "Grande Torino", the Torino side that dominated Italian football in the 1940s, winning five consecutive Serie A titles.

Career

Mazzola began his career with Alfa Romeo and Venezia, winning the Coppa Italia with Venice in 1941, before joining Torino in 1942. Renowned for his stamina, technique, leadership and trademark rolled-up sleeves when rallying his teammates, he led Torino to league titles in 1943 and 1946–1949, and won 12 caps for Italy, scoring 4 goals.

Death

Mazzola died in the Superga air disaster of 4 May 1949, when the aircraft carrying the Torino team home from a friendly in Lisbon struck the retaining wall of the Basilica of Superga above Turin, killing all 31 people aboard. His sons Sandro and Ferruccio Mazzola both became professional footballers; Sandro became an Inter Milan legend.
