Small Is Beautiful

Small Is Beautiful: A Study of Economics As If People Mattered is a collection of essays published in 1973 by German-born British economist E. F. Schumacher. The title originates from a phrase used by his teacher Leopold Kohr. It is often used to champion small, appropriate technologies that are believed to empower people more, in contrast to phrases such as "bigger is better".

Small Is Beautiful is ranked among the 100 most influential books published since World War II by The Times Literary Supplement. A further edition with commentaries was published in 1999.

Overview

The book is divided into four parts: "The Modern World", "Resources", "The Third World", and "Organization and Ownership". Schumacher challenges mainstream economic orthodoxies centred on ever-increasing production and consumption, arguing that modern economies have become dehumanised and unsustainable.

One of the main arguments is that we cannot consider the problem of production solved: a consumer society treats non-renewable resources and the natural environment as mere income items rather than capital. Schumacher advocates "intermediate technology" or "appropriate technology" — methods and machines cheap enough to be widely accessible, suitable for small-scale application, and encouraging of human creativity. His chapter on "Buddhist economics" contrasts Western materialist economics with an approach emphasising sufficiency and right livelihood.

Influence

The book became an important text for the environmental and appropriate-technology movements and helped popularise the idea that economic systems should serve human well-being rather than growth for its own sake.
