Edson Arantes do Nascimento (23 October 1940 – 29 December 2022), better known as Pelé, was a Brazilian professional footballer who played as a forward. Widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time, he was among the most successful and popular sports figures of the 20th century; in 1999 he was named Athlete of the Century by the International Olympic Committee.

Career

Pelé joined Santos at 15 and burst onto the world stage at the 1958 World Cup in Sweden, aged 17, scoring twice in the final against the hosts. With Brazil he won three World Cups (1958, 1962, and 1970) — a record for a player — and the 1970 team, in which he was the centrepiece, is often cited as the greatest national side ever. For Santos he scored the bulk of his career goals, winning two Copa Libertadores and two Intercontinental Cups. Retiring from Brazilian football in 1974, he joined the New York Cosmos of the NASL, enormously raising the sport's profile in the United States, before his final retirement in 1977. Guinness World Records credits him with 1,279 goals in 1,363 games including friendlies; in official matches his tally is usually reckoned at more than 750.

Later life and legacy

After football Pelé became a global ambassador for the sport and for UNICEF, and served as Brazil's Minister of Sport (1995–1998). He died of complications of colon cancer in December 2022, prompting a period of national mourning in Brazil and tributes worldwide; his funeral and wake were held at Santos's Vila Belmiro stadium, where he had begun his career.
