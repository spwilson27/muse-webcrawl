The Cossidae, the cossid millers or carpenter millers, are a family of moths in the order Lepidoptera. The family contains roughly 700 described species distributed around the world, mostly in the Old World tropics and Australia. Cossids are generally medium to very large moths with stout, hairy bodies and dull grey or brown wings; some, such as the Australian witjuti grub moths (genus Endoxyla), are among the largest moths in the world, with wingspans exceeding 20 cm.

Biology

The larvae are typically wood-borers: caterpillars tunnel into the trunks and branches of trees and shrubs, where they may feed for two or more years before pupating. Well-known examples include the goat moth (Cossus cossus) in Europe, whose reddish larvae smell faintly of goat, and the leopard moth (Zeuzera pyrina). Because of their tunnelling, several cossids are pests of orchards, timber trees and sugarcane. In Australia, the large larvae of some species — the famous witchetty grubs — were a traditional food for Aboriginal peoples.

Subfamilies

The family is divided into several subfamilies, including Cossinae, Zeuzerinae, Cossulinae, Hypoptinae and Chilecomadiinae. Adults mostly do not feed and are short-lived; they are nocturnal and come to lights.
