Flight number

A flight number, or flight designator, is a code for an airline service consisting of an airline's two-character IATA designator (or three-character ICAO designator) followed by a number of one to four digits — for example, UA 175 or BA249. It identifies a specific scheduled service between two airports, typically including the direction and approximate time of day of the flight.

FormatAirline code + 1–4 digit number
ExampleDL 106, LH 401

Conventions

By long-standing convention, eastbound and northbound flights are usually assigned even numbers and westbound and southbound flights odd numbers, and lower numbers are often given to an airline's most prestigious or longest-haul routes — many airlines reserve flight number 1 for a flagship service. The return leg of a route typically receives the next number after the outbound flight. Numbers are also reused under codeshare agreements, where marketing carriers sell seats on an operating carrier's flight under their own, different flight numbers.

Retired numbers

After fatal accidents, airlines commonly retire the flight number involved, out of respect for the victims; examples include the numbers of flights destroyed in major disasters, which are rarely used again by the same carrier. Flight numbers for special or charter services, ferry flights, and repositioning flights are often allocated from reserved ranges (for example, numbers above 3000 or in the 8000s and 9000s).
