Timbers Army

The Timbers Army is the principal organised supporters' group of the Portland Timbers, a Major League Soccer club based in Portland, Oregon. Occupying the north-end sections of Providence Park — especially Section 107 — the Army is known across North American soccer for its continuous chanting, elaborate hand-painted tifo displays, and the acronym RCTID ("Rose City 'Til I Die").

History

The group traces its origin to 2001, when fans of the minor-league Portland Timbers of the USL First Division began gathering as the Cascade Rangers. Renamed the Timbers Army in 2002, the group grew alongside the club through the USL years, helping demonstrate the depth of Portland's soccer culture during the city's successful bid for an MLS franchise, which began play in 2011. Membership is informal — supporters standing in the north end are considered part of the Army — while the affiliated 107 Independent Supporters' Trust (107IST) handles organisation, away travel, and community work.

Culture

The Army maintains a "no pyro, no party" ethos balanced by self-policing codes of conduct and an explicitly anti-racist, inclusive stance. Capos lead chants from stands at the front of the section, and volunteers spend weeks painting tifos that can cover the entire north end. The group has a celebrated rivalry with Seattle's Emerald City Supporters and Vancouver's Southsiders, contesting the Cascadia Cup, a supporter-created trophy that predates MLS involvement in the region. Timbers Army members also founded and support the fan culture around the Portland Thorns, where the corresponding group is the Rose City Riveters.

Activism

The Army has periodically clashed with league and club management over supporter rights, most prominently in 2019, when a league ban on the Iron Front symbol — displayed by many fans as an anti-fascist emblem — prompted silent protests and a partial walkout before the policy was reversed for Portland's supporters.
