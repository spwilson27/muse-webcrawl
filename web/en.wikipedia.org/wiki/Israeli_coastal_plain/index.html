Israeli coastal plain

The Israeli coastal plain is the narrow coastal plain along Israel's Mediterranean Sea coast, stretching about 187 kilometres from the Gaza Strip in the south to the Lebanese border in the north. It is home to a large majority of Israel's population and most of its industry and agriculture.

Geography

The plain widens from only a few kilometres in the north to about 40 kilometres in the south. It is traditionally divided into sections including the Plain of Sharon in the centre, the Judean plain in the south and the plain of Acre in the north. Sand dunes, kurkar (aeolianite) ridges and alluvial soils characterise its landscape. Major cities on the plain include Tel Aviv, Haifa, Ashdod, Ashkelon, Netanya and Rishon LeZion.

History

In antiquity the coastal plain carried the Via Maris, the trade route linking Egypt with Syria and Mesopotamia, and was contested among Philistines, Israelites, and successive empires. Citrus groves planted in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries made the region famous for Jaffa oranges.
