Mongoose

A mongoose is a small terrestrial carnivorous mammal belonging to the family Herpestidae. This family is currently split into two subfamilies, the Herpestinae and the Mungotinae. The Herpestinae comprises 23 living species that are native to southern Europe, Africa and Asia, whereas the Mungotinae comprises 11 species native to Africa. Mongooses are long-bodied, short-legged animals with small rounded ears, a pointed snout and a long, tapering tail; most are grizzled or brindled, ranging from a few hundred grams (the common dwarf mongoose) to several kilograms (the Egyptian mongoose and white-tailed mongoose).

A banded mongoose (Mungos mungo), a social Mungotinae species of Africa.

Behaviour and diet

Herpestinae species are mostly solitary, while Mungotinae species — such as meerkats, banded mongooses and dwarf mongooses — live in social groups of up to 50 individuals, cooperating in foraging, sentinel duty and pup-rearing. Mongooses feed on insects, crabs, earthworms, lizards, snakes, birds and rodents; some species crack eggs and shellfish by throwing them against rocks. Several species are famous for attacking venomous snakes such as cobras: agility, thick fur and modified acetylcholine receptors that reduce the effect of neurotoxic venom give them protection, though they are not fully immune.

Relationship with humans

Mongooses are celebrated in folklore — most famously Rikki-Tikki-Tavi in Rudyard Kipling's The Jungle Book (1894) — and in ancient Egypt the Egyptian mongoose was venerated. However, mongooses introduced to islands such as Hawaii, Jamaica and other Caribbean islands to control rats and snakes in sugarcane fields became destructive invasive predators of native birds, reptiles and amphibians, and in several places are a reservoir of rabies and leptospirosis. Importation is therefore banned or strictly controlled in countries such as the United States, Australia and New Zealand.

Taxonomy

The family Herpestidae belongs to the feliform side of Carnivora, and its closest living relatives are the hyenas (family Hyaenidae) and the Malagasy carnivorans of the family Eupleridae, which were once included among the mongooses. Fossil herpestids date back to the Oligocene, about 30 million years ago.
