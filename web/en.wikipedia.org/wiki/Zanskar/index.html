Zanskar

Zanskar is a high-altitude semi-desert region in the Kargil district of Ladakh, in northern India. It covers roughly 7,000 square kilometres at elevations between about 3,500 and 7,000 metres, and is drained by the Zanskar River, a tributary of the Indus. The administrative centre is Padum.

Geography

Zanskar is bounded by the Zanskar Range, part of the Great Himalayas, which separates it from the Suru valley and Kargil to the north, and from Lahaul in Himachal Pradesh to the south. The region is one of the coldest inhabited places in India; in winter, when the passes close with snow, the only route out is the famous Chadar trek, walking on the frozen surface of the Zanskar River.

History

Historically Zanskar formed a small Buddhist kingdom, at times independent and at times subordinate to Ladakh. Its royal line traced descent from the kings of Ladakh, and the region's monasteries preserved strong ties to Tibetan Buddhism. Zanskar was incorporated into the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir in the 19th century after the Dogra conquests.

Culture and religion

The population is roughly evenly divided between Buddhists, concentrated in the central valley, and Muslims, largely in Padum and the north. Important monasteries include Karsha, Phugtal (built into a cliffside cave), Stongdey, Sani and Zangla. Agriculture is based on barley, wheat, peas and apricots, grown during the short summer season.

Tourism

Zanskar attracts trekkers and rafters; the Zanskar River is noted for its dramatic gorge and white-water rafting. A road over the Pensi La connects it to Kargil, and newer road projects aim to provide year-round access to Himachal Pradesh.
