Moving Picture Experts Group (MPEG)
Founded1988
FoundersLeonardo Chiariglione, Hiroshi Yasuda
Parent bodiesISO/IEC JTC 1 (Working Group until 2020 restructuring)
StandardsMPEG-1, MPEG-2, MPEG-4, H.264/AVC, HEVC, MP3

The Moving Picture Experts Group (MPEG) is a working group of experts established in 1988 under ISO and IEC to set standards for the compression and transmission of audio and video. Chaired from its founding by Leonardo Chiariglione, MPEG produced the formats that underpin digital media, from the CD-ROM and MP3 to digital television and streaming.

Standards

MPEG-1 (1993) enabled Video CD and included the Layer III audio format — MP3 — which transformed music distribution. MPEG-2 (1995) became the basis of DVD and digital broadcast television. MPEG-4 (1998–99) introduced object-based coding and the widely used Part 10, better known as H.264/AVC, developed jointly with the ITU and foundational to online video. Later work includes HEVC (H.265) and VVC (H.266), plus "systems" standards such as the MP4 file format.

Organisation

MPEG operated for over three decades as ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 29/WG 11 before an ISO restructuring in 2020 dissolved the single working group into several, a change Chiariglione protested on leaving the organisation. Its standards are usually standardised jointly with ITU-T's video coding experts, and most MPEG technologies are patent-encumbered and licensed through patent pools — a model alternately credited with enabling interoperability and criticised for licensing costs.
