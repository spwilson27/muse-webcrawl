Passaic River

The Passaic River is a river, approximately 80 miles (129 km) long, in northern New Jersey. It rises in Mendham in Morris County and flows in a winding course through the Great Swamp, past Chatham, Livingston, and Fairfield, then through Paterson — where it drops over the Great Falls — and onward through Garfield, Passaic, and Clifton before joining the Hackensack River in Newark Bay.

Course and watershed

The Passaic drains a watershed of about 935 square miles. Major tributaries include the Whippany, Rockaway, Pompton, and Saddle rivers. The river's slow, meandering upper reaches pass through the Great Swamp National Wildlife Refuge, a remnant of glacial Lake Passaic.

Pollution and cleanup

The lower Passaic is one of the most polluted waterways in the United States, contaminated by a century of industrial discharges, including dioxin from a former Agent Orange production plant in Newark's Ironbound district. The lower 17 miles are a federal Superfund site, and in 2016 the EPA selected a remedy involving bank-to-bank dredging of the lower eight miles and a cap for the remainder.

Flooding

The basin is prone to severe flooding, notably in 1903, 1968, 1984, 2010, and during Hurricane Irene in 2011. Various flood-control proposals, including a large tunnel project, have been debated for decades.
