Eosin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Eosin is the name of several fluorescent acidic compounds which bind to and form salts with basic, or eosinophilic, compounds such as proteins containing amino acid residues such as arginine and lysine, and stains them dark red or pink. It is most widely used as a counterstain to haematoxylin in the H&E (haematoxylin and eosin) stain, the most common staining procedure in histology and diagnostic pathology.

Variants

The most common form is eosin Y (also known as eosin Y ws, eosin yellowish, Acid Red 87, or C.I. 45380), a tetrabrominated derivative of fluorescein with a slightly yellowish cast. Eosin B is a dibromo dinitro derivative with a faint blue cast. Eosins were first described in the 1870s and are named after Eos, the Greek goddess of dawn, for their pink hue.

Use

In an H&E-stained tissue section, cell nuclei stain blue-purple with haematoxylin, while cytoplasm, connective tissue, and other extracellular structures stain in varying shades of pink with eosin, allowing pathologists to distinguish cell types and diagnose disease. Eosin is also used as a red dye in inks and has applications in fluorescence studies.
