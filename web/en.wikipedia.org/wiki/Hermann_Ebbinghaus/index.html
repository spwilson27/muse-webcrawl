Hermann Ebbinghaus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hermann Ebbinghaus (24 January 1850 – 26 February 1909) was a German psychologist who pioneered the experimental study of memory. His 1885 monograph Memory: A Contribution to Experimental Psychology introduced the forgetting curve and the spacing effect, and he is credited with the nonsense-syllable method and the first sentence-completion test of intelligence.

Memory experiments

Working alone, Ebbinghaus used himself as the sole subject, memorising lists of nonsense syllables (consonant-vowel-consonant combinations such as "WUJ" and "ZAT") to study learning free of prior associations. By measuring how many repetitions were needed to relearn lists after various delays, he plotted the forgetting curve: memory decays rapidly at first, then levels off. He also documented the learning curve, serial-position effects, and the savings method.

Career

Ebbinghaus taught at the University of Berlin, where he co-founded a journal of psychology, and later at Breslau and Halle. In Breslau he developed a sentence-completion test used to assess schoolchildren's mental ability, a precursor of intelligence tests. He died of pneumonia in Halle in 1909.
