Hardness

[Image: Indentation hardness testing: a diamond indenter pressed into a material]

Overview

Hardness is a measure of the resistance to localised plastic deformation, such as indentation or scratching. It is not a single fundamental material property — different tests measure different combinations of yield strength, work hardening and fracture — so hardness is always qualified by the test used.

Scales

The Mohs scale (1812/1822) ranks minerals 1–10 (talc to diamond) by scratch resistance and is ordinal, not linear. Engineering scales measure indentation: Brinell (a ball), Rockwell (depth under load, the most common for metals), Vickers and Knoop (diamond pyramids, used for microhardness). Hardened steels reach about 60–65 HRC; ceramics and cermets far exceed metals, and diamond remains the hardest common natural material, though aggregated diamond nanorods and theoretical materials (lonsdaleite) rival it. Hardness correlates with wear resistance and, in steels, roughly with tensile strength.
