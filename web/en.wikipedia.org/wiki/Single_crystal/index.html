Single crystal

A single crystal (or monocrystal) is a material in which the crystal lattice is continuous and unbroken throughout the entire sample, with no grain boundaries. The opposite of a single crystal is a polycrystalline material, composed of many small crystallites.

Production and uses

Single crystals are grown artificially by methods such as the Czochralski process and Bridgman technique. Large silicon single crystals (boules) are sliced into wafers for semiconductor manufacturing. Single-crystal turbine blades of nickel superalloys withstand extreme conditions in jet engines, and single crystals are essential in optics, lasers and X-ray crystallography.

Natural single crystals include gemstones such as diamond and quartz.
