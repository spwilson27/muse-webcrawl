Mount Aragats

Mount Aragats (Armenian: Արագած) is an isolated four-peaked volcano massif in Armenia and the highest point in the country, with its northern summit reaching 4,090 metres (13,419 ft) above sea level. It lies in Aragatsotn Province, northwest of Yerevan.

Geography

The volcano has four summits arranged around a crater: the northern peak is the highest, followed by the western (4,080 m), eastern (3,916 m), and southern (3,879 m). Glacial cirques and alpine meadows cover its slopes. Lake Kari, at about 3,200 m on the mountain, is a popular starting point for ascents.

Points of interest

On Aragats's southern slopes stand the Byurakan Observatory, founded by Viktor Ambartsumian, and the 7th-century fortress of Amberd with its church. The mountain is named, according to legend, after Ara the Beautiful, and it holds a prominent place in Armenian culture and folklore. The slopes also feature the "Armenian alphabet park," where giant carved letters of the Armenian alphabet were installed in 2005.
