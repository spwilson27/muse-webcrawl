Foreign tax credit

A foreign tax credit is a non-refundable tax credit for income taxes paid to a foreign government as a result of foreign income tax withholdings or assessed foreign taxes. It is a mechanism used by countries that tax their residents on worldwide income to mitigate the effects of double taxation — the same income being taxed by both the country where it was earned (the source country) and the country of the taxpayer's residence.

Purpose and mechanism

Where a taxpayer earns income abroad, the source country typically taxes it first. Without relief, the residence country taxing the same income on a worldwide basis would impose a second layer of tax. The foreign tax credit allows the taxpayer to offset the foreign tax paid against the domestic tax otherwise due on that foreign income, generally up to the amount of domestic tax attributable to the foreign-source income. Excess credits may in some systems be carried back or forward to other years.

United States

The United States has allowed a foreign tax credit since 1918. Under the Internal Revenue Code (principally sections 901–909 and the limitation in section 904), US citizens and resident aliens — who are taxed on worldwide income regardless of where they live — may credit creditable foreign income taxes against US tax. The credit is subject to a limitation: it cannot exceed the proportion of total US tax that the taxpayer's foreign-source taxable income bears to total taxable income. Separate limitation categories ("baskets"), such as passive and general category income, apply. Taxpayers may instead deduct foreign taxes as an itemised deduction, but the credit is usually more valuable. Individuals use IRS Form 1116 to compute the credit.

Rather than the credit, US citizens abroad often use the foreign earned income exclusion, which excludes a limited amount of foreign earnings from US tax; the two provisions can sometimes be combined on different portions of income.

Other jurisdictions

Most countries that tax worldwide income provide unilateral foreign tax credit relief, and bilateral double taxation treaties typically specify which country has primary taxing rights over particular classes of income and require the other to give credit or exemption. Some countries instead use the exemption method, excluding foreign income from domestic tax altogether.
