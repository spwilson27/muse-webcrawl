Microeconomics

[Image: a single market stall pricing its goods, representing individual buyers, sellers and prices]

Microeconomics is the branch of Economics that studies the behaviour of individual agents — households, firms and industries — and how they make decisions about allocating scarce resources, above all through prices in individual markets. Where Macroeconomics takes the economy as a whole, microeconomics builds upward from choices: what to buy, what to produce, what price to charge.

Core topics

Its foundation is the supply and demand model and the concept of elasticity, used to analyse price controls, taxes and trade. Consumer theory explains demand through preferences, budget constraints and marginal Utility; the theory of the firm explains supply through production functions and costs. Market-structure analysis compares perfect Competition, Monopoly, Oligopoly and monopolistic competition. Later developments include game theory — the strategic interaction of decision-makers, pioneered by von Neumann and Nash — the economics of information (asymmetric information, adverse selection, moral hazard), and behavioural economics, which tests the rationality assumptions of the standard model experimentally.

Welfare and policy

Microeconomics underpins welfare economics: the first fundamental theorem states conditions under which competitive markets achieve Pareto efficiency, while the study of market failure — externalities, public goods, market power and information problems — explains where markets fall short and policy might help, at the risk of government failure of its own.
