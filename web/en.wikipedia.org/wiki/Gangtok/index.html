Gangtok


Gangtok, capital of Sikkim, spread across a ridge in the eastern Himalayas.

Gangtok is the capital and the largest town of the Indian state of Sikkim. It is situated in the eastern Himalayan range, at an average elevation of about 1,650 m (5,410 ft). The town, with a population of just over 100,000, belongs to the municipal district of East Sikkim. Gangtok first rose to prominence as a popular Buddhist pilgrimage site after the construction of the Enchey Monastery in 1840, and became the capital of the Kingdom of Sikkim in the mid-19th century.

History

Gangtok remained a small hamlet until Enchey Monastery was built in 1840. After the British invasion of Tibet in 1904, it became a major stopover on the trade route between Lhasa and ports such as Calcutta. Sikkim merged with India in 1975, and Gangtok continued as state capital.

Geography and climate

Gangtok lies in the Shivalik Hills and has a monsoon-influenced subtropical highland climate. Temperatures range from about 4 °C in winter to around 22 °C in summer. The town offers views of Mount Kangchenjunga, the world's third-highest peak.

Culture and tourism

The town is a centre of Tibetan Buddhist culture, home to the Rumtek and Enchey monasteries, the Namgyal Institute of Tibetology and Do-Drul Chorten. MG Marg, a pedestrianised shopping street, is a popular attraction. Gangtok is also known for its ropeway and as a base for visiting Nathu La and Lake Tsomgo.
