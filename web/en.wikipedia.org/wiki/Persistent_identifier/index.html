Persistent identifier

A persistent identifier (PI or PID) is a long-lasting reference to a document, file, web page, person, or other object. Unlike an ordinary URL, a persistent identifier is intended to remain valid and resolvable even when the object's location changes, typically because a registration agency maintains redirection and metadata.

Types

Widely used schemes include the Digital Object Identifier (DOI) for publications and datasets, ORCID iDs for researchers, Handles, Archival Resource Keys (ARKs), Persistent URLs (PURLs), and identifiers for physical and abstract entities such as ISBNs for books and ISNI for public identities.

Rationale

Link rot undermines scholarly citation and data reuse. PIDs separate an object's identity from its current location, supporting citation, provenance, credit and the FAIR principles for research data.

Governance

Persistence is an organisational commitment rather than a purely technical property: registration agencies such as Crossref and DataCite maintain the infrastructure and policies that keep identifiers resolving.
