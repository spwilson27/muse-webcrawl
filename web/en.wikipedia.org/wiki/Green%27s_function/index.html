Green's function

In mathematics, a Green's function (or fundamental solution) is the impulse response of an inhomogeneous linear differential operator defined on a domain with specified initial or boundary conditions. Solving the operator equation for a point impulse (a Dirac delta function) yields the Green's function, from which the solution for any source can be built by superposition as an integral.

Definition

For a linear operator L, the Green's function G satisfies L G(x, s) = δ(x − s). The solution of L u(x) = f(x) is then u(x) = ∫ G(x, s) f(s) ds, with boundary conditions handled by the choice of G.

History

The concept is named after the British mathematical physicist George Green, who first developed it in an 1828 essay on electricity and magnetism, though his work was little known until republication in the 1850s.

Uses

Green's functions are central in potential theory, wave propagation, quantum field theory (where they appear as propagators), and the solution of boundary-value problems in engineering and physics.
