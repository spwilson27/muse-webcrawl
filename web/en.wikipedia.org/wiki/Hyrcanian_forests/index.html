The Hyrcanian forests (Persian: جنگل‌های هیرکانی) form the Caspian Hyrcanian mixed forests ecoregion, a belt of lush lowland and montane temperate broadleaf forest along the southern shore of the Caspian Sea in northern Iran and southeastern Azerbaijan. Named after the ancient region of Hyrcania, the forests stretch roughly 850 km from the Talysh Mountains in the west to Golestan in the east, on the northern slopes of the Alborz range.

Ecology

Sheltered by the Alborz Mountains and moistened by Caspian air, the region escaped the worst effects of Quaternary glaciation and serves as a refugium for Arcto-Tertiary relict species. Lowland forests are dominated by chestnut-leaved oak, oriental beech, Persian ironwood, Caucasian elm, and alder, with dense undergrowth of boxwood and butcher's-broom; species such as the Caspian locust and velvet maple survive here long after vanishing from most of Eurasia. The forests support Persian leopards, brown bears, wild boar, red deer, and rich birdlife, and were historically a habitat of the now-extinct Caspian tiger.

Conservation

About 55,000 km² remain, much of it threatened by logging, grazing, and conversion to farmland and tea plantations. The Iranian portion was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2019 as "Hyrcanian Forests," and the listing was extended in 2023 to include sites in Azerbaijan.
