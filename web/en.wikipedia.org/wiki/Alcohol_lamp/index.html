Alcohol lamp

Alcohol lamp
TypeLaboratory burner
FuelEthanol or methanol (denatured alcohol / methylated spirit)
Flame temperatureapprox. 500 °C at the wick (cooler than a Bunsen burner)
UsesSterilising instruments, gentle heating, wax work, microbiology

An alcohol lamp (or spirit lamp) is a simple laboratory burner that burns liquid alcohol — usually denatured ethanol or methanol — drawn up through a wick by capillary action. The typical laboratory form is a small glass bottle with a metal wick holder and a ground-glass or metal cap used to extinguish the flame and prevent evaporation.

Design and operation

The lamp consists of a reservoir, a cotton or fibreglass wick passing through a short tube, and a cap. Fuel climbs the wick and burns at its tip with a pale blue, nearly smokeless flame. Because the flame is much cooler and gentler than that of a Bunsen burner (roughly 500 °C rather than over 1,000 °C), alcohol lamps are preferred where strong heating of glassware would risk cracking it, or where no gas supply is available.

Uses

Alcohol lamps are used in microbiology to flame-sterilise inoculating loops and forceps, in dentistry and jewellery for softening wax, in schools for gentle heating of solutions, and in field and home settings. Similar spirit burners power small camping and marine stoves.

Safety

Alcohol flames can be nearly invisible in bright light, and refilling a lamp while it is warm is a common cause of burns and fires; lamps should be extinguished, cooled and refilled away from ignition sources.
