Enterprise value

Enterprise value (EV), total enterprise value (TEV), or firm value is an economic measure reflecting the market value of a business. It is a sum of claims by all claimants: creditors (secured and unsecured) and equity holders (preferred and common). Enterprise value is one of the fundamental metrics used in business valuation, financial modeling, accounting, investment analysis and portfolio management.

Formula

A simplified calculation is:

EV = market capitalisation + preferred equity + total debt + minority interest − cash and cash equivalents

In effect, enterprise value is the theoretical takeover price of a company: the buyer must pay the market value of the equity, assume the company's debt and obligations, but gains the company's cash, which can be used to repay debt. EV therefore differs from market capitalisation, which values only the equity; two companies with the same market capitalisation can have very different enterprise values if one carries much more debt (or cash) than the other.

Uses

Because EV is (approximately) capital-structure neutral, it is used in valuation multiples that compare companies with different leverage: EV/EBITDA and EV/Sales are standard in merger and acquisition analysis and equity research. By contrast, price-based multiples such as the price–earnings ratio are equity values and are distorted by differences in gearing. EV is also used as the numerator in computing the total value of a firm's securities when applying option-pricing frameworks to equity as a claim on the company's assets.

Limitations

Calculating EV precisely requires care with items such as minority interests, pension deficits, operating leases, associate holdings and non-core assets, whose book values may not reflect market values; for firms with complex balance sheets analysts use a fuller definition summing the market values of all securities and other claims net of cash and non-operating assets.
