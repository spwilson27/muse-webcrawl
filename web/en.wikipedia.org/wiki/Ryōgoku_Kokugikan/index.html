Ryōgoku Kokugikan

Ryōgoku Kokugikan
Location: Yokoami, Sumida, Tokyo, Japan
Opened: January 1985
Capacity: approx. 11,000
Owner: Japan Sumo Association

Ryōgoku Kokugikan (両国国技館), also known as the Ryōgoku Sumo Hall, is an indoor sporting arena located in the Yokoami neighborhood of Sumida, Tokyo, Japan. Completed in 1985, it is the home of professional sumo in Tokyo, hosting three of the six annual honbasho tournaments — in January, May, and September — and is operated by the Japan Sumo Association. The building also contains the Sumo Museum.

History

The name Kokugikan ("hall of the national sport") was first given to an arena opened in Ryōgoku in 1909. That building was requisitioned during the Second World War and later burned down; postwar tournaments were held at the Kuramae Kokugikan from 1954 until 1984. The present arena, the third Kokugikan, opened in January 1985 a short distance from the original site and near the Edo-Tokyo Museum.

Design

The arena has a seating capacity of about 11,000. At its centre is the dohyō, a raised clay ring, above which hangs a giant Shinto-style roof (tsuriyane) weighing several tonnes, suspended from the ceiling rather than supported by pillars so as not to obstruct sightlines. Spectators sit in box seats (masu-seki) at ground level and chairs in the upper tiers. Enormous portraits of recent tournament champions decorate the upper walls.

Other uses

Besides sumo, the Kokugikan hosts boxing, professional wrestling, mixed martial arts, and concerts. It was the boxing venue for the 2020 Tokyo Olympic Games, held in 2021. The surrounding Ryōgoku district remains the heartland of sumo, home to many training stables (heya), sumo restaurants serving chankonabe, and the graves of the 47 rōnin at nearby Sengaku-ji are a popular associated attraction.
