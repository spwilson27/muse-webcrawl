Mineral processing

Mineral processing (also known as ore dressing) is the process of separating commercially valuable minerals from their ores. It is the first process that most ores undergo after mining, in order to provide a more concentrated material for the procedures of extractive metallurgy. The operations are commonly classified as comminution, sizing, concentration and dewatering.

Comminution

Comminution is the reduction of solid materials from one average particle size to a smaller average particle size, by crushing and grinding. Crushing is usually a dry process performed in stages by jaw, gyratory and cone crushers, while grinding — in rod and ball mills, often wet — completes the liberation of valuable mineral grains from gangue.

Sizing and classification

Sizing separates particles by dimension using screens, while classification exploits settling rates in fluids, as in hydrocyclones. These steps prepare feed of suitable size distribution for concentration.

Concentration

Concentration increases the proportion of valuable mineral by exploiting physical or surface-chemical properties: gravity separation (jigs, spirals, shaking tables), magnetic separation, electrostatic separation, and froth flotation, in which reagents render the target mineral hydrophobic so that it attaches to air bubbles. Leaching and other hydrometallurgical routes may follow.

Dewatering

Finally, concentrates are thickened, filtered and dried for transport or smelting, while tailings are disposed of in tailings dams. Modern plants emphasise water recycling and dry stacking to reduce environmental risk.
