Lebanon–Syria relations

Lebanon–Syria relations are the bilateral relations between the Lebanese Republic and Syria, two neighbouring states in the Levant whose history is deeply intertwined. The relationship has long been characterised by Syria's dominant influence over Lebanese affairs.

History

Both countries emerged from the French Mandate after the Second World War, yet for decades they did not exchange ambassadors; Syria did not formally recognise Lebanese independence in practice. Syria intervened militarily in the Lebanese Civil War in 1976 and maintained troops in Lebanon until 2005, when mass protests following the assassination of former prime minister Rafic Hariri forced their withdrawal. Formal diplomatic relations and embassies were finally established in 2008–2009.

Contemporary issues

The Syrian civil war that began in 2011 spilled across the border, brought large refugee flows into Lebanon, and drew in the Lebanese movement Hezbollah as a combatant on the Syrian government's side. Border demarcation, smuggling, and the fate of Syrian refugees in Lebanon remain central issues in the relationship.
