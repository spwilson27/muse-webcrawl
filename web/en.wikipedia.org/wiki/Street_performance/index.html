Street performance

Street performance or busking is the act of performing in public places for gratuities. In many countries the rewards are generally in the form of money but other gratuities such as food, drink or gifts may be given. Street performance is practised all over the world and dates back to antiquity. People engaging in this practice are called street performers or buskers — in the United States also called tip performers.

A street musician performing for a passing audience.

Forms

Street performance encompasses music, dance, acrobatics, juggling, magic, comedy, puppetry, living statues, caricature and portrait drawing, storytelling and street theatre. Performers distinguish between circle shows, which gather and hold a crowd for a complete act ending in a collection ("hatting" the audience), and walk-by or pitch acts such as musicians playing for passers-by who may drop coins without stopping.

History

Performing in public for reward is ancient: troubadours, minstrels, jongleurs and mountebanks worked the streets and fairs of medieval Europe, and analogous traditions exist worldwide. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries street music became woven into urban life — organ grinders, one-man bands and brass bands — and many noted musicians, from Édith Piaf to Ed Sheeran, began their careers partly by busking.

Legal status

Busking sits uneasily between art and nuisance in law. Many cities regulate it through permits, licensed pitches and time limits — famous regulated schemes include those of the London Underground and Covent Garden in London, and subway performance programmes in New York. Elsewhere bylaws restrict amplification or performing in certain places, and enforcement has repeatedly raised free-expression questions in the courts.

Terminology

The word busk derives from the Spanish buscar, "to seek", via a sense of seeking fame or fortune; it entered English in the nineteenth century, at first with disreputable overtones of itinerant performing. The performer's working spot is called a pitch, and a good crowd gathered around a show is an edge.
