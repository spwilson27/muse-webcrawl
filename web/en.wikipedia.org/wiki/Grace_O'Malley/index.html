Grace O'Malley (Irish: Gráinne Ní Mháille; c. 1530 – c. 1603), also known as Gráinne Mhaol ("Bald Grace"), was an Irish noblewoman, chieftain of the Ó Máille clan of County Mayo, and a famed seafarer frequently described in folklore as the "Pirate Queen" of Connacht. She commanded a fleet of ships in Clew Bay and along the western coast of Ireland during the later sixteenth century, a period of mounting English encroachment on Gaelic Ireland.

Early life

O'Malley was born into the seafaring Ó Máille family, lords of the barony of Murrisk. According to tradition she insisted on accompanying her father on a trading voyage to Spain, and when told her long hair would catch in the ship's ropes, she cut it short — earning the nickname Gráinne Mhaol. She married into the O'Flaherty and later the Bourke families, using both marriages to consolidate her power and, after the deaths of her husbands, returning to Mayo with her followers to lead from Clare Island.

Career at sea

From bases at Clare Island, Rockfleet (Carrickahowley) Castle and elsewhere in Clew Bay, O'Malley levied dues on shipping, traded with Scotland and Spain, and raided English and rival Irish vessels. Her activities drew the hostility of Sir Richard Bingham, the English governor of Connacht, who seized her lands and imprisoned members of her family.

Meeting with Elizabeth I

In 1593 O'Malley sailed to England and obtained a celebrated audience with Queen Elizabeth I at Greenwich Palace. Legend holds that she refused to bow, since she did not recognise Elizabeth as queen of Ireland, and that the two women conversed in Latin. The meeting resulted in the release of her son and brother and the restoration of much of her property, though Bingham's harassment continued to trouble her later years.

Death and legacy

O'Malley is believed to have died around 1603, probably at Rockfleet Castle. She remains one of the best-known figures of Irish folklore, celebrated in songs, plays and novels as a symbol of Irish resistance and female leadership.
