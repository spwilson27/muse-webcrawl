Lea & Perrins

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A bottle of Lea & Perrins Worcestershire sauce in its distinctive paper wrapper]
A bottle of Lea & Perrins Worcestershire sauce in its distinctive paper wrapper

Lea & Perrins is the English food company that created Worcestershire sauce in Worcester in the 1830s and remains its best-known producer. Founded by the chemists John Wheeley Lea and William Henry Perrins, the company sold its sauce worldwide from 1838; the brand — still made in Worcester, its US version bottled under licence — has been owned by Kraft Heinz since 2005 (via HP Foods). The orange-labelled, paper-wrapped bottle is a design icon.

The sauce's invention

The company's foundation legend holds that a nobleman (identified in tradition as Lord Sandys) returned from Bengal with a recipe, asked the chemists to make it, and found their attempt so harsh they abandoned the barrel — rediscovering it months later, fermented and mellowed, as a superb sauce. Whatever the tale's embellishments, the sauce — anchovy, tamarind, vinegar, molasses, garlic, spices — launched in 1837–38 and became a global condiment within the decade.

Company history

Lea & Perrins expanded into a Worcester institution, exporting by the shipload, opening a New York operation in 1839 onward, and fighting imitators in court (the name 'Worcestershire sauce' was ruled generic, but the brand's get-up was protected). Acquired by Imperial Tobacco's food arm in the twentieth century, passed through HP Foods and Danone, it joined Heinz in 2005. The Midland Road factory in Worcester still produces the sauce, its exact recipe (with regional variants, including a US formula with distilled vinegar) a guarded secret.
