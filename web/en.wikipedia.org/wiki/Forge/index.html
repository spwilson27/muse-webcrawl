Forge

A forge is a type of hearth used for heating metals, or the workplace (smithy) where such a hearth is located. The forge is used by the smith to heat a piece of metal to a temperature at which it becomes easier to shape by forging, or to the point where work hardening no longer occurs.

Types

Traditional forges burn coal, coke or charcoal, with air supplied by bellows or a blower to raise the fire's temperature. Modern smiths also use gas forges fuelled by propane, which are cleaner and easier to control, and induction heating for industrial work. The basic equipment of a forge includes the hearth, an anvil, hammers, tongs and a quench tub.

Process

In the forge the metal — typically iron or steel — is heated until it glows, ranging from dull red to bright yellow-white depending on the operation. At these temperatures it can be hammered, bent, drawn out, upset or forge-welded. The smith judges temperature largely by colour.

History

Forges have been central to metalworking since the Bronze and Iron Ages. Water-powered trip hammers and large industrial forge shops followed in the medieval and early modern periods, and forging remains an important manufacturing process for high-strength components.
