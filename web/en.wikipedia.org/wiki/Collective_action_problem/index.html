Collective action problem

A collective action problem or social dilemma is a situation in which all individuals would be better off cooperating but fail to do so because of conflicting interests between individuals that discourage joint action. The classic statements are the tragedy of the commons and the free-rider problem.

Theory

Mancur Olson's The Logic of Collective Action (1965) argued that large groups face greater difficulty organising for common interests because individuals can "free ride" on others' efforts. Elinor Ostrom showed empirically that communities often devise their own institutions — monitoring, graduated sanctions and clear boundaries — to govern common-pool resources successfully, work for which she won the 2009 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences.

Examples

Climate-change mitigation, funding of public goods, overfishing and voter turnout are standard examples where individual rationality and collective welfare diverge.
