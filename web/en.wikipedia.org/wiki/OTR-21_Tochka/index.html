OTR-21 Tochka

The OTR-21 Tochka (Russian: Точка, "point") is a Soviet tactical ballistic missile system, known in NATO reporting as the SS-21 Scarab. Designed in the 1970s to replace the unguided FROG-7 rocket, it carries a single-stage, solid-fuel missile on a wheeled transporter erector launcher and can deliver high-explosive, chemical, or nuclear warheads against targets such as command posts, airfields, and troop concentrations.

TypeTactical ballistic missile
OriginSoviet Union
Entered service1975 (Tochka); 1989 (Tochka-U)
Range~70 km (Tochka); ~120 km (Tochka-U)

Variants

The improved Tochka-U (SS-21 Scarab B) entered service in 1989 with greater range, inertial guidance refinements that improved accuracy, and additional warhead options including submunition and anti-radar types. The missile is launched from the 9P129 amphibious transporter erector launcher, supported by reload vehicles carrying spare missiles.

Service

The Tochka was exported to several Warsaw Pact and Middle Eastern states. It has been used in combat in Chechnya, the 2008 Russo-Georgian War, the Syrian Civil War, the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict, and the Russo-Ukrainian War, where both Russia and Ukraine employed Tochka-U missiles. In Russian service it is being replaced by the Iskander-M system.
