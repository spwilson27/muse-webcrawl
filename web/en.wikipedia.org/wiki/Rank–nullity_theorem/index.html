Rank–nullity theorem

Rank–nullity theorem
FieldLinear algebra
Statementdim(domain) = rank + nullity

The rank–nullity theorem is a theorem in linear algebra stating that the dimension of the domain of a linear map is the sum of its rank (the dimension of its image) and its nullity (the dimension of its kernel or null space).

Statement

For a linear map T : V → W between finite-dimensional vector spaces:
dim(V) = rank(T) + nullity(T)

Equivalently, for an m × n matrix A, n = rank(A) + nullity(A), where the nullity counts the free variables in the solution of the homogeneous system Ax = 0.

Proof sketch

Take a basis of the kernel of T and extend it to a basis of V. The images under T of the added basis vectors form a basis of the image of T, so the counts add up to dim(V).

Applications

The theorem underlies the discussion of the solutions of systems of linear equations: a linear system Ax = b can have at most one solution when the nullity is zero, and infinitely many solutions when the nullity is positive. It is a precursor of the more general first isomorphism theorem for vector spaces.
