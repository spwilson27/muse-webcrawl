Statistical mechanics is a branch of physics that applies probability theory and statistics to large ensembles of microscopic entities in order to explain the macroscopic behaviour of matter — temperature, pressure, heat and entropy — without tracking every particle.

Foundations

James Clerk Maxwell and Ludwig Boltzmann developed the kinetic theory of gases and the statistical interpretation of entropy in the 19th century; J. Willard Gibbs gave the subject its systematic ensemble formulation in 1902. Statistical mechanics explains phase transitions, underlies condensed-matter theory, and in the 20th century merged with quantum mechanics to describe everything from semiconductors to black-hole thermodynamics.
