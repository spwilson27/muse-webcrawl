Hamstring

In human anatomy, a hamstring is any one of the three posterior thigh muscles between the hip and the knee: the semimembranosus medially, the semitendinosus medially and superficial to it, and the biceps femoris laterally.

The hamstrings cross and act upon two joints – the hip and the knee – and as such are called bi-articular muscles. They play a crucial role in many daily activities such as walking, running, jumping, and controlling some movement in the trunk.

Structure

The three muscles of the posterior thigh originate on the ischial tuberosity of the pelvis and insert on the tibia and fibula below the knee. They are supplied by the tibial division of the sciatic nerve, with the short head of biceps femoris supplied by the common fibular division.

Function

The hamstrings act to flex the knee and extend the hip. In natural movement, they function most often in closed-chain conditions, decelerating the lower limb during activities such as running.

Clinical significance

Hamstring injuries are common in athletes who run fast and make up a significant proportion of acute sports trauma. Prevention programmes emphasise eccentric strengthening, such as the Nordic hamstring exercise.
