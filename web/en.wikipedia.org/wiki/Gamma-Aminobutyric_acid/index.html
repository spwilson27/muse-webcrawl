Gamma-Aminobutyric acid

γ-Aminobutyric acid (GABA)
Chemical formula: C4H9NO2
Molar mass: 103.12 g/mol
Role: Chief inhibitory neurotransmitter in the mammalian central nervous system
Biosynthesis: From glutamate by glutamate decarboxylase

Gamma-aminobutyric acid, or γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA), is the chief inhibitory neurotransmitter in the developmentally mature mammalian central nervous system. Its principal role is reducing neuronal excitability throughout the nervous system. GABA is sold as a dietary supplement in many countries.


Structure of GABA

Function

GABA acts at two classes of receptors: GABAA receptors, which are ligand-gated chloride channels producing fast inhibition, and GABAB receptors, which are G protein-coupled receptors producing slower, prolonged inhibition. Many sedative and anxiolytic drugs — including benzodiazepines, barbiturates and alcohol — act by enhancing GABAA signalling.

Biosynthesis

GABA is synthesised from glutamate by the enzyme glutamate decarboxylase (GAD) with pyridoxal phosphate (vitamin B6) as a cofactor. It is metabolised by GABA transaminase. GABA is also produced by some gut bacteria and is present in fermented foods.
