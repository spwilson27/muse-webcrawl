Sean-nós dance

Sean-nós dance (from Irish sean nós, "old style") is a traditional form of solo Irish step dancing, especially associated with Connemara in the west of Ireland. Unlike the formalised, competitive style of modern Irish dance, sean-nós is low to the ground, relaxed in the upper body, and largely improvised around a vocabulary of percussive steps performed in time with traditional music.

Characteristics

Dancers keep a loose, upright posture with arms held loosely (sometimes holding the edges of a door or dancing on a small surface, in folklore even on a barrel or half-door), while the feet beat out intricate rhythms — steps with names such as the "shuffle" and the "brush". Dancers traditionally perform one at a time, in a spirit of friendly exchange ("passing the steps" between dancers), to the music of a melodeon or fiddle.

Revival

The style nearly vanished outside Connemara but was revived from the 1970s onwards through teachers, workshops and competitions at festivals such as the Oireachtas, where sean-nós dance classes are now a major category. It has influenced the development of a looser, more rhythmic strand in contemporary Irish dance.
