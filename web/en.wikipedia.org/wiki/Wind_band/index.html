Wind band

A wind band, also called a concert band, wind ensemble, symphonic band, or wind orchestra, is a performing ensemble consisting primarily of members of the woodwind, brass, and percussion families of instruments, along with the double bass or bass guitar. Unlike an orchestra, a wind band typically contains no string section apart from the double bass. Wind bands perform in schools, universities, communities, and professional settings around the world.

Instrumentation

A standard wind band includes flutes and piccolos, oboes, bassoons, clarinets in several sizes, saxophones, trumpets and cornets, French horns, trombones, euphoniums, tubas, and a wide variety of percussion instruments. Many bands also include a string bass, harp, piano, or celesta when called for by a score.

History

The modern wind band descends from military bands of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, professional touring bands such as those led by Patrick Gilmore and John Philip Sousa popularized band concerts. The twentieth century saw the rise of the school and university band, and composers such as Gustav Holst, Ralph Vaughan Williams, Percy Grainger, and Vincent Persichetti wrote original works that elevated the wind band to a serious concert medium.

Repertoire

Wind band repertoire includes original compositions, transcriptions of orchestral works, marches, and arrangements of popular music. Prominent contemporary composers for the medium include John Mackey, Eric Whitacre, Julie Giroux, and Frank Ticheli.
