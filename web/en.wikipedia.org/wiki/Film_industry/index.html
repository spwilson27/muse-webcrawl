The film industry or motion picture industry comprises the technological and commercial institutions of filmmaking: production companies, film studios, cinematography, animation, film production, screenwriting, pre-production, post-production, film festivals, distribution, and actors. It is one of the world's major entertainment industries, measured by box-office revenue, number of films produced and global cultural influence.

History

Commercial cinema began in the 1890s with Edison's Kinetoscope and the Lumière brothers' Cinématographe. The industry centred first on New York and then on Hollywood, California, where the studio system of the 1920s–1940s vertically integrated production, distribution and exhibition. Antitrust decrees (the 1948 Paramount case), television, and later home video and streaming successively reshaped its economics.

Global industry

India produces the largest number of feature films annually, with major industries in Hindi (Bollywood), Telugu, Tamil and other languages. Nigeria's Nollywood is notable for its high output of video films. China became the largest box-office market in the world in 2020–2021. Hollywood remains dominant in global export revenue, driven by franchise blockbusters, while streaming services such as Netflix altered distribution by financing films for simultaneous online release.

Structure

Films are financed through studios, independent producers, pre-sales, tax incentives and co-productions. After production, distributors market and license films to cinemas, television and platforms; box-office grosses, awards and festival exposure influence a film's commercial life.
