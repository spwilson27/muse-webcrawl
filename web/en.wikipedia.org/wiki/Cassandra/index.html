Cassandra

In Greek mythology, Cassandra was a Trojan priestess of Apollo, daughter of King Priam and Queen Hecuba of Troy. Apollo granted her the gift of prophecy but, when she refused him, cursed her so that no one would ever believe her true predictions.

Myth

Cassandra foresaw the destruction of Troy, warned against the Trojan Horse, and predicted her own death, but was dismissed as mad. After the city's fall she was taken as a concubine by Agamemnon and murdered with him by Clytemnestra at Mycenae, a scene central to Aeschylus's Agamemnon.

Legacy

The "Cassandra syndrome" denotes a person whose valid warnings are disbelieved. She appears in works from Euripides' The Trojan Women to Christa Wolf's novel Kassandra (1983). See also Trojan War and Apollo.
