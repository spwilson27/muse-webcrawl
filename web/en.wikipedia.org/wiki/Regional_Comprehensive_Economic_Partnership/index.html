Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) is a free-trade agreement among fifteen Asia-Pacific countries: the ten ASEAN member states together with China, Japan, South Korea, Australia and New Zealand. Signed on 15 November 2020 after eight years of negotiation and in force from 1 January 2022, it covers about 30 per cent of world population and GDP, making it the world's largest trade bloc by those measures. India negotiated until 2019 but withdrew, citing concerns over import surges and deficits with China, and retains a facilitated path to rejoin.

RCEP consolidates the spaghetti bowl of ASEAN's separate agreements with its partners into common rules of origin (a single set of certificates letting components cross fifteen countries cumulatively), modest tariff liberalisation phased over twenty years (eliminating duties on roughly 90 per cent of goods in the long run), and chapters on services, investment, e-commerce and intellectual property — but no labour, environment or state-enterprise disciplines comparable to the rival CPTPP. Its strategic significance (China inside, the United States absent from both Asian mega-regionals) exceeds its shallow tariff cuts, per most trade economists' assessments.
