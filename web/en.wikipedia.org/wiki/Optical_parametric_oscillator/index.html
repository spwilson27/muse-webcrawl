Optical parametric oscillator

Schematic layout of an optical parametric oscillator.

An optical parametric oscillator (OPO) is a parametric oscillator that oscillates at optical frequencies. It converts an input laser wave (called "pump") into two output waves of lower frequency, the "signal" and "idler", by means of a second-order nonlinear optical interaction. The sum of the output waves' frequencies equals the input wave frequency. For historical reasons, the two output waves are called "signal" and "idler", where the output wave with higher frequency is the "signal".

Overview

The OPO consists essentially of an optical resonator and a nonlinear optical crystal. The nonlinear optical crystal plays the role of an active medium: the pump wave passes through the crystal and, through parametric down-conversion, gives rise to signal and idler waves. The optical resonator, formed by mirrors around the crystal, resonates one or both of the output waves, providing the feedback required for oscillation once the parametric gain exceeds the cavity losses, that is, above a certain pump threshold.

Tuning

Because the frequencies of the signal and idler are constrained only by energy conservation and phase matching, an OPO can be tuned over a wide wavelength range by adjusting the phase-matching conditions — for example by changing the crystal temperature, its orientation, or the period of a periodically poled crystal. This broad tunability makes OPOs valuable light sources for spectroscopy and other applications requiring wavelengths not directly available from lasers.

Types

OPOs are classified by which of the waves are resonant: singly resonant oscillators resonate only the signal (or only the idler), doubly resonant oscillators resonate both, and triply resonant designs also resonate the pump. They may operate with continuous-wave or pulsed pumping, and with ultrashort pulses they can generate femtosecond pulses across visible and infrared spectral regions. When the same nonlinear interaction is used without a resonator, the device is an optical parametric amplifier (OPA) or, when seeded, an optical parametric generator (OPG).

Applications

Applications include coherent light generation in the mid-infrared, spectroscopy, remote sensing and LIDAR, quantum optics experiments involving entangled photon pairs, and frequency conversion for laser systems.
