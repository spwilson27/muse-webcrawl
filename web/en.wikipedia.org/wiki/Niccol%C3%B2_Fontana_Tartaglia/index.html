Niccolò Fontana Tartaglia

Born1499/1500, Brescia, Republic of Venice
Died13 December 1557, Venice
Known forSolving cubic equations; Tartaglia's triangle

Niccolò Fontana Tartaglia (1499/1500 – 13 December 1557) was an Italian mathematician, engineer, surveyor and bookkeeper from the Republic of Venice. He published many books, including the first Italian translations of Archimedes and Euclid, and is best remembered for his method of solving cubic equations.

Life

As a boy during the French sack of Brescia in 1512, Niccolò suffered sabre wounds to his jaw and palate that left him with a speech impediment; "Tartaglia" (stammerer) became his surname. Largely self-taught, he earned his living teaching mathematics in Verona and Venice.

Cubic equations

In 1535 Tartaglia won a famous public contest against Antonio Maria Fior by solving cubic equations of a type Fior could not. He later confided his method to Gerolamo Cardano under an oath of secrecy, but Cardano published it in his Ars Magna (1545), sparking a bitter dispute. The episode is a landmark in the history of algebra.

Other work

Tartaglia's Nova Scientia (1537) was a pioneering study of ballistics, and his name is associated with the arithmetical triangle described in his General Trattato (1556).
