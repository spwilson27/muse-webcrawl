Respawn (video games)

In video games and other games, respawning is the term for the recreation of a character, NPC, enemy, building, or item after its death or destruction. In most games, a player's avatar will respawn after their character has been killed or dies. Respawning provides another chance to win and continue, even though death may be accompanied by costs such as losing money, equipment, or experience.

History

Respawning has been a characteristic of video games since their earliest days, being derived from pinball machines' extra balls and arcade games' extra lives. One of the first video games to feature the mechanic was the 1982 arcade game Joust, which is generally cited as the first video game to feature respawning.

Spawn points and spawn camping

Many games feature spawn points, specific locations where characters respawn. In multiplayer games, spawn points are usually scattered across the map and may change position based on the game's state. Controlling a spawn point is an advantageous position, since it allows a player to kill enemies as they spawn, a practice known as spawn camping. Spawn camping is generally considered poor sportsmanship because it gives the camper an unfair advantage over players who appear in the area without knowledge of the camper's presence.

Invulnerability

To counter spawn camping, many games give newly respawned characters a brief period of invulnerability, often called "spawn protection", during which they cannot be harmed. Some games also displace players from the spawn point once they begin moving or attacking.
