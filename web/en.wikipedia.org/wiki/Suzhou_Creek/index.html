Suzhou Creek

Suzhou Creek near its confluence with the Huangpu

Suzhou Creek (苏州河, Sūzhōu Hé), also known as the Wusong River in its upper course, is a waterway in Shanghai, China. It rises at Lake Tai in Jiangsu province and flows about 125 km eastward through the city, joining the Huangpu River at the northern end of the Bund, beside the Waibaidu (Garden) Bridge.

History

The creek takes its English name from the city of Suzhou, the major city it connects to Shanghai. In the treaty-port era it formed the boundary between the British and American settlements that merged into the Shanghai International Settlement. Its banks were lined with warehouses, mills, and factories; the Sihang Warehouse on its north bank became famous as the last stand of Chinese defenders in the 1937 Battle of Shanghai. By the late 20th century the creek was severely polluted, effectively an open sewer through the city centre.

Rehabilitation

A major clean-up programme launched in 1998 intercepted sewage, dredged the channel, and landscaped the banks; by the 2010s water quality had improved enough for dragon-boat races and riverside promenades. The M50 art district and converted warehouse districts along the creek in Putuo and Jing'an districts have made it a centre of Shanghai's creative scene, and a network of footbridges links its green banks.
