White Pass, also known as the Dead Horse Trail, is a mountain pass through the Boundary Ranges of the Coast Mountains on the border of the U.S. state of Alaska and the province of British Columbia, Canada. It lies within the limits of the Municipality of Skagway and is one of only two passes from Skagway to the headwaters of the Yukon River, the other being the better-known Chilkoot Pass.

The White Pass and Yukon Route near the summit

History

The White Pass was named in 1887 by the surveyor William Ogilvie after the Canadian Minister of the Interior, Thomas White. During the Klondike Gold Rush of 1897–98, thousands of stampeders crossed the pass carrying the ton of supplies required by the North-West Mounted Police. Conditions on the rough trail were so brutal that an estimated 3,000 pack horses died, giving the route its grim nickname, the Dead Horse Trail.

White Pass and Yukon Route

In 1898 construction began on the White Pass and Yukon Route, a narrow-gauge railway from Skagway over the pass to Whitehorse, Yukon. Completed in 1900, the railway largely replaced the trail. Today it operates as a heritage railway and is one of Alaska's most popular shore excursions, carrying cruise ship passengers from Skagway to the summit. The modern Klondike Highway also crosses near the pass, summitting somewhat to the east.
