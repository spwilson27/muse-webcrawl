Kshatriya (Sanskrit: क्षत्रिय, romanised kṣatriya, from Sanskrit kṣatra, "rule, authority") is one of the four varnas (social orders) of Hindu society, associated with warriorhood and rulership. The term includes the traditional roles of warriors, kings, princes, and administrators. In the classic varna hierarchy, Kshatriyas rank second, below Brahmins but above Vaishyas and Shudras.

Role and duties

According to the dharmashastra literature, the Kshatriya's duties are protection of the people, administration of justice, military leadership, and the study of the Vedas. The ideal of kshatriya dharma is expounded in the epics: in the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna instructs the warrior Arjuna that fighting a righteous war is the Kshatriya's sacred duty. Legendary Kshatriya dynasties — the Solar (Suryavamsha) and Lunar (Chandravamsha) lineages — claim descent from the sun and moon and include figures such as Rama and the Kuru princes.

History

The varna division is first described in the Purusha Sukta hymn of the Rigveda. During the Vedic period, the rajan (king) and warrior aristocracy formed the Kshatriya class. Notably, several founders of movements that challenged Brahminical orthodoxy — Mahavira of Jainism and Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha — were born into Kshatriya families, and early Buddhist texts sometimes place Kshatriyas above Brahmins. In later centuries, groups rose to power and claimed Kshatriya status through processes historians call "Kshatriyaization" or Sanskritization; the Rajputs of western India and the Marathas of the Deccan are prominent examples. The 17th-century coronation of Shivaji as a Kshatriya king famously required genealogical construction and Brahmin sanction.

Modern period

Under British rule, many Kshatriya-claiming castes sought recognition in censuses and served in colonial regiments. In modern India, communities classed as Kshatriya or claiming the status include Rajputs, Marathas, Khatris, Nairs, Bunts, and others; caste associations continue to negotiate status and political representation.
