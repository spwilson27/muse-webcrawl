Kunshan

Kunshan is a county-level city in southeastern Jiangsu Province, China, bordering Shanghai to the east and administered as part of the prefecture-level city of Suzhou. With a population of over two million, it is one of the most economically developed county-level cities in China and has repeatedly ranked first among China's county-level economies. It lies in the Yangtze River Delta and is about 50 km west of Shanghai's city centre.

Economy

Kunshan transformed from an agricultural county into a manufacturing and technology hub from the 1980s onward, attracting heavy investment from Taiwan and becoming a centre of electronics manufacturing — laptops, components and displays — with major plants and research facilities. It hosts an approved economic and technological development zone (established with State Council approval in 1992, one of the first at the county level), and since 2013 an experimental zone for deepening cross-strait (mainland–Taiwan) industrial cooperation. In 2014, a dust explosion at the Zhongrong Metal Production factory in Kunshan killed 146 people, one of China's worst industrial accidents of the decade.

Culture and transport

Kunshan is the birthplace of Kunqu, one of the oldest forms of Chinese opera, inscribed by UNESCO on its list of intangible heritage. The celebrated water town of Zhouzhuang, sometimes called the "Venice of the East", lies within its boundaries, as do the towns of Jinxi and Qiandeng, birthplace of the writer and dramatist Gu Yanwu's saying that "everyone bears responsibility for the fate of the world". The city is served by the Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway and by an extension of the Shanghai Metro (Line 11 to Huaqiao), China's first inter-provincial metro connection.
