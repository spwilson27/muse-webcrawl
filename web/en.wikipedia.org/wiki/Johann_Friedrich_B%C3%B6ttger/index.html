Johann Friedrich Böttger

Johann Friedrich Böttger (1682–1719) was a German alchemist who, while held by Augustus the Strong of Saxony in the hope he would make gold, instead discovered the secret of hard-paste porcelain — the first produced in Europe, at Meissen.

Life

Born in Schleiz, Böttger worked as an apothecary's apprentice in Berlin and claimed alchemical success, attracting the attention of Frederick I of Prussia. Fleeing to Saxony, he was detained by Augustus and put to work first on gold-making, then, with Ehrenfried Walther von Tschirnhaus, on ceramics experiments.

Porcelain

In 1708 the team produced a red stoneware and then true white hard-paste porcelain; the Meissen manufactory was founded in 1710 at Albrechtsburg Castle. Böttger remained effectively a prisoner and died in Dresden in 1719, aged 37.
