Hunedoara is a city in Hunedoara County, Transylvania, Romania, situated in the valley of the Cerna River near the Poiana Ruscă Mountains. It is best known for Corvin Castle (Castelul Corvinilor), one of the largest castles in Europe and a major Gothic-Renaissance landmark associated with John Hunyadi, the 15th-century voivode of Transylvania.

History

The area has been inhabited since the Stone Age and was an important centre of iron extraction from Dacian and Roman times. In the 20th century Hunedoara became a major centre of the Romanian steel industry, growing rapidly under communism; the industry declined sharply after 1989, and the city's population fell from over 80,000.

Corvin Castle

Built mainly in the 15th century on the site of an older fortification, the castle features tall towers, a drawbridge, a knights' hall and a prison tower. Legend associates its well with Turkish prisoners and links the castle to Vlad the Impaler, who was held there.
