Peace movement

A peace movement is a social movement which seeks to achieve ideals such as the ending of a particular war (or wars) or minimizing inter-human violence in a particular place or situation. They are often linked to the goal of achieving world peace. Some of the methods used to achieve these goals include advocacy of pacifism, nonviolent resistance, diplomacy, boycotts, peace camps, ethical consumerism, and support for anti-war political candidates.

History

Organized peace activism emerged in the early nineteenth century, with peace societies founded in the United States and Britain from 1815–16, and international peace congresses held from the 1840s. The Hague Peace Conferences of 1899 and 1907 promoted arbitration and international law. The catastrophe of the First World War spurred new movements, including conscientious objection and the Women's International League for Peace and Freedom (founded 1915).

Nuclear disarmament and the Cold War

After 1945 the movement focused on nuclear weapons: the Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament (CND), founded in Britain in 1957, popularised the peace symbol, and mass protests against the Vietnam War in the 1960s and 1970s mobilised millions in the United States and worldwide. In the 1980s, campaigns against the deployment of cruise and Pershing II missiles in Europe brought some of the largest demonstrations in postwar history.

Recent movements

The global protests against the Iraq War on 15 February 2003 were among the largest coordinated demonstrations ever held. Peace movements continue to campaign on arms exports, drones, military spending, and specific conflicts, often in coalition with religious groups, trade unions, and anti-militarist organisations.
