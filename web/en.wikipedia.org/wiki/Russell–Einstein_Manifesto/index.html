Russell–Einstein Manifesto

The Russell–Einstein Manifesto was a statement issued in London on 9 July 1955 by the philosopher Bertrand Russell, in the midst of the Cold War. It highlighted the dangers posed by nuclear weapons and called on world leaders to seek peaceful resolutions to international conflict.

Signatories

The manifesto was signed by eleven eminent scientists and intellectuals, most of them Nobel laureates. Albert Einstein signed it on 11 April 1955, just days before his death, making it his last public act. Other signatories included Max Born, Percy W. Bridgman, Leopold Infeld, Frédéric Joliot-Curie, Hermann J. Muller, Linus Pauling, Cecil F. Powell, Joseph Rotblat, and Hideki Yukawa.

Content and legacy

The manifesto famously asked: "Shall we put an end to the human race, or shall mankind renounce war?" and urged readers to "remember your humanity, and forget the rest." It directly inspired the first Pugwash Conference on Science and World Affairs, held in 1957 in Pugwash, Nova Scotia, and the Pugwash movement's later receipt of the Nobel Peace Prize in 1995.
