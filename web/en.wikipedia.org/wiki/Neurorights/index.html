Neurorights

Neurorights are proposed legal and human rights concerning the protection of the human brain and mental activity, advanced in response to advances in neurotechnology such as brain–computer interfaces, neuroimaging and consumer neurodevices. Advocates argue that existing rights to privacy, identity and free thought may need explicit extension to cover neural data and mental processes.

Proposed rights

Frameworks proposed by scholars and organisations such as the Neurorights Foundation commonly include the rights to mental privacy, personal identity, free will, equal access to mental augmentation, and protection from bias in neurotechnologies.

Legal developments

In 2021 Chile became the first country to enact neurorights protections, amending its constitution and passing a law on the protection of neural data and brain activity. Several US states, beginning with Colorado in 2024, have since amended privacy laws to cover neural data, and international bodies including the OECD and UNESCO have issued recommendations on neurotechnology governance.
