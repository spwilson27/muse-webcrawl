Findability

Findability is the ease with which information can be found. It is used both in the context of physical objects and of digital information, such as web pages, documents, and media files. The term is closely associated with information architect Peter Morville, whose work framed findability as a central concern of information architecture and ambient information design.

Concepts

Findability depends on factors including how information is labelled and organised (taxonomy and metadata), the quality of search and navigation systems, and factors external to the system itself — for example, search engine optimisation and inbound links that bring users to content from elsewhere. Morville described findability as preceding usability: you cannot use what you cannot find. In information science, the concept overlaps with retrievability in information retrieval research, which measures how easily documents in a collection can be located.

In libraries and archives, findability relates to cataloguing standards and discovery layers; in commerce, poor product findability on an e-commerce site directly reduces sales. Usability testing methods such as tree testing and first-click testing are used to evaluate it.
