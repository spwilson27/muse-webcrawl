Zernike polynomials

In mathematics, the Zernike polynomials are a sequence of polynomials that are orthogonal on the unit disk. Named after optical physicist Frits Zernike, who introduced them in 1934 in connection with his phase-contrast method, they play a central role in optics, where they are used to represent wavefront aberrations.

Definition

Zernike polynomials are defined in polar coordinates (ρ, θ) on the unit disk, where 0 ≤ ρ ≤ 1. Each polynomial is indexed by a radial degree n and an azimuthal frequency m, with n − m even and m ≤ n. The radial part Rnm(ρ) is a polynomial in ρ, and the full functions are either even or odd under reflection, involving cos(mθ) or sin(mθ) respectively.

Applications

Because the terms correspond to familiar aberration types, Zernike expansions are the standard language for describing optical aberrations: the lowest-order terms represent piston, tip and tilt, defocus, astigmatism, coma, and spherical aberration. They are used in telescope optics, adaptive optics, lithography lens testing, and ophthalmology, where Zernike coefficients measured from a patient's eye guide LASIK and other refractive procedures. Several index conventions exist, including the Noll and ANSI/OSA single-index schemes.
