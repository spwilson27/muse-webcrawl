Aaron Spelling (April 22, 1923 – June 23, 2006) was an American film and television producer and occasional actor. As of 2009, Spelling's eponymous production company, Spelling Television, holds the record as the most prolific television producer in US television history, with 218 producer and executive producer credits.

His productions included the TV series Family (1976–1980), Charlie's Angels (1976–1981), The Love Boat (1977–1986), Hart to Hart (1979–1984), Dynasty (1981–1989), Beverly Hills, 90210 (1990–2000), Melrose Place (1992–1999), 7th Heaven (1996–2007), and Charmed (1998–2006).

Early life

Spelling was born in Dallas, Texas, to Pearl (née Wald) and David Spelling, Russian Jewish immigrants. He served in the United States Army Air Corps as a pilot during World War II. He attended Southern Methodist University, graduating in 1949.

Career

Spelling began his career as an actor and scriptwriter, appearing in episodes of Dragnet and writing for The Dick Powell Show. He worked as a producer for Dick Powell's Four Star Television before forming his own companies, Aaron Spelling Productions and later Spelling Television. His glossy, escapist prime-time dramas dominated American television in the 1970s and 1980s.

Personal life and death

Spelling married actress Carolyn Jones in 1953; they divorced in 1964. In 1968 he married Carol Jean "Candy" Marer, with whom he had two children, Tori and Randy. His Holmby Hills mansion, known as "The Manor", was among the largest homes in Los Angeles County. He died on June 23, 2006, from complications of a stroke.
