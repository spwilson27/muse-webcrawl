Okanagan

The Okanagan (also spelled Okanagan Valley and often pronounced oh-kuh-NAH-gun) is a region in the Canadian province of British Columbia, defined by the basin of Okanagan Lake and the Canadian portion of the Okanagan River. The region is known for its sunny climate, dry landscapes and lakeshore communities, as well as its fruit orchards and wine industry, which have become hallmarks of the area.

The valley is home to several major communities, including Kelowna, the largest city in the region, as well as Vernon, Penticton, West Kelowna, Peachland, Summerland, Osoyoos and Oliver. The region's population has grown steadily, and it is a popular destination for tourism and retirement.

Geography

The Okanagan Valley runs roughly north–south for about 200 kilometres, from the area around Armstrong and Enderby in the north to Osoyoos at the Canada–United States border in the south. Okanagan Lake, about 135 kilometres long, dominates the central valley. South of the border, the river continues through Washington State as the Okanogan River, a tributary of the Columbia River.

Climate

The valley lies in the rain shadow of the Cascade Mountains and has a semi-arid climate, particularly in the south around Osoyoos, where Canada's only desert-like environment, the Okanagan Desert, is found. Summers are hot and dry, while winters are comparatively mild for Canada.

Economy

Agriculture has long been central to the Okanagan economy, with orchards producing apples, peaches, pears, cherries, plums and apricots. Since the late twentieth century, wine grapes have become increasingly important, and the Okanagan is one of Canada's premier wine-producing regions. Tourism, technology and health care are also major employers.

History

The region is the traditional territory of the Syilx (Okanagan) Nation, whose people have lived in the valley for thousands of years. European fur traders arrived in the early nineteenth century, followed by settlers, ranchers and orchardists. The establishment of irrigation in the early twentieth century transformed the valley into a major fruit-growing area.
