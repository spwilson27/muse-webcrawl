Photon

A photon is an elementary particle that is a quantum of the electromagnetic field, including electromagnetic radiation such as light and radio waves, and the force carrier for the electromagnetic force. Photons are massless, travel at the speed of light in vacuum, and have energy E = hν proportional to their frequency (Planck relation).

The concept emerged from Planck's quantisation (1900) and Einstein's explanation of the photoelectric effect (1905, for which he received the Nobel Prize). Photons exhibit wave–particle duality, demonstrated in the double-slit experiment: propagated as waves, detected as particles. They mediate all electromagnetic interactions — chemistry, sight, and every electronic technology.
