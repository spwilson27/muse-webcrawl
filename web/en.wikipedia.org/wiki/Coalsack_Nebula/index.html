The Coalsack Nebula (also known as the Coalsack Dark Nebula, or simply the Coalsack) is the most prominent dark nebula in the sky, visible to the naked eye as an conspicuous dark patch silhouetted against the rich star fields of the southern Milky Way in the constellation Crux, near the Southern Cross. It is catalogued as Caldwell 99.

Characteristics

The Coalsack is a cloud of interstellar dust and cold gas about 600 light-years from Earth. It spans roughly 30 to 35 light-years across and covers an area some 7 degrees by 5 degrees on the sky, overlapping the neighbouring constellations Musca and Centaurus. The dust particles in the cloud block and redden the light of the stars behind it, producing the nebula's inky appearance; the cloud is not completely opaque, and faint stars shine through its thinner edges.

The nebula is thought to be a future star-forming region. At present it shows no active star formation, but the cloud is slowly contracting, and within a few million years parts of it are expected to collapse and ignite as stars, at which point the Coalsack will cease to be a dark nebula.

In culture

The Coalsack has been known to southern hemisphere peoples for millennia. In Aboriginal Australian astronomy it forms the head of the "Emu in the Sky", a constellation defined by dark clouds of the Milky Way whose body stretches across the sky. European navigators noted it from the 15th century; the explorer Vicente Yáñez Pinzón observed it in 1499, and it was nicknamed the "Black Magellanic Cloud" by contrast with the bright Magellanic Clouds. John Herschel described it as "a vakuum in the heavens" — a blank hole in the sky.
