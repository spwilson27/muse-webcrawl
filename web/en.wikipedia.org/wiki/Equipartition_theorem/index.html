Equipartition theorem

Equipartition theorem
StatementEach quadratic degree of freedom holds average energy ½kBT
FieldClassical statistical mechanics
Key figuresMaxwell, Boltzmann, Kelvin

In classical statistical mechanics, the equipartition theorem relates the temperature of a system to its average energies: in thermal equilibrium, energy is shared equally among all accessible quadratic degrees of freedom, each contributing an average of ½kBT per particle, where kB is Boltzmann's constant.

Applications

The theorem predicts the heat capacities of gases (each translational and rotational degree of freedom adds to the molar heat capacity), the Dulong–Petit law for solids (3RT per mole from six vibrational degrees), and the thermal speed of particles. Einstein's analysis of Brownian motion rested on equipartition.

Limitations

Equipartition fails when quantum spacing of energy levels is comparable to kBT — degrees of freedom "freeze out" at low temperatures, as in the falling heat capacity of hydrogen gas or of solids approaching absolute zero. These failures were among the earliest hints of quantum theory.
