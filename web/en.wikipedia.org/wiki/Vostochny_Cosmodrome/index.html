Vostochny Cosmodrome

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Vostochny Cosmodrome
LocationAmur Oblast, Russian Far East
OperatorRoscosmos
First launch28 April 2016
PurposeCivilian launch site reducing reliance on Baikonur
Vostochny's Soyuz complex.

The Vostochny Cosmodrome is a Russian spaceport in the Amur Oblast of the Russian Far East, near the former Svobodny missile base. Built to give Russia a domestic civilian launch site — reducing dependence on the Baikonur Cosmodrome, which Russia leases from Kazakhstan — it hosted its first launch, a Soyuz-2 carrying satellites, on 28 April 2016, attended by President Vladimir Putin.

Construction was slow and dogged by corruption scandals and worker strikes over unpaid wages, but the complex grew: an Angara pad followed, with the heavy Angara-A5 making its first Vostochny flight in April 2024. Russia plans for crewed launches of its future Orel spacecraft from Vostochny, though the site's flight rate remains far below Baikonur's.

Geography

At about 51°N the site is less favourable for geostationary launches than Baikonur, but its trajectories over the Pacific and sparsely populated taiga suit polar and high-inclination missions, and its rail links to Russia's industrial base mirror Baikonur's. The nearby purpose-built town of Tsiolkovsky houses its workforce.
