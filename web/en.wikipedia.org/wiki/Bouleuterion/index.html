Bouleuterion

A bouleuterion (Greek: βουλευτήριον) was a building in ancient Greek cities housing the boulē, the council of citizens that prepared business for the assembly and handled daily administration.

Architecture

Typically located on or near the agora, bouleuteria were rectangular halls with tiered seats along walls, a roof supported by columns, and an altar. Examples survive at Athens (Old and New Bouleuterion on the Agora), Olympia, Priene and Miletus (one of the largest, seating about 1,500).

Function

In democratic Athens the Council of Five Hundred met in the bouleuterion to set the assembly agenda (probouleumata), oversee officials and finances, and receive envoys.
