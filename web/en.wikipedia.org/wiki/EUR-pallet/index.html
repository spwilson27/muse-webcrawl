EUR-pallet

The EUR-pallet, also known as the Euro-pallet or EPAL-pallet, is the standard European pallet as specified by the European Pallet Association (EPAL). It measures 1,200 × 800 × 144 mm (47.2 × 31.5 × 5.7 in) and is designed to fit efficiently through doorways and within the loading width of European trucks and standard shipping containers.

Specifications

The pallet is made of wood — typically eleven boards, nine blocks, and 78 nails in a specified pattern — and has a safe working load of about 1,500 kg when the load is evenly distributed, with a static load capacity of up to 4,000 kg when stacked on a solid surface. Conformity is indicated by markings on the blocks, including the EPAL logo and, for the International Union of Railways standard, the EUR oval. The design originated in 1961 when European railway companies standardized a common exchangeable pallet.

Exchange system

EUR-pallets circulate in an exchange pool: a loaded pallet delivered is swapped for an empty one of equivalent quality, reducing empty returns. There are an estimated several hundred million EPAL pallets in circulation. Related standardized sizes include the half pallet (800 × 600 mm) and quarter pallet. Quality is graded, and damaged pallets can be repaired only by licensed operations, after which they are re-marked with a repair nail.
