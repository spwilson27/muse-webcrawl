The Lincolnshire Wolds are a range of low chalk hills in the county of Lincolnshire, England, running roughly parallel to the North Sea coast between the Humber estuary and the town of Spilsby. They form the highest ground in lowland eastern England between Yorkshire and Kent.

Landscape

The Wolds are characterised by smooth rolling uplands dissected by steep-sided dry valleys, with chalk streams such as the headwaters of the Bain and the Lymn in the south. Much of the plateau is intensive arable farmland, but the valleys retain chalk grassland of high nature-conservation value. The area was designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty in 1973.

History and settlement

The hills are rich in prehistoric remains, including Neolithic long barrows and Bronze Age round barrows sited along the ancient ridgeway now followed by the Viking Way long-distance footpath. Market towns such as Louth, Horncastle, Alford and Market Rasen sit around the edges of the hills. Many villages shrank or were deserted in the later Middle Ages, and the landscape preserves numerous deserted medieval village sites.

Alfred, Lord Tennyson, Poet Laureate, was born in the Wolds village of Somersby in 1809, and the landscape of the hills recurs throughout his poetry.
