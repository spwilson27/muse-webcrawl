Bombardier Global Express

The Bombardier Global Express is a large-cabin, long-range business jet manufactured by Bombardier Aerospace (now Bombardier Aviation). Announced in 1991 and introduced in 1999, it was designed to fly intercontinental ranges of about 6,000 nautical miles at high speed, linking city pairs such as New York–Tokyo non-stop. The family grew to include the Global 5000, Global 6000, Global 5500/6500 and the larger, longer-range Global 7500 and 8000, making the Globals Bombardier's flagship business aircraft line.

Development

Bombardier, leveraging its Canadair subsidiary's Challenger programme, launched the Global Express to compete with the Gulfstream V in the ultra-long-range market created in the 1990s. The aircraft shares a fuselage cross-section with the Challenger 600 series and the CRJ regional jets but has a new supercritical wing with high-lift devices and Rolls-Royce BR710 engines. The first prototype flew in October 1996 and certification followed in 1998, with entry into service in 1999.

Variants and military use

The improved Global Express XRS (2004) added range and a premium cabin; it was rebranded Global 6000 in 2011 with upgraded avionics. Military derivatives include the Raytheon Sentinel R1 surveillance aircraft operated by the Royal Air Force, the US Air Force's E-11A battlefield communications node, and Sweden's GlobalEye airborne early warning platform based on the Global 6000/6500. The clean-sheet Global 7500, entering service in 2018, set range records for a purpose-built business jet at over 7,700 nautical miles.
