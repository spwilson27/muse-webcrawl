Districts of Afghanistan

The districts of Afghanistan (Pashto: ولسوالۍ, wuləswāləi; Dari: ولسوالی) are the second-level administrative subdivisions of the country, below the 34 provinces. There are roughly 400 districts — counts vary (about 387–421) because of boundary changes, newly created districts, and disputed listings — each headed by a district governor (wuluswal).

Administration

District governors are appointed and historically reported to provincial governors and the Independent Directorate of Local Governance. Districts form the level of government most directly encountered by rural Afghans, responsible for local security coordination, registration, and liaison with village shuras and community development councils.

Variability

Districts vary enormously in area and population: remote mountain districts may contain a few thousand inhabitants, while districts within Kabul and other cities hold hundreds of thousands. Successive governments created new districts partly to accommodate ethnic and tribal groups, and some districts existed "on paper" with limited state presence, especially during the insurgency after 2001 when control of many districts was contested or held by the Taliban.

Examples

Among the better-known districts are Arghandab and Panjwayi in Kandahar Province, Sangin and Musa Qala in Helmand Province (sites of heavy fighting after 2006), and the districts of Kabul, including Deh Sabz and Paghman.
