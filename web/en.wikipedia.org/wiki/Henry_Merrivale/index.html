Henry Merrivale

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sir Henry Merrivale is a fictional amateur detective created by John Dickson Carr, who wrote the Merrivale novels under the pseudonym Carter Dickson. Merrivale, known as "H.M." or "the Old Man", is the sleuth in 22 novels and several short stories published between 1934 and 1953, many of them classic "locked-room" or impossible-crime mysteries.

The character

Merrivale is a large, blustering, eccentric baronet and barrister, a former head of British military intelligence, with a formidable intellect hidden behind comic bluster and explosive temper. He is frequently aided — and exasperated — by Chief Inspector Humphrey Masters of Scotland Yard. His cases typically involve apparently impossible crimes, such as murders in sealed rooms or deaths in untouched snow.

Selected novels

Notable Merrivale novels include The Plague Court Murders (1934), The Three Coffins (1935, published in Britain as The Hollow Man), famous for its "locked-room lecture" in which Merrivale catalogues the ways such crimes can be committed, The Judas Window (1938), and She Died a Lady (1943).

Other media

Merrivale appeared in radio adaptations during the golden age of detective fiction; Anthony Boucher and other critics ranked the Merrivale books among the finest impossible-crime stories ever written.
