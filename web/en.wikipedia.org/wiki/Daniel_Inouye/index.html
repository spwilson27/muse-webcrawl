Daniel Inouye

Daniel Ken Inouye (September 7, 1924 – December 17, 2012) was an American attorney, soldier, and statesman who served as a United States senator from Hawaii from 1963 until his death in 2012. A member of the Democratic Party, he was the President pro tempore of the United States Senate from 2010 until his death, making him the highest-ranking Asian-American politician in U.S. history at the time.

Military service

Inouye served in the United States Army during World War II with the famed 442nd Regimental Combat Team, composed largely of Japanese Americans. In April 1945, during an assault in Italy, he was gravely wounded and lost his right arm; he was awarded the Medal of Honor for his actions in that battle. His wartime service ended his plans to become a surgeon.

Political career

After the war, Inouye earned a law degree and entered Hawaiian territorial politics. When Hawaii achieved statehood in 1959, he became its first U.S. representative. Elected to the Senate in 1962, he served on the Watergate Committee (1973–74) and chaired the Iran–Contra committee in 1987. As chairman of the Senate Appropriations Committee, he was one of the most influential senators of his era and a powerful advocate for Hawaii and for Native Hawaiian and Native American interests.

Legacy

Inouye received many honours, including the Presidential Medal of Freedom (posthumously, 2013). Honolulu International Airport was renamed Daniel K. Inouye International Airport in his honour.
