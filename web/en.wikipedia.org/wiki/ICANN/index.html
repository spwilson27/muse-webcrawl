ICANN

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
ICANN
Founded18 September 1998
TypeNon-profit organisation
PurposeCoordination of DNS, IP addresses and protocol parameters
HeadquartersLos Angeles, California, US
[Image: Photograph of an ICANN public meeting with rows of delegates and interpretation booths]

The Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers (ICANN) is an American multistakeholder group and nonprofit organisation responsible for coordinating the maintenance and procedures of several databases related to the namespaces and numerical spaces of the Internet, ensuring the network's stable and secure operation.

Through its IANA (Internet Assigned Numbers Authority) functions, ICANN coordinates the allocation of IP addresses to the regional registries, the assignment of protocol parameters defined by the Internet Engineering Task Force, and the DNS root zone — the master list of top-level domains and their operators. It accredits registrars, runs the new-gTLD programme and sets dispute policy (UDRP) for domain names.

ICANN was created in 1998 to take these functions over from the US government, which had funded the early network. For most of its life IANA operated under a contract with the US National Telecommunications and Information Administration; in October 2016 that stewardship was transferred to the global multistakeholder community, a transition long debated as a test of whether the Internet's core identifiers could be governed without any single government. ICANN's bottom-up, consensus-based policy development — and its occasional controversies, from the .xxx decision to the blocked sale of the .org registry in 2020 — make it the closest thing the decentralised Internet has to a government.
