Ōura Church

Ōura Church Formal name: Basilica of the Twenty-Six Holy Martyrs of Japan
Location: Nagasaki, Japan
Completed: 1864 (present wooden Gothic structure)
Status: National Treasure of Japan; UNESCO World Heritage Site (2018)
Ōura Church, Nagasaki — the oldest standing church in Japan.

The Ōura Church (大浦天主堂, Ōura Tenshudō), formally the Basilica of the Twenty-Six Holy Martyrs of Japan, is a Catholic church in Nagasaki, Japan, built in 1864 by French missionaries of the Paris Foreign Missions Society. It is the oldest standing Christian church building in Japan and has been designated a National Treasure.

The church was erected for the growing French and foreign community after Japan's opening to trade, and it faces in the direction of Nishizaka Hill, where twenty-six Christians had been crucified in 1597. Its fame rests on the events of 17 March 1865, when a group of villagers from Urakami approached the priest Bernard Petitjean and revealed themselves as descendants of the Kakure Kirishitan — Japan's "Hidden Christians", who had preserved their faith in secret through more than two centuries of persecution. The event is known as the "Discovery of the Christians".

Heritage

The original wooden Gothic Revival building, with its distinctive white walls and a statue of the Virgin Mary on the approach steps, survived the atomic bombing of 1945 despite lying close to the hypocentre. In 2018 the church was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the "Hidden Christian Sites in the Nagasaki Region".
