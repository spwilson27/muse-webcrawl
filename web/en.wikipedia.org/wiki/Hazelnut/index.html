Hazelnut

The hazelnut is the fruit of the hazel tree, especially the common hazel Corylus avellana and the filbert Corylus maxima. The nut is roughly spherical to oval, enclosed in a leafy husk, with a hard brown shell and an edible kernel rich in oil, protein and vitamin E.

Cultivation

Turkey is by far the world's largest producer, accounting for around 60–70 percent of global production, centred on the Black Sea provinces of Ordu, Giresun and Trabzon. Italy is the second-largest producer, followed by the United States (mainly Oregon), Azerbaijan, Georgia and Spain.

Uses

Hazelnuts are eaten raw or roasted and are widely used in confectionery: in praline, chocolate spreads such as Nutella, gianduja, nougat and baked goods. Hazelnut oil is used in cooking and cosmetics.

History

Hazelnuts have been gathered since prehistoric times; large quantities of charred shells found in Mesolithic sites in Scotland and elsewhere show they were an important food. The name filbert is traditionally associated with St Philbert's day, around the start of the harvest.
