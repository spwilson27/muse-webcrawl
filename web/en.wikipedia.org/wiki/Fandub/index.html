Fandub

A fandub is a fan-made dub of a media production — most commonly an anime episode, film, or video game — in which amateurs record their own voices to replace the original soundtrack, usually translating dialogue from a language in which an official dub is unavailable. Fandubs are typically distributed online and are created by enthusiasts motivated by affection for the source work rather than by profit.

History

Fan dubbing emerged in the 1980s and 1990s as anime fandom grew outside Japan and editing home video became feasible. Early fandubs were painstakingly assembled with VCRs and analogue audio equipment, and circulated at conventions and through tape trading. The spread of broadband internet, cheap microphones and free editing software in the 2000s made fandubbing far more accessible, and video-sharing sites allowed fandubs to reach large audiences.

Types

Straight fandubs attempt, as far as amateur resources allow, to reproduce the original script faithfully, with fans casting voices to match the characters. Parody fandubs (sometimes called "abridged series") deliberately rewrite dialogue for comic effect; popular abridged series have developed devoted followings and professional-quality production despite their unofficial status. Fandubs also cover songs, trailers and video game cutscenes.

Legal status

Fandubs are unauthorised derivative works and generally infringe the copyright of the original producers, though enforcement varies; rights holders often tolerate them where no official version exists or where the fandub plainly promotes the work, while taking down others. Some fandubbers have gone on to professional voice-acting careers.
