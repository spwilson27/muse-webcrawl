The Algonquin Hotel is a historic hotel in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, opened in 1902. It is famous in American literary history as the meeting place of the Algonquin Round Table, a celebrated circle of writers, critics and actors who lunched there through the 1920s.

The Round Table

Beginning in 1919, figures including Dorothy Parker, Robert Benchley, Alexander Woollcott, George S. Kaufman, Edna Ferber, Franklin P. Adams and Harpo Marx met almost daily in the hotel's Rose Room. Their witty conversation was quoted in newspaper columns nationwide, making the "Vicious Circle" synonymous with sophisticated New York humour. The hotel's longtime manager, Frank Case, encouraged the group; The New Yorker magazine was partly inspired by, and partly financed through, Round Table connections.

Later history

The Algonquin has long cultivated a literary image: it is said to have been home to a succession of resident cats since the 1920s, immortalised in the musical Cats connections of its famous lobby. Designated a New York City landmark in 1987 and a National Literary Landmark in 1996, it continues to operate as an upscale hotel under Marriott's Autograph Collection.
