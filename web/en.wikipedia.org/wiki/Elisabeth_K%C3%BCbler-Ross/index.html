Elisabeth Kübler-Ross

Elisabeth Kübler-Ross
Born: 8 July 1926, Zurich, Switzerland
Died: 24 August 2004, Scottsdale, Arizona
Occupation: psychiatrist, author
Known for: five stages of grief model

Elisabeth Kübler-Ross (1926–2004) was a Swiss-American psychiatrist and a pioneer in near-death studies, best known for her book On Death and Dying (1969), which introduced the model of the five stages of grief.

The five stages

Based on interviews with terminally ill patients, Kübler-Ross described a pattern of emotional responses to impending death: denial, anger, bargaining, depression, and acceptance. The model, later extended to grief generally, shaped hospice care and public understanding of dying, though researchers note the stages are not a strict sequence everyone follows.

Career

Trained in Zurich, she moved to the United States in 1958 and worked at hospitals in New York, Colorado, and Chicago, where her seminars with dying patients drew wide attention at a time when death was rarely discussed openly in medicine. She championed the hospice movement and the idea that the terminally ill should be listened to rather than avoided.

Later life and legacy

Kübler-Ross wrote more than twenty books, founded a healing centre in Virginia, and worked with AIDS patients and bereaved children. She was inducted into the National Women's Hall of Fame in 2007.
