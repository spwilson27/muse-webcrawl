Delaware Bay

Delaware Bay is the estuary outlet of the Delaware River on the northeast seaboard of the United States. Approximately 782 square miles (2,030 km²) in area, the bay's freshwater mixes with salt water from the Atlantic Ocean. It is bordered by the state of Delaware to the west (Cape Henlopen) and New Jersey to the east (Cape May), the two capes marking its mouth at the Atlantic.

Geography and ecology

The bay is an important shipping channel for the ports of Philadelphia, Wilmington, and Camden, and is reached by oceangoing vessels through a dredged channel. Its extensive tidal marshes and mudflats are of global ecological significance: each spring the bay shore hosts one of the world's great wildlife spectacles, when migratory shorebirds — most famously the red knot — stop to feed on the eggs of spawning horseshoe crabs. The bay is designated a Ramsar wetland of international importance and is known for its oyster beds, historically centred on the New Jersey side (the "Delaware Bay oyster" fishery).

History

The bay was explored for the Dutch by Henry Hudson in 1609 and named for Thomas West, 3rd Baron De La Warr, an early governor of Virginia. Dutch and Swedish colonies (including New Sweden, founded 1638) were established on its shores in the seventeenth century. The Cape May–Lewes Ferry now connects the two capes.
