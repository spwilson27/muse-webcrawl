Credit history

A credit history records how a borrower has managed credit over time

A credit history is a record of a borrower's responsible repayment of debts. It documents the credit accounts a person or business has held, how much has been borrowed, and whether payments were made on time. Credit histories are maintained by credit bureaus and summarised in credit reports, from which credit scores are calculated.

Contents of a credit history

A typical consumer credit history lists identifying information; each credit account (credit cards, mortgages, auto and student loans) with its opening date, credit limit or loan amount, balance, and payment record; recent credit inquiries made by lenders; and adverse public records such as bankruptcies, foreclosures, and accounts sent to collections. Late payments, defaults, and charge-offs are recorded and generally remain on a report for several years — in the United States, most negative items remain for seven years and bankruptcies for up to ten.

Use in lending decisions

Lenders use credit history as the primary evidence of a borrower's creditworthiness. A long history of on-time payments and moderate utilisation of available credit tends to produce a high credit score and access to lower interest rates, while missed payments and defaults make borrowing more expensive or unavailable. Landlords, insurers, and some employers also consult credit history where the law permits.

Building and repairing credit history

People with little or no credit history — young adults, recent immigrants, or those who have avoided borrowing — may find it difficult to obtain credit. Common ways to build a history include secured credit cards, credit-builder loans, and becoming an authorised user on another person's card. Disputed errors can be corrected through the bureau's dispute process under laws such as the US Fair Credit Reporting Act.
