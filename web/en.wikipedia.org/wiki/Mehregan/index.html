Mehregan

Mehregan (Persian: مهرگان) is an ancient Persian festival celebrating the god Mithra (Mehr in Middle and Modern Persian), the yazata of covenant, light and oath. It is the second most important festival of the Persian calendar after Nowruz, and is traditionally held around the autumn equinox season, on the day of Mehr in the month of Mehr — corresponding to early October in the Gregorian calendar.

Origins and observance

Mehregan dates back at least to the era of the Achaemenid Empire and is rooted in Zoroastrian tradition. Historically it marked the harvest season and, according to tradition, the day on which Fereydun defeated the tyrant Zahhak. Celebrations traditionally involve wearing new clothes, setting a table (sofreh) with items including marjoram, a mirror, pomegranates, rosewater and sweets, feasting, music and giving thanks for the harvest. The festival is still celebrated by Iranians and by Zoroastrian communities in Iran, Tajikistan and the diaspora.
