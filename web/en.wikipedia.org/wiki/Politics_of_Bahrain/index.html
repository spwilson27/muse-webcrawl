Politics of Bahrain

The politics of Bahrain take place within a constitutional monarchy headed by the King, Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa. Executive authority is exercised by the king together with the prime minister and cabinet; the king appoints the prime minister and ministers.

Government

The National Assembly is bicameral: the Council of Representatives has 40 elected members, while the Shura Council has 40 members appointed by the king. Political parties are not formally legalised, but political societies function as de facto parties. Bahrain's population is majority Shia, while the ruling Al Khalifa family is Sunni, a divide that has shaped the country's politics.

Recent history

Reforms under the 2001 National Action Charter led to elections from 2002. In 2011, Bahrain experienced large pro-democracy protests as part of the Arab Spring, which were suppressed with the help of Gulf Cooperation Council forces.
