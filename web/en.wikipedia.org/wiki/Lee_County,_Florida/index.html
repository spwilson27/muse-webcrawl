Lee County is a county located in southwestern Florida, on the Gulf Coast. As of the 2020 census, its population was 760,822. The county seat is Fort Myers, and the largest city is Cape Coral. Lee County comprises the Cape Coral–Fort Myers metropolitan statistical area.

History

Lee County was created in 1887 from Monroe County and named for Robert E. Lee, the Confederate general during the American Civil War. The county's early economy was based on cattle, citrus and fishing. Fort Myers became known as a winter retreat for Thomas Edison and Henry Ford, whose winter estates are preserved today. The completion of the Tamiami Trail and, later, Interstate 75 spurred rapid growth in the second half of the twentieth century, and Cape Coral — founded in 1957 as a planned community — grew into one of the largest cities in Florida by land area.

Geography

The county covers roughly 1,212 square miles, of which about 785 square miles are land and the remainder water. It includes a chain of barrier islands along the Gulf of Mexico, including Sanibel Island, Captiva Island, Pine Island and Estero Island (Fort Myers Beach), known for shelling beaches and wildlife refuges such as the J. N. "Ding" Darling National Wildlife Refuge. The Caloosahatchee River runs through the county from Lake Okeechobee to the Gulf.

Communities

Incorporated communities include Fort Myers, Cape Coral, Bonita Springs, Sanibel, Fort Myers Beach and Estero. Large unincorporated areas include Lehigh Acres, North Fort Myers and Pine Island Center. The county was severely impacted by Hurricane Ian in September 2022, which made landfall near Cayo Costa and caused catastrophic damage along the coast.

Transportation and education

Southwest Florida International Airport (RSW) serves the region. The county is crossed by Interstate 75 and U.S. Route 41. Institutions of higher education include Florida Gulf Coast University and Florida SouthWestern State College.
