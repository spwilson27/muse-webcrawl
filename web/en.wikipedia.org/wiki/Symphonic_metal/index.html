Symphonic metal

Stylistic originsHeavy metal, classical music, power metal, gothic metal
Cultural originsEarly-to-mid 1990s, Europe
Typical instrumentsElectric guitar, bass, drums, keyboards, orchestra, choir, operatic vocals

Symphonic metal is a cross-generic style of heavy metal music that combines elements of various metal subgenres with orchestral and symphonic arrangements drawn from classical music. It commonly features full orchestras or orchestral keyboards, choirs, and classically trained — often female — lead vocalists with operatic technique.

History

Early roots are found in bands that experimented with orchestration, such as Rainbow, and in the neoclassical playing of Yngwie Malmsteen. The style coalesced in the mid-1990s through Therion's Theli (1996), which joined death-metal roots to opera singers and orchestra, and through the Dutch and Finnish scenes: Within Temptation, Epica, and After Forever in the Netherlands, and Nightwish in Finland, whose albums Oceanborn (1998) and Once (2004) brought the genre mainstream success. Other major acts include Rhapsody of Fire, Xandria, Delain, and the symphonic black metal of Dimmu Borgir and Cradle of Filth.

Characteristics

Symphonic metal is defined less by a single rhythmic style than by its arrangements: sweeping string and brass parts, choirs, piano, and keyboards layered over metal instrumentation, frequently with concept-album narratives drawn from fantasy, mythology, and literature. It overlaps heavily with power metal and gothic metal and is one of the most commercially successful metal styles in Europe.
