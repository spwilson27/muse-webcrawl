Bún bò Huế

Bún bò Huế is a Vietnamese soup containing rice vermicelli (bún) and beef (bò), originating from Huế, the former imperial capital of Vietnam. It is known for its balance of spicy, sour, salty and sweet flavours and a distinctive lemongrass aroma.

Preparation

The broth is made by simmering beef and pork bones with lemongrass, seasoned with fish sauce, sugar and annatto oil for its characteristic orange-red colour, and spiced with chilli. Typical ingredients include sliced beef shank, pork knuckles, cubes of congealed pig's blood, and sometimes crab or pork meatballs (chả). The noodles are thicker and rounder than those used in phở.

Serving

The soup is garnished with onions, scallions, Vietnamese coriander and served with fresh herbs, bean sprouts, shredded banana blossom, lime wedges and split water spinach stems.

Bún bò Huế is widely eaten for breakfast in Vietnam and throughout the Vietnamese diaspora, and is regarded as the signature dish of Huế cuisine alongside cơm hến and bánh bèo.
