Gonbad-e Kavus

Gonbad-e Kavus (Persian: گنبد کاووس), also rendered Gonbad-e Kāvūs or Gonbad-e Qabus, is a city in the Central District of Gonbad-e Kavus County, Golestan province, Iran, serving as capital of both the county and the district. It lies near the historic site of Gorgan (Jurjan) in north-eastern Iran, close to the border with Turkmenistan.

Gonbad-e Qabus tower

The city is famous for the monumental brick tower of Gonbad-e Qabus, built in 1006–07 CE for Qabus ibn Wushmgir, the Ziyarid emir and man of letters. The cylindrical tomb tower rises about 53 metres (72 metres including its foundation hill) and is inscribed with one of the earliest dated examples of muqarnas-free brick calligraphy in Kufic script. It is the tallest wholly brick tower in the world and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2012.

Demographics and culture

The population is largely Iranian Turkmen, and the city is a centre of Turkmen culture in Iran, known for Turkmen horse breeding, carpet weaving and the nearby Khaled Nabi shrine and cemetery in the Turkmen Sahra hills.
