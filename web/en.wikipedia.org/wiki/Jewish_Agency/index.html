The Jewish Agency — formally the Jewish Agency for Palestine, expanded in 1929 from the Palestine Zionist Executive — was the body recognised under the League of Nations Mandate for Palestine as the public body to advise and cooperate with the British administration on matters affecting the establishment of the Jewish national home. It is the predecessor institution of today's Jewish Agency for Israel.

History

Article 4 of the Mandate provided for a Jewish agency, initially fulfilled by the Zionist Organization. At the 1929 Zionist Congress in Zurich, Chaim Weizmann broadened it to include non-Zionist Jewish philanthropists. During the 1930s and 1940s the Agency, led from 1935 by David Ben-Gurion, functioned as a proto-government of the Yishuv: organising immigration, settlement, education, and the Haganah defence force.

After the founding of the State of Israel in 1948, many Agency functions passed to the Israeli government, and the organisation was reconstituted as the Jewish Agency for Israel.
