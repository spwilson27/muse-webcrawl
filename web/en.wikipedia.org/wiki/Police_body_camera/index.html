Police body camera

A police body camera or body-worn camera (BWC) is a wearable audio, video, and photographic recording device used by law enforcement officers to record their interactions with the public. Typically worn on the chest or shoulder (and sometimes mounted on glasses or helmets), the cameras are promoted as a tool for accountability, transparency, and evidence gathering. Major suppliers include Axon (formerly TASER International), whose cameras and Evidence.com cloud platform dominate the US market, along with Motorola Solutions and Visual Labs.

History and adoption

British police trialled body cameras in Plymouth in 2005–2006, and the technology spread through UK forces in the following decade. Adoption in the United States accelerated sharply after 2014, following the shooting of Michael Brown in Ferguson, Missouri, and a White House programme under President Barack Obama that funded camera purchases for state and local departments. In 2021–2022, executive orders required US federal law enforcement officers to wear cameras during arrests and searches. Many countries in Europe and elsewhere have since equipped front-line officers.

Evidence and debate

An early randomized trial in Rialto, California (2012) reported large reductions in use-of-force incidents and citizen complaints after cameras were introduced, and a number of subsequent studies found reductions in complaints. However, larger multi-site trials, including a major study in Washington, D.C., found no statistically significant average effects on use of force or complaints, suggesting that outcomes depend heavily on departmental policy — especially whether officers must activate cameras during encounters and face sanctions for failing to do so. Policy debates continue over activation requirements, public release of footage under freedom-of-information laws, retention periods, privacy of bystanders and victims, and the cost of data storage. Critics also note that footage reflects the officer's viewpoint and may be interpreted accordingly, while supporters regard cameras as an indispensable record in contested encounters.
