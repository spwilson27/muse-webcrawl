Aberdaron is a village and community near the western tip of the Llŷn Peninsula in Gwynedd, Wales. Sometimes called "the Land's End of Wales", it was historically the embarkation point for pilgrims crossing to Bardsey Island (Ynys Enlli), one of the most sacred sites in Welsh Christianity.

History

The parish church of St Hywyn preserves a class of early Christian inscribed stones and was an important clas church. In the Middle Ages, three pilgrimages to Bardsey were said to equal one to Rome. The poet and priest R. S. Thomas was vicar of Aberdaron from 1954 to 1967 and wrote some of his best-known work inspired by the area's landscape and people.

Geography

The community covers the far end of the peninsula, including the headlands of Braich y Pwll and the offshore islands of Bardsey and the Gwylanod. The beach at Porthor, famous for its "whistling sands", lies nearby. The Wales Coast Path terminates at Aberdaron.

Culture and language

Aberdaron is one of the strongest remaining heartlands of the Welsh language, spoken by a large majority of residents. Tourism, agriculture, and fishing are the main activities. The village has been a focus of campaigns to protect Welsh-speaking communities from second-home pressures.
