The Fox River is a river in eastern and central Wisconsin in the United States. It flows about 200 miles (320 km) in two segments: the Upper Fox River, running southwest from its headwaters to Lake Winnebago, and the Lower Fox River, which drops about 164 feet (50 m) over 39 miles from Lake Winnebago northeast to Green Bay on Lake Michigan.

History

The river formed part of the Fox–Wisconsin Waterway, a historic route used by Native Americans and French explorers such as Marquette and Joliet, who in 1673 crossed from the Fox to the Wisconsin River at the Portage, reaching the Mississippi. The steep Lower Fox later powered a dense corridor of paper mills around Appleton, Kaukauna, and De Pere.

Environmental issues

Decades of industrial discharge left the Lower Fox contaminated with polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs) from carbonless copy paper recycling. One of the largest sediment cleanup projects in U.S. history, costing around a billion dollars, dredged and capped the contaminated stretches, largely completing in 2020.
