Sharp (music)

In music, sharp, dièse (from French), or diesis (from Greek) means "higher in pitch." More specifically, in musical notation a sharp is an accidental sign (♯, similar to the number sign #) placed before a note that raises its pitch by one chromatic semitone. The sharp symbol evolved from a stylized form of the letter b.

The opposite of a sharp is a flat (♭), which lowers a pitch by one semitone; a natural sign (♮) cancels a preceding sharp or flat. A double sharp (𝄪) raises a note by two semitones. In twelve-tone equal temperament, most sharpened notes are enharmonically equivalent to a neighbouring natural or flattened note — for example, F♯ sounds the same pitch as G♭ — though they are spelled differently to reflect harmony and voice-leading.

Usage

Sharps appear both in key signatures, where they apply throughout a piece (the major keys accumulate sharps in the order F♯, C♯, G♯, D♯, A♯, E♯, B♯), and as accidentals within a measure, where they remain in force for that measure only. By extension, musicians also say a note is "sharp" when it is played slightly above the intended pitch, and string players "sharpen" a note when raising it.
