Wet lease

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Wet lease (ACMI)
ProvidesAircraft · Crew · Maintenance · Insurance
Typical term1 month – 2 years
Related termsDry lease · Damp lease

A wet lease is a leasing arrangement whereby one airline (the lessor) provides an aircraft, complete crew, maintenance, and insurance (ACMI) to another airline or business (the lessee), which pays by the number of hours operated. The lessee provides fuel, airport fees, and other direct operating costs, sells the capacity under its own flight numbers, and bears the commercial risk.

Usage

Wet leases are used to cover short-term capacity needs: seasonal peaks, aircraft grounded for maintenance, launching new routes before an airline's own aircraft are delivered, or replacing capacity during strikes and fleet transitions. Specialist ACMI carriers such as Air Atlanta Icelandic, Titan Airways, Hi Fly, and SmartLynx operate fleets largely devoted to wet-leasing. Airlines in the European Union may wet-lease from third-country operators only under restricted conditions and approval, reflecting safety-oversight and labour concerns.

Related arrangements

A damp lease (or moist lease) is a wet lease without the full crew — typically the lessor supplies the aircraft, maintenance, insurance and cockpit crew while the lessee provides cabin crew. A dry lease, by contrast, supplies only the aircraft; the lessee provides its own crew, maintenance, and insurance and operates the aircraft under its own air operator's certificate. Wet-leased aircraft operate under the lessor's certificate and call signs, which is why regulators treat the arrangement as a form of chartering.
