Mixtón War

Date1540–1542
LocationNueva Galicia, New Spain (modern Jalisco, Zacatecas, Nayarit, Aguascalientes)
CombatantsSpanish Empire and indigenous allies vs. Caxcan and allied peoples
ResultSpanish victory

The Mixtón War (1540–1542) was a major indigenous uprising against Spanish rule in the region of Nueva Galicia in western New Spain, in what is now the Mexican states of Jalisco, Zacatecas, Nayarit and Aguascalientes. Led principally by the Caxcan people under leaders such as Francisco Tenamaztle, the rebels fortified themselves on steep hilltop strongholds (peñoles) such as Mixtón and Nochistlán.

Course of the war

The revolt was driven by the brutal encomienda regime of Nuño de Guzmán and abuses by encomenderos. Rebels killed Spanish settlers and missionaries and threatened Guadalajara. An initial Spanish counterattack under Cristóbal de Oñate suffered a serious defeat; the actor and conquistador Pedro de Alvarado was mortally injured retreating from Nochistlán in 1541. Viceroy Antonio de Mendoza personally led a large army of Spaniards and tens of thousands of indigenous allies to crush the uprising, storming the peñoles one by one through 1541 and 1542.

Aftermath

The suppression was followed by enslavement and deportation of many rebels. Tenamaztle escaped and continued resistance for years before surrendering; taken to Spain, he pleaded the Caxcan case before the Council of the Indies with the support of Bartolomé de las Casas. The war's brutality contributed to debate over the New Laws of 1542 limiting encomienda abuses.
