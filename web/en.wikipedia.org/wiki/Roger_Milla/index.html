Roger Milla (born Albert Roger Mooh Miller, 20 May 1952) is a Cameroonian former professional footballer who played as a striker. He was one of the first African players to become a star on the international stage, and achieved worldwide fame at the 1990 FIFA World Cup, where his goals and corner-flag dance celebrations helped Cameroon become the first African team to reach the quarter-finals.

Milla's corner-flag dance became an iconic image of the 1990 World Cup.

Club career

Milla spent most of his club career in France, with Valenciennes, Monaco, Bastia, Saint-Étienne and Montpellier, winning the Coupe de France with Bastia in 1981, after beginning his career in Cameroon with clubs including Tonnerre Yaoundé. He twice won the African Footballer of the Year award, in 1976 and 1990.

International career

Milla played at the 1982 World Cup and retired from international football in 1987, but was persuaded by Cameroon's president to return for Italia '90. Aged 38, he scored four goals as a substitute, two of them against Romania and two in the round of 16 against Colombia, celebrating each with a dance around the corner flag. In 1994, at 42, he became the oldest goalscorer in World Cup history with a goal against Russia — a record he still holds.

Legacy

Milla was named by Pelé in the FIFA 100 list of the world's greatest living players, and in 2007 was voted the best African player of the previous 50 years by the Confederation of African Football.
