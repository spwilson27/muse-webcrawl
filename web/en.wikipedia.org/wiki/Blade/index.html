Blade

A blade is the portion of a tool, weapon or machine with an edge that is designed to puncture, chop, slice or scrape surfaces or materials. Blades are usually made of materials harder than those they are to work on, historically including flint, obsidian, copper, bronze, iron and steel, and in modern times also ceramics and coated alloys.

Anatomy

A blade typically has a cutting edge, a point (tip), flat sides, a spine opposite the edge, and a tang by which it is attached to a handle or hilt. Many blades feature a fuller — a rounded groove along the flat — to lighten the blade without much loss of strength. The geometry of the cross-section (flat, hollow, convex or chisel grind) controls how the blade cuts and how durable its edge is.

Materials and manufacture

Prehistoric blades were knapped from stone; metal blades were cast and hammered in bronze and later forged from iron and steel. Modern blades may be heat-treated, laminated or coated to balance sharpness, toughness and corrosion resistance. Industrial blades include saw blades, razor blades, scalpel blades, and the rotating blades of turbines, propellers, fans and wind turbines, where "blade" denotes an airfoil-shaped vane rather than a cutting edge.

Uses

Blades are central to knives, swords, axes, scissors, razors, surgical instruments and countless machines. Because many blades are weapons, their manufacture, sale and carrying are regulated in many jurisdictions.
