Liberation Music Orchestra

The Liberation Music Orchestra is a jazz big band founded in 1969 by double bassist Charlie Haden and pianist/arranger Carla Bley. Conceived as an explicitly political ensemble, its recordings weave avant-garde jazz with folk and revolutionary songs from struggles around the world — the Spanish Civil War, the American civil rights movement, and liberation movements in Latin America, Africa, and Asia.

History

Haden, disturbed by the 1968 Democratic National Convention in Chicago and the Vietnam War, assembled an orchestra of leading free-jazz players — among them Don Cherry, Gato Barbieri, Dewey Redman, Paul Motian, and Roswell Rudd — with Bley's arrangements. Their debut album, Liberation Music Orchestra (Impulse!, 1970), juxtaposed songs of the Spanish Republic with original compositions such as Haden's "Song for Ché" and ended with a fragment of "We Shall Overcome" dissolving into sounds evoking the Chicago convention turmoil. It was named album of the year by Melody Maker and remains a landmark of political jazz.

Later recordings

The orchestra reconvened periodically rather than touring continuously. The Ballad of the Fallen (ECM, 1982) addressed El Salvador and won a Down Beat critics' poll; Dream Keeper (1990) set Langston Hughes and addressed apartheid South Africa; Not in Our Name (2005) protested the Iraq War through American patriotic songs; and Time/Life (2016), completed by Bley after Haden's death in 2014, closed the project with environmental themes and Haden's final recordings.

Legacy

The orchestra demonstrated that large-ensemble jazz could carry overt political content without sacrificing musical ambition, and its blend of hymn-like melody and free improvisation influenced generations of composer-bandleaders.
