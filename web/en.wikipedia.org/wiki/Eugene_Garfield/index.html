Eugene Garfield

Eugene Eli Garfield (16 September 1925 – 26 February 2017) was an American information scientist and linguist, regarded as one of the founders of bibliometrics and scientometrics. He created the Science Citation Index and founded the Institute for Scientific Information (ISI), whose databases underlie the modern practice of measuring scientific impact.

Career

Born in New York City, Garfield earned a degree in chemistry from Columbia University and a doctorate in structural linguistics from the University of Pennsylvania in 1961. After working on machine translation and on the Welch Medical Indexing Project at Johns Hopkins, he founded his own company in Philadelphia in 1955, which became the Institute for Scientific Information. In 1958 he launched Current Contents, reproducing the contents pages of scientific journals; in 1964 ISI published the first Science Citation Index, indexing not articles' subjects but the references they cited — an idea Garfield had set out in a landmark 1955 paper in Science.

Contributions

Garfield devised the journal impact factor, popularised through the Journal Citation Reports, and developed algorithms for historiographs of scientific ideas. He founded The Scientist magazine in 1986 and wrote hundreds of essays on information science, many in his "Current Comments" series. His work made citation analysis a standard tool for evaluating research, libraries and careers — and a subject of lasting controversy about the proper use of quantitative indicators.

Honours

Among many awards, Garfield received the Derek de Solla Price Memorial Medal for contributions to scientometrics. ISI became part of Thomson Reuters and later Clarivate, whose Web of Science descends directly from his citation indexes.
