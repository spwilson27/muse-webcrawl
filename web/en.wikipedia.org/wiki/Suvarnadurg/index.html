Suvarnadurg (also spelled Severndroog) is a fort built on a rocky island in the Arabian Sea, off the coast of Harnai in the Ratnagiri district of Maharashtra, India. The name means "golden fort" in Sanskrit and Marathi. The fort covers about two and a half hectares and is surrounded by massive basalt walls.

History

The fort was constructed by the Bijapur Sultanate in the sixteenth century and later repaired and strengthened. In 1660 it was captured by the Maratha ruler Shivaji, who had its fortifications rebuilt and made it part of the coastal defence of the Maratha Empire. Under the Maratha navy, commanded by commanders such as Kanhoji Angre, Suvarnadurg served as an important naval base. In 1713 the fort was handed over to Kanhoji Angre, and in 1755 it was attacked and captured by a combined Peshwa and British force; it passed to the British in 1818 along with the other forts of the Konkan coast.

Description

The island fort is accessible by boat from Harnai, weather permitting, and its entrance is concealed among the rocks on the eastern side. Inside stand the ruins of palaces, magazines and temples. A smaller land fort, Kanakdurg, and the forts of Goa Fort and Fattegad on the shore opposite, guarded the harbour approaches.
