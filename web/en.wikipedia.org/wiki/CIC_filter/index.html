Cascaded integrator–comb filter

CIC filter
Type: FIR filter
Invented by: Eugene B. Hogenauer (1981)
Uses: Decimation, interpolation
Multipliers required: None

A cascaded integrator–comb (CIC) filter is a class of finite impulse response (FIR) filter used in digital signal processing for efficient decimation and interpolation between different sampling rates. It was introduced by Eugene Hogenauer in 1981 and is sometimes called a Hogenauer filter.

Structure

A CIC filter consists of N integrator stages operating at the high sample rate, a down-sampler, and N comb (differentiator) stages at the low rate. Remarkably, the structure requires no multipliers — only additions and subtractions — making it very economical in hardware such as FPGAs and ASICs for digital down-converters.

Characteristics

The frequency response is a sinc-like shape with nulls at multiples of the output rate, providing anti-aliasing for decimation. Drawbacks include passband droop, usually corrected with a compensation FIR filter, and the need for wide internal registers.
