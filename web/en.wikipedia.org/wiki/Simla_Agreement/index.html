The Simla Agreement, also spelled Shimla Agreement, was a peace treaty signed between India and Pakistan on 2 July 1972 in Shimla, following the Indo-Pakistani War of 1971. It was signed by Indian Prime Minister Indira Gandhi and Pakistani President Zulfikar Ali Bhutto.

Provisions

The agreement committed both countries to settle their differences by peaceful means through bilateral negotiations, without recourse to force and without third-party intervention. In Jammu and Kashmir, the ceasefire line of 17 December 1971 was converted into the Line of Control, which both sides agreed to respect "without prejudice to the recognised position of either side" and not to alter unilaterally. India returned more than 13,000 km² of territory captured in the west, while Pakistan agreed to the repatriation arrangements under which over 90,000 Pakistani prisoners of war held by India were eventually released, and Pakistan recognised Bangladesh in 1974.

Significance

The Simla Agreement remains the foundational document of India–Pakistan bilateralism: India cites it to argue that the Kashmir dispute must be settled bilaterally, while Pakistan has continued to seek international involvement. The agreement also established summit-level diplomacy between the two states as the principal mechanism for crisis management.
