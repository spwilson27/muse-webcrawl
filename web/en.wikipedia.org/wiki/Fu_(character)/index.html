Fu (character)

Fú (Chinese: 福; pinyin: fú) is a Chinese character meaning "fortune", "good luck" or "blessing". One of the most ubiquitous auspicious symbols in Chinese culture, the character is displayed on doors, walls, lanterns and gifts, especially during the Lunar New Year, and forms with lu (prosperity) and shou (longevity) the traditional trio of blessings known as Fu-Lu-Shou, personified as three star gods.

Usage and customs

The character is commonly written in calligraphy on square red paper rotated 45 degrees into a diamond shape and pasted on entrances to homes and businesses. A widespread custom is to hang the character upside down: because the word for "upside down" (dào) is a homophone of the word for "arrive", an inverted fu signifies that good fortune has arrived. Legend attributes the practice to the household of a Ming dynasty prince or, in other versions, to an order of the Hongwu Emperor, after which inverting the character became customary.

The character itself is composed of radicals suggesting an altar, a mouth, and a field or measure, and in oracle-bone and bronze inscriptions it originally depicted offerings to ancestors or gods in prayer for blessing. During the Spring Festival, images of the God of Fortune, Caishen, and representations of fu in forms such as bats — whose name in Chinese (biānfú) puns on fu — decorate homes throughout China and the Chinese diaspora.
