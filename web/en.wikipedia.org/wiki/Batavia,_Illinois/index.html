Batavia is a city in DuPage and Kane counties in the U.S. state of Illinois. A suburb of Chicago, it lies along the Fox River about 40 miles west of the city and had a population of 26,098 at the 2020 census. Along with neighboring Geneva and St. Charles, it forms the "Tri-City" area.

History

Batavia was settled in 1833 and is among the oldest communities in Kane County. In the late 19th century it became a major center of the American windmill industry — several windmill manufacturers operated in the city, earning it the nickname "The Windmill City" — and its limestone quarries supplied building stone across the region. The Bellevue Place sanitarium in Batavia was where Mary Todd Lincoln was briefly a patient in 1875 after the death of her husband.

Fermilab

Batavia is best known as the home of Fermilab, the United States' premier particle physics laboratory, established in 1967 on land including the former village of Weston. The laboratory's accelerators and experiments have made the city a center of high-energy physics research.

Geography

The city is divided by the Fox River, with its historic downtown and riverwalk on both banks. Batavia's Challenger Learning Center and the Batavia Depot Museum preserve aspects of its railroad and industrial heritage.
