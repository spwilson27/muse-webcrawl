Largest remainder method

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The largest remainder method is a rule for allocating seats in proportional representation: parties first receive seats according to the integer part of their proportional entitlement under a quota (commonly the Hare quota — total votes divided by seats — or the Droop quota), and remaining seats are distributed to the parties with the largest fractional remainders.

The method is intuitive and exactly proportional in appearance, but vulnerable to paradoxes: increasing the house size can cost a party a seat (the Alabama paradox, observed in US apportionment in 1880), and population shifts can paradoxically move seats (the population paradox). These defects drove many systems toward divisor (highest-averages) methods such as D'Hondt and Sainte-Laguë, though largest-remainder with the Hare quota remains in use, for example in Hong Kong's former Legislative Council elections and in several Latin American and European systems, and is the method of the German-speaking literature's Hare/Niemeyer procedure.
