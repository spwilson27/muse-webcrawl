Mick Jones, guitarist and joint frontman of the Clash.

Michael Geoffrey Jones (born 26 June 1955) is an English musician, singer, and songwriter best known as the co-founder, lead guitarist, co-lead vocalist, and songwriter of the punk rock band the Clash, with whom he was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2003. After the Clash he founded Big Audio Dynamite and later Carbon/Silicon with Tony James.

The Clash

Jones grew up in south London, largely raised by his grandmother, and played in the proto-punk band London SS before forming the Clash in 1976 with Joe Strummer, Paul Simonon, and Terry Chimes (later Topper Headon). Jones was the band's principal musical arranger, writing the music to most songs while Strummer supplied most lyrics; he sang lead on tracks including "Train in Vain", "Should I Stay or Should I Go", and "Lost in the Supermarket". Friction with Strummer and Simonon led to his dismissal from the band in 1983.

Big Audio Dynamite and later work

In 1984 Jones formed Big Audio Dynamite (B.A.D.) with Don Letts, fusing punk with dance, hip-hop, and reggae; the group scored hits with "E=MC²" and "Rush". He later reunited B.A.D. for tours in 2011. With Tony James he formed Carbon/Silicon, which released music freely online. Jones also worked as a producer, notably on the Libertines' first two albums, and toured with Gorillaz. In 2013 he and Simonon produced The Rise and Fall of The Clash documentary project and opened the "Black Market Clash" exhibition drawn from Jones's extensive personal archive of rock memorabilia.
