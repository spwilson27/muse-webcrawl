Public ownership

Public ownership, also called state ownership or government ownership, is the ownership of an industry, asset, or enterprise by the state or a public body representing a community as a whole. It contrasts with private ownership by individuals or private companies and with common ownership. Public ownership commonly takes the form of state-owned enterprises, municipal services, and public infrastructure such as roads, libraries, and water systems.

Forms

Public ownership may be exercised at national, regional, or local level. It includes full state ownership of enterprises, majority shareholdings in otherwise commercial companies, and ownership of natural resources held in trust. The transfer of privately owned assets into public ownership is known as nationalization; the reverse process is privatization.

Rationale and criticism

Arguments for public ownership invoke natural monopolies, public goods, market failures, security of supply, and democratic accountability for essential services. Arguments against emphasize potential inefficiency, lack of competitive pressure, politicized management, and fiscal burden. Economists debate the performance of publicly owned firms in terms of allocative efficiency and X-efficiency.
