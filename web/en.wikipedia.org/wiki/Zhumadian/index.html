Zhumadian (simplified Chinese: 驻马店; traditional Chinese: 駐馬店; pinyin: Zhùmǎdiàn) is a prefecture-level city in southern Henan province, China. It borders Xinyang to the south, Nanyang to the west, Pingdingshan to the northwest, Luohe to the north, Zhoukou to the northeast, and the province of Anhui to the east.

History

The name Zhumadian, meaning "horse-relay station", recalls its historic role as a post station on imperial courier routes. The area was long an agricultural centre on the North China Plain. In August 1975, the failure of the Banqiao Dam and dozens of other dams in the region during Typhoon Nina caused one of the deadliest floods in history, devastating Zhumadian prefecture.

Administration

Zhumadian administers one district, Yicheng District, and nine counties including Xiping, Shangcai, Pingyu, Zhengyang, Queshan, Biyang, Runan, Suiping, and Xincai. The municipal government is seated in Yicheng.

Geography and economy

The west of the prefecture is hilly, part of the Funiu and Tongbai mountain fringes, while the east is flat alluvial plain drained by tributaries of the Huai River, including the Ru River. Agriculture — wheat, corn, peanuts, and sesame — dominates the economy, supplemented by food processing and manufacturing.
