Bad Kreuznach is a town in the Bad Kreuznach district in Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany. It is a spa town, well known for its medieval bridgehouses and its saline springs, and is the largest district town in the Nahe valley, with a population of roughly 50,000.

History

The area was settled in Roman times, when a vicus named Cruciniacum stood here. The town's saline springs were exploited from the Middle Ages, and Kreuznach received town rights in 1232. Its landmark Old Nahe Bridge, built around 1300, is one of the few remaining bridges in the world with houses built upon it. The town received the "Bad" (spa) designation in recognition of its radon brine baths, developed in the 19th century.

Sights

Landmarks include the Brückenhäuser (bridge houses) on the Alte Nahebrücke, the Pauluskirche (St Paul's Church), the ruins of Kauzenburg castle above the town, and the graduation towers (Salinen) of the spa quarter in Bad Münster am Stein-Ebernburg, incorporated in 2014.

Economy

Bad Kreuznach is a centre of wine trade for the Nahe wine region, of health tourism, and of manufacturing, historically including optical and chemical industries.
