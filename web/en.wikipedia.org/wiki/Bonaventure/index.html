Bonaventure

Born: 1221, Bagnoregio, Papal States
Died: 15 July 1274, Lyon
Canonised: 1482 by Pope Sixtus IV
Feast day: 15 July
Honours: Doctor of the Church ("Seraphic Doctor")

Saint Bonaventure (Italian: San Bonaventura da Bagnoregio; 1221 – 15 July 1274), born Giovanni di Fidanza, was an Italian Catholic Franciscan bishop, cardinal, scholastic theologian and philosopher. The seventh Minister General of the Order of Friars Minor, he organised and moderated the young Franciscan order, and as a theologian he sought to integrate faith and reason in the Augustinian tradition. He was canonised in 1482 and declared a Doctor of the Church, with the title Doctor Seraphicus, in 1588.

Life

Giovanni was born at Bagnoregio in central Italy. According to tradition, as a child he was healed of a serious illness through the intercession of Francis of Assisi, which led to his name — from buona ventura, "good fortune". He studied at the University of Paris, joined the Franciscans around 1243, and taught theology alongside his contemporary Thomas Aquinas, with whom he was associated in defending the mendicant orders against their critics in the university. In 1257 he was elected Minister General of the Franciscan order, then torn by controversy between rigorist "Spirituals" and moderates; his balanced rule and his official biography of St Francis, the Legenda Maior, helped hold the order together. Pope Gregory X made him cardinal bishop of Albano in 1273; he died during the Second Council of Lyon, where he had been working for reunion with the Greek church.

Works and thought

Bonaventure's masterpiece, the Itinerarium mentis in Deum ("The Journey of the Mind into God"), written in 1259 at La Verna, maps the soul's ascent through creation to contemplation of God. His other works include the Breviloquium, the commentaries on the Sentences of Peter Lombard, and the Collationes in Hexaëmeron. Influenced by Augustine, he taught the illumination of the mind by God, the plurality of forms, and a strongly Christ-centred metaphysics, standing in creative contrast to Aquinas's Aristotelian synthesis.

Legacy

Bonaventure is venerated throughout the Catholic Church; St. Bonaventure University in New York is named for him, as are numerous churches and the city of Buenaventura connections in the Franciscan tradition.
