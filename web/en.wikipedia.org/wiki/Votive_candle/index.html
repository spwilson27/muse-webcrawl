Votive candle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Votive candles lit in prayer in a Catholic church.

A votive candle or prayer candle is a small candle, typically white or beeswax yellow, intended to be burnt as a votive offering in an act of Christian prayer, especially within the Roman Catholic, Lutheran, and Anglican churches, among others. In Christianity, votive candles are commonplace in many churches, as well as home altars, and symbolize the prayers the worshipper is offering for him or herself, or for other people.

Use

Candles are commonly lit before statues or images of Jesus, the Virgin Mary or saints, often in tiered stands in a side chapel or shrine. The worshipper typically makes a small donation, lights the candle from an already-burning one, and offers a prayer — for a special intention, in thanksgiving, or in memory of the dead. The burning flame is understood to symbolise the prayer continuing after the person has left, as well as Christ as the light of the world.

Forms

Votive candles are usually short, thick candles in glass holders, which protect the flame and allow the candle to burn safely for many hours. Tall seven-day candles in coloured glass, printed with images of saints, are common in Catholic and folk-devotional practice. Electric votive candles have been adopted in some churches where open flames pose a fire risk.

Other traditions

Lighting candles as an offering also occurs in other religions: in Buddhism and Hinduism, lamps and candles accompany offerings at shrines, and in Judaism candles such as the yahrzeit candle are lit in memory of the dead. Secular candlelight vigils use candles in a comparable symbolic role.
