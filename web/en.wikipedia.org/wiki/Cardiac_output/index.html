Cardiac output (CO), also known as heart output, denoted by the symbol Q, is the volume of blood pumped by the heart per unit of time. It is a fundamental measure of cardiovascular function and is calculated as the product of stroke volume (the volume of blood ejected in one beat) and heart rate.

Definition

Cardiac output = stroke volume × heart rate. A healthy resting adult typically has a cardiac output of about 5 litres per minute. During maximal exercise this can rise to 20–25 L/min in untrained individuals and up to 40 L/min in elite endurance athletes.

Measurement

The gold standard is pulmonary artery catheterization with thermodilution. The Fick principle estimates output from oxygen consumption and the arteriovenous oxygen difference. Non-invasive methods include echocardiography, cardiac MRI, and pulse-contour analysis.

Regulation

Output is governed by preload, afterload, myocardial contractility, and heart rate, under autonomic control. The Frank–Starling law states that increased ventricular filling increases stroke volume. Catecholamines raise both rate and contractility during stress and exercise.

Clinical significance

Low cardiac output occurs in heart failure, hypovolaemia, and cardiogenic shock; unusually high output occurs in sepsis, hyperthyroidism, and severe anaemia. Cardiac index normalizes output to body surface area.
