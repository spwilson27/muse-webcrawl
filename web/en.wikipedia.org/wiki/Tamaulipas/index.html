Tamaulipas is a state in northeastern Mexico, bordering the U.S. state of Texas to the north along the Rio Grande (Río Bravo) and the Gulf of Mexico to the east. Its capital is Ciudad Victoria, and its major cities include Reynosa, Matamoros, Nuevo Laredo, and the port of Tampico.

Geography and economy

The state ranges from the coastal plain of the Gulf to the Sierra Madre Oriental in the west, and includes the El Cielo Biosphere Reserve, a notable cloud-forest ecosystem. Tamaulipas is an important centre of manufacturing — especially maquiladora plants in the border cities — agriculture (sorghum, citrus), cattle ranching, fishing, and energy, with significant natural gas production. The border crossings at Nuevo Laredo and Reynosa are among the busiest points for U.S.–Mexico trade.

History and security

Colonised by José de Escandón in the mid-18th century as Nuevo Santander, the state was named after the Sierra de Tamaulipas. In the 21st century Tamaulipas has been heavily affected by organised-crime violence associated with the Gulf Cartel and Los Zetas — including the 2010 San Fernando massacre of migrants — making it one of Mexico's most dangerous states, though violence has fluctuated over time.
