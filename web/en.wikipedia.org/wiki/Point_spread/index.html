Point spread


A sportsbook display with spreads

In sports betting, the point spread is a handicap set by bookmakers between two teams. The stronger team or player (the favourite) must win by more than the spread for a bet on them to pay out; the weaker team (the underdog) can lose by less than the spread, or win outright, for a bet on them to pay. Spread betting aims to attract equal action on both sides, allowing the bookmaker to profit from the commission (vig).

Mechanics

A spread is written as a number with a half-point to avoid ties, for example −7.5 for the favourite: the favourite must win by 8 or more points. If the game's margin lands exactly on a whole-number spread, the result is a "push" and stakes are refunded. Bookmakers adjust the spread as bets come in, and "buying points" lets bettors move the spread in exchange for worse odds.

History

Point-spread betting was developed in the United States in the 1940s, often credited to Chicago bookmaker Charles K. McNeil, a former mathematics teacher. It transformed American football and basketball betting, and the spread — together with the moneyline and the over/under total — remains one of the standard ways of wagering on team sports.
