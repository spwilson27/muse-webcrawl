Stonewall (officially Stonewall Equality Limited) is a lesbian, gay, bisexual and transgender (LGBT) rights charity in the United Kingdom. It is the largest LGBT rights organisation in Europe. Named after the 1969 Stonewall riots in New York, it was formed in 1989 by campaigners including Ian McKellen, Lisa Power, and Michael Cashman to lobby against Section 28 of the Local Government Act 1988, which prohibited the "promotion of homosexuality" by local authorities.

Campaigns and achievements

Stonewall campaigned successfully for the repeal of Section 28 (achieved in Scotland in 2000 and England and Wales in 2003), the equalisation of the age of consent, the lifting of the ban on LGBT people serving in the armed forces (2000), civil partnerships (2004), the Equality Act, and equal marriage (2013 in England and Wales). Its Diversity Champions programme advises employers on workplace inclusion, and its Workplace Equality Index ranks participating organisations. In 2015 Stonewall formally became trans-inclusive in its campaigning.

Controversy

Since the late 2010s Stonewall's positions on gender identity and its workplace schemes have attracted criticism and debate in the UK, and a number of public bodies have left the Diversity Champions programme; the charity maintains that its work supports inclusive workplaces and trans rights.
