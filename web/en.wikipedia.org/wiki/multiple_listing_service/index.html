Multiple listing service

A multiple listing service (MLS) is an organization with a suite of services that real estate brokers use to establish contractual offers of compensation and accumulate and disseminate information to facilitate real estate transactions. Its database of properties for sale allows brokers to see one another's listings and share commissions.

History and operation

The MLS concept originated in the United States in the late 1800s, when brokers met to exchange listing information and agreed to compensate cooperating brokers. Today hundreds of local and regional MLSs operate in the U.S. and Canada, most affiliated with the National Association of Realtors, and their data feeds power public portals such as Zillow and Realtor.com through IDX agreements.

Criticism and legal issues

The MLS system has faced antitrust scrutiny, including a 2008 U.S. Department of Justice settlement with the National Association of Realtors and major 2023–2024 class-action verdicts and settlements over commission practices that changed how buyer-agent compensation is offered.
