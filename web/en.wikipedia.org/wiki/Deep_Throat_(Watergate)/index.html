Deep Throat is the pseudonym given to the secret informant who provided information in 1972 and 1973 to Bob Woodward, who shared it with Carl Bernstein, implicating the administration of President Richard Nixon in what became known as the Watergate scandal. The reporter's most famous and influential anonymous source, Deep Throat's identity remained a closely guarded secret for more than thirty years, until in 2005 he was revealed to be Mark Felt, the former Associate Deputy Director of the FBI.

The meetings

Woodward met Felt in an underground parking garage in Rosslyn, Virginia, using a signalling system involving a red-flagged flowerpot on Woodward's apartment balcony and marks in his copy of The New York Times. Felt insisted on absolute confidentiality — Woodward called him only "my friend" and an "incurable gossip" on a sensitive confidential background basis — and used the meetings to confirm information and steer the reporters, insisting the story was far bigger than a single burglary, reaching to the CIA, the FBI and the Justice Department.

His most quoted advice, "Follow the money", was actually written for the 1976 film All the President's Men by screenwriter William Goldman; it does not appear in Woodward and Bernstein's book.

Unmasking

Speculation about Deep Throat's identity became a national guessing game; candidates proposed over the years included Alexander Haig, William Rehnquist, John Dean and George H. W. Bush. On 31 May 2005, Vanity Fair published an article by Felt's family's lawyer, John D. O'Connor, in which the 91-year-old Felt acknowledged: "I'm the guy they used to call Deep Throat." Woodward, Bernstein and former Post editor Ben Bradlee confirmed the identification the same day.
