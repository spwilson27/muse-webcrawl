William Bratton

William Joseph Bratton (born October 6, 1947) is an American law enforcement officer and businessman who served as chief of police of the Los Angeles Police Department, chief of the New York City Transit Police, commissioner of the Boston Police Department, and twice as New York City Police Commissioner (1994–1996 and 2014–2016). He is widely associated with the broken windows theory of policing and with the CompStat crime-management system.

Career

Bratton began his career with the Boston Police Department in 1970 after military police service in Vietnam. Rising through the ranks, he headed the Massachusetts Metropolitan District Commission Police and the MBTA police before becoming Boston police commissioner in 1993. As chief of the New York City Transit Police from 1990, he targeted fare evasion and disorder and began applying broken windows ideas.

Appointed NYPD commissioner by Mayor Rudy Giuliani in 1994, Bratton introduced CompStat, a data-driven system of mapping crime and holding precinct commanders accountable, and oversaw a sharp decline in New York's crime rates. He resigned in 1996 amid tensions with the mayor. From 2002 to 2009 he served as chief of the Los Angeles Police Department, working under a federal consent decree and presiding over continued crime reductions there.

Mayor Bill de Blasio returned Bratton to the NYPD commissionership in 2014. His second tenure coincided with historically low crime in New York but also debate over stop-and-frisk practices and police-community relations; he stepped down in 2016 and was succeeded by James P. O'Neill. Bratton has since worked in security consulting and co-authored books including his memoir Turnaround.
