Gao is a city in Mali and the capital of the Gao Region, situated on the left bank of the Niger River at the edge of the Sahara Desert, about 320 km (200 mi) east-southeast of Timbuktu. Historically one of the great trading cities of West Africa, Gao was the capital of the Songhai Empire, which dominated the western Sudan in the 15th and 16th centuries.

History

Founded around the 7th century by Songhai fishermen, Gao grew rich on trans-Saharan trade in gold, salt and slaves. It became the capital of the Songhai Empire under Sonni Ali in the late 15th century and flourished under Askia Muhammad, whose tomb — the Tomb of Askia, a great mud-brick pyramid — is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Moroccan forces destroyed Songhai power at the Battle of Tondibi in 1591, after which Gao declined. In 2012 the city was seized by Tuareg and Islamist rebels during Mali's northern conflict before being retaken by French and Malian forces in January 2013.

Geography and economy

Gao serves as a river port and market town for the surrounding region, connected to Timbuktu and Bamako by road and by seasonal river traffic. Its population, of roughly 87,000, is largely Songhai, with Tuareg and other communities.
