Information geometry

Information geometry is the study of families of probability distributions using the tools of differential geometry. A parametric family of distributions is treated as a smooth manifold — a statistical manifold — whose points are distributions and whose coordinates are the parameters. Geometric notions such as distance, curvature, and geodesics then acquire statistical meaning.

Fisher information metric

The central object is the Fisher information metric, a Riemannian metric defined by the Fisher information matrix. C. R. Rao proposed this geometric viewpoint in 1945, and a theorem of Chentsov established the Fisher metric as (up to a constant) the unique metric on statistical manifolds that is invariant under sufficient statistics. Distances measured with this metric relate to the distinguishability of nearby distributions, and the Cramér–Rao bound can be interpreted geometrically.

Dual connections

Shun-ichi Amari developed the modern theory in the 1980s, introducing pairs of dual affine connections (notably the exponential and mixture connections) that make exponential families dually flat. This duality underlies results in estimation theory and optimisation, and divergence functions such as the Kullback–Leibler divergence generalise squared distance on these manifolds.

Applications

Information geometry is applied in machine learning (natural gradient descent, which follows the Fisher-metric geometry of parameter space), signal processing, neural networks, and statistical physics.
