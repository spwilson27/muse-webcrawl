Polypill

A polypill is a single pill containing a fixed-dose combination of several medications, typically aimed at preventing cardiovascular disease. The concept was prominently proposed by Nicholas Wald and Malcolm Law in 2003, who suggested a pill combining a statin, three blood-pressure-lowering drugs at half dose, folic acid, and aspirin.

Rationale

Combining preventive drugs in one pill aims to improve adherence, simplify prescribing, and reduce cost, particularly in low- and middle-income countries. Wald and Law estimated such a pill could reduce ischaemic heart disease events by about 88 percent and strokes by about 80 percent in people over 55.

Evidence

Trials such as PolyIran and TIPS-3 have shown that polypill strategies reduce cardiovascular events compared with usual care or placebo in at-risk populations.
