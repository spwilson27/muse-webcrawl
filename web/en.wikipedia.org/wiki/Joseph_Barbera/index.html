Joseph Roland Barbera (March 24, 1911 – December 18, 2006) was an American animator and cartoon producer. With his partner William Hanna, he created Tom and Jerry at MGM and later co-founded Hanna-Barbera Productions, which dominated American television animation for three decades with series such as The Flintstones, Yogi Bear, The Jetsons and Scooby-Doo.

Early life and MGM

Barbera was born in Manhattan's Little Italy to Sicilian immigrant parents. After working as a tailor's assistant and accountant, he joined the Van Beuren Studios and then Terrytoons before moving to MGM in 1937, where he teamed up with William Hanna. Their first Tom and Jerry short, Puss Gets the Boot (1940), was nominated for an Academy Award; the series won seven Oscars during their tenure.

Hanna-Barbera

When MGM closed its animation studio in 1957, Hanna and Barbera founded their own company. Using cost-saving "limited animation" techniques for television, they produced The Huckleberry Hound Show (1958), the first animated series to win an Emmy, followed by The Flintstones (1960) — the first prime-time animated sitcom and a top-30 hit — Top Cat, The Jetsons, Jonny Quest and Scooby-Doo, Where Are You! Barbera, the team's gag man and sketch artist, developed stories and character designs while Hanna handled production.

Barbera remained active as an executive producer after the studio's sale, and was inducted into the Television Hall of Fame alongside Hanna in 1994.
