Cosa Nostra

Sicily, the birthplace and stronghold of Cosa Nostra.

Cosa Nostra (Italian: [ˈkɔːza ˈnɔstra]; "Our Thing"), also known as the Sicilian Mafia, is an organised crime syndicate and criminal society originating in Sicily, Italy, dating to the mid-19th century. It is a loose association of criminal groups ("families" or cosche) that share a common organisational structure and code of conduct, including the code of silence known as omertà. Its core activities are protection racketeering, extortion, arbitration of disputes between criminals, and the brokering and administration of illegal agreements and transactions.

History

The Mafia emerged in western Sicily in the decades after Italian unification, filling a vacuum left by a weak state and exploiting the lemon trade, land management and local politics. It survived Benito Mussolini's repression campaigns of the 1920s, revived after the Allied invasion of Sicily in 1943, and grew rich on post-war construction and the international heroin trade. Sicilian emigrants also transplanted the organisation to the United States, where the American Mafia developed as a related but separate entity.

Structure

The basic unit is the family (cosca), controlling a territory, usually a town or neighbourhood. Each family is headed by a boss (capofamiglia), with an underboss, a counsellor (consigliere), captains (capodecina) leading crews of soldiers ("men of honour"). A coordinating body, the Commission or Cupola, was established in the late 1950s to mediate between families in Palermo province.

War on the state and decline

Under the Corleonesi faction led by Salvatore "Totò" Riina, Cosa Nostra waged a violent campaign against the Italian state, culminating in the 1992 assassinations of judges Giovanni Falcone and Paolo Borsellino. The backlash led to mass arrests, the Maxi Trial convictions (1986–87), Riina's capture in 1993, and a lasting weakening of the organisation, though it remains active, particularly in extortion of businesses (the pizzo) and infiltration of public contracts.
