Meuse–Argonne offensive
Date26 September – 11 November 1918
LocationArgonne Forest and Meuse River, France
Part ofHundred Days Offensive, World War I
ResultAllied victory

The Meuse–Argonne offensive was a major part of the final Allied offensive of World War I that stretched along the entire Western Front. It was fought from 26 September 1918 until the Armistice of 11 November 1918, principally by the American Expeditionary Forces under General John J. Pershing, supported by French forces.

The battle

The offensive aimed to cut the German supply line at the Sedan–Mézières railway. Over 1.2 million American soldiers took part, making it the largest battle in United States military history at the time. Attacking through the dense Argonne Forest and across the Meuse River, American divisions faced strong German defences in depth. Notable episodes included the "Lost Battalion" of the 77th Division and the actions of Sergeant Alvin York. Progress was slow at first, but a renewed attack in November broke the German line and reached Sedan.

Casualties and legacy

American casualties numbered about 122,000, including some 26,000 dead — a toll commemorated by the Meuse-Argonne American Cemetery, the largest American military cemetery in Europe. German forces suffered comparable losses.
