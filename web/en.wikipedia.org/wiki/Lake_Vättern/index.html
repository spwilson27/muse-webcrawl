Lake Vättern (Swedish: Vättern) is the second-largest lake in Sweden by surface area, after Lake Vänern, and one of the largest lakes in Europe. It lies in south-central Sweden, in the historical land of Götaland, between the cities of Jönköping at its southern tip and Motala and Askersund to the north.

Geography

The lake occupies a deep graben (rift valley) and is famed for its exceptionally clear, cold, oligotrophic water — so transparent and pure that it supplies drinking water to surrounding municipalities with minimal treatment and is reserved as a strategic drinking-water source for a large part of Sweden. Its largest island, Visingsö, was a seat of early Swedish kings and is reached by ferry from Gränna, a town also known for its peppermint polkagris candy. The Göta Canal crosses the lake between Karlsborg and Motala.

Nature and use

Vättern's deep cold water supports glacial relict species such as Arctic char, grayling, and the giant form of lake trout known locally, and it is an important habitat for salmonids. The annual Vätternrundan, a 315-kilometre recreational cycling event around the lake held each June, is among the largest of its kind in the world. Military use of the lake, including live-fire exercises into its waters, has periodically drawn environmental protest from shoreline municipalities.
