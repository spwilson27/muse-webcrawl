Lorestan Province

Lorestan Province (Persian: استان لرستان, Ostān-e Lorestān) is one of the 31 provinces of Iran, located in the west of the country within the Zagros Mountains. Its capital and largest city is Khorramabad. The province covers about 28,294 square kilometres and has a population of roughly 1.76 million (2016 census). It is the historic homeland of the Lur people.

Geography

Lorestan is mountainous, with peaks of the Zagros range, including Oshtorankuh, rising above 4,000 metres. Deep river valleys — the Kashkan, Seymareh and Dez — cut through the province. The climate varies from cold and snowy in the highlands to mild in the southern lowlands. Waterfalls such as Bisheh and Nozhian are noted natural features, and the oak forests of the Zagros cover much of the terrain.

History

The region is famed for the "Luristan bronzes" — finely cast bronze artefacts of the early first millennium BC, including standards, pins and horse trappings, many looted from ancient burials. In antiquity the area lay within Elamite and later Kassite influence. The Lurs, an Iranian people speaking Luri languages, emerged in the medieval period; local dynasties of Atabegs ruled Lorestan until the Safavid era. The Falak-ol-Aflak Castle in Khorramabad dates in part to the Sasanian period.

Economy and culture

Agriculture and animal husbandry dominate the economy, alongside food processing and petrochemicals. Luri music, dance and distinctive clothing are central to the province's cultural identity, and many residents maintain semi-nomadic traditions. Counties include Khorramabad, Borujerd, Dorud, Aligudarz and Kuhdasht.
