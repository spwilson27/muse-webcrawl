Chhatrapati

Chhatrapati (Devanagari: छत्रपती) is an Indian royal and honorific title meaning "emperor" or "sovereign", literally derived from chhatra, a parasol or umbrella symbolising sovereignty, and pati, meaning lord or master — hence "lord of the (royal) umbrella". The title is most famously associated with the Maratha ruler Shivaji, who was crowned Chhatrapati in 1674.

Shivaji and the Maratha throne

Shivaji, founder of the Maratha kingdom in western India, was formally crowned at Raigad Fort on 6 June 1674 in an elaborate Vedic coronation ceremony conducted by the priest Gaga Bhatt, taking the title Chhatrapati. The coronation asserted his status as an independent Hindu sovereign rather than a subordinate of the Mughal Empire or the Deccan sultanates.

The title was inherited by his sons Sambhaji and Rajaram, and after civil war in the early 18th century the Maratha throne split between two lines of Chhatrapatis: the senior line at Satara and the junior line at Kolhapur, both descended from Shivaji. The Satara line was deposed by the British in 1848 after the death of Shahaji II without a recognised heir, while the Kolhapur line ruled as maharajas of the princely state of Kolhapur until Indian independence. The Bhonsle Chhatrapatis of Kolhapur — notably Shahu of Kolhapur (ruled 1894–1922), the social reformer — remained prominent figures in Maharashtra, and descendants of the two houses continue to use the title ceremonially.

Usage

In modern Maharashtra, "Chhatrapati" is used almost as a synonym for Shivaji himself: Mumbai's Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus (the former Victoria Terminus railway station), Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport, and the Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya (formerly Prince of Wales Museum) all bear his name. The district of Sambhajinagar's university and numerous institutions in Satara and Kolhapur also use the title in honour of the Maratha sovereigns.
