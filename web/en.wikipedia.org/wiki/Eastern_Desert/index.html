Eastern Desert

The Eastern Desert is the part of the Sahara that lies east of the Nile in Egypt and extends south into northeastern Sudan and Eritrea. It is bounded by the Nile Valley to the west and the Red Sea and Gulf of Suez to the east, covering roughly a fifth of Egypt's land area.

Geography and history

Unlike the flat sand seas of the Western Desert, the Eastern Desert is a rugged upland of igneous and metamorphic mountains — the Red Sea Hills — rising to over 2,000 m, dissected by dry wadis such as Wadi Hammamat. The region was exploited from pharaonic times for gold, granite, porphyry, and greywacke, and for the emerald mines of Mons Smaragdus. Roman roads and ports, including Berenice and Myos Hormos, linked the Nile to Red Sea trade with Arabia and India. In late antiquity the desert became home to Christian hermits and monasteries, including those of St Anthony and St Paul, among the oldest in the world.
