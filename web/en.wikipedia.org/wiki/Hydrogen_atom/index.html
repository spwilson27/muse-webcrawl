Overview

A hydrogen atom is an atom of the chemical element hydrogen, electrically neutral and containing a single positively charged proton and a single negatively charged electron bound by the Coulomb force. Atomic hydrogen constitutes about 75% of the baryonic mass of the universe. Because it is the simplest atom, its quantum-mechanical solution — exact for the Schrödinger equation — is the foundation of atomic physics and quantum chemistry.

Spectrum and models

Hydrogen's spectral lines (the Lyman, Balmer, Paschen and other series, summarised by the Rydberg formula) were the key evidence for Bohr's 1913 model and then for full quantum mechanics. Fine structure, the Lamb shift (1947) and hyperfine splitting (the 21-cm line, radio astronomy's workhorse) refined the picture and tested quantum electrodynamics to extraordinary precision. Isotopes deuterium and tritium carry one and two neutrons.
