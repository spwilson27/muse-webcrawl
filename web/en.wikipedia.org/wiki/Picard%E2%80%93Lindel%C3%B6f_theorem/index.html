Picard–Lindelöf theorem

Picard–Lindelöf theorem
FieldOrdinary differential equations
Named afterCharles Émile Picard, Ernst Lindelöf
Statement (informal)If f is continuous and Lipschitz in y, the initial value problem y′ = f(x, y), y(x₀) = y₀ has a unique local solution

The Picard–Lindelöf theorem (also called the Picard existence theorem or Cauchy–Lipschitz theorem) is a fundamental result on the existence and uniqueness of solutions to initial value problems for first-order ordinary differential equations. It guarantees that, under mild regularity conditions, a differential equation with a specified starting value has exactly one solution on some interval around the starting point.

Statement

For the problem y′ = f(x, y) with y(x₀) = y₀, if f is continuous in x and Lipschitz continuous in y on a rectangle around (x₀, y₀), then there exists an interval about x₀ on which a unique solution exists. The proof converts the equation into an integral equation and applies Picard iteration — repeated substitution starting from the constant function y₀ — showing the iterates converge uniformly to the solution, an early application of the contraction mapping principle.

Significance

The theorem is the bedrock of the theory of differential equations: it assures that models in physics and engineering posed as initial value problems are well-posed locally, while its hypotheses explain how non-uniqueness can arise when the Lipschitz condition fails (for example y′ = √y with y(0) = 0). Extensions cover systems of equations and Banach spaces.
