Egyptian hieroglyphs

Egyptian hieroglyphs were the formal writing system used in Ancient Egypt for the Egyptian language. Combining logographic, syllabic, and alphabetic elements, with roughly 1,000 distinct characters in later periods, they were used from c. 3200 BC until the 4th century AD.

Hieroglyphs
Type: Logography with phonetic elements
Period: c. 3200 BC – AD 400
Deciphered: Champollion, 1822, via Rosetta Stone
Related scripts: Hieratic, Demotic

System

Signs could be phonograms (one to three consonants), logograms, or determinatives clarifying meaning. Text could run left-to-right, right-to-left, or top-to-bottom. Cursive forms — hieratic for daily use and later demotic — evolved alongside monumental hieroglyphs.

Decipherment

Knowledge was lost after late antiquity. The Rosetta Stone (found 1799) with parallel Greek text enabled Thomas Young and Jean-François Champollion, who announced decipherment in 1822, to unlock the system.
