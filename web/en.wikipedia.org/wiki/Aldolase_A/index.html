Aldolase A

GeneALDOA
EC number4.1.2.13
ReactionFructose 1,6-bisphosphate ⇌ DHAP + glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate

Aldolase A (ALDOA), also known as fructose-bisphosphate aldolase A, is an enzyme that in humans is encoded by the ALDOA gene on chromosome 16. It is one of three fructose-bisphosphate aldolase isozymes (A, B and C) and is expressed mainly in muscle and red blood cells.

Function

Aldolase A catalyses the reversible cleavage of fructose 1,6-bisphosphate into the triose phosphates dihydroxyacetone phosphate and glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate, the fourth step of glycolysis. The enzyme is a tetramer and a class I aldolase, forming a Schiff base intermediate with its substrate.

Clinical significance

Deficiency of aldolase A is a rare autosomal recessive disorder that can cause episodes of haemolytic anaemia and rhabdomyolysis, often triggered by fever or exercise. Beyond glycolysis, aldolase A has "moonlighting" functions, including interactions with the cytoskeleton and roles in cell signalling, and it is overexpressed in several cancers, where it supports the Warburg effect.
