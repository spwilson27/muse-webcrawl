Louis-Hector de Callière

Born12 November 1648, Torigni-sur-Vire, France
Died26 May 1703, Quebec
OfficeGovernor General of New France (1698–1703)

Louis-Hector de Callière (1648–1703) was a French military officer and colonial administrator who served as Governor of Montreal and then as the 13th Governor General of New France from 1698 until his death. He is best remembered for negotiating the Great Peace of Montreal of 1701.

Career in Canada

The younger brother of the diplomat François de Callières, Louis-Hector arrived in Canada in 1684 as governor of Montreal, a post he held for some fifteen years. He directed the defence of the colony during King William's War, organised expeditions against the Iroquois and against English settlements, and served as acting governor general during Frontenac's absences, succeeding him on his death in 1698.

The Great Peace

Callière's great achievement was the Great Peace of Montreal, signed on 4 August 1701 with representatives of some 39 Indigenous nations, including the Haudenosaunee. The treaty ended the Beaver Wars that had devastated the colony for decades, established French mediation in inter-nation disputes, and opened a generation of relative stability in the St Lawrence valley. Callière died at Quebec in 1703; Pointe-à-Callière in Old Montreal commemorates the site associated with his estate.
