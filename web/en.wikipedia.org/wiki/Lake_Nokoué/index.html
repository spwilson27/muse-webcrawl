Lake Nokoué

Lake Nokoué (French: Lac Nokoué) is a lake in southern Benin, West Africa. It covers roughly 160 square kilometres and lies just north of Cotonou, the country's largest city. The lake is fed principally by the Ouémé River and is connected to the Atlantic Ocean by the Cotonou channel.

Geography and ecology

The lake is shallow, with depths typically between one and three metres, and its salinity varies seasonally with river inflow and tides. It is an important wetland, supporting fish, waterbirds, and extensive fishing activity.

Ganvié

On the lake stands Ganvié, one of the largest lake villages in Africa, whose Tofinu inhabitants live in houses built on stilts. The village was founded centuries ago, traditionally by people seeking refuge from slave raids, and is sometimes called the "Venice of Africa". It is a major tourist destination and has been proposed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Fishing

Traditional acadja brush-park fisheries are widely used on the lake, in which bundles of branches are placed in the water to attract fish. Fishing pressure, pollution from Cotonou, and the spread of water hyacinth pose environmental challenges.
