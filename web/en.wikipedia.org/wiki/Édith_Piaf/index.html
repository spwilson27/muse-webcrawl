Édith Piaf, France's "little sparrow".

Édith Piaf (19 December 1915 – 10 October 1963), born Édith Giovanna Gassion, was a French singer, songwriter and actress widely regarded as France's greatest popular singer and one of the most celebrated performers of the twentieth century. Her music was often autobiographical, specialising in chanson réaliste ballads of love, loss and sorrow.

Born into poverty in Belleville, Paris, Piaf was raised partly in a brothel run by her grandmother in Normandy and later accompanied her street-performer father. In 1935 she was discovered singing on the streets of Pigalle by nightclub owner Louis Leplée, who gave her the stage name La Môme Piaf — "the little sparrow" — for her short stature and nervous energy.

Famous songs and later life

Her signature songs include "La Vie en rose" (1946), written for the boxer Marcel Cerdan, the love of her life, who died in a plane crash in 1949; "Hymne à l'amour" (1949), dedicated to his memory; "Milord" (1959); and "Non, je ne regrette rien" (1960), which she dedicated to the French Foreign Legion. Years of alcohol and morphine addiction, compounded by car crashes, ruined her health. She died in 1963 and was buried in Père Lachaise Cemetery in Paris; tens of thousands lined the streets for her funeral procession. Her life was dramatised in the 2007 film La Vie en rose, for which Marion Cotillard won the Academy Award for Best Actress.
