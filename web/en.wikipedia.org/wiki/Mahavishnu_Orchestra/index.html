Mahavishnu Orchestra

The Mahavishnu Orchestra was a jazz fusion band formed in New York City in 1971 by English guitarist John McLaughlin. The group pioneered a high-energy blend of jazz improvisation, rock volume and virtuosity, Indian musical influences, and complex odd metres.

History

McLaughlin, who had played with Miles Davis on albums including Bitches Brew, adopted the name "Mahavishnu" from his guru Sri Chinmoy. The original line-up — McLaughlin on double-neck guitar, Billy Cobham on drums, Rick Laird on bass, Jan Hammer on keyboards, and Jerry Goodman on violin — released the acclaimed albums The Inner Mounting Flame (1971) and Birds of Fire (1973). Internal tensions dissolved the band by the end of 1973, after a third studio album was recorded but shelved (released in 1999 as The Lost Trident Sessions).

McLaughlin reformed the group in 1974 with violinist Jean-Luc Ponty, keyboardist Gayle Moran, and drummer Narada Michael Walden, recording Apocalypse (1974) with the London Symphony Orchestra and Visions of the Emerald Beyond (1975). A third version of the band toured and recorded in the mid-1980s.

Style and legacy

The Orchestra was noted for ferocious unison lines, asymmetric time signatures such as 5/4 and 9/8, and a spiritual intensity intended to convey McLaughlin's devotion. It remains one of the most influential fusion groups, shaping progressive rock and jazz-rock musicians for decades.
