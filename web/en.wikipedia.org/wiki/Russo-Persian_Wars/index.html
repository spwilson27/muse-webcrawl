The Russo-Persian Wars were a series of conflicts fought between the Russian Empire and Persia (Iran) over several centuries, principally over control of the Caucasus and the Caspian Sea littoral. Their outcome fixed the modern borders between Russia and Iran and transferred the South Caucasus — present-day Azerbaijan, Georgia, Armenia, and Dagestan — from Persian to Russian rule.

The conflicts

Early clashes (1651–1653) involved raids around the Terek and Caspian. Peter the Great's Persian campaign of 1722–1723 seized Derbent and Baku and the Caspian provinces, though most gains were handed back in the 1730s as Russia sought Persia as an ally against the Ottomans. Catherine the Great's punitive expedition of 1796 under Valerian Zubov was cut short by her death.

The decisive wars were fought in the nineteenth century. In the war of 1804–1813, sparked by Russia's annexation of Georgia and its advance into the khanates of the eastern Caucasus, Russian forces under generals such as Pavel Tsitsianov and Pyotr Kotlyarevsky repeatedly defeated much larger Persian armies led by Crown Prince Abbas Mirza; the Treaty of Gulistan (1813) confirmed Russia's possession of the khanates north of the Aras River. In the war of 1826–1828, Persia attempted to recover the lost territories but was defeated by Ivan Paskevich, who captured Erivan and Tabriz; the Treaty of Turkmenchay (1828) ceded the Erivan and Nakhchivan khanates, set the Aras as the frontier — still the border between Iran and Azerbaijan/Armenia — imposed a heavy indemnity, and granted Russia capitulatory rights in Persia.

Legacy

The treaties of Gulistan and Turkmenchay are remembered in Iran as national humiliations imposed by an expansionist empire; the grievances they created shaped Iranian foreign policy for a century and remain touchstones of Iranian historical memory.
