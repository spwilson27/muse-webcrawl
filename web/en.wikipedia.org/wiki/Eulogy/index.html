Eulogy

A eulogy (from Greek eulogia, "praise") is a speech or writing in praise of a person, especially one who has recently died or retired, or as a term of endearment. Eulogies are usually delivered by a family member or close friend at a funeral or memorial service.

History

The tradition descends from classical funeral orations, such as Pericles' funeral oration in ancient Athens. In modern Western funerals one or more eulogies recall the deceased's life, character, achievements and anecdotes, aiming to comfort mourners and celebrate a life. Eulogies may also be given for living persons on retirement or other occasions.

Famous eulogies

Well-known examples include Mark Antony's speech in Shakespeare's Julius Caesar ("Friends, Romans, countrymen"), Earl Spencer's eulogy for Diana, Princess of Wales in 1997, and Barack Obama's eulogy for Clementa Pinckney in Charleston in 2015, which closed with "Amazing Grace".
