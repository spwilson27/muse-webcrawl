Christina Maslach

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Christina Maslach (born January 21, 1946) is an American social psychologist and professor emerita of psychology at the University of California, Berkeley, known for her research on occupational burnout. She is the author of the Maslach Burnout Inventory (MBI), the most widely used instrument for measuring burnout.

Career

Maslach graduated from Radcliffe College and earned her Ph.D. at Stanford University. As a research assistant she played a key role in stopping Philip Zimbardo's Stanford prison experiment in 1971 after observing its conditions — she later married Zimbardo. At Berkeley she served in senior administrative roles including vice provost for undergraduate education.

Burnout research

Beginning in the 1970s, Maslach interviewed workers in human-services professions and identified burnout as a syndrome of emotional exhaustion, depersonalization (cynicism) and a reduced sense of personal accomplishment. The Maslach Burnout Inventory, published in 1981 with Susan E. Jackson, operationalized these three dimensions. Her later work, including the book The Burnout Challenge (2022) with Michael P. Leiter, emphasizes organizational causes of burnout and workplace redesign. Burnout was included as an occupational phenomenon in the WHO's ICD-11 in 2019.

Honors

Maslach has received numerous awards for her teaching and research and is a fellow of the American Association for the Advancement of Science.
