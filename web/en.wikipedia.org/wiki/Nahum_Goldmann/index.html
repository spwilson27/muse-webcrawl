Nahum Goldmann

Nahum Goldmann (Hebrew: נחום גולדמן; 10 July 1895 – 29 August 1982) was a German-born Zionist leader who served for many years as president of the World Jewish Congress and of the World Zionist Organization. He was one of the chief architects of the reparations agreement between Israel and West Germany.

Early life

Goldmann was born in Vishnevo, in the Russian Empire (now Belarus), and grew up in Frankfurt am Main, where his father was a teacher and Zionist writer. He studied law, history and philosophy at Heidelberg, Marburg and Berlin, and as a young man worked on the Encyclopaedia Judaica project in Berlin.

Career

Fleeing the Nazis in 1933, Goldmann settled first in Geneva and then in New York, becoming an American citizen in 1945. Together with Stephen S. Wise he led the World Jewish Congress, which he had helped found in 1936. After the Second World War he campaigned for the establishment of a Jewish state and represented the Jewish Agency before the United Nations.

As president of the Conference on Jewish Material Claims Against Germany, Goldmann negotiated the 1952 Luxembourg Agreements with Chancellor Konrad Adenauer, under which West Germany paid billions of marks in reparations to Israel and to Holocaust survivors. He also promoted dialogue between Jews and Germans and, controversially, advocated contacts with Arab leaders, at times criticising Israeli government policy. His memoirs, The Jewish Paradox (1976), set out his vision of a neutral, culturally creative world Jewry.
