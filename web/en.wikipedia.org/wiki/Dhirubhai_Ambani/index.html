Dhirajlal Hirachand Ambani (28 December 1932 – 6 July 2002), popularly known as Dhirubhai Ambani, was an Indian businessman who founded Reliance Industries, one of India's largest private-sector conglomerates, spanning petrochemicals, refining, textiles and telecommunications.

Early life

Born into a modest family in Chorvad, Gujarat, Ambani worked in Aden, Yemen, in the 1950s before returning to India in 1958 to start a spice and textile trading business in Bombay.

Reliance

He founded Reliance Industries in 1966, beginning with a textile mill in Naroda, Ahmedabad, whose "Vimal" brand became a household name. The company's 1977 initial public offering was a landmark in cultivating an equity culture among Indian small investors. Reliance expanded aggressively into petrochemicals and built the Jamnagar refinery in Gujarat, among the largest in the world.

Legacy

Ambani is regarded as a pioneer of India's modern capital markets and industrial growth. After his death, the Reliance empire was divided between his sons Mukesh and Anil Ambani.
