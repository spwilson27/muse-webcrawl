Codex Telleriano-Remensis

Codex Telleriano-Remensis
Created: c. 1562–1563
Place of origin: Mexico City
Material: European paper
Pages: 50 folios
Location: Bibliothèque nationale de France, Paris

The Codex Telleriano-Remensis is a sixteenth-century Aztec codex, produced in Mexico City around 1562–1563 on European paper. It is one of the finest surviving examples of Aztec manuscript painting, and combines indigenous pictorial writing with alphabetic glosses in Spanish written by the native painters and their Spanish annotators.

Contents

The manuscript is divided into three sections. The first is a divinatory almanac (tonalamatl) covering the ceremonies associated with the days of the 260-day ritual calendar. The second section is a calendar of the monthly festivals of the Aztec solar year. The third, and longest, section is a historical annals recording events from 1198 to 1562, including the migration of the Mexica, dynastic history, wars, and events of the Spanish conquest and early colonial period.

The historical section is closely related to the Codex Vaticano A (also known as the Codex Ríos), and both appear to derive from a lost prototype sometimes called the Codex Huitzilopochtli.

History

The codex takes its name from Charles-Maurice Le Tellier, Archbishop of Reims, who donated it to the Bibliothèque Sainte-Geneviève in Paris in 1700; "Remensis" refers to Reims. It is now held by the Bibliothèque nationale de France. The annals section was long attributed to the Dominican friar Pedro de los Ríos, whose name is associated with the related Vatican manuscript.

Significance

Because of its combination of images and explanatory text, the Telleriano-Remensis is an essential source for the study of Aztec religion, calendrics, and history. A facsimile edition with commentary by Eloise Quiñones Keber was published in 1995.
