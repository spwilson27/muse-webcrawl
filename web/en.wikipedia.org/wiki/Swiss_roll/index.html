A Swiss roll, also known as a jelly roll in the United States, is a type of rolled sponge cake filled with whipped cream, jam, chocolate, or icing. The cake is baked as a thin sheet of sponge, spread with filling, and rolled up to produce the characteristic spiral pattern visible when sliced.

History

Despite its name, the Swiss roll is not thought to originate in Switzerland; rolled cakes of this type appeared in Central Europe in the 19th century and became popular in Britain during the Victorian era. The earliest references to a rolled cake appear in the mid-19th century, and the term "jelly roll" was in use in the United States by the 1870s. Similar rolled cakes exist in many cuisines, including the French bûche de Noël (Yule log), the German Biskuitrolle, and various Asian roll cakes made with chiffon sponge.

Preparation

A basic Swiss roll is made from a whisked sponge of eggs and sugar with flour folded in, containing little or no fat so that it remains flexible. The batter is spread thinly in a shallow rectangular tin (a "Swiss roll tin") and baked briefly until just set. The warm sponge is turned onto sugared paper, spread with filling, and rolled tightly, often using the paper to guide it; rolling while warm helps prevent cracking. It is served sliced, revealing the spiral of cake and filling.

Variations

  • Chocolate Swiss roll – cocoa-flavoured sponge, often filled with cream and coated in chocolate.
  • Jelly roll – the American name, typically filled with fruit jelly or jam.
  • Bûche de Noël – a chocolate roll decorated to resemble a Yule log at Christmas.
  • Arctic roll – a British dessert of vanilla ice cream wrapped in a thin layer of sponge with raspberry jam.
  • Roll cake (Asia) – light chiffon rolls popular in Japan and elsewhere, often with fresh cream and fruit.
