Labour economics

[Image: workers on a factory floor assembling electronics]

Labour economics (labor economics) is the study of labour markets: how workers and employers interact to determine wages, employment, working conditions and income distribution. It applies Supply and demand — firms demand labour up to its marginal product; households supply it trading income against leisure — and then complicates the model with everything that makes labour unlike other goods.

Topics

Core questions include the effects of the Minimum wage, unions and collective bargaining; education and training as human capital (Becker, Mincer) and the returns to schooling; discrimination and gender pay gaps; unemployment insurance and job search; migration; and the changing structure of work — deindustrialisation, polarisation of jobs, and platform work in the gig economy. Institutions matter unusually much here, so labour economics blends theory with natural experiments (the Card–Krueger minimum-wage study; David Card's 2021 Nobel-recognised work on immigration and education) and detailed administrative data.
