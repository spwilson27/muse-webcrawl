Western Greece (Greek: Δυτική Ελλάδα, Dytikí Elláda) is one of the thirteen administrative regions of Greece. It comprises the western part of continental Greece and the northwestern part of the Peloponnese peninsula. Its capital and largest city is Patras, the third-largest city in Greece.

Geography

The region covers an area of about 11,350 square kilometres. It is divided into three regional units: Aetolia-Acarnania, Achaea, and Elis. The terrain is mountainous in the interior, with Mount Erymanthos and the Panachaiko range, and coastal plains along the Ionian Sea and the Gulf of Corinth. The region is connected to Central Greece by the Rio–Antirrio Bridge, one of the longest multi-span cable-stayed bridges in the world, opened in 2004.

Major towns include Agrinio, Aigio, Pyrgos, Amaliada, Messolonghi, and Nafpaktos. The region includes the archaeological site of ancient Olympia in Elis, birthplace of the Olympic Games, and the lagoon town of Messolonghi, celebrated for its role in the Greek War of Independence.

Economy

Agriculture — olives, citrus fruit, currants, tobacco, and cotton — is important in the coastal plains, alongside tourism, the port of Patras (a major ferry gateway to Italy), and the University of Patras. The region was established in the 1987 administrative reform and gained elected regional government under the 2010 Kallikratis reform.
