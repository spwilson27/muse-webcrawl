Tone (musical)

Tone
DefinitionA sound of definite pitch produced by regular vibrations
Related conceptsPitch, timbre, note, partials

In music, a tone is a sound characterized by its duration, pitch, intensity (or loudness), and timbre (or quality). In common usage, "tone" can refer to a musical note itself, to the interval of a whole tone (whole step), or to the quality of sound produced by an instrument or voice ("tone colour"). Simple tones, produced by sinusoidal vibration, combine to form the complex tones characteristic of real instruments.

Acoustics

A musical tone is generated by periodic vibrations, typically of a string, air column, membrane, or solid body. It consists of a fundamental frequency — which determines perceived pitch — and a series of overtones or partials (harmonics and inharmonic partials) whose relative strengths determine timbre. The evolution of a tone's amplitude over time, its envelope (attack, decay, sustain, release), further distinguishes instruments. By contrast, noise consists of aperiodic vibrations without definite pitch.

Whole tone and terminology

In Western music theory, a "tone" also denotes the interval of a major second (whole step), as opposed to a semitone; the whole-tone scale is built entirely from such intervals. In linguistics, "tone" refers to pitch used to distinguish lexical or grammatical meaning — see tone sandhi for changes in tone pronunciation. In organ building, "tone" may describe the speech quality of stops; in audio engineering, tone controls shape equalization.

Perception

Human pitch perception is roughly logarithmic in frequency, and listeners can perceive a "missing fundamental" when only upper partials are present. Cultural and training differences affect tone discrimination, which is also the basis of musical memory and absolute pitch.
