Hatha yoga

Haṭha yoga
Origin: India, c. 11th century onward
Key texts: Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā, Gheraṇḍa Saṁhitā, Śiva Saṁhitā
Practices: Āsana, prāṇāyāma, mudrā, cleansing

Haṭha yoga is a branch of yoga emphasising physical techniques — postures (āsana), breath control (prāṇāyāma), seals (mudrā) and cleansing — to preserve and channel vital energy and prepare for meditation and liberation.

History

Emerging in medieval India among Nāth yogis associated with Gorakṣanātha, haṭha was codified in texts such as Svātmārāma's 15th-century Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā. Originally an esoteric path to awakening kuṇḍalinī, it was practised by ascetics of several traditions.

Modern yoga

In the 20th century, teachers including Krishnamacharya, Iyengar and Pattabhi Jois transformed haṭha postures into the exercise-oriented yoga practised worldwide, often separated from its original soteriological aims. Modern 'hatha' classes are typically gentle, slow-paced posture and breathing classes.
