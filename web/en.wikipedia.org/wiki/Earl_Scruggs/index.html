Earl Scruggs

Earl Scruggs
Born: 6 January 1924, North Carolina
Died: 28 March 2012
Genre: Bluegrass
Instrument: Banjo

Earl Eugene Scruggs (1924–2012) was an American musician noted for popularising a three-finger style of playing the five-string banjo, now called Scruggs style, which became a defining characteristic of bluegrass music.

Career

Scruggs joined Bill Monroe's Blue Grass Boys in 1945, where his banjo playing, alongside Lester Flatt's guitar, helped create the classic bluegrass sound. In 1948 he and Flatt formed Flatt and Scruggs and the Foggy Mountain Boys, recording 'Foggy Mountain Breakdown' (1949) and 'The Ballad of Jed Clampett' (1962). After the duo split in 1969 he formed the Earl Scruggs Revue with his sons.

Legacy

His instructional book Earl Scruggs and the 5-String Banjo influenced generations. He won Grammy Awards, was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame, and received the National Medal of Arts. See also Bluegrass music.
