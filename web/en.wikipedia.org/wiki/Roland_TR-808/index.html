The Roland TR-808 Rhythm Composer, commonly known as the 808, is a drum machine manufactured by the Roland Corporation between 1980 and 1983. It was one of the first drum machines to allow users to program rhythms instead of using preset patterns. Unlike its nearest competitor, the more expensive Linn LM-1, the TR-808 generates sounds using analog synthesis rather than digital samples.

History

Roland founder Ikutaro Kakehashi, engineer Tadao Kikumoto and the team deliberately used cheap, out-of-specification transistors that gave the machine its distinctive "sizzling" sound. Selling for about US$1,195 on release, the 808 was initially a commercial failure, criticized for its unrealistic drum sounds; only around 12,000 units were made before Roland discontinued it when semiconductor quality improved and consistent faulty transistors could no longer be sourced.

Legacy

Second-hand 808s found their way into early hip hop, electro and house music. Marvin Gaye's "Sexual Healing" (1982) and Afrika Bambaataa and the Soulsonic Force's "Planet Rock" (1982) were early hits; the machine became foundational to hip hop, and its deep, tunable bass drum became a staple of trap music decades later. The 808 is arguably the most influential drum machine ever made, and original units are highly prized.
