Motorsport

Motorsport, also known as motor sports or motor racing, is a global term encompassing the group of competitive sporting events which primarily involve the use of motor vehicles, whether for racing or non-racing competition. It includes circuit racing, rallying, off-road racing, motorcycle racing, and many other disciplines, governed internationally by bodies such as the FIA (for automobiles) and the FIM (for motorcycles).

History

Motorsport began almost immediately after the invention of the petrol-fuelled internal combustion engine. The 1894 Paris–Rouen trial is often regarded as the first motor competition, and city-to-city races in France and America followed. Purpose-built circuits such as Brooklands (1907) and Indianapolis Motor Speedway (1909) replaced public roads after early safety catastrophes. The Formula One World Championship began in 1950, and the World Rally Championship in 1973; motorcycle Grand Prix racing began in 1949.

Major disciplines

  • Single-seater racing — Formula One, IndyCar, Formula E.
  • Sports car and endurance racing — the World Endurance Championship and the 24 Hours of Le Mans.
  • Stock car racing — dominated by NASCAR in the United States.
  • Touring cars — production-based saloon racing.
  • Rallying — timed special stages on closed public roads, plus rally-raid events such as the Dakar Rally.
  • Motorcycle racing — MotoGP, Superbike, motocross, and speedway.
  • Drag racing, drifting, karting and historic racing.

Safety and technology

Motorsport has driven advances in vehicle safety and technology — disc brakes, aerodynamics, hybrid powertrains, and crash structures — while itself becoming far safer through barriers, the HANS device, and the halo cockpit protection system. Events are licensed and regulated by national and international sanctioning bodies.
