Wrexham County Borough

Wrexham County Borough (Welsh: Bwrdeistref Sirol Wrecsam) is a county borough in north-east Wales. It borders Flintshire to the north, Shropshire to the east, Powys to the south, Gwynedd to the west and Denbighshire to the north-west. The principal settlement and county town is the city of Wrexham, which received city status in 2022 as part of the Platinum Jubilee honours.

Geography

The county borough covers about 504 square kilometres. The western part rises into the Berwyn Mountains and the Clwydian Range, while the east is low-lying farmland sloping toward the Cheshire Plain. The River Dee flows through the area, passing through the Vale of Llangollen nearby.

History

The county borough was established on 1 April 1996 under the Local Government (Wales) Act 1994, combining parts of the former districts of Wrexham Maelor and Glyndŵr in the county of Clwyd. Wrexham itself has a long history as a market town, and the surrounding area was important for coal mining and ironworking during the Industrial Revolution.

Settlements

Main settlements include Wrexham, Chirk, Cefn Mawr, Gresford, Holt, Ruabon and Rhosllanerchrugog. The Pontcysyllte Aqueduct, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, lies partly within the borough near Trevor.

Governance

Wrexham County Borough Council administers local services. For Senedd and UK Parliament elections the area is divided between the Wrexham and Clwyd South constituencies (with boundary changes over time).
