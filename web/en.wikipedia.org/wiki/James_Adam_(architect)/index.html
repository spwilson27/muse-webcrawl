Life

James Adam (21 July 1732 – 20 October 1794) was a Scottish architect and furniture designer, the younger brother and business partner of Robert Adam, with whom he developed the Adam style of Neoclassical interior design that dominated British taste in the later eighteenth century. Sons of the Kirkcaldy architect William Adam, James studied at the University of Edinburgh and made the obligatory Grand Tour of Italy (1760–63), studying classical ruins and collecting antiquities.

The Adam brothers

Returning to London, James joined Robert in the firm known as the Adelphi (from the Greek for "brothers"), which combined architecture, interior decoration, and furniture into a single integrated service — the first modern design practice of its kind. Together they published The Works in Architecture of Robert and James Adam (1773–79, with a posthumous third volume), the pattern book that broadcast their style of delicate grotesque ornament, pastel colour schemes, and integrated ceiling and carpet motifs.

Works

Joint commissions include the remodelling of Syon House and Kenwood House, the Adelphi Terrace development on the Thames (partly demolished 1930s), Portland Place, and the Royal Society of Arts building. Working increasingly in his own right, James designed the Old College buildings of Glasgow University and, with Robert, the Edinburgh register house (Register House). After Robert's death in 1792 James briefly carried the practice alone, dying two years later.
