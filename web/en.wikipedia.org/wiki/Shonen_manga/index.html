Shonen manga

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Shōnen manga (少年漫画, "boys' comics") is an editorial category of Japanese comics targeting an audience of young males, typically between the ages of 12 and 18. Along with shōjo (girls'), seinen (young adult men) and josei (adult women) manga, it is one of the main demographic categories by which manga magazines are marketed in Japan. Shōnen manga is the most commercially successful of these categories and has produced many of the medium's global best-sellers, including Dragon Ball, One Piece, Naruto, Bleach, Attack on Titan and Demon Slayer.

Characteristics

Shōnen works commonly feature a young male protagonist, action and adventure plots, martial arts or special powers, sports, science fiction or comedy, and themes of friendship, effort and victory — words famously represented in the reader-survey ethos of the leading magazine Weekly Shōnen Jump ("friendship, struggle, victory"). Stories typically proceed in long serial arcs of escalating challenges, tournament structures and power progression, and successful series are usually adapted into anime, films and merchandise.

History

Boys' magazines carrying manga date to the early twentieth century, but the modern shōnen magazine took shape after the Second World War with the success of Osamu Tezuka's work. Weekly Shōnen Magazine (Kodansha, 1959), Weekly Shōnen Sunday (Shogakukan, 1959) and Weekly Shōnen Jump (Shueisha, 1968) became the dominant titles; Jump reached a record circulation of 6.53 million copies in 1994–95, during the era of Dragon Ball, Slam Dunk and Yu-Gi-Oh!. The "Big Three" of the 2000s — One Piece, Naruto and Bleach — anchored the international anime and manga boom. Although aimed at boys, leading shōnen titles draw very large female and adult readerships, both inside and outside Japan.

Notable magazines and series

  • Weekly Shōnen Jump — Dragon Ball, One Piece, Naruto, Jujutsu Kaisen, My Hero Academia.
  • Weekly Shōnen Magazine — Ashita no Joe, Fairy Tail, The Seven Deadly Sins.
  • Weekly Shōnen Sunday — Detective Conan, Inuyasha, Urusei Yatsura.
  • Monthly Shōnen Magazine, Shōnen Ace and others.
