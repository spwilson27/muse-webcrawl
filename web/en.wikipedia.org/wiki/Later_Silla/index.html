Later Silla

Later Silla (Korean: 후신라; Hanja: 後新羅), sometimes called Unified Silla (통일신라), is the name given to the Korean kingdom of Silla after it unified most of the Korean Peninsula under its rule in 668, having conquered Baekje in 660 and Goguryeo in 668 with the aid of Tang China. The period runs from 668 until the kingdom's final collapse and submission to Goryeo in 935. Historians also use the term "North–South States Period" for this era, because Balhae ruled the former Goguryeo territories to the north while Silla governed the south.

Unification

Under kings Muyeol and Munmu, Silla allied with Tang China to destroy Baekje (660) and Goguryeo (668). When Tang attempted to impose direct rule over the peninsula, Silla fought the Silla–Tang War (670–676) and expelled Tang forces south of the Taedong River, completing a partial unification: Silla controlled the peninsula south of a line roughly from the Taedong to Wonsan, while Balhae emerged in the north.

Golden age

The eighth century is regarded as the golden age of Silla culture. The capital at Gyeongju may have held a million inhabitants. Buddhism flourished under state patronage, producing the Bulguksa temple and the Seokguram Grotto, now UNESCO World Heritage Sites, and the great bronze bell of King Seongdeok (the Emille Bell). King Gyeongdeok and King Hyegong presided over a highly aristocratic state ordered by the golpum (bone-rank) system, which rigidly prescribed office and status by birth. In 788 the state instituted a Confucian reading examination for officials, though bone rank continued to limit advancement.

Decline and fall

From the late eighth century, succession disputes, aristocratic factionalism, and the ambitions of provincial strongmen steadily weakened the throne. Peasant rebellions spread in the late ninth century under Queen Jinseong. Regional warlords — Gyeon Hwon, who proclaimed Later Baekje in 900, and Gung Ye, who proclaimed Later Goguryeo (Taebong) in 901 — plunged the peninsula into the Later Three Kingdoms period. In 935 the last Silla king, Gyeongsun, peacefully surrendered his kingdom to Wang Geon of Goryeo, ending nearly a thousand years of Silla rule.
