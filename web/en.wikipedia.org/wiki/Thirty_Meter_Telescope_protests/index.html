Thirty Meter Telescope protests
Date2014 – present (major escalations 2015 and 2019)
LocationMauna Kea, Hawaii, United States
CausePlanned construction of the Thirty Meter Telescope on land sacred to Native Hawaiians
MethodsBlockades, encampments, litigation, ceremonies
Key events2014 groundbreaking disrupted; Hawaii Supreme Court voids permit (2015) then upholds new permit (2018); arrests of kūpuna (elders) July 2019

The Thirty Meter Telescope protests are a series of demonstrations, blockades and legal challenges opposing the construction of the Thirty Meter Telescope (TMT) on Mauna Kea, a dormant volcano on the island of Hawaii. Many Native Hawaiians regard Mauna Kea as sacred — a realm of the gods and an ancestor — and the movement against the telescope has become a prominent focus of the Hawaiian sovereignty movement and of global debates about Indigenous rights, land use and science.

Background

Mauna Kea's summit, among the finest astronomical sites on Earth, already hosts thirteen telescopes. The TMT, a US$1.4 billion-plus international project with a 30-metre segmented mirror, received a conservation district use permit from Hawaii's Board of Land and Natural Resources in 2011. Opponents challenged the permit and the state's management of the mountain, which an earlier audit had criticised; supporters, including much of Hawaii's political and business establishment, argued the project would bring jobs, education funding and scientific prestige while following environmental rules.

Protests

Protests disrupted the project's groundbreaking ceremony in October 2014, and in spring 2015 blockades of the summit access road led to dozens of arrests and a halt to construction. In December 2015 the Hawaii Supreme Court invalidated the permit on due-process grounds; after new hearings, the court upheld a re-issued permit in October 2018. When construction was set to resume in July 2019, protectors (kiaʻi) established an encampment (Puʻuhonua o Puʻuhuluhulu) at the base of the access road; the arrest of 38 kūpuna (elders) on 17 July drew international attention, thousands joined the encampment, and the standoff continued for months. Construction remained stalled amid the COVID-19 pandemic and subsequent reviews.

Aftermath and significance

In 2022 Hawaii created the Mauna Kea Stewardship and Oversight Authority, with Native Hawaiian representation, to take over management of the summit lands from the University of Hawaii. The protests — conducted with strict nonviolence and ceremony, and amplified by social media and celebrity supporters — are widely seen as a watershed in Native Hawaiian activism, influencing telescope governance and Indigenous-consent debates worldwide.
