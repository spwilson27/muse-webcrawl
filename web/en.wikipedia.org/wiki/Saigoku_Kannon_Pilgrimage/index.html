The Saigoku Kannon Pilgrimage (Saigoku Sanjūsansho, "Thirty-three Places of the Western Provinces") is a pilgrimage circuit of thirty-three Buddhist temples in Japan's Kansai region, each dedicated to Kannon, the bodhisattva of compassion. It is the oldest and best known of Japan's many Kannon pilgrimage routes and is traditionally credited to the monk Tokudō Shōnin in the eighth century, revived by Emperor Kazan in the eleventh.

The route

The circuit runs from Seiganto-ji at Nachi Falls in Wakayama Prefecture through temples in Nara, Kyoto, Osaka, Hyōgo and Shiga, ending at Kegon-ji in Gifu Prefecture. Famous stops include Kiyomizu-dera (No. 16), Ishiyama-dera (No. 13), Hase-dera (No. 8), and Okadera (No. 7). The thirty-three temples correspond to the thirty-three forms Kannon is said to assume to save beings, as described in the Lotus Sutra. Along with the Bandō (33 temples, Kantō) and Chichibu (34 temples) circuits, the Saigoku route forms part of a combined 100-temple pilgrimage.

Practice

Pilgrims collect a temple seal (nōkyō) and a sacred song (goeika) for each temple in a stamp book or on a hanging scroll, and recite the temple's mantra and the Heart Sutra before the main image. Historically undertaken on foot over weeks, the route is now often travelled by train, bus or organised tour; pilgrims completing all thirty-three temples are recognised as having made the full circuit.
