Cornice

In architecture, a cornice (from the Italian cornice meaning "ledge") is generally any horizontal decorative moulding that crowns a building or furniture element — the cornice over a door or window, for instance, or the cornice around the top edge of a pedestal or along the top of an interior wall. A projecting cornice at the top of an exterior wall serves the practical purpose of throwing rainwater clear of the wall face below.

Classical architecture

In classical architecture, the cornice is the uppermost part of the entablature, above the architrave and frieze. Its form varies by order: the Doric cornice carries mutules on its underside, while the Ionic and Corinthian cornices are typically ornamented with dentils and modillions. The projecting lower surface of a cornice is called the corona, and its outer drip edge sheds water away from the building.

Interior use

Inside a room, a cornice (also called crown moulding or coving) covers the junction between wall and ceiling. Interior cornices range from simple cove profiles to elaborate plaster compositions with egg-and-dart, dentil and floral ornament. In modern construction they are commonly made of plaster, timber, MDF or expanded polystyrene.

Other meanings

In mountaineering, a snow cornice is an overhanging mass of wind-deposited snow at the edge of a ridge or cliff, dangerous because it can collapse under a climber's weight well back from the visible edge.
