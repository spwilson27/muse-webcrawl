Gísla saga

Gísla saga Súrssonar (listen) is one of the sagas of Icelanders, written in prose and set in the west of Iceland, principally in Haukadalur in Dýrafjörður, around the years 940–980 AD. It is traditionally dated to the thirteenth century and survives in three versions of differing length.

Synopsis

The saga tells the story of Gísli Súrsson, a poet and farmer who is drawn into a cycle of killings involving his brother Þorkell, his blood-brother Vésteinn and his brother-in-law Þorgrímr. After Vésteinn is murdered, Gísli avenges him by killing Þorgrímr, and is outlawed as a result. The saga follows Gísli through thirteen years of outlawry — the longest period of outlawry in the sagas — sustained by his loyal wife Auðr, until he is finally hunted down and killed in a celebrated last stand.

Gísla saga is notable for its exploration of fate, conflicting loyalties and the burden of the heroic code, and for the dream-verses attributed to Gísli in which a "good dream-woman" and a "bad dream-woman" appear to him. It is regarded as one of the finest and most psychologically complex of the Icelandic sagas.
