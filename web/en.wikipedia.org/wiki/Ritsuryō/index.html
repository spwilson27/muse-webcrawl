Ritsuryō

Ritsuryō (Japanese: 律令) is the historical legal system of ancient Japan, based on the Chinese legal codes of the Tang dynasty. The name combines ritsu (律, penal code) and ryō (令, administrative code). The system was the legal framework of the centralized imperial state built during the Asuka and Nara periods.

Codes

Major compilations include the Ōmi Code (c. 668), the Asuka Kiyomihara Code (689), the Taihō Code (701), and the Yōrō Code (compiled 718, enforced 757), the last of which remained nominally in force — with commentaries such as the Ryō no Gige and Ryō no Shūge — for over a thousand years. The codes established the Council of State (Daijō-kan), eight ministries, provincial governors, a census and land-allotment system (handen), and taxation in grain, cloth, and labour.

Decline

From the 9th century the allotment system decayed, tax-exempt estates (shōen) proliferated, and real power shifted to the Fujiwara regents and later the warrior government, though the ritsuryō framework survived formally until the Meiji Restoration of 1868.
