San Marco, Florence

San Marco is a religious complex in Florence, Italy, comprising a church and a Dominican convent that is now the Museo Nazionale di San Marco. It occupies a block in the historic centre of the city, on the Piazza San Marco just north of the cathedral. The convent is celebrated for the frescoes painted in its cells and corridors by the Dominican friar and painter Fra Angelico.

History

A church existed on the site from the 13th century, served first by Vallombrosan and then Silvestrine monks. In 1436 Pope Eugenius IV granted the complex to the Observant Dominicans of Fiesole, and Cosimo de' Medici financed a thorough rebuilding to the designs of his favourite architect, Michelozzo — one of the earliest great patronage projects of the Florentine Renaissance. The new church was consecrated on Epiphany night 1443 in the presence of the Pope.

Fra Angelico and his workshop decorated the chapter house, cloister, and each of the friars' cells with devotional frescoes between about 1438 and 1445, including the famous Annunciation at the head of the dormitory stairs. The convent's library, built by Michelozzo, was among the first public libraries of Renaissance Europe.

San Marco was the home of the fiery preacher Girolamo Savonarola, prior of the convent in the 1490s, whose cells are preserved in the museum. Suppressed under Napoleon and again after Italian unification, the convent became a museum in 1869.

The museum

The Museo Nazionale di San Marco displays the largest collection of Fra Angelico's works, panel paintings by other Renaissance masters, illuminated manuscripts from the convent library, and relics of Savonarola. The former hospice houses works by Fra Bartolomeo and the San Marco school of painting.
