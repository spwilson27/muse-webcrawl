Real estate broker

A real estate broker, real estate agent, or realtor is a person who represents sellers or buyers of real estate or real property. While a broker may work independently, an agent usually works under a licensed broker to represent clients. Brokers and agents are licensed by the state where they operate and are regulated to protect consumers in property transactions.

Roles

A seller's (listing) broker markets a property, advises on pricing, lists it — in North America typically on a Multiple Listing Service (MLS) — and negotiates on the seller's behalf. A buyer's broker helps purchasers find properties, arrange viewings, and negotiate offers. Some brokers act as transaction brokers or dual agents, subject to local law and disclosure requirements.

Licensing and compensation

In the United States, salespersons must work under a managing broker, who carries greater education and experience requirements and bears legal responsibility for the brokerage. Compensation is usually a commission, a percentage of the sale price, traditionally split between the listing and buyer's brokerages. "Realtor" is a trademarked term for members of the National Association of Realtors, who subscribe to its code of ethics. In the United Kingdom and Commonwealth countries, the equivalent professionals are usually called estate agents.
