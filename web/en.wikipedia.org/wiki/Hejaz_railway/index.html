Hejaz railway

The Hejaz railway (also spelled Hedjaz or Hijaz; Turkish: Hicaz Demiryolu) was a narrow-gauge railway (1,050 mm / 3 ft 5⅓ in) that ran from Damascus to Medina, through the Hejaz region of modern-day Saudi Arabia, with a branch line to Haifa on the Mediterranean Sea. It was built by the Ottoman Empire between 1900 and 1908 on the orders of Sultan Abdul Hamid II, largely funded by donations from Muslims worldwide and built with German engineering assistance under chief engineer Heinrich August Meissner.

The railway cut the Damascus–Medina journey from about 40 days to around four.

Purpose and construction

The railway's stated purpose was to ease the Hajj pilgrimage to Mecca — a projected extension to Mecca was never built — and to strengthen Ottoman military and administrative control of the remote Arabian provinces. Construction from Damascus southwards employed Ottoman soldiers as much of the workforce; the line opened to Medina on 1 September 1908, the anniversary of the Sultan's accession. Stations with fortified buildings and water towers dotted the desert route, including at Tabuk and Mada'in Saleh (Hegra).

First World War

During the First World War the railway became famous as the repeated target of Arab Revolt forces advised by T. E. Lawrence ("Lawrence of Arabia"), whose raids blew up bridges, track and locomotives in a guerrilla campaign designed to tie down Ottoman troops in the Hejaz. The line was severed and never restored south of the Jordanian frontier as a through route.

Today

Sections remain in operation: the line in Syria between Damascus and the Jordanian border, and in Jordan between Amman and Aqaba (used mainly for phosphate freight, with occasional heritage passenger trains from Amman to the desert stations). In Saudi Arabia the abandoned trackbed, stations and rusting locomotives survive as archaeological remains, and periodically discussed schemes propose rebuilding the line. Surviving Hejaz locomotives and stations are preserved at Medina, Mada'in Saleh, Tabuk and in Amman.
