Ruhollah Musavi Khomeini (24 September 1902 – 3 June 1989) was an Iranian Shia Muslim religious leader, revolutionary and politician who founded the Islamic Republic of Iran and served as its first Supreme Leader from 1979 until his death in 1989. He was the leader of the Iranian Revolution, which overthrew Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, the last Shah of Iran, and ended the millennia-old Persian monarchy.

Early life and education

Khomeini was born in Khomeyn, in present-day Markazi Province, and raised in a family of clerics and landowners. His father was murdered when Khomeini was an infant. He studied the Quran and Arabic from a young age, and in 1920 moved to Qom, where he studied Islamic philosophy, theology and jurisprudence under scholars such as Abdul-Karim Haeri Yazdi. He became a mujtahid and a teacher at the Fayzieh Madrasa in Qom, gaining prominence for his lectures on ethics and mysticism, though he largely avoided open politics until the 1960s.

Opposition to the Shah and exile

Khomeini emerged as a leading critic of the Shah's "White Revolution" reforms, denouncing them in 1963 as an assault on Islam. His fiery sermon of 3 June 1963 and subsequent arrest sparked the 15 Khordad uprising. Arrested again in 1964 after denouncing a status-of-forces agreement granting immunity to American military personnel, he was exiled, first to Turkey and then to Najaf in Iraq, where he spent thirteen years teaching. It was in Najaf that he developed and lectured on his doctrine of Velayat-e Faqih (Guardianship of the Jurist), arguing that in the absence of the Hidden Imam, qualified Islamic jurists should govern the state.

Expelled from Iraq in 1978, Khomeini settled in Neauphle-le-Château near Paris, where he became the focal point of the revolutionary movement, giving interviews and issuing statements that were smuggled into Iran on cassette tapes.

Revolution and rule

Following months of strikes and mass protests, the Shah left Iran in January 1979. Khomeini returned triumphantly on 1 February 1979 and, within days, the monarchy collapsed. A referendum established the Islamic Republic, and a new constitution, approved in December 1979, institutionalised his theory of jurist guardianship with Khomeini as Supreme Leader (Rahbar).

His decade in power was marked by the Iran hostage crisis (1979–1981), in which revolutionary students seized the US embassy in Tehran; the Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988), which he prosecuted long after Iraq's initial invasion had been repelled, accepting a ceasefire in 1988 that he described as "more deadly than taking poison"; the suppression of former revolutionary allies and other dissidents; and the mass executions of political prisoners in 1988. In February 1989 he issued a fatwa calling for the death of the British-Indian novelist Salman Rushdie over the novel The Satanic Verses.

Death and legacy

Khomeini died in Tehran on 3 June 1989 after undergoing surgery to stop internal bleeding. His funeral drew enormous crowds — by some estimates over ten million mourners, among the largest funerals in history — and scenes of chaos in which his coffin was nearly dropped. He was buried at the Behesht-e Zahra cemetery south of Tehran, where a large mausoleum complex was built. He was succeeded as Supreme Leader by Ali Khamenei.

Khomeini remains a deeply polarising figure: revered by supporters as the "Imam" who restored Islamic government and Iranian independence, and condemned by critics for authoritarianism, human-rights abuses and the suppression of political freedoms.
