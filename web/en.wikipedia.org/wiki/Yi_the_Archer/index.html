Yi the Archer

Chinese: 后羿 (Hòu Yì)
Domain: Chinese mythology
Known for: Shooting down nine of the ten suns

Yi the Archer, also known as Hou Yi (Chinese: 后羿), is a legendary archer and hero of Chinese mythology. Best known for the myth in which he shot down nine of ten suns that had risen together and scorched the earth, Yi is one of the most enduring figures of early Chinese folklore, and his story is closely intertwined with that of his wife Chang'e, the goddess of the Moon.

The ten suns

According to the myth, preserved in texts such as the Huainanzi, ten suns — the sons of the god Di Jun and the goddess Xihe — originally took turns rising each day, one at a time. One day all ten rose together, drying rivers and burning crops. The emperor Yao commissioned Yi, armed with a vermilion bow and ten arrows, to restore order. Yi shot down nine of the suns, leaving one, and is said to have spared the last at Yao's instruction or after his quiver was emptied by divine intervention.

Elixir of immortality

As a reward, Yi received the elixir of immortality from Xiwangmu, the Queen Mother of the West, obtained after he performed feats including slaying monstrous beasts that plagued humanity. In the best-known version of the tale, Yi entrusted the elixir to his wife Chang'e; when his apprentice Peng Meng (or Feng Meng) attempted to steal it, Chang'e drank it herself and floated up to the Moon, where she remains. In other versions, Yi himself is betrayed and killed by the apprentice, or Chang'e's flight is portrayed as desertion rather than sacrifice.

The historical Yi

Ancient histories also record a Hou Yi who was a tribal leader of the Dongyi people during the Xia dynasty and who usurped the throne from the king Xiang before being murdered by his minister Han Zhuo. Scholars debate whether the archer of myth and the usurper of history were originally the same figure. The myth of Yi and the ten suns remains a staple of the Mid-Autumn Festival tradition surrounding Chang'e.
