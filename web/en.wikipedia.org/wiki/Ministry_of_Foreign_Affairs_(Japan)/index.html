The Ministry of Foreign Affairs (外務省, Gaimu-shō) is a cabinet-level ministry of the Japanese government responsible for the country's foreign policy and international relations. It is headed by the Minister for Foreign Affairs and oversees Japan's embassies, consulates, and permanent missions abroad.

History

The ministry traces its origins to 1869, early in the Meiji era, when Japan began building a modern diplomatic service as it renegotiated the unequal treaties and expanded its international engagement. After the Second World War the ministry was rebuilt and played the central role in Japan's reintegration into the international community, the San Francisco Peace Treaty, and the development of Japan's post-war diplomacy centred on the alliance with the United States.

Organisation

The ministry is organised into regional bureaus (including the Asian and Oceanian Affairs Bureau and the North American Affairs Bureau) and functional bureaus dealing with economic affairs, international cooperation, treaties, consular services, and disarmament. It coordinates official development assistance policy together with the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA), runs the Japan Foundation's cultural diplomacy activities under its supervision, and is responsible for protecting Japanese nationals abroad.
