Harry Kendall Thaw

Harry Kendall Thaw (February 12, 1871 – February 22, 1947) was an American heir to a Pittsburgh coal and railroad fortune who became notorious for murdering the architect Stanford White in 1906 at Madison Square Garden in New York City.

The murder and trials

Thaw shot White during a performance of the musical Mam'zelle Champagne, claiming White had drugged and assaulted Thaw's wife, the actress and model Evelyn Nesbit, when she was a teenager. His two trials — the first ending in a hung jury, the second in a verdict of not guilty by reason of insanity — were sensationalised by the press as the "Trial of the Century".

Later life

Committed to an asylum at Beacon, New York, Thaw escaped to Canada in 1913, was extradited, and was eventually released after being judged sane in 1915. He was later involved in further legal troubles, including the whipping of a young man in 1916, and died in Florida in 1947.
