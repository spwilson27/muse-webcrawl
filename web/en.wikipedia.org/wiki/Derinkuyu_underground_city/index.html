Derinkuyu underground city

The Derinkuyu underground city (Turkish: Derinkuyu Yeraltı Şehri) is an ancient multi-level underground city in the town of Derinkuyu in Nevşehir Province, Turkey, in the historical region of Cappadocia. Extending to a depth of approximately 85 metres (280 ft), it is the largest excavated underground city in Turkey and one of several underground complexes found across Cappadocia. It was large enough to shelter as many as 20,000 people together with their livestock and food stores.

History

Caves in the soft volcanic rock of Cappadocia may first have been excavated by the Phrygians in the 8th–7th centuries BC. The complex was greatly enlarged in the Byzantine era, and the underground cities of the region were used extensively by early Christian inhabitants as hiding places during periods of persecution and during Arab raids between the 7th and 11th centuries. Derinkuyu remained in use for centuries and was a refuge during later conflicts; it was largely abandoned in the 1920s following the population exchange between Greece and Turkey, and was rediscovered in 1963 when a resident found a hidden room behind a wall of his cave house. The site opened to visitors in 1969.

Description

The city is organised over some 18 levels. The uppermost levels contained living quarters, stables and storage areas, while deeper levels included a missionary school, churches, communal spaces, wine and oil presses, cellars and refectories. A large cruciform church occupies the seventh level. Ventilation shafts also served as wells, and the passages between levels could be sealed from the inside by enormous circular rolling-stone doors, each weighing up to 500 kilograms. A tunnel several kilometres long is said to connect Derinkuyu with the neighbouring underground city of Kaymaklı.
