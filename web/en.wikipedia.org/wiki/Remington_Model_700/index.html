The Remington Model 700 is a series of bolt-action centerfire rifles manufactured by Remington Arms since 1962. It is a development of the Remington 721 and 722 series, and became one of the most popular hunting and precision rifles in the United States.

Design

The Model 700 uses a cylindrical receiver machined from solid steel, with the barrel recessed into the receiver — a configuration Remington advertised as "three rings of steel" around the cartridge case head. It is offered in a very large number of chamberings and configurations, from varmint rifles to the M24 and M40 sniper rifle variants adopted by the U.S. Army and Marine Corps.

Variants and use

Major variants include the ADL, BDL, SPS, Sendero, and the Model 700 Police. The action is widely used as the basis for custom rifles and many aftermarket parts. The rifle's trigger system was the subject of controversy and recalls concerning reports of firing without a trigger pull, leading to litigation and a voluntary recall of X-Mark Pro triggers.
