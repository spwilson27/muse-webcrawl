AOL

AOL (stylized as Aol., formerly America Online) is an American online service provider and media company. It was one of the pioneers of the consumer internet in the 1990s, bringing millions of users online through its dial-up service, distributed aggressively on free trial floppy disks and CD-ROMs, and popularized features such as email, chat rooms, instant messaging (AIM), and the greeting "You've Got Mail."

History

The company traces its origins to Control Video Corporation (1983) and Quantum Computer Services (1985), adopting the America Online name in 1991. At its peak around 2000, AOL had over 30 million subscribers. In 2000 it announced a merger with Time Warner in a deal valued at about $165 billion — the largest merger in history at the time and later widely regarded as one of the most disastrous, as the dial-up business collapsed with the rise of broadband. AOL was spun off in 2009, acquired by Verizon in 2015 for $4.4 billion, and in 2021 was sold with Yahoo to Apollo Global Management as part of Yahoo Inc.

Services

AOL offered a walled-garden content portal alongside internet access, AOL Mail, and the Huffington Post and TechCrunch, acquired in 2011 and 2010. In 2025 AOL announced the end of its dial-up internet service, closing a defining chapter of early consumer internet history.
