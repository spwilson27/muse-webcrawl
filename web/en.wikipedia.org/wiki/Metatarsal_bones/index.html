Metatarsal bones

Metatarsal bones
DetailsFive long bones of the midfoot
ArticulationsTarsals proximally; proximal phalanges distally
IdentifiersLatin: os metatarsale (pl. ossa metatarsi)

The metatarsal bones, or metatarsus, are a group of five long bones in the foot of humans and other tetrapods, located between the tarsal bones of the hind- and mid-foot and the phalanges of the toes. They are numbered from the medial side of the foot, so that the first metatarsal lies behind the big toe (hallux) and the fifth behind the little toe. Lacking individual names, they are analogous to the metacarpal bones of the hand.

Structure

Each metatarsal consists of a base (proximally), a shaft, and a rounded head (distally) that forms the "ball" of the foot. The first metatarsal is the shortest and thickest, bearing much of the load in walking and running, and has two sesamoid bones beneath its head. The second metatarsal is typically the longest, and its base is recessed into a mortise formed by the cuneiform bones, giving the midfoot stability. The fifth metatarsal has a prominent tuberosity on its lateral base, the site of a common avulsion injury.

Clinical significance

  • Stress fractures ("march fractures") commonly affect the second and third metatarsals in runners and soldiers.
  • Jones fracture — a fracture of the proximal fifth metatarsal, known for slow healing due to poor blood supply.
  • Lisfranc injuries disrupt the tarsometatarsal joints.
  • Metatarsalgia is pain in the ball of the foot, often related to overload of the metatarsal heads.

In other animals

In digitigrade and unguligrade animals the metatarsals are elongated and contribute to limb length; in horses the third metatarsal (cannon bone) is greatly developed while the second and fourth persist as splint bones.
