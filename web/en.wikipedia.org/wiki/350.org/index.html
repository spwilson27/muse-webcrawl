350.org is an international environmental organization dedicated to addressing the climate crisis. Its name derives from 350 parts per million (ppm) of carbon dioxide, a level identified by scientists including James Hansen as the maximum "safe" concentration of CO2 in the atmosphere if humanity wishes to preserve a planet similar to the one on which civilization developed.

History

350.org was founded in 2008 by American author and environmentalist Bill McKibben together with a group of recent graduates of Middlebury College. The organization grew out of the Step It Up 2007 campaign, which organized rallies across the United States calling for cuts in carbon emissions.

On 24 October 2009, 350.org coordinated the International Day of Climate Action, involving more than 5,200 events in 181 countries. It was described as one of the largest globally coordinated days of action on climate change at the time.

Campaigns

The organization has campaigned prominently against the Keystone XL pipeline, organizing mass protests and civil disobedience, including arrests outside the White House in 2011. Through its Fossil Free campaign, launched in 2012, 350.org promotes divestment from fossil fuel companies by universities, pension funds, religious institutions, and other investors.

350.org was a principal organizer of the People's Climate March in New York City in September 2014, which drew an estimated 400,000 participants ahead of a United Nations climate summit.
