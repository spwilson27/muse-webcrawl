Thant (22 January 1909 – 25 November 1974), known honorifically as U Thant ("U" being a Burmese honorific), was a Burmese diplomat and the third Secretary-General of the United Nations, serving from 1961 to 1971—the first non-European and first person from a developing country to hold the post. A devout Buddhist and former schoolteacher, he was chosen following the death of Dag Hammarskjöld in a plane crash.

Secretary-General

U Thant's decade in office spanned some of the Cold War's most dangerous episodes. He played a significant, if partly backstage, role in defusing the Cuban Missile Crisis of 1962 through correspondence with both Kennedy and Khrushchev. He oversaw the UN intervention in the Congo's aftermath, the admission of many newly independent states, and the creation of bodies such as the UN Development Programme and the UN Environment Programme groundwork. An early and staunch critic of the Vietnam War, he repeatedly offered to host peace talks, straining his relations with the United States; he also condemned apartheid and supported decolonisation.

He declined a third term and retired at the end of 1971, succeeded by Kurt Waldheim. In his farewell he expressed a "great sense of relief mingled with sadness".

Death and U Thant crisis

U Thant died of lung cancer in New York in 1974. Burma's military government under Ne Win refused him a state funeral or burial honours, prompting protests by students in Rangoon who seized his coffin and built a mausoleum on the former site of the Rangoon University Students' Union. The ensuing December 1974 "U Thant crisis" ended when the army stormed the campus, killing some students, and reburied him at the foot of the Shwedagon Pagoda. U Thant Island in New York's East River is named in his honour.
