Mein Kampf

Mein Kampf (German: "My Struggle") is a 1925 autobiographical manifesto by Adolf Hitler, written while he was imprisoned at Landsberg following the failed Beer Hall Putsch of 1923. The book combines elements of autobiography with an exposition of Hitler's ideology and his plans for Germany. It became a central text of the Nazi Party and a bestseller in Germany after the Nazis came to power.

Contents

The first volume recounts Hitler's youth in Austria, his years in Vienna and Munich, his service in World War I, and his early political awakening. The second volume, The National Socialist Movement, sets out the ideology of National Socialism: extreme nationalism, antisemitism, the concept of Lebensraum (living space) in Eastern Europe, and the Führer principle of absolute leadership. The book's antisemitic conspiracy theories and its advocacy of territorial expansion foreshadowed the policies of the Third Reich.

Publication history

Hitler dictated much of the book to Rudolf Hess in prison. After the Nazi seizure of power in 1933, Mein Kampf was widely distributed in Germany — given to newly married couples and soldiers — and translated into many languages. By 1945 some 10 million copies had been sold in Germany alone.

Copyright and later editions

After World War II, the copyright was held by the state of Bavaria, which refused to permit republication in Germany. The copyright expired at the end of 2015, seventy years after Hitler's death, and in January 2016 the Institute for Contemporary History in Munich published a heavily annotated scholarly edition, which became a bestseller. The book remains a subject of historical study, and its sale and display are restricted or contextualised in several countries.
