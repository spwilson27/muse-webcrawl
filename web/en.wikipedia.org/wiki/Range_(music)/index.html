Range (music)

Vocal and instrumental range
Concept inMusic theory, vocal music
Measured inNotes, octaves, or semitones between lowest and highest pitch

In music, range, or chromatic range, is the distance from the lowest to the highest pitch that a given musical instrument or voice can produce. It is distinct from tessitura, which describes the most comfortable part of the range, and from register, a specific timbral region of the voice or instrument.

Vocal range

A person's vocal range is all the pitches they can sing, typically classified into voice types: soprano, mezzo-soprano, contralto (or alto), tenor, baritone, and bass. A trained singer's usable range is usually about two octaves, though exceptional voices span three or more.

Instrumental range

Instruments have fixed ranges determined by their construction: the modern piano spans 88 keys (A0 to C8), the violin from G3 upward (extended by harmonics), and the piccolo sounds an octave above its written range. Transposing instruments are notated at a different pitch from their sounding range.

Compound and written ranges

The written range of a part may differ from its sounding range for transposing instruments. Orchestral scores account for each instrument's practical range when assigning parts, and composers exploit extremes of range for expressive effect.
