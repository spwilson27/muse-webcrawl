Concept

A concept is an abstract idea that serves as a building block of thought and belief. Concepts are central to philosophy, linguistics, psychology and the cognitive sciences. They are typically understood as mental representations that group objects, events or qualities sharing common features, enabling classification, reasoning and communication.

Concepts organise experience into categories that support reasoning.

In philosophy

Philosophers have long debated the nature and origin of concepts. Rationalists such as Descartes and Leibniz held that some concepts are innate, while empiricists such as Locke and Hume argued that concepts derive from experience. Kant proposed that concepts function as rules for synthesising experience, distinguishing concepts of the understanding from sensory intuitions. In contemporary philosophy of mind, the classical theory (concepts as definitions), prototype theory and theory-theory offer competing accounts of conceptual structure.

In cognitive science

Psychologists study how concepts are acquired, stored and used in categorisation. Eleanor Rosch's prototype theory showed that category membership is often graded, with some members judged more typical than others. Concepts also underpin language: words typically express concepts, and much of semantics concerns how linguistic expressions relate to them.
