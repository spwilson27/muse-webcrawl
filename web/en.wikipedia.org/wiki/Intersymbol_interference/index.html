Intersymbol interference

In telecommunications, intersymbol interference (ISI) is a form of distortion of a signal in which one symbol interferes with subsequent symbols. This is an unwanted phenomenon, as the previous symbols have a similar effect as noise, making communication less reliable. The spreading of the pulse beyond its allotted time interval causes it to interfere with neighbouring pulses.

Causes

ISI is usually caused by multipath propagation, in which copies of the signal arrive at different times, or by the inherent non-linear frequency response of a communication channel — for instance a band-limited channel that smears pulses in time. In a band-limited channel, the sharp cut-off causes ringing and spreading of each symbol's pulse into adjacent symbol periods.

Mitigation

Techniques to combat ISI include pulse shaping according to the Nyquist ISI criterion (for example raised-cosine filters), adaptive equalization at the receiver, error-correcting codes, multicarrier schemes such as OFDM that divide the data among narrow sub-channels, and maximum-likelihood sequence estimation.

Assessment

The severity of ISI can be judged from an eye diagram, in which overlaid symbol waveforms close the "eye" opening as interference increases; intentional ISI in the form of partial-response signalling is exploited in some systems to achieve spectral efficiency.
