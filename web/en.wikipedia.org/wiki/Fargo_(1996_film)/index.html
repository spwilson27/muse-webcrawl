Fargo is a 1996 black comedy crime film written, produced and directed by Joel Coen and co-written by Ethan Coen. Set in Minnesota and North Dakota in 1987, it follows Jerry Lundegaard (William H. Macy), a desperate car salesman who hires two criminals (Steve Buscemi and Peter Stormare) to kidnap his own wife so he can collect the ransom from his wealthy father-in-law. When the scheme unravels into murder, Brainerd police chief Marge Gunderson (Frances McDormand) investigates.

Plot

Jerry, deep in debt from embezzling dealership funds, arranges the kidnapping to extort $80,000 — actually demanding $1 million and planning to keep most of it. After the kidnappers kill a state trooper and two witnesses, Marge traces the case through methodical police work, eventually discovering the surviving kidnapper feeding his partner's body into a wood chipper, and arrests Jerry as he flees.

Reception and awards

Fargo premiered at the 1996 Cannes Film Festival, where Joel Coen won Best Director. It won two Academy Awards — Best Actress for McDormand and Best Original Screenplay — from seven nominations. In 2006 it was selected for the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically and aesthetically significant. Though filmed mostly in Minnesota and titled for Fargo, North Dakota, the film is celebrated for its wintry cinematography, dark humor and portrayal of Minnesota dialect.

Legacy

The film inspired the acclaimed anthology television series Fargo (2014–), created by Noah Hawley, which expands on its themes and setting with the Coens' blessing as executive producers.
