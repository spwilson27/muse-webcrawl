The Port of Shanghai (Chinese: 上海港) is the world's busiest container port, located at the mouth of the Yangtze River in and around Shanghai, China. It surpassed Singapore in 2010 to become the world's largest container port and has held the title since, handling over 47 million TEU in 2022 and more than 49 million TEU in 2023. The port is operated by Shanghai International Port Group (SIPG), a publicly listed company majority-owned by the Shanghai municipal government.

Geography and terminals

The port comprises facilities on the Yangtze estuary, along the Huangpu River through the city, and offshore at the Yangshan Deep-Water Port. The older Huangpu river terminals, once the heart of treaty-port Shanghai, have largely given way to urban redevelopment. The Waigaoqiao terminals on the south bank of the Yangtze estuary in Pudong handle large shares of container, vehicle, and bulk traffic. The Yangshan port, built on the Greater and Lesser Yangshan islands in Hangzhou Bay (administratively part of Zhejiang but operated by Shanghai) and connected to the mainland by the 32.5 km Donghai Bridge, opened in 2005 to give Shanghai true deep-water berths; its fourth phase, opened in 2017, is among the world's largest automated container terminals.

History

Shanghai became a treaty port after the Treaty of Nanking in 1842 and grew into China's principal gateway for foreign trade, with wharves along the Bund and the Huangpu serving the tea, silk, and later industrial trades. The port declined amid war and revolution in the 1940s, then revived under the People's Republic, especially after the economic reforms beginning in 1978 and the development of Pudong in the 1990s. China's accession to the WTO in 2001 triggered explosive growth in Yangtze Delta exports, driving the construction of Yangshan and propelling Shanghai past Hong Kong and Singapore in container rankings.

Economic significance

The port serves the vast manufacturing hinterland of the Yangtze River Delta — Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Anhui, and beyond — connected by the river itself, expressways, and rail. It anchors Shanghai's ambitions as an international shipping centre, complemented by the Yangshan free-trade zone arrangements and China's Maritime Silk Road policies, under which SIPG also operates terminals abroad, including in Haifa, Israel. Shanghai regularly ranks first in the world on connectivity indices measuring the number and reach of scheduled liner services.
