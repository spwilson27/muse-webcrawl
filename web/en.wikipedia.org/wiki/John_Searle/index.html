John Rogers Searle (31 July 1932 – 17 September 2025) was an American philosopher widely noted for his contributions to the philosophy of language, philosophy of mind, and social philosophy. He taught at the University of California, Berkeley, for most of his career.

Work

A student of J. L. Austin at Oxford, Searle developed speech act theory in Speech Acts (1969), analysing how utterances perform actions such as promising and asserting. In 1980 he proposed the Chinese room thought experiment, arguing that a computer manipulating symbols according to rules could never thereby understand a language — a landmark challenge to "strong AI".

His later work defended a position he called biological naturalism about consciousness and developed a theory of social reality in The Construction of Social Reality (1995) and Making the Social World (2010), centred on collective intentionality and status functions — how things like money, marriage and corporations exist by collective acceptance.

Controversy

In 2019, following student allegations and a university investigation finding that he had violated sexual harassment policies, Berkeley revoked his emeritus status and removed his name from a campus programme. Searle denied wrongdoing. He died in 2025.
