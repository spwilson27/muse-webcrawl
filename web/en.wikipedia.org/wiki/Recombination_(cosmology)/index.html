Recombination (cosmology)

In cosmology, recombination refers to the epoch during the early evolution of the universe when charged electrons and protons first combined to form electrically neutral hydrogen atoms. It occurred roughly 370,000 years after the Big Bang, when the universe had cooled to about 3,000 kelvin (redshift z ≈ 1,100). Before recombination, photons scattered constantly off free electrons, making the universe opaque; once neutral atoms formed, photons decoupled from matter and travelled freely, producing the radiation observed today as the cosmic microwave background (CMB).

At recombination the universe became transparent, releasing the cosmic microwave background.

Background

In the hot early universe, matter existed as an ionised plasma of protons, helium nuclei, and electrons. Hydrogen's ionisation energy is 13.6 eV, corresponding to about 158,000 K, yet recombination occurred at a much lower temperature: because photons outnumber baryons by about a billion to one, the high-energy tail of the photon distribution kept hydrogen ionised until the temperature fell to a few thousand kelvin. The process is described by the Saha equation in its early stages and by more detailed non-equilibrium ("Peebles") recombination theory as the expansion carried the plasma out of equilibrium.

Decoupling and the CMB

As free electrons disappeared into neutral atoms, the photon mean free path grew beyond the size of the observable universe: this "decoupling" defines the surface of last scattering from which the CMB originates. Small temperature fluctuations in the CMB, measured by satellites such as COBE, WMAP, and Planck, encode the density variations that later grew into galaxies and large-scale structure.

Aftermath

After recombination the universe entered the cosmic "dark ages," with no sources of light until the first stars ignited hundreds of millions of years later and reionised the intergalactic gas. The term "recombination" is a slight misnomer, since electrons and protons were combining for the first time, not re-combining.
