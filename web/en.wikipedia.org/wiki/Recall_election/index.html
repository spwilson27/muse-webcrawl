Recall election

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A recall election (also called a recall referendum) is a procedure by which, in certain polities, voters can remove an elected official from office through a referendum before that official's term has ended. Originating in the Swiss and US Progressive Era reforms (Los Angeles adopted it municipally in 1903; Oregon was the first state, 1908), recalls operate in 19 US states, several Latin American constitutions, Taiwan, parts of Canada and the UK in narrow statutory form (the Recall of MPs Act 2015, triggered by specified misconduct).

Operation and examples

Recalls typically require petitions signed by a set proportion of electors, then a vote on removal (sometimes combined with choosing a successor — California's two-question ballot). Famous cases include California 2003 (Governor Gray Davis recalled and replaced by Arnold Schwarzenegger) and 2021 (Governor Gavin Newsom retained), Wisconsin 2012, Venezuela's 2004 recall referendum (President Chávez retained), and Taiwan's legislative recall waves. Scholars debate recalls as accountability mid-course correction versus destabilising permanent campaigning exploited by defeated factions; design choices — grounds requirements, signature thresholds, timing bars — attempt the balance.
