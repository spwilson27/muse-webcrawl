Schmidt–Cassegrain telescope


Type: Catadioptric reflecting telescope
Designers: Bernhard Schmidt (corrector plate); Cassegrain reflector layout
Common apertures: 3.5–16 inches

The Schmidt–Cassegrain telescope is a catadioptric design that combines a spherical primary mirror with a Schmidt corrector plate to produce a compact optical tube with a long focal length. Light enters through the corrector plate, reflects off the primary mirror, then off a convex secondary mirror, and passes through a hole in the primary to an eyepiece at the back.

History

The design builds on Bernhard Schmidt's 1930 Schmidt camera corrector and the classical Cassegrain reflector. American manufacturers such as Celestron and Meade popularised it for amateur astronomy from the 1970s onward.

Characteristics

By folding the optical path, Schmidt–Cassegrains are far shorter than equivalent refractors or Newtonian reflectors, making them portable and well suited to computerised mounts. The design suffers some field curvature and image shift when focusing, and the central obstruction slightly reduces contrast.
