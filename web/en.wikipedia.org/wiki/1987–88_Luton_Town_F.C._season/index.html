The 1987–88 season was Luton Town Football Club's 102nd season in existence and its sixth consecutive season in the Football League First Division. It is the most celebrated season in the club's history: under manager Ray Harford, Luton won the League Cup (then sponsored as the Littlewoods Cup), the first major trophy in the club's history, and also reached the FA Cup semi-finals.

League Cup triumph

Luton's run to Wembley culminated in the final on April 24, 1988, against Arsenal, the holders and heavy favourites. Brian Stein gave Luton the lead, but Arsenal turned the match around to lead 2–1 and were awarded a penalty with ten minutes remaining. Substitute goalkeeper Andy Dibble—playing because Les Sealey was injured—saved Nigel Winterburn's penalty, and Luton rallied: Danny Wilson equalised and Stein scored a last-minute winner for a famous 3–2 victory.

League and FA Cup

In the First Division, Luton finished ninth, a creditable performance on one of the smallest budgets in the division, playing on the artificial pitch at Kenilworth Road. In the FA Cup, the Hatters reached the semi-finals, where they lost 2–1 to Wimbledon—the eventual winners—at White Hart Lane.

Legacy

The League Cup win should have brought European football, but English clubs remained banned from UEFA competitions following the Heysel disaster, so Luton never took up a UEFA Cup place. The 1988 final is still regarded as one of the greatest League Cup finals, and the team—including captain Mike Newell, Mick Harford, Ricky Hill and the Stein brothers—is remembered as the finest in the club's history.
