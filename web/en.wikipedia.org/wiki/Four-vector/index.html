Four-vector

In special relativity, a four-vector (or 4-vector) is an object with four components that transform in a specific way under Lorentz transformations. Four-vectors are the natural language of relativistic physics because they package space and time quantities together so that the laws of physics look the same in every inertial frame.

Definition

The prototypical four-vector is the spacetime position (or four-position), X = (ct, x, y, z), combining time multiplied by the speed of light with the three spatial coordinates of an event. Under a Lorentz boost, the components mix according to the Lorentz transformation, in exact analogy with how the components of an ordinary three-vector mix under rotations. The inner product of two four-vectors, computed with the Minkowski metric, is a Lorentz invariant: all observers agree on its value.

Examples

Important four-vectors include the four-velocity, obtained by differentiating position with respect to proper time; the four-momentum, (E/c, px, py, pz), whose invariant magnitude gives the rest mass of a particle; the four-current, combining charge density and current density; and the four-potential of electromagnetism, combining the scalar and vector potentials. Quantities such as the four-acceleration and four-force are likewise defined, and the wave four-vector combines angular frequency and wave vector.

Notation

Four-vectors are commonly written with Greek indices (Aμ, μ = 0, 1, 2, 3) in tensor notation, with contravariant components written with superscripts and covariant components with subscripts, related by the Minkowski metric tensor.
