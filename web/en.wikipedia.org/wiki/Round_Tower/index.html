Round Tower

The Round Tower (Danish: Rundetårn) is a 17th-century tower in central Copenhagen, Denmark, one of the many building projects of King Christian IV. Built as an astronomical observatory, it is famous for the broad spiral ramp — the equestrian staircase — that winds 7.5 times around its hollow core to the viewing platform at the top, 34.8 metres above the street.

History

Construction began in 1637 and the tower was completed in 1642 as part of the Trinitatis Complex, which combined the observatory with a university church and library. The astronomer Christen Sørensen Longomontanus, Tycho Brahe's former assistant, designed the observatory. Czar Peter the Great famously rode his horse up the ramp in 1716, with the czarina following in a carriage.

Today

The tower remains a public observatory and exhibition space; the former university library hall midway up hosts concerts and exhibitions. Its platform offers views over old Copenhagen and is referenced in Hans Christian Andersen's fairy tale "The Tinderbox".
