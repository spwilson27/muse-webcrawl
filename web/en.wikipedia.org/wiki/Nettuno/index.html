Nettuno is a town and comune in the Metropolitan City of Rome in the Lazio region of central Italy, on the Tyrrhenian Sea about 60 km south of Rome. It is a popular seaside resort and harbour, named after the Roman god Neptune. In antiquity the site near modern Nettuno was the Volscian and Roman port town of Antium, birthplace of the emperors Caligula and Nero.

History

The medieval town grew around a castle near the Saracen-era watchtowers of the coast; its walled old quarter, the Borgo Medievale, survives with narrow lanes, the church of San Giovanni and the Sangallo fortress (Forte Sangallo, 1501–1503), built by Antonio da Sangallo the Elder for the Borgia pope Alexander VI. Nettuno shares with neighbouring Anzio the history of the Allied landings of January 1944 (Operation Shingle) in the Second World War; the nearby Sicily–Rome American Cemetery commemorates the fallen of that campaign.

Main sights and culture

Landmarks include the Forte Sangallo, the medieval borgo, the Villa Bell'Aspetto (Villa Borghese), and the sanctuary of Santa Maria Goretti, who was born in the region and martyred nearby in 1902. Nettuno is also known in Italy as a historic centre of baseball: the town's club, Nettuno Baseball Club, founded in 1945 in the wake of the American presence, is among the most successful in Italian baseball, and the town is often called the capital of Italian baseball.
