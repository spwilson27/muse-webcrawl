The Piper PA-28 Cherokee is a family of two-seat or four-seat light aircraft built by Piper Aircraft and designed for flight training, air taxi and personal use. The PA-28 family of aircraft comprises the Archer, Warrior, Arrow, and Dakota, along with the discontinued Challenger, Charger, Pathfinder, and Pilot.

The PA-28 series competes with the high-winged Cessna 172 and the similarly low-winged Grumman American AA-5 series and Beechcraft Musketeer. The PA-28 includes both the original Cherokee series as well as the newer, tapered-wing designs.

Development

At the time of the Cherokee's introduction, Piper's primary single-engined, all-metal training aircraft was the Piper PA-24 Comanche, a larger, more expensive aircraft with retractable landing gear and a constant-speed propeller. Piper wanted a simpler, cheaper competitor that would do battle with the Cessna 172. The resulting aircraft had fixed gear, a fixed-pitch propeller, and a single door on the right side.

The PA-28 received its type certificate from the Federal Aviation Administration in October 1960, and entered production in 1961 as the Cherokee 150 and Cherokee 160, powered by 150 hp (112 kW) and 160 hp (119 kW) Lycoming O-320 engines respectively. In 1962, Piper added the Cherokee 180, powered by a Lycoming O-360-A4A engine with 180 hp (134 kW).

Design

The Cherokee is a low-wing, all-metal, unpressurized, single-engined piston-powered airplane with a tricycle landing gear and one door on the right side. Early models had a constant-chord, rectangular "Hershey bar" wing; later variants adopted a tapered wing with a longer span.

Variants

Variants range from the two-seat Cherokee 140 trainer to the turbocharged, retractable-gear PA-28R-201T Turbo Arrow and the PA-28-236 Dakota. Modern production models include the Archer TX and the Pilot 100i.
