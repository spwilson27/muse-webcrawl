Hellfire Pass

Hellfire Pass (Thai: ช่องเขาขาด, Chong Khao Khat) is the name of a railway cutting on the former Burma Railway ("Death Railway") in Kanchanaburi Province, Thailand. The pass was built during the Second World War by Allied prisoners of war and Asian labourers (romusha) forced to work for the Imperial Japanese Army.

Construction

The cutting, some 75 metres long and up to 25 metres deep, was hacked through solid rock largely by hand using drills, picks, and dynamite, during 1943. Prisoners worked gruelling "speedo" shifts of up to 18 hours; at night the cutting was lit by bamboo torches and oil lamps, whose flickering flames on the emaciated workers gave it the name "Hellfire Pass". Beatings, disease, malnutrition, and accidents killed many of those who worked on the railway: of roughly 60,000 Allied POWs and far larger numbers of Asian labourers employed overall, many thousands died.

Memorial

Today the site is preserved as a memorial, with a walking trail along the old railway bed and the Hellfire Pass Interpretive Centre, opened with Australian government support in the 1990s. It is a site of annual Anzac Day dawn services.
