Itria Valley

Itria Valley
Valle d'Itria
Country: Italy
Region: Apulia

The Itria Valley (in Italian: Valle d'Itria) is a karst depression in the central part of Apulia (Puglia), in southern Italy. The area, spanning parts of the Metropolitan City of Bari and the provinces of Brindisi and Taranto, is known for its concentration of trulli, the traditional dry-stone cone-roofed houses of the region.

Geography

The valley lies on the Murge plateau. Its main towns include Alberobello, Locorotondo, Martina Franca, Cisternino, Fasano and Ostuni. The landscape is characterized by red earth, olive groves, vineyards, and a dense network of dry-stone walls.

Architecture

The trulli of Alberobello were designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996. Built without mortar from local limestone, their conical roofs are often decorated with painted symbols. The valley also features masserie, fortified farmhouses typical of the Apulian countryside.

Economy

Agriculture — especially olive oil and wine production, including Locorotondo DOC wines — and tourism are the main economic activities.
