Kőnig's line coloring theorem

FieldGraph theory
Named afterDénes Kőnig
Proved1916

Kőnig's line coloring theorem, named after the Hungarian mathematician Dénes Kőnig, states that in any bipartite graph, the number of colours needed for an edge coloring equals the maximum degree of the graph. In other words, the edge chromatic number of a bipartite graph equals its maximum degree Δ.

Statement and significance

For general graphs, Vizing's theorem bounds the edge chromatic number between Δ and Δ + 1, but for bipartite graphs Kőnig's theorem shows the lower bound is always achievable. It implies, for example, that the edges of any regular bipartite graph can be coloured with exactly Δ colours, and hence such a graph decomposes into perfect matchings — a fact closely related to Kőnig's theorem on matchings and vertex covers and to Hall's marriage theorem.

Applications

The theorem is used in scheduling and timetabling problems, such as assigning teachers to classes or scheduling round-robin tournaments, where bipartite edge colouring models conflict-free time slots. Constructive proofs yield polynomial-time algorithms for optimal bipartite edge colouring.
