Middlesex County, Massachusetts

Middlesex County is a county in the Commonwealth of Massachusetts, in the northeastern United States. With a population of about 1.63 million at the 2020 census, it is the most populous county in New England and among the most populous counties in the country without a functioning county government: Massachusetts abolished Middlesex County's government in 1997, and the county survives as a historical and statistical region, with its two traditional seats at Lowell and Cambridge.

Geography and communities

The county extends northwest from Boston's inner suburbs to the New Hampshire border, covering 823 square miles. It contains 54 cities and towns, including Cambridge, Lowell, Somerville, Newton, Waltham, Framingham, Arlington, Medford, and Lexington and Concord — the latter famous as the sites of the first battles of the American Revolutionary War in 1775. Major rivers include the Charles, the Merrimack, the Concord, and the Sudbury.

Economy and education

Middlesex County is a major centre of higher education and technology, home to Harvard University and MIT in Cambridge, Tufts University, Brandeis University, and the University of Massachusetts Lowell. The Route 128 corridor through the county was one of the earliest high-technology zones in the United States, and the Kendall Square area of Cambridge is a global hub for biotechnology and software. Per-capita income in the county is among the highest of any large US county.

Government

Following financial difficulties, Middlesex became the first Massachusetts county to have its government abolished, effective 1997; most functions passed to the state, though elected offices such as sheriff, register of probate, and register of deeds continued. The county name remains in use for courts, districts, and statistics, and it should not be confused with Middlesex, the historic English county after which it was named.
