Race film

The race film or race movie was a genre of films produced in the United States between about 1915 and the early 1950s, consisting of films produced for black audiences, featuring black casts. In all, approximately five hundred race films were produced. Of these, only about one hundred survive; because most were produced outside the Hollywood studio system, they were largely forgotten by film historians until the 1980s, when scholars and archivists began recovering them as important cultural documents.

History

Race films were produced in response to the racism of mainstream cinema, exemplified by D. W. Griffith's The Birth of a Nation (1915), which glorified the Ku Klux Klan. The NAACP organised protests against the film, and Black filmmakers responded by creating films presenting positive, varied images of African American life. Oscar Micheaux, the most prolific and best-known of these filmmakers, directed more than forty features including Within Our Gates (1920), a direct reply to Griffith's film.

Characteristics

Produced on very low budgets, race films were shown in segregated theatres and at special "midnight rambles" in white theatres. They spanned every popular genre — westerns, musicals, gangster films, romances and sermons-in-film — and featured performers such as Paul Robeson, in his film debut in Micheaux's Body and Soul (1925), and the comic Mantan Moreland. Unlike mainstream Hollywood films of the period, race films portrayed Black characters as professionals, landowners and heroes, and directly addressed subjects such as lynching, segregation and passing that white studios avoided.

Decline and legacy

The genre declined after the Second World War as Hollywood began producing films on racial themes and integrating its casts, and as the economics of independent production worsened. Interest revived in the 1980s and 1990s: restored race films were screened in retrospectives, and titles such as Within Our Gates and Spencer Williams's The Blood of Jesus (1941) were selected for the National Film Registry.
