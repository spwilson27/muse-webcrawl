Châteaux of the Loire Valley

The Châteaux of the Loire Valley (French: châteaux de la Loire) are part of the architectural heritage of the historic towns of Amboise, Angers, Blois, Chinon, Orléans, Saumur, and Tours along the Loire River in France. More than 300 châteaux, built between the 10th and 20th centuries, illustrate the ideals of the Renaissance and the Age of Enlightenment on the design of gardens and great residences. The central part of the Loire Valley, between Sully-sur-Loire and Chalonnes, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2000.

History

Originally built as fortresses in the 10th century, the Loire châteaux evolved into splendid residences as the French royal court settled in the valley during the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries. Kings such as Charles VII, Louis XII, and Francis I made the region the heart of royal power, and Italian artists — including Leonardo da Vinci, who died at Clos Lucé in Amboise in 1519 — brought Renaissance ideas to France. When the court returned to Paris and Versailles in the seventeenth century, the great nobles continued to maintain Loire estates.

Notable châteaux

Among the most famous are the royal châteaux of Chambord, Amboise, Blois, and Chenonceau; the fortress of Chinon, where Joan of Arc met the Dauphin in 1429; the gardens of Villandry; and Azay-le-Rideau, built on an island in the Indre. Others include Cheverny, Chaumont-sur-Loire, Langeais, Loches, Ussé, and the Château de Valençay.
