New Development Bank

The New Development Bank (NDB), formerly known as the BRICS Development Bank, is a multilateral development bank established by the BRICS states — Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa — to finance infrastructure and sustainable development projects in emerging economies and developing countries. Agreed at the 2014 BRICS summit in Fortaleza and operational from 2016, the bank is headquartered in Shanghai, China, and was created partly in response to dissatisfaction with the governance of the World Bank and IMF. The concept of such a bank was championed by the economist Jim O'Neill, who coined the BRIC acronym.

Establishment

The proposal for a BRICS development bank was advanced at the 2012 Delhi summit, studied by finance ministers, and agreed at Durban in 2013. The Articles of Agreement were signed on 15 July 2014 at Fortaleza, alongside the treaty creating the BRICS Contingent Reserve Arrangement, a $100 billion pool of central bank reserves. The bank held its inaugural Board of Governors meeting in 2015, opened its Shanghai headquarters, and approved its first loans in 2016, initially for renewable energy projects in each member country.

Capital and governance

The NDB began with authorised capital of $100 billion and initial subscribed capital of $50 billion, shared equally among the five founders — a structure of formal equality unusual among development banks, with no single country holding a veto or dominant shareholding. The presidency rotates among the founding members: the first president was K. V. Kamath of India, followed by Marcos Troyjo of Brazil, and from 2023 Dilma Rousseff of Brazil. The bank has obtained investment-grade credit ratings and issues bonds in international and local currencies, including green bonds denominated in yuan.

Operations and membership expansion

The NDB lends for transport, water, energy, urban development, environmental protection and social infrastructure, and during the COVID-19 pandemic made fast-disbursing emergency loans to members. From 2021 the bank began admitting new members beyond the founders, including Bangladesh, the United Arab Emirates, Egypt and Uruguay, with further countries approved or expressing interest as BRICS itself expanded. Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the bank put new Russian transactions on hold and faced rating pressure, reflecting the difficulty of insulating a development lender from geopolitics.

Criticism

Commentators note that the NDB's lending volumes remain modest beside the World Bank's, that its project standards and transparency practices are less developed, and that sanctions on Russia complicate its operations. Supporters present it as a complementary, less conditional source of development finance that gives emerging economies greater voice in global economic governance.
