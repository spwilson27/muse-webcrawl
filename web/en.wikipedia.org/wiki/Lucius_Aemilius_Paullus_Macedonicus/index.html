Lucius Aemilius Paullus (c. 229 – 160 BC), later nicknamed Macedonicus for his victory over Macedon, was a Roman consul and general who twice held the consulship and triumphed in the Third Macedonian War, ending the Antigonid kingdom and establishing Roman supremacy in Greece.

Early career

The son of Lucius Aemilius Paullus, the consul killed at Cannae in 216 BC, Aemilius Paullus rose through the cursus honorum, serving in Hispania and Liguria. In his first consulship (182 BC) he campaigned successfully in Liguria.

Third Macedonian War

Elected consul a second time for 168 BC at the age of about sixty, Paullus was given command against King Perseus of Macedon. On 22 June 168 BC his army decisively defeated the Macedonian phalanx at the Battle of Pydna, exploiting gaps in the phalanx formation with the flexible Roman manipular legion. Perseus was captured and Macedon was divided into four republics; in 167 BC Paullus conducted the settlement of Greece and Epirus and celebrated a magnificent three-day triumph in Rome, in which Perseus walked in chains. The spoils were so vast that Roman citizens were freed from direct taxation for over a century.

Later life and family

Paullus was censor in 164 BC. A philhellene and patron of Greek culture, he nevertheless presided over harsh reprisals in Epirus. Two of his sons died around the time of his triumph, a loss described by Plutarch as the price of his fortune; two elder sons had been given in adoption — one becoming Scipio Aemilianus, the destroyer of Carthage and Numantia, and the other Quintus Fabius Maximus Aemilianus.

Legacy

Plutarch paired his Life of Aemilius Paullus with that of Timoleon in the Parallel Lives, praising his integrity: despite handling the treasures of Macedon, Paullus died modestly wealthy.
