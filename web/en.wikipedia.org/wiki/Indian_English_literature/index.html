Indian English literature

Indian English literature (IEL), also referred to as Indian Writing in English (IWE), is the body of work by writers from India who write in the English language and whose native or co-language may be one of the many languages of India. It is also associated with the Indian diaspora.

History

Early landmarks include Kashiprasad Ghosh and Henry Louis Vivian Derozio in poetry, and Bankim Chandra Chatterjee's novel Rajmohan's Wife (1864). In the twentieth century, the "big three" of the Indian English novel — Mulk Raj Anand, R. K. Narayan, and Raja Rao — brought international attention, with works such as Anand's Untouchable (1935) and Rao's Kanthapura (1938).

Contemporary writing

The publication of Salman Rushdie's Midnight's Children (1981), which won the Booker Prize, marked a watershed. Later internationally acclaimed authors include Vikram Seth, Arundhati Roy (The God of Small Things), Amitav Ghosh, Rohinton Mistry, Jhumpa Lahiri, and Kiran Desai. Indian English poetry is represented by figures such as Nissim Ezekiel, A. K. Ramanujan, and Kamala Das.
