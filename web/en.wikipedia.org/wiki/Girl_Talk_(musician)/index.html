Gregg Michael Gillis (born 26 October 1981), known by the stage name Girl Talk, is an American musician, DJ and record producer specialising in mashups and digital sampling. His albums construct dense collages from hundreds of uncleared samples of pop, rock and hip-hop recordings, and his work has become a prominent test case in debates over fair use and copyright in sample-based music.

Career

Gillis grew up in Pittsburgh and studied biomedical engineering at Case Western Reserve University in Cleveland, working as an engineer while developing Girl Talk as a side project. His early albums Secret Diary (2002) and Unstoppable (2004) mixed glitch and cut-and-paste techniques. Night Ripper (2006) broke through critically, layering more than 160 samples into a continuous party record; Pitchfork named Gillis to its staff lists and the album received widespread acclaim.

Feed the Animals (2008) was released on a pay-what-you-want basis through the label Illegal Art and was named among the year's best albums by Time magazine and Rolling Stone. All Day (2010), released as a free download, is structured as a single 71-minute track built from 373 samples. Gillis later moved toward production work and original collaborations, including the EP Broken Ankles (2014) with rapper Freeway and the album Full Court Press (2022) with Wiz Khalifa, Big K.R.I.T. and Smoke DZA.

Live performances

Girl Talk's concerts are known for their carnival atmosphere: Gillis performs from a laptop (often wrapped in plastic to protect it from sweat and water), invites audience members on stage, and deploys confetti, balloons and toilet-paper streamers. He has toured extensively and played festivals including Coachella, Bonnaroo and Lollapalooza.

Copyright and fair use

None of the samples on Gillis's mashup albums are licensed; his labels defend the works as fair use, arguing the brief fragments are transformative and do not substitute for the originals. Gillis testified before the U.S. Congress in 2007 during hearings on copyright and new technologies, and he appears in the documentaries Good Copy Bad Copy (2007) and RiP!: A Remix Manifesto (2008). Notably, despite the commercial success of the albums, no lawsuits have been brought against him.
