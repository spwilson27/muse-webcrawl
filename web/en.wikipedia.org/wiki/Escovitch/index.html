Escovitch
Place of originJamaica (with Spanish escabeche roots)
Main ingredientsFish (red snapper, kingfish), vinegar, Scotch bonnet peppers, onions, carrots, pimento
ServedOften with bammy (cassava flatbread) or festival dumplings

Escovitch (also escoveitch) is a Jamaican dish of fried fish topped with a spicy, vinegary pickle of vegetables. The fish — traditionally red snapper, kingfish or parrotfish — is seasoned, fried whole until crisp, and then dressed with a hot escovitch sauce or pickle made from vinegar, sliced onions, carrots (often julienned), Scotch bonnet peppers, pimento (allspice) berries and sometimes chayote (cho cho), which is poured over the fish and allowed to marinate.

Origins

The name and technique derive from the Spanish escabeche, a method of preserving fish or meat in an acidic marinade, which spread through the Spanish world to Latin America and the Philippines (where it became escabeche). In Jamaica the dish became wholly local in flavouring — pimento and Scotch bonnet heat — and it keeps well without refrigeration because of the vinegar, making it a traditional Easter dish (especially on Good Friday) and a staple of beach cook-shops such as those at Hellshire Beach, often served with bammy, a cassava flatbread, or festival dumplings.
