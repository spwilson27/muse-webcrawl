Cloaca

In animal anatomy, a cloaca (from the Latin verb cluo, "to cleanse", thus "sewer") is a single posterior opening that serves as the only opening for the digestive, reproductive and urinary tracts of many vertebrate animals. All amphibians, reptiles, birds, and a few mammals (monotremes, tenrecs, golden moles, and the marsupial mole) have this orifice, from which they excrete both urine and faeces; this is in contrast to most placental mammals, which have two or three separate orifices for evacuation and reproduction.

Function

The cloaca receives the products of the intestinal, urinary and genital tracts. In birds, mating typically involves brief contact between the cloacas of male and female, known as a "cloacal kiss", through which sperm is transferred. Some species, such as ratites, waterfowl and some others, retain an intromittent organ.

Development

In human embryonic development the cloaca is present transiently and divides into the urogenital sinus and the rectum; failure of this division results in congenital conditions such as persistent cloaca.
