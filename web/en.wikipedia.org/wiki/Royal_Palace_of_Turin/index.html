Royal Palace of Turin

Royal Palace of Turin (Palazzo Reale di Torino)
Location: Turin, Piedmont, Italy
Built: 16th–17th centuries
Architects: Ascanio Vittozzi, Carlo and Amedeo di Castellamonte
Status: Museum; UNESCO World Heritage Site

The Royal Palace of Turin (Italian: Palazzo Reale di Torino) is a historic palace in the city of Turin in northern Italy. It was the principal residence of the House of Savoy for over two centuries and is now part of the Royal Museums of Turin (Musei Reali). Together with other Savoy residences, it is listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The palace was built in the sixteenth century on the site of the former bishop's palace when Emmanuel Philibert, Duke of Savoy moved his capital to Turin. It was remodelled in the seventeenth century by architects including Cristina of France's court designers, with a façade by Amedeo di Castellamonte. Behind the palace lie the Royal Gardens, designed in the style of André Le Nôtre.

Interiors and collections

The palace is celebrated for its lavish Baroque and Neoclassical state rooms, including the Throne Room, the Swiss Hall (Salone degli Svizzeri) and the Daniel Gallery, painted by Daniel Seiter. The Chapel of the Holy Shroud, designed by Guarino Guarini, links the palace to Turin Cathedral, where the Turin Shroud is kept. The Royal Armoury (Armeria Reale), founded by Charles Albert in 1837, holds one of Europe's finest collections of arms and armour.

The palace complex also includes the Royal Library, housing Leonardo da Vinci's self-portrait drawing, and the Sabauda Gallery of paintings. The palace remained a royal residence until 1946, when Italy became a republic.
