Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act 1996

Enacted byParliament of India
Assented to24 December 1996
Short titlePESA Act, 1996
Applies toFifth Schedule (Scheduled) Areas in ten states

The Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996, commonly called the PESA Act, is a law enacted by the Government of India to extend the system of local self-governance under Part IX of the Constitution (introduced by the 73rd Amendment) to the Scheduled Areas listed in the Fifth Schedule, with certain modifications recognising the customary law, traditions and community resources of Scheduled Tribe communities. It received the assent of the President on 24 December 1996.

Background

The 73rd Constitutional Amendment of 1992 initially excluded the Scheduled Areas from its scope. A committee headed by Dileep Singh Bhuria examined how panchayati raj could be extended to these areas; on the basis of its recommendations, PESA was enacted to ensure self-rule for tribal communities through their traditional village assemblies.

Key features

PESA makes the Gram Sabha — the assembly of all adult voters of a village — the centre of governance in Scheduled Areas. Its main provisions include:

  • State laws on panchayats in Scheduled Areas must be consistent with customary law, social and religious practices, and traditional management of community resources.
  • Every village has a Gram Sabha competent to safeguard traditions, customs, cultural identity and community resources.
  • Gram Sabhas must be consulted before land acquisition in Scheduled Areas and before resettlement of project-affected people; their recommendation is mandatory for granting prospecting licences or mining leases for minor minerals.
  • Panchayats and Gram Sabhas are endowed with powers over minor water bodies, minor forest produce ownership, prevention of land alienation, control over village markets, moneylending to Scheduled Tribes, social sector institutions and local plans.

Coverage and implementation

The Act applies to Scheduled Areas in ten states: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha and Rajasthan. Implementation has been uneven, as states have been slow to harmonise their subject laws with PESA, and the Act is frequently invoked in debates over mining and development projects on tribal land.
