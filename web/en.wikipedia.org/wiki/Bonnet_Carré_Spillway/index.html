Bonnet Carré Spillway

The Bonnet Carré Spillway is a flood control structure in St. Charles Parish, Louisiana, that diverts water from the Mississippi River into Lake Pontchartrain and thence to the Gulf of Mexico. Built after the devastating Great Mississippi Flood of 1927, it protects the city of New Orleans from river flooding by lowering the river's level and reducing pressure on downstream levees. It is operated by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers as part of the Mississippi River and Tributaries Project.

Design

The spillway consists of a concrete weir about 7,700 feet (2,350 m) long containing 350 bays, each closed by wooden "needles" that are lifted by locomotive-mounted cranes to let water pass. The diverted water flows through a floodway roughly 5.7 miles (9.2 km) long, crossed by road and rail bridges, before emptying into Lake Pontchartrain. The structure takes its name (French for "square bonnet") from the shape of the bend in the river at the site.

Operation

The spillway is opened when the Mississippi's flow at New Orleans is forecast to exceed about 1.25 million cubic feet per second. It was first operated in 1937 and has been opened on a limited number of occasions, including 1973, 1983, 1997, 2008, 2011, 2016, and 2018. Unusually, it was opened twice in 2019 — the first back-to-back openings in its history — and again in 2020, reflecting a period of exceptional flooding on the river. Large openings introduce fresh water and sediment into the brackish lake and Mississippi Sound, events associated with algal blooms and mortality of oysters and other marine life, which has made the spillway's operation a subject of environmental controversy and litigation involving Gulf Coast fishing interests.
