Masyaf (Arabic: مصياف Miṣyāf) is a city in northwestern Syria, administratively part of the Hama Governorate, located in the foothills of the coastal Jabal Ansariyah mountains, about 40 kilometres (25 mi) west of Hama. It is famous for its medieval citadel and for its role as the headquarters of the Nizari Ismaili state in Syria — the order known in the West as the Assassins — under the leadership of Rashid al-Din Sinan, the "Old Man of the Mountain."

History

The castle of Masyaf was built in the Byzantine period and passed through Muslim and Crusader-era hands. The Nizari Ismailis acquired it around 1141, and it became the centre of their Syrian strongholds. Under Sinan (1163–1193), Masyaf's lord directed the fedayeen whose targeted killings of political figures made the Assassins legendary in both Muslim and Crusader sources. The Mongols took the castle in 1260, and the Mamluk sultan Baybars captured it in 1270, ending the independent Nizari state in Syria.

Under Ottoman rule Masyaf remained a modest garrison town. In the 20th century it grew into a local market centre with a mixed population of Ismailis, Alawites, Sunni Muslims, and Christians. During the Syrian civil war the town's vicinity saw fighting, and government forces used the area as a base for operations in the Hama countryside.

The castle

Masyaf Castle rises on a rocky outcrop above the town. It preserves Ismaili, Ayyubid, and Mamluk phases, and was restored in the 2000s with support from the Aga Khan Trust for Culture. The castle and town feature prominently in the Assassin's Creed video game series (2007), in which Masyaf serves as the headquarters of the Assassin brotherhood.
