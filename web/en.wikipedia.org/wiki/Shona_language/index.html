Shona language

Shona (chiShona)
Native to: Zimbabwe, Mozambique
Speakers: approx. 9–14 million
Family: Niger–Congo, Bantu
ISO 639-1: sn

Shona (chiShona) is a Bantu language of the Shona people of Zimbabwe and southern Mozambique. It is one of the official languages of Zimbabwe and the most widely spoken indigenous language in the country. Standard Shona, based mainly on the Zezuru, Karanga, and Manyika dialects, was codified in the 20th century, notably through the work of linguist Clement Doke.

Dialects

The Shona cluster includes Karanga, Zezuru, Manyika, Korekore, and Ndau, among others. Ndau, spoken in eastern Zimbabwe and central Mozambique, is sometimes treated as a separate language; Zimbabwe's constitution lists both Shona and Ndau among its officially recognised languages.

Grammar

Like other Bantu languages, Shona is agglutinative and tonal, with a system of noun classes marked by prefixes that govern agreement across the sentence. Verbs are built from a root with prefixes for subject and tense and can incorporate object markers and derivational extensions.

Literature

Shona has a rich oral tradition of proverbs (tsumo), praise poetry, and storytelling. The first Shona novel, Solomon Mutswairo's Feso (1956), opened a modern written literature that includes poets and novelists such as Charles Mungoshi, who wrote in both Shona and English.
