AuthorViktor E. Frankl
Published1946 (German); 1959 (English)
GenreMemoir, psychology

Man's Search for Meaning is a 1946 book by Viktor Frankl chronicling his experiences as a prisoner in Nazi concentration camps during World War II, and describing his psychotherapeutic method, logotherapy, which involves identifying a purpose in life and immersing oneself in imagining that outcome.

Contents

The book is divided into two parts. The first recounts Frankl's experiences in Auschwitz and other camps, analysing the psychology of prisoners and the ways in which the search for meaning sustained life. The second part introduces logotherapy, Frankl's theory that the primary human drive is not pleasure, as Freud proposed, but the pursuit of what he calls "meaning".

Reception

First published in German as ... trotzdem Ja zum Leben sagen ("...Saying Yes to Life in Spite of Everything"), the book has sold well over ten million copies and been translated into dozens of languages. A 1991 survey for the Library of Congress named it among the ten most influential books in the United States.
