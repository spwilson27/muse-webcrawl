Globe Theatre

The Globe Theatre was a theatre in London associated with William Shakespeare. It was built in 1599 by Shakespeare's playing company, the Lord Chamberlain's Men, in Southwark on the south bank of the Thames.

Globe Theatre
Location: Southwark, London
Opened: 1599
Destroyed: Fire 1613; demolished 1644
Company: Lord Chamberlain's Men

History

Built from timbers of the earlier Theatre in Shoreditch, the open-air polygonal Globe held about 3,000 spectators, with "groundlings" standing in the yard. On 29 June 1613 a stage cannon set the thatched roof alight during Henry VIII and the theatre burned down; it was rebuilt with a tiled roof in 1614. Puritans closed theatres in 1642 and the Globe was demolished in 1644.

Reconstruction

Actor Sam Wanamaker led the project to rebuild it; Shakespeare's Globe opened in 1997 about 230 metres from the original site, using traditional materials and staging plays in conditions approximating the original.
