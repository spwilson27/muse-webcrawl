Political union

A political union is a type of political entity composed of two or more states, regions or polities that unite under a common government. Political unions range from loose confederations to fully merged unitary states, and are classified by how power is shared between the centre and the constituent parts.

Types

  • Incorporating union — previously separate states merge into a single new state, as in the Acts of Union 1707 that created Great Britain from England and Scotland.
  • Federal union — states unite while retaining constitutionally protected self-government, as in the United States, Germany or Canada.
  • Confederation — sovereign states delegate limited powers to a common body they can overrule, as in the historical Articles of Confederation or the present-day European Union's confederal elements.
  • Real union / personal union — states remain legally distinct but share a head of state, as in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth or the Kalmar Union.
  • Supranational union — states pool sovereignty in shared institutions, the outstanding example being the European Union.

Formation and dissolution

Unions are typically created by treaty, act of union or constitution, motivated by defence, trade or dynastic inheritance. They may dissolve peacefully — as the United Arab Republic (Egypt and Syria, 1958–1961) or Czechoslovakia in 1993 — or through secession and war. See also federation and confederation.
