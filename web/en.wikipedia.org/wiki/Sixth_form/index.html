Sixth form

Sixth form
CountriesEngland, Wales, Northern Ireland and other systems based on the English model
Age range16–18
School yearsYear 12 (Lower Sixth) and Year 13 (Upper Sixth)
Typical qualificationsA-levels

The sixth form represents the final two years of secondary education in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (and in some Commonwealth countries), covering students aged 16 to 18. Students in the sixth form typically study for A-level qualifications (GCE Advanced Levels) in a small number of subjects, usually three or four, as preparation for university entrance or employment.

Structure

The first year is the Lower Sixth (Year 12) and the second the Upper Sixth (Year 13). Sixth forms may be part of a secondary school, or separate institutions such as sixth form colleges and further education colleges. Since 2015, young people in England have been required to participate in education or training until their 18th birthday, though not necessarily at school.

History

The term derives from the traditional form system of English public schools, in which the sixth form was the highest class. Historically, sixth-formers enjoyed privileges such as prefect roles, separate common rooms and relaxed uniform rules, customs that survive in many schools today.
