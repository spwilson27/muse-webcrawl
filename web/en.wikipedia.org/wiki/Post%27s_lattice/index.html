Post's lattice
Named afterEmil Leon Post
SubjectClones of Boolean functions
Published1941

Post's lattice is the lattice of all clones on a two-element set {0, 1}, ordered by inclusion. It was described by Emil Leon Post in his 1941 monograph The Two-Valued Iterative Systems of Mathematical Logic and gives a complete classification of the clones of Boolean functions.

Structure

Each node of the lattice represents a clone — a set of Boolean functions closed under composition and containing the projections. Familiar clones in the lattice include the monotone functions, the self-dual functions, the affine (linear) functions, and the 0-preserving and 1-preserving functions. The lattice is countable, and every clone is finitely generated.

Applications

Post's lattice underlies results on functional completeness: a set of Boolean connectives is functionally complete exactly when it is not contained in any of the five maximal clones. It also plays a central role in the algebraic approach to constraint satisfaction problems.
