Cramér–Rao bound

FieldStatistics, estimation theory
Named afterHarald Cramér, C. R. Rao
Related conceptFisher information

In estimation theory and statistics, the Cramér–Rao bound (CRB) relates to estimation of a deterministic parameter and expresses a lower bound on the variance of unbiased estimators of that parameter: the variance of any such estimator is at least as large as the reciprocal of the Fisher information.

Var(θ̂) ≥ 1 / I(θ)

Equivalently, the reciprocal of the Fisher information I(θ) is a lower bound on the mean squared error of any unbiased estimator of θ. An unbiased estimator that achieves this bound is said to be fully efficient. The result is named in honour of Harald Cramér and C. R. Rao, though it was also derived independently by Maurice Fréchet, Georges Darmois, and by Alexander Aitken and Harold Silverstone.

Statement

The bound applies to any unbiased estimator of an unknown parameter defined on a statistical model satisfying certain regularity conditions. For a biased estimator, a modified version of the bound incorporates the derivative of the bias.

Generalizations

Extensions include the multiparameter case, in which the Fisher information becomes a matrix and the covariance matrix of the estimator is bounded below by its inverse, and Bayesian versions such as the van Trees inequality.
