Subterranean river

A subterranean river or underground river is a river or stream that flows wholly or partially beneath the Earth's surface. Subterranean rivers form naturally in karst landscapes, where water dissolves limestone and other soluble rocks, creating caves and conduits; a surface stream may sink at a swallow hole and re-emerge at a resurgence some distance away. Other underground "rivers" are engineered, where an urban watercourse has been culverted or buried.

Natural examples

Famous natural examples include the Puerto Princesa Subterranean River in the Philippines, which flows about 8.2 kilometres underground before reaching the sea and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site; the Lost River in Indiana, which travels much of its course underground; and the Križna Jama system in Slovenia. Underground rivers support specialised ecosystems, including blind cave fish and other troglobitic species adapted to total darkness.

Mythology

Underground rivers feature prominently in myth and religion — the rivers of the Greek underworld (Styx, Acheron, Lethe, Phlegethon, and Cocytus) are among the best known examples — and they remain staples of adventure literature and film.
