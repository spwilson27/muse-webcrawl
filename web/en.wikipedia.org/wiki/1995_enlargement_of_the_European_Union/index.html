The 1995 enlargement of the European Union saw Austria, Finland and Sweden join the European Union on 1 January 1995, increasing its membership from twelve to fifteen countries. The three new members had previously belonged to the European Free Trade Association (EFTA) and were among the wealthiest states in Europe, making this the first enlargement in which the acceding countries were net contributors to the EU budget.

The EU-15 after 1 January 1995, with Austria, Finland and Sweden in the Union.

Background

All three countries had maintained neutrality during the Cold War, which had complicated membership of the European Communities. The end of the Cold War removed that obstacle. Austria applied for membership in July 1989, Sweden in July 1991 and Finland in March 1992. Norway also applied, and its accession treaty was signed, but Norwegian voters rejected membership in a referendum in November 1994, as they had in 1972.

Accession

Negotiations opened in early 1993 and concluded in spring 1994. The Treaty of Accession was signed at Corfu in June 1994. Referendums approved membership in Austria (66.6% in favour, June 1994), Finland (56.9%, October 1994) and Sweden (52.3%, November 1994). The three states joined the Union on 1 January 1995 and participated in the 1995 European Parliament elections later that year.

Impact

The enlargement extended the EU's territory to the Arctic, gave it a long land border with Russia through Finland, and strengthened the northern, environmentally conscious and transparency-oriented voice within the Union. The newcomers brought distinctive policy priorities, including the Northern Dimension initiative promoted by Finland. No further enlargement took place until the major eastern enlargement of 2004.
