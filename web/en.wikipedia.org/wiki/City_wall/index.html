City wall

Defensive wall / city wall

A city wall is a fortification used to protect a city or settlement from potential aggressors, forming an enclosure around the urban area.

A defensive wall is a fortification usually used to protect a city, town or other settlement from potential aggressors. The walls can range from simple palisades and earthworks to extensive military fortifications such as curtain walls with towers, bastions and city gates. A city wall specifically encloses an urban settlement. From ancient to modern times, they were used to enclose settlements in most parts of the world.

History

Among the earliest city walls are those of Jericho (around 8000 BC) and Uruk in Mesopotamia. Ancient and medieval cities from China to Europe ringed themselves with walls: the Aurelian Walls of Rome, the Theodosian Walls of Constantinople, and the Ming-dynasty walls of Xi'an and Nanjing are famous surviving examples. Walls controlled trade through gates where tolls could be levied, kept out raiders and armies, and marked the legal boundary of the city — many languages preserve the distinction between the city within the walls and suburbs outside.

The development of powerful artillery in the 15th–16th centuries rendered high medieval walls vulnerable, prompting the low, thick, angled bastions of the trace italienne. By the 19th century, growing cities often demolished their walls to allow expansion — Vienna's Ringstraße was built on the site of its demolished fortifications — although many historic circuits survive, partly or wholly, as in York, Dubrovnik, Lucca, and Carcassonne.

Features

City walls typically include a curtain wall topped by a parapet and wall walk, defensive towers at intervals, fortified gates, and often an external ditch or moat. Some walls incorporated earlier structures, and richer cities built multiple concentric circuits.
