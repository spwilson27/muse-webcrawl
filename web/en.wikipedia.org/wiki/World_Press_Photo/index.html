World Press Photo is an independent non-profit organization based in Amsterdam, Netherlands, best known for organizing the world's most prestigious annual contest in professional photojournalism and documentary photography. The winning images are exhibited in a travelling exhibition visited by millions of people in dozens of countries each year.

History

The contest was founded in 1955 by a group of Dutch photographers who invited international colleagues to compete. The first top prize was won by Danish photographer Mogens Haven for a photograph of a motorcycle racer crashing. Over the decades, winning images have become defining documents of their eras, from war and famine to sport and the environment.

Contest

The competition receives tens of thousands of entries from thousands of photographers worldwide. Since a 2022 restructuring, entries are first judged in six world regions — Africa, Asia, Europe, North and Central America, South America, and Southeast Asia and Oceania — across categories such as Singles, Stories, Long-Term Projects, and Open Format. Regional winners advance to the global judging for the World Press Photo of the Year.

Iconic winners

Famous Photo of the Year winners include the 1972 image of children fleeing a napalm attack in Vietnam (associated with the contest era's defining coverage), the 1987 photograph of a mother holding her malnourished child in Burkina Faso, and John Stanmeyer's 2013 image of African migrants on the shore of Djibouti City raising phones to capture a signal.
