Cnoidal wave

Cnoidal wave profiles for different values of the elliptic parameter.

In fluid dynamics, a cnoidal wave is a nonlinear and exact periodic wave solution of the Korteweg–de Vries equation. The solutions are expressed in terms of the Jacobi elliptic function cn(x, m), from which the name "cnoidal" derives, by analogy with sinusoidal waves described by sine functions. Cnoidal wave theory was developed by Diederik Korteweg and Gustav de Vries in 1895.

Characteristics

Cnoidal waves occur in shallow water, where the wavelength is much longer than the water depth. Compared with a sinusoidal wave of the same period, a cnoidal wave has sharper, narrower crests and longer, flatter troughs. The shape is governed by the elliptic parameter m: as m approaches zero the wave becomes sinusoidal, while as m approaches one the wavelength grows without bound and the cnoidal wave degenerates into a single solitary wave — a soliton — with a sech² profile.

Applications

Cnoidal wave theory is used in coastal and ocean engineering to describe long waves in shallow water, where linear (Airy) wave theory is inaccurate. It provides estimates of wave height, wavelength, and orbital velocities used in the design of coastal structures, and it describes phenomena such as undular bores in rivers and estuaries. Because the Korteweg–de Vries equation also arises in other physical settings, cnoidal waves appear in plasma physics, nonlinear optics, and solid-state physics as well.
