Online food ordering

Online food ordering is the process of ordering food, for delivery or pickup, from a local restaurant or food cooperative through a web page or mobile application. Much like ordering consumer goods online, many services allow customers to keep accounts with them in order to make frequent ordering convenient, and offer tracking of the meal as it is prepared and delivered.

History

The first online food order is often credited to a Pizza Hut pizza in 1994, via the chain's PizzaNet site. Dedicated platforms followed: Grubhub and Seamless in the United States, Just Eat in Denmark and the UK, and later app-based delivery marketplaces such as DoorDash, Uber Eats, Deliveroo, Meituan and Zomato, which added their own courier fleets to the older model in which restaurants delivered with their own drivers.

Models

Two broad models exist. In the platform-to-consumer model, a marketplace takes orders and organises logistics, charging restaurants commissions that can reach 20–30% and charging customers delivery and service fees. In the restaurant-to-consumer model, chains run their own ordering channels and fulfilment. The sector expanded dramatically during the COVID-19 pandemic, and has since seen consolidation, experiments with ghost kitchens (delivery-only restaurants), subscription delivery passes, and debate over courier employment status, restaurant margins, and commission caps imposed by some cities.
