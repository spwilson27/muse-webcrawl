True Grit is a 1969 American Western film directed by Henry Hathaway and starring John Wayne as U.S. Marshal Reuben J. "Rooster" Cogburn. The film is adapted from the 1968 novel of the same name by Charles Portis. Wayne won the Academy Award for Best Actor for his performance, the only Oscar of his long career.

Plot

Fourteen-year-old Mattie Ross (Kim Darby) hires the hard-drinking, one-eyed Marshal Rooster Cogburn to track down Tom Chaney, the hired hand who murdered her father. Insisting on accompanying the posse, Mattie joins Cogburn and Texas Ranger La Boeuf (Glen Campbell) on a trek into Indian Territory. The trio confronts Chaney and the outlaw gang of "Lucky" Ned Pepper, culminating in a celebrated climax in which Cogburn, reins in his teeth and a rifle in each hand, charges four outlaws alone.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major commercial success and received two Academy Award nominations, winning Best Actor for Wayne. A sequel, Rooster Cogburn, followed in 1975, pairing Wayne with Katharine Hepburn. In 2010 the Coen brothers released a second adaptation of Portis's novel, starring Jeff Bridges as Cogburn and Hailee Steinfeld as Mattie, which was also a critical success. The 1969 film remains one of the most beloved Westerns of Wayne's career.
