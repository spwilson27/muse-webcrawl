Khajuraho Group of Monuments

The Khajuraho Group of Monuments is a group of Hindu and Jain temples in Chhatarpur district, Madhya Pradesh, India. Built between about 885 and 1050 CE by the Chandela dynasty, the temples are famous for their nagara-style architecture and their intricate stone sculpture, including a celebrated minority of erotic figures. They are a UNESCO World Heritage Site (inscribed 1986).

History

Of the original 85 temples spread over 20 square kilometres, about 25 survive in various states of preservation, divided into western, eastern and southern groups. The largest and most ornate, the Kandariya Mahadeva Temple, is dedicated to Shiva. After the fall of the Chandelas the temples were abandoned and overgrown by forest, and were brought to wider attention in the 19th century.

Art and architecture

The temples are built of sandstone on high platforms, with soaring shikhara spires symbolising Mount Meru. Their walls are covered with thousands of carved figures — gods, warriors, musicians, dancers and scenes of daily life. The erotic sculptures, often emphasised in popular accounts, form only a small fraction of the total and are found on exterior walls.
