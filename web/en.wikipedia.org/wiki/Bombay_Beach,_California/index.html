Bombay Beach is a census-designated place (CDP) in Imperial County, California, United States. It lies on the eastern shore of the Salton Sea, along State Route 111, about 60 miles (97 km) southeast of Palm Springs. The population was 231 at the 2020 census, down from 295 in 2010.

History

Named for Bombay, India (according to local tradition, by an early promoter struck by the exotic-sounding name), the community developed as a resort during the Salton Sea's mid-20th-century tourism boom, with marinas, a yacht club, hotels and beachfront homes. As the sea grew more saline and polluted, fish kills and flooding in the 1970s destroyed the tourist economy; part of the town was abandoned behind a protective earthen berm. For decades Bombay Beach was cited as a symbol of the Salton Sea disaster, known for its rusting ruins, fish-bone beaches and low-income, largely elderly population.

Arts community

From about 2015, the Bombay Beach Biennale — an invitation-only arts festival founded by Stefan Ashkenazy, Sonia Campbell and others — brought artists, architects and musicians to the town, funding permanent installations such as a converted "opera house", an outdoor cinema of abandoned cars and dozens of sculptures and signs. The resulting attention has been credited with reviving interest in the town, though it has also sparked debate about gentrification in one of California's poorest communities.

Geography and demographics

According to the United States Census Bureau, the CDP has a total area of 0.94 square miles (2.4 km²), all of it land. At 223 feet below sea level it is one of the lowest communities in the United States. The 2010 census reported 295 residents with a median age well above the state figure, and Bombay Beach has frequently ranked among the poorest places in California by median household income. It is served by the Westmorland Union School District, and its ZIP code is 92257.

In popular culture

Bombay Beach is the setting of Alma Har'el's 2011 documentary Bombay Beach, which won the top documentary prize at the Tribeca Film Festival. The town and the Salton Sea shoreline have been used as filming and photography locations for numerous music videos, fashion shoots and independent films.
