Miracle of the Sun

The Miracle of the Sun (Portuguese: Milagre do Sol) is an event reported to have occurred on 13 October 1917 at Fátima, Portugal, before a crowd estimated in the tens of thousands. Many witnesses said the sun appeared to dance or zigzag in the sky, emit multicoloured light, and plunge toward the earth, before returning to its place. The event followed months of reported Marian apparitions to three shepherd children, Lúcia dos Santos and her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto, who had predicted a sign on that date.

Accounts

The day began with heavy rain, which witnesses said stopped abruptly; their soaked clothes were reported to be suddenly dry. Secular newspapers, including the Lisbon daily O Século, reported the phenomenon, though descriptions varied and some present said they saw nothing unusual.

Interpretations

The Catholic Church declared the Fátima apparitions "worthy of belief" in 1930. Critics and scientists have proposed explanations including a sundog (parhelion), atmospheric effects, retinal after-images from staring at the sun, and mass suggestion, while believers regard the event as a miracle authenticating the apparitions. Fátima has become one of the world's major Marian pilgrimage sites.
