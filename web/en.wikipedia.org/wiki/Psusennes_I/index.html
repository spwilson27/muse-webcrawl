Psusennes I

Psusennes I (Greek Ψουσέννης) was the third pharaoh of the Twenty-first Dynasty of Egypt, ruling from Tanis in the Nile Delta for roughly half a century around 1047–1001 BC. He is celebrated today for his intact royal tomb at Tanis, one of the very few undisturbed pharaonic burials ever found, and for his solid silver coffin, which earned him the epithet "the Silver Pharaoh".

Reign

Psusennes ruled the north of Egypt from Tanis while the High Priests of Amun governed the south from Thebes — the defining arrangement of the Third Intermediate Period. He built extensively at Tanis, enclosing the great temple of Amun with a massive mud-brick wall and reusing blocks from the earlier Ramesside capital of Pi-Ramesses nearby.

Tomb

The French Egyptologist Pierre Montet excavated Psusennes' tomb at Tanis in 1939–1940. Unlike the plundered tombs of Thebes, it had survived intact, containing a gold funerary mask, a coffin of solid silver — extraordinary in metal-poor Egypt — and abundant jewellery. The outbreak of the Second World War meant the discovery received little publicity compared with Tutankhamun's tomb, though its contents are now displayed in the Egyptian Museum in Cairo.
