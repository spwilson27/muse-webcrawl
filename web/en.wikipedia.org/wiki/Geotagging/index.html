Geotagging

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Geotagging, or geo-tagging, is the process of adding geographical identification metadata to media such as photographs, videos, websites, SMS messages or RSS feeds. The metadata usually consists of latitude and longitude coordinates, and may also include altitude, bearing, distance, accuracy data and place names.

Methods

Geotags can be applied automatically at the moment of capture — most smartphones and many cameras embed GPS coordinates in a photo's Exif data — or afterwards, by synchronising a tracklog from a separate GPS receiver with the timestamps of un-geotagged photos, or by manually placing media on a map in software such as photo-library applications.

Applications

Geotagged media can be searched and displayed by location, powering map-based photo browsing on services such as Flickr and Google Photos, location check-ins on social networks, citizen-science observations, journalism verification, and the geolocation of events in open-source investigations. Geotagging is also central to augmented reality and location-based games.

Privacy and safety

Because a geotag can reveal exactly where a person lives or where a photograph was taken, sharing geotagged images carries privacy and security risks; military authorities have warned that soldiers' geotagged posts can disclose positions, and wildlife agencies ask photographers not to geotag sensitive species. Major platforms typically strip location metadata from uploaded images or let users disable automatic geotagging.
