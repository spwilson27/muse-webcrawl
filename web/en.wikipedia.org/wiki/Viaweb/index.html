Viaweb

TypePrivate (1995–1998)
IndustryWeb application software
Founded1995
FoundersPaul Graham, Robert Morris, Trevor Blackwell
FateAcquired by Yahoo! in 1998
SuccessorYahoo! Store

Viaweb was a company founded in 1995 by Paul Graham, Robert Morris, and Trevor Blackwell that developed one of the first web applications: software that allowed users to build and operate their own online stores directly through a web browser. At a time when most software ran locally, Viaweb let merchants create storefronts entirely online, hosting them on Viaweb's servers for a monthly fee.

The software was written largely in the Lisp programming language, which Graham later credited as a major competitive advantage: it allowed the small team to add features more quickly than competitors. Graham's later essay "Beating the Averages" discusses Viaweb's use of Lisp as a case study in the strategic value of powerful programming languages.

Acquisition

In June 1998, Yahoo! acquired Viaweb in exchange for Yahoo! stock valued at roughly $49 million. The product was relaunched as Yahoo! Store, which became a popular platform for small-business e-commerce. The Viaweb founders' experience helped inspire Graham's later work co-founding the startup accelerator Y Combinator in 2005.
