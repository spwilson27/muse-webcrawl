Patanjali

Patanjali (Sanskrit: पतञ्जलि, Patañjali) was an ancient Indian sage credited as the author or compiler of the Yoga Sutras, the foundational text of classical yoga philosophy. Tradition also attributes to him the Mahabhasya, a great commentary on Panini's grammar, and a work on Ayurvedic medicine, though scholars debate whether these were the same person.

Dating and identity

Estimates for Patanjali's life range from the 2nd century BCE to the 4th century CE. The grammarian Patanjali who wrote the Mahabhasya is generally dated to the mid-2nd century BCE. Whether the yoga compiler was the same man has been argued for centuries; modern scholars mostly treat them as distinct or uncertain.

Yoga Sutras

The Yoga Sutras comprise 196 short aphorisms (sutras) defining yoga as the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind (yogas citta-vritti-nirodhah) and describing the eight limbs of practice (ashtanga): ethical restraints and observances, posture, breath control, sense withdrawal, concentration, meditation and absorption (samadhi). The text became the basis of Raja Yoga and was revived in global prominence in the 20th century.

Legacy

Patanjali is revered in India as an incarnation of the serpent Shesha. His name is given to institutions, universities and the popular Patanjali Ayurved company founded in the 21st century.
