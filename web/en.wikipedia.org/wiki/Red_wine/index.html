Red wine

Red wine
Type: Wine
Made from: Dark-coloured grape varieties, fermented with skins
Colour source: Anthocyanins from grape skins
Typical alcohol: 12–15% ABV

Red wine is a type of wine made from dark-coloured grape varieties. The colour of the wine can range from intense violet in young wines, through brick red for mature wines, to brown for older red wines. The juice from most purple grapes is greenish-white; the red colour comes from anthocyanin pigments present in the grape skins, extracted during fermentation by maceration.

Production

After crushing, red grapes ferment together with their skins, seeds, and sometimes stems, typically for one to two weeks at 25–30 °C. Contact with the skins extracts colour, tannins, and flavour compounds. The wine is pressed, may undergo malolactic fermentation to soften acidity, and is often aged in oak barrels or stainless steel before bottling. Techniques such as cold soaking, pump-overs, and extended maceration modulate extraction.

Varieties and styles

Major red varieties include Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Pinot noir, Syrah (Shiraz), Grenache, Tempranillo, Sangiovese, and Nebbiolo. Famous red-wine regions include Bordeaux and Burgundy in France, Rioja in Spain, Piedmont and Tuscany in Italy, the Barossa Valley in Australia, and Napa Valley in California. Styles range from light, chillable Beaujolais to tannic, long-lived Barolo.

Composition and health

Red wine contains polyphenols including tannins and the much-studied resveratrol, which prompted research into the "French paradox". Evidence for health benefits of moderate consumption remains debated, and alcohol itself carries recognised risks.
