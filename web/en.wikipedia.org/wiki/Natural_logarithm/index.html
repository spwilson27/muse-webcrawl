Natural logarithm

Notation: ln x, loge x
Base: e ≈ 2.71828
Inverse: Exponential function ex
Derivative: 1/x

The natural logarithm of a number is its logarithm to the base e, where e is the irrational mathematical constant approximately equal to 2.71828. It is written ln x or loge x. The natural logarithm of x is the power to which e must be raised to equal x: ln(ea) = a, and eln x = x for x > 0.

Definition and properties

Equivalently, ln x can be defined as the area under the curve 1/t from 1 to x, i.e. the integral ∫1x dt/t. It obeys the logarithm identities ln(xy) = ln x + ln y and ln(xr) = r ln x, has derivative 1/x, and tends slowly to infinity as x grows. Its series expansion ln(1 + x) = x − x²/2 + x³/3 − … underlies many calculations.

History and applications

The natural logarithm arose in the seventeenth century from the study of the area under a hyperbola (Grégoire de Saint-Vincent, Alphonse Antonio de Sarasa) and Mercator's series, before Euler's work in the eighteenth century established the modern theory centred on e. Because growth and decay processes — compound interest, radioactive decay, population growth — are governed by ex, the natural logarithm appears throughout science, statistics (log-likelihoods, entropy) and economics (continuously compounded returns, elasticity).
