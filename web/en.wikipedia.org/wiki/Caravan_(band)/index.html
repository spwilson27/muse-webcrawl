Caravan are an English rock band from the Canterbury area, founded by former Wilde Flowers members David Sinclair, Richard Sinclair, Pye Hastings, and Richard Coughlan in 1968. The band have never achieved the great commercial success that was widely predicted for them at the beginning of their career, but are nevertheless considered a key part of the Canterbury scene of progressive rock acts, blending psychedelic rock, jazz, and classical influences to create a distinctive sound.

The band were originally based in Whitstable, Kent, near Canterbury, and took early inspiration from the city's music scene that also produced Soft Machine.

History

Formation and early albums (1968–1972)

Caravan formed in 1968 from the remnants of The Wilde Flowers. Their self-titled debut album, Caravan, was released in 1968 on Verve Records — the band being, unusually for a British act, signed first to an American label. After Verve closed its rock and pop division, Caravan moved to Decca Records. Their second album, If I Could Do It All Over Again, I'd Do It All Over You (1970), brought them greater attention, and its title track became a minor hit single. The follow-up, In the Land of Grey and Pink (1971), is widely regarded as their finest work and a landmark of the Canterbury scene; it features the nine-minute suite "Nine Feet Underground" and the much-loved song "Golf Girl".

Later years

Following the departure of David Sinclair and the brief involvement of keyboardist Steve Miller, the classic line-up re-formed for For Girls Who Grow Plump in the Night (1973) and Cunning Stunts (1975). The band continued through the late 1970s with diminishing commercial returns before splitting in 1978. Caravan have re-formed several times since, led by guitarist and vocalist Pye Hastings, the band's one constant member, and continue to record and tour.
