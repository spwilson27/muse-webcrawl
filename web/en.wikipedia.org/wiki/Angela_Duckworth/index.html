Angela Lee Duckworth (born 1970) is an American academic, psychologist, and popular science author. She is the Rosa Lee and Egbert Chang Professor at the University of Pennsylvania, where she studies grit and self-control. She is also the founder and chief scientist of Character Lab, a not-for-profit organisation whose mission is to advance scientific insights that help children thrive.

Career

Duckworth studied neurobiology at Harvard College, neuroscience at the University of Oxford as a Marshall Scholar, and earned her PhD in psychology at the University of Pennsylvania under Martin Seligman. Before her academic career she taught mathematics and science in public schools in New York City, San Francisco and Philadelphia.

Grit

Duckworth defines grit as "perseverance and passion for long-term goals". Her studies of cadets at West Point, National Spelling Bee finalists, teachers and salespeople suggested that grit predicts success beyond measures of talent or intelligence. She received a MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship in 2013. Her 2016 book Grit: The Power of Passion and Perseverance was a New York Times bestseller. The concept has also attracted scholarly criticism, notably meta-analytic work suggesting that grit overlaps strongly with the established trait of conscientiousness and that only its perseverance facet robustly predicts performance.
