Zhouzhuang (Chinese: 周庄; pinyin: Zhōuzhuāng) is a famous ancient water town in Kunshan, under the administration of Suzhou, Jiangsu province, China. Located about 30 kilometres southeast of Suzhou and surrounded by lakes and rivers, Zhouzhuang is renowned for its well-preserved Ming and Qing dynasty architecture, network of canals, and stone bridges. It is often called the "Venice of the East" and is widely considered China's most famous water town.

History

Zhouzhuang was founded in 1086 during the Northern Song dynasty, when a local landowner donated land for a Buddhist temple and the settlement took the name of its benefactor, Zhou Digong. During the Ming dynasty, the wealthy silk merchant Shen Wansan enlarged the town and made it a prosperous trading centre for grain, silk and handicrafts in the Jiangnan region. Its waterways remained its main transport arteries well into the 20th century.

Attractions

  • Twin Bridges (Shuang Qiao) — the Shide and Yongan bridges, arranged at right angles over intersecting canals; made famous by a 1984 painting by artist Chen Yifei.
  • Fu'an Bridge — built in 1355, the oldest bridge in the town, combining a bridge with a tower.
  • Shen House — the vast private residence of Shen Wansan's descendants, built in 1742, with over 100 rooms.
  • Zhang House — a Ming dynasty residence with a courtyard and a section of canal running through it.
  • Chengxu Temple — a Taoist temple founded during the Song dynasty.

Culture

Zhouzhuang is known for its traditional crafts, Kunqu opera heritage, and local delicacies such as Wansan pork hock (braised pork knuckle named after Shen Wansan). Boat tours through its canals, with boatwomen singing local folk songs, are a signature visitor experience.
