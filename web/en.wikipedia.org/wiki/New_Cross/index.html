New Cross

New Cross is an area in south-east London, England, within the London Borough of Lewisham, about 4 miles (6.4 km) south-east of Charing Cross. It lies along the Old Kent Road and New Cross Road near Deptford, Brockley and Peckham.

History

The area was once known as Hatcham, recorded in the Domesday Book. Its modern name comes from a coaching inn, the Golden Cross, near a tollgate established in the 18th century. New Cross developed rapidly in the Victorian era around its two railway stations, New Cross and New Cross Gate.

Culture and landmarks

New Cross is home to Goldsmiths, University of London, a noted art and humanities institution whose alumni include many of the Young British Artists. The area has a strong music heritage — the Venue club and local pubs were important in the Britpop era, and bands such as Blur (formed at Goldsmiths) and Squeeze are associated with the district. In January 1981 a fire at a house party on New Cross Road killed 13 young Black people; the New Cross fire became a landmark event in British race relations, prompting the Black People's Day of Action march.

Transport

New Cross station and New Cross Gate station provide London Overground (East London line) and Southeastern services.
