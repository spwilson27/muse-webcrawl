NCAA Division III (D-III) is a division of the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) in the United States. D-III consists of athletic programmes at colleges and universities that choose not to offer athletic scholarships to their student-athletes. It is the NCAA's largest division by number of member institutions and by number of participants.

The division's philosophy holds that athletics should be primarily for the benefit of the participant and the institution, integrated into the educational mission rather than operated as a commercial enterprise. Division III athletes may receive financial aid only on the same basis as other students — need-based aid and academic merit awards — and schools must report aid patterns to ensure athletes are not favoured.

Division III athletics are integrated into campus life at mostly small colleges.

Philosophy and rules

The Division III philosophy statement emphasises academics, fair and equitable competition, and the primacy of the student-athlete's educational experience. Seasons are shorter, practice time is more limited, and out-of-season training restrictions are stricter than in Divisions I and II, and redshirting for athletic reasons is not permitted. Recruiting is permitted but without the financial inducement of athletic aid.

Membership

Most Division III members are small private liberal-arts colleges, though the division also includes large public institutions such as the University of Wisconsin system schools. Well-known members include Williams College, Amherst College, Johns Hopkins University, the University of Chicago, Washington University in St. Louis, and Emory University. The division is organised into more than 40 conferences, including the University Athletic Association, the New England Small College Athletic Conference (NESCAC), and the Wisconsin Intercollegiate Athletic Conference.

Championships

Division III crowns national champions in 28 sports. Its football championship game is the Stagg Bowl, named for Amos Alonzo Stagg, first played in 1973; the University of Mount Union and the University of Wisconsin–Whitewater have been the division's dominant football programmes. The men's basketball, baseball, and ice hockey championships likewise date to the 1970s reorganisation.

History

Division III was created in the NCAA's 1973 reorganisation, drawing mainly on the old College Division schools that did not award athletic scholarships. Membership has grown steadily, and proposals to subdivide the division by size or philosophy were debated in the 2000s but not adopted. Roughly 80% of members are private institutions.
