The Circus, Bath

The Circus
LocationBath, Somerset, England
ArchitectJohn Wood, the Elder; John Wood, the Younger
Built1754–1768
StyleGeorgian

The Circus is a historic ring of large townhouses in the city of Bath, Somerset, England, forming a circle with three entrances. Designed by the architect John Wood, the Elder, it was built between 1754 and 1768 and is regarded as a pre-eminent example of Georgian architecture. The name comes from the Latin circus, meaning a ring or oval.

Design

Wood was inspired by the Roman Colosseum, and the Circus's curved façades display the three classical orders — Doric, Ionic and Corinthian — on successive storeys, decorated with carved emblems including serpents, acorns and masonic symbols. The diameter of the circle is about 97 metres (318 ft), similar to that of Stonehenge, reflecting Wood's antiquarian interests. The central area was originally paved, later becoming a lawn planted with plane trees.

History

John Wood the Elder died shortly after construction began, and his son John Wood the Younger completed the project. Notable residents have included the painter Thomas Gainsborough and, in the 20th century, the actor Nicolas Cage. Together with the nearby Royal Crescent, the Circus forms part of the Bath UNESCO World Heritage Site.
