Cannery Row is a novel by American author John Steinbeck, published in 1945. Set during the Great Depression in Monterey, California, on a street lined with sardine canneries that is known as Cannery Row, the book revolves around the people living there: Lee Chong, the local grocer; Doc, a marine biologist based at Western Biological Laboratory; and Mack and the boys, a group of unemployed men living in the Palace Flophouse.

Plot summary

The novel's loose plot follows two attempts by Mack and the boys to throw a party for Doc, whom they regard as their benefactor. The first party, held in Doc's laboratory while he is away, gets disastrously out of hand; the second, planned with more care, is a success. Between these poles Steinbeck weaves vignettes about the Row's inhabitants — drifters, prostitutes, shopkeepers and Chinese fishermen — treating them with warmth and humour rather than judgement.

Themes

Steinbeck explores community, contentment outside material success, and the interdependence of human beings and the natural world, a theme connected to his long collaboration with the marine biologist Ed Ricketts, the model for Doc. The novel ends with Doc reading the poem "Black Marigolds" to his guests.

Legacy

The real Cannery Row street in Monterey, formerly Ocean View Avenue, was renamed Cannery Row in 1958 in honour of the book. A film adaptation appeared in 1982, and the novel remains one of Steinbeck's most popular works. A sequel, Sweet Thursday, followed in 1954.
