Meccano is a brand of construction set created in 1898 by Frank Hornby in Liverpool, England. The system consists of reusable metal strips, plates, girders, wheels, axles and gears, with nuts and bolts to connect the pieces, enabling the building of working models and mechanical devices. It was widely copied under names including Erector Set (in the United States) and Märklin Metallbaukasten.

History

Hornby devised the system, originally called "Mechanics Made Easy," to entertain his sons, and patented it in 1901. Production centred on the Binns Road factory in Liverpool, which operated from 1914 to 1979. Meccano was enormously popular in the first half of the 20th century and was exported worldwide, with factories in France and elsewhere. The brand passed through several owners, including Lines Bros (Tri-ang) and Airfix; today Meccano sets, relaunched with modern "Maker System" and robotics kits, are produced under Spin Master ownership.

The system

Standard Meccano parts are based on holes spaced at half-inch (12.7 mm) intervals, with threads of 5/32 inch Whitworth. Sets were sold in numbered outfits of increasing size, supplemented by accessory sets and motors (clockwork and electric). The hobby supported magazines, guilds, and elaborate models such as the giant block-setting crane built at the Binns Road factory.
