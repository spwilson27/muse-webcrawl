International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants

The International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants (ICN or ICNafp) is the set of rules and recommendations that govern the scientific names of all organisms traditionally treated as algae, fungi or plants, whether fossil or non-fossil. Before 2011 it was called the International Code of Botanical Nomenclature (ICBN); the name was changed at the International Botanical Congress in Melbourne in 2011 to reflect its coverage of fungi and algae as well as plants.

Principles

The Code rests on six principles: botanical nomenclature is independent of zoological and bacteriological nomenclature; the application of names is determined by nomenclatural types; nomenclature is based on priority of publication; each taxon has only one correct name, generally the earliest published in accordance with the rules; scientific names are treated as Latin regardless of their derivation; and the rules are retroactive unless expressly limited.

History and editions

The origins of the Code lie in Alphonse de Candolle's Lois de la nomenclature botanique adopted at the Paris congress of 1867. Successive International Botanical Congresses have revised the rules, with editions named after the host cities: Vienna, Brussels, Cambridge, Paris, Stockholm, Sydney, Berlin, Tokyo, St Louis, and Vienna again. The Melbourne Code (2012) introduced major changes, including allowing electronic publication and permitting descriptions in English as well as Latin. The Shenzhen Code, based on decisions at the 2017 congress in Shenzhen, China, was published in 2018.

Content

The Code regulates such matters as the ranks of taxa (species, genus, family and so on), the formation and grammar of names, valid publication and effective publication, typification, author citations, and the naming of hybrids and cultivated plants (the latter partly covered by a separate code, the ICNCP). For fungi, special provisions require the registration of new names in a recognised repository, such as MycoBank, for valid publication since 2013.

Governance

Changes to the Code are decided by the Nomenclature Section of an International Botanical Congress, held every six years, and the Code is maintained between congresses by committees under the International Association for Plant Taxonomy, which publishes the journal Taxon.
