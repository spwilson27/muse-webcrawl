National nature reserves (NNRs) in Scotland are areas of land or water designated under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 to protect some of the country's most important natural and semi-natural habitats and species. They are intended both as sanctuaries for wildlife and as places where the public can experience and study nature. There are 43 NNRs in Scotland, covering roughly 1.9% of the country's land area, and most are owned or managed by NatureScot, the Scottish Government's nature agency, or by partner organisations such as the National Trust for Scotland, RSPB Scotland, and the Scottish Wildlife Trust.

History

The first Scottish NNR, Beinn Eighe in Wester Ross, was declared in 1951 — the first national nature reserve in Great Britain — to protect a remnant of ancient Caledonian pinewood. The network expanded over the following decades under the Nature Conservancy and its successors to encompass mountain plateaux, Atlantic oakwoods, blanket bogs, estuaries, and island seabird colonies.

Notable reserves

  • Beinn Eighe — ancient pinewood and mountain terrain near Torridon.
  • Cairngorms — Britain's largest NNR by area, protecting arctic-alpine habitats on the Cairngorm plateau.
  • St Kilda — a remote archipelago famous for seabird colonies and its evacuated island community; also a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
  • Isle of May — island reserve at the mouth of the Firth of Forth, noted for puffins and grey seals.
  • The Great Trossachs Forest — one of the largest reserves, centred on woodland restoration near Loch Katrine.
  • Forsinard Flows — extensive blanket bog in the Flow Country of Caithness and Sutherland.
  • Tentsmuir and St Cyrus — coastal reserves protecting dune systems and dune grassland.

Management

Following a review in the 1990s, Scottish NNR policy emphasised that reserves should be primarily for nature conservation, secondarily for public enjoyment and education, and managed as exemplars of good practice. Many reserves overlap with Sites of Special Scientific Interest and with Natura 2000 designations. NatureScot promotes the network under the slogan of reserves being "where nature gives its finest show".
