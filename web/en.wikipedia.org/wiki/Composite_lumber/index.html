Composite lumber

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Outdoor decking is the most common application of composite lumber

Composite lumber is a material produced by combining wood fibre, plastic, and a binding agent into boards and other shapes used in place of traditional timber. The most common type is wood–plastic composite (WPC), typically made from recycled plastic (such as polyethylene) mixed with sawdust or wood flour, extruded or moulded into planks. Products include decking boards, railing, fencing, cladding, and outdoor furniture.

Manufacture

In a typical process, finely ground wood fibre is dried, blended with thermoplastic resin and additives (colourants, UV stabilizers, lubricants, and sometimes foaming agents), then extruded through a die into the desired profile and cooled. Some products are capped with a co-extruded polymer shell that improves resistance to staining, fading, and scratching. Fill ratios of wood to plastic vary widely, and some "composite" boards contain mineral fillers or no wood at all.

Properties

Composite lumber is marketed as more durable outdoors than untreated wood: it does not rot, splinter, or attract wood-boring insects in the same way, and it does not need periodic painting or sealing. Against this, it generally has lower stiffness and strength than structural timber of the same size, so it is rarely used for load-bearing framing; it expands and contracts with temperature more than wood; it can be heavier than comparable lumber; and its surface can become hot in direct sun. Quality and performance vary greatly between brands and product generations, and early products from the 1990s were subject to well-publicized failures, including mould growth and premature deterioration, leading to class-action lawsuits.

Environmental aspects

Manufacturers emphasize the use of recycled plastics and reclaimed wood waste, diverting material from landfill. However, the finished composite is itself difficult to recycle because the wood and plastic cannot easily be separated, and critics note that it ultimately persists as plastic waste. Life-cycle comparisons with pressure-treated timber are contested.

History

Wood–plastic composites were commercialized in Italy in the 1970s and grew rapidly in the United States in the 1990s, when companies such as Trex (formed in 1996 from a Mobil Chemical division) brought composite decking to the mass market. Decking remains by far the largest application.
