Internet rights

Internet rights are the claimed or recognized human rights and legal rights relating to access to and use of the Internet and digital communications. They extend established rights—such as freedom of expression, freedom of association and privacy—to the online environment, and include newer claims such as a right of access to the Internet itself.

Background

Advocacy for Internet rights grew with the mass adoption of the Internet in the 1990s and 2000s. Organizations such as the Electronic Frontier Foundation and Access Now campaign for digital rights, and initiatives including the Internet Rights and Principles Coalition have drafted charters applying human rights frameworks online. In 2016 the UN Human Rights Council adopted a resolution affirming that the same rights people have offline must also be protected online.

Principal issues

  • Access: claims that access to the Internet is a basic right or essential utility; a few countries, beginning with Estonia and Finland, have codified aspects of this.
  • Freedom of expression and censorship: limits on blocking, filtering and shutdowns.
  • Privacy and surveillance: protection of communications and personal data from state and corporate monitoring.
  • Net neutrality: equal treatment of Internet traffic by service providers.
  • Digital divide: inequalities in access between and within countries.
