Empty sella syndrome

Empty sella syndrome is the condition of a shrunken or flattened pituitary gland within the sella turcica, most commonly due to an intrasellar extension of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) accompanying an elevated intracranial pressure. The sella appears "empty" on imaging because it is filled with cerebrospinal fluid, which flattens the pituitary gland against the wall of the sella.

Classification

There are two types of empty sella syndrome: primary and secondary. Primary empty sella occurs when a small anatomical defect above the pituitary gland increases pressure in the sella turcica and causes the gland to flatten out along the interior walls of the sella turcica. It is associated with obesity and with idiopathic intracranial hypertension in women. Secondary empty sella is the result of the pituitary gland regressing within the cavity after an injury, surgery, or radiation therapy.

Signs and symptoms

Often there are no symptoms at all, and empty sella is an incidental finding on imaging performed for other reasons. When symptoms occur they may include headaches, hypertension, fatigue, and impotence. Low levels of pituitary hormones, including hypopituitarism, may be present. Rarely, cerebrospinal fluid may leak from the nose (CSF rhinorrhea).

Diagnosis

The condition is diagnosed by magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) or computed tomography (CT) of the brain, which shows the sella turcica filled with cerebrospinal fluid and a flattened pituitary gland. Hormone testing may be performed to assess pituitary function.

Treatment

Unless the syndrome results in other medical problems, treatment for endocrine dysfunction associated with pituitary dysfunction is symptomatic and supportive. Surgery may be needed if cerebrospinal fluid leaks from the nose, or if vision is affected.
