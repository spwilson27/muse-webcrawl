William Bundy

William Putnam Bundy (1917–2000), U.S. foreign policy adviser.

William Putnam Bundy (24 September 1917 – 6 October 2000) was an American attorney and intelligence expert who served as a foreign affairs advisor to presidents John F. Kennedy and Lyndon B. Johnson. He was a key figure in the planning of American policy during the Vietnam War. His brother, McGeorge Bundy, was National Security Advisor.

Career

Educated at Yale and Harvard Law School, Bundy worked on the Bletchley Park codebreaking effort liaison during the Second World War before joining the Central Intelligence Agency, where he helped prepare National Intelligence Estimates. In 1961 he moved to the Defense Department under Robert McNamara, then served as Assistant Secretary of State for Far Eastern Affairs from 1964 to 1969.

Vietnam

Bundy drafted important policy papers and resolutions during the escalation of the Vietnam War, including work connected with the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution. In later years he wrote a critical, unpublished history of the war and expressed regret over elements of the policy he had served.

Later life

After leaving government, Bundy was editor of the journal Foreign Affairs (1972–1984) and taught at Princeton. He died in 2000.
