Fundamental theorem on homomorphisms

In abstract algebra, the fundamental theorem on homomorphisms, also known as the first isomorphism theorem, relates the structure of two objects between which a homomorphism is given, and of the kernel and image of the homomorphism. It is one of the isomorphism theorems, first formulated in the work of Emmy Noether and others developing modern algebra in the early twentieth century.


The theorem induces a canonical isomorphism between the quotient by the kernel and the image.

Statement for groups

Let G and H be groups, and let f : G → H be a group homomorphism. Let ker f be the kernel of f and im f its image. Then ker f is a normal subgroup of G, the quotient group G / ker f is well defined, and there is a canonical isomorphism:

G / ker f  ≅  im f,    g ker f ↦ f(g).

In other words, the image of a homomorphism is an "exact copy" of the domain with precisely the kernel collapsed to the identity: a homomorphism factors through the quotient by its kernel, and the induced map is injective with image equal to im f.

Other structures

Analogous statements hold for rings (the quotient by the kernel, which is an ideal, is isomorphic to the image), for modules and vector spaces (where the theorem underlies the rank–nullity theorem: the dimension of the domain equals the dimension of the kernel plus the dimension of the image), and more generally for varieties of algebras in universal algebra, where congruences replace normal subgroups and ideals.

Applications

  • Identifying quotient groups concretely, e.g. showing that Z/nZ arises as the image of a map onto a cyclic subgroup.
  • Proving that the multiplicative group of a field modulo scalars relates to projective linear groups.
  • Establishing that for a linear map T : V → W, V/ker T ≅ im T.
