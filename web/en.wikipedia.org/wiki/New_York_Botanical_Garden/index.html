New York Botanical Garden
Location: The Bronx, New York City
Area: 250 acres (100 ha)
Established: 1891

The New York Botanical Garden (NYBG) is a botanical garden and plant research institution in the Bronx, New York City. Established in 1891 on part of the former Lorillard estate, its 250 acres contain more than one million living plants across some 50 gardens and collections, and it is a National Historic Landmark.

Grounds and collections

The garden's centrepiece is the Enid A. Haupt Conservatory, a Victorian glasshouse opened in 1902 and restored in the 1990s, housing palms, desert plants and seasonal exhibitions such as the annual Orchid Show. The grounds preserve the Thain Family Forest, some 50 acres of old-growth forest that is among the last remnants of the woodland that once covered New York City, and gardens devoted to roses, azaleas, conifers, native plants and a home gardening centre.

Research and education

NYBG is a major centre of botanical science. Its William and Lynda Steere Herbarium holds roughly 7.8 million specimens, among the largest collections in the world, and the LuEsther T. Mertz Library is one of the largest botanical libraries. The garden runs research programs in plant systematics, ecology and conservation and operates a graduate studies program with partner universities.
