Reggie Miller

Born: 24 August 1965, Riverside, California
Position: Shooting guard
Team: Indiana Pacers (1987–2005)
Honours: 5× NBA All-Star; Olympic gold (1996); Hall of Fame (2012)

Reginald Wayne Miller (born 24 August 1965) is an American former professional basketball player who played his entire 18-year National Basketball Association career with the Indiana Pacers. Renowned for his precision three-point shooting and clutch performances, especially against the New York Knicks, he is regarded as one of the greatest shooters in NBA history.

College and NBA career

Miller starred at UCLA before being drafted 11th overall by Indiana in 1987 — a choice initially unpopular with Pacers fans who wanted local hero Steve Alford. Miller became the face of the franchise, leading Indiana to the NBA Finals in 2000 and to five Eastern Conference finals. At his retirement in 2005 he held the NBA record for career three-point field goals made (2,560), a mark later surpassed by Ray Allen and Stephen Curry.

Famous moments

In Game 1 of the 1995 Eastern Conference semifinals against the Knicks, Miller scored eight points in 8.9 seconds to snatch an improbable victory, one of the most famous sequences in playoff history. His running rivalry with Knicks fan Spike Lee became part of NBA folklore.

International and later life

Miller won a gold medal with the United States at the 1996 Atlanta Olympics. After retiring he became a television analyst for TNT. His number 31 was retired by the Pacers, and he was inducted into the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame in 2012. His older sister Cheryl Miller is also a Hall of Fame player.
