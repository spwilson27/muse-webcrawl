Tiber Island, shaped in antiquity to resemble a ship.

Tiber Island (Italian: Isola Tiberina) is the only island in the stretch of the Tiber river that runs through Rome. About 270 metres long and 67 metres wide, it lies in the southern bend of the river, just west of the Capitoline Hill, and is joined to the banks by two ancient bridges. Since the third century BC it has been associated with healing, an association that continues today in the hospital that occupies its western half.

The temple of Aesculapius

During a plague in 293 BC, the Sibylline Books directed Rome to bring the cult of Aesculapius, the god of healing, from Epidaurus. The sacred snake carried on the Roman ship was said to have swum ashore at the island, choosing the temple's site; the Temple of Aesculapius was dedicated there in 291 BC. The island was subsequently monumentalised in travertine in the form of a ship, complete with a "prow" bearing reliefs of the god and an obelisk as its "mast" — a deliberate architectural image of the vessel that had brought salvation. Sick people came to sleep in the temple precinct (incubation) hoping for curative dreams.

Buildings today

The eastern end holds the church of San Bartolomeo all'Isola, founded around 998 by the emperor Otto III over the temple ruins to house the relics of St Bartholomew the Apostle. The western half is occupied by the Fatebenefratelli Hospital, founded in 1584 by the Brothers Hospitallers of Saint John of God, which still operates as a general hospital — thus continuing the island's medical vocation for more than two thousand years. A smaller church, San Giovanni Calibita, adjoins the hospital. The island is connected to the left bank by the Ponte Fabricio (62 BC, the oldest bridge in Rome still in use) and to Trastevere by the Ponte Cestio.

Legend and festivals

Roman legend explained the island itself variously: some said it formed from silt accumulating around the corpses and property of the hated king Tarquin the Proud, thrown into the river. In summer, the island hosts the Isola del Cinema open-air film festival, and its small piazza and ice-cream kiosk make it a quiet pause in the centre of the city.
