Erwin Neher (born 20 March 1944) is a German biophysicist, specialising in the field of cell physiology. Together with Bert Sakmann, he developed the patch clamp technique, which allows the detection of the minute electrical currents (of a few picoamperes) flowing through a single ion channel. For this discovery, Neher and Sakmann shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1991.

Career

Neher studied physics at the Technical University of Munich and the University of Wisconsin–Madison, and gained his doctorate at Munich in 1970. Working at the Max Planck Institute for Biophysical Chemistry in Göttingen with Sakmann in the 1970s, he refined the patch clamp method, first described in a landmark 1976 paper in Nature. The technique revolutionised the study of ion channels in cell membranes, with wide-ranging consequences for neuroscience, pharmacology and the understanding of diseases such as cystic fibrosis and epilepsy.

Neher later directed research on neurotransmitter release and synaptic transmission at Göttingen, becoming a director at the Max Planck Institute for Biophysical Chemistry and head of its Department of Membrane Biophysics.
