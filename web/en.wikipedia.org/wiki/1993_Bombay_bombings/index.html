The 1993 Bombay bombings were a series of 12 terrorist bombings that took place in Bombay (now Mumbai), India, on 12 March 1993. The single-day attacks killed 257 people and injured 1,400, making them the deadliest coordinated bomb attacks in Indian history at the time.

The explosions struck targets across the city over the course of about two hours, including the Bombay Stock Exchange, the Air India Building, hotels, markets, banks, and the regional passport office. The attacks were widely attributed to the D-Company criminal syndicate headed by Dawood Ibrahim, acting with the involvement of Tiger Memon and others, and are generally regarded as retaliation for the Bombay riots of December 1992 and January 1993, which followed the demolition of the Babri Masjid.

Investigation and trials

The investigation, led by the Central Bureau of Investigation, resulted in one of the longest trials in Indian legal history. In 2006–2007 the special TADA court convicted 100 of the 123 accused. Yakub Memon, convicted of conspiracy and financing, was executed in 2015, the only death sentence carried out in the case. Dawood Ibrahim and Tiger Memon remained fugitives.
