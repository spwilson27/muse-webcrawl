Strobe light

A strobe light or stroboscopic lamp, commonly called a strobe, is a device used to produce regular flashes of light. It is one of a number of devices that can be used as a stroboscope. The word originated from the Ancient Greek stróbos (στρόβος), meaning "act of whirling".

A typical commercial strobe light has a flash energy in the region of 10 to 150 joules, and discharge times as short as a few milliseconds, often resulting in a flash power of several kilowatts. Larger strobe lights can be used in "continuous" mode, producing extremely intense light.

Technology

Most strobe lights use a xenon flash lamp: a gas-discharge tube filled with xenon that emits a brief, intense pulse of white light when a high-voltage trigger ionizes the gas and a capacitor discharges through it. Modern designs increasingly use high-power LEDs. The flash rate is adjustable, from less than one flash per second to hundreds per second, depending on the application.

Applications

Stroboscopes are used in industry to inspect moving machinery by making rotating parts appear stationary, in science to study periodic motion and vibration, in photography as studio flash units, in aviation and marine navigation as beacon lights, and in entertainment lighting at concerts and nightclubs. Strobe tachometers measure rotational speed without contact.

Safety

Flashing lights in certain frequency ranges (roughly 3 to 30 flashes per second, most provocative around 15–25 Hz) can trigger seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy, which is why broadcast and web-content guidelines restrict flashing imagery.
