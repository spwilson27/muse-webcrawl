Acoustic fingerprint

An acoustic fingerprint is a condensed digital summary, a fingerprint, deterministically generated from an audio signal, that can be used to identify a sample or quickly locate similar items in an audio database. Unlike a raw audio recording, the fingerprint captures the perceptually significant features of the sound so that matching recordings can be recognised despite differences in format, bit rate or moderate noise.

How it works

Fingerprinting algorithms typically compute a spectrogram and extract salient events — for example, pairs of spectral peaks characterised by their frequencies and time difference, as in the system behind the music-identification service Shazam. These features are hashed into compact codes that are stored in a database and searched efficiently.

Applications

Uses include identifying songs playing in the environment, monitoring broadcasts for royalty collection, detecting duplicated or copyrighted content on media platforms, and synchronising second-screen experiences with television audio.
