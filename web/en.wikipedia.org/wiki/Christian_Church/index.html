Christian Church

The Christian Church is the body of all believers in Jesus Christ, understood in Christian theology as the community founded by Christ and the apostles. The term translates the Greek ekklēsia, meaning an assembly or congregation, and is used both for the universal community of Christians and for local congregations and church buildings.

Theology

The Nicene Creed describes the Church as "one, holy, catholic and apostolic". Different traditions understand its unity and structure differently: Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, and Protestant churches each claim continuity with the apostolic Church while differing on authority, sacraments, and governance.

History

The Church began in 1st-century Judea, spread through the Roman Empire despite periodic persecution, and became its state church in the 4th century. Major divisions followed: the East–West Schism of 1054 separated the Latin West from the Greek East, and the 16th-century Reformation divided Western Christianity into Catholic and Protestant traditions.

Usage of the term

"Christian Church" is also the name of specific denominations, notably the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ), a mainline Protestant tradition in North America that emerged from the Stone-Campbell Restoration Movement.
