Esperanza, Agusan del Sur

Esperanza

Municipality of Esperanza

Province: Agusan del Sur

Region: Caraga

Country: Philippines

Founded: September 11, 1953

Esperanza, officially the Municipality of Esperanza, is a municipality in the province of Agusan del Sur, Philippines. It lies along the Agusan River in the northeastern part of Mindanao, within the Caraga administrative region. The town's name is the Spanish word for "hope."

History

Esperanza was created as a municipality in 1953 from territory formerly belonging to the older settlement of the same name along the Agusan River, one of the earliest areas in the Agusan valley visited by Spanish missionaries in the seventeenth century. The area's inhabitants include Manobo peoples who have long lived along the river and its tributaries. The town celebrates its foundation anniversary each September.

Geography

The municipality is traversed by the Agusan River, the third-longest river in the Philippines, which drains the broad Agusan Marsh to the south. Much of the terrain is lowland plain subject to seasonal flooding, interspersed with forested uplands. Esperanza is subdivided into barangays, the smallest administrative units of Philippine local government, including the poblacion (town centre) and riverside settlements.

Economy

The local economy is agricultural, centred on rice, corn, bananas, and oil palm, with river transport and small-scale trade along the highway linking Butuan City with the interior of Agusan del Sur. Timber and rattan were historically important products of the surrounding forests.
