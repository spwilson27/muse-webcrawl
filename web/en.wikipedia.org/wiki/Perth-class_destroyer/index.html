The Perth-class destroyers were three guided missile destroyers operated by the Royal Australian Navy (RAN) from the 1960s into the early 21st century. Modified versions of the United States Navy's Charles F. Adams class, built in Michigan by the Defoe Shipbuilding Company, they were the RAN's first guided-missile-armed warships and its first ships designed for anti-aircraft missile warfare.

Design and armament

The three ships — HMAS Perth, HMAS Hobart and HMAS Brisbane — displaced around 4,700 tons at full load and were armed with a single-arm Mk 13 launcher for Tartar (later Standard) surface-to-air missiles, two 5-inch/54 calibre guns, an Ikara anti-submarine missile system (a distinctly Australian fitting, replacing the ASROC of the American design), and torpedo tubes. In later modernisations their combat systems and missiles were upgraded, including the Naval Combat Data System and, eventually, Harpoon anti-ship missiles.

Service

All three ships served on rotation in the Vietnam War, providing naval gunfire support off the Vietnamese coast between 1967 and 1971; Hobart was damaged in a 1968 friendly-fire incident involving US aircraft. The destroyers later formed the core of the RAN's surface combatant force for three decades, deploying widely through Asia and the Pacific. They were progressively decommissioned between 1999 and 2001 and replaced by upgraded Adelaide-class frigates until the Hobart-class air warfare destroyers entered service. All three were scuttled as dive wrecks off the Australian coast.
