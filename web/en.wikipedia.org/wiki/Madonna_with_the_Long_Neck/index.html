Madonna with the Long Neck

The Madonna with the Long Neck (Italian: Madonna dal Collo Lungo), also known as the Madonna and Child with Angels and St. Jerome, is an oil painting by the Italian Mannerist artist Parmigianino, dated to about 1535–1540. It is one of the most famous works of Mannerism and is held by the Uffizi Gallery in Florence.

Description

The Virgin is depicted seated, unusually elongated and elegant, holding the Christ Child, who sleeps stretched across her lap in a pose reminiscent of the Pietà. On the left, a row of angels crowding around an urn presents a startling perspective, while on the right the unfinished figure of St. Jerome stands beside a distant column that dwarfs him — a column without a capital that art historians read as a reference to the Immaculate Conception.

History

The painting was commissioned in 1534 for the funerary chapel of Elena Baiardi in the church of Santa Maria dei Servi in Parma. Parmigianino worked on it for years without finishing it; the painting remained incomplete at his death in 1540.

Interpretation

The deliberate distortions of proportion — the Virgin's long neck and fingers, the child's size — exemplify the Mannerist preference for grace and artifice over naturalistic accuracy, and the picture has been admired for its cool, refined strangeness.
