Fournier gangrene

Fournier gangrene is an acute, rapidly progressive necrotizing fasciitis of the perineum and external genitalia. It is a polymicrobial infection in which aerobic and anaerobic bacteria act synergistically to destroy soft tissue along fascial planes. It is named after Jean Alfred Fournier, the French venereologist who described five cases in 1883.

Presentation

The condition usually begins with pain, swelling, and redness of the scrotum or vulva, accompanied by fever and systemic toxicity. Subcutaneous gas (crepitus), dusky discoloration, and foul discharge develop as tissue dies. Progression can be measured in hours, and the infection may track along the abdominal wall and thighs. Risk factors include diabetes mellitus, alcohol use disorder, immunosuppression, perianal or urinary tract infection, and local trauma; a small number of cases have been reported in association with SGLT2 inhibitor drugs.

Treatment

Fournier gangrene is a surgical emergency. Treatment requires immediate resuscitation, broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics, and urgent, often repeated, surgical debridement of all necrotic tissue, sometimes with urinary or fecal diversion and later reconstructive surgery. Even with modern care, reported mortality ranges roughly from 20% to 40%, higher when diagnosis is delayed.
