Ganache


Ganache is a glaze, icing, or filling made from chocolate and cream.

Ganache (/ɡəˈnɑːʃ/) is a glaze, icing, sauce, or filling for pastries, made from chocolate and cream. It is a staple of French pâtisserie, said to have been created in Paris in the mid-19th century — legend holds that an apprentice who accidentally spilled hot cream into chocolate was called a ganache ("fool" or "jowl") by his master before the mixture was found to be delicious.

Preparation and proportions

Ganache is normally made by heating cream and pouring it over chopped chocolate, then stirring until smooth; butter is sometimes added for shine. The ratio of chocolate to cream determines the consistency: equal parts yields a pourable glaze or sauce; a higher proportion of chocolate (2:1 or more) gives a firm ganache suitable for rolling into chocolate truffles or piping; more cream produces a soft filling, and whipped chilled ganache becomes a light mousse-like frosting. Flavorings such as liqueurs, coffee, or fruit purées may be added.

Uses

Ganache is used as a glaze for cakes and éclairs (often poured as a mirror-smooth coating), a filling for cakes, tarts, and bonbons, the center of truffles, and a frosting when whipped. White and milk chocolate versions exist with adjusted ratios. See also icing and chocolate.
