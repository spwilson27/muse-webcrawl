The Kargil War, also known as the Kargil conflict, was an armed conflict fought between India and Pakistan from May to July 1999 in the Kargil district of Ladakh, along the Line of Control (LoC) in Kashmir. Pakistani soldiers of the Northern Light Infantry, together with Kashmiri militants, infiltrated across the LoC during the winter and occupied strategic high-altitude positions vacated seasonally by Indian forces, overlooking the vital Srinagar–Leh highway (NH 1).

Course of the conflict

The intrusion was detected in early May 1999 after a local shepherd reported movement in the Batalik sector. India responded with Operation Vijay, mobilising some 30,000 troops, while the Indian Air Force mounted air strikes under Operation Safed Sagar and the navy mobilised in the Arabian Sea. Fighting at altitudes above 5,000 metres (16,000 ft) — among the highest battlefields ever contested — centred on features such as Tololing, Tiger Hill and Point 4875 near Dras and Kargil. India progressively recaptured the heights, and under intense international pressure, particularly from United States President Bill Clinton at his 4 July meeting with Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif in Washington, Pakistan withdrew its remaining forces. India declared the operation complete on 26 July 1999, a date now commemorated as Kargil Vijay Diwas.

Casualties and aftermath

India officially reported 527 soldiers killed; Pakistani casualties are estimated at between several hundred and around 700, though figures remain disputed. The war was the first direct conventional conflict between two nuclear-armed states, both having tested nuclear weapons in 1998, and it drew worldwide concern over escalation. In Pakistan the debacle contributed to the October 1999 coup in which army chief Pervez Musharraf — widely identified as the operation's architect — overthrew Sharif's government. In India, the Kargil Review Committee led to sweeping reforms of intelligence and defence management.
