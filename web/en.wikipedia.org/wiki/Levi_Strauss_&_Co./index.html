Levi Strauss & Co.

Levi Strauss & Co. is an American clothing company known worldwide for its Levi's brand of denim jeans. It was founded in May 1853 when German-Jewish immigrant Levi Strauss moved from Buttenheim, Bavaria, to San Francisco, California, to open a West Coast branch of his brothers' New York dry goods business.

The company's corporate headquarters is located in Levi's Plaza in San Francisco. In 1873, Levi Strauss and tailor Jacob Davis patented the process of putting rivets in pants for strength, giving birth to the blue jean.

History

Strauss arrived in San Francisco in 1853 during the California Gold Rush and established a wholesale dry goods business. Davis, a customer, proposed riveted work pants for miners; the patent was granted on 20 May 1873. The company grew through the 20th century, popularising jeans globally, and went public in 1971, private again in 1985 in a leveraged buyout, and public again in 2019.

Brands

Its brands include Levi's, Dockers and Denizen. Levi's 501 jeans remain its most iconic product.
