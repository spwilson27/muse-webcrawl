Lorestan province

Lorestan province (Persian: استان لرستان) is a province of Iran situated in the Zagros Mountains of the country's west. Its capital is Khorramabad. The province, home to the Lur people, covers approximately 28,294 km² and counted about 1.76 million inhabitants in the 2016 census.

Overview

The terrain is dominated by parallel Zagros ridges separated by fertile intermontane plains, irrigated by the Kashkan and Seymareh rivers. Winters are cold in the north and east, while the south is warmer. Rainfall is comparatively high by Iranian standards, supporting dry farming of wheat and barley, orchards and oak woodlands.

History

Archaeology in Lorestan is best known for the Luristan bronzes of the Iron Age. The region corresponds in part to ancient Ellipi and was successively influenced by Elam, Media, the Achaemenids and the Sasanians. In the Islamic period the Luri atabegs governed the area, and the province took shape in its modern form under the Pahlavi monarchy in the twentieth century.

Administration

The province is divided into counties (shahrestan) including Khorramabad, Borujerd, Dorud, Aligudarz, Delfan, Selseleh, Kuhdasht, Pol-e Dokhtar, Chegeni, Rumeshkan and Azna. Khorramabad, watched over by the Falak-ol-Aflak fortress, is the administrative and commercial centre.

People

Most inhabitants are Lurs speaking Northern Luri; Laki speakers live in the north-east. Traditional transhumant pastoralism persists among some communities, and Luri music and dress remain prominent in provincial life.
