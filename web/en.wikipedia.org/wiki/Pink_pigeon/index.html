The pink pigeon (Nesoenas mayeri), endemic to Mauritius.

The pink pigeon (Nesoenas mayeri) is a species of pigeon in the family Columbidae, endemic to Mauritius. It is named for its pale pink head, neck and underparts, contrasting with brown wings and a broad rusty-red tail. Adults have dark red skin around the eyes and red feet.

Decline

Once widespread in the forests of Mauritius, the species was devastated by deforestation, introduced predators such as mongooses, macaques and rats, and disease. By 1991 only about ten individuals survived in the wild, making it one of the rarest birds in the world.

Recovery

A captive-breeding and reintroduction programme led by the Durrell Wildlife Conservation Trust and the Mauritian Wildlife Foundation — modelled on the earlier rescue of the Mauritius kestrel — bred birds at the Black River aviaries and released them into protected native forest, including on the offshore reserve of Île aux Aigrettes, with supplementary feeding and predator control. The wild population climbed to several hundred birds, and in 2018 the IUCN downlisted the pink pigeon from Endangered to Vulnerable, a rare conservation success story for an island bird. Inbreeding and disease remain concerns for its long-term survival.
