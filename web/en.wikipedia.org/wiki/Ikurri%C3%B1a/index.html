Ikurriña

The Ikurriña is the flag of the Basque Country, an autonomous community of northern Spain. It consists of a red field with a white Nordic-style cross (saltire-cross combination) and a green saltire superimposed. The name means simply "the flag" in Basque (from ikur, "symbol" or "sign").

History

The flag was designed in 1894 by the brothers Luis and Sabino Arana, the founder of the Basque Nationalist Party (PNV). It was originally intended as the flag of Biscay province, but it came to symbolise the Basque Country as a whole. The red field represents the Basque people, the green saltire the oak tree of Gernika (symbol of Basque liberties), and the white cross Christianity and the purity of law.

Legal status

The Ikurriña was adopted by the first Basque government in 1936, banned under the Franco regime, and legalised again during Spain's transition to democracy. It was officially recognised as the flag of the Basque Autonomous Community in the Statute of Autonomy of 1979. The flag is also commonly flown in the French Basque Country and by the Basque diaspora.
