A Baker's cyst, also known as a popliteal cyst, is a benign swelling of the semimembranosus bursa or, more generally, a fluid-filled distension at the back of the knee. It is named after the British surgeon William Morrant Baker, who first described it in the 19th century.

Signs and symptoms

Many Baker's cysts cause no symptoms and are found incidentally. When symptomatic, they produce a feeling of fullness or a bulge behind the knee, stiffness, and sometimes pain, particularly when the knee is fully bent or extended. A cyst may rupture, causing sudden calf pain and swelling that can mimic deep vein thrombosis.

Causes and diagnosis

In adults the cyst usually communicates with the knee joint and is associated with intra-articular pathology such as osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, or meniscal tears, which produce excess joint fluid. In children, cysts typically occur without joint disease. Diagnosis is usually clinical and can be confirmed by ultrasound or MRI.

Treatment

Treatment often focuses on the underlying knee condition. Options include rest, physiotherapy, anti-inflammatory medication, aspiration, and corticosteroid injection; surgical excision is reserved for persistent cases.
