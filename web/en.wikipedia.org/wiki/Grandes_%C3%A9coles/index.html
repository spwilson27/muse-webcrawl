Grandes écoles

The grandes écoles are a distinctive group of elite higher-education institutions in France, outside the main framework of the public university system. They are highly selective, admit students through competitive examinations (concours) typically taken after two years of preparatory classes (classes préparatoires), and train many of France's senior civil servants, engineers, executives, and researchers.

History

Many grandes écoles were founded in the 18th and 19th centuries to supply the state with technical and administrative elites: the École Polytechnique (1794), the École Normale Supérieure (1794), and the École des Ponts et Chaussées (1747) are among the oldest. Others, such as HEC Paris (1881) and Sciences Po (1872), focus on business and political science.

Notable institutions

Prominent grandes écoles include École Polytechnique, ENS Ulm, HEC Paris, ESSEC, Sciences Po, CentraleSupélec, Mines Paris, and the now-defunct École nationale d'administration (ENA), replaced in 2022 by the Institut national du service public (INSP), which trained many French presidents and prime ministers.

Criticism

The system is often praised for academic rigour but criticized for social reproduction and elitism, as admission via preparatory classes favours students from privileged backgrounds.
