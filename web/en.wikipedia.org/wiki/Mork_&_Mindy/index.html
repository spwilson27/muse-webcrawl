Mork & Mindy is an American television sitcom that aired on ABC from September 14, 1978, to May 27, 1982. A spin-off of Happy Days, the series stars Robin Williams as Mork, an extraterrestrial from the planet Ork sent to Earth to study human behavior, and Pam Dawber as Mindy McConnell, the young woman in Boulder, Colorado, who takes him in. The show was a breakthrough role for Williams, whose rapid-fire improvisational comedy became its defining feature.

Premise

Mork arrives on Earth in an egg-shaped spacecraft and is befriended by Mindy McConnell, who lets him live in her attic apartment above her father's music store. Unfamiliar with human customs, Mork misinterprets everyday behavior with literal-minded absurdity, and at the end of each episode he reports telepathically to Orson, his unseen superior on Ork, summarizing a gentle moral lesson about human life. Mork's Orkan customs include drinking through his finger, sitting on his head, and greeting people with the handshake and phrase "Na-Nu Na-Nu." His exclamations, such as "Shazbot!", entered popular slang.

Development

The character of Mork first appeared in a 1978 episode of Happy Days, "My Favorite Orkan," conceived after producer Garry Marshall's young son asked for a character from outer space. The episode was shot with an unknown stand-up comic, Robin Williams, whose improvised performance so impressed executives and audiences that ABC built a series around him within months. The Happy Days appearance was later retconned as a dream, while Mork & Mindy was set in the present day in Boulder.

Reception and changes

The first season was a sensation, ranking in the top three of the Nielsen ratings and making Williams a star; he won the Golden Globe Award for Best Actor in a Television Series – Musical or Comedy in 1979. In subsequent seasons the network repeatedly retooled the show — changing the supporting cast, the setting, and the time slot — and ratings fell sharply. In the fourth season Mork and Mindy married and hatched a son, Mearth (played by veteran comedian Jonathan Winters), who, because Orkans age in reverse, was born as an elderly man and grew younger. The series was cancelled in 1982 after 95 episodes.

Legacy

The show launched Robin Williams's screen career and remains a fondly remembered example of 1970s American sitcoms. Williams's largely improvised performance, with scripts reportedly leaving gaps marked for his ad-libs, set a pattern followed by many later star-driven comedies.
