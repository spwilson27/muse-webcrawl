CouchSurfing is a hospitality exchange service accessible via a website and mobile app. Members use the service to arrange homestays — sleeping on a host's couch, spare bed or floor — lodging, or to meet other travellers, traditionally without monetary exchange. The platform relies on profiles and a reference system in which hosts and guests review one another after a stay.

History

The concept originated in 1999 when American programmer Casey Fenton, planning a trip to Iceland, emailed students at the University of Iceland asking for a place to stay and received numerous offers. CouchSurfing International launched as a website in 2004, growing out of earlier hospitality networks such as Servas International and Hospitality Club. It operated as a non-profit for several years; a bid for US 501(c)(3) status was denied, and in 2011 the organisation converted to a for-profit benefit corporation, raising venture capital — a change that drew criticism from some members.

Paywall and later developments

In May 2020, citing financial pressure from the COVID-19 pandemic, CouchSurfing introduced a mandatory membership contribution for users in most countries, effectively ending free access for new and existing members who did not pay, which prompted significant backlash and migration of some users to non-profit alternatives such as BeWelcome, Trustroots and Couchers.

Safety

Trust mechanisms include references, vouching and optional identity verification. News reports have documented rare serious crimes involving stays arranged through the platform, and the service advises members to read references carefully and agree meeting details in advance.
