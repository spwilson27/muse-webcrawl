The Fête de la Musique, also known in English as Music Day, Make Music Day, or World Music Day, is an annual music celebration that takes place on 21 June, the day of the summer solstice in the Northern Hemisphere. On Music Day, citizens and residents are urged to play music outside in their neighbourhoods or in public spaces and parks. Free concerts are also organized, where musicians play for fun and not for payment.

History

The idea was first developed in the 1970s by the American musician Joel Cohen, who worked for France Musique radio in Paris, and was taken up by Jack Lang, France's Minister of Culture, together with the composer and music journalist Maurice Fleuret, Director of Music and Dance at the ministry. The first Fête de la Musique took place in Paris on 21 June 1982. Its founding slogan was "Faites de la musique" ("Make music"), a homophone of the festival's name.

The date was chosen deliberately: 21 June is the longest day of the year in the Northern Hemisphere, and the festival encourages performances to continue late into the evening.

International spread

Beginning in 1985, designated the European Year of Music, the festival was exported abroad. A charter signed in Budapest in 1997 committed participating cities to the principles of free, open-air performance by amateurs and professionals alike. The Fête de la Musique is now celebrated in well over a hundred countries and in hundreds of cities worldwide, including large celebrations in New York, London, Berlin, and across francophone Africa and Latin America.

Principles

The festival rests on a few simple rules: all concerts are free to the public, performers donate their time, and music of every genre — classical, jazz, rock, electronic, traditional — is welcomed in streets, squares, courtyards, gardens, and stations. Amateur participation is at the heart of the event, which aims to put musical practice within everyone's reach.
