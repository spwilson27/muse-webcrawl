Thibaw Min (1 January 1859 – 19 December 1916) was the last king of Burma and the final monarch of the Konbaung dynasty. His reign ended with the Third Anglo-Burmese War in 1885, after which Burma was annexed into British India and the monarchy abolished.

Reign

Thibaw succeeded his father, Mindon Min, in 1878. His accession, engineered in part by the queen dowager Hsinbyumashin, was accompanied by a massacre of royal princes and princesses who might contest the throne — an event that badly damaged the kingdom's standing abroad. Inexperienced and dominated by his formidable queen, Supayalat, Thibaw attempted to counter British influence by cultivating ties with France, a policy that alarmed the government of India.

Fall and exile

A dispute over a fine imposed on the Bombay Burmah Trading Corporation provided the pretext for the British ultimatum of 1885. When Thibaw refused its terms, British forces advanced up the Irrawaddy and occupied Mandalay almost without fighting. Thibaw surrendered on 29 November 1885 and was exiled with his family to Ratnagiri, on the west coast of India, where he lived on a British pension until his death in 1916. His palace at Ratnagiri, Thibaw Palace, still stands.
