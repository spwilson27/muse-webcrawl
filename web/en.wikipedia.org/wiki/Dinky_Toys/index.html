Dinky Toys

Dinky Toys was a brand of die-cast metal toys, mainly miniature vehicles, aircraft and military models, first produced by Hornby in Liverpool in 1934 as accessories for its O-gauge model railways under the name "Modelled Miniatures", and renamed Dinky Toys later that year. The range grew to hundreds of models and became the world leader in die-cast model vehicles until the 1960s, when it faced competition from its own stablemate — and later owner — Corgi Toys, and from Mattel's Hot Wheels.

History

The first Dinky models — a train set's worth of trackside figures, passengers and vehicles, and a series of cars, vans and aircraft — were made in the Meccano Ltd factory at Binns Road, Liverpool, from lead alloy (later zamak). Production stopped during the Second World War and resumed in 1946; a French factory at Bobigny, run by Meccano France, produced its own range from 1934. Post-war "Supertoys" (1947) were larger-scale models. In Britain the declining Hornby/Meccano group was taken over by Lines Bros (Tri-ang) in 1964; Dinky was sold to Airfix in 1979 and the Liverpool factory closed, ending UK production. The French range had passed to Mattel in the 1970s. The brand has since been revived several times — by Mattel and by DeAgostini/Atlas for partwork collectors' series — and original mint boxed models are highly collectible.

Legacy

Dinky Toys established conventions still used by model makers — standard scales, window glazing (introduced on the 1956 range), jewelled headlights and detailed liveries — and remains one of the most collected of all toy brands.
