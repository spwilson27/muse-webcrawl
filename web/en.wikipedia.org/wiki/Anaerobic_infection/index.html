Anaerobic infection

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anaerobic culture equipment used to identify oxygen-intolerant pathogens.

Anaerobic infections are infections caused by anaerobic bacteria — organisms that do not require oxygen for growth and may die in its presence. These bacteria are normal inhabitants of the skin, mouth, gastrointestinal tract, and female genital tract, and they cause disease when mucosal barriers are broken and they invade normally sterile tissues, often in mixed infections with aerobic bacteria.

Common organisms and syndromes

Important anaerobic pathogens include the Bacteroides fragilis group (intra-abdominal abscesses, peritonitis), Clostridium species (gas gangrene, tetanus, botulism, pseudomembranous colitis), Prevotella and Fusobacterium (oral, head and neck, and aspiration infections), and anaerobic cocci. Characteristic syndromes include brain and lung abscesses, aspiration pneumonia, pelvic inflammatory disease, diabetic foot infections, and necrotizing soft-tissue infections.

Diagnosis and treatment

Foul-smelling discharge, tissue necrosis, gas in tissues, and proximity to a mucosal surface are clinical clues. Specimens must be transported and cultured without oxygen exposure, which makes laboratory confirmation technically demanding. Treatment rests on surgical drainage and debridement plus antibiotics such as metronidazole, beta-lactam/beta-lactamase inhibitor combinations, or carbapenems; B. fragilis is typically resistant to penicillin alone.
