Telemarketing

Telemarketing is a method of direct marketing in which a salesperson solicits prospective customers to buy products or services, either over the telephone or through a subsequent face-to-face or web conferencing appointment. Telemarketing can also include recorded sales pitches programmed to be played over the phone via automatic dialing.

Categories

Telemarketing falls into two broad categories: business-to-business (B2B) and business-to-consumer (B2C). Outbound calls are initiated by the seller, while inbound calls come from customers responding to advertising or direct mail.

Regulation

Many jurisdictions regulate telemarketing because of consumer complaints about unsolicited calls. In the United States, the Federal Trade Commission's Telemarketing Sales Rule and the National Do Not Call Registry restrict calls to consumers who opt out. Similar regimes exist in Canada, the United Kingdom and Australia. Robocalls and caller-ID spoofing have prompted further enforcement.

Criticism

Telemarketing is often criticized for intrusiveness and has been associated with fraudulent "boiler room" schemes targeting vulnerable people.
