Artificial leather

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Rolls of synthetic leather

Artificial leather, also called synthetic leather, is a material intended to substitute for leather in upholstery, clothing, footwear, and other uses where a leather-like finish is desired but the actual material is cost-prohibitive or unsuitable for ethical reasons. Artificial leather is known under many names, including leatherette, faux leather, vegan leather, and pleather.

Manufacture

Most artificial leathers consist of a fabric backing coated with a plastic layer, typically polyvinyl chloride (PVC) or polyurethane (PU), embossed with a leather-like grain. Newer materials include microfiber-based synthetics such as Ultrasuede and plant- or fungi-derived alternatives based on pineapple leaf fibre (Piñatex), cactus, or mycelium.

Uses

Artificial leather is used extensively in automotive interiors, furniture upholstery, clothing, shoes, and accessories. Advantages include lower cost, ease of cleaning, and avoidance of animal products; disadvantages include lesser durability and breathability compared with genuine leather, and environmental concerns over plastic production and disposal.
