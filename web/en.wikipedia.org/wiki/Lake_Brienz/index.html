Lake Brienz (German: Brienzersee) is a lake just north of the Alps in the canton of Bern in Switzerland. It lies between the village of Brienz to the east and the town of Interlaken to the west, and is renowned for its striking turquoise colour, caused by fine glacial rock flour suspended in the water.

Geography

The lake is about 14 kilometres long and up to 2.8 kilometres wide, with a maximum depth of 260 metres. It is fed principally by the river Aare at its eastern end and by the Lütschine, which flows into it near Bönigen. The Aare leaves the lake at Interlaken and flows a short distance into neighbouring Lake Thun. Lake Brienz has no islands, and its steep shores leave little room for settlement; villages on its shores include Iseltwald, Giessbach (site of the Giessbach Falls), Ringgenberg, Niederried, and Oberried.

Transport and tourism

Passenger boats have operated on the lake since 1839; today services are run by BLS Schifffahrt, connecting Interlaken Ost with Brienz, and the historic paddle steamer Lötschberg (built 1914) still sails the lake. The Brünig railway line runs along the northern shore. The lake and its surroundings are popular for hiking, swimming, and sightseeing, and the village of Iseltwald gained international attention as a filming location for television dramas.

Ecology

Because the lake is deep, cold, and nutrient-poor, its fish stocks are modest, though it supports species such as perch, pike, and lake trout. The low nutrient levels contribute to its celebrated clarity and colour.
