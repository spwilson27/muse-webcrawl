Karl Gustav Jöreskog

Karl Gustav Jöreskog (born 25 April 1935) is a Swedish statistician best known for his pioneering contributions to structural equation modeling. He is professor emeritus at Uppsala University and is one of the developers of the LISREL software, the first widely used program for estimating structural equation models with latent variables.

Life and career

Jöreskog was born in Åmål, in Dalsland, Sweden. He studied at Uppsala University, where he earned his doctorate in 1968 with a thesis on factor analysis. His early work concerned methods for maximum likelihood estimation in factor models, work that laid much of the foundation for modern confirmatory factor analysis.

For many years he worked at the Educational Testing Service (ETS) in Princeton, New Jersey, where, together with Dag Sörbom, he developed LISREL (Linear Structural Relations), a computer program that made it practical for social scientists to estimate complex models involving multiple latent variables and measurement error. LISREL became the standard tool of an entire generation of researchers in psychology, sociology, education and marketing.

Jöreskog returned to Uppsala University, where he became professor of statistics and continued to develop methods for multivariate analysis, including the SIMPLIS command language designed to make LISREL models easier to specify. He is a member of the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences and a foreign honorary member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.

Contributions

Jöreskog's central insight was to unify factor analysis, path analysis and simultaneous equation models into a single general framework. He developed efficient algorithms for maximum likelihood estimation of covariance structure models and for the analysis of ordinal variables, and he co-authored influential textbooks including Advanced Structural Equation Modeling and the LISREL user guides.
