Mumtaz Mahal (born Arjumand Banu Begum; 27 April 1593 – 17 June 1631) was the empress consort of the Mughal Empire as the wife of Emperor Shah Jahan. The Taj Mahal in Agra, one of the most famous buildings in the world, was commissioned by Shah Jahan as her tomb. Her name, given by her husband, means "the exalted one of the palace" in Persian.

Life

Arjumand Banu was the daughter of the Persian nobleman Asaf Khan and the niece of Empress Nur Jahan. Tradition relates that the teenage Prince Khurram (the future Shah Jahan) first saw her selling silks and glass beads in Agra's Meena Bazaar in 1607; they were betrothed that year and married on 10 May 1612. Though Shah Jahan had other wives, Mumtaz was by all contemporary accounts his beloved companion and a trusted political adviser: she accompanied him on campaigns, held his imperial seal, and interceded for the poor and for prisoners condemned to death.

The couple had fourteen children, seven of whom survived to adulthood, including the heir-apparent Dara Shikoh, the princess Jahanara, and the future emperor Aurangzeb. Mumtaz died in Burhanpur on 17 June 1631 from postpartum haemorrhage after delivering her fourteenth child, while accompanying Shah Jahan on a campaign in the Deccan.

The Taj Mahal

Grief-stricken, Shah Jahan commissioned a mausoleum on the bank of the Yamuna at Agra; construction ran from about 1632 to 1653, employing some 20,000 artisans. Mumtaz's remains, first buried at Burhanpur, were moved to Agra and finally laid in the Taj's central chamber, beneath a cenotaph of white marble inlaid with precious stones; Shah Jahan himself was buried beside her after his death in 1666. A popular legend that Shah Jahan promised Mumtaz on her deathbed to build her the richest mausoleum ever known is repeated in many later accounts.

Legacy

Beyond the Taj, her memory is preserved in place names and in the Mumtaz Mahal pavilion of Agra Fort, where Shah Jahan was later imprisoned. Poets at court composed elegies in her name, and her UNESCO-listed tomb remains the defining monument of Mughal architecture.
