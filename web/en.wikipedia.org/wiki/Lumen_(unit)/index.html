The lumen (symbol: lm) is the SI unit of luminous flux, which measures the total quantity of visible light emitted by a source per unit of time, weighted by the luminosity function to reflect the sensitivity of the human eye to different wavelengths. One lumen is defined as the luminous flux of light produced by a light source that emits one candela of luminous intensity over a solid angle of one steradian: 1 lm = 1 cd⋅sr.

Definition and relations

Luminous flux differs from radiant flux (measured in watts) in that it accounts for the varying sensitivity of the eye. For monochromatic light at 555 nm, the wavelength of maximum sensitivity, one watt of radiant flux corresponds to 683 lumens. Related quantities include the lux (lumens per square metre, for illuminance) and the candela (lumens per steradian, for luminous intensity).

Typical values

  • Candle flame: about 13 lm
  • 60 W incandescent bulb: roughly 800 lm
  • Typical LED replacement bulb: 800–1,100 lm
  • ANSI lumens are used to rate video projectors, from a few hundred for portable units to tens of thousands for cinema projectors.

Lamp labelling

Since the phase-out of incandescent lamps, regulators in the EU and US have required light bulbs to be labelled by luminous output in lumens rather than electrical power in watts, encouraging consumers to compare "brightness per watt" (luminous efficacy).
