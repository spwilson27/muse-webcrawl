Mabo decision

The Mabo decision, formally Mabo v Queensland (No 2) [1992] HCA 23; (1992) 175 CLR 1, is a landmark decision of the High Court of Australia, delivered on 3 June 1992, which recognised that the Meriam people of the Murray Islands in the Torres Strait held native title to their traditional lands. In doing so, the Court rejected the doctrine of terra nullius — the notion that Australia had belonged to no one at the time of British colonisation — as it applied to the common law of Australia.

Background

Eddie Koiki Mabo, a Meriam man from Mer (Murray Island), began legal proceedings in 1982, together with fellow plaintiffs including David Passi and James Rice, seeking a declaration that the Meriam people owned their island lands under their traditional laws and customs. The claim was preceded by the earlier, unsuccessful Milirrpum v Nabalco (1971) decision concerning the Yolngu people of the Gove Peninsula, in which Justice Blackburn had held that communal native title was not recognisable in Australian law.

Queensland attempted to defeat the claim with the Queensland Coast Islands Declaratory Act 1985, which purported to extinguish any rights retrospectively. In Mabo v Queensland (No 1) (1988), the High Court held that Act inconsistent with the Commonwealth Racial Discrimination Act 1975 and therefore invalid, allowing the case to proceed.

The decision

By a 6–1 majority, the Court held that the common law of Australia recognises a form of native title which, in the cases where it has not been extinguished, reflects the entitlement of indigenous inhabitants, in accordance with their laws or customs, to their traditional lands. Justice Brennan's leading judgment reasoned that terra nullius had been extended beyond its proper scope: although the Crown acquired sovereignty over Australia, it did not thereby acquire absolute beneficial ownership of all land.

The Court held that native title is extinguished by valid government acts inconsistent with its continued enjoyment, such as the grant of freehold estates, but that mere regulation does not necessarily extinguish it.

Aftermath and significance

The decision overturned the long-standing assumption that Australia was legally "uninhabited" in 1788. It led the Keating Government to enact the Native Title Act 1993, which established a national framework for native title claims and determinations, and to the later Wik Peoples v Queensland (1996) decision on the coexistence of native title with pastoral leases. The anniversary of the decision, 3 June, is commemorated as Mabo Day.
