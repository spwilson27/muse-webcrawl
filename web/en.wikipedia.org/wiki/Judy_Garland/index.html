Judy Garland (born Frances Ethel Gumm; June 10, 1922 – June 22, 1969) was an American actress and singer. Renowned for her powerful contralto voice and emotional delivery, she attained international stardom as a teenager playing Dorothy Gale in The Wizard of Oz (1939), and went on to a career spanning more than four decades across film, concert stages, recordings, and television.

Early life and MGM years

Born into a vaudeville family in Grand Rapids, Minnesota, Garland performed from age two with her sisters as the Gumm Sisters. Signed by Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer in 1935, she made a series of musicals with Mickey Rooney, including the Andy Hardy films and Babes in Arms (1939). As Dorothy, singing "Over the Rainbow," she became a permanent part of film history and received an Academy Juvenile Award. Her 1940s MGM highlights included Meet Me in St. Louis (1944) — directed by Vincente Minnelli, whom she married — The Harvey Girls, Easter Parade (1948) with Fred Astaire, and Summer Stock (1950). Plagued by unhappiness over studio control of her weight and schedule, and by dependence on prescription medication begun in childhood, she was released from MGM in 1950.

Comeback and later career

Garland reinvented herself as a concert performer, triumphing at the London Palladium and New York's Palace Theatre in 1951–52. She returned to film in Warner Bros.' A Star Is Born (1954), earning an Academy Award nomination for Best Actress, and later received another nomination for Judgment at Nuremberg (1961). Her 1961 Carnegie Hall concert — released as Judy at Carnegie Hall — won Grammy Awards and is often called "the greatest night in show business history." She hosted the Emmy-nominated Judy Garland Show on CBS in 1963–64.

Personal struggles and death

Garland's adult life was marked by financial troubles, five marriages, and struggles with addiction and mental health. She died in London on June 22, 1969, of an accidental barbiturate overdose, twelve days after her 47th birthday. Her daughters Liza Minnelli and Lorna Luft both became performers. In 1997 Garland was posthumously awarded the Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award, and the American Film Institute ranked her among the greatest female stars of classic Hollywood cinema.

Legacy

Garland remains an icon — her rendition of "Over the Rainbow" was voted the greatest movie song of all time by the AFI, and she became a beloved figure in gay culture. The biopic Judy (2019), starring Renée Zellweger, dramatized her final months and won Zellweger the Academy Award for Best Actress.
