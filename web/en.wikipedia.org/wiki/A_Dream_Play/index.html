Original titleEtt drömspel
Written1901 (with prologue added later)

A Dream Play (Swedish: Ett drömspel) is a play by the Swedish playwright August Strindberg, written in 1901 and first performed in 1907. It is regarded as one of the founding works of modernist theatre, anticipating expressionism and surrealism in its dreamlike structure.

Plot and structure

In a preface, Strindberg wrote that he had sought to imitate "the disjointed yet seemingly logical form of a dream", in which characters "split, double, multiply, evaporate, condense, disperse, assemble". The protagonist is Agnes, daughter of the Hindu god Indra, who descends to Earth to learn why human beings complain so much. Moving through fluid, shifting scenes — the Growing Castle, the Officer forever waiting for his beloved Victoria, the Lawyer who wears the sufferings of his clients, Fingal's Cave, and the burning castle of the ending — Agnes repeats the refrain "Human beings are to be pitied" (Det är synd om människorna).

Influence

Notoriously difficult to stage, the play has attracted major directors; Ingmar Bergman staged it several times, and Robert Wilson and Caryl Churchill produced notable versions. Its free associative form influenced later experimental theatre and is often paired with Strindberg's To Damascus and The Ghost Sonata among his late "chamber" and dream works.
