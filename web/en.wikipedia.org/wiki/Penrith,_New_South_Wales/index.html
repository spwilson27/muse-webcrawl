Penrith is a city in New South Wales, Australia, about 50 kilometres west of the Sydney central business district, at the foot of the Blue Mountains. It is the administrative centre of the City of Penrith local government area.

Geography and history

Penrith lies on the banks of the Nepean River on the Cumberland Plain. The area is part of the traditional lands of the Darug people. European settlement followed the construction of roads west from Sydney in the early nineteenth century, and Penrith grew as a service town for travellers crossing the Blue Mountains. It was proclaimed a city in 1971 as Sydney's urban fringe expanded westward.

Sport and attractions

Penrith is home to the Penrith Panthers rugby league club, multiple National Rugby League premiership winners, and to the Sydney International Regatta Centre and Penrith Whitewater Stadium, built for the Sydney 2000 Olympic Games on former quarry lakes. The Nepean River, the Museum of Fire and the nearby Blue Mountains attract visitors year-round; Penrith is also noted for some of Sydney's highest summer temperatures.
