The Leica M is a rangefinder camera system introduced by Ernst Leitz in 1954 with the Leica M3. The "M" stands for Messsucher, German for rangefinder (literally "measuring viewfinder"). For seventy years the M series has been the flagship of the Leica system, famed for its quiet cloth shutter, bright-line viewfinder, compact fast prime lenses, and association with documentary and street photography.

Development

The M3 unified the viewfinder and rangefinder in a single bright window with automatically projected frame lines for 50, 90, and 135mm lenses, and replaced the earlier screw-thread lens mount with a quick-change bayonet. Successors added frame lines for wider lenses and through-the-lens metering: the M5 (1971) was the first M with a built-in meter, the M6 (1984) introduced popular LED metering in a fully mechanical body, and the M7 (2002) added aperture-priority autoexposure. Film production continued with the M-A (Typ 240's mechanical sibling) alongside the digital line.

Digital M cameras

The digital era began with the M8 (2006), which used an APS-H sensor, followed by the full-frame M9 (2009), the first full-frame digital rangefinder, and the monochrome-only Monochrom models. Later bodies — the M (Typ 240), M10, M11 — added live view, higher-resolution full-frame sensors, and Wi-Fi while retaining the classic rangefinder mechanism and full compatibility with M lenses dating to 1954.

Lenses and legacy

M-mount lenses such as the Summilux, Summicron, and Noctilux families are renowned for optical quality and compactness. The M system's longevity — virtually every M lens ever made works on every M body — is unique in the camera industry and underpins the cameras' status as long-term professional tools and collectibles.
