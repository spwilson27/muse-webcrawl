Halley's Comet, officially designated 1P/Halley, is a short-period comet visible from Earth every 75–76 years. It is the only known short-period comet regularly visible to the naked eye and can appear twice in a lifetime. It last appeared in the inner Solar System in 1986 and will next return in 2061.

History

Records of the comet date back to at least 240 BC. In 1705 Edmond Halley, applying Newton's laws of motion, recognised that the comets of 1531, 1607 and 1682 were the same object and correctly predicted its return in 1758. The comet appears in the Bayeux Tapestry, which depicts the Norman Conquest of 1066.

1986 apparition

During its 1986 return, Halley's Comet became the first comet to be observed in detail by spacecraft: the European Giotto probe and the Soviet Vega missions (the "Halley Armada") photographed its nucleus, revealing a dark, irregular body about 15 km long.
