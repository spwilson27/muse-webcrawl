OSCAR 1

OSCAR 1 (Orbiting Satellite Carrying Amateur Radio 1) was the first amateur radio satellite, launched on 12 December 1961, barely four years after Sputnik 1. Built by a group of amateur radio operators in California organised as Project OSCAR, it rode into orbit as a secondary payload aboard the Discoverer 36 satellite launch from Vandenberg Air Force Base.


Launch: 12 December 1961
Launch site: Vandenberg AFB, California
Orbit: Low Earth orbit, ~245 × 474 km
Operator: Project OSCAR / amateur radio community

Mission

The satellite, a small 10-pound box, carried a battery-powered beacon transmitter operating on 144.983 MHz in the two-metre amateur band. It transmitted the letters "HI" in Morse code at a rate that varied with the satellite's internal temperature, allowing listeners to infer thermal conditions on board. More than 570 amateur operators in 28 countries reported receiving its signals during its 22 days of operation, until its batteries failed on 3 January 1962. The satellite re-entered the atmosphere on 31 January 1962.

Legacy

OSCAR 1 demonstrated that the amateur community could build and fly space hardware, beginning a programme that has produced more than a hundred OSCAR-designated satellites and is coordinated today by AMSAT organisations worldwide. OSCAR 2 followed in June 1962, and OSCAR 3 in 1965 carried the first amateur transponder, allowing two-way communication through the satellite.
