Retinal

Retinal (also known as retinaldehyde) is a polyene chromophore. Retinal, bound to proteins called opsins, is the chemical basis of visual phototransduction, the light-detection stage of visual perception (vision). It is one of several forms of vitamin A and is the aldehyde derivative of retinol.

Role in vision

In vertebrate photoreceptor cells, 11-cis-retinal is bound to opsin to form the visual pigment rhodopsin (in rods) or the color-vision pigments (in cones). When a photon of light is absorbed, 11-cis-retinal photoisomerizes to all-trans-retinal. This change in molecular shape activates the opsin protein, triggering the phototransduction cascade that converts light into neural signals. The all-trans-retinal is then recycled back to the 11-cis form through the visual cycle in the retinal pigment epithelium, a process that depends on the enzyme RPE65.

Many invertebrates use similar retinal-based pigments, and retinal is also the chromophore of microbial rhodopsins such as bacteriorhodopsin, a light-driven proton pump found in archaea.

Chemistry

Retinal has the molecular formula C20H28O. It consists of a β-ionone ring attached to a conjugated polyene chain terminating in an aldehyde group. In animals, retinal is produced from dietary carotenoids such as β-carotene or from retinol stored in the liver.
