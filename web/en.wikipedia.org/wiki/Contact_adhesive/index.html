Contact adhesive

Contact adhesive (also called contact cement) is an adhesive applied to both surfaces to be bonded, allowed to dry, and then pressed together to form an immediate, high-strength bond. It is widely used for laminates, veneers, shoe soles, and upholstery.

Chemistry

Traditional contact adhesives are based on polychloroprene (neoprene) rubber dissolved in volatile solvents such as toluene, hexane or acetone. When the solvent evaporates, the rubber films on the two surfaces fuse on contact through auto-adhesion and crystallization. Water-based (latex) versions avoid flammable solvents.

Application

Both surfaces are coated, left until tacky-dry (typically 15–30 minutes), then carefully aligned — the bond is instant and repositioning is nearly impossible — and rolled or pressed firmly. Full strength develops over 24–72 hours.

Safety

Solvent-based contact cements are highly flammable and their vapors are narcotic; use requires ventilation and no ignition sources. Prolonged inhalation abuse of toluene-containing adhesives causes serious neurological harm.
