Battle of Delphi

The Battle of Delphi was fought in 279 BC during the Galatian invasion of Greece, when a Greek coalition army confronted the Celtic (Galatian) host of Brennus at Delphi, the sanctuary of Apollo. The Greeks claimed a victory that saved the sanctuary from plunder, though the Galatians had already sacked and plundered parts of the site.

Background

In 279 BC a large Galatian army under Brennus invaded Greece, defeating a Greek force at Thermopylae — the site of the famous Persian Wars stand — after finding the mountain path around the pass. Brennus then marched on Delphi, drawn by the sanctuary's legendary treasures, and hoping also to draw Greek forces away from guarding his escape route.

The battle

Ancient sources, especially Pausanias, describe storms, freezing cold, earthquakes and rockfalls from Mount Parnassus that the Greeks interpreted as the direct intervention of Apollo, Pan and the local heroes. The Greek defenders — Phocians, Aetolians and others — harried the Galatians on the slopes and drove them from the sanctuary. Brennus was wounded and the Galatians retreated north in disorder, suffering heavily from weather and pursuit; Brennus later died of his wounds.

Aftermath and legacy

The deliverance of Delphi was commemorated by the Aetolian League with the Soteria festival. The victory did much to raise Aetolian prestige in Greece. Survivors of the invasion crossed into Asia Minor, where they settled as the Galatians of Anatolia. Modern historians note that ancient accounts mix patriotic legend with fact, and some Galatian bands appear to have carried off treasure from the sanctuary.
