Pirate code

A pirate code is a code of conduct for governing pirates. Some codes are remembered in legend, such as the pirate articles of Bartholomew Roberts, which survive in documents from the 1720s. A pirate code was an agreement — a set of articles — sworn to or signed by the crew of a pirate vessel before a voyage, governing discipline, division of plunder, and compensation for injuries.

Bartholomew Roberts's articles

The articles of Bartholomew Roberts's crew, recorded in Captain Charles Johnson's A General History of the Pyrates (1724), included provisions that every man had a vote in affairs of the moment, that plunder was shared in fixed proportions, that lights and candles be put out at eight o'clock, that no boy or woman be allowed among the crew, and that desertion in battle be punished by death or marooning. Compensation for lost limbs was specified in pieces of eight paid from the common stock.

Features

Pirate codes were strikingly democratic for their era: captains were elected and could be deposed, and the quartermaster acted as a counterweight to the captain's authority, which was absolute only in battle or chase. Shares of plunder were relatively egalitarian compared with naval or merchant service.

In fiction

The "Pirates' Code" plays a prominent role in the Pirates of the Caribbean films, where it is presented as a formal book of law kept by the Brethren Court — a fictional elaboration of the historical articles.
