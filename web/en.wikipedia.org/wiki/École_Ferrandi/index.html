Ferrandi Paris, commonly known as École Ferrandi, is a French school of culinary arts and hospitality management in Paris, operated by the Paris Île-de-France Chamber of Commerce and Industry. Nicknamed "the Harvard of gastronomy", it trains chefs, pastry chefs, bakers, restaurant managers, and hospitality professionals, and is regarded as one of the most prestigious culinary schools in France and the world.

History

The school traces its origins to training schemes of the Paris Chamber of Commerce in 1920. It was reorganised and took its present name in honour of Jean Ferrandi, a former president of the chamber's apprenticeship commission. Its historic campus in the Saint-Germain-des-Prés district of the 6th arrondissement houses teaching kitchens, pastry laboratories, and two application restaurants — Le Premier and Le 28 — open to the public and staffed by students.

Programmes

Ferrandi offers French state qualifications from the CAP (vocational certificate) through bachelor's and master's-level degrees in culinary arts, pastry, baking, restaurant service, and hospitality management, alongside intensive programmes in English for international students and continuing education for professionals. The school also has a campus in Saint-Gratien and partnerships with institutions abroad.

Its alumni include numerous Michelin-starred chefs, Meilleurs Ouvriers de France, and champions of international competitions such as the Bocuse d'Or and the World Pastry Cup.
