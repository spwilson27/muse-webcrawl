Abu Musab al-Zarqawi (Arabic: أبو مصعب الزرقاوي; 30 October 1966 – 7 June 2006), born Ahmad Fadeel al-Nazal al-Khalayleh, was a Jordanian militant who founded and led al-Qaeda in Iraq (AQI), the organisation that later evolved into the Islamic State. He was one of the most wanted men in Iraq during the Iraq War.

Early life

Al-Zarqawi was born in Zarqa, Jordan, into a poor family of the Bani Hassan tribe. After a troubled youth and time in prison in Jordan during the 1990s, he travelled to Afghanistan, where he ran a militant training camp near Herat.

Iraq War

Following the 2003 invasion of Iraq, al-Zarqawi built a network that carried out numerous bombings and attacks against coalition forces, Iraqi security forces, and Shia civilians and religious sites. His group pledged allegiance to Osama bin Laden in 2004, becoming al-Qaeda in Iraq. His strategy of provoking sectarian conflict between Sunni and Shia Muslims contributed to the descent into civil war in 2006–2007.

Death

Al-Zarqawi was killed on 7 June 2006 in a United States airstrike on a safe house near Baqubah, Iraq. He was succeeded as leader of AQI by Abu Ayyub al-Masri.
