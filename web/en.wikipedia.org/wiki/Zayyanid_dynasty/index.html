Zayyanid dynasty

The Zayyanid dynasty (Arabic: الزيانيون) was a Berber dynasty centered in Tlemcen in what is now northwestern Algeria. The dynasty ruled the Kingdom of Tlemcen from 1235 to 1556, one of the successor states that emerged from the fragmentation of Almohad rule in the Maghreb, alongside the Marinids of Fez and the Hafsids of Tunis.

History

The dynasty was founded by Yaghmurasen ibn Zyan, a chief of the Zenata Berber Banu Abd al-Wad tribe, who established Tlemcen as his capital in 1235 (some accounts date the foundation to 1236 or 1240). The kingdom occupied a strategic position on the trade routes between the Sahara and the Mediterranean, and Tlemcen prospered as a commercial and cultural center.

Throughout its history the kingdom contended with, and periodically submitted to, the Marinids to the west and the Hafsids to the east, and survived two major Marinid sieges and occupations of Tlemcen in the 14th century. In the 16th century the Zayyanids fell under Spanish and then Ottoman pressure; the last ruler was deposed in 1556, and Tlemcen was absorbed into the Ottoman Regency of Algiers.

Legacy

The Zayyanids left a notable architectural heritage in Tlemcen, including mosques, madrasas and the Mechouar palace complex, built in a style closely related to the art of al-Andalus and the Marinids.
