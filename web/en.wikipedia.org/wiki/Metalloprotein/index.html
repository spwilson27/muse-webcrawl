Metalloprotein

A metalloprotein with a bound metal cofactor

A metalloprotein is a generic term for a protein that contains a metal ion cofactor. It is estimated that roughly half of all proteins contain a metal. The metal ions are usually coordinated by nitrogen, oxygen, or sulfur atoms belonging to amino acids in the polypeptide chain, and their functions are determined by the specific metal, its ligand environment, and the protein fold. Metalloproteins play many important roles in cells, such as in enzymes, transport and storage proteins, and signal transduction proteins.

Abundance and roles

Common metal cofactors include iron, zinc, copper, manganese, cobalt, nickel, molybdenum, vanadium, and calcium. Metalloproteins carry out an enormous range of tasks: hemoglobin and myoglobin transport and store oxygen via iron-containing heme groups; cytochromes transfer electrons; zinc finger proteins bind DNA; carbonic anhydrase uses zinc to hydrate carbon dioxide; and nitrogenase uses an iron–molybdenum cofactor to fix atmospheric nitrogen.

Classification

Metalloproteins are often classified by the function of the metal center: electron transfer (cytochromes, iron–sulfur proteins such as ferredoxins and rubredoxins), oxygen transport and activation (hemoglobin, hemocyanin, hemerythrin), catalysis (metalloenzymes such as superoxide dismutase and the metalloproteinases), and structural or regulatory roles (zinc fingers, metallothionein). The study of these systems falls within the field of bioinorganic chemistry.
