Person of Cultural Merit (文化功労者, bunka kōrōsha) is an official Japanese recognition and honour which is awarded annually to select people who have made outstanding cultural contributions. The honour is intended to provide a pension to those whose cultural activities have advanced Japanese culture, and it forms part of Japan's system of cultural honours alongside the Order of Culture.

History and selection

The system was created by the Pension Act for Persons of Cultural Merit in 1951. Recipients are selected by the Minister of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology after consultation with a selection committee, and are formally approved by the Cabinet. Selections are announced each year in late October or around Culture Day (3 November), when the Order of Culture is also presented by the Emperor to a small number of honourees — Order of Culture recipients are automatically designated Persons of Cultural Merit as well.

Pension and scope

Persons of Cultural Merit receive a lifetime annuity from the state (an annual pension of several million yen under modern revisions). Honourees come from fields including literature, theatre, film, music, fine arts, scholarship, science, sports, and the preservation of traditional arts. Notable recipients include novelists, kabuki actors, scientists such as Nobel laureates, and figures from popular culture; in some years the designation has recognised groups or individuals posthumously honoured through related schemes.

Related honours

The designation is distinct from, though related to, the title of Living National Treasure (an informal name for holders of Important Intangible Cultural Property) and from the Order of Culture itself.
