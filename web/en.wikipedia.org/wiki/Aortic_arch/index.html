Aortic arch

The aortic arch, or arch of the aorta, is the curved segment of the aorta between the ascending aorta and the descending aorta. It arches posteriorly over the root of the left lung and gives off the major arteries supplying the head, neck and upper limbs.

Branches

In the usual arrangement the arch gives rise to three branches: the brachiocephalic trunk, the left common carotid artery and the left subclavian artery. The brachiocephalic trunk then divides into the right common carotid and right subclavian arteries. Variations in this branching pattern are common and usually clinically insignificant.

Development and relations

The arch develops from the embryonic pharyngeal arch arteries; the left fourth arch contributes to the definitive aortic arch. Near the arch lie the trachea, oesophagus, thoracic duct and the left recurrent laryngeal nerve, which loops beneath the arch near the ligamentum arteriosum.

Clinical significance

Diseases of the arch include coarctation of the aorta, aneurysm and aortic dissection. These conditions may be investigated with echocardiography, CT angiography or MRI, and some require surgical or endovascular repair.
