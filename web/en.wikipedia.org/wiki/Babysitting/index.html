Babysitting

Babysitting is the practice of temporarily caring for a child on behalf of the child's parents or guardians. A babysitter is typically engaged for a few hours — commonly during an evening — while parents are away, and may be a family friend, neighbour, or a young person employed for the task. Unlike a nanny, who may be a long-term childcare professional, a babysitter's work is casual and short-term.

Duties

The babysitter's responsibilities usually include supervising play, preparing simple meals or snacks, following bedtime routines, and ensuring the children's safety. Requirements vary with the children's ages: infants need feeding, changing and constant watching, while older children may mainly need supervision and company.

The babysitter

In many countries babysitting is a common first job for teenagers and young adults, and training courses — often including first aid and child development basics — are offered by organisations such as the Red Cross. Modern parents frequently find babysitters through word of mouth, community networks or online platforms and apps that verify identities and reviews.

In popular culture

The babysitter is a recurring figure in fiction and film, from the children's book series The Baby-Sitters Club by Ann M. Martin to the horror trope of the babysitter menaced at home, popularised by films such as Halloween (1978) and the urban legend of "the babysitter and the man upstairs".
