Micelle

A micelle is an aggregate of surfactant molecules dispersed in a liquid, forming a colloidal suspension. In water, hydrophilic heads face outward and hydrophobic tails face inward.

Formation

Micelles form above the critical micelle concentration (CMC) and above the Krafft temperature. The structure solubilises oils in water, enabling detergents to lift grease.

Uses

Important in cleaning, emulsification, drug delivery, and biology — bile salts form micelles aiding fat digestion; casein micelles occur in milk. Reverse micelles form in non-polar solvents.
