Sphere spectrum

In stable homotopy theory, a branch of mathematics, the sphere spectrum S is the monoidal unit in the category of spectra. It is the fundamental object of the field: roughly speaking, it is built from the sequence of spheres S0, S1, S2, … in increasing dimensions, together with the natural structure maps relating them.

Definition

The sphere spectrum can be described as the suspension spectrum of the zero-dimensional sphere S0 (a space consisting of two points). Its homotopy groups are the stable homotopy groups of spheres: πn(S) = πnS, the stable stems, which are among the most studied and most difficult invariants in algebraic topology. The group π0(S) is the ring of integers Z, while the higher stable stems are mostly finite abelian groups whose complete determination remains out of reach.

Role in stable homotopy theory

Every spectrum admits maps from the sphere spectrum, and homotopy classes of such maps recover the homotopy groups of the target spectrum. Chromatic homotopy theory organises the sphere spectrum through its localisations and the associated Adams–Novikov spectral sequence. The sphere spectrum also plays a foundational role in the theory of highly structured ring spectra: it is the initial E∞-ring spectrum, so that every such ring spectrum receives an essentially unique map from it.
