1954 FIFA World Cup

The Wankdorf Stadium, Bern, scene of the "Miracle of Bern."

The 1954 FIFA World Cup was the fifth edition of the FIFA World Cup, held in Switzerland from 16 June to 4 July 1954. Sixteen teams took part. The tournament is remembered for the highest goals-per-game average in World Cup history (5.38), Hungary's legendary "Mighty Magyars," and one of the greatest upsets in football history in the final.

Tournament format

The format was unusual: each four-team group contained two seeded and two unseeded teams, and teams played only two group matches — seeded against unseeded — with play-offs used to break ties. The quarter-final between Austria and Switzerland, a 7–5 thriller in Lausanne, remains the highest-scoring match in World Cup history. Hungary, unbeaten in four years and Olympic champions, thrashed South Korea 9–0 and West Germany 8–3 in the group stage, then beat Brazil in the violent "Battle of Berne" and defending champions Uruguay in the semi-finals.

Final: the Miracle of Bern

In the final at the Wankdorf Stadium on 4 July, Hungary raced into a 2–0 lead within eight minutes through Ferenc Puskás and Zoltán Czibor. West Germany, managed by Sepp Herberger, hit back immediately: Max Morlock and Helmut Rahn levelled the score by the 18th minute, and Rahn scored the winner six minutes from time for a 3–2 victory. The result, known in Germany as the Miracle of Bern (Das Wunder von Bern), gave West Germany its first World Cup title and became a symbol of the country's post-war recovery. Sandor Kocsis of Hungary finished as top scorer with 11 goals.

Legacy

The tournament marked the first World Cup to be televised. Hungary's defeat remains one of the sport's great shocks; the team never won the World Cup. Questions have since been raised about possible use of methamphetamine injections by the West German team, though no conclusive proof of wrongdoing has been established.
