Perspectivism

Perspectivism (German: Perspektivismus) is the philosophical view that all perception, knowledge, and evaluation take place from some perspective, and that there is no perception from a "view from nowhere". It is most closely associated with Friedrich Nietzsche, who argued that truth and values are always apprehended through the interpretive perspectives of living beings.

Nietzsche

In works such as On the Genealogy of Morality (1887), Nietzsche wrote that "there is only a perspective seeing, only a perspective 'knowing'", and urged multiplying perspectives — using "more eyes, different eyes" — to arrive at a more complete conception of a thing. For Nietzsche this was not mere relativism: perspectives can be ranked by their honesty, their affirmation of life, and their explanatory power, and his genealogy of morality itself aimed to expose the standpoints from which moral values arose.

Earlier and later uses

Elements of perspectivism appear in Leibniz, whose monads each mirror the universe from a unique point of view, and in Gustav Teichmüller, who coined the term. In the twentieth century, José Ortega y Gasset developed a "doctrine of the point of view" in which perspective is a component of reality itself. Anthropologist Eduardo Viveiros de Castro later adapted the term for Amerindian cosmologies ("multinaturalism"), in which different beings share a culture but inhabit different natures.
