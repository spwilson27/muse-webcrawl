Tankōbon

OriginJapan
Meaning"Standalone book"
Typical formatPaperback, about 200 pages
Used forCollected editions of manga series

Tankōbon (Japanese: 単行本, "independent/standalone book") is the Japanese term for a book that is complete in itself rather than part of a series of books, most commonly used in the West to refer to collected volumes of a manga series.

Manga publishing

Manga chapters are typically serialized first in weekly or monthly anthology magazines. Popular series are then collected into tankōbon volumes — usually around 200 pages, containing four to twelve chapters — printed on higher-quality paper with new covers. Best-selling tankōbon can sell millions of copies. Related formats include the larger aizōban (collector's edition) and the compact bunkoban.

Usage outside Japan

In English-language publishing, "tankōbon" is often used loosely for any trade paperback collection of comics chapters, though publishers more often simply call them graphic novels or collected editions.
