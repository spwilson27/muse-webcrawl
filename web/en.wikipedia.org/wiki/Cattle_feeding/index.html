Cattle feeding

Cattle feeding is the practice of managing the diet of beef cattle during the various phases of production — from cow–calf grazing through backgrounding (growing) to finishing in a feedlot — so as to produce beef efficiently and to target specifications.

Phases

Most beef calves are born and raised on pasture with their dams, then weaned and "backgrounded" on forage or light grain rations to build frame. In the finishing phase, cattle are fed energy-dense rations — in North America typically corn-based, with silage, distillers' grains, minerals, and additives — for 90–300 days in feedlots (concentrated animal feeding operations, CAFOs) until reaching slaughter weight and desired fat cover (marbling).

Nutrition

Rations are balanced for energy, protein, fibre, and minerals; effective fibre maintains rumen function, while abrupt shifts to high-grain diets can cause acidosis, bloat, and liver abscesses, managed with ionophores such as monensin. Growth-promoting implants and beta-agonists are used in some countries and prohibited in others (notably the EU).

Alternative systems

Grass-finished beef is raised entirely on forage, taking longer to finish and typically yielding leaner meat with a different flavour profile. Feeding practices are central to debates over cost, animal welfare, antibiotic use, and environmental footprint.
