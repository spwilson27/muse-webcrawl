Wives of Muhammad

The Islamic prophet Muhammad is traditionally reported to have married eleven women during his lifetime. In Islamic tradition his wives are honoured with the title "Mothers of the Believers" (Umm al-Mu'minin), a term derived from the Quran (33:6). Muhammad's first marriage was to Khadija bint Khuwaylid, a Meccan merchant, in 595 CE; he remained monogamously married to her until her death in 619 CE.

List of wives

Following Khadija's death, Muhammad married Sawda bint Zam'a, and later Aisha bint Abi Bakr, Hafsa bint Umar, Zaynab bint Khuzayma, Umm Salama, Zaynab bint Jahsh, Juwayriyya bint al-Harith, Umm Habiba, Safiyya bint Huyayy, Maymunah bint al-Harith, and (according to some accounts) Rayhana bint Zayd. Maria al-Qibtiyya is counted by some sources as a wife and by others as a concubine.

Historical discussion

Historians note that many of the marriages had social, tribal and political dimensions, forming alliances, protecting widows, and integrating clans into the early Muslim community. Muhammad's marriages are a subject of extensive discussion in both traditional Islamic scholarship and modern academic writing.
