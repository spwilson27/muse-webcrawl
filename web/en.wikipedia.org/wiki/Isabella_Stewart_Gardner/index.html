Isabella Stewart Gardner
Born: April 14, 1840, New York City, U.S.
Died: July 17, 1924 (aged 84), Boston, Massachusetts, U.S.
Known for: Art collecting; Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum
Spouse: John Lowell Gardner II (m. 1860; d. 1898)

Isabella Stewart Gardner (April 14, 1840 – July 17, 1924) was an American art collector, philanthropist, and patron of the arts. She founded the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum in Boston.

Early life

Gardner was born in New York City, the daughter of David Stewart, who made a fortune in iron and mining. In 1860 she married John Lowell "Jack" Gardner II, a prominent Bostonian. The couple traveled widely; after the death of their infant son, travel and art collecting became central to her life.

Collecting

Advised by the connoisseur Bernard Berenson, Gardner assembled a major collection of European art, including works by Titian, Rembrandt, Raphael, Botticelli, Vermeer, and Velázquez, as well as Asian art, manuscripts, and decorative objects. She was the first American to own a painting by Matisse and acquired Vermeer's The Concert.

Fenway Court

After her husband's death in 1898, Gardner built Fenway Court in Boston's Fenway neighborhood, designed in the style of a 15th-century Venetian palazzo around a flower-filled courtyard. It opened to the public in 1903. She lived in an apartment on the fourth floor and stipulated in her will that the collection remain displayed exactly as she arranged it.

1990 theft

On March 18, 1990, thirteen works — including Vermeer's The Concert and three Rembrandts — were stolen from the museum in the largest art theft in American history. The works have never been recovered; their empty frames still hang in the museum.

Legacy

Gardner was a flamboyant figure in Boston society, nicknamed "Belle" and "Mrs. Jack." Her museum remains a major cultural institution, expanded with a new wing designed by Renzo Piano in 2012.
