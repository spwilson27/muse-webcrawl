Pulmonary function testing

Pulmonary function tests (PFTs) are a group of non-invasive tests that measure how well the lungs move air and transfer gases into the blood. They are used to diagnose and monitor diseases such as asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and interstitial lung disease, to assess preoperative risk and disability, and to measure response to treatment.

Spirometry

Spirometry, the most common test, records the volume and flow of air during forced breathing manoeuvres. Key values are the forced vital capacity (FVC), the forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1), and their ratio: a reduced FEV1/FVC ratio indicates obstruction, while a reduced FVC with a normal ratio suggests restriction. Improvement of FEV1 by at least 12% and 200 mL after a bronchodilator ("reversibility") supports asthma. Results are compared with reference values from the Global Lung Function Initiative equations, adjusted for age, height and sex, and expressed as percent predicted and z-scores.

Other tests

Lung volumes (total lung capacity, residual volume) are measured by body plethysmography, helium dilution or nitrogen washout, and confirm restriction or air trapping. The diffusing capacity (DLCO) measures gas transfer using carbon monoxide and falls in emphysema, fibrosis and pulmonary vascular disease. The six-minute walk test and cardiopulmonary exercise testing assess functional capacity. Because manoeuvres are effort-dependent, ATS/ERS standards govern technique, acceptability and repeatability.
