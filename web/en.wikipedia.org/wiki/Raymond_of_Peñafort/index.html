Raymond of Peñafort

Saint Raymond of Peñafort (Catalan: Sant Ramon de Penyafort; c. 1175 – 6 January 1275) was a Catalan Dominican friar and canon lawyer, one of the foremost legal scholars of the Middle Ages. He is the patron saint of canon lawyers.

Life and works

Raymond studied and taught canon law at Bologna before returning to Barcelona. At the request of Pope Gregory IX he compiled the Decretals (the Liber extra, promulgated in 1234), a systematic collection of papal decretal letters that became a principal source of Catholic canon law for centuries. He also wrote the influential Summa de casibus poenitentiae, a manual for confessors dealing with cases of conscience.

Elected third Master General of the Dominican Order in 1238, he resigned after two years and devoted himself to preaching, mission, and the care of souls. A famous legend recounts that, forbidden to leave Majorca by King James I of Aragon, he spread his cloak on the sea and sailed back to Barcelona upon it. He was canonised in 1601; his feast day is 7 January.
