ISO character encodings

A number of character encodings have been standardized by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO), most famously the ISO/IEC 8859 family of single-byte encodings and ISO/IEC 10646, the Universal Character Set underlying Unicode.

ISO/IEC 8859

ISO/IEC 8859 defines a series of 8-bit character encodings, each covering a set of scripts: part 1 (Latin-1) for Western European languages, part 2 for Central European languages, part 5 for Cyrillic, part 7 for Greek, and so on. Each part encodes 191 printable characters and is a superset of ASCII in its lower half.

Other standards

ISO 646 defined national variants of a 7-bit code, while ISO 2022 provided escape-sequence mechanisms for switching between encodings, widely used in East Asian encodings such as ISO-2022-JP. In modern practice, UTF-8 has largely superseded the ISO encodings on the web.
