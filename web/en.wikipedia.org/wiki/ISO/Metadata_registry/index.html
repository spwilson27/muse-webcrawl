Metadata registry

A metadata registry is a central location in an organization where metadata definitions are stored and maintained in a controlled method. The concept is standardised by ISO/IEC 11179, which specifies the kinds of items a registry records, their attributes, and the metamodel relating data elements, concepts, and value domains.

Purpose

Metadata registries promote semantic interoperability: by registering administered items with unique identifiers, precise definitions, and mappings between the vocabularies of different systems, organisations can exchange data without ambiguity about meaning. Registered items pass through lifecycle statuses (for example, from "candidate" to "standard" to "retired") under the governance of a registration authority.

Examples

Notable registries include the US National Cancer Institute's Cancer Data Standards Registry and Repository (caDSR), the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare's METeOR, and various national health and environmental data registries built on the ISO/IEC 11179 framework. The standard's metamodel in Part 3 is the normative core, while other parts address naming conventions, classification, and formulation of definitions.
