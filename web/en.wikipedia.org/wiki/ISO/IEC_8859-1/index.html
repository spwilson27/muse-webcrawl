ISO/IEC 8859-1

ISO/IEC 8859-1
Alias: Latin-1
Type: Single-byte encoding
First published: 1987

ISO/IEC 8859-1:1998, Information technology — 8-bit single-byte coded graphic character sets — Part 1: Latin alphabet No. 1, is part of the ISO/IEC 8859 series of ASCII-based character encodings, first published in 1987. Informally called Latin-1, it was for decades the default encoding for many documents and systems in Western Europe and the Americas.

Coverage

The encoding assigns printable characters to the byte range 0xA0–0xFF in addition to ASCII, covering letters needed for most Western European languages, including French, German, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, Dutch and the Scandinavian languages — characters such as à, é, î, ñ, ö, ü, ß and ø. The first 32 code points of its Unicode counterpart (U+00A0–U+00FF) are identical, which made migration to Unicode straightforward.

Use and successors

Latin-1 was the default encoding of early HTML and many Unix and X Window systems. The closely related Windows-1252 encoding superseded it in practice on Windows and in web browsers (the HTML5 specification treats documents labelled ISO-8859-1 as Windows-1252). UTF-8 has since become the dominant encoding on the web.
