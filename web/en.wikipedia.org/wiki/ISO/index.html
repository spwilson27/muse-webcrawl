The International Organization for Standardization (ISO) is an independent, non-governmental international standard development organization composed of representatives from the national standards organizations of member countries. Founded in 1947 and headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland, it develops and publishes voluntary international standards covering nearly every industry, from technology and manufacturing to food safety, healthcare and agriculture.

Name

"ISO" is not an acronym of the organisation's name in any one language. It derives from the Greek word ísos (ἴσος), meaning "equal", chosen so the short name would be the same in every language.

Standards development

ISO standards are developed by technical committees of experts from member bodies, proceeding through proposal, preparatory, committee, enquiry, approval and publication stages, with drafts (such as DIS and FDIS) circulated for ballot. Well-known standards include ISO 9001 (quality management), ISO 14001 (environmental management), ISO/IEC 27001 (information security), ISO 8601 (dates and times) and the ISO country codes (ISO 3166). ISO works closely with the IEC (electrotechnology) and ITU (telecommunications).

Membership and funding

Each member country has one member body. Funding comes from member subscriptions and the sale of standards documents, which are copyrighted and sold rather than freely distributed — a model that has attracted both support and criticism.
