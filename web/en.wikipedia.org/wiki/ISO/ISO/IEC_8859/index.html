ISO/IEC 8859

TypeCharacter encoding standard
Parts16 (one withdrawn)
Encoding8-bit single byte
OrganizationsISO, IEC

ISO/IEC 8859 is a joint ISO and IEC series of standards for 8-bit character encodings. The standard consists of numbered parts, such as ISO/IEC 8859-1 and ISO/IEC 8859-2, each specifying an encoding for a group of languages and scripts, built on the 7-bit ASCII repertoire in the first 128 code points.

Parts

Part 1 (Latin-1) covers Western European languages; Part 2 covers Central and Eastern European languages; Part 5 covers Cyrillic; Part 6 Arabic; Part 7 Greek; Part 8 Hebrew; Part 9 Turkish; Part 15 (Latin-9) revised Part 1 to include the euro sign and Finnish and French letters. Part 12, intended for Devanagari, was abandoned.

Use

ISO/IEC 8859-1 was historically the default encoding for many protocols and documents, though in practice the closely related Windows-1252 was often used under its name. Unicode and UTF-8 have since become dominant.
