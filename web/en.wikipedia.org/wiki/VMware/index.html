VMware

VMware LLC is an American cloud computing and virtualization technology company headquartered in Palo Alto, California. Founded in 1998, VMware was the first company to successfully virtualize the x86 architecture, and its hypervisor products — above all ESXi and the vSphere platform — became foundational to modern data centres, enabling many virtual machines to run on a single physical server. In November 2023, VMware was acquired by Broadcom in a deal valued at about $61 billion (approximately $69 billion including debt), one of the largest technology acquisitions ever.

History

VMware was founded in 1998 by Diane Greene, Stanford professor Mendel Rosenblum, and others, growing out of research on operating systems and virtual machine monitors. Its first product, VMware Workstation (1999), let users run multiple operating systems on a desktop computer; the server products GSX Server and ESX Server followed. EMC Corporation acquired VMware in 2004, and the company went public in 2007 in one of that year's most successful IPOs. Dell Technologies gained control through its acquisition of EMC in 2016 and spun VMware off in 2021, before Broadcom's acquisition two years later, after which Broadcom overhauled VMware's licensing toward bundled subscriptions — a change that drew criticism from some customers and partners.

Products

VMware's core product is the ESXi bare-metal hypervisor, managed through vSphere and vCenter, with companion technologies including vSAN (software-defined storage), NSX (network virtualization), and the Tanzu platform for Kubernetes. On the desktop, VMware Workstation (for Windows and Linux) and Fusion (for macOS) remain widely used by developers and IT professionals.
