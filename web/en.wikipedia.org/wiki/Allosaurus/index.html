Allosaurus

Temporal rangeLate Jurassic, 155–145 Ma
FamilyAllosauridae
Length8.5–9.7 m (28–32 ft)
Named byOthniel Charles Marsh, 1877

Allosaurus is a genus of large carnosaurian theropod dinosaur that lived 155 to 145 million years ago during the Late Jurassic period. The name means "different lizard", referring to its light, concave vertebrae. It was one of the first dinosaurs described from reasonably complete material, and remains among the best-known theropods.

Description

Allosaurus was a large bipedal predator, averaging about 8.5 metres in length, with a massive skull bearing dozens of sharp serrated teeth and distinctive ridges above its eyes. It had three-fingered hands with sharp claws and powerful hind limbs.

Discovery and classification

Named by Othniel Charles Marsh in 1877 during the Bone Wars, Allosaurus fossils are abundant in the Morrison Formation of North America; the Cleveland-Lloyd Dinosaur Quarry in Utah has yielded remains of dozens of individuals. The most famous specimen, "Big Al", shows numerous healed injuries and infections. The best-known species is A. fragilis.

Paleobiology

As the most common large predator of the Morrison ecosystem, Allosaurus preyed on ornithopods, stegosaurids, and possibly young sauropods. Studies of its skull suggest a relatively weak bite but strong neck muscles, leading to a proposed "hatchet" style of attack.
