La bohème

La bohème is an opera in four acts by Giacomo Puccini to an Italian libretto by Luigi Illica and Giuseppe Giacosa, based on Henri Murger's 1851 novel Scènes de la vie de bohème. It premiered on 1 February 1896 at the Teatro Regio in Turin, conducted by the young Arturo Toscanini. It is one of the most frequently performed operas in the world.

Synopsis

In a freezing Parisian garret on Christmas Eve, the poet Rodolfo meets the seamstress Mimì, who has come to relight her candle; they fall in love ("Che gelida manina," "Sì, mi chiamano Mimì," "O soave fanciulla"). Rodolfo's friends — the painter Marcello, the musician Schaunard, and the philosopher Colline — carouse at the Café Momus, where Marcello's former lover Musetta makes a flamboyant entrance. Months later, Mimì, ill with consumption, has left Rodolfo, who cannot support her. In the final act the friends' bohemian horseplay turns to grief as Musetta brings the dying Mimì back to the garret, where she dies in Rodolfo's arms.

Reception and influence

The premiere received mixed reviews, but the opera soon triumphed internationally and has never left the repertoire. Leoncavallo composed a rival La bohème (1897) after a public dispute over rights to Murger's novel. Puccini's opera later inspired the musical Rent (1996), which transposed the story to New York's East Village during the AIDS crisis.
