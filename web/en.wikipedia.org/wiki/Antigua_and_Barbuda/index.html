Antigua and Barbuda is an island country in the Caribbean, in the Leeward Islands of the Lesser Antilles. It consists principally of two islands, Antigua and Barbuda, along with several smaller islands including the uninhabited Redonda. The capital and largest city is St. John's, on Antigua.

History

The islands were inhabited by Amerindian peoples before Christopher Columbus reached Antigua in 1493 and named it after Santa María de la Antigua. English colonists settled in 1632 and established sugar plantations worked by enslaved Africans, who were emancipated in 1834. Antigua and Barbuda became an associated state in 1967 and gained full independence from the United Kingdom on 1 November 1981, remaining a Commonwealth realm with the British monarch as head of state.

Geography and economy

Antigua is largely low-lying limestone and volcanic terrain with numerous beaches; Barbuda, about 60 km north, is a flat coral island centred on the Codrington Lagoon, devastated by Hurricane Irma in 2017. Tourism dominates the economy, supplemented by offshore financial services and a citizenship-by-investment programme. Antigua is known for cricket — producing players such as Viv Richards — and for Nelson's Dockyard, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
