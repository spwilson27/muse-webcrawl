Indio, California

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Indio (Spanish for "Indian") is a city in Riverside County, California, United States, in the Coachella Valley of the Colorado Desert. With a population of roughly 89,000 at the 2020 census, it is the largest city in the valley and one of the fastest-growing in Southern California. Indio is known worldwide as the host of the Coachella Valley Music and Arts Festival and the Stagecoach Festival, both held at the Empire Polo Club.

History

Indio began in 1876 as a railroad siding and water stop on the Southern Pacific line across the desert—originally called Indian Wells, then renamed Indio because too many stops had similar names. Irrigation from artesian wells and the Colorado River aqueduct turned the settlement into an agricultural centre, most famously for dates: the valley came to produce the great majority of the United States date crop, and the United States Department of Agriculture maintained a date experiment station in the area. Indio incorporated in 1930 and is home to the Riverside County Fair and National Date Festival, held since the 1920s–1940s in evolving form.

Festivals

The Coachella festival, founded in 1999, draws over 100,000 attendees per day across two weekends each April, and its country counterpart Stagecoach follows shortly after. The city is also known for its International Tamale Festival, one of the largest food festivals in California.

Geography and climate

Indio lies below sea level (about 13 feet below) on the floor of the Coachella Valley, between the San Bernardino Mountains to the north and the Santa Rosa and San Jacinto Mountains to the west. It has a hot desert climate with summer highs routinely above 110°F (43°C) and mild winters, which underpin its agriculture and winter tourism.
