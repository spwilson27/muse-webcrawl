Nicholas Pileggi

Born February 22, 1933, New York City, U.S.

Occupation Author, screenwriter, journalist

Spouse Nora Ephron (m. 1987; died 2012)

Nicholas Pileggi (born February 22, 1933) is an American author and screenwriter. He wrote the non-fiction book Wiseguy (1985) about the life of mobster Henry Hill, and co-wrote its film adaptation, Goodfellas (1990), with director Martin Scorsese; the screenplay was nominated for an Academy Award. He also wrote the book Casino: Love and Honor in Las Vegas and co-wrote its 1995 film adaptation, also with Scorsese.

Early life and career

Pileggi was born and raised in New York City, the son of Italian immigrants. His father owned a shoe store. He began his career as a journalist, working for the Associated Press and New York magazine, where he specialized in reporting on crime and the Mafia. His reporting on organized crime gave him access to figures such as Henry Hill, a former associate of the Lucchese crime family who became the subject of Wiseguy.

Film work

Scorsese adapted Wiseguy into Goodfellas (1990), widely regarded as one of the greatest films ever made. Pileggi and Scorsese collaborated again on Casino (1995), based on Pileggi's book about Frank "Lefty" Rosenthal and the mob's control of Las Vegas casinos. Pileggi also served as an executive producer on the television series Vegas (2012–2013), and he wrote the screenplay for the 1996 film City Hall. He was among the executive producers of Scorsese's The Irishman (2019), based on the book I Heard You Paint Houses by Charles Brandt.

Personal life

Pileggi was married to writer and filmmaker Nora Ephron from 1987 until her death in 2012.
