Assassination of John F. Kennedy
Date22 November 1963, 12:30 p.m. CST
LocationDealey Plaza, Dallas, Texas, U.S.
TargetJohn F. Kennedy, 35th President of the United States
PerpetratorLee Harvey Oswald (per Warren Commission)

The assassination of John F. Kennedy took place on Friday, 22 November 1963, in Dallas, Texas, when the 35th president of the United States was shot while riding in a presidential motorcade through Dealey Plaza. Kennedy was pronounced dead at Parkland Memorial Hospital about thirty minutes later; Texas Governor John Connally, riding in the same limousine, was seriously wounded.

The shooting and the suspect

Shots were fired as the motorcade passed the Texas School Book Depository. Lee Harvey Oswald, a Depository employee and former Marine who had lived in the Soviet Union, was arrested that afternoon for the murder of Dallas police officer J. D. Tippit and was charged with Kennedy's assassination that night. Oswald denied the charges. Two days later, on 24 November, he was himself shot dead by nightclub owner Jack Ruby while being transferred between jails, live on television.

Investigations

The Warren Commission (1964) concluded that Oswald acted alone, firing three shots from the Depository's sixth floor. The House Select Committee on Assassinations (1979) agreed that Oswald fired the shots that killed Kennedy but concluded there was a "probable conspiracy", based largely on acoustic evidence later disputed. The Zapruder film, an amateur home movie of the motorcade, is the most studied record of the event. The assassination remains the subject of numerous conspiracy theories, though official reviews have consistently identified Oswald as the gunman.

Aftermath

Vice President Lyndon B. Johnson was sworn in as president aboard Air Force One the same day. Kennedy's state funeral in Washington, attended by leaders from around the world, became an enduring image of national mourning.
