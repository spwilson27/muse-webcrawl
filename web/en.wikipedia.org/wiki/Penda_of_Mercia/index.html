Penda (died 15 November 655) was a 7th-century king of Mercia, the Anglo-Saxon kingdom in the English Midlands. A pagan at a time when the other English kingdoms were converting to Christianity, he became one of the most powerful rulers of his age, defeating and killing two kings of Northumbria—Edwin at Hatfield Chase (633, with Cadwallon of Gwynedd) and Oswald at Maserfield (642)—as well as Ecgfrith's predecessors' ally Anna of East Anglia.

Reign

Penda came to power around 626 (some sources suggest later) and spent nearly three decades extending Mercian supremacy over the Welsh borders, the Hwicce, the Magonsæte and the Middle Angles, where he installed his son Peada. The Venerable Bede, writing from a Northumbrian Christian perspective, depicts Penda as an implacable enemy, though he also notes Penda did not forbid Christian preaching in Mercia; several of Penda's children became notable Christians.

Death

In 655 Penda invaded Bernicia with a large coalition, but Oswiu of Northumbria, cornered, offered tribute and—according to Bede—vowed his daughter to God if granted victory. At the Battle of the Winwaed, fought in flooded conditions, Penda's army was destroyed and he was killed; his head was reportedly displayed at York. Mercia briefly submitted to Oswiu before regaining independence under Penda's son Wulfhere.
