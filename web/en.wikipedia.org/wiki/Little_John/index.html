Little John

Little John is a legendary fellow outlaw of Robin Hood, and his chief lieutenant and second-in-command among the Merry Men. His name is an ironic nickname: in the ballads he is a giant of a man, often described as seven feet tall, renowned for his strength and for his skill with the quarterstaff.

In the ballads

Little John appears in the earliest surviving Robin Hood ballads, including A Gest of Robyn Hode and Robin Hood and the Monk. In the most famous of his stories — told in Robin Hood and Little John — Robin meets a huge stranger on a narrow bridge over a stream; they fight with quarterstaffs for the crossing, Robin is knocked into the water, and, laughing, he recruits the stranger, who is baptised (in jest) "Little John", a reversal of his presumed name John Little.

Within the band, Little John serves as Robin's loyal and sometimes more prudent deputy: in Robin Hood and the Monk he rescues Robin from captivity in Nottingham after Robin's capture at St Mary's Church. In some versions Little John is the only mourner at Robin's death, and he is sometimes said to have buried Robin where his last arrow fell.

Possible historical origins

Like Robin Hood himself, Little John may have roots in a real outlaw. A grave at Hathersage in Derbyshire, in the churchyard of St Michael's, is traditionally said to be his; in the 18th century an excavation reportedly found very long thigh bones. Records mention several "John Littles" and similar figures, but no identification is certain.

In later literature and film

Little John is a staple of Robin Hood retellings, from the children's novels of the 19th century to film — memorably played, in Disney's animated Robin Hood (1973), as a bear voiced by Phil Harris, and by Nick Brimble in Robin Hood: Prince of Thieves (1991). His quarterstaff duel with Robin on the bridge remains one of the signature scenes of the legend.
