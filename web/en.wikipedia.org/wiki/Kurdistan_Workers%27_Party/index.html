Kurdistan Workers' Party

The Kurdistan Workers' Party, commonly known by its Kurdish acronym PKK (Partiya Karkerên Kurdistanê), is a Kurdish militant political organization and armed guerrilla movement founded in Turkey in 1978. It has historically sought Kurdish autonomy and rights, and is designated as a terrorist organization by Turkey, the United States, the European Union and several other countries.

History

The PKK was founded on 27 November 1978 in the village of Fis, near Lice in Diyarbakır Province, under the leadership of Abdullah Öcalan. In 1984 it launched an armed insurgency against the Turkish state. The conflict has resulted in tens of thousands of deaths over four decades. Öcalan was captured in Kenya in 1999 and has been imprisoned on İmralı Island since then; from prison he developed the ideology of democratic confederalism.

Recent developments

Ceasefires and peace processes were attempted several times, most notably from 2013 until the process collapsed in 2015. In 2025, following a call by Öcalan to lay down arms, the PKK announced its decision to dissolve itself and end its armed struggle.
