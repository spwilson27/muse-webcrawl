Dark ride

A dark ride or ghost train is an indoor amusement ride on which passengers aboard guided vehicles travel through specially lit scenes that typically contain animation, sound, music, and special effects. Appearing as early as the late 19th century, dark rides have evolved from simple haunted-house attractions with painted scenery to technologically advanced narrative experiences.

History

Early dark rides, such as the "Old Mill" rides and Leon Cassidy's Pretzel rides of the 1920s, carried riders in the dark past illuminated stunts and tableaus. The classic spook-house rides, with their banging doors, tilting floors, and air blasts, became fixtures of American amusement parks and travelling fairs. In the 1950s and 1960s, Walt Disney's Imagineers transformed the form: attractions such as Peter Pan's Flight, Pirates of the Caribbean, and The Haunted Mansion used Audio-Animatronic figures, elaborate sets, and continuous-loading Omnimover systems to tell stories in three dimensions.

Modern dark rides

Contemporary dark rides often combine physical sets with projection mapping, trackless vehicles, and interactive elements. Examples include The Amazing Adventures of Spider-Man, Harry Potter and the Forbidden Journey, Transformers: The Ride, and Star Wars: Rise of the Resistance. "Interactive dark rides," in which riders shoot at targets for points, began with rides such as Buzz Lightyear's Space Ranger Spin.

Terminology

In the United Kingdom and Australia, the traditional term is "ghost train," regardless of the ride's theme. Dark rides that rely on scares are often classed as haunted attractions.
