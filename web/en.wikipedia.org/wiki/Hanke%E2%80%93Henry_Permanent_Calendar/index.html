Hanke–Henry Permanent Calendar

Proposed bySteve Hanke and Richard Henry
Proposed2011–2012
TypePerennial calendar reform

The Hanke–Henry Permanent Calendar is a proposed reform of the Gregorian calendar by Johns Hopkins economist Steve H. Hanke and astrophysicist Richard Conn Henry. It is a perennial calendar: every date falls on the same day of the week every year.

Design

The calendar has four quarters of 91 days, each made of two 30-day months followed by one 31-day month (30, 30, 31), totalling 364 days. To stay in step with the solar year, an extra seven-day "leap week" (a mini-month sometimes called Xmas week) is inserted at the end of December every five or six years, in years divisible by 5 with a remainder condition.

Claimed advantages

Proponents argue that fixed dates for holidays, simpler scheduling, and stable quarterly accounting would bring economic benefits; the proposal also advocates abolishing time zones in favour of universal time. It has not been adopted by any government.
