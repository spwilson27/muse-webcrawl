The Wall Street Journal (WSJ) is an American business-focused daily newspaper founded on 8 July 1889 and published by Dow Jones & Company, part of Rupert Murdoch's News Corp since 2007. It is among the largest US papers by circulation and is known for its sober broadsheet design, market coverage and conservative editorial page.

History

The Journal grew from Dow Jones' 1880s news bulletins for traders. Under Bernard Baruch-era predecessors and later editors such as Vermont Royster, it pioneered explanatory business journalism for a national audience, launching Asian and European editions.

News and opinion

The paper famously separates its news columns from its editorial page; the latter's free-market conservatism has been influential in Republican politics. Its reporting has won dozens of Pulitzer Prizes; the site was an early adopter of the hard paywall.
