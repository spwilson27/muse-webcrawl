Joseph Bates (Adventist)

Joseph Bates (July 8, 1792 – March 19, 1872) was an American sea captain, temperance reformer and evangelist who became one of the founders of the Seventh-day Adventist Church. He is credited with introducing the seventh-day Sabbath doctrine to the early Adventist movement.

Early life and seafaring career

Bates was born in Rochester, Massachusetts, and grew up in the whaling port of Fairhaven. He went to sea as a cabin boy in 1807 and spent more than two decades in maritime life, surviving shipwreck, storms and impressment into the British Royal Navy during the War of 1812, when he was held as a prisoner at Dartmoor Prison. He rose to the rank of captain and retired from the sea in 1828 with a modest fortune.

Reform work and Millerism

On returning home Bates became active in the temperance movement, helping to organize one of America's first temperance societies, and he campaigned against tobacco and other stimulants. Around 1840 he accepted William Miller's teaching that Christ would return in the early 1840s, and he spent much of his fortune promoting the Millerite message. After the Great Disappointment of October 1844 he continued to study prophecy with the small group of Advent believers who remained.

The Sabbath doctrine

In 1845 Bates read a tract by Thomas M. Preble and became convinced that the seventh day, Saturday, was the biblical Sabbath. In 1846 he published his own tract, The Seventh-day Sabbath, a Perpetual Sign, and traveled to Washington, New Hampshire, where his arguments persuaded the believers there. That same year he met the young James and Ellen White, who accepted the Sabbath teaching after studying his tract; the three became the principal founders of the Sabbatarian Adventist movement, formally organized as the Seventh-day Adventist Church in 1863.

Later life

Bates remained an energetic evangelist into old age, preaching across the American Midwest and helping to plant congregations. He also championed health reform, having given up alcohol, tobacco, tea, coffee and rich foods decades earlier. He died in Battle Creek, Michigan, in 1872.
