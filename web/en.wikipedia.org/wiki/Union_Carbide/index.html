Union Carbide

Union Carbide Corporation is an American chemical corporation founded in 1917. It became a wholly owned subsidiary of Dow Chemical Company (now Dow Inc.) in 2001. The company was a major producer of petrochemicals, industrial gases, plastics and graphite products, and was known for consumer brands such as Eveready batteries and Glad food storage products (both later divested).

History

The Union Carbide and Carbon Corporation was formed in 1917 from a merger of several companies making calcium carbide, gases and carbon products. It grew into one of the largest chemical companies in the world, developing processes such as the UNIPOL polyethylene process.

Bhopal disaster

The company is widely known for the Bhopal disaster of 2–3 December 1984, in which a methyl isocyanate gas leak at a pesticide plant owned by its subsidiary Union Carbide India Limited killed thousands of people and injured hundreds of thousands. Union Carbide reached a settlement with the Government of India in 1989 for US$470 million. The disaster remains one of the world's worst industrial accidents and a major case in discussions of corporate responsibility.
