Common Rule

The Common Rule governs federally funded research involving human subjects in the United States.

The Common Rule is a rule of ethics in the United States regarding human subject research, formally the Federal Policy for the Protection of Human Subjects, codified at 45 CFR part 46, subpart A. It was adopted in 1991 by multiple federal departments and agencies, each of which adopted the same regulatory text, hence the name.

Provisions

  • Institutions engaged in covered research must give a written assurance of compliance and designate an Institutional Review Board (IRB).
  • Research protocols must be reviewed and approved by an IRB before human subjects are enrolled.
  • Researchers must obtain and document the informed consent of subjects, covering required elements such as the purpose of the research, risks, benefits, and the right to withdraw.
  • Additional protections apply to vulnerable populations, addressed in separate subparts for pregnant women, prisoners, and children.

History and revision

The Common Rule grew out of the National Research Act of 1974 and the ethical framework of the Belmont Report (1979). A comprehensive revision, published in 2017 and generally applicable from January 2019, updated consent requirements, introduced broad consent for stored identifiable data and biospecimens, and exempted some low-risk research categories.
