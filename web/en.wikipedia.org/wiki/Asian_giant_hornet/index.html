Asian giant hornet

Scientific name: Vespa mandarinia
Family: Vespidae
Native range: East and Southeast Asia
Wingspan: up to 7.5 cm

Vespa mandarinia, the world's largest hornet.

The Asian giant hornet (Vespa mandarinia) is the world's largest hornet, native to temperate and tropical East Asia, South Asia and parts of the Russian Far East. Queens can exceed 5 cm in body length with a wingspan of about 7.5 cm. The species is a formidable predator of other insects, most notoriously honey bees, and its powerful sting can be dangerous to humans.

Description

The hornet has a broad orange head, dark brown antennae, a dark thorax and an abdomen banded in orange and dark brown. Its sting is about 6 mm long and delivers a potent venom containing a cytolytic peptide, mastoparan, capable of dissolving tissue; the sting can be used repeatedly.

Predation on honey bees

Asian giant hornets mark honey bee colonies with pheromones and attack in coordinated "slaughter phase" assaults in which a few dozen hornets can destroy a colony of tens of thousands of European honey bees within hours, decapitating the bees and carrying the protein-rich thoraxes back to feed their larvae. Japanese honey bees (Apis cerana japonica) have evolved a defence: they swarm an invading scout hornet and vibrate their flight muscles to form a "bee ball" whose internal temperature and carbon dioxide levels kill the hornet.

North American detections

In 2019 the species was detected in British Columbia, Canada, and in Washington State, USA, prompting eradication efforts and widespread media coverage under the sensational name "murder hornets". Several nests were located and destroyed. No detections have been reported since 2021, and in December 2024 authorities announced the species had been eradicated from the United States.

Danger to humans

Although not especially aggressive away from the nest, the hornet's sting is intensely painful and can cause anaphylaxis or, in rare cases involving multiple stings, death. In Japan, the hornets kill a number of people each year, and they are also collected as food and used in traditional beverages.
