Commonwealth Games

The Commonwealth Games, often referred to as the Friendly Games, is a quadrennial international multi-sport event among athletes from the Commonwealth of Nations, which consists mostly of territories of the former British Empire. It is the world's first fully inclusive international multi-sport event, integrating para-sports into the programme.

History

First held in 1930 in Hamilton, Ontario, as the British Empire Games, the event has been staged every four years since except 1942 and 1946 (cancelled due to World War II). It was renamed the British Empire and Commonwealth Games in 1954, the British Commonwealth Games in 1970, and the Commonwealth Games in 1978.

Participating nations

There are 56 member nations of the Commonwealth, but 72 teams compete because England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland, and various territories and island nations send separate teams. Australia has been the most successful nation overall, followed by England and Canada.

Sports

Core sports include athletics, swimming, track cycling, gymnastics, netball, rugby sevens, and weightlifting. Hosts may add optional sports such as cricket — women's T20 cricket debuted at Birmingham 2022.

Recent and future Games

Birmingham hosted in 2022. Victoria, Australia, was due to host in 2026 but withdrew in 2023 citing costs; Glasgow agreed to host a scaled-down 2026 edition.
