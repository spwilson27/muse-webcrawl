Four-wave mixing

FieldNonlinear optics
NonlinearityThird-order (χ(3))
EffectsNew frequencies generated from interacting waves

Four-wave mixing (FWM) is an intermodulation phenomenon in nonlinear optics, whereby interactions between two or three wavelengths produce a new wavelength. It is a third-order nonlinear process arising from the χ(3) susceptibility of a medium, in which photons from the input fields are annihilated and new photons are created at mixed frequencies, conserving energy and momentum (phase matching).

Mechanism

In the degenerate case, two pump photons at frequency ωp generate a signal photon and an idler photon whose frequencies satisfy 2ωp = ωs + ωi. Efficient generation requires phase matching so the interacting waves stay in step through the medium.

Applications and impairments

Four-wave mixing is exploited for wavelength conversion, optical phase conjugation, parametric amplification, and supercontinuum generation. In wavelength-division multiplexed fiber-optic systems, however, FWM between channels creates crosstalk and is a major impairment in dense channel plans, particularly near the fiber's zero-dispersion wavelength.
