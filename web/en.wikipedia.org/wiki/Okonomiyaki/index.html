Okonomiyaki

Okonomiyaki (お好み焼き) is a Japanese savoury pancake dish consisting of wheat flour batter cooked on a griddle (teppan) with cabbage and a variety of ingredients. The name derives from okonomi, "as you like it," and yaki, "grilled." Renowned as the signature food of Osaka and Hiroshima, it is often cooked at the table in restaurants or prepared at home.

Styles

In the Kansai (Osaka) style, shredded cabbage and other ingredients — pork belly, seafood, cheese, mochi — are mixed into the batter before cooking. The cake is finished with a sweet brown okonomiyaki sauce, Japanese mayonnaise, aonori (green laver), and katsuobushi flakes. In the Hiroshima style, ingredients are layered rather than mixed, and the dish invariably includes fried yakisoba or udon noodles (modan-yaki) and often a fried egg. Tokyo has its own runnier relative, monjayaki.

History

Okonomiyaki evolved from earlier griddled batter snacks of the Edo and Meiji periods, such as funoyaki and senbei-jiru, and took its modern form in the pre-war and post-war years, when it provided an inexpensive, filling meal built around cheap cabbage and flour. It is now celebrated at festivals and in dedicated restaurant districts, notably Dōtonbori in Osaka and Okonomi-mura in Hiroshima.
