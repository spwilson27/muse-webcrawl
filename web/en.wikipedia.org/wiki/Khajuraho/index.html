Khajuraho

Location: Chhatarpur district, Madhya Pradesh, India
Known for: Khajuraho Group of Monuments
UNESCO World Heritage Site (1986)

Khajuraho is a town in the Indian state of Madhya Pradesh, renowned for the Khajuraho Group of Monuments: a group of Hindu and Jain temples famous for their nagara-style architecture and intricate stone sculptures, including celebrated erotic carvings.

History

The temples were built between about 885 and 1050 CE by the Chandela dynasty. Of an original 85 temples, about 25 survive, spread over roughly 20 square kilometres. After the Chandelas' decline the temples were largely abandoned and overgrown until their rediscovery in the 19th century.

Architecture and art

The temples rise on high platforms with soaring shikharas (spires) symbolising Mount Meru. Their walls are covered with thousands of sculptures depicting deities, celestial maidens (surasundaris), musicians, warriors, everyday life and, on a minority of panels, erotic scenes whose meaning scholars link to tantric practice and the four aims of life in Hindu thought.
