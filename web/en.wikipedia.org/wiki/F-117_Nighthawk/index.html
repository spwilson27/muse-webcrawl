Lockheed F-117 Nighthawk

The Lockheed F-117 Nighthawk is a retired American single-seat, subsonic, twin-engine stealth attack aircraft developed by Lockheed's secretive Skunk Works division. It was the first operational aircraft designed around stealth technology, using faceted surfaces to scatter radar waves. Despite the "F" (fighter) designation, the F-117 was purely an attack aircraft with no air-to-air capability.

Development

The design grew out of the experimental Have Blue demonstrator of 1977, which proved that a faceted airframe, based on radar-cross-section calculations derived from the work of Soviet physicist Pyotr Ufimtsev, could achieve very low observability. The F-117 first flew in 1981 at Groom Lake, Nevada, and its existence was not officially acknowledged until 1988, when a grainy photograph was released.

Operational history

The Nighthawk saw its first combat in Panama in 1989 and became famous during the 1991 Gulf War, flying some of the opening strikes on Baghdad. It also flew in Kosovo in 1999, where one F-117 (82-0806) was shot down by a Yugoslav S-125 surface-to-air missile — the only Nighthawk lost in combat. The type was formally retired by the United States Air Force in 2008, although a small number have since been flown in research and aggressor roles.
