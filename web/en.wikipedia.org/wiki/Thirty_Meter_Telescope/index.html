The Thirty Meter Telescope (TMT) is a planned extremely large telescope (ELT) that has become controversial due to its planned location on Mauna Kea, on the island of Hawaiʻi in the United States, a site considered sacred by many Native Hawaiians. The TMT would become the largest visible-light telescope on Mauna Kea if constructed.

Design

The telescope's primary mirror consists of 492 hexagonal segments, each 1.44 metres across, forming a 30-metre aperture with about nine times the collecting area of the largest existing telescopes. With adaptive optics, it is designed to achieve spatial resolution more than twelve times sharper than the Hubble Space Telescope at near-infrared wavelengths.

Partnership

The project is an international partnership of institutions in the United States, Canada, Japan, China and India, coordinated by the TMT International Observatory.

Protests and site dispute

Construction, which broke ground in 2014, was halted by protests in 2015; the Hawaiʻi Supreme Court invalidated the original construction permit on due-process grounds, and a new permit was issued in 2018. Large protests in 2019 blocked the access road to Mauna Kea for months. The observatories on La Palma in the Canary Islands are the project's designated alternative site. As of the mid-2020s the project remained unbuilt, with funding and site decisions pending.
