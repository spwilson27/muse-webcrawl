Franz Ritter von Soxhlet (12 January 1848 – 5 May 1926) was a German agricultural chemist best known for inventing the Soxhlet extractor, a piece of laboratory glassware that remains in routine use for extracting compounds from solid materials.

Life and career

Soxhlet was born in Brno (then Brünn) and studied agricultural chemistry, earning his doctorate in Leipzig in 1872. He held posts in Vienna and later became professor at the Technical University of Munich, where he directed research on dairy chemistry and infant nutrition.

Contributions

In 1879 Soxhlet described his extractor, which continuously washes a solid sample with freshly distilled solvent; it became the standard apparatus for determining the fat content of milk and other foodstuffs. He also developed the Soxhlet–Henkel titration method for measuring the acidity of milk, and in 1886 proposed applying pasteurization to milk intended for infants, advocating the sterilization of milk for babies.

Death

Soxhlet died in Munich on 5 May 1926.
