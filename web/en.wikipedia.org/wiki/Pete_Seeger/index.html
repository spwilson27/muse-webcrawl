Peter Seeger (May 3, 1919 – January 27, 2014) was an American folk singer, songwriter and social activist. A fixture of American music for seven decades, he co-founded the Almanac Singers with Woody Guthrie and, later, the Weavers, whose 1950 recording of "Goodnight, Irene" topped the charts before the group was blacklisted during the McCarthy era.

Career

Seeger, the son of musicologist Charles Seeger, was introduced to the five-string banjo as a teenager and worked as an assistant to Alan Lomax at the Archive of American Folk Song. He wrote or popularised songs that became standards of the folk revival and the civil rights movement, including "If I Had a Hammer", "Turn! Turn! Turn!", "Where Have All the Flowers Gone?" and his adaptation of the spiritual "We Shall Overcome". Subpoenaed by the House Un-American Activities Committee in 1955, he refused to answer questions about his associations on First Amendment grounds and was indicted for contempt of Congress; the conviction was overturned on appeal in 1962.

Later life

From the 1960s Seeger campaigned for peace, civil rights and the environment; he founded the Clearwater organisation to clean up the Hudson River, sailing the sloop Clearwater as a floating classroom. He received the National Medal of Arts, induction into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, and performed at President Barack Obama's inaugural celebration in 2009, aged 89. His banjo head bore the inscription: "This machine surrounds hate and forces it to surrender."
