Perge

Perge (Greek: Πέργη) was an ancient Greek city in Pamphylia, in present-day Antalya Province, Turkey, one of the region's richest and best-preserved archaeological sites.

Perge
Location: Aksu, Antalya, Turkey
Region: Pamphylia
Notable: Stadium, theatre, Hellenistic towers
Visited by: Paul the Apostle (Acts 13–14)

History

Settled from the Bronze Age and refounded in the Hellenistic period, Perge prospered under Rome. The mathematician Apollonius of Perga was born here. Paul and Barnabas preached in Perge on the first missionary journey. Wealthy citizens such as the benefactress Plancia Magna embellished the city in the 2nd century AD. It declined in late antiquity but remained a bishopric.

Site

Surviving remains include twin Hellenistic gate towers, a colonnaded main street with a water channel, a large stadium (about 12,000 seats), a theatre, baths, agora, nymphaeum and city walls. Excavations since 1946 have uncovered notable mosaics and sculpture, now in Antalya Museum.
