Georgian uprising on Texel

The Georgian uprising on Texel (Dutch: Opstand der Georgiërs) was an insurrection by the 882nd Infantry Battalion Königin Tamara (Queen Tamar Battalion) of the Georgian Legion — a German Wehrmacht unit manned by Georgian Soviet prisoners of war and émigrés — stationed on the occupied Dutch island of Texel. Beginning on 5 April 1945, it was one of the last battles of the Second World War in Europe and continued even after the German surrender, until Canadian troops landed on the island on 20 May 1945.

Background

The battalion, commanded on the Georgian side by Shalva Loladze, a former Soviet air force officer, was posted to Texel as part of the Atlantic Wall defences. Learning that they were to be transferred to the mainland to fight the Allies in Germany, the Georgians resolved to seize the island, hoping to hold it until Allied forces arrived.

The uprising

On the night of 5–6 April 1945 the Georgians rose and killed some 400 German soldiers, many in their sleep, seizing most of the island except the coastal artillery batteries at the northern and southern ends. The expected Allied support never came. German reinforcements, including artillery and troops of the 163rd Marine-Schützenregiment, landed from the mainland and retook the island after weeks of brutal fighting. The Germans executed captured Georgians and carried out reprisals against Dutch islanders who had helped the rebels.

Casualties and remembrance

Around 800 Germans, 565 Georgians, and 120 Texel civilians were killed. The 228 surviving Georgians were repatriated to the Soviet Union, where many were sent to labour camps as suspected collaborators. The Georgian dead are buried in the Loladze war cemetery on the Hoge Berg near Den Burg, visited in later years by both Dutch and Georgian officials. Because fighting raged until well after VE Day, the episode is sometimes called "Europe's last battle."
