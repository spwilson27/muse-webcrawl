Soldier's Medal


Type: Military decoration
Awarded for: Heroism not involving conflict with an enemy
Presented by: United States Army
Established: 2 July 1926

The Soldier's Medal is a military decoration of the United States Army. It is awarded to any person of the Armed Forces of the United States, or of a friendly foreign nation, who while serving with the Army distinguishes himself or herself by heroism not involving actual conflict with an enemy.

Criteria

The performance must have involved personal hazard or danger and a voluntary risk of life under conditions not involving conflict with an armed enemy. Awards are not made solely on the basis of having saved a life; the heroism must have been at a level comparable to that required for the Distinguished Flying Cross. The medal was established by Act of Congress on 2 July 1926.

Design

The medal is a bronze octagon bearing an eagle with raised wings standing on a fasces, and is suspended from a ribbon of red and white stripes flanked by blue bands.

Notable recipients

Recipients include soldiers who rescued comrades from burning vehicles and crashed aircraft, among them Colin Powell, who received the medal for rescuing fellow soldiers from a burning helicopter in Vietnam.
