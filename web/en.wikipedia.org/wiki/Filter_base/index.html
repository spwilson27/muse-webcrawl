Filter base

In mathematics, a filter base (also called a filter basis) is a collection of sets that generates a filter: a family B of nonempty sets is a filter base if, for any two sets in B, there is a third set in B contained in their intersection. The filter generated by B consists of all supersets of members of B.

Purpose

Filter bases allow filters to be specified conveniently without listing every set in the filter. In topology, the neighbourhoods of a point have natural bases — for example, open balls of radius 1/n around a point — and convergence can be described in terms of such bases. A sequence in a topological space gives rise to a filter base of "tails" of the sequence, which is how filters generalize the notion of convergence beyond metric spaces.

Related concepts

A filter base that is closed under finite intersections and taking supersets within the base is essentially a filter itself. Subbases, countable bases, and Cauchy filter bases are used throughout analysis and topology, notably in defining completeness of uniform spaces and in the theory of nets and ultrafilters.
