Maria Karolina Zofia Felicja Leszczyńska (23 June 1703 – 24 June 1768) was Queen of France as the wife of King Louis XV. The daughter of Stanisław Leszczyński, the deposed King of Poland, she married Louis XV in 1725 and became the longest-serving queen consort of France, holding the position for nearly 43 years.

Early life

Maria was born in Trzebnica in Silesia, the second daughter of Stanisław Leszczyński and Katarzyna Opalińska. Her father's fortunes fluctuated dramatically: briefly King of Poland with Swedish support, he was deposed and the family lived in exile in Sweden, Zweibrücken, and finally Wissembourg in Alsace, in reduced circumstances. Maria received a good education and was known for her piety, kindness, and fluency in several languages.

Marriage

When the engagement of the young Louis XV to the Spanish infanta Mariana Victoria was broken off for political reasons, a bride who could quickly produce an heir was needed. Maria, twenty-two and of proven health, was chosen from a list of candidates partly because her family had no political entanglements. They were married by proxy in Strasbourg on 15 August 1725 and in person at Fontainebleau on 5 September 1725. The marriage produced ten children, eight of whom survived infancy, including Louis, Dauphin of France, father of Louis XVI.

Queen of France

Maria was popular with the French public for her charity, religious devotion, and patronage of music, painting, and Polish culture at Versailles. She introduced Polish dishes and the polonaise at court. After the birth of her last child in 1737, her relationship with the king grew distant as Louis took a series of famous mistresses, including Madame de Pompadour and Madame du Barry, but the queen maintained her dignity and the respect of the court. Her father was installed as Duke of Lorraine in 1737, and she corresponded with him and with Voltaire.

Death

Maria died at Versailles on 24 June 1768, a day after her sixty-fifth birthday. She was buried at the Basilica of Saint-Denis; her heart was placed in the Church of Notre-Dame de Bonsecours in Nancy, near her parents.
