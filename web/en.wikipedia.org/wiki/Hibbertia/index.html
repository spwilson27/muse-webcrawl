Hibbertia, commonly known as guinea flowers, is a genus of flowering plants in the family Dilleniaceae. It is among the largest genera of Australian native plants, with several hundred described species and more awaiting description; species also occur in New Guinea, New Caledonia, Fiji and Madagascar. The genus is named for George Hibbert, an English botanist and plant collector.

Description

Hibbertias are mostly shrubs, from small prostrate groundcovers to plants several metres tall. The flowers of nearly all species are bright yellow, with five deeply notched petals and numerous stamens, often arranged asymmetrically around the carpels. Leaves are usually small and simple, sometimes with toothed margins or a downy underside.

Distribution and ecology

Species are found in nearly every Australian habitat, with the greatest diversity in the heathlands and forests of south-western Western Australia and the Sydney sandstone region. Many are specialised to nutrient-poor soils, and several are pollinated by native bees that perform buzz pollination. Some species are rare and threatened by habitat clearing and dieback disease.

Cultivation

A number of species and cultivars are grown in gardens for their profuse yellow flowers. They generally require sharply drained, acidic soil and full sun or light shade, and are usually propagated from cuttings.
