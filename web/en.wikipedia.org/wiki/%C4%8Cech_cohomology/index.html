Čech cohomology

In mathematics, specifically algebraic topology, Čech cohomology is a cohomology theory based on the properties of open covers of a topological space. It is named after the Czech mathematician Eduard Čech, who introduced it in the 1930s. Given an open cover of a space and a sheaf (or, classically, a coefficient group), the Čech construction assembles a cochain complex from data on finite intersections of cover sets, whose cohomology groups are the Čech cohomology groups.

Construction

Let {Ui} be an open cover of a space X and F a presheaf of abelian groups. The degree-n Čech cochains assign to each ordered (n+1)-fold intersection Ui₀ ∩ … ∩ Uiₙ a section of F over it. An alternating coboundary operator d, built from restriction maps, satisfies d² = 0, giving cochain complexes; their cohomology is the Čech cohomology of the cover. Taking a limit over refinements of covers yields the Čech cohomology of X itself.

Relation to other theories

For paracompact spaces and constant sheaves, Čech cohomology agrees with singular cohomology; more generally it agrees with sheaf cohomology, and for the "good covers" of manifolds (all intersections contractible) it can be computed concretely — a consequence of Leray's theorem. Čech cohomology is a standard computational tool in algebraic geometry, where it computes sheaf cohomology from affine covers, and in complex geometry and the theory of characteristic classes, such as the classification of line bundles by Ȟ¹(X, O*).
