Iraqi Turkmen

The Iraqi Turkmen (also spelled Turkoman or Turcoman) are an ethnic Turkic group native to northern Iraq, principally in the cities of Kirkuk, Tal Afar, Mosul, Erbil, and Altun Kupri. They are generally considered the third-largest ethnic group in Iraq, after Arabs and Kurds, though estimates of their population vary widely.

History

Turkmen presence in Iraq dates to successive waves of Oghuz Turkic settlement beginning under the Umayyad and Abbasid caliphates and expanding under the Seljuks and Ottomans, when Turkmen served as soldiers and administrators. The region they inhabit is sometimes called Turkmeneli.

Language and culture

Iraqi Turkmen speak a dialect of Azerbaijani (South Azerbaijani), though official writing uses Istanbul Turkish. The community is predominantly Muslim, divided between Sunni and Shia, with a small Catholic minority. Under Ba'athist Arabization policies, particularly in Kirkuk, Turkmen faced land confiscation and forced displacement; their political status within disputed territories remains contested since 2003.
