Orthocenter

The orthocenter of a triangle is the point where the triangle's three altitudes intersect. An altitude is the perpendicular segment from a vertex to the line containing the opposite side. The orthocenter is one of the classical triangle centres, usually denoted H. Its position depends on the shape of the triangle: in an acute triangle it lies in the interior; in a right triangle it coincides with the vertex at the right angle; and in an obtuse triangle it lies outside the triangle.

Properties

The orthocenter, the centroid and the circumcenter of any triangle are collinear, lying on the Euler line, with the centroid dividing the segment between the other two in the ratio 2:1. The reflection of the orthocenter across each side of the triangle lies on the circumcircle, and the nine-point circle passes through the feet of the three altitudes. Together with the triangle's vertices, the orthocenter forms an orthocentric system, in which each point is the orthocenter of the triangle formed by the other three.
