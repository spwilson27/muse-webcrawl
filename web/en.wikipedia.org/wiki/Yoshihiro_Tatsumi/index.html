Yoshihiro Tatsumi

Born: 10 June 1935, Osaka, Japan
Died: 7 March 2015 (aged 79), Tokyo, Japan
Occupation: Manga artist
Known for: Founding the gekiga movement

Yoshihiro Tatsumi (Japanese: 辰巳 ヨシヒロ, 10 June 1935 – 7 March 2015) was a Japanese manga artist widely credited with founding gekiga, the movement for serious, adult-oriented "dramatic pictures" that emerged in the late 1950s as an alternative to mainstream children's manga. His bleak, naturalistic short stories about the working poor of postwar Japan have earned him comparison to a comics equivalent of the short-story masters of literature.

Career

Born in Osaka, Tatsumi began publishing manga as a teenager, encouraged by Osamu Tezuka, who visited his home. Feeling constrained by the conventions of children's comics, in 1957 he coined the term gekiga and in 1959 helped found the Gekiga Workshop with fellow artists. Working for the rental-book (kashihon) market, he produced crime stories and noir dramas that drew on film noir and postwar hardship.

Major works

His mature short stories of the late 1960s and 1970s — collected in English as The Push Man and Other Stories, Abandon the Old in Tokyo, and Good-Bye — depict factory workers, prostitutes, and salarymen in tales of alienation and quiet desperation. His sprawling autobiographical graphic novel A Drifting Life (2008), covering his youth and the birth of gekiga, won the Tezuka Osamu Cultural Prize and two Eisner Awards, and was adapted into an animated film by Eric Khoo in 2011.

Legacy

Tatsumi's influence extends across Japanese alternative manga and international art comics; his collected stories, published in English by Drawn & Quarterly under editor Adrian Tomine, introduced gekiga to a broad Western audience late in his life. He died of cancer in Tokyo in 2015.
