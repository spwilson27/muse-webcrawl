1080i is a combination of frame resolution and scan type used in high-definition television (HDTV). The "1080" denotes 1,080 horizontal lines of vertical resolution and the "i" stands for interlaced scan, in which each frame is drawn as two alternating fields of odd and even lines.

Characteristics

A 1080i signal carries 1920 × 1080 pixels in a 16:9 aspect ratio at 25 or 30 frames (50 or 60 fields) per second. Interlacing halves the bandwidth compared with progressive scan at the same frame rate but can produce artefacts such as interline twitter and combing on motion; displays must deinterlace it for modern panels.

Broadcasting

1080i was adopted widely for terrestrial, cable and satellite HDTV broadcasts, including by major networks in the United States, while others chose 720p. It contrasts with 1080p, the progressive-scan format used by Blu-ray and streaming services.
