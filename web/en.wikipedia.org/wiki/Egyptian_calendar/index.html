Ancient Egyptian calendar

The ancient Egyptian calendar was a solar calendar with a 365-day year, consisting of three seasons of 120 days each, plus an intercalary month of five epagomenal days. It was the first known calendar with a 365-day year.

Egyptian calendar
Year: 365 days (12×30 + 5)
Seasons: Akhet, Peret, Shemu
Epoch: c. 3000 BC

Structure

Seasons were Akhet (inundation), Peret (growing/emergence), and Shemu (harvest/low water), each of four 30-day months, with weeks of 10 days (decans). Five extra days celebrated the births of Osiris, Isis, Set, Nephthys, and Horus.

Wandering year

With no leap year, the calendar drifted one day every four years against the solar year — a "wandering year" — completing a Sothic cycle of 1,460 years when it realigned with the heliacal rising of Sirius (Sothis). The Canopus Decree (238 BC) attempted a leap day, but reform only succeeded under Augustus, creating the Coptic/Alexandrian calendar.
