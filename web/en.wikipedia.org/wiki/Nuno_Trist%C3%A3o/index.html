Nuno Tristão (died c. 1446) was a Portuguese navigator and explorer of the early Age of Discovery, a knight of the household of Prince Henry the Navigator. He commanded some of the first Portuguese expeditions to push beyond Cape Bojador and Cape Blanc along the West African coast in the 1440s.

Voyages

In 1441 Tristão, together with Antão Gonçalves, captained an expedition that reached Cape Blanc and returned to Portugal with the first African captives seized south of the Sahara — an event chronicled by Gomes Eanes de Zurara and marking the beginning of the Portuguese slave trade from Guinea. In 1443 he reached the island of Arguin, where a trading post and fort were soon established. In a later voyage, conventionally dated 1446, Tristão sailed far to the south, reaching the vicinity of the Geba River in what is now Guinea-Bissau — then the southernmost point reached by Europeans on the African coast.

Death

According to Zurara's chronicle, Tristão and most of his landing party were killed when local inhabitants attacked their boats with poisoned arrows as they explored a river estuary. A handful of survivors, including young crew members, navigated the caravel back across the Atlantic to Portugal, a feat celebrated in the chronicles.

Legacy

Tristão's voyages opened the Guinea coast to Portuguese trade in gold and enslaved people, with grave consequences for West African societies. His name is commemorated in geography and in Portuguese historiography of the discoveries.
