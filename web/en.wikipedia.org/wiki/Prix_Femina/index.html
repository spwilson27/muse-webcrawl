Prix Femina

The Prix Femina is a French literary prize created in 1904 by 22 writers for the magazine La Vie heureuse (today known as Femina). The prize is decided each year by an exclusively female jury, although the authors of the winning works do not have to be women. It is awarded annually, generally at the beginning of November, alongside prizes for foreign literature (Prix Femina étranger) and for essays (Prix Femina essai).


The Prix Femina is one of France's major autumn literary prizes.

History

The prize was created in reaction to the Prix Goncourt, whose jury was all male and which had rarely honoured women writers. Its founders intended it as an alternative honouring literary merit as judged by women of letters. The first winner, in 1904, was Myriam Harry for La Conquête de Jérusalem. Notable later winners include works by Colette, Antoine de Saint-Exupéry (for Wind, Sand and Stars, in the foreign/essay sphere of the prize's history), and many leading figures of modern French literature.

Selection

The jury, composed of women writers and critics, announces its shortlist in the autumn and awards the prize at the Hôtel de Crillon in Paris. Together with the Prix Goncourt and the Prix Renaudot, the Prix Femina forms the trio of major French literary prizes awarded each autumn, and winning it substantially boosts a book's sales.
