PlayStation Portable

The PlayStation Portable (PSP-1000)

The PlayStation Portable (PSP) is a handheld game console developed by Sony Computer Entertainment, released in Japan in December 2004 and in North America and Europe in 2005. It was Sony's first handheld system and competed directly with Nintendo's DS.

Features

The PSP featured a 4.3-inch widescreen LCD, analogue nub controller, Wi-Fi connectivity, and games distributed on the proprietary Universal Media Disc (UMD), an optical format also used briefly for films. It could play music and video, display photos and browse the web, positioning it as a portable multimedia device as much as a games machine. Several hardware revisions followed, including the slimmer PSP-2000 and PSP-3000, the download-only PSP Go (2009) and the budget PSP-E1000.

Reception and legacy

The PSP was a substantial commercial success, selling over 80 million units worldwide, and hosted acclaimed games such as God of War: Chains of Olympus, Monster Hunter Freedom Unite, Grand Theft Auto: Liberty City Stories and Crisis Core: Final Fantasy VII. Production ended in 2014; its successor was the PlayStation Vita.
