Pen and ink

MediumInk applied with pen or brush on paper
TechniquesHatching, cross-hatching, stippling, wash
Notable artistsRembrandt, Van Gogh, Aubrey Beardsley

Pen and ink (or pen-and-ink) is a drawing technique involving the use of a pen or similar implement to apply ink to a surface, traditionally paper. The resulting work is usually monochrome, with tone created through line alone.

Techniques

Because a pen line is essentially uniform in value, artists build light and shade with hatching (parallel lines), cross-hatching (intersecting sets of lines), stippling (dots) and controlled variation of line density and weight. Ink washes, applied with a brush, may supplement the line work. Errors are difficult to correct, so the medium rewards a decisive hand.

History and use

Pen and ink has been used since antiquity and flourished in the sketchbooks and studies of Renaissance masters. Rembrandt's reed-pen drawings and Van Gogh's reed-pen landscapes are celebrated examples. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries the medium became standard for book and newspaper illustration, cartoons and comics, where strong reproducible lines were essential.
