Escabeche

Escabeche is an Ibero-American dish of fried or poached fish or meat marinated and served in an acidic sauce — typically vinegar with oil, onions, and spices such as bay leaf, peppercorns and pimentón — eaten cold or at room temperature. The name derives, via Catalan escabetx, from Persian sikbaj, a vinegar stew transmitted through Arabic cooking.

Escabeche
OriginIberia, from Persian/Arab roots
Main ingredientsFish or meat, vinegar, oil, onion, spices
ServingCold or room temperature

History

Marinating cooked food in vinegar preserved it before refrigeration — escabeche kept for days or weeks, making it shipboard and travel food. Spain and Portugal spread versions worldwide: sardines or mackerel en escabeche are canned staples in Iberia.

Regional varieties

In the Philippines, escabeche is whole fried fish in a sweet-sour sauce thickened with cornstarch, with bell peppers and ginger. Latin American versions include Peruvian escabeche de pescado with ají peppers, Mexican pickled vegetables en escabeche (jalapeños, carrots), and Caribbean pickled fish, the root of Jamaican escovitch fish served with festival bread.
