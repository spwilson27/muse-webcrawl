The osprey (Pandion haliaetus), also called sea hawk, river hawk and fish hawk, is a diurnal, fish-eating bird of prey with a cosmopolitan range. It is a large raptor, reaching more than 60 cm (24 in) in length and 180 cm (71 in) across the wings. It is brown on the upperparts and predominantly greyish on the head and underparts.

The osprey tolerates a wide variety of habitats, nesting in any location near a body of water providing an adequate food supply. It is found on all continents except Antarctica, although in South America it occurs only as a non-breeding migrant.

Diet and hunting

As its other common names suggest, the osprey's diet consists almost exclusively of fish. It possesses specialised physical characteristics: reversible outer toes, closable nostrils, barbed pads on the soles and backward-facing scales on the talons to grip slippery fish. Ospreys spot prey from 10 to 40 metres above the water, hover, then plunge feet-first, sometimes submerging completely, before carrying the fish head-first to reduce wind resistance.

Distribution and migration

Ospreys breed near freshwater lakes and rivers and coastal brackish waters, building large stick nests on trees, poles or artificial platforms. Northern populations are migratory: European birds winter in Africa, while North American birds fly to South America. The Australian population is largely sedentary.

Conservation

Osprey populations crashed in the mid-20th century because the pesticide DDT thinned their eggshells. Following DDT bans in the 1970s and nest-platform programmes, numbers recovered strongly, and the species is now listed as Least Concern—a frequently cited conservation success story.
