M51 (missile)

Place of originFrance

DesignerArianeGroup (formerly EADS Astrium / Airbus DS)

Range8,000–10,000 km (estimated)

The M51 is a French submarine-launched ballistic missile, the sea-based core of France's nuclear deterrent (force de dissuasion). It replaced the M45 aboard the Triomphant-class ballistic missile submarines of the French Navy and entered service in 2010.

Development

Development began in the 1990s to provide greater range, payload and accuracy than the M45. The three-stage solid-fuel missile is about 12 metres long and weighs over 50 tonnes. The first flight test took place in November 2006 from the Biscarrosse test centre; an early test from the submarine Le Vigilant in May 2013 failed, but subsequent tests succeeded and the programme continued.

Versions

The initial M51.1 carries TN 75 warheads inherited from the M45. The improved M51.2, fielded from the mid-2010s, carries the new tête nucléaire océanique (TNO) warhead and has increased range. A further development, the M51.3, with an improved third stage, has been under development for the 2020s. Each missile carries multiple independently targetable re-entry vehicles fitted with penetration aids; exact performance figures remain classified.
