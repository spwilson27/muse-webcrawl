TWA Flight Center

The TWA Flight Center, also known as the Trans World Airlines Flight Center, is the airport terminal and hotel complex at John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) in New York City. The original terminal building, designed by Finnish-American architect Eero Saarinen and opened in 1962, is a celebrated example of mid-century modern architecture, its thin-shell concrete roof shaped like a bird in flight symbolising the optimism of the Jet Age.

History

Saarinen's design, executed with associates Kevin Roche and John Dinkeloo, featured a soaring central terminal flanked by two red-carpeted departure-arrival tubes. The building served as Trans World Airlines' hub terminal until TWA ceased operations in 2001, after which it was preserved as a New York City designated landmark and listed on the National Register of Historic Places, though left vacant for many years while JetBlue's Terminal 5 was built behind it.

TWA Hotel

After a long preservation campaign, the terminal was restored and reopened in May 2019 as the headhouse of the TWA Hotel, a 512-room hotel with restaurants, event space and a rooftop pool overlooking the runways. The restoration retained the restored split-flap departures board, sunken lounge and other original details.
