Siege of Limerick (1690)

The siege of Limerick of 1690 was the climactic failure of William III's first Irish campaign in the Williamite War. After his victory at the Battle of the Boyne, William marched on Limerick, the strongest remaining Jacobite stronghold in Ireland, defended by Patrick Sarsfield and a French-officered garrison. Despite bombardment and a costly assault on a breach, the Williamite army was repulsed and forced to raise the siege on 31 August 1690, partly because Sarsfield's daring cavalry raid had destroyed William's siege train at Ballyneety.

Background

Following the Boyne (1 July 1690) the Jacobite army fell back behind the Shannon, and Limerick — split by the river into Englishtown and Irishtown and defended by medieval walls strengthened with earthworks — became its principal base, garrisoned by some 14,000–20,000 men. William arrived before the city on 17 August with about 25,000 troops but only a light battering train, his heavy artillery still on the road behind him.

Sarsfield's raid

On the night of 11–12 August, before the siege proper, Sarsfield led a picked force of horse across the Shannon and fell upon the Williamite siege train at Ballyneety, near Cullen, County Tipperary. The Jacobites spiked or blew up the guns, destroyed the powder and pontoons, and escaped — a blow that crippled William's ability to breach the walls and became a legend of the war.

The assault

With a few guns William's engineers nonetheless opened a breach in the Irishtown walls by 27 August. The storming assault that day was met by fierce resistance — famously joined by the women of Limerick, who hurled stones and bottles at the attackers in the breach — and was beaten off with heavy losses after hours of fighting. With autumn rains beginning, his army sickening, and his siege artillery ruined, William raised the siege on 31 August and soon left Ireland, delegating command to Godert de Ginkel. Limerick finally fell a year later, after the Battle of Aughrim, and was surrendered under the Treaty of Limerick of October 1691.
