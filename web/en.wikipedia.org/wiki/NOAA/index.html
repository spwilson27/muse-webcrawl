The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) is a United States scientific and regulatory agency within the Department of Commerce. Its mission is to understand and predict changes in climate, weather, oceans and coasts; to share that knowledge; and to conserve and manage coastal and marine ecosystems and resources.

History

NOAA was created in 1970 under President Richard Nixon, consolidating older bodies including the Coast and Geodetic Survey (founded 1807, often cited as the oldest US scientific agency), the Weather Bureau (1870) and the Bureau of Commercial Fisheries. The reorganisation gathered the nation's principal ocean and atmosphere functions into a single administration.

Organisation

NOAA's major line offices include the National Weather Service, which issues forecasts and severe-weather warnings; the National Ocean Service, responsible for nautical charts, tides and marine sanctuaries; NOAA Fisheries (the National Marine Fisheries Service), which manages federal fisheries and protected species; the National Environmental Satellite, Data, and Information Service, operating the GOES and polar-orbiting weather satellite fleets; and the Office of Oceanic and Atmospheric Research, which runs laboratories studying climate, air quality and the oceans. The NOAA Commissioned Officer Corps, one of the US uniformed services, operates the agency's ships and aircraft, including the famous "Hurricane Hunter" planes.

Activities

NOAA produces the official US hurricane outlooks and storm-surge warnings, maintains the national climate record through its National Centers for Environmental Information, monitors phenomena such as El Niño, charts US waters, manages thirteen national marine sanctuaries and the Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument jointly with other agencies, and publishes fisheries statistics and stock assessments. Its weather data underpin most commercial forecasting in the United States.
