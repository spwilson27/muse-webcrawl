Fairfax County, Virginia

Fairfax County, officially the County of Fairfax, is a county in the Commonwealth of Virginia, in the United States. It is part of Northern Virginia and the Washington metropolitan area. With a population of about 1.15 million (2020 census), it is Virginia's most populous jurisdiction, and one of the most affluent counties in the United States, with a median household income well above $100,000. The county seat is the independent City of Fairfax, which is an enclave within the county.

History

The county was formed in 1742 from Prince William County and named for Thomas Fairfax, 6th Lord Fairfax of Cameron, proprietor of the Northern Neck. George Washington lived at Mount Vernon in the county, and George Mason at Gunston Hall. During the American Civil War the county saw fighting at Chantilly and two battles around Fairfax Court House. After the Second World War, suburbanisation driven by federal employment transformed it from farmland into one of the largest suburban counties in the country.

Economy and communities

Fairfax County hosts major employment centres at Tysons, Reston and Herndon along the Dulles Technology Corridor, home to numerous defence contractors, technology firms and the headquarters of companies such as Northrop Grumman innovation centres, Capital One and Hilton Worldwide. Large communities include Annandale, Burke, Centreville, Chantilly, McLean, Reston, Springfield and Vienna. The county is served by Fairfax County Public Schools, one of the largest school divisions in the U.S., and by Washington Dulles International Airport, part of which lies in the county.
