Anti-access/area denial

Anti-access/area denial (A2/AD) is a military strategy used by a weaker opponent to prevent a stronger adversary from entering or operating within a contested area. Anti-access actions aim to stop an adversary from deploying forces into a theater, typically at long range, while area denial actions impede freedom of movement within the area, usually at shorter range.

Capabilities

Modern A2/AD networks integrate long-range sensors, anti-ship ballistic and cruise missiles, advanced air defense systems, submarines, naval mines, and cyber and electronic warfare capabilities. The concept is most often discussed in relation to China's military modernization in the Western Pacific and Russia's deployments around Kaliningrad, Crimea, and its coasts, where layered defenses are intended to raise the cost of intervention by the United States and its allies.

Responses

Counter-strategies have included long-range strike systems, stealth aircraft, unmanned platforms, dispersed basing, and operational concepts such as the U.S. AirSea Battle and its successor, the Joint Concept for Access and Maneuver in the Global Commons (JAM-GC). Analysts debate the extent to which A2/AD "bubbles" are genuinely impenetrable versus porous to a determined, well-equipped opponent.
