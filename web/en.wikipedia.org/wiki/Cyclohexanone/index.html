Cyclohexanone

Cyclohexanone, (CH₂)₅CO, is a six-membered cyclic ketone.

Cyclohexanone is the organic compound with the formula (CH2)5CO. The molecule consists of a six-carbon cyclic ring with a ketone functional group. This colourless, oily liquid has an odour reminiscent of acetone and peppermint. Over time, samples assume a yellow colour due to oxidation. Cyclohexanone is slightly soluble in water and miscible with common organic solvents.

Production

Billions of kilograms are produced annually, mainly as a precursor to nylon. Cyclohexanone is produced by the air oxidation of cyclohexane, which also yields cyclohexanol (the mixture is called KA oil, for ketone–alcohol), or by the hydrogenation of phenol. The KA oil is further processed — cyclohexanol being dehydrogenated to more cyclohexanone — and then oxidised with nitric acid to adipic acid, or converted via its oxime to caprolactam.

Uses

The great majority of cyclohexanone is consumed in producing precursors to nylon 6 and nylon 6,6: oxidation gives adipic acid, and conversion to cyclohexanone oxime followed by a Beckmann rearrangement gives caprolactam. Cyclohexanone is also used as a solvent and in smaller-volume chemical syntheses.

Safety

Cyclohexanone is flammable and an irritant; workplace exposure is regulated, and like other ketones it can act as a central nervous system depressant at high concentrations.
