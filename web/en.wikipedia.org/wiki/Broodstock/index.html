Broodstock

Broodstock
FieldAquaculture, animal husbandry
PurposeBreeding stock for producing offspring (seed) for rearing or restocking

Broodstock, or broodfish in the case of fish, are a group of mature individuals used in aquaculture for breeding purposes. Broodstock can be a population of animals maintained in captivity as a source of replacement parents, or specifically as a source of eggs and juveniles ("seed") for production. They are generally kept in ponds or tanks holding conditions such as photoperiod, temperature and pH optimised to promote gonadal development and spawning.

Management

Broodstock management involves maintaining environmental conditions that promote maximum survival, growth and gonad development, and supplying a nutritionally complete diet, since egg and larval quality depend heavily on broodstock nutrition — particularly essential fatty acids. In fish hatcheries, spawning may be natural, induced by hormone treatment, or achieved by stripping eggs and milt and fertilising them artificially. Selection of broodstock also has a genetic dimension: hatcheries select fast-growing or disease-resistant individuals, but must avoid inbreeding depression by maintaining sufficiently large, genetically diverse stocks and periodically introducing wild-caught animals.

Uses

Beyond food production, broodstock are maintained for conservation hatcheries that rear endangered species for release, for stock enhancement of wild fisheries, and in terrestrial animal husbandry, where the term corresponds to breeding stock generally.
