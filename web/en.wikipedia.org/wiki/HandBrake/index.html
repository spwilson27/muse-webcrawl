HandBrake

HandBrake is a free and open-source transcoder for digital video files. It was originally developed in 2003 by Eric Petit to make ripping a film from a DVD to a data storage device easier. HandBrake's backend contains comparatively little original code; the program is an integration of many third-party audio and video libraries, including codecs such as x264, x265, and FFmpeg.

Features

HandBrake runs on Linux, macOS, and Windows. It can convert video from nearly any format to a selection of modern, widely supported codecs (notably H.264, H.265, and AV1) in MP4, MKV, and WebM containers. Features include preset profiles for devices, chapter markers, subtitle support, video filters, and batch encoding.

History

Development was dormant between 2006 and 2007 before being revived by the community. Version 1.0.0 was released in December 2016, adding VP9 and other modern codec support. HandBrake does not decrypt copy-protected discs on its own.
