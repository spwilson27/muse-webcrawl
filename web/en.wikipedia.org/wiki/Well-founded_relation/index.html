Well-founded relation

In mathematics, a well-founded relation is a binary relation R on a set X such that every non-empty subset S of X has an element that is minimal with respect to R; that is, an element m of S such that no element of S stands in the relation R to m. Equivalently, a well-founded relation has no infinite descending chains.

Definition

A relation R on a set X is called well-founded if every non-empty subset of X has an R-minimal element. If the relation is also a total order, it is called a well-order. Well-founded relations need not be transitive or total, and the notion generalises the idea behind mathematical induction.

Induction and recursion

Well-founded relations support a principle of well-founded induction, also called structural induction in many applications: to prove a property for all elements of X, it suffices to show that whenever the property holds for all elements below x, it holds for x. Well-founded recursion likewise allows functions to be defined by cases on smaller elements, guaranteeing termination.

Examples

The usual ordering of the natural numbers is well-founded. The "divides" relation on positive integers, suitably restricted, is well-founded, as is the subterm relation used in term rewriting and the membership relation in set theory, whose well-foundedness is asserted by the axiom of foundation.
