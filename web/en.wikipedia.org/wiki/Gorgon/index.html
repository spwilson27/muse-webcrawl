The Gorgons (Greek: Γοργών, Gorgṓn) are three monstrous sisters in Greek mythology — Stheno, Euryale, and Medusa — described as having hair of living, venomous snakes and a gaze that turned onlookers to stone. They were the daughters of the sea deities Phorcys and Ceto.

Of the three, only Medusa was mortal, and she was slain by the hero Perseus, who beheaded her while avoiding her direct gaze by means of a mirrored shield. From her severed neck sprang the winged horse Pegasus and the giant Chrysaor.

Mythology

Gorgoneia — depictions of a Gorgon's head — were widely used in ancient Greek art and architecture as protective, apotropaic symbols, notably on shields and temples. In later tradition, Medusa's head was mounted on Athena's aegis. Classical sources vary in describing the Gorgons as hideous or, in later art, as beautiful yet terrifying.

In later culture

The Gorgons, especially Medusa, have remained popular figures in art, literature, and popular culture, from Caravaggio's painted shield to modern fantasy games and films, where "gorgon" often denotes a petrifying monster.
