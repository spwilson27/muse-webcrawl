The Royal Standard of the United Kingdom is the personal flag of the British monarch. It is the Royal Arms in banner form, divided into four quarters: the first and fourth show the three golden lions of England on a red field, the second shows the red lion rampant of Scotland on gold, and the third shows the golden harp of Ireland on blue. A different version is used in Scotland, where the Scottish lion takes precedence in the first and fourth quarters.

Use

The Royal Standard is flown above Buckingham Palace, Windsor Castle and other royal residences when the monarch is in residence, and is lowered when they are absent; it is replaced by the Union Flag at Buckingham Palace when the monarch is away. Unlike the Union Flag, the Royal Standard is never flown at half-mast, because there is always a monarch on the throne — a deceased sovereign is immediately succeeded. When flown on a building, the standard indicates the monarch's presence; it is also draped over the monarch's coffin at a state funeral.

History

The modern standard evolved from the royal banners of the medieval kings of England. Following the union of England and Scotland in 1707 and of Great Britain and Ireland in 1801, the arms were re-quartered, reaching essentially their present form in the reign of Queen Victoria in 1837, when the Hanoverian arms were removed on her accession. Individual standards exist for other members of the Royal Family, differenced with labels and other marks of cadency.
