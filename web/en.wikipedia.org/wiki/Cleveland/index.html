Cleveland

Cleveland is a city in northeastern Ohio, United States, the seat of Cuyahoga County, situated on the southern shore of Lake Erie at the mouth of the Cuyahoga River. With a population of about 372,000 in the 2020 census, it is the second-most populous city in Ohio after Columbus, and the centre of a metropolitan area of roughly two million.

History

Founded in 1796 by surveyors of the Connecticut Land Company led by Moses Cleaveland, the city grew with the Ohio and Erie Canal and railroads into a major Great Lakes manufacturing centre for steel, automobiles and oil refining — John D. Rockefeller built Standard Oil there. Twentieth-century deindustrialisation brought steep population decline from a 1950 peak near 915,000, along with episodes such as the 1969 Cuyahoga River fire that helped spur the U.S. environmental movement.

Culture and economy

Cleveland is home to the Cleveland Clinic, one of the world's leading hospitals; the Cleveland Orchestra; the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame; Playhouse Square; and major-league teams the Guardians (baseball), Browns (football) and Cavaliers (basketball), whose 2016 NBA championship ended the city's 52-year title drought.

Geography

The city lies on glacial lake plains cut by the Cuyahoga valley, with a humid continental climate and lake-effect snow in winter.
