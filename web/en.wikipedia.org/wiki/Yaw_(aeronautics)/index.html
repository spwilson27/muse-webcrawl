Yaw (aeronautics)

A yaw rotation is a movement around the yaw axis of a rigid body that changes the direction it is pointing, to the left or right of its direction of motion. The yaw rate (or yaw velocity) measures the angular velocity of this rotation, typically in degrees per second or radians per second. In aeronautics, yaw is one of the three principal rotations of an aircraft in flight, alongside pitch and roll.

Axes of rotation

An aircraft rotates about three mutually perpendicular axes passing through its centre of gravity: the longitudinal axis (roll), the lateral axis (pitch) and the vertical or normal axis (yaw). Yaw is rotation about the vertical axis, swinging the nose left or right. The angles describing these rotations are a form of Euler angles, and together with position they define the aircraft's attitude.

Control

Yaw is primarily controlled by the rudder, a movable surface on the vertical stabiliser (fin) at the tail. Deflecting the rudder generates a side force at the tail that rotates the nose in the desired direction. Unwanted yaw arises from adverse yaw, a side effect of aileron deflection in turns, which pilots counteract with coordinated rudder input. Directional stability about the yaw axis is provided by the vertical stabiliser, and many aircraft are fitted with a yaw damper to suppress the oscillatory Dutch roll mode.

Measurement

Yaw rate is measured by rate gyroscopes and, in modern aircraft, by inertial reference systems. The heading indicator and turn coordinator present yaw-related information to the pilot in flight.
