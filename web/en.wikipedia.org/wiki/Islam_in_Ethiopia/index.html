Islam in Ethiopia dates to the very beginnings of the faith in the seventh century CE. Ethiopia (Abyssinia) is also significant in Islamic tradition as the destination of the First Hijra, when persecuted followers of Muhammad sought refuge at the court of the Christian king of Aksum. Today Muslims form roughly one-third of Ethiopia's population, making the country home to one of the largest Muslim communities in Africa.

History

The First Hijra

Around 615–616 CE, a group of Muhammad's followers emigrated to the Aksumite Empire, whose ruler — remembered in Islamic tradition as the Negus (al-Najāshī) — refused Meccan demands to hand them over. The village of Negash in Tigray is traditionally associated with these first migrants and contains what is regarded as one of Africa's oldest mosques and cemeteries.

Medieval sultanates

Trade across the Red Sea spread Islam along the coast and into the eastern highlands. Muslim states such as the Sultanate of Shewa, Ifat and later the Adal Sultanate rose on the margins of the Christian Ethiopian empire. In the sixteenth century the Adal general Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi (Ahmad Gragn) conquered much of the highlands before being defeated in 1543 with Portuguese assistance.

Harar and later centuries

The walled city of Harar became a major centre of Islamic learning and commerce, sometimes called the fourth holiest city of Islam. Under the nineteenth-century expansion of the Ethiopian empire, Muslim regions including Harar (1887) were incorporated. In the twentieth century Muslims campaigned for equal recognition; the imperial and Derg regimes both restricted religious life at times, while the post-1991 federal constitution guarantees freedom of religion.

Demographics and practice

Ethiopian Islam is overwhelmingly Sunni, following mainly the Shafi'i school, with strong Sufi traditions including the Qadiriyya and Tijaniyya orders. Pilgrimage centres such as Sheikh Hussein in Bale draw large gatherings. Major Muslim populations live in the Oromia, Somali and Afar regions and in cities including Addis Ababa, Dire Dawa and Jimma.
