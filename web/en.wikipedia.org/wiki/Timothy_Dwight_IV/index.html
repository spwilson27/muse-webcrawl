Timothy Dwight IV (May 14, 1752 – January 11, 1817) was an American academic and educator, a Congregationalist minister, theologian, and author. He was the eighth president of Yale College (1795–1817).

Early life

Dwight was a grandson of the theologian Jonathan Edwards. A child prodigy, he graduated from Yale in 1769 at age 17, later serving as a tutor and as a chaplain in the Continental Army during the American Revolutionary War.

Presidency of Yale

As president of Yale, Dwight transformed the college: he expanded the curriculum, championed the study of science, and led a religious revival among students that formed part of the Second Great Awakening. His orthodox Calvinist preaching countered the influence of Enlightenment skepticism on campus.

Writings

Dwight wrote the epic poem The Conquest of Canaan (1785), a satirical work The Triumph of Infidelity, and the widely read Travels in New-England and New-York, published posthumously.
