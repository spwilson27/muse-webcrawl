Sample-rate conversion

Sample-rate conversion is the process of changing the sampling rate of a discrete signal to obtain a new discrete representation of the underlying continuous signal. It is a routine operation in digital signal processing and digital audio, for example when audio recorded at 48 kHz (video and broadcast standard) must be played on a system running at 44.1 kHz (CD audio standard), or when a digital communication receiver must match a transmitter's symbol rate.

Processes

The two basic operations are:

  • Decimation (downsampling): reducing the sample rate by an integer factor M, preceded by a low-pass anti-aliasing filter that removes frequencies above the new Nyquist limit to prevent aliasing.
  • Interpolation (upsampling): increasing the sample rate by an integer factor L, by inserting zeros between samples and then low-pass filtering (an anti-imaging filter) to reconstruct the values of the new samples.

Combining the two — upsampling by L, filtering, then downsampling by M — achieves conversion by any rational factor L/M. Conversion by arbitrary or time-varying ratios (asynchronous sample-rate conversion) uses polyphase filter banks or interpolation filters such as windowed-sinc filters whose coefficients are computed or selected on the fly.

Quality considerations

The quality of a converter is measured by its passband ripple, stopband attenuation (aliasing and imaging rejection), phase linearity and transient response. High-quality converters may achieve alias rejection of 120 dB or more, while cheap converters can introduce audible artefacts. In oversampling A/D and D/A converters, sample-rate conversion is an integral part of the conversion chain, trading a high internal sample rate for relaxed analogue filtering.

Applications

  • Digital audio workstations, media players and broadcast chains mixing 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz material.
  • Software-defined radio and digital communications receivers.
  • Image resizing and video frame-rate conversion (the two-dimensional analogue of the same problem).
