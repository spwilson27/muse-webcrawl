Hans Christian Andersen (2 April 1805 – 4 August 1875) was a Danish author. Although a prolific writer of plays, travelogues, novels, and poems, he is best remembered for his literary fairy tales, which have been translated into more than 125 languages and have become culturally embedded in the West's collective consciousness, readily accessible to children while presenting lessons of virtue and resilience in the face of adversity for mature readers as well.

His most famous fairy tales include "The Emperor's New Clothes", "The Little Mermaid", "The Nightingale", "The Steadfast Tin Soldier", "The Red Shoes", "The Princess and the Pea", "Thumbelina", "The Snow Queen", "The Ugly Duckling", and "The Little Match Girl". His stories have inspired ballets, plays, and animated and live-action films.

Early life

Andersen was born in Odense, Denmark, the only child of a poor shoemaker and an illiterate washerwoman. His father introduced him to literature, reading him the Arabian Nights. After his father's early death, Andersen worked in a factory and at fourteen moved alone to Copenhagen to seek his fortune at the Royal Danish Theatre, where he sang as a boy soprano and briefly danced and acted. Patrons including Jonas Collin arranged for his education at grammar schools in Slagelse and Elsinore, an unhappy period he later described as the darkest of his life.

Career

Andersen published his first story in 1822 and gained recognition with the poem "The Dying Child" and the 1829 playbill tale A Walking Tour from the Holmen Canal to the Eastern Point of the Amager. His first novel, The Improvisatore (1835), was an international success. The same year he published the first instalment of his Fairy Tales Told for Children, which initially sold poorly but grew in fame through the 1840s. He travelled extensively in Europe, met Charles Dickens in England in 1847 (and overstayed his welcome on a return visit in 1857), and became a celebrated figure across the continent.

Death and legacy

Andersen fell out of bed in 1872 and never fully recovered; he died of liver cancer on 4 August 1875 at Rolighed, near Copenhagen. The Hans Christian Andersen Awards, given every two years, are the highest international recognition for creators of children's books. His birthday, 2 April, is celebrated as International Children's Book Day, and his statue and the Little Mermaid statue are landmarks of Copenhagen.
