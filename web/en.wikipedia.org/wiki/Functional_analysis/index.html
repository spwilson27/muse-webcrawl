Functional analysis

Functional analysis is a branch of mathematical analysis whose core is the study of vector spaces endowed with some kind of limit-related structure (such as an inner product, norm, or topology) and the linear functions defined on these spaces. It grew out of the study of function spaces and transformations such as the Fourier transform, and of differential and integral equations, in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

History

The field coalesced around the work of Vito Volterra, David Hilbert (Hilbert spaces and spectral theory), and Maurice Fréchet (abstract metric spaces). Stefan Banach's 1920s work — including his 1932 monograph Théorie des opérations linéaires — laid out the axiomatic theory of complete normed spaces, now called Banach spaces. The theory matured with Laurent Schwartz's theory of distributions and applications to partial differential equations.

Key concepts

Central objects include normed and Banach spaces, Hilbert spaces, bounded and compact operators, dual spaces and linear functionals, and spectra of operators. Cornerstone theorems are the Hahn–Banach theorem, the uniform boundedness principle, the open mapping and closed graph theorems, and the spectral theorem. Weaker topologies (weak and weak-* topologies) and convexity play essential roles.

Applications

Functional analysis provides the mathematical language of quantum mechanics, where states are vectors in a Hilbert space and observables are operators on it (formalised by John von Neumann). It is also fundamental to partial differential equations, signal processing, optimisation, numerical analysis, and mathematical economics.
