Illuminations is an unfinished collection of prose poems by the French poet Arthur Rimbaud, largely written between 1872 and 1875. First published in part by the Paris review La Vogue in 1886 — arranged by Rimbaud's former lover Paul Verlaine — the collection comprises some forty-two pieces, mostly prose poems with a handful of verse poems interspersed.

Content and style

The title may refer both to coloured engravings (in the sense of illuminated plates) and to mystical illumination. The poems abandon narrative and logical structure in favour of hallucinatory imagery, abrupt juxtapositions and dreamlike scenes: flooded cities, processions, childhood memories and apocalyptic visions. Pieces such as "Villes", "Barbare", "Aube" and "Génie" are among the most celebrated.

Composition and publication

Because Rimbaud abandoned literature by the age of twenty-one and travelled abroad, the manuscript's history is obscure. Verlaine obtained the texts and published them without the author's involvement; the order of the poems in modern editions remains debated, and no autograph manuscript of the complete collection survives.

Influence

The Illuminations profoundly influenced Symbolism, Surrealism and modern prose poetry, and Rimbaud's visionary style later inspired writers and musicians including Henry Miller, André Breton and Jim Morrison.
