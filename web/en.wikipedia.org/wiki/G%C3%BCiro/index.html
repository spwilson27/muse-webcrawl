Güiro

The güiro is a Latin American percussion instrument consisting of a hollow gourd or modern equivalent with parallel notches cut in one side. It is played by rubbing a stick or tines along the notches to produce a ratcheting, rasping sound.

History

The instrument derives from Taíno and other indigenous Caribbean traditions, and similar scrapers exist in African and pre-Columbian American cultures. The Puerto Rican güiro is traditionally carved from the higüero (gourd of the calabash tree).

Use

The güiro is a staple of salsa, merengue, cumbia, son cubano and plena, providing a steady rhythmic pattern. Related instruments include the Dominican güira (metal scraper), the Brazilian reco-reco, and the frog-shaped guiro rasped in some Asian traditions.
