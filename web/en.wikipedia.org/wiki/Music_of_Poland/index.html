The music of Poland spans a rich tradition from medieval plainchant and Renaissance polyphony through the Romantic piano works of Frédéric Chopin to contemporary classical, jazz, and popular music.

Folk music

Polish folk dances — the stately polonaise, the lively mazurka, the kujawiak, the oberek, and the krakowiak — influenced composers across Europe. Regional traditions, such as the music of the Tatra highlanders (Podhale), retain distinctive fiddle styles.

Art music

Chopin, born in Żelowa Wola, transformed Polish dance forms into concert masterpieces. In the twentieth century, Witold Lutosławski, Krzysztof Penderecki, and Henryk Górecki gained international renown; Górecki's Symphony No. 3 became a worldwide bestseller in the 1990s.

Popular music

Poland developed strong jazz and rock scenes even under communist rule, and the festival culture continues with events such as Open'er Festival and the Chopin International Piano Competition, held in Warsaw every five years since 1927.
