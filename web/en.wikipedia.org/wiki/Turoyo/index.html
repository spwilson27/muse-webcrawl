Turoyo, also known as Surayt, is a Central Neo-Aramaic language traditionally spoken by Syriac Christians in the Tur Abdin region of southeastern Turkey and northern Syria. It is the vernacular counterpart of Classical Syriac, the liturgical language of the Syriac churches, and one of the few Neo-Aramaic languages with a substantial speaker community.

History and speakers

Turoyo descends from the Aramaic dialects of upper Mesopotamia. Its speakers are concentrated around Midyat and the villages of Tur Abdin, but since the late 20th century most speakers live in the diaspora — in Germany, Sweden, the Netherlands, Syria, and elsewhere — following emigration driven by conflict and economic pressures. Estimates of speakers range from tens of thousands to over one hundred thousand. UNESCO classifies the language as endangered.

Features

Like other Neo-Aramaic languages, Turoyo has undergone extensive restructuring compared with Classical Syriac, including a split-ergative verbal system in past tenses and heavy lexical borrowing from Turkish, Arabic, and Kurdish. It is written both in the Syriac script (Serto) and, increasingly, in a Latin-based alphabet used in diaspora teaching materials.

Revitalisation

Diaspora communities, particularly in Sweden (where the language is called Surayt and taught to children of Assyrian/Syriac immigrants) and Germany, support language classes, media, and publications. Academic documentation is centred at institutions including the Free University of Berlin.
