Religious discrimination

Religious discrimination is treating a person or group differently because of the particular beliefs which they hold about a religion. This includes instances when adherents of different religions, denominations or non-religions are treated unequally due to their particular beliefs, either by law or in institutional settings, such as employment or housing.

Forms

Discrimination may be direct — for example laws restricting the worship, dress, dietary practice or civil rights of a religious group — or indirect, where facially neutral rules disproportionately burden a faith. Historical examples include the persecution of early Christians, European antisemitism culminating in the Holocaust, anti-Catholic penal laws, and restrictions on religious minorities in various states. Contemporary concerns include discrimination against Muslims, Sikhs, Jews, Christians, Hindus, atheists and others in different regional contexts.

Legal protections

Freedom of religion is protected by Article 18 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights. Many constitutions prohibit religious discrimination; in the United States, the First Amendment and Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 provide protections, while the European Convention on Human Rights addresses it in Article 9 and Article 14.
