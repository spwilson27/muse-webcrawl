Overview

Chiptune, also known as chip music or 8-bit music, is a style of electronic music made using the programmable sound generator chips of early video game consoles, home computers and arcade machines, or modern software emulating them. Its composers work within strict limits on channels and waveforms, producing the genre's distinctive bright, arpeggiated sound.

History

Chiptune grew out of 1980s video game music and the European demoscene, where programmers and musicians competed to extract impressive sound from hardware such as the Commodore 64's SID chip and the Amiga. As original hardware aged, trackers and emulators preserved the practice, and from the 2000s acts such as Anamanaguchi and musicians using Nintendo Game Boys with software like Little Sound Dj (LSDj) brought chiptune into live performance and popular music.

Characteristics

Typical features include square-, triangle- and noise-wave voices, rapid arpeggios that fake chords on single channels, and compositions written in tracker software. Related microgenres include bitpop, which mixes chip sounds with conventional instruments and vocals, and skweee and other hardware-focused electronic scenes.
