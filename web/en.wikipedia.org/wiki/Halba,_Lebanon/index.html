Halba (Arabic: حلبا) is a town in northern Lebanon and the capital of the Akkar Governorate, the country's northernmost and least developed governorate. It lies on the plain of Akkar near the Syrian border, northeast of Tripoli, at the crossroads of routes to the coast, the Beqaa and Homs in Syria.

Geography and life

Halba serves as the administrative and market centre for the surrounding agricultural plain of Akkar, an area known for farming, olive groves and citrus. The town hosts government offices, schools, hospitals and souks serving dozens of surrounding villages. Its population is predominantly Sunni Muslim, with Christian and Alawite minorities, reflecting the mixed demography of the Akkar region.

Recent history

Halba saw tensions and clashes during spillovers of the Syrian civil war in the early 2010s, and the region has hosted a large number of Syrian refugees. Protests over Lebanon's economic collapse also took place in the town from 2019 onward.
