Écrins National Park (French: Parc national des Écrins) is a French national park in the Dauphiné Alps of south-eastern France, spanning the departments of Hautes-Alpes and Isère. It protects the high mountain massif of Les Écrins, one of the great glaciated ranges of the European Alps.

History

A reserve around Mont Pelvoux was created in 1913 — a forerunner sometimes called the Bérarde or Pelvoux park — making the Écrins area France's first proto-national park. The present national park was formally established by decree in 1973, becoming one of the country's earliest and largest parks.

Geography and nature

The park's crowning summit, the Barre des Écrins (4,102 m), is the highest peak of the Massif des Écrins and was France's highest mountain before the annexation of Savoy brought Mont Blanc into the country. More than a hundred summits exceed 3,000 m, with major glaciers including the Glacier Blanc and Glacier Noir. Wildlife includes chamois, reintroduced Alpine ibex, marmots, golden eagles, and bearded vultures, and the flora ranges from larch forest to rare high-alpine species.

Access

Gateway villages include La Grave, Vallouise, Saint-Christophe-en-Oisans, and Le Bourg d'Oisans. The park is a major destination for mountaineering, ski touring, and hiking, with a network of staffed mountain refuges.
