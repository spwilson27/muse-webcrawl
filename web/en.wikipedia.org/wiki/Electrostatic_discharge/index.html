Electrostatic discharge

Electrostatic discharge
AbbreviationESD
CauseSudden flow of electricity between objects at different potentials
Common causeTriboelectric charging (contact electrification)
Notable riskDamage to microelectronics; ignition of flammables

An electrostatic discharge (ESD) is a sudden and momentary flow of electric current between two electrically charged objects caused by contact, an electrical short or dielectric breakdown. A buildup of static electricity can be caused by tribocharging (friction between materials) or by electrostatic induction. The ESD occurs when differently charged objects are brought close together or when the dielectric between them fails, often creating a visible spark.

Causes

When two different materials come into contact and separate, electrons may transfer, leaving one object positively charged and the other negatively charged — the triboelectric effect. Walking across a carpet, removing a synthetic garment, or sliding plastic packaging can generate potentials of many thousands of volts, although the total energy is small. If the electric field exceeds the breakdown strength of air (about 3 kV/mm), a spark discharges the objects.

Effects and damage

ESD is a major reliability concern in the electronics industry: sensitive components such as MOSFETs can be destroyed or latently damaged by discharges far below the threshold of human perception (a person typically feels a shock only above roughly 3,000 V, while devices can fail at tens of volts). ESD can also ignite flammable gases, vapours and dusts, a serious hazard in fuel handling and munitions, and it was implicated in historical airship and industrial accidents.

Protection

Electronics are protected by ESD-safe workstations with antistatic mats, wrist straps and ionisers; components are shipped in shielding bags and handled in electrostatic protected areas. Devices incorporate on-chip protection circuits, and standards such as the human-body model (HBM) and charged-device model (CDM) define test methods. Humidity control reduces charge accumulation.

Natural ESD

Lightning is the largest-scale natural electrostatic discharge, transferring enormous charge between clouds and the ground. Smaller corona discharges and brush discharges occur around sharp conductors in strong fields.
