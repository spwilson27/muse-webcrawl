Satavahana dynasty

The Satavahana dynasty (also called the Andhra dynasty in the Puranas) was an ancient Indian dynasty that ruled much of the Deccan and central India from roughly the late 2nd century BCE to the early 3rd century CE. At its height under Gautamiputra Satakarni and his successors, the empire stretched from coast to coast, controlling trade routes between northern India and the peninsula.

History

The dynasty's founder, Simuka, is dated by different traditions to between the 2nd and 1st centuries BCE. Early Satavahana kings ruled from Pratishthana (modern Paithan) on the Godavari river. The dynasty fought repeatedly with the Western Kshatrapas over the western Deccan and the lucrative ports of the Arabian Sea coast. Gautamiputra Satakarni (reigned c. 106–130 CE) destroyed the Kshaharata family and restored Satavahana power; his mother Gautami Balashri's Nashik inscription celebrates him as a model Brahmin king. His son Vasishthiputra Pulumavi extended influence to the Coromandel coast.

Culture and economy

The Satavahanas patronised both Brahmanical Hinduism and Buddhism, endowing cave temples at Karle, Nashik and Kanheri and contributing to the stupa complexes at Amaravati and Sanchi (whose southern gateway bears a Satavahana-period inscription). They issued coins in lead, potin, copper and silver — some bilingual — and their ports traded with the Roman world; the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea describes their harbours. The Prakrit anthology Gatha Saptashati is traditionally attributed to the Satavahana king Hala. Their administration used amatyas and districts called aharas.

Decline

By the early 3rd century CE the empire fragmented into successor states — the Abhiras, Ikshvakus, Vakatakas and others inherited its territories.
