La Rioja

La Rioja (Spanish: [la ˈrjoxa]) is an autonomous community and province in northern Spain, bordering the Basque Country, Navarre, Aragon, and Castile and León. Its capital and largest city is Logroño. Covering about 5,045 km² (1,948 sq mi) with a population of around 320,000, it is Spain's smallest autonomous community by population and second smallest by area. It is famous internationally above all for its wines, produced under the Rioja DOCa designation.

Geography

The river Ebro crosses the north of the region; south of it, seven valleys — among them the Najerilla, Iregua, and Cidacos — descend from the Iberian System mountains, including the Sierra de la Demanda, whose San Lorenzo peak (2,271 m) is the region's highest point. The name Rioja is usually traced to the Río Oja, a tributary of the Ebro.

Wine

Rioja is Spain's flagship wine region, its wines — traditionally red blends dominated by Tempranillo, aged in oak and classified as Crianza, Reserva, and Gran Reserva by aging time — known worldwide since the 19th century, when phylloxera in France drove Bordeaux merchants to the region. The DOCa (Denominación de Origen Calificada, awarded 1991) spans La Rioja and parts of Álava and Navarre, and more than 600 wineries operate there; notable bodegas range from historic houses in Haro's Barrio de la Estación to architectural showpieces designed by Frank Gehry and Santiago Calatrava.

History and culture

The monasteries of San Millán de la Cogolla, where some of the earliest written Spanish (the Glosas Emilianenses) was traditionally said to originate, are a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with Yuso. The region was part of the medieval kingdoms of Navarre and Castile before becoming a modern province; it received its autonomy statute in 1982. The Way of St. James (Camino de Santiago) passes through Logroño and Nájera, and important dinosaur-track sites are preserved in the southern valleys.
