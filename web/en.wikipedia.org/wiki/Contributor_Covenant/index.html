Contributor Covenant

The Contributor Covenant is a code of conduct for open source projects, created by Coraline Ada Ehmke in 2014. It is widely used by open source communities to set expectations for respectful participation and to provide a framework for addressing harassment and unacceptable behaviour. The document has been adopted by tens of thousands of projects, including major ones associated with the Linux kernel, Ruby on Rails, Swift, Angular and many projects hosted by foundations such as the Eclipse Foundation.

Content

The covenant pledges to make participation in a project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation. It lists examples of behaviour that contributes to a positive environment — such as welcoming language, respect for differing viewpoints, and graceful acceptance of criticism — and unacceptable behaviour, including harassment, trolling, insults and publishing others' private information. Project maintainers are made responsible for clarifying and enforcing the standards, with enforcement responses scaled from correction and warning to temporary or permanent bans.

Reception

Supporters credit the Contributor Covenant with making open source communities more welcoming and professional. Critics have argued that codes of conduct can be used to police political views or import disputes into technical projects. Version 2.0 was released in 2020, and version 2.1 followed in 2021, incorporating wording aligned with the Mozilla Community Participation Guidelines.
