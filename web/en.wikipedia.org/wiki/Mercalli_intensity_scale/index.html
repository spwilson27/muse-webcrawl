The Mercalli intensity scale is a seismic intensity scale used for measuring the intensity of shaking produced by an earthquake at a given location, based on its observed effects on people, buildings and the natural environment.

History

Italian volcanologist Giuseppe Mercalli published his scale in 1902, expanding the earlier Rossi–Forel scale. The version revised by Harry Wood and Frank Neumann in 1931 became the Modified Mercalli intensity scale, still used by the United States Geological Survey, which reports intensities in Roman numerals from I to XII.

Intensity versus magnitude

Unlike magnitude scales such as the Richter scale, which describe the energy released at the source, intensity varies from place to place with distance, depth and local geology. Intensity maps therefore show how shaking was distributed, and historical earthquakes can be assigned magnitudes by analysing reported intensities.
