Judgment

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: scales of justice]

Judgment (also spelled judgement) is the evaluation of given circumstances to make a decision. The term has several distinct uses: in psychology, it is the cognitive process of forming an opinion or conclusion; in law, it refers to a court's formal decision in a case; and in religion, it may refer to divine judgment of humanity.

In psychology

Judgment and decision-making research studies how people assess probabilities, weigh evidence, and choose between options. Heuristics and biases — such as availability, anchoring, and overconfidence — systematically shape human judgment, as demonstrated in the work of Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky.

In law

A judgment is the final determination of the rights and obligations of parties in a lawsuit. It may be rendered after trial or by default, and can typically be appealed. A money judgment creates a legal obligation to pay damages.

In religion

Many religions teach of a final or "Last Judgment" in which the deeds of every person are weighed. In Christianity this is associated with the Second Coming; similar concepts appear in Islam (Yawm al-Qiyamah) and other traditions.
