Non-rebreather mask

A non-rebreather mask is a medical device used to deliver high concentrations of oxygen to a spontaneously breathing patient. It consists of a face mask connected to a reservoir bag, with one-way valves that prevent the patient from rebreathing exhaled air.

Design and use

Oxygen flows into the reservoir bag (typically at 10–15 litres per minute, enough to keep the bag inflated). On inhalation the patient draws oxygen from the bag through a one-way valve; on exhalation, valves direct expired gas out through side ports. The mask can deliver an inspired oxygen fraction (FiO₂) of roughly 60–90%, the highest available from a low-flow mask device.

Indications

Non-rebreathers are used in emergencies such as severe hypoxemia, major trauma, carbon monoxide poisoning and cluster headache treatment, as a bridge while definitive airway management is arranged. They require an adequate respiratory effort and the reservoir must not be allowed to collapse fully.
