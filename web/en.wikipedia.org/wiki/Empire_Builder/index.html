The Empire Builder is a daily long-distance passenger train operated by Amtrak between Chicago and the Pacific Northwest. The train runs from Chicago Union Station through Milwaukee, St. Paul, Fargo, the northern Great Plains and the Rocky Mountains to Spokane, where it splits into sections serving Seattle and Portland. It is one of Amtrak's most popular long-distance routes, carrying several hundred thousand passengers a year.

History

The train was inaugurated by the Great Northern Railway on June 10, 1929, named in honour of the railway's founder James J. Hill, known as the "Empire Builder". Streamlined in 1947 and re-equipped with dome cars in the 1950s, it was the Great Northern's flagship. Amtrak took over the service in 1971 and has operated it since, largely over BNSF Railway trackage that follows the historic Great Northern main line, passing through Glacier National Park at Marias Pass.

Route and operation

The journey covers about 2,206 miles (3,550 km) to Seattle and takes roughly 46 hours. Highlights include the Mississippi River valley, the plains of North Dakota and Montana, Glacier National Park, and the Cascade Tunnel through the Cascade Range. The train typically consists of Superliner double-deck coaches and sleeping cars, a dining car and a Sightseer Lounge.
