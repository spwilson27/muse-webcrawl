General Creighton W. Abrams.

Creighton Williams Abrams Jr. (15 September 1914 – 4 September 1974) was a United States Army general who commanded military operations in the Vietnam War from 1968 to 1972, and then served as Chief of Staff of the United States Army from 1972 until his death in 1974. The M1 Abrams main battle tank is named after him.

Career

A 1936 graduate of West Point, Abrams served as a tank commander in the Second World War under George S. Patton, leading the 37th Tank Battalion that relieved Bastogne during the Battle of the Bulge; Patton called him the best tank commander in the Army. He fought in the Korean War and commanded at several levels in Europe before becoming deputy commander of Military Assistance Command, Vietnam (MACV) in 1967.

Vietnam and Chief of Staff

Abrams succeeded William Westmoreland as MACV commander in June 1968. He shifted emphasis from large-unit "search and destroy" operations toward pacification, population security and the "Vietnamization" programme of building up South Vietnamese forces as U.S. troops withdrew from over 540,000 to fewer than 30,000 by 1972. Historians debate how far his "one war" approach might have succeeded had it begun earlier. As Chief of Staff he began rebuilding the post-Vietnam Army and oversaw the creation of the all-volunteer force structure. He died in office of complications following surgery for lung cancer.
