Satya Narayan Goenka (30 January 1924 – 29 September 2013), commonly known as S. N. Goenka, was an Indian teacher of Vipassanā meditation in the tradition of the Burmese master Sayagyi U Ba Khin. Though born in Burma to a Hindu family and himself a successful businessman, he presented Vipassanā as a universal, non-sectarian technique. The movement he founded operates more than 200 meditation centres worldwide, all offering standardised ten-day residential courses free of charge, funded by donations from former students.

Life

Goenka was born in Mandalay to Marwari parents from India and became a leading businessman in Burma. Suffering from severe migraines, he met U Ba Khin in 1955 seeking relief; he studied meditation under him for 14 years. After U Ba Khin's death he moved to India in 1969 and began teaching, at first to small groups in Bombay. In 1976 he established the Vipassana International Academy at Igatpuri near Nashik, and from 1979 began teaching abroad. He addressed the Millennium World Peace Summit at the United Nations in 2000.

Teaching

Courses last ten days in silence. Students spend the first three and a half days practising ānāpānasati (awareness of breathing), then learn Vipassanā — systematic observation of bodily sensations with equanimity — and close with mettā (loving-kindness) meditation. Instruction is delivered largely through Goenka's own recorded audio and video discourses, even after his death, with assistant teachers present to answer questions. Goenka insisted the technique was open to people of any religion or none.

Death and legacy

Goenka died in Mumbai on 29 September 2013, aged 89. India's government awarded him the Padma Bhushan in 2012. The Global Vipassana Pagoda near Mumbai, inaugurated in 2009, was built as a monument to the tradition he taught.
