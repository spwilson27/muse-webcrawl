Jin dynasty (1115–1234)

The Jin dynasty (1115–1234), officially the Great Jin, was an imperial dynasty of China founded by the Jurchen people, ancestors of the Manchus. Founded by Wanyan Aguda, it conquered the Liao dynasty and much of northern China, ruling alongside the Southern Song dynasty until its destruction by the Mongol Empire.

History

The Jurchens revolted against their Khitan overlords of the Liao dynasty in 1114. In alliance with the Song dynasty, they destroyed Liao by 1125, then turned on Song, capturing Kaifeng in the Jingkang incident (1127) and forcing Song south of the Huai River. Treaties such as the Shaoxing Treaty (1141) fixed the border.

Emperor Hailing moved the capital to Zhongdu (modern Beijing) in 1153 and promoted sinicisation. Under Emperors Shizong and Zhangzong the dynasty reached cultural and economic prosperity, developing Jurchen script, examinations, and Chinese-style bureaucracy while trying to preserve Jurchen identity.

Fall

Weakened by war with the revived Southern Song and flooding of the Yellow River, Jin faced the Mongol invasions under Genghis Khan from 1211. Zhongdu fell in 1215. The last emperor, Modi, died at Caizhou in 1234 during a joint Mongol–Song siege, ending the dynasty.

Culture and society

Jin ruled a multi-ethnic population of Jurchen, Khitan, Bohai, and Han Chinese. Buddhism flourished, printing expanded, and Jurchen translations of Chinese classics were produced. Its legal and administrative systems influenced the later Yuan and Qing dynasties.
