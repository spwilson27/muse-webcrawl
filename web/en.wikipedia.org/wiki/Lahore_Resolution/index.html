Lahore Resolution

The Lahore Resolution, later called the Pakistan Resolution, was a formal political statement adopted by the All-India Muslim League at its annual session in Lahore, British India, on 23 March 1940. Presented by the Bengali leader A. K. Fazlul Huq and drafted principally by a committee under Muhammad Ali Jinnah's leadership, it rejected a united federal India and demanded that the Muslim-majority regions of the north-west and east be grouped into "independent states". It is regarded as the founding demand of the Pakistan Movement and is commemorated in Pakistan on 23 March as Pakistan Day.

Background

By the late 1930s the Muslim League's experience of Congress provincial governments after the 1937 elections under the Government of India Act, and the failure of negotiations over power-sharing, convinced Jinnah and the League leadership that Muslims would be a permanent minority in a unified democratic India. The two-nation theory — which held that Hindus and Muslims were distinct nations — became League doctrine; the philosopher Muhammad Iqbal had sketched a consolidated Muslim state in the north-west in his Allahabad Address of 1930, and the word "Pakistan" had been coined by Choudhry Rahmat Ali in 1933.

Text and meaning

The resolution declared that no constitutional plan would be acceptable to Muslims unless the predominantly Muslim north-western and eastern zones were grouped to constitute "independent states in which the constituent units shall be autonomous and sovereign", with safeguards for minorities on a reciprocal basis. It did not use the name "Pakistan", and its plural "states" wording has generated long scholarly debate over whether it envisaged one state or two, and whether it was a maximalist bargaining position rather than an absolute demand. A 1946 convention of League legislators in Delhi clarified the demand as a single sovereign state of Pakistan.

Aftermath

The resolution transformed the League's programme and set the course that ended with the Partition of India and the creation of Pakistan on 14 August 1947. The tower of Minar-e-Pakistan was erected in the 1960s in Iqbal Park (formerly Minto Park), Lahore, on the spot where the resolution was adopted, and 23 March is observed as a national holiday.
