Thai script

The Thai script (Thai: อักษรไทย, akson thai) is the abugida used to write Thai, Southern Thai and many other languages spoken in Thailand. It has 44 consonant symbols, 16 vowel symbols that combine into at least 32 vowel forms, four tone marks, and other diacritics.

History

The script is traditionally said to have been created in 1283 by King Ram Khamhaeng the Great of Sukhothai, as recorded in the Ram Khamhaeng Inscription. It was derived, via Old Khmer, from the Brahmic scripts of India. Unlike neighbouring scripts, Thai does not use spaces between words; spaces serve roughly the function of commas and periods, marking clauses and sentences.

Characteristics

Thai is written left to right. Consonants are written in a main line while vowels can be at the front, back, top, bottom, or around the consonant. The script is tonal: the combination of the consonant class (high, mid, or low), syllable length, final consonant, and tone mark determines each syllable's tone. Thai numerals (๐๑๒๓๔๕๖๗๘๙) exist alongside the Arabic numerals now commonly used.

The script has no letter case and does not distinguish between capital and small letters. Obsolete letters such as ฃ and ฅ remain part of the traditional alphabet count but are no longer used.
