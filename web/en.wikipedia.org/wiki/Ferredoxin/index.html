Ferredoxin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ferredoxins (from Latin ferrum, iron, + redox) are iron–sulfur proteins that mediate electron transfer in a range of metabolic reactions. These soluble proteins contain iron–sulfur clusters that accept and donate single electrons, and they are found in bacteria, archaea, algae, plants, and animals (in which the related protein is often called adrenodoxin). The term was coined in 1962 when the first such protein was isolated from the anaerobic bacterium Clostridium pasteurianum. Ferredoxins are among the most ancient electron carriers in biology and are thought to have functioned in the metabolism of the earliest cells.

Classes

Ferredoxins are classified by the nature of their iron–sulfur cluster:

  • 2Fe–2S ferredoxins — including the plant-type ferredoxins of chloroplasts and cyanobacteria, and animal adrenodoxin. In photosynthesis, ferredoxin is the first soluble electron acceptor of photosystem I.
  • 4Fe–4S ferredoxins — typical of anaerobic bacteria; often low-potential, powerful reductants.
  • 3Fe–4S and 8Fe–8S (dicluster) ferredoxins — found in various bacteria, some with distinct specialized roles.

Roles in metabolism

Photosynthesis

In oxygenic photosynthesis, electrons from photosystem I reduce ferredoxin on the stromal side of the thylakoid membrane. Reduced ferredoxin then donates electrons (via the enzyme ferredoxin–NADP⁺ reductase) to make NADPH for carbon fixation, and also distributes electrons to pathways of nitrogen and sulfur assimilation and to cyclic electron flow. Ferredoxin therefore sits at the branch point between light-driven electron transport and cellular biosynthesis.

Other roles

Bacterial ferredoxins feed electrons into nitrogen fixation (the nitrogenase complex receives electrons from ferredoxin or flavodoxin), hydrogen production by hydrogenases, and anaerobic respiration. In animal mitochondria, adrenodoxin transfers electrons from adrenodoxin reductase to cytochrome P450 enzymes that synthesize steroid hormones. Related small iron–sulfur carriers, such as rubredoxin, perform similar redox chemistry with a single iron atom.

Properties

Ferredoxins are small (roughly 6–14 kDa), acidic, soluble proteins, typically with strongly negative reduction potentials (about −240 to −430 mV), which makes them effective low-potential electron donors. Their intensely coloured iron–sulfur chromophores give characteristic visible absorption spectra (bands near 390 and 420 nm) used to follow their oxidation state spectroscopically.
