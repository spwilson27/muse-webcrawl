Ariwara no Narihira (在原 業平, 825 – July 9, 880) was a Japanese courtier and waka poet of the early Heian period. He was named one of the Six Poetic Sages and one of the Thirty-six Poetic Sages. He is generally regarded as the hero of The Tales of Ise, whose anonymous author drew on his life and loves; many of the poems in that work are attributed to him.

Life

Narihira was the fifth son of Prince Abo, and thus a grandson of Emperor Heizei. His mother was Princess Ito, daughter of Emperor Kanmu. Along with his brothers, he was removed from imperial succession and given the clan name Ariwara. Despite his imperial birth, his court career was modest: he served in the Imperial Guard and as a provincial governor late in life, never rising above junior fourth rank. Legend gave him a far more glamorous career as an irresistible lover, including a scandalous affair with the imperial consort Fujiwara no Takaiko.

Poetry

Thirty of Narihira's poems appear in the imperial anthology Kokin Wakashū, and more in later collections; one of his poems is included in the Ogura Hyakunin Isshu. His famous poem on the cherry blossoms — "If there were no cherry blossoms in this world, how much more tranquil our hearts would be in spring" — epitomises his emotional, lyrical style. His poems are noted for their intensity and occasional obscurity, sometimes criticised by the Kokinshū compilers as having "too much feeling in too few words".

Legacy

Through The Tales of Ise, Narihira became the archetype of the refined amorous courtier in Japanese culture, referenced for a millennium in literature, Noh drama, and painting.
