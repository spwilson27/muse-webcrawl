Food safety

Food safety (or food hygiene) is used as a scientific method and discipline describing the handling, preparation, and storage of food in ways that prevent foodborne illness. The occurrence of two or more cases of a similar illness resulting from the ingestion of a common food is known as a foodborne disease outbreak. Food safety includes the routines that should be followed to avoid potentially severe health hazards, from production and processing through transport, retail, and preparation in the home.

Hazards

Food hazards are conventionally grouped into three classes:

  • Biological hazards — pathogenic bacteria (such as Salmonella, Listeria monocytogenes, and E. coli O157:H7), viruses (such as norovirus and hepatitis A), parasites, and prions.
  • Chemical hazards — pesticide residues, heavy metals, natural toxins such as mycotoxins, allergens, and cleaning chemicals.
  • Physical hazards — foreign objects such as glass, metal fragments, or bone.

The World Health Organization estimates that unsafe food causes about 600 million cases of foodborne illness and 420,000 deaths worldwide each year.

Prevention and control

Key consumer-level practices promoted by the WHO's "Five Keys to Safer Food" are: keep clean; separate raw and cooked food; cook thoroughly; keep food at safe temperatures; and use safe water and raw materials. In industry and food service, hazard analysis and critical control points (HACCP) provides a systematic preventive framework, supplemented by good manufacturing practice, traceability, labelling, and recall systems. Temperature control — keeping cold foods below about 5 °C and hot foods above about 60 °C — limits the growth of most pathogens in the "danger zone" between those temperatures.

Regulation

Food safety is regulated nationally — for example by the Food and Drug Administration and Department of Agriculture in the United States, and the Food Standards Agency in the United Kingdom — and coordinated internationally through the Codex Alimentarius Commission, established by the FAO and WHO to set food standards.
