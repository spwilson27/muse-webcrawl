Antibiotics

[Image: A petri dish of bacteria with antibiotic discs creating clear zones of inhibition]

Antibiotics are medicines that kill bacteria or stop their growth, used to treat and prevent bacterial infections. Alexander Fleming's 1928 observation that Penicillium mould killed staphylococci led, through Florey and Chain's work, to penicillin's mass production in the Second World War and a revolution in medicine.

Resistance

Antibiotics do not work on viruses such as those behind colds or the COVID-19 pandemic. Overuse in medicine and agriculture has driven antimicrobial resistance, which health agencies rank among the top global health threats; stewardship, new drug development and vaccination are the main defences.
