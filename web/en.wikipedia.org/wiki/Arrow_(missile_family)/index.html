The Arrow or Hetz (Hebrew: חֵץ, "arrow") is a family of anti-ballistic missiles designed by Israel Aerospace Industries to fulfil Israel's requirement for a theatre missile defence system able to intercept ballistic missiles at high altitude. Funded and produced jointly with the United States, it is a central layer of Israel's multi-tiered air and missile defence alongside Iron Dome and David's Sling.

Development

The programme began in 1986 under a US–Israel memorandum connected with the Strategic Defense Initiative, and gained urgency after Iraqi Scud missiles struck Israel during the 1991 Gulf War. Flight testing of the Arrow 1 demonstrator began in 1990, and the improved Arrow 2 became operational in 2000. The system uses the Green Pine radar for detection and tracking and the Citron Tree battle management centre.

Variants

Arrow 2 intercepts missiles within the atmosphere and lower space using a blast-fragmentation warhead. Arrow 3, declared operational in January 2017, intercepts ballistic missiles in space during their exo-atmospheric flight using a hit-to-kill vehicle, giving greater range and the ability to engage multiple targets; it was developed jointly with Boeing. An Arrow 4, intended to counter hypersonic and manoeuvring threats, was announced as under development.

Operational use

Arrow 2 scored its first combat interception in March 2017, destroying a Syrian S-200 missile fired at Israeli aircraft. Arrow 3 made its first operational exo-atmospheric interception on 9 November 2023, destroying a ballistic missile launched from Yemen toward Eilat during the Gaza war, and Arrow systems were used extensively against Iranian ballistic missile attacks in April and October 2024. Germany selected Arrow 3 in 2023 for the European Sky Shield Initiative, the system's first major export sale.
