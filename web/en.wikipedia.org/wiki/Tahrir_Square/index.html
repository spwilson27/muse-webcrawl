Tahrir Square

Tahrir Square (Arabic: ميدان التحرير, Mīdān at-Taḥrīr, "Liberation Square"), also known as "Martyr Square", is a major public town square in downtown Cairo, Egypt. It is located near the Nile, the Qasr al-Nil Bridge, the Egyptian Museum and the Mogamma government building.

History

The square was laid out in the nineteenth century during the modernisation of Cairo under Khedive Ismail, who modelled the new districts on Paris, and was originally named Ismailia Square. After the Egyptian revolution of 1919 it became known popularly as Liberation Square, a name made official after the revolution of 1952. Around it stand major institutions: the Egyptian Museum (opened 1902), the former Nile Ritz-Carlton hotel, the Qasr El Nil Bridge guarded by its lion statues, Cairo University's downtown campus, the Arab League headquarters and the Mogamma, the vast former centre of Egypt's bureaucracy.

2011 revolution

Tahrir Square became world famous as the focal point of the Egyptian revolution of 2011. Beginning on 25 January 2011, protesters occupied the square in huge numbers, maintaining an encampment through clashes with police and pro-government attackers, until President Hosni Mubarak resigned on 11 February after thirty years in power. The square remained a symbolic site of protest during the subsequent years of political upheaval, including the mass demonstrations of June–July 2013 that preceded the removal of President Mohamed Morsi.

Renovation

In 2020 the square was redeveloped with an obelisk of Ramesses II and four ram-headed sphinxes from Karnak installed at its centre, surrounded by new landscaping and lighting.
