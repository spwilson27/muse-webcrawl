Louis Stephen St. Laurent (February 1, 1882 – July 25, 1973) was a Canadian lawyer and politician who served as the 12th prime minister of Canada from 1948 to 1957. Affectionately known as "Uncle Louis" (Oncle Louis), he led the Liberal Party through a period of postwar prosperity and oversaw Newfoundland's entry into Confederation.

Early life and legal career

St. Laurent was born in Compton, Quebec, to a French-Canadian father and an Irish-Canadian mother, and was fluently bilingual. He became one of Canada's most prominent corporate lawyers, served as president of the Canadian Bar Association, and twice declined offers to enter politics before accepting Mackenzie King's invitation in 1941.

Minister and successor to King

As Minister of Justice and later Secretary of State for External Affairs, St. Laurent helped build the postwar international order, representing Canada at the founding of the United Nations. He succeeded William Lyon Mackenzie King as Liberal leader and prime minister in 1948, and won decisive election victories in 1949 and 1953.

Prime minister

St. Laurent's government admitted Newfoundland as Canada's tenth province in 1949, supported the founding of NATO, committed Canadian troops to the Korean War, and launched major national projects including the Trans-Canada Highway, the St. Lawrence Seaway and the TransCanada pipeline. His administration also expanded social programs and equalization payments.

The Liberals' controversial use of closure to force through the pipeline bill in 1956 damaged the government, and in the 1957 election St. Laurent was narrowly defeated by John Diefenbaker's Progressive Conservatives. He retired as Liberal leader in 1958.
