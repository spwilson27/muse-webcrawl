Fellow

A fellow is a concept whose exact meaning depends on context. In learned or professional societies, it is the highest grade of membership, usually reserved for those who have made a distinguished contribution to their field. In academia, a fellow is a member of a college's governing body at universities such as Oxford and Cambridge, or a recipient of a fellowship — funding or a position supporting research, study or training — such as a postdoctoral research fellow. The word derives from the Old Norse félagi, a partner or shareholder in a joint venture, and in general usage simply means a companion or a man.

Learned societies

Many societies designate senior members as fellows, often with postnominal letters: Fellows of the Royal Society (FRS), of the British Academy (FBA), of the Royal Academy of Engineering (FREng), and of professional bodies in medicine, law, engineering and other disciplines. Election to fellowship is generally by peer nomination and is an honour as well as a membership grade.

Universities and medicine

At Oxford and Cambridge, fellows form the college in its corporate sense, typically teach and conduct research, and govern the college. In the United States, "fellow" also names a physician undergoing subspecialty training after residency, supported by a fellowship programme, and graduate students holding funded fellowships. Large philanthropic programmes, such as the MacArthur Fellowship and the Guggenheim Fellowship, also call their recipients fellows.
