Battle of Cape Hermaeum

The Battle of Cape Hermaeum (also spelled Cape Hermaea) was a naval battle fought in 255 BC, during the First Punic War, off Cape Hermaeum (modern Ras al-Tib / Cap Bon) on the coast of Africa. A Roman fleet returning from the invasion of Africa — after rescuing the survivors of Regulus's defeated army at Aspis (Clupea) — defeated a Carthaginian fleet that attempted to intercept it. Shortly afterward, the Roman fleet was largely destroyed by a storm off Camarina.

Background

The Roman invasion of Africa in 256 BC had begun triumphantly with the victory at Cape Ecnomus and Regulus's advance on Carthage, but in 255 BC the Carthaginian general Xanthippus destroyed Regulus's army at the Battle of Tunis (Bagradas River). Rome dispatched a fleet of some 350 ships under the consuls Marcus Aemilius Paullus and Servius Fulvius Paetinus Nobilior to evacuate the survivors holding out at Aspis.

Battle

Having embarked the survivors, the Roman fleet encountered the Carthaginian fleet — about 200 ships — off Cape Hermaeum. In the ensuing engagement the Romans, fighting with their customary corvus boarding tactics, defeated the Carthaginians decisively; Polybius reports 114 Carthaginian ships captured along with their crews and 16 sunk.

Aftermath

The victory was immediately overshadowed by catastrophe: against the advice of their pilots, the consuls sailed along the exposed southern coast of Sicily, where a great storm wrecked the fleet off Camarina — Polybius says only 80 of 364 ships survived, with losses of life in the tens of thousands, one of the worst naval disasters of the ancient world.
