Felon (medical)

A felon is a bacterial infection of the fingertip pulp, the closed compartment of fat and fibrous septa at the pad of a digit. It is usually caused by Staphylococcus aureus introduced through a minor puncture, splinter or hangnail, and most often affects the thumb and index finger.

Signs and symptoms

Because the pulp space is divided by fibrous septa anchored to the distal phalanx, pus collects under pressure, causing intense throbbing pain, redness, swelling and a tense, tender fingertip. Untreated, rising pressure can cut off blood flow, leading to tissue necrosis, osteomyelitis of the fingertip bone, or spread of infection to the flexor tendon sheath.

Treatment

Early cases without a formed abscess may respond to warm soaks, elevation and antibiotics covering staphylococci and streptococci. Once an abscess has formed, prompt incision and drainage is required, typically through a longitudinal incision that avoids the neurovascular bundles and tactile pad, followed by packing and oral antibiotics. Delayed treatment risks permanent loss of sensation or function in the fingertip.
