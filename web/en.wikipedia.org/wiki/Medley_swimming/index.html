Medley swimming is a form of competitive swimming in which swimmers use four different strokes in a single race. In the individual medley (IM), one swimmer completes legs of butterfly, backstroke, breaststroke, and freestyle in that order. In the medley relay, four swimmers each swim one stroke; because a backstroke start begins in the water, the relay order is backstroke, breaststroke, butterfly, freestyle.

Rules and turns

Each leg must be swum according to the rules of its stroke, and the transitions between strokes are governed by specific turn rules. Notably, the backstroke-to-breaststroke turn has generated controversy: swimmers must touch the wall on their back, but may then turn in any manner, which led to the development of the crossover turn and disputes over underwater dolphin kicks during the turn.

Events

The Olympic program includes the 200 m and 400 m individual medley for men and women, and the 4 × 100 m medley relay, including a mixed-gender relay added at the Tokyo 2020 Games. Distances in short-course (25 m pool) competition also include the 100 m individual medley.

Notable swimmers

Michael Phelps dominated the individual medley in the 2000s, winning four consecutive Olympic gold medals in the 200 m IM (2004–2016) and setting long-standing world records. Other prominent medley swimmers include Ryan Lochte, Katinka Hosszú, and Léon Marchand, who won the 200 m and 400 m IM at the Paris 2024 Olympics.
