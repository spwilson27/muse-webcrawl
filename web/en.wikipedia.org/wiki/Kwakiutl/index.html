Kwakiutl

RegionNorthern Vancouver Island and opposite mainland, British Columbia
LanguageKwak'wala

The Kwakiutl are an Indigenous people of the Pacific Northwest Coast, centred historically around Fort Rupert on northern Vancouver Island and the adjacent mainland of British Columbia, Canada. "Kwakiutl" properly refers to one group of the broader Kwakwaka'wakw peoples, though the name was long applied to all of them in anthropological literature.

Culture

Kwakiutl society was organised into ranked kin groups (numayms) holding hereditary privileges, crests, and ceremonial dances. The winter ceremonial season featured elaborate masked dances, and the potlatch redistributed wealth and validated status. The Kwakiutl were the subject of extensive ethnographic study by Franz Boas, whose work with George Hunt documented their language and culture in detail.
