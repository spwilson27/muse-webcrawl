Scuttling of the French fleet at Toulon

The scuttling of the French fleet at Toulon took place on 27 November 1942, when French naval officers deliberately sank their own warships at the naval base of Toulon to prevent them from falling into German hands.

Background

Under the 1940 armistice, much of the French fleet lay demilitarised at Toulon in the unoccupied zone of Vichy France, under standing orders to scuttle rather than surrender the ships. After the Allied landings in North Africa (Operation Torch), German forces occupied the free zone in Case Anton, and on 27 November German troops moved to seize the fleet in Operation Lila.

The scuttling

Admiral Jean de Laborde, commanding the High Seas Force, ordered the scuttling as German forces entered the arsenal. Seventy-seven vessels were sunk or destroyed, including three battleships (Strasbourg, Dunkerque and Provence), seven cruisers, fifteen destroyers and twelve submarines, though a handful of submarines escaped to sea. The Germans captured only a few small, damaged vessels, and the main fleet never served the Axis.
