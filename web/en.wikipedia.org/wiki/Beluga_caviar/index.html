Beluga caviar

SourceRoe of the beluga sturgeon (Huso huso)
Native rangeCaspian Sea, Black Sea and connected rivers
Egg sizeUp to about 3.5 mm — the largest of sturgeon roes
ColourPale silver-grey to black
StatusCritically endangered source species; trade restricted under CITES

Beluga caviar is caviar consisting of the roe (or eggs) of the beluga sturgeon, Huso huso. It is the largest-grained and traditionally the most prized and expensive of all caviars, found primarily in the Caspian Sea, the world's largest salt-water lake, bordered by Iran, Russia, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan. The roe can be harvested only from mature females, which may take 20 years to reach egg-bearing age.

Characteristics

Beluga caviar pearls are large, soft and delicate, ranging in colour from pale silver-grey (the rarest and most expensive, sometimes marketed as "royal" or "imperial" grades) to near-black; contrary to lesser caviar grading, for beluga the lightest eggs command the highest prices. The flavour is buttery, creamy and mildly nutty, with a delicate skin that bursts in the mouth. Prime beluga can retail for many thousands of US dollars per kilogram; a special Iranian "Almas" grade from albino beluga is among the most expensive foods in the world.

Conservation and bans

Overfishing, poaching and habitat loss caused the collapse of wild beluga sturgeon stocks in the late 20th century. The species is listed as critically endangered, and international trade is controlled under CITES. The United States Fish and Wildlife Service banned imports of Caspian beluga caviar in 2005. Since then, farmed beluga and hybrid sturgeon (such as "bester") caviar from aquaculture operations has increasingly supplied the legal market.
