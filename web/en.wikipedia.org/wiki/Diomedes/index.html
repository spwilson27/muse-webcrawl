Diomedes

Diomedes (Greek: Διομήδης) is a hero in Greek mythology, known for his participation in the Trojan War. Son of Tydeus and king of Argos, he was one of the finest Achaean warriors and a favourite of the goddess Athena.

In the Iliad

Book 5 of the Iliad is devoted to Diomedes' aristeia: emboldened by Athena, he wounds the gods Aphrodite and Ares themselves. He duels Aeneas and Pandarus, exchanges armour with the Lycian Glaucus upon discovering a bond of guest-friendship, and with Odysseus steals the horses of Rhesus and later the Palladium from Troy in episodes told outside the Iliad.

After the war

Returning to Argos to find his wife unfaithful and plots against him, Diomedes in later traditions emigrated to Italy, founding cities in Apulia (Daunia), where the Diomedean islands and local cults preserved his memory. Dante places his shade, with Odysseus, among the fraudulent counsellors in the Inferno.
