Vincent Massey

Born20 February 1887, Toronto, Ontario, Canada
Died30 December 1967 (aged 80), London, England
OfficeGovernor General of Canada (1952–1959)

Charles Vincent Massey (20 February 1887 – 30 December 1967) was a Canadian lawyer and diplomat who served as Governor General of Canada, the 18th since Confederation, from 1952 to 1959. Massey was the first Canadian-born individual to be appointed to the post. Born in Toronto, he was a member of the prominent Massey family, founders of the Massey agricultural machinery company.

Massey, the first Canadian-born governor general

Career

Educated at the University of Toronto and Balliol College, Oxford, Massey held a series of diplomatic and public posts: he was Canada's first envoy to the United States as ambassador in Washington (1926–1930), high commissioner to the United Kingdom (1935–1946), and chancellor of the University of Toronto. From 1949 to 1951 he chaired the Royal Commission on National Development in the Arts, Letters and Sciences — the Massey Commission — whose recommendations led to the creation of the Canada Council for the Arts and support for the national library and universities.

Governor general

As governor general, Massey travelled widely within Canada, promoting Canadian culture, bilingualism and unity, and encouraging the arts. He established awards including the Governor General's Awards for architecture and promoted the founding of Massey College at the University of Toronto, which opened in 1963 with his endowment. The Massey Lectures, an annual series of lectures on radio, also bear his name.
