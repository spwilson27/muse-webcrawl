Tolerance stack up

Tolerance stack up (also called tolerance stacking or tolerance analysis) is the process of calculating the cumulative effect of part tolerances in an assembly to determine whether the assembled product will meet its dimensional and functional requirements.

Methods

In a worst-case analysis, each dimension is assumed to be at its extreme limit simultaneously, guaranteeing interchangeability but often requiring unnecessarily tight (and expensive) tolerances. In a statistical analysis, typically using the root sum square (RSS) method, tolerances are combined in quadrature on the assumption that individual dimensions follow a distribution (often normal) centred on the nominal; this permits looser component tolerances for the same overall risk of assembly failure.

Application

Tolerance stack-up is central to geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T) practice in mechanical design. Designers identify the chain of dimensions ("tolerance loop") that contributes to a critical gap, clearance, or interference—such as the flushness of a door panel or the endplay of a shaft—and allocate tolerances among the contributing parts so the loop closes within specification at acceptable cost and yield.
