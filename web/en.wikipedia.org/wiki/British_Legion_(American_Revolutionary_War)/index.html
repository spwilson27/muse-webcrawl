The British Legion was a Loyalist military unit of the British Army during the American Revolutionary War, composed of American Loyalist cavalry and light infantry. Formed in New York in 1778 from several smaller Loyalist corps, the Legion is best known for its service in the southern campaigns of 1780–1781 under Lieutenant Colonel Banastre Tarleton.

Service

Under Tarleton the Legion won a fearsome reputation for speed and ferocity, most notoriously at the Battle of Waxhaws (29 May 1780), where Continental troops were cut down after resistance collapsed — an episode Patriots remembered as "Tarleton's quarter". The Legion screened Cornwallis's army in the Carolinas, fought at Blackstock's Farm and Cowpens (17 January 1781, where it suffered heavy losses), and took part in the race to the Dan, Guilford Court House, and the final campaign ending at Yorktown, where the surviving Legion surrendered with Cornwallis in October 1781.

Aftermath

The unit was disbanded in 1783. Many of its officers and men, as United Empire Loyalists, resettled in Nova Scotia and New Brunswick in British North America.
