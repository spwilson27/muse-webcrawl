Ankle-foot orthosis

[Image: A plastic ankle-foot orthosis worn inside a shoe]

An ankle-foot orthosis (AFO) is an orthotic device worn on the lower leg and foot to support the ankle, hold the foot and ankle in the correct position, and compensate for weakness or deformity. AFOs are commonly used for foot drop following stroke, cerebral palsy, multiple sclerosis, peripheral nerve injury and other neuromuscular conditions.

Types

Designs range from flexible posterior leaf-spring AFOs — which assist toe clearance during the swing phase of walking — to solid-ankle and hinged designs with adjustable stops, and ground-reaction AFOs that help stabilise the knee. They are made from moulded thermoplastics or carbon fibre composites, prefabricated or custom-cast to the wearer's leg.

Function

By controlling ankle motion, an AFO improves gait safety and energy efficiency, prevents trips and falls caused by toes catching on the ground, and can reduce spasticity-related deformity over time.
