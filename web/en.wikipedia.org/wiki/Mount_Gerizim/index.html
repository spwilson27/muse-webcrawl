Elevation881 m (2,890 ft)

SignificanceHoliest site of the Samaritans

Mount Gerizim (Hebrew: הַר גְּרִזִים; Samaritan Hebrew: ʾĀrgārīzēm) is one of two mountains flanking the city of Nablus in the West Bank, facing Mount Ebal across the valley of ancient Shechem. It is the most sacred site of the Samaritans, who regard it — rather than Jerusalem — as the place chosen by God for His sanctuary.

Biblical tradition

In Deuteronomy, Gerizim is the "mountain of blessing": Moses instructed that six tribes stand on Gerizim to pronounce blessings when Israel entered the land, and Joshua later built an altar there. The Samaritan Pentateuch names Gerizim as the site of the first altar in the land and the place God "has chosen". In the New Testament, the Samaritan woman at the well alludes to the dispute: "Our ancestors worshipped on this mountain" (John 4).

History

A Samaritan temple stood on the summit from the Persian or early Hellenistic period until it was destroyed by the Hasmonean king John Hyrcanus around 110 BCE. The Romans later built a temple to Zeus on the peak, and a Byzantine octagonal church of the Theotokos, erected under Emperor Zeno in 475 CE, whose ruins still crown the mountain. Today the Samaritan community of Kiryat Luza lives on the summit plateau, where the Passover sacrifice is still performed annually.
