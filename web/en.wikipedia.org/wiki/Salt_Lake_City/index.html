Salt Lake City

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Salt Lake City
State: Utah, USA (capital and largest city)
Founded: 1847, by Mormon pioneers under Brigham Young
Population: about 210,000 (city); 1.2 million (metro)

Salt Lake City is the capital and most populous city of Utah, in the western United States, set between the Wasatch Range and the Great Salt Lake from which it takes its name. It is the world headquarters of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (the Mormons), whose pioneers founded it in 1847.

History

Brigham Young led the first pioneer company into the Salt Lake Valley on 24 July 1847 — "This is the right place," in the famous (perhaps polished) account — and laid out a grid city around Temple Square, from which all addresses are still reckoned. The city became the hub of a Mormon commonwealth of irrigation and settlement across the interior West, surviving federal conflict over polygamy (abandoned by the church in 1890, enabling Utah statehood in 1896).

Modern city

Salt Lake City hosted the 2002 Winter Olympics (and will again in 2034), leveraging the ski resorts of the Wasatch — "the Greatest Snow on Earth" — minutes from downtown. Its economy spans finance, biotech and the "Silicon Slopes" tech corridor. Its crises are environmental: the Great Salt Lake has shrunk to record lows, exposing toxic dust-laden lakebed, and winter inversions trap some of America's worst air in the valley — a desert city founded on irrigation now negotiating with its drying lake.
