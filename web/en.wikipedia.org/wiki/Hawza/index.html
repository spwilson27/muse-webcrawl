Hawza

Ḥawza ʿilmiyya
Tradition: Twelver Shia Islam
Major centres: Najaf (Iraq), Qom (Iran), Karbala
Subjects: Jurisprudence (fiqh), principles (uṣūl), theology, Arabic

A hawza (Arabic: حوزة علمية, ḥawza ʿilmiyya) is a Shia Islamic seminary — a circle of scholars and students centred on senior jurists (marjaʿs) — where students train to become clerics.

Study

Students progress through preliminary, intermediate and advanced 'lessons beyond the text' (dars al-khārij) stages in jurisprudence, legal theory, hadith, theology, philosophy and Arabic grammar, often over decades. Funding traditionally comes from religious taxes such as khums paid to a marjaʿ.

Centres

The hawza of Najaf in Iraq is the oldest major centre, associated in recent decades with Grand Ayatollah Ali al-Sistani. The hawza of Qom in Iran grew after 1922 and became central after the 1979 Iranian Revolution, alongside historic rivalries and differing views on clerical rule (velāyat-e faqīh).
