Accreditation Council for Continuing Medical Education

The Accreditation Council for Continuing Medical Education (ACCME) is a nonprofit organization based in Chicago, Illinois, that accredits institutions providing continuing medical education (CME) for physicians in the United States. Founded in 1981, it succeeded earlier AMA-coordinated mechanisms for approving CME sponsors and sets the standards by which accredited providers plan, implement, and evaluate educational activities for which physicians earn AMA PRA Category 1 Credit.

Role and structure

The ACCME accredits several hundred organizations directly — including medical schools, hospitals, medical societies, and education companies — while state medical societies recognized by the ACCME accredit intrastate providers; together the system encompasses roughly 1,700 accredited organizations. Its seven member organizations are the American Board of Medical Specialties, the American Hospital Association, the American Medical Association, the Association of American Medical Colleges, the Association for Hospital Medical Education, the Council of Medical Specialty Societies, and the Federation of State Medical Boards.

Standards

Accredited providers must comply with the ACCME's Accreditation Criteria and its Standards for Integrity and Independence in Accredited Continuing Education (revised in 2020, replacing the 2004 Standards for Commercial Support). These require that education be based on valid content, free of commercial bias, and that financial relationships between planners/speakers and ineligible companies be identified, mitigated, and disclosed. Providers must also analyze changes in learners' competence, performance, or patient outcomes.

Criticism

The ACCME and the CME system have been scrutinized over pharmaceutical and device industry funding of education; the standards restricting commercial support were tightened in response to congressional and academic criticism that industry-funded CME can bias prescribing.
