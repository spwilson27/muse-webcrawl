Chitral District

Chitral District (Urdu: ضلع چترال) was a district of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province in northern Pakistan, the largest district in the province by area, covering about 14,850 square kilometres. Its capital was the town of Chitral on the Kunar River. In 2018 the district was divided for administrative purposes into Upper Chitral District and Lower Chitral District.

Geography

Chitral lies in the Hindu Kush mountain range, bordered by Afghanistan's Wakhan Corridor to the north and west, Gilgit-Baltistan to the east, and the districts of Dir and Swat to the south. Tirich Mir, at 7,708 metres the highest peak of the Hindu Kush, rises in the north of the district. The Chitral River (upper Kunar) flows south through the main valley. Access from the south is by the Lowari Pass (3,118 m), made all-weather by the Lowari Tunnel, opened in stages from 2017; the Shandur Pass (3,700 m) connects Chitral with Gilgit to the east.

History

Chitral was historically an independent princely state ruled by the Mehtar of the Katur dynasty. It came under British influence in the late 19th century, notably during the Siege of Chitral in 1895, and acceded to Pakistan in 1947; the state's separate status was finally abolished in 1969, when it was incorporated into the North-West Frontier Province (now Khyber Pakhtunkhwa) as a district.

People and culture

The majority population are the Kho people, speakers of Khowar (Chitrali), an Indo-Aryan Dardic language that serves as the region's lingua franca. Many Kho follow Ismaili Islam. The district's three Kalasha valleys (Bumburet, Rumbur and Birir) are home to the Kalash people, who retain a polytheistic religion and distinctive dress and festivals such as Chilam Joshi and Uchal. Wakhi, Gujari, Dameli, Gawar-Bati and other languages are also spoken; the overall population was about 447,000 in the 2017 census.

Chitral is famous for polo played in its freestyle mountain form; the annual Shandur Polo Festival, held on the world's highest polo ground between teams from Chitral and Gilgit, is a major tourist event. Tourism centres on the valleys, trout fishing, mountaineering in the Hindu Kush, and the Kalasha festivals.
