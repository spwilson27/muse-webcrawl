Kissimmee River

Kissimmee River Location: Florida, United States
Length: about 134 miles (216 km) originally; channelised to 56 miles
Source: Lake Kissimmee
Mouth: Lake Okeechobee
Known for: Channelisation (1960s) and major restoration project

The Kissimmee River is a river in south-central Florida, in the United States, flowing from Lake Kissimmee southward into Lake Okeechobee. Originally it meandered for about 134 miles through a broad floodplain of wetlands that sustained abundant fish and waterfowl and filtered water flowing into Okeechobee and the Everglades.

Channelisation

After devastating floods from a 1947 hurricane, the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers channelised the river between 1960 and 1971, dredging it into the straight, 30-foot-deep C-38 canal only 56 miles long. The project drained some 35,000 acres of floodplain wetlands, waterfowl populations collapsed, and nutrient-laden water flowed rapidly into Lake Okeechobee, worsening its water quality. The channelisation became a notorious example of environmentally destructive flood control.

Restoration

In response, Congress authorised the Kissimmee River Restoration Project, one of the largest river restoration efforts in the world. Beginning in 1999, engineers backfilled long stretches of the canal, removed water-control structures and re-carved historic meanders, restoring flow to about 24 miles of the original river channel and roughly 12,000 acres of floodplain wetlands. Completed in 2021 at a cost of around one billion dollars, the project has seen the return of wading birds, sports fish and floodplain wildlife, and is cited as proof that large river ecosystems can recover when natural hydrology is restored.
