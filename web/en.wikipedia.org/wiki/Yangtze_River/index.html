Yangtze River

The Yangtze River (Chinese: 长江; pinyin: Cháng Jiāng, literally "long river") is the longest river in Asia and the third-longest in the world, flowing about 6,300 km (3,900 mi) from the glaciers of the Tibetan Plateau eastward through China to the East China Sea near Shanghai. It drains one-fifth of China's land area, and its basin is home to roughly one-third of the country's population.

Course

The river rises in the Tanggula Mountains of Qinghai, where its uppermost reach is called the Tongtian. As the Jinsha it descends through deep gorges along the borders of Tibet, Sichuan, and Yunnan, then crosses the Sichuan Basin and cuts the famous Three Gorges before entering the lowland plains of central China, passing the great lakes Dongting and Poyang and the cities of Wuhan and Nanjing on its way to the sea.

Importance

The Yangtze has been central to Chinese agriculture, transport, and culture for millennia; its fertile delta is one of the world's most productive rice-growing regions. The Three Gorges Dam, completed in 2006, is the world's largest power station by installed capacity and also serves flood control and navigation. The river's ecology — home to species such as the Chinese paddlefish (declared extinct in 2020) and the Yangtze finless porpoise — has been heavily affected by damming, shipping, and pollution, prompting a ten-year fishing ban imposed in 2021.
