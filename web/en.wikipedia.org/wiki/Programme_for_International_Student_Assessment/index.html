Programme for International Student Assessment

The Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) is a worldwide study by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) evaluating education systems by measuring 15-year-old school pupils' performance in mathematics, science, and reading. First conducted in 2000 and repeated every three years, PISA tests students' ability to apply knowledge to real-life problems rather than recall curriculum content, and collects contextual data on students, schools, and systems.

Design

Each cycle emphasises one major domain (reading in 2000 and 2009, mathematics in 2003 and 2012, science in 2006 and 2015, reading in 2018, mathematics in 2022; the 2021 cycle was postponed to 2022 because of the COVID-19 pandemic). Around half a million students, representing tens of millions of 15-year-olds, take the two-hour computer-based test in each cycle, and complete questionnaires on their background, attitudes, and learning environments. Results are reported on scales with an OECD mean originally set at 500 points.

Findings and influence

East Asian systems (Singapore, Shanghai and later the four participating Chinese provinces, Japan, Korea) and countries such as Finland, Estonia, and Canada have frequently topped the rankings. PISA results have driven major policy debates and reforms — notably the "PISA shock" in Germany after 2000, which prompted national education standards — and the OECD's analyses link performance to factors such as early childhood education, socio-economic equity, and teacher status. The 2022 results showed an unprecedented decline in mathematics and reading scores across many countries, attributed largely to pandemic disruption.

Criticism

Academics have criticised PISA's methodology and influence: the translation and cultural equivalence of test items, the narrowing of education policy to measurable outcomes, the statistical treatment of sampling and ranking uncertainty, and the OECD's policy prescriptions. The OECD responds that its technical standards, field trials, and scaling methods are robust and transparent.
