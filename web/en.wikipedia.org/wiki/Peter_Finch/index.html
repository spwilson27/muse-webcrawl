Peter Finch

BornFrederick George Peter Ingle Finch, September 28, 1916, London, England
DiedJanuary 14, 1977, Beverly Hills, California, U.S.
OccupationActor

Frederick George Peter Ingle Finch (September 28, 1916 – January 14, 1977) was an English-Australian actor of theatre, film and radio. Raised largely in Australia, he built his career in British films before achieving international acclaim in Hollywood. He is best remembered for his performance as the deranged television news anchor Howard Beale in Network (1976).

Career

Finch worked in Australian radio and film in the 1930s and 1940s, and served in the Australian Army during the Second World War. Discovered by Laurence Olivier, he moved to Britain and gained recognition in films such as A Town Like Alice (1956), The Nun's Story (1959) opposite Audrey Hepburn, and The Trials of Oscar Wilde (1960), in which he played Wilde. He won five BAFTA Awards for Best Actor, more than any other performer.

Network and death

His portrayal of Howard Beale, whose on-air breakdown includes the famous declaration "I'm as mad as hell, and I'm not going to take this anymore!", earned universal acclaim. Finch died of a heart attack in January 1977, while promoting the film, and became the first actor to win the Academy Award for Best Actor posthumously. His widow, Eletha Barrett, accepted the award on his behalf.
