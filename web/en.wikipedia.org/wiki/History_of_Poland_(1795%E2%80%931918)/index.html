History of Poland (1795–1918)

The history of Poland from 1795 to 1918 covers the period when the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth had been partitioned among Prussia, Austria, and Russia, and Poland existed as a nation without a state. The Third Partition of 1795 erased the Commonwealth from the map of Europe for 123 years.

Napoleonic era

Napoleon created the Duchy of Warsaw in 1807, a Polish client state that briefly restored partial statehood. After his fall, the Congress of Vienna (1815) created Congress Poland under the Russian tsar, the Free City of Kraków, and confirmed Prussian and Austrian holdings.

Uprisings

Poles repeatedly rose against the partitioning powers, notably in the November Uprising (1830–31) and the January Uprising (1863–64), both crushed by Russia, followed by intensified Russification and Germanization. Thousands emigrated in the "Great Emigration" to France and beyond.

Towards independence

Cultural and economic "organic work" preserved Polish identity. During the First World War, the partitioning empires collapsed, and in November 1918 Poland regained independence as the Second Polish Republic under leaders including Józef Piłsudski. See also Partitions of Poland.
