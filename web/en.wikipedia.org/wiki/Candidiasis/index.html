Candidiasis

Candidiasis is a fungal infection caused by yeasts of the genus Candida, most commonly Candida albicans. It is commonly known as a yeast infection or thrush.

Types and symptoms

Oral candidiasis (oral thrush) produces white patches in the mouth. Vulvovaginal candidiasis causes itching, soreness and discharge, and is very common. Cutaneous candidiasis affects moist skin folds. In people with weakened immune systems, Candida can invade the bloodstream (candidaemia) or deep organs, a serious and potentially fatal condition.

Causes

Candida species normally live harmlessly on skin and mucous membranes. Overgrowth is favoured by antibiotics, diabetes, pregnancy, immunosuppression, dentures and moist environments.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually clinical, supported by microscopy and culture. Superficial infections are treated with topical antifungals such as clotrimazole or nystatin, or oral fluconazole. Invasive candidiasis requires systemic antifungals such as echinocandins and removal of infected catheters.

Epidemiology

Most women experience vulvovaginal candidiasis at least once. Oral thrush is common in infants, denture wearers and people with HIV/AIDS or receiving inhaled corticosteroids.
