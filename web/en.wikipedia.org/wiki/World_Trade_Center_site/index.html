World Trade Center site

Area16 acres (6.5 ha)
LocationBounded by Vesey, Liberty, Church and West Streets, Manhattan, New York City
OwnerPort Authority of New York and New Jersey
Also known asGround Zero (after 2001)

The World Trade Center site, often called Ground Zero after the September 11 attacks, is a 16-acre (6.5 ha) plot in Lower Manhattan, New York City, bounded by Vesey Street to the north, the West Side Highway to the west, Liberty Street to the south, and Church Street to the east. The Port Authority of New York and New Jersey owns the site.

History

The site was created in the 1960s by land reclamation along the Hudson River, on land that had been the Radio Row electronics district. The original World Trade Center complex, including the Twin Towers, was built there between 1966 and 1985. The "bathtub" — a slurry-wall enclosure that keeps out the Hudson River — survived the 2001 attacks and determined the layout of everything rebuilt afterward.

After the attacks

Recovery and cleanup of the site, involving the removal of some 1.8 million tons of debris, was completed in May 2002. Human remains continued to be identified from recovered fragments for many years. The Lower Manhattan Development Corporation oversaw planning, and Daniel Libeskind's "Memory Foundations" master plan was selected in February 2003 after a public design competition.

Present layout

The western half of the site is occupied by the National September 11 Memorial plaza and museum. The eastern half holds the office towers of the new World Trade Center, the Transportation Hub (Oculus), and St. Nicholas Greek Orthodox Church, rebuilt at the site's southeastern corner in Liberty Park. The site excludes the separate parcels of 7 World Trade Center to the north and the former Deutsche Bank Building site to the south.
