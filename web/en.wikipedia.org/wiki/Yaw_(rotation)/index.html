Yaw (rotation)

Yaw
AxisVertical (normal) axis
RotationAbout the vertical axis, side to side
Companion anglesPitch, Roll
Primary controlRudder (aircraft)

Yaw is a term used in aviation, and more generally in dynamics, to describe a rotation about an object's vertical axis. A yaw rotation is a movement around the yaw axis of a rigid body that changes the direction it is pointing, to the left or right of its direction of motion. The yaw rate, or yaw velocity, is the angular velocity of this rotation, commonly measured in degrees per second.

Together with pitch and roll, yaw is one of the three principal rotations — often described by Euler angles or Tait–Bryan angles — that define the orientation (attitude) of a vehicle such as an aircraft, ship, spacecraft, or automobile in three-dimensional space.

Aviation

In flight dynamics, yaw is controlled primarily by the rudder, a hinged surface on the trailing edge of the vertical stabilizer. Deflecting the rudder creates a side force on the tail that swings the nose left or right. Because a rolling aircraft tends to yaw in the opposite direction (a phenomenon called adverse yaw), coordinated turns require the pilot — or a yaw damper in modern airliners — to apply rudder together with aileron input.

Yawing also arises from asymmetric thrust, crosswinds, and engine failure in multi-engine aircraft, where the loss of thrust on one side produces a strong yawing moment toward the dead engine that must be countered with rudder.

Ships and vehicles

For a ship, yaw is the deviation of the bow from its intended course, caused by waves, wind, and steering, and is counteracted by the rudder and by autopilot systems. In automotive dynamics, the yaw rate of a car — measured by yaw-rate sensors used in electronic stability control — is compared with the driver's steering input to detect understeer or oversteer.

Measurement

Yaw angle is measured relative to a reference heading, such as true north or a runway centerline. Gyroscopes, magnetometers, and inertial measurement units provide the yaw information used in attitude and heading reference systems.
