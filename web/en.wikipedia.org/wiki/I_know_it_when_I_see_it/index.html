I know it when I see it

"I know it when I see it" is a colloquial expression by which a speaker attempts to categorise an observable fact or event, while admitting the category is subjective or lacks clearly defined parameters. The phrase is best known from United States Supreme Court Justice Potter Stewart's concurring opinion in Jacobellis v. Ohio (1964).

Jacobellis v. Ohio

The case concerned whether the French film The Lovers was obscene and thus unprotected by the First Amendment. Stewart wrote that hard-core pornography was not protected, but that he could not succeed in intelligibly defining it: "But I know it when I see it, and the motion picture involved in this case is not that."

Legacy

The phrase became a famous shorthand for subjective standards in law and beyond, and it is frequently quoted — and parodied — in debates about obscenity, art and other hard-to-define categories. The Court replaced the underlying obscenity test in Miller v. California (1973).
