Riverside County is a county located in the southern portion of the U.S. state of California. As of the 2020 census, the population was 2,418,185, making it the fourth-most populous county in California and the tenth-most populous in the United States. The county seat and largest city is Riverside. The county was named for the city of Riverside, so called because of its location beside the Santa Ana River.

Geography

Riverside County is one of the largest counties in California by area, covering 7,303 square miles (18,920 km²). It stretches from the Greater Los Angeles area in the west nearly to the Colorado River and the Arizona border in the east. The western part of the county forms part of the Inland Empire metropolitan area together with San Bernardino County, while the centre and east comprise the Coachella Valley resort cities (including Palm Springs, Palm Desert and Indio) and the deserts beyond, including part of Joshua Tree National Park and the Salton Sea shoreline in the southeast.

History

Riverside County was formed in 1893 from parts of San Bernardino County and San Diego County. The area was historically inhabited by Cahuilla, Luiseño, Serrano and other Indigenous peoples. Citrus growing — especially the Washington navel orange, propagated in Riverside in the 1870s — drove early prosperity. In the 20th century the county grew with suburbanisation, logistics and warehousing in the west, and tourism in the Coachella Valley, home to the Coachella and Stagecoach music festivals.

Communities

Major cities include Riverside, Moreno Valley, Corona, Murrieta, Temecula, Hemet, Indio, Palm Desert, Palm Springs, Cathedral City, Lake Elsinore, Perris, Menifee and Jurupa Valley. Much of the eastern portion is unincorporated desert, including the community of Blythe on the Colorado River.
