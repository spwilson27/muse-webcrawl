The Producers Guild of America (PGA) is a trade association representing producers in film, television and new media in the United States. It grew out of the Screen Producers Guild (founded 1950) and the Television Producers Guild, which merged in 1962. Unlike the Writers Guild or Directors Guild, the PGA is not a labour union and does not negotiate collective bargaining agreements; membership is open to qualified producers across media.

Producers Guild Awards

Since 1990 the Guild has presented the Producers Guild Awards, whose top honour, the Darryl F. Zanuck Award for Outstanding Producer of Theatrical Motion Pictures, is regarded as a strong predictor of the Academy Award for Best Picture, since both use a preferential ballot over a similar field.

Producers Mark

The Guild campaigned for decades against "vanity credits" and in 2012 introduced the Producers Mark — the lowercase letters "p.g.a." after a producer's name in screen credits — certifying that the individual performed the majority of producing functions on the production. The mark requires an application and verification process.

Activities

The PGA maintains a code of credits, runs the annual Produced By conference, advocates on issues such as production incentives and workplace safety, and has promoted sustainable production through its PGA Green initiative.
