Synthetic biology

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Synthetic biology is an interdisciplinary field that applies engineering principles to biology: designing and building new biological parts, circuits and organisms, or redesigning natural systems, for useful purposes. It draws on genetic engineering, systems biology and Bioinformatics.

Approaches and milestones

Practitioners assemble standardised DNA 'parts' into genetic circuits that switch genes on and off, build minimal genomes to find the smallest workable set of genes, and engineer microbes to manufacture fuels, chemicals and medicines — for example yeast engineered to produce the antimalarial drug precursor artemisinic acid. In 2010 a team at the J. Craig Venter Institute reported a bacterial cell controlled by a chemically synthesised genome.

Governance

Creating novel organisms raises biosafety and biosecurity questions, including the risk of engineered pathogens and the environmental release of modified organisms. International debate continues over governance, especially as DNA synthesis and genome editing become cheaper and more widely available.
