Master James of Saint George (c. 1230 – 1309), known in French as Maître Jacques de Saint-Georges, was a master mason and military architect from Savoy who designed most of the great castles built by Edward I of England in Wales, including Conwy, Harlech, Caernarfon and Beaumaris. He is regarded as one of the greatest architects of castles in medieval Europe.

Origins and Savoyard career

James came from Saint-Georges-d'Espéranche in the county of Savoy, and was probably the son of a master mason named John (Jean) who worked for Count Peter II of Savoy. Peter was a close ally of the English crown — he was uncle by marriage to Queen Eleanor of Provence — and Savoyard masons were familiar figures at Edward's court. James himself worked on Savoyard castles such as Yverdon and Chillon, developing the geometric, concentric planning and round-towered designs he later brought to Wales.

Service to Edward I

Edward I recruited James after his first Welsh war in 1277. He was appointed master of the king's works in Wales, supervising an enormous programme of construction employing thousands of workmen. His castles are distinguished by their compact, symmetrical plans, concentric lines of defence, and gatehouses of exceptional strength. After the second Welsh war he designed Edward's finest castles: Conwy (1283–87), Harlech, Caernarfon — whose polygonal towers and banded masonry deliberately evoked Constantinople and imperial Rome — and, from 1295, Beaumaris on Anglesey, the most perfectly concentric castle of the series, though never finished to its intended height.

James later served Edward in Scotland, working on fortifications there, and was granted land and a pension. He died in 1309. The Welsh castles he created were inscribed by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site, the Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd, in 1986.
