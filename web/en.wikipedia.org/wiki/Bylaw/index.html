A bylaw (also spelled by-law or bye-law) is a rule or law established by an organisation or community to regulate itself, as allowed or provided for by some higher authority. The higher authority, generally a legislature or, in the case of a corporation, the jurisdiction in which it is incorporated, gives the primary entity the power to enact bylaws on specified matters.

Municipal bylaws

In many countries, including Canada, South Africa and New Zealand, municipal bylaws are local laws made by city and town councils under powers delegated by provincial or national legislation. Common subjects include zoning and land use, noise, parking, animal control, building standards and public health. Municipal bylaws are enforced by bylaw enforcement officers and are punishable by fines; they must not conflict with higher-level law.

Organisational bylaws

For corporations, non-profit organisations, condominium associations and clubs, bylaws are the internal rules governing the body's own operation: the powers and election of directors and officers, membership, meetings, quorums, voting procedures and amendment processes. In the United States and Canada, corporate bylaws, together with the articles of incorporation or constitution, form the core governing documents of the entity. The word derives from Old Norse býr (town) + lǫg (law), reflecting its origin in the laws of Scandinavian towns.
