Radical axis

The radical axis (or power line) of two non-concentric circles is the set of points that have equal power with respect to both circles — that is, points from which tangents drawn to the two circles have equal length. It is a straight line perpendicular to the line joining the centres of the circles.

Properties

If the two circles intersect at two points, the radical axis is the line through those points; if they touch at one point, it is their common tangent there. For circles that do not intersect, the axis can be constructed using auxiliary circles. When the two circles are concentric the radical axis does not exist (or is regarded as the line at infinity in projective terms).

Radical centre

For three circles with non-collinear centres, the three radical axes meet in a single point called the radical centre, which has equal power with respect to all three circles. The radical axis concept generalises, in coordinate geometry, by subtracting the equations of the two circles to obtain a linear equation.
