Potsdamer Platz

Potsdamer Platz is a public square and traffic intersection in the centre of Berlin, Germany, named after the city of Potsdam. Before the Second World War it was one of the busiest traffic junctions in Europe and a centre of Berlin nightlife, home to grand hotels, department stores, and one of Europe's first traffic lights, installed in 1924.

Division and reunification

The square was heavily damaged by Allied bombing and then lay directly on the border between the Soviet and Western sectors of the divided city; the Berlin Wall crossed it from 1961, leaving the area a derelict wasteland within the Wall's death strip for nearly three decades.

Redevelopment

After reunification in 1990, Potsdamer Platz became the largest building site in Europe. A masterplan led by Renzo Piano produced a new quarter of offices, shops, cinemas, and the Sony Center, completed around 2000. The rebuilt square is again a major commercial and transport hub, served by regional, S-Bahn, and U-Bahn stations, and hosts the Berlinale film festival at the nearby Theater am Potsdamer Platz.
