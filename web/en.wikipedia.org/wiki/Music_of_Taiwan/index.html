Music of Taiwan

The music of Taiwan reflects the island's diverse population: indigenous Austronesian peoples, Hoklo and Hakka settlers from Fujian and Guangdong, Japanese colonial influence (1895–1945), and post-1949 mainland Chinese culture. Taiwan became the centre of the global Mandopop industry.

Indigenous musicians performing traditional polyphonic songs.

Indigenous music

Taiwan's indigenous peoples, including the Amis, Bunun, and Paiwan, maintain rich vocal traditions. The Bunun's eight-part polyphonic singing (pasibutbut), a prayer for the millet harvest built from gradually expanding chords, is internationally renowned. In 1993, a recording of Amis elder Difang's singing was sampled in Enigma's "Return to Innocence", bringing global attention (and a legal settlement) to indigenous music rights.

Folk and traditional music

Hoklo folk music includes nanguan (elegant chamber music with pipa and dongxiao), beiguan, and Taiwanese opera (koa-á-hì), performed at temple festivals. Hakka mountain songs and tea-picking opera are distinctive Hakka forms. During the Japanese era, songs such as "Rainy Night Flower" (1934) became classics of Taiwanese-language song.

Popular music

Teresa Teng, born in Taiwan, became one of the most beloved singers across Asia in the 1970s–80s. After martial law ended in 1987, Taiwanese-language (Taiyupop) and indigenous and Hakka pop flourished. Taiwan's Mandopop industry produced stars including A-Mei, Jolin Tsai, Jay Chou — whose blend of pop, R&B, and classical elements redefined Mandopop in the 2000s — and the rock band Mayday. The Golden Melody Awards, established in 1990, are the premier honours for Chinese-language music.

Taiwan also has vibrant indie rock, electronic, and hip-hop scenes, centred on Taipei venues and festivals such as Spring Scream in Kenting.
