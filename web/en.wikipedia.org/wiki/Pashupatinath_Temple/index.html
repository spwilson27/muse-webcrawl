Pashupatinath Temple

The Pashupatinath Temple is a Hindu temple complex in Kathmandu, Nepal, dedicated to Pashupati, a form of Shiva as lord of the animals. Located on the banks of the Bagmati River in the Deopatan area east of central Kathmandu, it is one of the most sacred Hindu pilgrimage sites in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Kathmandu Valley listing.

History

A temple is believed to have existed on the site from at least the early centuries CE, with the present main pagoda-style temple largely dating to the late 17th century after earlier structures were damaged. The complex grew to include hundreds of shrines, ashrams, and rest houses for pilgrims along both banks of the river.

Architecture and worship

The main temple is built in the Nepalese pagoda style with a gilded roof, four silver-plated doors, and rich wood carvings. Only Hindus are permitted to enter the main temple courtyard; the large lingam inside is bathed daily and worshipped by hereditary Bhatta priests from Karnataka. Cremation ghats along the Bagmati, particularly at Arya Ghat, are used for Hindu funerals.

Festivals

The major festival is Maha Shivaratri, which draws hundreds of thousands of devotees and sadhus from Nepal and India. Teej and other Shaivite observances are also celebrated.
