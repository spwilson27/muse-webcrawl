Alfonso XII (28 November 1857 – 25 November 1885), nicknamed El Pacificador ("the Peacemaker"), was King of Spain from 1874 until his death in 1885. His reign marked the restoration of the Bourbon monarchy after the collapse of the First Spanish Republic, and the beginning of the period known as the Restoration.

Early life and exile

Alfonso was the son of Queen Isabella II and, nominally, her husband Francis, Duke of Cádiz. When the Glorious Revolution of 1868 deposed his mother, the royal family went into exile, first in Paris. The young prince was educated at the Theresianum in Vienna and later at the Royal Military College at Sandhurst in England. In 1870 Isabella formally abdicated in his favour, making Alfonso the legitimate Bourbon claimant to the throne.

Restoration

The politician Antonio Cánovas del Castillo orchestrated Alfonso's return, promoting the Sandhurst Manifesto of 1874, in which the prince promised a constitutional monarchy. On 29 December 1874 Brigadier Martínez Campos proclaimed Alfonso king at Sagunto in a pronunciamiento, and the Bourbon Restoration began. Alfonso entered Madrid in January 1875.

Reign

Under Cánovas's system, power alternated peacefully between the Conservative and Liberal parties — the turno pacífico — stabilising Spanish politics after decades of civil war and military intervention. Alfonso ended the Third Carlist War in 1876, earning his nickname "the Peacemaker", and presided over the adoption of the Constitution of 1876. The Ten Years' War in Cuba ended with the Pact of Zanjón in 1878.

Personal life and death

Alfonso married his first cousin Mercedes of Orléans in 1878, but she died of tuberculosis only months later. In 1879 he married Archduchess Maria Christina of Austria, who bore two daughters and a posthumous son. Alfonso died of tuberculosis at the Royal Palace of El Pardo on 25 November 1885, three days before his twenty-eighth birthday. Maria Christina acted as regent until the birth of Alfonso XIII in May 1886.

Legacy

Alfonso XII is remembered as a relatively popular and conciliatory monarch whose early death cut short a reign that had brought Spain a measure of political stability. Numerous towns, streets and the battleship Alfonso XIII commemorated the restored dynasty.
