Bipartite Kneser graph

In graph theory, a bipartite Kneser graph H(n, k) is a graph in which the vertices represent the k-element and (n − k)-element subsets of a set of n elements, and two vertices are connected by an edge if they correspond to sets that are disjoint (equivalently, in this setting, if one set is the complement of the other up to inclusion: each k-set is joined to the (n − k)-sets containing its complement).

Definition

Formally, for integers n and k with n ≥ 2k, the bipartite Kneser graph H(n, k) has as its bipartition the family of k-subsets and the family of (n − k)-subsets of an n-element set. A k-set A and an (n − k)-set B are adjacent when A ⊆ B or, equivalently, when they are disjoint after taking complements. The ordinary Kneser graph KG(n, k) can be recovered from the bipartite Kneser graph: the bipartite graph is in effect the bipartite double cover of the Kneser graph.

Examples

The bipartite Kneser graph H(5, 2) is the utility-like incidence graph associated with the Petersen graph; the Petersen graph is its quotient under complement symmetry. H(n, 1) is a crown-type graph on two copies of an n-set, joining each element to all (n − 1)-sets that contain it.

Properties

Bipartite Kneser graphs are regular: each k-set is contained in exactly one (n − k)-supersets structure counted by binomial coefficients, giving degree equal to the binomial coefficient C(n − k, 0) scaled appropriately — in fact each vertex on the k-side has degree 1 in the containment description when the (n − k)-set must be exactly the complement, while in the disjointness description the degrees are given by binomial coefficients. These graphs are studied for their symmetry, connectivity and colouring properties, and they relate to conjectures on the chromatic number of Kneser graphs established by Lovász using topological methods.
