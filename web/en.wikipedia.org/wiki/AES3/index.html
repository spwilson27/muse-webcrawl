AES3

AES3 is a standard for the exchange of digital audio signals between professional audio devices. It is often called AES/EBU because it was jointly developed by the Audio Engineering Society and the European Broadcasting Union. An AES3 signal carries two channels of pulse-code modulated (PCM) audio over a single balanced line using XLR connectors, with no separate word clock required because the signal is self-clocking.

Technical details

The interface uses biphase mark code to encode the data and clock together. Audio is transmitted in frames of two subframes (one per channel), each containing up to 24 bits of audio plus validity, user, channel status and parity bits. Frames are grouped into blocks of 192 frames, with the channel status bytes conveying metadata such as sampling frequency, emphasis, and whether the content is professional or consumer material.

The standard physical layer is a balanced 110-ohm twisted pair terminated in XLR-3 connectors at 2–7 volts peak-to-peak. A consumer variant, S/PDIF, uses unbalanced 75-ohm coaxial cable or optical TOSLINK and a slightly different channel status format; the audio data formats are otherwise nearly identical.

Variants

Related standards include AES3id (unbalanced BNC, 75 Ω), MADI (AES10), which multiplexes many AES3 channels, and AES67 and Dante, which carry AES3-compatible audio over IP networks. Sampling rates commonly supported include 44.1 kHz, 48 kHz, 88.2 kHz, 96 kHz and 192 kHz.
