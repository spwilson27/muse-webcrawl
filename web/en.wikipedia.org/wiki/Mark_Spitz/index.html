Mark Andrew Spitz (born 10 February 1950) is an American former competitive swimmer and nine-time Olympic champion. He was the most successful athlete at the 1972 Summer Olympics in Munich, winning seven gold medals, all in world-record time — an achievement that stood as the single-Games record until Michael Phelps won eight golds at Beijing in 2008.

Career

Spitz grew up in Sacramento and Honolulu and was a teenage prodigy, holding numerous age-group records. At the 1968 Mexico City Olympics, aged eighteen, he won two team golds, an individual silver and a bronze, falling short of his own prediction of six golds. He then attended Indiana University, training under coach Doc Counsilman and winning eight NCAA titles.

Munich 1972

At Munich, Spitz won the 100 m and 200 m freestyle, the 100 m and 200 m butterfly, and all three relays, setting a world record in each of his seven events. After the Munich massacre of Israeli athletes, Spitz, who is Jewish, was evacuated from Germany for his safety and retired from competition shortly afterwards, aged 22. He briefly attempted a comeback for the 1992 Barcelona Olympics but failed to qualify.

Legacy

His famous moustache — unusual among swimmers — became an iconic image of the Games. Spitz was inducted into the International Swimming Hall of Fame and remains one of the most decorated Olympic swimmers in history.
