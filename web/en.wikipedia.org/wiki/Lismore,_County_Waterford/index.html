Lismore (Irish: Lios Mór, meaning "great ringfort") is a historic town in County Waterford, Ireland, situated on the River Blackwater at the foot of the Knockmealdown Mountains. It was founded by Saint Mochuda (Saint Carthage) in the seventh century, when he established an abbey that became one of Ireland's most celebrated early monastic schools.

Lismore Castle overlooking the River Blackwater.

History

The monastery of Lismore, founded around 634 AD, attracted students from across Europe. The town later became a Norman stronghold; a castle was begun in the twelfth century and served as the seat of the bishops of Lismore. The present Lismore Castle was largely rebuilt in the nineteenth century by the 6th Duke of Devonshire and remains the Irish home of the Dukes of Devonshire. The chemist and physicist Robert Boyle was born at the castle in 1627. St Carthage's Cathedral, with its stained glass by Edward Burne-Jones, stands on the site of the ancient monastery.

Today

Lismore is a heritage town noted for its salmon fishing on the Blackwater, its annual arts and music events, and attractions including the castle gardens, the heritage centre, and the Lady Louisa's Walk along the river. The Book of Lismore, a fifteenth-century manuscript compiled at the castle, is now held by the Royal Irish Academy.
