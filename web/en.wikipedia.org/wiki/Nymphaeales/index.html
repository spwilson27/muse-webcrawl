Nymphaeales

The Nymphaeales are an order of flowering plants consisting of aquatic herbs, and include the water lilies. Together with the orders Amborellales and Austrobaileyales, they form the ANA grade of basal angiosperms — the earliest-diverging lineages of living flowering plants. The order contains three families: Hydatellaceae, Cabombaceae, and Nymphaeaceae.

Characteristics

Members of the Nymphaeales are aquatic or semi-aquatic herbs rooted in the mud of ponds, lakes, and slow rivers, with floating or submerged leaves. Many lack true xylem vessels in their stems, a feature once taken as primitive. Their flowers are often large and showy, as in the water lilies (Nymphaea) and lotus-like Nelumbo relatives historically included in the group, though the sacred lotus is now placed in the Proteales. The tiny, grass-like Hydatellaceae were only recognised as relatives of water lilies in 2007, a finding that reshaped ideas about early flower evolution.

Evolution

Fossil flowers attributed to the Nymphaeales date to the Early Cretaceous, around 125 million years ago, and molecular clock studies suggest the lineage may be considerably older. Because of their position near the base of the angiosperm tree, the order is of great interest in reconstructing the features of the earliest flowers.
