Telomere

A telomere is a region of repetitive nucleotide sequences associated with specialized proteins at the ends of linear chromosomes (see DNA). Telomeres are a widespread genetic feature most commonly found in eukaryotes. In most, if not all species possessing them, they protect the terminal regions of chromosomal DNA from progressive degradation and ensure the integrity of linear chromosomes by preventing DNA repair systems from mistaking the very ends of the DNA strand for a broken strand.

During cell division, enzymes that duplicate DNA cannot continue their duplication all the way to the end of a chromosome, so in each duplication the end of the chromosome is shortened (the "end-replication problem"). Telomeres are disposable buffers at the ends of chromosomes which are truncated during cell division; their presence protects the genes in front of them from being truncated instead.

Shortening and ageing

Over time, due to each cell division, the telomere ends become shorter. They are replenished by an enzyme, telomerase reverse transcriptase, which is active in germ cells, some stem cells, and most cancer cells, but is absent or low in most somatic cells. When telomeres become critically short, the cell can enter senescence or undergo apoptosis—a phenomenon linked to the Hayflick limit described by Leonard Hayflick in 1961. The existence of telomeres was inferred from work by Hermann Joseph Muller and Barbara McClintock in the 1930s; the repeat sequence was identified by Elizabeth Blackburn and Joseph Gall in the ciliate Tetrahymena in 1978, and Blackburn, Carol Greider and Jack Szostak received the 2009 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for discovering how telomeres are protected and how telomerase works.

Telomeres and disease

Short telomeres are associated with age-related diseases and premature ageing syndromes such as dyskeratosis congenita, caused by mutations in telomere-maintenance genes. Conversely, most human cancers reactivate telomerase, enabling unlimited cell division, which has made telomerase a target of research into cancer therapies.
