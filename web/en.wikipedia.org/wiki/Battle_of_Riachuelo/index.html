The Naval Battle of Riachuelo was fought on 11 June 1865 on the Paraná River between the Imperial Brazilian Navy and the Paraguayan Navy, during the Paraguayan War (War of the Triple Alliance). The Paraguayan squadron descended the river to surprise and capture the Brazilian fleet at anchor near the Riachuelo stream; after a confused and savage close action — decided when Admiral Barroso's flagship Amazonas rammed and sank three Paraguayan vessels — Brazil won its greatest naval victory. The battle secured the Allied blockade of Paraguay and doomed its offensive into Argentina.

Background

In 1865 President Francisco Solano López of Paraguay invaded the Argentine province of Corrientes, seeking to carry the war to Brazil's allies. Paraguayan strategy depended on the rivers: if the Brazilian squadron blockading the Paraná could be destroyed or captured by boarding, Paraguay could supply its armies and contest control of the waterways. Commodore Pedro Ignacio Meza planned a dawn surprise, his steamers towing flat armed barges (chatas) with troops to board the anchored Brazilians.

The battle

Mechanical trouble delayed the Paraguayans, and the attack came in daylight on 11 June. The Brazilian squadron of nine steamers under Admiral Barroso weighed anchor and fought back; the leading Brazilian ship Jequitinhonha grounded under the Paraguayan shore batteries, and for hours the action was a mêlée of gunnery and attempted boarding. Barroso then hoisted the famous signal "Brazil expects that every man will do his duty" and "Sustain the fire, that victory is ours," and drove his flagship Amazonas at full speed into the Paraguayan line, ramming and sinking three vessels in succession. The Paraguayan squadron broke and fled upriver, leaving its barges and shore guns behind; Meza was mortally wounded.

Aftermath

Paraguay never again mounted a serious river offensive; the Allies retained command of the Paraná and Paraguay rivers, enabling the blockade and the slow invasion that destroyed Paraguay by 1870. Riachuelo is celebrated as the Brazilian Navy's defining battle, and 11 June is commemorated as Brazilian Navy Day.
