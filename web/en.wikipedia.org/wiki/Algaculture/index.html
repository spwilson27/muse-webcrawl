Algaculture

Algaculture is a form of aquaculture involving the farming of species of algae. Cultivated species include macroalgae such as seaweeds and microalgae such as phytoplankton, grown for food, feed, chemicals, cosmetics and biofuel.

ProductsNori, kelp, spirulina, agar, alginate, carrageenan
Major producersChina, Indonesia, Philippines, South Korea, Japan
SystemsSea farms, raceway ponds, photobioreactors

Macroalgae

Seaweed farming is centuries old in East Asia: nori (Porphyra/Pyropia), kombu kelp and wakame are grown on nets and ropes in coastal waters. Tropical farming of Kappaphycus and Eucheuma supplies carrageenan, and Gracilaria supplies agar.

Microalgae

Microalgae such as Spirulina, Chlorella, Dunaliella (for beta-carotene) and Haematococcus (for astaxanthin) are grown in open raceway ponds or closed photobioreactors. They serve health-food, aquaculture-feed and pigment markets.

Uses and prospects

Algae yield hydrocolloids (alginate, agar, carrageenan), fertilisers, and feedstocks researched for algal biofuel and carbon capture, since algae grow fast and need no arable land — though harvesting and dewatering costs remain a barrier to commodity-scale fuel.

Environmental issues

Farms can absorb nutrients and CO2, but introduced species, disease and coastal-use conflicts require management.
