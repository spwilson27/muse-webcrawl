King David

David (Hebrew: דָּוִד, romanised: Dāwīḏ) was, according to the Hebrew Bible, the second king of the United Kingdom of Israel and Judah, reigning c. 1010–970 BCE. He is depicted as a righteous king, a warrior, a musician and a poet, and is traditionally credited with composing many of the Psalms.

Biblical account

In the Books of Samuel, David is introduced as the youngest son of Jesse of Bethlehem, anointed by the prophet Samuel while Saul still reigned. He gains fame by defeating the Philistine champion Goliath with a sling, becomes a commander and then, fleeing Saul's jealousy, an outlaw leader. After Saul's death David is proclaimed king at Hebron and later conquers Jerusalem, making it his capital. His reign is marred by his adultery with Bathsheba, the arranged death of her husband Uriah, and the rebellion of his son Absalom. He is succeeded by Solomon, his son by Bathsheba.

Historicity

The Tel Dan stele, a 9th-century BCE Aramaic inscription discovered in 1993, refers to the "House of David" (bytdwd), the earliest extra-biblical evidence widely accepted as referring to a Davidic dynasty. Scholars debate the scale of David's kingdom: some view him as a historical local ruler whose deeds were later magnified, while others accept the broad outline of a 10th-century Judahite kingdom.

Religious significance

David is venerated in Judaism as the model king and ancestor of the Messiah; in Christianity, Jesus is described as a descendant of David; in Islam, Dawud is honoured as a prophet and king and the recipient of the Zabur (Psalms).
