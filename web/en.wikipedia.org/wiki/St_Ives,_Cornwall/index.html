St Ives, Cornwall

St Ives (Cornish: Porth Ia, meaning "St Ia's cove") is a seaside town, civil parish and port in Cornwall, England, on the Celtic Sea coast west of Camborne and north of Penzance. Traditionally a fishing port — once one of the busiest pilchard fisheries in Cornwall — it is now best known as a holiday resort and as a long-standing artists' colony.

History

The town takes its name from Saint Ia, a 5th- or 6th-century Irish missionary. The medieval parish church of St Ia stands above the harbour, whose pier, Smeaton's Pier, was built in the 1760s–70s. Pilchard fishing and exporting, along with mining in the surrounding district, drove the town's growth in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. The arrival of the railway branch line from St Erth in 1877 — one of the most scenic in Britain — opened St Ives to tourism.

Art colony

From the late nineteenth century, artists were drawn by the quality of the light. The St Ives School came to include figures such as Bernard Leach, Barbara Hepworth, Ben Nicholson, Naum Gabo and Patrick Heron. Tate St Ives, opened in 1993 overlooking Porthmeor Beach, and the Barbara Hepworth Museum and Sculpture Garden anchor the town's artistic identity.

Today

Tourism dominates, with beaches including Porthmeor (a surfing beach), Porthgwidden and the harbour beach. Popularity has brought debate about second homes and housing costs for local residents, and the town held a referendum in 2016 supporting restrictions on new-build homes being sold as second homes.
