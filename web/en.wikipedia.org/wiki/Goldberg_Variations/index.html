Goldberg Variations

ComposerJohann Sebastian Bach
CatalogueBWV 988
Published1741
StructureAria and 30 variations

The Goldberg Variations, BWV 988, are a keyboard work by Johann Sebastian Bach consisting of an aria and a set of 30 variations. Published in 1741 as the fourth part of Bach's Clavier-Übung, the work is considered one of the most important examples of the variation form and a pinnacle of Baroque keyboard music.

Title and legend

The variations are named after Johann Gottlieb Goldberg, a young harpsichordist in the service of Count Hermann Karl von Keyserlingk, the Russian ambassador to Saxony. According to Bach's early biographer Johann Nikolaus Forkel, the Count commissioned the work so that Goldberg could play it during his sleepless nights — an anecdote now regarded with scepticism by scholars, though the dedication of the published edition to the Count is genuine.

Structure

Unlike most variation sets, the variations are built not on the aria's melody but on its bass line and harmonic progression. Every third variation is a canon, progressing systematically from canon at the unison to canon at the ninth. Variation 16 is a French overture, variation 25 — a deeply expressive adagio in G minor — was called by Wanda Landowska "the black pearl," and variation 30 is a quodlibet weaving together popular tunes, after which the aria returns da capo.

Performance and recordings

Written for a two-manual harpsichord, the work is now performed on both harpsichord and piano. Glenn Gould's recordings of 1955 and 1981 are among the most celebrated interpretations and helped establish the work's modern popularity.
