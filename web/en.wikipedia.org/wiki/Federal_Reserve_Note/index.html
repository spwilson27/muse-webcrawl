Federal Reserve Note

Federal Reserve Notes are the paper currency of the United States. Issued by the twelve Federal Reserve Banks under authority of the Federal Reserve Act of 1913, they are the only form of US banknote currently produced, circulating in denominations of $1, $2, $5, $10, $20, $50 and $100.

Characteristics

Each note is an obligation of the United States, is legal tender for all debts public and private, and bears the seal and letter of the issuing Federal Reserve Bank, which also appears in its serial number prefix. Notes are printed by the Bureau of Engraving and Printing on a cotton–linen paper and are backed by assets held by the Federal Reserve, principally US Treasury securities. Since 1963 the notes have carried the motto "In God We Trust" and, following the end of gold and silver convertibility, are a pure fiat currency.

History

The first Federal Reserve Notes appeared in 1914, initially alongside gold certificates, silver certificates and United States Notes. Large-size notes gave way to the modern small size in 1929. High denominations of $500 to $10,000 were discontinued in 1969. From the 1990s the notes were progressively redesigned with anti-counterfeiting features including watermarks, security threads, colour-shifting ink and, in the most recent $100 note, a blue 3-D security ribbon.
