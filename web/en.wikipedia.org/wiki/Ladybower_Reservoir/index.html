Ladybower Reservoir is a large Y-shaped reservoir in the Upper Derwent Valley in the Peak District of Derbyshire, England. The River Ashop flows into it from the west, and the River Derwent flows south, initially through Howden Reservoir and then Derwent Reservoir. Ladybower is the lowest of the three reservoirs in the valley and was built between 1935 and 1945 to supply water to the cities of the East Midlands and South Yorkshire.

Construction

Building Ladybower was a major undertaking involving a purpose-built village, Birchinlee — nicknamed "Tin Town" — to house the workers. Unlike the stone-faced dams of Howden and Derwent, Ladybower's dam is a clay-cored earth embankment. Construction began in 1935 but was slowed by the Second World War; the reservoir was completed in 1945 and officially opened by King George VI on 25 September 1945.

The creation of the reservoir required the flooding of the villages of Derwent and Ashopton, whose inhabitants were rehoused. Most of the buildings were demolished before flooding, but the tower of Derwent church was left standing and remained visible above the waterline until it was demolished for safety reasons in 1947. During droughts, when the water level falls, the remains of the villages reappear and draw crowds of visitors.

The Dambusters connection

During the Second World War, the similar appearance of the Derwent Valley reservoirs to the Ruhr dams made them ideal practice targets for the RAF's 617 Squadron — the "Dambusters" — preparing for Operation Chastise, the 1943 raid on the German dams. A memorial to the squadron stands at the Derwent Dam.

The plug holes

Ladybower is famous for its two spectacular overflow "plug holes" — bell-mouth spillways some 24 m (80 ft) in diameter that drain excess water through tunnels under the dam when the reservoir is full. They are among the largest examples of their kind and are a well-known sight in wet winters.

Recreation

The reservoir is used for fishing and is surrounded by popular walking and cycling routes in the Peak District National Park. The Ladybower Inn and the nearby Fairholmes visitor centre serve the many visitors to the valley.
