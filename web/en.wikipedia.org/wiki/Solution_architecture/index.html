Solution architecture

Solution architecture is the practice of designing and describing a specific information system solution that addresses particular business problems and requirements, typically within the constraints set by an organisation's enterprise architecture. A solution architecture describes the structure, components, interfaces, and technologies of a specific system or set of changes, and provides a blueprint for its development and delivery.

Scope

Whereas enterprise architecture concerns the organisation-wide landscape and long-term direction, solution architecture focuses on a single project or programme: it selects applications and technologies, defines how they integrate, and ensures that non-functional requirements such as security, scalability, performance, and resilience are met. The solution architect typically produces architecture descriptions, options analyses, and design decisions that guide engineers during implementation.

Role of the solution architect

The solution architect bridges business stakeholders and technical teams, translating requirements into a workable design, evaluating build-versus-buy options, and governing delivery against the design. The role overlaps with those of software architect, systems architect, and technical architect, and in frameworks such as TOGAF the discipline is situated within a broader architecture practice that includes business, data, application, and technology domains.

Deliverables

Common artifacts include the solution architecture document or high-level design, architecture decision records, interface specifications, data models, and deployment diagrams. In agile and cloud environments, these may be maintained as living documents rather than produced once up front.
