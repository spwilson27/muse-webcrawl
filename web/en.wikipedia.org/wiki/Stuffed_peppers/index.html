Stuffed peppers

Stuffed peppers is a dish common in many cuisines. It consists of hollowed or halved peppers (typically bell peppers or chili peppers) filled with any of a variety of fillings, often including meat, vegetables, cheese, rice, or sauce. The dish is usually assembled by filling the cavities of the peppers and then cooking them.

Regional varieties

  • Mediterranean and Balkan: In Greece (gemista), Turkey (biber dolması), the Balkans (punjena paprika, filovana paprika), and Romania (ardei umpluți), peppers are stuffed with minced meat and rice, often cooked in a tomato-based sauce.
  • Spain: Pimientos rellenos may be filled with manchego cheese, cod, or morcilla (blood sausage).
  • United States: Green bell peppers are commonly stuffed with ground beef, rice, and tomato sauce, topped with cheese and baked.
  • Mexico: Chiles rellenos are poblano peppers stuffed with cheese or meat, battered and fried, while chiles en nogada are served in walnut sauce with pomegranate seeds.
  • India: Bharva mirch are peppers stuffed with spiced ground meat or potatoes.
  • Middle East: Peppers are included among the dolma family of stuffed vegetables, filled with rice, herbs, and sometimes meat.

Preparation

The tops of the peppers are cut off and the seeds and membranes removed. Fillings are usually pre-cooked or mixed raw and cooked together with the pepper by baking, simmering in sauce, or grilling. Because peppers collapse as they cook, some recipes parboil or char the peppers before filling.
