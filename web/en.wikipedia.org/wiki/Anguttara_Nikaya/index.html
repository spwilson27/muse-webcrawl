Anguttara Nikaya

The Anguttara Nikāya (Pali: Aṅguttara Nikāya, "Collection of Discourses Increasing by One [Factor]") is a Buddhist scripture, the fourth of the five Nikāyas, or collections, in the Sutta Pitaka, which is one of the three "baskets" comprising the Pali Canon, the scriptures of Theravada Buddhism. It is translated by F. L. Woodward and E. M. Hare as The Book of the Gradual Sayings, and more recently by Bhikkhu Bodhi as The Numerical Discourses of the Buddha.

Contents

The collection contains several thousand discourses (suttas) attributed to the Buddha and his chief disciples, arranged in eleven books (nipātas) according to the number of items they treat: the Book of Ones deals with single items, the Book of Twos with pairs, and so on up to the Book of Elevens. This numerical arrangement made the material convenient for preaching and memorisation.

Parallels

A corresponding collection, the Ekottara Āgama, survives in Chinese translation in the canon of Chinese Buddhism, though its arrangement and contents differ considerably from the Pali version, reflecting the different early Buddhist schools that preserved them.

Significance

The Anguttara Nikāya is a principal source for early Buddhist doctrine on ethics, meditation, and the stages of the path, and its systematic lists underlie much of the later scholastic organisation of the teaching. Well-known discourses include the Kalama Sutta, in the Book of Threes, often cited for its counsel on testing teachings in experience.
