The Basilica Ulpia was an ancient Roman civic basilica in Trajan's Forum in Rome, and the largest basilica ever built in the city. It was inaugurated in AD 112 as part of Trajan's monumental forum complex, constructed with spoils from the Dacian Wars, and took its name from the emperor's nomen, Ulpius.

Architecture

The basilica formed the northern side of the forum's great piazza, opposite the equestrian statue of Trajan. Its central nave measured roughly 117 by 58 metres in total, flanked by double aisles on the long sides and terminating in two large semicircular apses; sources also describe two-aisled exedrae. Rows of columns — grey granite in the central nave, with coloured marbles including cipollino and pavonazzetto elsewhere — supported a timber roof rather than vaults. Behind it stood the Column of Trajan, flanked by the Greek and Latin libraries, and beyond them Trajan's temple.

Function

Like other Roman civic basilicas, the Basilica Ulpia served law courts, commerce and official business, including the formal manumission of slaves. In late antiquity and the Middle Ages the building was progressively quarried for stone, and its site was built over; surviving granite and marble column shafts have been re-erected in modern times.

Excavation

The site was excavated under Napoleonic occupation in the early nineteenth century and again in the 1930s for the creation of the Via dei Fori Imperiali; substantial portions remain buried beneath modern roads. New excavations in the 2020s have exposed further sections, and anastylosis work has raised several columns again on the ancient pavement.
