Simon Smith Kuznets (30 April 1901 – 8 July 1985) was a Russian-born American economist and statistician. He is best known for developing the modern system of national income and product accounts — the basis of gross domestic product (GDP) measurement — and for his empirical work on economic growth and income inequality. He received the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 1971.

Career

Kuznets emigrated to the United States in 1922 and earned his PhD from Columbia University in 1926, working with Wesley Clair Mitchell at the National Bureau of Economic Research. His 1934 report to the US Senate, National Income, 1929–32, laid the foundation for the national accounts. He taught at the University of Pennsylvania, Johns Hopkins, and Harvard.

Contributions

Kuznets identified long "swings" of roughly 15–25 years in economic activity (Kuznets cycles) and proposed the inverted-U hypothesis of inequality — the Kuznets curve — suggesting that inequality first rises and then falls as economies develop. He also cautioned that national income measures should not be equated with economic welfare, a warning frequently quoted in debates about GDP.
