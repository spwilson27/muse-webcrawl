Quadratic formula

The quadratic formula gives the solutions of a quadratic equation of the form ax2 + bx + c = 0, where a ≠ 0. The solutions are:

x = (−b ± √(b2 − 4ac)) / 2a

The discriminant

The expression under the square root, b2 − 4ac, is called the discriminant. If it is positive, the equation has two distinct real roots; if it is zero, the equation has one repeated real root; if it is negative, the equation has two complex conjugate roots. The formula is derived by completing the square on the general quadratic equation.

History

Methods for solving quadratic equations were known to Babylonian mathematicians nearly 4,000 years ago. Geometrical approaches appear in Euclid, and the general algebraic solution developed through the work of Indian and Persian mathematicians such as Brahmagupta and al-Khwarizmi, taking its modern symbolic form after the introduction of algebraic notation in the 16th and 17th centuries.
