Melanocortin 2 receptor

MC2R (melanocortin 2 receptor)

Also known as: ACTH receptor

Gene: MC2R, chromosome 18p11.21 (human)

Family: G protein-coupled receptors

The melanocortin 2 receptor (MC2R), also known as the ACTH receptor, is a G protein-coupled receptor expressed primarily in the adrenal cortex. Unlike the other melanocortin receptors, MC2R is activated exclusively by adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) and not by the melanocyte-stimulating hormones.

Function

Binding of ACTH — released from the pituitary as part of the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis — stimulates the adrenal cortex to synthesise and secrete glucocorticoids (cortisol in humans, corticosterone in rodents) and adrenal androgens. MC2R requires the accessory protein MRAP (melanocortin-2 receptor accessory protein) for trafficking to the cell surface and for signalling.

Clinical significance

Inactivating mutations in MC2R or MRAP cause familial glucocorticoid deficiency (FGD), an autosomal recessive disorder characterised by cortisol deficiency with preserved mineralocorticoid function, recurrent hypoglycaemia, and hyperpigmentation from chronically elevated ACTH. Excess ACTH drive through MC2R in pituitary adenomas (Cushing's disease) produces glucocorticoid excess instead.
