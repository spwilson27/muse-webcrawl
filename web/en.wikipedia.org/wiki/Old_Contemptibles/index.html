Old Contemptibles

The Old Contemptibles were the members of the British Expeditionary Force (BEF) who served in France and Flanders during the early months of the First World War, in 1914. The name derives from a remark attributed to Kaiser Wilhelm II of Germany, who is said to have referred to the British force as a "contemptible little army" in an order of the day issued on 19 August 1914. British soldiers adopted the phrase with pride, calling themselves the Old Contemptibles.

Origin of the name

The Kaiser's alleged order exhorted his troops to "exterminate first the treacherous English and walk over General French's contemptible little army." Although the authenticity of the document was questioned in Germany after the war, the phrase was widely publicised in Britain and became a badge of honour for the regular soldiers of the original BEF — professional long-service troops, many of them reservists recalled to the colours.

The 1914 campaign

The Old Contemptibles fought at Mons, Le Cateau, the Marne, the Aisne and the First Battle of Ypres in 1914. By the end of that year the original force had suffered very heavy casualties, and the pre-war regular army was largely destroyed, to be replaced by the volunteer New Armies and later conscripts.

Association

Veterans formed the Old Contemptibles' Association in 1925, with branches (called "chums") across Britain and the Commonwealth. Membership was restricted to those who had served in the BEF in 1914 and were holders of the 1914 Star, sometimes called the "Mons Star". The association was disbanded in the 1970s as its membership dwindled; the last surviving Old Contemptible died in the 2000s.
