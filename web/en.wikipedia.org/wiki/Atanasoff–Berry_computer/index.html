Atanasoff–Berry computer

DeveloperJohn Vincent Atanasoff, Clifford Berry
Built1939–1942, Iowa State College

The Atanasoff–Berry computer (ABC) was the first automatic electronic digital computer, built at Iowa State College between 1939 and 1942 by physicist John Vincent Atanasoff and graduate student Clifford Berry. It was designed to solve systems of linear equations using binary arithmetic and vacuum tubes.

Design

The ABC used about 300 vacuum tubes, binary mathematics, regenerative capacitor memory on rotating drums, and punched-card input/output. It was not programmable or Turing-complete, but pioneered elements later used in electronic computing.

Legal significance

In the 1973 US court case Honeywell v. Sperry Rand, the ABC was cited in invalidating the ENIAC patent, the judge finding that ENIAC inventor John Mauchly had derived ideas from Atanasoff. A working replica was completed at Iowa State in 1997.
