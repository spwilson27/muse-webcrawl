Blood, Sweat & Tears

Origin: New York City, United States
Genres: Jazz rock, psychedelic rock
Years active: 1967–present
Founders: Al Kooper, Bobby Colomby

Blood, Sweat & Tears (also styled BS&T) is an American jazz-rock group founded in New York City in 1967 by Al Kooper. The band pioneered the combination of a rock rhythm section with a horn section in the big-band tradition.

History

After Kooper's departure, singer David Clayton-Thomas fronted the group's second album, Blood, Sweat & Tears (1968), which won the Grammy Award for Album of the Year and yielded the hits "Spinning Wheel", "And When I Die" and "You've Made Me So Very Happy".

Later years

The band's commercial fortunes declined in the 1970s amid frequent personnel changes, but a touring version of the group, led for decades by drummer Bobby Colomby's organisation, has continued to perform.
