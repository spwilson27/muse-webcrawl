Deep River (North Carolina)

The Deep River is a tributary of the Cape Fear River in central North Carolina, United States, approximately 125 miles (201 km) long. It rises in the Piedmont near Kernersville in Forsyth County and flows generally southeast through Guilford, Randolph, Moore, Chatham, and Lee counties before joining the Haw River near Moncure to form the Cape Fear River.

Course

The Deep River begins in western Forsyth County and passes through or near the towns of High Point, Jamestown, Randleman, Ramseur, and Sanford. Its valley is relatively broad and level in its upper reaches, but below Ramseur the river cuts through resistant rocks of the Carolina slate belt, forming gorges that were historically exploited for water power.

History

The river's fall-line gorges powered numerous textile mills, gristmills, and iron works from the eighteenth century onward. Coal was mined in the Deep River valley around Egypt (Cumnock) and Coal Glen in Chatham County during the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, in one of North Carolina's few significant coalfields. Communities such as Carbonton, a former resort town, grew along its banks.

Tributaries

Major tributaries include Muddy Creek, Polecat Creek, Richland Creek, Buffalo Creek, and Rocky River (which joins the Deep River in Chatham County).
