WWVH is the call sign of the United States National Institute of Standards and Technology's shortwave radio time signal station located at Kekaha on the island of Kauai in Hawaii. WWVH is the Pacific sister station to WWV in Fort Collins, Colorado, and broadcasts the same time signal format on the same frequencies, carefully coordinated so the two stations do not interfere with one another.

The station continuously broadcasts time announcements, standard time intervals, standard frequencies, geophysical alerts and marine storm warnings. Its voice announcements use a female voice, which distinguishes them from WWV's male voice, and are made a few seconds ahead of the corresponding WWV announcements.


The WWVH station site at Kekaha, Kauai

Transmission system

WWVH broadcasts on 2.5, 5, 10 and 15 MHz with transmitter powers of 5 kW on 2.5 MHz and 10 kW on the other frequencies. The signal includes seconds pulses, a 440 Hz tone marking the hour, other audio tones, digital time codes, and recorded voice announcements of the time of day in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). The site occupies a 30-acre (12 ha) plot near Kekaha, on the Pacific Missile Range Facility.

History

WWVH began broadcasting in November 1948 from Kihei on the island of Maui. In July 1971, because of encroaching development and interference, the station was moved to its present location on Kauai. Along with WWV and the longwave station WWVB, WWVH is operated by NIST's Time and Frequency Division and provides the official time of the United States to users across the Pacific region, including mariners, scientists, broadcasters and radio amateurs.
