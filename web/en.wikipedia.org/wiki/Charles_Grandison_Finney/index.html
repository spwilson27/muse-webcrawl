Charles Grandison Finney (29 August 1792 – 16 August 1875) was an American Presbyterian minister and leader of the Second Great Awakening in the United States. He has been called the "Father of Modern Revivalism" for his innovative revival methods.

Revival ministry

Finney trained as a lawyer before a dramatic conversion in 1821 led him to preaching. His revivals in upstate New York's "Burned-over District" and in Rochester (1830–31) used "new measures" — protracted meetings, the anxious bench, and allowing women to pray aloud — that sparked controversy among Old School Presbyterians and Congregationalists such as Asahel Nettleton and Lyman Beecher. His Lectures on Revivals of Religion (1835) codified his methods.

Oberlin

From 1835 Finney taught theology at Oberlin College in Ohio, becoming its second president (1851–66). Oberlin became a centre of abolitionism, perfectionist theology, and coeducation. Finney's theology emphasised human free will and moral government, departing from strict Calvinism. He died in Oberlin in 1875.
