Meatloaf is a dish of ground meat that has been combined with other ingredients, formed into the shape of a loaf, and then baked or smoked. The final shape is formed by cooking it in a loaf pan or by hand on a baking pan. It is usually topped with a sauce or glaze before or during cooking.

History

Dishes of minced meat shaped into loaves date back centuries. A recipe resembling meatloaf appears in the Roman cookbook Apicius. The German dish Hackbraten uses ground beef or pork mixed with breadcrumbs and onions. In the United States, meatloaf became especially popular during the Great Depression, when cooks stretched scarce meat with inexpensive fillers such as breadcrumbs, oatmeal, and cracker crumbs. During the Second World War it was promoted as an economical way to use rationed meat. By the 1950s it had become a staple of American home cooking.

Ingredients and preparation

A typical meatloaf combines ground beef (sometimes mixed with pork or veal) with a binder such as breadcrumbs soaked in milk, beaten egg, diced onion, garlic, salt, pepper, and herbs. The mixture is pressed into a loaf pan or shaped free-form, often topped with a glaze of ketchup, tomato sauce, or barbecue sauce, sometimes sweetened with brown sugar. It is baked until the internal temperature reaches a safe level, then rested and sliced.

Variations

Regional and family variations include Italian-style meatloaf with Parmesan and herbs, meatloaf stuffed with cheese or hard-boiled eggs, bacon-wrapped loaves, and vegetarian versions made from lentils, beans, or plant-based meat substitutes. Leftover meatloaf is commonly served cold in sandwiches.
