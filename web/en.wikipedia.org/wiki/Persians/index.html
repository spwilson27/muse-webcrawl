Persians

Persians
Total population: c. 55–60 million (incl. related Persian-speaking groups)
Regions: Iran, Afghanistan, Tajikistan, diaspora worldwide
Languages: Persian (Farsi, Dari, Tajik)
Religion: Predominantly Shia Islam

The Persians are an Iranian ethnic group who comprise over half of the population of Iran. They share a common cultural system and are native speakers of Persian (Farsi) and closely related languages. Persian-speaking peoples also form major populations in Afghanistan (Dari speakers) and Tajikistan (Tajik speakers), with large diaspora communities in North America, Europe, and elsewhere.

History

The Persians descend from ancient Iranian peoples who entered the Iranian plateau in the early first millennium BCE. From their homeland in Persis (Fars), they founded the Achaemenid Empire under Cyrus the Great in 550 BCE — the largest empire of the ancient world to that date — followed in later centuries by the Parthian and Sasanian empires. After the Arab conquest of the seventh century CE, Persians gradually adopted Islam while preserving and reviving their language, which re-emerged in New Persian written in Arabic script by the ninth and tenth centuries.

Culture

Persian identity centres on the Persian language and a literary heritage including Ferdowsi, Rumi, Saadi, and Hafez. Cultural practices include the celebration of Nowruz, Persian cuisine, miniature art, carpet weaving, and classical music. Most Persians in Iran adhere to Twelver Shia Islam, adopted as the state religion under the Safavid dynasty from 1501.

Diaspora

Substantial Persian diaspora communities formed after the 1979 Iranian Revolution, notably in Los Angeles (sometimes called "Tehrangeles"), Toronto, London, and Stockholm.
