Edward VIII (Edward Albert Christian George Andrew Patrick David; 23 June 1894 – 28 May 1972), later known as the Duke of Windsor, was King of the United Kingdom and Emperor of India from 20 January 1936 until his abdication that December — the only British monarch to voluntarily abdicate.

Early life and Prince of Wales

The eldest son of George V, Edward served in the First World War and undertook popular Empire tours as Prince of Wales. His glamorous, modern image contrasted with his father's conservatism.

Abdication crisis

Edward wished to marry Wallis Simpson, an American socialite who had divorced one husband and was divorcing a second. As head of the Church of England, the King could not marry a divorced woman with living former spouses; the government of Stanley Baldwin opposed the match. Edward signed the Instrument of Abdication on 10 December 1936 and broadcast his decision — that he could not discharge his duties "without the help and support of the woman I love." He was succeeded by his brother, George VI.

Later life

Created Duke of Windsor, he married Simpson in France in 1937. Controversy followed over his 1937 tour of Nazi Germany and alleged sympathies. He served as Governor of the Bahamas (1940–45), then lived mainly in Paris, writing memoirs. He died in 1972 and is buried at Frogmore.
