Gull

Gulls, or colloquially seagulls, are seabirds of the family Laridae in the suborder Lari. They are most closely related to terns and skimmers, and distantly related to auks and waders. Around fifty species are recognised, spread across the genera Larus, Chroicocephalus, Ichthyaetus, Hydrocoloeus, Rissa, Pagophila, Xema and Creagrus.

Gulls are typically medium to large birds, usually grey or white, often with black markings on the head or wings. They have stout, longish bills, webbed feet and a characteristic raucous call. Most are ground-nesting carnivores or opportunistic scavengers that take live food such as crabs and fish, or scavenge human refuse; many species have learned to coexist near humans and thrive in coastal towns.

Species range in size from the little gull, at about 120 grams, to the great black-backed gull, at up to 1.75 kilograms. Gulls breed on every continent, including the margins of Antarctica, and a few inhabit the high Arctic.
