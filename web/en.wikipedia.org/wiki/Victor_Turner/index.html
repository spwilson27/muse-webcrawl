Victor Turner

Victor Witter Turner (28 May 1920 – 18 December 1983) was a British cultural anthropologist best known for his work on symbols, rituals, and rites of passage. His work, along with that of Clifford Geertz and others, helped define the field of symbolic and interpretive anthropology. Turner is especially associated with the concepts of liminality and communitas.

Life and career

Turner studied English literature at University College London, served as a non-combatant in the Second World War, and then turned to anthropology, completing his PhD at the University of Manchester under Max Gluckman in the Manchester School. His doctoral fieldwork among the Ndembu people of Zambia (then Northern Rhodesia) produced his first book, Schism and Continuity in an African Society (1957), which introduced the "social drama" as a unit of analysis. He later taught at Cornell University, the University of Chicago — where he was central to the interdisciplinary Committee on the History of Culture — and the University of Virginia.

Major concepts

Liminality

Building on Arnold van Gennep's threefold structure of rites of passage (separation, transition, incorporation), Turner developed the middle, "liminal" phase: a threshold state in which initiands are "betwixt and between", stripped of status and social identity. In The Ritual Process: Structure and Anti-Structure (1969) he generalised liminality into a theory of the dialectic between social structure and its periodic suspension. He later distinguished the obligatory, collective liminal from the voluntary, playful liminoid phenomena of industrial leisure.

Communitas

In liminal situations Turner identified communitas: an intense, egalitarian sense of human solidarity and togetherness that contrasts with the hierarchies of ordinary social structure. He distinguished spontaneous, ideological, and normative forms of communitas.

Legacy

Turner's concepts have been widely adopted beyond anthropology — in performance studies (notably through his collaboration with Richard Schechner), religious studies, organisational studies, and tourism research. The Society for Humanistic Anthropology awards an annual Victor Turner Prize for ethnographic writing.
