Tim Spicer

Timothy Simon Spicer (born 1952) is a British former army officer and founder of the private military company Aegis Defence Services. He is a prominent — and controversial — figure in the modern private security industry.

Military career

Spicer was commissioned into the Scots Guards and served in the Falklands War and Northern Ireland. As commanding officer of the 1st Battalion Scots Guards in Belfast in 1992, two of his soldiers were convicted of murdering teenager Peter McBride; Spicer publicly defended them, attracting controversy.

Private military companies

After leaving the army, Spicer was involved with Sandline International, whose 1997 arms shipment to Sierra Leone — in breach, critics said, of an arms embargo — led to the "Arms to Africa" affair and a parliamentary inquiry in the UK. He later founded Aegis Defence Services in 2002, which won large US government contracts in Iraq, including coordinating private security firms there.

Views

Spicer published an autobiography, An Unorthodox Soldier (1999), arguing that private military companies can be a legitimate and regulated tool of foreign policy.
