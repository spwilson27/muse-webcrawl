The Black Book of the Admiralty (Latin: Liber Niger Admiralitatis) is a medieval compilation of English maritime law and the practice of the High Court of Admiralty. Named for the black leather binding of the manuscript, it is one of the most important sources for the early development of admiralty law in England.

Contents

The book is a miscellany assembled from the fourteenth century onward. It preserves the ordinances traditionally attributed to kings from Richard I onward, texts derived from the Rolls of Oléron (the influential sea code associated with the Atlantic wine trade), articles of inquiry for admiralty courts, oaths of court officers, and precedents concerning wreck, prize, salvage and the discipline of mariners. The earliest surviving manuscript dates from about 1450, though parts of its contents are considerably older.

Publication

The work was printed for the first time in the nineteenth century in the Rolls Series edition prepared by Sir Travers Twiss, Monumenta Juridica: The Black Book of the Admiralty (4 volumes, 1871–1876), which remains the standard scholarly edition and includes parallel material from other maritime compilations.
