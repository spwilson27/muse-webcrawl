Music of Kazakhstan

The music of Kazakhstan reflects the traditions of the Kazakh people, a nomadic culture of the Central Asian steppe. Its central instrument is the dombra, a long-necked two-stringed lute, and its best-known classical form is the instrumental küi.

Traditional music

Traditional Kazakh music is largely solo and improvisatory. The küi is a short instrumental piece, usually for dombra or kobyz (a bowed instrument with horsehair strings associated with shamanic practice), that depicts scenes, stories and emotions; famous küi composers include Kurmangazy Sagyrbayuly and Dina Nurpeisova. Singers (änshi) perform epic and lyrical songs, while the aitys is a competitive improvisational singing and oratory contest between akyns, or poet-bards.

Soviet era and classical music

Under Soviet rule, Kazakh folk music was harmonised and arranged for large orchestras of modified folk instruments, and conservatories were founded in Almaty and elsewhere. Composers such as Akhmet Zhubanov and Yevgeny Brusilovsky worked folk material into operas like Abai and Kyz Zhibek.

Contemporary music

Modern Kazakhstan has a varied popular scene including Kazakh-language pop (Q-pop), rock, and hip hop. The singer Dimash Kudaibergen, noted for his exceptionally wide vocal range, gained international fame after appearing on a Chinese singing competition in 2017. The ethno-folk group Turan and others blend traditional instruments with modern arrangements.
