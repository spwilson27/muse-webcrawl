Kha b-Nisan (Syriac: ܚܕ ܒܢܝܣܢ, "First of April"), also known as Resha d'Sheta ("Head of the Year"), is the Assyrian New Year festival, celebrated on 1 April, coinciding with the start of spring. The festival has roots in the ancient Mesopotamian Akitu festival and is today the largest gathering of Assyrians worldwide.

History

The New Year celebration descends from the ancient Akitu festival of Babylon and Nineveh, which marked the spring equinox season, the sowing of barley, and the renewal of kingship, and was dated by tradition to the legendary founding era of Mesopotamian civilisation—Assyrians reckon the year count from 4750 BC, so that 1 April 2024 marked the year 6774. With Christianisation the festival was retained in Christian Assyrian communities of northern Iraq, southeastern Turkey, northwestern Iran, and eastern Syria.

Celebrations

Traditional observances include village parades in traditional dress (joma), dancing, feasting, and the gathering of flowers and herbs, which are hung over doorways in a bundle called daqna d-Nisan ("the beard of April") to bring blessings for the year. In the diaspora, large public parades are held in cities such as Chicago and in Sydney's Fairfield district. The largest celebration takes place in Duhok in Iraqi Kurdistan, where tens of thousands of Assyrians from Iraq and the diaspora march in traditional clothing carrying Assyrian flags.

In modern times

The festival serves as a focal point of Assyrian ethnic identity, especially following the displacements of the twentieth century and the persecution of Christians in Iraq and Syria. Attendance has grown in recent decades, and the event has drawn international media attention as a symbol of the survival of one of the world's oldest continuous cultures.
