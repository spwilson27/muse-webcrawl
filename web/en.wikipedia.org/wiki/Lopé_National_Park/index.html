Lopé National Park is a national park in central Gabon, in the valley of the Ogooué River. It protects a distinctive mosaic of tropical rainforest and ancient remnant savannas that survived the dry periods of the last Ice Age, and it is one of the most important sites in Central Africa for primate research and conservation.

Wildlife

The park hosts significant populations of western lowland gorillas, central chimpanzees, mandrills (in troops that can number over a thousand animals), forest elephants, forest buffalo, red river hogs and several species of duiker. Long-term research at the park's Station d'Études des Gorilles et Chimpanzés, founded in 1983, has made Lopé a key site for the study of great ape behaviour and the ecology of the forest–savanna boundary.

Archaeology and recognition

The Lopé-Okanda landscape contains an unusually dense concentration of archaeological remains spanning some 400,000 years, including Stone Age and Iron Age sites and nearly 2,000 rock engravings (petroglyphs) documenting the migration of Bantu-speaking peoples. In recognition of this combination of natural and cultural values, UNESCO inscribed the "Ecosystem and Relict Cultural Landscape of Lopé-Okanda" on the World Heritage List in 2007. The park had first been protected as a wildlife reserve in 1946, making it Gabon's oldest protected area, before becoming one of thirteen national parks created in 2002.
