Daniel Gaston Ash (born 31 July 1957) is an English guitarist, songwriter and singer. He achieved prominence as the guitarist of the gothic rock band Bauhaus, and later of the bands Tones on Tail and Love and Rockets, the latter of which scored an international hit with "So Alive" in 1989.

Career

Ash formed Bauhaus in Northampton in 1978 with Peter Murphy, David J and Kevin Haskins. The band's debut single "Bela Lugosi's Dead" (1979) became a defining record of gothic rock, and its albums In the Flat Field (1980) and Mask (1981) established Ash's distinctive guitar style — textural, echo-laden and influenced by glam rock and dub.

After Bauhaus split in 1983, Ash formed Tones on Tail with Glenn Campling, releasing the club favourite "Go!". In 1985 he reunited with David J and Kevin Haskins as Love and Rockets; the band's self-titled 1989 album yielded the US number-three single "So Alive", written by Ash. His solo career began with Coming Down (1991), which included a hit version of "This Love". Ash has participated in Bauhaus reunions (2005–2008, 2019–2022) and later formed Poptone with Haskins and Diva Dompe, performing material from all three of his bands.

Style

Ash is known for his use of effects, sustained feedback, the e-bow, and a Telecaster-style guitar, as well as saxophone playing — an inheritance from his early idol Mick Ronson and his love of glam rock.
