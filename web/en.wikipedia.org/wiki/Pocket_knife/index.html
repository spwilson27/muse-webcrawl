Pocket knife

A pocket knife is a knife with a blade (or blades) that folds into the handle, small enough to be carried in a pocket. The typical pocket knife has one or more folding blades contained within its handle and is usually no longer than about 12 cm (5 in) when closed. Pocket knives are general-purpose tools used for whittling, opening packages, preparing food, fishing, hunting, and countless everyday cutting tasks.

History

Folding knives date to the Iron Age; folding utility knives were common in the Roman Empire. The modern pocket knife developed in Europe in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries—English "penny knives" and French and German patterns led to famous regional styles such as the Laguiole (France), the Opinel (France, from 1890), and the knives of Solingen (Germany) and Sheffield (England). In the United States, companies such as Case and Buck built large followings, and the Swiss Army knife—produced by Victorinox and (formerly) Wenger—became an international icon of the multi-tool folding knife.

Types and mechanisms

Simple friction folders and slipjoint knives are held open only by spring pressure. Locking knives add a mechanism for safety: liner locks, frame locks, lockbacks, and axis locks are common. Traditional patterns include the stockman, trapper, Barlow, canoe, and congress knives, each with characteristic blade combinations. Finer distinctions include penknives (originally for trimming quill pens), pruning knives, and electrician's knives. Some pocket knives are also multi-tools, combining blades with screwdrivers, corkscrews, scissors, and other implements.

Legality

The legality of carrying a pocket knife varies widely by jurisdiction. Many places restrict blade length, prohibit certain opening mechanisms (automatic/"switchblade" knives are heavily regulated in many countries and were long the subject of federal restriction in the United States), or ban carrying knives in specific places. The United Kingdom, for example, generally permits carrying a folding knife with a blade under 3 inches without needing a reason, but prohibits most other knives in public absent a good reason.
