Dessert

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: An assortment of desserts including cake, fruit and chocolate]

Dessert is a course that concludes a meal, typically consisting of sweet foods such as cakes, pastries, Ice cream, fruits, custards and confections, and often a sweet drink. The word derives from the French desservir, "to clear the table".

Sweet courses became distinct as sugar grew cheaper from the 17th century; French pâtisserie set the professional standard with creations from the croquembouche to the soufflé. Other traditions include Indian milk sweets (gulab jamun, rasmalai), Turkish baklava and lokum, Japanese wagashi served with tea, and the cheese course that in France may precede or replace dessert.

Contents
1. Types

Types

Desserts are classed as baked goods (cakes, pies, tarts), frozen desserts, custards and puddings, gelatin desserts, fried doughs (churros, doughnuts), and confectionery. Modern pastry chefs combine these with savoury and acidic notes, and tasting menus often end with several dessert courses.
