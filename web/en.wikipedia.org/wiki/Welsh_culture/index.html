Welsh culture

Welsh culture is the culture of Wales, a Celtic country of the United Kingdom, shaped by its Welsh language, its literary and musical traditions, its chapel and industrial heritage, and by the distinctive geography and history of the nation.

Language

Welsh (Cymraeg), a Brythonic Celtic language, is spoken by roughly a fifth of the population and has official status in Wales. It is the language of one of Europe's oldest living literary traditions, including the Mabinogion, medieval praise poetry and the hymn tradition. The 20th century saw a revival driven by education, the Welsh-medium schools movement, and the broadcaster S4C, launched in 1982.

Literature and music

Wales has a rich bardic tradition: the eisteddfod, a festival of poetry, music and literature presided over by the Gorsedd of Bards, is held annually as the National Eisteddfod of Wales. Welsh literature includes the work of Dylan Thomas, R. S. Thomas, and Kate Roberts, while popular music has produced acts from Tom Jones and Shirley Bassey to the Manic Street Preachers and Super Furry Animals. Male-voice choirs are a hallmark of Welsh musical life.

Traditions and symbols

National symbols include the red dragon (Y Ddraig Goch) on the Welsh flag, the daffodil and the leek, and Saint David, patron saint, celebrated on 1 March. Rugby union is the national sport, and Wales's Millennium Stadium in Cardiff has hosted the Six Nations and major events. Traditional dishes include cawl (a lamb stew), bara brith and Welsh rarebit.
