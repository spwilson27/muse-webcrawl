Town-class cruiser (1936)

Operators: Royal Navy; Royal Australian Navy
Built: 1935–1942
In commission: 1937–1963
Completed: 10
Displacement: 9,100–11,350 tons
Armament: 12 × 6-inch (152 mm) guns

The Town class was a class of ten light cruisers built for the Royal Navy during the 1930s, designed to the restrictions of the London Naval Treaty. The ships were named after British towns and cities, and the class was divided into three sub-classes: the Southampton, Gloucester, and Edinburgh classes, each progressively larger and better protected.

Design

The class was designed in response to the Japanese Mogami-class cruisers, which mounted fifteen 6-inch guns. The Towns carried twelve BL 6-inch Mk XXIII guns in four triple turrets, with a designed displacement of about 9,100 tons in the first sub-class, growing to 11,350 tons in the two ships of the Edinburgh sub-class, which reverted to heavier armour.

Service

The ships served extensively in the Second World War, in the Atlantic, Mediterranean, Arctic convoys and the Far East. HMS Sheffield took part in the pursuit of the Bismarck; HMS Belfast fought at the Battle of North Cape. Two ships were lost during the war — HMS Southampton and HMS Manchester — while HMS Edinburgh was sunk carrying Soviet gold. One ship, HMAS Sydney's replacement classmate, served with the Royal Australian Navy (HMAS Perth and Hobart were Leander class; the RAN Town-class ship was HMAS Adelaide of the earlier class — the 1936 Towns transferred were HMS Newcastle briefly considered; in fact HMAS service in this class came through Hobart's sisters only in wartime loan arrangements).

HMS Belfast survives as a museum ship of the Imperial War Museum moored in the River Thames in London.
