Gestational diabetes

Oral glucose tolerance testing during pregnancy

Gestational diabetes is a condition in which a pregnant woman without previous diabetes develops high blood sugar levels. It usually shows few symptoms, but increases the risk of pre-eclampsia, depression and caesarean delivery, and babies born to mothers with poorly treated gestational diabetes are at higher risk of excessive birth weight (macrosomia), low blood sugar after birth and jaundice.

Causes and risk factors

During pregnancy the placenta produces hormones that impair insulin action; gestational diabetes develops when the pancreas cannot produce enough extra insulin to compensate. Risk factors include overweight, previous gestational diabetes, a family history of type 2 diabetes, polycystic ovarian syndrome and older maternal age.

Diagnosis and management

Screening is usually performed between 24 and 28 weeks of pregnancy with an oral glucose tolerance test. Management centres on dietary modification, exercise and blood glucose monitoring; insulin or oral agents such as metformin may be added if targets are not met. Blood sugar usually returns to normal after delivery, but affected women and their children have an increased lifetime risk of type 2 diabetes, so follow-up testing is recommended.
