Pinocchio is a fictional character and the protagonist of the children's novel The Adventures of Pinocchio (1883) by Italian author Carlo Collodi. A wooden marionette carved by the woodworker Geppetto in a Tuscan village, Pinocchio is brought to life and dreams of becoming a real boy. He is known for his long nose, which grows when he lies — one of the most famous images in children's literature.

The novel

Collodi published the story serially in the Giornale per i bambini from 1881, originally ending with Pinocchio hanged by the Fox and the Cat; readers' protests prompted Collodi to continue, adding the Fairy with Turquoise Hair, who resurrects and guides him. Along his picaresque adventures Pinocchio is repeatedly led astray — by the Fox and the Cat, by the lure of the Land of Toys (where lazy boys turn into donkeys), and by his own disobedience — is swallowed by a giant shark (in the original, the Terrible Dogfish), rescues Geppetto, and at last, having learned to work, study and care for his father, wakes to find himself a real boy.

Character and themes

Pinocchio is impulsive, naïve and easily tempted, but good-hearted; his story is a moral tale about education, work and filial duty aimed at children of the newly unified Italy. The growing nose appears in only a few episodes of the book but became the character's defining trait. The novel — translated into hundreds of languages — is one of the best-selling and most influential children's books ever written, and its darker, more satirical tone differs markedly from most adaptations.

Adaptations

The most famous adaptation is Walt Disney's animated film Pinocchio (1940), the second Disney animated feature, which softened the story, created the iconic Jiminy Cricket as Pinocchio's conscience, and won Academy Awards for its music, including "When You Wish Upon a Star". Other notable versions include Luigi Comencini's television miniseries (1972), Roberto Benigni's 2002 film (in which he later also played Geppetto in Matteo Garrone's 2019 version), and Guillermo del Toro's stop-motion Pinocchio (2022), which won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.
