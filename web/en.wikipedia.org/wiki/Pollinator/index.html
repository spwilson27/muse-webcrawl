Pollinator

A pollinator is an animal that moves pollen from the male anther of a flower to the female stigma of a flower. This helps to bring about fertilisation of the ovules in the flower by the male gametes from the pollen grains.

Insects are the major pollinators of most plants, and insect pollinators include all families of bees and most families of aculeate wasps, ants, many families of flies, many lepidopterans (butterflies and moths), and many families of beetles. Vertebrates, mainly bats and birds, pollinate certain plant species. Sometimes the terms pollinator and pollinizer are confused: a pollinizer is a plant that provides pollen, while a pollinator is the agent that moves it.

Types of pollinators

  • Bees — the most important pollinators worldwide, including honey bees, bumblebees, carpenter bees, mason bees and many solitary species.
  • Butterflies and moths — important for many wildflowers; some moths specialise in night-blooming flowers.
  • Flies — hoverflies and others are significant pollinators, especially in cool climates.
  • Beetles — among the oldest pollinators, associated with magnolias and other ancient plant lineages.
  • Birds — hummingbirds in the Americas, sunbirds in Africa and Asia, and honeyeaters in Australia.
  • Bats — pollinate many tropical plants, including agave, mango and banana relatives.

Ecological and economic importance

Pollinators are responsible for assisting in the reproduction of roughly one out of every three bites of food humans eat. Crops such as apples, almonds, blueberries, coffee and many vegetables depend wholly or partly on animal pollination. The global economic value of pollination services is estimated in the hundreds of billions of dollars annually.

Decline

Many pollinator populations are declining due to habitat loss, pesticide use (especially insecticides such as neonicotinoids), disease, parasites such as the Varroa mite, invasive species, and climate change. Colony collapse disorder in honey bees has drawn particular public attention. Conservation responses include planting pollinator-friendly habitats, reducing pesticide use, and monitoring schemes for wild pollinators.
