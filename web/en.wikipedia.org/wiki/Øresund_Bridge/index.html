The Øresund Bridge (Danish: Øresundsbroen; Swedish: Öresundsbron) is a combined railway and motorway cable-stayed bridge across the Øresund strait between Denmark and Sweden. Together with the artificial island of Peberholm and the Drogden Tunnel on the Danish side, it forms the Øresund Link, connecting Copenhagen with Malmö and creating a single cross-border metropolitan region.

Design and construction

The crossing is about 16 kilometres long in total: an 8-kilometre bridge from the Swedish coast to the artificial island of Peberholm, built to transition between bridge and tunnel, and a 4-kilometre immersed tunnel beneath the Drogden shipping channel to the Danish island of Amager. The bridge itself is a double-deck structure, with the motorway above and the railway below, and its main cable-stayed span of 490 metres is carried on pylons 204 metres high. Construction began in 1995 and the link was inaugurated on 1 July 2000 by King Carl XVI Gustaf of Sweden and Queen Margrethe II of Denmark.

Traffic and significance

The link carries the E20 motorway and the Øresund Line railway, used by commuter trains and long-distance services between the two countries. It is a prominent symbol of Scandinavian cooperation and was featured prominently in the Danish–Swedish television crime series The Bridge (Broen/Bron).
