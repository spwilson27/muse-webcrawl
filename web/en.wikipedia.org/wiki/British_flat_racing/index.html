British flat racing

Flat racing in Great Britain is the traditional summer code of British horse racing, run over distances from five furlongs (1,000 m) to two and a half miles (4,000 m) on turf racecourses without obstacles. Together with National Hunt (jump) racing it forms the dual structure of the British racing calendar, which is regulated by the British Horseracing Authority.

The Classics

The five Classics, restricted to three-year-old thoroughbreds, are the season's foundation races: the 2000 Guineas and 1000 Guineas at Newmarket in spring; the Derby and the Oaks at Epsom in June; and the St Leger at Doncaster in September. Winning all three colts' Classics — the Triple Crown — has not been achieved since Nijinsky in 1970.

Major meetings

The flat season runs broadly from the Lincoln Handicap at Doncaster in March to British Champions Day at Ascot in October. Its highlights include Royal Ascot, the July Meeting at Newmarket, Glorious Goodwood, the Ebor Festival at York and the St Leger festival. Britain's most valuable flat race is the Derby; the richest day is Champions Day. All-weather racing on artificial surfaces at tracks such as Lingfield and Kempton extends the flat programme through the winter.

Race classification

The best races are Pattern races — Group 1, 2 and 3 — followed by Listed races and handicaps, in which official ratings determine the weights horses carry. The champion jockey, trainer and owner titles are decided across the championship period from the Guineas meeting to Champions Day.
