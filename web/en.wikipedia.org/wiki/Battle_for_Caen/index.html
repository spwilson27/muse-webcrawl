Battle for Caen

The Battle for Caen was a series of operations fought by British and Canadian forces of the Second Army against German Panzer divisions in the summer of 1944 for control of the Norman city of Caen and its surroundings during the Battle of Normandy. Caen, a D-Day objective, was not taken until mid-July, after operations including Perch, Epsom, Windsor, Charnwood and Goodwood.

Background

Caen was a key road and rail hub whose capture was assigned to the British I Corps landing at Sword Beach on 6 June 1944. Strong German resistance by the 21st Panzer Division and then the 12th SS Panzer Division blocked a quick capture, and the struggle around the city drew in the bulk of German armour in Normandy — armour that consequently was not available to oppose the American breakout in the west (Operation Cobra).

The operations

After the failed thrust at Villers-Bocage (Perch) and the Odon offensive (Epsom, 26 June–1 July), the northern outskirts were taken in Operation Windsor, and Operation Charnwood (8–9 July), preceded by a controversial heavy bomber raid that devastated much of the city and killed hundreds of French civilians, captured Caen north of the Orne. The remainder fell after Operations Atlantic and Goodwood (18–20 July). The two-month battle left Caen largely in ruins; around 2,000 of its civilians died, and the city's liberation came to symbolise both Allied persistence and the cost of the Normandy campaign.

Assessment

Historians debate whether Caen's slow capture represented failure against the original plan or a deliberate attritional strategy by Montgomery to fix German armour while the Americans built up. Either way, by late July the German position in Normandy was collapsing.
