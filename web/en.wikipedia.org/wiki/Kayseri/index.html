Kayseri

Placeholder image: Kayseri and Mount Erciyes.

Kayseri (Turkish pronunciation: [ˈkajseɾi]) is a large industrialised city in Central Anatolia, Turkey. It is the seat of Kayseri Province. The city has been continuously inhabited since around 3000 BC and was known in antiquity as Caesarea (and before that as Mazaka). It lies at the foot of the extinct volcano Mount Erciyes, which rises to 3,916 metres.

History

Kayseri was a major trading centre on the ancient Silk Road and served as the capital of the kings of Cappadocia. Under Roman rule it was renamed Caesarea in Cappadocia and became an important Christian centre, home to Saint Basil the Great. The city later passed through Seljuk, Mongol and Ottoman hands, and its skyline retains notable Seljuk-era mosques, madrasas and tombs.

Economy

Modern Kayseri is one of the so-called "Anatolian Tigers", noted for its furniture manufacturing, textiles, food processing and a famously entrepreneurial business culture. The city is also renowned for its cuisine, particularly pastırma (cured beef) and mantı (small dumplings).

Geography and climate

Kayseri sits on a plateau at roughly 1,054 metres elevation. It has a continental climate with hot, dry summers and cold, snowy winters; the nearby Mount Erciyes hosts a ski resort. The metropolitan municipality covers a wide area and had a population of over 1.4 million in recent censuses.
