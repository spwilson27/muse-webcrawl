Sulfur trioxide

FormulaSO3
Molar mass80.06 g/mol
AppearanceColourless liquid or white crystalline solid; fumes in air
Melting point16.9 °C (γ-form)
Boiling point45 °C
HazardsHighly corrosive; reacts violently with water

Sulfur trioxide (SO3) is the chemical compound that is the anhydride of sulfuric acid: it reacts with water to form H2SO4. It is produced industrially on a vast scale as the key intermediate in the manufacture of sulfuric acid by the contact process. Sulfur trioxide is a potent oxidising and sulfonating agent and a serious pollutant when emitted to the atmosphere, where it contributes to acid rain.

Structure and forms

In the gas phase, SO3 is a trigonal planar molecule with sulfur in the +6 oxidation state. On condensation it forms several solid polymorphs: the γ-form consists of cyclic trimers (S3O9) and melts at 16.9 °C, while the α- and β-forms are asbestos-like polymeric chains of corner-sharing SO4 tetrahedra that form in the presence of traces of water. The liquid readily fumes in air as escaping SO3 combines with moisture to make a mist of sulfuric acid droplets, which makes accidental releases particularly hazardous.

Production

Industrially, sulfur trioxide is made by the catalytic oxidation of sulfur dioxide with air over a vanadium(V) oxide catalyst at around 450 °C: 2 SO2 + O2 ⇌ 2 SO3. In the contact process the SO3 is absorbed not into water directly — which would generate an uncontrollable acid mist — but into concentrated sulfuric acid, forming oleum (fuming sulfuric acid), which is then diluted to the desired acid strength. In the laboratory SO3 can be prepared by dehydrating sulfuric acid with phosphorus pentoxide or by thermal decomposition of certain sulfates.

Reactions and uses

Sulfur trioxide hydrates violently to sulfuric acid and sulfonates aromatic compounds, a reaction used in making detergents (linear alkylbenzene sulfonates), dyes, and drugs. It also reacts with sulfur dichloride to give thionyl chloride, and with bases to give sulfates. Dissolved in sulfuric acid it forms oleum, an important industrial sulfonating medium.

Environmental role

Volcanic emissions and the oxidation of sulfur dioxide from fossil-fuel combustion generate sulfur trioxide and sulfuric acid aerosols in the atmosphere; these are principal components of acid deposition and of the sulfate particles implicated in respiratory harm and in stratospheric effects after large eruptions.
