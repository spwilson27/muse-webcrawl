A self-balancing scooter (also known as a hoverboard, self-balancing board or swagboard) is a portable, rechargeable battery-powered scooter consisting of two motorised wheels connected by a pair of articulated foot pads, on which the rider stands. Gyroscopes and tilt sensors detect the rider's lean: leaning forward drives both wheels ahead, leaning one foot more than the other turns the device. Despite the popular name, the device rolls on the ground and does not hover.

History

The device appeared in China around 2013–2014, produced by numerous manufacturers, and became a global consumer craze in 2015, heavily promoted by celebrity sightings and social media. Its origins were disputed in patent litigation involving companies such as Chic Intelligent Technology, Inventist and Ninebot/Segway.

Safety and regulation

The craze was accompanied by widely reported incidents of lithium-ion battery fires during charging, prompting recalls, airline carriage bans, and the creation of the UL 2272 safety standard for electrical systems of personal e-mobility devices. Several jurisdictions also restricted where the scooters may be ridden, generally treating them as motor vehicles not permitted on pavements or public roads.
