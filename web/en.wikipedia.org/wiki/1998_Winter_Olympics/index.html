1998 Winter Olympics

The 1998 Winter Olympics, officially the XVIII Olympic Winter Games and commonly known as Nagano 1998, were a winter multi-sport event held from 7 to 22 February 1998, primarily in Nagano, Japan. They were the third Olympic Games held in Japan and the second Winter Olympics, after Sapporo 1972. A record 72 nations and 2,176 athletes took part in 68 events in 14 sports.

Nagano hosted the last Winter Olympics of the 20th century.

Highlights

The Nagano Games saw several notable firsts. Snowboarding made its Olympic debut, as did women's ice hockey; curling returned to the official programme for the first time since 1924. Professional players from the National Hockey League took part in the men's ice hockey tournament for the first time; the Czech Republic, led by goaltender Dominik Hašek, won the gold medal, defeating Russia in the final.

Norwegian cross-country skier Bjørn Dæhlie won three gold medals, becoming the first winter Olympian to reach twelve career medals, including eight golds. Fifteen-year-old American figure skater Tara Lipinski became the youngest individual gold medallist in Winter Olympic history by winning the ladies' singles, defeating Michelle Kwan. Austrian alpine skier Hermann Maier crashed spectacularly in the downhill yet recovered to win gold in the super-G and giant slalom days later. Japan's ski jumping team won the large-hill team event before ecstatic home crowds.

Bid scandal

The Nagano bid later became entangled in the wider Olympic bid scandal that erupted in late 1998 over the Salt Lake City bid for the 2002 Winter Games. Nagano officials acknowledged that bid records had been destroyed, and the city's bid spending — reported at tens of millions of dollars — drew sustained criticism. The scandal led to the expulsion of several IOC members and major reforms of the bidding process.
