Verdun is a town in the Meuse department in Grand Est, north-eastern France, on the banks of the river Meuse. It is a subprefecture and a small commercial centre, but its name resonates far beyond its size: Verdun was the site of the Treaty of Verdun in 843, which divided Charlemagne's empire, and of the Battle of Verdun in 1916, one of the longest and costliest battles of the First World War.

History

Verdun was a Gallic oppidum (Verodunum, "strong fort") and later a Roman town, and a bishopric from the fourth century. The Treaty of Verdun (843), agreed between the three surviving sons of Louis the Pious, split the Carolingian Empire into West, Middle and East Francia — a division often taken as a starting point for the separate histories of France and Germany. As one of the Three Bishoprics, Verdun was annexed by France in 1552 and formally ceded by the Holy Roman Empire in the Peace of Westphalia (1648). Vauban fortified the town, which held out in the Prussian invasion of 1792 and was occupied after a siege in 1870 during the Franco-Prussian War.

Battle of Verdun, 1916

In February 1916 Germany attacked the Verdun salient, intending, in General Falkenhayn's strategy, to bleed the French army white. The battle lasted until December 1916 — 302 days — with artillery fire of unprecedented density; combined casualties are commonly estimated at around 700,000. Forts Douaumont and Vaux became symbols of the struggle, and the defence, organized by Philippe Pétain under the slogan "Ils ne passeront pas" ("They shall not pass"), was sustained along the Voie Sacrée supply road. Verdun became an enduring French national symbol of sacrifice and endurance; the Douaumont Ossuary and vast war cemeteries overlook the scarred battlefield.

Today

Modern Verdun has a population of roughly 17,000. It hosts the Centre Mondial de la Paix (World Centre for Peace) in the former bishop's palace, the Verdun Memorial museum, and each year commemorates the battle. The town is also known for its dragées (sugared almonds), manufactured there since the 18th century.
