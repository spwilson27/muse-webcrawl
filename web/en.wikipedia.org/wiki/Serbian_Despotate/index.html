Serbian Despotate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Serbian Despotate (Serbian: Српска деспотовина, Srpska despotovina) was a medieval Serbian state of the first half of the 15th century, the last medieval Serbian polity to be conquered by the Ottoman Empire. It succeeded the fragmented realm left after the dissolution of the Serbian Empire and the Battle of Kosovo (1389).

History

After the death of Prince Lazar at Kosovo, his son Stefan Lazarević initially ruled as an Ottoman vassal, fighting for Sultan Bayezid I at Nicopolis (1396) and Ankara (1402). After the Ottoman defeat at Ankara, Byzantium granted him the title of despot in 1402, and Hungary ceded Belgrade, which he rebuilt as a flourishing capital. Stefan promoted literature and art (the Resava School of scribes at Manasija Monastery) and codified mining law for the rich silver mines of Novo Brdo.

His successor Đurađ Branković returned Belgrade to Hungary and built the great fortress of Smederevo (1430) as the new capital. The Despotate was first occupied by the Ottomans in 1439–1444, restored by the Crusade of Varna settlement, and finally conquered when Sultan Mehmed II took Smederevo on 20 June 1459, ending medieval Serbian statehood for centuries.
