Guelders

Duchy of Guelders
Status: County (c. 1096–1339), then Duchy of the Holy Roman Empire
Capital: Geldern
Today part of: Netherlands and Germany

Guelders or Gelderland (Dutch: Gelre; German: Geldern) was a historical county and later duchy in the Low Countries, roughly corresponding to the present-day Dutch province of Gelderland, with its original core around the town of Geldern in present-day Germany.

History

The county of Guelders emerged around 1096 and was raised to a duchy in 1339. It was divided into four "quarters": Nijmegen, Roermond, Zutphen and Arnhem. The duchy was repeatedly a prize in the wars between the Habsburgs and France; its last independent duke, Charles of Egmond, resisted Habsburg rule until his death in 1538. In 1543 the duchy passed to the Habsburg Netherlands by the Treaty of Venlo, though Zutphen and the northern parts later joined the Dutch Republic.

Legacy

The name survives in the modern province of Gelderland, the largest province of the Netherlands by land area, and in the German town of Geldern in North Rhine-Westphalia.
