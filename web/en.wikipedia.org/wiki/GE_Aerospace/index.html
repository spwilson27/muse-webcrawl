GE Aerospace

General Electric Company, doing business as GE Aerospace, is an American aerospace company which is a provider of jet and turboprop engines, as well as integrated systems for commercial, military, business and general aviation aircraft. General Electric was incorporated in New York in 1892 as a merger of several electric companies; the modern GE Aerospace is the remaining company after the spin-offs of GE HealthCare (2023) and GE Vernova (2024).

The company traces its aircraft-engine work to 1917, when the U.S. government sought a maker of turbosuperchargers for military aircraft. During the Second World War GE built the first American jet engine, the I-A, flown in 1942, and went on to power generations of military and civil aircraft.

History

General Electric grew from the merger of Edison General Electric and Thomson-Houston Electric in 1892, with Charles Coffin as its first president. Through the 20th century it became a vast conglomerate spanning power generation, appliances, broadcasting (NBC), finance (GE Capital) and healthcare. Under Jack Welch (1981–2001) it became one of the world's most valuable companies, but after the 2008 financial crisis it progressively divested businesses.

Restructuring and renaming

In November 2021 GE announced it would split into three public companies. GE HealthCare was spun off in January 2023 and GE Vernova (energy) in April 2024, leaving the aviation business — renamed GE Aerospace in July 2022 — as the continuing company under CEO H. Lawrence Culp Jr.

Products

Through CFM International, its joint venture with Safran Aircraft Engines, GE produces the CFM56 and LEAP families, the best-selling commercial jet engines in history. Other products include the GE90 and GE9X for the Boeing 777 family, the GEnx for the 787, and military engines such as the F404, F110 and F414. The company also supplies avionics and electrical power systems.
