Jim Lee (born August 11, 1964) is a Korean-born American comic book artist, writer, editor and publisher. He is currently the Publisher and Chief Creative Officer of DC Comics. Lee first rose to prominence at Marvel Comics, where his work on The Uncanny X-Men made him one of the most popular artists in the industry; X-Men #1 (1991), which he co-created with writer Chris Claremont, remains one of the best-selling comic books of all time.

Early life and career

Lee was born in Seoul, South Korea, and immigrated with his family to the United States, growing up in St. Louis, Missouri. He studied psychology at Princeton University and originally intended to become a physician before pursuing comics. After early work for Marvel on titles such as Alpha Flight and Punisher War Journal, he took over Uncanny X-Men, redesigning several characters whose looks remain iconic.

Image Comics and WildStorm

In 1992 Lee was one of the seven artists who left Marvel to found Image Comics. His studio, WildStorm Productions, launched WildC.A.T.S and Gen¹³. In 1998 Lee sold WildStorm to DC Comics and eventually moved into executive roles, while continuing to draw major projects such as Batman: Hush (2002–2003, written by Jeph Loeb) and Superman: For Tomorrow.

Executive career

Lee became co-publisher of DC Comics in 2010 and was a principal designer of the company's 2011 "New 52" relaunch, for which he redesigned the costumes of the Justice League. He was named Chief Creative Officer in 2018 and sole Publisher in 2023. His highly detailed, dynamic art style has influenced generations of comic book artists.
