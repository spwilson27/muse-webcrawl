Greater Manchester is a ceremonial county and metropolitan and combined authority area in North West England, with a population of roughly 2.9 million. It comprises ten metropolitan boroughs — Bolton, Bury, Manchester, Oldham, Rochdale, Salford, Stockport, Tameside, Trafford and Wigan — together with the cities of Manchester and Salford at its core. It is one of the United Kingdom's largest urban areas and a major centre of commerce, culture and higher education.

History

The county was created on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, drawn from parts of Lancashire and Cheshire plus several independent county boroughs. The area had grown explosively during the Industrial Revolution as the world centre of cotton manufacture — "Cottonopolis" — served by canals and, from 1830, the Liverpool and Manchester Railway. Greater Manchester County Council was abolished in 1986, and since 2011 most strategic functions have been exercised by the Greater Manchester Combined Authority, led since 2017 by a directly elected Mayor of Greater Manchester.

Economy and culture

Greater Manchester is home to two globally famous football clubs, Manchester United and Manchester City, major universities including the University of Manchester, and the MediaCityUK media hub in Salford. Its economy spans financial services, digital industries, manufacturing and logistics, and it is frequently cited as the UK's most important economic centre outside London.
