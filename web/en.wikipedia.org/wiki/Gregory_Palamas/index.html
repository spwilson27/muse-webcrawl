Gregory Palamas

Gregory Palamas (Greek: Γρηγόριος Παλαμᾶς; c. 1296 – 1359) was a Byzantine Greek theologian and hesychast monk of Mount Athos who became Archbishop of Thessalonica. He is venerated as a saint in the Eastern Orthodox Church and is best known for defending hesychast spirituality and formulating the distinction between God's essence and energies.

Life

Born in Constantinople to a noble family, Palamas was educated at the imperial court before renouncing a political career for monastic life on Mount Athos around 1316. After periods in various monasteries, he settled in Thessalonica and was ordained a priest. During the 1330s and 1340s he became the leading theological defender of the hesychast monks against the Calabrian scholar Barlaam of Seminara, who derided hesychast prayer practices.

The hesychast controversy

Barlaam attacked Palamas's defence that humans can experience the uncreated light of God — the light of the Transfiguration on Mount Tabor. Palamas responded with the Triads in Defense of the Holy Hesychasts (c. 1338–1341), distinguishing between God's inaccessible essence and His communicable energies: God is wholly present in His energies yet remains beyond creaturely comprehension in essence. Councils in Constantinople in 1341 and 1351 upheld Palamas's teaching, which is now regarded as normative Orthodox doctrine.

Later years and legacy

Palamas was consecrated Archbishop of Thessalonica in 1347, was briefly held captive by Turkish pirates in 1354, and died on 14 November 1359. He was canonized in 1368. His feast day, the Second Sunday of Great Lent, is styled the "Second Triumph of Orthodoxy." His theology of theosis (deification) remains central to Eastern Orthodox spirituality.
