The Hardangerfjord (Norwegian: Hardangerfjorden) is the fifth longest fjord in the world, and the second longest fjord in Norway. It is located in Vestland county in the Hardanger region, stretching about 179 kilometres (111 mi) from the Atlantic Ocean into the mountainous interior.

Geography

The fjord runs from islands near Bømlo northeast to Odda at the head of its Sørfjorden branch. Along its shores lie the Folgefonna glacier, the Trolltunga rock formation, and some of Norway's most important fruit-growing districts, famous for apple, plum, and cherry orchards and for cider production.

Crossings and industry

The Hardanger Bridge, opened in 2013, carries Norwegian National Road 13 across the fjord near its Eidfjorden branch and is one of the longest suspension bridges in the world. The fjord is also used for fish farming, shipping, and hydroelectric power in its side valleys.

Tourism

Hardanger has been a tourist destination since the nineteenth century, promoted for its waterfalls – including Vøringfossen and Steinsdalsfossen – glaciers, and blossoming orchards in spring.
