Taxonomy of Banksia
Genus: Banksia
Family: Proteaceae
Species: About 170 recognised

The taxonomy of Banksia concerns the classification of the Australian wildflower genus Banksia in the family Proteaceae. The genus was formally published by Carl Linnaeus the Younger in 1782, named in honour of Sir Joseph Banks, who had collected the first specimens at Botany Bay in 1770 during James Cook's first voyage.

Background

Banksia species range from prostrate shrubs to trees of 30 metres, almost all endemic to Australia, with one species extending to New Guinea and the Aru Islands. They are readily recognised by their characteristic flower spikes and woody follicle-bearing cones. Nearly all species were once divided between Banksia and the related genus Dryandra; DNA studies at the turn of the 21st century showed Banksia to be paraphyletic with respect to Dryandra, and in 2007 all Dryandra species were transferred into Banksia.

Historical arrangements

Major classifications include those of Robert Brown (1810), Carl Meissner (1856), George Bentham in Flora Australiensis (1870), and the influential 1981 monograph by Alex George, which organised the genus into subgenera, sections and series based largely on floral and fruit characters. George's arrangement, updated in 1999, long remained the accepted framework, while cladistic analyses by Kevin Thiele, Pauline Ladiges and later Austin Mast proposed alternative groupings.

Current status

Following the merger of Dryandra, the genus comprises around 170 species, and workers continue to debate whether the expanded genus should instead be split. The taxonomy remains an active area of research in Australian botany.
