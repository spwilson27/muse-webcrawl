Kodiak bear

Scientific nameUrsus arctos middendorffi
RangeKodiak Archipelago, southwestern Alaska
Mass270–635 kg (males, typical)
StatusLeast Concern (as part of brown bear)

The Kodiak bear (Ursus arctos middendorffi), also known as the Kodiak brown bear or Alaskan brown bear, is a large subspecies of brown bear inhabiting the islands of the Kodiak Archipelago in southwestern Alaska. It is one of the largest living land carnivorans, rivalled in size only by the polar bear.

Description

Kodiak bears have been isolated from mainland brown bears for roughly 10,000–12,000 years, since the end of the last Ice Age. Large males may weigh over 600 kg and stand about 1.5 m at the shoulder on all fours and around 3 m when upright. Their size is supported by an abundant diet, especially spawning salmon, sedges, berries and marine mammal carcasses.

Behaviour and conservation

The bears are generally solitary except for females with cubs and temporary gatherings at rich feeding sites such as salmon streams. Much of their habitat lies within the Kodiak National Wildlife Refuge, established in 1941. The population, estimated at around 3,500 animals, is carefully managed through limited hunting permits and viewing programmes, and is considered healthy and stable.
