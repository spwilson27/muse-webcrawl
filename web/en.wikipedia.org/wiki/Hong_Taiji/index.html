Hong Taiji (28 November 1592 – 21 September 1643), also rendered Huang Taiji, was the second ruler of the Manchu-led Later Jin dynasty and the founding emperor of the Qing dynasty. The eighth son of Nurhaci, he consolidated Manchu rule, reorganised its government on Chinese lines, and in 1636 proclaimed the Qing dynasty — setting the stage for the Qing conquest of Ming China, completed under his son and successor, the Shunzhi Emperor.

Accession

On Nurhaci's death in 1626, Hong Taiji was chosen as khan by the council of beile (princes), prevailing over rivals including his elder brother Daišan, whose support proved decisive. He progressively weakened the co-ruling princes, centralising authority in his own person.

Consolidation and reform

Hong Taiji strengthened the Eight Banners system, adding Mongol and Chinese (Hanjun) banners, and built a civil administration modelled on the Ming, with Six Ministries and Han Chinese officials such as Fan Wencheng in senior roles. He commissioned a revised Manchu script (the "old Manchu" script with diacritics added by Dahai) and in 1635 decreed that his people be called "Manchu" rather than Jurchen. Campaigns subdued Inner Mongolia and, in invasions of 1627 and 1636–37, forced Joseon Korea to transfer allegiance from the Ming to the Qing.

Proclaiming the Qing

In 1636 Hong Taiji proclaimed himself emperor, renaming his state from (Later) Jin to Qing and his era Chongde. Historians note the change distanced the dynasty from the earlier Jurchen Jin dynasty that had been an enemy of the Song. From 1639 his armies won the decisive battles of Songshan and Jinzhou (1640–42), capturing the Ming commander Hong Chengchou, though Hong Taiji died suddenly in September 1643 — months before Qing forces entered Beijing in 1644.

Succession and legacy

After a brief succession contest, his ninth son Fulin became the Shunzhi Emperor, with Hong Taiji's brother Dorgon as regent. Hong Taiji is buried in the Zhao Mausoleum (Beiling) in Shenyang (Mukden), his capital. He is regarded as the Qing dynasty's true organisational founder, transforming a frontier confederation into a multi-ethnic empire capable of ruling China.
