Heavy metal poisoning

Heavy metal poisoning is the accumulation of toxic amounts of certain metals in the body, causing damage to organs and the nervous system. The metals most commonly involved are lead, mercury, arsenic, cadmium and thallium. Exposure may be occupational, environmental, dietary or from contaminated medicines and consumer products.

Examples

Lead poisoning affects the brain, kidneys and blood, and is especially harmful to children's development; sources include old paint, contaminated water and some traditional remedies. Mercury poisoning (as in Minamata disease) damages the nervous system. Arsenic in groundwater is a major public-health problem in parts of South Asia. Cadmium accumulates in kidneys and bone, as in Japan's itai-itai disease.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on blood and urine metal levels and clinical history. Treatment centres on removing the source of exposure and, in severe cases, chelation therapy with agents such as EDTA, dimercaprol or succimer, along with supportive care.
