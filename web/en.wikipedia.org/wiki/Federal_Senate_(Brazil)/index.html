Federal Senate (Brazil)

SeatNational Congress Palace, Brasília
Members81 senators
Term8 years
Representation3 senators per state and the Federal District

The Federal Senate (Portuguese: Senado Federal) is the upper house of the National Congress of Brazil. Together with the Chamber of Deputies, it exercises the legislative power of the federal government.

Composition

The Senate has 81 members: three senators for each of Brazil's 26 states and the Federal District, representing the states equally regardless of population. Senators serve eight-year terms, with elections staggered so that the chamber renews one-third and then two-thirds of its seats in alternating elections. Candidates must be at least 35 years old.

Powers

In addition to sharing ordinary legislation with the Chamber, the Senate has exclusive powers: it tries the President and other high officials in impeachment proceedings, approves presidential nominees for the Supreme Federal Court, ambassadorships, and the central bank, authorizes foreign loans and the federal debt ceiling, and can suspend laws declared unconstitutional. The President of the Senate also serves as President of the National Congress and stands in the line of presidential succession.

History

The Senate dates to the Empire of Brazil in 1826, when senators served for life. It became an elective body under the republic in 1891 and has sat since 1960 in Oscar Niemeyer's National Congress Palace in Brasília, in the chamber beneath the complex's smaller dome.
