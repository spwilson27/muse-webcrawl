The MG Rover Group was a British car manufacturer formed in 2000 when the Phoenix Consortium bought the Rover car business from BMW. Operating from the historic Longbridge plant in Birmingham, it built cars under the MG and Rover marques until it collapsed into administration in April 2005, ending volume car production by a British-owned company.

Background

BMW had acquired the Rover Group in 1994, investing heavily but failing to return it to sustained profit. In 2000 BMW broke the group up: the Land Rover business was sold to Ford, the Mini brand was retained for a new model, and the remaining MG/Rover volume car operations at Longbridge were sold for a nominal £10 to the Phoenix Consortium, a group of businessmen led by John Towers.

Operations

MG Rover's range centred on the Rover 25, 45 and 75 and on sporting MG derivatives — the ZR, ZS and ZT "Z-cars" — plus the MG TF roadster. The company facelifted its line-up, launched the Rover 75 Tourer and the V8-powered MG ZT 260, and developed the MG XPower SV supercar through its acquisition of the Italian manufacturer Qvale. However, the model range was ageing, sales volumes fell well below what was needed to fund replacement models, and a long-sought joint venture with China's Shanghai Automotive Industry Corporation (SAIC) failed to materialise in time.

Collapse

On 8 April 2005 MG Rover entered administration with debts of over £1 billion, and production at Longbridge ceased. Around 5,000–6,000 workers lost their jobs, with thousands more affected in the supply chain. Nanjing Automobile of China subsequently purchased the company's remaining assets; SAIC had already acquired intellectual property rights to the Rover 75 and other models, which it later built in China under the Roewe name. A government-commissioned inquiry into the collapse, published in 2009, was critical of the conduct of the Phoenix directors, who had taken substantial remuneration from the business.
