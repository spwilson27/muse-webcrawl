Castelo Branco, Portugal

Castelo Branco is a city and municipality in central-eastern Portugal, capital of the district of the same name in the Centro region. It lies near the Spanish border, inland from the coast, and is known for its Templar castle, embroidered bedspreads and the olive oil and cheese of the surrounding Beira Baixa.

History

The settlement grew around a castle founded by the Knights Templar in the early 13th century, from which it takes its name — "white castle". It received royal charters in the Middle Ages and later became a seat of bishops, whose palace and garden, the Jardim do Paço Episcopal, with its baroque staircases and statues, are among its landmarks.

Economy and culture

Castelo Branco is a service and agro-industrial centre for a rural hinterland producing olive oil, cheese (queijo de Castelo Branco, with protected designation) and cork. Its traditional colchas — silk-embroidered linen bedspreads with symbolic motifs — are a celebrated craft. The city hosts a polytechnic institute and a museum of contemporary art in the former episcopal palace area.
