Lake Mariout (Arabic: بحيرة مريوط, Buhayrat Maryut), also spelled Maryut or Mareotis, is a brackish lake in the Nile Delta of northern Egypt, lying immediately south of the city of Alexandria. In antiquity, as Lake Mareotis, it was larger and connected by canals to the Nile and the Mediterranean, serving as a busy harbour for wine and goods shipped from the Egyptian interior.

History

In the Ptolemaic and Roman periods the lake's ports, including Marea and Taposiris Magna, handled trade in wine, grain, and papyrus. In 1801 British forces cut the dike between the lake and the sea during the siege of Alexandria, flooding the basin with seawater and destroying farmland; the breach was later repaired, leaving the lake brackish and partially saline. Through the 19th and 20th centuries the lake shrank as land was reclaimed for agriculture and Alexandria expanded.

Present day

The modern lake is divided into basins used for fish farming and salt production. It receives agricultural drainage and has suffered pollution from industrial and municipal effluent, though rehabilitation projects have aimed to restore its fisheries, which historically supplied much of Alexandria's fish.
