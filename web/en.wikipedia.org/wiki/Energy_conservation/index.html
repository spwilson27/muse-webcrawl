Energy conservation

Energy conservation is the effort made to reduce wasteful energy consumption by using fewer energy services, through either using energy more efficiently or reducing the amount of service used. It differs from efficient energy use, which achieves the same service with less energy; conservation may also involve behavioral change, such as turning off lights or lowering thermostats. Energy conservation is pursued to reduce costs, increase energy security, and limit greenhouse gas emissions and other pollution.

Methods

In buildings — which account for a large share of energy use — conservation measures include insulation, air sealing, efficient windows, LED lighting, smart thermostats, and passive solar design. In transport, measures include public transit, carpooling, cycling infrastructure, and driving practices such as eco-driving. Industry conserves energy through process optimization, waste-heat recovery, and cogeneration.

Policy

Governments promote conservation through building codes, appliance efficiency standards (such as Energy Star and EU energy labels), fuel-economy standards, utility demand-side management programs, and public campaigns. The 1970s oil crises spurred major conservation policies in OECD countries. Analysts note the rebound effect (Jevons paradox), in which efficiency gains partly offset conservation by increasing total use of a service.

Outcomes

The International Energy Agency has called energy efficiency the "first fuel," as avoided energy demand is often the cheapest and cleanest source of "supply."
