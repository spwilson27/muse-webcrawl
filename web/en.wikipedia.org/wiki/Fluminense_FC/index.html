Fluminense Football Club is a Brazilian sports club based in the Laranjeiras neighbourhood of Rio de Janeiro. Founded on 21 July 1902 by Oscar Cox, a Brazilian of English descent, it is the oldest football club in Rio de Janeiro still in activity. The club is known as Tricolor for its maroon, green and white vertical stripes, and its classic rivalry with Flamengo is the famous Fla-Flu derby, while it also contests derbies with Botafogo and Vasco da Gama as one of Rio's "big four".

Fluminense supporters at the Maracanã.

History

Fluminense dominated early Carioca football, winning the first Campeonato Carioca in 1906 and many state titles thereafter. The club built the Estádio das Laranjeiras, which hosted the first match of the Brazil national team in 1914. Nationally, Fluminense has won the Brazilian championship four times (1970, 1984, 2010 and 2012) and the Copa do Brasil in 2007. The club suffered a dramatic fall in the 1990s, reaching the third division in 1998, then completed a celebrated recovery culminating in winning its first Copa Libertadores in 2023, defeating Boca Juniors 2–1 after extra time in the final at the Maracanã. In 2023 it also reached the FIFA Club World Cup final, losing to Manchester City.

Stadium

The club owns the historic Estádio das Laranjeiras but plays its major home matches at the Maracanã, which it shares with Flamengo under a joint management arrangement.

Honours

  • Copa Libertadores: 2023
  • Campeonato Brasileiro Série A: 1970, 1984, 2010, 2012
  • Copa do Brasil: 2007
  • Campeonato Carioca: more than 30 titles
  • Copa Rio (1952), recognised by the club as a world title
