The American Whiskey Trail is a cultural heritage and tourism initiative launched in 2004 by the Distilled Spirits Council of the United States (DISCUS) to promote the history of distilled spirits in American life. The trail links operating distilleries and historic sites associated with American whiskey — particularly bourbon and Tennessee whiskey — and encourages tourism, education, and preservation.

Description

Participating sites include major bourbon distilleries in Kentucky, Tennessee whiskey distilleries such as Jack Daniel's and George Dickel, George Washington's rebuilt distillery at Mount Vernon in Virginia, and historic sites in Pennsylvania associated with the Whiskey Rebellion of 1794. The trail begins symbolically at the Fraunces Tavern in New York, where Washington bid farewell to his officers, and traces whiskey's role from colonial times through Prohibition to the modern craft-distilling revival.

Relation to other trails

The American Whiskey Trail inspired the Kentucky Bourbon Trail, founded earlier (1999) by the Kentucky Distillers' Association, which covers a concentrated group of Kentucky bourbon distilleries and has become a major tourist attraction drawing over a million visitors in some years.
