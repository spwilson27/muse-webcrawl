Rec. 709

ITU-R Recommendation BT.709, usually abbreviated Rec. 709 or BT.709, is a standard developed by the Radiocommunication Sector of the International Telecommunication Union (ITU-R) in regard to image encoding and signal characteristics of high-definition television (HDTV).

Technical details

The standard specifies the colour primaries, white point (D65), transfer characteristics and luma/chroma encoding for HDTV images of 1920 × 1080 pixels with a 16:9 aspect ratio. Its colour gamut is identical to that of Rec. 601 used for standard-definition television and to the sRGB gamut used for computer displays, though the transfer function differs slightly. Rec. 709 defines frame rates from 24 to 60 frames per second, in both progressive and interlaced scanning.

History

Recommendation BT.709 was first approved in 1990. Because HDTV was developed internationally, the standard aimed at a common world format for production and programme exchange. The most recent version is BT.709-6, released in 2015.

Successors

For ultra-high-definition television, Rec. 709 was supplemented by Rec. 2020, which specifies a much wider colour gamut and higher bit depths, and Rec. 2100 for high-dynamic-range television.
