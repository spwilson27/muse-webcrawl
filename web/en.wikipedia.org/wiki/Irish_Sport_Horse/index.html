The Irish Sport Horse (ISH) is a type of sport horse bred in Ireland, traditionally produced by crossing the Irish Draught horse with the Thoroughbred, and in more recent decades with continental European warmblood bloodlines. Renowned for its athleticism, bravery and temperament, the Irish Sport Horse has for many years been ranked among the world's leading studbooks for eventing, and is also successful in show jumping.

Breeding

The classic Irish Sport Horse combines the bone, substance and sensible temperament of the Irish Draught with the speed, refinement and gallop of the Thoroughbred. The resulting horse — long marketed abroad as the "Irish Hunter" — proved ideally suited to crossing country at speed and jumping large, natural obstacles. The Irish Horse Board, now part of Horse Sport Ireland, maintains the Irish Sport Horse Studbook and has operated a stallion inspection and mare classification system aimed at improving quality.

From the later twentieth century, breeders increasingly introduced warmblood sires to improve dressage and show-jumping ability, prompting debate about preserving the traditional Draught–Thoroughbred cross that underpinned Ireland's eventing success.

Performance

The Irish Sport Horse Studbook has repeatedly topped the World Breeding Federation for Sport Horses (WBFSH) rankings for eventing, and Irish-bred horses have won medals at Olympic Games, World Equestrian Games and five-star three-day events such as Badminton, Burghley and Kentucky. Famous Irish Sport Horses include Supreme Rock, Custom Made (individual gold medallist at the Sydney 2000 Olympics), and eventing sires such as King of Diamonds and Clover Hill, whose descendants dominate pedigrees in Ireland and abroad.

Characteristics

Irish Sport Horses typically stand between 15.2 and 17 hands high, in any solid colour. They are noted for soundness, a generous and careful jump, stamina, and an amenable temperament that makes them popular with both professional and amateur riders.
