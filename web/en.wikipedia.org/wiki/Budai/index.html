Budai (Chinese: 布袋; pinyin: Bùdài; Japanese: Hotei) is a semi-historical Chinese monk and folk deity, traditionally depicted as a fat, bald, laughing man carrying a large cloth sack. He lived around the 10th century in the Wuyue kingdom during the Later Liang dynasty and is identified in Chan Buddhism as an incarnation of Maitreya, the future Buddha.

Legend

Budai's original name is said to have been Qici (契此), a wandering Chan monk of Fenghua, Zhejiang, notable for his jovial nature, his large belly and the sack from which he produced food and gifts for children and the poor. According to legend, before dying he revealed himself as an emanation of Maitreya. His iconography — the exposed belly, the sack, beads and an ingot or gourd — made him a symbol of abundance, contentment and good fortune.

In East Asian culture

In Japan, Hotei is one of the Seven Lucky Gods (Shichifukujin). In the West, statues of Budai are frequently confused with images of Siddhartha Gautama, the historical Buddha, leading to the widespread "Laughing Buddha" or "Fat Buddha" figure used as a good-luck charm; rubbing his belly is said to bring prosperity. In Taoism and folk religion he is also worshipped as a god of abundance.
