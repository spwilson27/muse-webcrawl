Bard the Bowman

Bard the Bowman is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's The Hobbit (1937). A grim-faced archer of Lake-town (Esgaroth), he is a descendant of Girion, the last lord of Dale. Bard kills the dragon Smaug with his Black Arrow and afterwards becomes king of the refounded kingdom of Dale.

Role in The Hobbit

When Smaug attacks Lake-town, Bard organises the defence after the Master flees. Aided by a thrush that repeats Bilbo's report of a bare patch in Smaug's armour, Bard shoots his last arrow — the Black Arrow inherited from his forefathers — into the hollow of the dragon's left breast, killing him. After the Battle of Five Armies, Bard receives a share of the treasure of Erebor and rebuilds Dale, becoming its first king. His great-grandson Brand rules Dale at the time of The Lord of the Rings.

Conception and analysis

Critics note Bard appears late in the novel yet performs its decisive heroic deed, functioning as a traditional Germanic hero figure set against the mercantile Master of Lake-town. His lineage and restoration of Dale tie the children's story into Tolkien's wider legendary history of the North.

Adaptations

Bard is played by Luke Evans in Peter Jackson's Hobbit film trilogy (2012–2014), where his role is expanded to include a family and a confrontation with the Master of Lake-town and his deputy Alfrid.
