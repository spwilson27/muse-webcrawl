The Lockheed P-38 Lightning is a World War II American twin-piston-engined fighter aircraft with a distinctive twin-boom, central-nacelle design. Developed for the United States Army Air Corps by Lockheed, the P-38 had counter-rotating propellers and turbo-superchargers that made it one of the fastest and highest-performing fighters of its era. German pilots reportedly dubbed it the Gabelschwanz-Teufel ("fork-tailed devil"), while the Japanese called it "two planes, one pilot".

Design and development

The P-38 originated in a 1937 Army specification for a high-altitude interceptor capable of 360 mph at 20,000 feet. Hall Hibbard and Clarence "Kelly" Johnson chose a radical twin-boom layout to house two turbo-supercharged Allison V-1710 engines, the pilot, and nose armament of four .50-calibre machine guns and a 20 mm cannon — concentrated firepower that needed no synchronisation with propellers. The XP-38 first flew on 27 January 1939 and soon demonstrated speeds above 400 mph. Early problems included dangerous compressibility effects in high-speed dives, later mitigated with dive flaps, and tail flutter, cured with mass balances.

Operational history

The P-38 served in every major theatre. It excelled in the Pacific, where its long range, twin-engine safety over water, and heavy firepower made it the mount of the two highest-scoring American aces, Richard Bong (40 victories) and Thomas McGuire (38). On 18 April 1943, P-38s of the 339th Fighter Squadron flew the long-range mission that intercepted and killed Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto. In Europe the type flew escort, ground-attack and — in unarmed F-4/F-5 form — photographic reconnaissance; Charles Lindbergh, flying as a civilian technical adviser in the Pacific, famously demonstrated fuel-saving cruise techniques to P-38 pilots. Over 10,000 aircraft were built before production ended in 1945; a handful survive in museums and as warbirds.

Specifications (P-38L)

  • Crew: 1
  • Powerplant: 2 × Allison V-1710 liquid-cooled V-12, about 1,600 hp each
  • Maximum speed: about 414 mph (666 km/h) at 25,000 ft
  • Range: about 1,300 mi (2,100 km) combat; ferry range over 2,260 mi
  • Armament: 1 × 20 mm Hispano cannon, 4 × 0.50 in M2 Browning machine guns; up to 4,000 lb of bombs or rockets
