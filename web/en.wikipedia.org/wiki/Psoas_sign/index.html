Psoas sign

The psoas sign, also known as the iliopsoas test or Cope's psoas test, is a clinical sign used in the physical examination of the abdomen. It indicates irritation of the iliopsoas group of hip flexor muscles, classically by an inflamed retrocaecal appendix in acute appendicitis.

Technique

The sign can be elicited in two ways. With the patient lying on the left side, the examiner slowly extends the patient's right thigh, stretching the iliopsoas muscle; alternatively, with the patient supine, the patient is asked to actively flex the right hip against resistance from the examiner's hand. Pain in the right lower quadrant during either maneuver constitutes a positive sign.

Interpretation

A positive psoas sign suggests that an inflamed structure lies against the psoas muscle. The most common cause is a retrocaecal appendix, which lies on the psoas in roughly a third of individuals. Other causes include a psoas abscess, retroperitoneal hemorrhage, or other retroperitoneal inflammation. The sign has modest sensitivity and specificity and is used together with other findings, such as McBurney's point tenderness and the obturator sign.
