The Saxon State and University Library Dresden (German: Sächsische Landesbibliothek – Staats- und Universitätsbibliothek Dresden), known by its acronym SLUB Dresden, is the regional library of the Free State of Saxony and the university library of the Technische Universität Dresden. It is one of the largest public research libraries in Germany, holding more than five million volumes along with extensive special collections.

History

The library traces its origins to 1556, when Elector Augustus of Saxony founded the electoral court library (Hofbibliothek) in Dresden. It grew over the centuries through the collecting zeal of the Saxon electors and kings, becoming famous for treasures such as the Dresden Codex, a pre-Columbian Maya manuscript, and the autograph scores of Dresden court composers. The library suffered heavy losses in the bombing of Dresden in February 1945. In 1996 the Saxon State Library merged with the library of the Technical University to form the present SLUB, and in 2002 it moved into a new central building designed by the architects Ortner & Ortner on the university campus.

Collections and digitization

Besides its general research collections, the SLUB maintains the German Photographic Library (Deutsche Fotothek) with millions of images, the Stenographic Institute's collections, and major holdings of maps, manuscripts and early printed books. It is a leader in mass digitization in Germany; its digital collections portal makes millions of digitized pages freely available, and the library coordinates national digitization infrastructure projects.
