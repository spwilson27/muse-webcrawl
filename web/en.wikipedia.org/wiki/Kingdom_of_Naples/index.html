The Kingdom of Naples (Italian: Regno di Napoli) was a state in southern Italy from 1282 to 1816. Created when the island of Sicily revolted against Charles I of Anjou (the Sicilian Vespers, 1282) and took the Aragonese side, leaving the Angevins with the mainland territories — which confusingly retained the formal title "Kingdom of Sicily" — the kingdom was successively ruled by Angevin, Aragonese, Spanish Habsburg, Bourbon, and Napoleonic regimes before merging with Sicily in 1816 to form the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies.

History

Angevin kingdom (1282–1442)

Charles I had conquered the Norman-Hohenstaufen Regno from Manfred in 1266 (Battle of Benevento) and Charles II lost Sicily definitively while holding Naples. Under Robert the Wise (r. 1309–1343) Naples was a leading centre of Italian culture, patronised by Petrarch and Boccaccio. The reign of Joanna I and the disputed succession that followed produced civil wars, Hungarian invasions, and the eventual triumph in 1442 of Alfonso V of Aragon, who reunited Naples with Sicily in his person.

Aragonese and Spanish rule (1442–1700)

Alfonso the Magnanimous made Naples a brilliant Renaissance court. On his death (1458) Naples passed to his illegitimate son Ferrante, whose dynasty faced baronial revolts and French invasion: Charles VIII's entry in 1495 opened the Italian Wars, and by 1504 Ferdinand II of Aragon had secured Naples for Spain. For two centuries the kingdom was governed by Spanish viceroys as part of Spain's Italian system, heavily taxed for imperial wars — a burden that provoked the revolt of Masaniello (1647) and a short-lived Neapolitan republic. Despite fiscal pressure, Spanish Naples was one of Europe's largest cities and a centre of baroque art, music, and philosophy (Giordano Bruno, Tommaso Campanella, Giambattista Vico).

Bourbons, Napoleon, and the Two Sicilies

After the War of the Spanish Succession and a brief Austrian period (1707–1734), Charles of Bourbon conquered Naples in 1734, founding a resident Bourbon dynasty. His son Ferdinand IV faced the French Revolutionary wars: the Parthenopaean Republic (1799), restored monarchy, and then the Napoleonic kingdom under Joseph Bonaparte and Joachim Murat (1806–1815). After the Congress of Vienna, Ferdinand united his realms in December 1816 as the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies, ending the separate existence of the Kingdom of Naples.
