Narrative arc

A narrative arc is the chronological construction of a plot in a novel or story; it can also apply to the structure of episodes in television series and video games. As a structural term, it originated with Aristotle's Poetics and was formalised in Gustav Freytag's 19th-century analysis of drama, Freytag's pyramid, which divides a play into exposition, rising action, climax, falling action and dénouement (resolution).

Structure

In the conventional account: the exposition introduces characters, setting and the central conflict; rising action builds tension through complications; the climax is the point of highest tension and the turning point of the story; falling action shows the consequences; and the resolution (dénouement) ties up the conflict's outcome. Variants include the three-act structure (setup, confrontation, resolution) common in screenwriting.

In serial media

In television, the term "story arc" distinguishes a storyline spanning multiple episodes from self-contained episodic plots. Series such as Babylon 5 and later prestige dramas used season-long or series-long arcs, a practice that became dominant in the streaming era.
