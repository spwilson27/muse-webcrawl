Émile Léonard Mathieu

Died: 19 October 1890 (aged 55), Nancy, France

Émile Léonard Mathieu, discoverer of the Mathieu groups.

Émile Léonard Mathieu (15 May 1835 – 19 October 1890) was a French mathematician. He is known both for his work in group theory and mathematical physics.

Mathieu was born in Metz and showed precocious talent, mastering Latin and Greek as a schoolboy and completing the course at the École Polytechnique with exceptional speed. He obtained his doctorate in 1859 and eventually became professor of mathematics at the University of Nancy, where he taught for the rest of his career.

Work

In papers of 1860–1873 Mathieu described a family of multiply transitive permutation groups, the first examples of what are now called sporadic simple groups: the Mathieu groups M11, M12, M22, M23 and M24. They remained curiosities for a century until their rediscovery in the 1960s, when they took their place among the 26 sporadic simple groups of the classification of finite simple groups; M24 is intimately connected with the Golay code and the Steiner system S(5,8,24).

In mathematical physics he studied the vibrations of an elliptical membrane, which led him to the partial differential equation whose solutions are the Mathieu functions. He also wrote a multi-volume treatise on mathematical physics covering elasticity, heat conduction, electromagnetism and capillarity.
