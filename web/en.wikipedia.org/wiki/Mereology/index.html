Mereology

Mereology
FieldMetaphysics / logic
SubjectRelations between parts and wholes
Term coined byStanisław Leśniewski (1916)
[Image: Nested wooden dolls illustrating part-whole relations]

Mereology is the formal study of parts and the wholes they form — the branch of Ontology and logic concerned with parthood. It asks deceptively simple questions: when do parts compose a further object (does a heap of planks make a ship? do atoms compose a table?), whether composition is unrestricted (any plurality forms a whole), and how objects persist through part-change — puzzles classed since Plutarch under the Ship of Theseus.

Positions

Mereological universalism says any two things have a sum; nihilism says only simples exist; moderate views restrict composition to cases like living organisms (van Inwagen). The logic of parthood underlies work in formal ontology, computer science and linguistics (mass nouns, plurals).
