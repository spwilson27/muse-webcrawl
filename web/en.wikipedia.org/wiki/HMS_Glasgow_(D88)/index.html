HMS Glasgow was a Type 42 destroyer of the Royal Navy. The sixth ship to bear the name, she was laid down by Swan Hunter in 1974, launched in 1976, and commissioned in 1979. During fitting out, she was damaged by fire in 1976 that killed eight workers.

Falklands War

Glasgow was part of the task force sent to the South Atlantic during the Falklands War of 1982. Together with HMS Brilliant she formed the "Type 64" pairing of Sea Dart and Sea Wolf equipped ships providing layered air defence. On 12 May 1982, while in company with Brilliant, she was attacked by Argentine A-4 Skyhawks; a 1,000 lb bomb passed through her stern without exploding, damaging steering and an engine, and she was withdrawn for repairs before returning to the war zone. Her Sea Dart system engaged several aircraft during the campaign.

Later service

After the war, Glasgow undertook patrols worldwide, including Armilla patrol duties in the Gulf, deployments to the Adriatic during the Yugoslav wars, and in 1999–2000 service off East Timor with the INTERFET peacekeeping taskforce, one of her last major deployments. She was decommissioned on 31 January 2005, towed away in 2008, and broken up at Portsmouth in 2009.
