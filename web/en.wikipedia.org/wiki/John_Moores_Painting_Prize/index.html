John Moores Painting Prize

John Moores Painting Prize
First awarded: 1957
Location: Walker Art Gallery, Liverpool, England
Founder: Sir John Moores
Frequency: Biennial (currently)

The John Moores Painting Prize is a British art prize for painting, established in 1957 by Sir John Moores, founder of the Littlewoods retail and football pools empire. It is held in association with the Walker Art Gallery in Liverpool and is regarded as one of the United Kingdom's most prestigious awards for painters.

History

The first John Moores Exhibition was held in 1957 and was among the first major open competitions for contemporary painting in Britain; entries are submitted anonymously and judged by a changing jury of artists and critics. The prize aimed to bring the best contemporary painting to Liverpool and to encourage public engagement with modern art. Winning works have often been purchased for the Walker Art Gallery's permanent collection.

Notable past winners include some of the most significant British painters of the post-war period. Jack Smith won the first prize in 1957; later winners include David Hockney, Richard Hamilton, John Hoyland, and Peter Doig, whose win in 1993 with Blotter helped cement his reputation. The exhibition became biennial and forms part of the Liverpool Biennial of contemporary art.

Format

The competition is open to artists working in the UK, and entries are selected by an independent jury. A first prize is awarded along with additional smaller prizes, and a visitors' choice award is voted on by exhibition visitors. Because selection is anonymous and open, the exhibition is known for placing emerging artists alongside established names.
