Henri de Mondeville

Henri de Mondeville (c. 1260 – 1320) was a French surgeon and physician to kings Philip IV of France and Louis X. He is regarded as one of the founders of modern surgery in France and the author of an influential treatise, the Chirurgia ("Surgery"), written in Latin between 1306 and 1320 and left unfinished at his death.

Life and teaching

Mondeville studied medicine at Montpellier and Bologna, where he learned from Theodoric Borgognoni, whose wound-care methods he championed. He taught surgery in Paris and at Montpellier, delivering lectures illustrated with anatomical charts — among the earliest known anatomical teaching images in medieval Europe.

Surgical ideas

Following Theodoric, Mondeville opposed the prevailing doctrine of "laudable pus", arguing that wounds should be cleaned, kept dry, and closed promptly to heal without suppuration — views centuries ahead of standard practice. He advocated careful haemostasis, the use of wine as an antiseptic wash, and humane, attentive patient care, and he also wrote on cosmetic surgery and the professional ethics and status of surgeons.
