An associate degree is an undergraduate academic degree awarded by colleges and universities upon completion of a course of study lasting usually two years. It is most common in the United States, where it is offered principally by community colleges, and equivalent qualifications exist in Canada, Australia (associate degree and advanced diploma), and parts of Europe as short-cycle higher education within the Bologna Process framework.

Types

In the US the main titles are the Associate of Arts (AA) and Associate of Science (AS), generally designed for transfer into a bachelor's degree, and the Associate of Applied Science (AAS) and similar occupational degrees, designed for direct entry into employment in fields such as nursing, dental hygiene, paralegal studies and computer networking. A typical programme requires about 60 semester credit hours, combining general education with major requirements.

Role and outcomes

Associate degrees serve as an affordable, open-access first step in higher education: community college tuition is far below that of four-year institutions, and articulation agreements in many states guarantee junior-level standing for transfer graduates. Holders earn substantially more on average than workers with only a high-school diploma, though less than bachelor's degree holders. Roughly a million associate degrees are conferred annually in the United States.
