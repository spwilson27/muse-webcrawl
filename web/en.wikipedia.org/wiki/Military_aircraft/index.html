Military aircraft

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A formation of military jets trailing vapour over an airfield]
Military jets in formation.

Military aircraft are aircraft operated by armed forces for combat, support and training. They include fighters, bombers, attack aircraft, transports, tankers, helicopters, maritime patrol and surveillance types, and unmanned systems — from the B-52 bomber (in service since 1955) to stealth aircraft and armed drones. Military aviation began with observation balloons and came of age in the First World War.

Roles

Combat aircraft fight for air superiority and strike ground and naval targets; enablers multiply their effect: airborne early warning (E-3, E-7), tankers (KC-135, A330 MRTT), electronic warfare (EA-18G) and strategic airlift (C-17, A400M). Helicopters add attack (Apache), transport and anti-submarine roles; UAVs now perform surveillance and strike at scale.

Development

The Cold War drove rapid advances (jets, missiles, radar, stealth — the F-117 of 1983 was revealed in 1988). Post-Cold-War consolidation merged Western industry into giants (Lockheed Martin, Boeing, BAE Systems, Airbus); China\'s PLAAF now fields the world\'s largest combat-aircraft fleet by some counts.

Operations

Air power proved decisive or central in conflicts from the Second World War and Korea to the Gulf War (1991) and the Russo-Ukrainian war, where drones and air defences have constrained crewed aviation. Air shows (Paris, Farnborough, RIAT) showcase the types.
