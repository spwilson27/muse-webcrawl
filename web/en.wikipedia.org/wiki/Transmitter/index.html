In electronics and telecommunications, a transmitter or radio transmitter is an electronic device which produces radio waves with an antenna. The transmitter itself generates a radio frequency alternating current, which is applied to the antenna. The information to be transmitted — sound, pictures, or data — is imposed on the carrier wave by a process called modulation.

Operation

A transmitter typically contains a power supply, an oscillator that generates the carrier frequency, a modulator that varies the carrier in accordance with the information signal, and an amplifier that increases the power. The output is delivered through a transmission line to an antenna, which radiates the energy as electromagnetic waves. In most applications the transmitter is combined with a receiver in a transceiver.

Applications

Transmitters are used in broadcasting (radio and television), two-way radio communications, mobile phones, radar, navigation beacons, wireless networking, garage door openers and many other systems. Their power, frequency and emissions are regulated by national authorities and international agreements administered by the International Telecommunication Union.

History

The first practical radio transmitters were spark-gap transmitters used for wireless telegraphy in the early 1900s. They were superseded by vacuum-tube transmitters capable of continuous-wave and voice transmission, and later by solid-state designs.
