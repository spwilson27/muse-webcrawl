51 Eridani b is a gas giant exoplanet orbiting the young F-type star 51 Eridani, about 96 light-years from Earth in the constellation Eridanus. It was the first exoplanet discovered with the Gemini Planet Imager and is notable as one of the faintest planets directly imaged, resembling a young, cooler version of Jupiter.

Discovery

The planet was detected in December 2014 during the Gemini Planet Imager Exoplanet Survey and announced in 2015. Direct imaging in the near-infrared revealed a methane-rich atmosphere — the clearest spectroscopic signature of methane then seen in a directly imaged planet, marking it as a transitional object between hotter imaged planets and the Solar System's gas giants.

Properties

51 Eridani b orbits at roughly 13 astronomical units from its star, within a system about 20 million years old that also hosts a debris disk. Its effective temperature of roughly 600–750 K makes it the coldest directly imaged planet known at the time of discovery. Mass estimates depend strongly on formation models, ranging from about 2 Jupiter masses for "cold start" scenarios to higher values for "hot start" assumptions, making the system a key test of giant-planet formation theories.
