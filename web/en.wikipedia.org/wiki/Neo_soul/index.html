Neo soul

Neo soul is a genre of popular music that emerged in the mid-1990s, combining contemporary R&B with elements of 1970s soul, jazz, funk, hip hop and African music. The term was coined by music industry entrepreneur Kedar Massenburg to market artists whose style diverged from the mainstream, sample-driven R&B of the time in favour of live instrumentation, analogue warmth and idiosyncratic songwriting.

Key artists and albums

Landmark recordings include D'Angelo's Brown Sugar (1995) and Voodoo (2000), Erykah Badu's Baduizm (1997), Lauryn Hill's The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill (1998), and work by Maxwell, Jill Scott, Bilal, India.Arie, Raphael Saadiq, Angie Stone and the collective Soulquarians. British acts such as Sade (an important precursor) and later Amy Winehouse also drew on the style.

Characteristics and legacy

Neo soul is characterised by a conscious, often bohemian aesthetic, jazz-inflected harmony, gospel touches, and lyrics addressing spirituality, identity and relationships. Although its commercial peak passed in the early 2000s, the genre profoundly influenced subsequent alternative R&B artists.
