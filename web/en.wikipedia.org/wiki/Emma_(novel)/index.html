Emma (novel)

Emma is a novel by Jane Austen, published in December 1815 (dated 1816), the last of her novels published during her lifetime. A comedy of manners set in the fictional Surrey village of Highbury, it follows Emma Woodhouse, a wealthy, clever and beautiful young woman who, convinced she will never marry, amuses herself by matchmaking among her neighbours — with results that repeatedly expose her misjudgements.

Plot

Emma Woodhouse lives with her hypochondriac father at Hartfield. After successfully matching her governess to Mr Weston, she takes up the naive Harriet Smith as a protégée, persuading her to refuse the farmer Robert Martin and aiming her at the vicar Mr Elton — who in fact proposes to Emma herself. Further entanglements involve Frank Churchill, the charming but secretly engaged stepson of Mr Weston, and Jane Fairfax, the accomplished young woman Emma irrationally dislikes. Emma's errors culminate in her public rudeness to the poor spinster Miss Bates at Box Hill, for which her friend and brother-in-law Mr Knightley rebukes her. Realising she loves Knightley herself, and that Harriet's affections are also fixed on him, Emma fears she has ruined her happiness — but Harriet settles with Robert Martin, Frank's engagement to Jane is revealed, and Emma and Knightley marry.

Themes and reception

Austen described Emma before publication as "a heroine whom no one but myself will much like". The novel is admired for its free indirect discourse, its intricate plotting built on Emma's restricted point of view, and its moral comedy about self-deception and social class. Dedicated, by permission, to the Prince Regent, who admired Austen's work, Emma is now widely considered her most perfectly constructed novel.

Adaptations

The novel has been filmed many times, notably the 1996 film starring Gwyneth Paltrow, the 1995 teen film Clueless (a modern retelling), the BBC serial of 2009, and the 2020 film starring Anya Taylor-Joy.
