Moguer is a municipality and small city in the province of Huelva, Andalusia, in southwestern Spain, close to the Atlantic coast and near the mouth of the Tinto River, opposite the neighbouring town of Palos de la Frontera. It has a population of roughly 21,000. Moguer is known historically for its role in the first voyage of Christopher Columbus and culturally as the birthplace of the Nobel Prize-winning poet Juan Ramón Jiménez and the setting of his celebrated prose work Platero and I.

History

The area has been inhabited since antiquity, and the town's origins lie in a Roman and then Moorish farmstead and fortification; its name may derive from an Arabic or pre-Roman root. After the Christian conquest of the region in the 13th century, Moguer passed to various noble houses, eventually to the Portocarrero family. By the late Middle Ages it was a prosperous river port exporting wine, oil and other produce.

In 1492 Moguer played a direct part in the discovery of the Americas: the caravel Niña, one of Columbus's three ships, was built in Moguer's shipyards and owned by the local Niño brothers, who sailed on the voyage — Juan Niño as master of the Niña and Pedro Alonso Niño as pilot of the flagship. Before departing, Columbus and his crew attended mass and took communion at the Monastery of Santa Clara in Moguer, and on his return in March 1493 Columbus spent a night of vigil there fulfilling a vow made during a storm. The monastery, founded in the 14th century, is one of the town's principal monuments, alongside the Church of Nuestra Señora de la Granada and the castle.

Juan Ramón Jiménez

The poet Juan Ramón Jiménez (1881–1958), winner of the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1956, was born in Moguer, and the town is immortalised in his lyric prose-poem Platero y yo (1914; Platero and I), an elegiac portrait of the poet and his little grey donkey wandering the whitewashed streets and countryside of Moguer. His birthplace is preserved as a museum-house, and statues of Platero are a local emblem.

Economy

Modern Moguer's economy is driven by intensive agriculture, above all strawberries and other berries grown under plastic in one of Europe's largest soft-fruit producing areas, as well as by tourism linked to the Columbian sites (the "Lugares Colombinos") and to the nearby beaches of Mazagón, which the municipality shares with Palos de la Frontera.
