Moderator

This article is about the general term. For other uses, see the sections below.

Moderator
RoleFacilitation, chairing, content oversight
ContextsDebates, churches, online communities, nuclear engineering

A moderator is a person who presides over an assembly, meeting, or discussion, acting as a neutral participant who facilitates dialogue, keeps proceedings orderly and on schedule, and ensures that rules are observed. The term is used in several distinct domains, including debates and broadcasting, ecclesiastical office, online content moderation, and nuclear physics.

In debates and discussions

In a formal debate, panel discussion, town hall or television programme, the moderator introduces participants, poses questions, manages speaking time, maintains civility, and may summarise points for the audience. Moderators of political debates — such as those in United States presidential elections, organised since 1987 by the Commission on Presidential Debates — are usually journalists chosen for their neutrality and their ability to press candidates with follow-up questions. Skilled moderation is generally expected to be even-handed: the moderator facilitates rather than participates in the argument.

In churches

In several Presbyterian and Reformed churches, the moderator is the presiding officer of a governing body such as a general assembly, synod or presbytery. The Moderator of the General Assembly of the Church of Scotland, for example, is elected for a one-year term, chairs the Assembly, and serves as the church's public representative during that year; the office carries no extra doctrinal authority over other ministers. Similar offices exist in the Presbyterian Church (USA), the United Church of Canada, and other denominations.

Online moderation

On internet forums, social media platforms, chat services and multiplayer games, moderators ("mods") enforce community rules: they review reported content, remove spam and abusive posts, lock or move threads, and warn, suspend or ban users. Moderation may be carried out by volunteers (as with subreddit moderators on Reddit and administrators on Wikipedia), by professional trust-and-safety teams, or increasingly with the assistance of automated filters. Content moderation at scale — balancing free expression, user safety and legal obligations — has become a major policy and research topic.

In nuclear engineering

In nuclear reactor physics, a neutron moderator is a medium that reduces the speed of fast neutrons, turning them into thermal neutrons capable of sustaining a nuclear chain reaction in fissile material such as uranium-235. Common moderators include ordinary ("light") water, heavy water, and graphite; light water is used in most commercial reactors worldwide.
