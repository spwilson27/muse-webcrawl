Steatosis

Steatosis, also called fatty change, is the abnormal retention of fat (lipids) within cells or organs. It most commonly affects the liver — where it is known as fatty liver — but can also occur in the heart, kidneys and skeletal muscle.

Causes

Hepatic steatosis arises when the balance between lipid uptake and synthesis on one side, and lipid oxidation and export (as very-low-density lipoprotein) on the other, is disrupted. Major causes include excessive alcohol consumption, obesity, insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome, certain medications, and malnutrition. The non-alcoholic form is termed metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD, formerly NAFLD).

Pathology

Steatosis may be macrovesicular, with large fat droplets displacing the cell nucleus, or microvesicular, with numerous small droplets. Simple steatosis can progress to steatohepatitis (fat with inflammation), fibrosis, cirrhosis and liver failure. It is often reversible if the underlying cause is removed.
