The Legend of Zelda is a video game series created by Japanese game designers Shigeru Miyamoto and Takashi Tezuka, and developed and published by Nintendo. The series is set in the fantasy kingdom of Hyrule and generally follows the hero Link, who is called upon to rescue Princess Zelda and defeat the demon king Ganon (or his human form, Ganondorf), who seeks the Triforce, a sacred relic that grants its holder's wishes.

Gameplay

The games blend exploration, puzzle-solving, combat and role-playing elements. Most entries feature an explorable overworld punctuated by dungeons, in which Link acquires new items that open up further areas. Beginning with Ocarina of Time (1998), the series moved into 3D; later landmarks include The Wind Waker (2002), Twilight Princess (2006), Skyward Sword (2011), and the open-world Breath of the Wild (2017) and its sequel Tears of the Kingdom (2023).

Timeline and lore

Although most games star incarnations of the same three characters, Nintendo's official timeline — published in the Hyrule Historia (2011) — splits into three branches after the events of Ocarina of Time. Recurring elements include the Master Sword, the goddesses Din, Nayru and Farore, and the races of Hyrule: Hylians, Gorons, Zora, Gerudo and others.

Reception and legacy

The series has sold more than 160 million copies worldwide and is regarded as one of the most influential in the history of the medium; several of its titles, particularly Ocarina of Time, are routinely ranked among the greatest video games of all time. Its music, composed largely by Koji Kondo, has been performed in concert series such as the Symphony of the Goddesses.
