Slavic peoples

The Slavic peoples (or Slavs) are an ethnolinguistic group native to Central, Eastern and Southeastern Europe who speak the Indo-European Slavic languages and share, to varying degrees, cultural traits and historical backgrounds. They are the largest ethnolinguistic grouping in Europe, numbering well over 300 million people. The Slavs are traditionally divided into West Slavs, East Slavs and South Slavs.

Branches

  • West Slavs: Poles, Czechs, Slovaks, Silesians, Kashubians and Sorbs.
  • East Slavs: Russians, Ukrainians, Belarusians and Rusyns.
  • South Slavs: Serbs, Croats, Bosniaks, Bulgarians, Macedonians, Montenegrins and Slovenes.

Origins and expansion

Scholars place the early Slavic homeland in east-central Europe, commonly in the region of the Pripyat marshes and middle Dnieper (in today's Ukraine and Belarus), in the early centuries AD. Slavic-speaking groups appear clearly in Byzantine sources of the 6th century as the Sclaveni and Antes. During the Migration Period they expanded rapidly: westward to the Elbe and the Alps, eastward across the East European Plain, and southward into the Balkans, where they settled extensively in lands of the Byzantine Empire, reaching the Peloponnese.

History

Early Slavic polities included Samo's tribal union (7th century), Great Moravia and the First Bulgarian Empire (9th century), the medieval kingdoms of Poland, Bohemia, Serbia and Croatia, and Kievan Rus'. In the 860s the Byzantine missionaries Saints Cyril and Methodius devised the Glagolitic alphabet and translated Christian texts into Old Church Slavonic, shaping the religious divide between largely Orthodox East and South Slavs and largely Catholic West Slavs (with notable exceptions). Later centuries brought Mongol domination in the east, Ottoman rule in the Balkans, Habsburg and Prussian rule in the west, and in the 19th century the Pan-Slavism movement and the emergence of modern Slavic nation-states. In the 20th century most Slavic peoples lived in states associated with the Eastern Bloc, and the wars in Yugoslavia in the 1990s and the Russo-Ukrainian War from 2014 are among the most significant recent events in Slavic history.

Languages and religion

The Slavic languages descend from Proto-Slavic and are written in the Latin alphabet (mainly by Catholic and western groups) or the Cyrillic alphabet (mainly by Orthodox groups). Most Slavs are Christians — Orthodox, Catholic, or Protestant historically in some communities — and there are significant Muslim Slavic communities among Bosniaks, Pomaks and Torbeši, a legacy of Ottoman rule.
