Aviation call signs

Used byAirlines, air forces, general aviation
Assigned byICAO (airline designators); national aviation authorities; military commands
FormatCompany call sign + flight number (e.g. "Speedbird 249")

Aviation call signs are communication call signs assigned as unique identifiers for aircraft in radio telephony communication with air traffic control. For commercial flights, a call sign typically consists of an airline telephony designator followed by the flight number, such as "Speedbird 249" for a British Airways flight. Call signs prevent confusion between similarly numbered flights and between aircraft with similar registration marks.

Airline call signs

The International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) assigns three-letter airline designators and telephony designators, published in ICAO Document 8585. Some call signs are obvious shortenings of the airline name (e.g. "American", "Delta", "Lufthansa"), while others are historical or branding choices:

When two flights have similar call signs in the same airspace, regulators may require the use of alphanumeric call signs to reduce the risk of confusion and blocked transmissions.

Military and general aviation call signs

Military aircraft use tactical call signs assigned by their commands, which may identify the unit, the mission, or an individual aircrew nickname. Well-known examples include "Air Force One" for any U.S. Air Force aircraft carrying the President of the United States. General aviation aircraft usually identify themselves by their registration (tail number), sometimes shortened after first contact with a controller.
