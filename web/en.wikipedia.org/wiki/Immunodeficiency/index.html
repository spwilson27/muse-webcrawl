Immunodeficiency

Immunodeficiency
Specialty: Immunology
Symptoms: Recurrent or unusual infections
Causes: Genetic disorders; infections, drugs, malnutrition

Immunodeficiency, also known as immunocompromise, is a state in which the immune system's ability to fight infections and other diseases is weakened or absent. People with immunodeficiency suffer infections that are more frequent, more severe or caused by organisms that rarely trouble healthy people.

Types

Primary immunodeficiencies are inherited disorders — more than 400 are recognised, including severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID), X-linked agammaglobulinaemia and common variable immunodeficiency. Secondary immunodeficiencies are acquired, most famously from HIV infection progressing to AIDS, but also from immunosuppressive drugs, chemotherapy, malnutrition and certain cancers.

Diagnosis and treatment

Investigation includes blood counts, immunoglobulin levels, lymphocyte subsets and genetic testing. Treatment ranges from immunoglobulin replacement and prophylactic antibiotics to haematopoietic stem-cell transplantation and gene therapy for some primary disorders.
