Osculating plane

In the geometry of curves in three-dimensional space, the osculating plane at a point of a curve is the plane that most closely "kisses" the curve at that point — from the Latin osculari, to kiss. It is the plane spanned by the tangent vector and the principal normal vector to the curve at the point, and it contains the osculating circle, the circle that best approximates the curve locally.

Definition

For a regular space curve r(t) with unit tangent T and principal normal N, the osculating plane at a point is the plane through the point spanned by T and N. Its normal direction is the binormal vector B = T × N. Equivalently, the osculating plane is the limit of the planes through three points of the curve as the three points coalesce, and it is the unique plane having at least second-order contact with the curve.

Properties

Together with the normal plane and the rectifying plane, the osculating plane forms the Frenet–Serret frame that travels along the curve. Where the curvature vanishes (an inflection point), the osculating plane is not defined by the standard construction. As one moves along the curve, the rotation of the osculating plane is measured by the torsion of the curve: the plane stays constant along a planar curve, for which torsion is zero everywhere.
