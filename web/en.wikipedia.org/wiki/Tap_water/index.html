Tap water

Tap water (also known as faucet water, running water, or municipal water) is water supplied to a tap (valve). Its uses include drinking, washing, cooking, and the flushing of toilets. Indoor tap water is distributed through "indoor plumbing", which has existed since antiquity but was available to very few people until the second half of the 19th century when it began to spread in popularity in what are now developed countries. Tap water became common in many regions during the 20th century, and is now lacking mainly among people in poverty, especially in developing countries.

Usage

Everything in the built environment requires water. In a typical affluent household, water is used for flushing toilets, showering and bathing, washing clothes, washing dishes, drinking and cooking. In many less affluent places, people may collect their household water at a central tap or other water source.

Where tap water is available, it is often drawn from a municipal distribution system fed by reservoirs, rivers or aquifers. Water companies treat the raw water to remove pathogens and contaminants before distribution.

Treatment and safety

Governments in the developed world usually require tap water to meet drinking water quality standards. In the United States, the Environmental Protection Agency sets standards under the Safe Drinking Water Act. Treatment typically includes coagulation, filtration and disinfection with chlorine or chloramine, and in many places fluoridation for dental health.

Contamination incidents, such as the lead leaching crisis in Flint, Michigan, have highlighted the risks posed by ageing infrastructure, particularly lead service pipes.

Comparison with bottled water

Tap water is far cheaper per litre than bottled water and avoids much of the plastic waste associated with it, though taste and perceived quality vary by locality. Public-health authorities in developed countries generally consider properly treated tap water safe to drink.
