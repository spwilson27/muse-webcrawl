Quanjude (Chinese: 全聚德; pinyin: Quánjùdé) is a Chinese restaurant chain famous for its Peking roast duck and its long history, founded in Beijing in 1864 during the Qing dynasty. It is one of China's best-known "China Time-honored Brand" restaurants.

History

Quanjude was founded by Yang Quanren, who had worked as a chicken and duck dealer. In 1864 he bought a failing dried-fruit shop in the Qianmen district of Beijing and converted it into a roast duck restaurant, hiring a cook formerly of the imperial kitchens who perfected the hanging-oven roasting technique. The restaurant's reputation grew rapidly, and its duck became a delicacy associated with Beijing.

Peking duck

Quanjude's ducks are raised specially, roasted in an open oven fuelled by fruitwood, producing crisp, glossy skin and tender meat. The duck is carved at the table and served with thin pancakes, spring onion, cucumber, and sweet bean sauce. The chain claims to serve millions of ducks each year.

Expansion

From its original Qianmen location, Quanjude has expanded to many branches across China and overseas, including in Canada, Australia, and Japan. The company listed on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange in 2007.
