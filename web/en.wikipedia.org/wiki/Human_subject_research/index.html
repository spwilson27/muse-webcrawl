Human subject research


A clinical research setting.

Human subject research is systematic, scientific investigation that can be either interventional (a "trial") or observational (no "test article") and involves human beings as research subjects, commonly known as test subjects. Human subject research can be either medical (clinical) research or non-medical (e.g., social science) research.

Ethics and regulation

In the United States, human subject research is governed by the Common Rule and oversight by institutional review boards (IRBs), which review research protocols to protect the rights and welfare of subjects. Key ethical principles — respect for persons, beneficence, and justice — were articulated in the Belmont Report (1979). International standards include the Declaration of Helsinki and the Nuremberg Code, which emerged from the prosecution of Nazi medical experiments.

Historical abuses

Notorious cases of unethical research, including the Tuskegee syphilis study, Nazi human experimentation, and the Guatemala syphilis experiments, led to modern safeguards such as informed consent, independent review, and the right of subjects to withdraw from research at any time.
