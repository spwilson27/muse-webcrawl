Shin Kokin Wakashū

The Shin Kokin Wakashū (新古今和歌集, "New Collection of Poems Ancient and Modern"), often shortened to Shin Kokinshū, is the eighth imperial anthology of waka poetry of Japan. It was commissioned in 1201 by the retired Emperor Go-Toba and completed around 1205. It is regarded, along with the Kokin Wakashū and the Man'yōshū, as one of the three great anthologies of Japanese poetry.

Compilers and contents

The editorial committee included Fujiwara no Teika, Fujiwara no Ariie, Fujiwara no Ietaka, the priest Jakuren, Minamoto no Michitomo, and Fujiwara no Kunifusa; Go-Toba himself took an active role in selecting and revising poems. The anthology contains roughly 2,000 poems arranged in twenty books covering the seasons, travel, love, grief, and miscellaneous topics.

Style and reputation

The Shin Kokinshū is noted for its polished, allusive style, making extensive use of honkadori (allusive variation on earlier poems) and embodying the aesthetic ideals of yūgen—subtle profundity and suggestion—favored by Teika. It represents the culmination of court waka of the medieval period, and its poems have been central to Japanese literary culture, including the one hundred poems selected for the popular Hyakunin Isshu card game.
