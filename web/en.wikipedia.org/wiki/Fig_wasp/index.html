Fig wasp

Fig wasp
Blastophaga psenes, pollinator of the common fig
FamilyAgaonidae (pollinators); others in Chalcidoidea
SizeTypically 1–3 mm
HostFigs (Ficus species)
RelationshipObligate mutualism (pollinators)

Fig wasps are wasps of the family Agaonidae (and a number of non-pollinating chalcid families) that spend their larval stages inside figs. The pollinating fig wasps have an obligate mutualism with fig trees (Ficus): each of the roughly 750 fig species is typically pollinated by one or a few specific wasp species, and the wasps can breed only inside their partner fig.

Life cycle

A mature female wasp, covered in pollen, enters a receptive fig through the ostiole — a tight passage that often strips her wings and antennae. Inside the syconium she pollinates the female flowers while laying eggs in some of them, then dies inside the fig. Her offspring develop in galls; the wingless males hatch first, mate with females, chew an exit tunnel and die. The winged females emerge laden with pollen and fly off in search of another receptive fig, completing the cycle.

Mutualism and non-pollinators

The fig–wasp partnership is a classic example of coevolution and has persisted for tens of millions of years. Alongside the pollinators there are many non-pollinating fig wasps — gallers, parasitoids and inquilines — that exploit the fig without pollinating it. The common fig's pollinator is Blastophaga psenes; caprifigs host the wasp while edible Smyrna-type figs require its visit to set fruit.
