Hornet


A European hornet, Vespa crabro

Hornets are insects of the genus Vespa, the largest of the eusocial wasps. They are closely related to the yellowjackets (genera Vespula and Dolichovespula), from which they are generally distinguished by their larger size. There are about 22 recognised species, native to Europe, Asia, and North Africa; the European hornet (Vespa crabro) was introduced to North America.

Biology

Like other social wasps, hornets build papery nests from chewed wood fibres, located in hollow trees, wall cavities, attics, or underground. A colony is founded in spring by a single fertilised queen and may grow to several hundred workers. Hornets feed on sugary substances and prey on other insects, including bees, which they feed to their larvae. The Asian giant hornet (Vespa mandarinia), the world's largest hornet, can reach 5 cm in length and is notorious for attacks on honey bee colonies.

Stings

Hornet stings are painful and, because of the insects' size, deliver relatively large quantities of venom, which contains acetylcholine and enzymes. However, hornets are generally less aggressive than yellowjackets unless their nest is threatened. Stings are medically significant mainly for people allergic to wasp venom, for whom they can cause anaphylaxis, or when many stings occur at once.
