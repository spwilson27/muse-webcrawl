The Avengers are a fictional team of superheroes in the Marvel Cinematic Universe (MCU) media franchise, based on the Marvel Comics team of the same name. Assembled under the Avengers Initiative conceived by S.H.I.E.L.D. director Nick Fury, the team brings together super-powered and highly skilled individuals to fight threats beyond the capacity of conventional forces. The Avengers are the central protagonists of the Infinity Saga, appearing in The Avengers (2012), Avengers: Age of Ultron (2015), Avengers: Infinity War (2018), and Avengers: Endgame (2019), as well as in numerous other MCU films and series.

History within the franchise

The founding members — Tony Stark (Iron Man), Steve Rogers (Captain America), Thor, Bruce Banner (Hulk), Natasha Romanoff (Black Widow), and Clint Barton (Hawkeye) — first unite to stop Loki and his Chitauri invasion of New York in the Battle of New York. The roster expands in Age of Ultron with Wanda Maximoff, Vision, Sam Wilson, and James Rhodes. The team fractures over the Sokovia Accords in Captain America: Civil War (2016), leaving it divided when Thanos attacks in Infinity War; after Thanos's Snap kills half of all life, the surviving Avengers, joined by Carol Danvers, Scott Lang, and Nebula, execute the "Time Heist" in Endgame, reverse the Snap, and defeat Thanos at the cost of Stark's and Romanoff's lives, while Rogers retires.

Following Endgame, the team's status is left uncertain in-universe, with individual heroes operating independently or in new groups, while the franchise builds toward new Avengers films in the Multiverse Saga, including Avengers: Doomsday (2026) and Avengers: Secret Wars (2027).

Reception

The Avengers films are among the highest-grossing of all time; Endgame briefly became the highest-grossing film ever released. Critics have praised the team's ensemble dynamics as the centerpiece of the MCU's crossover storytelling model.
