The Zollverein Coal Mine Industrial Complex (German: Zeche Zollverein) is a large former industrial site in Essen, in Germany's Ruhr region, long known as the "world's most beautiful coal mine". Named after the German customs union (the Zollverein), the colliery operated from the mid-19th century until 1986 and epitomised the heavy industry of the Ruhr.

Shaft 12

The complex is dominated by Shaft 12, built between 1928 and 1932 to designs by Fritz Schupp and Martin Kremmer. Rationally planned in the Bauhaus-influenced New Objectivity style, it centralised the mine's entire extraction on one site and was regarded as the most modern and architecturally ambitious colliery in Europe — its symmetrical steel-frame halls and iconic double A-frame winding tower remain landmarks of industrial architecture.

After closure

Saved from demolition, the site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2001. It now houses the Ruhr Museum, the Red Dot Design Museum (installed in the former boiler house, refitted by Norman Foster), cultural venues, studios and parkland laid out over the former works. Zollverein has become a symbol of the Ruhr's transition from coal and steel to culture and services, and an anchor point of the European Route of Industrial Heritage.
