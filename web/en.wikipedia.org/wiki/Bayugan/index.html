Bayugan, officially the City of Bayugan, is a component city in the province of Agusan del Sur in the Caraga region of Mindanao, Philippines. It is the only city in the province and the third most populous settlement in Caraga, serving as a commercial and agricultural centre for the Agusan valley.

History

The name is said to derive from the Manobo word bayug, a kind of tree once abundant in the area. The settlement began as a barrio of Esperanza populated by Manobo people and later by migrants from the Visayas and Luzon. Bayugan became a municipality in 1961 under an executive order, and its status was confirmed by Republic Act in 1967. It was converted into a city in 2007; like several other new Philippine cities, its cityhood was contested in the courts before the Supreme Court finally upheld it in 2011.

Geography and economy

Bayugan lies along the Pan-Philippine Highway (Maharlika Highway) and is a gateway between Butuan and the interior of Agusan del Sur. Its flat alluvial lands are intensively farmed: rice is the principal crop, alongside corn, coconut, abaca, rubber, banana and oil palm. The city celebrates the Kahimunan Festival every January in honour of its patron, the Santo Niño, giving thanks for the harvest.

Administration

The city is subdivided into 43 barangays, including the poblacion districts of Taglatawan, Poblacion itself and several large rural barangays along the highway. Bayugan belongs to the first legislative district of Agusan del Sur.
