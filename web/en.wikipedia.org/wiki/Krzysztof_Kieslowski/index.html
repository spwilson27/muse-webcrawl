Krzysztof Kieślowski (27 June 1941 – 13 March 1996) was a Polish film director and screenwriter, widely regarded as one of the most influential European filmmakers of the late 20th century. His films explore moral dilemmas, chance, fate, and the inner lives of ordinary people, moving from the social documentaries of communist-era Poland to metaphysical features made in France.

Career

After several rejections, Kieślowski graduated from the Łódź Film School in 1968. He first made documentaries for Polish television, such as Workers '71 and From the Point of View of a Night Porter, before turning to fiction with features including The Scar (1976), Camera Buff (1979), Blind Chance (completed 1981, released 1987), and No End (1985), the start of his collaboration with screenwriter Krzysztof Piesiewicz and composer Zbigniew Preisner.

Dekalog and international success

His ten-part television series Dekalog (1988), loosely inspired by the Ten Commandments and set in a Warsaw housing estate, won worldwide acclaim; two episodes were expanded into the features A Short Film About Killing and A Short Film About Love. The Double Life of Véronique (1991) and the Three Colours trilogy — Blue (1993), White (1994), and Red (1994), themed on liberty, equality, and fraternity — brought major festival prizes, including the Golden Lion at Venice and the Silver Bear at Berlin, and an Academy Award nomination for Best Director for Red.

Death and legacy

Kieślowski announced his retirement after Red and died of a heart attack in 1996 following cardiac surgery. His spare visual style, moral seriousness, and use of coincidence profoundly influenced later filmmakers, and his films regularly appear on critics' lists of the greatest ever made.
