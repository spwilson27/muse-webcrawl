The Champ de Mars massacre took place on 17 July 1791 in Paris at the Champ de Mars during the French Revolution. A crowd of protesters, republicans and others, had gathered to sign a petition demanding the deposition — and effectively the abolition of the position — of King Louis XVI. When the National Guard under the Marquis de Lafayette opened fire on the crowd, between twelve and fifty people were killed. The massacre was a key turning point that deepened the split between moderate and radical revolutionaries.

Background

In June 1791, Louis XVI and his family attempted to flee Paris in the Flight to Varennes, but were captured and returned. The National Assembly provisionally suspended the king, then reinstated him after he accepted the new constitution. Republican agitation grew: the Cordeliers club organized a petition calling for the king's abdication, to be signed on the altar of the fatherland at the Champ de Mars on 17 July 1791.

The massacre

Tens of thousands gathered to sign the petition. After two men found hiding under the altar platform were lynched by the crowd, the municipal authorities proclaimed martial law. Mayor Jean Sylvain Bailly ordered the crowd to disperse; Lafayette's National Guard arrived with the red flag of martial law. After stones were thrown and a shot was fired at the troops, the Guards fired volleys into the crowd, killing dozens.

Aftermath

The authorities cracked down on the radical movement: the Cordeliers club was closed for a time, and hundreds of protesters were arrested. Danton fled to England and Marat went into hiding. Bailly and Lafayette's reputations were badly damaged; Bailly was guillotined in November 1793. The massacre convinced many that the monarchy could not be reconciled with the Revolution, fuelling the movement that overthrew it in August 1792.
