Kazakh cuisine

Beshbarmak, widely regarded as the national dish of Kazakhstan.

Kazakh cuisine is the traditional food of the Kazakh people of Kazakhstan and neighbouring regions of Central Asia. Rooted in the nomadic pastoral lifestyle of the steppe, it centres on meat — especially mutton, beef, horse and camel — and on dairy products made from sheep's, mare's, cow's and camel's milk.

Main dishes

  • Beshbarmak — boiled meat served over large flat noodles with onion broth; the national dish, traditionally eaten with the hands.
  • Kazy — sausage made from horse rib meat stuffed into intestines.
  • Shashlik — skewered, grilled marinated meat, common across Central Asia.
  • Manti — steamed dumplings filled with minced meat.
  • Baursaki — fried dough pieces served with tea.
  • Laghman — hand-pulled noodles, shared with Uyghur and Dungan cuisines.

Drinks

Traditional beverages include kumys (fermented mare's milk), shubat (fermented camel's milk) and black tea, often served with milk. Dairy products such as kurt (dried salty cheese balls) and irimshik (hard cheese) were essential portable foods for nomads.

Customs

Hospitality (qonaqasy) is central to Kazakh culture. Guests are served the best portions; at a feast the head of a sheep may be presented to an honoured guest, who carves and distributes parts of it according to custom.
