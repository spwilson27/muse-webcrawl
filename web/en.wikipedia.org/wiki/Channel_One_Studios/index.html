Channel One Studios

Channel One is a recording studio in Kingston, Jamaica, best known for the distinctive reggae and dub records produced there in the 1970s and early 1980s, and for the Channel One record label. The studio's drum-and-bass sound, created by drummer Sly Dunbar and bassist Robbie Shakespeare of the Revolutionaries, was central to the development of the "rockers" style of reggae.

History

Channel One was established in 1972 at 29 Maxfield Avenue in Kingston by the four Hoo Kim brothers — Joseph ("Jo Jo"), Ernest, Paul and Kenneth — whose parents ran an ice-cream parlour and jukebox business. Joseph Hoo Kim, the eldest, had worked in the jukebox and slot-machine trade and decided to enter record production, buying an API mixing console and building a 16-track studio, at the time the most advanced in Jamaica.

The studio's first major hit was "Right Time" by the Mighty Diamonds (1975). With house band the Revolutionaries — anchored by Sly Dunbar and Robbie Shakespeare, with musicians such as keyboardist Ansel Collins and guitarist Rad Bryan — Channel One produced a stream of influential records for artists including the Mighty Diamonds, Black Uhuru, Israel Vibration, John Holt, Leroy Smart, the Wailing Souls and Dillinger, whose "CB 200" and "Cokane in My Brain" became international hits.

Sound and legacy

The "Channel One sound" featured Dunbar's hard, syncopated drumming — including the rimshot-heavy rockers beat and later the influence of drum machines — Shakespeare's melodic basslines, and spacious mixes often passed to dub mixer Scientist (Hopeton Brown), whose celebrated dub albums of the early 1980s, cut at Channel One rhythms, were released by Greensleeves Records. Ernest Hoo Kim served as the studio's principal engineer during its classic period.

The studio's fortunes declined in the mid-1980s as digital dancehall production shifted work elsewhere and after Kenneth Hoo Kim was killed in a shooting. The Channel One label has continued to reissue its catalogue, which remains one of the most important bodies of recorded reggae.
