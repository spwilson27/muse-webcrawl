Plantar fascia

The plantar fascia (plantar aponeurosis) spans the sole of the foot from heel to toes.

The plantar fascia, also called the plantar aponeurosis, is the thick connective tissue (fascia) which supports the arch on the bottom (plantar side) of the foot. It runs from the tuberosity of the calcaneus (heel bone) forward to the heads of the metatarsal bones (the bone between each toe and the bones of the mid-foot).

Structure

The plantar fascia is a broad band of dense connective tissue, mostly collagen, divided into central, medial and lateral parts. The thick central portion attaches proximally to the medial process of the calcaneal tuberosity and distally divides into five processes, one for each toe, blending with the flexor tendon sheaths and the skin of the sole. Together with the intrinsic foot muscles and the spring ligament, it acts as a tie-rod for the longitudinal arch: tension in the fascia increases as the toes dorsiflex during walking (the "windlass mechanism"), stiffening the foot for push-off.

Clinical significance

Inflammation or degeneration of the plantar fascia, plantar fasciitis, is one of the most common causes of heel pain, estimated to affect about one in ten people at some point in their lives. It typically causes stabbing pain near the heel that is worst with the first steps in the morning. Risk factors include prolonged standing, running, obesity, and limited ankle dorsiflexion. Most cases resolve with conservative treatment: rest, stretching of the fascia and calf, footwear changes, orthoses, and night splints. A plantar heel spur is a common accompanying radiographic finding, though it is often incidental.
