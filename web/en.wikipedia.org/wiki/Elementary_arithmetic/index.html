Elementary arithmetic

Elementary arithmetic is the simplified portion of arithmetic that includes the operations of addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. It is typically taught in elementary school and provides the foundation for later study of mathematics.

Numerals and place value

Elementary arithmetic begins with the natural numbers and the written symbols (numerals) that represent them. The Hindu–Arabic numeral system uses ten digits (0 through 9) and a place-value structure in which the position of a digit determines its value.

Addition and subtraction

Addition combines two or more numbers into a sum. Subtraction is the inverse operation, finding the difference between two numbers. Students learn algorithms for adding and subtracting multi-digit numbers, including carrying and borrowing (regrouping).

Multiplication and division

Multiplication can be thought of as repeated addition; students typically memorize the multiplication table up to 10 × 10 or 12 × 12. Division is the inverse of multiplication, and the long division algorithm is a standard part of the elementary curriculum.

Fractions and decimals

Elementary arithmetic also introduces fractions, decimals, and percentages, along with basic operations on them, preparing students for algebra and higher mathematics.
