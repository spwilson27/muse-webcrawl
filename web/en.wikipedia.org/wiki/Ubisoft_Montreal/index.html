Ubisoft Montreal (legally Ubisoft Divertissements Inc.) is a Canadian video game developer based in Montreal and a studio of French video game publisher Ubisoft. Founded in April 1997, it is one of the largest video game studios in the world, employing more than 4,000 people, and is best known as the lead developer of the Assassin's Creed, Far Cry, Watch Dogs, and Tom Clancy's Rainbow Six franchises.

The Ubisoft Montreal studio in the Mile End district.

History

The studio was established in 1997 as part of Ubisoft's expansion into North America, helped by subsidies and incentives from the Government of Quebec designed to create multimedia jobs. The studio initially worked on children's games before achieving its breakthrough with Tom Clancy's Splinter Cell (2002) and Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time (2003).

In 2007 the studio released the first Assassin's Creed, which became one of Ubisoft's flagship franchises. Ubisoft Montreal went on to lead development of Far Cry 3 (2012), Watch Dogs (2014), For Honor (2017), and numerous Assassin's Creed entries including Origins, Odyssey, and Valhalla (with support from other Ubisoft studios).
