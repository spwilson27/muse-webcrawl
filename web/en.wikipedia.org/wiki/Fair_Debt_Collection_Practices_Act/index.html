Fair Debt Collection Practices Act

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA), Pub. L. 95-109; 91 Stat. 874, codified as 15 U.S.C. § 1692 through 1692p, is a United States federal law enacted in 1977 that protects consumers from abusive, deceptive, and unfair debt collection practices. It governs the conduct of third-party debt collectors who regularly collect debts owed to others.

Enacted1977 (effective March 20, 1978)
Codification15 U.S.C. §§ 1692–1692p
Administered byConsumer Financial Protection Bureau; Federal Trade Commission

Prohibited practices

The FDCPA prohibits debt collectors from, among other things: calling consumers before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m.; contacting consumers at work if the collector knows the employer disapproves; using threats of violence, obscene language, or repeated harassing calls; falsely implying they are attorneys or government officials; misrepresenting the amount owed; and threatening arrest or legal action they do not intend to take. Collectors generally may not discuss a consumer's debt with third parties such as family members or employers.

Consumer rights

Within five days of first contact, a collector must send a validation notice stating the amount owed and the creditor's name. The consumer has 30 days to dispute the debt in writing, after which collection must pause until the debt is verified. A consumer may also demand in writing that the collector cease further communications. Consumers injured by violations may sue within one year for actual damages, statutory damages of up to $1,000, and attorney's fees; class actions carry additional statutory damages.

Scope and enforcement

The Act applies to "debt collectors" collecting consumer debts — debts incurred primarily for personal, family, or household purposes — and generally does not cover original creditors collecting their own debts, though some state laws (such as California's Rosenthal Act) extend similar rules to creditors. Enforcement rests with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and the Federal Trade Commission, and the CFPB's Regulation F, effective November 30, 2021, implemented the Act for modern communications including email and text messages.
