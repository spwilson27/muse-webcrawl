Paresthesia

Paresthesia is an abnormal sensation of the skin — tingling, pricking, chilling, burning or numbness — with no apparent physical cause. It is most familiar as the "pins and needles" feeling experienced when pressure on a nerve temporarily interrupts blood supply to a limb, which is then said to have "fallen asleep".

Transient paresthesia

Brief paresthesias are usually caused by temporary pressure on a nerve or brief hyperventilation, and resolve once the pressure is relieved. The tingling intensifies as circulation returns and then fades within minutes.

Chronic paresthesia

Persistent paresthesia may indicate underlying nerve damage (peripheral neuropathy) from causes such as diabetes, vitamin B12 deficiency, alcohol use, chemotherapy, carpal tunnel syndrome, multiple sclerosis or stroke. Diagnosis involves neurological examination and, where needed, nerve conduction studies.

Treatment

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause. Symptomatic relief may include medications used for neuropathic pain, physical therapy and management of contributing metabolic conditions.
