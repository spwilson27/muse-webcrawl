Bülent Ecevit

Mustafa Bülent Ecevit (28 May 1925 – 5 November 2006) was a Turkish politician, statesman, poet, writer, scholar and journalist who served as the Prime Minister of Turkey four times between 1974 and 2002. He was leader of the Republican People's Party (CHP) from 1972 to 1980 and of the Democratic Left Party (DSP) from 1987 until his death.

Early life

Ecevit was educated at Robert College in Istanbul and later studied at SOAS in London and Harvard University. He worked as a journalist and as press attaché, and published poetry and translations of T. S. Eliot and Rabindranath Tagore.

Political career

First elected to parliament in 1957, Ecevit became Minister of Labour in the 1960s and moved the CHP toward democratic socialism under the slogan "left of centre". As Prime Minister in 1974 he ordered the Turkish intervention in Cyprus following the Greek-backed coup, which made him a national hero at home. His later governments struggled with economic crisis and political violence; after the 1980 coup he was banned from politics for a decade.

Final premiership

Returning as Prime Minister in 1999 at the head of a coalition, Ecevit oversaw the capture of PKK leader Abdullah Öcalan, Turkey's recognition as an EU candidate, and the severe 2001 financial crisis. Ill health forced his withdrawal after the 2002 election. He died in Ankara in 2006 and was given a state funeral.
