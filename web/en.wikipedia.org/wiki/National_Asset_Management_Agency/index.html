National Asset Management Agency (NAMA)
Type: State agency
Founded: 2009
Location: Dublin, Ireland
Parent: Government of Ireland

The National Asset Management Agency (NAMA) is a state agency of the Government of Ireland, created in late 2009 as part of the response to the Irish financial crisis. NAMA functioned as a "bad bank", acquiring property development loans from Irish banks in return for government bonds. Its purpose was to remove risky assets from the banks' balance sheets, restore their capacity to lend, and maximise the value recovered for the Irish State.

Background

Following the collapse of the Irish property bubble in 2008, Irish banks were left heavily exposed to loans made to property developers. The government established NAMA under the National Asset Management Agency Act 2009, and the agency began acquiring loans in 2010.

Operations

NAMA acquired loans with a nominal value of about €74 billion for approximately €31.8 billion, applying substantial discounts ("haircuts") to reflect the impaired value of the underlying assets. It managed a large portfolio of property and development assets in Ireland, the United Kingdom and elsewhere, selling assets over time and using the proceeds to redeem its debt.

Wind-down

NAMA redeemed its senior debt ahead of schedule and returned a surplus to the State. Having substantially completed its mandate, the agency was wound down, with its remaining activities and assets transferred as its operations concluded in the mid-2020s.
