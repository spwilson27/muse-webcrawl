Mine detector

A mine detector is a device, typically a specialized metal detector, used to locate buried land mines and unexploded ordnance by detecting the metallic components of a mine or by sensing disturbances in the soil. Handheld mine detectors consist of a search head on a pole, electronics in a control unit, and audio output to headphones, allowing a trained operator to pinpoint metal objects a short distance below the surface.

History

The first practical portable mine detector was developed in the winter of 1941–42 by Polish officer Józef Kosacki, serving with Polish forces in Britain. His design doubled the speed of minefield clearance and was used by British forces from the Second Battle of El Alamein onward; remarkably, Kosacki never patented it, giving it freely to the war effort. Versions of the basic design remained in service for decades.

Challenges and technology

Modern minimum-metal mines contain very little metal, making detection difficult, and battlefields are littered with metallic fragments that generate many false alarms — deminers may excavate hundreds of harmless fragments for every mine found. Newer systems combine electromagnetic induction with ground-penetrating radar in dual-sensor detectors, which substantially reduce false alarm rates. Vehicle-mounted and robotic detection systems are used for route clearance and area reduction.
