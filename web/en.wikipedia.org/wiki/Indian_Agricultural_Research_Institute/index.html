The Indian Agricultural Research Institute (IARI), commonly known as the Pusa Institute, is India's national institute for agricultural research, education, and extension. Located in New Delhi, it is administered by the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) and was responsible for the research leading to the "Green Revolution in India" of the 1970s.

History

The institute was established in 1905 at Pusa, Bihar, with a generous grant from the American philanthropist Henry Phipps. It was originally called the Agricultural Research Institute. After a devastating earthquake damaged the buildings at Pusa in 1934, the institute was shifted to New Delhi in 1936; the new campus neighbourhood retains the name Pusa. In 1958 the institute was granted the status of a deemed university.

Research and the Green Revolution

IARI scientists, including plant breeder B. P. Pal and, in the 1960s, the geneticist M. S. Swaminathan, developed high-yielding dwarf varieties of wheat adapted from Norman Borlaug's Mexican lines, along with improved varieties of rice, maize, millets, and pulses. These varieties, combined with expanded irrigation and fertiliser use, transformed India from a food-importing country into one self-sufficient in grain. The institute's postgraduate school, established in 1958, has trained generations of Indian agricultural scientists.

Organisation

IARI has a number of disciplinary divisions — including agronomy, genetics, plant pathology, entomology, soil science, and agricultural extension — together with regional stations across India. Its campus in west Delhi covers experimental farms, laboratories, and the institute's central library, one of the largest agricultural libraries in Asia.
