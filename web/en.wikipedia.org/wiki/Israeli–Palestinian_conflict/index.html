The Israeli–Palestinian conflict is an ongoing political and armed conflict over land and self-determination in the Levant. It began in the mid-20th century, growing out of competing national movements—Zionism and Palestinian nationalism—in the territory of Mandatory Palestine under British rule.

The 1947 UN Partition Plan proposed separate Jewish and Arab states; its rejection by Arab leaders led to the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, Israel's establishment, and the displacement of over 700,000 Palestinians in what Palestinians call the Nakba ("catastrophe"). In the 1967 Six-Day War, Israel occupied the West Bank, Gaza Strip, East Jerusalem, the Golan Heights, and the Sinai Peninsula (the latter returned to Egypt in 1982).

Peace process

The First Intifada (1987–1993) led to the Oslo Accords, under which Israel and the Palestine Liberation Organization recognized each other and created the Palestinian Authority with limited self-government. Final-status issues were left unresolved. The Second Intifada (2000–2005) brought renewed violence. Hamas won Palestinian legislative elections in 2006 and took control of Gaza in 2007, leading to an Israeli-Egyptian blockade and repeated wars in Gaza.

Recent escalation

On 7 October 2023, Hamas-led militants attacked southern Israel, killing about 1,200 people and taking roughly 250 hostages. Israel responded with a large-scale military campaign in Gaza causing tens of thousands of Palestinian deaths and widespread destruction, amid a severe humanitarian crisis. The war drew international legal proceedings and widespread protests, and the conflict's core disputes—borders, Jerusalem, Israeli settlements, Palestinian refugees' right of return, and security—remain unresolved.
