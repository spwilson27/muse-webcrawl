Damselfly

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Animalia
Phylum:Arthropoda
Class:Insecta
Order:Odonata
Suborder:Zygoptera

A damselfly at rest, holding its wings together above the body.

Damselflies are flying insects of the suborder Zygoptera in the order Odonata. They are similar to dragonflies (which constitute the other odonatan suborder, Anisoptera) but are smaller and have slimmer bodies. Most species fold the wings along, and parallel to, the body when at rest.

Description

The general body plan of a damselfly is similar to that of a dragonfly. The compound eyes are large but more widely separated than in dragonflies and relatively smaller. Above the eyes is the frons or forehead. Below this is the clypeus and below that the labrum, an extensible organ used in the capture of food. The top of the head bears three simple eyes (ocelli), which may measure light intensity, and tiny antennae that serve no olfactory function but may measure air speed.

Damselflies are found on every continent except Antarctica. They are associated with freshwater habitats: their nymphs are aquatic and live in ponds, lakes, streams and wetlands, where they prey on small invertebrates. Adults are weak fliers compared with dragonflies and typically stay close to the water.

Life cycle

Damselflies undergo incomplete metamorphosis. Females lay eggs in water, often on submerged vegetation or directly into plant tissue. The aquatic nymph (naiad) passes through a series of moults before climbing out of the water onto a plant stem and emerging as an adult. Adults feed on mosquitoes, midges and other small flying insects caught in flight.

Families

The suborder Zygoptera includes families such as Calopterygidae (demoiselles), Coenagrionidae (pond damsels), Lestidae (spreadwings), Platycnemididae (white-legged damselflies) and many others.
