Kansai region

The Kansai region (関西地方, Kansai-chihō), also called the Kinki region (近畿地方), lies in the southern-central part of Japan's main island, Honshū. It comprises the prefectures of Nara, Wakayama, Kyoto, Osaka, Hyōgo and Shiga, often also including Mie. Its population of about 22–23 million is concentrated in the Keihanshin metropolitan area of Osaka, Kyoto and Kobe, Japan's second-largest urban region.

History

Kansai is the historical heartland of Japan: the Yamato state arose in the Nara basin, Buddhism entered through its temples, and Japan's first permanent capitals, Nara (710) and Kyoto (794), stood here for over a millennium. Osaka grew into the nation's commercial "kitchen" during the Edo period, while Kobe opened as a treaty port in 1868. The name Kansai means "west of the barrier", referring to the toll barriers east of the old capital region that separated it from Kantō, "east of the barrier".

Culture

The region is famous for its distinct dialect, Kansai-ben, its comedy tradition of manzai centred on Osaka, and a cuisine featuring takoyaki, okonomiyaki and kitsune udon. Kansai people are stereotyped within Japan as humorous, direct and merchant-minded, in contrast to Kantō. Major attractions include Kyoto's temples, Himeji Castle, Nara's great Buddha, and Universal Studios Japan in Osaka, which hosted Expo 2025.

Economy

Kansai's economy, comparable in size to that of the Netherlands, combines Osaka's commerce and manufacturing, Kobe's port and heavy industry, and Kyoto's electronics and traditional crafts. Kansai International Airport, built on an artificial island in Osaka Bay, opened in 1994.
