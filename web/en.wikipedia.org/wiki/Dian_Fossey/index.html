Dian Fossey

Dian Fossey
Born: (1932-01-16) January 16, 1932, San Francisco, California, U.S.
Died: December 26, 1985 (aged 53), Virunga Mountains, Rwanda
Known for: Study and conservation of mountain gorillas

Dian Fossey (January 16, 1932 – December 26, 1985) was an American primatologist and conservationist known for undertaking an extensive study of mountain gorilla groups from 1966 until her murder in 1985. She studied them daily in the mountain forests of Rwanda, initially encouraged to work there by paleoanthropologist Louis Leakey.

Work in Africa

In 1967 Fossey founded the Karisoke Research Center in Volcanoes National Park, Rwanda, between Mount Karisimbi and Mount Bisoke. Living in isolation in the Virunga Mountains, she habituated gorilla groups to her presence, documented their social structure, vocalisations and individual personalities, and named and chronicled individual animals. Her favourite gorilla, Digit, was killed by poachers in 1977, an event that intensified her militant anti-poaching campaign; she founded the Digit Fund (now the Dian Fossey Gorilla Fund) to finance anti-poaching patrols.

Death and legacy

Fossey was murdered in her cabin at Karisoke on December 26, 1985; the case remains unsolved. Her book Gorillas in the Mist (1983), combining her scientific study with her personal story, was adapted into a 1988 film starring Sigourney Weaver. Fossey is regarded, along with Jane Goodall and Biruté Galdikas, as one of the foremost primatologists of the 20th century, and her work is credited with helping save the mountain gorilla from extinction.
