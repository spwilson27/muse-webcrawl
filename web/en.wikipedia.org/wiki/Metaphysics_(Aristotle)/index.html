The Metaphysics is one of the principal works of Aristotle and the source of the name of the branch of philosophy now called metaphysics. It is a compilation of fourteen books, likely assembled by later editors — traditionally Andronicus of Rhodes in the 1st century BC — from Aristotle's lectures and treatises. The title, ta meta ta physika ("the things after the Physics"), originally referred to the work's place in the arrangement of Aristotle's writings, not its subject matter.

Overview of the books

  • Book Alpha (I) — philosophy as the science of first causes and principles; a survey of predecessors.
  • Book Beta (III) — a catalogue of puzzles (aporiai) that metaphysics must solve.
  • Book Gamma (IV) — being qua being, and the principle of non-contradiction.
  • Book Delta (V) — a philosophical lexicon of key terms.
  • Book Epsilon (VI) — the divisions of theoretical science; accidental being.
  • Books Zeta, Eta, Theta (VII–IX) — substance (ousia), form and matter, actuality and potentiality; the core of the work.
  • Book Iota (X) — unity and opposites.
  • Books Mu and Nu (XIII–XIV) — criticism of Plato's theory of Forms and of mathematical objects.
  • Book Lambda (XII) — the unmoved mover and Aristotle's theology.

Central themes

Aristotle defines the subject as the study of "being qua being" — being in general, as opposed to the particular beings studied by the special sciences. Running through the work are the doctrines of the four causes (material, formal, efficient, final), the distinction between potentiality and actuality, and the analysis of substance as a compound of matter and form (hylomorphism). Book Lambda concludes that the motion of the heavens requires an eternal, immaterial first mover that moves as an object of thought and desire.

Influence

The Metaphysics profoundly shaped medieval philosophy in the Islamic world and Christian Europe, where commentators such as Avicenna, Averroes, and Thomas Aquinas built systems around it. Its concepts remain reference points in contemporary metaphysics and philosophy of science.
