The Catalogue of Life is an online database that provides an index of known species of animals, plants, fungi and microorganisms. It was created in 2001 as a partnership between the global Species 2000 consortium and the American Integrated Taxonomic Information System (ITIS). The catalogue aims to list every known species — more than two million in recent editions — assembling expert-curated taxonomic checklists from hundreds of contributing databases into a single classification.

Structure and use

For each species the catalogue gives its accepted scientific name, synonyms, classification from kingdom down to species, and references to the source database that manages that group. Annual and monthly editions are released; the data are freely available online and through application programming interfaces. The Catalogue of Life underpins other biodiversity infrastructures, serving as the taxonomic backbone for the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF), contributions to the Encyclopedia of Life, and occurrence and conservation databases. Because taxonomy is continually revised, the catalogue is a dynamic working checklist rather than a fixed authority, and gaps and disagreements among source databases are a continuing challenge.
