Empirical distribution function

In statistics, an empirical distribution function (or empirical cumulative distribution function, eCDF) is the distribution function associated with the empirical measure of a sample. Given n independent observations X1, …, Xn from a distribution with cumulative distribution function F(x), the empirical distribution function is the step function

F̂n(x) = (1/n) Σ 1{Xi ≤ x},

that is, the proportion of sample points less than or equal to x. It jumps by 1/n at each observation (or by a multiple of 1/n at tied values) and is a right-continuous staircase converging to the true distribution function.

Properties

The empirical distribution function is an unbiased and consistent estimator of F. The Glivenko–Cantelli theorem states that it converges uniformly to F almost surely, and the Donsker theorem describes its asymptotic fluctuations as a Brownian bridge. The maximum deviation between the empirical and a hypothesised distribution function is the test statistic of the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test.
