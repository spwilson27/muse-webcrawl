Treacle tart

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Treacle tart is a traditional British dessert. The earliest known recipe for the dessert is from English author Mary Jewry, in her cookbooks from the late 19th century. It is prepared using shortcrust pastry, with a thick filling made from golden syrup (also known as light treacle), breadcrumbs, and lemon juice or zest. A modern alternative recipe uses a biscuit (cookie) base instead of pastry.

Description

Despite its name, the classic treacle tart contains no dark treacle (molasses): in older British usage "treacle" referred to any syrup, and when golden syrup — invented in the 1880s by Abram Lyle's company — became widely available it became the standard filling. The breadcrumbs absorb the syrup as it bakes, giving a set, chewy, caramel-sweet filling balanced by lemon. The pastry case is often finished with a lattice of pastry strips across the top. Treacle tart is usually served hot with a jug of custard, or with clotted cream, whipped cream, or ice cream.

History and culture

Tarts of syrup and crumbs appear in late Victorian cookery books and became a staple of British school dinners and home baking through the twentieth century, valued because the ingredients were cheap and shelf-stable. It is famously the favourite dessert of Harry Potter in J. K. Rowling's novels — the first food he notices at the Hogwarts start-of-term feast — which introduced the dish to a global audience. Regional and family variants add grated apple, ginger, or a pinch of spice, and a related pudding uses suet pastry or a sponge topping with the same syrup base.
