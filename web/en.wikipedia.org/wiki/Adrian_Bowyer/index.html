Adrian Bowyer (born 1952 in London) is a British engineer and mathematician who was a senior lecturer in the Department of Mechanical Engineering at the University of Bath. He is best known as the inventor of the RepRap project, the first open-source, self-replicating 3D printer design, which helped to launch the modern desktop 3D-printing and maker movements.

Early life and education

Bowyer studied mechanical engineering and later completed a PhD in tribology at Imperial College London. Before joining the University of Bath he worked in research roles in industry and academia, developing interests in geometry, computation and manufacturing. He also co-founded research work in the area of geometric modelling and set-valued (voxel) representations of solid objects.

Career

At Bath, Bowyer's research covered tribology (the study of friction, wear and lubrication), geometric computing and the mathematics of manufacturing. His work on modelling solids using function representations influenced later design tools for additive manufacturing.

RepRap

In 2005 Bowyer founded the RepRap project (short for "replicating rapid prototyper") with the goal of creating a machine that could print most of its own plastic parts, so that owners could make copies for friends at low cost. The first design, called Darwin, was released openly in 2007, followed by Mendel (2009) and Prusa Mendel derivatives. Because the designs and software were released under the GNU General Public License, RepRap seeded a large ecosystem of derivative printers and companies, and is widely credited with driving the price of desktop 3D printers down from tens of thousands of dollars to a few hundred.

Bowyer has argued that self-replicating, liberally licensed manufacturing technology can have a "wealth without money" effect, spreading productive capacity in the same way that open-source software spread computing capability. He directed the RepRap project and its spin-out activities from Bath until his retirement from the university.
