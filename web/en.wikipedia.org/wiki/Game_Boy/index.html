The Game Boy is an 8-bit handheld game console developed and manufactured by Nintendo. Released in Japan on 21 April 1989 and later that year in North America, it was designed by Gunpei Yokoi's team, creators of the earlier Game & Watch handhelds. Despite a monochrome, backlight-free screen and modest hardware compared with colour rivals such as the Atari Lynx and Sega Game Gear, the Game Boy's long battery life, durability, low price and strong game library made it an enormous commercial success.

Hardware

The console uses a custom 8-bit processor running at about 4.19 MHz, 8 KB of RAM, and a 160 × 144 pixel reflective liquid-crystal display showing four shades. Games are stored on removable ROM cartridges. Four AA batteries provide roughly 15–30 hours of play. A link cable port enabled two-player games, a feature central to the success of the Pokémon games in the late 1990s.

Games

The Game Boy launched with titles including Super Mario Land and Tetris, the latter bundled with the console in North America and Europe and widely credited with driving its adoption across all ages. Its library of more than a thousand games includes The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening, Kirby's Dream Land, Metroid II, and the first-generation Pokémon games.

Legacy

Combined with its colour successor, the Game Boy Color (1998), the line sold about 118.7 million units, making it one of the best-selling game systems of all time. It established Nintendo's dominance of the handheld market for three decades and became a defining cultural icon of 1990s gaming.
