Hungry i

LocationJackson Street, San Francisco, California
TypeNightclub
Opened1950
Closed1970s (as a folk/comedy club)
OwnerEnrico Banducci

The hungry i was a nightclub in San Francisco, California, originally located in the North Beach neighbourhood and later at the International Hotel. Operated by impresario Enrico Banducci from 1950, it was one of the most important launching pads of the American folk music revival and of modern stand-up comedy. The name is usually explained as standing for "hungry intellectual", although Banducci gave varying accounts.

The club's bare brick wall became an iconic backdrop for stand-up comedy.

Performers

The club's intimate cellar, with its exposed brick wall, hosted Mort Sahl, whose topical monologues pioneered modern stand-up; Lenny Bruce; Dick Gregory; Phyllis Diller; Bill Cosby; and Woody Allen. Folk and popular acts included the Kingston Trio, who recorded live albums there, the Limeliters, Peter, Paul and Mary, Glenn Yarbrough, and Vince Guaraldi; Barbra Streisand appeared in 1963, early in her career. Many acts recorded "live at the hungry i" albums, which helped define the era's entertainment.

Later history

Banducci moved the club in the 1960s and eventually sold the name; the venue became a strip club, and the hungry i name survives on a Columbus Avenue strip club in North Beach. The original club's influence on American comedy and folk music is widely acknowledged, and it was chronicled in the 2013 documentary The hungry i Reunion.
