Makizushi (巻き寿司, "rolled sushi"), also known as makimono or norimaki, is a type of sushi in which sushi rice and various fillings are rolled up in a sheet of nori (dried seaweed) using a bamboo mat (makisu) and cut into bite-sized pieces. It is one of the most familiar forms of sushi outside Japan.

Types

  • Hosomaki — thin rolls, about 2.5 cm in diameter, usually with a single filling such as tuna (tekkamaki), cucumber (kappamaki) or fermented soybeans (nattōmaki).
  • Futomaki — thick rolls, 5–6 cm across, with several fillings chosen for colour and flavour; associated with the Ehōmaki eaten whole at Setsubun.
  • Uramaki — "inside-out" rolls, with rice on the outside and nori around the filling; the California roll is the classic example, developed in North America.
  • Temaki — hand rolls: a cone of nori filled with rice and ingredients, eaten uncut by hand.

Preparation

A sheet of nori is placed on the bamboo mat, covered with a thin layer of seasoned rice, and the fillings arranged in a line before the mat is used to roll and compress the cylinder. The roll is then sliced with a wet, sharp knife into six or eight pieces and served with pickled ginger, wasabi and soy sauce.
