Cream of tartar

Potassium bitartrate
IUPAC namePotassium hydrogen tartrate
FormulaKC4H5O6
AppearanceWhite crystalline powder
SourceWine-making sediment (argol)

Cream of tartar is the common name for potassium bitartrate (potassium hydrogen tartrate), the potassium acid salt of tartaric acid. It is a byproduct of winemaking: during fermentation, crude deposits called argol crystallize on the inside of wine barrels and casks, and purification of these deposits yields the fine white powder sold for culinary use. Its chemical formula is KC4H5O6.

Culinary uses

Cream of tartar is best known as a stabilizer for beaten egg whites: a small amount added before whipping increases the foam's stability and volume by lowering pH and strengthening the protein network, which helps prevent over-beating and weeping in meringues and soufflés. Combined with baking soda and a starch, it forms single-acting baking powder, releasing carbon dioxide when moistened and heated. It also prevents sugar syrups from crystallizing (acting as an inverting agent in candies and frostings), and gives snickerdoodle cookies their characteristic tang.

Other uses

Historically, potassium bitartrate was used in medicine as a laxative and diuretic, as a mordant in dyeing, and in electroplating baths. At home it is used in cleaning pastes for copper, brass, and aluminum. Rochelle salt (potassium sodium tartrate) and tartar emetic are related tartrate compounds.
