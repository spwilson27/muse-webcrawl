The Notitia Dignitatum ("Register of Offices") is a unique document of the late Roman Empire listing the empire's civil and military offices, their staffs, and the units of the late Roman army with their stations. Compiled around the turn of the 5th century AD, it is one of the most important sources for the administrative and military organisation of the late empire — though historians debate how far it describes reality rather than an official ideal.

Contents

The Notitia is divided into Eastern (partes Orientis) and Western (partes Occidentis) sections. For each it names, in order of precedence, the great officers of state — praetorian prefects, the urban prefect, masters of the soldiers (magistri militum), counts and dukes (comites and duces) commanding frontier forces — together with their subordinate staffs (officia) and, for the army, every legion, cavalry vexillation, ala and cohort with its posting. It also lists palatine officials, the imperial arms factories (fabricae), and the shield devices (insignia) of the regiments, illustrated in the manuscript tradition with hundreds of shield patterns.

Compilation and dating

The Eastern list reflects the situation about 395 AD, at the death of Theodosius I; the Western list was repeatedly updated, with its latest entries datable to the 420s (the era of Valentinian III), even as provinces such as Britain (abandoned after c. 410) continued to be listed — evidence that the document was a living office register of the imperial chancery, kept partly for prestige even when out of date.

Transmission

The original is lost. All surviving copies descend from the Carolingian-era Codex Spirensis, formerly at Speyer Cathedral and lost after the 17th century, which was itself a copy of the late antique original. Several illustrated Renaissance copies (in Oxford, Paris, Munich, Vienna and elsewhere) preserve the text and most of the illustrations. The standard modern edition is that of Otto Seeck (1876).

Significance

For Britain, Gaul, the Danube and the East alike, the Notitia provides the framework for reconstructing late Roman garrisons (for example, the commands of the Dux Britanniarum and the Count of the Saxon Shore) and is constantly compared with archaeology and with Ammianus Marcellinus. Its regiment lists underpin estimates of late Roman army size, and its colourful shield patterns are a key source for late Roman military iconography.
