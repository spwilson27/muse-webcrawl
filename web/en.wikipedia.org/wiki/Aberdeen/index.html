Aberdeen (Scottish Gaelic: Obar Dheathain) is a city in north-east Scotland, situated between the mouths of the rivers Dee and Don. It is Scotland's third most populous city and the centre of the Aberdeenshire region.

History

Aberdeen grew from two burghs — Old Aberdeen by the Don and New Aberdeen by the Dee — united in 1891. It became a major fishing, shipbuilding, textile and granite-quarrying centre; its silvery mica-flecked granite gave it the name "Granite City" and built landmarks such as Marischal College, among the world's largest granite buildings.

Oil era

North Sea oil from the 1970s made Aberdeen the "Oil Capital of Europe", transforming its economy and population, though it now works to transition toward offshore renewables.

Education

The University of Aberdeen (founded 1495) and Robert Gordon University anchor education; the city's two medieval universities episodes shaped its scholarly reputation.
