New World vultures are a group of scavenging birds of prey found in the Americas, comprising seven species in five genera, including the turkey vulture, black vulture, king vulture, condors and the California condor. They belong to the family Cathartidae.

Characteristics

New World vultures resemble the Old World vultures of Africa and Asia, but the resemblance results from convergent evolution rather than close relationship. Unlike most birds, several species — notably the turkey vulture — have a keen sense of smell used to locate carrion.

They have bald heads that stay clean while feeding, weak feet unsuited to carrying prey, and they often soar for hours on thermals with wings held in a shallow V. Many species urinate on their legs to cool themselves and disinfect them, a behaviour called urohidrosis.

Taxonomy and conservation

The taxonomic placement of Cathartidae has been debated; they were once associated with storks, but are now generally placed within or near the order Accipitriformes with hawks and eagles.

The California condor became extinct in the wild in 1987 and was reintroduced from captive breeding; it remains critically endangered. The Andean condor is also threatened. Like other scavengers, New World vultures provide an important ecosystem service by removing carcasses.
