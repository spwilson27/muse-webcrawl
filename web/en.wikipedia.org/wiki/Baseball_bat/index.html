Baseball bat

Wooden baseball bats.

A baseball bat is a smooth wooden or metal club used in the sport of baseball to hit the ball after it is thrown by the pitcher. By regulation it may be no more than 2.75 inches (7.0 cm) in diameter at the thickest part and no more than 42 inches (1.067 m) in length. Although historically bats weighing up to 3 pounds (1.4 kg) were used, today most bats weigh around 33 ounces (0.94 kg).

Construction

A bat consists of the barrel (the thick hitting end), the handle, the knob that prevents the bat sliding from the hands, and sometimes a cupped indentation in the barrel end. Professional wooden bats are traditionally made of white ash or hard maple; bamboo and composite-wood bats are also produced. Metal bats, introduced in the 1970s, are hollow aluminium or composite shells; they are standard in youth, school and amateur play but are banned in Major League Baseball and most professional leagues, where only solid one-piece wood (or approved composite wood) is permitted.

History

Nineteenth-century bats were often homemade, sometimes with flat hitting surfaces in the era when rules permitted them. By the 1880s manufacturers such as J. F. Hillerich (later Hillerich & Bradsby, maker of the Louisville Slugger, first produced for Pete Browning in 1884) industrialised production on lathes. The bat's shape standardised into the modern tapered round form, with individual players developing signature "models" distinguished by barrel profile, handle thickness and weight distribution, quantified by the "drop" (length minus weight).

Performance and rules

Metal bats outperform wood because their hollow construction concentrates mass distribution and produces a "trampoline effect"; amateur governing bodies therefore certify bats to standards such as BBCOR to keep performance near wood levels. Altering a bat — corking, for instance — is illegal in professional play.
