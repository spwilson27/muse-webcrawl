Henry III of France

Henry III (French: Henri III; 19 September 1551 – 2 August 1589) was King of France from 1574 until his assassination in 1589, and briefly King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1573 to 1575 as Henry of Valois. The last French king of the House of Valois, he reigned during the French Wars of Religion and was caught between the Catholic League, the Huguenots and his own government. His murder by a Dominican friar made him the first French king assassinated since the Merovingian era and extinguished the Valois line.

Early life and Poland

The fourth son of Henry II and Catherine de' Medici, Henry was his mother's favourite. As Duke of Anjou he won military fame against the Huguenots at Jarnac and Moncontour (1569). In 1573 he was elected king of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, but after scarcely a year he secretly fled Poland on learning of the death of his brother Charles IX, returning to France to take the throne.

Reign

Henry's reign saw successive wars of religion, the formation of the ultra-Catholic League under Henry of Guise, and the War of the Three Henrys (1587–89). After the Day of the Barricades (May 1588) drove him from Paris, Henry had the Duke and Cardinal of Guise assassinated at Blois in December 1588. He then allied with the Protestant Henry of Navarre to besiege League-held Paris. On 1 August 1589, at Saint-Cloud, the friar Jacques Clément stabbed the king, who died the next day, naming Navarre as his successor — the future Henry IV.

Legacy

Henry founded the Order of the Holy Spirit (1578), France's premier chivalric order, and was known for his court of favourites, the mignons. Long portrayed by League propaganda as weak and decadent, modern historians credit him with serious efforts at fiscal and administrative reform in impossible circumstances.
