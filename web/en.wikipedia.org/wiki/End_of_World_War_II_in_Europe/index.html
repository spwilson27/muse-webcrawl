End of World War II in Europe

The end of World War II in Europe occurred in May 1945. Following the suicide of Adolf Hitler on 30 April 1945 during the Battle of Berlin, Germany's unconditional surrender was authorised by his successor, Reichspräsident Karl Dönitz, on 7 May in Reims, France, and signed by representatives of the German High Command. A second signing took place in Berlin on 8 May 1945 at the insistence of the Soviet Union.

The signing of the German Instrument of Surrender, May 1945.

Background

By early 1945, Allied forces were advancing into Germany from both east and west. The Soviet Red Army captured Berlin after a fierce battle in April–May 1945, while Western Allied forces linked up with Soviet troops at the Elbe River on 25 April 1945, an event known as Elbe Day.

Surrender

The first Instrument of Surrender was signed at Reims on 7 May 1945 by General Alfred Jodl on behalf of the German High Command, in the presence of representatives of the United States, the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom and France. Because the Soviet representative considered the ceremony insufficient, a second, ratified signing was held in Berlin-Karlshorst late on 8 May, signed by Field Marshal Wilhelm Keitel. The surrender took effect at 23:01 Central European Time on 8 May 1945.

Commemoration

8 May is celebrated as Victory in Europe Day (VE Day) in many Western countries, while Russia and several other states commemorate Victory Day on 9 May, owing to the time difference when the surrender took effect. Fighting in some areas, such as the Courland Pocket and the Channel Islands, ended in the following days.
