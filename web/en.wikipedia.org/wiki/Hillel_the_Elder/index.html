Hillel the Elder

Hillel the Elder (c. 110 BCE – c. 10 CE) was a Jewish religious leader, sage, and scholar associated with the development of the Mishnah and the Talmud. He is traditionally regarded as the founder of the House of Hillel (Beit Hillel), whose interpretations of Jewish law were generally more lenient than those of the rival House of Shammai.

Teachings

Hillel is famous for his summary of the Torah: "What is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow: this is the whole Torah; the rest is commentary; go and learn." He is also credited with the saying, "If I am not for myself, who will be for me? And being only for myself, what am I? And if not now, when?" recorded in Pirkei Avot.

Life

According to tradition, Hillel was born in Babylonia and migrated to Jerusalem to study Torah, working as a woodcutter and day labourer. He became Nasi (president) of the Sanhedrin and is said to have lived to the age of 120.
