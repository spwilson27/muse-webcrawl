The opening sequence of The Simpsons is one of the most recognisable title sequences in television. First appearing with the series' second episode in January 1990, it introduces the Simpson family and the town of Springfield to the accompaniment of Danny Elfman's theme music, composed in 1989 in a style Elfman described as deliberately retro.

Description

The sequence zooms through the clouds to the show's title, then follows each family member home at the end of the school and work day: Bart writes lines on a classroom chalkboard, Homer leaves the nuclear power plant (with a rod of uranium down his back), Marge and Maggie pass through a supermarket checkout, and Lisa is expelled from band practice while playing an improvised saxophone solo. The family converges on their house, races for the sofa, and settles in front of the television.

Variable elements

Four elements change from episode to episode and have become famous in their own right: the chalkboard gag (the lines Bart writes), the couch gag (the family's arrival at the sofa, sometimes an elaborate set-piece), Lisa's saxophone solo, and the price shown on the supermarket register when Maggie is scanned — long displayed as $847.63, a figure said to be the monthly cost of raising a baby. A high-definition version of the sequence, introduced in 2009, added further variable elements, including the town sign and a billboard gag. Shortened and extended versions of the sequence are used depending on episode length, and elaborate guest-directed couch gags have been contributed by animators and artists including Banksy and Guillermo del Toro.
