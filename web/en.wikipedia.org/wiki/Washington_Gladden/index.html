Solomon Washington Gladden (1836–1918) was an American Congregational minister and a leading figure of the Social Gospel movement, which applied Christian ethics to the social problems of industrial society.

Career

Ordained in 1860, Gladden served pastorates in New York, Massachusetts and, most famously, at the First Congregational Church of Columbus, Ohio, from 1882 until his death in 1918. As a member of the Columbus city council he fought municipal corruption, and his church became a model of the "institutional church" offering social services to its community.

Thought and influence

In books such as Applied Christianity (1886) and Social Salvation (1902), Gladden argued that salvation was social as well as individual, supporting labour unions, municipal reform, civil rights and temperance while opposing both unrestrained capitalism and revolutionary socialism. He also wrote the popular hymn "O Master, Let Me Walk with Thee" (1879). A bridge figure between evangelicalism and progressive reform, he helped prepare American Protestantism for the social activism of the twentieth century.
