Rosenborg Castle

Rosenborg Castle (Danish: Rosenborg Slot) is a Renaissance castle located in Copenhagen, Denmark. Built by King Christian IV in the Dutch Renaissance style, it was originally a summer residence and is now a museum housing the Danish Crown Jewels and regalia.

Location: Copenhagen, Denmark
Built: 1606–1634
Architectural style: Dutch Renaissance
Owner: Danish state

History

Christian IV, one of Denmark's most celebrated monarchs, commissioned the castle in 1606 as a pleasure palace outside the city walls. Construction proceeded in stages; by 1624 the main building was complete, and a final expansion in 1633–1634 gave the castle essentially its present form. It served as a royal residence until around 1710, when Frederick IV abandoned it in favour of Frederiksberg Palace, though it was used twice as an emergency residence after fires at Christiansborg.

Collections

The castle museum, open to the public since 1838, displays the chronologically arranged collections of the Danish royal family from the 16th to the 19th century. Highlights include the Knights' Hall with its coronation thrones, silver lions, and tapestries depicting Christian V's victories in the Scanian War. The basement treasury holds the Danish Crown Jewels, including the crown of Christian IV and the crown of Queen Sophie Amalie, as well as the Rosenborg wine and a celebrated narwhal-tusk throne.

Gardens

The castle stands in Kongens Have (the King's Garden), Copenhagen's oldest royal garden and a popular public park, laid out in the early 17th century.
