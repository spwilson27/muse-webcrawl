Tulsidas

Goswami Tulsidas (c. 1532–1623) was an Indian Hindu saint and poet, renowned for his devotion to the deity Rama. He is best known as the author of the Ramcharitmanas, a retelling of the Sanskrit Ramayana in the Awadhi vernacular.

Life

Tradition holds that Tulsidas was born Rambola Dubey in Rajapur, in present-day Uttar Pradesh. After the death of his wife, he renounced worldly life and became an ascetic, spending much of his life in Varanasi and Ayodhya. He is credited with founding the Sankat Mochan Hanuman Temple in Varanasi.

Works

Besides the Ramcharitmanas (1574–1577), written largely at Varanasi, his works include Vinaya Patrika, Dohavali, Kavitavali, Gitavali, and the popular Hanuman Chalisa, a 40-verse hymn to Hanuman. By writing in Awadhi rather than Sanskrit, Tulsidas made the Rama story accessible to ordinary people; the Ramcharitmanas remains one of the most beloved texts of northern India.
