The Westminster Assembly of Divines was a council of theologians and members of the English Parliament appointed by the Long Parliament to restructure the Church of England. It met from 1643 to 1653 during the English Civil War, initially in the Henry VII Chapel of Westminster Abbey and later in the Jerusalem Chamber.

History

Parliament summoned the Assembly to advise on reforming church government, doctrine and worship, in the context of the Solemn League and Covenant with Scotland, which committed England to a Presbyterian settlement. Scottish commissioners, including Alexander Henderson and Samuel Rutherford, attended and influenced the debates. Members debated Presbyterian, Independent (congregational) and Erastian views of church government.

Documents

The Assembly produced the documents collectively known as the Westminster Standards: the Westminster Confession of Faith (1646), the Shorter Catechism and Larger Catechism (1647), the Directory for Public Worship, and forms of church government and discipline. The Confession and catechisms were adopted by the Church of Scotland and became foundational standards for Presbyterian churches worldwide.
