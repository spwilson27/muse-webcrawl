Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (born 27 October 1945), known mononymously as Lula, is a Brazilian politician who has served as president of Brazil since 2023, having previously held the office from 2003 to 2011. A former metalworker and trade union leader, he co-founded the Workers' Party (PT) in 1980.

Early life and union career

Born into poverty in Pernambuco, Lula migrated with his family to São Paulo as a child. Working in metalworking factories, he lost a finger in an industrial accident and rose through the metalworkers' union, leading the great ABC strikes of the late 1970s against Brazil's military regime — a movement that helped catalyse the country's return to democracy.

Presidencies

After three unsuccessful runs, Lula won the 2002 presidential election. His first two terms combined market-friendly macroeconomic policy with expanded social programmes — notably Bolsa Família — during a commodity boom, lifting millions from poverty; he left office in 2011 with record approval ratings. In 2017 he was convicted of corruption in the Operation Car Wash scandal and imprisoned in 2018; the convictions were annulled by the Supreme Federal Court in 2021 on grounds of improper jurisdiction and judicial bias. Lula defeated incumbent Jair Bolsonaro in the 2022 election and began his third term on 1 January 2023.
