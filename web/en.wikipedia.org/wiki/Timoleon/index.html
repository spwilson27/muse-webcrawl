Timoleon

Timoleon (c. 411 – 334 BC) was a Greek statesman and general of Corinth who became famous as a liberator of Sicily from tyranny and Carthaginian domination. His life is recorded by Plutarch in the Parallel Lives and by Cornelius Nepos.

Life

Timoleon was the brother of Timophanes, who made himself tyrant of Corinth; according to tradition Timoleon, a devoted opponent of tyranny, was complicit in or acquiesced to his brother's assassination. In 345/344 BC the Corinthians, responding to appeals from Syracuse, sent Timoleon with a small force to Sicily. Despite being heavily outnumbered, he defeated the tyrant Dionysius II and the Carthaginians, winning a decisive victory at the Battle of the Crimissus around 339 BC.

Legacy

After expelling tyrants from the Sicilian Greek cities, Timoleon restored democratic constitutions, rebuilt the island's prosperity, and then retired into private life at Syracuse, where he was honoured as a founder and hero. Plutarch presents him as an exemplar of disinterested virtue and good fortune.
