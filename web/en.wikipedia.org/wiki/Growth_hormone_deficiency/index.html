Growth hormone deficiency

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Growth hormone deficiency (GHD) is a medical condition in which the pituitary gland does not produce enough growth hormone (somatotropin). In children it results in slow growth and short stature; in adults it causes a syndrome of reduced muscle mass, increased body fat, low energy, and impaired quality of life. It may be congenital or acquired, and may occur in isolation or with deficiencies of other pituitary hormones.

Causes

Congenital GHD can result from genetic mutations affecting pituitary development or the growth hormone gene itself, or from structural abnormalities of the brain. Acquired causes include pituitary tumours (such as craniopharyngioma in children), head injury, infection, cranial irradiation, and infiltrative diseases. In many childhood cases no cause is found, and the deficiency is termed idiopathic.

Signs and symptoms

Children with GHD typically have normal birth length but grow slowly thereafter, falling progressively below their expected height percentiles while retaining normal body proportions. They often have a cherubic appearance with a prominent forehead and delayed tooth eruption. In adults, symptoms are subtler: central obesity, reduced bone density, fatigue, and adverse cardiovascular risk profiles.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis combines growth measurement, bone-age X-ray, blood tests for IGF-1, and provocative stimulation tests in which a stimulus (such as arginine, clonidine, or insulin-induced hypoglycaemia) is used to measure the pituitary's ability to release growth hormone. MRI of the pituitary is often performed.

Treatment is with recombinant human growth hormone, given by daily subcutaneous injection. Historically, hormone was extracted from cadaver pituitaries, a practice abandoned in the 1980s after cases of Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease. Early treatment in children can allow near-normal adult height. Long-acting weekly preparations have been introduced more recently.
