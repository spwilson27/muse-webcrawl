Journey is an American rock band formed in San Francisco in 1973 by former members of Santana and Frumious Bandersnatch. Initially a progressive jazz-rock group, the band achieved worldwide fame after vocalist Steve Perry joined in 1977, shifting to a melodic arena-rock sound that produced a string of hit singles and multi-platinum albums.

History

Founded by guitarist Neal Schon and keyboardist Gregg Rolie, both Santana alumni, Journey released three fusion-influenced albums with modest success. The arrival of Perry transformed their fortunes: Infinity (1978), Evolution (1979) and Departure (1980) yielded hits including "Wheel in the Sky", "Lovin', Touchin', Squeezin'" and "Any Way You Want It". Keyboardist Jonathan Cain replaced Rolie in 1981, and the album Escape (1981) became their biggest studio success, featuring "Don't Stop Believin'", "Who's Crying Now" and "Open Arms". Frontiers (1983) added "Separate Ways (Worlds Apart)" and "Faithfully".

The band went on hiatus in 1987; Perry's departure became permanent in 1998. Journey continued with other vocalists, most notably Arnel Pineda, discovered via YouTube in 2007. "Don't Stop Believin'" enjoyed a huge revival after its use in the finale of The Sopranos (2007) and in Glee, becoming one of the best-selling digital tracks of the twentieth century.

Legacy

Journey has sold roughly 80 million records worldwide and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2017.
