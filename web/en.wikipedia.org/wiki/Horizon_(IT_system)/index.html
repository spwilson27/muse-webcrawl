Horizon (IT system)

Horizon
DeveloperICL Pathway / Fujitsu
Introduced1999–2000
Used byPost Office Limited (UK)

Horizon is an electronic point-of-sale and accounting system developed by ICL Pathway, later part of Fujitsu, for Post Office Limited in the United Kingdom. Rolled out across the branch network from 1999, it handled transactions, stock, and branch accounts. Horizon became notorious because defects in the software produced phantom shortfalls in branch accounts, for which hundreds of subpostmasters were blamed, prosecuted, or ruined in what became known as the British Post Office scandal.

Development

Horizon began as a government project to computerise benefit payments and post office counters. After the benefits card element was cancelled in 1999, the Post Office adopted the system for branch automation under a private finance initiative contract with ICL. A successor version, Horizon Online, followed from 2010, and a further replacement programme has been under way since the 2020s.

The scandal

From the early 2000s, subpostmasters reported unexplained losses shown by Horizon. The Post Office insisted the system was robust and required operators to make good the shortfalls; between 1999 and 2015 more than 900 subpostmasters were prosecuted, many on evidence derived from Horizon data. Litigation led by Alan Bates culminated in a 2019 High Court judgment finding that bugs in Horizon could and did cause shortfalls. Criminal convictions were subsequently overturned in large numbers, a statutory public inquiry was established, and in 2024 Parliament passed legislation exonerating convicted subpostmasters en masse.
