Johannes Carolus (Hans) Clevers (born 27 March 1957) is a Dutch molecular geneticist and stem cell researcher. He is internationally recognised for his work on Wnt signalling, the identification of Lgr5 as a marker of adult stem cells, and the development of organoid technology — three-dimensional miniature organs grown from stem cells in the laboratory.

Career

Clevers studied medicine and biology at Utrecht University, obtaining his PhD in 1985. After postdoctoral work, including a period at Harvard Medical School, he became professor of molecular genetics at Utrecht and later director of the Hubrecht Institute for developmental biology and stem cell research. From 2012 to 2015 he served as president of the Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences. He subsequently became research director of the Princess Máxima Center for pediatric oncology in Utrecht, and in 2022 joined Roche as head of its Pharma Research and Early Development organisation while retaining academic ties.

Research

Clevers's group showed that TCF transcription factors are the nuclear effectors of the Wnt signalling pathway, whose disruption drives colorectal cancer. In 2007 his laboratory identified the protein Lgr5 as a marker of the actively dividing stem cells of the intestine. Building on this, in 2009 they reported that single Lgr5-positive stem cells could be grown in culture into self-organising "mini-guts" — the first organoids derived from adult stem cells. The method was extended to liver, pancreas, lung, and many other tissues, and to organoids grown from patients' tumour tissue for drug testing and personalised medicine.

Recognition

Clevers has received numerous honours, including the Spinoza Prize (2001), the Heineken Prize for Medicine (2012), and a Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences (2013). He is a member of the Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences and a foreign associate of the US National Academy of Sciences.
