Holy Week in the Philippines

Holy Week (Filipino: Mahal na Araw) in the Philippines is one of the most important religious observances in the predominantly Roman Catholic country, blending Spanish colonial Catholic traditions with local folk practices. It is marked by solemn processions, the chanting of the Pasyon, church visits, and in some places public acts of penitence including self-flagellation and ritual crucifixion.

Observances

Observance begins on Palm Sunday (Domingo de Ramos), when palm fronds (palaspás) are blessed and waved in imitation of Christ's entry into Jerusalem. During the week, many Filipinos read or chant the Pasyon, the epic narrative of Christ's life, Passion and death, in a tradition called pabasa that can continue day and night in homes and chapels. On Holy Thursday, Catholics practise Visita Iglesia, visiting seven (or fourteen) churches to pray. Maundy Thursday and Good Friday are national public holidays; much of the country effectively shuts down, and television and radio stations sign off or broadcast religious programming.

Good Friday traditions

Good Friday is the most solemn day. Parishes stage the Senakulo, traditional street plays of the Passion, and processions carry life-size, often centuries-old images of Christ, the Virgin Mary (as Our Lady of Sorrows) and saints on decorated carriages (carrozas). In San Pedro Cutud, San Fernando, Pampanga, devotees undergo actual crucifixion in the Cutud Lenten Rites, a practice the official Church discourages but does not prohibit. In Marinduque, the Moriones Festival features penitents in Roman soldier masks and costumes. Flagellant processions (magdarame) are held in several provinces as a form of panata (vow).

Easter Sunday

The week culminates before dawn on Easter Sunday with the Salubong (in some regions Encuentro), a ritual re-enactment of the meeting of the risen Christ and his mother, in which separate processions of men and women converge at the church as a child dressed as an angel removes the Virgin's black veil of mourning.
