Yuji Horii (堀井 雄二, Horii Yūji, born January 6, 1954) is a Japanese video game designer and scenario writer best known as the creator of the Dragon Quest series of role-playing video games. He has been credited with popularising the console role-playing game genre in Japan, and Dragon Quest is regarded as one of the most influential video game franchises of all time.

Early life and career

Horii was born in Sumoto, on Awaji Island in Hyogo Prefecture. He graduated from Waseda University's Department of Literature. Before entering the games industry he worked as a freelance writer for newspapers and magazines, and wrote manga. In 1982 he won a game programming contest sponsored by Enix with a tennis game, which led to his involvement with the company.

Horii created The Portopia Serial Murder Case (1983), a pioneering adventure game, before designing Dragon Quest (1986) for the Famicom, working with character designer Akira Toriyama and composer Koichi Sugiyama. The game's accessible design brought the role-playing genre to a mass audience in Japan.

Other works

Horii also wrote the scenario for Chrono Trigger (1995), developed by Square, which is frequently cited among the greatest video games ever made. He founded the development company Armor Project, through which he continues to oversee the Dragon Quest series, published by Square Enix. He also created the Itadaki Street board game video game series.
