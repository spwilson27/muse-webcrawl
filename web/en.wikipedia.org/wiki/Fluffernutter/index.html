Fluffernutter

A fluffernutter is a sandwich made with peanut butter and marshmallow creme, usually served on white bread. A New England, especially Massachusetts, childhood staple, it is sometimes called the “liberty sandwich”. The related term also describes other foods pairing peanut butter and marshmallow.

Fluffernutter
Type: Sandwich
Region: New England, USA
Main ingredients: Peanut butter, marshmallow creme, bread

History

Marshmallow creme was popularised by Archibald Query of Somerville, Massachusetts, who sold his recipe to Durkee-Mower in Lynn, makers of Marshmallow Fluff. A peanut-butter-and-marshmallow sandwich recipe appeared in print during World War I; Emma Curtis promoted a “Liberty Sandwich” in 1918. The name “fluffernutter” was coined in the 1960s by an advertising agency for Durkee-Mower.

Cultural status

Somerville hosts the annual “What the Fluff?” festival. In 2006 a Massachusetts legislator’s proposal to ban fluffernutters from school lunches provoked a defence of it as a state symbol; bills have proposed making it the official state sandwich. Variations add banana, bacon, or grilled preparation.
