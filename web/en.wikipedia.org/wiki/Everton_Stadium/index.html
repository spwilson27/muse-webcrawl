Everton Stadium is a football stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock in Vauxhall, Liverpool, England, built on the site of a former dock within the city's waterfront. It is the home ground of Everton Football Club, replacing Goodison Park, the club's home since 1892. The stadium, with a capacity of 52,888, opened for the start of the 2025–26 season. For sponsorship reasons during its first years it has also been known by a naming-rights title (the Hill Dickinson Stadium).

Development

Everton had explored leaving Goodison Park for decades, with earlier schemes at King's Dock and Kirkby failing to proceed. In 2017 the club selected the Bramley-Moore Dock site, part of the Liverpool Waters development area. The dock was infilled with sand dredged from the Irish Sea and the River Mersey as part of land reclamation works before construction of the stadium began in 2021; the main build was carried out by Laing O'Rourke. The brick, glass, and steel design, by architect Dan Meis and Pattern Design, was intended to evoke the maritime heritage of the docks, with a steep single-tier south stand designed to hold around 13,000 supporters.

Features

The stadium was built to be capable of hosting major international fixtures and was selected as a venue for UEFA Euro 2028. Its construction was criticised by heritage bodies because the works contributed to UNESCO's decision in 2021 to remove Liverpool's Maritime Mercantile City from the World Heritage List. Test events were held in early 2025 before the stadium's competitive opening in August 2025.

Transport

The stadium is served by Sandhills station on the Merseyrail Northern Line, with additional bus and walking routes along the waterfront to Liverpool city centre.
