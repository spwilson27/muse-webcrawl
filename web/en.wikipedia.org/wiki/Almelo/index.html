Almelo

Almelo (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈɑlməloː]) is a municipality and a city in the eastern Netherlands, in the province of Overijssel, in the region of Twente. It had a population of around 73,000 in recent years. Almelo lies on the borders of the Overijsselse Vecht river valley and is crossed by canals linking it to Zwolle, Deventer, and Germany via the Twentekanaal and the Almelo–De Haandrik canal.

History

Almelo developed around the Huis Almelo, the manor of the lords (later counts) of Almelo, whose family, the Van Rechteren Limpurgs, still own the estate. The settlement received city rights in 1394. For centuries its economy rested on linen weaving and the textile industry, which expanded greatly in the 19th century with steam-powered factories; the decline of textiles in the 20th century led to diversification into machinery, plastics, and high-tech manufacturing.

Transport and sport

Almelo has a main railway station with connections to Zwolle, Deventer, Enschede, and Hengelo, and lies near the A1 and A35 motorways. The city is home to the professional football club Heracles Almelo, which plays at the Erve Asito stadium.

Landmarks

Points of interest include the Huis Almelo estate and its park, the late-Gothic Grote Kerk (St. George's Church), the Bolletje bakery museum and factory shop, and the Stadsmuseum Almelo. Several windmills and historic warehouses survive in and around the centre.
