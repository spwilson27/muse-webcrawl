Shivkumar Sharma (13 January 1938 – 10 May 2022) was an Indian classical musician and santoor player, credited with adapting the santoor — a hammered dulcimer traditionally associated with the folk music of Jammu and Kashmir — into a respected instrument of Hindustani classical music. He is widely honoured with the title Pandit.

Early life and training

Sharma was born in Jammu into a musical family; his father, Uma Dutt Sharma, a vocalist and tabla player, recognised the potential of the santoor and trained him in vocals and tabla before introducing him to the instrument at about the age of thirteen. The santoor, with its many strings struck by light wooden mallets, presented formidable difficulties for rendering the slides (meend) central to raga music, and Sharma modified both the instrument's tuning and his technique to sustain and bend notes.

Career

Sharma gave his first major public performance in Bombay in 1955. His 1967 album Call of the Valley, recorded with flautist Hariprasad Chaurasia and guitarist Brij Bhushan Kabra, became one of the best-loved and best-selling Indian classical recordings, evoking a day in Kashmir through ragas. He performed worldwide for over six decades and trained numerous disciples, including his son Rahul Sharma, who also became a noted santoor player.

With Hariprasad Chaurasia, as the composing duo Shiv–Hari, he wrote music for successful Hindi films including Silsila (1981), Chandni (1989), Lamhe (1991) and Darr (1993).

Awards

Sharma received the Sangeet Natak Akademi Award in 1986, the Padma Shri in 1991, and the Padma Vibhushan, India's second-highest civilian honour, in 2001. He died of cardiac arrest in Mumbai on 10 May 2022, aged 84.
