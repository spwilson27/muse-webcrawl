Satoshi Ōmura

Satoshi Ōmura (大村 智, born 12 July 1935) is a Japanese biochemist known for the discovery and development of hundreds of pharmaceuticals originally occurring in microorganisms. He shared the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with William C. Campbell for their work on avermectin and its derivative ivermectin, a therapy that dramatically reduced river blindness and lymphatic filariasis. The prize was shared with Tu Youyou, recognised for artemisinin.

Career and discoveries

Ōmura grew up in Yamanashi Prefecture and studied at the University of Yamanashi and the Tokyo University of Science, earning doctorates in pharmaceutical science and chemistry. He built his career at the Kitasato Institute in Tokyo, developing methods to culture and screen soil microorganisms for useful compounds, and pioneered collaborations between academia and industry, notably with Merck & Co.

In the 1970s his group isolated a Streptomyces culture from soil near a golf course in Shizuoka that produced the avermectins; Campbell showed their potency against parasitic worms. The derivative ivermectin (Mectizan) was donated free by Merck for the treatment of onchocerciasis from 1987, protecting hundreds of millions of people in Africa and Latin America, and is on the WHO list of essential medicines. Ōmura's teams also discovered or developed many other agents, including staurosporine and cerulenin, widely used as research tools.

Honours

Besides the Nobel Prize, Ōmura received the Japan Academy Prize (1990), the Order of Culture (2012), and the Canada Gairdner Global Health Award. He is also an accomplished artist and benefactor of art in his home prefecture.
