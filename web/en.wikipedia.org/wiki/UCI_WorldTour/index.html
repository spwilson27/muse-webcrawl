UCI WorldTour

The UCI WorldTour is the premier annual series of elite men's road cycling races, organised by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI). Introduced in its current form in 2011, merging the former UCI ProTour and the historical world rankings calendar, the WorldTour brings together the sport's most prestigious events — the three Grand Tours, the five Monuments, and other leading stage races and one-day classics.

Structure

The calendar comprises roughly 35 races held mainly in Europe, with events also in Australia, Canada, China and the Middle East. The highest-ranked teams, licensed as UCI WorldTeams (18 teams in recent seasons), are automatically invited — and generally obliged — to enter every WorldTour race, guaranteeing that the best riders compete against each other throughout the season. Riders and teams accumulate points in the UCI world rankings based on results.

Principal events

The Grand Tours — the Tour de France, Giro d'Italia and Vuelta a España — headline the calendar, alongside the Monuments: Milan–San Remo, the Tour of Flanders, Paris–Roubaix, Liège–Bastogne–Liège and Il Lombardia. Other major races include Paris–Nice, Tirreno–Adriatico, the Critérium du Dauphiné, the Tour de Suisse, the Amstel Gold Race, La Flèche Wallonne, the Clásica de San Sebastián, and the Tour de Pologne.

History

The WorldTour succeeded the UCI ProTour (2005–2010), a period marked by conflict between the UCI and the organisers of the Grand Tours, notably ASO. The 2011 merger produced a unified ranking, and reforms from 2017 expanded the calendar with new races. A women's equivalent, the UCI Women's WorldTour, was launched in 2016.
