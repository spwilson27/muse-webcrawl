Yama (Sanskrit: यम, romanized: Yama) is the Hindu deity of death and justice, responsible for the dispensation of law and punishment of sinners in his abode of Yamaloka. He is often identified with Dharmadeva, the god of dharma, and is regarded as the first mortal to die, who blazed the path of mortality down which all humans have since followed. He is the father of Yudhishthira of the Mahabharata and the elder brother of the river goddess Yamuna.

Origins and mythology

Yama appears in the Rigveda as the son of the sun god Vivasvant (Surya) and Saranyu. Together with his twin sister Yami, he is counted among the first humans; having chosen death, he became king of the departed. In later Hindu tradition he presides over Naraka, the realm of the dead located beneath the earth and in the south, the direction he guards as one of the Lokapalas (guardians of the directions).

Yama is depicted as green or dark in complexion, riding a water buffalo, and armed with a staff (danda) and a noose (pāśa) with which he draws the soul from the body. His scribe and assistant Chitragupta maintains a record of every human being's deeds, on the basis of which Yama judges the dead and assigns them reward or punishment according to their karma.

Famous episodes involving Yama include the story of Savitri, who by her devotion and wit won back the life of her husband Satyavan from the god of death, and the Katha Upanishad, in which the boy Nachiketa travels to Yama's abode and receives from him the teaching on the nature of the self (atman) and liberation.

In other traditions

With the spread of Buddhism and Hinduism, Yama entered many Asian religions: as Yanluo Wang in Chinese tradition, Enma in Japan, and Yamaraja in Tibetan Buddhism, where he is a wrathful dharmapala. His Iranian counterpart, Yima, reflects the common Indo-Iranian origin of the figure.
