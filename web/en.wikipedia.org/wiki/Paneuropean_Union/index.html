Paneuropean Union

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Paneuropean Union (German: Paneuropa-Union), founded in 1922, is the oldest European unification movement. It advocates a politically and economically united Europe grounded in Christian and liberal values. Its founder was the Austrian-Japanese philosopher and politician Richard von Coudenhove-Kalergi, whose 1923 manifesto Pan-Europa launched the movement.

History

Coudenhove-Kalergi founded the movement in Vienna in the aftermath of the First World War, arguing that only a federated "Pan-Europe" could prevent another war and hold its own between an Anglo-American bloc and Soviet Russia. The first Paneuropean Congress met in Vienna in 1926; supporters included Aristide Briand, who became honorary president, and figures such as Konrad Adenauer, Georges Pompidou, and Franz Josef Strauss. The movement's flag — twelve golden stars' forerunner symbolism of a sun disc bearing a red cross on a blue field — later influenced the design of the European flag, whose circle of twelve stars Coudenhove-Kalergi also championed.

Banned in Nazi Germany and the Soviet sphere, the Union was revived after 1945, though the practical work of integration passed to the Council of Europe and the European Communities. Coudenhove-Kalergi was the first recipient of the Charlemagne Prize in 1950. After his death in 1972, Otto von Habsburg — the last crown prince of Austria-Hungary — led the movement for decades and used it to champion the cause of Central and Eastern European nations behind the Iron Curtain; the organization co-inspired the Paneuropean Picnic of 1989. The Union still exists, with national sections in many European countries, advocating European unity and the accession of remaining European states.

Principles

The Union's stated principles include subsidiarity, the preservation of Europe's cultural and religious heritage, a social market economy, and the defence of human rights and democracy — historically framed in anti-communist terms and open, in principle, to all European peoples.
