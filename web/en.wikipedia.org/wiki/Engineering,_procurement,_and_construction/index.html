Engineering, procurement, and construction

AbbreviationEPC
IndustriesEnergy, infrastructure, process industries

Engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) is a contracting arrangement in which a single contractor is responsible for designing a facility, procuring all necessary materials and equipment, and constructing it, typically delivering a completed, operational plant to the owner for a fixed price and date — a so-called "turnkey" delivery.

Characteristics

Under an EPC contract the owner specifies performance requirements, and the contractor assumes most design, schedule, and cost risk. EPC contracts are commonly let on a lump-sum turnkey (LSTK) basis and are standard in the oil and gas, petrochemical, power generation, mining, and large infrastructure sectors. The EPC contractor in turn subcontracts specialised packages of work.

Related models

Variants include EPCM (engineering, procurement, and construction management), in which the contractor manages construction on the owner's behalf without taking full delivery risk, and EPCC, which adds commissioning. The choice of model reflects how risk is allocated between owner and contractor.
