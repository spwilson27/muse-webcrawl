Ganvié is a lake village in Benin, Africa, lying in Lake Nokoué, near the capital region of Cotonou and the commune of Sô-Ava. It is the largest water-based settlement in Africa, built entirely on stilts, and is sometimes called the "Venice of Africa". Its population is commonly estimated between roughly 20,000 and 45,000 people.

History

The village was founded in the 16th or 17th century by the Tofinu people. According to tradition, the Tofinu settled on the lake to escape slave raids by the Fon kingdom of Dahomey. Fon religious prohibitions forbade their warriors from attacking communities on the water, so the lake offered protection. The name Ganvié is commonly interpreted as "we are saved" or "the community of those who finally found peace" in the Tofinu language.

Geography and daily life

Ganvié sits in the shallow waters of Lake Nokoué, a lagoon connected to the Atlantic Ocean near Porto-Novo and Cotonou. All buildings — houses, shops, a school, a church and mosque, and a market — are built on wooden stilts above the water. Transport is entirely by dugout canoe (pirogue), and residents navigate a network of waterways between neighbourhoods. Artificial islands of earth have been created in a few places for facilities such as a football pitch and a cemetery.

Economy

The economy is based primarily on fishing, including the distinctive acadja brush-park fish enclosures planted in the lake bed, as well as fish farming, trade and increasingly tourism. Visitors reach the village by boat, typically from the jetty at Abomey-Calavi. Ganvié has been on Benin's tentative list for UNESCO World Heritage status, and its culture and stilt architecture attract growing numbers of tourists each year.
