Sea kayak

A sea kayak or touring kayak is a kayak developed for the sport of paddling on open waters of lakes, bays, and the ocean. Sea kayaks are seaworthy small boats with a covered deck and one or more cockpits, each seating one paddler. They are typically long (5–5.5 metres) and relatively narrow, with storage compartments fore and aft sealed by hatches and bulkheads for flotation and carrying expedition gear.

Origins

The sea kayak descends from the baidarka and qajaq of the Aleut, Inuit and Yup'ik peoples of the Arctic, who built skin-on-frame hunting boats for use in rough seas. Modern recreational sea kayaking developed in Britain and North America in the 1970s, with designers such as Derek Hutchinson and Frank Goodman adapting traditional Greenland forms in fibreglass, later supplemented by polyethylene and composite construction.

Features and skills

Sea kayaks commonly feature retractable skegs or rudders for tracking in wind, deck lines and hatches, and upturned "rocker" for manoeuvrability. Paddlers use spray decks and learn self-rescue techniques such as wet exits, rolls, and assisted rescues, and must plan for tides, weather and cold-water immersion. Sea kayaking ranges from day trips to major expeditions, including circumnavigations of Britain, Iceland and other coastlines.
