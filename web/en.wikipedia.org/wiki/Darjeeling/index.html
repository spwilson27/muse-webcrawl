Darjeeling is a town and municipality in the Indian state of West Bengal, in the Lesser Himalayas at an elevation of about 2,045 metres (6,709 ft). It is famous internationally for the Darjeeling tea grown on the estates around it, for views of Kangchenjunga, the world's third-highest mountain, and for the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, a narrow-gauge "toy train" line designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The name Darjeeling derives from the Tibetan Dorje-ling, "land of the thunderbolt". The hill tract was ceded by the Chogyal of Sikkim to the British East India Company in 1835 and developed as a sanatorium and hill station for British officials escaping the heat of the plains. Commercial tea cultivation began in the 1850s, and the town grew with a mixed population of Nepali, Tibetan, Lepcha and plains Indian communities.

Tea

Darjeeling tea, produced from the small-leaved Chinese variety of the tea plant grown at altitude, is prized for its muscatel flavour and is sold in seasonal "flushes" — first, second, and autumn. It holds a Geographical Indication tag, the first granted to an Indian product. Tea tourism and the estate gardens remain central to the local economy.

Politics and culture

Darjeeling is the centre of the Gorkhaland movement, which has periodically agitated for a separate state for the Nepali-speaking Gorkha population; the semi-autonomous Gorkhaland Territorial Administration now administers the hills. The town is also known for its boarding schools founded in the colonial era and for its mountaineering institute, the Himalayan Mountaineering Institute, co-founded by Tenzing Norgay.
