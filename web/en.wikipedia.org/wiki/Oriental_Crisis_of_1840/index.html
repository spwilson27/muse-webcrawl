Oriental Crisis of 1840

The Oriental Crisis of 1840 was an episode in the Eastern Question, the long-running contest over the declining Ottoman Empire. It arose from the second war between Sultan Mahmud II and Muhammad Ali of Egypt, whose forces under his son Ibrahim Pasha had conquered Syria and threatened Anatolia.

The crisis

After the Ottoman defeat at Nezib in 1839 and the defection of the Ottoman fleet at Alexandria, Britain, Austria, Prussia and Russia intervened to preserve the Ottoman Empire. By the Convention of London (1840) they offered Muhammad Ali hereditary rule of Egypt if he withdrew from Syria. France, which supported Muhammad Ali, was isolated and threatened war, but backed down after British and Austrian forces bombarded Beirut and captured Acre.

Settlement

Muhammad Ali accepted the terms in 1841, gaining hereditary rule over Egypt and Sudan while giving up Syria and Crete. The Straits Convention of 1841 closed the Bosporus and Dardanelles to foreign warships in peacetime, and the crisis reshaped the European balance of power in the Near East.
