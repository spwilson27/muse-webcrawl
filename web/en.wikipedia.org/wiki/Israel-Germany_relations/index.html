Israel–Germany relations

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Israel–Germany relations are the bilateral ties between the State of Israel and the Federal Republic of Germany. Emerging from the shadow of the Holocaust, they have developed into close cooperation in politics, security, science and culture, though historical memory remains central to the relationship.

Early years and the Reparations Agreement

Formal diplomatic relations were established in 1965. The decisive early step was the 1952 Luxembourg Agreement (Reparations Agreement), under which West Germany paid the new State of Israel some 3.45 billion Deutsche Marks (about $822 million) for the material losses and costs of the Holocaust; Israel's Knesset ratified it amid fierce domestic debate. German Chancellor Konrad Adenauer and Israeli prime ministers David Ben-Gurion and later Levi Eshkol forged the personal trust on which the relationship was built.

Security and political cooperation

Germany is one of Israel's closest European partners and a major arms supplier, including the supply of Dolphin-class submarines built by ThyssenKrupp. Chancellor Angela Merkel declared Israel's security part of Germany's Staatsräson (reason of state) in her 2008 address to the Knesset. The countries hold regular intergovernmental consultations, cooperate in intelligence, counter-terrorism and technology, and commemorate the victims of the Munich massacre of 1972 together.

Culture and society

German funding and institutions support Holocaust remembrance and education in Israel; youth exchanges between the two countries are among the largest in the world. A large German-speaking Jewish community history is recalled at sites such as Yad Vashem, while Berlin has become a major centre of Israeli expatriate life.
