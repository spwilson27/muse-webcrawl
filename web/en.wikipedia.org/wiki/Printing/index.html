Printing

Placeholder image: a 19th-century printing press

Printing is a process for mass reproducing text and images using a master form or template. The earliest non-paper products involving printing include cylinder seals and objects such as the Cyrus Cylinder. The earliest known form of printing as applied to paper was woodblock printing, which appeared in China before 220 AD.

History

Woodblock printing was used in China for centuries; the Diamond Sutra (868 AD) is the oldest dated printed book. Movable type was invented by Bi Sheng around 1040 using ceramic pieces, and metal movable type was developed in Korea, where the Jikji was printed in 1377. In Europe, Johannes Gutenberg developed a system of movable type, oil-based ink, and a screw press around 1440, dramatically lowering the cost of books and fuelling the spread of the Renaissance, Reformation, and Scientific Revolution.

Printing processes

Conventional printing processes include letterpress (relief), offset lithography (planographic — now dominant for books and newspapers), gravure (intaglio, used for long runs and packaging), flexography, and screen printing. Each uses an image carrier from which ink is transferred directly or indirectly to the substrate.

Digital printing

From the late 20th century, digital methods — principally inkjet and laser (electrophotographic) printing — allowed direct output from computer files, enabling short runs and print-on-demand. 3D printing extended the concept to fabricating objects layer by layer.
