Gulf War

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The **Gulf War** was an armed conflict between Iraq and a 42-country coalition led by the United States that liberated Kuwait. It began with Iraq's invasion of Kuwait on 2 August 1990 under Saddam Hussein, prompting UN sanctions and the build-up of coalition forces in Saudi Arabia (Operation Desert Shield). The coalition air campaign (Operation Desert Storm) began on 17 January 1991, followed by a ground offensive on 24 February that freed Kuwait in just 100 hours; a ceasefire was declared on 28 February 1991.

The war showcased precision-guided weapons, stealth aircraft and live television war coverage (CNN). Iraqi forces set fire to hundreds of Kuwaiti oil wells as they retreated, causing an environmental catastrophe. Coalition deaths were under 400; Iraqi military deaths are estimated in the tens of thousands, with the 'Highway of Death' bombings of retreating convoys remaining controversial. Saddam remained in power, suppressing Kurdish and Shia uprisings the coalition had encouraged; sanctions and no-fly zones followed, and disputes over Iraq's weapons programmes led toward the 2003 Iraq War.

Causes

Iraq, indebted after the Iran–Iraq War, accused Kuwait of slant-drilling into the Rumaila oil field and of depressing oil prices; Saddam also pressed historic claims to Kuwait as Iraq's 'nineteenth province'. Misread signals about the likely American response preceded the invasion.
