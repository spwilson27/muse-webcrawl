Khorsabad

Khorsabad is a village in northern Iraq, in Nineveh Governorate, built on the site of the ancient Assyrian city of Dur-Sharrukin ("Fortress of Sargon"). Founded by Sargon II of Assyria in the late 8th century BCE, the city served briefly as the capital of the Neo-Assyrian Empire.

History

Sargon II began construction around 717 BCE on a previously sparsely inhabited site. The city was laid out as a vast square enclosed by walls, with a citadel containing temples and an enormous royal palace decorated with carved reliefs and colossal lamassu statues. After Sargon's death in battle in 705 BCE, his son Sennacherib moved the capital to Nineveh, and Dur-Sharrukin was largely abandoned.

Archaeology

The site was excavated by Paul-Émile Botta, the French consul at Mosul, beginning in 1843 — work that effectively launched Assyriology. Many sculptures were shipped to the Louvre in Paris, though one transport convoy was lost in the Tigris in 1855. Later excavations were conducted by the Oriental Institute of Chicago. The modern village of Khorsabad overlies the ancient remains.
