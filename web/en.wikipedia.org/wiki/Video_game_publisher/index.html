Video game publisher

A video game publisher is a company that publishes video games that have been developed either internally by the publisher or externally by a game developer. Publishers generally finance development, handle manufacturing and distribution, marketing and advertising, localisation, licensing and platform certification, in return for a share of revenue and often ownership of the intellectual property.

Video game publisher
FunctionsFunding, production oversight, marketing, distribution, localisation
Major examplesNintendo, Sony, Microsoft, Electronic Arts, Activision Blizzard, Ubisoft, Take-Two

Role

Publishers evaluate pitches, set milestones and budgets, provide development kits and quality assurance, negotiate retail and digital storefront placement, and manage age ratings (ESRB, PEGI) and legal clearance. First-party publishers publish for their own consoles; third-party publishers release across platforms; independent developers may self-publish digitally.

History

Early publishers such as Atari and Activision (founded 1979 as the first third-party developer-publisher) shaped the model. The North American video game crash of 1983 led Nintendo to impose strict licensing and quality seals. From the 2000s, digital distribution (Steam, app stores, console networks) lowered barriers, while consolidation created large groups owning many studios and franchises.

Business models

Revenue comes from unit sales, subscriptions, downloadable content, microtransactions and licensing. Publishing deals range from full financing with IP transfer to distribution-only and co-publishing arrangements.
