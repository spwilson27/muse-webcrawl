Bernard Charles Ecclestone (born 28 October 1930) is a British business magnate. He is the former chief executive of the Formula One Group, which manages Formula One motor racing, and effectively controlled the sport's commercial rights for nearly four decades, building it into a multibillion-dollar global enterprise.

Early life and racing career

Ecclestone was born in Bungay, Suffolk, the son of a fisherman. He left school at 16 and built a successful motorcycle parts business. He raced briefly in Formula Three in the late 1940s and later managed drivers including Stuart Lewis-Evans and Jochen Rindt. In 1972 he bought the Brabham team, which won world championships with Nelson Piquet in 1981 and 1983.

Control of Formula One

As head of the Formula One Constructors' Association, Ecclestone negotiated control of the sport's television rights in the late 1970s, transforming its finances. He remained the dominant figure in Formula One until Liberty Media's takeover in 2017, when he was replaced as chief executive by Chase Carey and given the honorary title of chairman emeritus.

Legal issues

In October 2023 Ecclestone pleaded guilty in a London court to fraud by false representation over undeclared overseas assets and agreed to repay more than £650 million in tax, interest and penalties, receiving a suspended prison sentence.
