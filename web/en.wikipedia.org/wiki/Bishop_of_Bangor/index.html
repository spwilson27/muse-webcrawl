The Bishop of Bangor is the ordinary of the Church in Wales Diocese of Bangor in the Province of Wales. The see is based in the city of Bangor in north-west Wales, and the bishop's seat (cathedra) is located at Bangor Cathedral, dedicated to Saint Deiniol.

History

The Diocese of Bangor is traditionally traced to about 546, when Saint Deiniol founded a monastery at Bangor and was consecrated as its first bishop. The see survived the Norman invasion and the medieval period as one of the four historic Welsh dioceses, within the province of Canterbury. Following the Welsh Church Act 1914, the Welsh dioceses were disestablished (from 1920) and formed the Church in Wales, of which Bangor remains a diocese.

The diocese covers the historic counties of Anglesey, most of Caernarvonshire and Merionethshire, and a portion of Montgomeryshire. Until the disestablishment settlement, the bishops of Bangor sat in the House of Lords as Lords Spiritual; today the bishop is elected by an electoral college of the diocese. Notable bishops include Nicholas Robinson (1566–1585), the scholar-bishop William Morgan — translator of the 1588 Welsh Bible — held the see from 1601, and in the modern era Barry Morgan served as Bishop of Bangor (1993–1999) before becoming Archbishop of Wales. The arms of the see are gules, a bend or gutty de poix between two mullets argent.
