John Sigismund, Elector of Brandenburg

John Sigismund (German: Johann Sigismund; 8 November 1572 – 23 December 1619) was a Prince-elector of the Margraviate of Brandenburg from the House of Hohenzollern. He became the Duke of Prussia through his marriage to Duchess Anna, the eldest daughter of Duke Albert Frederick of Prussia, who left no surviving sons. His acquisition of the Duchy of Cleves and other territories laid the foundations of the later Brandenburg-Prussian state.

Reign

John Sigismund succeeded his father Joachim Frederick as Elector in 1608. In the same year he became regent of the Duchy of Prussia for his mentally ill father-in-law, inheriting it outright in 1618 — creating the personal union known as Brandenburg-Prussia, though the two territories remained legally separate.

Jülich-Cleves succession

On the death of the childless Duke John William of Jülich-Cleves-Berg in 1609, John Sigismund pressed his wife's family's claim to the Rhineland duchies. The resulting War of the Jülich Succession ended with the Treaty of Xanten (1614), which gave Brandenburg Cleves, Mark and Ravensberg — the dynasty's first territories in western Germany.

Conversion

In 1613 John Sigismund converted from Lutheranism to Calvinism, but he did not compel his subjects to follow, granting freedom of worship in what became a characteristic Hohenzollern policy of a Calvinist ruler over Lutheran subjects. He suffered a stroke in 1617 and died in Berlin in 1619, succeeded by his son George William.
