Water gap

A water gap is a gap in a mountain ridge through which a river or stream flows. Water gaps are often sought out as routes for roads and railways because they offer low-gradient passages through otherwise difficult terrain.

Water gap
FormationAntecedence, superimposition, or overflow erosion
ExamplesDelaware Water Gap; Columbia River Gorge; Manawatu Gorge

Formation

Geologists explain water gaps by antecedence — a river maintaining its course as a ridge is uplifted across its path — or by superimposition, in which a river established on softer overlying strata erodes down into a buried resistant ridge. A related feature, the wind gap, is a former water gap no longer occupied by a stream, typically after stream capture diverts its drainage.

Examples

The Delaware Water Gap, cut by the Delaware River through the Kittatinny Ridge on the Pennsylvania–New Jersey border, and the Columbia River Gorge through the Cascade Range are well-known American examples; both carry major highways and rail lines.
