Forestry

A managed forest plantation

Forestry is the science and craft of creating, managing, planting, using, conserving and repairing forests and woodlands for associated resources for human and environmental benefits. Forestry is practiced in plantations and natural stands. The science of forestry has elements that belong to the biological, physical, social, political and managerial sciences.

History

Formal forest management developed in Europe, notably in Germany in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, where the concept of sustained yield — harvesting no more timber than the forest grows — was articulated. Scientific forestry was exported widely through colonial administrations and influenced forest services around the world, including the United States Forest Service, founded in 1905 under Gifford Pinchot.

Practice

Forestry includes silviculture, the practice of controlling the establishment, growth, composition, and health of forests; forest protection against fire, insects, and disease; forest inventory and planning; and the harvesting and transport of timber. Modern forestry increasingly emphasises ecosystem services beyond timber, including watershed protection, carbon sequestration, recreation, and biodiversity conservation.

Education and professions

Foresters typically hold degrees in forestry or natural resource management, and in many jurisdictions are licensed or registered professionals. Forestry roles exist in government agencies, the timber industry, conservation organisations, and consulting firms.
