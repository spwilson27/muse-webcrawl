Wildfire suppression

Airtanker dropping fire retardant

Wildfire suppression is a range of firefighting tactics used to suppress wildfires. Firefighting efforts in wildland areas require different techniques, equipment, and training from the more familiar structure fire fighting found in populated areas. Fire suppression is organised under incident management systems, and in the United States most large incidents are managed using the Incident Command System.

Tactics

Suppression strategies include direct attack at the fire's edge, using hand crews, engines, and water or retardant drops; parallel and indirect attack, in which a control line is constructed at a distance and the intervening fuel is burned out (see backfire); and confinement or monitoring strategies for fires in remote areas. Bulldozers, hand tools, and chainsaws are used to construct firelines that remove fuel down to mineral soil.

Aviation

Fixed-wing airtankers drop long-term fire retardant ahead of the fire, while helicopters drop water with buckets or internal tanks. Smokejumpers parachute into remote fires in roadless areas, and hotshot crews hike in to construct line in steep terrain.

Policy

For much of the twentieth century, agencies pursued aggressive suppression of all fires, which in fire-adapted ecosystems allowed fuels to accumulate — the so-called fire paradox. Modern policy increasingly combines suppression of fires threatening communities with prescribed fire and managed wildfire to restore fire's ecological role.
