Backfire (firefighting)

A firefighter sets a backfire with a drip torch

A backfire or back burn is a fire set intentionally by wildland firefighters in the path of an oncoming wildfire, from a secured control line such as a road, river, or hand-cut fireline. The fire burns inward toward the approaching main fire, consuming the fuel between the line and the wildfire so that when the main fire arrives there is nothing left for it to burn, and it dies out at the line.

Technique

Backfiring is an indirect attack method used when a wildfire is too intense for direct attack. Crews ignite vegetation along the inner edge of the control line, often using drip torches. As the main fire approaches, its convection column and indrafts pull the backfire toward it; the two fires meet and, deprived of fuel, burn out. The operation must be timed carefully: igniting too early can waste the opportunity, while igniting too late risks the main fire crossing the line first.

Risks

Backfires can escape control if winds shift or strengthen, and the technique requires experienced personnel and careful coordination with weather forecasts. A smaller-scale version of the technique, used to consume fuel immediately adjacent to a fireline, is called burning out.
