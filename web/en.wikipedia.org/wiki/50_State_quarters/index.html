50 State quarters

ProgramUnited States Mint commemorative coin series
Years1999–2008
DenominationQuarter dollar (25 cents)

The 50 State quarters was a series of circulating commemorative quarters released by the United States Mint from 1999 to 2008. Five designs were issued each year, each honouring one of the fifty U.S. states in the order they joined the Union, with a design representing the state on the reverse of the coin.

Program

Authorized by the 50 States Commemorative Coin Program Act of 1997, the series began with Delaware in 1999 and ended with Hawaii in 2008. The obverse retained a modified portrait of George Washington. Each state chose its design through a public process involving governors, artists, and sometimes popular votes.

Impact

The program was the most popular commemorative coin initiative in U.S. history, credited with drawing an estimated 147 million Americans into coin collecting and generating roughly billion for the Treasury through seigniorage. Its success led to follow-on series for the District of Columbia and U.S. territories, the America the Beautiful quarters, and American Innovation dollars.
