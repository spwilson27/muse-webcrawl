The United States Army Ranger School is a 61-day small-unit tactics and leadership course based at Fort Benning (now Fort Moore), Georgia. It is widely regarded as one of the most physically and mentally demanding courses in the US military. Graduates earn the Ranger Tab; completing the school makes a soldier "Ranger qualified", distinct from assignment to the 75th Ranger Regiment, whose members wear the tan beret.

Overview

The course is open to soldiers, and on a limited basis to members of other US services and allied militaries. Its purpose is to teach small-unit leadership under conditions of severe stress: students average very little sleep and limited rations while conducting continuous patrols. Historically only about half of each class graduates without repeating ("recycling") a phase, and overall completion rates are lower still.

Phases

  • Benning Phase (Fort Benning, ~20 days): begins with the Ranger Assessment Phase (RAP week) — fitness tests, a 12-mile ruck march, land navigation, water confidence and combat lifesaver tests — followed by patrolling instruction and graded patrols around Camp Darby.
  • Mountain Phase (~21 days): conducted at Camp Frank D. Merrill near Dahlonega, Georgia, covering mountaineering, knots, belays and squad/platoon patrols in mountainous terrain.
  • Florida Phase (~18 days): conducted at Camp James E. Rudder at Eglin Air Force Base, covering swamp and jungle operations, small-boat work and a multi-day field exercise in the Yellow River swamp.

History

The course grew out of Ranger training at Fort Benning beginning in 1950 during the Korean War, and was standardised under the Ranger Department; the first class graduated in 1952. In August 2015, Captain Kristen Griest and First Lieutenant Shaye Haver became the first women to graduate, after the course was opened to women on a trial basis; it has been fully gender-integrated since.
