Highland Lakes

The Highland Lakes are a chain of seven fresh water reservoirs in Central Texas formed by dams on the Colorado River. Created and managed by the Lower Colorado River Authority (LCRA) primarily for flood control, water supply and hydroelectric power, they stretch for about 85 miles northwest of Austin through the Texas Hill Country.

The lakes

From upstream to downstream, the lakes and their dams are: Lake Buchanan (Buchanan Dam, completed 1938), Inks Lake (Inks Dam), Lake Lyndon B. Johnson (Wirtz Dam; formerly Lake Granite Shoals), Lake Marble Falls (Max Starcke Dam), Lake Travis (Mansfield Dam, the largest, completed 1942), Lake Austin (Tom Miller Dam), and Lady Bird Lake in Austin (Longhorn Dam). Lake Travis is the primary flood-control reservoir, with variable levels, while the "constant-level" lakes serve recreation and lakeside communities such as Marble Falls, Kingsland and Horseshoe Bay.

Use and management

The lakes supply water to Austin and downstream users including rice farmers, generate hydroelectricity, and are a major Central Texas recreation destination for boating, fishing and swimming. The Hill Country communities around them are also known for spring bluebonnet displays.
