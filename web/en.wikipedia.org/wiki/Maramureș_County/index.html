Maramureș County
Country: Romania
Capital: Baia Mare
Area: 6,304 km²
Population: approx. 450,000

Maramureș County (Romanian pronunciation: [maraˈmureʃ]) is a county (județ) in northwestern Romania, in the historical region of Maramureș along the border with Ukraine. Its capital and largest city is Baia Mare. The county corresponds to the southern part of historical Maramureș; the northern part lies across the Tisza River in Ukraine's Zakarpattia Oblast.

Geography and culture

The county is ringed by the Eastern Carpathians — the Rodna, Maramureș and Țibleș mountains — and is noted for its preserved rural culture: carved wooden gates, traditional dress, and above all its wooden churches. Eight Maramureș wooden churches, in Bârsana, Budești, Desești, Ieud, Plopiș, Poienile Izei, Rogoz and Șurdești, are inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The village of Săpânța is famous for its Merry Cemetery, with brightly painted crosses bearing humorous epitaphs.

Economy

Baia Mare was historically a mining centre (gold, silver, lead and copper), and mining's legacy includes the 2000 Baia Mare cyanide spill, a major environmental disaster in the Tisza basin. Forestry, agriculture, food processing and tourism are now central to the economy. The population is predominantly Romanian, with Hungarian and Ukrainian minorities.
