The Yarkand Khanate, also known as the Yarkent Khanate or Khanate of Kashgaria, was a Sunni Muslim Turkic state ruled by the Mongol-descended Chagatai khans from 1514 to 1705. Centred on the oasis city of Yarkand, it controlled the western Tarim Basin in what is now Xinjiang, including Kashgar, Khotan, Aksu and, at times, Turpan.

Foundation

The khanate was founded by Sultan Said Khan, a descendant of the Chagatai Khanate rulers of Moghulistan, who seized Kashgar and Yarkand in 1514 from the Dughlat emir Mirza Abu Bakr. Said Khan (r. 1514–1533) also campaigned in Ladakh and Baltistan and patronised Islamic scholarship; his court historian Mirza Muhammad Haidar Dughlat wrote the Tarikh-i Rashidi, a principal source for the region's history.

Later history

Under Abdurashid Khan and his successors the khanate maintained trade and diplomatic contact with Ming China, Mughal India and Bukhara. From the later 17th century, power increasingly passed to rival Naqshbandi Sufi khoja factions — the Afaqi ("White Mountain") and Ishaqi ("Black Mountain") parties — whose feuds invited intervention by the Dzungar Khanate. In 1678 the Afaqi khoja Afaq Khoja took power with Dzungar backing, and the Yarkand khans were reduced to puppets; the khanate effectively ended by 1705.

Culture

The khanate's court supported Chagatai Turkic literature and music; the queen consort Amanni Shahan is credited with systematising the Twelve Muqam, the great cycle of Uyghur classical music now recognised by UNESCO.
