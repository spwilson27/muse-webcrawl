Kom Ombo (Arabic: كوم أمبو) is an agricultural town in southern Egypt, in the Aswan Governorate, located on the east bank of the Nile about 50 kilometres north of Aswan. In antiquity it was known as Ombos, a garrison town controlling trade routes between Nubia and the Nile Valley and the mines of the Eastern Desert.

Temple of Kom Ombo

The town is famous for the Temple of Kom Ombo, an unusual double temple built during the Ptolemaic dynasty (principally 180–47 BC, with later Roman additions). The temple is perfectly symmetrical along its main axis and was dedicated to two sets of gods: the crocodile god Sobek, associated with fertility and the Nile, and the falcon god Horus the Elder (Haroeris). Twin entrances, courts, and sanctuaries served the two cults side by side.

Reliefs in the temple include a celebrated scene often interpreted as depicting surgical and medical instruments. A small chapel nearby houses mummified crocodiles, now displayed in the adjacent Crocodile Museum, reflecting the cult of Sobek in the region.

Modern town

Today Kom Ombo is a centre for sugarcane cultivation and sugar refining. Many of its inhabitants are Nubians displaced by the flooding of Lower Nubia after the construction of the Aswan High Dam. The temple, dramatically sited on a bend of the Nile, is a standard stop for Nile cruise boats between Luxor and Aswan.
