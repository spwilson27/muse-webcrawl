Fagaceae

Acorns of an oak, a member of Fagaceae

Fagaceae is a family of flowering plants in the order Fagales, comprising about 900–1,000 species of trees and shrubs, both deciduous and evergreen, distributed mainly across temperate and tropical regions of the Northern Hemisphere. The family includes some of the world's most economically and ecologically important hardwoods.

Characteristics

Members of Fagaceae have simple, alternate leaves with pinnate venation, unisexual flowers (plants are monoecious), and fruit in the form of nuts partly or wholly enclosed in a cupule — the acorn cup of oaks, the husk of beechnuts and the spiny burr of chestnuts. Pollination is by wind in most genera, though some tropical members are insect-pollinated.

Ecology and uses

Oaks and beeches dominate many temperate forests and their mast crops feed wildlife. The family yields valuable timber (oak and beech wood), cork (from the cork oak, Quercus suber), edible nuts (chestnuts, acorns as livestock forage), tannins and ornamental trees.
