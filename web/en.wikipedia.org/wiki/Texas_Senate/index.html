Texas Senate

The Texas Senate is the upper house of the Texas Legislature. It consists of 31 members, each representing a single-member district of roughly 940,000 people, who are elected to four-year staggered terms. The Lieutenant Governor of Texas serves as President of the Senate — a position often described as the most powerful in Texas state government, because the lieutenant governor controls the flow of legislation, appoints committees and their chairs, and casts tie-breaking votes.

Composition and elections

Senators serve four-year terms, with elections staggered so that all seats are contested after each redistricting. To be eligible, a candidate must be at least 26 years old, a qualified voter, and a resident of Texas for five years and of the district for one year immediately preceding the election. Regular sessions of the Legislature convene for 140 days in odd-numbered years; the governor may call special sessions.

Procedure

A distinctive feature of the Texas Senate is the traditional "two-thirds rule", under which a bill ordinarily needed the support of two-thirds of the senators (21 of 31) to be brought up for debate out of order; in 2015 the threshold was lowered to three-fifths (19 of 31). Filibusters are permitted in the chamber, and must be conducted standing, on-topic, and without food or drink, leading to celebrated marathon speeches in Texas political history.

History

The Senate traces its origins to the Congress of the Republic of Texas and was carried into statehood in 1845. Republicans gained a majority in the chamber following the 1996 elections, ending more than a century of Democratic control, and have held the majority since.
