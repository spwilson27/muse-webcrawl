Battle of al-Qadisiyyah

The Battle of al-Qādisiyyah was fought in 636 AD (15 AH) between the army of the Rashidun Caliphate under Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and the forces of the Sasanian Empire commanded by Rostam Farrokhzad. The Muslim victory at al-Qadisiyyah, on the western edge of the Iraqi alluvial plain, opened Mesopotamia to conquest and led within a few years to the fall of Ctesiphon and the eventual collapse of Sasanian Persia.

Background

After the Muslim victory at the Battle of the Bridge (634) checked earlier gains, Caliph Umar sent a major expedition under Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas into Iraq. Yazdegerd III dispatched his spahbed Rostam with the empire's main field army. The armies faced each other near the canal town of al-Qadisiyyah, west of the Euphrates.

The battle

Arabic tradition describes four days of fighting. Sasanian war elephants initially disrupted the Arab infantry and cavalry; Syrian reinforcements under al-Qa'qa ibn Amr arrived on the second day. On the final day a sandstorm blew into the Persians' faces, Rostam was killed — the circumstances are variously reported — and the Persian army disintegrated. The sacred Sasanian standard, the Derafsh Kaviani, was captured.

Aftermath

The Arabs advanced on the Sasanian capital Ctesiphon, which fell in 637 after the battle of Jalula secured the region. Al-Qadisiyyah remains a powerful symbol in Arab and Iranian memory, invoked in modern times — notably by Saddam Hussein, who framed the Iran–Iraq War as a new Qadisiyyah. The historical details, including army sizes and the exact date, are debated because the early Arabic sources are late and legendary in places.
