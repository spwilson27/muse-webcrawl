Washington Mutual

Washington Mutual, Inc. (often abbreviated to WaMu) was an American savings bank holding company based in Seattle, Washington. Founded in 1889 as the Washington National Building Loan and Investment Association, it grew through aggressive acquisitions to become the largest savings and loan association in the United States, with more than 2,200 branches and about $307 billion in assets at its peak.

Failure

During the 2000s housing boom, WaMu became a major originator of high-risk mortgages, including subprime and option adjustable-rate loans. As house prices fell, losses mounted, and in September 2008 depositors withdrew $16.7 billion over ten days. On 25 September 2008 the Office of Thrift Supervision seized the bank and placed it in FDIC receivership — the largest bank failure in American history. Its banking operations were sold the same day to JPMorgan Chase for $1.9 billion, while the holding company filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy the following day.

Aftermath

The closure wiped out holders of WaMu's shares and bonds and generated years of litigation over the division of assets between the FDIC, JPMorgan Chase and the bankruptcy estate. Investigations by the U.S. Senate later criticized the bank's lending practices and its regulator's oversight.
