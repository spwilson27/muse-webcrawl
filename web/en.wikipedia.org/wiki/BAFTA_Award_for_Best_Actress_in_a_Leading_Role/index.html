BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role

BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role
Presented by: British Academy of Film and Television Arts
First awarded: 1952 (film year)
Country: United Kingdom

The BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role is an award presented annually by the British Academy of Film and Television Arts to recognise an actress who has delivered an outstanding leading performance in a film.

History

From 1952 to 1967 the category was split into awards for British and foreign actresses. From 1968 they were merged into a single Best Actress award, renamed Best Actress in a Leading Role in 1985 to match the creation of a supporting category.

Notable winners

Early winners included Vivien Leigh, Audrey Hepburn and Simone Signoret. Multiple winners include Judi Dench, Maggie Smith, Katharine Hepburn and Frances McDormand. Recent winners in the 2020s have included Joanna Scanlan for After Love, Cate Blanchett for Tár and Emma Stone for Poor Things.

The award is one of the major acting prizes of the British film awards season, alongside the Academy Award for Best Actress. See British Academy Film Awards.
