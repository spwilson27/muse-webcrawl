Allen Newell

Allen Newell (March 19, 1927 – July 19, 1992) was an American computer scientist and cognitive psychologist, a pioneer of artificial intelligence. With Herbert A. Simon he created the Logic Theorist (1956) and the General Problem Solver (1957), founded the field's research program at the RAND Corporation and Carnegie Mellon University, and shared the 1975 Turing Award with Simon.

Career

Newell studied physics at Stanford and mathematics at Princeton before joining RAND in 1950, where he met Simon and J. C. Shaw. Their Logic Theorist, often called the first AI program, proved theorems from Whitehead and Russell's Principia Mathematica. The trio's General Problem Solver introduced means–ends analysis, and their work founded the physical symbol system hypothesis: that a physical symbol system has the necessary and sufficient means for general intelligent action.

At Carnegie Mellon from 1961, Newell built a wide research program spanning problem solving, production-system architectures such as Soar, human–computer interaction, and speech recognition. His 1990 book Unified Theories of Cognition argued that cognitive science should aim at complete, integrated theories of the mind.
