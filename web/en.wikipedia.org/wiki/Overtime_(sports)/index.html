Overtime (sports)

Overtime or extra time is an additional period of play specified under the rules of a sport to bring a game to a decision and avoid a tie or draw where the format requires a winner, such as in knockout competitions.

Forms

Methods vary widely. Association football uses two periods of extra time followed, if necessary, by a penalty shoot-out; historically it also experimented with the golden goal and silver goal. North American sports use overtime periods of fixed length: the NFL employs a modified sudden-death system, the NBA plays five-minute periods until a winner emerges, and the NHL uses a short 3-on-3 overtime in the regular season and full sudden-death periods in the playoffs. Baseball and softball use extra innings.

Sudden death

In sudden-death formats, the first score wins immediately, a system that heightens drama but is criticised where the team that first gains possession has a decisive advantage. Many leagues have modified their rules to guarantee both teams a possession in important games.
