Proper length

Proper length or rest length is the length of an object in the object's rest frame. It is the measurement of space-like intervals in special relativity. For an object at rest in an inertial frame, the proper length is the ordinary measured length. Observers in relative motion measure the object to be shorter, a phenomenon known as length contraction.

Length contraction

If the proper length of an object is L0, an observer moving at speed v relative to the object measures its length, parallel to the motion, as:

L = L0 √(1 − v²/c²) = L0/γ

where γ is the Lorentz factor. The concept was developed as part of Albert Einstein's 1905 theory of special relativity, building on earlier work by Lorentz and FitzGerald.

Proper distance

The related concept of proper distance generalizes the idea to cosmology and curved spacetime: it is the distance between two events measured along a surface of constant proper time, providing a physical length scale for the expansion of the universe. Proper length is invariant under Lorentz transformations for objects at rest in the frame in which the length is evaluated, making it a frame-independent property of the object itself rather than of the observer.
