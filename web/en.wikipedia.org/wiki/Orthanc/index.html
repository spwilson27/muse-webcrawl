Orthanc

Orthanc is a fictional tower in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium, appearing in The Lord of the Rings. It stands at the centre of the Ring of Isengard, in the valley of Nan Curunír at the southern end of the Misty Mountains. Orthanc is the stronghold of the wizard Saruman during the War of the Ring, and one of the great fortresses built by the Men of Gondor in the early Third Age.

Orthanc, the tower of Isengard.

Description

The tower was built by the engineers of Gondor from four many-sided columns of hard black stone joined together and crowned with sharp peaks, rising some 500 feet above the plain of Isengard. Its rock was impervious to damage — even the Ents could not break it. A stair of 27 steps led to its single door; above lay the chambers of the tower and an observatory at the summit. The name is deliberately ambiguous in Tolkien's fiction: in Sindarin it means "Mount Fang", while in the language of the Mark it can be read as "the Cunning Mind".

History within the legendarium

Orthanc was raised in the days of Gondor's strength and held one of the seven palantíri (seeing-stones). As Gondor waned, the tower was effectively abandoned; in TA 2759 the Steward Beren formally granted the keys of Orthanc to Saruman, who made it his home. From Orthanc, Saruman spied on his neighbours through the palantír, was ensnared by Sauron, bred an army of Uruk-hai and Orcs, and industrialised the valley, felling its trees. During the War of the Ring, the Ents destroyed Isengard's works but could not harm the tower, imprisoning Saruman within it. After Saruman's departure the tower was returned to the King of the Reunited Kingdom, and the palantír — thrown down by Gríma Wormtongue and recovered — was restored to use by King Elessar.
