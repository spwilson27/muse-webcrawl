Zigbee is an IEEE 802.15.4-based specification for a suite of high-level communication protocols used to create personal area networks with small, low-power digital radios. It is designed for applications requiring low data rates, long battery life, and secure networking, such as home automation, sensor networks, industrial control, and smart lighting. The name refers to the waggle dance of honey bees after their return to the beehive.

Overview

Zigbee builds on the physical layer and media access control defined by IEEE 802.15.4, adding network and application layers. It operates in the unlicensed 2.4 GHz band worldwide, with additional regional bands at 915 MHz (Americas) and 868 MHz (Europe). Data rates range from 20 kbit/s to 250 kbit/s. A key feature is mesh networking: devices can relay data for one another, extending range and improving reliability across networks that may contain hundreds or thousands of nodes.

Device types

  • Coordinator — forms and manages the network; one per network.
  • Router — a full-function device that forwards data for other devices.
  • End device — a reduced-function, low-power device that communicates through a parent router or coordinator and can sleep most of the time.

Applications

Zigbee is widely used in smart home products — including lighting systems such as Philips Hue, door locks, thermostats and sensors — as well as in smart metering, building automation, and healthcare monitoring. The Zigbee 3.0 standard unified earlier application profiles to improve interoperability. The Connectivity Standards Alliance (formerly the Zigbee Alliance) oversees the specification, and has also developed the newer Matter standard, which can operate alongside Zigbee networks.
