American League

The American League of Professional Baseball Clubs, known simply as the American League (AL), is one of the two leagues that make up Major League Baseball in the United States and Canada. Founded as a major league in 1901 under Ban Johnson, it has fifteen teams divided into East, Central and West divisions.

History

The league grew from the Western League and challenged the established National League by signing star players and placing clubs in major cities. Peace between the leagues in 1903 led to the first World Series. The AL became famous for franchises including the New York Yankees, Boston Red Sox, Chicago White Sox and Detroit Tigers.

Rules and play

For most of the period from 1973 to 2021, the American League differed from the National League by using the designated hitter. MLB adopted a universal designated hitter with the 2022 season, reducing the practical distinction between the leagues, though separate league identities, championships and awards remain.
