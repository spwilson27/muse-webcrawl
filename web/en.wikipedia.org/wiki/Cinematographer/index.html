Cinematographer

A cinematographer (also called a director of photography, DP or DOP) is the person responsible for the visual look of a film, television production, or other live-action piece. The cinematographer works closely with the director to realise the artistic vision of the work through camera, lighting, lens, and framing choices.

Role

The cinematographer heads the camera and lighting departments, choosing cameras, lenses, film stock or digital settings, filters, and lighting design. Working with the director and production designer, they establish the colour palette, contrast, and camera movement language of the film during pre-production and supervise the grade in post-production.

History

In early cinema the camera operator was often also the director. As productions grew, the roles separated, and cinematographers such as Gregg Toland — famed for the deep-focus work in Citizen Kane (1941) — became recognised as major creative artists. Professional societies include the American Society of Cinematographers (ASC) and the British Society of Cinematographers (BSC).

Recognition

The Academy Award for Best Cinematography has been presented since the first Academy Awards in 1929. Multiple winners include Roger Deakins, Emmanuel Lubezki, and Vittorio Storaro.
