Style guide

A style guide (also style manual) is a set of standards for the writing, formatting, and design of documents. It is often called a style sheet, though that term has multiple meanings. A style guide can be developed for general use or for a specific publication, organization, or field, and it establishes standard style to help ensure consistency within a document and across multiple documents.

Scope

Style guides typically cover:

  • Language and grammar: preferred spellings, capitalization, punctuation, and usage.
  • Citations: formats for references, footnotes, and bibliographies.
  • Numbers and units: how to present dates, measurements, currencies, and statistics.
  • Design: typography, layout, colors, and logo usage (in brand style guides).
  • Inclusive language: guidance on respectful and unbiased terminology.

Notable style guides

  • The Chicago Manual of Style — widely used in book publishing.
  • APA style — the Publication Manual of the American Psychological Association, used in the social sciences.
  • MLA Handbook — used in the humanities.
  • The Associated Press Stylebook — standard for American journalism.
  • The Elements of Style by Strunk and White — a concise classic on English prose.
  • MHRA Style Guide and New Hart's Rules — used in British academic and trade publishing.

House style

Many publishers and organizations maintain their own house style, which supplements or overrides a general style guide. Wikipedia itself maintains a Manual of Style to standardize its articles.
