Kruger National Park

Kruger National Park is one of Africa's largest game reserves, located in the provinces of Limpopo and Mpumalanga in north-eastern South Africa. It covers an area of 19,623 km² (7,576 sq mi), stretching 360 km (220 mi) from north to south and 65 km (40 mi) from east to west. The park is administered by South African National Parks (SANParks) and is the flagship of South Africa's national park system.

History

The area was first protected in 1898, when President Paul Kruger of the South African Republic proclaimed the Sabie Game Reserve to control hunting and protect dwindling wildlife. The reserve was greatly expanded and renamed in Kruger's honour when the National Parks Act of 1926 formally created Kruger National Park, South Africa's first national park. Its first warden, James Stevenson-Hamilton, served from 1902 to 1946 and shaped its early development. The park first opened to motorists in 1927.

Wildlife and landscape

The park's habitats range from the granite hills of the south-west and the Lebombo Mountains in the east to tropical forests in the north. Its biodiversity is exceptional: roughly 147 mammal species, more than 500 bird species, and 114 reptile species. It is renowned for the "Big Five" — lion, leopard, elephant, rhinoceros and buffalo — and supports one of the world's largest rhino populations, as well as notable numbers of African wild dogs, giraffes, hippos and many antelope species. Major rivers, including the Sabie, Olifants, Crocodile, Letaba, Luvuvhu and Limpopo, cross the park from west to east.

Since 2002 the park has formed part of the Great Limpopo Transfrontier Park, linked with parks in Mozambique and Zimbabwe. In recent decades the park has faced major challenges from rhino poaching for the illegal horn trade, and recurrent droughts.
