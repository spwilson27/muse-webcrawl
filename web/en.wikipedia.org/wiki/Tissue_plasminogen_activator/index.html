Tissue plasminogen activator

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) is a protein involved in the breakdown of blood clots. It is a serine protease found on endothelial cells that converts plasminogen to plasmin, the enzyme that digests fibrin, the main structural protein of clots. It is classified as a thrombolytic (clot-busting) drug when used therapeutically.

Physiology

tPA is released from endothelial cells in response to stimuli including exercise, bradykinin and shear stress. It binds preferentially to fibrin, so plasmin formation is localised to the clot and its circulating level and activity are tightly controlled by inhibitors such as plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1).

Medical use

Recombinant forms of tPA — alteplase, and the genetically engineered variants reteplase and tenecteplase — are used in acute ischemic stroke, myocardial infarction and massive pulmonary embolism. In stroke, alteplase given within 4.5 hours of symptom onset improves the chance of a good functional outcome, but the same treatment carries a risk of intracranial haemorrhage, and patients must be screened carefully for bleeding risk. Tenecteplase, which can be given as a single bolus, has increasingly replaced alteplase in many stroke and cardiac protocols.
