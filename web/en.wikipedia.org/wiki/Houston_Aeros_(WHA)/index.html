Houston Aeros (WHA)

The Houston Aeros were a professional ice hockey team based in Houston, Texas, that played in the World Hockey Association (WHA) from 1972 to 1978. One of the league's most successful franchises, the Aeros won the Avco World Trophy as WHA champions in 1974 and 1975. The team's name was later revived by minor-league clubs in Houston, including an American Hockey League team that played from 1994 to 2013.

History

The franchise was originally awarded to Dayton, Ohio as the Dayton Aeros, but moved to Houston before playing a game because of the lack of a suitable arena. In 1973 the Aeros achieved the WHA's biggest coup by signing Gordie Howe, who came out of retirement to play alongside his sons Mark and Marty Howe. With "Mr. Hockey" in the lineup, Houston won consecutive championships in 1974 and 1975, defeating the Chicago Cougars and the Quebec Nordiques, and reached the finals again in 1976.

Dissolution

Despite their success on the ice and at the gate, the Aeros were excluded from the 1979 NHL–WHA merger negotiations, in part because the NHL had little interest in the Houston market and the Summit arena arrangements complicated admission. Owner Kenneth Schnitzer folded the team in July 1978 rather than continue in a diminished WHA.

Legacy

Gordie Howe's six seasons in the WHA, mostly with Houston, extended his professional career to 1980. Mark Howe went on to a Hall of Fame career in the NHL. The Aeros name remains strongly associated with hockey in Houston.
