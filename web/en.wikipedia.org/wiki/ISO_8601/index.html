DomainRepresentations of dates and times

ISO 8601 is an international standard covering the worldwide exchange and communication of date- and time-related data. Published by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO), it provides a unique, unambiguous notation for dates, times, and durations, designed to avoid the misinterpretations that arise from differing national conventions.

The standard's most familiar contribution is the all-numeric date format YYYY-MM-DD, for example 2025-03-14 for 14 March 2025. Dates are written from the most significant to the least significant unit, so that alphabetical ordering coincides with chronological ordering. Extended formats use separators (hyphens and colons) while basic formats omit them (e.g. 20250314T093000Z).

Representations

Dates: YYYY-MM-DD; ordinal dates like 2025-073 for the 73rd day of the year; week dates such as 2025-W11-5 (the fifth day of week 11). Times: hh:mm:ss, with decimal fractions permitted (hh:mm:ss.sss), and times of day may include a designator for the time zone — Z for UTC ("zulu time") or an offset such as +02:00. Combined: date and time are joined by T, e.g. 2025-03-14T09:30:00Z. Durations: PnYnMnDTnHnMnS, e.g. P3Y6M4DT12H30M5S. Intervals may be given with a solidus separating start and end.

History and adoption

ISO 8601 originated in 1988 from ISO 2014, 2015, 2711, 3307 and 4031, and was last revised as ISO 8601-1:2019 (fundamental rules) and ISO 8601-2:2019 (extensions, including imprecise dates, seasons, and set expressions). It underlies timestamps on the internet — a profile of ISO 8601 is used in RFC 3339 — and in computing broadly, from file naming to database storage. Monday is designated the first day of the week (week 01 is the week containing the year's first Thursday).
