Hotel California is the fifth studio album by American rock band the Eagles, released by Asylum Records on December 8, 1976. It was the first Eagles album with guitarist Joe Walsh, who replaced founding member Bernie Leadon, and the last to feature bassist Randy Meisner. The album became the band's best-selling studio release, with more than 26 million copies sold in the United States and over 32 million worldwide.

Content

The album is a loose concept record about the excesses and disillusionment of American life in the 1970s, particularly the Los Angeles music scene. Its title track, "Hotel California", with its famous dual-guitar coda by Don Felder and Joe Walsh, won the Grammy Award for Record of the Year. Other well-known songs include "New Kid in Town", which also won a Grammy, "Life in the Fast Lane", "Victim of Love", and the closing epic "The Last Resort".

Cover

The cover photograph shows the Beverly Hills Hotel at sunset, shot by David Alexander; the gatefold image of the band and friends in the hotel lobby fuelled decades of fan speculation about hidden figures and meanings.

Reception and legacy

Hotel California topped the US Billboard 200 and is consistently ranked among the greatest albums of all time, appearing in Rolling Stone's list of the 500 greatest albums. In 2017 a 40th-anniversary deluxe edition was released with remastered audio and live recordings.
