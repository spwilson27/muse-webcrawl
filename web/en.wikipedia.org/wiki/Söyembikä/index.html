Söyembikä (Tatar: Сөембикә; Russian: Сююмбике, Syuyumbike; 1516 – after 1554) was a Tatar noblewoman and regent of the Khanate of Kazan. A daughter of the Nogai bey Yusuf, she is one of the most celebrated figures in Tatar history and a national heroine of Tatarstan.

Life

Söyembikä was first married to the Kazan khan Canğali. After his murder in 1535 she was married to his successor, Safagäräy. When Safagäräy died in 1549, their infant son Ütämeşgäräy became khan and Söyembikä ruled as regent. Her government faced a pro-Moscow faction among the Kazan nobility, and in 1551, under the terms of a treaty imposed by Ivan the Terrible, she and her son were handed over to Moscow. She was then forcibly married to the Kasimov khan Şahğäli, a Russian vassal — an act remembered in Tatar tradition as a profound humiliation. She died in captivity, separated from her son, who was baptized and raised at the Russian court.

Legacy

Popular legend holds that Ivan the Terrible wished to marry Söyembikä and that she agreed only if he built a tower in Kazan in seven days; when the Söyembikä Tower was finished, she climbed it and threw herself from the top rather than submit. The leaning tower in the Kazan Kremlin that bears her name — in fact built later, probably in the late 17th or early 18th century — remains the architectural symbol of Kazan and of Tatar identity.
