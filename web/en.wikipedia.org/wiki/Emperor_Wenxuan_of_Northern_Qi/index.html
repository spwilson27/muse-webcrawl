Emperor Wenxuan of Northern Qi

Emperor Wenxuan of Northern Qi (北齊文宣帝) (526–559), personal name Gao Yang (高洋), courtesy name Zijin (子進), was the founding emperor of the Northern Qi dynasty of China. The second son of the Eastern Wei paramount general Gao Huan, he forced Emperor Xiaojing of Eastern Wei to yield the throne to him in 550, ending Eastern Wei and establishing Northern Qi.

Reign

Early in his reign, Gao Yang was a capable and diligent ruler. He strengthened the military, campaigned successfully against the Göktürks to the north and the Liang dynasty to the south, reformed the legal code, and—though of Xianbei descent himself—promoted Chinese officials and institutions. Northern Qi was at this time the strongest of the Chinese successor states.

Later years

In his later years, the emperor descended into alcoholism and episodes of extreme cruelty and erratic behaviour, murdering officials, princes, and concubines on impulse and undertaking extravagant building projects. He died in 559 at the age of 33, reportedly from the effects of alcohol. He was succeeded by his son Gao Yin (Emperor Fei), whom his brother Gao Yan deposed within a year. Despite his violent end, historians credit Wenxuan's early administration with laying the foundations of the Northern Qi state.
