Kuril Islands dispute

The Kuril Islands dispute, known as the Northern Territories dispute in Japan, is a territorial dispute between Japan and Russia over four islands at the southern end of the Kuril chain: Iturup (Etorofu), Kunashir (Kunashiri), Shikotan and the Habomai group.

History

The 1855 Treaty of Shimoda placed the border between Japan and Russia between Iturup and Urup, confirming the four islands as Japanese. The Soviet Union occupied the islands in the closing days of the Second World War in 1945, and expelled the Japanese inhabitants. The 1951 San Francisco Peace Treaty, which the USSR did not sign, had Japan renounce the Kuril Islands without naming a beneficiary, and Japan maintains the four islands are not part of the Kurils renounced.

Status

A 1956 joint declaration restored diplomatic relations and envisaged transferring Shikotan and Habomai after a peace treaty, but no treaty has ever been concluded. Russia administers the islands as part of Sakhalin Oblast; Japan claims them as its Northern Territories, administered from Hokkaido. The dispute remains the principal obstacle to a formal post-war settlement between the two countries, and talks have stalled further since 2022.
