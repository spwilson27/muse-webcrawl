National Memorial Arboretum

National Memorial Arboretum
Location: Alrewas, Staffordshire, England
Area: 150 acres (61 ha)
Opened: 16 May 2001

The National Memorial Arboretum is a British site of national remembrance at Alrewas, near Lichfield, Staffordshire. Comprising 150 acres of woodland on reclaimed gravel workings beside the River Tame, it contains more than 400 memorials to the armed forces, emergency services, and civilian organizations, and some 25,000 trees planted as living tributes.

History

The arboretum was inspired by Commander David Childs, who founded the project after visiting Arlington National Cemetery and the National Arboretum in Washington, D.C. Backed by Group Captain Leonard Cheshire and the Royal British Legion, planting began in 1997, and the site opened to the public on 16 May 2001. It is managed by the National Memorial Arboretum Company, part of the Royal British Legion family of charities.

Armed Forces Memorial

The centrepiece is the Armed Forces Memorial, a large circular Portland stone structure designed by Liam O'Connor, opened by Queen Elizabeth II in 2007. It bears the names of over 16,000 British service personnel killed on duty or by terrorism since the end of the Second World War; its design allows a shaft of sunlight to fall on the central wreath at the 11th hour of the 11th day of the 11th month.

Other memorials

Other prominent memorials include the Shot at Dawn memorial, the Basra Memorial Wall (relocated from Iraq), the Polar Bear Memorial, and the Burma Railway and Royal National Lifeboat Institution memorials. The Millennium Chapel of Peace and Forgiveness holds a daily act of remembrance.
