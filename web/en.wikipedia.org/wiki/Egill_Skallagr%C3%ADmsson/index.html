Egill Skallagrímsson

Egill Skallagrímsson (Old Norse: [ˈeɣelː ˈskalːaˌɡriːmsˌson]; Modern Icelandic: [ˈeiːjɪtl̥ ˈskatlaˌkrimsˌsɔːn]; c. 904 – c. 995) was a Viking Age war poet, sorcerer, berserker, and farmer. He is known mainly as the protagonist of Egils saga, an Icelandic saga which may have been written by Snorri Sturluson. He is regarded as one of the greatest skalds of the Viking Age.

Life

Egill was born in Iceland, the son of Skalla-Grímr Kveldúlfsson and Bera Yngvarsdóttir. His family had fled Norway after conflicts with King Harald Fairhair. According to the saga, Egill composed his first poem at the age of three and committed his first killing at seven. As a young man he travelled widely, fighting and composing poetry in Norway, England, and elsewhere in Scandinavia, and he became embroiled in a long feud with King Eric Bloodaxe of Norway.

In one of the saga's most famous episodes, Egill, captured and condemned to death by Eric at York, composed the praise poem Höfuðlausn ("Head-Ransom") overnight and recited it in the morning, winning his freedom. Another celebrated poem, Sonatorrek ("Loss of Sons"), laments the deaths of his sons Böðvarr and Gunnarr. Egill eventually settled at Borg in Iceland, lived to a great age, and died around 995.

Poetry

Egill's surviving works include Höfuðlausn, Sonatorrek, and Arinbjarnarkviða, a poem in praise of his friend Arinbjörn Þórisson, along with numerous single stanzas (lausavísur). His verse is noted for its complex kennings, emotional intensity, and personal voice.
