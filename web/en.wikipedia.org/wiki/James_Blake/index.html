James Blake Litherland (born 26 September 1988) is an English singer, songwriter, and record producer. He first gained recognition for a series of 2010 EPs blending dubstep-influenced production with soul singing, and his Mercury Prize-winning second album Overgrown (2013) established him internationally.

Career

Blake studied popular music at Goldsmiths, University of London. His self-titled debut album (2011), featuring a celebrated cover of Feist's "Limit to Your Love," was shortlisted for the Mercury Prize. Overgrown (2013) won the prize and included the single "Retrograde."

Later work

Subsequent albums — The Colour in Anything (2016), the more upbeat Assume Form (2019), Friends That Break Your Heart (2021), and Playing Robots into Heaven (2023) — saw him collaborate widely. As a producer and featured artist he has worked with Beyoncé, Kendrick Lamar, Jay-Z, Frank Ocean, and Travis Scott, winning a Grammy for his contribution to the Black Panther soundtrack era of Lamar's catalogue.

Style

Blake's music is characterized by sparse, bass-heavy electronic production, pitched and looped vocal fragments, piano, and a falsetto delivery influenced by gospel and R&B.
