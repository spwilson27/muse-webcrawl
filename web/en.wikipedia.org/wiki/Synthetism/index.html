Synthetism

Synthetism is a term used by Post-Impressionist artists, principally Paul Gauguin, Émile Bernard, and their associates at Pont-Aven in Brittany in the late 1880s, to distinguish their work from Impressionism. Synthetist works are characterised by flat areas of colour, bold outlines (related to cloisonnism), and a synthesis of the outward appearance of a subject with the artist's feelings about it and with purely aesthetic considerations.

Characteristics

The Synthetists rejected the faithful transcription of nature in favour of distilling its essence: simplified forms, expressive and non-naturalistic colour, and subjects drawn from memory and imagination. Gauguin's Vision of the Sermon (1888) and The Yellow Christ (1889) exemplify the style, which was showcased at the Volpini Exhibition of 1889 in Paris.

Influence

Synthetism influenced the Nabis, Fauvism, and the development of modernist abstraction by legitimising the expressive distortion of form and colour.
