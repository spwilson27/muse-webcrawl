Tooth development

FieldDevelopmental biology, dentistry
Also calledOdontogenesis
StagesBud, cap, bell, apposition, maturation

Tooth development, or odontogenesis, is the complex process by which teeth form from embryonic cells, grow, and erupt into the mouth. In humans it begins in the sixth week of embryonic development and continues into early adulthood with the third molars.

Stages

Tooth formation is conventionally divided into the bud, cap and bell stages, followed by apposition and maturation. It begins with the dental lamina, a band of epithelial tissue in the developing jaw. Buds of epithelium grow into the underlying mesenchyme and are progressively shaped by signalling centres such as the enamel knot. Cells differentiate into ameloblasts, which secrete enamel, and odontoblasts, which secrete dentin; other tissues formed include cementum, the pulp and the periodontal ligament.

Timeline

The first deciduous (primary) teeth begin to calcify in the second trimester and typically erupt from around six months of age. The permanent dentition begins forming before birth, with first molars erupting around age six and the full set, excluding third molars, usually in place by the early teens. Defects in tooth development can produce missing teeth (hypodontia), extra teeth, or disorders of enamel and dentin such as amelogenesis imperfecta and dentinogenesis imperfecta.
