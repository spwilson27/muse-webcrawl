Triangle choke

A triangle choke, or sankaku-jime in judo, is a type of figure-four chokehold applied from the guard position in grappling arts such as Brazilian jiu-jitsu and judo, in which the practitioner's legs encircle the opponent's neck and one arm, forming a triangle that compresses the carotid arteries.

Mechanism

The attacker traps the opponent's head and one arm between their legs, locks the ankles in a figure-four configuration, and squeezes while raising the hips. The opponent's own shoulder is driven into one side of the neck while the attacker's thigh compresses the other, restricting blood flow to the brain.

Use

The triangle choke is one of the most common submissions in mixed martial arts, famously used by Royce Gracie in the early Ultimate Fighting Championship events. Variations include the rear triangle and the mounted triangle. See Brazilian jiu-jitsu and chokehold.
