Hassan Ahmed Abdel Rahman Muhammed al-Banna (17 October 1906 – 12 February 1949), known as Hassan al-Banna, was an Egyptian schoolteacher and imam who founded the Muslim Brotherhood in 1928, one of the largest and most influential Islamist movements of the twentieth century. As the Brotherhood's first "General Guide" (al-Murshid al-'Am), he shaped its ideology of comprehensive Islamic revival encompassing religion, society, and politics.

Early life

Al-Banna was born in Mahmoudia, in the Nile Delta, the son of a watch repairer and imam who had studied at al-Azhar. Deeply influenced by Sufi practice in his youth and by reformist thinkers such as Rashid Rida, he trained as a teacher in Cairo and graduated from Dar al-Ulum in 1927. He was posted to Ismailia, a city dominated by the British-controlled Suez Canal Company, where the contrast between foreign privilege and Egyptian poverty sharpened his critique of colonialism and secularisation.

Founding of the Muslim Brotherhood

In March 1928, al-Banna and six others founded the Society of the Muslim Brothers in Ismailia. The movement preached a return to the Qur'an and Sunna as a total way of life — his famous formulation held Islam to be "a creed and worship, a homeland and nationality, a religion and a state." Through preaching, schools, clinics, scout troops, and publications, the Brotherhood grew into a mass movement with hundreds of branches across Egypt by the 1940s, and spread to other Arab countries.

Final years and assassination

The Brotherhood's paramilitary wing, the "Secret Apparatus," was implicated in political violence in the late 1940s, including assassinations; after the murder of Prime Minister Mahmoud Fahmi al-Nuqrashi in December 1948, the government dissolved the movement. Al-Banna condemned the violence and sought negotiations, but on 12 February 1949 he was shot in Cairo and died, widely believed to have been killed by government agents. His writings, including his memoirs and the Epistles (Rasa'il), remain foundational texts for Islamist movements. His grandson is the scholar Tariq Ramadan.
