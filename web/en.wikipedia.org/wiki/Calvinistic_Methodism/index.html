Calvinistic Methodism

Calvinistic Methodism was a strand of the 18th-century Methodist revival that, unlike the Arminian Methodism of John Wesley, held to the Calvinist doctrines of predestination and particular redemption. It was associated especially with Wales and with the preaching of George Whitefield.

Origins

The Welsh revival began with Howell Harris and Daniel Rowland in the 1730s. In England, Whitefield's open-air preaching carried Calvinistic Methodism widely, and the Countess of Huntingdon's Connexion supported chapels and a college at Trevecca. The breach with Wesley became formal over the doctrine of election.

Wales

In 1811 the Welsh Calvinistic Methodists ordained their own ministers, breaking from the Church of England and forming a Presbyterian-style denomination, later known as the Presbyterian Church of Wales. The movement profoundly shaped Welsh religious and cultural life in the 19th century.
