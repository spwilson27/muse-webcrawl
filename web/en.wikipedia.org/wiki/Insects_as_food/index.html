Insects as food

Insects as food, or edible insects, are insect species used in human nutrition, a practice known as entomophagy. Around 2 billion people worldwide are estimated to eat insects as part of their traditional diets, with more than 2,000 documented edible species.

History

Humans have consumed insects since prehistoric times. Edible insects remain common in parts of Asia, Africa, Latin America and Oceania, where species such as grasshoppers, crickets, silkworm pupae, mopane worms and palm weevil larvae are eaten as delicacies or staple protein sources.

Nutrition

Edible insects are generally high in protein, healthy fats, fibre, and micronutrients such as iron, zinc and B vitamins. Their nutritional content varies widely by species and life stage.

Modern markets

Since the 2010s, insect-based foods such as cricket flour and protein bars have entered Western markets, promoted as sustainable alternatives to conventional meat. The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations has encouraged research into insects for food security.
