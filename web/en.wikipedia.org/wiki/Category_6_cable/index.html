Category 6 cable (Cat 6) is a standardised twisted-pair cable used for Ethernet and other network physical layers, defined in the ANSI/TIA-568 and ISO/IEC 11801 standards. It is backward compatible with the earlier Category 5 and Category 5e standards.

Characteristics

Cat 6 cable is specified for channel bandwidths up to 250 MHz, roughly double that of Cat 5e. It supports Gigabit Ethernet over the full 100-metre channel length and 10 Gigabit Ethernet over reduced distances (up to about 55 metres, depending on the alien-crosstalk environment). The tighter crosstalk specifications are achieved through stricter manufacturing tolerances, heavier-gauge conductors (typically 23 AWG), and, in many designs, a plastic spline separating the four twisted pairs.

Category 6A

The augmented Category 6A standard (Class EA) extends bandwidth to 500 MHz and supports 10 Gigabit Ethernet over the full 100 metres. Cat 6A cabling is typically thicker and less flexible than Cat 6 because of additional shielding and pair separation.

Termination

Like its predecessors, Cat 6 uses the 8P8C ("RJ45") modular connector in structured cabling installations, terminated to either the T568A or T568B pin assignments.
