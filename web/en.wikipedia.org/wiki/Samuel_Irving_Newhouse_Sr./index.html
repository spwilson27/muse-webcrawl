Samuel Irving Newhouse Sr. (May 24, 1895 – August 29, 1979) was an American broadcasting businessman and publisher. He built one of the largest newspaper chains in the United States and founded Advance Publications, the media empire that later acquired the Condé Nast magazine group.

Early life

Newhouse was born Solomon Neuhaus on Manhattan's Lower East Side, the eldest of eight children of Jewish immigrants from Russia and Austria-Hungary. He left school early to work as an office boy at a law firm in Bayonne, New Jersey, studied at night, and passed the bar; a successful turnaround assignment for the struggling Bayonne Times redirected his career into newspapers.

Newspaper empire

In 1922 Newhouse bought the Staten Island Advance, the nucleus of Advance Publications. Over the following decades he acquired papers including the Newark Star-Ledger, the Cleveland Plain Dealer, the New Orleans Times-Picayune, the Syracuse papers and the Portland Oregonian, typically buying undervalued properties, cutting costs and ploughing profits into further purchases. He also entered broadcasting and cable television.

Condé Nast and legacy

In 1959 Newhouse bought Condé Nast, publisher of Vogue and Vanity Fair, reportedly as a gift for his wife Mitzi, and later added Random House (sold in 1980, after his death). On his death in 1979 the business passed to his sons, S. I. Newhouse Jr. and Donald Newhouse. His name is commemorated by the S. I. Newhouse School of Public Communications at Syracuse University.
