Petit Le Mans (French for "Little Le Mans") is a sports car endurance race held annually at Road Atlanta in Braselton, Georgia, United States. It was founded by entrepreneur Don Panoz and first run in 1998, inspired by the 24 Hours of Le Mans in France.

The race was originally contested over 1,000 miles (1,600 km) or ten hours, whichever came first; it is now a ten-hour race that serves as the season finale of the IMSA SportsCar Championship. Class winners at Petit Le Mans once received automatic invitations to the following year's 24 Hours of Le Mans.

History

Panoz created the event in 1998, and in 1999 it became the flagship race of the new American Le Mans Series. The race attracted factory teams from Audi, Porsche, Peugeot, and others; Audi's R8 and R10 TDI prototypes dominated much of the 2000s, with Rinaldo Capello among the most successful drivers.

Following the merger of the American Le Mans Series and the Grand-Am Rolex Sports Car Series, Petit Le Mans joined the calendar of the unified IMSA championship in 2014, maintaining its role as the season-ending endurance classic alongside the 24 Hours of Daytona and the 12 Hours of Sebring.
