Eger Castle

Eger Castle

Location: Eger, Heves County, Hungary

Built: 13th century (after the Mongol invasion); repeatedly rebuilt

Famous event: Siege of 1552

Today: István Dobó Castle Museum

Eger Castle (Hungarian: Egri vár) is a historic fortress in the city of Eger in northern Hungary. The present castle, built on Castle Hill above the old town, is celebrated above all for the heroic defence of 1552, when its garrison withstood a major Ottoman siege.

History

The first castle on the site was an early medieval episcopal stronghold destroyed during the Mongol invasion of 1241. The bishops of Eger rebuilt it in stone in the following decades, and the fortress was progressively modernised with Italianate bastions in the sixteenth century as the Ottoman threat grew.

Siege of 1552

In the autumn of 1552 an Ottoman army — traditionally numbered in the tens of thousands — besieged Eger for 39 days. The garrison of about 2,000 soldiers and armed townsfolk, commanded by Captain István Dobó, repelled repeated assaults until the attackers withdrew with the onset of winter. The defence became one of Hungary's most cherished national legends, retold in Géza Gárdonyi's novel Eclipse of the Crescent Moon (1899), in which Gergely Bornemissza, Dobó's lieutenant and an ingenious maker of explosive devices, is a central figure.

Ottoman period and after

The castle finally fell to the Ottomans in 1596 and remained in their hands until 1687. After the reconquest its military role declined, and parts were demolished in the early eighteenth century to prevent use by rebels. Excavation and restoration began in the twentieth century.

The castle today

The castle houses the István Dobó Castle Museum, with exhibitions on the fortress's history, a picture gallery, lapidarium and casemates. Gárdonyi himself is buried within the castle grounds. The complex is one of Hungary's most visited historic sites and hosts festivals and re-enactments of the siege.
