Relativity (M. C. Escher)

Relativity is a lithograph print by the Dutch artist M. C. Escher, first printed in December 1953. The work depicts a paradoxical world in which the normal laws of gravity do not apply: within the same architectural structure, three separate gravitational pulls operate, so that figures can walk on staircases and floors oriented in different directions while remaining upright relative to their own world.

Description

The building shown contains many staircases — sixteen in total — and numerous human figures, featureless android-like inhabitants who move through the space in different gravitational orientations. Three communities coexist, each oblivious to the others, each using different walls as floors. Doors and staircases connect the worlds in ways that appear impossible from any single viewpoint.

Interpretation and influence

Relativity is one of Escher's best-known works and a classic example of his exploration of impossible objects and multiple perspectives. The image has been widely reproduced and referenced in popular culture, including in films, television and album art, and is frequently used to illustrate concepts of perspective and relative frames of reference.
