Jabberwocky

"Jabberwocky" is a nonsense poem written by Lewis Carroll about the killing of a creature named "the Jabberwock". It was included in his 1871 novel Through the Looking-Glass, and What Alice Found There, the sequel to Alice's Adventures in Wonderland (1865). It is widely considered one of the greatest nonsense poems written in English.

John Tenniel's illustration of the Jabberwock, 1871
'Twas brillig, and the slithy toves
Did gyre and gimble in the wabe;
All mimsy were the borogoves,
And the mome raths outgrabe.

Structure and story

The poem's seven quatrains (in ballad meter, largely iambic tetrameter) tell a miniature heroic quest: a father warns his son of the Jabberwock "with eyes of flame," the boy takes his vorpal sword, finds the monster beneath the Tumtum tree, slays it in combat ("One, two! One, two! And through and through"), and returns with its head to his father's joy ("O frabjous day! Callooh! Callay!"). The opening stanza is repeated verbatim at the close.

Nonsense vocabulary

Many of Carroll's invented words — "chortle," "galumphing," "frabjous," "vorpal," "manxome," "uffish" — entered the English lexicon. In the novel, Humpty Dumpty glosses the first stanza for Alice, explaining portmanteau coinages such as "slithy" (lithe + slimy) and "mimsy" (flimsy + miserable). The poem has been translated into dozens of languages, a notable feat for a work whose words are largely invented.
