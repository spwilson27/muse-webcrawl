The Khunjerab Pass (Urdu: درہ خنجراب; Chinese: 红其拉甫口岸) is a mountain pass at 4,693 metres (15,397 ft) on the border between Pakistan and China, in the Karakoram mountains. It is the highest paved international border crossing in the world and the highest point on the Karakoram Highway, connecting Pakistan's Gilgit-Baltistan with China's Xinjiang region. The name derives from the Wakhi language and is usually translated as "valley of blood".

Geography

The pass sits on a broad, high plateau of alpine steppe, grazed by yaks and home to wildlife including Himalayan ibex and, rarely, snow leopards. On the Pakistani side the pass lies within Khunjerab National Park, created to protect the Marco Polo sheep and other high-altitude fauna. The watershed here divides the Indus drainage to the south from the Tarim Basin to the north.

The Karakoram Highway

The crossing was opened to trade in 1982 and to foreign tourists in 1986, following the completion of the Karakoram Highway, jointly built by Pakistan and China between 1959 and 1979. Historically the border post closed in winter because of snow and extreme cold, but since 2024 Pakistan and China have moved to keep the pass open year-round to support trade under the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC).

Traffic side

The pass is notable as the point where traffic changes sides: Pakistan drives on the left, China on the right. Signs mark the switchover at the border. Nearby monuments include the China–Pakistan friendship gate, one of the highest ATMs in the world (installed by the National Bank of Pakistan at about 4,690 m), and altitude warnings for travellers.
