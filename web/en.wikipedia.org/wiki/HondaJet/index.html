The HondaJet is a light business jet produced by the Honda Aircraft Company in Greensboro, North Carolina, United States. It is the first production aircraft of Honda and is distinguished by its unusual over-the-wing engine mount configuration, a natural laminar flow wing and nose, and a fuselage made largely of composite materials.

Development

Honda began secret research into a small business jet in the 1980s under engineer Michimasa Fujino; a proof-of-concept aircraft first flew on 3 December 2003. The programme was publicly announced in 2005, and the Honda Aircraft Company was established in 2006. The first FAA-conforming aircraft flew on 20 December 2010, and the type received Federal Aviation Administration certification in December 2015, with first deliveries that month.

Design

The over-the-wing engine mount frees fuselage space, reduces cabin noise and vibration, and lowers drag compared with rear-fuselage-mounted engines. The aircraft is powered by two GE Honda HF120 turbofans of about 2,050 lbf thrust each, developed jointly with GE Aviation. The HondaJet cruises at about 422 knots (782 km/h) at up to 43,000 ft (later models 47,000 ft), with a range of roughly 1,200–1,500 nautical miles depending on variant, and seats six passengers in a typical executive layout plus one or two crew. The avionics are based on the Garmin G3000 flight deck.

Variants

The improved HondaJet Elite (2018) added range and a quieter cabin; the Elite II (2022) added autothrottle, greater range and increased gross weight. The larger HondaJet 2600 / Echelon concept, announced in 2021, aims at transcontinental range with up to 11 occupants. The HondaJet was the best-selling aircraft in the very light jet category for several consecutive years after its introduction.
