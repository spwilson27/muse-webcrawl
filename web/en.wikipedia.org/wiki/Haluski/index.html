Haluski

Haluski (Polish: hałuski; Slovak: halušky) is a traditional Central and Eastern European dish of noodles or small dumplings cooked with fried cabbage, onions and butter. In its best-known Polish and Polish-American form it consists of egg noodles tossed with cabbage that has been slowly fried in butter (sometimes with bacon, kielbasa or onion) until soft and caramelised. The dish is a staple of church suppers, festivals and Lenten tables, where it is often served without meat.

Variations

The name derives from words for dumplings across the Slavic languages, and the term covers several related preparations. In Polish cuisine, hałuski may denote the combination of noodles and cabbage or a type of small potato or flour dumpling. Slovak bryndzové halušky — dumplings with sheep's cheese and bacon — is a different, nationally emblematic dish, while cabbage-based kapustové halušky is closer to the Polish version. In the industrial regions of the United States, especially Pittsburgh and the surrounding coal and steel towns, haluski became a signature comfort food of Polish, Slovak, Hungarian and other immigrant communities, commonly sold at parish festivals and food stands alongside pierogi.

Preparation

Sliced cabbage and onion are fried in butter or oil until tender and lightly browned, seasoned with salt and black pepper, and then mixed with boiled egg noodles or dumplings. Bacon fat, kielbasa slices or caraway seed may be added. The dish is inexpensive, filling and easily made in large quantities, which accounts for its enduring popularity as festival and fundraiser food.
