Sexual violence

Sexual violence is any sexual act, attempt to obtain a sexual act, or other act directed against a person's sexuality using coercion, by any person regardless of their relationship to the victim, in any setting. The World Health Organization (WHO) uses this definition in its World Report on Violence and Health (2002). The category includes rape, sexual assault, sexual abuse of children, forced marriage, sexual harassment involving physical contact, and sexual exploitation, and it occurs in peacetime and in armed conflict.

Prevalence

WHO estimates that globally about one in three women has experienced physical and/or sexual intimate partner violence or non-partner sexual violence in her lifetime, and that 7% of women have been sexually assaulted by someone other than a partner. Men and boys are also victims, including in detention and conflict settings, though data are sparser and under-reporting is severe for all victims. Most sexual violence is committed by persons known to the victim — partners, relatives, acquaintances, or authority figures.

Health consequences

Documented consequences include physical injury, sexually transmitted infections including HIV, unintended pregnancy, and long-term mental-health effects such as depression, anxiety, post-traumatic stress disorder, and elevated suicide risk. The WHO and UN agencies emphasise comprehensive post-rape care, including emergency contraception, STI prophylaxis, and psychosocial support.

Conflict-related sexual violence

Rape and other sexual violence have been used as tactics of war and instruments of terror and ethnic cleansing; since UN Security Council Resolution 1820 (2008) they have been recognised as threats to international peace and security, and specific acts have been prosecuted as war crimes and crimes against humanity at the ICTY, ICTR, and International Criminal Court.

Prevention and response

Evidence-based prevention frameworks, such as the WHO/UN Women RESPECT framework, focus on relationship skills, challenging harmful gender norms, education (including comprehensive sexuality education), economic empowerment, and legal reform. Victim services include hotlines, shelters, forensic examination, and legal aid.
