Ofqual

The Office of Qualifications and Examinations Regulation (Ofqual) is a non-ministerial government department of the United Kingdom that regulates qualifications, examinations, and assessments in England. Established in 2008 as part of the Qualifications and Curriculum Authority and made independent in April 2010 under the Apprenticeships, Skills, Children and Learning Act 2009, Ofqual is responsible for maintaining standards and confidence in qualifications such as GCSEs, A levels, and a wide range of vocational and technical qualifications.

Role and functions

Ofqual accredits and monitors awarding organisations (exam boards such as AQA, Pearson Edexcel, OCR, and WJEC Eduqas), sets rules for the design, delivery, marking, and grading of regulated qualifications, and intervenes where standards are at risk. Its statutory objectives include securing qualification standards, promoting public confidence, and ensuring efficiency and value for money. In Northern Ireland it also regulates vocational qualifications, while Scotland and Wales have their own regulators (the SQA and Qualifications Wales respectively).

Controversies and notable events

Ofqual gained wide public attention in 2020, when the COVID-19 pandemic led to the cancellation of summer exams and the use of an algorithm to standardise teacher-assessed grades. Following public protest over downgraded results, the algorithm was abandoned in favour of centre-assessed grades. Exams resumed in 2022 with mitigations, and grading standards were returned progressively toward pre-pandemic levels by 2023.

Leadership

Ofqual is headed by a Chief Regulator, appointed by the Secretary of State for Education, and governed by a board. Its policies on grade standards, including the use of comparable outcomes, are central to debates about grade inflation and assessment reform in England.
