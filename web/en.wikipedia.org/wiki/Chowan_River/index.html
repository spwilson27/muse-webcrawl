Chowan River


Placeholder image: the lower Chowan River

The Chowan River is a blackwater river formed by the confluence of the Blackwater and Nottoway rivers at the Virginia–North Carolina line, near Franklin, Virginia. It flows generally southeastward for about 50 miles (80 km) through northeastern North Carolina before emptying into Albemarle Sound near Edenton. Counting the Nottoway, its longest tributary system extends well over 100 miles into southern Virginia.

Course

The river forms the boundary between several North Carolina counties, including Hertford, Gates, Chowan and Bertie. Its principal tributaries include the Meherrin River and the Wiccacon River. In its lower reaches the river widens considerably into a broad, slow-moving estuary lined with cypress swamps. U.S. Route 17 crosses the river between Edenton and Hertford on a long bridge; an earlier crossing, the Albemarle Sound Bridge to the east, links Edenton with Mackeys.

History and name

The river's name derives from the Chowanoke (Chowanoc) people, an Algonquian-speaking tribe that was among the largest and most powerful in the region when English colonists arrived in the 17th century. The Chowan basin supported an important herring and shad fishery; Edenton and other river towns were early centres of the colonial fish trade, and pound-net fishing remained a local industry into the 21st century.

Ecology

The Chowan is a blackwater system, stained dark by tannins from the extensive swamp forests of its basin. It experienced severe algal blooms in the 1970s, which prompted pioneering state nutrient-management programmes. The basin supports anadromous fish including river herring and striped bass, and its bottomland hardwood wetlands are significant wildlife habitat.
