Khanate of Kazan

The Khanate of Kazan (Tatar: Qazan xanlığı) was a medieval Tatar state that existed from 1438 to 1552 in the area of the former Volga Bulgaria, in present-day Russia. It was one of the successor states of the Golden Horde. Its capital, Kazan, stood near the confluence of the Volga and Kazanka rivers. In 1552 the khanate was conquered by Ivan the Terrible of Russia, a landmark in Russian expansion.

History

The khanate was founded by Ulugh Muhammad, a claimant to the Golden Horde throne who seized Kazan in 1438. At its height it covered much of the middle Volga region, including the lands of the Tatars, Mari, Chuvash, Mordvins, Udmurts and Bashkirs. The khanate controlled important trade routes along the Volga and traded furs, honey, slaves and craft goods.

Relations with the Grand Duchy of Moscow alternated between alliance, vassalage and war. Kazan khans raided Russian lands, and Moscow repeatedly intervened in Kazan succession disputes, at times installing client khans from the allied Qasim Khanate. The Crimean Giray dynasty also competed for influence at the Kazan court.

Conquest

After failed campaigns in 1547–48 and 1549–50, Ivan IV built the fortress of Sviyazhsk near Kazan and in 1552 laid siege to the city with a large army and siege artillery. Kazan fell on 2 October 1552 after mines exploded beneath its walls; much of the population was killed or enslaved and the khanate was annexed. Resistance in the countryside continued for several years. The conquest opened the Volga to Russian colonisation and is commemorated in Moscow's Saint Basil's Cathedral, built to celebrate the victory.

Society

The khan was advised by a council (diwan) of leading clans. Society included a landowning aristocracy, Muslim clergy, merchants and artisans in the towns, and a largely non-Muslim tributary peasantry in the countryside. The ruling dynasty changed frequently, and enthronement of outside candidates — from Crimea, Astrakhan or Qasim — was common.
