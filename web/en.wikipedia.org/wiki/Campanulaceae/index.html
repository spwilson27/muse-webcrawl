The Campanulaceae, commonly called the bellflower family, are a family of flowering plants in the order Asterales, containing about 84 genera and roughly 2,380 species. The family takes its name from the genus Campanula, whose name derives from the Latin for "little bell", referring to the bell-shaped flowers.

Distribution and habitat

Members of the family are found on all continents except Antarctica, with the greatest diversity in temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere and in southern Africa. They occur in habitats ranging from alpine meadows and cliffs to forests and deserts.

Characteristics

Most species are herbs or shrubs with milky sap. The flowers are typically bisexual, with a fused corolla of five lobes, often bell-shaped, urn-shaped or tubular, and commonly blue or purple. The fruit is usually a capsule or berry. The family includes the subfamily Lobelioideae, containing the lobelias, which have bilaterally symmetrical flowers.

Uses

Many Campanula species are popular garden ornamentals, and the rampion bellflower was historically grown for its edible roots and leaves. Some Lobelia species have been used in traditional medicine, though several contain potent alkaloids.
