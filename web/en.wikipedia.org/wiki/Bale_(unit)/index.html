Bale (unit)

Compressed cotton bales ready for shipment.

A bale is a large compressed and bound bundle of material, and the word is also used as a unit of measurement or trade quantity for several commodities, most notably cotton, hay, wool, and paper. The mass or dimensions of a bale vary widely by commodity, country, and historical period, so a bale is a customary rather than a standardised scientific unit.

Cotton

In the international cotton trade, bale sizes have been partially standardised. A bale of cotton in the United States is defined by the United States Department of Agriculture as weighing 480 pounds (about 218 kg) net of lint cotton, and the "universal density bale" measures approximately 55 by 28 by 21 inches. Historically, bale weights differed by producing country: Indian and Egyptian bales were typically heavier, around 720 lb and 680 lb respectively in some periods, while later conventions moved toward the 480 lb statistical bale used by international bodies such as the International Cotton Advisory Committee.

Baling

Bales are produced by baling presses or agricultural balers, which compress loose material and bind it with wire, twine, netting, or strapping. Compression reduces volume and makes bulky, low-density materials such as cotton, hay, and recycled paper economical to store and transport.
