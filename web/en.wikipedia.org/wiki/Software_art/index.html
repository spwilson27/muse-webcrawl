Software art

Software art is a work of art where the creation of software, or concepts from software, plays an important role; for example, software applications which were created by artists, which were intended as artworks. As an artistic discipline, software art has attained growing attention since the late 1990s. It is closely related to Internet art since it often relies on the Internet, most notably the World Wide Web, for dissemination and critical discussion of the works.

Contemporary software art overlaps with net art, generative art, and new media art, but where net art foregrounds the network, software art foregrounds the code, algorithm, and the behavior of machines as the artistic material.

History

Precursors include early computer art of the 1960s and instruction-based conceptual art such as Sol LeWitt's wall drawings. The term gained currency around 2000 through festivals and prizes including transmediale in Berlin, which awarded a software art prize, and the online repository Runme.org (founded 2003), which collected and theorized examples of the genre.

Characteristics

Software artworks may distribute as executable programs, source code, browser-based works, viruses, bots, or modified video games. Because software is processual, works often generate their output in real time, involve user input, and resist collection as static objects, raising questions of preservation and emulation for museums.

Notable artists

Artists associated with the genre include JODI, Netochka Nezvanova, Florian Cramer, Adrian Ward (developer of the Auto-Illustrator), LIA, and Cory Arcangel.
