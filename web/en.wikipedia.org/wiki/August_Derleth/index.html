August William Derleth (February 24, 1909 – July 4, 1971) was an American writer and anthologist. Though best remembered as the first publisher of the writings of H. P. Lovecraft, and for his own contributions to the Cthulhu Mythos and the Solar Pons detective stories, Derleth was a leading regional writer of his day, producing more than 150 books across genres including the multi-volume Sac Prairie Saga set in his native Wisconsin.

Derleth at his Sauk City home, "Place of Hawks".

Arkham House

In 1939 Derleth and Donald Wandrei founded Arkham House, the first American specialist weird-fiction press, to preserve Lovecraft's work in hardcover, beginning with The Outsider and Others. Arkham House went on to publish Ray Bradbury, Robert E. Howard, Clark Ashton Smith and many others, becoming one of the most influential small presses in fantasy literature.

The Cthulhu Mythos

Derleth corresponded with Lovecraft from 1926 until Lovecraft's death in 1937 and coined the term "Cthulhu Mythos". His own Mythos stories and "posthumous collaborations" — completed from Lovecraft fragments and notes — imposed a more systematic, good-versus-evil framework on Lovecraft's cosmos, an approach later scholars such as S. T. Joshi have criticised, even while crediting Derleth with saving Lovecraft's work from obscurity.

Other work

Derleth wrote the Solar Pons stories, pastiches of Sherlock Holmes begun in his teens and praised by Ellery Queen and Vincent Starrett; historical and regional novels of the Sac Prairie Saga (Wind Over Wisconsin, Restless Is the River); poetry; biographies of Thoreau and others; and the Steve Grendon and Judge Peck mysteries. He also edited many anthologies and founded the magazine The Arkham Sampler. He died of a heart attack in 1971.
