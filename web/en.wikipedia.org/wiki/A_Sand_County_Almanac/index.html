A Sand County Almanac

A Sand County Almanac and Sketches Here and There is a 1949 non-fiction book by American ecologist, forester and environmentalist Aldo Leopold. Published posthumously by Oxford University Press a year after Leopold's death, it describes the land around the author's farm near Baraboo, Wisconsin, and articulates his "land ethic" — the idea that humans have a moral responsibility to the natural world of which they are members. It is considered a foundational text of the modern conservation movement and has sold more than two million copies.

Contents

The book is in four parts. The first, the "Almanac" proper, is a month-by-month account of Leopold's observations of nature at "the Shack", a rehabilitated worn-out farm on the Wisconsin River in Sauk County ("Sand County") that he and his family restored from the 1930s onward. The second part, "Sketches Here and There", recounts episodes from Leopold's forty years of fieldwork across North America, including the famous essay "Thinking Like a Mountain", in which killing a wolf teaches him the ecological role of predators. The third part collects occasional pieces on conservation, and the final part, "The Upshot", presents his philosophy.

The land ethic

In the concluding essay Leopold writes his most-quoted maxim: "A thing is right when it tends to preserve the integrity, stability, and beauty of the biotic community. It is wrong when it tends otherwise." The land ethic enlarges the boundaries of the community to include soils, waters, plants and animals — collectively, the land — and recasts humans from conquerors of the land into "plain member and citizen" of it.

Legacy

Initially modest in sales, the book grew in influence through the environmental movement of the 1960s and 1970s, alongside Rachel Carson's Silent Spring. Leopold's son Luna edited the volume for publication, and later expanded editions added further essays. The Leopold family shack and farm are now preserved by the Aldo Leopold Foundation.
