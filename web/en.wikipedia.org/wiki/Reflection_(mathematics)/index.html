Reflection (mathematics)

In mathematics, a reflection (also spelled reflexion) is a mapping from a Euclidean space to itself that is an isometry whose set of fixed points is a hyperplane; this set is called the axis (in dimension 2) or plane (in dimension 3) of reflection. The image of a figure by a reflection is its mirror image in the axis or plane of reflection.

Reflection of a figure across a line (the axis of reflection)

Properties

A reflection is an involution: applying it twice returns every point to its original position. Reflections reverse orientation — a figure and its mirror image are congruent but cannot be superimposed by translations and rotations in the plane alone. In two dimensions, the reflection of a point (x, y) across the x-axis is (x, −y), and across the y-axis is (−x, y).

The composition of two reflections across parallel lines is a translation, while the composition of two reflections across intersecting lines is a rotation about the intersection point through twice the angle between the lines. By the Cartan–Dieudonné theorem, every orthogonal transformation of n-dimensional space can be expressed as a composition of at most n reflections.

Formula

In linear algebra, the reflection of a vector v across a hyperplane through the origin with unit normal vector a is given by the Householder transformation:

v − 2(a·v)a.

Reflections play a central role in the theory of reflection groups and Coxeter groups, and in the study of symmetry.
