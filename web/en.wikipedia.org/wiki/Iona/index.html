Iona (Scottish Gaelic: Ì Chaluim Chille) is a small island in the Inner Hebrides off the western coast of Scotland, separated from the Isle of Mull by the Sound of Iona. It was a centre of Gaelic monasticism for four centuries and is today known for its tranquility and natural beauty, as well as for the Iona Abbey and the ecumenical Iona Community. The island is in the care of the National Trust for Scotland and Historic Environment Scotland, and is a major place of Christian pilgrimage.

History

In 563 AD St Columba (Colum Cille) arrived on Iona from Ireland with twelve companions and founded a monastery. From Iona, Columba and his monks set out to convert the Picts and the Scots of Dál Riata to Christianity, and the abbey's influence spread across Scotland and into Northumbria, where monks from Iona founded Lindisfarne. The monastery became a renowned centre of learning; the Book of Kells is believed to have been begun on Iona around 800 before being taken to Ireland for safety.

From 794 the island suffered repeated Viking raids — the monastery was burned, and in 806 sixty-eight monks were massacred at Martyrs' Bay. Many Scottish kings, including Macbeth, Duncan I and Kenneth mac Alpin, are traditionally said to be buried on Iona in the Reilig Odhráin. In the 13th century a Benedictine abbey and an Augustinian nunnery were founded; the abbey church was rebuilt in the 15th century. After the Scottish Reformation the buildings fell into ruin.

The modern abbey and Iona Community

In 1899 the Duke of Argyll transferred the abbey ruins to the Church of Scotland, and restoration of the church was completed in the early 20th century. In 1938 the Reverend George MacLeod founded the Iona Community, an ecumenical Christian community of men and women from different traditions, which rebuilt the abbey's residential quarters using unemployed craftsmen from Glasgow and remains based on the island, running the Abbey and the MacLeod Centre and welcoming tens of thousands of visitors and pilgrims each year.

Geography and the island today

Iona is about 3 miles long and 1.5 miles wide at most, characterised by sandy bays, small rocky hills and fertile machair. Most residents live in the village of Baile Mòr, linked by ferry to Fionnphort on Mull. Historic sites include the Abbey, the ruined nunnery, St Oran's Chapel (the oldest church building, from which the Reilig Odhráin burial ground takes its name), St Columba's Bay at the south end — traditionally the saint's landing place — and the marble quarry in the south-east. Cars are largely restricted to residents, giving the island a notably peaceful atmosphere.
