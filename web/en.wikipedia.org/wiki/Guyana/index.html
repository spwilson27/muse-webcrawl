Guyana, officially the Co-operative Republic of Guyana, is a country on the northern mainland of South America. It is bordered by the Atlantic Ocean to the north, Brazil to the south and southwest, Venezuela to the west, and Suriname to the east. With an area of about 214,970 square kilometres, Guyana is the third-smallest sovereign state on mainland South America after Uruguay and Suriname, and the second-least populous.

History

The region was inhabited by Indigenous peoples including the Arawak, Carib and Warao before European colonisation. The Dutch established colonies at Essequibo, Demerara and Berbice; these passed to Britain in the early nineteenth century and were united as British Guiana in 1831. After the abolition of slavery, indentured labourers were brought from India, shaping Guyana's ethnic makeup. Guyana gained independence in 1966 and became a republic in 1970 under Forbes Burnham, whose "co-operative socialism" dominated the following decades. Venezuela claims the Guayana Esequiba region west of the Essequibo River, a dispute that has intensified since major offshore oil discoveries.

Geography and economy

Much of the country is covered by the Guiana Shield rainforest, among the most intact tropical forests in the world; the population is concentrated on the narrow coastal plain. Historically dependent on sugar, rice, gold, bauxite and timber, Guyana's economy has been transformed since 2019 by offshore petroleum production led by ExxonMobil in the Stabroek Block, making it one of the fastest-growing economies in the world. Guyana is the only South American country where English is the official language, though Guyanese Creole is widely spoken, and it is a member of the Commonwealth and hosts the headquarters of the Caribbean Community (CARICOM) in Georgetown.
