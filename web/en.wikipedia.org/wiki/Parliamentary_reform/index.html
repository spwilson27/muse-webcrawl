Parliamentary reform

Parliamentary reform refers to changes to the composition, franchise, procedures and powers of a parliament. The term is most often associated with the long campaign to reform the British House of Commons in the 18th and 19th centuries.

Parliamentary reform

Key UK acts: Reform Acts 1832, 1867, 1884
Issues: Rotten boroughs, franchise, redistribution

British reform

Before 1832, representation was grossly unequal: "rotten boroughs" with few voters returned MPs while industrial cities such as Manchester had none, and the franchise was narrow and varied. Agitation by reformers, radicals and movements such as Chartism led to the Great Reform Act 1832, followed by the Acts of 1867 and 1884–85, the secret ballot (1872), and later equal franchise measures in 1918 and 1928 extending votes to women.

Wider usage

The term also covers procedural modernisation, devolution, electoral-system change, and reform movements in other countries' legislatures.
