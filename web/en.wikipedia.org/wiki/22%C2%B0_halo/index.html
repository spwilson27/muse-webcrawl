22° halo

22° halo
TypeAtmospheric optical phenomenon
CauseRefraction through hexagonal ice crystals
Angular radiusApproximately 22°

A 22° halo is an atmospheric optical phenomenon consisting of a bright ring of light around the Sun or the Moon with an angular radius of approximately 22 degrees. It is produced when light is refracted through the randomly oriented hexagonal prism ice crystals suspended in cirrostratus cloud high in the troposphere.

Formation

Light entering one face of a hexagonal ice crystal and leaving through another is deviated by a minimum angle of about 22°, so rays pile up at that angle and form a ring. Because red light is refracted slightly less than blue, the inner edge of the halo often appears reddish while the outer edge is bluish or white.

Frequency

The 22° halo is the most common of all halo phenomena and can be visible many days per year at a given location, whenever thin cirrostratus covers the sky.
