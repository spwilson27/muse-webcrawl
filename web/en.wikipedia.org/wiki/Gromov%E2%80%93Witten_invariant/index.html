Gromov–Witten invariant

In mathematics, Gromov–Witten (GW) invariants are rational numbers that, in certain situations, count pseudoholomorphic curves meeting prescribed conditions on a given symplectic manifold. They were inspired by work of Mikhail Gromov on pseudoholomorphic curves (1985) and by Edward Witten's formulation in the context of topological sigma models in physics.

Definition

GW invariants are defined using moduli spaces of stable maps from nodal Riemann surfaces into a symplectic manifold. Because the expected dimension of the moduli space may differ from its actual dimension, the invariants are constructed via a virtual fundamental class, using techniques developed by Li–Tian, Behrend–Fantechi, Fukaya–Ono, and others. The invariants are assembled into a generating function known as the Gromov–Witten potential, central to quantum cohomology.

Applications

Gromov–Witten theory gives modern foundations for enumerative geometry, computing numbers of rational curves on varieties such as the quintic threefold, and is a key ingredient of mirror symmetry, which relates GW invariants of a Calabi–Yau manifold to period integrals on its mirror.
