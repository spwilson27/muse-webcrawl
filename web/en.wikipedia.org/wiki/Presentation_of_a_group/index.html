Presentation of a group

In mathematics, a presentation is one method of specifying a group. A presentation of a group G comprises a set S of generators and a set R of relations among the generators, written ⟨S | R⟩. Informally, G has the given presentation if it is the "freest" group generated by S subject only to the relations R. Formally, G is the quotient of the free group on S by the normal closure of R.

Examples

The cyclic group of order n has presentation ⟨a | an = 1⟩. The free group on two generators is ⟨a, b | ⟩, with no relations. The Klein four-group is ⟨a, b | a2 = b2 = (ab)2 = 1⟩. The symmetric group S3 can be presented as ⟨r, s | r3 = s2 = 1, srs = r−1⟩.

Properties

Every group has a presentation — indeed, one can always take the underlying set of the group itself as generators, with the multiplication table as relations. A group is finitely presented if it has a presentation with finitely many generators and relations. The word problem for groups — deciding whether a product of generators equals the identity — is undecidable in general for finitely presented groups (Novikov–Boone theorem), though it is solvable for many classes of interest.

Tietze transformations

Any two presentations of the same group are related by a sequence of Tietze transformations, which add or remove generators and relations without changing the group.
