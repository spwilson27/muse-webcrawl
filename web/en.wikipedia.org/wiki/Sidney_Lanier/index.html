Sidney Lanier

BornFebruary 3, 1842, Macon, Georgia
DiedSeptember 7, 1881, Lynn, North Carolina
OccupationPoet, musician, scholar

Sidney Clopton Lanier (1842–1881) was an American musician, poet, and author. A Confederate veteran of the Civil War, he contracted tuberculosis while a prisoner at Point Lookout, an illness that shortened his life and career.

Works

Lanier served as first flutist of Baltimore's Peabody Orchestra and lectured on English literature at Johns Hopkins University. His best-known poems include "The Marshes of Glynn," "Sunrise," and "Corn," noted for their musicality; his scholarly work The Science of English Verse (1880) applied musical notation to poetic metre. He also wrote the novel Tiger-Lilies and popular retellings of Arthurian tales for boys.

Legacy

Lake Lanier in Georgia and numerous schools bear his name.
