Horse Guards Parade

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Horse Guards Parade is a large parade ground off Whitehall in central London, in the City of Westminster. It is the site of the annual ceremonies of Trooping the Colour, held on the sovereign's official birthday, and Beating Retreat, as well as other ceremonial events.

History

The ground was originally the tiltyard of the Palace of Whitehall, where jousts were held in the time of Henry VIII. It takes its name from the adjoining Horse Guards building, the headquarters of the Household Division, built in the mid-18th century to designs by William Kent and John Vardy. The parade ground is bounded by the Horse Guards building to the east, St James's Park to the west, and the gardens of 10 Downing Street to the south.

Ceremonial use

Each June, the Trooping the Colour ceremony takes place on Horse Guards Parade, with over a thousand soldiers and hundreds of horses participating, before the sovereign travels in procession along the Mall to Buckingham Palace. The ground is also used for the daily Changing of the Queen's Life Guard (now the King's Life Guard), which has taken place since the Restoration of Charles II.

Modern uses

For the 2012 London Olympics, Horse Guards Parade was converted into an arena for beach volleyball, with temporary seating for 15,000 spectators. Memorials on and around the ground include the Guards Memorial, commemorating the five regiments of Foot Guards, and a Turkish cannon captured in Egypt in 1801.
