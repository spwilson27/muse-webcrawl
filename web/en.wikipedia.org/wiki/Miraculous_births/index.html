Miraculous births

Miraculous births are a common theme in world mythology and religious texts, in which a hero, deity or religious founder is conceived or born in a manner that departs from ordinary reproduction — by a virgin mother, by divine intervention on behalf of a barren couple, by parthenogenesis, or by some other supernatural means. Such stories typically mark the child as set apart and destined for greatness.

The miraculous birth is among the most widespread motifs in mythology. (Labelled placeholder image.)

Virgin births

The best-known example is the virgin birth of Jesus, described in the Gospels of Matthew and Luke, in which Mary conceives by the Holy Spirit. Earlier traditions include the births of figures fathered by gods on mortal women in Greek myth (for example Perseus, born to Danaë), the birth of the Buddha, who in legend entered his mother Maya's womb in the form of a white elephant in a dream, and Miraculous conceptions in Hindu tradition such as the birth of Karna to the unmarried Kunti in the Mahabharata.

Births to barren or elderly parents

A second large class involves children granted to parents long childless or past childbearing age: Isaac, born to Abraham and the aged Sarah in Genesis; Samuel, born to Hannah after prayer; and John the Baptist, born to Zechariah and Elizabeth. The Quran recounts several such births, including that of Yahya (John) and, uniquely, of Isa (Jesus) to the virgin Maryam.

Other forms

Some traditions describe birth without conception at all: Athena springing fully armed from the head of Zeus, Dionysus born from Zeus's thigh, or the Chinese sage Laozi said to have been born with white hair after an exceptionally long gestation. Scholars of comparative mythology, from Otto Rank (The Myth of the Birth of the Hero, 1909) onward, have catalogued recurring patterns in these tales.
