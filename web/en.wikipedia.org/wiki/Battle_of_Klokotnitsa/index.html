Battle of Klokotnitsa

The Battle of Klokotnitsa (Bulgarian: Битка при Клокотница) was fought on 9 March 1230 near the village of Klokotnitsa (in present-day Haskovo Province, Bulgaria) between the Second Bulgarian Empire, led by Tsar Ivan Asen II, and the Empire of Thessalonica under Theodore Komnenos Doukas. The Bulgarian victory destroyed Thessalonica's bid for Balkan supremacy and made Bulgaria the dominant power of southeastern Europe.

Background

Theodore Komnenos Doukas, ruler of Epirus and, from 1224, of Thessalonica, had rebuilt a powerful Greek state in western Greece and Macedonia and had himself crowned emperor, rivalling Nicaea for the inheritance of Byzantium. Around 1230, having secured his other frontiers by treaties, Theodore broke his peace with Ivan Asen II and invaded Bulgaria along the Maritsa valley, reportedly confident that the Bulgarian tsar would not resist. According to tradition, Ivan Asen had the text of the broken treaty fastened to a spear as his banner when he marched to meet the invaders with a comparatively small force, hastily joined by Cuman auxiliaries.

The battle

The armies met near Klokotnitsa on 9 March 1230, the feast of the Forty Martyrs of Sebaste. Details are sparse, but the sources agree the result was total: the Epirote army was annihilated, with most of its soldiers killed or captured. Theodore himself was taken prisoner. Ivan Asen treated the common prisoners with notable generosity, releasing many of the rank and file to return home — a gesture that won loyalty across the conquered territories — while the captured aristocracy was taken to Tarnovo. Theodore, initially honoured at court, was later blinded after allegedly plotting against the tsar.

Aftermath

Bulgaria annexed most of Macedonia, Albania and Thrace down to the environs of Constantinople, and Ivan Asen II adopted the title "Tsar of the Bulgarians and the Greeks". The Empire of Thessalonica collapsed, passing to Theodore's brother Manuel as a Bulgarian dependency. In commemoration of the victory, Ivan Asen built and endowed the Church of the Forty Holy Martyrs in Tarnovo, whose famous inscription column records the campaign and the battle's date.
