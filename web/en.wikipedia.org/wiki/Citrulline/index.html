Citrulline

Citrulline is an α-amino acid that is not incorporated into proteins during translation but is a key intermediate in the urea cycle. It takes its name from Citrullus, the Latin word for watermelon, from which it was first isolated in 1914; watermelon remains a significant dietary source.

Citrulline
IUPAC name2-Amino-5-(carbamoylamino)pentanoic acid
FormulaC6H13N3O3
Molar mass175.19 g/mol
RoleUrea cycle intermediate; nitric oxide precursor

Biochemistry

In the mitochondria of liver cells, citrulline is formed from ornithine and carbamoyl phosphate by ornithine transcarbamylase, then converted in the cytosol through argininosuccinate to arginine. Citrulline is also produced as a co-product when nitric oxide synthases convert arginine to nitric oxide. Inherited defects of its metabolism cause citrullinemia. Citrullinated proteins, produced by peptidylarginine deiminase enzymes, are targets of the anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide (anti-CCP) antibodies characteristic of rheumatoid arthritis.

Dietary supplement use

Citrulline and its malate salt are sold as sports supplements, on the theory that raising plasma arginine increases nitric oxide production and blood flow; evidence for performance benefits is mixed.
