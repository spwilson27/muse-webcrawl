Somatotopy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Somatotopy is the point-for-point correspondence of an area of the body with a specific point on the central nervous system, such that stimulation or activity at a particular locus corresponds to sensation or movement in a particular body part. Somatotopic maps are a central organising principle of sensory and motor cortex.

Cortical maps

The best-known examples are the sensory and motor homunculi described by Wilder Penfield, in which the primary somatosensory cortex (postcentral gyrus) and primary motor cortex (precentral gyrus) contain distorted maps of the body: regions with fine discrimination or control, such as the lips, tongue and hands, occupy disproportionately large cortical areas.

Other structures

Somatotopic organisation is also found in the dorsal columns and spinothalamic tract, the thalamus, the cerebellum and the midbrain. The maps are plastic and can reorganise after injury or amputation, a phenomenon implicated in phantom limb sensation.
