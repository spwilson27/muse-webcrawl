Güyüyük Khan

Güyük Khan (c. 1206 – 1248), also rendered Güyüyük, was the third Khagan-emperor of the Mongol Empire, the eldest son of Ögedei Khan and a grandson of Genghis Khan. He reigned from 1246 to 1248, after a long regency by his mother Töregene Khatun, who engineered his election at a kurultai near Karakorum attended by foreign emissaries including the papal envoy Giovanni da Pian del Carpine.

Güyük reversed many of his mother's appointments, demanded submission from the Kievan Rus' princes and the Seljuks, and famously replied to Pope Innocent IV's letter by demanding the Pope's submission. His reign was cut short by his death in 1248, possibly while preparing for conflict with Batu Khan, paving the way for Möngke's accession.
