Automatic Warning System

The Automatic Warning System (AWS) is a form of limited train protection and driver vigilance system used in the United Kingdom and some other railways. Its purpose is to assist drivers by giving them an audible and visual indication of the aspect of the next signal, and to apply the brakes automatically if the driver fails to acknowledge a restrictive indication.

History

AWS evolved from the automatic train control system installed by the Great Western Railway from 1906, which used a ramp between the rails to sound a bell or siren in the cab. British Rail standardised AWS nationally in the 1950s, replacing the contact ramp with a permanent magnet set between the rails, so that no electrical supply was needed at the trackside equipment.

As a train passes over an AWS magnet, typically about 185 metres (200 yards) before a signal, a detector on the locomotive responds. If the signal is clear, a bell rings and the visual indicator, nicknamed the "sunflower", shows black. If the signal is at caution or danger, a horn sounds instead and the driver must press a reset button within about 2.5 seconds; the sunflower then displays alternating black and yellow petals. Failure to acknowledge causes an automatic emergency brake application.

AWS gives no protection against a driver who acknowledges the warning and then proceeds past a red signal, a weakness addressed by the later Train Protection & Warning System (TPWS) and, on newer routes, by ETCS.
