Scientific computing

Scientific computing (also computational science) is an interdisciplinary field that constructs mathematical models and uses numerical analysis and computer simulation to solve complex scientific and engineering problems. It is often regarded as a third pillar of science, alongside theory and experiment.

Methods

Core techniques include numerical methods for ordinary and partial differential equations, such as finite element, finite volume and finite difference methods; numerical linear algebra for large sparse systems; Monte Carlo methods for stochastic problems; and optimisation. Because many models involve enormous data sets and computations, scientific computing relies heavily on high-performance computing, parallel algorithms and supercomputers.

Applications

Applications include weather and climate modelling, computational fluid dynamics for aircraft and vehicle design, molecular dynamics and quantum chemistry, astrophysical simulation, structural engineering, and computational biology such as protein folding. Standard software building blocks include the BLAS and LAPACK linear algebra libraries and message-passing standards such as MPI.

Concerns

Practitioners must manage rounding error, numerical stability and the reproducibility of results, and verify and validate models against experiments. Languages historically used include Fortran and C/C++, with Python now widespread as a front end to compiled libraries.
