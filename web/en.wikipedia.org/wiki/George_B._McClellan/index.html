George B. McClellan

George Brinton McClellan

Born: 3 December 1826, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania

Died: 29 October 1885, Orange, New Jersey

Allegiance: United States (Union)

Commands: Army of the Potomac

Later offices: Governor of New Jersey (1878–1881)

George Brinton McClellan (3 December 1826 – 29 October 1885) was an American military officer and politician. A graduate of West Point, he served with distinction in the Mexican–American War and as a railroad executive before the American Civil War, in which he became a major general of the Union Army.

Civil War command

After the Union defeat at First Bull Run in 1861, McClellan organized the Army of the Potomac into a well-trained fighting force and briefly served as general-in-chief of all Union armies. His Peninsula Campaign of 1862 failed to capture Richmond, and his caution — driven in part by faulty intelligence that overestimated Confederate strength — frustrated President Abraham Lincoln. He checked Robert E. Lee's invasion of Maryland at the Battle of Antietam in September 1862, the bloodiest single day in American history, but failed to destroy Lee's army, and Lincoln removed him from command that November.

Political career

McClellan was the Democratic Party's nominee for president in 1864, running on a peace platform against Lincoln; he lost the election but carried three states. He later served as the 24th Governor of New Jersey from 1878 to 1881.

Legacy

Historians credit McClellan's organizational skill in building the Army of the Potomac while criticizing his excessive caution and reluctance to engage the enemy decisively.
