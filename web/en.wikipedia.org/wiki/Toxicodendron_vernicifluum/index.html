Toxicodendron vernicifluum

Toxicodendron vernicifluum (formerly Rhus verniciflua), commonly called the Chinese lacquer tree, is a species of tree in the cashew family (Anacardiaceae) native to China and the Indian subcontinent, and cultivated in Japan and Korea. It is the principal source of East Asian lacquer.

Description

The tree grows up to about 20 metres tall, with large pinnate leaves. Its sap contains urushiol, the same allergenic oil found in poison ivy and poison oak, and contact can cause severe dermatitis in sensitised people.

Lacquer production

Tapping the bark yields a grey-brown sap that, after filtering, heating, and controlled exposure to humid air, polymerises into urushi lacquer — a hard, glossy, chemically resistant coating used for centuries on wood, ceramics, and metal in China, Japan (as urushi), and Korea. Lacquerware made from the tree's sap is among the oldest luxury crafts in East Asia. The wax from its fruits has also been used for candles and cosmetics.
