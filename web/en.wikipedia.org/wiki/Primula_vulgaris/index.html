Primula vulgaris

Primula vulgaris
Family: Primulaceae
Genus: Primula
Common name: Common primrose

Primula vulgaris, the common primrose, is a species of flowering plant in the family Primulaceae, native to western and southern Europe, northwest Africa, and parts of southwest Asia. It is one of the earliest spring flowers in Europe.

Description

It is a semi-evergreen perennial growing to about 10 cm tall, with a basal rosette of wrinkled oval leaves. The pale yellow, occasionally white or pink-tinged, flowers appear singly on hairy stems in early spring.

Habitat and ecology

The primrose grows in woods, hedgerows, and open grassland. Its flowers are heterostylous, occurring in "pin" and "thrum" forms — a classic example, studied by Charles Darwin, of a mechanism promoting cross-pollination by insects.

Cultivation and uses

Numerous cultivars and hybrids (often sold as polyanthus) are grown as ornamental spring bedding plants. Historically the flowers and leaves were used in folk medicine, and the plant is the county flower of Devon in England.
