Ethereal wave

Ethereal wave artists favour atmosphere, reverb and otherworldly soprano vocals.

Ethereal wave, also called ethereal goth or ethereal darkwave, is a subgenre of dark wave music that emerged in the early 1980s, principally in the United Kingdom. It is characterised by shimmering, heavily processed guitars, synthesisers, echoing drum machines and high, often wordless female vocals, creating a dreamlike, otherworldly atmosphere.

History

The style coalesced around artists on the 4AD label, above all the Cocteau Twins, whose singer Elizabeth Fraser pioneered the genre's glossolalic soprano style, and This Mortal Coil, the label collective whose 1983 cover of "Song to the Siren" became a defining recording. Dead Can Dance brought neoclassical and world-music influences to the sound.

1990s and Projekt Records

In the United States, Sam Rosenthal's Projekt Records championed ethereal wave through acts such as Black Tape for a Blue Girl, Lycia and Love Spirals Downwards, and the term became common in American goth scenes. The genre overlaps with dream pop and shoegaze, from which it differs by its darker, more melancholic tone and gothic associations.

Characteristics

Ethereal wave emphasises texture over rhythm: layered synthesiser pads, chorused guitars, generous reverb and vocals treated as an instrument. Lyrics, when intelligible, dwell on longing, mysticism and melancholy. The genre later fed into styles such as witch house and modern dream pop revivals.
