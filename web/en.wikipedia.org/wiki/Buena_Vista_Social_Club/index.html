Buena Vista Social Club is a studio album by the Cuban ensemble of the same name, produced by American guitarist Ry Cooder and released in 1997 on World Circuit Records. The ensemble brought together veteran Cuban musicians — many of them in their seventies, eighties, and nineties, and some of whom had been retired for years — to record traditional Cuban music including son, bolero, and danzón. The album became an international success, won the Grammy Award for Best Tropical Latin Performance in 1998, and sparked a worldwide revival of interest in traditional Cuban music.

Background

The project grew out of a planned collaboration between Cuban and Malian musicians organised by World Circuit's Nick Gold. When the Malian musicians were unable to obtain visas, Cooder and Gold instead recorded with local Havana musicians assembled by bandleader Juan de Marcos González. The name came from a defunct members' club in Havana's Buenavista neighbourhood, a popular venue for musicians in the 1940s.

Music and performers

Key performers included vocalists Ibrahim Ferrer, Compay Segundo, Omara Portuondo, and Eliades Ochoa, pianist Rubén González, and tres player Segundo himself, who contributed the album's signature song "Chan Chan". Other tracks include "Dos Gardenias", "El Cuarto de Tula", and "Candela". The recordings were made in a matter of days at Havana's Egrem Studios.

Reception and legacy

The album sold over eight million copies worldwide and appeared on numerous "greatest albums" lists. A 1999 documentary film of the same name, directed by Wim Wenders and featuring concert footage from Amsterdam and Carnegie Hall, was nominated for an Academy Award. A 25th-anniversary edition with previously unreleased tracks appeared in 2021. Most of the original members have since died, but the ensemble continued touring in various configurations, and successor projects such as the Orquesta Buena Vista Social Club carried the repertoire forward.
