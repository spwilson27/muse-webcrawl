Procrustes analysis

Field: Statistics, shape analysis, morphometrics

Named after: Procrustes of Greek mythology

Generalisation: Generalised Procrustes analysis (GPA)

Procrustes analysis is a form of statistical shape analysis used to analyse the distribution of a set of shapes. The name derives from Procrustes, the bandit of Greek mythology who stretched or cut his victims to fit an iron bed. In the analysis, landmark configurations are optimally superimposed by translation, rotation and uniform scaling so that differences in shape can be compared.

Ordinary Procrustes analysis

In ordinary (two-shape) Procrustes analysis, one configuration of landmarks is fitted to another by minimising the sum of squared distances between corresponding points, known as the Procrustes distance. The optimal rotation is obtained via the orthogonal Procrustes problem, solved using the singular value decomposition: the rotation matrix is derived from the SVD of the cross-product of the centred configurations.

Generalised Procrustes analysis

Generalised Procrustes analysis (GPA) extends the method to a whole sample of shapes. All configurations are iteratively aligned to an evolving mean shape until convergence; the resulting mean and the residuals provide the basis for subsequent statistical analysis, often via principal component analysis in tangent space.

Applications

Procrustes methods are fundamental in geometric morphometrics, where they are used to study biological form in evolutionary biology, anthropology and medicine, and also appear in computer vision and pattern matching.
