Television in Italy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Television in Italy began with regular broadcasts by the public service broadcaster RAI on 3 January 1954. Television is the most important mass medium in Italy and is characterised historically by a duopoly of RAI and Silvio Berlusconi's Mediaset group.

History

Experimental broadcasts took place in the 1930s, but regular service began in 1954 with a single channel. RAI expanded to a second channel in 1961 and a third in 1979. Constitutional Court rulings in the 1970s ended RAI's monopoly at the local level, unleashing hundreds of private stations; in the 1980s Berlusconi's Fininvest built the first national commercial networks (Canale 5, Italia 1, Rete 4), forming Mediaset.

Broadcasters

Major free-to-air broadcasters include RAI (Rai 1, Rai 2, Rai 3 and thematic channels), Mediaset, La7 (Cairo Communication), and Sky Italia for pay satellite. Italy completed its digital terrestrial switchover in 2012 and later migrated to DVB-T2. The licence fee (canone) funding RAI has been collected through electricity bills since 2016.

Regulation

The sector is overseen by AGCOM, the communications regulatory authority. Political influence over RAI appointments (lottizzazione) has been a recurring topic of debate.
