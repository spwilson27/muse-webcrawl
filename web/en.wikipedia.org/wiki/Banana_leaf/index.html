Banana leaf

The banana leaf is the leaf of the banana plant, which may produce up to 40 leaves over its lifetime. The leaves are among the largest in the plant kingdom — commonly 2 metres long and 60 cm wide — with a pronounced midrib and parallel veins. Because of their size, flexibility and waxy, water-shedding surface, banana leaves have a wide range of culinary and cultural uses across the tropics, where the plant is cultivated throughout Asia, Africa, Latin America and the Pacific.

Culinary uses

In many cuisines banana leaves are used as a natural, disposable wrapping for food that is steamed, grilled or baked: examples include Filipino bibingka and suman, Indonesian pepes and lemper, Mexican tamales in some regions, and South Indian dishes. The leaf imparts a subtle aroma to the food and protects it from direct heat. In southern India and Sri Lanka, meals are traditionally served on whole banana leaves, with dishes arranged in a prescribed order; the practice is also common at festivals and temple feasts. Leaves are softened by briefly passing them over a flame or through hot water before use.

Other uses

Banana leaves serve as disposable plates, thatching, umbrellas and packaging for market produce. In Thailand the leaves are fashioned into decorative krathong floats for the festival of Loi Krathong. The leaves and their fibres are also used in traditional crafts, and wax extracted from the leaf surface has been studied for food and cosmetic applications.
