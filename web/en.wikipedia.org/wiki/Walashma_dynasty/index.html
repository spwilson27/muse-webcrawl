The Walashma dynasty was a Muslim noble family who ruled parts of the Horn of Africa in the late medieval period. They were the ruling dynasty of the Sultanate of Ifat and later the Sultanate of Adal. The dynasty was founded by Umar Walashma, who established the Ifat Sultanate around 1285 in the eastern Ethiopian highlands and the Awash valley.

History

According to tradition, Umar Walashma united a group of small Muslim principalities in the region east of the Ethiopian Christian highland kingdom. Ifat became the leading Muslim power in the Horn, controlling trade routes between the highlands and the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden ports such as Zeila. Its rulers bore the title of Sultan and maintained extensive commercial and religious ties with the wider Islamic world.

Ifat's rise brought it into conflict with the expanding Solomonic Empire of Ethiopia. In 1332, Emperor Amda Seyon I defeated and killed the Ifat sultan Haqq ad-Din I. Conflict continued under his successors; the sultan Sa'ad ad-Din II was defeated and killed by Emperor Dawit I around 1403, after which his children fled to Yemen. The family returned under his son Sabr ad-Din II, and in the 15th century the seat of the sultanate shifted eastward to Harar and the Adal region, whose fertile lands and strategic position made it the new centre of Walashma power.

Adal and decline

As sultans of Adal, the Walashma presided over a period of renewed war with Ethiopia. Their authority was increasingly challenged by powerful emirs; in the early 16th century the emir Mahfuz of Harar effectively dominated the court, and after his death the imam Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi ("Ahmad Gragn") rose to power and sidelined the Walashma sultan Abu Bakr ibn Muhammad. Ahmad's invasion of the Ethiopian highlands (1529–1543) was conducted in the sultanate's name but largely beyond Walashma control.

After Ahmad's death at the Battle of Wayna Daga in 1543, the dynasty briefly regained authority under sultan Nur ibn Mujahid, builder of the walls of Harar. Adal declined in the later 16th century under pressure from Ethiopia, the Ottoman presence on the Red Sea coast, and Oromo migrations; the capital was moved from Harar to Aussa in 1577, marking the effective end of Walashma rule, with the successor Imamate of Aussa passing to another family.
