Wat Pho

Wat Pho (Thai: วัดโพธิ์), officially Wat Phra Chetuphon Wimon Mangkhalaram Rajwaramahawihan, is a Buddhist temple complex in Bangkok, Thailand, famous for its giant Reclining Buddha and as a centre of traditional Thai massage.

History

The temple was rebuilt and greatly enlarged by King Rama I in the late eighteenth century on the site of an older temple, and further expanded under Rama III, who inscribed texts and treatises on medicine, history and literature on stone plaques around the grounds — a collection recognised by UNESCO's Memory of the World programme and often called Thailand's first public university.

Reclining Buddha

The temple's phra ubosot and the vihara housing the Reclining Buddha, built under Rama III, are its best-known features. The gold-plated statue, 46 metres long, depicts the Buddha entering nirvana; its feet are inlaid with mother-of-pearl auspicious symbols. Wat Pho is also regarded as the birthplace of traditional Thai massage, and its massage school continues to train practitioners.
