Outer space

Outer space is the expanse that exists beyond Earth's atmosphere and between celestial bodies. It is not completely empty, but consists of a hard vacuum containing a low density of particles, predominantly a plasma of hydrogen and helium, as well as electromagnetic radiation, magnetic fields, neutrinos, dust, and cosmic rays.

There is no clear boundary where outer space begins. The Kármán line, at an altitude of 100 km above sea level, is conventionally used as the start of outer space for aerospace purposes, a convention reflected in the Outer Space Treaty.

Regions of space include interplanetary space, interstellar space and intergalactic space. Human exploration began with balloon flights and rocketry, leading to satellites, crewed spacecraft and deep-space probes.
