Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite

Pseudo-Dionysius
Flourished: c. late 5th–early 6th century AD
Language: Greek
Works: Celestial Hierarchy, Mystical Theology, Divine Names
Tradition: Neoplatonism, Christian theology

Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite is the pen name of a Greek author who wrote a set of influential theological works around the late fifth to early sixth century AD, claiming to be Dionysius the Areopagite, the Athenian convert of St Paul mentioned in Acts 17.

Works

The Corpus Dionysiacum comprises The Celestial Hierarchy, The Ecclesiastical Hierarchy, The Divine Names, The Mystical Theology, and ten letters. The writings fuse Christian doctrine with late Neoplatonism, especially Proclus, and introduce the hierarchy of angels (seraphim, cherubim, thrones, etc.) and the via negativa — apophatic theology describing God by what God is not.

Influence and authorship

Quoted at a Constantinople colloquy in 532, the corpus gained immense authority in both East and West, shaping Maximus the Confessor, John Scotus Eriugena (its Latin translator), and Thomas Aquinas, who cites it extensively. Doubts raised by Lorenzo Valla and Erasmus were confirmed by 19th-century scholarship, which showed dependence on Proclus (d. 485), dating the author centuries later than the apostolic age.
