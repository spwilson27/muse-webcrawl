Typing

Typing is the process of writing or inputting text by pressing keys on a typewriter, computer keyboard, mobile phone, or calculator. It can be distinguished from other means of input, such as handwriting and speech recognition. Text can be in the form of letters, numbers and other symbols.

Techniques

Touch typing

Touch typing is a style of typing in which the typist does not look at the keyboard. The fingers rest on a home row of keys — on a QWERTY keyboard, A, S, D, F for the left hand and J, K, L, ; for the right — and each finger is responsible for a set of nearby keys. Skilled touch typists can reach speeds of 75–80 words per minute or more, and competitive typists can exceed 120 words per minute.

Hunt and peck

Hunt and peck (also called two-fingered typing) is a common informal style in which the typist presses each key individually, usually watching the keyboard. It is generally slower than touch typing, though some practitioners develop considerable speed.

Speed measurement

Alphanumeric typing speeds are typically measured in words per minute (WPM). For the purposes of WPM measurement, a word is standardized to five characters or keystrokes. Net speed takes into account errors, which are sometimes counted through gross WPM minus error penalties.

History

Typing skills spread with the commercial typewriter in the late 19th century, when typing became a distinct clerical profession. Touch typing was popularized in the 1880s, notably by Frank Edward McGurrin, a court stenographer from Salt Lake City who won a famous typing competition in 1888. Typing instruction became a standard part of business and school curricula, and continued into the computer era.
