Animal testing on non-human primates

Animal testing on non-human primates (NHPs) involves experiments using monkeys and apes in biomedical and psychological research. Because of their genetic and physiological closeness to humans, primates are used in neuroscience, infectious disease, vaccine development, toxicology and transplantation research — and their use is among the most controversial areas of animal research.

Species usedRhesus and cynomolgus macaques, marmosets, baboons
FieldsNeuroscience, HIV/AIDS, vaccines, toxicology

Use and regulation

Tens of thousands of primates are used annually worldwide; the United States and China are major users, while the European Union restricts great ape research, effectively banning it. Chimpanzee research in the US was largely ended in the 2010s, with surviving animals retired to sanctuaries. UK facilities such as those formerly at Huntingdon Life Sciences have been targets of protest.

Controversy

Supporters argue primate models remain essential for diseases such as Parkinson's, Ebola and HIV. Critics, including animal rights groups, cite primates' intelligence and social needs, the suffering involved, and improving alternatives such as organoids and computational models. Notorious cases include the Silver Spring monkeys (1981) and Harry Harlow's maternal-deprivation experiments.
