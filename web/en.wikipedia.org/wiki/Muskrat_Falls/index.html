Muskrat Falls

Muskrat Falls is a natural waterfall and a hydroelectric generating station located on the lower Churchill River, about 25 kilometres west of Happy Valley-Goose Bay in Labrador, Newfoundland and Labrador, Canada. The generating station, with an installed capacity of 824 megawatts, is the centrepiece of the Lower Churchill Project developed by the provincial Crown corporation Nalcor Energy (now part of Newfoundland and Labrador Hydro). Power is transmitted to the island of Newfoundland via the Labrador–Island Link, a high-voltage direct current line that crosses the Strait of Belle Isle, and onward to Nova Scotia via the Maritime Link.

Project history

The project was sanctioned by the government of Danny Williams in 2012 at an estimated capital cost of about $7.4 billion. Costs subsequently escalated dramatically, reaching over $13 billion, and the project became the subject of a provincial public inquiry (the Muskrat Falls Inquiry, 2018–2020), which found that the business case was misrepresented and that risks were concealed from the public. Construction suffered from disputes with contractors, including the Italian firm Astaldi, and the first power was produced in 2021, with the station reaching full operation in 2023.

Controversies

Indigenous groups and local residents raised concerns about methylmercury contamination of the reservoir and downstream Lake Melville food webs, leading to protests, including a hunger strike and occupation of the site in 2016, and commitments to clear vegetation from the reservoir area. The stability of the North Spur, a natural clay formation forming part of the dam, was also a source of public concern.
