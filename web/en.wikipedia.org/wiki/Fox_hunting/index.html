Fox hunting

Fox hunting is an activity involving the tracking, chase and, sometimes, killing of a fox, traditionally a red fox, by trained scent hounds. Followed by a group of unarmed followers led by a "master of foxhounds" (master of hounds), the hounds follow the scent of the fox rather than the pursuit and catching of it. A group of riders follow the hounds across the countryside.

History

Fox hunting in its modern form developed in England in the 18th century, as deer numbers declined and enclosure created suitable country; Hugo Meynell, master of the Quorn Hunt from 1753, is credited with breeding faster hounds and shaping the modern sport. It spread to Ireland, North America and elsewhere in the British world. In the United States and Canada, the emphasis is on the chase rather than the kill ("fox chasing").

Controversy and law

Fox hunting has long been controversial on animal-welfare grounds. Scotland restricted it with the Protection of Wild Mammals (Scotland) Act 2002, and the Hunting Act 2004 banned hunting wild mammals with dogs in England and Wales; trail hunting (following an artificial scent) and drag hunting remain legal. Debates continue over enforcement and alleged illegal hunting.
