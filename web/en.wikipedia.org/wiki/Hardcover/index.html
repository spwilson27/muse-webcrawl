Hardcover

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Image placeholder: Hardcover

A hardcover, hardback or hardbound book is bound with rigid protective covers, typically made of binder's board covered in cloth, heavy paper or leather, with the pages sewn together (case binding) rather than glued. Hardcover editions are more durable and collectible than paperbacks and are usually the first format in which a book is published, sold at a higher price; a paperback edition commonly follows months later.

Construction

In case binding, folded signatures of pages are sewn together to form a text block, which is glued into a separately made case of boards and covering material, joined by endpapers. Many hardcovers are issued with a printed dust jacket, which originally protected the binding and later became a principal surface for cover art and marketing copy. Elements such as headbands, ribbon markers, foil stamping and embossed spines are more common on hardcovers than on other formats.

History and publishing practice

Until the 19th century, books were commonly sold in temporary bindings and bound to the buyer's order; publishers' cloth bindings became standard in the 1820s–30s. The modern practice of releasing a hardcover first grew with the economics of publishing in the 20th century: the "hardcover first" model segments the market by price and time, with libraries, collectors and eager readers buying the durable edition. Some genres — including children's picture books, reference works and art books — are issued predominantly in hardcover, and the term "library binding" denotes an especially reinforced variant.
