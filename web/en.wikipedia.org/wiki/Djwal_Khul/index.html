Djwal Khul

Djwal Khul, known as "the Tibetan", in Theosophical tradition.

Djwal Khul (also spelled Djwhal Khul, Djwal Kul, or simply "D.K."), known as "the Tibetan", is a figure in Theosophy and the writings of Alice Bailey, said to be a member of the "Spiritual Hierarchy", or Brotherhood, of Mahatmas — enlightened masters guiding human evolution. He is described as a Tibetan lama of advanced spiritual attainment, living in Shigatse, Tibet.

In Theosophical literature

Djwal Khul was first mentioned in the works of Helena Blavatsky and was said to have assisted in the production of The Secret Doctrine (1888). He became far more prominent through Alice Bailey, who claimed that between 1919 and 1949 he telepathically dictated to her the series of esoteric books published under her name, including A Treatise on Cosmic Fire and Esoteric Healing. In Bailey's system, Djwal Khul is a Master of the Second Ray of Love-Wisdom, a disciple of the Master Koot Hoomi.

Influence

The Bailey teachings, attributed largely to Djwal Khul, became foundational for parts of the later New Age movement, popularising concepts such as the seven rays and the anticipated "reappearance of the Christ". Later writers, including Benjamin Creme, also claimed contact with the Masters. Belief in Djwal Khul's existence is a matter of faith within these movements; there is no independent evidence for a historical person of this description.
