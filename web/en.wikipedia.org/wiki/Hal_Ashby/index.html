Hal Ashby

William Hal Ashby (September 2, 1929 – December 27, 1988) was an American film director and editor. He began his career as an editor, winning the Academy Award for Best Film Editing for In the Heat of the Night (1967), before becoming one of the key directors of the New Hollywood era of the 1970s.

Career

Mentored by Norman Jewison, Ashby made his directorial debut with The Landlord (1970). His celebrated run of 1970s films includes the dark comedy Harold and Maude (1971), The Last Detail (1973), Shampoo (1975), Bound for Glory (1976), the Vietnam War drama Coming Home (1978) — for which he was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Director — and the satire Being There (1979), starring Peter Sellers. Ashby was known for his gentle, character-driven style, unconventional editing rhythms, and use of popular music on soundtracks, notably Cat Stevens's songs in Harold and Maude.

Later years

His output declined in the 1980s amid studio conflicts and personal difficulties with drugs; later films such as Lookin' to Get Out (1982) and 8 Million Ways to Die (1986) were commercial failures. Ashby died of pancreatic cancer in 1988, aged 59. His work has since been widely reappraised, and Harold and Maude in particular has become a cult classic.
