Jesus Prayer

The Jesus Prayer, also known as the Prayer of the Heart, is a short formulaic prayer esteemed and advocated especially within the Eastern Orthodox Church and Eastern Catholic Churches. The most common form of the prayer is "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner." It is rooted in the biblical pleas for mercy of the blind men and the publican in the Gospels, and is at the centre of the hesychast tradition of contemplative prayer.

Practice

The prayer is repeated continuously, often counted on a prayer rope (komboskini or chotki), and may be coordinated with breathing. Through practice, teachers of the tradition hold, the prayer becomes "self-acting" in the heart, fulfilling the command to "pray without ceasing" (1 Thessalonians 5:17). The classic guide to the practice is the Philokalia, an anthology of texts from the 4th to 15th centuries, and its popularisation owed much to the anonymous 19th-century Russian spiritual classic The Way of a Pilgrim.

History

Short invocations of Jesus were used by the Desert Fathers of Egypt; the developed prayer is attested by Diadochos of Photiki (5th century) and John Climacus (7th century). On Mount Athos the practice was systematised by the hesychasts, whose theology of uncreated light was defended by Gregory Palamas against Barlaam of Calabria in the 14th century. The prayer spread widely in Russia in the 19th century and, in the 20th, among Western Christians and in ecumenical settings.
