Emmanuel de Grouchy, marquis de Grouchy

Emmanuel de Grouchy, 2nd Marquis de Grouchy (French pronunciation: [ɛmanɥɛl də ɡʁuʃi]; 23 October 1766 – 29 May 1847) was a French military leader who served during the French Revolutionary Wars and the Napoleonic Wars. He was the last Marshal of the Empire created by Napoleon, promoted in 1815, and is chiefly remembered for his role in the Waterloo campaign.

Early career

Grouchy was born in Paris to an aristocratic family and entered the artillery at the age of fourteen. Despite his noble birth he embraced revolutionary principles, served with distinction in the Vendée and in Ireland's attempted expedition of 1796, and became a general of division. He fought in Italy under Moreau and commanded the cavalry of the Grande Armée in the campaigns of 1805–1807, distinguishing himself at Eylau and Friedland.

Later Napoleonic service

Grouchy led cavalry in the Peninsular War, in the invasion of Russia in 1812, and in the German campaign of 1813, where he was wounded at Leipzig. In the defence of France in 1814 he fought at Brienne and Craonne. After the first restoration he was sidelined by the Bourbons, but rallied to Napoleon during the Hundred Days and was made a Marshal and a Peer of France.

Waterloo

During the Waterloo campaign Grouchy commanded the right wing of the Armée du Nord. After the Battle of Ligny on 16 June 1815, Napoleon ordered him to pursue the Prussians with some 33,000 men. Grouchy followed slowly toward Wavre and engaged the Prussian rearguard there on 18–19 June, while the main battle was decided at Waterloo. Critics, including Napoleon in exile, blamed his failure to march to the sound of the guns for the defeat; his defenders note that he obeyed his written orders and that his force was engaged against a strong Prussian corps.

After the second restoration Grouchy was exiled and lived in Philadelphia until an amnesty allowed his return to France in 1821. His rank of Marshal was restored in 1831. He died at Saint-Étienne in 1847.
