Rhinitis medicamentosa

Rhinitis medicamentosa is persistent nasal congestion caused or worsened by prolonged use of topical nasal decongestants. It is often called rebound congestion: as each dose wears off, the nasal tissues swell again, prompting another dose and a cycle of dependence.

Common triggers
Oxymetazoline, xylometazoline and phenylephrine sprays used beyond a few days
Main symptom
Nasal obstruction with relatively little discharge in many cases

Mechanism

Topical decongestants constrict blood vessels in the nasal mucosa through alpha-adrenergic stimulation. With repeated use, receptors become less responsive and natural vascular tone changes. When the drug fades, vessels dilate and tissue engorgement can exceed the original congestion. Chronic use may also impair ciliary function and inflame the lining.

Symptoms and diagnosis

The dominant complaint is blocked breathing through the nose, sometimes with dryness, irritation or reduced smell. Diagnosis rests mainly on a history of frequent decongestant use and exclusion or treatment of allergic rhinitis, sinus disease, septal deviation and other causes. The exact number of days required varies; product labels commonly advise limiting use to about three days unless directed by a clinician.

Treatment

The essential treatment is stopping the offending spray. Congestion may temporarily worsen before improving. Saline irrigation, intranasal corticosteroids and management of underlying allergy or infection can help. Some clinicians taper use in one nostril at a time or use a short course of systemic medication in selected patients, although evidence and suitability vary.

Rhinitis medicamentosa is associated especially with topical decongestants. Intranasal corticosteroids are not considered to cause the same rebound cycle.

Prevention

Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest labelled period, avoid sharing sprays, and seek evaluation for congestion that persists. Education is important because the immediate relief makes repeated use feel necessary even as it perpetuates the problem.
