Stax Records is an American record company, originally based in Memphis, Tennessee. Founded in 1957 as Satellite Records, the label changed its name to Stax Records in 1961. It was influential in the creation of Southern soul and Memphis soul music.

Stax released recordings by Otis Redding, Isaac Hayes, Rufus and Carla Thomas, Sam & Dave, Booker T. & the M.G.'s, Albert King, the Staple Singers, and many others. Its integrated house band, Booker T. & the M.G.'s, and its raw, groove-centred sound distinguished it from its Detroit rival Motown.

History

Siblings Jim Stewart and Estelle Axton founded the company; the name "Stax" combines the first two letters of their surnames (Stewart and Axton). The studio at 926 East McLemore Avenue—"Soulsville, U.S.A."—was a converted movie theatre whose sloped floor shaped its distinctive acoustics. A distribution deal with Atlantic Records (1960–1968) brought national success; its termination cost Stax its back catalogue and helped spark a rebuilding era under executive Al Bell.

The "Soul Explosion" of 1969 released 27 albums at once. Isaac Hayes's soundtrack to Shaft (1971) won an Academy Award, and the Wattstax concert of 1972 drew 100,000 people to the Los Angeles Coliseum. Financial difficulties, a burdensome distribution deal with CBS and banking problems forced the original company into involuntary bankruptcy in December 1975.

Legacy

The Stax Museum of American Soul Music opened on the original site in 2003, alongside the Stax Music Academy. The label has been revived several times, most recently as an imprint of Concord.
