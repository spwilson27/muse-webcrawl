Govardhan Puja

Govardhan Puja (Sanskrit: गोवर्धन पूजा), also known as Annakut or Annakoot (meaning a "mountain of food"), is a Hindu festival in which devotees worship Govardhan Hill and prepare and offer a large variety of vegetarian food to Krishna as a mark of gratitude. For Vaishnavas — especially devotees of Krishna — this festival commemorates the incident in the Bhagavata Purana when Krishna lifted Govardhan Hill to provide shelter to the villagers of Vrindavan from torrential rains.

The festival is observed on the Pratipada Tithi of Kartik Shukla Paksha, the day after Diwali, and marks the beginning of the Vikram Samvat New Year in Gujarat and some other regions.

Legend

According to the Bhagavata Purana, the residents of Vrindavan traditionally made offerings to Indra, god of rain, for good harvests. The young Krishna persuaded them instead to worship Govardhan Hill, which provided their cattle with grass and their village with bounty. Enraged, Indra unleashed a deluge of rain lasting seven days and nights. Krishna lifted the entire hill on his little finger and held it like an umbrella over the villagers and their cattle until Indra, humbled, acknowledged Krishna's supremacy and sought his forgiveness.

Celebrations

Devotees build small hillocks of cow dung representing Mount Govardhan, decorate them with flowers, and worship them with circumambulation (parikrama). Temples prepare the Annakut — a mountain of cooked food, often hundreds of dishes — which is offered to the deity and afterwards distributed to devotees as prasad. In Braj, thousands of pilgrims perform the 21 km circumambulation of Govardhan Hill itself. The festival also celebrates the bond between husband and wife in some regions, and is associated with the worship of cattle.
