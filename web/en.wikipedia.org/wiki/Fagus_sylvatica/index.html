Fagus sylvatica

European beech
Kingdom:Plantae
Order:Fagales
Family:Fagaceae
Genus:Fagus
Species:F. sylvatica
Binomial nameFagus sylvatica L.

Fagus sylvatica, the European beech or common beech, is a large, graceful deciduous tree in the beech family Fagaceae. It is native to much of Europe, from southern Sweden to northern Sicily, and east to northwest Turkey. It is a dominant tree of European temperate forests, forming extensive pure stands on well-drained, often calcareous soils.

Description

The European beech is a large tree, capable of reaching heights of up to 50 m (160 ft) and 3 m (10 ft) trunk diameter, though more typically 25–35 m (82–115 ft) tall. It has a typical lifespan of 150–200 years, though sometimes up to 300 years. The bark is smooth and grey. The leaves are alternate, simple, and entire or with a slightly crenate margin, 5–10 cm long and 3–7 cm broad, with 6–7 veins on each side. The buds are long and slender, 15–30 mm long, a distinctive feature of the genus.

The flowers are small catkins which appear shortly after the leaves in spring. The seeds, called beechnuts, are small triangular nuts 15–20 mm long, enclosed in a soft-spined cupule. They are edible but bitter due to tannins, and were traditionally used as fodder for pigs and pressed for oil.

Distribution and habitat

Natural range extends across western and central Europe. Beech requires a humid atmosphere and well-drained soil; it is sensitive to spring frost and drought in its early years. In the southern part of its range it grows mainly in mountainous regions at 600–1,800 m altitude. Beech forests are noted for their dense canopy, which casts deep shade and supports a characteristic ground flora including bluebells, wood anemone and wild garlic.

Uses

Beech wood is an excellent firewood and is used for furniture, flooring, tool handles and charcoal. The timber is hard, strong and fine-grained but not durable outdoors without treatment. Ornamental cultivars, such as the copper beech (Fagus sylvatica f. purpurea) and the fern-leaved beech, are widely planted in parks and large gardens. Primeval beech forests of the Carpathians and other regions of Europe are inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.
