Proportions of the human body

The study of the proportions of the human body — the ratios between the sizes of body parts — has been a central concern of artists, anatomists, and anthropologists since antiquity. Artists' canons express total height in units of the head: an average adult is about 7 to 7.5 heads tall, while idealised figures in art are often drawn at 8 heads, and heroic or fashion figures at 8.5–9 heads. A newborn, by contrast, is only about 4 heads tall, since the head accounts for roughly a quarter of infant length (about an eighth in adults).

Historical canons

Egyptian artists used grid systems dividing the standing figure into a fixed number of units (18 fists from hairline to sole in the classical canon). The Greek sculptor Polykleitos wrote a treatise, the Canon, and embodied his system of symmetria in the Doryphoros. The Roman architect Vitruvius recorded that the body is eight heads (or ten faces) tall and that a man's outstretched arms equal his height — the relations Leonardo da Vinci drew in the Vitruvian Man (c. 1490). Dürer's Four Books on Human Proportion (1528) documented a wide range of body types, and Le Corbusier's Modulor (1948) later based architectural dimensions on body proportions and the golden ratio.

Common proportions (adult)

  • The navel divides height in approximately the golden ratio in some idealised schemes; the span of outstretched arms ≈ height.
  • The forearm plus hand ≈ the length of the foot; the head from chin to crown ≈ 1/8 of height in the classical canon.
  • The eye-line sits roughly halfway down the head; the bottom of the nose is about halfway between the eyes and the chin.
  • Shoulder width ≈ 2 heads (men) to 1.5 heads; hip width ≈ 1.5 heads.

Modern study

Quantitative study of body proportions falls under anthropometry and allometry. Ratios vary systematically with age, sex, and population, and are used in ergonomics, medicine (assessing growth and syndromes), forensic reconstruction, and the sizing systems of the clothing industry. The waist–hip ratio and digit ratio (2D:4D) are among the proportions studied for health and developmental correlations.
