Sumatra

Sumatra is one of the Sunda Islands of western Indonesia, the sixth-largest island in the world (about 482,000 square kilometres) and the largest island entirely within Indonesia. It lies along the Strait of Malacca, one of the world's busiest shipping lanes, opposite the Malay Peninsula.

The Barisan Mountains run down its western side, including Mount Kerinci (3,805 metres), while the east is low swampy plain drained by rivers such as the Musi and Batang Hari. Sumatra's rainforests are home to critically endangered Sumatran orangutans, tigers, elephants and rhinos, and Lake Toba, the world's largest volcanic lake, formed by a supervolcano eruption about 74,000 years ago. In 2004 the undersea megathrust earthquake off Sumatra triggered the Boxing Day tsunami that killed some 170,000 people in Aceh alone.
