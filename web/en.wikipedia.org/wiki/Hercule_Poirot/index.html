Hercule Poirot

Hercule Poirot is a fictional Belgian detective created by the English writer Agatha Christie. One of Christie's most famous and long-running characters, he appeared in 33 novels, 2 plays and more than 50 short stories published between 1920 and 1975, and solved cases using his "little grey cells" and his insistence on order and method.

Character

Poirot is a retired Belgian police officer who fled to England as a refugee during the First World War. Short, dapper and fastidious, with an egg-shaped head and a magnificent waxed moustache, he is vain about his appearance and his intellect alike. He frequently works with his friend Captain Arthur Hastings, who narrates many of the early stories, and is grudgingly assisted by Chief Inspector Japp of Scotland Yard.

Notable cases

His most famous investigations include The Murder of Roger Ackroyd (1926), Murder on the Orient Express (1934), Death on the Nile (1937) and The A.B.C. Murders (1936). His final case, Curtain, was written in the 1940s but held back and published in 1975, shortly before Christie's death; Poirot remains the only fictional character to have received an obituary in The New York Times.

Adaptations

Poirot has been played on screen by actors including Austin Trevor, Peter Ustinov, Albert Finney and Kenneth Branagh, but the portrayal most widely regarded as definitive is David Suchet's in the ITV series Agatha Christie's Poirot (1989–2013), which adapted nearly the entire canon.
