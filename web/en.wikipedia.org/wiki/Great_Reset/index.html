Great Reset

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Great Reset is an initiative launched by the World Economic Forum (WEF) in June 2020, proposing that the COVID-19 pandemic be used as an opportunity to reform global economic and social systems. It was introduced by the Prince of Wales (now Charles III) and WEF founder Klaus Schwab, and formed the theme of the WEF's 2021 annual meeting at Davos. The initiative is set out in Schwab's book COVID-19: The Great Reset (2020), co-authored with Thierry Malleret.

Proposals

The initiative rests on three main components:

  • Steering the market toward fairer outcomes through government policy on taxation, regulation, and fiscal programmes.
  • Stakeholder capitalism and ESG investment — directing investment toward shared goals of equity and sustainability, with metrics beyond GDP and corporate profit.
  • Harnessing the Fourth Industrial Revolution — using technological innovation to address health, environmental, and social challenges.

Reception

Supporters, including a number of governments and business leaders, presented the Great Reset as a framework for "building back better" after the pandemic, linking recovery spending to climate goals and digital infrastructure. Critics across the political spectrum objected that it was an unelected body proposing sweeping economic change, that its proposals expanded managerial and state control of markets, or conversely that they amounted to greenwashing by the corporations involved. The phrase also became the centre of online conspiracy theories alleging a secret plan to abolish private property — claims drawn largely from a misreading of a speculative WEF essay ("you'll own nothing and be happy") that fact-checkers and the WEF itself have rejected. "Great Reset" has since been used more loosely in political debate as a label for post-pandemic economic interventionism.
