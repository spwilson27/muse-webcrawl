Grand hotel (United States)

A grand hotel in the United States is a large, luxurious hotel, typically a destination in itself, distinguished by extensive grounds, elaborate public rooms, fine dining and recreational amenities. The American grand hotel tradition flourished in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, closely tied to railroad expansion and the rise of leisure tourism among the wealthy.

History

Early exemplars included the great resort houses of Saratoga Springs and White Sulphur Springs, where elite society gathered for the "season". The railroad age produced monumental hotels at scenic destinations: the Grand Hotel on Mackinac Island, Michigan (1887), famous for its 660-foot porch — the longest in the world; the Hotel del Coronado near San Diego (1888), a vast wooden Victorian beach resort; The Greenbrier in West Virginia; the Mohonk Mountain House in New York (1869); and the mountain houses of the White Mountains and the Adirondack Great Camps' hotel cousins. In the national parks, railroad companies built rustic grand lodges such as Yellowstone's Old Faithful Inn (1904), the Grand Canyon's El Tovar (1905) and Glacier National Park's Many Glacier Hotel (1915) — a style later dubbed "parkitecture". Urban palace hotels, including New York's Waldorf-Astoria and Plaza Hotel, extended the model to the city.

Characteristics

Grand hotels typically offered hundreds of rooms, ballrooms, orchestras, promenades, mineral-spring or seaside "cures", golf, and formal dining under celebrated chefs, with service rituals and dress codes that made the hotel stay itself a social performance. Many declined with the Great Depression, the automobile age and changing tastes; some burned or were demolished, while survivors were often restored in the late twentieth century and are listed on the National Register of Historic Places or designated National Historic Landmarks.

Notable examples

  • Grand Hotel, Mackinac Island, Michigan (1887)
  • The Greenbrier, White Sulphur Springs, West Virginia (1778/1913)
  • Hotel del Coronado, Coronado, California (1888)
  • The Broadmoor, Colorado Springs, Colorado (1918)
  • The Breakers, Palm Beach, Florida (1896/1926)
  • The Ahwahnee Hotel, Yosemite National Park (1927)
  • Mohonk Mountain House, New Paltz, New York (1869)
