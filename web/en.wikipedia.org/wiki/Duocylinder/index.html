Duocylinder

Duocylinder
Type: 4-dimensional geometric object
Cartesian product: two disks

The duocylinder is a geometric object in 4-dimensional Euclidean space defined as the Cartesian product of two disks of respective radii r1 and r2: D = {(x, y, z, w) : x² + y² ≤ r₁², z² + w² ≤ r₂²}.

Properties

It is the 4-dimensional analogue of the cylinder in 3 dimensions and the rectangle in 2 dimensions. The boundary of the duocylinder consists of two "surfaces" — each the Cartesian product of a disk with a circle — which meet at a toroidal ridge. Its hypervolume is π²r₁²r₂², and it is closely related to the Clifford torus, which is the set of points equidistant in both planes; the Clifford torus forms the ridge of the duocylinder when the radii are equal.

Projections

Under parallel projection into three dimensions, the duocylinder appears as overlapping cylinders or tori depending on orientation. It is used in popular accounts of four-dimensional geometry alongside the tesseract and hypersphere. See four-dimensional space and Clifford torus.
