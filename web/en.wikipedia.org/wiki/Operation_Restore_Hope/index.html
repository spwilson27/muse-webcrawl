Operation Restore Hope

Operation Restore Hope was a United States military operation, conducted with the support of the United Nations, in Somalia from December 1992 to May 1993. The operation, formally carried out by the Unified Task Force (UNITAF) authorized under UN Security Council Resolution 794, aimed to create a secure environment for the delivery of humanitarian aid during the Somali Civil War and the famine of 1992.

Background

After the overthrow of President Siad Barre in 1991, Somalia collapsed into clan-based warfare. Fighting between factions, particularly those of Mohamed Farrah Aidid and Ali Mahdi Muhammad, obstructed relief efforts, and hundreds of thousands of Somalis faced starvation. The initial UN mission, UNOSOM I, proved unable to operate effectively.

The operation

On 9 December 1992, US Marines landed near Mogadishu, the first of some 37,000 troops, mostly American, deployed under UNITAF. The force secured ports, airfields and food distribution centres, significantly improving the flow of relief supplies. In May 1993, UNITAF handed over to UNOSOM II, whose broader nation-building mandate led to increasing conflict with Somali factions, culminating in the Battle of Mogadishu in October 1993.
