Treprostinil, sold under the brand names Remodulin, Tyvaso and Orenitram among others, is a vasodilator used in the treatment of pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH). It is a synthetic analogue of prostacyclin (PGI2). Treprostinil was approved for medical use in the United States in 2002, initially as a continuous subcutaneous infusion.

Medical uses

Treprostinil is indicated to improve exercise capacity and symptoms in patients with WHO Group 1 pulmonary arterial hypertension. Different formulations are approved for different routes: continuous subcutaneous or intravenous infusion, inhaled solution delivered by nebuliser, and oral extended-release tablets. The inhaled formulation is also approved for pulmonary hypertension associated with interstitial lung disease (WHO Group 3), following the INCREASE trial.

Mechanism of action

As a prostacyclin analogue, treprostinil binds prostacyclin (IP) receptors — and also DP1 and EP2 receptors — on vascular smooth muscle and platelets, increasing intracellular cyclic AMP. This produces vasodilation of the pulmonary and systemic arterial beds, inhibition of platelet aggregation, and antiproliferative effects on vascular smooth muscle.

Adverse effects

Common side effects are those of prostacyclin-class drugs: headache, flushing, jaw pain, diarrhoea, nausea and rash. Infusion-site pain and reactions are very common with subcutaneous administration and are a frequent reason for switching routes. Abrupt discontinuation of infused treprostinil can cause rebound worsening of pulmonary hypertension, and the intravenous form carries a risk of bloodstream infection from the indwelling catheter. Its half-life of about four hours is longer than that of epoprostenol, making it more forgiving of brief interruptions.

History

Treprostinil was developed by United Therapeutics, which was founded to develop treatments for pulmonary arterial hypertension after founder Martine Rothblatt's daughter was diagnosed with the disease. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
