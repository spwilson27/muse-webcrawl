The 2006 FIFA World Cup was the eighteenth FIFA World Cup, held in Germany from 9 June to 9 July 2006. Italy won their fourth world title, beating France 5–3 on penalties after a 1–1 draw in the final at the Olympiastadion, Berlin. The final is remembered for the dismissal of France's Zinedine Zidane in his last professional match, sent off in extra time for headbutting Marco Materazzi.

Tournament summary

Hosts Germany, under Jürgen Klinsmann and built around captain Michael Ballack and strikers Miroslav Klose and Lukas Podolski, energised the home "summer fairy tale" before losing a tense semi-final to Italy, whose late extra-time goals by Fabio Grosso and Alessandro Del Piero became iconic. France, written off after a slow group stage, eliminated Spain, defending champions Brazil and Portugal behind veteran performances from Zidane, Lilian Thuram and Claude Makélélé. Italy conceded only two goals all tournament — an own goal and a penalty — behind goalkeeper Gianluigi Buffon and captain Fabio Cannavaro.

Final

Zidane converted an audacious chipped penalty in the seventh minute; Materazzi equalised with a header twelve minutes later. After a goalless extra time in which Zidane was sent off, Italy won the shootout 5–3, Fabio Grosso converting the decisive kick after David Trezeguet's effort hit the crossbar.

Legacy

Germany 2006 was a commercial and organisational triumph, credited with reshaping Germany's international image. It was the first World Cup in which the defending champions had to qualify; the last in which the winners' native continent hosted consecutively in Europe; and it launched the golden generation of German players who won the title eight years later.
