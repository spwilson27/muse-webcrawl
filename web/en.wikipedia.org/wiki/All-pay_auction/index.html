All-pay auction

In economics and game theory, an all-pay auction is an auction in which every bidder must pay their bid regardless of whether they win the prize, which goes to the highest bidder. Unlike a standard auction, losing bids are not refunded.

Examples

Everyday analogues include raffles and lotteries in which all tickets are paid for but only one wins, penny auctions online, and, more loosely, contests such as lobbying, political campaigns and research-and-development races, where all competitors sink costs but only one obtains the reward. The format is also closely related to models of conflict such as the war of attrition.

Analysis

Because bids are sunk, bidders shade their bids relative to their valuation of the prize. In the classic model with complete information and risk-neutral bidders, equilibrium bidding involves mixed strategies, and expected total revenue equals the value of the prize — a result connected with rent dissipation in rent-seeking contests and the Tullock contest model. All-pay auctions are used in mechanism design, including for charity auctions, where the fact that all payments are collected can raise more money for a cause than a conventional auction.
