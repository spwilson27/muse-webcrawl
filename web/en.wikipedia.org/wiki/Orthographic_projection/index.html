Orthographic projection

Orthographic projection is a means of representing three-dimensional objects in two dimensions. It is a form of parallel projection in which all projection lines are orthogonal (perpendicular) to the projection plane, so that the view shows the true shape and size of faces parallel to that plane, undistorted by perspective. The term derives from the Greek orthos (straight, upright) and graphē (drawing).

Multiview projection

In engineering and technical drawing, an object is depicted by a set of aligned views — typically front, top and side — arranged either in first-angle projection (standard in Europe and Asia) or third-angle projection (standard in North America). Because each view preserves measurable distances, multiview orthographic drawings are the working documents of manufacturing and architecture; conventions for them are codified by ISO and national standards bodies.

Axonometric forms

Axonometric projections rotate the object so that several faces appear in a single view while keeping projection lines parallel: isometric projection (equal foreshortening on three axes), dimetric and trimetric projections. These remain "orthographic" in the strict sense because the projectors are perpendicular to the picture plane; oblique projections, by contrast, use projectors at an angle.

In cartography and graphics

Mapmakers use an orthographic projection of the Earth that shows the planet as if viewed from an infinite distance — a hemisphere as it would appear from space. In computer graphics the orthographic camera is one of the two standard view transforms (the other being perspective), defined by a box-shaped viewing volume, and is used in CAD, strategy games and 2D-style rendering where parallel lines must stay parallel and sizes must not shrink with distance.

History

The principles were formalized in Gaspard Monge's descriptive geometry (late 18th century) and were exploited for industrial drawing throughout the 19th century; plan and elevation views in architecture are older still.
