Jonathan Kolia Favreau (born October 19, 1966) is an American actor and filmmaker. He first gained attention as the writer and star of the independent comedy Swingers (1996) and went on to direct major studio films including Elf (2003), Iron Man (2008) and The Jungle Book (2016). He is a central creative figure in the Marvel Cinematic Universe and the Star Wars franchise.

Early life

Favreau was born in Flushing, Queens, New York City, the son of Madeleine, an elementary school teacher, and Charles Favreau, a special education teacher. He attended the Bronx High School of Science and studied at Queens College before leaving to pursue acting and comedy, including a stint with Chicago's ImprovOlympic.

Career

After roles in Rudy (1993) and Mrs. Parker and the Vicious Circle (1994), Favreau wrote and starred in Swingers (1996), directed by Doug Liman, which became a cult success. He directed his first feature, Made (2001), then scored a major hit with the Christmas comedy Elf (2003) starring Will Ferrell.

In 2008 he directed Iron Man, which launched the Marvel Cinematic Universe; he also played Happy Hogan in the films and directed the sequel Iron Man 2 (2010). He wrote, directed and starred in the food-truck drama Chef (2014), directed Disney's photorealistic remakes The Jungle Book (2016) and The Lion King (2019), and created the Star Wars series The Mandalorian (2019), pioneering the use of LED-volume virtual production.
