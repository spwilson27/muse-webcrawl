Constitution of Georgia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia. This article is about the constitution of the country of Georgia.

The Constitution of Georgia (Georgian: საქართველოს კონსტიტუცია) is the supreme law of Georgia. It was approved by the Parliament of Georgia on 24 August 1995 and entered into force on 17 October 1995. The constitution replaced the decrees and interim arrangements that had governed the country since independence from the Soviet Union in 1991, and defines Georgia as a democratic, unitary republic with a separation of powers among legislative, executive, and judicial branches.

History and amendments

Georgia's first modern constitution was adopted in 1921 by the Democratic Republic of Georgia, shortly before the Red Army invasion. The 1995 text drew on that earlier document as well as European models. Major amendments in 2004 strengthened the presidency under Mikheil Saakashvili, introducing a prime minister and cabinet accountable to both president and parliament. Amendments adopted in 2010 and taking effect in 2013 shifted power toward the prime minister and parliament. A comprehensive revision in 2017–2018, enacted by the Georgian Dream government, moved Georgia to a fully parliamentary system: the president became a largely ceremonial head of state elected by an electoral college, direct presidential elections were abolished after 2018, and the country committed to a fully proportional parliamentary electoral system, phased in for the 2024 elections.

Contents

The constitution guarantees fundamental human rights and freedoms, defines the territory of Georgia (while noting the unresolved status of Abkhazia and the Tskhinvali region/South Ossetia), establishes the Constitutional Court, and declares the country's aspiration toward European and Euro-Atlantic integration; a 2018 amendment obliges state bodies to act to ensure full integration into the European Union and NATO. Georgian is the state language, with Abkhaz also recognised in Abkhazia.
