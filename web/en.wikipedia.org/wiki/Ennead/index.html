Ennead

The Ennead (from Greek enneas, "group of nine"; Egyptian psḏt) is a group of nine deities in ancient Egyptian mythology, worshipped principally at Heliopolis. The best known, the Great Ennead, forms a family tree of the gods descending from the creator god Atum, and its genealogy underpins the central Egyptian creation myth of the Osiris cycle.

The nine gods

  1. Atum — the self-created creator god, who emerged from the primordial waters of Nun.
  2. Shu — god of air, produced by Atum.
  3. Tefnut — goddess of moisture, Shu's twin and consort.
  4. Geb — god of the earth, child of Shu and Tefnut.
  5. Nut — goddess of the sky, Geb's sister and consort.
  6. Osiris — god of the afterlife and resurrection, eldest child of Geb and Nut.
  7. Isis — goddess of magic and motherhood, Osiris's sister and wife.
  8. Set — god of chaos, storms and the desert, who murdered Osiris.
  9. Nephthys — goddess of mourning, sister of Isis and consort of Set.

Mythology

According to the Heliopolitan cosmogony, Atum arose from the benben mound in the waters of chaos and, being alone, produced Shu and Tefnut. Their children Geb and Nut were born locked in an embrace and had to be separated by Shu, creating the space between earth and sky in which life could exist. The youngest generation of the Ennead — Osiris, Isis, Set and Nephthys — star in the great myth of Osiris's murder by Set, his resurrection through Isis's magic, and the struggle of his son Horus (not himself a member of the Ennead) to claim the throne of Egypt. The sequence of the Ennead thus moves from cosmic creation to the divine model of Egyptian kingship.

Other enneads

"Ennead" was a flexible term: Egyptian theology recognised several groupings of nine gods, and the number nine also carried the sense of a plural of plurals — three times three — meaning "all the gods". Temples at other cities arranged their own enneads around their local chief deity.
