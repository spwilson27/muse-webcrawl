Poor Clares

The Poor Clares, officially the Order of Saint Clare (Latin: Ordo Sanctae Clarae), are a contemplative order of nuns in the Roman Catholic Church. Founded by Saint Clare of Assisi and Saint Francis of Assisi in 1212, it was the second Franciscan order established, and its members — known originally as the Poor Ladies — devote themselves to prayer, poverty, and enclosure.

History

Clare, a young noblewoman of Assisi, left her family on Palm Sunday 1212 to follow Francis's way of life. She gathered a community at the church of San Damiano, where she remained abbess until her death in 1253. Clare fought for and finally received papal approval for her Rule — the first rule written by a woman for a religious order — including the "privilege of poverty", the right to own nothing even in common.

Observances and branches

Over the centuries the order divided into several observances differing in the strictness of poverty and enclosure, including the Colettine Poor Clares, the Capuchin Poor Clares (Clarisses Capucines), and the Poor Clares of Perpetual Adoration. Today there are roughly 20,000 Poor Clare nuns in over 900 monasteries across 76 countries, living a cloistered life centred on the Liturgy of the Hours, Eucharistic adoration, and manual work such as altar-bread baking.
