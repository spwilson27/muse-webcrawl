Q-Pochhammer symbol

In mathematics, in the area of combinatorics and special functions, the q-Pochhammer symbol, also called the q-shifted factorial or q-factorial, is the q-analog of the Pochhammer symbol. It is defined as

(a; q)n = ∏k=0n−1 (1 − a qk), with (a; q)0 = 1.

The infinite q-Pochhammer symbol is defined for |q| < 1 by (a; q)∞ = ∏k=0∞ (1 − a qk).

Combinatorics

The q-Pochhammer symbol is a major building block in the construction of q-analogs. In combinatorics, (q; q)n counts weighted permutations, and the symbol appears in the generating functions for integer partitions: ∏k=1∞ 1/(1 − qk) = 1/(q; q)∞ generates the partition numbers. It is fundamental to the theory of basic hypergeometric series, q-series, and identities such as Euler's pentagonal number theorem.
