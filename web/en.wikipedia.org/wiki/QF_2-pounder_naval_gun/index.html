QF 2-pounder naval gun

The QF 2-pounder naval gun, commonly called the "pom-pom", was a British 40 mm automatic cannon used as an anti-aircraft gun on Royal Navy and Commonwealth warships from the 1930s through the Second World War. Its nickname came from the distinctive sound of its firing.

Development

The gun was developed by Vickers from the earlier 2-pounder of the First World War era. It fired a 2-pound (40 mm) high-explosive shell and was mounted singly and in quadruple and octuple mountings, which could deliver a dense barrage against low-flying aircraft. The octuple mounting, controlled by a director, was fitted to many cruisers and capital ships.

Service

By the Second World War the pom-pom's relatively low muzzle velocity and short effective range made it less effective than the 40 mm Bofors gun, but it remained in widespread service throughout the war. It was gradually replaced by Bofors mountings in the later war years.
