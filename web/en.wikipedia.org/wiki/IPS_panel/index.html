IPS (in-plane switching) is a screen technology for liquid-crystal displays (LCDs). In IPS panels, the liquid-crystal molecules switch parallel to the plane of the panel rather than perpendicular to it, which solves the main weaknesses of earlier twisted nematic (TN) panels: poor colour reproduction and narrow viewing angles.

History

The in-plane switching principle was patented in the 1970s but only became commercially practical in the 1990s. Hitachi developed and commercialised IPS in 1996, with major improvements under names such as Super-IPS and Advanced Super-IPS. LG Display later became the largest producer of IPS panels, and the technology became standard in smartphones, tablets and professional monitors. Apple's Retina displays popularised IPS in mobile devices.

Characteristics

Because the crystals rotate within the plane of the glass, light passes consistently regardless of viewing angle, giving IPS panels viewing angles approaching 178 degrees with minimal colour shift. IPS panels are known for strong colour accuracy and consistency, making them popular for photo and video editing. Drawbacks historically included slower response times and higher cost than TN panels, though modern variants (such as LG's Nano IPS and fast-IPS gaming panels) have largely closed the response-time gap. IPS panels can exhibit "IPS glow", a silvery sheen visible on dark screens at angles.

Variants

Related technologies include PLS (Plane-to-Line Switching) by Samsung and AHVA by AU Optronics, which offer similar behaviour. VA (vertical alignment) panels are the main competing LCD technology, offering higher contrast but narrower viewing angles.
