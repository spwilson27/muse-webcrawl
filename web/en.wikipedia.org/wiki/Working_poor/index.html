Working poor

The working poor are working people whose incomes fall below a given poverty line due to low-income jobs and low familial household income. These are people who spend at least 27 weeks in a year working or looking for employment, but remain under the poverty threshold.

Definition

In the United States, the official measurement is based on the Bureau of Labor Statistics definition. Similar concepts are used in Europe, where in-work poverty is measured relative to median income, often at 60% of the median.

Causes

Low wages, part-time or precarious employment, single-earner households, high housing and childcare costs, and limited access to benefits all contribute. Structural factors include deindustrialisation, growth of service work, and weakening collective bargaining in some countries.

By country

Rates vary widely with welfare systems, minimum wages and labour protections. The phenomenon exists in both developed and developing economies, though its forms differ — informal work dominates in much of the Global South.

Responses

Policies discussed include minimum-wage increases, earned-income tax credits, in-work benefits, affordable housing, childcare support and training.
