Crown Dependencies

The Crown Dependencies are three offshore island territories in the British Isles that are self-governing possessions of the British Crown: the Bailiwick of Guernsey and the Bailiwick of Jersey, both in the English Channel off the coast of Normandy, and the Isle of Man in the Irish Sea. They are not part of the United Kingdom, nor are they British Overseas Territories. Their heads of state are the Lord of Mann (in the Isle of Man) and the Duke of Normandy (in the Channel Islands) — titles held by the British monarch — represented in each island by a lieutenant governor.

The Isle of Man lies in the Irish Sea; Jersey and Guernsey lie in the English Channel near the French coast.

The Bailiwick of Guernsey comprises the island of Guernsey itself and the smaller islands of Alderney, Sark, Herm and others, each with a degree of internal self-government. Tynwald, the Manx legislature, claims to be the oldest continuously operating parliament in the world, with origins in the Norse period over a thousand years ago.

Constitutional status

Each dependency has its own legal system, legislature, courts, taxation and stamps and coins; they share the pound sterling, issuing their own notes and coins. The United Kingdom Parliament does not normally legislate for them without their consent, although the UK is responsible for their defence and international representation. Island laws receive royal assent through the Privy Council. The territories are not represented in the UK Parliament, and their residents are generally British citizens.

Relationship with the UK and EU

Historically the Channel Islands are the surviving fragments of the Duchy of Normandy held by the English Crown; the Isle of Man was purchased by the Crown in 1765 (the "Revestment"). The dependencies were never members of the European Union, but while the UK was a member they were within the EU customs union for goods under Protocol 3 of the UK's accession treaty, and after Brexit they entered into customs arrangements with the United Kingdom. All three have developed substantial financial services sectors and have been the subject of international scrutiny as low-tax jurisdictions.
