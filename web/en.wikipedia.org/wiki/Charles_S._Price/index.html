Charles S. Price

Charles Sydney Price, Pentecostal evangelist and faith healer.

Charles Sydney Price (1887 – 8 March 1947) was a Welsh-born Canadian-American Pentecostal evangelist and faith healer. Originally a Congregational minister, Price became one of the most prominent healing evangelists associated with the tradition of the Azusa Street Revival after his own Pentecostal conversion in 1921, and conducted large campaigns across the United States and Canada. He also founded the magazine Golden Grain, which he edited until his death.

Early life and conversion

Price was born in England and emigrated to Canada, where he practised law briefly before entering the ministry. He was ordained in the Congregational Church and pastored a church in Lodi, California. Originally sceptical of Pentecostalism, he attended meetings held by Aimee Semple McPherson in San Jose in 1921 to gather material against the movement, but was instead converted and baptized in the Holy Spirit, an experience that transformed his preaching and led him to resign his pastorate and begin evangelistic work.

Healing ministry

Price's campaigns in the 1920s and 1930s, particularly on the Pacific coast and in western Canada — including a well-attended Vancouver campaign in 1923 — drew very large crowds, and his meetings were noted for testimonies of healing. He later pastored in Ashland, Oregon, and conducted campaigns in the eastern United States during the 1940s. Price was noted among Pentecostal evangelists for combining healing ministry with an emphasis on consecration and devotional life, themes developed in his books, including The Real Faith, which circulated widely after his death and influenced later healing revivalists of the late 1940s and 1950s.

Death and legacy

Price died in March 1947, shortly before the post-war healing revival in which figures such as William Branham and Oral Roberts became prominent. His writings, especially The Real Faith, continued to be reprinted and are regarded as an early statement of the faith teaching characteristic of that later movement.
