The Beast (Revelation)

The Beast (Greek: Θηρίον, Thērion) may refer to one of three beasts described in the Book of Revelation, the final book of the New Testament. Most commonly it denotes the Beast from the Sea of Revelation 13, a creature with seven heads and ten horns, given authority by the dragon (Satan), which demands worship and bears the mysterious "number of the beast", 666. A second creature, the Beast from the Earth — later called the False Prophet — performs signs and compels people to worship the first beast, while the great red dragon of Revelation 12 is sometimes counted as a third.

The Beast from the Sea, with seven heads and ten horns, as described in Revelation 13.

Description

Revelation 13 describes the first beast rising from the sea, resembling a leopard with the feet of a bear and the mouth of a lion — imagery echoing the four beasts of Daniel 7 — with seven heads, ten horns, and ten crowns, and blasphemous names on its heads. One head receives a mortal wound that is healed, astonishing the world. The beast speaks blasphemies and makes war on the saints for 42 months. The second beast rises from the earth, has two horns like a lamb but speaks like a dragon, performs miraculous signs including calling down fire, and causes all people to receive a mark on the right hand or forehead, without which they cannot buy or sell; the mark is the name of the beast or its number, 666 (some manuscripts read 616).

Interpretations

Interpretations fall broadly into four schools. Preterists read the beast as a figure of the first century — commonly the Roman Empire or the emperor Nero, whose name in Hebrew letters (Neron Kaisar) sums by gematria to 666. Historicists spread the symbolism across church history, often identifying the beast with papal Rome. Futurists expect a future end-times ruler, frequently identified with the Antichrist. Idealists treat the beast as a timeless symbol of political power opposed to God and of persecution of the faithful. The number 666 has generated centuries of speculation, with candidates ranging across emperors, popes, and modern political figures.

Cultural impact

The beast and its number have permeated Western culture, appearing in literature, heavy-metal music, film, and conspiracy theories, and "666" remains a widely recognised symbol of evil. The phrase "mark of the beast" is used metaphorically for stigmatising systems of control.
