Developed market

A developed market is a country that is most developed in terms of its economy and capital markets. Such countries have high income, open and liquid financial markets, strong regulatory institutions, and stable access for foreign investors. The term is used by index providers such as MSCI, FTSE Russell, and S&P Dow Jones Indices to classify national stock markets, in contrast to emerging markets and frontier markets.

Criteria

Classification considers economic development (sustained high gross national income per capita), market size and liquidity, and market accessibility, including openness to foreign ownership, ease of capital flows, efficiency of the operational framework, and stability of institutions.

Examples

Developed markets include the United States, Japan, the United Kingdom, Germany, France, Canada, Australia, Switzerland, and most of Western Europe, among roughly two dozen countries in the MSCI World Index universe. Classification changes occur periodically: for example, South Korea is classed as developed by FTSE but emerging by MSCI, while countries such as Greece have been downgraded from developed to emerging status.
