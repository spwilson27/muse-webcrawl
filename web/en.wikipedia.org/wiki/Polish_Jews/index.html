History of the Jews in Poland

Polish Jews
Arrival10th–13th centuries
Population 1939~3.3 million (largest in Europe)
Key centresWarsaw, Łódź, Kraków, Vilnius, Lwów, Lublin
LanguagesYiddish, Polish, Hebrew

The history of the Jews in Poland dates back more than a thousand years. For centuries, Poland was home to the largest and most significant Jewish community in the world: the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth was a haven of relative tolerance, and Polish Jews developed a vibrant culture in Yiddish, founding dynasties of Hasidism, great yeshivas, and political movements from Bundism to Zionism.

The Old Synagogue (Stara Synagoga) in Kazimierz, Kraków, built in the 15th century.

From settlement to the Commonwealth

Jewish merchants and refugees from German lands settled in Poland from the 10th century onward; the Statute of Kalisz (1264) granted Jews legal protection and communal autonomy. Under the Commonwealth, the Council of Four Lands (1580–1764) coordinated Jewish self-government across regions. By the mid-16th century, most of the world's Jews lived in Poland-Lithuania.

Partitions, interwar period, and the Holocaust

After the partitions of Poland, Jews were divided among Russia, Austria, and Prussia, facing the Pale of Settlement, pogroms, and mass emigration. The reborn Polish Republic in 1918 counted about 3.3 million Jews — roughly 10% of the population — with Warsaw the largest Jewish city in Europe. During the Second World War, Nazi Germany murdered about 90% of Polish Jewry in the Holocaust, operating death camps including Auschwitz and Treblinka on occupied Polish soil.

Postwar

Survivors faced the Kielce pogrom of 1946 and the state antisemitic campaign of 1968, which drove most remaining Jews to emigrate. Since 1989 a small Jewish revival has taken place, marked by Kraków's Jewish Culture Festival and the POLIN Museum of the History of Polish Jews in Warsaw (opened 2013).
