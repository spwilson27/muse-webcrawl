Gusztáv Sebes

Gusztáv Sebes
Born: 22 January 1906, Budapest
Died: 30 January 1986, Budapest
Occupation: Football manager
Teams managed: Hungary (1949–1956)

Gusztáv Sebes (22 January 1906 – 30 January 1986) was a Hungarian football manager and player, best known as the coach of Hungary's legendary "Golden Team" (Mighty Magyars) of the early 1950s, featuring Ferenc Puskás and Sándor Kocsis.

Career

As a player, Sebes was a midfielder for clubs including MTK Budapest. After World War II he became deputy minister for sport and head coach of the national team, giving him unusual control over player selection and training.

The Golden Team

Under Sebes, Hungary won gold at the 1952 Helsinki Olympics, went unbeaten for years, defeated England 6–3 at Wembley in 1953 ("Match of the Century"), and reached the 1954 World Cup final, losing to West Germany in the "Miracle of Bern."

Legacy

Sebes pioneered the deep-lying centre-forward role and intensive national-team preparation, influencing modern football management.
