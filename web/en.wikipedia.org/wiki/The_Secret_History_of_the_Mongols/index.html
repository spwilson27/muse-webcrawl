The Secret History of the Mongols is the oldest surviving literary work in the Mongolian language and the principal native source for the life of Genghis Khan and the early history of the Mongol Empire. Written by an anonymous author, probably a member of the imperial court, shortly after Genghis Khan's death in 1227, it was apparently intended for circulation only within the imperial family — hence its "secret" title.

Content

The work combines myth, genealogy, epic poetry, and history. It opens with the legendary descent of the Mongols from a blue-grey wolf and a fallow doe, traces the ancestry of Temüjin (the future Genghis Khan), and narrates his difficult youth, his unification of the steppe tribes, his proclamation as khan in 1206, and his conquests, concluding with the reign of his successor Ögedei. It is notable for its candid treatment of its hero, recording his fears, family conflicts, and ruthless decisions as well as his triumphs.

Transmission

The original text, written in the Uyghur-derived Mongolian script, is lost. The work survives through a Ming-dynasty Chinese transcription, in which the Mongolian sounds were represented with Chinese characters, accompanied by interlinear glosses and abridged Chinese translations. This text was reconstructed by scholars in the 19th and 20th centuries and has been translated into many languages, notably by Francis Woodman Cleaves and Igor de Rachewiltz in English.
