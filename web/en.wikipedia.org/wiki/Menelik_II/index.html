Overview

Menelik II (1844–1913) was King of Shewa and, from 1889, Emperor of Ethiopia. He doubled Ethiopia's territory by conquest southward, founded Addis Ababa as his capital (1886), and is celebrated for defeating Italy at the Battle of Adwa on 1 March 1896 — the decisive African victory of the Scramble for Africa, preserving Ethiopian independence when Italy tried to convert the Treaty of Wuchale into a protectorate on the basis of a discrepancy between its Amharic and Italian texts.

Modernisation

Menelik introduced Ethiopia's first modern schools, hospitals, bank, postal service and (planned) railway from Djibouti, imported arms and telegraph lines, and began Addis Ababa's eucalyptus planting. His later years were clouded by illness and succession manoeuvring around Iyasu V; his widow Empress Taytu Betul and Ras Makonnen were among the powers of his court. His great-nephew Ras Tafari — the future Haile Selassie — rose in the regency that followed.
