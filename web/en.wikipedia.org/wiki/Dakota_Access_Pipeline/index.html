Dakota Access Pipeline

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Dakota Access Pipeline (DAPL), also known as the Bakken pipeline, is a 1,172-mile-long (1,886 km) underground oil pipeline in the United States. It carries crude oil from the Bakken shale fields of northwestern North Dakota to a storage and distribution hub near Patoka, Illinois, passing through South Dakota and Iowa. The pipeline was developed by Dakota Access, LLC, a subsidiary of Energy Transfer Partners, at a cost of around $3.8 billion, and entered service on 1 June 2017 with a capacity of roughly 470,000 barrels per day, later optimised to over 700,000 barrels per day.

Protests

The pipeline became the focus of major protests in 2016–2017, centred on its crossing of the Missouri River at Lake Oahe, just upstream of the Standing Rock Sioux Reservation in North Dakota. Members of the Standing Rock Sioux Tribe argued that the pipeline threatened their drinking water and sacred burial sites, and that the federal government had failed to consult them adequately. The protest camps drew thousands of Native American "water protectors" and supporters from around the world, and the movement became a prominent symbol of Indigenous rights and environmental activism, organised under slogans including "Water is Life" (Mni Wiconi).

Confrontations between protesters and law enforcement in late 2016 drew national attention. In December 2016 the Obama administration denied the final easement pending further environmental review, but the incoming Trump administration reversed the decision in early 2017, and construction was completed. The main protest camps were cleared in February 2017.

Legal proceedings

The Standing Rock Sioux and Cheyenne River Sioux tribes continued to challenge the pipeline in court. In 2020 a federal judge ordered a full environmental impact statement and briefly ordered the pipeline shut down and emptied, though the shutdown order was overturned on appeal and the pipeline continued operating while the review proceeded. Litigation and regulatory review have continued into the 2020s.
