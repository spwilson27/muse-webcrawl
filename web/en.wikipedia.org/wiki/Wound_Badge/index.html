The Wound Badge (German: Verwundetenabzeichen) was a German military decoration awarded to soldiers wounded in combat. Instituted by Kaiser Wilhelm II on 3 March 1918, during the First World War, it was re-established by Adolf Hitler in 1939 for the Second World War, and a special version was struck for the survivors of the 20 July 1944 assassination attempt on Hitler. The badge depicted a German steel helmet (a 1935-pattern helmet with swastika in the Second World War issue) before crossed swords, within a laurel wreath.

Grades and criteria

The badge was awarded in three grades reflecting the number and severity of wounds:

  • Black — for one or two wounds;
  • Silver — for three or four wounds, or for a single serious wound such as loss of a hand, foot or eye, or severe disfigurement;
  • Gold — for five or more wounds, or for total disability such as blindness or severe brain damage; it was also awarded posthumously to the next of kin of those killed in action after 1939.

Wounds were certified in a soldier's pay book, and badges were typically produced in stamped steel, brass or zinc by many licensed manufacturers. In the First World War an equivalent badge was also created for naval personnel and for colonial troops, and a version in cut-out ("Durchbrochen") style was privately purchased by officers.

The 20 July 1944 badge

After the failed 20 July plot to assassinate Hitler at the Wolf's Lair, a special Wound Badge was instituted on 20 August 1944, bearing the date "20 Juli 1944" and Hitler's signature beneath the helmet. It was awarded in the three grades to the roughly two dozen military and civilian personnel wounded in the explosion, with gold badges presented to the families of those killed; Hitler himself, who was among the injured, received one. It is among the rarest of Third Reich awards.

Later use

After 1945, wearing Nazi-era decorations was restricted in Germany; the 1957 law on titles and decorations authorised de-Nazified versions of wartime awards — including the Wound Badge, with the swastika removed — for wear by Bundeswehr veterans. Unlike some other nations' awards such as the American Purple Heart, no equivalent federal wound badge has been newly instituted in the Federal Republic.
