Transmission (mechanics)
DefinitionAny mechanism that transmits mechanical power from a source to a load
ElementsGears, belts, chains, shafts, couplings, friction drives
GovernsSpeed ratio, torque ratio, direction of rotation

In mechanics, a transmission is any system of components that transmits mechanical power from a prime mover to a driven machine, usually while modifying speed, torque and direction of rotation. The term covers simple elements such as gears, belts and chains, shafts and couplings, as well as complete gearbox assemblies; the vehicle gearbox is treated separately at Transmission (mechanical device).

Principles

A transmission trades speed against torque: neglecting losses, power is conserved, so reducing output speed increases output torque in the same ratio. The ratio may be fixed (a single gear pair), stepped (a multi-ratio gearbox) or continuously variable (belt CVTs, hydrostatic and friction drives). Flexible elements — flat and V-belts, timing belts, roller chains — transmit power between separated shafts and tolerate misalignment, while rigid gear meshes give compact, precise ratios. Shafts carry power over distance, with universal joints accommodating angular offset.

Applications and history

Mechanical power transmission underpinned the Industrial Revolution: water wheels and steam engines drove whole factories through line shafts and belts before individual electric motors displaced the system in the early 20th century. Today transmissions remain essential in vehicles, machine tools, agricultural and construction equipment, wind turbines and robotics, and their design balances efficiency, noise, durability and backlash.
