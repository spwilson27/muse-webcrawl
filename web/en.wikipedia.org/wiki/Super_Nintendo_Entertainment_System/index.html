The Super Nintendo Entertainment System, commonly shortened to Super Nintendo, Super NES or SNES, is a 16-bit home video game console developed by Nintendo that was released in 1990 in Japan (as the Super Famicom) and North America in 1991, and in Europe and Australia in 1992. It was Nintendo's second programmable home console, following the Nintendo Entertainment System, and introduced advanced graphics and sound capabilities, including sprite scaling and rotation via its "Mode 7" graphics mode.

History

Designed by Masayuki Uemura — who had also designed the Famicom/NES — the SNES was launched to compete with Sega's Mega Drive/Genesis, sparking the fiercest phase of the "console wars" of the early 1990s. Its launch title Super Mario World became its best-selling game. The console's relatively slow CPU was offset by enhancement chips built into cartridges — the Super FX chip powered the polygonal Star Fox (1993) — and by an acclaimed Sony-designed SPC700 sound chip. Despite a late start against Sega, the SNES ultimately won its generation, selling 49.1 million units worldwide.

Legacy

The SNES library is considered one of the strongest in gaming history, including The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past, Super Metroid, Donkey Kong Country, Final Fantasy VI, Chrono Trigger, EarthBound, Street Fighter II and Super Mario Kart, which launched the kart-racing genre. The console's controller, with its shoulder buttons and four-button diamond layout, set the template for modern gamepads. Nintendo released a miniature plug-and-play version, the SNES Classic Edition, in 2017, and SNES games remain available through Nintendo Switch Online.
