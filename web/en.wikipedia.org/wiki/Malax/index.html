Malax

Malax (Finnish: Maalahti) is a municipality of Finland in the region of Ostrobothnia, on the west coast of the country facing the Gulf of Bothnia. The population of Malax is about 5,500, and the municipality covers an area of roughly 1,950 square kilometres, more than half of which is water. It is a bilingual municipality: the large majority of the population speak Swedish as their first language, with a Finnish-speaking minority, and both languages have official status.

Geography

Malax lies south of the city of Vaasa. The municipality includes mainland villages and a large archipelago in the Kvarken area, where post-glacial land uplift — among the fastest in the world — is continually reshaping the coastline; parts of the nearby Kvarken Archipelago form a UNESCO World Natural Heritage site shared with Sweden's High Coast. The Petalax and Övermark areas joined the municipality in earlier municipal mergers. The administrative centre is the village of Malax (Yttermalax).

Economy and life

Historically a farming, fishing and seafaring community — boat-building was a noted local craft — Malax today has small industry, agriculture, and commuters to Vaasa. The population density is low, and the municipality maintains Swedish- and Finnish-language schools and services.
