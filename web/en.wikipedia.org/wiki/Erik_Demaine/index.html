Erik Demaine

Erik D. Demaine (born February 28, 1981) is a Canadian-American professor of computer science at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and a former child prodigy. His research centres on algorithms and computational geometry, especially the mathematics of folding.

Career

Demaine was home-schooled by his father, the artist Martin Demaine, and earned a bachelor's degree from Dalhousie University at age 14 and a PhD from the University of Waterloo at 20. He joined MIT in 2001, at 20 becoming one of the youngest professors in the institute's history. In 2003 he received a MacArthur Fellowship ("genius grant").

Work

Demaine is a leading figure in computational origami; his books Geometric Folding Algorithms (2007, with Joseph O'Rourke) and Games, Puzzles, and Computation codified the field. With his father he creates curved-crease paper sculptures held in museum collections, including the Museum of Modern Art. His research also covers data structures, algorithms, and the computational complexity of video games.
