Paphos (Greek: Πάφος) is a coastal city in southwest Cyprus and the capital of Paphos District. In classical antiquity two locations were called Paphos: Old Paphos (Palaepaphos, at modern Kouklia), the site of the great sanctuary of Aphrodite, and New Paphos, founded about 12 km west and the island's Roman-era capital.

Mythology and history

Paphos was famed in the ancient world as the birthplace of Aphrodite, who was said to have risen from the sea foam near Petra tou Romiou ("Aphrodite's Rock"). The Sanctuary of Aphrodite at Old Paphos was one of the most celebrated pilgrimage shrines of the Greek world. New Paphos flourished under the Ptolemies and Romans; the Apostle Paul visited in the 1st century AD, when, according to Acts, the proconsul Sergius Paulus converted. The city later passed through Byzantine, Lusignan, Venetian, and Ottoman hands.

Sights

The Kato Paphos Archaeological Park preserves Roman villas with celebrated mosaics (Houses of Dionysos, Theseus, Aion, and Orpheus), the Odeon, agora, and the Tombs of the Kings — a large Hellenistic-Roman necropolis carved into rock. Paphos was a European Capital of Culture in 2017 and is a major resort city with a harbour dominated by a medieval castle.
