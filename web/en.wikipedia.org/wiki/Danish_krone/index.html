Danish krone

The krone (Danish: krone, plural kroner; sign: kr.; code: DKK) is the official currency of Denmark, together with its autonomous territories Greenland and the Faroe Islands. It was introduced on 1 January 1875 as part of the Scandinavian Monetary Union with Sweden and Norway.

History

The krone replaced the rigsdaler at a rate of two kroner to one rigsdaler when Denmark joined the Scandinavian Monetary Union, which fixed the krone, the Swedish krona and the Norwegian krone at par on a gold standard. The union dissolved in practice during the First World War, but the three currencies retained their names and now circulate independently at different values.

Exchange rate policy

Denmark participates in the European Exchange Rate Mechanism (ERM II); since the euro's launch in 1999 the krone has been pegged at a central rate of 7.46038 kroner per euro with a narrow band of ±2.25%, and Danmarks Nationalbank in practice holds it far more tightly. Danish voters rejected adopting the euro in a referendum in 2000, so the opt-out from the single currency remains in force.

Coins and banknotes

Coins are issued in denominations of 50 øre and 1, 2, 5, 10 and 20 kroner (100 øre = 1 krone). Current banknotes (the 2009 "bridges" series) are 50, 100, 200, 500 and 1,000 kroner, pairing Danish bridges with prehistoric artefacts. The Faroe Islands issue their own banknotes of equal value decorated with Faroese fauna, while Greenland uses ordinary Danish currency.
