Dirichlet distribution

In probability and statistics, the Dirichlet distribution (after Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet), often denoted Dir(α), is a family of continuous multivariate probability distributions parameterized by a vector α of positive reals. It is a multivariate generalization of the beta distribution. The Dirichlet distribution is the conjugate prior of the categorical distribution and multinomial distribution in Bayesian statistics, and it is commonly used as a prior distribution in Bayesian statistics and as the component distribution in Latent Dirichlet allocation.

ParametersK ≥ 2 number of categories; concentration parameters α1,…,αK > 0
Supportx1,…,xK ≥ 0 with Σxi = 1
MeanE[xi] = αi / α0

Probability density function

The probability density function of the Dirichlet distribution of order K ≥ 2 with parameters α1, …, αK > 0 is

f(x1,…,xK; α1,…,αK) = (1 / B(α)) ∏ xiαi − 1,

where the normalizing constant B(α) is the multivariate beta function, expressible in terms of gamma functions:

B(α) = ∏ Γ(αi) / Γ(Σ αi).

The support is the open (K − 1)-dimensional simplex { (x1, …, xK) ∈ ℝK : all xi ≥ 0 and Σxi = 1 }.

Properties

Marginals and aggregation

Each component marginally follows a beta distribution: xi ~ Beta(αi, α0 − αi). The distribution has the aggregation property: if components are grouped and summed, the grouped vector is again Dirichlet with the summed parameters.

Conjugate prior

If data are drawn from a categorical or multinomial distribution with unknown probabilities, placing a Dirichlet prior on those probabilities yields a Dirichlet posterior whose parameters are increased by the observed counts. This conjugacy makes the Dirichlet distribution the standard prior for multinomial models, including smoothed estimates such as Laplace smoothing.

Related distributions

The generalized Dirichlet distribution relaxes the independence structure among components. The Dirichlet-multinomial distribution results from integrating a multinomial likelihood against a Dirichlet prior. The Dirichlet process is an infinite-dimensional analogue used in nonparametric Bayesian models.

Applications

Dirichlet distributions are used to model compositional data, in topic models for text, in Bayesian mixture models, in population genetics, and in the analysis of vote shares and market shares across categories.
