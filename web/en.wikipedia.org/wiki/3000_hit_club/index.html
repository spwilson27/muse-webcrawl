3,000 hit club

The 3,000 hit club is the group of batters who have collected 3,000 or more regular-season hits in Major League Baseball (MLB). Reaching 3,000 hits has long been considered one of baseball's great milestones and an almost certain qualification for the National Baseball Hall of Fame, for players otherwise eligible and not implicated in gambling or performance-enhancing drug controversies.

Members

The club has 33 members. Pete Rose is the all-time leader with 4,256 hits, followed by Ty Cobb (4,189 or 4,191 by differing counts). Cap Anson is generally credited as the first member, reaching 3,000 in 1897. Derek Jeter (3,465) recorded his 3,000th hit as a home run in 2011; Albert Pujols and Miguel Cabrera are among the most recent members, joining in 2018 and 2022 respectively.

Significance

Because a batter needs both elite ability and some 15–20 seasons of durability, membership is rarer than membership in the 500 home run club. Ichiro Suzuki amassed 3,089 MLB hits in addition to 1,278 in Japan's Nippon Professional Baseball.
