Trophy (countermeasure)

Trophy (Hebrew: מעיל רוח, "Windbreaker"; also known as ASPRO-A) is an Israeli military active protection system (APS) for armoured vehicles, developed by Rafael Advanced Defense Systems in cooperation with Israel Aerospace Industries' Elta division. It is designed to intercept and destroy incoming anti-tank missiles, rocket-propelled grenades, and other projectiles with a shotgun-like blast of small projectiles.

Operation

Four flat-panel Elta EL/M-2133 radar antennas mounted around the vehicle provide 360-degree detection and tracking of incoming threats. The system computes the projectile's trajectory and, if it threatens the vehicle, fires a tight pattern of small explosive projectiles ("kinetic energy defeat") from rotating launchers to neutralize the warhead a short distance from the hull. Threats that will miss are not engaged, reducing collateral danger to nearby infantry.

Service history

Trophy achieved its first combat interception on 1 March 2011 near the Gaza border, destroying an anti-tank missile aimed at a Merkava Mk IV tank, and has since been used extensively on Israeli Merkava and Namer vehicles. It has been adopted or selected for the US Army's M1 Abrams, the German Leopard 2, and the British Challenger 3, making it the most widely deployed hard-kill APS in the world.
