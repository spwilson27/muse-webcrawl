El Bulli

El Bulli (Catalan pronunciation: [əl ˈβuʎi]) was a Spanish 3-star Michelin Guide restaurant near the town of Roses, Catalonia, in Spain, run by chef Ferran Adrià and driven by the culinary ideas of Albert Adrià. The small restaurant overlooked Cala Montjoi, a bay on Catalonia's Costa Brava, and was described as "the best restaurant in the world" by numerous publications.

History

The name "El Bulli" came from the French bulldogs (bullis) kept by the restaurant's first owners, German couple Hans and Marketta Schilling, who opened a beach bar on the site in 1961. The restaurant received its first Michelin star in 1975. Ferran Adrià joined the staff in 1984 and became head chef in 1987.

El Bulli held three Michelin stars and was voted the world's best restaurant a record five times by Restaurant magazine (2002, 2006, 2007, 2008 and 2009). The restaurant was legendary both for its creative avant-garde cuisine — including foams, spherification and other techniques associated with molecular gastronomy — and for the extreme difficulty of securing a reservation: it served around 8,000 diners per season while receiving millions of requests.

Closure and legacy

The restaurant closed on 30 July 2011 and reopened as the elBulliFoundation, a creativity centre and museum known as elBulli1846, which opened on the site in 2023. Adrià's techniques and the training of chefs who passed through its kitchen influenced restaurants worldwide.
