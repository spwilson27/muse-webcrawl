Hanging

Hanging is the suspension of a person by a noose or ligature around the neck. The Oxford English Dictionary distinguishes hanging in three senses: the first is the manner in which the noose is tied; the second is as a method of execution carried out by state authority; and the third is suspension as a method of suicide. Hanging has been a common method of capital punishment since the Middle Ages and remains in use in a number of countries.

Method of execution

Judicial hanging methods include the short drop, suspension hanging, the standard drop, and the long drop. The long drop, developed in Britain in the 19th century, uses a calculated drop length based on the condemned person's weight to fracture the neck vertebrae, causing rapid unconsciousness and death. Official tables of drops were published in Britain in 1888 and 1913.

History

Hanging was the standard method of execution in much of Europe and its colonies for centuries. In England it was the customary penalty for many felonies, carried out publicly at sites such as Tyburn and Newgate Prison until public executions ended in 1868; the last executions in Britain took place in 1964, and capital punishment for murder was abolished in 1965 (permanently in 1969).

Present day

Hanging remains a legal method of execution in countries including Japan, Iran, Singapore, and Malaysia, and is a secondary method in a small number of U.S. states, though it has not been used there since 1996.
