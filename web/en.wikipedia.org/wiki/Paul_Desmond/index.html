Paul Desmond

Paul Desmond (born Paul Emil Breitenfeld, November 25, 1924 – May 30, 1977) was an American jazz alto saxophonist and composer, best known for his work with the Dave Brubeck Quartet and for composing its biggest hit, "Take Five." His light, airy, melodic improvising — the antithesis of Charlie Parker's bebop velocity — made him one of the most recognizable alto voices in cool jazz.

Desmond described his sound as that of "a dry martini."

Career

Born in San Francisco, Breitenfeld studied clarinet before switching to alto saxophone and changed his surname to Desmond, reportedly picking it from a phone book. He joined forces with pianist Dave Brubeck in the late 1940s, and after a period of smaller groups the Dave Brubeck Quartet was formed in 1951, achieving its classic line-up with Joe Morello and Eugene Wright. The quartet toured colleges to great success and appeared on the cover of Time in 1954. Its 1959 album Time Out featured Desmond's composition "Take Five," written in 5/4 time; released as a single in 1961, it became the best-selling jazz single of its era.

Later work and style

After the quartet disbanded in 1967, Desmond recorded as a leader (including albums with guitarist Jim Hall), collaborated with Gerry Mulligan and Chet Baker, and performed with modern jazz groups in the 1970s. His playing was lyrical and logical, built on motivic development, a feather-light tone, and wit — he was also famous for his dryly humorous liner notes and interviews. A heavy smoker, he died of lung cancer in 1977; characteristically, he reportedly expressed delight that his liver was found to be healthy.
