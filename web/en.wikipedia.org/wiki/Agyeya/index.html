Sachchidananda Hirananda Vatsyayan (7 March 1911 – 4 April 1987), popularly known by his pen name Agyeya, was an Indian writer, poet, novelist, literary critic, journalist, and editor in the Hindi language. A pioneer of modern Hindi literature, he introduced existentialist and experimental currents into Hindi fiction and poetry and founded the Prayogvad (experimentalism) movement through the anthology Tar Saptak (1943).

Life

Born in Kasia (now Kushinagar, Uttar Pradesh) to a noted archaeologist, Vatsyayan was educated at Madras Christian College and Lahore. As a young man he joined the revolutionary movement against British rule and was imprisoned for several years for his association with Bhagat Singh's Hindustan Socialist Republican Association. He later served in the Indian Army during the Second World War, worked for All India Radio, and edited influential journals including Sainik, Vishal Bharat, and the weekly Dinmaan, as well as founding the literary quarterly Prateek.

Works

His best-known novel, Shekhar: Ek Jivani ("Shekhar: A Biography", 1941–44), is a landmark of psychological fiction in Hindi; later novels include Nadi Ke Dweep ("Islands in the Stream", 1951) and Apne Apne Ajnabi (1961), the latter winning the Sahitya Akademi Award. His poetry collections, travelogues (including Agyeya's account of travels in the Northeast), and essays made him one of the most versatile men of letters of modern India. He received the Jnanpith Award in 1978.
