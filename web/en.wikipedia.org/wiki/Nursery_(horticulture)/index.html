Nursery (horticulture)

A plant nursery is a place where plants are propagated and grown to a desired size, mainly for sale to gardeners, landscapers, farmers and forestry operations. Nurseries may be retail businesses selling directly to the public, wholesale nurseries selling to other businesses, or private nurseries supplying the needs of estates and institutions. The term also refers to the section of a nursery or garden where young plants are raised from seed, cuttings or grafts before being transplanted.

Types of nurseries

  • Retail nurseries sell plants, tools and supplies to home gardeners; larger establishments are often called garden centres.
  • Wholesale nurseries grow plants in bulk for retailers, landscapers and other commercial customers.
  • Forest nurseries raise tree seedlings for reforestation, conservation and timber planting.
  • Propagation nurseries specialise in starting new plants from seed, cuttings, layering, grafting or tissue culture.

Methods

Nursery plants may be grown in open fields or in containers. Field-grown stock is lifted and sold either bare-root (in the dormant season) or balled-and-burlapped, while container-grown plants can be sold year-round. Greenhouses, polytunnels, shade houses and cold frames are used to control temperature, light and humidity, and automated irrigation, misting systems and climate control are common in large operations. Young plants require careful management of watering, feeding, pruning and pest and disease control, as well as hardening off before they are planted out. Nurseries are important suppliers for horticulture, agriculture, forestry and ecological restoration, and they play a role in conserving rare and native plant species.
