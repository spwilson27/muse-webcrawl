Mujeres Libres

Founded: April 1936, Madrid/Barcelona

Founders: Lucía Sánchez Saornil, Mercedes Comaposada, Amparo Poch y Gascón

Ideology: Anarcha-feminism; associated with the CNT-FAI

Membership: approx. 30,000 (1938)

Publication: Mujeres Libres journal

Mujeres Libres (Spanish for "Free Women") was an anarchist women's organisation in Spain during the 1930s, founded in 1936 shortly before the Spanish Civil War. Its founders — the poet and journalist Lucía Sánchez Saornil, the lawyer Mercedes Comaposada and the doctor Amparo Poch y Gascón — sought to address what they called the "triple enslavement" of women: to ignorance, to capital and to men.

Aims and activities

Mujeres Libres argued that women's emancipation had to proceed together with the anarchist social revolution, and that women could not simply wait for liberation to follow from it. The organisation ran literacy and training programmes, apprenticeships, schools, and cultural and health centres for working-class women, and published a journal of the same name (13 issues, 1936–1938). At its height it claimed some 30,000 members. During the Civil War its members organised women's work in war industries and services, childcare collectives, and fought — sometimes literally, in the militias — while resisting pressure to confine women to auxiliary roles.

Relations with the anarchist movement

Although closely tied to the anarcho-syndicalist CNT and the FAI, Mujeres Libres insisted on autonomy, and its request for recognition as an equal branch of the libertarian movement was repeatedly refused by the male leadership. The organisation was suppressed after the Nationalist victory in 1939; many members went into exile or prison. It was later recovered as a key historical example of anarcha-feminism, notably through the research of historian Martha Ackelsberg.
