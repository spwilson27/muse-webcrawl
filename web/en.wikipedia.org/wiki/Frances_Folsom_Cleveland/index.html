Frances Clara Folsom Cleveland (July 21, 1864 – October 29, 1947) was the wife of President Grover Cleveland and First Lady of the United States from 1886 to 1889 and again from 1893 to 1897. At 21 years old, she remains the youngest First Lady in American history and the only one to be married in the White House.

Life and marriage

Frances Folsom was the daughter of Oscar Folsom, a Buffalo lawyer who was Cleveland's law partner and close friend. After Oscar's death in 1875, Cleveland became administrator of the estate and a guardian figure to Frances. She graduated from Wells College in 1885. On June 2, 1886, she married the 49-year-old president in the Blue Room of the White House — the only presidential wedding held there. The Clevelands had five children, including Ruth Cleveland ("Baby Ruth", for whom the candy bar is sometimes said, disputedly, to be named) and Esther Cleveland, the only child of a president born in the White House.

A popular and gracious hostess, she held receptions accessible to working women on Saturdays. After Grover Cleveland's death in 1908, she remarried in 1913 to Thomas J. Preston Jr., a Princeton archaeology professor. She is buried beside her first husband in Princeton Cemetery.
