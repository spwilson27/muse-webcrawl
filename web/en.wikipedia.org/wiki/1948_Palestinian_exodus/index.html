The 1948 Palestinian exodus, known in Arabic as the Nakba (Arabic: النكبة, romanized: an-Nakba, lit. "the catastrophe"), refers to the flight and expulsion of more than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs from their homes during the 1948 Palestine war. The exact number is disputed, but most estimates place the figure at over half of the Arab population of Mandatory Palestine. Refugees fled or were driven to the West Bank, the Gaza Strip, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria, and Egypt.

Course of the exodus

The exodus unfolded in waves. The first, from December 1947 to March 1948, involved mainly urban middle- and upper-class families leaving mixed cities amid escalating communal violence following the UN Partition Plan. A second, much larger wave accompanied the Haganah and later Israeli offensives of April–June 1948, including the capture of Tiberias, Haifa, Jaffa, Safed, and Acre; the massacre at Deir Yassin on 9 April 1948 spread fear widely. Further waves accompanied the July 1948 offensives — including the expulsion of the inhabitants of Lydda and Ramle — and the operations in the Galilee and the Negev in October–November 1948.

Causes

The causes of the exodus remain one of the most contested subjects in the historiography of the conflict. Historians describe a mixture of factors varying by place and time: flight from fighting, fear of massacre, collapse of Palestinian leadership and services, direct expulsion by Israeli forces in some localities, and psychological warfare. Scholars debate the extent to which a premeditated plan existed; Plan Dalet, the Haganah's April 1948 operational plan, is central to that debate.

Aftermath

After the war Israel refused to allow the refugees to return, and the Abandoned Areas Ordinance and the Absentees' Property Law (1950) transferred refugee property to the state. UN General Assembly Resolution 194 (December 1948) resolved that refugees wishing to return and live at peace should be permitted to do so. UNRWA was established in 1949 to provide relief; today the registered refugee population, including descendants, numbers several million. The status of the refugees and the claimed Palestinian right of return remain core issues of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict. In Israel, more than 400 depopulated Palestinian villages were demolished or repopulated over the following decades.
