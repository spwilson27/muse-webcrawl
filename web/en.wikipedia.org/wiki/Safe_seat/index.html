Safe seat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A safe seat is an electoral district regarded as fully secure for a particular party or incumbent, because its majority is so large that the outcome is effectively predetermined. Under plurality systems, most seats in many legislatures are safe: in the United Kingdom a majority of constituencies rarely change hands, and in the United States most House districts are safe for one party, a fact attributed to residential sorting and to gerrymandering in redistricting.

Safe seats shift political competition inward: the decisive contest becomes the dominant party's nomination (in the US, the primary), which can reward ideological purity and incumbency protection, while general-election voters in such districts have little leverage. Reformers argue safe seats depress turnout and accountability; defenders reply that they reflect genuine community preferences and stable representation. The opposite is a marginal (swing) seat, where small shifts change the winner and campaign resources concentrate.
