Crunk

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Crunk's shouted call-and-response vocals were designed for club crowds.

Crunk is a subgenre of hip hop music that emerged in the Southern United States in the 1990s and achieved mainstream popularity in the mid-2000s. The word is generally regarded as a portmanteau of "crazy" and "drunk", and was used in Atlanta slang to describe an energetic, rowdy state. Crunk music is characterized by heavy, stripped-down drum machine beats — often built around the Roland TR-808 — repetitive synthesizer riffs, and shouted, chant-like vocals frequently delivered in call-and-response.

History

Crunk developed out of Southern hip hop scenes in Memphis and Atlanta, drawing on Miami bass, Memphis underground rap, and New Orleans bounce. Memphis acts such as Three 6 Mafia incorporated dark, chant-driven production, while Atlanta producer and performer Lil Jon became the genre's central figure. With his group the East Side Boyz, Lil Jon brought crunk to national attention with albums such as Kings of Crunk (2002) and the hit single "Get Low" (2003) with the Ying Yang Twins.

By 2004 crunk dominated American pop and R&B charts. Lil Jon produced or featured on major hits including Usher's "Yeah!", Ciara's "Goodies", and the Ying Yang Twins' "Wait (The Whisper Song)". The related style crunk&B blended crunk production with contemporary R&B vocals. Other notable crunk artists include Lil Scrappy, Crime Mob, D4L, Dem Franchize Boyz, and David Banner.

Characteristics

Crunk tracks are typically built for clubs and parties: tempos are often slower than other hip hop, but the energy comes from thunderous 808 kick drums, handclaps, sparse synth melodies, and group chants meant to be shouted by a crowd. Lyrics are usually simple, repetitive, and focused on partying and bravado rather than narrative or wordplay. The genre's confrontational energy has sometimes drawn comparisons to punk rock.

Legacy

Crunk's commercial peak faded by the late 2000s, but its emphasis on minimal, bass-heavy production and chant hooks strongly influenced later Southern styles, especially trap music, as well as EDM-influenced pop. Lil Jon's later collaboration with DJ Snake on "Turn Down for What" (2013) carried crunk's shouted vocal style into electronic dance music.
