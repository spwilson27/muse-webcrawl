Voretigene neparvovec

Voretigene neparvovec, sold under the brand name Luxturna, is a gene therapy medication for the treatment of Leber congenital amaurosis, an inherited blinding disease caused by mutations in the RPE65 gene. It was developed by Spark Therapeutics.

Mechanism

Voretigene neparvovec is a modified adeno-associated virus serotype 2 (AAV2) vector carrying a normal copy of the human RPE65 gene. The vector is delivered by subretinal injection during vitrectomy surgery into each eye, weeks apart. The gene restores production of the RPE65 enzyme in retinal pigment epithelium cells, enabling the visual cycle and improving the retina's ability to respond to light.

Approval

In December 2017 voretigene neparvovec became the first directly administered gene therapy approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration, and the first treatment for any inherited retinal disease. It was approved in the European Union in 2018. It is indicated for patients with confirmed biallelic RPE65 mutation-associated retinal dystrophy who retain sufficient viable retinal cells.

Trials and cost

In the pivotal phase III trial, treated patients showed significantly improved ability to navigate a standardised mobility course in low light. At launch, the US list price was $850,000 for the pair of one-time treatments (one per eye), making it one of the most expensive medicines in the world and a focus of debates over the pricing of gene therapies.
