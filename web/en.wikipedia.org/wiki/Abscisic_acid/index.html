Abscisic acid

Abscisic acid (ABA) is a plant hormone (phytohormone) with the chemical formula C15H20O4. It is a sesquiterpenoid synthesised in plastids from carotenoid precursors. ABA regulates many aspects of plant growth and development, most notably seed dormancy, germination, and responses to environmental stresses such as drought, salinity and cold — hence its common description as the plant "stress hormone."

Discovery

ABA was identified in the 1960s by two groups working independently: one isolated "abscisin II" from cotton fruit, a substance that accelerated leaf abscission, and another isolated "dormin" from sycamore leaves, which induced bud dormancy. The compounds proved identical, and the name abscisic acid was adopted in 1967 — although later research showed ABA is not the primary driver of abscission, which is governed mainly by ethylene.

Functions

ABA accumulates in maturing seeds, imposing dormancy and preventing precocious germination (vivipary); the balance between ABA and gibberellins controls the dormancy–germination decision. Under water stress, ABA synthesized in roots and leaves triggers closure of stomata by guard cells, reducing transpiration. It also mediates acclimation to salinity, cold and desiccation by switching on large suites of stress-responsive genes.

Signalling

The core ABA signalling pathway was elucidated in 2009: ABA binds PYR/PYL/RCAR receptor proteins, which then inhibit clade-A PP2C phosphatases, releasing SnRK2 kinases to phosphorylate downstream transcription factors and ion channels. This double-negative module is now a textbook example of hormone signal transduction and a target for engineering drought-tolerant crops; synthetic ABA agonists such as opabactin have been developed.
