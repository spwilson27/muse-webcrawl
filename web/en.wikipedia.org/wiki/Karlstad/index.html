Karlstad

Karlstad (Swedish pronunciation: [ˈkɑːɭsta]) is a city in Värmland County, Sweden, situated on the northern shore of Lake Vänern, the largest lake in Sweden, where the Klarälven river delta enters the lake. It is the seat of Karlstad Municipality and the capital of Värmland County, with a population of roughly 70,000 in the city and about 100,000 in the municipality.

History

Karlstad was granted city privileges in 1584 by Duke Charles, later King Charles IX of Sweden, after whom it is named. The city was repeatedly damaged by fires, most devastatingly in 1865, after which it was rebuilt with wide tree-lined streets. In 1905, the negotiations that peacefully dissolved the union between Norway and Sweden were held in Karlstad. Karlstad University gained full university status in 1999.

Culture and sport

The city is home to the Värmland Museum, the Wermland Opera, and the Sandgrund Lars Lerin art museum. Its ice hockey team, Färjestad BK, is one of Sweden's most successful clubs. Karlstad enjoys a local reputation for sunny weather, symbolised by "Sola i Karlstad" (the Sun in Karlstad), after a popular 19th-century innkeeper's daughter.
