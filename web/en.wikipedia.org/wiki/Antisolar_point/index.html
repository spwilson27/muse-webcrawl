Antisolar point

The antisolar point lies directly opposite the Sun in the sky, below the horizon when the Sun is up.

The antisolar point is the abstract point on the celestial sphere directly opposite the Sun from an observer's perspective. It lies on the line that passes through the centres of the Sun and the Earth and through the observer, extended beyond the observer in the direction away from the Sun. Because it is defined relative to the observer, it has no fixed position in space.

When the Sun is above the horizon, the antisolar point is below the horizon, and vice versa. At sunset and sunrise the antisolar point lies exactly on the horizon, in the east at sunset and in the west at sunrise. It is closely related to the anthelion, a rare atmospheric optical phenomenon.

Atmospheric phenomena

Several optical phenomena are centred on or near the antisolar point:

  • Rainbows — the primary rainbow is a circle of angular radius about 42° centred on the antisolar point. When the Sun is low, the observer's shadow marks the centre of the rainbow's arc.
  • Glory — a set of coloured rings seen around the shadow of the observer's head cast onto clouds or fog, centred on the antisolar point.
  • Heiligenschein — a bright halo around the shadow of the observer's head on dew-covered grass, caused by retroreflection.
  • Anthelion — a faint white spot at the same altitude as the Sun but 180° away in azimuth, sometimes accompanied by arcs.

Shadow geometry

The antisolar point is the point toward which an observer's shadow converges at great distances: the shadow of the observer's head always falls on the antisolar point. This is why a person's shadow on a cloud bank (as seen from a mountain or aircraft, the Brocken spectre) appears at the centre of a glory.

Astronomy

In astronomy, the antisolar point is relevant to phenomena such as the gegenschein, a faint brightening of the night sky in the direction opposite the Sun, caused by sunlight backscattered from interplanetary dust. The Moon passes near the antisolar point at full moon, and a lunar eclipse occurs when it passes through the Earth's shadow close to this point.
