The River Lea (also spelled Lee) is a river in south-east England, rising near Luton in Bedfordshire and flowing south-east through Hertfordshire and then along the Essex–London boundary to join the River Thames at Leamouth, opposite Greenwich. The lower valley, the Lee Valley, contains a chain of reservoirs supplying London's drinking water and the Lee Valley Regional Park.

Course

The Lea rises at Leagrave Common near Luton and flows through Luton, Harpenden (via its tributary), Wheathampstead, Welwyn Garden City, Hertford — where it is joined by the Mimram, Beane and Rib — and Ware. Below Ware the river is paralleled by the Lee Navigation, the canalised channel used by boats past Hoddesdon, Broxbourne, Waltham Abbey and Enfield. In London it flows past the Walthamstow and Tottenham marshes, Hackney Marshes and the Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park at Stratford, before reaching the Thames through Bow Creek at Leamouth.

The spelling "Lea" is generally used for the natural river and "Lee" for the navigation and the Lee Valley bodies, though usage is inconsistent.

History

The Lea formed the boundary between the Saxon kingdoms and later between Danelaw and English territory under the treaty between Alfred the Great and Guthrum. In 895 Alfred trapped a Viking fleet in the river. Gunpowder mills at Waltham Abbey and the Royal Small Arms Factory at Enfield drew on its power and water transport. The river was also the source of the New River, opened in 1613 to carry drinking water to London, and of the great reservoir chain built in the valley from the 19th century. The lower Lea was heavily industrialised, but regeneration associated with the 2012 Olympic Games transformed the Stratford reaches.

Water supply

A succession of reservoirs along the valley, including the King George V and William Girling reservoirs, stores a substantial part of London's water supply, abstracted under the Lee Valley water scheme.
