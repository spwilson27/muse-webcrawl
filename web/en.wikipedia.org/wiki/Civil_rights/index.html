Civil rights

Civil and political rights are a class of rights that protect individuals' freedom from infringement by governments, social organizations, and private individuals. They ensure one's entitlement to participate in the civil and political life of society and the state.

Civil rights
Examples: right to vote, freedom of speech, freedom of assembly, due process, freedom from discrimination

History

The term derives from Latin ius civis (rights of a citizen). Early milestones include Magna Carta (1215), the English Bill of Rights (1689), and the French and American declarations of the 18th century. The modern concept expanded through abolition, women's suffrage, and the civil rights movement in the United States (1954–1968).

Legal protection

Civil rights are protected by constitutions and statutes such as the U.S. Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (1966). They are closely related to natural rights and racial equality.

Issues

Contemporary debates concern voting rights, policing, privacy, digital rights, and equality regardless of race, sex, disability, or sexual orientation.
