Chevrolet

Chevrolet (colloquially "Chevy") is an American automobile division of General Motors (GM), founded on November 3, 1911, by Louis Chevrolet and ousted GM founder William C. Durant. It is GM's best-selling brand and one of the world's largest car marques, known for models such as the Corvette, Camaro, Silverado pickup and Suburban SUV.

History

Swiss-born racing driver Louis Chevrolet and investor William C. Durant founded the Chevrolet Motor Company in 1911. The brand's first car, the Series C Classic Six, was a large, expensive touring car, but Durant soon used Chevrolet's success to regain control of General Motors, merging Chevrolet into GM in 1918. Louis Chevrolet himself left the company in 1915 after disputes with Durant.

Through the 1920s Chevrolet overtook Ford as America's best-selling make, competing with the slogan "a product of General Motors". The famous gold "bowtie" logo, introduced in 1913, remains the brand's emblem. Iconic postwar models include the 1955–57 "Tri-Five" cars, the Corvette sports car (launched 1953), the Camaro pony car (1966, a rival to Ford's Mustang), and the long-running Silverado and Suburban nameplates — the Suburban, in continuous production since 1935, is the longest-running automobile nameplate in the world.

Electric vehicles

Chevrolet has been a key part of GM's electrification strategy: the Volt plug-in hybrid (2010), the Bolt EV (2016), and later Ultium-platform vehicles including the Silverado EV, Blazer EV and Equinox EV.

Motorsport

Chevrolet is a major presence in NASCAR, IndyCar and drag racing, and the Corvette competes internationally in endurance racing, including the 24 Hours of Le Mans.
