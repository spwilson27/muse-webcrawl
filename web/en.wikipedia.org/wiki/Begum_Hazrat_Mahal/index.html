Begum Hazrat Mahal

Begum Hazrat Mahal (c. 1820 – 1879), regent of Awadh during the rebellion of 1857.

Begum Hazrat Mahal (c. 1820 – 7 April 1879), also known as the Begum of Awadh, was the second wife of Nawab Wajid Ali Shah of Awadh and a leading figure of the Indian Rebellion of 1857. After the British annexed Awadh and exiled the Nawab to Calcutta, she remained in Lucknow and took charge of the state as regent for her young son, Birjis Qadr.

Role in the rebellion

When the rebellion broke out in 1857, Hazrat Mahal declared her son ruler of Awadh, organised resistance, and worked with rebel leaders including Nana Saheb. Her forces controlled Lucknow for an extended period during the siege of the Residency; contemporary British accounts acknowledged her energy and ability. She also issued a famous counter-proclamation to Queen Victoria's Proclamation of 1858, rebutting its promises point by point.

Later life

After British forces retook Lucknow in 1858, she retreated through the countryside of Awadh and eventually fled to Nepal, where she refused British offers of a pension and amnesty and died in Kathmandu in 1879. She is remembered in India as one of the foremost women leaders of the rebellion, and a park and memorial in Lucknow bear her name.
