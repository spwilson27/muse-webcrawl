February Revolution

The February Revolution (March 1917 in the Gregorian calendar) was the first of two revolutions in Russia in 1917. Centred in Petrograd, it ended three centuries of Romanov rule, forced the abdication of Tsar Nicholas II, and created a Provisional Government alongside the Petrograd Soviet — the "dual power" that lasted until the October Revolution.

Date8–16 March 1917 (O.S. 23 Feb – 3 Mar)
LocationPetrograd, Russian Empire
ResultAbdication of Nicholas II; Provisional Government formed

Causes

World War I losses, food shortages, inflation, strikes and military mutinies eroded the Tsarist regime. Bread queues in Petrograd on International Women's Day, 23 February (O.S.), sparked demonstrations that grew into a general strike.

Events

Troops of the Petrograd garrison mutinied and joined workers; the Duma formed a Provisional Committee while workers and soldiers formed the Petrograd Soviet, whose Order No. 1 undermined officers' authority. Nicholas II, at Stavka, abdicated on 2 March (O.S.) for himself and his son, then his brother Michael refused the throne pending a Constituent Assembly.

Aftermath

The Provisional Government, led first by Prince Georgy Lvov and then Alexander Kerensky, continued the war and delayed land reform and elections, while soviets and Bolshevik agitation grew — setting the stage for October.
