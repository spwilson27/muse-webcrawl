Yunnan

Yunnan is a landlocked province in southwestern China. Its capital and largest city is Kunming. The province spans approximately 394,000 square kilometres (152,000 sq mi) and has a population of roughly 47 million. Yunnan is known for its striking geographic and ethnic diversity: it borders Myanmar, Laos, and Vietnam, and is home to 25 recognized ethnic minority groups, more than any other Chinese province.

Geography

Yunnan's terrain descends from the high plateaus and snow-capped ranges of the northwest — where the Yangtze, Mekong, and Salween rivers run in deep parallel gorges through the Hengduan Mountains — to subtropical lowlands in the south. The province's elevation ranges from about 76 metres in the Red River valley to 6,740 metres at Kawagarbo in the Meili Snow Mountains.

History

The region was home to the Dian Kingdom in the Bronze Age, famous for its intricate bronze drums and metalwork. It was later ruled by the Nanzhao kingdom and the Dali Kingdom before Mongol forces under Kublai Khan conquered it in 1253, incorporating it into the Yuan dynasty administration. During the Second Sino-Japanese War the province was a lifeline for China via the Burma Road.

Economy and culture

Yunnan is a major producer of tobacco, tea (including the famed Pu'er), coffee, flowers, and nonferrous metals. Tourism is significant, drawn by sites such as the old town of Lijiang, Dali, Shangri-La, and the Stone Forest near Kunming. The province's cuisine is noted for dishes such as crossing-bridge noodles (guoqiao mixian).
