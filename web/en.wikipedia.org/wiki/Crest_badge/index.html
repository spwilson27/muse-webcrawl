Crest badge

A crest badge is a badge worn by members of a clan or followers of a chief, most closely associated with Scottish clans. It consists of the crest of the clan chief surrounded by a strap and buckle bearing the chief's motto or slogan. Unlike the crest itself, which is the personal property of the chief, the crest badge in its strap-and-buckle form may be worn by clansmen to show their allegiance.

Usage

Crest badges are conventionally worn as cap badges on the bonnet, or pinned to the plaid or sash. In Scottish heraldry the right to grant and matriculate crests rests with the Lord Lyon King of Arms under the Court of the Lord Lyon. The strap-and-buckle design distinguishes a follower's badge from the chief's own crest, which is shown without the surrounding strap (or, for clan chiefs and armigers, within a simple circlet).

Related badges

Related items include the plant badge, a sprig of a particular plant traditionally associated with a clan and worn in the bonnet, and the clan badge in a wider sense. In England, livery badges served a comparable purpose for the retainers of a magnate.
