Josquin des Prez

Josquin Lebloite dit des Prez (c. 1450–1455 – 27 August 1521) was a Franco-Flemish composer of the Renaissance, widely considered the first master of the high Renaissance style of polyphonic vocal music. Martin Luther praised him as "the master of the notes."

Life

Josquin was born in the region near the Franco-Flemish border, probably in Picardy or Hainaut. He sang at Milan Cathedral and served the Sforza family, spent time in the papal chapel in Rome, and later worked at the court of Ercole I d'Este in Ferrara, where he composed the Missa Hercules dux Ferrariae. He ended his life as provost of the collegiate church of Notre-Dame in Condé-sur-l'Escaut.

Music

Josquin composed masses, motets, and secular chansons. His style is marked by clear text declamation, pervasive imitation, and paired imitation between voices. Famous works include the motets Ave Maria ... virgo serena and Miserere mei, Deus, and the chansons Mille regretz and El grillo. His music was widely disseminated through the new technology of music printing, notably by Ottaviano Petrucci.

Influence

Josquin's reputation grew enormously after his death; so many works were falsely attributed to him that attribution remains a scholarly problem. He remains the most famous European composer of his era, a bridge between the medieval and modern musical worlds.
