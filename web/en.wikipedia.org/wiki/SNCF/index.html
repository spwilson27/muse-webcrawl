Overview

The Société nationale des chemins de fer français (SNCF, "National Society of the French Railways") is France's national state-owned railway company. Founded in 1938 by the nationalisation and merger of the major private railway companies, it operates most passenger and freight rail services in France, including the TGV high-speed network — the world's first dedicated high-speed system to enter service (Paris–Lyon, 1981) — as well as TER regional trains, Intercités and the Paris RER lines it shares with RATP.

Structure and reform

EU railway liberalisation reorganised the SNCF repeatedly: infrastructure was split to RFF (1997) and reunified within the SNCF group (2015), and the 2018 reform converted SNCF into a state-owned joint-stock group (SNCF, SNCF Réseau, SNCF Voyageurs, etc.), ending new hires on the historic cheminot statute — reforms contested by major strikes. SNCF also runs international services (Eurostar/Thalys brands via subsidiaries) and low-cost Ouigo trains, and competes abroad through Keolis. The TGV holds the world rail speed record, 574.8 km/h, set in 2007.
