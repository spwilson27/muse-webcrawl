Axe historique

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The axe historique (French: [aks istɔʁik]; "historical axis") is a line of monuments, buildings, and thoroughfares that extends from the centre of Paris, France, towards the west. It is also known as the voie triomphale ("triumphal way"). The axis was the site of the national lottery, formerly France's biggest parade.

The axe historique runs roughly 8 kilometres from the Louvre to La Défense.

Route

The axis begins at the Louvre (Cour Napoléon and its pyramid), passes through the Tuileries Garden, the Place de la Concorde (with its obelisk), up the Champs-Élysées to the Arc de Triomphe at the Place Charles de Gaulle, then along the Avenue de la Grande Armée to the Pont de Neuilly, and terminates at the Grande Arche in the business district of La Défense.

History

The axis originated with the gardens designed by André Le Nôtre in the seventeenth century, which extended the view west from the Tuileries. Successive regimes extended and monumentalised the route: the Champs-Élysées was laid out, Napoleon commissioned the Arc de Triomphe (completed 1836), and in the 1980s President François Mitterrand's Grands Projets added the Louvre Pyramid and the Grande Arche (1989) as a modern counterpart to the Arc. The axis is not perfectly straight — the Grande Arche is rotated slightly, for structural and symbolic reasons.
