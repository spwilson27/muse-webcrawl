Walter Cronkite

Walter Cronkite
Born4 November 1916, Saint Joseph, Missouri
Died17 July 2009, New York City
OccupationBroadcast journalist, CBS News anchor
[Image: Cronkite at the CBS anchor desk in the 1960s]

Walter Cronkite (1916–2009) was an American broadcast journalist who anchored the CBS Evening News from 1962 to 1981, becoming — in a famous poll phrase — 'the most trusted man in America'. His career spanned the Second World War (as a United Press war correspondent), the space programme, the Kennedy assassination and Vietnam.

Landmarks

Cronkite announced President Kennedy's death to the nation in 1963; his televised 1968 editorial concluding the Vietnam War was stalemated is said to have influenced President Johnson; he covered Apollo 11's landing with open wonder in 1969.

Legacy

His sign-off, 'And that's the way it is', and his journalistic authority made him the archetype of the American anchor — a figure of a three-network era that later fragmented media never reproduced.
