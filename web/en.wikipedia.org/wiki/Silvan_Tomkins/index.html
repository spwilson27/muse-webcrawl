Silvan Tomkins

Born1911, Philadelphia
Died1991
FieldPsychology

Silvan Solomon Tomkins (1911–1991) was an American psychologist and personality theorist, best known for developing affect theory and script theory. His four-volume work Affect Imagery Consciousness (1962–1991) laid out his account of the basic affects.

Affect theory

Tomkins proposed a small set of innate affects — including interest–excitement, enjoyment–joy, surprise–startle, distress–anguish, fear–terror, anger–rage, shame–humiliation, disgust and dissmell — which he argued are the primary motivational system of human beings, amplifying the experiences tied to them. His work influenced later research on facial expressions of emotion, including that of his student Paul Ekman, and the study of shame by Donald Nathanson and others.

Script theory

In his later work Tomkins developed script theory, describing how people build "scripts" — rules for interpreting and responding to affect-laden scenes — that organise personality and behaviour over a lifetime.
