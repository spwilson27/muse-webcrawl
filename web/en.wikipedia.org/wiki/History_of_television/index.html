The history of television traces the invention of television technology in the 1920s and 1930s, its spread as a mass medium after the Second World War, and its transformation by colour, cable, satellite, digital broadcasting and internet streaming.

By the 1950s television had become the dominant home entertainment medium in much of the world.

Invention

Television grew from 19th-century ideas for transmitting images, including Paul Nipkow's scanning disk (1884). In the 1920s John Logie Baird demonstrated mechanical television in London, while Philo Farnsworth (1927) and Vladimir Zworykin developed fully electronic systems using cathode-ray tubes. The BBC began the world's first regular high-definition service from Alexandra Palace in 1936. Development was interrupted by the Second World War.

The broadcast era

After 1945 television expanded rapidly, led by the United States, where the networks NBC, CBS, ABC and DuMont built national audiences around news, variety, drama and sport. In Britain, the BBC was joined by commercial ITV in 1955; much of Europe adopted public-service broadcasting models. The medium reshaped politics (the 1960 Kennedy–Nixon debates), advertising and everyday life. Colour standards — NTSC in North America, PAL and SECAM in Europe — were adopted in the 1950s–60s, and satellite relays (Telstar, 1962) made live transcontinental broadcasts possible; some 600 million people watched the Apollo 11 Moon landing in 1969.

Cable, satellite and video

From the 1970s, cable television multiplied channels and created dedicated services such as HBO (1972) and the 24-hour news channel CNN (1980). Direct-broadcast satellite, videocassette recorders and later DVDs gave viewers control over timing. Remote controls and channel surfing changed viewing habits.

Digital and streaming eras

Digital terrestrial television, high-definition (HDTV) and flat-panel displays replaced analogue broadcasts in most countries between the 2000s and 2010s. Broadband internet then enabled streaming services — YouTube (2005), Netflix streaming (2007) and many rivals — shifting television toward on-demand viewing on multiple devices, while traditional broadcasters adapted with catch-up and streaming platforms. Smart TVs and global hits distributed simultaneously worldwide mark the medium's current form.
