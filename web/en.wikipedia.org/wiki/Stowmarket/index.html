Stowmarket

County: Suffolk, England
District: Mid Suffolk
Population: approx. 21,000

Stowmarket is a market town in Suffolk, England, lying on the River Gipping between Ipswich and Bury St Edmunds. Its name derives from Old English "stow" (a place of assembly) with a market charter granted in 1347.

History

The navigable Gipping and later the railway (1846) made Stowmarket a centre for the agricultural trade; the town had major fertiliser, brewing, and explosives industries — the 1871 Stowmarket gun-cotton explosion killed 28 people.

Attractions

The Museum of East Anglian Life (now the Food Museum) occupies a large site in the town centre. The church of St Peter and St Mary is a prominent landmark, and the town is linked to Milton's relative? John Milton's namesake tree stood in the rectory garden.
