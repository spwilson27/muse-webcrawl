Intercity Express Programme
Type: Train procurement programme
Country: United Kingdom
Operator: Department for Transport
Trains: Class 800, Class 801, Class 802

The Intercity Express Programme (IEP) is an initiative of the Department for Transport in the United Kingdom to procure new trains to replace the InterCity 125 and InterCity 225 fleets on the East Coast Main Line and Great Western Main Line. The programme led to the development of the Hitachi AT300 family of trains, introduced in Britain as the Class 800, Class 801 and related Class 802 units.

History

The programme was announced in 2005, and in 2009 the Agility Trains consortium, led by Hitachi, was named preferred bidder. After a review by the incoming government in 2010, the scheme was scaled back but proceeded, with financial close reached in 2012 for the Great Western fleet and in 2014 for the East Coast fleet. A new factory was built at Newton Aycliffe in County Durham to assemble the trains.

Trains

The Class 800 is a bi-mode multiple unit able to run on electric power from overhead wires or on diesel power elsewhere; the Class 801 is an electric multiple unit for fully electrified routes. Trains were ordered in five-car and nine-car formations. Great Western Railway began operating Class 800s in October 2017, and London North Eastern Railway introduced its trains, branded "Azuma", in May 2019.

Criticism

The programme was criticised by the National Audit Office and others for its cost, complexity and the long procurement process. Supporters argue the trains delivered a step change in capacity, reliability and journey times on Britain's principal inter-city routes.
