Sun Jian

Sun Jian, the "Tiger of Jiangdong"

Sun Jian (155 – 191 or 192), courtesy name Wentai, was a Chinese military general, politician and warlord who lived during the late Eastern Han dynasty of China. A fearless commander nicknamed the "Tiger of Jiangdong", he rose from minor office through service against the Yellow Turban rebels and became a leading member of the coalition against the tyrant Dong Zhuo. His sons Sun Ce and Sun Quan went on to found the state of Eastern Wu in the Three Kingdoms period, and Sun Jian was posthumously honoured as Emperor Wulie.

Early career

Born in Fuchun County (in present-day Hangzhou, Zhejiang), Sun Jian was said to be a descendant of the strategist Sun Tzu, though this is unverified. As a youth of seventeen he famously scattered a band of pirates by bluffing that a large force approached. He served as a minor official and distinguished himself fighting the rebel Xu Chang and later the Yellow Turbans under Zhu Jun, earning promotion to Administrator of Changsha.

Coalition against Dong Zhuo

In 190 Sun Jian joined the coalition of warlords assembled to depose Dong Zhuo, who had seized the capital and the boy emperor. Serving under Yuan Shu, Sun Jian proved the coalition's most effective general: he defeated Dong Zhuo's forces, recovered the ruined capital Luoyang in 191, and — according to legend — recovered the lost Imperial Seal of China from a palace well, a find later central to his family's claims.

Death and legacy

Later in 191 (some sources say 192), Yuan Shu sent Sun Jian to attack Liu Biao in Jing Province. Near Xiangyang he was ambushed by troops of Liu Biao's general Huang Zu and killed — in the historical records, by an arrow in battle; the novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms embellished his death with a boulder trap. His eldest son Sun Ce inherited his troops and carved out the Jiangdong territories that became the Wu kingdom.
