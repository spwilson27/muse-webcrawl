Ammit (Ancient Egyptian: ꜥm-mwt, "devourer of the dead"; also rendered Ammut or Ahemait) was a demoness and goddess in ancient Egyptian religion with the head of a crocodile, the front legs and body of a lion, and the hind legs and body of a hippopotamus — the three largest "man-eating" animals known to the Egyptians.

Role in the afterlife

Ammit appears in funerary texts such as the Book of the Dead beside the scales in the Hall of Two Truths, where the heart of the deceased was weighed against the feather of Maat. If the heart was judged impure and heavier than the feather, Ammit would devour it, condemning the soul to restlessness and a "second death" — ceasing to exist — rather than entering the blissful Field of Reeds. Ammit was thus not worshipped, but feared as the embodiment of divine retribution, though she was also understood as an instrument of justice acting on the verdict of the gods.
