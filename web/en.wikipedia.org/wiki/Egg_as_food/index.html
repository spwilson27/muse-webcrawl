Egg as food

Egg (chicken, large)

Energy~155 kcal per 100 g
Protein~13 g
Fat~11 g
ComponentsWhite, yolk, shell

Humans and other animals have eaten eggs for millennia. The most widely consumed eggs are those of the chicken, but eggs of ducks, quail, geese and other birds are also eaten, as are fish eggs (roe and caviar). A bird egg consists of a protective shell, the egg white (albumen) and the yolk, contained in thin membranes.

Eggs are prepared in many ways, including frying and boiling.

Culinary use

Eggs can be scrambled, fried, poached, boiled (hard or soft), pickled and baked, and appear in countless dishes from omelettes and quiches to cakes and pasta. Beaten egg whites form foams used in meringues and soufflés; yolks act as an emulsifier in mayonnaise and hollandaise sauce. Egg dishes are common breakfast foods in many cultures.

Nutrition and health

Eggs are a rich source of high-quality protein, B vitamins (including B12), vitamin D, choline and the antioxidants lutein and zeaxanthin. They were once restricted in dietary advice because of their cholesterol content, but later research weakened the link between dietary cholesterol and blood cholesterol for most people, and guidance moderated accordingly. Egg allergy is among the most common food allergies in children.

Production

Most commercial eggs come from laying hens kept in battery cages, barn, free-range or organic systems — differences that have generated extensive animal-welfare debate and regulation, including an EU ban on conventional battery cages in 2012. Eggs are graded by size and quality and are usually sold in cartons of six, ten or twelve.
