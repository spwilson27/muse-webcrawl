Brian Thomas Swimme (born 1950) is an American evolutionary cosmologist, mathematical cosmologist, and author. He is a professor at the California Institute of Integral Studies (CIIS) in San Francisco, where he teaches in the Philosophy, Cosmology, and Consciousness program. Swimme is best known for his work interpreting the scientific story of the universe as a source of meaning and for his collaborations with the historian of religions Thomas Berry.

Education and career

Swimme received his Ph.D. in gravitational physics from the University of Oregon. His early research concerned mathematical cosmology and the dynamics of the universe. Over time his interests shifted toward the cultural and philosophical implications of the modern scientific account of cosmic evolution, a field sometimes called the "epic of evolution" or "Universe Story."

He joined the faculty of the California Institute of Integral Studies, where he helped develop graduate programs exploring the intersection of cosmology, ecology, and spirituality.

Works

With Thomas Berry, Swimme co-authored The Universe Story: From the Primordial Flaring Forth to the Ecozoic Era (1992), which presents the 13.8-billion-year history of the universe as a single coherent narrative intended to give modern readers a shared cosmological mythos grounded in science. His earlier book, The Universe Is a Green Dragon (1984), presents similar themes in dialogue form.

Swimme also co-created, with Mary Evelyn Tucker, the multimedia project Journey of the Universe, which includes a film (2011), a book published by Yale University Press, and a series of educational conversations. The film won an Emmy Award for documentary programming.

Ideas

Swimme's work emphasizes that the discoveries of modern cosmology — the Big Bang, the formation of galaxies and stars, and the emergence of life on Earth — constitute a creation story for humanity as a whole. He argues that understanding this story can reorient human values toward ecological responsibility, a theme shared with Thomas Berry's notion of the "Ecozoic era."
