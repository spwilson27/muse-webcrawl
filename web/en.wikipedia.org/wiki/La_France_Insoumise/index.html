La France Insoumise (LFI; "France Unbowed") is a left-wing political movement and party in France, founded in 2016 by Jean-Luc Mélenchon to support his candidacy in the 2017 presidential election. It grew out of the Left Party (Parti de gauche) and adopted a decentralised structure of local action groups rather than a traditional party membership model.

Programme

The party's programme, L'Avenir en commun ("The Future in Common"), proposes convening a constituent assembly to found a Sixth Republic, lowering the retirement age, large public investment in ecological transition, withdrawal from NATO's integrated command, and renegotiation of or disobedience to European Union budgetary rules. Its positions are commonly described as left-wing populist, democratic socialist and eco-socialist.

Electoral history

Mélenchon won 19.6% of the vote in the first round of the 2017 presidential election and 21.95% in 2022, finishing third on both occasions. In the 2022 legislative elections LFI formed the core of the NUPES alliance of left parties, which became the largest opposition bloc in the National Assembly. In the snap legislative elections of 2024 the party campaigned within the New Popular Front, which finished first in seats. LFI is commonly characterised by opponents and some commentators as far-left, a label the party disputes.

Organisation

LFI's symbol is the Greek letter phi (φ) in red and purple. It sits in the European Parliament within The Left group. Prominent figures besides Mélenchon include Mathilde Panot, Manuel Bompard and Clémence Guetté.
