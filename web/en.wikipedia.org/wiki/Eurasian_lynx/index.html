The Eurasian lynx (Lynx lynx) is a medium-sized wild cat native to vast forested regions from Western Europe through Siberia to Central and East Asia. It is the largest of the four species in the genus Lynx and is characterised by its short tail, tufted ears, large padded paws and a coat that varies from greyish to reddish, often with dark spots.

Description

Adults stand 55–75 cm at the shoulder and weigh typically 18–30 kg, males being larger than females. The prominent ear tufts and cheek ruffs, together with large paws that act like snowshoes, adapt the species to life in deep-snow boreal forests. Its winter coat is thicker and paler than the summer coat.

Behaviour and diet

The Eurasian lynx is a solitary, largely nocturnal predator. Its principal prey in Europe is the roe deer, which can form the bulk of its diet; it also takes red deer calves, chamois, mountain hares, and small mammals and birds. An adult may kill a deer every few days, caching the carcass and returning to feed. Home ranges are large — often 100–300 km² — and are marked with scent and scrapes.

Distribution and conservation

Hunted for fur and persecuted as a livestock predator, the species was exterminated from much of Western and Central Europe by the early 20th century. Reintroduction programmes in the Alps, Carpathians, Balkans, and elsewhere have restored populations, and the lynx has also recolonised parts of Scandinavia and the Baltic naturally. The global population is estimated in the tens of thousands, and the IUCN lists the species as Least Concern, though several European subpopulations remain small and isolated.

Subspecies

Several subspecies have been proposed, including the nominate L. l. lynx of Scandinavia and Russia, the Carpathian L. l. carpathicus, the Balkan L. l. balcanicus, the Caucasian L. l. dinniki, and the Turkestan L. l. isabellinus of Central Asia.
