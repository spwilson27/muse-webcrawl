The tree of life (Hebrew: עֵץ הַחַיִּים, ʿĒṣ ha-Ḥayyīm) is a tree planted by God in the midst of the Garden of Eden in the biblical Book of Genesis, whose fruit grants — or sustains — eternal life. It appears alongside the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, the eating from which brings about the Fall of man.

In Genesis

Genesis 2 places the tree of life "in the midst of the garden"; Adam and Eve are permitted to eat of every tree except the tree of knowledge. After the Fall, God expels them from Eden precisely "lest he reach out his hand and take also of the tree of life and eat, and live forever" (Genesis 3:22), and stations cherubim with a flaming sword to guard the way to the tree.

Later references

In the Book of Proverbs the phrase becomes a metaphor: wisdom, the fruit of the righteous, a wholesome tongue and fulfilled desire are each called "a tree of life". In the New Testament's Book of Revelation, the tree of life reappears in the New Jerusalem, bearing twelve kinds of fruit monthly, with leaves "for the healing of the nations", and the risen Christ promises the victorious the right to eat of it.

In other traditions

In Judaism the Torah itself is called a "tree of life" (after Proverbs 3:18), and the phrase names the wooden rollers of a Torah scroll. Latter-day Saint scripture gives the tree a prominent role in Lehi's vision in the Book of Mormon, where its fruit symbolises the love of God. Parallels to life-giving trees occur throughout ancient Near Eastern and world mythology.
