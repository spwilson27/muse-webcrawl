Tegea (Greek: Τεγέα) was an important city of ancient Arcadia in the Peloponnese, situated in a fertile plain south of modern Tripoli. Founded, according to tradition, by King Aleus, it was one of the oldest and most powerful Arcadian communities and long resisted Spartan domination before becoming a Spartan ally.

History

In the sixth century BC Sparta's attempts to annex Tegea failed; Herodotus relates the oracle of the "fetters" that ironically came true when Spartan prisoners were set to work Tegean fields in chains. Tegea fought on the Spartan side at Plataea in 479 BC, where its troops held a place of honour. After the Peloponnesian War it was manipulated by Sparta, temporarily dissolved into villages, and later rebuilt; it joined the Arcadian League and fought Sparta at Mantinea. The city persisted into Roman and Byzantine times, when it was known for a period as Nikli.

Temple of Athena Alea

Tegea's glory was the sanctuary of Athena Alea. After the archaic temple burned in 395/4 BC, it was rebuilt to designs by the sculptor Scopas of Paros, in the Doric order with Ionic and Corinthian interior columns; its pediments depicted the Calydonian boar hunt and the duel of Achilles and Telephus. Pausanias described it as surpassing all other temples in the Peloponnese in size and workmanship. Excavations by French and later Norwegian and Greek teams revealed the temple foundations, the altar, and remains of the theatre and agora.
