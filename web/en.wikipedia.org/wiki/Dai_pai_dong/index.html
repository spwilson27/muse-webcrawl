Dai pai dong

A dai pai dong (Cantonese: 大牌檔) is a type of open-air food stall in Hong Kong, characterised by a large green-painted steel kitchen box, folding tables and stools, and wok-cooked Cantonese dishes served quickly and cheaply.

The name means "big licence stall", referring to the larger licence plates issued to such stalls after the Second World War. Once numbering in the hundreds, dai pai dong declined as licences ceased to be transferable and hygiene policy tightened; only around twenty remain in areas such as Central, Sham Shui Po and Tai Hang, and they are valued as part of Hong Kong's food heritage.

Cuisine

Typical dishes include fried noodles, congee, milk tea, French toast Hong Kong style, and stir-fries cooked over very high heat.
