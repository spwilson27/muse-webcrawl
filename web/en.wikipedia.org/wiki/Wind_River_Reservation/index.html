Wind River Reservation

The Wind River Reservation is an Indian reservation in the central part of the U.S. state of Wyoming, shared by two federally recognized tribes: the Eastern Shoshone and the Northern Arapaho. It is the seventh-largest Indian reservation by area in the United States, encompassing more than 2.2 million acres in Fremont County and Hot Springs County. The reservation is named for the Wind River, which flows through it.

History

The reservation was established for the Eastern Shoshone by the Fort Bridger Treaty of 1863, which set aside a vast territory of some 44.6 million acres; a second treaty in 1868 reduced it to its roughly present Wind River location. In 1878, the Northern Arapaho — traditional enemies of the Shoshone — were placed on the reservation, a move the Shoshone contested for decades; in 1939 the Arapaho were granted an equal interest in the reservation's lands and resources by court decision.

Communities and governance

The principal communities include Fort Washakie, seat of the Eastern Shoshone tribal government, and Ethete and Arapahoe, associated with the Northern Arapaho. The tribes jointly operate the Wind River Casino and other enterprises, and the reservation is known for oil, gas, and uranium resources as well as cattle ranching. Sacajawea, the Shoshone guide of the Lewis and Clark Expedition, is traditionally said to be buried at Fort Washakie.
