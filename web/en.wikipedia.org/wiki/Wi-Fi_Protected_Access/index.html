Wi-Fi Protected Access (WPA) is a family of security certification programs developed by the Wi-Fi Alliance to secure wireless computer networks. It was created in response to the serious weaknesses found in the original Wired Equivalent Privacy (WEP) standard. Three generations exist: WPA, WPA2, and WPA3.

WPA and WPA2

The original WPA (2003) was an interim standard implementing most of IEEE 802.11i, notably the Temporal Key Integrity Protocol (TKIP) for per-packet key mixing on existing hardware. WPA2 (2004) implemented the full 802.11i standard, mandating AES-based CCMP encryption. Both offer Personal (pre-shared key) and Enterprise (802.1X/RADIUS authentication) modes. WPA2 was shown vulnerable in 2017 to the KRACK key-reinstallation attacks against the four-way handshake, largely mitigated by software patches.

WPA3

Announced in January 2018, WPA3 replaces the pre-shared key exchange with Simultaneous Authentication of Equals (SAE), providing forward secrecy and resistance to offline dictionary attacks; it also adds 192-bit security mode for enterprise networks and Easy Connect provisioning for devices without displays. WPA3 support is required for Wi-Fi 6 certification.
