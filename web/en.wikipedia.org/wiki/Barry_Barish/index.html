Barry Clark Barish (born 27 January 1936) is an American experimental physicist and Nobel laureate. As director of the LIGO Laboratory from 1994, he transformed a struggling research project into a large, disciplined scientific collaboration, secured the construction of the two observatories, and created the LIGO Scientific Collaboration — leadership recognised by his share of the 2017 Nobel Prize in Physics.

Barish, a Berkeley-trained particle physicist who worked on neutrino experiments at Fermilab and led a search for magnetic monopoles, brought big-project management to gravitational-wave science; the design he championed (initial LIGO followed by a planned Advanced LIGO) delivered the first detection in 2015, months after the upgrade's completion. He has since been a leading figure in planning future detectors.

Role at LIGO

Taking over in 1994 after the project's early difficulties, Barish wrote the proposal that won NSF construction funding, built the two-site observatory model, and professionalised the collaboration of over a thousand scientists. The 2017 Nobel citation paired Weiss's invention and Thorne's theory with Barish's decisive organisational contribution — a rare formal recognition of scientific leadership.
