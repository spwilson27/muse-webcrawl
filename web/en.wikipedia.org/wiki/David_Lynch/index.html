David Lynch

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: David Lynch in 2016, silver pompadour immaculate, seated in his painting studio — the artist as serene surrealist]

David Keith Lynch (20 January 1946 – 15 January 2025) was an American filmmaker, visual artist, musician and actor, the defining surrealist of American cinema. His work — small-town surfaces over abysses of dream and violence — produced the adjective 'Lynchian', entered in dictionaries of film criticism if not yet of record everywhere: Eraserhead (1977), The Elephant Man (1980), Blue Velvet (1986), Wild at Heart (1990, Palme d'Or), Mulholland Drive (2001, voted the greatest film of the twenty-first century in a 2016 BBC critics' poll) and the television series Twin Peaks (1990–91, with Mark Frost; revived 2017).

Born in Missoula, Montana, an Eagle Scout and painter trained at the Pennsylvania Academy, Lynch made Eraserhead over five years at the AFI Conservatory; it became the midnight-movie circuit's sacred text. Mel Brooks, of all producers, hired him for The Elephant Man and called him 'Jimmy Stewart from Mars' (in the famous phrase).

Method

Lynch painted, built furniture, recorded weather reports and albums, and practised Transcendental Meditation from 1973 — his foundation promotes TM in schools and prisons, and his book Catching the Big Fish (2006) argues creativity as fishing in consciousness. On set he directed by music, mood and 'eye of the duck' scene theory, refusing to explain his films' meanings: the work, he insisted, is the talking.

Death and standing

An honorary Academy Award (2019) recognised a career that bent American film and television permanently — every 'weird small town' series descends from Twin Peaks. Lynch died in January 2025 of complications of emphysema, having been evacuated from his home days earlier during the Los Angeles wildfires; the tributes treated him as a national artist.
