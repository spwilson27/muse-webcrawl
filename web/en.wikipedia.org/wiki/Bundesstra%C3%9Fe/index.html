Bundesstraße (German for "federal highway"), designated "B" followed by the road's number, is a class of long-distance road in Germany and Austria, administered by the federal government (in Germany, through the federal states acting on its behalf) and ranking below the Autobahn in the road hierarchy. In Germany, Bundesstraßen are identified by rectangular signs with black numbers on a yellow background.

Germany

The German Bundesstraße network comprises roughly 38,000 km of roads connecting medium and larger cities and regions where no autobahn runs. Unlike autobahns, they may pass directly through towns and villages, have at-grade intersections and varying speed limits, though many sections are built as bypasses or dual carriageways ("gelbe Autobahn"). The numbering system dates to the Reichsstraßen of 1932, whose numbers were largely retained.

Austria

In Austria, the term was historically applied to federal roads, which since 2002 have been administered by the states and renamed Landesstraßen B, though they retain the "B" numbering and their yellow signage; famous examples include the Großglockner High Alpine Road (B 107).

Switzerland

The German-speaking cantons of Switzerland sometimes also use the word informally for main roads (Hauptstraßen), though these are not federal highways in the German sense.
