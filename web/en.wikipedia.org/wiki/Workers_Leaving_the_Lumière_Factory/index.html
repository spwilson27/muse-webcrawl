Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory

Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory (French: La Sortie de l'usine Lumière à Lyon) is an 1895 French short black-and-white silent documentary film directed and produced by Louis Lumière. It is often cited as the first motion picture ever made.

Content

In a single static shot lasting about 46 seconds, workers — mostly women — stream out of the gates of the Lumière photographic factory in Lyon at the end of the working day, dispersing on foot and bicycle until the gates close.

Significance

The film was first shown on 22 March 1895 to a small audience, and later formed part of the Lumières' famous public Cinématographe programme on 28 December 1895 in Paris. Three versions were shot, differing in details such as a horse-drawn carriage. It exemplifies early 'actuality' filmmaking.
