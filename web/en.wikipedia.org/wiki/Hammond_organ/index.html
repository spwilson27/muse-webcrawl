The Hammond organ is an electric organ invented by Laurens Hammond and John M. Hanert and first manufactured in 1935. Multiple models have been produced, most of which use sliding drawbars to vary sounds. Until 1975, Hammond organs generated sound by creating an electric current from rotating a metal tonewheel near an electromagnetic pickup, and amplifying the resulting signal through a speaker cabinet. The organ is commonly used with — and its sound is often associated with — the Leslie speaker.

History

Laurens Hammond, a clock manufacturer, adapted his synchronous electric motor technology to musical sound generation, patenting the tonewheel organ in 1934. The Model A went on sale in 1935, marketed to churches as a cheaper alternative to pipe organs. The Federal Trade Commission forced Hammond to stop claiming it could fully reproduce a pipe organ, but sales flourished anyway. The most famous models are the B-3 console (1954) and its church variant the C-3 and home variant A-100, all using the same tone generator. Jimmy Smith revolutionised the instrument in jazz in the 1950s, and the Hammond sound became central to gospel, soul, rock, and progressive rock — played by artists from Booker T. Jones to Jon Lord of Deep Purple and Keith Emerson.

Sound and features

Each manual has nine drawbars per set, controlling the levels of harmonic partials from the sub-octave to high harmonics, giving the player additive control over timbre. Distinctive features include the "percussion" effect (a fast-decaying harmonic ping on attack), key click (an artefact of the electromechanical keying that musicians came to prize), vibrato and chorus generated by a mechanical scanner, and the warm distortion produced when the organ drives a Leslie speaker, whose rotating horns produce a characteristic Doppler chorus.

Legacy

Tonewheel production ended in 1975 as transistor instruments took over, but original B-3s remain sought after. Hammond (now owned by Suzuki) and other makers produce digital clonewheel organs that simulate the tonewheel sound, and Hammond emulation is a standard feature of stage keyboards and software instruments.
