International Workers' Day, also known as Labour Day in some countries and often referred to as May Day, is a celebration of labourers and the working classes promoted by the international labour movement and held every year on 1 May, or the first Monday in May.

Origins

The date was chosen by the Second International in 1889 to commemorate the Haymarket affair in Chicago, where a labour demonstration for the eight-hour day in May 1886 ended in violence and the deaths of workers and police. The first International Workers' Day was observed in 1890, and the observance spread worldwide, becoming a focus for demonstrations demanding workers' rights.

Observance

1 May is an official public holiday in many countries across Europe, Asia, Africa and Latin America, marked by parades, marches and union rallies. In the United States and Canada, Labour Day is instead celebrated on the first Monday of September, a separation that dates to official efforts to distance the holiday from its radical origins.
