Mehmed IV (Ottoman Turkish: محمد رابع, Meḥmed-i rābiʿ; 2 January 1642 – 6 January 1693), also known as Mehmed the Hunter (Avcı Mehmed), was Sultan of the Ottoman Empire from 1648 to 1687. His 39-year reign, one of the longest in Ottoman history, began when he was six years old and encompassed the Köprülü era of grand-vizierial government, the conquest of Crete, the failed Siege of Vienna in 1683, and the disastrous War of the Holy League that led to his deposition.

Accession and regency

Mehmed ascended the throne in August 1648 after his father Ibrahim was deposed and killed in a palace revolt. Effective power lay first with his grandmother Kösem Sultan and then, after her murder in 1651, with his mother Turhan Sultan. The empire suffered chronic fiscal and military disorder, culminating in the Çınar Incident of 1656, after which Turhan appointed the elderly Köprülü Mehmed Pasha as Grand Vizier with near-absolute powers.

Köprülü era

Under Köprülü Mehmed (1656–1661) and his son Fazıl Ahmed Pasha (1661–1676) the empire's fortunes revived: rebellions were suppressed, the navy was rebuilt, and the long war for Crete ended with the fall of Candia (Heraklion) in 1669. Ottoman arms advanced in Ukraine and Podolia was annexed in 1672. After Fazıl Ahmed's death, his foster-brother Kara Mustafa Pasha became Grand Vizier and pursued an ambitious policy against the Habsburgs.

Mehmed himself was devoted to hunting and spent much of his reign at Edirne, leaving government largely to his viziers. His court chronicler and companions, and his support for the Kadızadeli-influenced preacher Vani Mehmed Efendi, shaped the religious tone of the court.

Vienna and deposition

In 1683 Kara Mustafa led a vast army against Vienna; the siege was broken on 12 September 1683 by the relief army of John III Sobieski, and the ensuing War of the Holy League turned catastrophically against the Ottomans. Buda fell in 1686 and the Ottoman army was crushed at the second Battle of Mohács in August 1687. Mutinous troops marched on the capital, and Mehmed IV was deposed on 8 November 1687 in favour of his brother Suleiman II. He spent his remaining years in confinement at Edirne, where he died in 1693.
