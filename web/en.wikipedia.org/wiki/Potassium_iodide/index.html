Potassium iodide

Potassium iodide is a chemical compound, medication, and dietary supplement with formula KI. It is a metal-halide salt featuring the potassium cation and the iodide anion. As a medication it is used to treat hyperthyroidism, in radiation emergencies to protect the thyroid gland, and as an expectorant.

Medical uses

In a nuclear accident, potassium iodide tablets saturate the thyroid with stable iodine, blocking uptake of radioactive iodine-131 and reducing the risk of thyroid cancer; this use was notable after the Chernobyl disaster, where Poland distributed KI to millions of children. Saturated solution of potassium iodide (SSKI) is used as an expectorant and to treat the fungal infection sporotrichosis. Lugol's iodine and tincture of iodine are related antiseptic preparations containing KI.

Chemistry and production

Potassium iodide is made by reacting iodine with hot concentrated potassium hydroxide, or by treating iron filings with iodine to give iron(II,III) iodide and converting it with potassium carbonate. It is an important source of the iodide ion in organic synthesis, for example in the Finkelstein reaction, and is used in analytical chemistry in iodometric titrations.

Other uses

KI is added to table salt to make iodized salt, preventing iodine deficiency. It is also used in photography, in cloud-seeding alternatives research, and as a nutrient in animal feed.
