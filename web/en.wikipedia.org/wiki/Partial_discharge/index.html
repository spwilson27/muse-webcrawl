Partial discharge

In electrical engineering, a partial discharge (PD) is a localised dielectric breakdown of a small portion of a solid or liquid electrical insulation system under high-voltage stress, which does not completely bridge the space between the two conductors. PD activity is an early indicator of insulation deterioration in transformers, cables, switchgear and rotating machines.

Causes

Partial discharges typically occur in gas-filled voids within solid insulation, at delaminated interfaces, around sharp conducting points (corona discharge in air), or along contaminated surfaces. Because the electric field in a gas void exceeds that in the surrounding dielectric, the void breaks down at a voltage below the system's overall withstand level.

Effects and detection

Each discharge is a tiny current pulse that erodes the insulation chemically and mechanically, producing ozone and acids that eventually tree through the material and cause complete failure. PD is therefore measured during factory acceptance tests and in-service monitoring — electrically (in picocoulombs, per IEC 60270), acoustically, or via ultra-high-frequency sensors — and trended as a diagnostic of asset health.
