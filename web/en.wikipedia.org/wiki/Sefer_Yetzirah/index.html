Sefer Yetzirah

Sefer Yetzirah (Hebrew: סֵפֶר יְצִירָה, "Book of Formation" or "Book of Creation") is the title of the earliest extant book on Jewish mysticism, sometimes called the "Book of Abraham." It describes how God created the universe through the ten sefirot (numbers or emanations) and the twenty-two letters of the Hebrew alphabet.

Date and authorship

The work is pseudepigraphically attributed to the patriarch Abraham, and tradition ascribes its editing to Rabbi Akiva. Modern scholars date the text variously between the second and sixth centuries CE, with some proposing dates as early as the second century BCE. It exists in short and long recensions and was the subject of commentaries by Saadia Gaon, Judah Halevi, and many later Kabbalists.

Contents

The brief text expounds a cosmology in which the thirty-two "paths of wisdom" — ten sefirot and twenty-two letters — form the building blocks of creation. The letters are divided into three "mother" letters (Aleph, Mem, Shin), seven "double" letters, and twelve "simple" letters, correlated with the elements, planets, zodiac signs, months, and parts of the body. By combining letters according to mathematical permutations — the text enumerates combinations of two, three, and more letters — God "engraved, carved, permuted, weighed, and transformed" all that exists. Later legend held that adepts could use these techniques to create a golem.

Influence

Sefer Yetzirah became a foundational text of Kabbalah and of Jewish letter mysticism, and its scheme of sefirot prepared the way for the elaborate Kabbalistic tree of life. It also influenced medieval Jewish philosophy, Hebrew linguistics, and later Western esotericism.
