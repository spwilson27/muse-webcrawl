In relativistic physics, Born coordinates are a coordinate system adapted to a family of observers who rotate uniformly in flat Minkowski spacetime — the so-called Langevin observers. Named after Max Born, who introduced the notion of Born rigidity in 1909, the chart is the relativistic analogue of rotating cylindrical coordinates and is central to discussions of the Ehrenfest paradox and the Sagnac effect.

Definition

Starting from ordinary cylindrical coordinates on Minkowski spacetime, one passes to a frame rotating with constant angular velocity ω by the transformation φ′ = φ − ωt. In these coordinates the line element takes the form

ds² = −(1 − ω²r²) dt² + 2ωr² dt dφ + dr² + r² dφ² + dz².

The cross term reflects the fact that the rotating chart is not time-orthogonal: Langevin observers cannot consistently synchronise clocks around a closed loop, which is the geometric root of the Sagnac effect.

Born rigidity and the light cylinder

The rotating congruence is Born-rigid: neighbouring Langevin observers maintain constant radar distance from one another, so the quotient geometry of their worldlines is time-independent. However, the chart is valid only inside the light cylinder r < 1/ω, where the observers' speed remains below the speed of light. The spatial geometry measured by the rotating observers is non-Euclidean — the circumference of a rotating disk exceeds 2π times its radius — resolving the Ehrenfest paradox without contradiction.

Applications

Born coordinates are a standard pedagogical tool for showing that non-inertial frames in special relativity already exhibit many features — horizons, non-Euclidean spatial geometry, failure of global simultaneity — often associated only with general relativity.
