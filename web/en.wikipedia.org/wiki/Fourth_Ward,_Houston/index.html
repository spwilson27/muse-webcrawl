Fourth Ward, Houston

Fourth Ward is a historical political district and neighbourhood in Houston, Texas, United States. It is one of the six historic wards created by the city in the 19th century. The Fourth Ward is located west of downtown Houston and includes the area historically known as Freedmen's Town, a community founded by emancipated African Americans after the Civil War.

History

Houston's ward system was established in 1839; wards served as political districts until the city moved to a commission government in 1905, but the names remain in common use. After emancipation in 1865, freed slaves settled Fourth Ward land along Buffalo Bayou, building Freedmen's Town — a self-sufficient community of churches, schools and businesses, whose residents paved some streets with hand-laid bricks that survive today. From the mid-20th century, urban renewal, highway construction and later gentrification dramatically reduced the African American population and demolished much historic housing; the area was designated a historic district and parts are listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Landmarks

Landmarks include Antioch Missionary Baptist Church (founded 1866), the African American Library at the Gregory School, and the remaining brick streets of Freedmen's Town.
