Boniface I, Marquis of Montferrat

Boniface I of Montferrat
Born: c. 1150
Died: 4 September 1207
Title: Marquis of Montferrat (from 1192)
Known for: Leader of the Fourth Crusade; King of Thessalonica
Father: William V of Montferrat

Boniface I (c. 1150 – 4 September 1207), usually known as Boniface of Montferrat, was an Italian nobleman, Marquis of Montferrat from 1192, and the elected leader of the Fourth Crusade. He came from a family with long-standing crusading connections in the Latin East: his father William V had fought in the Kingdom of Jerusalem, and his brothers William Longsword and Conrad both married into the Jerusalem royal house.

Fourth Crusade

Boniface was chosen to lead the Fourth Crusade in 1201 after the death of Count Theobald of Champagne. He had hoped to be crowned emperor after the crusaders captured Constantinople in 1204, but the Venetians vetoed his candidacy in favour of Baldwin of Flanders. In compensation Boniface claimed Thessalonica and much of Macedonia and Greece, founding the Kingdom of Thessalonica and granting fiefs to his followers.

Death and legacy

Boniface campaigned against the Bulgarians under Tsar Kaloyan and was killed in an ambush near Mosynopolis in the Rhodope Mountains on 4 September 1207. His young son Demetrius inherited Thessalonica, which was soon besieged and eventually lost to the Despotate of Epirus in 1224. Boniface was also a patron of troubadour poetry and himself associated with the court culture of his native Piedmont.
