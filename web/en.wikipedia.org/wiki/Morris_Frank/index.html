Morris Frank

Born: March 23, 1908, Nashville, Tennessee
Died: November 22, 1980, Morristown, New Jersey
Known for: Co-founding The Seeing Eye; first American guide dog user

Morris Frank (March 23, 1908 – November 22, 1980) was an American co-founder of The Seeing Eye, the first guide dog school in the United States, and is generally credited as the first American to use a guide dog. Blinded in both eyes by accidents — one in a childhood boxing match, the other at age 16 — Frank was frustrated by dependence on human guides. In 1928 he travelled to Switzerland to train at Fortunate Fields, Dorothy Harrison Eustis's guide dog school, and returned to the United States with a German Shepherd named Buddy, demonstrating guide dog work to skeptical American journalists by crossing a busy New York street.

The Seeing Eye

With Eustis's backing, Frank and others founded The Seeing Eye in Nashville, Tennessee, in January 1929; the school relocated to Whippany and later Morristown, New Jersey. Frank served as its vice president and travelling ambassador for decades, promoting the independence that trained guide dogs offered blind people. The school's name comes from Proverbs 20:12: "The seeing eye, and the hearing ear, the Lord hath made even both of them."

Advocacy

Frank campaigned for laws and policies allowing guide dogs in public places, restaurants, hotels, and public transportation, at a time when animals were broadly excluded. He and a succession of dogs — all his guide dogs were named Buddy — travelled the country demonstrating guide dog work. His efforts contributed to access legislation and to acceptance of service animals in the United States; in 1936 alone he is reported to have travelled some 50,000 miles promoting the cause. He was also involved in organizing the American Foundation for the Blind's efforts on travel and employment for blind veterans.

Legacy

Frank's life with the first Buddy was dramatized in the 1984 television film Love Leads the Way. The Seeing Eye continues to operate in Morristown, and Frank is remembered as the man who brought the guide dog movement to America.
