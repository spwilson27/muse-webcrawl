Harry Braverman

Harry Braverman (December 9, 1920 – August 2, 1976) was an American Marxist, worker, political economist, and revolutionary. Born in New York City, Braverman worked in a variety of metal-smithing industries beginning in 1937. He became a socialist activist and a leader of the Socialist Workers Party, and later worked in publishing, eventually becoming director of Monthly Review Press.

Born: December 9, 1920, New York City, U.S.

Died: August 2, 1976 (aged 55), Honesdale, Pennsylvania, U.S.

Occupation: Metalworker, editor, writer

Notable work: Labor and Monopoly Capital (1974)

Life

Braverman grew up in a working-class family in Brooklyn and left college during the Great Depression to work as a coppersmith's apprentice in shipyards and steel fabrication shops. His years in industry gave him firsthand knowledge of craft work and of management's efforts to control it. Politically active in the Trotskyist movement, he wrote under pseudonyms during the McCarthy period and was dismissed from employment under political pressure. He became an editor at Grove Press and then, from 1967, director of Monthly Review Press, where he published works of Marxist scholarship.

Labor and Monopoly Capital

Braverman's best-known work, Labor and Monopoly Capital: The Degradation of Work in the Twentieth Century (1974), revived analysis of the labour process within Marxist economics. Drawing on Marx and on Frederick Winslow Taylor's Principles of Scientific Management, Braverman argued that under monopoly capitalism management systematically separates conception from execution, deskilling workers by transferring knowledge of production into managerial systems and machinery. The thesis extended to clerical and service work, which he saw being routinised in the same way as factory labour.

The book became a foundational text of labour process theory, provoking both extensive scholarly debate — critics questioned whether deskilling was a universal tendency — and renewed interest in the politics of work design, automation, and technology.
