New Orleans

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

New Orleans
State: Louisiana, USA
Founded: 1718, by the French
Population: about 360,000 (city)
Known for: Jazz, Mardi Gras, Creole culture and cuisine

New Orleans is a major port city in southeastern Louisiana, at the mouth of the Mississippi River, known worldwide as the birthplace of jazz and for its Creole culture, cuisine, architecture and Mardi Gras carnival. Founded by the French in 1718, ruled by Spain for four decades, and sold to the United States with the Louisiana Purchase in 1803, it is the most European — and the most African — of American cities.

Culture

In Congo Square, enslaved Africans were permitted Sunday gatherings whose rhythms fed directly into the music that became jazz around 1900 (Buddy Bolden, then Louis Armstrong). The French Quarter's iron balconies, the streetcars of St. Charles Avenue, gumbo and jambalaya, second-line parades and jazz funerals define a culture of pleasure with deep roots in survival. Mardi Gras — "the greatest free show on earth" — crowns its calendar.

Struggle and resilience

Much of the city lies below sea level, ringed by levees. Hurricane Katrina (29 August 2005) breached them catastrophically: some 80% of the city flooded and over a thousand people died in Louisiana, in the costliest disaster in US history, exposing failures of engineering and government seared into national memory. Rebuilt neighbourhood by neighbourhood, with population partly recovered, New Orleans remains the delta's living archive of the blues, of river trade down to the Gulf past St. Louis's rival claims, and of a Creole civilisation unlike any other in America.
