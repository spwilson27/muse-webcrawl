Crown Royal is a blended Canadian whisky produced by Diageo. The brand was created in 1939 by Samuel Bronfman, president of Seagram, to honour the royal tour of Canada by King George VI and Queen Elizabeth — the first reigning monarchs to visit the country. It is distilled in Gimli, Manitoba, and remains one of the best-selling Canadian whiskies in the United States, recognizable by its crown-shaped bottle and the purple velvet bag in which it is sold.

The whisky remained available only in Canada until 1964, when it was introduced to the United States and other markets. Crown Royal's flagship "Deluxe" blend is made from a selection of several dozen whiskies matured in oak and blended for smoothness.

History

To mark the 1939 royal visit, Bronfman created a special blend, presented to the King and Queen aboard the royal train in a decanter in a purple bag with gold drawstrings — the packaging that defines the brand to this day. Crown Royal was premium-priced and marketed as fit for royalty. After its US launch in 1964 it grew steadily, and by the 2000s it was regularly the top-selling Canadian whisky brand in the US. When Seagram's drinks business was broken up in 2000–2001, Diageo acquired Crown Royal, and the brand expanded into numerous line extensions.

Production

Crown Royal is produced at Diageo's Gimli distillery on the western shore of Lake Winnipeg in Manitoba, chosen for its limestone-filtered water and prairie grain supply. The distillery produces several distinct whisky styles — distilled in column and pot stills and matured in new, charred, and refill oak — which master blenders combine for the flagship blend, traditionally described as a blend of around 50 individual whiskies. Bottling uses water from the region; the signature purple bag is included with the Deluxe and most premium expressions.

Varieties

Beyond the flagship Deluxe, major expressions include Crown Royal Reserve, the cognac-cask-finished XO, the higher-proof Black, Northern Harvest Rye (named World Whisky of the Year in Jim Murray's 2016 Whisky Bible, a celebrated upset), the limited Crown Royal Noble Collection, and a highly successful line of flavoured whiskies led by Regal Apple (2014), followed by Vanilla, Peach, and others that became major sellers in the US market.

Marketing and culture

The purple bag has entered North American popular culture as a ubiquitous keepsake — used for coins, dice, and games pieces — and Crown Royal has been prominent in NASCAR sponsorship (notably the "Your Name Here" 400-era races honouring military members), rodeo, and country music marketing. Its advertising commonly emphasizes generosity and gratitude ("Live Generously").
