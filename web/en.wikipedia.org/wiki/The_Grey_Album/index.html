The Grey Album is a mashup album by the musician and producer Danger Mouse, released in 2004. It combines an a cappella version of rapper Jay-Z's The Black Album with samples and instrumentation drawn entirely from the Beatles' 1968 self-titled double album, commonly known as the White Album — hence the "grey" of the title.

Grey Tuesday

Danger Mouse produced the album as a limited promotional item, but EMI, holder of the Beatles' recordings, issued cease-and-desist orders against its distribution. In protest, on 24 February 2004 the activist group Downhill Battle organised "Grey Tuesday", in which hundreds of websites hosted the album for free download — a landmark early act of online civil disobedience over sampling and copyright.

Reception

Despite (and partly because of) its legal suppression, the album received wide critical acclaim and is credited with popularising the mashup genre; it launched Danger Mouse's mainstream career, and Jay-Z and Paul McCartney were reported to have reacted approvingly.
