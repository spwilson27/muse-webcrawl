Pulled pork

Pulled pork
Place of originUnited States
RegionSouthern United States
Main ingredientsPork shoulder (Boston butt)
Cooking methodSlow smoking / barbecuing

Pulled pork is an American barbecue dish, most common in the Southern United States, in which pork — typically the shoulder cut known as the Boston butt — is slow-cooked at low temperature until the meat becomes tender enough to be "pulled", or shredded, into strands.

Preparation

The shoulder is usually coated with a dry rub of spices (commonly including salt, sugar, paprika and black pepper) and smoked over wood for many hours at around 110–130 °C (225–275 °F). During the long cook, collagen in the meat converts to gelatin, so that when the internal temperature approaches 90 °C the meat can be shredded by hand or with forks. It is commonly served on a bun with coleslaw and barbecue sauce, or alongside baked beans and cornbread.

Regional styles

In eastern North Carolina, whole-hog barbecue is dressed with a thin vinegar-and-pepper sauce; the Lexington style of the Piedmont uses shoulder meat with a tomato-tinged vinegar dip. In Memphis, pulled pork is served in sandwiches with sauce and slaw, while in Kansas City sweeter, thicker tomato-based sauces predominate.
