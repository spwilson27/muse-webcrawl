Techno is a genre of electronic dance music which generally produces a repetitive instrumental track in 4/4 time, typically around 120–150 beats per minute. The genre emerged in Detroit, Michigan, in the mid-1980s, pioneered by Juan Atkins, Derrick May and Kevin Saunderson — the "Belleville Three" — who fused Chicago house and electro with the futuristic synth music of Kraftwerk and synth-pop.

History

Atkins's project Cybotron released the electro classic "Clear" (1983) and "Techno City" (1984), from which the genre took its name; May's "Strings of Life" (1987, with Michael James) and Atkins's Model 500 defined the Detroit sound. The 1988 UK compilation Techno! The New Dance Sound of Detroit introduced the genre to Europe, where a harder "second wave" (Underground Resistance, Jeff Mills, Robert Hood) and Belgian and German scenes developed.

After the fall of the Berlin Wall, techno exploded in Germany: clubs such as Tresor and the Love Parade (which drew over a million people) made Berlin the genre's world capital. Juan Atkins's dancefloor descendants include minimal techno, acid techno and dub techno. Machines central to the sound include the Roland TR-808 and TR-909 drum machines and the TB-303 bass synthesizer.
