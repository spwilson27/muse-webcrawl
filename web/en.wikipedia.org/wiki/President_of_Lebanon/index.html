The President of Lebanon is the head of state of the Lebanese Republic. Under the country's confessional power-sharing system, established by the unwritten National Pact of 1943 and confirmed by the Taif Agreement of 1989, the presidency is reserved for a Maronite Christian. The prime minister, by the same convention, is a Sunni Muslim, and the speaker of parliament a Shia Muslim.

Election and term

The president is elected by the Parliament of Lebanon for a term of six years and may not be re-elected until six years have passed after the end of the previous term. Election requires a two-thirds quorum of parliament, and a two-thirds majority in the first round of voting, falling to an absolute majority in subsequent rounds. The difficulty of assembling these majorities has repeatedly produced long presidential vacancies, during which the council of ministers exercises presidential powers collectively.

Powers

The president promulgates laws, negotiates and ratifies international treaties (in agreement with the prime minister), accredits ambassadors, grants pardons on the recommendation of the minister of justice, and is the supreme commander of the armed forces, although the forces are in practice subject to the authority of the cabinet. After parliamentary consultations, the president designates the prime minister, who then forms a government. Before the Taif Agreement the presidency was considerably stronger; Taif shifted much executive power to the council of ministers collectively.

History

The office was created under the French Mandate constitution of 1926, with Charles Debbas as the first president. After independence in 1943, Bechara El Khoury became the first president of the independent republic. Presidents have included Camille Chamoun, Fouad Chehab, Charles Helou, Suleiman Frangieh, Elias Sarkis, Bashir and Amine Gemayel, René Moawad, Elias Hrawi, Émile Lahoud, Michel Suleiman, and Michel Aoun. The country has experienced several extended vacancies, most notably during the civil war years and after 2022, when parliament required many sessions to elect a successor to Michel Aoun; Joseph Aoun, the army commander, was elected in January 2025.
