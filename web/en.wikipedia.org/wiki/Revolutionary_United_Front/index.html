The Revolutionary United Front (RUF) was a rebel army that fought the government of Sierra Leone in the Sierra Leone Civil War (1991–2002). Founded and led by Foday Sankoh, a former army corporal, the RUF invaded from Liberia in March 1991 with support from Charles Taylor's National Patriotic Front of Liberia. The movement claimed to fight corruption and the marginalisation of the countryside, but became notorious internationally for atrocities, above all the amputation of civilians' limbs and the mass recruitment of child soldiers.

The civil war

The RUF seized the diamond fields of Kono District early in the war, financing itself through illicit "blood diamond" sales channelled through Liberia. Successive peace agreements collapsed: the Abidjan Accord (1996) was followed by the AFRC military coup of 1997, in which the RUF joined a short-lived junta in Freetown. West African ECOMOG forces restored President Ahmad Tejan Kabbah in 1998. In January 1999 RUF and AFRC fighters attacked Freetown in Operation No Living Thing, killing thousands.

The Lomé Peace Accord (July 1999) made Sankoh vice-president and head of the diamond commission, and granted a blanket amnesty. The settlement unravelled in May 2000 when the RUF took hundreds of UN peacekeepers hostage; British forces intervened (Operation Palliser), Sankoh was captured, and a renewed disarmament process, backed by a strengthened UNAMSIL mission, finally ended the war in January 2002.

Atrocities and the Special Court

The RUF was indicted for crimes against humanity and war crimes by the Special Court for Sierra Leone. Senior leaders including Issa Sesay, Morris Kallon and Augustine Gbao were convicted in 2009. Sankoh died in custody in 2003 before his trial. Charles Taylor was convicted in 2012 of aiding and abetting RUF crimes and sentenced to 50 years' imprisonment.

After the war

The RUF reorganised as the Revolutionary United Front Party (RUFP) and contested elections, winning few votes. The war killed an estimated 50,000–70,000 people and displaced more than a third of the country's population.
