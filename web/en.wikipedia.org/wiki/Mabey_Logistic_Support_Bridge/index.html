The Mabey Logistic Support Bridge (LSB) is a modular steel panel bridge system manufactured by the British company Mabey. It is a modern development of the famous Bailey bridge of the Second World War, designed for rapidly installed, high-capacity line-of-communication bridging by military engineers, and also for semi-permanent civil and emergency use. The system is in service with numerous armed forces including those of the United States, the United Kingdom, and many NATO and partner nations.

Design

The LSB uses prefabricated, interchangeable steel panels pinned together to form trusses, with transoms and a steel deck system. Components are sized for transport in standard ISO containers or palletised loads. Structures can be erected by hand or with light equipment and launched from one bank using a launching nose and rollers, in the manner of its Bailey ancestor. Depending on configuration — single or double storey, extra-wide deck, and the addition of piers — it can carry heavy main battle tanks and loaded transporters over spans that would be beyond lighter assault bridging.

Use

As a "logistic" bridge, the LSB is intended to keep supply routes open behind the front line: replacing battle-damaged permanent bridges, bypassing washed-out crossings, and providing durable crossings in theatre infrastructure. The US Army adopted the system as the Dry Support Bridge-family counterpart for longer spans, and Mabey bridges have been used in Iraq and Afghanistan and in disaster relief operations around the world. Civil variants derived from the same panel technology, marketed for permanent rural and highway bridges, have been installed in more than a hundred countries.
