Traditional Irish singing
Stylistic originsIrish folk song, sean-nós, ballad tradition
Cultural originsIreland
LanguagesIrish (Gaeilge) and English

Traditional Irish singing is the singing of traditional songs in the native styles of Ireland. It encompasses songs in both the Irish language and English, performed solo and usually unaccompanied, in settings ranging from the fireside and pub session to the concert stage and competition platform (the Fleadh Cheoil).

Sean-nós

The most distinctive style is sean-nós ("old style"), a highly ornamented, free-rhythm form of singing in Irish, originating in the Gaeltacht regions, especially Connemara. Sean-nós singing uses melodic ornamentation such as grace notes and melisma, subtle variation between verses, and a restrained delivery in which the song's story takes precedence. Renowned exponents include Seosamh Ó hÉanaí (Joe Heaney), Darach Ó Catháin and Nioclás Tóibín.

English-language tradition

Songs in English include broadside ballads, bothy and emigration songs, rebel songs and locally composed pieces. The "traditional" style in English tends toward a straighter, narrative delivery with less ornament than sean-nós, though the boundary is fluid. Singers such as Margaret Barry, Paddy Tunney and Len Graham were influential collectors' sources and performers.

Revival and organisation

Comhaltas Ceoltóirí Éireann (founded 1951) organises competitions in traditional singing in both languages. Singing clubs, festivals such as the All-Ireland Fleadh, and the work of collectors including Tom Munnelly preserved a large repertoire during the 20th-century folk revival.
