The Curtiss JN-4 "Jenny" was a series of biplane aircraft built by the Curtiss Aeroplane and Motor Company of Hammondsport, New York, later the Curtiss Aeroplane and Motor Corporation. It was the standard primary trainer of the United States Army during the First World War, and after the war thousands of surplus Jennies were sold cheaply to the public, becoming the iconic aircraft of the barnstorming era.

Development and service

The JN series combined features of Curtiss's earlier Model J and Model N trainers, designed with the help of British engineer B. Douglas Thomas. The JN-4, ordered in quantity from 1916, trained an estimated 95 percent of American and Canadian pilots in the First World War. The Canadian-built version, the JN-4 (Can) or "Canuck", differed in several details. A JN-4 also made the first flight of the United States Air Mail service in May 1918.

Barnstorming

After the Armistice, the US government sold surplus Jennies for as little as a few hundred dollars. Travelling pilots — barnstormers — bought them to give rides and perform stunts at county fairs across North America, introducing aviation to the general public. Charles Lindbergh began his flying career with a JN-4.

Specifications

  • Length: 27 ft 4 in (8.33 m); wingspan: 43 ft 7 in (13.28 m).
  • Powerplant: one Curtiss OX-5 water-cooled V-8 of 90 hp.
  • Range: about 143 miles; service ceiling: 6,500 ft.

Many Jennies survive in museums, and a small number remain airworthy.
