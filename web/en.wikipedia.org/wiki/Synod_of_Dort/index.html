Date13 November 1618 – 29 May 1619

The Synod of Dort (Dutch: Synode van Dordrecht) was an international synod of the Dutch Reformed Church held in Dordrecht in 1618–1619, convened by the States General of the Dutch Republic to settle the theological controversy between Calvinist (Counter-Remonstrant) and Arminian (Remonstrant) factions. It rejected the Five Articles of Remonstrance and issued the Canons of Dort, one of the "Three Forms of Unity" of Reformed churches, whose five main points are popularly summarised by the acronym TULIP.

Background

After the death of Jacobus Arminius (1609), his followers presented the Five Articles of Remonstrance (1610) to the States of Holland, moderating orthodox Calvinist teaching on predestination and grace. Their opponents, led by Franciscus Gomarus, demanded a national synod. The dispute became entangled with the political rivalry between Grand Pensionary Johan van Oldenbarnevelt, who protected the Remonstrants, and Stadtholder Maurice of Nassau, who backed the Counter-Remonstrants; Maurice's coup of 1618 cleared the way for the synod and for Oldenbarnevelt's arrest, trial and execution in May 1619.

Proceedings and outcome

The synod included Dutch delegates and representatives from Reformed churches in England, Scotland, the Palatinate, Hesse, Bremen, Switzerland and elsewhere. After the Remonstrant delegates, led by Simon Episcopius, were expelled from the assembly in January 1619 for refusing to accept the synod's procedural terms, the synod examined their articles in their absence and condemned them. The Canons of Dort affirm, in response to the five Remonstrant articles: unconditional election, limited (definite) atonement, total depravity, irresistible grace, and perseverance of the saints. About 200 Remonstrant ministers were deposed and leading figures banished, though toleration gradually returned in practice by the 1630s. The synod also authorised the Dutch States Translation of the Bible (Statenvertaling, published 1637) and addressed matters such as baptism and catechism instruction.

Legacy

The Canons of Dort remain a confessional standard for many Reformed denominations worldwide. The synod's 400th anniversary in 2018–2019 prompted historical reassessment and, in the Netherlands, public reflection and reconciliation gestures toward the Remonstrant tradition.
