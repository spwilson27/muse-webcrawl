Kitchen brigade

The kitchen brigade (brigade de cuisine) is a system of hierarchy found in restaurants and hotels employing extensive staff, commonly referred to as "kitchen staff" in English-speaking countries. The concept was developed by Georges Auguste Escoffier in the late 19th century, drawing on his military experience to organise the chaotic kitchens of grand hotels into a disciplined, specialised workforce.

Positions

  • Chef de cuisine — the executive chef, responsible for all kitchen operations, menus and staff.
  • Sous-chef de cuisine — the second-in-command, directly responsible for production and the kitchen team.
  • Chef de partie — a station chef in charge of a particular area of production.
  • Saucier — prepares sauces, stocks and hot appetisers; a prestigious station.
  • Poissonnier — the fish chef.
  • Rôtisseur — prepares roasted and braised meats.
  • Grillardin — the grill chef.
  • Friturier — the fry chef.
  • Entremétier — prepares soups, vegetables, pasta and egg dishes.
  • Garde manger — in charge of cold foods, salads, pâtés and the pantry.
  • Pâtissier — the pastry chef, responsible for desserts, breads and pastries.
  • Tournant — a swing cook who fills in at any station as needed.
  • Commis — a junior cook working under a chef de partie.
  • Plongeur — the dishwasher and kitchen porter.

History

Before Escoffier, kitchens were often organised around the older partie system of the great French houses, but staff were frequently versatile and stations loosely defined. Escoffier rationalised the structure at the Savoy Hotel in London and the Ritz in Paris, assigning each cook a defined speciality so that the components of complex dishes could be produced simultaneously and assembled quickly — the foundation of classical French haute cuisine service.

Modern use

Large hotel and fine-dining kitchens still use the brigade in full or adapted form, while smaller restaurants compress several stations into a handful of roles. The titles remain standard vocabulary in professional culinary training worldwide.
