Country: Turkey

Ankara is the capital of Turkey, located in the central Anatolian plateau. It became the seat of the Turkish national movement during the War of Independence and was declared the capital of the new Republic on 13 October 1923, shortly before the Republic itself was proclaimed, succeeding Istanbul, the Ottoman capital.

History

The city has ancient roots — Hittite, Phrygian, Galatian and Roman layers lie beneath it. As Ancyra it was a major Roman and Byzantine provincial centre; the Temple of Augustus there preserves the Res Gestae, Augustus's own account of his reign, inscribed on its walls. It became Ottoman in the fourteenth century and was long famous for the soft mohair of its Angora goats, cats and rabbits.

Modern capital

Mustafa Kemal Atatürk chose Ankara in 1919–1920 as the base of resistance to the post-First World War occupation partly for its inland safety and rail links. His mausoleum, Anıtkabir, completed in 1953, dominates a hill in the city and is the ceremonial heart of the Republic. Modern Ankara is Turkey's second-largest city, home to the Grand National Assembly, the ministries, foreign embassies and major universities, with a planned-capital core of broad boulevards.
