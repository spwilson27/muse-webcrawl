Overview

Gypsum is a soft sulfate mineral composed of calcium sulfate dihydrate, CaSO₄·2H₂O. With a Mohs hardness of 2 (scratched by a fingernail), it is one of the most common sulfate minerals, formed in evaporite sequences alongside halite and anhydrite, and in the oxidation zones of sulfide deposits.

Uses

Gypsum is the basis of plaster of Paris — made by heating gypsum to drive off part of its water — and hence of plaster, drywall (gypsum board), moulds and casts; it also conditions soil and sets Portland cement. Fine-grained white gypsum is alabaster, carved since antiquity. Spectacular crystals occur in Mexico's Cave of the Crystals at Naica (selenite beams up to 12 m) and in Spain's Pulpí geode. Gypsum dunes form the White Sands of New Mexico.
