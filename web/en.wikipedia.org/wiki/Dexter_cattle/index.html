Dexter cattle

Dexter cattle
Country of origin: Ireland
Use: Dual-purpose, milk and beef
Traits: Small stature; black, red or dun

Dexter cattle are a breed of cattle originating in Ireland. The smallest of the European cattle breeds, they are about half the size of a traditional Hereford and about one third the size of a Holstein dairy cow. They are a dual-purpose breed, used for both milk and beef production.

History

The breed originated in the south-west of Ireland in the 18th century, developed from the hardy mountain cattle of the region. The Dexter breed was promoted by a Mr Dexter, an agent of Lord Hawarden, from whom the breed takes its name. Dexters were introduced to England in 1882, and the breed society was founded in 1900.

Dexter cattle nearly became extinct in the 20th century as larger breeds became favoured, but the breed has recovered with support from rare breed organisations such as the Rare Breeds Survival Trust.

Characteristics

Dexters are naturally small cattle, with cows typically standing 92 to 107 cm at the hip. They come in three colours: black, red and dun. Some carry the chondrodysplasia (bulldog) dwarfism gene, and breeding programmes manage this carefully. The breed is known for its hardiness, efficient grazing, good mothering ability and high-quality marbled beef.
