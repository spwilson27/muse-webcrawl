Buy Nothing Christmas

Buy Nothing Christmas is a movement that began in Canada as a protest against the commercialization of Christmas and the materialism of consumer culture. Founded in 1992 by Ted Dave, a former advertising artist from Winnipeg working with the Adbusters network, the campaign encourages people to reconsider holiday spending and to celebrate Christmas without buying gifts, or by giving handmade, second-hand, or non-material gifts such as time, services, and donations to charity.

History

The idea grew out of the annual Buy Nothing Day, observed on Black Friday in North America since 1992. Mennonite churches in Canada helped popularize Buy Nothing Christmas in the late 1990s, developing resources and a website promoting a simpler, more spiritual celebration of the holiday. In the United Kingdom, the campaign was promoted by churches and activists as an alternative to debt-fuelled seasonal consumption.

Practices

Participants adopt strategies such as drawing names for a single gift exchange instead of buying for everyone, making food or craft gifts, giving "experience" gifts, pooling money for charitable donations, or holding gift-free gatherings focused on shared meals and activities. The movement emphasizes reducing waste, avoiding holiday debt, and recovering time with family and community.

Criticism and reception

Retailers and some commentators have argued that reduced Christmas spending would harm the economy and retail workers, while supporters counter that the campaign targets excess and marketing pressure rather than livelihoods. The movement is typically presented as a personal and spiritual choice rather than a boycott of specific companies.
