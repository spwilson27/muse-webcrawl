Aymara people

The Aymara are an Indigenous people of the Altiplano — the high plateau of the Andes — in Bolivia, Peru and Chile, numbering around two million. They speak the Aymara language, one of the few Native American languages with over a million speakers, and are recognised constitutionally in Bolivia, whose former president Evo Morales is Aymara.

History

Aymara-speaking kingdoms flourished around Lake Titicaca after the decline of Tiwanaku, around the 12th to 15th centuries, before being absorbed into the Inca Empire and then the Spanish colonial system. Aymara communities endured the colonial mining economy centred on Potosí and took part in major rebellions, including that of Túpac Katari, who besieged La Paz in 1781. Land reform after Bolivia's 1952 revolution and later indigenous movements reshaped Aymara political life.

Traditional Aymara life centres on the ayllu, a kin-based community holding land collectively, and on reciprocal labour practices. High-altitude agriculture grows potatoes, quinoa and oca, and herds of llamas and alpacas provide wool and transport. Religious life blends Catholicism with devotion to Pachamama (Mother Earth) and mountain spirits; the Aymara New Year, on the June solstice, is a public holiday in Bolivia. The multicoloured Wiphala flag represents the Indigenous peoples of the Andes and is a national symbol of Bolivia alongside the state flag.
