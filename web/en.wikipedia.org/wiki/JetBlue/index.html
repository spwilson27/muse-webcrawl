JetBlue

JetBlue Airways Corporation, stylised as jetBlue, is a major American low-cost airline headquartered in Long Island City, in the New York City borough of Queens. Founded by entrepreneur David Neeleman, the airline began operations in February 2000 and grew rapidly on the strength of low fares combined with amenities unusual for a budget carrier, including leather seats, free live television at every seat and generous legroom. Its main hub is John F. Kennedy International Airport in New York, with focus cities including Boston, Fort Lauderdale, Orlando, Los Angeles and San Juan.

History

Neeleman, who had previously co-founded Morris Air and WestJet, incorporated the company in 1998 as "New Air" and raised substantial start-up capital. The airline launched service from JFK to Buffalo and Fort Lauderdale in February 2000 with Airbus A320 aircraft. It remained profitable through the industry downturn following the September 11 attacks, but suffered a highly publicised operational meltdown during an ice storm in February 2007, which led to a passenger bill of rights and Neeleman's replacement as chief executive later that year.

Services and fleet

In 2014 JetBlue introduced Mint, a premium cabin with lie-flat seats, on transcontinental routes, later extending it to services to London (launched 2021) and Paris (2023) using A321LR aircraft. The fleet has centred on the Airbus A320 family; the airline was the launch customer for the A220-300 in U.S. service, taking the type to replace its Embraer E190s. Its loyalty programme is TrueBlue, and it has codeshare and partnership arrangements with a number of international carriers. A proposed merger with Spirit Airlines, announced in 2022, was blocked by a federal judge on antitrust grounds in January 2024 and abandoned shortly afterwards.
