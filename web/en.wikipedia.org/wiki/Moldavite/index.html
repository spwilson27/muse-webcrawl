TypeTektite (natural glass)

AgeAbout 15 million years
SourcesCzech Republic (Bohemia and Moravia), Germany, Austria

Moldavite is a forest green, olive green or blue-greenish vitreous silicate projectile glassy rock formed by a meteorite impact in southern Germany (the Nördlinger Ries crater) that occurred about 15 million years ago. It is a type of tektite, found principally in the Czech Republic along and near the Vltava River, from which its name derives (the river is called the Moldau in German).

Formation and distribution

During the Ries impact, molten material was ejected hundreds of kilometres and fell as droplets that solidified into glass in the strewn fields of Bohemia, Moravia, Lusatia, and Austria. Moldavites typically show sculptured, wrinkled surfaces from natural etching and have a distinctive moss-green colour caused by their chemical composition.

The total amount of moldavite ever formed is estimated at only a few thousand tonnes, making fine specimens prized by collectors.

Use

Moldavite has been fashioned into jewellery and amulets since the Stone Age; polished pieces are popular gemstones. It is also credited in folklore and modern crystal-healing practice with various properties, though such claims lack scientific support. Concern exists that many pieces sold are imitation glass.
