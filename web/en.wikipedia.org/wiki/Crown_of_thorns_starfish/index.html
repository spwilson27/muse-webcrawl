Crown-of-thorns starfish

Scientific classification
Phylum: Echinodermata
Class: Asteroidea
Order: Valvatida
Family: Acanthasteridae
Genus: Acanthaster
Species: A. planci

The crown-of-thorns starfish (Acanthaster planci) is a large starfish native to coral reefs in the Red Sea and across the Indian and Pacific Oceans. It is covered in long, sharp, venomous spines and preys on hard, or stony, coral polyps (order Scleractinia). Adults can reach 25–35 cm in diameter and may have as many as 21 arms.

Ecology

The starfish feeds by extruding its stomach over coral and digesting the living tissue, leaving behind white feeding scars. A single adult can consume several square metres of coral per year. Periodic population outbreaks, in which densities rise dramatically, have caused extensive damage to reefs including the Great Barrier Reef; whether outbreaks are natural or amplified by human activity such as nutrient runoff and removal of predators remains debated.

Control

Control measures include manual collection and injection with bile salts or household vinegar, which kills the starfish without harming other reef organisms. Natural predators include the giant triton snail, the humphead wrasse, and certain pufferfish and triggerfish. See also Great Barrier Reef and Starfish.
