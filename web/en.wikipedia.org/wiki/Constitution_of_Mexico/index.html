The Political Constitution of the United Mexican States (Spanish: Constitución Política de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos) is the current constitution of Mexico. It was drafted in Santiago de Querétaro by a Constituent Congress during the Mexican Revolution, approved on February 5, 1917, and promulgated by President Venustiano Carranza. February 5 is now a Mexican national holiday, Constitution Day.

Background

The constitution succeeded the liberal Constitution of 1857. Revolutionary leaders sought a charter that would enshrine the social demands of the Revolution — land reform, labour rights and limits on the power of the Catholic Church and foreign landowners — going well beyond Carranza's initial, more conventional draft.

Key provisions

Article 3 mandates free, secular public education. Article 27 vests ownership of land, water and subsoil resources in the nation, enabling agrarian reform through communal ejidos and restricting foreign ownership. Article 123 guarantees labour rights including the eight-hour day, a minimum wage, the right to strike and the abolition of child labour. Other articles establish a federal republic of free and sovereign states, separation of powers, a ban on presidential re-election, and extensive individual guarantees; Article 40 declares Mexico a representative, democratic, secular and federal republic.

Amendments

The constitution has been amended hundreds of times, making it one of the most frequently revised national constitutions. Reforms have covered electoral institutions, energy, education, human rights (notably the 2011 reform elevating treaty rights) and the structure of the judiciary.

Significance

The 1917 Constitution is often described as the first social constitution of the 20th century, predating the Russian (1918) and Weimar (1919) constitutions in incorporating social and labour rights alongside classical liberal guarantees.
