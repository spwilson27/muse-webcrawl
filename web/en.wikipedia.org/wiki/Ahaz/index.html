Ahaz (Hebrew: אָחָז, "he has grasped") was the twelfth king of Judah and the son and successor of Jotham. According to the Hebrew Bible he began to reign at the age of twenty and ruled for sixteen years, in the late 8th century BC. He is remembered unfavourably by the biblical writers for his idolatry and for making Judah a tributary vassal of the Assyrian Empire.

Syro-Ephraimite War

Early in his reign, Ahaz was attacked by Rezin of Aram-Damascus and Pekah of Israel, who sought to force Judah into an anti-Assyrian coalition — the Syro-Ephraimite War. The prophet Isaiah urged Ahaz to trust in God rather than seek foreign help, delivering the famous sign prophecy of the child Immanuel. Ahaz instead appealed to Tiglath-Pileser III of Assyria, sending him the treasures of the Temple as a gift. The Assyrians crushed Damascus and Israel, but Judah became an Assyrian vassal owing annual tribute.

Religious policy

The Books of Kings and Chronicles accuse Ahaz of introducing foreign cults, sacrificing his own son "in the fire" after the manner of the nations, and installing in the Temple in Jerusalem an altar modelled on one he had seen in Damascus. He is also associated with a famous early water-clock or sundial — the "dial of Ahaz" mentioned in connection with Isaiah's sign to Hezekiah.

Legacy

Ahaz was succeeded by his son Hezekiah, who reversed his father's religious policies. Ahaz is listed in the genealogy of Jesus in the Gospel of Matthew.
