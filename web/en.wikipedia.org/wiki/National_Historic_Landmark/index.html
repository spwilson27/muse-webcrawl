National Historic Landmark

A National Historic Landmark (NHL) is a building, district, object, site or structure in the United States that the federal government officially recognises for its outstanding historical significance. The programme is administered by the National Park Service. Of the more than 90,000 places listed on the National Register of Historic Places, only about 2,600 — under three per cent — are designated National Historic Landmarks, a distinction reserved for properties of exceptional value in illustrating the heritage of the United States.

History

The Historic Sites Act of 1935 authorised the Secretary of the Interior to survey and identify nationally significant historic properties. The National Historic Landmarks programme was formally launched in 1960, when the first 92 designations were announced, including sites such as Independence Hall in Philadelphia and the Alamo in San Antonio. The National Historic Preservation Act of 1966 created the National Register of Historic Places, into which all Landmarks are automatically entered.

Criteria

To be designated, a property must possess exceptional value or quality in illustrating or interpreting the heritage of the United States and a high degree of integrity of location, design, setting, materials, workmanship, feeling and association. Categories include places associated with events that have made a significant contribution to the broad patterns of American history; properties associated with the lives of persons nationally significant in the past; places embodying the distinguishing characteristics of an architectural type or period; sites that have yielded, or may yield, information important in history or prehistory; and districts composed of integral parts of notable significance. Ordinarily, religious properties, moved structures, reconstructed buildings, graves and places achieving significance within the last fifty years are excluded, though exceptions exist.

Designation and protection

Designation is made by the Secretary of the Interior after review by the National Park Service and the National Park System Advisory Board. NHL status does not itself restrict what a private owner may do with a property, but federal undertakings affecting Landmarks trigger review under Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act, and Landmarks threatened with damage may be placed on a watch list; designation can be withdrawn if a property loses its historic character. Some Landmarks, such as the Statue of Liberty, are also units of the National Park System.
