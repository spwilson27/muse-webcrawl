Indian name

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Indian names follow a variety of conventions based on the region, religion, language and community of the person. Unlike the standard Western pattern of given name followed by family surname, many Indian naming systems use patronymics, village or ancestral place names, caste or community names, or a single given name with initials. Consequently, the order and function of the parts of an Indian name vary widely across the country.

North India

In much of northern India, a person typically has a given name followed by a family surname (upnaam), which may derive from a clan, caste, occupation or place. Among Hindus, names are often drawn from deities, epics and virtues; a middle name may be the father's name (common in Maharashtra and Gujarat). Sikh men commonly take the religious surname Singh ("lion") and Sikh women Kaur ("princess"), followed optionally by a clan surname. Many women traditionally adopt their husband's surname after marriage, though practices vary.

South India

In Tamil Nadu, names commonly consist of the father's given name (often reduced to an initial placed before) followed by the person's given name, with no family surname; for example, R. Kumar means Kumar, son of Ramaswamy. In Kerala, names may combine a house or family name (tharavad), the father's name and the given name, in varying order. In Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, a family name (intiperu) usually precedes the given name. In Karnataka, naming patterns blend village names, father's names and given names.

East and Northeast

Bengali names generally follow the given-name–surname pattern, with surnames such as Banerjee, Mukherjee, Chatterjee and Ghosh derived historically from village names and titles. In the Northeast, many communities use clan names and, among Christian-majority states such as Mizoram and Nagaland, biblical given names are common.

Naming ceremonies

Traditional Hindu naming takes place at the namakarana ceremony, usually on the 11th or 12th day after birth, in which the child's name is often chosen according to the syllable suggested by the birth star (nakshatra). Muslim, Christian, Jain, Buddhist and Parsi communities follow their own customary naming practices, often combining religious and regional elements.
