The Apology Resolution is a United States joint resolution (Public Law 103-150) adopted by Congress and signed by President Bill Clinton on 23 November 1993, apologising to Native Hawaiians for the role of United States agents and citizens in the January 1893 overthrow of the Kingdom of Hawaii and the deprivation of Native Hawaiians' rights to self-determination.

Background

The resolution was passed in the centennial year of the overthrow of Queen Liliʻuokalani. It acknowledges that the overthrow was illegal, that the United States minister John L. Stevens and U.S. naval forces participated, and that Native Hawaiians never relinquished their claims to sovereignty or lands. Senators Daniel Akaka and Daniel Inouye of Hawaii were its principal sponsors.

Legal significance

A disclaimer in the resolution states that nothing in it is intended to serve as a settlement of any claims against the United States. Its legal weight was tested in Hawaii v. Office of Hawaiian Affairs (2009), in which the U.S. Supreme Court held that the resolution's findings did not strip Hawaii of authority over the ceded lands. The resolution remains an important reference in the Hawaiian sovereignty movement and debates over federal recognition of Native Hawaiians.
