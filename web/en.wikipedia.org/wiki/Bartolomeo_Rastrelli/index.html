Francesco Bartolomeo Rastrelli (1700 – 29 April 1771) was an Italian architect who worked in the Russian Empire and defined the exuberant late Baroque style of Elizabethan Russia. The son of sculptor Carlo Bartolomeo Rastrelli, he arrived in Saint Petersburg in 1716 and became court architect to the empresses Anna and Elizabeth.

Works

Rastrelli's masterpieces include the Winter Palace in Saint Petersburg (1754–1762), the Catherine Palace at Tsarskoye Selo with its vast gilded facade, the Smolny Convent with its sky-blue cathedral, the Stroganov and Vorontsov palaces, the Anichkov Palace, and, outside the capital, Rundāle Palace and Jelgava Palace in Latvia and contributions to the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra and St Andrew's Church in Kyiv.

Later years

His style fell from favour under Catherine the Great, who preferred Neoclassicism; Rastrelli retired in 1763 and died in 1771. His turquoise, white, and gold palaces remain defining images of imperial Saint Petersburg.
