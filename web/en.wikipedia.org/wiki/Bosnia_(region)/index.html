Bosnia (region)

Bosnia (Bosnian/Croatian/Serbian: Bosna) is the northern region of the modern country of Bosnia and Herzegovina, encompassing roughly 80 percent of its territory. Its southern counterpart is the region of Herzegovina. Together the two regions form the state, although they have never been formal administrative units with strictly defined borders.

Geography

Bosnia is largely mountainous, crossed by the Dinaric Alps in its western and central parts, while the north along the Sava River opens onto the flat Pannonian Plain. Major rivers include the Bosna (from which the region takes its name), the Vrbas, the Una and the Drina, which forms much of the eastern border with Serbia. The largest cities are Sarajevo (the national capital, lying on the border with Herzegovina), Banja Luka, Tuzla, Zenica and Bihać. The climate is predominantly continental, with cold winters and warm summers.

History

A Bosnian polity emerged in the medieval period, developing into the Kingdom of Bosnia in 1377 under Tvrtko I. The region was conquered by the Ottoman Empire in the fifteenth century (1463) and remained under Ottoman rule for over four centuries, during which Islam spread widely among the local Slavic population. Austria-Hungary occupied Bosnia in 1878 and annexed it in 1908, a crisis that contributed to the tensions leading to the assassination in Sarajevo that sparked the First World War. After 1918 Bosnia was part of Yugoslavia, and in 1992 it became part of the newly independent Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina, followed by the Bosnian War of 1992–1995.
