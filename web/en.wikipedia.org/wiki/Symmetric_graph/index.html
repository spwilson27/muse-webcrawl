Symmetric graph

In graph theory, a branch of mathematics, a graph is symmetric (or arc-transitive) if its automorphism group acts transitively on its ordered pairs of adjacent vertices — that is, for any two arcs (directed edges) u → v and x → y there is an automorphism mapping one to the other. Every arc-transitive graph is also vertex-transitive and edge-transitive, but the converse does not hold in general. Symmetric graphs are among the most highly symmetric combinatorial objects and have been studied extensively, particularly in the cubic (3-regular) case.

The Petersen graph, a well-known symmetric graph.

Properties

A connected arc-transitive graph must be regular, since automorphisms can map any vertex to any other. Symmetric graphs include complete graphs, complete bipartite graphs, cycles, the Petersen graph, the utility graph K3,3, and the Platonic solids' skeletons (tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron and icosahedron). A graph that is edge-transitive and vertex-transitive but not arc-transitive is called half-transitive; the smallest example is the Holt graph, with 27 vertices.

Cubic symmetric graphs

The cubic symmetric graphs have been completely catalogued. In 1947, William Thomas Tutte proved that a cubic symmetric graph can be at most 5-arc-transitive, and that the order of its automorphism group is tightly bounded in terms of its size. Ronald Foster began compiling the "Foster census" of cubic symmetric graphs, work completed by later researchers; the list shows that cubic symmetric graphs exist on every number of vertices divisible by certain patterns, and the complete catalogue, including all such graphs up to large orders, is maintained in modern databases by Marston Conder and others.

Algebraic formulation

Many symmetric graphs are Cayley graphs of groups, and the study of arc-transitive graphs connects graph theory with group theory, especially the theory of finite permutation groups and the classification of finite simple groups. Results such as the theorem of Tutte on the stabiliser of a vertex in a cubic arc-transitive graph — a 3-group of bounded order — are milestones of algebraic graph theory.
