A Single Man (novel)

AuthorChristopher Isherwood
Published1964 (Simon & Schuster)
GenreNovel
SettingLos Angeles, 1962

A Single Man is a 1964 novel by the English-American writer Christopher Isherwood. It follows a single day in the life of George Falconer, a middle-aged British professor of English at a Los Angeles college, who is grieving the sudden death of his partner Jim in a car accident. It is considered one of the earliest and finest works of the post-Stonewall gay liberation literary tradition, notable for its matter-of-fact portrayal of a gay protagonist's ordinary life.

Plot

Over the course of one day in 1962, George goes through the motions of his routine: dressing, teaching a seminar on Aldous Huxley, exercising, visiting a friend in hospital, enduring a dinner with his friend Charlotte, and drinking with a student, Kenny, who seeks him out. Beneath the surface he contemplates despair and suicide, yet the day's small connections — and a late-night swim and conversation with Kenny — complicate his resolve. The novel ends ambiguously, as George sleeps and the narrator contemplates the possibility that his heart has stopped.

Adaptation

The novel was adapted into the 2009 film A Single Man, directed by fashion designer Tom Ford in his directorial debut, starring Colin Firth as George — a performance that won the Volpi Cup and a BAFTA and was nominated for an Academy Award — and Julianne Moore as Charlotte.
