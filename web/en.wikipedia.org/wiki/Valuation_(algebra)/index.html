Valuation (algebra)

In algebra, a valuation is a function on a field (or ring) that provides a measure of the size or multiplicity of elements, generalising notions such as the exponent of a prime in the factorisation of an integer. Formally, a valuation on a field K is a map v : K* → Γ, from the non-zero elements of K to an ordered abelian group Γ (the value group), satisfying v(xy) = v(x) + v(y) and the ultrametric-type inequality v(x + y) ≥ min(v(x), v(y)). Setting v(0) = ∞ extends it to the whole field.

Examples

The prototype is the p-adic valuation on the rational numbers: for a prime p, vp(a/b) counts the exponent of p in the factorisation of a/b. On a field of rational functions, the order of vanishing of a function at a point is a valuation. Valuations of rank one with value group the integers are called discrete valuations; their associated valuation rings are discrete valuation rings, central objects in local algebra and algebraic geometry.

Valuation rings and places

The set of elements with non-negative valuation forms the valuation ring, a local ring whose maximal ideal consists of elements of positive valuation, with a residue field attached. Valuations connect to absolute values: exponentiating a real-valued valuation yields a non-Archimedean absolute value, and Ostrowski-type theorems classify these on number fields. The concept, developed by Kürschák, Krull and others in the early 20th century, underlies p-adic analysis, the theory of algebraic function fields, and modern approaches to singularities and birational geometry, where valuations on function fields correspond to prime divisors and exceptional loci.
