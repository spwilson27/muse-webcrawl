Hilo, Hawaii

Hilo
Census-designated place, seat of Hawaii County
Population: 44,186 (2020 census)
Airport: Hilo International Airport (ITO)

Hilo is the largest settlement and census-designated place in Hawaii County, Hawaii, United States, which encompasses the Island of Hawaii. It is the county seat and the largest town on the island, with a population of 44,186 at the 2020 census. Hilo overlooks Hilo Bay on the windward (eastern) coast of the island, at the foot of Mauna Loa and Mauna Kea.

Climate

Hilo is famous for its rain: it is one of the wettest cities in the United States, receiving around 130 inches (3,300 mm) of precipitation a year, driven by the trade winds forced up the slopes of the volcanoes. The lush vegetation fed by the rain supports attractions such as Rainbow Falls and the gardens along Banyan Drive.

Tsunamis

The low-lying bayfront has twice been devastated by tsunamis. On 1 April 1946 a tsunami generated by an earthquake in the Aleutian Islands killed dozens in Hilo; on 23 May 1960 the great Chilean earthquake sent waves that killed 61 people in the town. The disasters led to the creation and expansion of tsunami warning systems and to the redevelopment of the bayfront as open parkland; the Pacific Tsunami Museum in downtown Hilo commemorates the events.

Economy and culture

Hilo grew as a sugar plantation port and administrative centre. Today government, the University of Hawaii at Hilo, tourism and agriculture (including macadamia nuts, papaya and orchids) dominate its economy. Each spring the town hosts the Merrie Monarch Festival, the world's pre-eminent hula competition, honouring King David Kalākaua. Hilo is also the gateway to Hawaii Volcanoes National Park, about 30 miles (48 km) to the south-east.
