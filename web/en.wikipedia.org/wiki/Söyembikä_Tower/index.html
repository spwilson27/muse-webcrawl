The Söyembikä Tower (Tatar: Сөембикә манарасы; Russian: Башня Сююмбике) is a leaning watchtower in the Kazan Kremlin and the best-known landmark of Kazan, Russia. The seven-tiered red-brick tower, 58 metres tall, leans noticeably—by roughly 2 metres from vertical—and is traditionally named for Söyembikä, the last queen regent of the Khanate of Kazan.

History and legend

The tower's date is disputed: some scholars place its construction in the late 17th or early 18th century, after Russia's conquest of Kazan, while others argue for earlier, khanate-era origins. Popular legend holds that Ivan the Terrible built the tower in seven days after conquering Kazan in 1552, and that Söyembikä—whom he wished to marry—threw herself from its top rather than submit, a story that has made the tower a symbol of Tatar identity. In the 20th century the lean was stabilised by underpinning, and the tower became part of the Kremlin's UNESCO World Heritage listing (2000).

Architecture

The tower rises in seven tiers—square lower storeys giving way to octagonal upper levels—crowned by a pyramidal tent roof with a small watch turret. Bricks bearing marks associated with 18th-century Russian construction, together with Tatar decorative motifs, reflect the region's mixed heritage. Local wedding custom holds that touching the tower brings luck to newlyweds.
