Well logging

Well logging, also known as borehole logging, is the practice of making a detailed record (a well log) of the geologic formations penetrated by a borehole. The log may be based either on visual inspection of samples brought to the surface (geological logs) or on physical measurements made by instruments lowered into the hole (geophysical logs).

History

The first electrical well log was recorded in 1927 in a small oil field in France by the Schlumberger brothers, whose resistivity survey launched the modern well-logging industry. Nuclear logs, sonic logs and many other tool types followed over the twentieth century.

Types of logs

Common logs include the gamma ray log, which measures natural radioactivity and helps distinguish shale from sandstone; resistivity logs, sensitive to pore fluids; density and neutron porosity logs, used to estimate formation porosity and lithology; and the sonic log, which records acoustic travel time. Measurements may be taken while drilling (LWD/MWD) or afterwards on wireline.

Applications

Well logs are central to petroleum geology, groundwater exploration, mining, geothermal development and scientific drilling, allowing geologists to identify rock types, estimate porosity and permeability, and locate hydrocarbon-bearing zones.
