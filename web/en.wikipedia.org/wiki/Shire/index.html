Shire

A shire is a traditional term for a division of land, found in Great Britain, Australia, New Zealand, and some other English-speaking countries. It was first used in Wessex from the beginning of the Anglo-Saxon settlement, and spread to most of the rest of England in the tenth century. In some rural parts of Australia the term continues as the designation of a local government area.

History

In early medieval England, shires were administrative units governed on behalf of the king by an ealdorman and later a sheriff (shire-reeve). Each shire had its own court and raised military levies. Over time, many shires became the historic counties of England, and their names often survive in county names ending in "-shire", such as Yorkshire, Hampshire, and Shropshire.

Shire counties

In modern England, "shire county" informally denotes a non-metropolitan county with a two-tier structure. The term is also used in phrases like "the Shires" for the rural counties of the English Midlands.

The Shire in fiction

The Shire is also the homeland of the hobbits in J. R. R. Tolkien's The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings, depicted as a peaceful, agricultural region of Middle-earth.
