Molding (process)

Molding (American English) or moulding (British and Commonwealth English) is the process of manufacturing by shaping liquid or pliable raw material using a rigid frame called a mold or matrix. The mold is a hollow container, usually made of metal or other durable material, whose cavity is the negative shape of the part to be produced. The liquid material hardens inside the mold, adopting its shape, and is then ejected or removed.

Types of molding

  • Injection molding – molten plastic or metal is injected under pressure into a closed mold; the dominant process for mass-produced plastic parts.
  • Blow molding – a heated plastic tube is inflated inside a mold to form hollow parts such as bottles.
  • Compression molding – preheated material is placed in an open mold cavity and compressed into shape.
  • Rotational molding – a heated mold is rotated so material coats its inner surface, forming large hollow parts.
  • Extrusion molding – material is forced through a shaped die.
  • Casting – liquid metal or other material is poured into a mold and allowed to solidify.

Materials and applications

Molding is used with plastics, metals, glass, ceramics, and composite materials. Molded products range from bottles, toys, and household goods to automotive components, medical devices, and aerospace parts. Mold design must account for material shrinkage, draft angles for part removal, and the flow of material into the cavity.
