Conceptual history

Conceptual history (German: Begriffsgeschichte) is a branch of historical study that examines the historical evolution of concepts and the ways in which their meanings change over time. It treats concepts not as fixed labels but as both indicators of, and factors in, historical change, and it draws on historical semantics, social history, and the history of ideas.

Origins

The approach is most associated with the German historian Reinhart Koselleck and the monumental lexicon Geschichtliche Grundbegriffe ("Basic Concepts in History", published 1972–1997), which traced concepts such as revolution, state, and society. Koselleck argued that between roughly 1750 and 1850 (the Sattelzeit or "saddle period") many European political and social concepts were democratised, temporalised, ideologised, and politicised.

Related approaches

In the Anglophone world, the Cambridge School associated with Quentin Skinner and J. G. A. Pocock developed a related contextualist method for the history of political thought, emphasising speech acts and the recovery of what historical authors were doing in writing. Conceptual history has since been applied internationally, including global and transnational studies of travelling concepts.
