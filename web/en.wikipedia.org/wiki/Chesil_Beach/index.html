Overview

Chesil Beach in Dorset, England, is one of Britain's three major shingle beach structures and one of the finest examples of a tombolo in the world. The beach stretches about 29 kilometres (18 miles) from West Bay to the Isle of Portland, enclosing the Fleet, a shallow tidal lagoon, for much of its length.

Geology

The beach is composed of flint and chert pebbles graded in size along its length — small pea-sized shingle near West Bay growing to large cobbles at Portland — a sorting traditionally attributed to wave action, which allowed local fishermen to judge their position at night by the size of the pebbles. The ridge reaches about 15 metres in height at its eastern end. Chesil Beach forms part of the Jurassic Coast, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History and ecology

Storms periodically overtop the beach; the Great Storm of 1824 destroyed much of the village of Fleet. Behind the beach, the Fleet lagoon is a protected nature reserve noted for mute swans at the Abbotsbury Swannery and for little terns nesting on the shingle. Ian McEwan's 2007 novella On Chesil Beach took its setting from the beach.
