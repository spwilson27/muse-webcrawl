Media of Scotland


BBC Scotland's headquarters at Pacific Quay, Glasgow

The media of Scotland comprise a distinct mix of Scottish-produced newspapers, broadcasting, and digital media alongside UK-wide outlets. Scotland has its own editions of British newspapers, its own BBC and ITV services, and a vigorous tradition of national and local journalism.

Newspapers

Scotland's best-known daily newspapers include The Herald (founded 1783, among the world's oldest national titles), The Scotsman (1817), the Daily Record, the Press and Journal in Aberdeen, and The Courier in Dundee. The National, launched in 2014, supports Scottish independence. Scottish editions of UK papers such as The Scottish Sun and the Scottish Daily Mail also circulate widely. Like print media elsewhere, circulations have declined with the shift to digital.

Broadcasting

BBC Scotland provides television and radio from its Pacific Quay headquarters in Glasgow, including the BBC Scotland channel (launched 2019), BBC One Scotland, BBC Radio Scotland, and the Gaelic-language BBC Alba and BBC Radio nan Gàidheal. STV holds the ITV franchises for central and northern Scotland. Commercial and community radio stations, and the Gaelic media service MG Alba, complete the landscape. Broadcasting is reserved to Westminster, though its role in Scottish political debate — particularly around the 2014 independence referendum — has been intensely discussed.

Magazines and digital

Scotland publishes magazines such as The Big Issue Scotland (a separate edition), Holyrood magazine for politics, and many online-only news sites, while Scottish publishers produce books in English, Scots, and Scottish Gaelic.
