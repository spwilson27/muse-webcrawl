Restless legs syndrome

Restless legs syndrome (RLS), also known as Willis–Ekbom disease, is a generally long-term neurological disorder that causes an overwhelming urge to move one's legs. There is often an unpleasant feeling in the legs that improves somewhat by moving them. The feelings generally happen when at rest, and therefore can make it hard to sleep.

Signs and symptoms

The sensations are described as aching, throbbing, pulling, itching, or crawling. Symptoms occur primarily at night, are worse in the evening, and are temporarily relieved by movement such as walking or stretching. Periodic limb movements during sleep frequently accompany the condition.

Causes

The cause is generally unknown but is believed to result from brain iron deficiency and dysfunction of the dopaminergic system. RLS often runs in families, and genome-wide association studies have identified risk variants in genes such as MEIS1 and BTBD9. Secondary causes include iron deficiency, kidney failure, pregnancy, and certain medications such as antidepressants and antihistamines.

Treatment

Treatment includes lifestyle changes, treating underlying causes such as iron deficiency, and submission to various medications such as alpha-2-delta calcium channel ligands (gabapentin, pregabalin) and dopamine agonists. Alcohol and sleep deprivation can trigger or worsen symptoms and are best avoided.
