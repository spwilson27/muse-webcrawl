William F. Dean

William Frishe Dean Sr. (August 1, 1899 – August 24, 1981) was a United States Army major general during the Korean War. He commanded the 24th Infantry Division at the outbreak of the war and led its delaying action at the Battle of Taejon in July 1950, during which he was captured — becoming the highest-ranking American prisoner of the war. He received the Medal of Honor for his conduct in the battle.

Military career

Dean was commissioned in the infantry in 1923 after joining the California National Guard and graduating from the University of California, Berkeley. In the Second World War he served as assistant division commander and then commander of the 44th Infantry Division in France and Germany, earning the Distinguished Service Cross.

In 1950 Dean's 24th Infantry Division, on occupation duty in Japan, was the first American division rushed to Korea. Understrength and ill-equipped, it fought a series of delaying actions to buy time for reinforcements to reach the Pusan Perimeter. At Taejon (Daejeon) on 20 July 1950, Dean personally led tank-hunting teams against North Korean armour before being wounded and separated from his command; he evaded capture for 35 days before being taken prisoner. Presumed dead, he was awarded the Medal of Honor; he spent three years as a prisoner of war and was released in September 1953 after the armistice.

Dean retired in 1955 and published his memoir, General Dean's Story (1954). He is buried at San Francisco National Cemetery.
