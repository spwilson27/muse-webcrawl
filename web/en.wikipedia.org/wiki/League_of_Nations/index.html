League of Nations

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The League of Nations was the first worldwide intergovernmental organisation whose principal mission was to maintain world peace. Founded on 10 January 1920 by the Paris Peace Conference that ended the First World War, it was headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland. The League's Covenant — part of the Treaty of Versailles — bound members to collective security, disarmament and the peaceful settlement of disputes, and created mandates for former colonies, a Permanent Court of International Justice, and agencies for refugees, health and labour (the ILO survives as a UN agency).

History

Championed by US President Woodrow Wilson, the League was weakened from the start by the US Senate's refusal to ratify membership. It scored successes in the 1920s — settling the Åland Islands and Upper Silesia disputes, combating trafficking and disease — but failed against major aggression in the 1930s: Japan's seizure of Manchuria (1931–33), Italy's invasion of Ethiopia (1935–36) and the slide to the Second World War. The Soviet Union was expelled in 1939 for attacking Finland; the League lingered formally until 1946, transferring assets and functions to the United Nations.

Assessment

Historians debate whether the League "failed": it normalised permanent multilateral diplomacy, technical international cooperation and the idea of international accountability, but unanimity rules, absent great powers and unenforceable sanctions doomed its security role — lessons deliberately addressed in the UN Charter's design, including Security Council vetoes to keep great powers inside.
