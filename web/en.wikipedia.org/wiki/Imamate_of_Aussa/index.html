The Imamate of Aussa was an early modern Islamic state of the Afar people in the lower Awash River valley of the Horn of Africa, centred on the oasis town of Aussa. It emerged after the decline of the Adal Sultanate in the later 16th century, when Afar groups migrating into the Awash valley organised themselves under religious leaders titled Imam.

History

Tradition credits the imamate's formation to around 1577, as Afar pastoralists settled the fertile riverine plains. The imams of Aussa controlled caravan routes between the Ethiopian highlands and the Red Sea coast at ports such as Tadjoura, levying tolls on the salt and slave trades. The imamate was chronically weakened by succession disputes and feuding among Afar clan lineages (the Mudaito and rival groups).

In 1672 the last imam, Umar Din bin Adam, was overthrown by the Mudaito clan, which established the Sultanate of Aussa under Kadafo; the sultanate endured, in reduced form, into the 20th century.
