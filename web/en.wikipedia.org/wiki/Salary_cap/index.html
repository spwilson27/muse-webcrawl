Salary cap

Salary cap
Used byNFL, NBA, NHL, MLS and other leagues
PurposeCost control and competitive balance

In professional sports, a salary cap (or wage cap) is an agreement or rule that places a limit on the amount of money that a team can spend on players' salaries. It exists as a per-player limit or per-team limit, and is a league-wide figure agreed under a collective bargaining agreement.

Rationale

Salary caps are intended to keep costs under control and to maintain competitive balance by preventing wealthier teams from signing far more top talent than poorer rivals. Supporters argue they make leagues more exciting and financially stable; critics argue they suppress players' earnings and transfer money from players to owners.

Hard and soft caps

A hard cap cannot be exceeded for any reason (as in the NFL and NHL). A soft cap, such as the NBA's, contains exceptions that allow teams to exceed the threshold in defined circumstances — for example to re-sign their own players (the "Larry Bird exception") — and may pair the cap with a luxury tax paid on payroll above a higher threshold. Leagues may also impose a salary floor requiring minimum team spending.

Examples

  • NFL — hard cap introduced in 1994; the 2024 cap was about US$255 million per team.
  • NBA — soft cap since 1984–85, with a luxury tax and, from 2023, "apron" restrictions.
  • NHL — hard cap adopted after the 2004–05 lockout.
  • MLS — salary budget with Designated Player exceptions.
  • Rugby and Australian rules — caps in Super Rugby, the NRL and AFL.
