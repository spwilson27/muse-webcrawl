The NCAA Division II football championship is an American college football tournament played annually to determine the champion of NCAA Division II. It was first held in 1973, when the NCAA reorganised its divisions and replaced the College Division. Louisiana Tech won the inaugural title.

Format

The championship is decided by a single-elimination playoff. The field, expanded over the decades from 8 to 16, 24 and most recently 28 teams, is drawn from four super regions; conference champions and at-large selections qualify, and the top seeds receive byes and home games in the early rounds. Since 2018 the championship game has been played at the McKinney ISD Stadium in McKinney, Texas; earlier long-term homes included Florence, Alabama, and Kansas City, Kansas.

Champions

Northwest Missouri State has won a record six championships (1998, 1999, 2009, 2013, 2015, 2016). Other multiple champions include Grand Valley State (4), Valdosta State (4), North Dakota State (5, before moving up to Division I FCS) and North Alabama (3). Ferris State won three consecutive titles in 2021, 2022 and 2024, while Harding won its first championship in 2023.

Before the playoff era, Division II champions were crowned through polls and regional bowl games; the Camellia Bowl served as the western playoff final in the 1960s and early 1970s.
