National Capital Region (Japan)

The National Capital Region (Japanese: 首都圏, Shuto-ken) is the metropolitan region surrounding Tokyo, Japan, defined under the National Capital Region Development Act of 1956. It comprises Tokyo Metropolis and the seven surrounding prefectures of Saitama, Chiba, Kanagawa, Ibaraki, Tochigi, Gunma and Yamanashi. With a population of about 44 million, it is the largest metropolitan region in the world by population and the core of Japan's economy, politics and culture.

Definition and composition

Japanese law uses several overlapping definitions of the Tokyo area. The National Capital Region is the broadest official planning unit, used for national land-use and infrastructure planning. Narrower definitions include the "Tokyo metropolitan area" of Tokyo and the three adjacent prefectures of Saitama, Chiba and Kanagawa (sometimes called the Greater Tokyo Area in international usage), and the built-up urban employment area centred on the special wards of Tokyo. The region includes the major cities of Yokohama, Kawasaki, Chiba and Saitama, as well as the ports of Tokyo Bay.

Planning and development

The 1956 Act and successive National Capital Region Development Plans sought to manage explosive post-war growth through green belts (largely never realised), new towns such as Tama New Town and Tsukuba Science City, suburban railway development, and the dispersal of government functions. The region's economy accounts for roughly a third of Japan's GDP. Persistent policy debates concern over-concentration of population and functions in Tokyo (Tokyo ikkyoku shūchū), earthquake risk on the Kantō plain, and proposals to relocate some national institutions outside the capital.

Transport

The region is served by one of the world's most extensive rail networks, centred on the Yamanote loop line and radiating JR and private railway lines, two international airports (Haneda and Narita), and expressway rings including the Ken-Ō and Tokyo Gaikan motorways. The Port of Tokyo, Yokohama and Chiba form a combined port complex among the busiest in Asia.
