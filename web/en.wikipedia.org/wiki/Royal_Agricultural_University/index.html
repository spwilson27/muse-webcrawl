Royal Agricultural University

The Royal Agricultural University (RAU) is a public university in Cirencester, Gloucestershire, England. Founded in 1845 as the Royal Agricultural College, it was the first agricultural college in the English-speaking world and received its royal charter from Queen Victoria the same year; it gained full university status in 2013.

History and campus

The college was established to train landowners and farmers in scientific agriculture. Its Gothic Revival main building stands on a campus at the edge of Cirencester, with university farms and the Rural Innovation Centre nearby. Patronage has traditionally come from the monarch; King Charles III is the university's president.

Academics

The RAU teaches agriculture, food production, land management, equine science, agribusiness and rural real estate to around a thousand students. It is known for strong links to the land-based professions and for its Innovation Village development supporting agri-tech start-ups.
