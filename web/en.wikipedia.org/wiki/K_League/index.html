K League

The K League (Korean: K리그) is South Korea's professional association football league system, operated by the Korea Professional Football League. It consists of the top-flight K League 1 and the second-tier K League 2, between which clubs are promoted and relegated.

The league was founded in 1983 as the Korean Super League, making it one of Asia's oldest professional football leagues. It adopted the K League name in 1997–98 and introduced a two-division structure in 2013. Historically successful clubs include Jeonbuk Hyundai Motors, FC Seoul, Pohang Steelers and Ulsan HD.

K League clubs have won more AFC Champions League titles than those of any other nation, and the league is regularly ranked among the strongest in Asia.
