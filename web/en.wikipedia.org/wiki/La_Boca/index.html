La Boca is a neighbourhood (barrio) of Buenos Aires, Argentina, located in the city's southeast near the old port, at the mouth (Spanish: boca) of the Riachuelo River. It is famous for its colourful houses, its association with the tango, and as the home of the football club Boca Juniors.

History

La Boca grew up around the port and was settled heavily by Italian immigrants, particularly from Genoa, in the late 19th century. Dockworkers and labourers built houses of corrugated metal and wood, painting them with leftover ship paint in bright colours — a tradition that defines the district's appearance today. The neighbourhood was a centre of working-class politics and the birthplace of the painter Benito Quinquela Martín, who championed its preservation.

Caminito and culture

The pedestrian street Caminito, an open-air museum of coloured buildings, tango dancers, and artists' stalls, is one of Buenos Aires' best-known tourist attractions; its name comes from a 1926 tango composed by Juan de Dios Filiberto. The district's stadium, La Bombonera (Estadio Alberto J. Armando), home of Boca Juniors, is another major landmark.
