Malolos, officially the City of Malolos (Filipino: Lungsod ng Malolos), is a component city and the capital of the province of Bulacan, Philippines. According to the 2020 census, it has a population of about 262,000 people. Malolos is best known in Philippine history as the capital of the First Philippine Republic in 1898–1899 and the site of the Malolos Congress, which promulgated the Malolos Constitution.

History

Founded as a Spanish colonial town in the 16th century, Malolos became the seat of the revolutionary government of Emilio Aguinaldo in September 1898 after the capital was moved from Bacoor, Cavite. The revolutionary congress met at Barasoain Church, where it ratified the declaration of independence and adopted the Malolos Constitution on 21 January 1899; the First Philippine Republic was inaugurated two days later. American forces captured Malolos in March 1899 during the Philippine–American War. The Women of Malolos, a group of twenty young women who petitioned in 1888 for permission to open a night school to learn Spanish, were praised by José Rizal in a famous letter.

Landmarks

Barasoain Church, sometimes called the "Cradle of Democracy in the East", is the city's most famous landmark; its image appeared on the Philippine ten-peso bill. Other sites include the Casa Real Shrine, which housed the revolutionary printing press, and Malolos Cathedral.
