Project Gutenberg

Project Gutenberg (PG) is a volunteer effort to digitize and archive cultural works, providing free electronic books (eBooks) whose copyright has expired in the United States. Founded by Michael S. Hart in 1971, it is the oldest digital library. Its collection comprises tens of thousands of titles — over 70,000 free eBooks — available in formats such as plain text, HTML, EPUB, and Kindle-compatible files.

History

Hart began the project on December 4, 1971, by typing the United States Declaration of Independence into a mainframe computer at the University of Illinois, making it available to other users of the network — an early instance of sharing texts electronically. The project grew slowly at first, as volunteers typed and later scanned and proofread texts; the 10,000th title was released in 2003, and growth accelerated with optical character recognition and distributed proofreading.

Collection and copyright

The library focuses on works in the public domain under U.S. copyright law, including classics of Western literature, and includes audiobooks and texts in many languages; affiliated partner projects operate in other countries. Texts are proofread by volunteers, including through Distributed Proofreaders, founded in 2000. Hart led the project until his death in 2011; it is administered by the Project Gutenberg Literary Archive Foundation.

Influence

Project Gutenberg pioneered the free eBook and influenced later initiatives for open access to cultural heritage, while copyright term extensions have limited the flow of newer works into the public domain it draws upon.
