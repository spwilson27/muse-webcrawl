Set (also spelled Seth, Setesh, or Sutekh) is a god of deserts, storms, disorder, violence, and foreigners in ancient Egyptian religion. He is usually depicted as a man with the head of the mysterious "Set animal" — a creature with a curved snout, tall square-tipped ears, and a forked tail that does not match any known animal. Set was the son of the earth god Geb and the sky goddess Nut, and brother of Osiris, Isis, and his wife Nephthys.

Mythology

In the Osiris myth, Set murders his brother Osiris — in one version drowning him in a coffer, in another dismembering his body and scattering the pieces across Egypt — and seizes his throne. Osiris's son Horus contends with Set for the kingship in a long series of contests, recounted in texts such as The Contendings of Horus and Set; the gods eventually award the throne to Horus, while Set is assigned a role as defender of the sun god Ra, standing at the prow of Ra's barque to spear the serpent Apophis each night. Set's violence was thus ambivalent: dangerous and chaotic, yet harnessed to protect cosmic order.

Cult and history

Set was worshipped from Egypt's earliest periods and was patron of Upper Egypt in the archaic dual kingship. The Hyksos rulers identified him with their storm god Baal and made Avaris his cult centre; Ramesside pharaohs, whose family came from the eastern Delta, honoured him prominently, and kings bore names such as Seti ("man of Set") and Setnakhte. From the Third Intermediate Period onward, as Egypt suffered foreign invasions associated in myth with Set, he was increasingly demonised: statues were defaced, his animals persecuted, and in some late texts he was equated with the Greek monster Typhon. In the Greco-Roman world, "Seth" also appears in Gnostic texts as a distinct figure, a son of Adam.
