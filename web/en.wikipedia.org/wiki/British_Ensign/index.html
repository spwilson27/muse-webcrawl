British Ensign

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The British ensigns are the flags flown by British ships and establishments, distinguished by the colour of their field: the Red Ensign, the Blue Ensign and the White Ensign. Each carries the Union Flag in the canton (the upper hoist-side quarter). The system, settled by an Order in Council of 1864, allocates the plain White Ensign to the Royal Navy, the plain Blue Ensign to government (state) vessels, and the plain Red Ensign to the merchant navy and private vessels.

History

British naval ensigns originated in the 17th century, when English fleets were divided into squadrons distinguished by red, white and blue ensigns bearing the cross of St George in the canton; after the Union of 1603 and 1707, and again in 1801, the canton was replaced successively by the combined Union Flag. Until 1864 the three colours marked the rank of the admiral commanding a squadron rather than distinct services — a survival of this system is the Royal Navy's order of seniority of the colours (white, red, blue). The 1864 reform regularised usage: the White Ensign became exclusively naval, the Blue Ensign was assigned to ships in public service, and the Red Ensign to the mercantile marine, becoming affectionately known as the "Red Duster."

Defaced ensigns

Many organisations and territories fly ensigns defaced with a badge or emblem in the fly. Defaced Blue Ensigns are used by numerous British government departments and were historically the basis of most British colonial flags; many present-day flags of Commonwealth realms and former colonies — including those of Australia and New Zealand — derive from the defaced Blue Ensign. Defaced Red Ensigns form the civil ensigns of several British overseas territories and Crown dependencies, and are used by organisations such as Trinity House, the Royal National Lifeboat Institution and many yacht clubs holding Admiralty warrants. The Royal Air Force has its own sky-blue ensign, charged with the RAF roundel in the fly.

Use today

British merchant ships are required to fly the Red Ensign (or a permitted defaced version). The White Ensign is flown by Royal Navy warships and shore establishments, and by the Royal Yacht Squadron. Special ensigns are granted by warrant to members of certain yacht clubs. In vexillological terms the British ensigns are among the most influential flag designs in the world, having generated a large "family" of national and regional flags across the former British Empire.
