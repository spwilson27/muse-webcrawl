Dukkha

Dukkha (Pali: dukkha; Sanskrit: duḥkha) is a fundamental concept in Buddhism, commonly translated as "suffering," "pain," "unsatisfactoriness" or "stress." It refers to the inherent unsatisfactoriness and pain of mundane existence (samsara). The Buddha taught that dukkha arises from craving (tanha) and can be ended (nirodha) by following the Noble Eightfold Path — the teaching known as the Four Noble Truths.

Meaning

Dukkha is broader than ordinary pain. Buddhist texts analyse it in three categories: the dukkha of physical and mental suffering (dukkha-dukkha), the dukkha of change (viparinama-dukkha) — pleasant states are impermanent — and the dukkha of conditioned existence (sankhara-dukkha), the pervasive unsatisfactoriness of all conditioned phenomena.

Four Noble Truths

The Buddha's first sermon set out the Four Noble Truths: there is dukkha; dukkha has an origin (craving); dukkha has a cessation (nirvana); and there is a path to its cessation. Understanding dukkha fully — "suffering should be fully understood" — is the first task in the path to liberation.
