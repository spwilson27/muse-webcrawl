Quine–Putnam indispensability argument

The Quine–Putnam indispensability argument is an argument in the philosophy of mathematics for the existence of abstract mathematical objects such as numbers and sets. Associated with W. V. O. Quine and Hilary Putnam, it holds that because mathematics is indispensable to our best scientific theories, and because we ought to believe in the entities indispensable to those theories, we ought to believe in mathematical entities.

The argument

The argument rests on two Quinean doctrines: confirmational holism (scientific theories are confirmed as wholes, so the mathematics embedded in them shares in their confirmation) and ontological commitment (a theory commits us to whatever must exist for the theory to be true, as revealed by its quantifiers). Putnam presented the argument as the only serious ground for mathematical realism that does not rest on intuition.

Objections

Nominalists have replied in several ways: Hartry Field's Science Without Numbers attempted to reformulate Newtonian physics without quantifying over mathematical objects; others challenge holism, deny that indispensability confers ontological commitment, or develop fictionalist accounts on which mathematical statements can be useful without being literally true. The argument remains a central point of contention between Platonists and nominalists.
