Vivien Leigh

Vivien Leigh (1913–1967), born Vivian Mary Hartley in Darjeeling, India, was a British actress who won two Academy Awards for Best Actress: as Scarlett O'Hara in Gone with the Wind (1939) and as Blanche DuBois in A Streetcar Named Desire (1951).

Career

Leigh achieved stage success in London before worldwide fame as Scarlett after an exhaustive casting search. She was married to Laurence Olivier (1940–1960), with whom she frequently performed, including celebrated Shakespeare productions. Her other notable films include Waterloo Bridge (1940), That Hamilton Woman (1941), Caesar and Cleopatra (1945), Anna Karenina (1948) and The Roman Spring of Mrs. Stone (1961).

Health and death

Much of Leigh's later life was overshadowed by bipolar disorder and recurrent tuberculosis, which caused her death in London in 1967, aged 53. She remains regarded as one of the great screen and stage actresses of the twentieth century.
