Tarsal tunnel syndrome

Tarsal tunnel syndrome (TTS) is a compression neuropathy caused by entrapment of the tibial nerve as it passes through the tarsal tunnel on the inside of the ankle, beneath the flexor retinaculum. It is analogous to carpal tunnel syndrome of the wrist, though considerably less common.

Symptoms

Patients report aching, burning, tingling or numbness along the inner ankle and the sole of the foot, often worse after prolonged standing or at night. Tapping over the nerve behind the medial malleolus may reproduce the symptoms (Tinel's sign).

Causes

Anything that reduces space within the tunnel can compress the nerve: ganglion cysts, lipomas, enlarged veins, tenosynovitis, ankle fractures, severe flat feet (overpronation) or systemic conditions such as diabetes and rheumatoid arthritis. In many cases no clear cause is found.

Treatment

Conservative measures — rest, ice, anti-inflammatory medication, shoe modification and custom orthotics — are tried first. Persistent cases may need corticosteroid injection or surgical decompression by division of the flexor retinaculum, with better results when a specific space-occupying lesion is present.
