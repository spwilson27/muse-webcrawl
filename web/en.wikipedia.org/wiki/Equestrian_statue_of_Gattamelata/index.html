Equestrian statue of Gattamelata

Artist: Donatello
Year: c. 1447–1453
Medium: Bronze
Location: Piazza del Santo, Padua

The equestrian statue of Gattamelata is a bronze sculpture by the Italian early Renaissance artist Donatello, erected in the Piazza del Santo in Padua, Italy, in honour of the Venetian condottiero Erasmo da Narni, better known as Gattamelata ("Honey-Cat"). Completed around 1453, it was the first full-size equestrian statue cast since antiquity.

Description

The monument stands about 3.4 metres high atop an 8-metre pedestal designed to resemble a sepulchral chamber. Gattamelata sits calmly on a war horse, wearing classical-style armour and holding a commander's baton; the horse's raised foreleg rests on a cannonball. In contrast to the heroic antique model — the statue of Marcus Aurelius in Rome — the figure conveys quiet psychological realism rather than triumph.

Significance

The statue was a landmark of Renaissance sculpture: the largest bronze casting of its time, it revived the classical genre of the equestrian monument and directly inspired Verrocchio's later monument to Bartolomeo Colleoni in Venice. Its commission by the heirs of a mercenary captain, and its placement near the tomb of Saint Anthony, reflected the status condottieri had achieved in Italian society.
