Chagatai Khan

Chagatai Khan (c. 1183 – 1242) was the second son of Genghis Khan and Börte. He inherited the lands of Central Asia — roughly modern Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Xinjiang — which became the Chagatai Khanate.

Life

Chagatai campaigned with his father in the conquest of Khwarezmia, taking part in the sieges of Otrar, Bukhara and Samarkand. Known as a strict guardian of Mongol customary law, the Yassa, he quarrelled with his brother Jochi and opposed the succession of Ögedei before accepting it. He advised Ögedei as Khan and administered his ulus from near Almaliq, remaining notably resistant to conversion to Islam while ruling many Muslim subjects.

Legacy

The Chagatai Khanate endured, in split eastern and western forms, for centuries, and the Chagatai language — a Turkic literary language of Central Asia — bears his name.
