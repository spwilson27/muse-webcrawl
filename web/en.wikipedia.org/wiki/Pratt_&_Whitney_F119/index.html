Pratt & Whitney F119

The Pratt & Whitney F119 is an afterburning turbofan engine developed by Pratt & Whitney for the United States Air Force's fifth-generation fighter, the Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptor. Two F119-PW-100 engines power each F-22, providing more than 35,000 pounds (156 kN) of thrust each in afterburner and enabling the aircraft to supercruise — to fly supersonically without afterburner — at speeds above Mach 1.5.

Design and development

The F119 was developed under the Advanced Tactical Fighter programme, competing against the General Electric YF120; the Pratt & Whitney YF119 was selected in 1991 alongside the Lockheed YF-22. The engine features a three-stage fan driven by a single-stage low-pressure turbine and a six-stage compressor driven by a single-stage high-pressure turbine, with counter-rotating spools. Bladed disks ("blisks") reduce part count and weight, and the engine is controlled by a dual-redundant full authority digital engine control (FADEC).

Thrust vectoring

The F119's rectangular two-dimensional convergent-divergent nozzle can vector thrust up to 20 degrees in pitch, contributing to the F-22's exceptional manoeuvrability. The nozzle design also reduces infrared and radar signatures, supporting the aircraft's low observability. The F119 served as the basis for the F135 engine that powers the F-35 Lightning II.
