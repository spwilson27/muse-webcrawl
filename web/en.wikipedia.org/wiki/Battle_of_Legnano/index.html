The Battle of Legnano was fought on 29 May 1176 near the town of Legnano in Lombardy, between the army of the Holy Roman Emperor Frederick I Barbarossa and the forces of the Lombard League, an alliance of northern Italian cities led by Milan. The League's victory forced Barbarossa to abandon his attempt to subdue the Lombard communes and to recognize their autonomy in the Peace of Constance (1183).

Background

Frederick's repeated Italian expeditions aimed to restore imperial authority over the wealthy Lombard cities, whose privileges had been asserted against him at the Diet of Roncaglia (1158). After the destruction of Milan in 1162, the cities formed the Lombard League in 1167, rebuilt Milan, and founded the fortress city of Alessandria, named for Pope Alexander III, Frederick's opponent in the imperial-papal struggle.

The battle

During his fifth Italian expedition, Frederick, reinforced by knights from Germany who had crossed the Alps by the Splügen Pass, moved toward the Ticino. Lombard forces advanced to block him and met the imperial army near Legnano. The Lombard cavalry was driven off in the first clash, and the imperial knights pressed against the League's infantry, drawn up around the carroccio — the sacred war-cart bearing Milan's standard and altar. The infantry, including the Milanese company later celebrated as the Company of Death, held its ground; returning Lombard cavalry from Brescia and Milan then struck the imperial army, which broke. Frederick himself was unhorsed and for a time believed dead, reappearing at Pavia days later.

Aftermath and legacy

Defeated, Frederick negotiated: the Peace of Venice (1177) reconciled him with the Pope, and the Peace of Constance (1183) granted the Lombard cities extensive rights of self-government while preserving nominal imperial sovereignty. Legnano became an enduring symbol of Italian civic resistance and is celebrated in Verdi's opera La battaglia di Legnano and in a line of the Italian national anthem ("dall'Alpi a Sicilia dovunque è Legnano" — "from the Alps to Sicily, Legnano is everywhere").
