Canadian Soccer Association

The Canadian Soccer Association (French: Association canadienne de soccer), operating as Canada Soccer, is the governing body of soccer in Canada. Founded in 1912 and headquartered in Ottawa, it is a member of FIFA and of CONCACAF, and oversees the men's and women's national teams, futsal and beach soccer, coach and referee development, and domestic competitions including the Canadian Championship.

History

The association was founded in Winnipeg on May 24, 1912, as the Dominion of Canada Football Association, uniting provincial associations that had formed over the preceding decades. It joined FIFA in 1913, withdrew in 1928 during the international dispute over broken-time payments to amateur players, and rejoined in 1948. It was a founding member of CONCACAF in 1961.

National teams

The men's national team qualified for the FIFA World Cup in 1986 and 2022, and Canada co-hosts the 2026 FIFA World Cup with the United States and Mexico. The women's national team is one of the program's greatest successes, winning bronze medals at the 2012 and 2016 Olympics and the gold medal at the Tokyo 2020 Olympic Games, in addition to CONCACAF championships in 1998 and 2010.

Domestic competitions

Canada Soccer sanctions the Canadian Championship, the cup competition contested by professional clubs including teams from Major League Soccer and the Canadian Premier League, which launched in 2019. It also runs national amateur championships at several age levels and the Futsal Canadian Championship.

Governance

The association is governed by a board of directors and works through provincial and territorial member associations. Its funding and labour relations, including negotiations with the national-team players' associations, have been subjects of public scrutiny in the 2020s.
