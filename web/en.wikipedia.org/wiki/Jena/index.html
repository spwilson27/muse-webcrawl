Jena (German pronunciation: [ˈjeːna]) is a city in the German state of Thuringia, situated on the river Saale. With a population of about 110,000, it is the second largest city in Thuringia after Erfurt. Jena is best known as a centre of the optical and precision engineering industries and for its university, the Friedrich Schiller University, founded in 1558.

History

Jena was first mentioned in a document in 1182. In the late 13th century it received town rights. The University of Jena was founded in 1558 by Elector John Frederick I of Saxony and became one of Germany's leading universities; around 1800 it was a centre of German idealism and early Romanticism, associated with figures including Fichte, Schelling, Hegel and Schiller. In October 1806 the Battle of Jena–Auerstedt, fought nearby, saw Napoleon's forces decisively defeat Prussia.

Industry and science

In 1846 Carl Zeiss founded his optical workshop in Jena; with Ernst Abbe and Otto Schott the city became a world centre for microscopes, glass and precision optics. The Carl Zeiss and Schott companies remain major employers, alongside the Jenoptik group. The JenTower, a 159-metre cylindrical high-rise completed in 1972, is the city's landmark.

Education

The Friedrich Schiller University Jena enrols around 18,000 students, and the Ernst Abbe University of Applied Sciences adds several thousand more. Jena also hosts numerous research institutes, including Max Planck and Fraunhofer institutes, and is sometimes called a "city of science" (Stadt der Wissenschaft, a title it held in 2008).
