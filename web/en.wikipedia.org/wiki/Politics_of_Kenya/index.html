Politics of Kenya

Parliament Buildings, Nairobi

The politics of Kenya take place in a framework of a presidential republic, in which the President of Kenya is both head of state and head of government. The constitution of 2010 created a devolved system of government with 47 counties, a bicameral parliament consisting of the National Assembly and the Senate, and an independent judiciary headed by the Supreme Court.

History

Kenya became independent from Britain in 1963 under Jomo Kenyatta. His successor, Daniel arap Moi, ruled from 1978 to 2002, much of it under a de jure one-party system. Multi-party politics returned in the 1990s, and the 2002 election brought Mwai Kibaki to power. The disputed 2007 presidential election triggered widespread violence in which more than 1,100 people were killed; a power-sharing government was formed with Raila Odinga as prime minister. The crisis prompted the new constitution of 2010, designed to disperse power and protect rights.

Recent politics

Uhuru Kenyatta, son of the founding president, won the 2013 and 2017 elections; the Supreme Court's nullification of the 2017 result—the first such ruling in Africa—was a landmark for judicial independence. In 2022 William Ruto won the presidency on a "hustler" populist platform promising a bottom-up economic model. His government faced major youth-led protests in 2024 against proposed tax increases, which forced withdrawal of the finance bill and a reshuffle of the cabinet to include opposition figures.

Elections

General elections are held every five years for the presidency, parliament, and county governments. Kenyan politics is strongly shaped by ethnic coalition-building, and parties tend to form as alliances around leading personalities rather than durable ideological organisations.
