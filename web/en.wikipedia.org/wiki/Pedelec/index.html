Pedelec

A pedelec with a down-tube battery and hub motor.

A pedelec (from pedal electric cycle) is a type of electric bicycle in which the electric motor provides assistance only while the rider is pedalling. When the rider stops pedalling, the motor cuts out, distinguishing pedelecs from e-bikes controlled by a throttle. In many jurisdictions, pedelecs that meet certain power and speed limits are legally classified as ordinary bicycles rather than motor vehicles.

Technical characteristics

Pedelecs use a torque, cadence, or speed sensor—commonly located in the bottom bracket, crankset, or rear hub—to detect pedalling and modulate motor output accordingly. Motors may be mounted in the front or rear wheel hub or centrally at the crank (mid-drive). Most use rechargeable lithium-ion batteries mounted on the frame or rack.

Regulation

In the European Union, under Regulation (EU) No 168/2013 and the EN 15194 standard, a pedelec whose motor has a maximum continuous rated power of 250 watts and whose assistance cuts off at 25 km/h (15.5 mph) is treated as a bicycle: no licence, insurance, or helmet mandate applies in most member states. Faster "speed pedelecs", with assistance up to 45 km/h, are classified as mopeds in most jurisdictions. Similar distinctions exist in other countries, although rules vary widely.

Usage

Pedelecs became the dominant form of electric bicycle in Europe, particularly in Germany and the Netherlands, where they account for a large and growing share of bicycle sales. They are widely used for commuting, cargo carrying, and recreational riding, and studies associate them with increased cycling distances among older riders.
