Yellow Magic Orchestra

OriginTokyo, Japan
GenresElectronic, synth-pop, technopop
Years active1978–1984, 1992–1993, 2007–present

Yellow Magic Orchestra (YMO) is a Japanese electronic music band formed in Tokyo in 1978 by Haruomi Hosono (bass, keyboards, vocals), Ryuichi Sakamoto (keyboards, vocals), and Yukihiro Takahashi (drums, lead vocals, occasional keyboards). Alongside Kraftwerk, the band is widely considered a pioneer of electronic music and a foundational influence on synth-pop, techno, hip hop, and video game music.

History

Hosono, already well known in Japan for his work with Happy End and as a session bassist, conceived the project as a satirical take on exotica, using then-new synthesizer and sampler technology to rework the "oriental" clichés of Western pop. The trio's self-titled debut album (1978) featured a computer-game-inspired cover of Martin Denny's "Firecracker" and became a major hit in Japan; a remixed US release followed in 1979.

The 1979 album Solid State Survivor sold over two million copies and yielded the hits "Technopolis," "Rydeen," and a cover of the Beatles' "Day Tripper." YMO toured the United States and Europe in 1979 and 1980, performing on television programs such as Soul Train. Their 1981 album BGM is notable as one of the first releases to use the Roland TR-808 Rhythm Composer, which became a cornerstone of hip hop and dance music production.

Influence

YMO's use of loops, samples, and programmed rhythms directly influenced Afrika Bambaataa's electro-funk classic "Planet Rock," the Detroit techno pioneers Juan Atkins and Derrick May, and ambient house acts such as The Orb. Their video game-inspired sounds — Takahashi and Sakamoto famously sampled arcade sounds on the debut album — anticipated the chiptune genre. The members also maintained celebrated solo careers: Sakamoto won an Academy Award for the score of The Last Emperor (1987).
