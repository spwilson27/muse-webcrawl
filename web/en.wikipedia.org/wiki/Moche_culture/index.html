Moche culture

Periodc. 100–800 CE
RegionNorth coast of Peru
Major sitesHuacas del Sol y de la Luna, Sipán, El Brujo
Preceded bySalasaca / Vicús traditions
Followed byWari influence; Lambayeque (Sicán)

The Moche culture (also called Mochica) flourished on the arid north coast of present-day Peru from about 100 to 800 CE. Organised in a series of valley-based polities rather than a single empire, the Moche built monumental adobe pyramid-platforms (huacas), extensive irrigation systems, and produced some of the most accomplished ceramics and metalwork of the ancient Americas.

Art and society

Moche stirrup-spout vessels include lifelike portrait heads of individuals, as well as scenes of warfare, ritual, hunting and daily life. Fine-line painted ceramics depict a ceremonial narrative known as the Sacrifice Ceremony or Presentation Theme. Elite burials such as the tomb of the Lord of Sipán (excavated 1987) and the priestess known as the Lady of Cao revealed gold, silver and copper regalia of extraordinary richness.

Decline

From the later 6th century, severe El Niño floods and droughts, together with internal pressures, contributed to political fragmentation and the abandonment of major centres by about 800 CE.
