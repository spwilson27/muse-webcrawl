Stemmatics

Stemmatics (also stemmatology) is an approach to textual criticism that reconstructs the relationships among surviving manuscript witnesses of a text in order to recover the lost original (archetype). The result is drawn as a family tree called a stemma codicum.

Method

The method, associated with Karl Lachmann, proceeds by comparing witnesses and identifying shared errors (errores coniunctivi) that must descend from a common ancestor copy. Witnesses derived entirely from another surviving witness are eliminated (eliminatio codicum descriptorum), and the archetype is reconstructed from the agreements of the independent branches, with the editor's judgement resolving the remaining variants (examinatio and emendatio).

Modern developments

Contamination — copyists consulting more than one exemplar — complicates strict stemmatics, and since the late twentieth century scholars have adapted computational phylogenetic methods from evolutionary biology to build stemmata automatically.
