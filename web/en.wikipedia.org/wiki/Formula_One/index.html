Formula One (abbreviated F1) is the highest class of international racing for open-wheel single-seater formula racing cars, sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA). A Formula One season consists of a series of races, known as Grands Prix, held on purpose-built circuits and, in a few cases, closed city streets, around the world. Drivers and teams compete for the World Drivers' Championship and World Constructors' Championship respectively.

History

The World Championship began in 1950, with Giuseppe Farina its first champion and Juan Manuel Fangio dominating the 1950s with five titles. Technical revolutions followed: rear-engined cars, wings and aerodynamics, turbocharged engines in the 1980s, and today's hybrid power units (introduced 2014). Ayrton Senna, Alain Prost, Michael Schumacher — who won seven titles, five consecutively with Ferrari — and Lewis Hamilton, who equalled the record of seven championships, define successive eras. Safety improved dramatically after the deaths of Senna and Roland Ratzenberger at Imola in 1994, and modern F1 cars are among the safest racing machines ever built, while also being the fastest regulated road-course cars in the world.

The sport today

Ten teams each field two cars; a season of around 24 races (as of the mid-2020s) spans five continents, including street races in Monaco, Singapore and Las Vegas. Points are awarded to the top ten finishers (25 for a win), and sprint races at selected events add extra points. A budget cap, introduced in 2021, aims to level competition. F1's popularity surged in the United States in the 2020s, helped by the documentary series Drive to Survive.

Technology

F1 cars are extreme machines: hybrid V6 turbo engines plus electric motors produce around 1,000 horsepower, aerodynamic downforce lets them corner at over 5 g, and they can accelerate from 0–100 km/h in about 2.6 seconds. Innovations such as carbon-fibre construction, active suspension (later banned), kinetic energy recovery and the halo head-protection device often begin in F1 and influence road cars.
