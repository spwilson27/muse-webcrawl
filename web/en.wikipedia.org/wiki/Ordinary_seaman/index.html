Ordinary seaman

Department: Deck department

Ranks below: Deckhand (entry)

Ranks above: Able seaman

Abbreviation: OS

An ordinary seaman (OS) is an entry-level rating in the deck department of a merchant ship, ranking above a deckhand or cadet and below an able seaman (AB). The ordinary seaman performs the routine manual work of the vessel under supervision while gaining the sea time and skills required for advancement.

Duties

Duties include chipping, scraping and painting to control corrosion; cleaning decks and accommodation spaces; assisting with mooring lines when arriving and departing port; standing gangway and anchor watches; and helping the boatswain (bosun) with cargo gear and maintenance. Ordinary seamen also form part of emergency parties for firefighting, lifeboat launching and man-overboard response.

Certification

Under the STCW Convention, a seafarer begins with basic safety training and a Rating Forming Part of a Navigational Watch (RFPNW) endorsement; after sufficient sea service — commonly six months to a year in the deck department — an ordinary seaman may qualify as an able seafarer deck (able seaman). In the United States the Coast Guard issues a Merchant Mariner Credential with the ordinary seaman endorsement as the usual first entry into the industry.

Historical usage

In the age of sail the Royal Navy rated men as landsman, ordinary seaman or able seaman according to experience: an ordinary seaman was expected to know the ropes and steer but not yet to be fully proficient aloft, the distinction Herman Melville and Richard Henry Dana Jr. described in classic sea narratives such as Two Years Before the Mast.
