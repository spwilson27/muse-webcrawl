Demetrius Poliorcetes

Bust of Demetrius I of Macedon, called Poliorcetes, "the Besieger"

Demetrius I of Macedon (337–283 BC), known as Poliorcetes ("the Besieger of Cities"), was a Macedonian nobleman and military leader who became king of Macedon. The son of Antigonus I Monophthalmus, he was one of the Diadochi, the rival generals who fought over the empire of Alexander the Great after his death.

Career

Demetrius first distinguished himself commanding his father's forces; his defeat at the Battle of Gaza in 312 BC was offset by later victories. In 307 BC he "liberated" Athens from Cassander's garrison and was worshipped there as a god. He won a great naval victory over Ptolemy at the Battle of Salamis in Cyprus in 306 BC, after which Antigonus and Demetrius assumed the royal title. Demetrius earned his nickname for his famous siege of Rhodes (305–304 BC), which failed despite the construction of the enormous siege tower known as the Helepolis.

King of Macedon and fall

After Antigonus was killed at the Battle of Ipsus in 301 BC, Demetrius retained a powerful fleet and possessions in Greece. In 294 BC he seized the throne of Macedon, but his grandiose plans and unpopularity led to his expulsion by Pyrrhus and Lysimachus in 288 BC. Captured by Seleucus in 285 BC, he died in comfortable captivity in Syria in 283 BC. His son Antigonus II Gonatas eventually secured the Macedonian throne for the Antigonid dynasty.
