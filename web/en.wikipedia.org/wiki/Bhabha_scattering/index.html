Bhabha scattering

Leading-order Feynman diagrams for Bhabha scattering: annihilation (left) and scattering (right).

In quantum electrodynamics, Bhabha scattering is the electron–positron scattering process e+ e− → e+ e−, named after the Indian physicist Homi J. Bhabha, who first calculated its cross-section in 1935 using the Dirac equation.

Description

Two tree-level Feynman diagrams contribute to the process: an annihilation diagram, in which the electron and positron annihilate into a virtual photon that re-creates an electron–positron pair (the s-channel), and a scattering diagram, in which they exchange a virtual photon (the t-channel). The interference between the two amplitudes produces an angular distribution strongly peaked at small scattering angles, dominated by the t-channel photon pole.

Applications

Because its rate can be calculated very precisely in QED, small-angle Bhabha scattering is used as the standard process for measuring the luminosity of electron–positron colliders, including LEP, and was employed at many earlier machines. At larger angles, the process is sensitive to weak-interaction effects and to possible new physics, making it a precision test of the Standard Model.
