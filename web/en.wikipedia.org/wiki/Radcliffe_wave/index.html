Radcliffe Wave

The Radcliffe Wave is a neighbouring coherent gaseous structure formed of a series of interacting stellar nurseries in the Milky Way galaxy. It is one of the largest known structures in the galaxy, stretching about 9,000 light-years in length and some 400 light-years in width, and lying a few hundred light-years from the Sun at its closest point.

The structure was announced in January 2020 by astronomers at Harvard University's Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Study (for which it is named) and the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian. It was discovered using three-dimensional dust mapping based on data from the Gaia space observatory. The wave links star-forming regions including the Orion, Perseus, Taurus, and Cepheus molecular cloud complexes in a single undulating filament that rises above and dips below the galactic plane.

Significance

The Radcliffe Wave replaced the older picture of Gould's Belt, a ring-like arrangement of young stars around the Sun, with a long, narrow, wavy filament. Its origin is debated; later studies found the structure is oscillating through the galactic plane like a travelling wave. The Sun is thought to have crossed the wave roughly 13 million years ago.
