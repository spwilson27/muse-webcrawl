5.56×45mm NATO

TypeRifle cartridge
OriginBelgium / United States
Standardized1980 (STANAG 4172)
Parent case.223 Remington

The 5.56×45mm NATO is a rimless, bottlenecked, intermediate rifle cartridge developed in the late 1970s by FN Herstal in Belgium and adopted as the standard service cartridge of NATO forces. It derives from the .223 Remington but is loaded to higher pressures with a heavier bullet in its SS109/M855 form.

History

The cartridge grew out of the U.S. experience with the M16 rifle and its .223 Remington ammunition during the Vietnam War. NATO standardized the Belgian SS109 projectile in 1980, leading to widespread adoption in rifles such as the M16, M4 carbine, FN FNC, and L85.

Characteristics

The small, high-velocity round offers low recoil, flat trajectory, and light weight, allowing soldiers to carry more ammunition. Its terminal effectiveness and performance at long range have been debated, prompting development of enhanced rounds such as the M855A1.
