Hi-hat

A hi-hat is a standard part of a drum kit: a pair of matched cymbals mounted face-to-face on a stand with a foot pedal. Pressing the pedal clashes the cymbals together ("chick"); the drummer can also strike them with a stick while closed, partly open, or fully open for different sounds.

History

It evolved in the 1920s from the low "sock cymbal" pedal as drummers such as Baby Dodds and Jo Jones raised it to playing height, becoming central to jazz timekeeping and later to rock, funk and electronic music, where the open/closed hi-hat pattern is a defining element of house music.

Technique

Sounds range from a tight "ts" closed, through a sloshy half-open "tsh", to a sustained open crash stopped by closing the pedal.
