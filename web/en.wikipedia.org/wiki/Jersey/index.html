Jersey

Official name: Bailiwick of Jersey
Capital: Saint Helier
Status: Crown Dependency
Area: 119.6 km² (46.2 sq mi)
Population: approx. 103,000 (2021)
Currency: Pound sterling; Jersey pound
Languages: English, French, Jèrriais

Jersey, officially the Bailiwick of Jersey, is a self-governing island in the English Channel, the largest of the Channel Islands. It lies about 22 kilometres (14 mi) off the Cotentin Peninsula of France and about 161 km (100 mi) south of mainland England. Although not part of the United Kingdom, Jersey is a Crown Dependency, with the British monarch as head of state, represented by a Lieutenant Governor. Its capital is Saint Helier.

History

Jersey has been inhabited since the Palaeolithic era and was part of the Duchy of Normandy; when William the Conqueror took the English throne in 1066 the island became linked to the English Crown. Although mainland Normandy was lost to France in 1204, the Channel Islands remained with the Crown. Jersey developed its own legal system rooted in Norman customary law and its own parliament, the States Assembly. During the Second World War the island was occupied by German forces from 1940 to 1945, the only part of the British Isles to be occupied.

Economy and governance

Historically agricultural — famous for Jersey Royal potatoes and the Jersey cow — the island's economy is now dominated by financial services, supplemented by tourism. Jersey issues its own banknotes and coins and maintains a distinct tax regime. The island is divided into twelve parishes. Its indigenous Norman language, Jèrriais, is endangered but officially recognised.

Geography

Jersey has a mild oceanic climate and a varied coastline of cliffs, bays, and sandy beaches. Tides in the surrounding waters are among the largest in the world, exposing extensive reefs and sandbanks at low water. The island's wildlife includes seabird colonies on its offshore islets.
