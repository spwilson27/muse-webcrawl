Other Backward Class

Other Backward Class (OBC) is a collective term used by the Government of India to classify castes which are educationally or socially disadvantaged. It is one of several official classifications of the population of India, along with the General category (unreserved), Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. The OBCs were found to comprise a large share of the country's population — the Mandal Commission (1980) estimated 52% — though estimates vary widely.

Reservation

Under India's system of affirmative action ("reservation"), OBCs are entitled to 27% of seats and posts in central government jobs and admissions to central educational institutions. The reservation for OBCs at the central level was recommended by the Mandal Commission, set up in 1979 under B. P. Mandal, and implemented in 1990 by Prime Minister V. P. Singh, triggering nationwide protests and a major political realignment. The Supreme Court upheld the policy in Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992), while directing that the "creamy layer" — the wealthier and more advanced sections within OBCs — be excluded from benefits, and that total reservations generally not exceed 50%.

Lists and administration

There is a Central List of OBCs maintained by the National Commission for Backward Classes, and separate state lists; a caste may be listed as OBC in one state but not another. In 2018 the National Commission for Backward Classes was given constitutional status by the 102nd Amendment. The 103rd Amendment (2019) added a separate 10% reservation for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS) of the general category.

Sub-categorisation debate

Because benefits are unevenly distributed among the thousands of listed communities, there has been sustained debate about sub-categorising OBCs so that the most backward groups receive a fairer share. A commission headed by Justice G. Rohini was appointed in 2017 to examine sub-categorisation of the central OBC list.
