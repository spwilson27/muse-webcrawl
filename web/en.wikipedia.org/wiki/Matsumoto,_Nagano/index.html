Matsumoto (松本市, Matsumoto-shi) is a city in Nagano Prefecture, Japan. Matsumoto is designated as a core city and serves as a commercial centre for the region. It is picturesquely situated in a fertile basin, surrounded on all sides by mountains — the Hida Mountains (Northern Alps) to the west — and is perhaps most famous for Matsumoto Castle, one of Japan's premier historic castles and a listed National Treasure.

History

Matsumoto developed as a castle town of the Ogasawara clan during the Sengoku period; the present castle keep was built in the 1590s under Toyotomi Hideyoshi's rule. In the Edo period it was the seat of Matsumoto Domain. The modern city was founded in 1889 and expanded repeatedly through mergers, most recently absorbing neighbouring villages in 2005 and 2010. The Matsumoto City Museum of Art displays works of the city's most famous artist, Yayoi Kusama, who was born in Matsumoto.

Culture and education

Matsumoto is renowned for music education: the Suzuki method for teaching children music was founded here by Shinichi Suzuki, and the city hosts the Saito Kinen Festival Matsumoto, founded by conductor Seiji Ozawa. It is home to Shinshu University's main campus. The preserved streets of Nakamachi and Nawate-dōri retain the atmosphere of the Edo-period merchant town.

Geography and transport

The city lies in the Matsumoto Basin at an elevation of about 600 metres, at the confluence of the Metoba and Narai rivers. The Kamikōchi highland valley and the Norikura and Utsukushigahara plateaus lie within its borders, making it a gateway for alpine tourism. Matsumoto is served by JR Shinonoi and Ōito line trains, the Nagano Expressway, and Matsumoto Airport.
