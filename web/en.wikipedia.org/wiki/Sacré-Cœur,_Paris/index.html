Sacré-Cœur, Paris

The Basilica of the Sacred Heart of Paris (French: Basilique du Sacré-Cœur de Montmartre), commonly known as Sacré-Cœur, is a Roman Catholic basilica in Paris, France, dedicated to the Sacred Heart of Jesus. It stands at the summit of the butte of Montmartre, the highest point in the city, and is one of the most visited monuments in Paris.

History

The basilica was proposed as a national vow after France's defeat in the Franco-Prussian War and the turmoil of the Paris Commune of 1871. The National Assembly authorised its construction in 1873 as an act of penance and a symbol of hope. Architect Paul Abadie won the design competition; the foundation stone was laid in 1875. Construction, financed largely by private donations, proceeded slowly, and although the building was essentially complete by 1914, its consecration was delayed by the First World War until 1919.

Architecture

Built in Romano-Byzantine style, the basilica is constructed of travertine limestone quarried at Château-Landon, which exudes calcite when wet and so continually bleaches itself a brilliant white. Its silhouette is dominated by a great central dome flanked by smaller domes, rising some 83 metres. Inside, the apse contains one of the world's largest mosaics, Christ in Glory (Christ the King), depicting the resurrected Christ in white garments with arms outstretched. The basilica maintains a perpetual adoration of the Blessed Sacrament, which has continued unbroken since 1885.

Visiting

The basilica is open daily and free to enter; visitors may climb to the dome for a panoramic view of Paris or descend into the crypt. The square before the church, reached by the Funiculaire de Montmartre or by long flights of steps, is a popular gathering place for tourists and street performers.
