The Sting is a 1973 American caper film directed by George Roy Hill and written by David S. Ward. Set in 1936, it follows two professional grifters, Henry Gondorff (Paul Newman) and Johnny Hooker (Robert Redford), who conspire to con a mob boss, Doyle Lonnegan (Robert Shaw), out of a large sum of money. The film reunited Newman and Redford four years after Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid.

Plot and reception

After Lonnegan's men murder their friend Luther Coleman in revenge for a petty scam, Hooker seeks out the veteran Gondorff, who assembles a team to stage an elaborate fake off-track betting parlour — the classic "wire" con — to fleece Lonnegan.

The film was a critical and commercial success and won seven Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Director and Best Original Screenplay. Its soundtrack, arranged by Marvin Hamlisch from Scott Joplin's ragtime compositions, revived popular interest in ragtime; Joplin's "The Entertainer" became a top-ten hit four decades after the composer's death. The film is structured into titled chapters in the style of the Saturday Evening Post illustrations of the era.
