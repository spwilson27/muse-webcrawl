St John's College, Cambridge

St John's College University: University of Cambridge
Founded: 1511
Founder: Lady Margaret Beaufort
Named after: St John the Evangelist
Students: ~1,000 (undergraduates and graduates)
Location: St John's Street, Cambridge

St John's College is a constituent college of the University of Cambridge. It was founded in 1511 by Lady Margaret Beaufort, mother of King Henry VII, on the site of the Hospital of St John the Evangelist (founded c. 1200). The college is one of the largest in Cambridge by area, endowment and student numbers.

The Bridge of Sighs (1831), one of Cambridge's best-known landmarks

Buildings and grounds

The college's courts span both banks of the River Cam, linked by the Bridge of Sighs and the Kitchen Bridge. The Old Library (1624) and the distinctive New Court (1831) on the west bank are among its architectural glories, as is the Victorian chapel designed by Sir George Gilbert Scott.

Notable members

Alumni and fellows include the poet William Wordsworth, abolitionist Thomas Clarkson, prime ministers including Lord Palmerston and Manmohan Singh, and numerous Nobel laureates — among them physicists and economists associated with the college across the 20th century. The college choir, the Choir of St John's College, is internationally renowned.
