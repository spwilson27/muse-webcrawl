Elementary abelian group

The Klein four-group is an elementary abelian group of order 4

In abstract algebra, an elementary abelian group is a finite abelian group in which every nontrivial element has order a prime number p. Equivalently, it is a group in which every element other than the identity has the same prime order. Such a group is sometimes called an elementary abelian p-group.

Structure

By the fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups, every elementary abelian group is a direct product of cyclic groups of order p. Hence an elementary abelian group of order pn is isomorphic to (Z/pZ)n. Conversely, every such direct product is an elementary abelian group.

Vector space structure

An elementary abelian group of order pn can naturally be regarded as an n-dimensional vector space over the finite field Fp = Z/pZ: scalar multiplication is given by repeated addition. Under this correspondence, subgroups correspond to subspaces, and automorphisms of the group correspond to invertible linear transformations, so the automorphism group is the general linear group GL(n, p). This viewpoint makes elementary abelian groups central to representation theory and the study of p-groups.

Examples

  • The Klein four-group V4 ≅ (Z/2Z)2 is the smallest elementary abelian group that is not cyclic.
  • The additive group of the field Fpn is elementary abelian.
  • Every cyclic group of prime order is elementary abelian.
