Attack on Mers-el-Kébir

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The attack on Mers-el-Kébir (Battle of Mers-el-Kébir) on 3 July 1940, during the Second World War, was a British naval attack on French Navy ships moored at the naval base at Mers El Kébir, near Oran in French Algeria. The attack was part of Operation Catapult, a British plan to neutralise or destroy French warships to prevent them falling into German hands after the Franco-German armistice of 22 June 1940.

Background

Britain feared that the French fleet — the fourth largest in the world — might be seized by Germany despite armistice terms promising it would not be used against Britain. Force H presented the French commander with an ultimatum offering options including sailing to British ports, demilitarisation in the French West Indies, or scuttling. Miscommunication and mistrust marred the negotiations, and the full terms were reportedly not conveyed to the French admiralty in time.

The attack

When the deadline expired, Force H — built around HMS Hood, Valiant, Resolution and the carrier Ark Royal — opened fire. The battleship Bretagne exploded and sank with heavy loss of life; Dunkerque and Provence were badly damaged, though Strasbourg escaped to Toulon. In total 1,297 French servicemen were killed.

Aftermath

The attack shocked France, led Vichy France to sever diplomatic relations with Britain, and long poisoned Anglo-French relations. It also demonstrated to the United States Britain's determination to fight on. Days later, British aircraft returned to finish off the grounded Dunkerque.
