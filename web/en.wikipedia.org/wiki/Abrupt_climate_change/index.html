Abrupt climate change
Ice-core records from Greenland reveal past abrupt warming events
DefinitionA climate transition occurring faster than the change in its forcing
Key examplesYounger Dryas; Dansgaard–Oeschger events; 8.2-kiloyear event
MechanismsOcean circulation changes, ice-sheet dynamics, feedbacks and tipping points

Abrupt climate change occurs when the climate system is forced to transition into a new state at a rate faster than that of the forcing itself, often within decades. Evidence from ice cores, sediments, and other palaeoclimate records shows that such rapid shifts have happened repeatedly in Earth's past, sometimes involving temperature changes of several degrees within a human lifetime.

Past events

The best-known examples are the Dansgaard–Oeschger events of the last glacial period, in which Greenland warmed by as much as 8–16 °C within decades, and the Younger Dryas (c. 12,900–11,700 years ago), a sudden return to near-glacial conditions in the North Atlantic region after the initial deglacial warming. The 8.2-kiloyear event, a sharp cooling lasting about 150 years, is linked to the catastrophic drainage of glacial lakes Agassiz and Ojibway into the North Atlantic, freshening the ocean and slowing the Atlantic meridional overturning circulation (AMOC).

Mechanisms

Abrupt changes are thought to arise from thresholds and feedbacks within the climate system: changes in ocean circulation, ice-sheet instability, shifts in atmospheric circulation, vegetation feedbacks, and methane release. Because these processes can pass tipping points, the response to gradual forcing can be sudden and partially irreversible.

Present-day concerns

Scientists monitor several potential sources of future abrupt change, including weakening of the AMOC, collapse of parts of the Greenland and West Antarctic ice sheets, permafrost carbon release, and shifts in monsoon systems. While the timing and likelihood of such events remain uncertain, their potentially large impacts make them a focus of risk assessment in climate policy.
