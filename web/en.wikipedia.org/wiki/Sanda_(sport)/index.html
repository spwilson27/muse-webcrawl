Sanda (Chinese: 散打; pinyin: Sǎndǎ), formerly known competitively as Sanshou (散手), is a Chinese combat sport and self-defence system developed from traditional kung fu under the modern wushu programme. It combines punches, kicks, knee strikes (in professional rules), and — distinctively among kickboxing styles — wrestling throws and takedowns drawn from Chinese shuai jiao and chin na.

Competition

Matches take place on a raised platform called a leitai (擂台), traditionally without ropes, so that pushing or throwing an opponent off the platform can score heavily or decide the bout. Amateur bouts use protective gear and are scored over rounds; professional sanda in China has featured televised events such as the Sanda King tournaments. Sanda is contested at the World Wushu Championships and the Asian Games as part of wushu.

Techniques and training

Sanda training emphasises fast, side-on kicking (especially roundhouse and side kicks), straight punching, and catching kicks to execute throws. Because of its blend of striking and takedowns, sanda has produced notable crossover fighters in mixed martial arts, including Cung Le, Pat Barry, and Zhang Weili, China's first UFC champion, whose base style is sanda.
