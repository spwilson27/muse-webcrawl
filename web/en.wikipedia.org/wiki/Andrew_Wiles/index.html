Andrew Wiles

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Portrait photograph of Andrew Wiles, seated, in later life]

Sir Andrew John Wiles (born 11 April 1953) is an English mathematician and a Royal Society Research Professor at the University of Oxford, specialising in number theory. He is best known for proving Fermat's Last Theorem, announcing the proof in 1993 and completing it in 1994 with Richard Taylor.

Proof of Fermat's Last Theorem

Wiles worked in secrecy for about six years on the Taniyama–Shimura–Weil conjecture for semistable elliptic curves, which implied Fermat's Last Theorem. A flaw in his 1993 announcement was found in peer review; after well over a year of further work with his former student Richard Taylor, he completed the proof, published in the Annals of Mathematics in 1995.

Early life and honours

Wiles first encountered Fermat's Last Theorem as a ten-year-old in a library book. He studied at Oxford and Cambridge, and later worked at Princeton. He was knighted in 2000 and received the Abel Prize in 2016.
