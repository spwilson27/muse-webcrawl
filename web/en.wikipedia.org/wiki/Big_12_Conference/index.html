The Big 12 Conference is a collegiate athletic conference headquartered in Irving, Texas. It is a member of NCAA Division I, and its football teams compete in the Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS). The conference was formed in 1994 by the merger of the Big Eight Conference with four Texas schools from the Southwest Conference, and began competition in 1996 with twelve members.

History

The original twelve members were Baylor, Colorado, Iowa State, Kansas, Kansas State, Missouri, Nebraska, Oklahoma, Oklahoma State, Texas, Texas A&M, and Texas Tech. A first wave of realignment in 2010–2012 saw Nebraska leave for the Big Ten, Colorado for the Pac-12, and Missouri and Texas A&M for the SEC; TCU and West Virginia joined, leaving the league with ten members despite its name. The conference survived a second crisis in 2021 when Texas and Oklahoma announced moves to the SEC (effective 2024), responding by adding BYU, Cincinnati, Houston, and UCF in 2023. In 2024, following the collapse of the Pac-12, Arizona, Arizona State, Colorado (returning), and Utah joined, bringing membership to sixteen.

Sports and profile

The Big 12 sponsors championships in 25 sports. It is especially renowned for men's and women's basketball — Kansas is a traditional power, and Baylor won the men's national title in 2021 and Houston reached the national final in 2025 — as well as football, softball, and wrestling. Conference football and basketball championships are held in the Dallas–Fort Worth area and Kansas City respectively.
