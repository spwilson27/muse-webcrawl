Seasoning

Seasoning is the process of supplementing food with salt, herbs, spices, or other flavor enhancers to improve its taste. A seasoning may also refer to the mixture itself — any combination of aromatic ingredients added to food, typically ground or whole spices, dried herbs, salt, pepper, and sometimes sugar, acid, or umami-rich compounds such as monosodium glutamate.

General meaning

Salt is the most basic seasoning, enhancing flavor and suppressing bitterness; in many cuisines the phrase "season to taste" primarily means adjusting salt and pepper. Seasonings are added at various stages: before cooking (as in marinades, rubs, and brines), during cooking, or at the table as condiments. Famous blended seasonings include garam masala, herbes de Provence, Cajun seasoning, Old Bay, za'atar, shichimi tōgarashi, and Chinese five-spice powder.

Herbs and spices

Herbs — usually the leafy parts of plants, used fresh or dried — and spices — seeds, bark, roots, fruits, or flowers, usually dried and ground — form the bulk of most seasoning traditions. Historical trade in spices such as black pepper, cinnamon, and nutmeg shaped global commerce and colonization.

Other uses of the term

"Seasoning" also describes the preparation of cookware: seasoning cast iron or carbon steel involves baking thin layers of oil onto the surface to form a protective, non-stick coating. Similarly, new wooden or ceramic items may be seasoned before use. In finance, a "seasoned" security is one that has traded in the secondary market for some time.

Health considerations

Excess sodium from salt-heavy seasoning blends is a dietary concern linked to hypertension, prompting the development of salt-free seasoning mixtures based on herbs, citrus, and potassium chloride.
