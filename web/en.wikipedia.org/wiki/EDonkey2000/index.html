eDonkey2000

Developer: MetaMachine
Initial release: 2000
Type: Peer-to-peer file sharing
Successor network: eDonkey network (eD2k)

eDonkey2000 (nicknamed "ed2k") was a peer-to-peer file-sharing application developed by US company MetaMachine, which created the eDonkey network. The original client pioneered multi-source downloads and content hashing for large-file distribution, and competed with Napster-era and post-Napster sharing systems. Alternative clients — most importantly the open-source eMule — came to dominate the network.

Features

Unlike earlier services, eDonkey2000 did not index files on a single central server: a federation of independently run servers indexed shared files, while transfers happened directly between clients. Files were split into chunks (9.28 MB "parts"), each verified by MD4-based hash sets (the "ed2k hash"), allowing a download to be assembled from many sources simultaneously ("swarming") and corrupted chunks to be re-fetched individually. Content was linked with ed2k:// URLs containing filename, size, and hash, widely pasted on link sites and forums.

History

MetaMachine was founded by Jed McCaleb and Sam Yagan; the client was released in 2000. The network grew rapidly, and by 2004 eDonkey was among the most-used file-sharing networks in the world, especially in Europe. In September 2005, following the U.S. Supreme Court's MGM v. Grokster decision, the RIAA sent cease-and-desist letters to MetaMachine; the company settled for $30 million and shut down the official client and website in September 2006, displaying a warning that users risked copyright lawsuits. The underlying eDonkey network continued to operate for years through eMule and surviving servers, though server raids (notably the Razorback2 seizure in 2006) and the rise of BitTorrent diminished it.
