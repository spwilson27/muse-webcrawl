Inveraray

Inveraray, on the western shore of Loch Fyne in Argyll, Scotland.

Inveraray (Scottish Gaelic: Inbhir Aora) is a town in Argyll and Bute, Scotland. It is on the western shore of Loch Fyne, near its head, and on the A83 road. It is a former royal burgh, the traditional county town of Argyll, and the ancestral home of the Duke of Argyll.

History

The town was rebuilt in the second half of the 18th century by Archibald Campbell, 3rd Duke of Argyll, as part of the construction of the new Inveraray Castle. Much of the town, with its distinctive whitewashed buildings, was designed by architects including John Adam and Robert Mylne. The planned town is a notable example of 18th-century Scottish town planning and is protected as a conservation area.

Attractions

Visitor attractions include Inveraray Castle, the seat of the Duke of Argyll, chief of Clan Campbell; Inveraray Jail, a museum in the former county courthouse and prison; and the Celtic crosses and bell tower of All Saints Church. The town is also a popular stopping point for travellers touring the West Highlands.
