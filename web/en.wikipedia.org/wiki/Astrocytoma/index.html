Astrocytoma

Astrocytoma
Specialty: Neuro-oncology, neurosurgery
Types: Grades I–IV (WHO)
Treatment: Surgery, radiation, chemotherapy

Astrocytomas are a type of brain tumour. They originate in a particular kind of glial cell called an astrocyte, in the cerebrum. The tumour may also occur in other parts of the central nervous system, including the cerebellum, brain stem, and spinal cord. Astrocytomas are the most common type of glioma.

Classification

The World Health Organization (WHO) classifies astrocytomas into four grades based on histological features such as cellularity, nuclear atypia, mitotic activity, and necrosis. Grade I tumours, such as pilocytic astrocytoma, are slow-growing and often curable by surgery; grade II are diffuse low-grade tumours; grade III is anaplastic astrocytoma; and grade IV, glioblastoma, is the most aggressive. Modern WHO classification also incorporates molecular markers, notably IDH mutation status.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms depend on the location and size of the tumour and commonly include headaches, seizures, focal neurological deficits, and changes in personality or cognition. Raised intracranial pressure may cause nausea, vomiting, and visual disturbances.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is by neuroimaging, usually magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), followed by biopsy or surgical resection to obtain tissue for histopathological and molecular examination.

Treatment and prognosis

Treatment typically involves maximal safe surgical resection, often followed by radiotherapy and chemotherapy, such as temozolomide, for higher-grade tumours. Prognosis varies greatly with grade, age, extent of resection, and molecular profile: pilocytic astrocytomas have an excellent prognosis, while glioblastoma remains difficult to treat, with a median survival of roughly 15 months despite therapy.
