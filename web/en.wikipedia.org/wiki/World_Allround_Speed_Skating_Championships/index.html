The World Allround Speed Skating Championships are a series of speed skating events held annually to determine the best allround speed skater in the world. The event is organized by the International Skating Union (ISU). Unlike the single-distance championships, skaters compete over four distances, and the champion is the skater with the lowest total points based on the samalog system, in which each race time is converted to points.

History

The first unofficial world championships for men were held in Amsterdam in 1889, and the event became official in 1893. The women's championships were first held unofficially in 1933 and officially from 1936. For much of their history the men's and women's events were held at separate venues and on separate weekends, but they have increasingly been combined, and in recent years the allround championships have been staged together with the World Sprint Speed Skating Championships at a single venue.

Format

Men skate the 500 m, 1500 m, 5000 m and 10,000 m, while women skate the 500 m, 1500 m, 3000 m and 5000 m. After three distances, only the best skaters in the standings qualify for the final, longest distance. Points are calculated by dividing each time by the number of 500 m segments in the race, so that all distances are comparable.

Notable champions

Norwegian skaters dominated the early decades, with Oscar Mathisen and Ivar Ballangrud among the most successful. In the modern era, the Dutch skater Sven Kramer won a record nine men's titles, while Germany's Gunda Niemann-Stirnemann won eight consecutive women's titles. Other multiple champions include Ard Schenk, Eric Heiden, and Shani Davis for the men, and Karin Enke and Ireen Wüst for the women.
