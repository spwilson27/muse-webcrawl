Mojo

A mojo, in the African-American folk magic tradition of hoodoo, is an amulet consisting of a flannel bag containing one or more magical items. It is a "prayer in a bag", a spell that can be carried with or on the host's body. The mojo is also known as a mojo hand, mojo bag, conjure hand, lucky hand, or gris-gris among many regional names.

Origins and contents

The mojo derives from West African practices of carrying spiritually charged objects, adapted under slavery in the Americas. A mojo bag is typically made for a specific purpose — luck in gambling, love, protection, or success — and filled with corresponding roots, herbs, minerals, coins, or personal concerns, then dressed with oils and "fed" to keep its spirit active. The contents are traditionally concealed, since a mojo's power is believed to fail if the bag is seen or touched by another person.

In popular culture

The mojo entered broader American culture through blues music, notably Muddy Waters' "Got My Mojo Working" (1957), and in modern slang "mojo" has come to mean personal charisma, luck, or magical confidence more generally.
