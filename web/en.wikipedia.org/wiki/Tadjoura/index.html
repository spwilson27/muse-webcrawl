Tadjoura (also spelled Tadjourah or Tajura; Afar: Tagórri) is the oldest town in Djibouti and the capital of the Tadjourah Region. It lies on the northern shore of the Gulf of Tadjoura, roughly opposite Djibouti City, to which it is connected by ferry, and is sometimes called the "City of the Seven Mosques" or the white city for its whitewashed buildings.

History

Tadjoura was for centuries the seat of Afar sultans and an important port for trade between the Ethiopian highlands and the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden, exporting goods such as coffee, ivory and hides — and, in earlier centuries, it was also a significant centre of the slave trade. In the 1880s the town came under French influence as France established its protectorate over the region, and treaties with the local sultans made it part of French Somaliland. The decline of its caravan trade with the rise of Djibouti City and its railway to Ethiopia reduced Tadjoura's commercial importance in the twentieth century.

Geography and economy

The town sits on a coastal plain backed by the green Mabla Mountains. Its economy rests on fishing, salt production from nearby Lake Assal — one of the lowest and saltiest lakes in the world, lying in the Tadjourah Region — local trade, and a modernised port facility developed to serve trade with Ethiopia. The surrounding region includes the Day Forest National Park on Mount Goda, one of Djibouti's few areas of highland vegetation.

Transport

Tadjoura is linked to Djibouti City by a ferry crossing the gulf and by road via the RN9 highway, part of which was upgraded to serve the new port. A small airstrip serves the town.
