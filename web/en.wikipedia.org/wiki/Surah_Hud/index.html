Hud (Arabic: هود, Hūd) is the eleventh chapter (surah) of the Quran, containing 123 verses (ayat). It is named after the prophet Hud, who is mentioned in the chapter as having been sent to the people of ʿĀd. Like the preceding surah Yunus and the following surah Yusuf, it opens with the disconnected letters Alif Lām Rā and is generally classified as a Meccan surah, revealed before the Hijra.

Content

The surah opens with a declaration of the Quran's authority and a call to worship God alone and seek forgiveness. Its central portion recounts, in sequence, the stories of earlier prophets and their peoples: Nuh (Noah) and the flood; Hud and the destruction of ʿĀd; Salih and the she-camel of Thamud; Ibrahim (Abraham) and the announcement of Isaac; Lut (Lot) and the fate of his people; Shuayb and the people of Madyan; and Musa (Moses) and Pharaoh. Each story follows the pattern of a messenger rejected by his people, who are then punished, and each is presented as a lesson and a consolation for Muhammad and the early Muslim community facing opposition in Mecca.

The surah also contains a well-known description of the establishment of prayer and patience (verse 114) and the statement that God does not let the reward of those who do good be lost. Verse 118–119 discusses why humanity was not created as a single community.

In tradition

A saying attributed to Muhammad reports that "Surah Hud and its companions have aged me," commonly explained by the surah's stern commands — in particular the command in verse 112, "So remain on a right course as you have been commanded," together with its accounts of the punishment of earlier nations.
