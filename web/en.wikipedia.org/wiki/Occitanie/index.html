Occitanie (French pronunciation: [ɔksitani]; Occitan: Occitània) is an administrative region of France in the south of the country, created on 1 January 2016 by the merger of the former regions of Languedoc-Roussillon and Midi-Pyrénées. Its capital and largest city is Toulouse. The region is the second largest in metropolitan France by area.

Geography

Occitanie stretches from the Rhône valley in the east to the borders of Nouvelle-Aquitaine in the west, and from the Massif Central in the north to the Pyrenees and the Mediterranean Sea in the south. It includes Mediterranean coastline around Montpellier, Sète and Perpignan, the mountainous Cévennes, and parts of the Pyrenean chain along the Spanish border. Major cities include Toulouse, Montpellier, Nîmes, Perpignan, Béziers and Montauban.

Name

The name was chosen in 2016 following a public consultation and refers to the historical region of Occitania, the area of southern Europe where the Occitan language (langue d'oc) was traditionally spoken. The regional council styles the region "Occitanie / Pyrénées-Méditerranée" in some contexts.

History

The territory corresponds broadly to the Roman province of Gallia Narbonensis and, in the Middle Ages, to the County of Toulouse and the lands affected by the Albigensian Crusade against the Cathars. The modern region was formed as part of France's 2014–2016 territorial reform, which reduced the number of metropolitan regions from 22 to 13.

Economy

Toulouse is a major centre of the European aerospace industry, home to Airbus headquarters and a large cluster of aeronautics and space companies. Agriculture, wine production (the region contains some of the largest vineyard areas in France), tourism and higher education are also important sectors.
