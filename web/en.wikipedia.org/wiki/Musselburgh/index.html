Musselburgh is a town in East Lothian, Scotland, on the coast of the Firth of Forth, about 5 miles (8 km) east of Edinburgh city centre. It is the largest settlement in East Lothian apart from the county town of Haddington, and is sometimes described as being on the edge of Edinburgh's built-up area. The town's name derives from the mussel beds (shellfish) found in the Firth of Forth and the Old Scots word burgh, meaning a town or borough. Its motto is Honestas — "honesty" — and Musselburgh is traditionally known as "The Honest Toun".

History

The area was settled in Roman times; the invading Roman army built a fort at nearby Inveresk, and a Roman bridge crossed the River Esk at Musselburgh. The town grew around the harbour at Fisherrow and the crossing of the Esk. Musselburgh was made a burgh of barony in the 14th century and a royal burgh later. The Battle of Pinkie Cleugh, a decisive and bloody defeat for the Scots at the hands of the English, was fought just outside the town in 1547.

The Old Bridge, also known as the Roman Bridge, still stands over the Esk, and the New Bridge was built in the 19th century. Musselburgh Links, on the coast, is reputed to be one of the oldest golf courses in the world, with golf documented there as early as 1672; the Open Championship was played there in the 19th century, and the course hosts the annual race meeting of the Honest Toun tradition. Musselburgh Racecourse, established in 1816, remains a popular horse racing venue.

Geography and landmarks

The town sits on the south shore of the Firth of Forth at the mouth of the River Esk. Landmarks include the Tolbooth (1590), Pinkie House — now part of Loretto School, an independent school founded in 1827 — and the Brunton Theatre. Newhailes House, a 17th-century country house, lies on the town's western edge. Levenhall Links, reclaimed from ash lagoons of the former Cockenzie power station, is now a bird sanctuary and recreation area.

Transport

Musselburgh railway station, opened in 1988, is on the East Coast Main Line and the North Berwick branch, with trains to Edinburgh Waverley, North Berwick and Dunbar. The town is served by the A1 road and by buses to Edinburgh and East Lothian towns. See also Edinburgh and East Lothian.
