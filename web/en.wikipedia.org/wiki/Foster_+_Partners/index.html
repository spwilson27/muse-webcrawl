Foster + Partners is a British international architecture and design practice founded in London in 1967 by Norman Foster. One of the largest architectural firms in the world, it is known for high-tech architecture integrating structure, services and environmental design, and for landmark projects on every inhabited continent.

Major works

The practice first won wide attention with the Willis Faber and Dumas headquarters in Ipswich (1975) and the Sainsbury Centre for Visual Arts in Norwich (1978). Subsequent landmarks include the Hongkong and Shanghai Bank headquarters in Hong Kong (1986), then the most expensive building in the world; Stansted Airport; the refurbished Reichstag in Berlin with its glass dome (1999); the Great Court of the British Museum (2000); 30 St Mary Axe, the "Gherkin" (2003); the Millau Viaduct in France, the world's tallest bridge; Hearst Tower in New York; Beijing Capital Airport Terminal 3; and Apple Park, Apple's headquarters in Cupertino. The practice has also produced masterplans, the Zayed National Museum, and spaceport and lunar-habitat studies.

Recognition

Norman Foster received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 1999, and the practice has twice won the Stirling Prize, for the Gherkin and for Bloomberg's European headquarters in London. The firm is organised as a partnership with studios in London and offices worldwide, employing architects, engineers and designers from dozens of countries.
