Duke of Richmond is a title in the Peerage of England, now held with the dukedoms of Lennox (Scotland) and Gordon (United Kingdom). The current creation dates from 1675, when Charles II created his illegitimate son by Louise de Kérouaille, Charles Lennox, Duke of Richmond.

History

Earlier creations included the dukedom granted to Henry FitzRoy, son of Henry VIII, in 1525. The Lennox dukes settled at Goodwood House in Sussex, which became famous for the Glorious Goodwood race meeting. The 4th Duke died as Governor General of British North America in 1819, reportedly from rabies after a fox bite. The 6th Duke was created Duke of Gordon in 1876.

The current holder is Charles Gordon-Lennox, 11th Duke of Richmond, known for founding the Goodwood Festival of Speed and the Goodwood Revival. The heir apparent is Charles Henry Gordon-Lennox, Earl of March and Kinrara.
