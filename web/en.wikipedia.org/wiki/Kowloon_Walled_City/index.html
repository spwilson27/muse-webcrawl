Kowloon Walled City

LocationKowloon City, Hong Kong
Area2.6 ha (6.4 acres)
Population peakc. 33,000–50,000 (by 1987)
Demolished1993–1994

Kowloon Walled City (Chinese: 九龍寨城) was an extremely densely populated and largely ungoverned fortified settlement in Kowloon City, Hong Kong. Originally a Chinese military fort, it became an enclave after the New Territories were leased to Britain in 1898, and grew into a maze of high-rise buildings housing tens of thousands of residents on just 2.6 hectares (6.4 acres), giving it one of the highest population densities ever recorded.

History

The settlement began as a Song dynasty outpost and was fortified in 1847 with walls and cannons against British influence. After 1898, China retained nominal jurisdiction over the walled city even as Britain administered the surrounding territories, creating a legal vacuum neither side effectively policed. After the Second World War, squatters and refugees flooded in, and following unsuccessful attempts to evict them, the city grew without building regulation, its structures rising to 10–14 storeys and interlocking so tightly that sunlight rarely reached the lower alleys.

Life in the city

Despite a reputation for triad activity in the 1950s and 1960s, by the 1970s the city had become a settled community with factories, workshops, hundreds of small food producers, and unlicensed dentists and physicians serving residents and outsiders alike. It was served by only a handful of water standpipes and by mail carriers threading its passageways.

Demolition and legacy

In 1987 the Hong Kong and Chinese governments jointly announced the city's clearance and demolition, carried out in 1993–94 after compensating residents and businesses. The site reopened in 1995 as Kowloon Walled City Park, preserving the old yamen building and other relics. The walled city remains an enduring subject of fascination, inspiring works of fiction, film, architecture, and video games with its dense, labyrinthine aesthetic.
