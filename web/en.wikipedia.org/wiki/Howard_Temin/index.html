Howard Temin

Howard Martin Temin (December 10, 1934 – February 9, 1994) was an American geneticist and virologist. He shared the 1975 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Renato Dulbecco and David Baltimore for discoveries concerning the interaction between tumour viruses and the genetic material of the cell.

Work

Working at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, Temin studied Rous sarcoma virus, an RNA tumour virus. His experiments suggested that the virus somehow imposed a stable, heritable change on infected cells, and in 1964 he proposed the provirus hypothesis: that the RNA genome is copied into DNA, which is integrated into the host chromosome. The idea contradicted the then-standard "central dogma" that information flows only from DNA to RNA, and was widely doubted.

In 1970 Temin and Satoshi Mizutani, and independently David Baltimore, discovered the enzyme reverse transcriptase, which synthesises DNA from an RNA template — vindicating the provirus hypothesis and founding the study of retroviruses, later including HIV. Temin died of lung cancer in 1994, despite never having smoked, and campaigned in his later years against smoking.
