Byzantine music

Byzantine music is the liturgical chant tradition of the Byzantine Empire and, today, of the Eastern Orthodox Church. It is a monophonic vocal music, sung a cappella, organised in eight modes (the Octoechos), accompanied in performance by a sustained drone (ison). In 2019 UNESCO inscribed Byzantine chant on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

History

The tradition developed from early Christian chant in the Greek-speaking East, flowering in Constantinople with hymnographers such as Romanos the Melodist and John of Damascus. After 1453 it was preserved in monasteries, notably on Mount Athos, and codified in the 1814 "New Method" reform by the Three Teachers, which simplified its notation.

Characteristics

Chant is built from formulaic phrases in eight echoi, uses microtonal intervals distinct from Western scales, and is sung by a psaltis (cantor) and choir. Repertoire includes troparia, kontakia, stichera, and the great hymn the Akathist.
