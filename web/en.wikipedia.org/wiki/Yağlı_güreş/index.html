Yağlı güreş

Yağlı güreş (Turkish pronunciation: [ˈjaːɫɯ ˈɟyɾeʃ]), or oil wrestling, is a traditional Turkish sport in which wrestlers, known as pehlivan ('hero' or 'champion'), compete while doused in olive oil. It is the national sport of Turkey. The wrestlers wear a specially made pair of leather breeches called a kıspet and wrestle on grass. Because the oil makes the wrestlers slippery, great strength and technique are required to gain a hold.

History

Oil wrestling dates back thousands of years to ancient Turkic and earlier Near Eastern traditions, and was practised throughout the Ottoman Empire, spreading across the Balkans. The most famous tournament is the Kırkpınar oil wrestling festival held annually in Edirne, which claims a history reaching back to the fourteenth century and is recognised by UNESCO as part of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. The winner at Kırkpınar receives the title of başpehlivan (chief wrestler) and a golden belt.

Rules and ritual

Before a bout, wrestlers perform the peşrev, a ritualised warm-up and introduction accompanied by the music of drums (davul) and shawms (zurna), led by a master of ceremonies, the cazgır, who recites verses praising the wrestlers. The bout is won by pinning the opponent's back to the ground, lifting him and carrying him several steps, or by other traditional throws. Wrestlers are categorised by size and experience into classes, from beginners up to the baş (head) category.
