Indus Waters Treaty

Indus Waters Treaty
Signed19 September 1960, Karachi
PartiesIndia; Pakistan; World Bank (signatory for certain purposes)
SubjectSharing of the waters of the Indus system

The Indus Waters Treaty is a water-distribution treaty between India and Pakistan, arranged and negotiated by the World Bank, to use the water available in the Indus River and its tributaries. It was signed in Karachi on 19 September 1960 by Indian prime minister Jawaharlal Nehru and Pakistani president Ayub Khan.

Provisions

The treaty allocated control of the three "eastern rivers" — the Beas, Ravi, and Sutlej — to India, and the three "western rivers" — the Indus, Chenab, and Jhelum — to Pakistan, with India permitted limited non-consumptive and specified uses of the western rivers, including run-of-the-river hydroelectric projects built to strict design criteria. It established the Permanent Indus Commission, with a commissioner from each country, to implement and manage the treaty.

Disputes

Despite three wars between the signatories, the treaty long survived and is often cited as one of the most successful water-sharing agreements in the world. Disputes have arisen over Indian hydroelectric projects on the western rivers, including the Baglihar Dam on the Chenab and the Kishanganga project on a Jhelum tributary, which were taken to a neutral expert and the Permanent Court of Arbitration respectively. In January 2023 India issued a notice seeking modification of the treaty. Following the April 2025 Pahalgam terror attack, India announced it was holding the treaty "in abeyance", a step Pakistan rejected as unlawful.
