Gong Ji-young

Gong Ji-young (Korean: 공지영; born January 31, 1963) is a South Korean novelist and social activist, one of the most widely read contemporary Korean authors. Her work addresses labour, feminism, disability and human rights, and her books have sold millions of copies in Korea.

Career

Gong studied Korean literature at Yonsei University and made her literary debut in 1988 with the novella Dawn Rising (Dong teuneun saebyeok), informed by the student and labour movements of the 1980s. Her novel Go Alone Like a Rhino's Horn (1993) became a landmark feminist bestseller and was adapted for film and theatre.

Major works

Her novel Our Happy Time (2005), about a suicidal woman and a death-row inmate, was adapted into the film Maundy Thursday. The Crucible (Dogani, 2009), based on the real sexual-abuse scandal at the Gwangju Inhwa School for the deaf, was adapted into the 2011 film Silenced; the public outcry it generated led to the repeal and strengthening of statutes of limitation for sex crimes against minors and the disabled — the so-called "Dogani Law". Other works include My Sister Bongsoon and The Scale of Joy.
