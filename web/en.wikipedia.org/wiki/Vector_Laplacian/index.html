Vector Laplacian

The vector Laplacian is a differential operator acting on vector fields, generalising the scalar Laplace operator. It appears throughout physics, notably in the wave equation for electromagnetic fields, in fluid dynamics, and in the vector calculus form of Maxwell's equations.

The vector Laplacian measures the difference between a vector field's value at a point and its average nearby.

Definition in Cartesian coordinates

In Cartesian coordinates, the vector Laplacian of a vector field A = (Ax, Ay, Az) is simply the vector whose components are the scalar Laplacians of the components:

∇²A = (∇²Ax, ∇²Ay, ∇²Az)

Relation to grad, div and curl

The operator is connected to the other second-order operators of vector calculus by the identity

∇²A = ∇(∇·A) − ∇×(∇×A),

which expresses the Laplacian of a vector field as the gradient of its divergence minus the curl of its curl. This identity is the key step in deriving the electromagnetic wave equation from Maxwell's equations in free space.

Other coordinate systems

In curvilinear coordinates such as cylindrical and spherical coordinates, the formula is more complicated: unlike the Cartesian case, one cannot simply apply the scalar Laplacian component-wise, because the unit vectors themselves vary with position. Extra terms involving derivatives of the components appear; for example, the φ-component in cylindrical coordinates includes terms such as (2/r²) ∂Ar/∂φ and −Aφ/r².

Generalisation

On Riemannian manifolds, the natural generalisation is the connection (rough) Laplacian on vector bundles, and its close relative the Hodge Laplacian acting on differential forms; the two are related by the Weitzenböck formula, which differs by curvature terms. The vector Laplacian is also fundamental to the Helmholtz decomposition of vector fields.
