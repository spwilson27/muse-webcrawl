Pole vault

Pole vault
Category: Field event (jumping)
Olympic sport since: 1896 (men); 2000 (women)
Men's world record: 6.26 m — Armand Duplantis (2024)
Women's world record: 5.06 m — Yelena Isinbayeva (2009)

The pole vault is a track and field event in which an athlete uses a long, flexible pole to jump over a bar set at a measured height. Competitors sprint down a runway carrying the pole, plant its tip in a metal box at the base of the uprights, and convert their speed and the pole's bending into upward lift, clearing the crossbar without dislodging it.

Technique and equipment

Modern poles are made of fiberglass and carbon fiber composites, rated by the vaulter's weight and flex; earlier eras used bamboo and metal poles. The vault consists of the approach, plant and take-off, swing, rock-back and turn, and bar clearance. Athletes are eliminated after three consecutive failed attempts; the winner is whoever clears the greatest height.

History

Pole jumping has roots in practical pole-assisted leaping over canals and marshes in the Netherlands and England, and became a competitive event in the 19th century. It has been contested at every modern Olympic Games for men since 1896, with the women's event added in 2000. Sergey Bubka dominated the event in the 1980s–90s, and Armand "Mondo" Duplantis has repeatedly raised the world record since 2020.
