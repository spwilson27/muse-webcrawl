Emmanuel de Grouchy

Emmanuel de Grouchy, marquis de Grouchy (23 October 1766 – 29 May 1847) was a French military leader who served during the Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars. He was made a Marshal of the Empire in 1815 — the last marshal appointed by Napoleon — and commanded the right wing of the army during the Waterloo campaign.

Career

Born into an old noble family, Grouchy entered the artillery and later transferred to the cavalry. Despite his aristocratic origins he embraced the Revolution, serving with distinction in Ireland's intended expedition, at Hohenlinden (1800), and through the campaigns of the Grande Armée. He was badly wounded at Eylau and at Wagram, and commanded the cavalry of the Army of Italy and later the famed "Sacred Squadron" during the retreat from Russia.

Waterloo

After Napoleon's return in 1815, Grouchy was promoted to marshal and given command of some 33,000 men, tasked with pursuing the Prussians after the Battle of Ligny. On 18 June 1815, while Napoleon fought at Waterloo, Grouchy engaged the Prussian rearguard at Wavre. His failure to march to the sound of the guns — obeying, as he insisted, his written orders — allowed Blücher's corps to reach Waterloo and decide the battle. The question of blame has been debated by historians ever since.

Later life

Exiled after the Bourbon Restoration, Grouchy lived in the United States before being permitted to return to France, where his marshal's rank was restored in 1831. He died at Saint-Étienne in 1847.
