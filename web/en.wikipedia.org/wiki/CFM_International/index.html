CFM International

CFM International is a Franco-American aircraft engine manufacturer, a 50/50 joint venture between GE Aerospace (formerly GE Aviation) of the United States and Safran Aircraft Engines (formerly Snecma) of France. It was founded in 1974 to develop and produce the CFM56 series of turbofan engines. The name CFM derives from the parent companies' engine designations: GE's CF6 and Snecma's M56.

Products

The CFM56, entering service in 1982, became the best-selling turbofan engine in history, powering the Boeing 737 Classic and Next Generation families, the Airbus A320 family, the A340-200/300, and military types including the KC-135R. More than 33,000 CFM56 engines were delivered.

Its successor, the LEAP engine (Leading Edge Aviation Propulsion), was developed with extensive use of composite fan blades and ceramic matrix composites. The LEAP-1A powers the Airbus A320neo family, the LEAP-1B the Boeing 737 MAX, and the LEAP-1C the Comac C919. The venture was extended in 2021 to 2050, and the partners launched the RISE (Revolutionary Innovation for Sustainable Engines) open-fan demonstrator programme.

History

The partnership originated in 1971 discussions between GE and Snecma and survived an early export-licence dispute in the United States, resolved after diplomatic intervention in 1973. The joint company was formally established in 1974. Work is shared roughly evenly: GE produces the core, and Safran the fan, low-pressure components and accessories.
