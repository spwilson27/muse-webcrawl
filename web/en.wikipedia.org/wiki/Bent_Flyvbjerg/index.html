Bent Flyvbjerg

Bent Flyvbjerg is a Danish social scientist known for his research on megaprojects, planning and decision-making. He coined or popularised concepts including the planning fallacy in project management, optimism bias and strategic misrepresentation as explanations for systematic cost overruns and benefit shortfalls in large infrastructure projects, and he developed reference class forecasting as a remedy.

Career

Flyvbjerg held the first Chair of Major Programme Management at the University of Oxford's Saïd Business School, and previously taught at Aalborg University in Denmark, where his Aalborg study of urban politics became the book Rationality and Power. He later took a professorship at the IT University of Copenhagen.

Research

Building large databases of transport and other projects, Flyvbjerg documented that cost overruns and demand shortfalls are the norm rather than the exception — summarised in his "iron law of megaprojects": over budget, over time, under benefits, over and over again. He attributes the pattern both to cognitive biases and to deliberate strategic misrepresentation by promoters competing for funding. Reference class forecasting — estimating outcomes from the distribution of results in a class of similar completed projects, an application of Daniel Kahneman's "outside view" — was endorsed by the American Planning Association and adopted in UK and Danish appraisal guidance.

In social science methodology he advocates phronetic research focused on values, power and practice, and defended the case-study method against claims that it cannot support generalisation.
