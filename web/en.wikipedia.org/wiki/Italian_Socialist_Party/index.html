Italian Socialist Party

The Italian Socialist Party (Partito Socialista Italiano, PSI) was a social-democratic political party in Italy, founded in Genoa in 1892. It was one of the country's major parties for a century before collapsing in 1994 amid the Tangentopoli corruption scandal.

History

The PSI grew out of workers' leagues and mutual aid societies. It opposed Italy's entry into the First World War, split with its communist wing in 1921, and was suppressed under Mussolini. After 1943 it re-emerged in the resistance and the post-war republic.

Post-war era

Under Pietro Nenni the party entered centre-left governments in the 1960s. Under Bettino Craxi, secretary from 1976 and Prime Minister 1983–1987, the PSI modernised its image and reached about 14% of the vote, but Craxi's fall in the Mani Pulite investigations destroyed the party, which dissolved in 1994.
