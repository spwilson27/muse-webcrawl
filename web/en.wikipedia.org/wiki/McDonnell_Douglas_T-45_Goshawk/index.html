The McDonnell Douglas (now Boeing) T-45 Goshawk is a highly modified version of the British BAE Systems Hawk land-based training jet aircraft. Manufactured by McDonnell Douglas (now Boeing) and British Aerospace (now BAE Systems), the T-45 is used by the United States Navy as an aircraft carrier-capable trainer.

Development

The T-45 was developed from the Hawk Mk.60 for the US Navy's VTXTS advanced trainer programme. To operate from aircraft carriers, the design received a strengthened airframe, enlarged tail surfaces, a more powerful Rolls-Royce Turbomeca Adour engine, an arrestor hook, and reinforced landing gear with a twin-wheel nose gear and catapult launch bar. It first flew on 16 April 1988.

Operational history

The T-45 entered service in 1991 and serves with Training Air Wings at NAS Meridian, NAS Kingsville, and NAS Pensacola, training naval aviators and naval flight officers. The improved T-45C variant features a digital "glass cockpit." A total of 221 Goshawks were produced. The aircraft has no combat capability, though it has provision for training weapons.
