Audi Le Mans quattro

The Audi Le Mans quattro is a concept car developed by German automobile manufacturer Audi, first shown at the 2003 Frankfurt Motor Show, to celebrate Audi's three successive wins at the arduous 24 Hours of Le Mans endurance race in 2000, 2001, and 2002. It was the third and final of a series of Audi concepts shown in 2003, following the Pikes Peak quattro and the Nuvolari quattro.

Manufacturer: Audi AG

Production: 2003 (concept car)

Class: Sports car / supercar concept

Engine: 5.0 L twin-turbocharged V10 FSI

Power: 449 kW (610 PS; 602 hp)

Layout: Mid-engine, all-wheel drive

Design and specifications

The mid-engined two-seater used Audi's aluminium space frame (ASF) construction and quattro all-wheel drive. Its 5.0-litre V10 engine with FSI direct injection and twin turbochargers produced about 610 PS (602 hp) and 750 N·m of torque, giving a claimed 0–100 km/h time of 3.7 seconds and a top speed of around 345 km/h. Distinctive styling features included very short overhangs, prominent wheel arches, LED headlights, and a large rear diffuser.

Production successor

The Le Mans quattro directly previewed the production Audi R8, launched in 2006, which retained the concept's proportions, mid-engine layout, and much of its styling, though the production car initially used a 4.2-litre V8 in place of the twin-turbo V10. The car also became widely known to gamers as a featured vehicle in racing games such as Gran Turismo 4.
