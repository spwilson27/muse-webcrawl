Velites

Velites (singular: veles) were a class of infantry in the Roman army of the mid-Republic (roughly the 4th to 2nd centuries BC). They were light skirmishers drawn from the youngest and poorest citizens who could not afford the equipment of the heavy infantry of the legion.

Equipment

According to Polybius, each veles carried a bundle of light javelins (hastae velitares) about 120 cm long with thin iron points that bent on impact, a short sword (gladius), a small round shield (parma) roughly 90 cm in diameter, and a simple helmet. Many wore a wolf skin over the helmet so that their officers could recognise their conduct in battle and reward bravery.

Role in battle

In the manipular legion each maniple of heavy infantry had a detachment of velites attached; in total a legion fielded about 1,200 velites. At the start of a battle they advanced in a loose screen ahead of the main line, throwing their javelins to harass the enemy, then retired through the intervals between the maniples of hastati to the rear or flanks, where they might guard the baggage or threaten the enemy's flanks.

History

The velites emerged as Rome organised its citizen levy by wealth and age, a system traditionally associated with the Servian reforms and refined during the Samnite and Punic Wars. As equipment was standardised in the late 2nd century BC — a change associated with the reforms of Gaius Marius — the distinction between velites, hastati, principes, and triarii disappeared, and light troops were increasingly supplied by auxiliaries.
