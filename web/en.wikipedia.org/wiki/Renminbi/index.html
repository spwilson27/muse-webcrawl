The renminbi (Chinese: 人民币; pinyin: Rénmínbì; lit. "People's Currency"; symbol ¥; ISO code CNY) is the official currency of the People's Republic of China. It is issued by the People's Bank of China, the country's central bank. The basic unit of the renminbi is the yuán (元), which is subdivided into ten jiǎo (角) and one hundred fēn (分). In colloquial speech, the unit is often called kuài.

History

The first renminbi series was issued in December 1948, shortly before the founding of the People's Republic of China in 1949. Successive series of banknotes followed, with the fifth series introduced from 1999. The currency was long pegged closely to the US dollar; in 2005 China moved to a managed floating exchange rate based on a basket of currencies.

International use

Since the 2010s, China has promoted the internationalization of the renminbi, establishing offshore trading (denoted CNH) centered in Hong Kong. In 2016 the renminbi joined the IMF's Special Drawing Rights basket. A central bank digital currency, the digital yuan (e-CNY), has been piloted since 2020.
