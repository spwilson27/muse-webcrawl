Taunton is the county town of Somerset, England. It lies on the River Tone in the Vale of Taunton Deane, between the Quantock Hills, Blackdown Hills and Somerset Levels. The town has a long history as a market and administrative centre and is known for its connection with the Monmouth Rebellion of 1685.

History

A Saxon settlement grew around a minster founded in the early 8th century, and a castle was established in the 12th century. Taunton received its first charter before 904 and prospered on the woollen cloth trade. In 1685 the Duke of Monmouth was proclaimed king in Taunton before his defeat at the Battle of Sedgemoor; the subsequent "Bloody Assizes" of Judge Jeffreys were held in the Great Hall of Taunton Castle, now part of the Museum of Somerset.

Modern Taunton

Today Taunton is a regional service and retail centre and home to the headquarters of Somerset Council, the United Kingdom Hydrographic Office, and 40 Commando, Royal Marines, at Norton Manor Camp. Somerset County Cricket Club plays at the County Ground, Taunton. The town is served by the M5 motorway and a station on the Great Western main line to London Paddington.
