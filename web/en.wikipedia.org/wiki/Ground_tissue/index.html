Ground tissue
One of the three plant tissue systems, alongside dermal and vascular tissue. Comprises parenchyma, collenchyma, and sclerenchyma.

The ground tissue of plants includes all tissues that are neither dermal nor vascular. It can be divided into three types based on the nature of the cell walls: parenchyma, collenchyma, and sclerenchyma. Together with the dermal and vascular tissues, the ground tissue system forms the body of the plant.

Parenchyma

Parenchyma cells have thin primary walls and usually remain alive at maturity. They form the bulk of many plant structures, including the pith and cortex of stems and roots, the mesophyll of leaves, and the flesh of fruits. Parenchyma cells carry out photosynthesis, storage of starch and water, and wound healing.

Collenchyma

Collenchyma cells have unevenly thickened primary walls and provide flexible mechanical support to growing parts of the plant, such as young stems and leaf petioles — the ridges of a celery stalk are a familiar example.

Sclerenchyma

Sclerenchyma cells have thick, lignified secondary walls and are usually dead at maturity. They include long fibres and short sclereids (stone cells), and provide rigid support and protection, as in seed coats and the gritty texture of pears.

Functions

Ground tissue fills the spaces between other tissues, stores food reserves, carries out photosynthesis in stems and leaves, and provides structural support throughout the plant body.
