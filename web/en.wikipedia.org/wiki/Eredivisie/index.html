Eredivisie

The Eredivisie (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈeːrədivizi]; "Honour Division" or "Premier Division") is the highest professional football league in the Netherlands. Founded in 1956, it consists of 18 clubs; at the end of each season the bottom clubs are relegated to the second-tier Eerste Divisie, with promotion and relegation play-offs deciding additional places.

History

Before 1956 Dutch football championships were decided by play-offs between regional champions. The Eredivisie unified the national competition. The league has historically been dominated by the "big three" clubs—Ajax, PSV Eindhoven, and Feyenoord—with Ajax the most successful club in league history. Dutch clubs have also enjoyed major European success, winning European Cups in the 1970s and 1995.

Format

Each club plays the others twice (home and away) over a season running from August to May. The champion qualifies for the UEFA Champions League, with other European places determined by league position and play-offs; the bottom club is relegated automatically, and the 16th- and 17th-placed clubs enter promotion/relegation play-offs.
