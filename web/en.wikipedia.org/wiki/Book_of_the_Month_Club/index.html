Book-of-the-Month Club

The Book-of-the-Month Club (BOMC) is an American subscription-based book club founded in 1926 by Harry Scherman. For much of the twentieth century it was one of the most powerful forces in American publishing: selection by its editorial board could guarantee a book large sales, and membership gave middle-class readers across the country curated access to new fiction and non-fiction by post. It is now operated as an online subscription service, relaunched in 2015 with a focus on early releases of new hardcovers.

History

Scherman, an advertising copywriter, had earlier experimented with mail-order sets of classics through the Little Leather Library. With the Book-of-the-Month Club he added the crucial device of a distinguished panel of judges — figures such as Henry Seidel Canby, Dorothy Canfield Fisher, and later Clifton Fadiman — who chose a featured book each month, lending cultural authority to the selections. The club's first selection was Sylvia Townsend Warner's novel Lolly Willowes. Membership passed a million by the late 1940s, and the club helped popularise landmark works including The Story of Civilization and many bestselling novels.

Operations and influence

Members received a monthly news bulletin describing the selections and, unless they declined, the main selection was mailed automatically — the negative option plan that became standard for subscription clubs. A BOMC selection or alternate selection often meant print runs in the hundreds of thousands, and publishers competed keenly for the judges' favour. The club became part of larger publishing and mail-order groups, passing through Time Inc. and later Bertelsmann's Doubleday direct-marketing operations, before its 2015 relaunch as a streamlined monthly subscription aimed at younger readers, offering a choice of five new-release hardcovers each month.
