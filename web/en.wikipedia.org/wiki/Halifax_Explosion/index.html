The Halifax Explosion was a devastating maritime disaster in Halifax, Nova Scotia, Canada, on the morning of 6 December 1917. The French cargo ship SS Mont-Blanc, laden with high explosives, collided with the Norwegian vessel SS Imo in the Narrows of Halifax Harbour. A fire aboard Mont-Blanc ignited her cargo, producing the largest human-made explosion at the time and one of the largest non-nuclear explosions in history.

The collision

Halifax was a major wartime port. Mont-Blanc had arrived from New York carrying some 2,900 tonnes of explosives — TNT, picric acid, guncotton and benzole — bound for the war in Europe, but was not flying the red flag required of explosives carriers. Imo, outbound in ballast, was travelling too fast and on the wrong side of the channel. After a series of conflicting whistle signals, the ships collided at about 8:45 a.m. Sparks ignited benzole vapours, and Mont-Blanc's crew abandoned ship and rowed for the Dartmouth shore, shouting warnings that few bystanders understood.

The explosion

At 9:04:35 a.m. the burning ship detonated. The blast — estimated at 2.9 kilotons of TNT equivalent — levelled the Richmond district of Halifax, sent a mushroom cloud 3.6 km high, generated a tsunami up to 18 metres in the harbour, and flung Mont-Blanc's anchor shaft 5.6 km and one of her guns nearly 10 km. About 1,782 people were killed outright and some 9,000 injured, many blinded by flying glass as they watched the fire from windows. Roughly 1,600 buildings were destroyed and 12,000 damaged.

Aftermath

A blizzard the following day hampered relief, which arrived from across Canada and the United States — notably from Boston, a generosity Halifax still honours by sending Boston a Christmas tree each year. The disaster unfolded weeks after the Mi'kmaq community of Tufts Cove was destroyed. Legal proceedings initially blamed both captains; charges were eventually dropped. The explosion remained the benchmark for accidental blast damage until the atomic age, and it directly prompted modern hazardous-cargo port regulations.
