Dummy tank
Type: Military decoy
Materials: Wood, canvas, inflatable rubber
Purpose: Deception of enemy reconnaissance

Dummy tanks are decoy vehicles built to resemble real tanks, used to deceive enemy reconnaissance, inflate the apparent size of an armoured force, draw fire away from real vehicles, or mislead an opponent about the location of an attack. They range from crude wooden frames covered in canvas to sophisticated inflatable replicas that mimic a tank's visual and even thermal signature.

History

Simple dummy tanks appeared in the First World War. In the Second World War they were used on a large scale: British forces in North Africa built dummy armour to mask the build-up before El Alamein, and the Allies' Operation Fortitude employed an entirely fictitious First United States Army Group in Kent, complete with inflatable Sherman tanks, to convince Germany the D-Day landings would come at Calais. The U.S. "Ghost Army" (23rd Headquarters Special Troops) used dummy tanks with sound and radio effects to simulate armoured divisions.

Modern use

Inflatable decoys were used by Serbia in the Kosovo War and by Russia and Ukraine in the Russo-Ukrainian War, where cheap replicas draw expensive precision strikes and complicate satellite and drone surveillance.
