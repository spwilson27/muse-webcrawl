History of Nova Scotia

The history of Nova Scotia covers the homeland of the Mi'kmaq people, French Acadia founded in 1605, and British rule after 1713. Halifax, founded in 1749, became the colonial capital.

Colonial era

French settlers (Acadians) farmed dyked marshlands until their expulsion by the British beginning in 1755 during the Seven Years' War era. New England Planters, Loyalists after the American Revolution, and Scottish immigrants repopulated the province; New Brunswick was split off in 1784.

Confederation and after

Nova Scotia was one of the four founding provinces of Canada in 1867, despite significant anti-Confederation sentiment. Shipbuilding, coal, steel, and fisheries drove its 19th-century economy; the Halifax Explosion of 1917 was a defining disaster.
