Lumber

Lumber (in North American English) — generally called timber in other English-speaking regions — is wood that has been processed into uniform sizes such as planks, boards and beams, primarily for use in construction and carpentry. It is produced in sawmills by cutting logs into rectangular sections, then drying, planing and grading the pieces.

Production

Logs are debarked and sawn by methods such as live sawing or cant sawing, then the rough lumber is kiln-dried or air-dried to stabilise it, and surfaced (planed) to final dimensions. Lumber is divided into softwood (from conifers such as pine, spruce and fir) and hardwood (from broad-leaved trees such as oak and maple); the terms do not strictly reflect the wood's hardness. Grading rules classify pieces by strength and appearance.

Sizes

In North America, dimensional lumber is sold by nominal sizes (e.g. a "two-by-four", 2 in × 4 in) that are larger than its actual finished dimensions (1.5 in × 3.5 in). Structural lumber is measured in board feet in the United States and in cubic metres in metric markets. Products range from studs and joists to glulam beams, I-joists and other engineered wood products made from lumber, veneer or strands.

Uses and trade

Lumber is the primary material of wood-frame construction, dominant in North American housing, and is also used for furniture, pallets, fencing and formwork. Canada and the United States have disputed softwood lumber trade for decades under the recurring "softwood lumber dispute". Lumber prices spiked dramatically during the COVID-19 pandemic housing boom, becoming an economic indicator.
