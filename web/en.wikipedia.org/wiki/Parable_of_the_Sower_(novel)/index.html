Parable of the Sower is a science fiction novel by American writer Octavia E. Butler, published in 1993. Set in a dystopian California of the 2020s, devastated by climate change, inequality and drug-fuelled violence, it follows teenager Lauren Olamina — who suffers from "hyperempathy", a condition making her share the pain and pleasure of those around her — as she develops a new faith, Earthseed, and journeys north after her walled community is destroyed. A sequel, Parable of the Talents, followed in 1998 and won the Nebula Award for Best Novel. The novel reached The New York Times Best Seller list in 2020, decades after publication, amid renewed attention to its prescience.

Plot

In 2024, Lauren Olamina lives in Robledo, a cul-de-sac community outside Los Angeles protected by a wall against the arsonist gangs addicted to the drug "pyro", which makes watching fire pleasurable. Lauren's father, a Baptist minister, leads the community, but Lauren secretly rejects his God and writes verses of her own scripture, Earthseed: The Books of the Living, whose central tenet is that "God is Change". When her father disappears and the neighbourhood is attacked and burned in 2027, Lauren escapes dressed as a man and travels Highway 101 north with two companions, gathering followers including the physician Bankole, her future partner. The novel ends with the group reaching Bankole's land in Humboldt County, where Lauren founds "Acorn", the first Earthseed community, and quotes the biblical Parable of the Sower.

Themes

The novel explores survival, community, race, gender and the construction of religion; Earthseed's destiny — "to take root among the stars" — reframes humanity's future as adaptation to change rather than escape to heaven. Critics have noted the novel's anticipation of "make America great again"-style politics (developed in the sequel's President Jarret), climate migration and privatised, walled inequality.

Reception and adaptations

Parable of the Sower was nominated for the Nebula Award. Toshi Reagon's folk-opera adaptation, Octavia E. Butler's Parable of the Sower, premiered in 2015 and has toured internationally. The novel's readership surged in the 2020s as its fictional 2024 setting arrived and its themes resonated with contemporary events.
