Nick Carter is a fictional private detective who first appeared in the story paper New York Weekly on 18 September 1886 in the story The Old Detective's Pupil, written by John R. Coryell and published by Street & Smith. One of the longest-lived characters in American popular fiction, Carter starred in thousands of dime novels, pulp magazines, radio programmes, films and, from the 1960s, a long-running series of spy paperbacks.

Dime novel detective

In his original incarnation Carter was a clean-living, square-jawed master detective, trained from boyhood by his father, the detective Sim Carter. A master of disguise, boxing, languages and deductive reasoning, he solved crimes in weekly stories written by a stable of house authors under the "Nicholas Carter" byline. At his peak, Nick Carter stories were translated worldwide and the character became a generic term for a detective in several languages.

Radio, film and pulps

The character headlined his own pulp magazine, Nick Carter Magazine (1933–1936), and the long-running Mutual radio drama Nick Carter, Master Detective (1943–1955), famous for its opening cry of "Nick Carter — back in the nick of time!" Walter Pidgeon played Carter in three MGM films beginning with Nick Carter, Master Detective (1939).

Killmaster

In 1964 the character was reinvented as a suave American secret agent, codenamed N3, in the Nick Carter – Killmaster series of paperback spy thrillers. More than 260 Killmaster novels appeared over three decades, written by numerous authors under the house name Nick Carter.
