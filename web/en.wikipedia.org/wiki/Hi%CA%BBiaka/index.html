Hiʻiaka

Hiʻiaka, in full Hiʻiakaikapoliopele ("Hiʻiaka in the bosom of Pele"), is a Hawaiian goddess, the youngest and favourite sister of the volcano goddess Pele, and a principal figure in the Pele epic cycle of Hawaiian mythology. She is the patron goddess of the hula dance and of the island of Hawaiʻi's forests and healing.

Mythology

According to tradition, Hiʻiaka was born from an egg incubated in Pele's bosom. Pele sent her on the great journey to Kauaʻi to fetch Pele's lover Lohiʻau, a quest forming the longest epic in Hawaiian literature, in which Hiʻiaka battles moʻo (lizard beings) and other dangers, aided by her companions including the healer Pāuliuli and the woman Hōpoe, her dear friend. When Pele, suspecting betrayal, destroyed Hōpoe and Hiʻiaka's beloved lehua groves, Hiʻiaka defied her sister and embraced Lohiʻau at the crater's edge.

Legacy

The epic of Hiʻiaka was recorded in Hawaiian-language newspapers and in the Hoʻoulu Lāhui edition published under King Kalākaua's programme; it has been translated in full into English by Puakea Nogelmeier. Several species and the hula tradition continue to honour her.
