Vertex operator algebra

A vertex operator algebra (VOA) is an algebraic structure that plays an important role in conformal field theory and string theory. The notion was introduced by Richard Borcherds in 1986, formalising ideas of Igor Frenkel and others from the construction of the Monster group.

Definition

A VOA consists of a graded vector space V, a vacuum vector, a conformal vector, and a state-field correspondence assigning to each vector a formal power series (a vertex operator) with coefficients that are operators on V. The axioms include locality (mutual commutativity of vertex operators after multiplication by a suitable power of the difference of variables) and encode the operator product expansion of two-dimensional conformal field theory.

Examples and applications

Examples arise from lattice theory, affine Kac–Moody algebras at positive integer level, and Virasoro algebra representations. Frenkel, Lepowsky and Meurman constructed the Moonshine module, a VOA whose automorphism group is the Monster, providing the framework for Borcherds's proof of the Monstrous Moonshine conjectures, recognised by the Fields Medal in 1998.
