Week

A week is a time unit equal to seven days. It is the standard period used in most of the world for cycles of rest days alongside the work week and weekend. Unlike days, months, and years, the week has no direct basis in astronomy; it likely derives from ancient Near Eastern traditions, including the Jewish seven-day cycle and Babylonian association of seven with the visible "planets".

Days of the week

In English the days are Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, and Sunday — several named for Norse and Roman deities. ISO 8601 numbers the days 1 (Monday) to 7 (Sunday) and defines week dates used in commerce and planning.

History

The seven-day week spread through the Roman Empire, where it became standard by the time of Constantine, and via Judaism and Christianity around the world. Other cultures historically used weeks of different lengths — the French Revolutionary calendar used ten-day décades, and the Soviet Union briefly experimented with five- and six-day weeks — but the seven-day week is now nearly universal.
