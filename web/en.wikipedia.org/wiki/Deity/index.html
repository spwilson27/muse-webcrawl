Deity

A deity or god is a supernatural being considered divine or sacred. Deities are often conceived as creators, rulers of natural forces or realms, or embodiments of concepts, and are objects of worship in polytheistic, monotheistic, henotheistic and other religions. The Oxford English Dictionary defines a deity as "a god or goddess (in a polytheistic religion)", or anything revered as divine.

Conceptions

Monotheism recognises one God (capitalised), often omnipotent, omniscient and creator of the universe, as in Judaism, Christianity and Islam. Polytheism recognises many gods with distinct domains—Greek Olympians, Hindu devas, Norse Æsir, Egyptian and Aztec pantheons. Other views include pantheism (God identical with the universe), deism (a non-intervening creator), animism and ancestor veneration.

Attributes and roles

Deities may be anthropomorphic or abstract, immortal or mortal (as in Norse myth), benevolent, capricious or ambivalent. They receive prayer, sacrifice and offerings, mediate morality and fate, and appear in creation myths and epic literature. Demigods and divinised humans (e.g. Roman emperors, Egyptian pharaohs) blur the boundary.

Study

Comparative religion, anthropology and cognitive science study god concepts, their cultural evolution and psychological bases. Philosophical theology debates the existence and attributes of God (ontological, cosmological and teleological arguments).
