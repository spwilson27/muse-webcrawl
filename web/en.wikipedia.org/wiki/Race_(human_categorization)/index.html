Race (human categorization)

Race is a categorization of humans based on shared physical or social qualities generally viewed as distinct within a given society. Modern scholarship regards race as a social construct — an identity assigned on perceived shared ancestry and phenotype — rather than a valid biological classification, since genetic variation within groups exceeds that between them.

Race
Concept: Social categorization
Based on: Phenotype, ancestry, social criteria
Scientific consensus: Not a biological taxonomy
Related: Ethnicity, racism, eugenics

Historical development

Early modern European thinkers — Linnaeus, Blumenbach, Kant — proposed racial taxonomies tied to geography and supposed temperament. In the 19th and early 20th centuries, scientific racism underpinned colonialism, slavery's legacies, and eugenics. After World War II, UNESCO statements and genetic research repudiated biological race concepts.

Contemporary views

Anthropologists and biologists note human variation is clinal and non-concordant. Yet race remains socially and politically real: censuses (e.g. in the US and Brazil) record racial categories, and racism — discrimination based on perceived race — continues to shape health, wealth and law.

By country

Definitions differ: US categories derive from census and "one-drop" history; Brazil uses colour continuum; South Africa's apartheid classifications persist in statistics; many European states avoid racial data.
