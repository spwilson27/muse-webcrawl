Isoroku Yamamoto

Born: 4 April 1884, Nagaoka, Niigata, Japan

Died: 18 April 1943 (aged 59), near Buin, Bougainville

Allegiance: Empire of Japan

Service: Imperial Japanese Navy, 1904–1943

Rank: Marshal Admiral (posthumous)

Commands: Combined Fleet

Battles: Russo-Japanese War; Pearl Harbor; Midway

Isoroku Yamamoto (山本 五十六, Yamamoto Isoroku; 4 April 1884 – 18 April 1943) was a Marshal Admiral of the Imperial Japanese Navy and Commander-in-Chief of the Combined Fleet during the first two years of the Pacific War. He planned the attack on Pearl Harbor and the Battle of Midway, and is regarded as one of Japan's most capable naval strategists, whose early death was a heavy blow to Japanese morale.

Early life and career

Born Isoroku Takano in Nagaoka, he was later adopted into the Yamamoto family. He graduated from the Imperial Japanese Naval Academy in 1904 and was wounded at the Battle of Tsushima in the Russo-Japanese War, losing two fingers. Yamamoto studied at Harvard University in 1919–21 and served as naval attaché in Washington, D.C., acquiring fluent English and a first-hand appreciation of American industrial power. A pioneer of naval aviation, he commanded the carrier Akagi, headed the Navy's aeronautics bureau, and rose to Vice-Minister of the Navy. He opposed Japan's alliance with Germany and Italy and war with the United States, receiving death threats from militarists, yet as a loyal officer he planned for war once it was decided.

Pacific War

Appointed Commander-in-Chief of the Combined Fleet in 1939, Yamamoto designed the surprise carrier strike on Pearl Harbor of 7 December 1941, hoping to cripple the US Pacific Fleet and buy time for Japan to fortify a defensive perimeter. He reportedly warned that he could "run wild" for six months to a year but had "utterly no confidence" thereafter. The stunning tactical success at Pearl Harbor was followed in June 1942 by the disastrous defeat at Midway, where four Japanese carriers were sunk. Yamamoto thereafter directed a war of attrition around Guadalcanal that Japan could not sustain.

Death

On 18 April 1943, American codebreakers, having decrypted his itinerary, enabled P-38 Lightning fighters from Henderson Field to ambush his aircraft over Bougainville in Operation Vengeance. Yamamoto's Mitsubishi G4M bomber was shot down; his body was recovered by a Japanese search party and he was cremated and given a state funeral in Tokyo. His ashes were divided between Tama Cemetery and his birthplace in Nagaoka.
