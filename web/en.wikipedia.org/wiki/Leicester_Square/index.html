Leicester Square

Leicester Square is a pedestrianised square in the West End of London, England. Laid out in the 1670s on land belonging to the Earl of Leicester, it became one of London's principal entertainment districts and is regarded as the heart of British cinema, hosting major film premieres at its large cinemas. The square and its surroundings include theatres, hotels, restaurants, nightclubs, and the TKTS theatre ticket booth.

History

The square was developed from Leicester Fields after Robert Sidney, 2nd Earl of Leicester, built Leicester House on the site in the 1630s; residential squares followed in the 1670s. During the eighteenth century the area was fashionable, home to artists including William Hogarth and Joshua Reynolds, and Leicester House briefly housed Frederick, Prince of Wales. In the nineteenth century the square took on a more commercial and theatrical character with music halls and exhibitions, a trajectory that continued in the twentieth century as cinemas replaced earlier venues.

Cinemas and entertainment

Leicester Square is home to several major cinemas, including the Odeon Luxe Leicester Square, which opened in 1937 and hosts many national and world film premieres, the Empire (now part of Cineworld), Vue West End, and the Prince Charles Cinema, known for repertory programming. Red-carpet premiere events regularly close off the square. Nearby are the theatres of Shaftesbury Avenue and the attractions of London's Chinatown to the south.

Features

The centre of the square is a public garden containing a statue of William Shakespeare, installed in the nineteenth century. Following a major redevelopment completed in time for the 2012 London Olympics, the square received new paving, water features, and statues of film characters including Charlie Chaplin, Mary Poppins, Paddington Bear, and Harry Potter, reflecting its cinema heritage. Leicester Square tube station, on the Piccadilly and Northern lines, serves the square.
