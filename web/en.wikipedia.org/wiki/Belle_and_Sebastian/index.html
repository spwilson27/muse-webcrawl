Belle and Sebastian

OriginGlasgow, Scotland
Formed1996
GenresIndie pop, chamber pop
Lead singer/songwriterStuart Murdoch
LabelsJeepster, Rough Trade, Matador

Belle and Sebastian are a Scottish indie pop band formed in Glasgow in 1996. Led by singer-songwriter Stuart Murdoch, the band has released a string of acclaimed albums characterised by literate, wistful songwriting, gentle melodies and lush, 1960s-influenced arrangements. The name comes from Belle et Sébastien, the 1965 novel by French writer Cécile Aubry about a boy and his dog.

History

Murdoch assembled the band while recovering from chronic fatigue syndrome, recording the debut album Tigermilk as a college project in 1996; its initial pressing of 1,000 vinyl copies became highly collectable. The follow-up, If You're Feeling Sinister (1996), is widely regarded as one of the finest albums of the 1990s. Initially reclusive — refusing interviews and publicity photographs — the band nevertheless won Best Newcomer at the 1999 BRIT Awards, famously beating acts such as Steps in a public vote.

The Boy with the Arab Strap (1998) reached the UK top 20, and the band's sound became more expansive on Dear Catastrophe Waitress (2003, produced by Trevor Horn) and The Life Pursuit (2006). Murdoch also wrote and directed the musical film God Help the Girl (2014). Later albums include Girls in Peacetime Want to Dance (2015), How to Solve Our Human Problems (2018), A Bit of Previous (2022) and Late Developers (2023).

Style and influence

The band's delicate chamber pop, drawing on Nick Drake, the Smiths, Felt and 1960s girl groups, helped define the sound of indie pop in the late 1990s and influenced a generation of artists. Their early records on the independent Jeepster label built a devoted cult following, and If You're Feeling Sinister regularly appears on critics' lists of the greatest albums ever made.
