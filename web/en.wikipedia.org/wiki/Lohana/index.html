Lohana

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Lohanas are a Hindu trading community (jāti) native principally to Gujarat and Sindh, in present-day India and Pakistan. Historically merchants, traders, and administrators, Lohanas speak Gujarati or Sindhi depending on the region, and over the last two centuries have formed a notable diaspora in East Africa, the United Kingdom, and elsewhere.

Origins and history

Community traditions trace Lohana origins variously to the region of Lohargadh and to figures of the Ramayana era, while historians generally regard them as an old mercantile grouping of north-western India whose members, at various periods, were classified among Kshatriya or Vaishya varṇas as they moved between ruling, military, and commercial occupations. Under the British Raj, Gujarati Lohanas became prominent in trade between western India and East Africa; Lohana merchants were instrumental in commerce in Uganda, Kenya, and Tanzania, where many families settled until upheavals such as the 1972 expulsion of Asians from Uganda drove them to Britain and Canada. Sindhi Lohanas, the largest Hindu community of Sindh, migrated in large numbers to India at the Partition of 1947.

Culture

Lohanas are predominantly Hindu, with devotion to Krishna and, among Sindhi Lohanas, to Jhulelal, the patron deity of Sindhi Hindus; some families also follow Swaminarayan or Jain traditions. Common Lohana surnames include Thakkar, Gadhia, Kotak, Madhani, Ruparel, Nathwani, Popat, and (among Sindhi Lohanas) names ending in -ani, such as Advani. Community associations and caste councils (mahajans) historically regulated trade and welfare, and Lohana-run trusts continue to operate schools, halls, and charities in Gujarat and in the diaspora. The businessman and political figure Dhirubhai Ambani's community background was Modh Bania rather than Lohana, but other Gujarati industrial families of Lohana origin, such as those behind several East African trading houses, are frequently cited in community histories.
