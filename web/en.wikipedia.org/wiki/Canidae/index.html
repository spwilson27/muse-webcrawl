Canidae is a biological family of dog-like carnivorans, colloquially referred to as dogs, and constitutes a clade. A member of this family is a canid. The family includes three subfamilies: the Caninae (all living canids), and the extinct Borophaginae and Hesperocyoninae. Living canids include wolves, foxes, coyotes, jackals, and domestic dogs.

Canids are found on all continents except Antarctica, having arrived independently or accompanying humans over extended periods. They range in size from the 2-metre (6.6 ft) grey wolf to the 24-centimetre (9.4 in) fennec fox.

Characteristics

The body forms of canids are similar, typically having long muzzles, upright ears, teeth adapted for cracking bones and slicing flesh, long legs, and bushy tails. They are mostly social animals, living together in family units or small groups and behaving cooperatively. Canids communicate by scent signals, vocalisations (including howling, barking, and whining), and body language.

Evolution

The Canidae first appeared in North America during the Eocene, roughly 40 million years ago. The family diversified into the fox-like Vulpini and the true dog tribe Canini. The domestic dog was domesticated from wolves at least 15,000 years ago, becoming the first domesticated species.
