Bornc. 260–265, probably Caesarea, Roman Palestine

OccupationBishop of Caesarea, historian, theologian

Eusebius of Caesarea (c. 260/265 – c. 339), also known as Eusebius Pamphilus, was a Greek historian of Christianity, exegete, and bishop of Caesarea (from c. 313). He is often called the "father of church history" for his Ecclesiastical History, the earliest surviving full-scale history of the Christian Church, and his Chronicle, a foundational work of chronology that synchronized biblical and pagan history.

Life

Eusebius studied at Caesarea under the scholar Pamphilus, a disciple of Origen, whose great library shaped his work; he took the name "Pamphilus" in his teacher's honour. He witnessed the Diocletianic persecution, survived it, and became a close associate of the emperor Constantine, whose biography he wrote (Life of Constantine) and whom he praised as a divinely appointed ruler. He participated in the Council of Nicaea (325), where he presented a baptismal creed and initially accommodated himself to its settlement, though his theology had subordinationist tendencies associated with Origenism.

Works

Besides the Ecclesiastical History and Chronicle, his works include the Onomasticon (a gazetteer of biblical place names, still used by archaeologists), the Praeparatio evangelica and Demonstratio evangelica (apologetic works quoting many otherwise-lost pagan texts), works against Marcellus of Ancyra, and commentaries. Legend also credits his scriptorium with producing fifty Bibles commissioned by Constantine.
