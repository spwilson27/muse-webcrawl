Enhanced Fujita scale

The Enhanced Fujita scale (abbreviated as EF-Scale) rates tornado intensity based on the severity of the damage they cause. It is used in some countries, including the United States and France. The scale was developed in recognition that the Fujita scale, which had been in use since 1971, required revision to better align wind speeds with the damage caused by tornadoes.

The Enhanced Fujita scale replaced the Fujita scale in the United States on 1 February 2007. Like the original scale, it has six categories, from EF0 to EF5, representing increasing severity of damage.

History

The original Fujita scale was introduced by T. Theodore Fujita in 1971. Over the following decades, meteorologists and engineers noted inconsistencies: some tornadoes rated highly had produced less damage than expected, and the wind speeds assigned to the original categories were believed to be too high. A forum of experts convened in 2001 began work on a revised scale, which was formally unveiled at a meeting of the American Meteorological Society in 2004 and implemented by the National Weather Service in 2007.

Damage indicators and degrees of damage

The scale is a set of wind estimates based on damage. It uses 28 damage indicators (DIs), including small barns and outbuildings, trees, strip malls, schools, and one- or two-family houses. Each indicator is evaluated against a set of degrees of damage (DoD) ranging from visible damage to total destruction. When tornado-related damage is surveyed, the indicator and degree of damage are compared with a table of estimated wind speeds, and the highest rating applicable to the path of the storm is assigned.

Classification

Because the scale is damage-based, a tornado that strikes no damage indicators cannot be rated above EF0 even if radar measurements suggest much stronger winds. This limitation has been debated following events such as the 2013 El Reno tornado, whose mobile radar-measured winds exceeded 300 mph (480 km/h) but whose damage supported only an EF3 rating.
