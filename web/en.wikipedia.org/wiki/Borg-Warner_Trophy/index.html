The Borg-Warner Trophy is the trophy presented to the winner of the Indianapolis 500 automobile race. Named for its sponsor, the automotive supplier BorgWarner, it has been awarded since 1936. The trophy is famous for bearing the sculpted likeness of every winning driver, making it one of the most recognizable awards in motorsport.

Design

The trophy stands 5 feet 4 inches tall and weighs about 110 pounds. It is made of sterling silver and mounted on a multi-tiered base. Each winner's face is sculpted in bas-relief on one of the squares covering the trophy's body and base, along with the winner's name, the year, and the winning speed. Unusually for a major sports trophy, it also carries the likeness of Anton "Tony" Hulman, the longtime owner of the Indianapolis Motor Speedway, rendered in gold.

History

The trophy was commissioned in 1935 and first awarded to Louis Meyer in 1936. As the squares filled with faces, the base was enlarged in 1986 and again in 2004, when a new base added space for winners through 2034. Because the trophy is too large and valuable to travel extensively, winners receive a smaller replica, known as the "Baby Borg," for permanent keeping.

Ceremony

The trophy is presented in Victory Lane immediately after the race, where the winning driver traditionally celebrates with a bottle of milk. It remains on permanent display at the Indianapolis Motor Speedway Museum except for ceremonial appearances each May.
