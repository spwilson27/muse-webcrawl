Boarding (attack)

Boarding
TypeNaval tactic
EraAntiquity to present
PurposeCapture of an enemy vessel

Boarding is an attack in naval warfare in which personnel from one vessel board an opposing vessel, usually to capture it. It was one of the oldest tactics at sea, dominant in the age of galleys and again in the age of sail, before long-range gunnery made closing with an enemy ship hazardous.

History

Roman forces used the corvus, a spiked boarding bridge, to turn sea battles into infantry fights during the First Punic War. In the age of sail, boarding followed attempts to disable a ship's rigging or crew with cannon fire; attackers swarmed aboard with cutlasses, pistols and pikes. Famous boarding actions include the capture of the HMS Serapis by John Paul Jones in 1779.

Modern use

Today boarding is conducted mainly for law enforcement, customs inspection, anti-piracy and visit, board, search and seizure (VBSS) operations, often by special forces or coast guard teams using fast boats or helicopters.
