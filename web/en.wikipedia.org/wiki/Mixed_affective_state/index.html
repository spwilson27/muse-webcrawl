Mixed affective state

A mixed affective state, historically known as a mixed episode or mixed state, is a condition in which features of both mania (or hypomania) and depression occur simultaneously or in rapid sequence. It is most often associated with bipolar disorder, though mixed features can also be specified in major depressive disorder.

Features

A person in a mixed state may experience elevated or racing energy and agitation together with a depressed mood, guilt, or hopelessness. Common presentations include dysphoric mania — an agitated, irritable elevated state — and depressive episodes with racing thoughts, pressured speech, or psychomotor agitation.

Clinical significance

Mixed states are associated with a higher risk of suicide than purely depressive or manic episodes, and they can be harder to treat: antidepressants used alone may worsen agitation, so mood stabilizers and atypical antipsychotics are commonly used. The concept descends from Emil Kraepelin's early descriptions of mixed forms of manic-depressive illness.

Diagnostic criteria

The DSM-5 replaced the older "mixed episode" category with a "with mixed features" specifier that can be applied to manic, hypomanic, or depressive episodes, requiring the presence of a threshold number of symptoms from the opposite pole.
