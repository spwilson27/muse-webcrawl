Particular judgment

In Christian theology, particular judgment is the divine judgment that each human soul undergoes immediately after death, in contradistinction to the general or Last Judgment, when all humanity will be judged together at the end of time. According to the doctrine, particularly as taught by the Roman Catholic Church, at the moment of death each person receives their eternal retribution: admission to heaven (directly or after purification in purgatory) or condemnation to hell.

Doctrine

The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that every person receives their eternal recompense in their immortal soul at the very moment of death, in a particular judgment that refers their life to Christ. The doctrine was formally defined by Pope Benedict XII in the constitution Benedictus Deus (1336), which affirmed that souls are rewarded or punished immediately after death, before the resurrection of the body and the general judgment.

Biblical basis

Key references
Hebrews 9:27"It is appointed for mortals to die once, and after that the judgment"
Luke 16Parable of the rich man and Lazarus
Luke 23:43"Today you will be with me in Paradise"

Supporters cite the parable of Lazarus and the rich man, in which both men find themselves in their respective states immediately upon dying, and the words of Jesus to the penitent thief on the cross. The particular judgment is distinguished from the general judgment not in outcome — the two are held to agree — but in scope: the first concerns the individual soul alone, the second the whole person, body and soul, and the public, social consequences of every life.

In other traditions

Orthodox Christianity also teaches a judgment of the soul after death, though with varying emphases and imagery, while many Protestant traditions affirm an immediate post-mortem destiny while rejecting purgatory. Some traditions instead teach soul sleep until the general resurrection and judgment.
