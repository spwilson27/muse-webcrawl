Vestibulo-ocular reflex

The vestibulo-ocular reflex (VOR) is a reflex that acts to stabilise images on the retinas of the eyes during head movement. It produces an eye movement in the direction opposite to head movement, thus preserving the image on the centre of the visual field. For example, when the head moves to the right, the eyes move to the left, meaning the image that a person sees stays in the same place even though their head has turned. Since slight head movements are present all the time, the VOR is important for stabilising vision.

The VOR relies on information about head rotation and translation provided by the vestibular organs in the inner ear: the semicircular canals and the otolith organs. Signals from these organs are carried by the vestibular nerve to vestibular nuclei in the brainstem, which coordinate compensatory movements of the extraocular muscles through the abducens, trochlear, and oculomotor nuclei. The VOR is extremely fast, with a latency of only about 7–10 milliseconds, and is vital for clear vision during locomotion.

Mechanism

The reflex arc has three main components: a sensory receptor in the semicircular canals that detects head acceleration, a central processing component in the vestibular nuclei of the brainstem, and motor neurons that drive the eye muscles. When the head turns, endolymph in the canals lags behind, deflecting hair cells whose signals increase on one side and decrease on the other. The resulting imbalance drives the eyes in the equal and opposite direction, with a gain close to 1.0 in darkness under ideal conditions.

Clinical significance

Damage to the vestibular system can cause the VOR gain to become abnormally low or high, producing oscillopsia — the sensation that the visual world moves or bounces during head movement. The head impulse test is a bedside method for assessing VOR function: a rapid, small-amplitude head turn is applied, and a corrective saccade indicates a deficient reflex on the side toward which the head was turned. The VOR can also be tested with caloric stimulation and rotational chair testing.
