Kerr–Newman metric

The Kerr–Newman metric is the most general asymptotically flat, stationary solution of the Einstein–Maxwell equations in general relativity. It describes the geometry of spacetime around a charged, rotating mass and is characterised by three parameters: mass, angular momentum, and electric charge.

History

Roy Kerr found the solution for uncharged rotating black holes in 1963, and Ezra Newman generalised it to the charged case in 1965. The solution reduces to the Kerr metric when charge is zero, to the Reissner–Nordström metric when rotation is zero, and to the Schwarzschild metric when both vanish.

Properties

Like the Kerr solution, the Kerr–Newman geometry possesses an ergosphere and ring singularity, and admits a ring-shaped region allowing closed timelike curves inside the inner horizon. The "no-hair theorem" states that a black hole in equilibrium is fully described by these three quantities.
