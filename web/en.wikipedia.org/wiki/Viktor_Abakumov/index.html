Viktor Abakumov

Viktor Semyonovich Abakumov (Russian: Виктор Семёнович Абакумов; 24 April 1908 – 19 December 1954) was a high-ranking Soviet security official, head of SMERSH military counterintelligence from 1943 to 1946, and Minister of State Security (MGB) of the USSR from 1946 to 1951. He was one of the most powerful and feared figures of the late Stalin period.

Career

Abakumov joined the Cheka as a teenager and rose through the NKVD during the Great Purge. During the Second World War he headed SMERSH ("Death to Spies"), the Red Army's counterintelligence organisation, answering directly to Stalin, and organised both the hunt for German agents and repression within the ranks. After the war he became minister of state security, overseeing the post-war purges, the Leningrad Affair, and the early stages of the anti-"cosmopolitan" campaign.

Fall and death

Abakumov was arrested in July 1951, accused of failing to act on the alleged "Doctors' plot" conspiracy. After Stalin's death in 1953 he remained in prison; following the fall of Lavrentiy Beria, he was tried in Leningrad in December 1954 — formally for his role in fabricating the Leningrad Affair — sentenced to death, and shot.
