Papunya is a small Aboriginal community in the Northern Territory of Australia, about 240 km northwest of Alice Springs. It is internationally famous as the birthplace of the Western Desert art movement, which transformed Aboriginal painting into one of the world's most celebrated contemporary art movements.

History

Papunya was established in 1959 as a government ration station and settlement, to which Pintupi, Luritja, Warlpiri, Arrernte and Anmatyerre people were brought, often forcibly, from surrounding desert country. Conditions were difficult, and overcrowding and disease were severe in the early years.

The art movement

In 1971 schoolteacher Geoffrey Bardon encouraged senior men at Papunya to paint a traditional design on the school wall; led by painters such as Kaapa Tjampitjinpa, the men soon began painting Dreaming (Tjukurrpa) designs on boards and canvas. In 1972 they founded the artists' company Papunya Tula ("Honey Ant Dreaming place"), which became the flagship of an art movement that now supports communities across the desert and whose works hang in major galleries worldwide.
