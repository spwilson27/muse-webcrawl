Submanifold

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, a submanifold of a manifold M is a subset S which is itself a manifold with a lower dimension, together with a compatible way of regarding S as sitting inside M. Submanifolds generalise the notion of a curve lying on a surface, or a surface lying in three-dimensional space.

Definitions

An immersed submanifold is the image of an immersion — a smooth map whose derivative is everywhere injective — from a manifold S into M. The image need not be a manifold in the subspace topology: a figure-eight curve produced by an immersed circle in the plane is the classic example.

An embedded submanifold satisfies the stronger condition that the inclusion map is an embedding, so that S with its own manifold topology agrees with the topology it inherits from M. Every embedded submanifold is locally "flat": around each of its points there is a chart of M in which S appears as a coordinate slice. Embedded submanifolds arise naturally as level sets of smooth functions — by the implicit function theorem, the solution set of equations F(x) = 0 at points where the derivative of F is surjective is an embedded submanifold.

Examples

Open subsets of a manifold are submanifolds of the same dimension. Curves in three-dimensional space and surfaces in three-dimensional space are submanifolds of codimension two and one respectively; a submanifold of codimension one is called a hypersurface. The sphere, considered as the set of unit vectors in Euclidean space, is an embedded submanifold defined by a single equation.
