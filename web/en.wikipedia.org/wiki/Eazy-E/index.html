Eric Lynn Wright (September 7, 1964 – March 26, 1995), known professionally as Eazy-E, was an American rapper who propelled West Coast rap and gangsta rap by leading the group N.W.A and its label, Ruthless Records. He is often referred to as the "Godfather of Gangsta Rap".

Early life

Eric Lynn Wright was born to Richard and Kathie Wright on September 7, 1964, in Compton, California, a Los Angeles suburb once known for gang activity and crime. His father was a postal worker and his mother was a grade-school administrator. Wright dropped out of high school in the tenth grade, but later earned a general equivalency diploma (GED). He supported himself primarily by selling drugs before investing in Ruthless Records.

Career

In 1987, Wright founded Ruthless Records with manager Jerry Heller, using money he had earned from drug dealing. He recruited Dr. Dre, Ice Cube, DJ Yella, MC Ren, and Arabian Prince to form N.W.A, whose 1988 album Straight Outta Compton became a landmark of gangsta rap, drawing controversy for its explicit lyrics and the track "Fuck tha Police". Eazy-E's own solo debut, Eazy-Duz-It (1988), was also a commercial success.

After Ice Cube and Dr. Dre left N.W.A amid financial disputes, Eazy-E feuded publicly with Dre, a rivalry played out in diss tracks on both sides. Ruthless Records also launched the career of the group Bone Thugs-n-Harmony in the mid-1990s.

Death and legacy

In February 1995, Wright was admitted to Cedars-Sinai Medical Center in Los Angeles with what he believed to be asthma; he was instead diagnosed with HIV/AIDS. He announced his illness in a public statement on March 16, 1995, and died from complications of AIDS on March 26, 1995, at the age of 30. His posthumous album Str8 off tha Streetz of Muthaphukkin Compton was released in 1996. Eazy-E is widely credited with helping to bring gangsta rap into the mainstream.
