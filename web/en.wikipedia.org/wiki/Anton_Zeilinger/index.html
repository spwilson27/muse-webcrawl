Anton Zeilinger

Anton Zeilinger

[Image: Photograph of Anton Zeilinger with his white beard in a quantum optics laboratory]
Zeilinger in Vienna.

Anton Zeilinger (born 20 May 1945) is an Austrian physicist and Nobel laureate. From the 1990s his Vienna group turned Quantum entanglement into technology: the first Quantum teleportation of an independent qubit (1997), entanglement swapping, dense coding, and ever-longer distances — culminating in satellite-based entanglement distribution and quantum communication with China's Micius satellite.

He shared the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics with John Clauser and Alain Aspect for experiments with entangled photons. Zeilinger's group also performed the loophole-conscious Bell tests and matter-wave interference with large molecules (fullerenes), and he has served as president of the Austrian Academy of Sciences.

Career highlights

After neutron interferometry work at Vienna and MIT, Zeilinger built Austria's quantum optics effort from scratch. Landmark results include teleportation across the Danube (2004) and across 143 km between Canary Islands (2012), multi-photon GHZ entanglement, and the 2017 Micius experiments distributing entanglement over 1,200 km of space-to-ground links — foundations of any future quantum internet.
