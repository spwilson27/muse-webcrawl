Otto III (980 – 23 January 1002) was Holy Roman Emperor from 996 until his death, and King of Germany from the age of three. The son of Otto II and the Byzantine princess Theophanu, he pursued an ambitious programme of imperial renewal — a "renovation of the Roman Empire" — that made Rome the centre of his rule and briefly reshaped the politics of Latin Christendom.

Childhood and regency

Otto was crowned King of Germany at Aachen at Christmas 983, days before news arrived of his father's death in Rome. Control of the child king was contested between the dowager empress Theophanu, who governed effectively as regent until her death in 991, and his grandmother Adelaide of Italy thereafter. Otto assumed personal rule in 994.

Rule and the Renovatio

Otto intervened repeatedly in Italy: he installed his cousin Bruno of Carinthia as Pope Gregory V in 996 and was crowned emperor by him, then, after Gregory's expulsion, restored him by force and installed his former tutor Gerbert of Aurillac as Pope Sylvester II (999) — a name evoking the age of Constantine. Otto took up residence on the Aventine in Rome and revived Roman court titles and ceremonial, styling his project the renovatio imperii Romanorum, "renewal of the Empire of the Romans".

In the year 1000 he made a celebrated pilgrimage to the tomb of his friend St. Adalbert of Prague at Gniezno, where he recognised an independent Polish archbishopric and treated Duke Bolesław the Brave as a partner, and he opened the tomb of Charlemagne at Aachen. His diplomacy envisaged a family of Christian realms — Francia, Germania, Italia, and Sclavinia, the four provinces depicted offering homage in his Gospel book.

Death

Roman revolts in 1001 forced Otto out of the city. While preparing to retake Rome he died at the castle of Paterno near Civita Castellana on 23 January 1002, probably of malaria, aged twenty-one and unmarried. His body was carried back over the Alps amid fighting and buried beside Charlemagne at Aachen. He was succeeded by his cousin Henry II, and his grand design for a Rome-centred empire died with him.
