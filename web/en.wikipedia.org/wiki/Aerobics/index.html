Aerobics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A group aerobics class (image placeholder)

Aerobics is a form of physical exercise that combines rhythmic aerobic exercise with stretching and strength training routines, with the goal of improving all elements of fitness — flexibility, muscular strength, and cardiovascular fitness. It is usually performed to music and may be practised in a group setting led by an instructor, although it can be done solo and without musical accompaniment.

History

The term and the method were developed by Dr. Kenneth H. Cooper, a physician and former Air Force officer, whose 1968 book Aerobics introduced a point system for improving the cardiovascular system through running, walking, swimming, and cycling. In the late 1970s and 1980s, dance-based aerobic classes popularized by figures such as Judi Sheppard Missett (Jazzercise) and Jane Fonda, whose 1982 workout video became a bestseller, turned aerobics into a mass fitness phenomenon.

Styles

  • Step aerobics — choreography performed on an elevated platform, introduced by Gin Miller in the late 1980s.
  • Water aerobics — low-impact exercise performed in a pool, popular for rehabilitation and older adults.
  • Dance aerobics — classes modelled on dance styles, later including programs such as Zumba.
  • Sport aerobics — a competitive discipline governed by the FIG, judged on choreographed routines demonstrating strength, flexibility, and aerobic movement patterns.

Health effects

Regular aerobic exercise is associated with improved cardiovascular health, reduced risk of chronic diseases such as type 2 diabetes, improved mood, and better weight management. Health authorities generally recommend at least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity for adults.
