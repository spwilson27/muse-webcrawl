Chimney sweep

OccupationTradesperson
TasksRemoving soot and creosote from chimneys and flues
ToolsBrushes, rods, vacuums

A chimney sweep is a tradesperson who cleans chimneys, flues and fireplaces to remove soot and creosote, reducing the risk of chimney fires and carbon monoxide poisoning.

History

In 18th- and 19th-century Britain, master sweeps employed small boys as "climbing boys" to ascend chimneys; the practice caused injuries and a distinctive cancer, and was progressively restricted by law, finally becoming an offence in 1875 after the death of 12-year-old George Brewster. In parts of Europe, sweeps in traditional dress with top hats are considered symbols of good luck.

Modern practice

Modern sweeps use rotary brushes, cameras and industrial vacuums, and professional bodies such as the Chimney Safety Institute of America certify sweeps and promote annual inspections.
