Methylene blue

FormulaC16H18ClN3S
Molar mass319.85 g/mol
AppearanceDark green crystals; deep blue solution
UsesBiological stain, medication, redox indicator

An aqueous solution of methylene blue

Methylthioninium chloride, commonly called methylene blue, is a salt used as a dye and as a medication. As a medication, it is mainly used to treat methemoglobinemia and can be used as an antidote to cyanide poisoning; it also has roles in surgery as a dye for sentinel lymph node mapping and in the treatment of vasoplegia. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines. As a biological stain, it is used in microscopy, for example to stain cells and nuclei and in the Gram stain procedure's alternatives; it is also a redox indicator in chemistry demonstrations such as the "blue bottle" experiment.

Chemistry

Methylene blue is a heterocyclic aromatic compound, a thiazine dye first prepared by Heinrich Caro in 1876. It dissolves in water to give an intense blue solution. It is reversibly reduced to a colorless form, leucomethylene blue, which underlies both its redox-indicator behavior and its therapeutic action in reducing methemoglobin (Fe³⁺) back to hemoglobin (Fe²⁺) at low doses; paradoxically, at high doses it can induce methemoglobinemia.

History

Methylene blue is considered the first fully synthetic drug used in medicine: Paul Ehrlich and Paul Guttmann used it to treat malaria in 1891, which influenced Ehrlich's development of chemotherapy. It was later used as an antipsychotic probe and antidepressant research tool because of its monoamine oxidase-inhibiting activity, which also creates a risk of serotonin syndrome when combined with serotonergic drugs.
