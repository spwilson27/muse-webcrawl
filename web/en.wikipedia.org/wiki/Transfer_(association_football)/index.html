Transfer (association football)

In professional football, a transfer is the action taken whenever a player under contract moves between clubs. It refers to the transferring of a player's registration from one association football club to another. In general, the buying club pays a fee (the transfer fee) to the selling club as compensation for releasing the player from his contract.

Transfer windows

Under FIFA regulations, transfers may only take place during designated transfer windows set by each national association: typically a long window before the season (in summer in Europe) and a shorter one mid-season (in January in Europe). A move outside a window generally can only be completed when the next window opens. Players out of contract may sign as free agents at any time.

Types of transfer

Permanent transfers may include buyout or release clauses fixed in a player's contract. Loan transfers move a player temporarily, sometimes with an option or obligation to buy. Free transfers occur when a contract expires — a freedom greatly expanded by the 1995 Bosman ruling of the European Court of Justice. Other mechanisms include swap deals, co-ownership (historically used in Italy), and sell-on clauses entitling the selling club to a share of a future fee.

Record fees

The world record transfer fee is the €222 million paid by Paris Saint-Germain to Barcelona for Neymar in 2017, which activated his release clause. Other landmark fees include Kylian Mbappé's move to PSG and Philippe Coutinho's transfer to Barcelona. Transfer activity is tracked and celebrated in media coverage, increasingly driven by agents, data analytics, and FIFA's Transfer Matching System, which since 2010 has required international transfers to be recorded centrally.
