Hindu mythology is the body of narratives found in Hindu texts — the Vedas, the epics Mahābhārata and Rāmāyaṇa, and the Purāṇas — that explain the origins of the world, the deeds of gods, goddesses, heroes and demons, and the ideals of dharma. Transmitted orally for millennia and constantly retold in regional languages, temple sculpture, dance-drama and popular media, it forms a shared narrative world across Hindu traditions.

Gods and cosmic order

The Trimūrti assigns creation to Brahma, preservation to Vishnu and destruction/transformation to Shiva, though devotees of each may exalt their god as supreme. Vishnu descends in avatars — including Rama, hero of the Rāmāyaṇa, and Krishna, counsellor of the Bhagavad Gita — whenever dharma declines. The Goddess (Devi) in forms such as Durga, Kali (goddess) and Lakshmi is worshipped in her own right, especially in Shaktism. Time is cyclical, measured in yugas and kalpas, with creation and dissolution endlessly repeated.

Stories and themes

Signature narratives include the churning of the ocean of milk by gods and demons for the nectar of immortality; Shiva's marriage to Parvati and the birth of their sons Ganesha and Kartikeya; Krishna's childhood exploits and the Kurukshetra war; and Rama's exile, the abduction of Sita and the defeat of Ravana. Mythological stories encode ritual, geography — countless places claim events of the epics — and moral teaching, and their retelling (from Tulsidas's Rāmcaritmānas to televised serials watched by tens of millions) remains a living practice.
