La Bamba

"La Bamba" (pronounced [la ˈβamba]) is a Mexican folk song, originally from the state of Veracruz, best known from a 1958 adaptation by Ritchie Valens, a Top 40 hit in the U.S. charts. Valens's version is ranked number 345 on Rolling Stone magazine's list of the 500 Greatest Songs of All Time, and it is the only song on the list sung in Spanish. In 1987, a cover by Los Lobos, recorded for the biographical film La Bamba about Valens, reached number one in several countries.

Origins

The song is a classic of the son jarocho genre, the regional folk music of Veracruz that blends Spanish, African, and indigenous influences. Its exact origins are unknown, but it is generally traced to the late 17th or 18th century; the name may derive from a Bantu word for a dance or from the Spanish verb bambolear, "to sway." Traditionally the song is performed with jarana, requinto jarocho, and harp, and dancers tie a ribbon into a bow with their feet while dancing on a wooden platform (the tarima).

Ritchie Valens version

Ritchie Valens recorded the song in 1958, transforming the folk tune into a rock and roll number with a distinctive guitar riff. Released as the B-side of "Donna," it reached number 22 on the Billboard Hot 100. Valens, who did not speak Spanish fluently, learned the lyrics phonetically. The song helped open American popular music to Spanish-language rock and is regarded as a foundational recording of Chicano rock.

Lyrics

The best-known refrain is: "Para bailar la Bamba, para bailar la Bamba se necesita una poca de gracia…" ("To dance the Bamba, you need a bit of grace"). The traditional song has countless improvised verses.
