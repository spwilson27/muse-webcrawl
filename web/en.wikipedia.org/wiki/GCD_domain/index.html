GCD domain

In mathematics, a GCD domain is an integral domain R with the property that any two non-zero elements have a greatest common divisor (GCD) — that is, there is a unique minimal principal ideal containing the ideal generated by the two elements. Every unique factorization domain (UFD) and every Bézout domain is a GCD domain, but the converse statements do not hold.

Properties

In a GCD domain, any two elements also have a least common multiple, and the usual identities relating gcd and lcm hold. A GCD domain is integrally closed. An integral domain is a GCD domain if and only if every finite intersection of principal ideals, when non-empty, is principal. Schreier domains and GCD domains are related refinements: a GCD domain is a Schreier domain, and the class sits between Prüfer domains and Bézout domains in the hierarchy of commutative rings studied in multiplicative ideal theory.

Examples

Examples include polynomial rings over UFDs, valuation domains that are not fields, and certain non-Noetherian rings such as the ring of entire functions on the complex plane (a Bézout domain).
