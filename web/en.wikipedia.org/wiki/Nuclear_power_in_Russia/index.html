Nuclear power in Russia

Nuclear power in Russia accounts for roughly one fifth of the country's electricity generation. Russia operates a fleet of more than 35 reactors at about a dozen nuclear power plants, managed by Rosenergoatom, a subsidiary of the state corporation Rosatom. Russia is also a leading exporter of nuclear reactor technology.

History

The world's first grid-connected nuclear power plant, the Obninsk Nuclear Power Plant (5 MWe), began operation in the Soviet Union in 1954. The Soviet programme deployed RBMK and VVER designs at scale. The 1986 Chernobyl disaster — at an RBMK plant in Soviet Ukraine — led to major safety reforms. After a post-Soviet slowdown, construction resumed in the 2000s with VVER-1000 and VVER-1200 units.

Fleet and technology

Modern Russian plants use the VVER-1200 (AES-2006) design with passive safety features and a core catcher. Russia operates the world's only floating nuclear power plant, Akademik Lomonosov, moored at Pevek in Chukotka since 2020, and fast breeder reactors (BN-600 and BN-800 at Beloyarsk). Rosatom has built or is building reactors in countries including Turkey, Bangladesh, Egypt, India, and China.

Policy

The Russian government supports expanding nuclear generation, developing closed fuel-cycle technology with fast reactors, and maintaining a large share of the global market for enrichment services and fresh fuel supply.
