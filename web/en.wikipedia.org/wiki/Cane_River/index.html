The Cane River (French: Rivière aux Cannes) is a roughly 30-mile (48 km) waterway in Natchitoches Parish, Louisiana, United States. It is an oxbow lake formed from a former channel of the Red River of the South, which changed course in the 19th century, leaving the Cane River as a long, narrow lake running through the city of Natchitoches and south past plantations and Creole communities.

History and culture

The river takes its name from the river cane that once grew along its banks. Under French and Spanish colonial rule, plantations were established along its banks, and the area became home to a distinctive community of Louisiana Creoles of color, centred on Isle Brevelle. Historic plantation houses along the river — including Magnolia and Oakland plantations — are preserved within the Cane River Creole National Historical Park, and the region forms part of the Cane River National Heritage Area.

Geography

The Cane River runs roughly parallel to the modern Red River. Dams and weirs regulate its level, and it is used for recreation, fishing and local events in Natchitoches, including the annual Christmas Festival of Lights along the downtown riverbank.
