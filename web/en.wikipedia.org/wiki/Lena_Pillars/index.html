Lena Pillars

LocationSakha Republic (Yakutia), Russia
RiverLena River
Height150–300 m
RockCambrian limestone
UNESCOWorld Heritage Site (2012, natural)

The Lena Pillars (Russian: Ленские столбы) are a natural rock formation along the banks of the Lena River in far eastern Siberia, in the Sakha Republic of Russia. Towering limestone pillars between roughly 150 and 300 metres high stretch for some 80 kilometres along the river, about 200 km upstream from the city of Yakutsk.

Geology

The pillars formed from Cambrian marine limestones laid down over 500 million years ago, sculpted by the extreme continental climate of Yakutia — with annual temperature swings approaching 100 °C between summer and winter — through freeze-thaw erosion and river action. The surrounding Lena Pillars Nature Park also yields important Cambrian fossils documenting the early diversification of animal life.

Recognition

The site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2012 under natural criteria for its outstanding beauty and scientific significance. It is one of the best-known natural landmarks of the Russian Far East, typically visited by boat from Yakutsk.
