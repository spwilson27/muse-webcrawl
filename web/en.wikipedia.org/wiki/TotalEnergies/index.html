TotalEnergies SE is a French multinational integrated energy and petroleum company founded in 1924 and one of the seven supermajor oil companies. Its businesses cover the entire oil and gas chain, from crude oil and natural gas exploration and production to power generation, transportation, refining, petroleum product marketing, and international crude oil and product trading. It is also a large-scale chemicals manufacturer.

TotalEnergies has its head office in the Tour Coupole in La Défense district in Courbevoie, west of Paris. The company is a component of the Euro Stoxx 50 stock market index. In the 2023 Forbes Global 2000, TotalEnergies was ranked as the 21st largest company in the world.

History

The company was founded in 1924 as the Compagnie française des pétroles (CFP), at the initiative of the French government, to secure France's oil supplies after the First World War. It adopted the name Total in 1985 for its retail network and became Total SA in 1991. Following mergers with Belgium's Petrofina (forming TotalFina) and France's Elf Aquitaine (forming TotalFinaElf), the company reverted to the name Total in 2003.

In May 2021 the company changed its name from Total to TotalEnergies, reflecting its strategy of transforming into a broad energy company with growing investments in renewables and electricity, including solar, wind, hydrogen and battery storage through subsidiaries such as Saft.

Operations

TotalEnergies operates in more than 130 countries and employs around 100,000 people. Its segments include Exploration & Production, Integrated Gas, Renewables and Power, Refining & Chemicals, and Marketing & Services. The company operates a large service-station network under the TotalEnergies brand, particularly in Europe and Africa.

Controversies

The company has faced criticism and legal action over environmental damage, oil spills such as the 1999 Erika disaster off the coast of Brittany, and over its continued investment in fossil fuels amid the climate crisis. Its liquefied natural gas project in Mozambique and the East African Crude Oil Pipeline (EACOP) in Uganda and Tanzania have drawn opposition from environmental and human rights groups.
