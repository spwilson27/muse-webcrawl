Solaris (novel)

Solaris is a 1961 science fiction novel by Polish writer Stanisław Lem. It follows psychologist Kris Kelvin arriving at a station orbiting the planet Solaris, whose sentient ocean creates physical manifestations of the crew's memories.

Plot

Kelvin finds the station in disarray; one scientist has died by suicide, and the others are haunted by 'visitors' — Kelvin's visitor is his late wife Harey (Rheya in some translations), who died by suicide years earlier. The crew cannot rid themselves of the visitors and must confront guilt and the impossibility of truly knowing an alien intelligence.

Themes

The novel critiques anthropocentrism and the limits of science and communication: humanity seeks mirrors of itself rather than genuine contact. 'Solaristics', the fictional discipline studying Solaris, has accumulated vast data without understanding.

Adaptations

Filmed by Andrei Tarkovsky (1972), widely regarded as a classic, and by Steven Soderbergh (2002). Lem was dissatisfied with both adaptations' focus on human relationships over his philosophical intent.

Legacy

Solaris is among the most famous works of Polish literature and of philosophical science fiction.
