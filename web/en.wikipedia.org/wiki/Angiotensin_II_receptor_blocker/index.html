Angiotensin II receptor blocker

Angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs), formally AT1 receptor antagonists and also called sartans, are a class of pharmaceutical drugs used primarily to treat hypertension (high blood pressure) and heart failure. They block the activation of angiotensin II AT1 receptors, preventing the hormone angiotensin II from constricting blood vessels and stimulating aldosterone release. Losartan was the first ARB marketed, in 1995.

Mechanism of action

The renin–angiotensin system regulates blood pressure and fluid balance. Angiotensin II, acting through AT1 receptors, causes vasoconstriction, sympathetic activation, and aldosterone secretion. ARBs selectively block AT1 receptors, leaving AT2 receptors unopposed, which may contribute additional vasodilatory and antiproliferative effects. Unlike ACE inhibitors, ARBs do not inhibit the breakdown of bradykinin, so the persistent dry cough associated with ACE inhibitors is much less common.

Medical uses

ARBs are first-line therapy for hypertension and are commonly prescribed to patients intolerant of ACE inhibitors. They are also used in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, after myocardial infarction, and to slow the progression of kidney disease in patients with diabetes and proteinuria. Several are available in fixed-dose combinations with thiazide diuretics or calcium channel blockers.

Adverse effects

ARBs are generally well tolerated. Potential adverse effects include dizziness, hyperkalemia (raised potassium), and reduced kidney function, particularly in patients with bilateral renal artery stenosis. Like ACE inhibitors, they are contraindicated in pregnancy because of fetal toxicity. Combining an ARB with an ACE inhibitor is generally not recommended after trials showed added risk without benefit.

History

Following the success of ACE inhibitors, researchers sought a more selective way to block the renin–angiotensin system at the receptor. Losartan, developed at DuPont, was approved in 1995; valsartan, irbesartan, candesartan, telmisartan, eprosartan, olmesartan and azilsartan followed. Some ARBs, notably telmisartan, also act as partial agonists of PPAR-gamma, which has been studied for metabolic benefits.
