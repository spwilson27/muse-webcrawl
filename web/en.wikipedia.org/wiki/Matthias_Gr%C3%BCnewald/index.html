Matthias Grünewald (c. 1470–1475 – 31 August 1528) was a German Renaissance painter of religious works who ignored Renaissance classicism in favour of the expressive, emotional style of late medieval Central European art. His real name was Mathis Gothart; "Grünewald" was a 17th-century biographical invention.

Life

Little is documented of Grünewald's life. He worked for the archbishops of Mainz, painted in Frankfurt and Aschaffenburg, and also served as a hydraulic engineer. Suspected Lutheran sympathies cost him his post at Mainz in 1526, and he died in Halle in 1528.

Works

Only about ten paintings and some 35 drawings survive. His masterpiece is the Isenheim Altarpiece (1512–1516), painted for the hospital chapel of the Monastery of St Anthony at Isenheim, now in the Musée Unterlinden in Colmar. Its Crucifixion — an anguished, lacerated Christ against a darkened sky — is among the most powerful images in Western art, and its multi-winged construction unfolds scenes from the Annunciation to the Resurrection for patients suffering from ergotism.

Reputation

Forgotten for centuries, Grünewald was rediscovered by German Expressionists and modernists in the early 20th century; his work inspired Paul Hindemith's opera and symphony Mathis der Maler.
