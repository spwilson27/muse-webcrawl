James Chadwick

Sir James Chadwick (20 October 1891 – 24 July 1974) was a British physicist who received the 1935 Nobel Prize in Physics for his discovery of the neutron in 1932. A student and later colleague of Ernest Rutherford at Manchester and Cambridge, he was interned in Germany during the First World War while there on a scholarship visit.

Chadwick's neutron — a neutral particle of about proton mass, inferred from the penetrating 'beryllium radiation' of Bothe and the Joliot-Curies — completed the modern picture of the nucleus and opened the road to fission. During the Second World War Chadwick led the British Mission to the Manhattan Project and was a key liaison in Los Alamos, later becoming Master of Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge.
