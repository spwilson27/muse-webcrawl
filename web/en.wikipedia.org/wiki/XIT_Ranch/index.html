XIT Ranch

The XIT Ranch was a cattle ranch in the Texas Panhandle which operated from 1885 to 1912. Comprising about 3,000,000 acres (over 4,600 square miles) of land across ten counties, it was the largest ranch in the world under fence and is often described as the largest single cattle operation of its time.

Origins

In 1879 the Texas legislature appropriated three million acres of Panhandle land to finance a new state capitol building. The Capitol Freehold Land and Investment Company, an investor syndicate associated with Chicago businessmen Charles B. Farwell and John V. Farwell and British investors, accepted the land in exchange for building the granite Texas Capitol in Austin. To work the land, the syndicate organised the XIT Ranch; the brand XIT was famously (though probably apocryphally) later said to stand for "Ten in Texas", for the ten counties it covered.

Operation

At its height the ranch ran some 150,000 head of cattle, maintained over 1,500 miles of fence and employed several hundred cowboys across seven divisions, headquartered at Buffalo Springs and elsewhere. The land was progressively sold off after 1901 as investors sought returns, and the last cattle were sold in 1912; parcels of the former ranch are commemorated by the XIT Ranch Museum in Dalhart and the annual XIT Rodeo and Reunion.
