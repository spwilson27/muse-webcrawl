Estragole

Estragole (p-allylanisole, methyl chavicol) is a phenylpropene, a natural organic compound. It is a colorless to pale yellow liquid, although impure samples may appear yellow. It is a component of various trees and plants, including turpentine, basil, fennel, and tarragon, from which it takes its name, derived from Artemisia dracunculus.

Estragole
IUPAC: 1-allyl-4-methoxybenzene
Formula: C10H12O
Molar mass: 148.20 g/mol
CAS: 140-67-0

Occurrence and use

Estragole is the primary component of tarragon oil (60–75%) and is present in basil oil, pine oil, and anise. It is used in perfumes and as a food flavoring, imparting an anise-like, sweet flavor.

Safety

Estragole is suspected to be carcinogenic and genotoxic in rodents, leading food safety authorities such as the European Food Safety Authority to scrutinize its use as a flavoring additive, though exposure through normal culinary herbs is considered low.
