A Security Force Assistance Brigade (SFAB) is a specialised formation of the United States Army dedicated to advising, training and assisting the armed forces of partner nations. Six SFABs were established beginning in 2017 — five active-duty brigades aligned with the geographic combatant commands and one brigade in the Army National Guard — so that advisory missions would no longer require breaking apart conventional brigade combat teams.

Organisation

Each SFAB numbers roughly 800 senior non-commissioned officers and officers organised into small advisor teams mirroring the structure of a conventional brigade: infantry, armour, artillery, engineer, signal and logistics advisor elements. Members are volunteers screened for experience and maturity, trained at the Military Advisor Training Academy at Fort Moore (formerly Fort Benning), Georgia, and wear a brown beret with a distinctive unit patch.

Deployments

The 1st SFAB deployed to Afghanistan in 2018, the first operational employment of the concept. Since then SFABs have maintained persistent, regionally aligned deployments, sending company- and battalion-sized advisor packages to dozens of countries in Africa, Europe, the Middle East, South America and the Indo-Pacific to build partner capacity and provide a forward United States presence.

Purpose

The brigades institutionalise security force assistance — a mission previously carried out by ad hoc teams and by Special Forces — within the conventional Army, freeing brigade combat teams to focus on large-scale combat operations while still competing for influence and access with partner forces worldwide.
