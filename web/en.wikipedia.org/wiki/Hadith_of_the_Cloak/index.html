The Hadith of the Cloak (Arabic: حديث الكساء, Hadith al-Kisa) is a narration in Islamic tradition in which the prophet Muhammad gathered his daughter Fatimah, his cousin and son-in-law Ali, and his grandsons Hasan and Husayn under his cloak (kisa) and prayed for them as his Ahl al-Bayt ("People of the House"), in connection with the Quranic Verse of Purification (33:33). The five are collectively revered especially in Shia Islam as the Ahl al-Kisa ("People of the Cloak") or the Panjtan.

The narration

In the version recorded in Sahih Muslim, Aisha reported that Muhammad came out one morning wearing a striped black cloak of camel hair. Hasan came and he took him under it; then Husayn came and joined him; then Fatimah came and he brought her in; then Ali came and he brought him in. Then he recited:

"God only desires to remove defilement from you, O People of the House, and to purify you completely." (Quran 33:33)

Another famous version, narrated by Umm Salama, one of Muhammad's wives, adds that when she asked whether she too was among the Ahl al-Bayt, she was told — in some versions — that she was "on the path of good" but was not taken under the cloak. Shia narrations expand the story with a long prayer of Muhammad and a conversation between God and the angels about the five.

Significance

In Shia Islam

The Hadith of the Cloak is a foundation text for the special status, purity and, by extension, the infallibility (isma) of the five under the cloak, and thus of the Imams descended from them. A long devotional version, Hadith-e-Kisa, is regularly recited in Shia gatherings in Arabic, Persian and Urdu.

In Sunni Islam

Sunni collections include the narration (notably Sahih Muslim), and Sunni scholars accept it as authentic praise of the Prophet's household. Sunni interpretation generally includes the Prophet's wives within the Ahl al-Bayt on the basis of the surrounding verses of Surah al-Ahzab, a point long debated between the schools.

Related concepts

The five of the cloak are also central to the Event of Mubahala, in which Muhammad is said to have brought Ali, Fatimah, Hasan and Husayn to a proposed prayer-curse encounter with the Christians of Najran. Their names figure prominently in Shia art, banners and lamentation poetry, above all in connection with the martyrdom of Husayn at Karbala in 680.
