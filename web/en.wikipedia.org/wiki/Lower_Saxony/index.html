Lower Saxony (German: Niedersachsen) is a German state in northwestern Germany. It is the second-largest state by land area, covering 47,624 square kilometres, and the fourth-most populous, with around eight million inhabitants. Its capital is Hanover, and other major cities include Braunschweig, Oldenburg, Osnabrück, Wolfsburg, Göttingen, and Hildesheim.

Geography

Lower Saxony borders the North Sea to the northwest and stretches from the East Frisian Islands and the Wadden Sea coast across the North German Plain to the Harz mountains in the southeast, where the Brocken rises to 1,141 metres. Important rivers include the Ems, Weser, Aller, and Elbe. The Lüneburg Heath, a large area of heathland, forest, and bog, is one of the state's best-known landscapes.

History

The state takes its name from the Saxons, a Germanic people who settled the region in the early Middle Ages. The modern state was created in 1946 by the British military government through the merger of the former states of Hanover, Brunswick, Oldenburg, and Schaumburg-Lippe. Hanover itself had been a kingdom until its annexation by Prussia in 1866.

Economy

Lower Saxony is a major agricultural region and an important centre of the German automotive industry: Volkswagen is headquartered in Wolfsburg and operates several plants in the state. Other significant sectors include shipbuilding, energy — particularly offshore wind power along the North Sea coast — and food processing. The ports of Wilhelmshaven, Cuxhaven, and Emden link the state to international shipping.
