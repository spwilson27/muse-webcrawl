Galileo probe

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Galileo probe
MissionGalileo (orbiter + probe)
Entry date7 December 1995
TargetJupiter's atmosphere
Survived~58 minutes in atmosphere
The probe descends into Jupiter.

The Galileo probe was an atmospheric entry probe carried by NASA's Galileo orbiter to Jupiter. On 7 December 1995 it became the first spacecraft to enter the atmosphere of a gas giant, plunging into Jupiter at about 47 km/s — among the fastest human-made entries ever — and transmitting data for nearly an hour as it descended by parachute through the cloud tops.

Protected by the most capable heat shield yet flown — nearly half its 339 kg mass was ablative shield — the probe survived deceleration forces over 200 g and measured temperature, pressure, composition, winds and radiation before contact was lost about 160 km below the cloud tops, where it was eventually melted and vaporised by the heat and pressure.

Findings

The probe descended into an unexpectedly dry, windy "hot spot", finding far less water than predicted — a sampling quirk of Jupiter's meteorology that initially misled composition estimates. It measured helium abundance, detected strong winds increasing with depth, and found noble gases that constrained theories of Jupiter's formation. No dedicated Jupiter entry probe has flown since; the measurement remains unique.
