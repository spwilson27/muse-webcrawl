Tomb of the Unknown Soldier

Tomb of the Unknown Soldier
ConceptMemorial grave for an unidentified serviceman, symbolizing all war dead
First examplesLondon (Westminster Abbey) and Paris (Arc de Triomphe), 1920

A Tomb of the Unknown Soldier or Tomb of the Unknowns is a monument dedicated to the services of an unknown soldier and to the common memories of all soldiers killed in war. Such tombs can be found in many nations and are usually high-profile national monuments. Throughout history, many soldiers have died in war with their remains being unidentified. Following World War I, a movement arose to commemorate these soldiers with a single tomb containing the body of one such unidentified soldier.

Origins in World War I

The idea is often credited to the Reverend David Railton, an army chaplain, who in 1916 saw a grave marked by a rough cross inscribed "An Unknown British Soldier" near Armentières. In 1920 Britain interred the Unknown Warrior in Westminster Abbey and France placed its Unknown Soldier beneath the Arc de Triomphe in Paris, both ceremonies held on 11 November 1920. The United States followed in 1921, burying an unknown from World War I at Arlington National Cemetery; the tomb's white marble sarcophagus bears the inscription "Here rests in honored glory a soldier known but to God." Unknowns from World War II, Korea, and Vietnam were added there; the Vietnam unknown was later identified by DNA as Michael Blassie and removed, leaving that crypt empty but rededicated to missing service members.

Around the world

Many countries maintain similar memorials, including Italy (the Milite Ignoto at the Altare della Patria, Rome), Russia (Tomb of the Unknown Soldier by the Kremlin Wall, Moscow, with an eternal flame), Poland (Warsaw), Greece (Syntagma Square, Athens), Turkey (Çanakkale Martyrs' Memorial), Australia (Australian War Memorial, Canberra), Canada (National War Memorial, Ottawa, 2000), and India (Amar Jawan Jyoti, New Delhi). Tomb guards of honour — most famously the United States Army's 3rd Infantry Regiment ("The Old Guard") at Arlington, which has kept a continuous vigil since 1937 — are a hallmark of many such sites.

Symbolism

The unknown soldier embodies every serviceman and woman who died without recognition, and gives bereaved families of the missing a place to mourn. State funerals, wreath-layings, and remembrance ceremonies at these tombs remain central to national commemoration in many countries.
