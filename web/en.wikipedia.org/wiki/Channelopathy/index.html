Channelopathy

Channelopathy
SpecialtyNeurology, cardiology, medical genetics
CauseMutations in ion channel genes; autoimmune attack
TypesSodium, potassium, calcium, chloride channelopathies

Channelopathies are a group of diseases caused by the dysfunction of ion channel subunits or the proteins that regulate them. They can be inherited, arising from mutations in genes encoding ion channels, or acquired, for example through autoimmune attack on the channels. Because ion channels are found throughout the body, channelopathies affect many organ systems, including the nervous system, muscles, heart, and lungs.

Examples

Well-known channelopathies include cystic fibrosis (a chloride channel disorder caused by mutations in CFTR), long QT syndrome and Brugada syndrome (cardiac sodium, potassium, and calcium channel disorders), episodic ataxia, periodic paralyses such as hypokalemic periodic paralysis, myotonia congenita (a chloride channel disorder of skeletal muscle), and certain forms of epilepsy and migraine, such as familial hemiplegic migraine.

Mechanisms

Pathogenic variants may cause gain-of-function or loss-of-function changes in channel gating, conductance, or expression. The resulting disturbance of membrane excitability can produce episodic symptoms triggered by exercise, rest after exercise, fever, or emotional stress — a hallmark pattern of many channelopathies.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis often combines clinical history, electrophysiological testing, and genetic sequencing. Treatment is disease-specific and may include channel-blocking or channel-opening drugs, avoidance of triggers, and, for cardiac channelopathies, implantable defibrillators.
