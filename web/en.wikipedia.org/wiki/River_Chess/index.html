River Chess

CountryEngland, United Kingdom
Lengthabout 18 km (11 mi)
SourceChesham, Buckinghamshire
MouthRiver Colne at Rickmansworth, Hertfordshire

The River Chess is a chalk stream in Buckinghamshire and Hertfordshire, England. It rises from springs and the Pednor Vale near Chesham, flows south-east through the Chess Valley past Chenies and Latimer, and joins the River Colne at Rickmansworth. Despite being only about 18 kilometres long, the Chess is notable as a classic Chilterns chalk stream, a globally rare habitat type dependent on the chalk aquifer.

Course and character

The Chess is fed predominantly by groundwater, giving it the clear water, stable flow and constant cool temperature characteristic of chalk streams, supporting watercress beds, brown trout, and invertebrates such as mayflies and the white-clawed crayfish (now threatened by invasive signal crayfish). The river powered numerous water mills historically — corn, paper and fulling mills — of which several mill buildings survive along the valley. The Chess Valley Walk, a waymarked trail of about 10 miles, follows the river from Chesham to Rickmansworth, passing the restored Chorleywood area and the nature reserves around Sarratt Bottom.

Water supply and pressures

The Chess catchment supplies drinking water via groundwater abstraction, and the river has suffered low-flow periods linked to abstraction during droughts; campaigners and the Chilterns Chalk Streams Project have worked on habitat restoration, fish passage and flow monitoring. Watercress farming, once extensive around Sarratt and Chorleywood, has declined but survives as a local speciality. The upper river at Chesham runs in culvert through part of the town before emerging below the Moor, where its clear pools are a traditional local paddling spot.
