Winnie-the-Pooh

Winnie-the-Pooh is a fictional anthropomorphic teddy bear created by English author A. A. Milne and English illustrator E. H. Shepard. The character first appeared in the book Winnie-the-Pooh (1926), followed by The House at Pooh Corner (1928). Pooh lives in the Hundred Acre Wood with his friends Piglet, Eeyore, Owl, Rabbit, Kanga, Roo, and Tigger, and the boy Christopher Robin, named after Milne's son.

Origins

The character was named after a Canadian black bear named Winnie (after Winnipeg) at London Zoo and a swan called "Pooh." The original stuffed toys belonging to Christopher Robin Milne — Pooh, Piglet, Eeyore, Kanga, and Tigger — are on display at the New York Public Library.

Stories and adaptations

Milne's two story collections, together with his poetry books When We Were Very Young and Now We Are Six, became classics of children's literature. The Walt Disney Company acquired rights in 1961 and developed the characters into one of the most successful media franchises in the world, beginning with the featurette Winnie the Pooh and the Honey Tree (1966). The original books entered the public domain in the United States in 2022, leading to new adaptations, including the 2023 horror film Winnie-the-Pooh: Blood and Honey.

Characters

Pooh is a gentle, honey-loving bear of "very little brain"; Piglet is his timid best friend; Eeyore is a gloomy donkey; Owl is a pompous know-it-all; Rabbit is brisk and practical; and Tigger, introduced in the second book, is a bouncy tiger.
