Cyanuric acid

Formula: C3H3N3O3
Molar mass: 129.07 g·mol−1

Cyanuric acid is an industrial chemical with the formula (CNOH)3 — formally the cyclic trimer of isocyanic acid. It is a white, odorless solid. Large quantities are produced annually, mainly for use in swimming-pool chemistry and as a precursor to chlorinated disinfectants.

Uses

Cyanuric acid is added to outdoor swimming pools as a "stabilizer" or "conditioner": it binds free chlorine in a reversible equilibrium, shielding it from rapid destruction by sunlight while reducing its immediate activity, so excessive levels can impair disinfection. It is also a component of the dichlor and trichlor sanitizers themselves. Other uses include herbicide manufacture, crosslinking agents, and bleaches. Combined with melamine it forms melamine cyanurate, a poorly soluble crystal implicated in the 2007 pet food and 2008 milk adulteration scandals, where it contributed to kidney failure.

Synthesis

It is manufactured principally by heating urea, which eliminates ammonia and trimerizes isocyanic acid intermediates.
