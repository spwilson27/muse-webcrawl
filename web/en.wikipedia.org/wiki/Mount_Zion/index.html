Mount Zion (Hebrew: הַר צִיּוֹן, Har Ṣīyyōn) is a hill in Jerusalem, just outside the walls of the Old City near the Zion Gate. The name "Zion" first appears in the Hebrew Bible for the Jebusite fortress captured by David, and later became a synonym for Jerusalem itself and for the Land of Israel; the modern hill acquired the name through a later shift of tradition.

Sites and traditions

The summit area holds some of Jerusalem's most venerated places. The complex traditionally identified as the Tomb of David stands beneath the Cenacle, the room venerated as the site of the Last Supper and of Pentecost. Nearby rises the great Benedictine Dormition Abbey (completed 1910), marking the tradition of the Virgin Mary's "falling asleep". Other sites include the Chamber of the Holocaust (Martef HaShoah), the Protestant Mount Zion Cemetery — where Oskar Schindler is buried — and several churches and monasteries.

History

Between 1948 and 1967 Mount Zion was Israel's closest point to the Old City and a place of pilgrimage overlooking the then Jordanian-held shrines; worshippers climbed to the roof of David's Tomb to view the Temple Mount. Access to the Western Wall's restoration after 1967 diminished that role, but the hill remains a major destination for Jewish, Christian and Muslim visitors.
