Contradanza (also known as contradanza criolla) is a Cuban musical genre that arrived from Europe — via Spain, itself influenced by the English country dance and the French contredanse — and flourished in Cuba from the late 18th century.

Overview

The contradanza is an orchestral dance form written in two-part structure, often introduced by a paseo. Cuban composers added Afro-Cuban syncopation, producing a distinct creole style.

Development in Cuba

Composers such as Manuel Saumell (1818–1870), called the father of the Cuban contradanza, wrote dozens of short contradanzas whose rhythms — the cinquillo and tresillo — became foundational in Cuban music. Ignacio Cervantes later refined the form into art music.

Legacy

The contradanza evolved into the danza and then the danzón, Cuba's national dance. Its characteristic rhythms also fed the habanera and, through New Orleans and ragtime, early jazz.
