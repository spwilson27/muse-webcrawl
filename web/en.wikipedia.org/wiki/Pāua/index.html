Pāua

Pāua is the Māori name given to three species of large edible sea snails, marine gastropod molluscs belonging to the family Haliotidae (the abalones), found only in New Zealand. The species are Haliotis iris (blackfoot pāua), Haliotis australis (yellowfoot pāua), and Haliotis virginea (whitefoot pāua).

Description

Pāua have oval shells with a row of respiratory holes along one edge. The inside of the shell is lined with iridescent nacre (mother-of-pearl) in vivid blues, greens, and purples, which is highly prized for jewellery and ornamentation; in Māori culture the shells were traditionally used to represent the eyes in carvings (wheku and tekoteko figures).

Fishery

The blackfoot pāua supports a commercial fishery managed under New Zealand's Quota Management System, and recreational gathering is subject to bag limits and a minimum size of 125 mm. Pāua may only be gathered by free-diving — using scuba equipment to take pāua is prohibited. Overharvesting and poaching are persistent threats to the fishery.
