Wassily Kandinsky

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Composition VIII (1923): circles, triangles and checkerboard arcs arranged across a pale ground in precise geometric harmony

Wassily Wassilyevich Kandinsky (16 December 1866 – 13 December 1944) was a Russian painter and art theorist. Kandinsky is generally credited as one of the pioneers of abstraction in western art. Born in Moscow, he trained as a lawyer and economist before abandoning academia at 30 to study painting in Munich.

In 1911 he co-founded Der Blaue Reiter with Franz Marc and published Concerning the Spiritual in Art, arguing that colour and form could communicate like music. His work moved from expressive landscapes through the improvisations and compositions to the geometric style of his Bauhaus years (1922–33), where he taught alongside Paul Klee. After the Nazis closed the Bauhaus he settled in Paris, developing the biomorphic abstraction of his final decade.
