Massa (Italian pronunciation: [ˈmassa]) is a town and comune in Tuscany, central Italy, the administrative centre of the province of Massa and Carrara. It is situated in the valley of the Frigido River, near the Alpi Apuane (Apuan Alps), some 5 kilometres (3.1 mi) from the Tyrrhenian Sea coast, where its seaside district Marina di Massa lies. Together with neighbouring Carrara, Massa is world-famous for the white marble quarried in the surrounding mountains.

History

Massa is mentioned in documents from the 9th century. From the 15th to the 19th century it was the capital of the independent Duchy (later Principality) of Massa and Carrara, ruled by the Malaspina and then the Cybo-Malaspina families. The Malaspina Castle (Rocca Malaspina), which dominates the town from a hill, dates largely to this period. In 1829 the states passed to the Dukes of Modena, and in 1859 Massa was annexed to the Kingdom of Sardinia on the way to Italian unification.

During the Second World War the town lay close to the Gothic Line and suffered heavy bombing; the surrounding mountains saw intense partisan activity and reprisals.

Main sights

  • Rocca Malaspina — the castle of the Malaspina marquises, with medieval and Renaissance parts.
  • Massa Cathedral (Santi Pietro e Francesco) — dating in part to the 14th–15th centuries.
  • Palazzo Ducale — the ducal palace of the Cybo-Malaspina, built from the 16th century on Piazza Aranci.
  • Marina di Massa — the town's seaside resort district on the Tyrrhenian coast.

Economy

The marble industry, shared with Carrara, remains central: quarrying, cutting, and exporting stone from the Apuan Alps. Tourism, both cultural and seaside, is also significant.
