Magnetorquer

A magnetorquer or magnetic torquer (also known as a torque rod) is a satellite subsystem for attitude control and stabilization. It works by generating a magnetic dipole that interacts with the ambient magnetic field — usually Earth's — so that magnetic forces produce a mechanical torque on the spacecraft, allowing it to be oriented without expending propellant.

Operation

Magnetorquers are typically coils of wire, sometimes wound around a ferromagnetic core to strengthen the dipole. By controlling the current in coils aligned along different axes, controllers generate torque about any axis perpendicular to the local magnetic field; torque about the field direction itself cannot be produced, so magnetorquers are often combined with reaction wheels, which they can also desaturate ("momentum dumping").

Advantages and limitations

Magnetorquers are simple, lightweight, consume only electrical power (often supplied by solar panels), and have no moving parts or exhaustible propellant. Their torque is weak, however, and diminishes with altitude as Earth's field weakens (falling with the cube of distance), so they are most useful in low Earth orbit and in small satellites such as CubeSats.
