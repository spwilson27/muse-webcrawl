Parotitis

Parotitis is inflammation of the parotid glands, the major salivary glands located on either side of the face, just below and in front of the ears. It causes painful swelling of the cheeks and jaw.

Causes

The best-known cause is mumps, a viral infection that was a common childhood illness before widespread MMR vaccination. Parotitis can also be bacterial — often due to Staphylococcus aureus, particularly in dehydrated or elderly patients with poor oral hygiene — or result from blocked salivary ducts, autoimmune conditions such as Sjögren syndrome, or radiation therapy.

Treatment

Viral parotitis is usually self-limiting and managed supportively. Bacterial parotitis requires antibiotics, hydration, warm compresses, and measures to stimulate saliva flow; abscess formation may need surgical drainage.
