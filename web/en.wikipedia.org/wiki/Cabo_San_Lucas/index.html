Cabo San Lucas

Cabo San Lucas (Spanish for "Cape Saint Luke") is a resort city at the southern tip of Mexico's Baja California Peninsula, in the municipality of Los Cabos, Baja California Sur. It lies where the Pacific Ocean meets the Sea of Cortés, and is famed for the natural rock arch El Arco at Land's End.

History and development

Indigenous Pericú people inhabited the area for thousands of years. After Spanish colonization the cape served as a landmark and occasional pirate anchorage on the Manila galleon route. Sport fishing drew visitors in the mid-20th century, and after the completion of the Transpeninsular Highway in 1973 and the development of the neighbouring town of San José del Cabo and the Los Cabos International Airport, Cabo San Lucas grew into one of Mexico's foremost beach resorts, known for marlin fishing, golf, diving, and nightlife.

Geography and climate

The city has a hot desert climate tempered by the ocean, with most rain falling during late-summer tropical storms; hurricanes occasionally strike, notably Odile in 2014. Nearby beaches include Médano Beach on the bay side and the rougher Pacific-facing beaches near Land's End, while the Cabo Pulmo marine reserve to the northeast protects one of the oldest living coral reefs on the west coast of the Americas.
