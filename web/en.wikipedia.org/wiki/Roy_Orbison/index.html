Roy Orbison

Roy Orbison Born: 23 April 1936, Vernon, Texas, US
Died: 6 December 1988 (aged 52), Hendersonville, Tennessee
Genres: Rock, pop, country
Instruments: Vocals, guitar
Years active: 1953–1988

Roy Kelton Orbison (April 23, 1936 – December 6, 1988) was an American singer, songwriter and guitarist known for his impassioned singing style, complex song structures and dark, emotional ballads. His music was described by critics as operatic, earning him the nickname "the Caruso of Rock" and "the Big O". His instantly recognisable image—jet-black hair, dark sunglasses and black clothing—matched the themes of loneliness and heartbreak in his songs.

Early career

Born in Vernon, Texas, Orbison began singing in a rockabilly and country band in high school. He signed with Sun Records in 1956, where he recorded "Ooby Dooby", but mainstream success came after he moved to Monument Records. Between 1960 and 1964 he had a string of international hits, including "Only the Lonely", "Running Scared", "Crying", "In Dreams" and "Oh, Pretty Woman", the last of which became his signature song and a transatlantic number one in 1964.

Style

Orbison possessed a powerful three-to-four-octave vocal range and wrote songs that broke with the standard verse-chorus formulas of the time, following the emotional logic of a story instead. His ballads often build to dramatic crescendos, and his trembling, vulnerable delivery influenced generations of singers, from the Beatles to Bruce Springsteen, who called him a profound influence.

Later life and comeback

Orbison's career faltered in the late 1960s, compounded by personal tragedy: his wife Claudette died in a motorcycle accident in 1966, and two of his sons died in a house fire in 1968. He enjoyed a major revival in the 1980s. The use of "In Dreams" in David Lynch's film Blue Velvet (1986) brought renewed attention, and in 1987 he was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame. In 1988 he joined the supergroup the Traveling Wilburys with Bob Dylan, George Harrison, Tom Petty and Jeff Lynne. He died of a heart attack on 6 December 1988, at the height of his comeback, shortly after recording the solo album Mystery Girl, which was released posthumously and became his biggest-selling record in decades.
