Masaccio
Born: Tommaso di Ser Giovanni di Mone Cassai, December 21, 1401, San Giovanni Valdarno, Republic of Florence
Died: Summer 1428 (aged 26), Rome, Papal States
Movement: Early Renaissance
Known for: Linear perspective, naturalism

Masaccio (born Tommaso di Ser Giovanni di Mone Cassai; December 21, 1401 – summer 1428) was an Italian painter of the Quattrocento, regarded as the first great artist of the Italian Renaissance. In a career of barely six years he revolutionized painting with his use of linear perspective, naturalistic light and shade, and emotionally expressive figures.

Major works

His major works include the Brancacci Chapel frescoes in Santa Maria del Carmine, Florence (c. 1424–1427, painted with Masolino da Panicale), which include The Tribute Money, The Expulsion from the Garden of Eden, and The Baptism of the Neophytes; the Holy Trinity (c. 1427) in Santa Maria Novella, one of the earliest applications of systematic linear perspective; and the Madonna and Child with St. Anne (1424).

Death and influence

Masaccio died in Rome in 1428 at the age of 26, reportedly from unknown causes. Though his surviving body of work is small, he profoundly influenced later artists including Leonardo da Vinci, Raphael, and Michelangelo, all of whom studied his frescoes in the Brancacci Chapel.
