Power ballad

A power ballad is a type of song that typically features an emotional, slow-paced ballad structure combined with the powerful instrumentation of rock or heavy metal. Characterised by a quiet verse building to a loud, anthemic chorus with electric guitars and drums, power ballads became a staple of rock radio in the 1970s and 1980s.

Characteristics

Power ballads usually deal with romantic or sentimental themes and follow a formula of acoustic or clean-toned openings, swelling dynamics, a dramatic guitar solo, and a final, full-band climax. Lyrically they favour universal themes of love, loss and longing, which helped them cross over to pop audiences.

History

The style coalesced in the 1970s with songs such as Led Zeppelin's "Stairway to Heaven", Aerosmith's "Dream On" and Lynyrd Skynyrd's "Free Bird". It reached commercial zenith in the 1980s with hits including Journey's "Open Arms", Foreigner's "I Want to Know What Love Is", Heart's "Alone", and numerous glam metal ballads by bands such as Poison, Bon Jovi and Whitesnake. A power ballad became an almost obligatory track on hard rock albums of the era.

Cultural impact

The ritual of holding cigarette lighters (later phone lights) aloft during power ballads became a defining image of arena rock concerts. Although the format declined with 1990s alternative rock, it persists in pop and metal and is frequently revived in film, television and talent-show performances.
