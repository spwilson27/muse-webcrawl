Meat packing industry

The meat packing industry (also spelled meatpacking industry) handles the slaughtering, processing, packaging, and distribution of meat from animals such as cattle, pigs, sheep, and other livestock. It is among the largest segments of the food industry worldwide, and in the United States it is dominated by a small number of large firms—Tyson Foods, JBS USA, Cargill, and Smithfield Foods among them.

History

Meat packing as an industry began with colonial-era salt pork packed in barrels for shipping. In the early 19th century Cincinnati earned the nickname "Porkopolis" as a packing center. The development of railroads and the opening of Chicago's Union Stock Yards in 1865 made Chicago the industry's hub; firms such as Armour and Swift pioneered refrigerated rail cars, byproduct utilization ("everything but the squeal"), and disassembly-line mass production—methods that inspired Henry Ford's assembly lines in reverse. Gustavus Swift and Philip Armour built national distribution networks, and the "Big Five" packers dominated until antitrust consent decrees in 1920.

Upton Sinclair's 1906 novel The Jungle, exposing wretched conditions in Chicago's stockyards, helped prompt the U.S. Federal Meat Inspection Act and Pure Food and Drug Act of 1906.

Modern structure

In the 1960s–1980s, Iowa Beef Processors (IBP) revolutionized the industry with "boxed beef," rural plants near cattle feedlots, single-species specialized lines, and a largely non-union immigrant workforce; the established firms adopted the model or were acquired. Concentration increased, and by the 21st century four firms controlled roughly 80% of U.S. beef processing. Major centers shifted to the Great Plains (e.g., Garden City and Dodge City, Kansas; Grand Island, Nebraska; Amarillo, Texas).

Issues

The industry faces persistent criticism over worker safety (high injury and repetitive-strain rates and fast line speeds), labor practices, environmental effects (manure and wastewater, odors, greenhouse-gas emissions), animal welfare, consolidation and price-setting power over ranchers, and food-safety risks such as E. coli contamination. In 2020, COVID-19 outbreaks in packing plants became a major public-health issue in the United States and Germany, prompting plant closures and government intervention.
