Pseudoscalar

Type: Quantity in linear algebra and physics
Behaviour: Changes sign under parity inversion
Examples: Magnetic helicity; pseudoscalar mesons in particle physics

In linear algebra and physics, a pseudoscalar is a quantity that behaves like a scalar under proper rotations but changes sign under an improper transformation such as a parity inversion (reflection through the origin). Pseudoscalars arise naturally as products of vectors and pseudovectors, or from the volume element in geometric algebra.

In physics

Examples include the scalar triple product of three polar vectors, magnetic helicity, and the Chern–Pontryagin density. In particle physics, a pseudoscalar meson is a meson with spin zero and odd parity (JP = 0−), such as the pion and kaon; the Higgs boson was tested against a pseudoscalar alternative before being established as predominantly scalar.

In mathematics

In geometric (Clifford) algebra, the pseudoscalar is the highest-grade element of the algebra — in three dimensions the trivector e1e2e3 — which squares to −1 and plays the role of the unit imaginary in duality operations.
