Dzhokhar Dudayev

Born: 15 February 1944, Yalkhoroy, Chechen-Ingush ASSR, Soviet Union
Died: 21 April 1996 (aged 52), Gekhi-Chu, Chechnya
Office: President of the Chechen Republic of Ichkeria (1991–1996)

Dzhokhar Musayevich Dudayev (Chechen: Дудин Муса-кӀант Джохар; 15 February 1944 – 21 April 1996) was a Chechen politician, military leader and statesman who served as the first President of the Chechen Republic of Ichkeria, a breakaway state in the North Caucasus, from 1991 until his death in 1996. A former Soviet Air Force general — the first Chechen to reach that rank — Dudayev led Chechnya's declaration of independence from Russia and commanded its forces during the First Chechen War.

Early life and military career

Dudayev was born in the village of Yalkhoroy in the Galanchozh district of Chechnya in February 1944, days before his family was deported to Kazakhstan in Stalin's forced resettlement of the Chechen people. He returned to Chechnya in 1957. He joined the Soviet Air Force, trained as a strategic bomber pilot, and rose through the ranks, eventually commanding a heavy bomber division based at Tartu in Estonia with the rank of major general — commanding nuclear-capable aircraft. During his posting in Estonia he sympathised with the Baltic independence movement and refused orders to suppress it.

Presidency and war

In 1990 Dudayev resigned from the Soviet military and returned to Chechnya, where he was elected head of the executive committee of the All-National Congress of the Chechen People. In October 1991 he was elected president, and on 1 November 1991 he signed a decree declaring Chechnya's independence. Russian president Boris Yeltsin's attempt to impose emergency rule failed, and for three years Ichkeria existed as a de facto independent, though unrecognised, state.

In December 1994 Russia invaded, beginning the First Chechen War. Russian forces captured the capital, Grozny (renamed Dzhokhar by the separatists), in early 1995, but Chechen fighters continued guerrilla resistance from the mountains.

Death

On 21 April 1996, Russian forces pinpointed Dudayev's location by intercepting the signal of his satellite telephone while he was reportedly negotiating a call with a Russian mediator; two laser-guided missiles fired by Russian aircraft struck his position near the village of Gekhi-Chu, killing him. He was succeeded by his vice president, Zelimkhan Yandarbiyev, and later by Aslan Maskhadov.

Legacy

Dudayev remains a national hero to Chechen separatists. Streets and squares bearing his name exist in several countries, including Riga, Vilnius, Warsaw, and Kyiv, where streets were renamed in his honour — a practice that has drawn periodic protest from Moscow.
