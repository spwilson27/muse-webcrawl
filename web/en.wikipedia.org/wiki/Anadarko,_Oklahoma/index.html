Anadarko is a city in Caddo County, Oklahoma, United States, and serves as the county seat. Often called the "Indian Capital of the Nation", the city is home to the headquarters of several Native American tribes and to the Bureau of Indian Affairs agency serving the region.

History

Anadarko developed after the 1901 land lottery opened the Kiowa-Comanche-Apache reservation to non-Indian settlement. Its name derives from the Nadaco (Anadarko) people, a Caddoan tribe. The city became a centre for the trade and administration of the surrounding tribal lands.

Culture

The Southern Plains Indian Museum in Anadarko displays traditional and contemporary Native American art. The city also hosts the annual American Indian Exposition, a large intertribal gathering with dancing, parades and exhibitions. Anadarko is the tribal headquarters of the Delaware Nation and is closely associated with the Wichita and Affiliated Tribes, the Apache Tribe of Oklahoma and other nations.

Geography

The city lies in west-central Oklahoma near the Washita River, roughly 60 miles southwest of Oklahoma City. Its economy is based on agriculture, oil and gas services, and tribal and federal employment.
