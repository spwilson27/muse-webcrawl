Fremont Street

Fremont Street is a street in downtown Las Vegas, Nevada, that was the city's original gambling and entertainment centre long before the rise of the Las Vegas Strip. Its most famous section, nicknamed "Glitter Gulch", is now covered by the Fremont Street Experience, a pedestrian mall and LED canopy attraction.

History

Fremont Street was laid out in 1905 when Las Vegas was founded as a railroad town, and named after explorer and politician John C. Frémont. It was the first paved street in the city (1925) and received one of Nevada's first traffic lights. After gambling was legalized in Nevada in 1931, Fremont Street became home to landmark casinos including the Northern Club, the Golden Nugget (1946), Binion's Horseshoe, the Golden Gate, and the Fremont Hotel and Casino (1956, once the tallest building in Nevada).

The street's dense neon signage — including the cowboy figure "Vegas Vic," erected in 1951 — made it one of the most photographed streets in America. As large resorts concentrated on the Strip from the 1960s onward, downtown declined, prompting revitalization efforts.

Fremont Street Experience

In 1995 several blocks of Fremont Street were closed to traffic and covered with a barrel-vault canopy equipped with a huge LED display (later upgraded and now known as Viva Vision), with nightly light-and-sound shows. The attraction draws millions of visitors annually and is accompanied by the SlotZilla zip line. East of the canopy, the Fremont East district has developed as an area of bars, restaurants, and the Downtown Container Park.
