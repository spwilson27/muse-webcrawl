Anatevka is a fictional village — a shtetl — in the Pale of Settlement of Imperial Russia around 1905. It is the home of Tevye the dairyman and his family, first in the stories of the Yiddish writer Sholem Aleichem, and most famously in the musical Fiddler on the Roof (1964) and its 1971 film adaptation, which take their plot from the Tevye tales. The name is sung in the musical's song "Anatevka", in which the villagers lament leaving their poor but beloved home.

In the stories and the musical

In Sholem Aleichem's stories Tevye lives in an unnamed village near the town of Yehupetz (a fictionalised Kyiv). For the musical, librettist Joseph Stein set the action in Anatevka, a small community of Jews and Orthodox Christians living side by side under the distant authority of the Tsar. The plot follows Tevye's attempts to marry off his daughters according to tradition amid poverty, generational change and rising antisemitism; it ends with the village's Jews ordered to leave within three days under an edict of expulsion, scattering the community to America and the Holy Land.

Real-world echoes

In 2015 a real village named Anatevka was established near Kyiv in Ukraine as a refuge and community for Jewish families displaced by the war in the Donbas, led by Ukraine's chief rabbi Moshe Azman — a deliberate borrowing of the fictional name. The original Anatevka itself is generally modelled on the shtetls of the Kyiv region, including Boyarka, which Sholem Aleichem knew well.
