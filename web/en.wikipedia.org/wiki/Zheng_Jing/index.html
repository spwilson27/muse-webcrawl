Zheng Jing

Zheng Jing (鄭經)
Born: 25 October 1642, Nan'an, Fujian
Died: 17 March 1681, Tungning (Taiwan)
Reign: King of Tungning, 1662-1681
Father: Koxinga (Zheng Chenggong)

Zheng Jing (1642-1681) was a Ming loyalist leader who ruled the Kingdom of Tungning on Taiwan from 1662 to 1681, succeeding his father Koxinga (Zheng Chenggong), who had expelled the Dutch from the island. He maintained the Zheng regime's claim to be the legitimate continuation of the Ming dynasty against the Qing.

Succession and rule of Taiwan

On Koxinga's death in 1662, Zheng Jing overcame a rival claim by his uncle Zheng Shixi and consolidated power from his bases at Xiamen (Amoy) and Jinmen on the Fujian coast. After Qing pressure and a Dutch-assisted campaign cost him his mainland footholds in 1664, he withdrew to Taiwan, where he organised the island's administration along Ming lines, promoted settlement, agriculture and trade — including commerce with the English East India Company, with which he signed an agreement in 1670.

Revolt of the Three Feudatories

When the Revolt of the Three Feudatories broke out in 1674, Zheng Jing crossed to the mainland in alliance with the rebel Geng Jingzhong, recovering Xiamen and coastal prefectures. Alliance disputes and Qing counter-offensives, including defeats inflicted by the Qing commander Shi Lang, drove his forces back; by 1680 he had abandoned the mainland for good.

Death and succession

Zheng Jing died in 1681. A succession struggle followed: his elder son Zheng Kezang was killed in a coup, and the twelve-year-old Zheng Keshuang was installed. Two years later Shi Lang's fleet destroyed the Zheng navy at the Battle of Penghu, and the kingdom surrendered to the Qing, ending the Zheng regime in 1683.
