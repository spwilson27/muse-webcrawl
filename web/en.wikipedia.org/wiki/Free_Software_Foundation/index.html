The Free Software Foundation (FSF) is a non-profit organisation founded by Richard Stallman on 4 October 1985 to support the free software movement, which promotes the universal freedom to study, distribute, create and modify computer software. The FSF is based in Boston, Massachusetts.

History

Stallman founded the FSF shortly after announcing the GNU Project in 1983, to provide legal, organisational and funding support for the development of a completely free operating system. From its founding until the mid-1990s, FSF funds were mostly used to employ programmers to write free software for the GNU Project. Since the mid-1990s, when many companies and individuals began writing free software independently, the FSF has concentrated on legal and advocacy work.

Projects and activities

GNU Project

The FSF sponsors the GNU Project, which produces the GNU operating system components, including the GNU Compiler Collection (GCC), the GNU C Library (glibc) and the GNU core utilities. With the Linux kernel, these form the widely used GNU/Linux systems.

Licensing

The FSF publishes and maintains the GNU General Public License (GPL), the Lesser GPL (LGPL), the Affero GPL (AGPL) and the GNU Free Documentation License (GFDL), and operates a licensing compliance lab that investigates reported violations of its licenses. It also maintains the Free Software Definition and a directory of free software.

Campaigns

The FSF campaigns against digital restrictions management (through its Defective by Design campaign), software patents, and proprietary software in education, and promotes campaigns such as "Free Your Android". It certifies hardware products that respect user freedom under its "Respects Your Freedom" programme.

Governance and membership

The FSF is a membership organisation funded largely by donations and associate member dues. Its board has included Stallman, who served as president from 1985 until his resignation in September 2019 following controversial public remarks; he returned to the board in 2021, a move that prompted public criticism and calls for his removal from some free software organisations. The FSF presents the annual Free Software Awards and hosts the LibrePlanet conference.
