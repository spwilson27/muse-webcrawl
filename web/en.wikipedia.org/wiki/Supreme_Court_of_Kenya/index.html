Supreme Court of Kenya

The Supreme Court of Kenya is the highest court in Kenya, established under Article 163 of the Constitution of Kenya 2010. It comprises the Chief Justice (who is its president), the Deputy Chief Justice, and five other judges. The Court sits in Nairobi.

Jurisdiction

The Court has exclusive original jurisdiction to hear and determine disputes relating to presidential elections, and appellate jurisdiction over decisions of the Court of Appeal involving the interpretation or application of the Constitution, as well as other matters of general public importance certified for appeal. It may also give advisory opinions at the request of the national government, any state organ, or any county government concerning county government matters.

History

The Court was created by the 2010 Constitution, which restructured the Kenyan judiciary after the post-election violence of 2007–08 and long-standing public distrust of the courts. Willy Mutunga became its first Chief Justice in 2011. In 2017, under Chief Justice David Maraga, the Court made history by annulling the August presidential election — the first such annulment in Africa — and ordering a fresh election, a decision that drew worldwide attention. Martha Koome became Chief Justice in 2021, the first woman to hold the office.

Notable cases

Beyond the 2017 presidential petition, the Court has ruled on the constitutionality of the Building Bridges Initiative constitutional amendments (2021–22), devolution and revenue disputes between national and county governments, and fundamental rights questions, shaping Kenya's post-2010 constitutional order.
