Sean Phillips, British comics artist

Sean Phillips (born 27 January 1965) is a British comics artist, best known for his long-running collaboration with writer Ed Brubaker on acclaimed crime and noir series including Sleeper, Criminal, Fatale, The Fade Out, Kill or Be Killed, Pulp and the Reckless graphic novels. His moody, naturalistic artwork, frequently inked and coloured with painterly restraint, has made him one of the most respected illustrators in modern American comics.

Early life and career

Phillips began working professionally in British comics while still a teenager, contributing to girls' comics such as Bunty and Judy before moving into the British adult comics scene. He drew for Crisis and 2000 AD, including work on Judge Dredd-related strips, and co-created characters that helped establish his reputation for gritty, character-driven storytelling.

In the 1990s he crossed over to American publishers, working for DC Comics and its Vertigo imprint on titles such as Hellblazer and The Invisibles, and for Marvel Comics on various series. His early American work demonstrated a versatility that ranged from horror to superhero material.

Collaboration with Ed Brubaker

Phillips first teamed with Ed Brubaker on Sleeper (2003–2005), a superhero noir published under DC's WildStorm imprint. Their partnership deepened with Criminal (2006 onwards), published first by Marvel's Icon imprint and later by Image Comics. The series, a collection of interlocking crime stories, won widespread critical praise and multiple Eisner Awards.

Subsequent collaborations include Fatale (2012–2014), which blended noir with Lovecraftian horror; The Fade Out (2014–2016), set in 1940s Hollywood; Kill or Be Killed (2016–2018), a vigilante thriller; the original graphic novel Pulp (2020); and the Reckless series of graphic novels beginning in 2020, which adopted a direct-to-book format that proved commercially successful.

Style and technique

Phillips is noted for his grounded, cinematic compositions, expressive character acting and restrained use of colour, often working with colourist Elizabeth Breitweiser on later projects. He frequently works from photographic reference for settings and props while keeping his figures drawn rather than traced, giving his pages a distinctive blend of realism and graphic clarity.

Awards

Phillips has won multiple Eisner Awards, including Best Artist/Penciller honours, and his work with Brubaker has repeatedly won Best New Series and Best Limited Series categories. The Fade Out won the Eisner Award for Best Limited Series in 2016.
