Psychological thriller

Psychological thriller is a genre combining thriller and psychological fiction, emphasising the mental states of characters — perception, obsession, paranoia, and unstable narration — over physical action. Suspense arises from uncertainty about motives, memory and reality itself.

Characteristics

Common devices include unreliable narrators, plot twists, doppelgängers, gaslighting, and protagonists whose grip on truth falters. Themes often overlap with psychological horror and crime fiction but tension is intellectual and emotional rather than gore-driven.

Examples

In film, Alfred Hitchcock's Vertigo (1958) and Psycho (1960) are foundational; later landmarks include The Silence of the Lambs (1991), Se7en (1995), Memento (2000), Shutter Island (2010) and Gone Girl (2014), adapted from Gillian Flynn's novel. In literature, Patricia Highsmith, Ruth Rendell and contemporary domestic-noir authors exemplify the form.
