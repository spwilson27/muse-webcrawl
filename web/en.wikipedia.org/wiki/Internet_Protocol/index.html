Internet Protocol

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Diagram of an IP packet being routed hop-by-hop across several networks from source to destination]

The Internet Protocol (IP) is the network layer communications protocol in the Internet protocol suite for relaying datagrams across network boundaries. Its routing function enables internetworking and essentially establishes the Internet.

IP has the task of delivering packets from the source host to the destination host based solely on the IP addresses in the packet headers. For this purpose, IP defines packet structures that encapsulate the data to be delivered, and addressing methods that label the datagram with source and destination information. IP makes no guarantees: packets may be lost, duplicated, reordered or corrupted — a 'best effort' service on which the transport protocols, principally TCP, build reliability.

Versions

The first major version, IPv4, still carries a large share of traffic decades after its 1981 standardisation. Its successor, IPv6, with 128-bit addresses and a simplified header, was designed in the 1990s and is deployed alongside it. (Version numbers 5 and 7–9 were assigned to experimental or abandoned designs, which is why the successor to version 4 is version 6.)

IP was designed by Vint Cerf and Robert Kahn as part of their 1974 paper on packet network intercommunication — the paper often called the birth certificate of the Internet.
