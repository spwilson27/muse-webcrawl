Sustainable Development Goal 6

Sustainable Development Goal 6 (SDG 6 or Global Goal 6) is about "clean water and sanitation for all" and is one of the 17 Sustainable Development Goals established by the United Nations in 2015. Its official wording is: "Ensure availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all."

Targets

The eight targets include safe and affordable drinking water for all by 2030; adequate sanitation and hygiene, ending open defecation; improved water quality by reducing pollution and untreated wastewater; increased water-use efficiency and sustainable withdrawals; integrated water resources management, including across transboundary basins; protection of water-related ecosystems such as wetlands, rivers, and lakes; expanded international cooperation and capacity building; and participation of local communities in water and sanitation management.

Progress

Progress is tracked by UN-Water through indicators such as the proportion of the population using safely managed drinking water services. While billions of people have gained access to improved water and sanitation since 2000, billions still lack safely managed services, and water stress, pollution, and climate-change-driven scarcity threaten further gains. Global assessments have repeatedly found that the world is not on track to achieve SDG 6 by 2030 without a major acceleration of investment and reform.
