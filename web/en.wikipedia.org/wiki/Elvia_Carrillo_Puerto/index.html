Elvia Carrillo Puerto

Elvia Carrillo Puerto (December 6, 1878 – April 15, 1968) was a Mexican socialist politician and feminist who fought for women's suffrage in Mexico. In 1923 she became one of the first three women elected to a Mexican state legislature, serving in the state congress of Yucatán.

Early life and activism

Carrillo Puerto was born in Motul, Yucatán. Her brother, Felipe Carrillo Puerto, became a socialist governor of Yucatán. Influenced by the reformist atmosphere of the Mexican Revolution, she founded feminist leagues (Ligas Feministas) that organized women in towns and villages across Yucatán, promoting education, birth control, and political rights.

Political career

Following her brother's election as governor, she organized the First Feminist Congress of Yucatán in 1916 in Mérida, one of the earliest such gatherings in Mexico. In 1923, she, Beatriz Peniche, and Raquel Dzib Cicero were elected to the Yucatán state legislature — the first women deputies in Mexico. After her brother's assassination in 1924, she was forced from office and her election was effectively annulled amid conservative backlash.

She continued to campaign for women's suffrage nationally for decades. Mexican women finally won the right to vote in federal elections in 1953. Carrillo Puerto is known in Mexico as the "Red Nun" (La Monja Roja) for her devotion to socialist causes.
