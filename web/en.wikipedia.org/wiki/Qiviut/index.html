Qiviut

Qiviut (pronounced "kiv-ee-oot") is the Inuktitut word for the soft underwool of the musk ox (Ovibos moschatus). Unlike sheep's wool, qiviut has no scales or crimp, does not shrink in water of any temperature, and is about eight times warmer than sheep's wool with a much finer texture. It is among the most expensive natural fibres in the world.

Production

Musk oxen shed their qiviut each spring; a single animal yields roughly 2.5–3.5 kg (5–7 lb). The fibre is collected from domesticated herds — such as those of the Musk Ox Farm in Palmer, Alaska — or gathered from the ground after wild animals shed. The wool is cleaned, dehaired, carded, and spun, often by hand by Indigenous knitters' cooperatives in Alaska, who produce scarves, caps, and tunics (nachaq) with regional patterns.

Properties

Qiviut fibres are extraordinarily fine (about 11–13 micrometres in diameter), hypoallergenic because they lack lanolin, and extremely light. Finished garments are prized for warmth without bulk, but their cost is high because supply is small and processing is labour-intensive.
