Anne Arundel County is a county in the U.S. state of Maryland, located south of Baltimore on the western shore of the Chesapeake Bay. Its county seat is Annapolis, the state capital. With a population of about 588,000 in the 2020 census, it is among Maryland's most populous counties.

History

The county was established in 1650 and named for Anne Arundell (1615/16–1649), wife of Cecilius Calvert, 2nd Baron Baltimore, the proprietor of the Maryland colony. Puritan settlers, invited under the Maryland Toleration Act, founded Providence (later Annapolis) in 1649.

Geography

The county has over 500 miles of Chesapeake shoreline, including the Magothy, Severn, South and West rivers. Its northern edge borders Baltimore, and it contains Baltimore/Washington International Airport and Fort George G. Meade, headquarters of the National Security Agency. The United States Naval Academy is in Annapolis.

Communities

Major communities include Glen Burnie, Severna Park, Odenton, Crofton, Arnold, Pasadena, Severn and Maryland City, alongside the incorporated cities of Annapolis and Highland Beach. The county government adopted a charter form of government in 1964, headed by a county executive and a seven-member county council.
