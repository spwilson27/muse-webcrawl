Diriyah

Diriyah (Arabic: الدِرْعِيّة) is a city in Saudi Arabia located on the north-western outskirts of the capital, Riyadh, in the valley of Wadi Hanifa. It was the original home of the Saudi royal family and served as the capital of the Emirate of Diriyah, the First Saudi State, from 1744 until 1818, when it was destroyed by Ottoman forces. Its historic district of At-Turaif was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2010, and the city is the focus of a major modern redevelopment programme.

History

The settlement was established in 1446–47 by Mani' ibn Rabi'a al-Muraydi, an ancestor of the Al Saud, who received land along Wadi Hanifa. For centuries it was one of several rival oasis towns of Najd. In 1744 its ruler, Muhammad ibn Saud, formed an alliance with the religious reformer Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab — the "Diriyah pact" — joining political authority to the reform movement and founding the First Saudi State. From Diriyah the new emirate expanded until it controlled most of the Arabian Peninsula, including Mecca and Medina.

The expansion provoked the Ottoman Empire, whose Egyptian forces under Ibrahim Pasha campaigned into Najd and besieged Diriyah in 1818. After a six-month siege the city surrendered; its ruler Abdullah ibn Saud was sent to Constantinople and executed, and Diriyah was systematically destroyed. The Saudi capital moved to Riyadh, and old Diriyah remained largely in ruins, though a new town grew nearby.

At-Turaif

The At-Turaif district, founded in the 15th century, contains the remains of multi-storey mud-brick palaces — including the Salwa Palace complex — built in the traditional Najdi architectural style with triangular windows and rooftop ornamentation. Inscribed by UNESCO in 2010, it was the first Saudi site in the Riyadh region on the World Heritage List. Since 2017 the Diriyah Gate development has been restoring the district as a cultural and tourist destination adjoining the modern city.
