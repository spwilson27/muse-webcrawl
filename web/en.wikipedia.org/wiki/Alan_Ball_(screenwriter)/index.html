Alan Erwin Ball (born 13 May 1957) is an American writer, director and producer for television, film and theatre. He won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay for American Beauty (1999) and created the HBO drama series Six Feet Under (2001–2005) and True Blood (2008–2014), both widely acclaimed and commercially successful.

Early life and theatre

Ball was born in Marietta, Georgia, and studied theatre at Florida State University. In New York he worked as a playwright — his plays include the one-act Five Women Wearing the Same Dress (1993) — and wrote advertising copy before moving into television comedy, writing for sitcoms such as Grace Under Fire and Cybill, an experience he later described as creatively stifling.

American Beauty and Six Feet Under

His screenplay for American Beauty, directed by Sam Mendes, anatomised suburban despair and won five Academy Awards including Best Picture. Ball then created Six Feet Under for HBO, about a Los Angeles family running a funeral home; each episode began with a death, and the series was praised for its treatment of mortality and family life. Its final episode, "Everyone's Waiting" (2005), is frequently cited among the greatest series finales ever made.

True Blood and later work

Adapting Charlaine Harris's Southern Vampire novels, Ball created True Blood, a steamy, allegorically charged vampire drama set in Louisiana that became one of HBO's biggest hits. He wrote and directed the feature film Towelhead (2007), executive-produced the Cinemax series Banshee, and created the short-lived HBO drama Here and Now (2018). His feature Uncle Frank (2020), which he wrote and directed, premiered at Sundance.

Themes

Ball's work recurrently explores death, repression, family dysfunction and the gap between suburban respectability and inner life, often from an openly gay perspective and with a mixture of black comedy and melodrama.
