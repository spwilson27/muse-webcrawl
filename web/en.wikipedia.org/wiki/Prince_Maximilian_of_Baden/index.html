Prince Maximilian of Baden (Maximilian Alexander Friedrich Wilhelm Prinz von Baden; 10 July 1867 – 6 November 1929), also known as Max von Baden, was a German prince, general, and statesman. He was heir presumptive to the throne of the Grand Duchy of Baden, and in October 1918 briefly served as the last Imperial Chancellor of the German Empire and minister president of Prussia. He sued for peace on Germany's behalf at the end of the First World War, based on the Fourteen Points of US President Woodrow Wilson, and was largely responsible for transforming the empire into a parliamentary democracy. He announced the abdication of Emperor Wilhelm II on 9 November 1918, before it had in fact been granted.

Early life

Maximilian was born in Baden-Baden, the second son of Prince Wilhelm of Baden and Princess Maria Maximilianovna of Leuchtenberg, a granddaughter of Eugène de Beauharnais and niece of Tsar Alexander II of Russia. He studied law at Heidelberg University, where he joined the Corps Saxo-Borussia. After a career in the Prussian Army, reaching the rank of major general, he retired in 1911.

First World War

Known for his liberal views, he campaigned for a negotiated peace during the war. In 1917 he became president of the First Chamber of the Baden estates. As the military situation collapsed in autumn 1918, the German leadership turned to him in the hope that his reputation as a moderate would secure better armistice terms from the Allies.

Chancellorship

Emperor Wilhelm II appointed Maximilian Chancellor on 3 October 1918. His government included representatives of the majority parties of the Reichstag, among them Social Democrats such as Philipp Scheidemann and Gustav Bauer, making it in effect the first parliamentary government in German history. Constitutional amendments in late October formally transferred control of the military to the civilian government and made the Chancellor responsible to the Reichstag.

Maximilian's government sent the first German note to President Wilson requesting an armistice on the basis of the Fourteen Points. When revolution broke out in November, and fearing a Bolshevik-style uprising, he announced the Emperor's abdication on 9 November 1918 without Wilhelm's consent, and resigned, handing the chancellorship to Friedrich Ebert, leader of the Social Democratic Party.

Later life

Maximilian spent the remainder of his life attempting to distance himself from the role imputed to him, collaborating with the historian Erich Eyck on a memoir defending his chancellorship. He died at Salem on 6 November 1929.
