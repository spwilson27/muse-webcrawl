James Brindley

James Brindley (1716 – 27 September 1772) was an English engineer. Born into a family with little formal education, he became one of the most notable engineers of the 18th century and a pioneer of the canal age in Britain.

Early life

Brindley was born in Tunstead, Derbyshire. He was apprenticed to a millwright in Sutton, Macclesfield, and soon showed exceptional mechanical ability. He set up his own business as a wheelwright and millwright and became known for his work on steam engines and mills.

Canal engineering

Brindley's reputation led Francis Egerton, 3rd Duke of Bridgewater, to employ him on the Bridgewater Canal, which opened in 1761 to carry coal from the Duke's mines at Worsley into Manchester. Its most famous feature was the Barton Aqueduct, which carried the canal over the River Irwell. The success of the canal halved the price of coal in Manchester and demonstrated the commercial value of canals.

Brindley went on to design much of the early canal network, including the Trent and Mersey Canal, the Staffordshire and Worcestershire Canal, the Oxford Canal and the Coventry Canal. His canals were built along contour lines with locks avoided where possible, and he favoured puddled clay for waterproofing. He is credited with involvement in over 365 miles (587 km) of canal.

Death

Brindley died at Turnhurst, Staffordshire, in 1772, after becoming ill while surveying the Caldon Canal, and was buried at Newchapel.
