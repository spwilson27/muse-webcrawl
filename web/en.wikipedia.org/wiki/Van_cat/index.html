Van cat
OriginLake Van region, eastern Turkey
Common traitsWhite coat; odd eyes (one blue, one amber); fondness for swimming

The Van cat (Turkish: Van kedisi; Armenian: Վանա կատու, Vana katu) is a distinctive landrace of domestic cat native to the Lake Van region of eastern Anatolia, in present-day Turkey. It is characterised by a predominantly white coat and striking eye colouration: many Van cats are odd-eyed, with one blue eye and one amber eye, a trait celebrated locally and protected by Turkish authorities.

Characteristics

Van cats are large, muscular and agile, with a chalk-white coat that may carry small markings on the head and tail. Their eyes may be both blue, both amber, or one of each; odd-eyed white cats of the region were already noted by travellers centuries ago. Unusually for cats, Van cats are reported to enjoy water and to swim in Lake Van, earning them the nickname "the swimming cats", though the behaviour is variable among individuals.

History and conservation

Cats of this type have lived around Van for centuries and appear in local folklore and jewellery designs of great antiquity. The modern pedigree breed known as the Turkish Van was developed in Britain from cats taken from the region in the 1950s, with colour restricted to the head and tail. The landrace at home faced declining numbers, and in the 1990s Yüzüncü Yıl University in Van established a Van Cat Research Centre (the "Van Cat House") to breed and conserve the all-white odd-eyed population. Export of Van cats from Turkey is restricted.
