Contortion

Contortion (sometimes contortionism) is a performance art in which performers called contortionists showcase their skills of extreme physical flexibility. Contortion acts often accompany acrobatics and circus acts, street performers and other live stage performances, and are frequently portrayed in film and television.

Skills

Most contortionists are naturally flexible, though the skill can be developed through intensive training. Performances generally fall into categories such as frontbending (folding the body forward), backbending (arching the spine backwards, including the Marinelli bend, performed while supported only by the teeth) and dislocations and enterology (passing the body through small frames or hoops). Many contortionists also perform hand-balancing, aerial acts and dance within their routines.

History

Contortion has ancient roots: flexible poses appear in the art and religious practice of ancient Egypt, Greece, India, China and Mongolia. Mongolian contortion, developed over centuries and taught from childhood, is particularly renowned and is performed by troupes around the world. Contortion became a staple of the 19th- and 20th-century circus and vaudeville stage, and figures such as the "human pretzel" acts of early sideshows popularised it in the West.

Health considerations

Extreme flexibility is associated with conditions such as hypermobility and Ehlers–Danlos syndromes. Training typically begins in childhood, when joints are most pliable, and professional contortionists work with coaches and medical supervision to reduce the risk of injury.
