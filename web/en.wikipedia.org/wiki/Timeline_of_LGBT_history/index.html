Timeline of LGBT history

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The following is the timeline of lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and queer (LGBTQ) history. It records major events concerning same-sex love, gender diversity, and the legal and social status of LGBT people across cultures, from antiquity to the present day.

Antiquity and the Middle Ages

  • Ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia: early references to same-sex relations appear in Egyptian and Assyrian texts and law codes.
  • Ancient Greece: same-sex relationships, including institutionalized pederasty in some city-states and the love poetry of Sappho of Lesbos (source of the word "lesbian"), are widely documented.
  • Roman Empire: same-sex relations were tolerated within status conventions under the Republic and early Empire; in 342 and 390 AD, Christian emperors criminalized same-sex marriage and homosexual acts.
  • China: same-sex relations among elites were recorded for centuries, with the "cut sleeve" story of Emperor Ai of Han (1st century BC) becoming proverbial.
  • Middle Ages: sodomy became a grave sin and capital crime in much of Christian Europe; in 1533 England enacted the Buggery Act, the first secular anti-sodomy statute.

18th and 19th centuries

  • 1791: Revolutionary France decriminalizes sodomy; the Napoleonic Code (1804) spreads decriminalization across much of Europe.
  • 1861: England reduces the penalty for buggery from death to life imprisonment.
  • 1869: Karl-Maria Kertbeny coins the terms "homosexual" and "heterosexual."
  • 1895: Oscar Wilde is convicted of gross indecency in London.
  • 1897: Magnus Hirschfeld founds the Scientific-Humanitarian Committee in Berlin, an early homosexual-rights organization.

20th century

  • 1933–45: Nazi Germany persecutes homosexuals; thousands perish in concentration camps, marked with pink triangles.
  • 1950: The Mattachine Society is founded in Los Angeles, one of the first sustained gay organizations in the United States.
  • 1952: Christine Jorgensen's transition becomes a major international news story in the United States.
  • 1967: England and Wales partially decriminalize male homosexuality (Sexual Offences Act).
  • 1969: The Stonewall riots in New York City catalyze the modern gay liberation movement; the first Pride marches follow in 1970.
  • 1973: The American Psychiatric Association removes homosexuality from its list of mental disorders.
  • 1981 onward: The AIDS epidemic devastates gay communities and spurs organizations such as ACT UP (1987).
  • 1989: Denmark establishes the world's first legal same-sex registered partnerships.

21st century

  • 2001: The Netherlands becomes the first country to legalize same-sex marriage.
  • 2003: In Lawrence v. Texas, the U.S. Supreme Court strikes down remaining state sodomy laws.
  • 2015: In Obergefell v. Hodges, the U.S. Supreme Court legalizes same-sex marriage nationwide.
  • 2018: India's Supreme Court decriminalizes consensual same-sex relations.
  • 2019: Taiwan becomes the first place in Asia to legalize same-sex marriage; Botswana and Ecuador decriminalize or recognize same-sex unions.
  • 2020s: Marriage equality expands to countries including Switzerland, Mexico (nationwide), Thailand (2025), and others, while anti-LGBT legislation and criminalization persist or intensify in a number of states.
