Ashdod
Hebrew: אַשְׁדּוֹד
District: Southern District, Israel
Founded: Bronze Age (ancient); 1956 (modern)
Population: ~225,000

Ashdod is the sixth-largest city in Israel, located in the Southern District on the Mediterranean coast, some 32 kilometres south of Tel Aviv. In antiquity Ashdod was one of the five principal cities of the Philistine pentapolis. The modern city was founded in 1956 on sand hills near the ancient site.

History

Ancient Ashdod is mentioned in Ugaritic texts and in the Hebrew Bible, where the Philistines placed the captured Ark of the Covenant in the temple of Dagon. The city was conquered successively by Egyptians, Israelites, Assyrians, Greeks, Romans and Arabs. The modern city grew rapidly with immigration, particularly from Morocco, Egypt, Georgia and the former Soviet Union.

Port

The Port of Ashdod is one of Israel's two main cargo ports, handling a large share of the country's imported goods. A second port, the Southport terminal, opened in the 2020s.

Culture

The city is home to the Ashdod Performing Arts Center and a large Haredi community. Its football club plays at the Yud-Alef Stadium.
