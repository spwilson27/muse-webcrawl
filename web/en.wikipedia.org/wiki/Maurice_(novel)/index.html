Maurice is a novel by E. M. Forster about homosexual love in early-20th-century England. Written in 1913–1914, it was revised intermittently over Forster's lifetime but published only posthumously, in 1971, a year after his death, because of the legal and social risks surrounding its subject during his lifetime.

Plot

Maurice Hall, a conventional suburban stockbroker, falls in love at Cambridge with his friend Clive Durham, who initiates him into Plato's ideal of same-sex love but later renounces it and marries. Maurice, despairing and briefly seeking a hypnotist's "cure", eventually finds happiness with Alec Scudder, a gamekeeper on Clive's estate, defying class and social convention to live with him.

Background

Forster was inspired by a visit in 1913 to the writer and campaigner Edward Carpenter at Millthorpe, where Carpenter's partner George Merrill touched Forster in a way he later described as the creative spark for the novel. Forster insisted the story end happily, remarking in a terminal note that a happy ending was imperative. He circulated the manuscript among trusted friends, and Christopher Isherwood later helped ensure its preservation.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted into the acclaimed 1987 Merchant Ivory film Maurice, starring James Wilby and Hugh Grant, and has also been adapted for the stage.
