Ashoka Chakra

Type: Depiction of the dharmachakra

Spokes: 24

Colour: Navy blue

Used on: Flag of India

The Ashoka Chakra is a depiction of the Buddhist and Hindu dharmachakra ("wheel of dharma") with 24 spokes, named after the Mauryan emperor Ashoka, on whose pillar capitals the wheel appears. It is most widely known as the central device of the national flag of India, adopted on 15 August 1947, where it replaced the spinning wheel (charkha) of earlier flag designs.

Symbolism

The wheel's 24 spokes are commonly interpreted as representing the eternal wheel of law and motion: "There is life in movement and death in stagnation." The navy-blue colour signifies universal sky and the depth of the ocean, and the wheel's regular spokes symbolise that the nation should move forward in all directions of dharma (righteousness).

Uses

  • Centre of the flag of India, in navy blue on the white band.
  • The Ashoka Chakra is also the name of India's highest peacetime gallantry award, instituted in 1952 and awarded for valour, courageous action or self-sacrifice away from the battlefield.
  • The wheel appears on the Lion Capital of Ashoka, India's national emblem, at its base.
