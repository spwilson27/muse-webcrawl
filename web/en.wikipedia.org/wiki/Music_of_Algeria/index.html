Music of Algeria

The music of Algeria is rich and diverse, shaped by the country's Amazigh (Berber), Arab, and Mediterranean heritage and by centuries of exchange with Andalusi, Ottoman, and French culture. Algiers, Oran, Constantine and Tlemcen are historic centres of distinct regional styles.

Andalusi music

Andalusi classical music, descended from the court music of Muslim Spain, is preserved in three main schools: the sanaa of Algiers, the gharnati of Tlemcen, and the malouf of Constantine. These traditions are built around suites called nouba, performed with instruments such as the oud, rebab, kwitra, violin, mandole, and derbouka, and settings of classical Arabic and muwashshah poetry.

Chaabi

Chaabi ("popular") music developed in Algiers in the 1930s from Andalusi sources, blending classical poetry with colloquial lyrics. Its best-known exponent is El Hadj M'Hamed El Anka, the "master of chaabi," whose students and successors included Dahmane El Harrachi, famous for the song "Ya Rayah."

Raï

Raï originated in Oran in western Algeria among Bedouin shepherds and urban working-class performers. Modernised in the 1970s and 1980s with synthesisers and drum machines, "pop raï" became Algeria's most famous musical export through stars such as Cheb Khaled, Cheb Mami, and Rachid Taha. The singer Cheb Hasni, a beloved figure of romantic raï, was assassinated in 1994 during the Algerian Civil War.

Other styles

Kabyle music from the Berber region of Kabylie, Tuareg desert blues related to the music of Mali, gnawa healing music, staifi from Sétif, and modern Algerian hip hop all contribute to the country's musical landscape.
