Nobuo Yoneda

Nobuo Yoneda (Japanese: 米田 信夫, Yoneda Nobuo; 28 March 1930 – 22 April 1996) was a Japanese mathematician and computer scientist, best known in mathematics for the Yoneda lemma, one of the most fundamental results in category theory, and in computer science for early work on the ALGOL programming language.

Biography

Yoneda was born in 1930 and studied mathematics at the University of Tokyo, where he completed his doctorate. His research in algebraic topology and homological algebra led him to category theory, then a young subject developed by Samuel Eilenberg and Saunders Mac Lane.

In 1954, while in Paris, Yoneda communicated the result now known as the Yoneda lemma to Mac Lane. According to Mac Lane's later recollections, Yoneda explained the lemma during a conversation at the Gare du Nord railway station. The lemma was published in Yoneda's 1954 paper on functors and became a cornerstone of category theory: it states that an object in a locally small category is determined up to isomorphism by the network of morphisms into (or out of) it, formalised by the fully faithful embedding of the category into its functor category of presheaves.

Computer science

Yoneda was also a pioneer of computer science in Japan. At the University of Tokyo he led work on programming languages and compilers, and he was a member of the international committee that designed ALGOL 68. The University of Tokyo's computer science department traces part of its origins to his group.

Legacy

Beyond the Yoneda lemma, his name attaches to the Yoneda embedding and the Yoneda product in homological algebra. In category-theoretic folklore, the lemma is often summarised by the slogan that an object is best understood through its relationships with all other objects.
