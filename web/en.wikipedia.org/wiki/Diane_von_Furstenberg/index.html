Diane von Furstenberg (born Diane Simone Michele Halfin; 31 December 1946) is a Belgian fashion designer best known for her wrap dress. Introduced in 1974, the knitted jersey dress — which wraps in front and ties at the waist — became an emblem of women's liberation in the 1970s and an enduring classic, held in the collection of the Metropolitan Museum of Art's Costume Institute.

Early life

She was born in Brussels to a Romanian-born father and a Greek-born Jewish mother, Liliane Nahmias, a survivor of the Holocaust who had been liberated from the camps shortly before Diane's birth — an experience the designer has often credited for her philosophy of strength and freedom. She studied economics at the University of Geneva, where she met Prince Egon von Fürstenberg, heir to the Fiat fortune, whom she married in 1969.

Career

Moving to New York, she began designing with an investment of $30,000, and in 1974 launched the wrap dress with the slogan "Feel like a woman. Wear a dress!" By 1976 she had sold millions of dresses, appeared on the cover of Newsweek, and built a licensing empire. After selling and later regaining control of her brand, she relaunched the wrap dress in 1997 to a new generation. She served as president of the Council of Fashion Designers of America (CFDA) from 2006 to 2019 and, with her husband Barry Diller, is a notable philanthropist through the Diller–von Furstenberg Family Foundation, including the annual DVF Awards honouring women leaders.
