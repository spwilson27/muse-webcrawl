International Bank Account Number

The International Bank Account Number (IBAN) is an internationally agreed system of identifying bank accounts across national borders to facilitate the communication and processing of cross-border transactions with a reduced risk of transcription errors. It was originally adopted by the European Committee for Banking Standards (ECBS) and has since been designated as an international standard under ISO 13616.

Structure

An IBAN consists of up to 34 alphanumeric characters comprising: a two-letter ISO 3166-1 country code; two check digits, calculated using the mod-97 algorithm; and the Basic Bank Account Number (BBAN), which contains the domestic bank identifier and account number in a country-specific format of up to 30 characters. For example, a German IBAN has 22 characters, a French IBAN 27, and a British IBAN 22. In print, IBANs are conventionally written in groups of four characters separated by spaces; electronically they are transmitted without spaces.

Check digits

The two check digits allow a bank receiving a payment order to validate the IBAN before processing: the number, with letters converted to digits (A=10 through Z=35) and the first four characters moved to the end, must leave a remainder of 1 when divided by 97. This catches most transcription errors, such as swapped or mistyped digits, though validation of the IBAN does not by itself confirm that the account exists.

Adoption

The IBAN is mandatory for payments within the Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA), where it is used together with — and increasingly instead of — the BIC (Business Identifier Code) of the beneficiary's bank. More than 80 countries, concentrated in Europe, the Middle East and parts of the Caribbean and elsewhere, have adopted the standard. Notable exceptions include the United States, Canada, Australia and New Zealand, which continue to use domestic routing systems for account identification.

History

The standard was developed in the 1990s to reduce errors in cross-border payments within Europe, where incompatible national account formats made straight-through processing difficult. SWIFT acts as the registration authority for the IBAN format registry under ISO 13616.
