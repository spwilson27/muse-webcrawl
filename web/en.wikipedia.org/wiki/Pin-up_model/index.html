Pin-up model

A pin-up model (also known as a pin-up girl for women) is a model whose mass-produced pictures see wide appeal within popular culture. Pin-up models are usually glamor models, actresses, or fashion models. Pictures of pin-ups are intended for informal display, i.e. meant to be "pinned up" on a wall, and are sometimes called cheesecake in American slang.

History

The pin-up tradition grew out of 19th-century burlesque posters and French postcards. It reached mass popularity during World War II, when American servicemen pinned photographs and paintings of actresses and models in barracks, lockers, and aircraft nose art. Artists such as Alberto Vargas and Gil Elvgren produced iconic painted pin-ups, while photographic pin-ups featured stars such as Betty Grable and Rita Hayworth.

Post-war and modern era

After the war, pin-ups remained popular in calendars and magazines. Bettie Page became one of the most famous pin-up and fetish models of the 1950s. In recent decades, a retro pin-up subculture has revived the style in fashion, photography, and tattoo art, often with an emphasis on nostalgia and body positivity.
