Japan is an island country in East Asia, situated in the northwest Pacific Ocean. It comprises an archipelago of thousands of islands stretching roughly 3,000 kilometres from the Sea of Okhotsk in the northeast to the East China Sea in the southwest, along the Pacific coast of Asia. The five main islands, from north to south, are Hokkaido, Honshu, Shikoku, Kyushu and Okinawa; Honshu is the largest and most populous and is sometimes called the mainland.

Terrain and geology

About 73 per cent of Japan is mountainous, with a backbone of ranges running the length of each main island and only narrow coastal plains — the largest, the Kantō Plain around Tokyo — for intensive settlement and agriculture. The archipelago sits where the Pacific, Philippine Sea, Eurasian and Okhotsk plates converge, making it one of the world's most tectonically active regions, with 111 active volcanoes, frequent earthquakes, and attendant tsunami risk. Mount Fuji (3,776 m), itself an active stratovolcano, is the country's highest peak and a cultural icon. Forests cover about two-thirds of the land area.

Climate and natural hazards

The climate ranges from cool temperate in Hokkaido to subtropical in Okinawa, with most of the country humid temperate and marked by a June–July rainy season (tsuyu), hot humid summers, and heavy snow on the Sea of Japan side in winter. Southern and central Japan are struck by typhoons in late summer and early autumn. Major rivers are short and steep; the longest is the Shinano at 367 km.

Extent and resources

Japan's land area of about 377,975 km² is slightly smaller than California; its exclusive economic zone, by contrast, is among the world's largest at roughly 4.5 million km². Lacking significant domestic mineral and fossil-fuel resources, Japan imports most of its energy and raw materials, while its surrounding seas support major fisheries.
