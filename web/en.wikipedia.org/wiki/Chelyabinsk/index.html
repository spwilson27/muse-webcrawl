Chelyabinsk is a city in west-central Russia, the administrative centre of Chelyabinsk Oblast, situated just east of the Ural Mountains on the Miass River. With about 1.2 million inhabitants, it is one of Russia's largest cities and a major industrial and transport hub on the Trans-Siberian Railway.

History

Chelyabinsk was founded in 1736 as a frontier fortress on Russia's expanding Siberian frontier. It grew slowly until the arrival of the Trans-Siberian Railway in the 1890s turned it into a boom town and a gateway to Siberia, handling enormous flows of settlers and grain.

During the Second World War, factories evacuated from western Russia transformed the city into a vast armaments centre. The Chelyabinsk Tractor Plant and associated works produced T-34 and IS tanks in such numbers that the city earned the nickname Tankograd ("Tank City"). Chelyabinsk Oblast later hosted key Soviet nuclear facilities, including Mayak near Ozersk, site of the 1957 Kyshtym disaster.

The 2013 meteor

On 15 February 2013 a meteor about 18–20 metres across entered the atmosphere over the southern Urals and exploded in an airburst near Chelyabinsk with an energy estimated at roughly 500 kilotons of TNT. The shock wave blew out windows across the city and injured about 1,500 people, mostly from flying glass, though no deaths were reported. Widely captured on dashboard cameras, the Chelyabinsk meteor was the largest recorded meteor airburst since the 1908 Tunguska event, and fragments — including a large piece recovered from Lake Chebarkul — are displayed in the local museum.

Industry and culture

Modern Chelyabinsk is a centre of ferrous metallurgy, pipe production, tractor and machinery building, and food processing, with the pollution legacy that heavy industry implies. The city has several universities, theatres, and an ice-hockey tradition embodied by the club Traktor Chelyabinsk, which has produced notable NHL players. A metro system, under construction intermittently since the 1990s, was long a byword for stalled Soviet-era megaprojects.
