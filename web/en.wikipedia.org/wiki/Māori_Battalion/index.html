Māori Battalion

Active: 1940–1946
Country: New Zealand
Branch: New Zealand Army
Official name: 28 (Māori) Battalion
Engagements: Greece, Crete, North Africa, Italy

The 28 (Māori) Battalion, often simply called the Māori Battalion, was a battalion of the New Zealand Army that served during the Second World War. It was raised in 1940 following lobbying by Māori leaders such as Sir Āpirana Ngata, who saw military service as a way for Māori to assert their place as citizens — "the price of citizenship".

Service

The battalion served as part of the 2nd New Zealand Division in Greece and Crete in 1941, in the North African campaign — including the Battle of El Alamein — and in the Italian campaign, fighting at Cassino and on the advance to Trieste. Its soldiers won a formidable reputation; around 3,600 men served in the battalion, of whom 649 were killed and over 1,700 wounded, among the highest casualty rates of any New Zealand battalion.

Legacy

Members of the battalion received numerous gallantry awards, including a Victoria Cross awarded to Second Lieutenant Te Moananui-a-Kiwa Ngarimu. The battalion was disbanded in 1946. Its marching song, "Māori Battalion March to Victory", remains well known, and the battalion holds a central place in Māori and New Zealand military remembrance.
