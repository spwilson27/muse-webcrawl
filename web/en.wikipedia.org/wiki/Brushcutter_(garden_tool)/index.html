Brushcutter (garden tool)

A brushcutter (also called a brush saw, clearing saw or strimmer in some contexts) is a powered garden or agricultural tool used to trim weeds, small trees, and other foliage not accessible by a lawn mower or rotary mower. Various blades or trimmer heads can be attached to the machine for specific applications.

The term "brushcutter" usually refers to heavier-duty machines than the string trimmer (grass trimmer), which uses a flexible monofilament line instead of a blade. Brushcutters generally have a straight, solid drive shaft and a gearbox at the cutting head, allowing them to drive rigid metal blades capable of cutting through woody stems and saplings.

Design

A brushcutter consists of:

  • A power unit, either mounted near the body as part of a harness or at the end of a pole;
  • A drive shaft (pole) transmitting power to the cutting head;
  • A cutting head, typically a metal blade (such as a circular saw blade, a three- or four-toothed blade, or a mulching blade) or a heavy-duty nylon line head;
  • Handlebars — often bicycle-style "cow horn" handles on larger machines, or a loop handle on smaller ones;
  • A harness or shoulder strap to support the weight of the machine.

Power sources

Most brushcutters are powered by two-stroke or four-stroke petrol engines, typically between 25 and 60 cc. Electric models, both corded and battery-powered, are increasingly common for domestic use, and large professional machines may be mounted on the operator's back with a flexible shaft (backpack brushcutters).

Uses

Brushcutters are used in gardening, landscaping, agriculture and forestry for clearing undergrowth, maintaining paths and firebreaks, cutting back brambles and scrub, and managing vegetation on steep or rough ground where wheeled mowers cannot operate. With saw-tooth blades they can fell small trees and are used in young forest stands for pre-commercial thinning.

Safety

Because metal blades can throw stones and debris and can cause serious injury, operators typically wear protective equipment including a helmet with visor and ear defenders, gloves, and stout footwear. A blade that strikes a hard object can also cause violent kickback of the machine.
