Amish

The Amish are a group of traditionalist Anabaptist Christian church fellowships with Swiss German and Alsatian origins. They are known for simple living, plain dress, Christian pacifism, and reluctance to adopt many conveniences of modern technology, aiming instead to preserve family, community and humility.

RegionsUnited States (Ohio, Pennsylvania, Indiana) and Ontario, Canada
Populationc. 400,000 (2020s)
LanguagesPennsylvania Dutch, English, Bernese German
ReligionAnabaptist Christianity

History

The movement derives from a 1693 schism among Swiss Mennonites led by Jakob Ammann, whose followers became known as Amish. Persecuted in Europe, many emigrated to Pennsylvania from the early 18th century. A major division in the 1860s produced Old Order Amish and more progressive Amish Mennonites.

Beliefs and practices

Adult baptism, nonresistance, separation from the world, and church discipline (including shunning) are central. Each congregation follows an unwritten code, the Ordnung. Most Old Order Amish use horse-drawn buggies, limit electricity from public utilities, dress plainly, and educate children in one-room schools through eighth grade — a practice protected by Wisconsin v. Yoder (1972). Youth experience Rumspringa before choosing baptism.

Population

High birth rates and retention have produced rapid growth, doubling roughly every twenty years, centred in Holmes County, Ohio, Lancaster County, Pennsylvania, and northern Indiana.
