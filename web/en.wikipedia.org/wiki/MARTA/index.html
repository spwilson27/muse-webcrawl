The Metropolitan Atlanta Rapid Transit Authority (MARTA) is the principal public transport operator in the Atlanta metropolitan area, Georgia, United States. Formed in 1971, it operates a network of bus routes, a heavy rail rapid transit system, the Atlanta Streetcar and paratransit services, primarily serving Fulton, DeKalb and Clayton counties.

History

MARTA was created by an act of the Georgia General Assembly in 1965 and funded after voters in Fulton and DeKalb counties approved a 1% sales tax in 1971. Bus operations began in 1972 after acquiring the Atlanta Transit Company, and the first rail line opened in 1979. The rail system grew to four lines — Red, Gold, Blue and Green — with 38 stations and about 48 miles (77 km) of track, including a direct station at Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport.

Services

In addition to rail and an extensive bus network, MARTA launched the Atlanta Streetcar in 2014 and has planned bus rapid transit lines, including the region's first BRT on Campbellton Road and a line in Clayton County. Fares are collected using the reloadable Breeze Card. MARTA is notable among major US transit agencies for historically receiving no regular operating funding from the state of Georgia, relying instead on county sales taxes and fares.
