Evangeline

Evangeline, A Tale of Acadie is an epic poem by the American poet Henry Wadsworth Longfellow, published in 1847. It follows an Acadian girl named Evangeline and her search for her lost love Gabriel, set during the Expulsion of the Acadians (1755–64).

AuthorHenry Wadsworth Longfellow
Published1847
FormEpic poem, dactylic hexameter
SettingAcadia, Louisiana and North America

Synopsis

Evangeline Bellefontaine and Gabriel Lajeunesse, betrothed in the village of Grand-Pré in Nova Scotia, are separated when British forces expel the Acadians. Evangeline wanders for years through America, eventually becoming a Sister of Mercy in Philadelphia, where she at last finds the dying Gabriel in a hospital during an epidemic and he dies in her arms.

Influence

The poem was immensely popular, made Longfellow famous internationally, and became a defining symbol of Acadian identity and of Cajun culture in Louisiana, where the "Evangeline Oak" in St. Martinville is a tourist landmark.
