Eric S. Raymond

Eric Steven Raymond
Born: 4 December 1957, Boston, Massachusetts, U.S.
Other names: ESR
Occupation: Software developer, author
Known for: The Cathedral and the Bazaar; open-source advocacy

Eric Steven Raymond (born December 4, 1957), often referred to as ESR, is an American software developer, open-source software advocate, and author of the 1997 essay and 1999 book The Cathedral and the Bazaar, credited with helping to launch the open source movement. He has been the editor of the Jargon File and served as a leading figure in hacker culture.

Career

Raymond began programming in the 1970s and contributed to numerous free software projects, including fetchmail, the ncurses library, and GPSD. He co-founded the Open Source Initiative in 1998 with Bruce Perens, after Netscape announced it would release its browser source code — a decision Raymond's essay is widely credited with influencing. He served as OSI president from its founding until 2005.

Writings

The Cathedral and the Bazaar contrasts two models of software development: the carefully planned "cathedral" model and the informal, bazaar-like model of the Linux community, from which he draws lessons such as "given enough eyeballs, all bugs are shallow" (Linus's law). His other books include The Art of Unix Programming (2003), a survey of Unix design philosophy, and The New Hacker's Dictionary, the published form of the Jargon File. Raymond is also known for essays on hacker culture such as "How to Become a Hacker".

Views and controversy

Raymond is a prominent libertarian and gun-rights advocate, and his outspoken political writings and commentary have drawn both support and criticism within the technology community. He has written on topics ranging from software economics to the politics of open source.
