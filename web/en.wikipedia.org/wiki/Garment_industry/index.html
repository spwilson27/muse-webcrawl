Garment industry


Workers sewing garments in an export factory.

The garment industry (also called the clothing, apparel or fashion industry) is the sector of manufacturing concerned with producing clothes and accessories from textiles and other materials. Together with the textile industry, it forms one of the oldest and largest manufacturing sectors in the world, employing tens of millions of workers — the great majority of them women, and most in Asia.

Production

Garment production typically follows an assembly-line process: design and pattern-making, fabric spreading and cutting, sewing, pressing, quality inspection and packing. Because sewing remains difficult to automate, the industry is highly labour-intensive, and production has repeatedly shifted to countries with lower labour costs, from 19th-century Britain and New England to modern Bangladesh, Vietnam, China and India — the world's largest exporters.

Labour conditions and history

The industry has a long history of "sweatshop" conditions, from the 1911 Triangle Shirtwaist Factory fire in New York, which killed 146 workers and spurred safety reforms, to the 2013 Rana Plaza collapse in Bangladesh, which killed more than 1,100 garment workers and led to international safety accords. Fast fashion — rapid, cheap turnover of styles — has intensified pressure on wages and raised concerns about waste and environmental impact.
