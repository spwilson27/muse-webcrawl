Perelman's soul theorem

Soul theorem

Field: Riemannian geometry

Proved (general case): Grigori Perelman, 1994

Earlier results: Cheeger & Gromoll (1972)

In Riemannian geometry, the soul theorem states that every complete, non-compact Riemannian manifold with non-negative sectional curvature contains a compact, totally geodesic submanifold — its soul — such that the whole manifold is diffeomorphic to the normal bundle of the soul. The theorem was proved by Jeff Cheeger and Detlef Gromoll in 1972, who conjectured that the soul could always be reduced to a point when the curvature is strictly positive somewhere in every direction.

Perelman's result

That conjecture — the soul conjecture — stood for over twenty years. In 1994 Grigori Perelman proved it with a remarkably short argument: if the curvature is positive at one point, then any soul must be a single point, so the manifold is diffeomorphic to Euclidean space of the same dimension. Perelman's proof built on Alexandrov geometry and the concavity properties of distance functions.

Significance

The result essentially completes the classification picture for open manifolds of non-negative curvature, showing their topology is governed by a compact core. It was an early demonstration of the techniques Perelman later used in his celebrated proof of the Poincaré conjecture.
