Fernet

Fernet is a style of amaro, a bitter, aromatic spirit from Italy. It is made from a number of herbs and spices — commonly including myrrh, rhubarb, chamomile, cardamom, aloe, and saffron — macerated in grape spirits and typically aged in oak for about a year. Fernet is usually around 39–45% alcohol by volume and is intensely bitter and nearly black.

Brands and consumption

The best-known brand is Fernet-Branca, produced by Fratelli Branca in Milan since 1845. Fernet is traditionally drunk as a digestif, neat or with coffee. In Argentina — the world's largest consumer — Fernet-Branca is mixed with cola (fernet con coca), a combination popularized in Córdoba. It is also a favored shot among bartenders in San Francisco and other cities.
