Multiplex (movie theater)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A multiplex is a movie theater complex with multiple screens within a single complex. They are usually housed in a specially designed building. Sometimes, an existing venue undergoes a renovation where the existing auditoriums are split into smaller ones, or more auditoriums are added in an extension. A complex with more than 20 screens is sometimes called a megaplex.

History

The multiplex concept is generally credited to Stanley Durwood of American Multi-Cinema (AMC), who realised in the 1960s that one lobby, box office and concession operation could serve several auditoriums at once; his Parkway Twin in Kansas City (1963) was among the first purpose-built twin cinemas, and AMC went on to open the first four-screen and larger complexes. In Canada, Nat Taylor's Elgin Theatre in Toronto was converted to dual screens in 1947, and Taylor's Cineplex chain popularised the multiplex widely in the late 1970s and 1980s. The first megaplex is often identified as the Kinepolis in Brussels, opened in 1988 with 25 screens.

Features and impact

Multiplexes offer staggered showtimes of many films, shared concessions and staff, and later amenities such as stadium seating, digital projection, IMAX screens, recliner seats and licensed food service. Concentrating screens made suburban cinema-going economically viable and allowed blockbuster films to open on thousands of screens simultaneously, but the model is also blamed for the decline of independent single-screen cinemas and for a programming emphasis on mainstream releases. The largest complexes in the world include the AMC Dine-In in Ontario Mills, California, and megaplexes in South Korea and India with 30 or more screens.
