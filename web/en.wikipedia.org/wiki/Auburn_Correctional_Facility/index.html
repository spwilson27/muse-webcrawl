Auburn Correctional Facility

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Auburn Correctional Facility is a state prison on State Street in Auburn, New York, United States. Built in 1816–1817, it is the oldest operating prison in New York and one of the oldest in the United States. It is a maximum security facility for male inmates, operated by the New York State Department of Corrections and Community Supervision.

The Auburn system

Auburn gave its name to the "Auburn system" of penology, an influential alternative to the solitary Pennsylvania system: prisoners slept in separate small cells at night but worked together in silence in workshops by day, under strict discipline enforced by flogging and the striped uniform — which originated here. The regime was widely copied in the United States and abroad.

Electric chair

On 6 August 1890, Auburn hosted the first execution by electric chair, when convicted murderer William Kemmler was electrocuted; the device was dubbed "Old Sparky". The prison's famous statue of a Continental soldier atop the front wall, "Copper John", has become a local landmark.

Notable inmates

Historical inmates include Chester Gillette, whose crime inspired An American Tragedy; Leon Czolgosz, assassin of President William McKinley, executed at Auburn in 1901; and, in the women's prison era, serial killer Lizzie Halliday.
