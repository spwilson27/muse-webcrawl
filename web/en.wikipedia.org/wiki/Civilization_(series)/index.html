Civilization (series)

The Civilization series logo and artwork from several mainline entries.

Civilization is a series of turn-based strategy video games, first released in 1991. Sid Meier developed the first game in the series and has had creative input for most of the series since, and for that reason the games are often referred to as "Sid Meier's Civilization". There are six main installments in the series, a number of expansion packs and spin-off games, as well as board games inspired by the video game series.

All titles in the series share similar gameplay, centred on building a civilization on a macro-scale from prehistory up to the near future. Each turn allows the player to move their units on the map, build or improve new cities and units, and initiate negotiations with the human or computer-controlled players. The player will also choose technologies to research. These reflect the cultural, intellectual, and technical sophistication of the civilization, and usually allow the player to build new units or to improve their cities with new structures. In most games in the series, one may win by military conquest, achieving a certain level of culture, reaching the Alpha Centauri star system, or achieving the highest score, among other means.

History

The original Sid Meier's Civilization was developed by Meier and Bruce Shelley at MicroProse and released in 1991 for MS-DOS. It established the template of the 4X genre — explore, expand, exploit, and exterminate — that the series would refine over subsequent decades. Civilization II followed in 1996, designed by Brian Reynolds, and is frequently cited among the greatest video games of all time.

After internal disputes at MicroProse, Meier co-founded Firaxis Games, which acquired the rights to the series and released Civilization III in 2001. Civilization IV (2005), designed by Soren Johnson, introduced religion and a fully 3D engine. Civilization V (2010) switched the map to a hexagonal grid and changed combat so units could no longer be stacked. Civilization VI, released in 2016, unstacked cities into districts on the map. A seventh mainline entry, Civilization VII, was released in February 2025, restructuring the campaign into distinct Ages with the ability to change civilizations between them.

Gameplay

Players take on the role of a historical leader guiding a civilization from a single settler unit in 4000 BC through thousands of years of development. Core systems include city management, technology research, diplomacy, warfare, and the pursuit of wonders of the world. The series is famous for the "one more turn" phenomenon, in which the open-ended structure of a game encourages players to continue long past their intended stopping point.

Spin-offs and related games

Spin-offs include Civilization: Call to Power (1999) by Activision, Sid Meier's Alpha Centauri (1999), Civilization Revolution (2008) for consoles, and Civilization: Beyond Earth (2014). The board game adaptations include Civilization: The Board Game (2010).
