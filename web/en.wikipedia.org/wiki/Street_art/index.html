Street art

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A large mural covering a building gable in vivid colour, painted with municipal permission during a street-art festival]

Street art is artwork created in public locations, usually unsanctioned, encompassing stencil graffiti, wheatpasted posters, sticker art, murals, yarn bombing, LED art and street installations. Distinct from territorial graffiti 'writing' yet grown from the same culture, it gained worldwide recognition in the 2000s as artists like Banksy, Shepard Fairey and Invader brought its images into galleries, auctions and advertising — to the suspicion of purists.

Precursors and pioneers include Blek le Rat's Parisian rats (1980s), Keith Haring's subway drawings, Jean-Michel Basquiat's SAMO tags, and the mural traditions of Mexico and Northern Ireland. Fairey's Obey Giant (from 1989) and his Obama 'Hope' poster (2008) are emblematic works.

Legality and the market

Street art lives in legal tension: property owners and councils variously prosecute, preserve behind Perspex, or commission it. Pieces are cut from walls for auction, festivals from Bristol's Upfest to Wynwood Walls in Miami institutionalise the form, and social media — location tags, instant global reach — remade its economics without ending its anonymous, ephemeral core.

Notable centres and artists

Berlin, Bristol, Melbourne, São Paulo and Los Angeles are noted centres. Alongside Banksy, prominent artists include Os Gêmeos, JR (giant photographic pastings), Vhils (drilled relief portraits), Lady Pink and Swoon. In 2022–23 Banksy's murals on war-damaged buildings in Ukraine showed the form's continuing news-making power.
