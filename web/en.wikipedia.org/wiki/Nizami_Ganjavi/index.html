Nizami Ganjavi (c. 1141 – c. 1209), born Ilyas ibn Yusuf, was a 12th-century Persian poet, regarded as the greatest romantic epic poet in Persian literature. He spent his life in Ganja (in modern Azerbaijan) and brought a colloquial and realistic style to the Persian epic. His heritage is widely appreciated across Iran, Azerbaijan, Afghanistan and Tajikistan, where claims to his legacy sometimes overlap.

The Khamsa

Nizami's fame rests on the Khamsa ("Quintet"), five long narrative poems (masnavis) totalling some 30,000 couplets:

  • Makhzan al-Asrar ("The Treasury of Mysteries", 1163) — a didactic poem of sermons and anecdotes.
  • Khosrow and Shirin (1180) — the romance of the Sasanian king Khosrow II and the Armenian princess Shirin.
  • Layla and Majnun (1188) — the tragic Arabian love story, which Nizami transformed into an epic of mystical longing; written at the request of the Shirvanshah.
  • Haft Peykar ("The Seven Portraits", 1197) — the adventures and loves of the Sasanian king Bahram Gur.
  • Iskandarnama ("The Book of Alexander", c. 1200) — Alexander the Great as warrior, philosopher and prophet.

Style and influence

Nizami combined courtly romance with ethics, astronomy, medicine and philosophy, and was famed for inventive metaphor and psychological depth. The Khamsa became the model for later quintets by Amir Khusrow and Jami, and its scenes are among the most frequently illustrated in Persian miniature painting. Goethe praised Nizami in the West-Eastern Divan. The year 1991 was declared by UNESCO the Year of Nizami.

Legacy

His mausoleum stands outside Ganja, a major Azerbaijani monument; streets, the Baku metro station and institutions in several countries bear his name.
