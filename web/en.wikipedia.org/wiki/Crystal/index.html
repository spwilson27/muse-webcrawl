A crystal or crystalline solid is a solid material whose constituents (atoms, molecules or ions) are arranged in a highly ordered microscopic structure forming a crystal lattice that extends in all directions. Crystals are classified into seven crystal systems by their symmetry (cubic, hexagonal, tetragonal, orthorhombic, rhombohedral, monoclinic, triclinic) and into 14 Bravais lattices.

Many familiar substances are crystalline: quartz, diamond, table salt, snowflakes, metals. Their growth from solution, melt or vapour, their defects (dislocations, vacancies) and their properties (cleavage, piezoelectricity, birefringence) are the subjects of crystallography — revolutionised by X-ray diffraction (Laue, the Braggs, 1912–13), the method that revealed DNA's structure (Photo 51, Rosalind Franklin). Quasicrystals (1982, Dan Shechtman) broke the classical rules with ordered but non-periodic patterns.
