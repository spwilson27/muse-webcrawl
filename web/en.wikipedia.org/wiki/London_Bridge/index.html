London Bridge

London Bridge is a road bridge over the River Thames in London, connecting the City of London with Southwark. Several bridges have carried the name over roughly two thousand years; the current box-girder bridge, opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 16 March 1973, stands on the site of the historic crossing, just upstream of Tower Bridge (with which it is sometimes confused).

History

The Romans built the first permanent bridge near the site around 50 AD. The medieval stone bridge, begun in 1176 under Peter of Colechurch and completed in 1209, stood for over 600 years. It carried a street of houses and shops, the chapel of St Thomas Becket, and — on its gatehouse — the heads of executed traitors on spikes. Its 19 narrow arches obstructed the river so much that the Thames could freeze upstream in cold winters.

The 1831 bridge

John Rennie's five-arch granite bridge, completed by his son John Rennie the Younger in 1831, replaced the medieval bridge, which was demolished in 1832. By the 1960s the Rennie bridge was sinking and too narrow for traffic. In 1968 it was sold to American entrepreneur Robert P. McCulloch for about $2.46 million and re-erected in Lake Havasu City, Arizona, where it remains a tourist attraction.

The modern bridge

The present bridge, designed by engineer Harold N. King and architect Lord Holford, was built between 1967 and 1972 using some components cantilevered from the existing structure so traffic could continue. At its north end stands the Monument to the Great Fire of London; at the south end is Southwark Cathedral and Borough Market.

In culture

The falling of the old bridge is commemorated in the nursery rhyme "London Bridge Is Falling Down". In June 2017 the bridge and nearby Borough Market were the scene of a terrorist attack in which eight people were killed.
