Vaishya

Vaishya (Sanskrit: वैश्य, vaiśya) is one of the four varnas of the Hindu social order in India. Vaishyas are classed third in the traditional hierarchy, below Brahmins (priests and scholars) and Kshatriyas (rulers and warriors) and above Shudras (labourers and service providers). Their traditional occupations are trade, commerce, agriculture, and cattle rearing.

Traditional duties

The Bhagavad Gita (18.44) describes the natural work of the Vaishya as kṛṣi-gorakṣya-vāṇijyam — agriculture, cow protection, and trade. The Manusmriti assigns to Vaishyas trade, lending, farming, and the keeping of cattle, along with study of the Vedas, charity, and sacrifice. Like Brahmins and Kshatriyas, Vaishya males are "twice-born" (dvija), entitled to the sacred-thread ceremony (upanayana) and Vedic study.

Communities and history

Merchant and trading castes (jatis) historically associated with the Vaishya varna include Banias, Agarwals, Maheshwaris, Marwaris, Chettiars, and Komatis, though varna classification of particular communities has always varied by region and has often been contested. During the colonial and modern periods, Vaishya-associated business communities became prominent in Indian commerce and industry, and figures such as Mahatma Gandhi were born into Vaishya (Modh Bania) families.
