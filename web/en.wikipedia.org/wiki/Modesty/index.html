Modesty

Modesty is a mode of dress and deportment that avoids encouraging sexual attraction in others or revealing oneself immodestly. The term also describes a quality of humility — a modest assessment of one's own abilities and achievements — but this article concerns modesty in dress and behavior.

Standards

Standards of modest dress vary greatly between cultures, religions, and historical periods. They may regulate which parts of the body must be covered, the looseness or fit of garments, hairstyles and veiling, and the circumstances — such as bathing or sport — in which different standards apply.

Religious traditions

Many religions teach principles of modest dress. In Islam, concepts such as hijab govern clothing and conduct for women and men. Orthodox Judaism prescribes tzniut, covering rules of modesty in dress and behavior. Various Christian traditions, including Anabaptist groups such as the Amish and some conservative denominations, practice plain or modest dress, and Hindu, Sikh, and other traditions maintain their own customs.

Social context

Debates about modesty intersect with questions of gender, personal freedom, public decency laws, school dress codes, and the design of swimwear and sportswear. Sociologists note that standards have shifted dramatically over time even within a single culture.
