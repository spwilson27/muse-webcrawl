Gambling Commission


The Gambling Commission is the regulator of most commercial gambling in Great Britain, headquartered in Birmingham.

The Gambling Commission is an executive non-departmental public body of the United Kingdom Government, sponsored by the Department for Culture, Media and Sport. Established under the Gambling Act 2005, it assumed most of its regulatory functions in 2007, taking over from the Gaming Board for Great Britain. It is based in Birmingham.

Responsibilities

The Commission licenses and regulates commercial gambling in Great Britain — including casinos, bingo, betting, gaming machines, gambling software, and remote (online) gambling offered to British consumers — and also regulates the National Lottery (a function it took over from the National Lottery Commission in 2013). Its licensing objectives, set by the 2005 Act, are to prevent gambling from being a source of crime or disorder, to ensure gambling is conducted fairly and openly, and to protect children and other vulnerable persons from being harmed or exploited by gambling.

Powers and activity

The Commission sets licence conditions and codes of practice, investigates and fines operators, and can suspend or revoke licences. Notable actions include multimillion-pound enforcement penalties against major online operators for anti-money-laundering and social-responsibility failures, and measures such as the 2020 ban on gambling with credit cards and the GAMSTOP self-exclusion scheme. It does not regulate spread betting (Financial Conduct Authority) or gambling in Northern Ireland.
