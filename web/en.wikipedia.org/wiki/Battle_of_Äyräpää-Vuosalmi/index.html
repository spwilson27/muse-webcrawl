Battle of Äyräpää–Vuosalmi

The Battle of Äyräpää–Vuosalmi, fought in July 1944, was a series of battles during the Continuation War between Finland and the Soviet Union. After the Soviet Vyborg–Petrozavodsk Offensive broke the Finnish lines on the Karelian Isthmus, Finnish forces under Lieutenant General Karl Lennart Oesch stabilised the front along the Vuoksi river at Äyräpää and Vuosalmi, stopping the Soviet advance and helping to preserve Finnish independence in the ensuing peace negotiations.

Background

The Soviet summer offensive of 1944 recaptured Vyborg (Viipuri) in June and drove the Finns back to the VKT-line along the Vuoksi waterway. The Soviet 23rd Army sought to cross the Vuoksi at Vuosalmi and rupture the Finnish front, while the 21st Army pushed from the direction of Äyräpää.

The battle

From 4 July the Soviets established and fiercely defended a bridgehead on the northern bank at Äyräpää, supported by enormous artillery concentrations. Finnish artillery — directed with the fire-correction methods refined earlier in the war — and counter-attacks kept the bridgehead contained and inflicted very heavy losses on troops packed into the crossing sites. At Vuosalmi the Finnish line along the river held against repeated crossing attempts. By mid-July the Soviet offensive on the Isthmus had culminated without achieving a breakthrough.

Aftermath

Together with the Finnish defensive victories at Tali–Ihantala and in other sectors, Äyräpää–Vuosalmi demonstrated that the Red Army could not conquer Finland quickly or cheaply. The front stabilised, and the Moscow Armistice of September 1944 ended the Continuation War on terms that, while harsh, preserved Finland's independence.
