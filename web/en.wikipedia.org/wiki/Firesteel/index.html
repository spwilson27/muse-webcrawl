Firesteel

A firesteel is a fire-starting tool. In its historical form it is a piece of high-carbon steel used with flint: striking the steel's edge against a sharp flint edge shaves off tiny particles of iron which ignite in the air, producing sparks that can light tinder such as char cloth. In its modern form, "firesteel" (or ferro rod) refers to a rod of ferrocerium alloy scraped with a striker to produce a shower of hot sparks.

History

Flint-and-steel was a dominant fire-making method from the Iron Age until the widespread adoption of friction matches in the 19th century. Steels were often forged in decorative shapes and carried in tinderboxes with flint and tinder. The method is preserved in heraldry—the firesteel appears in the coat of arms of Serbia.

Modern use

Ferrocerium rods are standard equipment in survival kits, bushcraft, and military issue because they work when wet and produce sparks of about 3,000 °C. They are distinct from, but related to, the flints used in cigarette lighters.
