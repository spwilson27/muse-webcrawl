Germania

Germania (Latin: [ɡɛrˈmaːnia]), also called Magna Germania (English: Great Germania), Germania Libera (English: Free Germania), or Germanic Barbaricum to distinguish it from the Roman provinces of the same name, was a large historical region in north-central Europe during the Roman era. It was associated with the Germanic peoples, stretching from the Middle and Lower Rhine in the west to the Vistula in the east, and from the North Sea and the Baltic in the north to the upper and middle Danube in the south.

Roman provinces

Two provinces along the Rhine frontier were also named Germania: Germania Inferior ("Lower Germania"), roughly the southern Netherlands, Belgium and the lower Rhineland with its capital at Colonia Claudia Ara Agrippinensium (modern Cologne); and Germania Superior ("Upper Germania"), covering the upper Rhine, Alsace and southwestern Germany, with its capital at Mogontiacum (modern Mainz). Both were created in the late first century AD from the earlier military districts of Gallia Belgica.

History

Rome attempted to conquer Germania Magna under Augustus, establishing a presence as far east as the Weser and Elbe. The Roman defeat at the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest in AD 9, in which Arminius destroyed three legions under Varus, ended ambitions to annex the region between Rhine and Elbe. Thereafter the Rhine–Danube limes formed the frontier, and relations between Rome and the free Germanic tribes alternated between trade, subsidy, punitive expeditions and war — as in the Marcomannic Wars under Marcus Aurelius.

Sources and ethnography

The region takes its name from the Germani as described by Roman authors. The most important surviving ethnographic account is the Germania of Tacitus (c. AD 98), which describes the customs, religion and tribal geography of the Germanic peoples. Other sources include Caesar's De Bello Gallico, Strabo, Pliny the Elder and Ptolemy's Geography. In later centuries the peoples of Germania — Goths, Vandals, Franks, Saxons and others — played a central role in the transformation of the Roman world during the Migration Period.
