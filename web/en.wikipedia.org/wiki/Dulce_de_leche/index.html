Dulce de leche

Dulce de leche (Spanish for "sweet [made] of milk"), also known as manjar in Chile and Peru, arequipe in Colombia and Venezuela, and cajeta in Mexico, is a confection from Latin America prepared by slowly heating sweetened milk until it caramelizes, changing colour and flavour. It is used as a spread, a sauce, and a filling for cakes, cookies such as alfajores, churros, ice cream and flan.

Preparation

The basic preparation simmers milk with sugar (and often vanilla and a pinch of baking soda) for several hours with constant stirring until the Maillard reactions and caramelization produce a thick, amber paste. An easier home method boils a sealed can of sweetened condensed milk. Variants include dulce de leche made from goat's milk (Mexican cajeta) and firmer, sliceable forms.

Origins

Accounts of origin are contested between Argentina, Uruguay and other countries of the former Spanish and Portuguese empires; similar milk sweets exist in France (confiture de lait), Norway and the Philippines, and the underlying technique may trace to Southeast Asian routes by which the dish reached Latin America in the colonial period. Argentina and Uruguay both claim the invention as a point of culinary pride.

Uses

Dulce de leche fills alfajores, pancakes (panqueques), milhojas and suspiro de limeña; it flavours ice cream, coffee and liqueurs, and is spread on toast for breakfast in the Río de la Plata region.
