Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff

The chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff (CJCS) is the presiding officer of the Joint Chiefs of Staff of the United States Armed Forces and, by law, the highest-ranking and most senior military officer in the United States Armed Forces. The chairman is the principal military advisor to the president of the United States, the National Security Council, the Homeland Security Council, and the secretary of defense.

Role and responsibilities

Under Title 10 of the U.S. Code, the chairman acts as the principal military advisor to civilian leadership. The chairman convenes and presides over the meetings of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, assigns duties to its members and to the Joint Staff (whom the chairman directs), and serves as the spokesperson for the commanders of the combatant commands, conveying their views and recommendations to the secretary of defense and the president.

Crucially, the chairman is not in the chain of command. Operational command of U.S. forces runs from the president through the secretary of defense directly to the commanders of the combatant commands. The chairman functions as an advisor and as a conduit for orders and communications, a structure cemented by the Goldwater–Nichols Act of 1986, which greatly strengthened the chairman's role, made the Joint Staff answerable to the chairman, and created the position of vice chairman.

Appointment and tenure

The chairman is nominated by the president from among the regular officers of the armed forces — generally from among serving or former service chiefs and combatant commanders — and must be confirmed by a majority vote of the Senate. By statute, the chairman serves in the grade of general or admiral (four stars). Amendments to the law effective 1 January 2021 set the term at four years, served as a single term beginning with an odd-numbered year.

History

The Joint Chiefs originated in the Second World War, when Admiral William D. Leahy served as Chief of Staff to the Commander in Chief, effectively chairing the body. The National Security Act of 1947 formalised the Joint Chiefs, and a 1949 amendment created the statutory office of chairman, first held by General Omar Bradley, who was later promoted to General of the Army. The chairman historically serves alongside the service chiefs, the chief of the National Guard Bureau, and (since 2020) the chief of space operations as members of the Joint Chiefs of Staff.

List of chairmen (selected)

  • Omar Bradley (1949–1953) — first chairman.
  • Arthur W. Radford (1953–1957).
  • Maxwell D. Taylor (1962–1964).
  • William Westmoreland-era chairman: Earle Wheeler (1964–1970).
  • Colin Powell (1989–1993) — chairman during the Gulf War.
  • Peter Pace (2005–2007) — first Marine to hold the office.
  • Martin Dempsey (2011–2015); Joseph Dunford (2015–2019).
  • Mark Milley (2019–2023).
  • Charles Q. Brown Jr. (2023–2025).
