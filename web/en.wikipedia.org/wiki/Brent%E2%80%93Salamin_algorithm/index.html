Brent–Salamin algorithm

The Brent–Salamin algorithm, also known as the Gauss–Legendre algorithm, is an iterative method for computing π to very high precision. It was independently published by Richard Brent and Eugene Salamin in 1976, based on the arithmetic–geometric mean studied by Gauss and Legendre.

Algorithm

Starting with a₀ = 1, b₀ = 1/√2, t₀ = 1/4, and p₀ = 1, the iteration repeatedly replaces a and b by their arithmetic and geometric means, updates t by subtracting p(a_old − a_new)², and doubles p. The estimate π ≈ (a + b)² / (4t) converges quadratically — the number of correct digits roughly doubles with each step.

Significance

The algorithm underpinned many record computations of π in the late twentieth century and remains a standard benchmark for arbitrary-precision arithmetic.
