Gothenburg

Country: Sweden
Province: Västergötland
County: Västra Götaland
Founded: 1621
Population (city): c. 604,000 (2023)
Population (metro): c. 1,080,000

Gothenburg (Swedish: Göteborg) is the second-largest city in Sweden, the fifth-largest in the Nordic countries, and the capital of Västra Götaland County. It is situated by the Kattegat, on the west coast of Sweden, and has a population of approximately 600,000 in the city proper and about 1.1 million inhabitants in the metropolitan area.

Gothenburg was founded as a heavily fortified, primarily Dutch, trading colony, by royal charter in 1621 by King Gustavus Adolphus. In addition to the generous privileges given to traders from the Netherlands, the town also attracted the Scots, Germans and English. At a key strategic location at the mouth of the Göta älv river, where Scandinavia's largest drainage basin empties into the sea, the Port of Gothenburg is now the largest port in the Nordic countries.

History

The city was founded in 1621 on the site of earlier settlements and fortified with Dutch-built canals and ramparts; Dutch planners laid out its classic canal grid, which survives in the old town. Gothenburg grew through trade — the Swedish East India Company, founded in 1731, brought great wealth — and through 19th-century industrialization in shipbuilding, engineering and textiles. The Gothenburg system of regulated alcohol sales was an influential model of temperance policy.

Economy

Gothenburg is a major centre for the Swedish automotive industry: Volvo Cars and the Volvo Group were founded in the city in 1927 and remain headquartered there. Other major employers include SKF (ball bearings, founded 1907), the pharmaceutical company AstraZeneca, and the Port of Gothenburg, Scandinavia's largest, handling a large share of Sweden's foreign trade. The city is also a centre for life sciences, information technology, and events and tourism.

Culture and education

The city is home to the University of Gothenburg and Chalmers University of Technology, and to Scandinavia's largest film festival and book fair. The Liseberg amusement park is one of Sweden's most visited attractions. Gothenburg is known for its seafood, its café culture (the Swedish fika), and its music scene. The harbour cranes, the Älvsborg Bridge, and the Poseidon statue at Götaplatsen are prominent landmarks.

Sport

The city hosts the Gothia Cup, the world's largest youth football tournament, and the Göteborgsvarvet, one of the world's largest half-marathon races. IFK Göteborg, based at Ullevi stadium, is one of Sweden's most successful football clubs, having won the UEFA Cup in 1982 and 1987.
