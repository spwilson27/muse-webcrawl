Uterine cancer

Uterine cancer is a term used for several types of cancer that develop in the uterus. The two main types are endometrial cancer, which begins in the lining of the uterus (the endometrium), and uterine sarcoma, which forms in the muscle or supporting tissues of the uterus. Endometrial cancer is by far the more common of the two.

Signs and symptoms

The most common symptom of endometrial cancer is abnormal vaginal bleeding, particularly bleeding after menopause. Other symptoms may include pelvic pain, pain during intercourse, and unusual vaginal discharge. Uterine sarcomas may cause similar symptoms along with a rapidly enlarging uterus.

Risk factors and diagnosis

Risk factors for endometrial cancer include obesity, exposure to estrogen without progesterone, diabetes, polycystic ovary syndrome, and the inherited condition Lynch syndrome. Diagnosis usually begins with ultrasound and is confirmed by endometrial biopsy or dilation and curettage. Cancers are staged according to the FIGO system based on how far they have spread.

Treatment

Treatment typically involves surgery, usually a hysterectomy, sometimes combined with radiation therapy, chemotherapy, or hormone therapy depending on the stage and grade of the tumour. Most endometrial cancers are detected early because of postmenopausal bleeding, and early-stage disease has a favourable prognosis.
