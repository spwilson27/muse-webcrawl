Transition metals are the elements of the d-block of the periodic table (groups 3–12), including iron, copper, zinc, silver, gold, nickel, platinum and titanium. IUPAC defines them as elements whose atoms or ions have an incomplete d subshell.

Their partially filled d orbitals give them their character: variable oxidation states, coloured compounds and ions, the ability to form complex ions with ligands, and widespread catalytic activity — from iron in the Haber process to platinum in catalytic converters and enzymes containing copper, zinc and molybdenum.

Properties

Most transition metals are hard, dense and high-melting (tungsten melts at 3,422 °C) and are excellent conductors. Many are ferromagnetic (iron, cobalt, nickel) or form useful alloys such as steel, brass and bronze.

Colours

The colours of transition-metal compounds arise from d-electron transitions: copper(II) sulfate is blue, potassium dichromate orange, potassium permanganate purple — colours exploited in pigments, glass and analytical tests.
