The Grand Duchy of Berg (German: Großherzogtum Berg) was a territorial grand duchy established in 1806 by Napoleon Bonaparte after his victory at the Battle of Austerlitz. It was fashioned from territories east of the Rhine formerly belonging to Prussia and various smaller states, centred on the old Duchy of Berg with its capital at Düsseldorf.

History

Napoleon assigned the grand duchy to his brother-in-law, Marshal Joachim Murat, who ruled until 1808, when he left to become King of Naples. Napoleon then governed it in personal union before transferring it in 1809 to his young nephew Napoleon Louis Bonaparte, with Napoleon himself as regent. The Grand Duchy was a member of the Confederation of the Rhine and an important model state for Napoleonic reforms: feudal privileges and serfdom were abolished, the Napoleonic Code was introduced, and administration and justice were modernised on French lines.

Dissolution

The state collapsed after Napoleon's defeat at the Battle of Leipzig in 1813. The Congress of Vienna assigned most of its territory to Prussia, where it became part of the Rhine Province.
