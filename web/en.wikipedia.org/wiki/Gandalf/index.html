Gandalf is a protagonist in J. R. R. Tolkien's novels The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings. He is a wizard, one of the Istari order, sent to Middle-earth in the Third Age to rally its free peoples against the Dark Lord Sauron. Known first as Gandalf the Grey, he falls in battle with a Balrog in Moria and returns as Gandalf the White, chief of his order.

Character

Tolkien described Gandalf as an angelic being (a Maia) incarnated in human form, subject to hunger, pain and fear. He is a wanderer and counsellor rather than a ruler, manipulating events through wisdom and encouragement instead of force. In The Hobbit he recruits Bilbo Baggins for the quest of Erebor; in The Lord of the Rings he uncovers the identity of the One Ring, forms the Fellowship, and directs the defence of Gondor. After Sauron's fall he sails West with the Ring-bearers from the Grey Havens.

Portrayals

Ian McKellen portrayed Gandalf in Peter Jackson's The Lord of the Rings and The Hobbit film trilogies, earning an Academy Award nomination for The Fellowship of the Ring (2001). The character's name derives from the Old Norse Dvergatal, a list of dwarves in which "Gandálfr" appears.
