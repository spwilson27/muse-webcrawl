CC cream

CC creams market colour correction with light foundation coverage.

CC cream is a marketing term for a cosmetic product, "CC" standing variously for Colour Control, Colour Correcting, or Complete Correction. CC creams were developed as a refinement of BB creams (beauty balms), the hybrid skincare-makeup products popularised in South Korea. They are typically lighter in texture than BB creams and are marketed as providing coverage together with colour-correcting pigments that neutralise redness, sallowness, or dark spots, along with moisturising ingredients and sun protection.

Formulation and claims

CC creams combine a lightly tinted emulsion with pigments — for example green-tinted particles to offset redness or lilac to counter yellow tones — and often include skincare ingredients such as hyaluronic acid, niacinamide, vitamin C, or antioxidants, plus SPF from chemical or mineral sunscreen agents. Brands claim the products even out skin tone with a more natural, less mask-like finish than full foundation while requiring less precise shade-matching, since the pigments are said to adapt to the wearer's skin tone.

History

The category emerged around 2010–2011, with brands such as Rachel K in Singapore and Channel's Complete Correction line among the early launches, and spread rapidly through Asian and then Western beauty markets alongside the BB cream boom. The concept was promptly extended into further "alphabet creams" — DD ("daily defence") and EE ("even effect") creams — illustrating the marketing-driven proliferation of the category.

Criticism

Reviewers and dermatologists note that the differences between BB and CC creams are largely matters of marketing rather than composition, that the colour-correcting effect is modest, and that the sunscreen in such products is usually applied too thinly to deliver its labelled SPF, so a dedicated sunscreen remains advisable. Claims of skin-adapting pigments rest mainly on light-scattering optical effects.
