Pyrgi

Pyrgi (Greek: Πύργοι, Latin: Pyrgi) was an ancient Etruscan port town in Etruria, northwest of Rome. It served as the principal harbour of the city-state of Caere (modern Cerveteri) and stood near the modern village of Santa Severa, on the Tyrrhenian coast of Lazio, Italy.

History

Pyrgi was one of the three ports of Caere, alongside Alsium and Punicum. It is mentioned by Strabo, who noted its famous sanctuary of the goddess Leukothea (in Etruscan Uni), reputedly founded by Pelasgians and plundered by Dionysius I of Syracuse in 384 BC, who reportedly carried off 1,000 talents of plunder. The town grew rich from maritime trade between Etruria, Greece, Phoenicia and Carthage.

The Pyrgi Tablets

In 1964 excavations at the temple complex uncovered three gold tablets, now known as the Pyrgi Tablets, dating to about 500 BC. Two are inscribed in Etruscan and one in Phoenician, recording a dedication by Thefarie Velianas, ruler of Caere, to the goddess Uni (identified with the Phoenician Astarte). The bilingual texts provided important clues for understanding the Etruscan language.

Archaeology

The temple area includes two temples: Temple A (around 460 BC), possibly dedicated to Leukothea, and the older Temple B (around 510 BC). The site is open to visitors and is associated with the nearby castle of Santa Severa.
