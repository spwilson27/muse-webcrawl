Elocution

Elocution is the study of formal speaking in pronunciation, grammar, style, and tone, and the skill of clear and expressive speech, especially of distinct pronunciation and articulation. The term derives from the Latin eloquentia, speaking out.

History

In Western classical rhetoric, elocution was one of the five core disciplines of pronuntiatio, which was the art of delivering speeches. Orators were trained not only in proper diction but also in the management of the voice, gesture, and facial expression appropriate to the content.

In the 18th and 19th centuries, elocution became a popular subject of instruction in Britain and the United States. Figures such as Thomas Sheridan and John Walker promoted systematic methods of delivery, and elocution lessons were widely offered as a means of social refinement and professional advancement. The movement was closely associated with public recitation, school examinations in speech, and the cultivation of a standard pronunciation.

Modern usage

Today elocution training survives in speech and drama education, public-speaking courses, and competitive speaking events. Examinations offered by bodies such as LAMDA include elocution and spoken performance grades.
