Haakon Eiriksson

Haakon Eiriksson (Old Norse: Hákon Eiríksson; c. 998 – c. 1029) was a Norwegian earl, son of Eirik Håkonsson of the Earls of Lade dynasty. He ruled Norway as a vassal of the Danish king Cnut the Great from about 1028 until his death, and had earlier served Cnut in England as Earl of Worcester.

Life

Haakon's father Eirik had governed Norway for Cnut's father Sweyn Forkbeard and later became an earl in England. Haakon fled Norway when Olaf Haraldsson (Saint Olaf) seized power in 1015, joined Cnut in England, and was made Earl of Worcester in 1017. After Cnut's conquest of Norway in 1028 drove Olaf into exile, Haakon was installed as Cnut's viceroy in Norway. He drowned in a shipwreck in the Pentland Firth around 1029 while returning from England, an event that opened the way for Olaf's attempted return and death at the Battle of Stiklestad in 1030.
