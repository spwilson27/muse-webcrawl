United States Minor Outlying Islands

The United States Minor Outlying Islands is a statistical designation defined by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO 3166-1) consisting of eleven United States insular areas in the Pacific Ocean and the Caribbean Sea. None of the islands has a permanent civilian population; several host temporary military or scientific personnel.

The islands

The Pacific territories are Baker Island, Howland Island, Jarvis Island, Johnston Atoll, Kingman Reef, Midway Atoll, Palmyra Atoll, and Wake Island; the Caribbean territories are Bajo Nuevo Bank, Navassa Island, and Serranilla Bank. Many were claimed under the Guano Islands Act of 1856. Most are now National Wildlife Refuges and form part of large marine national monuments; Johnston Atoll was a chemical-weapons storage and destruction site, and Wake and Midway were famous Second World War battle sites. Palmyra is unique in being an incorporated (but unorganized) territory, largely owned by The Nature Conservancy.

Status

The grouping exists chiefly for statistical and standards purposes; the territories are administered individually, several claims are disputed (notably Navassa by Haiti, and the Caribbean banks by Colombia and neighbours), and the islands have the internet country-code top-level domain .um, which is unused.
