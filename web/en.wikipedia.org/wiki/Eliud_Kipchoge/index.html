Eliud Kipchoge (born 5 November 1984) is a Kenyan long-distance runner who competes in the marathon and formerly specialized in the 5000 metres. Regarded as perhaps the greatest marathon runner of all time, he is the 2016 and 2020 Olympic marathon champion, and he held the marathon world record, set at the 2022 Berlin Marathon with a time of 2:01:09, until it was broken by Kelvin Kiptum in 2023.

Early career

Kipchoge grew up near Kapsabet in Kenya's Rift Valley and was coached from 2001 by Patrick Sang, a former Olympic steeplechase medallist who remained his coach throughout his career. In 2003 he won the world junior cross country title and the 5000 m at the World Championships in Paris at age 18, beating Hicham El Guerrouj and Kenenisa Bekele. He won Olympic bronze in the 5000 m in 2004 and silver in 2008.

Marathon career

Moving to the marathon in 2013, Kipchoge won in Hamburg on his debut and went on to win almost every marathon he entered, including multiple victories in Berlin and London and Olympic gold in Rio de Janeiro (2016) and Tokyo (2021, held in Sapporo). He set a world record of 2:01:39 in Berlin in 2018, improving it to 2:01:09 in 2022.

Sub-two-hour marathon

On 12 October 2019 in Vienna, at the Ineos 1:59 Challenge, Kipchoge became the first person to run a marathon distance in under two hours, clocking 1:59:40.2. Because the event used rotating pacemakers and other aids not permitted in competition, the time was not recognized as a world record, but it was hailed as a landmark in human endurance performance.
