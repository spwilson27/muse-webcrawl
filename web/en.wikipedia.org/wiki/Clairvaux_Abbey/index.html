Clairvaux Abbey (French: Abbaye de Clairvaux) was a Cistercian monastery founded in 1115 by St. Bernard of Clairvaux in the Val d'Absinthe, now Ville-sous-la-Ferté in the Aube département of France. It became one of the most influential monasteries of medieval Europe.

History

Sent from Cîteaux Abbey with twelve companions, the 25-year-old Bernard built a house of extreme austerity in a remote valley. From Clairvaux, more than 350 daughter houses were founded across Europe, and Bernard's prestige made the abbey a center of monastic reform, papal politics, and the Second Crusade.

The community declined in the later Middle Ages but revived partially under commendatory abbots. During the French Revolution the abbey was seized and its church largely destroyed. In 1808 the site was converted into a prison, Centrale de Clairvaux, which operated until 2023.

Architecture

The surviving medieval buildings include the 12th-century lay brothers' building (with its vast vaulted cellar and refectory), the cloister, and the abbot's chapel. A new Cistercian community re-established itself nearby at the end of the 20th century.
