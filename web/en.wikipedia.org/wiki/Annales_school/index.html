Annales school

The Annales school is a group of French historians associated with the journal Annales. Histoire, Sciences Sociales, founded in 1929 by Marc Bloch and Lucien Febvre. It revolutionised 20th-century historiography by emphasising long-term social, economic and geographical structures over political events and great individuals.

FoundersMarc Bloch, Lucien Febvre
JournalAnnales (founded 1929)
Key conceptsLongue durée, histoire totale, mentalités

Generations

First-generation leaders Bloch and Febvre promoted interdisciplinary "total history". After the Second World War, Fernand Braudel led a second generation, theorising the longue durée in The Mediterranean and the Mediterranean World in the Age of Philip II (1949). A third generation, including Emmanuel Le Roy Ladurie and Jacques Le Goff, turned to the history of mentalities, climate, and microhistory, institutionalised at the EHESS in Paris.

Influence

The school influenced world-systems analysis, historical sociology, and history worldwide, shifting attention to peasants, demography, material life and collective psychology.
