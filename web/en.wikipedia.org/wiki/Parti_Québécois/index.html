The Parti Québécois (PQ; French: [paʁti kebekwa]; "Quebec Party") is a sovereignist and social democratic provincial political party in Quebec, Canada. The PQ advocates national sovereignty for the province of Quebec and for promoting the French language and the culture of the Quebec nation in all their activities. The PQ has governed Quebec on several occasions since its foundation in 1968.

History

The party was founded by René Lévesque, who left the Quebec Liberal Party in 1967 and merged his Mouvement Souveraineté-Association with other sovereignist groups in 1968. Elected to government in 1976, the PQ enacted the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101) and held the 1980 referendum on sovereignty-association, which was defeated 60–40. It returned to power in 1994 under Jacques Parizeau and held the 1995 referendum, which failed by the narrowest of margins—50.58% No to 49.42% Yes. Later governments were led by Lucien Bouchard, Bernard Landry and Pauline Marois, whose 2012–2014 minority government proposed the controversial Charter of Quebec Values. The party suffered severe defeats in 2018 and 2022, reduced to a handful of seats, before reviving under leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon.

Ideology

The PQ's central goal is the political independence of Quebec, to be submitted to voters by referendum; since 2011, its program describes independence as the party's "raison d'être" alongside social democratic governance. Its federal counterpart tradition is represented by the Bloc Québécois.
