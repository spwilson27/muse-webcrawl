Phlogopite

CategoryPhyllosilicate (mica group)
FormulaKMg3(AlSi3O10)(F,OH)2
ColourBrown, yellow, greenish, reddish brown
Crystal systemMonoclinic
CleavagePerfect basal {001}
Mohs hardness2.0–2.5

Phlogopite is a yellow, greenish, or reddish-brown member of the mica group of phyllosilicate minerals, with the chemical formula KMg3(AlSi3O10)(F,OH)2. It is the magnesium endmember of the biotite solid solution series, the iron endmember being annite. Phlogopite was named in 1841 by August Breithaupt from the Greek phlogopos, meaning "fiery-looking", in reference to its reddish, flame-like glints.

Properties

Like other micas, phlogopite has perfect basal cleavage, splitting into thin, flexible, elastic sheets. It is monoclinic and commonly forms pseudohexagonal tabular crystals. Its low iron content distinguishes it visually from the darker biotite.

Occurrence

Phlogopite occurs in magnesium-rich rocks: metamorphosed dolomitic limestones, serpentinites, ultramafic rocks, and kimberlites, in which it can be an important indicator mineral for diamond exploration. It is also characteristic of lamproites and some potassic igneous rocks. Notable localities include Ontario and Quebec in Canada, Madagascar, Russia, and Sri Lanka.

Uses

Phlogopite mica is valued as an electrical and thermal insulator, particularly in high-temperature applications where muscovite is unsuitable, and as a filler and reinforcement in plastics and drilling muds. Large sheet crystals have been used in electrical equipment.
