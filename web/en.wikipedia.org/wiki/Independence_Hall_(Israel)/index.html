Independence Hall (Israel)

Independence Hall (Hebrew: בית העצמאות, Beit Ha'Atzmaut) is the site of the signing of Israel's Declaration of Independence. It is located on Rothschild Boulevard in Tel Aviv, Israel, in the building originally constructed as the home of Meir Dizengoff and his wife Zina, and later known as the Dizengoff House.

History

The plot of land at 16 Rothschild Boulevard was won by the Dizengoff family in a lottery held by the Ahuzat Bayit society in 1909, and the house was built in 1910. Meir Dizengoff, who became the first mayor of Tel Aviv, donated the house to the city after his wife's death in 1930, requesting that it become a museum. It served as the Tel Aviv Museum of Art from 1932 until 1971.

The declaration ceremony

On the afternoon of Friday, 14 May 1948 (5 Iyar 5708), hours before the British Mandate for Palestine was set to expire, some 350 invited guests gathered in the main hall. At 4:00 p.m., David Ben-Gurion struck the table with a gavel and read out the Declaration of the Establishment of the State of Israel, announcing the creation of the State of Israel. The ceremony lasted 32 minutes, closing with the singing of "Hatikvah", the national anthem. The hall has since been restored to its appearance on that day, with the original furnishings and a portrait gallery of the signatories.

Museum

Independence Hall operates as a museum and national historic site, administered first by the Israel Museum and later by the Eretz Israel Museum and the Council for Conservation of Heritage Sites. In 2009 the Knesset passed the Independence Hall Law to secure the site's preservation. The site underwent extensive renovation in the 2010s and reopened to visitors with new exhibitions on the drafting and signing of the declaration and the life of Meir Dizengoff.
