Harold Gregory Moore Jr. (13 February 1922 – 10 February 2017) was a United States Army lieutenant general, best known for commanding the 1st Battalion, 7th Cavalry Regiment at the Battle of Ia Drang in 1965, the first major battle of the Vietnam War between regular American and North Vietnamese forces.

Early life and career

Moore was born in Bardstown, Kentucky. Determined to attend the United States Military Academy, he moved to Washington, D.C., to improve his chances of an appointment, and graduated from West Point in 1945. He served in the Korean War as a company commander and made experimental parachute jumps as a test jumper, surviving multiple malfunctions.

Battle of Ia Drang

In November 1965, Moore led his battalion into Landing Zone X-Ray in the Ia Drang Valley, where it was surrounded and heavily attacked by North Vietnamese regiments. Over three days of fighting, Moore's leadership — summarized in his pledge to be the first man on the battlefield and the last to leave, and never to leave a dead or wounded soldier behind — became legendary. He was awarded the Distinguished Service Cross for his actions.

Later career and writing

Moore held senior posts including commanding general of Fort Ord and the Military Personnel Center before retiring in 1977. With journalist Joseph L. Galloway, who had been present at Ia Drang, he co-authored the best-selling We Were Soldiers Once… and Young (1992), adapted into the 2002 film We Were Soldiers, in which Moore was portrayed by Mel Gibson. A follow-up, We Are Soldiers Still, appeared in 2008. Moore died in 2017 and was buried at Fort Benning.
