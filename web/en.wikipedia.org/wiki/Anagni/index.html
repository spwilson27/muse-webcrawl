Anagni is an ancient town and comune in the province of Frosinone, Latium, central Italy, in the hills east-southeast of Rome. It is a historical and artistic centre famous as the birthplace of several medieval popes and the site of the "Outrage of Anagni" in 1303.

History

Anagni, the Anagnia of antiquity, was the capital of the Hernici people before falling under Roman control. In the Middle Ages it became a favoured residence of the popes. Four popes were born in or closely associated with the town, including Innocent III, Gregory IX and Boniface VIII.

In September 1303, agents of King Philip IV of France — Guillaume de Nogaret and Sciarra Colonna — seized Pope Boniface VIII in his palace at Anagni. According to tradition, Colonna struck the pope with his mailed fist, the famous "slap of Anagni" (schiaffo di Anagni). Boniface was freed by the townspeople after three days but died in Rome a month later. The episode symbolised the conflict between the medieval papacy and the rising national monarchies, and Dante placed both Boniface and the incident prominently in the Divine Comedy.

Sights

The Cathedral of Santa Maria Annunziata, begun in 1072, is a Romanesque church famous for its crypt, frescoed in the 13th century with cycles depicting the creation of the universe, the zodiac, miracles of St Magnus and biblical scenes — often called the "Sistine Chapel of the Middle Ages". The Palazzo Bonifacio VIII and the medieval walls are other notable monuments. The town's patron, St Magnus of Anagni, a 3rd-century bishop and martyr, is celebrated each August.
