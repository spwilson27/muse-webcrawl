Vespa

Vespa is an Italian luxury brand of scooters and mopeds manufactured by Piaggio. The name means "wasp" in Italian, reflecting the vehicle's shape: a thick rear body joined to the front by a narrow waist, with a flat floorboard and leg shield.

History

Designed by aeronautical engineer Corradino D'Ascanio and launched by Enrico Piaggio in 1946, the Vespa offered enclosed, clean, easy-to-ride transport ideal for post-war Italy. Its pressed-steel unibody, gear shift on the handlebar and small wheels made it revolutionary, and it became a global cultural icon — famously ridden by Gregory Peck and Audrey Hepburn in the film Roman Holiday (1953) and adopted by Britain's Mod subculture.

Models

Classic models include the Vespa 98, the 150 GS, the PX series (1977–2017) with manual gears, and modern automatic ranges such as the Primavera, Sprint, GTS and the electric Elettrica. Licensed production, notably by Bajaj in India, spread the design worldwide; millions of Vespas have been sold.
