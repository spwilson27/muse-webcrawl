Mister Rogers' Neighborhood

Fred Rogers on the set of the Neighborhood

Mister Rogers' Neighborhood (sometimes shortened to Mister Rogers) is an American half-hour educational children's television series created and hosted by Fred Rogers. Produced at WQED in Pittsburgh for public television, it ran from 1968 to 2001, with 895 episodes, making it one of the longest-running children's series in American television.

Format

Each episode began with Rogers arriving home, changing into sneakers and a cardigan sweater while singing "Won't You Be My Neighbor?". Episodes addressed childhood feelings and real-world topics — anger, divorce, death, the assassination of Robert F. Kennedy — with honesty and calm. A trolley carried viewers to the Neighborhood of Make-Believe, where puppet characters such as King Friday XIII, X the Owl, Henrietta Pussycat and Lady Elaine Fairchilde acted out storylines continued across a week of episodes.

Legacy

The series won four Emmy Awards and a Peabody Award; Rogers received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2002. The 2018 documentary Won't You Be My Neighbor? and the 2019 film A Beautiful Day in the Neighborhood renewed public appreciation.
