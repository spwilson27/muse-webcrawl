Indo-Caribbean

Indo-Caribbean refers to people of the Caribbean who trace their ancestry to the Indian subcontinent. They are descendants of the jahaji indentured labourers brought by the British, Dutch and French colonial powers during the 19th and early 20th centuries to work on sugar plantations after the abolition of slavery.

Indo-Caribbean culture blends South Asian and Caribbean traditions.

History

The first Indians arrived in the Caribbean in 1838, when the ships Whitby and Hesperus brought labourers to British Guiana (present-day Guyana). Between 1838 and 1917, when the indenture system was abolished, roughly half a million Indians were transported to colonies including Trinidad and Tobago, Guyana, Jamaica, Suriname, Grenada, Guadeloupe and Martinique. Most came from the Bhojpuri-speaking regions of present-day Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, with a smaller number from South India, arriving at depots in Calcutta and Madras.

Demographics

Indo-Caribbeans form the largest ethnic group in Guyana and Suriname and a major group in Trinidad and Tobago. Large diaspora communities now live in Toronto, New York City, London, the Netherlands and Florida.

Culture

Indo-Caribbean culture retains strong South Asian roots reshaped by Caribbean life: Hinduism, Islam and Christianity are the main religions; festivals such as Diwali, Phagwah (Holi) and Hosay are publicly celebrated. Cuisine blends Indian techniques with Caribbean ingredients, producing dishes such as roti, doubles and curry goat. Musical forms including chutney music and its modern offshoot chutney soca fuse Indian and Afro-Caribbean styles.

Notable people

Prominent Indo-Caribbeans include Nobel laureate V. S. Naipaul, cricketer Shivnarine Chanderpaul, and politicians such as Cheddi Jagan of Guyana and Basdeo Panday of Trinidad and Tobago.
