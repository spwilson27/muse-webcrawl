Coffee pod

A coffee pod, also called a coffee pad, is a pre-packaged single serving of ground coffee in its own filter medium, designed to be brewed in a compatible machine. Related but distinct are coffee capsules, rigid sealed containers of aluminium or plastic used by systems such as Nespresso and Keurig K-Cups. Pods standardise dose and grind, trading freshness and flexibility for convenience and consistency.

Types

The 44 mm Easy Serving Espresso (ESE) pod, an open industry standard developed in Italy in the 1970s, places 7 grams of compressed coffee in a paper filter disc usable in many espresso machines. Philips and Douwe Egberts launched the Senseo system in 2001, using larger, softer paper pads for filter-style coffee. Keurig's K-Cup (from the 1990s) encloses coffee in a plastic cup with an internal filter, while Nespresso capsules are sealed aluminium; the two capsule systems are proprietary, though the expiry of key patents opened markets to third-party and refillable alternatives.

Environmental impact

Billions of pods and capsules are discarded annually. Aluminium capsules are recyclable but only through dedicated return schemes; plastic K-Cups long went to landfill, prompting Keurig to shift to recyclable polypropylene, and some cities have banned single-serve pods in municipal buildings. Compostable paper pods and reusable refillable capsules are marketed as lower-waste options, and the inventor of the K-Cup, John Sylvan, publicly expressed regret at the waste generated.

Market

Single-serve systems became one of the fastest-growing segments of the coffee market in the 2010s, at their peak accounting for a substantial share of coffee sold in North America and western Europe, despite costing several times more per cup than bulk ground coffee.
