Force H

Active: 1940–1943
Country: United Kingdom
Branch: Royal Navy
Base: Gibraltar
Notable commanders: James Somerville

Force H was a British naval formation during the Second World War. It was formed in 1940, based at Gibraltar, to replace French naval power in the western Mediterranean after the fall of France, and was commanded for most of its existence by Vice-Admiral Sir James Somerville.

Operations

Force H's first and most controversial operation was the attack on the French fleet at Mers-el-Kébir in July 1940 (Operation Catapult), intended to prevent the ships falling into German hands. The force subsequently escorted convoys to Malta, carried out the bombardment of Genoa, and launched carrier aircraft in raids against Sardinian targets.

In May 1941 Force H took part in the hunt for the German battleship Bismarck; aircraft from HMS Ark Royal torpedoed and crippled the battleship's steering, enabling the Home Fleet to destroy her. In 1942 Force H supported Operation Torch, the Allied landings in North Africa, and escorted the vital Operation Pedestal convoy to Malta. Elements of the force later operated in the Indian Ocean and supported the Salerno landings before Force H was effectively dissolved in late 1943.

Composition

The force's core typically included the battlecruiser HMS Hood (initially), the battleships HMS Resolution and HMS Valiant, and the aircraft carrier HMS Ark Royal, with cruisers and destroyers in support. Ships were frequently detached and replaced as operations demanded.
