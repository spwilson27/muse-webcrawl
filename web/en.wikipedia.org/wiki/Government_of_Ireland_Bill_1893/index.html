Government of Ireland Bill 1893

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

William Ewart Gladstone, prime minister when the 1893 bill was introduced.

The Government of Ireland Bill 1893, commonly known as the Second Home Rule Bill, was a proposal by the Liberal government of William Gladstone to grant home rule to Ireland within the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland. It followed the defeat of the first Home Rule Bill in 1886.

Provisions

The bill proposed a bicameral Irish legislature in Dublin — a Legislative Council of 48 members elected on a property franchise and a Legislative Assembly of 103 members — with an executive under the Lord Lieutenant. Ireland would retain 80 MPs at Westminster, though unlike in the 1886 bill they were to be excluded from voting on purely British matters (the so-called "in and out" problem). Westminster retained control of foreign affairs, defence, trade, and customs, while Ireland would contribute a fixed share of imperial expenditure.

Fate of the bill

After lengthy debates, the bill passed the House of Commons on 1 September 1893 by a majority of 34. A week later the House of Lords rejected it overwhelmingly, by 419 votes to 41. Gladstone resigned the following year, and home rule was not revived by a government until the third bill of 1912.
