Mashgiach ruchani

A mashgiach ruchani (Hebrew: משגיח רוחני), literally "spiritual supervisor", is a position in a Jewish yeshiva responsible for the spiritual guidance and personal development of the students, complementing the rosh yeshiva who directs Talmudic study. The role developed within the Mussar movement founded by Rabbi Israel Salanter in 19th-century Lithuania.

Role

The mashgiach oversees students' prayer, character development, and adherence to the yeshiva's standards. Traditionally he delivers regular mussar talks (schmuessen) on ethics and self-improvement, meets students individually for personal guidance (vekn), and may be consulted on religious and personal questions. In the classic Lithuanian yeshivas, the mashgiach was sometimes as influential as the rosh yeshiva.

Notable mashgichim

Famous mashgichim include Rabbi Yeruchom Levovitz of Mir (the "Mashgiach"), Rabbi Eliyahu Eliezer Dessler of Ponevezh, author of Michtav me-Eliyahu, and Rabbi Shlomo Wolbe, author of Alei Shur. In contemporary yeshivas the role varies: some mashgichim retain the traditional supervisory and spiritual functions, while others act mainly as counsellors and mentors.

Distinction from kashrut supervision

The title should not be confused with a mashgiach of kashrut, who supervises food production for kosher certification — a separate profession sharing only the Hebrew root meaning "one who watches".
