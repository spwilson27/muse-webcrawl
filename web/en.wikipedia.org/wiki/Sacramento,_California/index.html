Sacramento, California

Sacramento is the capital city of the U.S. state of California and the seat of Sacramento County. Located at the confluence of the Sacramento and American Rivers in the Sacramento Valley, it is the sixth-largest city in California, with a population of about 526,000 (2020) and a metropolitan area of roughly 2.4 million.

History

The city grew out of Sutter's Fort, founded in 1839 by the Swiss-Mexican pioneer John Sutter. The discovery of gold at Sutter's Mill in 1848 ignited the California Gold Rush, and Sacramento became a booming supply centre, the western terminus of the Pony Express and, from 1863, the western anchor of the First Transcontinental Railroad, built by the Central Pacific under the "Big Four", including Leland Stanford. Sacramento became state capital in 1854. Devastating floods led the city to raise its street level in the 1860s and 1870s, leaving a network of underground passages in what is now Old Sacramento.

Government and economy

As the state capital, Sacramento's economy is anchored by government, alongside healthcare, education and food processing — the surrounding valley is one of the world's most productive agricultural regions, and the city brands itself "America's Farm-to-Fork Capital". Landmarks include the California State Capitol, the Crocker Art Museum (the oldest public art museum west of the Mississippi) and the Golden 1 Center, home of the NBA's Sacramento Kings.

Climate

Sacramento has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers moderated by the "Delta breeze" from San Francisco Bay, and mild, wet winters with frequent tule fog.
