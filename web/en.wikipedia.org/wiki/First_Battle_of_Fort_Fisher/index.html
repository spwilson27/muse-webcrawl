First Battle of Fort Fisher

The First Battle of Fort Fisher was a naval siege in the American Civil War, when Union forces attacked the Confederate fortifications of Fort Fisher outside Wilmington, North Carolina, on December 24–27, 1864. The fort protected blockade runners bringing supplies to Wilmington, the last major Atlantic port open to the Confederacy.

Background

By late 1864, Wilmington was the Confederacy's principal remaining port for blockade runners carrying supplies from Europe. Fort Fisher, an extensive earthwork fortification at the mouth of the Cape Fear River, commanded the approaches and was nicknamed the "Gibraltar of the South." Union planners organized a joint army-navy expedition under Maj. Gen. Benjamin Butler and Rear Adm. David Dixon Porter to close the port.

The powder boat

The expedition began with an unusual scheme: the USS Louisiana, packed with some 215 tons of gunpowder, was towed close to the fort on the night of December 23–24 and exploded, in the hope that the blast would demolish the fort's walls. The explosion, though spectacular, did little damage to the earthworks.

Bombardment and landing

Porter's fleet of more than fifty warships bombarded the fort on December 24 and 25. Union troops under Brig. Gen. Adelbert Ames were put ashore on December 25, but Butler, judging the fort undamaged and fearing the approach of Confederate reinforcements under Maj. Gen. Robert F. Hoke, ordered a withdrawal after a partial reconnaissance. The expedition was abandoned by December 27.

Aftermath

The failure caused friction between Butler and Porter and contributed to Butler's removal from command. A second, better-coordinated expedition under Maj. Gen. Alfred Terry captured the fort in the Second Battle of Fort Fisher in January 1865, closing Wilmington to blockade running.
