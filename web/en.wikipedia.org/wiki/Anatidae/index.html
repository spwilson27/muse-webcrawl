Anatidae is the biological family of water birds that includes ducks, geese, and swans. Members of the family are distributed worldwide across all continents except Antarctica and are strongly associated with aquatic habitats such as marshes, lakes, rivers, and coastal waters. The family contains roughly 170 living species in about 50 genera.

Ducks, geese, and swans together form the family Anatidae

Characteristics

Anatids share waterproof plumage maintained by preen oil, webbed feet for swimming, and (in most species) flattened bills with lamellae used to filter food from water or mud. Body size ranges from the small pygmy geese to the trumpeter swan, among the heaviest flying birds. Many species are strongly migratory, travelling thousands of kilometres between breeding and wintering grounds, while others are sedentary.

Feeding and behaviour

Diets vary from grazing on grasses and sedges (geese), to dabbling for seeds and invertebrates (dabbling ducks), to diving for molluscs, fish, and aquatic vegetation (diving ducks and mergansers). Most anatids are socially monogamous for at least a season; swans and geese often form long-term pair bonds. Females typically build the nest and incubate, and the young are precocial, able to swim and feed themselves shortly after hatching.

Relationship with humans

Several species have been domesticated, notably the domestic duck (descended from the mallard, and the Muscovy duck) and domestic geese (from the greylag and swan goose). Anatids are important game birds, are widely kept in ornamental waterfowl collections, and face conservation pressures from wetland loss and hunting; a number of island species have become extinct since human settlement.
