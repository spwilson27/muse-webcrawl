Cricket field

Cricket field
ShapeOval or circular
Typical diameter137–150 m (boundary to boundary)
Key featuresPitch, creases, boundary, inner circle

A cricket field or cricket ground is a large grass field on which the game of cricket is played. Although generally oval in shape, there is a wide variety within this: some are almost perfect circles, some elongated ovals, and some entirely irregular shapes. Unlike most sports, there are no fixed dimensions for a cricket field other than prescribed minimum boundary distances in professional cricket.

Parts of the field

At the centre of the field is the pitch, a rectangular strip 22 yards (20.12 m) long and 10 feet (3.05 m) wide, with a wicket — three stumps topped by two bails — at each end. Around the pitch the creases are marked: the bowling crease, popping crease, and two return creases at each end, defining the protected areas for batters and bowlers.

The area around the pitch is divided into the infield and the outfield, separated in limited-overs cricket by an ellipse of white markers 30 yards (27.4 m) from the centre of the pitch, inside which fielding restrictions apply during powerplay overs. The edge of the playing area is the boundary, often marked by a rope; a ball struck over it on the full scores six runs, and after bouncing, four.

Fielding positions

The field is conceptually split into the off side and leg side (on side) by the line of the pitch, and into areas in front of and behind the batter's wicket — described as "square", "fine", "deep", "long", "short" and "silly" in the names of fielding positions such as slip, gully, point, cover, mid-off, mid-on, square leg, fine leg and third man.

Dimensions and conditions

Under the ICC's playing conditions for international matches, boundaries should be between 59.43 m (65 yards) and 82.29 m (90 yards) from the centre of the pitch. Grass length, moisture and wear differ from ground to ground and form part of home advantage in the sport; famous grounds include Lord's in London, the Melbourne Cricket Ground, Eden Gardens in Kolkata and Newlands in Cape Town.
