One-world government

The United Nations is often discussed in debates about world government

A one-world government is a hypothetical single political authority exercising sovereignty over the entire Earth and its population. The idea has been advanced in many forms — as an ideal to prevent war, as a prediction about political evolution, and, in conspiracy theories, as an alleged secret project of global elites. No such government exists; international authority today operates through treaties and organisations such as the United Nations, whose powers derive from member states.

History of the idea

Aspirations to universal political order appear in ancient thought, from Stoic cosmopolitanism to the universal empires of Rome and China. Dante Alighieri argued for a world monarchy in De Monarchia (c. 1313), and Immanuel Kant's essay Perpetual Peace (1795) proposed a federation of free states as the path to ending war. The horrors of the two world wars revived serious advocacy: the League of Nations and then the United Nations were created as steps toward collective security, though both stopped well short of world government.

In the 1940s, world federalists such as Emery Reves, author of The Anatomy of Peace (1945), and the United World Federalists movement campaigned for a democratic world federation with limited but real powers. Figures including Albert Einstein publicly supported supranational authority over atomic weapons. The movement declined as the Cold War froze international politics.

Arguments

Supporters argue that global problems — nuclear war, climate change, pandemics, and ungoverned technologies — cannot be solved by nation-states acting alone, and that a federal world authority modelled on systems like the United States or the European Union could address them while preserving local self-government. Critics respond that a world state would risk global tyranny with no possibility of exit or resistance, that cultural and political diversity makes a single legitimate government impracticable, and that accountability would be impossibly distant from citizens.

In conspiracy theories and fiction

"New World Order" conspiracy theories allege that a secretive elite is conspiring to impose a totalitarian one-world government, a narrative often attached to the United Nations, Agenda 21, or international financial institutions. World government also appears widely in fiction, from the United Earth governments of science fiction such as Star Trek to dystopian portrayals of planetary dictatorship.
