Cosine

In mathematics, cosine is a trigonometric function of an angle. The cosine of an acute angle in a right triangle is defined as the ratio of the length of the adjacent side to the length of the hypotenuse:

cos(θ) = adjacent / hypotenuse

The function is abbreviated cos and extends to all real (and complex) numbers, taking values between −1 and 1. Cosine is an even function, cos(−θ) = cos θ, and is related to sine by cos θ = sin(π/2 − θ) and the Pythagorean identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.

Unit circle definition

For an angle θ measured from the positive x-axis, cos θ is the x-coordinate of the corresponding point on the unit circle. Its graph is a shifted version of the sine wave, with period 2π, maxima of 1 at θ = 2πn and minima of −1 at θ = π + 2πn.

Series and calculus

Cosine has the Taylor series cos x = 1 − x²/2! + x⁴/4! − x⁶/6! + ⋯. Its derivative is d/dx cos x = −sin x, and its integral is ∫cos x dx = sin x + C. Together with sine it parametrises circles, describes simple harmonic motion and appears in Fourier analysis and Euler's formula, eiθ = cos θ + i sin θ.
