B-boying, also known as breaking (and popularly, though not by practitioners, as "breakdancing"), is a style of street dance that originated among African American and Puerto Rican youth in the Bronx, New York City, during the 1970s. It is one of the foundational elements of hip hop culture. Its practitioners are called b-boys and b-girls.

History

Breaking developed at block parties where DJ Kool Herc looped the percussive "breaks" of funk and soul records using two turntables. Dancers who performed during these breaks became known as break-boys and break-girls. Crews such as the Rock Steady Crew and the Zulu Kings popularized the form in the late 1970s and early 1980s, and media coverage carried it worldwide.

Techniques

The dance is generally organized into four kinds of movement: toprock, upright dancing and footwork performed standing; downrock (or footwork), performed on the floor with hands supporting the body; power moves, acrobatic spins and rotations such as the windmill, headspin, and flare; and freezes, poses in which the dancer stops, often balanced on the hands or head. Battles — competitive exchanges between individual dancers or crews — are judged on technique, musicality, originality, and character.

Competitive scene and the Olympics

International competitions such as Red Bull BC One, the UK B-Boy Championships, and Battle of the Year emerged as the art form globalized. Breaking made its Olympic debut at the Paris 2024 Summer Games, where b-boys and b-girls competed in judged one-on-one battles.
