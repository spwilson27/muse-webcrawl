Equality of opportunity

Equality of opportunity is a political ideal that is opposed to caste hierarchy but not necessarily to inequality of outcome. It holds that people should be able to compete on equal terms, with success depending on talent and effort rather than arbitrary factors such as birth, race, sex, or wealth of parents.

Concepts

Formal equality of opportunity requires that careers be open to talents and that discrimination be prohibited. Substantive versions go further, requiring measures — such as universal education — to offset disadvantages of upbringing. John Rawls defended 'fair equality of opportunity' in A Theory of Justice alongside the difference principle.

Applications

The ideal underlies anti-discrimination law, meritocratic recruitment, equal opportunity policies in employment and education, and debates over affirmative action, legacy admissions, and inheritance.
