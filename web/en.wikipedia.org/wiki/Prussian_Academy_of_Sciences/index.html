Prussian Academy of Sciences

The Prussian Academy of Sciences (German: Preußische Akademie der Wissenschaften) was the state academy of Prussia, founded in Berlin in 1700 on the initiative of Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz. For more than two centuries it was one of Europe's leading learned societies, numbering among its members Euler, the Humboldt brothers, Helmholtz, Planck and Einstein. Its institutional successor is the Berlin-Brandenburg Academy of Sciences and Humanities.

History

Chartered in 1700 as the Electoral Brandenburg Society of Sciences, the Academy was realised only slowly; Leibniz served as its first president from afar. Frederick the Great reorganised it in 1744 as the Académie Royale des Sciences et des Belles-Lettres, working largely in French, with Maupertuis as president and Euler as its leading mathematician. In the 19th century the Academy became the centre of German historical scholarship under figures such as Theodor Mommsen, who organised its great source-edition projects (the Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum and the Monumenta Germaniae Historica connection).

Twentieth century

The Academy awarded the first Nobel-calibre physics of the quantum era: Max Planck and Albert Einstein were members, and Einstein resigned in 1933 after the Nazi seizure of power. Renamed the German Academy of Sciences at Berlin in 1946, it became the Academy of Sciences of the GDR, the East German state academy, and was dissolved after reunification. Its scholarly projects and library passed largely to the Berlin-Brandenburg Academy founded in 1992.
