James W. Gordon is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics, most commonly in association with the superhero Batman. Created by Bill Finger and Bob Kane, he debuted in Detective Comics #27 (1939)—the same issue that introduced Batman—and was the first Batman supporting character.

Character history

Gordon is typically portrayed as the commissioner of the Gotham City Police Department and one of Batman's most trusted allies, summoning him with the Bat-Signal and sharing information despite the vigilante's extralegal status. Interpretations range from the largely ineffectual official of early stories to Frank Miller's Batman: Year One (1987), which recast Gordon as an honest transfer cop battling corruption within his own department, a version that profoundly influenced later portrayals.

Family

Gordon's daughter Barbara becomes the superhero Batgirl (and later the information broker Oracle). His son, James Gordon Jr., is portrayed in modern stories as a psychopathic murderer, providing a dark counterpoint to his father.

In other media

The character has been played by Pat Hingle in the Tim Burton and Joel Schumacher Batman films, by Gary Oldman in Christopher Nolan's Dark Knight trilogy, and by J. K. Simmons in the DC Extended Universe. Ben McKenzie portrayed a young Gordon as the lead character of the television series Gotham, and Jeffrey Wright played Gordon in The Batman (2022).
