Kongōbu-ji

Kongōbu-ji

Location: Mount Kōya, Wakayama, Japan

School: Shingon Buddhism

Founded: 816 (site); temple 1593

Status: Head temple of Kōyasan Shingon

Kongōbu-ji (金剛峯寺) is the ecclesiastical head temple of the Kōyasan sect of Shingon Buddhism, located on Mount Kōya (Kōyasan), Wakayama Prefecture, Japan. Its name means "Temple of the Diamond Mountain Peak".

History

The mountain complex was founded by the monk Kūkai (Kōbō Daishi) in 816. The temple building bearing the name Kongōbu-ji was originally constructed in 1593 by Toyotomi Hideyoshi to commemorate his mother; it later came to serve as the headquarters of the whole Mount Kōya complex.

Features

The temple is famed for the Banryūtei, Japan's largest rock garden, and for painted fusuma doors by the Kanō school. With the other sacred sites of Mount Kōya, it is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range.
