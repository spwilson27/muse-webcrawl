Eloi

The Eloi are a fictional species in H. G. Wells's 1895 novella The Time Machine. They are one of two human descendant species encountered by the Time Traveller in the year AD 802,701, the other being the subterranean, ape-like Morlocks, who prey upon them.

Description

The Eloi are small, slender, beautiful but frail beings who live above ground in decaying communal halls, wearing identical garments and eating only fruit. They speak a simple language, show little curiosity or strength, and spend their days in childlike play. The Time Traveller initially supposes them to be the descendants of a leisured aristocracy that, having eliminated danger and hardship, degenerated into weak, intellectually diminished creatures; the Morlocks he takes to be descendants of the labouring classes, adapted to darkness and machine-tending underground.

Relationship with the Morlocks

The Traveller eventually discovers the truth of the relationship: the blind, nocturnal Morlocks, who maintain machinery underground, come to the surface at night and carry off Eloi as food. His friend among the Eloi, Weena, is killed in a forest fire during his escape. Wells's division of humanity into Eloi and Morlocks is widely read as a satire and warning about Victorian class inequality.

In adaptations

The Eloi appear in the film adaptations of the novel, notably George Pal's The Time Machine (1960), in which they are portrayed as passive blond youths summoned by air-raid sirens to the Morlocks' caverns, and the 2002 remake.
