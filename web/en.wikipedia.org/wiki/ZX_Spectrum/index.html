The ZX Spectrum is an 8-bit home computer developed and marketed by Sinclair Research. Released in the United Kingdom on 23 April 1982, it became one of the best-selling and most influential British computers of all time, launching the UK home computer industry and a generation of game developers.

The original 48K ZX Spectrum, with its distinctive rubber keyboard and rainbow stripe.

Hardware

The machine was built around a Zilog Z80A CPU running at 3.5 MHz and came in 16 KB and 48 KB RAM versions. Its compact black case, designed by Rick Dickinson, featured a rubber chiclet keyboard and a rainbow stripe. Video output was to a domestic television at 256 × 192 pixels with a distinctive colour system — eight colours at two brightness levels, with only two colours permitted per 8 × 8 pixel attribute cell, causing the characteristic "colour clash". Sound on the original model was a single-channel beeper. Programs loaded from cassette tape.

Models and successors

The Spectrum+ (1984) added an improved keyboard; the Spectrum 128 (1985) added memory and an AY sound chip. After Amstrad bought Sinclair's computer business in 1986 it produced the +2 and +3 models, the latter with a built-in floppy disk drive.

Legacy

More than 25,000 software titles were released, including classics such as Manic Miner, Jet Set Willy, and Elite-era bedroom-coding hits. The machine was cloned widely in Eastern Europe and remains a focus of retrocomputing; the ZX Spectrum Next (2020) revived the design. Its low price — £125 for the 16K model — made it a cultural phenomenon, and Clive Sinclair was knighted in 1983 partly for its success.
