Uncle Sam

Uncle Sam is a national personification of the United States and its federal government, typically depicted as an elderly man with white hair and a goatee, dressed in clothing resembling the American flag.

Uncle Sam (with the initials U.S.) is a common national personification of the federal government of the United States or the country in general. Since the early 19th century, Uncle Sam has been a popular symbol of the US government in American culture and a manifestation of patriotic emotion.

Origin

Tradition links the figure to Samuel Wilson, a meat packer from Troy, New York, who supplied barrels of beef to the United States Army during the War of 1812. The barrels were stamped "U.S.," and soldiers reportedly joked that the initials stood for "Uncle Sam" Wilson. The story was popularized in the press in 1813, and in 1961 Congress formally recognized Wilson as the progenitor of the symbol. Cartoonists developed the image through the 19th century, and Thomas Nast gave the character many of his familiar features.

The famous poster

The best-known depiction appeared on a 1917 Army recruiting poster by illustrator J. M. Flagg, showing Uncle Sam pointing at the viewer above the words "I Want YOU for U.S. Army." Based on the earlier British Lord Kitchener poster, Flagg's design — for which he used his own face as the model — was reused in the Second World War and has been endlessly imitated since.

Use

Uncle Sam is used worldwide as shorthand for the US government, whether approvingly in patriotic contexts or critically in editorial cartoons and propaganda abroad, where the figure often represents American power and foreign policy.
