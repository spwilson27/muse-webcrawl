The Battle of Oriskany, fought on 6 August 1777, was one of the bloodiest engagements of the American Revolutionary War. It took place when a relief column of New York militia marching to besieged Fort Stanwix was ambushed by Loyalist and Native American forces, and it helped unravel both the British campaign in the Mohawk Valley and the unity of the Iroquois Confederacy.

Background

As part of the 1777 Saratoga campaign, Lieutenant Colonel Barry St. Leger led a mixed force of British regulars, Loyalists and Iroquois warriors down the Mohawk Valley to besiege Fort Stanwix. Brigadier General Nicholas Herkimer raised some 800 Tryon County militiamen, accompanied by Oneida scouts, and marched to relieve the fort.

The ambush

On 6 August, against the advice of his officers and scouts, Herkimer led his column into a swampy ravine near the Oneida village of Oriskany, where a force of Loyalists under Sir John Johnson and Native warriors under Joseph Brant and Sayenqueraghta had laid an ambush. The militia were caught in a devastating crossfire; Herkimer, his leg shattered, directed the fight propped against a tree, smoking his pipe. A thunderstorm interrupted the battle, after which the militia fought in pairs from behind trees. Meanwhile a sortie from Fort Stanwix raided the British camp, and the attackers eventually withdrew. Herkimer died of his wounds days later, after an amputation.

Aftermath

Nearly half the militia were killed, wounded or captured, and the Iroquois suffered serious losses too — a catastrophe that ignited a civil war within the Six Nations, as Oneida and Tuscarora sided with the Americans against the other nations. St. Leger, shaken by the sortie and desertions, abandoned the siege of Fort Stanwix later in August, fatally weakening Burgoyne's campaign.
