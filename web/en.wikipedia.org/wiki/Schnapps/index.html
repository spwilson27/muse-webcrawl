Schnapps (German: Schnaps) refers to several kinds of alcoholic beverages. In German-speaking countries, the term denotes clear, colorless distilled spirits made from fermented fruit, similar to fruit brandy (Obstler). In North America, "schnapps" more commonly refers to sweetened, flavored liqueurs such as peppermint or peach schnapps, which differ significantly from the European originals.

European schnapps

True European schnapps is made by fermenting fruit — apples, pears, plums, cherries, apricots, or raspberries — and distilling the fermented mash, without added sugar. The resulting spirit is typically bottled at 80 proof or higher and is not sweet. Varieties include Williamsbirne (pear), Kirschwasser (cherry), Zwetschgenwasser (plum), and Himbeergeist (raspberry spirit, made by maceration rather than fermentation because of the fruit's low sugar content).

American schnapps

American schnapps are flavored liqueurs created by combining neutral grain spirit with fruit flavorings, spices, or herbs, and substantial sweetener. Common flavors include peppermint, butterscotch, cinnamon, and peach. They typically contain 15–25% alcohol by volume and are often used in cocktails.

Etymology

The word derives from the Low German snap, referring to a quick gulp or dram of strong liquor.
