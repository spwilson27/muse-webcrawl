Lamella (mycology)

Lamella

Lamellae on the underside of a gilled mushroom

A lamella (pl. lamellae), or gill, is a papery rib under the cap of some mushroom-forming fungi, most often of the order Agaricales. The gills are used by the mushrooms as a means of spore dispersal, and are important for species identification. The spores are produced in the hymenium that covers the gill surfaces and are released as the mushroom matures.

Short gills that do not reach the stem are called lamellulae. The arrangement, colour, spacing and manner of attachment of gills to the stipe are standard diagnostic characters in mushroom field guides.

Classification by attachment

Gill attachment to the stem is described with terms including:

  • Adnate — broadly attached to the stem;
  • Adnexed — narrowly attached;
  • Decurrent — running down the stem;
  • Emarginate (notched) — abruptly indented before attachment;
  • Free — not attached to the stem;
  • Sinuate — smoothly curving up before attachment;
  • Subdecurrent — running only slightly down the stem.

Function

Packing the spore-bearing surface into thin vertical plates greatly increases the area available for spore production compared with a flat surface, while keeping the plates vertical ensures that released spores fall clear of the cap into air currents below.

Gill colour and spore print

Gill colour often changes as spores mature and relates to the colour of the spore print — white, pink, brown or black prints being major identification groupings among agarics.
