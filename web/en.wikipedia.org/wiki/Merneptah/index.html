Merneptah
Reign: 1213–1203 BC
Predecessor: Ramesses II
Successor: Seti II
Dynasty: 19th Dynasty

Merneptah (reigned 1213 BC – 1203 BC) was the fourth pharaoh of the Nineteenth Dynasty of Ancient Egypt. He ruled Egypt for almost ten years, from late July or early August 1213 BC until his death on May 2, 1203 BC. He was the thirteenth son of Ramesses II and the only one of Ramesses' sons to succeed him because all his older brothers had predeceased him. By the time he ascended to the throne, he was almost seventy years old.

Reign

Merneptah's reign was dominated by military campaigns. In his fifth year he defeated an invasion of Egypt by the Libyans in alliance with the Sea Peoples at the Battle of Perire in the western Nile Delta. He also campaigned in Canaan and Nubia. His inscriptions record the subjugation of Ashkelon, Gezer, and Yenoam in the Levant.

Merneptah Stele

His most famous monument is the Merneptah Stele (also known as the Israel Stele), a victory inscription discovered at Thebes in 1896 by Flinders Petrie. It contains the earliest certain extra-biblical reference to "Israel," named among the peoples defeated in Canaan: "Israel is laid waste and his seed is not." Because of this line, the stele is one of the most discussed artifacts in the study of early Israel.

Death and tomb

Merneptah suffered from arthritis and arteriosclerosis in old age. He was buried in tomb KV8 in the Valley of the Kings, and his mummy was later moved by priests to the tomb of Amenhotep II for safekeeping, where it was found in 1898. His mortuary temple stands at Thebes, built largely with stone reused from the temple of Amenhotep III.
