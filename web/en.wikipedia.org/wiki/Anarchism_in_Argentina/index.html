Anarchism in Argentina

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Argentine anarchists built one of the strongest anarchist labor movements in the world.

Anarchism in Argentina was, in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, the dominant ideological current in the Argentine labor movement. Carried largely by Spanish and Italian immigrants, Argentine anarchism built powerful trade unions, newspapers, athenaeums, and theaters, and it shaped the country's working-class culture for decades.

The FORA and the early movement

Exiles such as Errico Malatesta, who lived in Argentina in the 1880s, seeded early groups. In 1901 workers founded the Federación Obrera Argentina, which as the FORA (Federación Obrera Regional Argentina) adopted anarcho-communism in 1905. The FORA organized general strikes that paralyzed Buenos Aires, while the anarchist daily La Protesta reached mass circulation. The state answered with the Residence Law (1902), the Social Defense Law (1910), and violent repression, including the Semana Trágica of 1919 and the military suppression of striking rural workers in Patagonia (1920–22), in which hundreds were executed.

Expropriators and decline

The 1920s saw fierce internal debates and the phenomenon of "expropriator" anarchists such as Severino Di Giovanni, who financed the movement through robberies and carried out bombings. Military dictatorship after 1930 crushed the movement's organizations; the rise of Peronism in the 1940s absorbed much of the union movement into state-aligned Peronist unions, and anarchism survived mainly in publishing, cultural institutions, and small federations such as the Federación Libertaria Argentina.
