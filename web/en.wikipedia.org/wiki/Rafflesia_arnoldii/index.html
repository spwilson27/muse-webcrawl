Rafflesia arnoldii

The giant flower of Rafflesia arnoldii, the largest individual flower in the world.

Rafflesia arnoldii, commonly called the corpse flower or giant padma, is a species of flowering plant in the parasitic genus Rafflesia. It produces the largest individual flower on Earth, growing to a diameter of about one metre (3 ft) and weighing up to 11 kilograms (24 lb).

Description

The plant has no stems, leaves, or true roots; it lives as a thread-like parasite inside vines of the genus Tetrastigma in the rainforests of Sumatra and Borneo, emerging only to flower. The five fleshy, reddish petals are covered with wart-like spots, and the flower emits a strong odour of rotting flesh that attracts carrion flies, which pollinate it. Flowers are unisexual and last only about five to seven days.

Discovery

The species was described in 1818 following an expedition led by Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles and named in honour of Raffles and the botanist Joseph Arnold, who died shortly after the discovery.

Conservation

Rafflesia arnoldii is rare and threatened by habitat loss; its host-dependent life cycle makes cultivation extremely difficult. It is one of the three national flowers of Indonesia. See also Rafflesia and parasitic plant.
