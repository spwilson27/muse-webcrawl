Ship commissioning

Ship commissioning is the act or ceremony of placing a ship in active service. The term is derived from the practice of commissioning naval officers and assigning a warship to duty under a commanding officer; the vessel is not considered officially active until this milestone, which follows its construction, launching, fitting out, and sea trials.

Process

Commissioning generally occurs after a ship has been launched, outfitted, and successfully completed builder's and acceptance trials. At the commissioning ceremony, the ship's sponsor and senior officers attend, orders are read placing the ship in commission, the commissioning pennant is hoisted, and the crew boards and "brings the ship to life" in a traditional ceremony.

Naval practice

In the United States Navy, a ship is designated "USS" only when in commission and is commanded by a commissioned officer. The commissioning ceremony traditionally includes the setting of the first watch. A ship may be decommissioned at the end of its service and, in some cases, recommissioned after refit or return from reserve.

The opposite of commissioning is decommissioning, which formally removes a vessel from active service. For civilian vessels, the analogous milestone is entry into service following delivery and christening.
