Politics of Switzerland

The politics of Switzerland take place in a framework of a multi-party federal directorial republic, whereby the Federal Council of Switzerland is the head of state and government. Executive power is exercised by the government and the federal administration and is not concentrated in any one person. Federal legislative power is vested in both the government and the two chambers of the Federal Assembly. The judiciary is independent of the executive and the legislature.

Direct democracy

Switzerland is an example of direct democracy. At all levels — municipal, cantonal, and federal — citizens can propose constitutional amendments (popular initiative) or request a referendum on laws passed by parliament (optional referendum). Federal popular votes are held several times a year. The long tradition of direct democracy shapes the political system, giving voters a direct say in many matters of policy.

Federal Council

The Federal Council is a seven-member collegial body elected by the Federal Assembly for a four-year term. Since 1959, its composition followed the "magic formula" (2:2:2:1) distributing seats among the four major parties: the Swiss People's Party, the Social Democratic Party, The Liberals, and The Centre.

Cantons

Switzerland is a federation of 26 cantons, each with its own constitution, parliament, government, and courts. The cantons retain all powers not delegated to the federation by the federal constitution.
