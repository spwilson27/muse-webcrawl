2020–2021 Belarusian protests

Protest details
Date24 May 2020 – 2021
LocationBelarus, especially Minsk
CausesDisputed 2020 presidential election, authoritarianism, economic grievances

The 2020–2021 Belarusian protests were a series of mass demonstrations against the government of Alexander Lukashenko, in power since 1994. The protests erupted around the presidential election of 9 August 2020, whose official result — a landslide for Lukashenko — was widely regarded domestically and internationally as fraudulent.

Election and outbreak

After popular opposition candidates were jailed or barred, Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya emerged as the main challenger, drawing enormous rallies. When official results credited Lukashenko with about 80 percent of the vote, protests broke out across the country and were met with mass arrests, beatings and torture in detention, drawing global condemnation. Tsikhanouskaya fled to Lithuania.

Sunday marches

For months, weekly Sunday marches in Minsk drew crowds of 100,000 or more, featuring the white-red-white flag and flowers carried by women's marches. Workers at major state factories staged strikes, and a Coordination Council was formed to negotiate a transfer of power.

Outcome

With Russian backing, Lukashenko remained in power. The movement's street phase faded under sustained repression through 2021, with tens of thousands detained overall, opposition media shuttered, and hundreds recognised as political prisoners.
