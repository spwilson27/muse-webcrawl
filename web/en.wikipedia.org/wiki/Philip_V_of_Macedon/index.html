Philip V (238 – 179 BC) was king of Macedon from 221 BC until his death. The son of Demetrius II, he came to the throne at seventeen and proved an energetic and capable commander, but his ambitions brought Macedon into repeated conflict with the rising power of the Roman Republic, beginning the long process that ended with Rome's annexation of his kingdom.

Reign

Philip's early reign was marked by the Social War (220–217 BC) against the Aetolian League. During the Second Punic War he allied with Hannibal of Carthage in 215 BC, provoking the First Macedonian War with Rome, which ended inconclusively with the Peace of Phoenice (205 BC). His aggression in the Aegean and Asia Minor then triggered the Second Macedonian War (200–197 BC), in which the Roman general Titus Quinctius Flamininus decisively defeated his phalanx at the Battle of Cynoscephalae in 197 BC. The ensuing settlement stripped Philip of his possessions outside Macedon and imposed a heavy indemnity.

In his later years Philip cooperated cautiously with Rome against Antiochus III, regained some territory in Thrace, and rebuilt Macedon's finances, army and population. A succession dispute between his sons Perseus and Demetrius ended with Demetrius executed on suspicion of pro-Roman treason — a loss Philip is said to have bitterly regretted. He died at Amphipolis in 179 BC and was succeeded by Perseus, the last king of Macedon.
