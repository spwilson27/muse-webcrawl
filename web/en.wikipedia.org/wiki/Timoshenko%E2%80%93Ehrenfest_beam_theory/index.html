Timoshenko–Ehrenfest beam theory

The Timoshenko–Ehrenfest beam theory was developed by Stephen Timoshenko and Paul Ehrenfest early in the 20th century. It is a refinement of the classical Euler–Bernoulli beam theory that accounts for the effects of shear deformation and rotational inertia, making it suitable for describing the behaviour of short, deep beams and sandwich composite beams.

Kinematics

Unlike Euler–Bernoulli theory, in which cross-sections remain perpendicular to the deformed neutral axis, Timoshenko theory allows the cross-section to rotate independently of the slope of the deflection curve; the difference between the two angles is the shear strain. The governing equations couple the transverse deflection and the rotation of the cross-section through the bending moment and shear force.

Applications

The theory is widely used in structural and mechanical engineering for stubby beams, high-frequency vibration analysis, and layered or composite beams where shear flexibility cannot be neglected. A shear correction factor, often taken as 5/6 for a rectangular cross-section, adjusts the shear stiffness.
