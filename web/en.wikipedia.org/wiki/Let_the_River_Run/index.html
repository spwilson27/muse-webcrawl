Let the River Run

"Let the River Run" is a song written, composed, and performed by American singer-songwriter Carly Simon as the theme for the 1988 film Working Girl. With its hymn-like melody, imagery of New York City ("the New Jerusalem"), and a chorus sung over surging synthesizers and percussion, the song became an anthem of ambition and empowerment.

Awards

The song achieved a rare triple: it won the Academy Award for Best Original Song, the Golden Globe Award for Best Original Song, and the Grammy Award for Best Song Written for Visual Media. Simon was the first artist to win all three awards with a song written for a film for which she was the sole composer, lyricist, and performer. The track reached the top 40 of the Billboard Hot 100 in 1989 and also appears on Simon's compilation albums.

In popular culture

"Let the River Run" has been widely covered and licensed; following the September 11 attacks it was used in tributes to New York City, and the Working Girl soundtrack, also featuring Simon's music, became a platinum seller. The song remains one of Simon's signature recordings.
