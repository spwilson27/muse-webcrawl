Army basing

Army basing refers to the permanent stationing of land forces, together with their equipment, families and support services, in garrisons and military installations, and to the planning processes by which armed forces decide where units should be located. Basing policy balances operational readiness, training access, cost, recruitment and retention, and the political and economic interests of host regions.

Concepts

A station is the location where a unit is ordinarily based in peacetime, while deployment describes temporary movement to an operational theatre. Modern armies concentrate support functions — maintenance depots, medical care, schools and housing — at large "super-garrisons" rather than dispersing small detachments. See also military base and garrison.

Planning and review

Basing decisions are usually made through periodic defence reviews that assess the estate (the land and buildings an army owns) against future force structure. In the United States, major changes are scrutinised through the Base Realignment and Closure (BRAC) process, while the British Army's basing programme has consolidated units onto fewer sites such as Salisbury Plain and Catterick. Redeployment of formations from overseas stations, such as the withdrawal of British forces from Germany completed in 2020, requires large construction programmes at receiving garrisons.

Impact

Garrisons are major local employers, and closures can have significant effects on surrounding communities, while new concentrations of troops drive demand for housing, schools and infrastructure. Environmental considerations, including the management of protected habitats on training areas, are also integral to basing plans.
