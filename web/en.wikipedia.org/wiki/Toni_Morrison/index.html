Toni Morrison

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Toni Morrison in later life: the Nobel laureate with silver-grey hair, seated for a portrait]

Toni Morrison (born Chloe Ardelia Wofford; 18 February 1931 – 5 August 2019) was an American novelist, editor and professor, the first African-American woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature (1993). Her novels — rich in folklore, history and an oral, incantatory prose — centre Black lives, especially Black women's, and interrogate how slavery and its aftermath inhabit memory and home.

Her works include The Bluest Eye (1970), Sula (1973), Song of Solomon (1977, National Book Critics Circle Award), Tar Baby (1981), and Beloved (1987), which won the Pulitzer Prize and is frequently ranked the greatest American novel of its era.

Editor and novelist

Born in Lorain, Ohio, Morrison studied at Howard and Cornell, taught, and then as an editor at Random House championed Black writers (Angela Davis, Toni Cade Bambara, Gayl Jones) and compiled The Black Book (1974), a documentary history whose newspaper clipping about Margaret Garner seeded Beloved. She wrote at dawn around a day job and two sons; fame came in her late forties.

Beloved and the Nobel

Beloved — dedicated to the 'Sixty Million and more' of the slave trade — tells of Sethe, an escaped slave haunted by the baby she killed rather than see recaptured. The Nobel citation praised her novels' 'visionary force'; she later delivered the laureate lecture on language as a living, endangered act. Professor at Princeton from 1989, she received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2012.
