Asfotase alfa

Brand name: Strensiq
Type: Enzyme replacement therapy
Indication: Hypophosphatasia
Manufacturer: Alexion Pharmaceuticals

Asfotase alfa is a recombinant alkaline phosphatase fusion protein

Asfotase alfa, sold under the brand name Strensiq, is a medication used to treat hypophosphatasia (HPP), a rare inherited disorder caused by deficiency of the enzyme tissue-nonspecific alkaline phosphatase. It was the first treatment approved for the disease, receiving approval in Japan, the European Union, Canada and the United States in 2015.

Mechanism

HPP results from mutations in the ALPL gene; without functional alkaline phosphatase, substrates such as inorganic pyrophosphate accumulate and bone mineralisation fails, causing rickets-like deformity, fractures and, in perinatal cases, death. Asfotase alfa is a recombinant fusion protein — the enzyme linked to an immunoglobulin Fc fragment and a deca-aspartate tail that targets it to bone — administered by subcutaneous injection as long-term enzyme replacement.

Clinical use and cost

Trials showed improved survival in infants with life-threatening HPP and better growth and mobility in children. Because HPP is ultra-rare, Strensiq has been among the world's most expensive medicines, with annual costs reaching several hundred thousand dollars or more depending on patient weight.
