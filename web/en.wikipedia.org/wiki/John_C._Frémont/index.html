John C. Frémont

John Charles Frémont (21 January 1813 – 13 July 1890) was an American explorer, military officer, and politician. Known as "the Pathfinder," he led several expeditions into the American West in the 1840s, and was the first presidential candidate of the newly formed Republican Party in 1856.

Exploration

Frémont was born in Savannah, Georgia. As a lieutenant in the U.S. Army Corps of Topographical Engineers, he led expeditions to the Rocky Mountains, Oregon, and California between 1842 and 1846, producing maps and reports that encouraged westward migration. His wife, Jessie Benton Frémont, helped write the expedition reports, which became bestsellers.

California and politics

During the Mexican–American War, Frémont played a controversial role in the Bear Flag Revolt and the conquest of California, and was court-martialed for insubordination. He later served briefly as a U.S. Senator from California, and in 1856 ran for president as the Republican nominee, losing to James Buchanan.

Civil War and later life

During the Civil War, Frémont commanded the Department of the West but was relieved by Lincoln after issuing an unauthorized emancipation proclamation in Missouri. He later served as governor of the Arizona Territory (1878–1881) and died in New York City in 1890.
