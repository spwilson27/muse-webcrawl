George Bass

George Bass (30 January 1771 – after 5 February 1803) was a British naval surgeon and explorer of Australia and Tasmania. With Matthew Flinders, he circumnavigated Tasmania in 1798–99, proving it an island; the strait between Tasmania and the mainland is named Bass Strait.

George Bass
Born: 30 January 1771, Aswarby, Lincolnshire
Died: after 1803 (disappeared at sea)
Occupation: Naval surgeon, explorer

Exploration

Bass arrived in Sydney in 1795 aboard HMS Reliance with Matthew Flinders. In a tiny boat, the Tom Thumb, they explored Botany Bay and the Georges River. In 1797 Bass sailed south in a whaleboat, reaching Western Port and discovering evidence of a strait. In 1798–99, in the sloop Norfolk, Bass and Flinders circumnavigated Van Diemen's Land (Tasmania), confirming the strait.

Later life and disappearance

Bass turned to merchant venturing in the Pacific — trading, and a planned venture to South America. He sailed from Sydney in February 1803 aboard the brig Venus and was never seen again; his fate remains unknown, with theories ranging from wreck to capture by the Spanish. Places named for him include Bass Strait, Bass Point, the town of Bass in Victoria and the Bass Highway.
