Ghost

In folklore and popular belief, a ghost is the soul or spirit of a dead person or animal that can appear to the living. Descriptions of ghosts vary widely, from an invisible presence or faint murmuring to translucent figures and lifelike apparitions. A place in which ghosts are said to appear or which they are believed to frequent is described as haunted. Belief in ghosts is found in cultures all over the world and is closely connected with ancestor veneration, funerary ritual and ideas of an afterlife.

History of belief

Belief in the spirits of the dead returning to the world of the living is ancient. Homer's Odyssey depicts shades in Hades; Roman writers such as Pliny the Younger recorded haunted-house stories; and medieval literature abounds in revenants. Ghosts figure prominently in the religious traditions of China, India, Africa and the indigenous Americas, where rituals often aim to appease or guide the dead. In the nineteenth century, Spiritualism and organisations such as the Society for Psychical Research (founded 1882) attempted to investigate apparitions systematically, while stage magic and sceptical writers exposed fraudulent mediums.

Characteristics and typology

Folklorists classify ghosts by their behaviour: some are said to be bound to a location, re-enacting past events ("residual hauntings"), others to interact with witnesses ("intelligent hauntings"), and poltergeists are associated with physical disturbances such as rapping and thrown objects. Ghosts are typically described as wearing the clothes of their era, appearing at night, and vanishing when approached; many traditions hold that a ghost lingers because of an unfulfilled task, an injustice, or improper burial.

Science and scepticism

No scientific evidence supports the existence of ghosts. Psychologists attribute reported experiences to sleep paralysis, suggestion, infrasound, carbon monoxide poisoning, the ideomotor effect and the human tendency to perceive agency in ambiguous stimuli. Despite this, surveys regularly find that a substantial minority of the public believes in ghosts, and ghost lore remains a staple of literature, theatre and film — from the ghost of Hamlet's father to modern horror cinema.
