The 2016 World Series was the championship series of Major League Baseball's 2016 season, played between the National League champion Chicago Cubs and the American League champion Cleveland Indians. The Cubs won the best-of-seven series four games to three, claiming their first World Series title since 1908 and ending the longest championship drought in North American professional sports. The decisive Game 7, an 8–7 extra-inning victory after a rain delay, is frequently ranked among the greatest games in baseball history.

Background

The matchup featured the two clubs with the longest active title droughts in MLB: the Cubs had not won since 1908 nor appeared in a World Series since 1945, while Cleveland's last championship came in 1948. Chicago, managed by Joe Maddon, won 103 games in the regular season, the best record in baseball. Cleveland, under Terry Francona, reached the Series despite injuries to key pitchers, relying on ace Corey Kluber and a dominant bullpen led by Andrew Miller.

Summary of games

Cleveland took a 3–1 series lead behind Kluber's pitching and strong relief work, winning Games 1, 3 and 4. The Cubs staved off elimination in Game 5 at Wrigley Field thanks to Jon Lester and closer Aroldis Chapman, then won Game 6 in Cleveland 9–3, powered by Addison Russell's grand slam.

In Game 7, Chicago built a 6–3 lead, but Cleveland rallied in the eighth inning — capped by Rajai Davis's two-run home run off Chapman — to tie the game 6–6. A 17-minute rain delay before the tenth inning broke the tension; in the top of the tenth, Ben Zobrist doubled home Albert Almora Jr. with the go-ahead run and Miguel Montero added an insurance single. Cleveland scored once in the bottom of the inning, but Mike Montgomery induced a ground out from Michael Martínez to Kris Bryant, whose throw to Anthony Rizzo ended the game and the 108-year wait.

Legacy

An estimated five million people attended the Cubs' victory parade in Chicago. Game 7 drew more than 40 million television viewers in the United States, the most for a baseball game in a quarter-century. Cleveland's drought, meanwhile, extended beyond the series and remains the longest in MLB following the Cubs' victory.
