The Federal Parliament of Nepal (Nepali: संघीय संसद नेपाल, Saṅghīya Sansada Nēpāla) is the federal and supreme legislature of Nepal. It is bicameral, consisting of an upper house, the National Assembly (Rastriya Sabha), and a lower house, the House of Representatives (Pratinidhi Sabha), alongside the President of Nepal as part of the legislature under the Constitution of Nepal 2015.

Composition

The House of Representatives has 275 members: 165 elected by first-past-the-post voting from single-member constituencies and 110 through proportional representation. Members serve five-year terms. The National Assembly has 59 members elected by provincial electoral colleges and presidential appointees, serving staggered six-year terms, with a third retiring every two years.

History

The Federal Parliament was established under the 2015 constitution, which transformed Nepal from a unitary state into a federal democratic republic following the abolition of the monarchy in 2008 and the end of the Constituent Assembly process. The first elections under the new system were held in 2017.

Functions

The parliament enacts federal laws, approves the budget, ratifies treaties, and can amend the constitution with a two-thirds majority. It elects the president and can remove the prime minister through a vote of no confidence.
