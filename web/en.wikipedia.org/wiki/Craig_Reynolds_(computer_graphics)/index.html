Craig Reynolds (computer graphics)

Craig W. Reynolds (born March 15, 1953) is an American computer graphics researcher best known for creating the Boids artificial life model of flocking behaviour, presented at SIGGRAPH in 1987 in the paper "Flocks, Herds, and Schools: A Distributed Behavioral Model".

Boids

Reynolds demonstrated that the coordinated motion of bird flocks, fish schools and herds could emerge from three simple local rules applied by each individual agent ("boid"): separation (steer to avoid crowding neighbours), alignment (steer towards the average heading of neighbours) and cohesion (steer towards the average position of neighbours). The model became a landmark in artificial life and complex systems, and was used to animate flocks of bats and penguins in films such as Batman Returns (1992) and stampedes in The Lion King (1994).

Career

Reynolds worked at Symbolics, Inc. during the Lisp machine era, and later at Electronic Arts and Sony Computer Entertainment America, researching autonomous character steering behaviours — his 1999 paper "Steering Behaviors for Autonomous Characters" is widely used in game development. He received a Scientific and Technical Award from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences in 1998 for pioneering work in the development of three-dimensional computer animation for motion pictures.
