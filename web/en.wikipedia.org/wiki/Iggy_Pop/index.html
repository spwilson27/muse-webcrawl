James Newell Osterberg Jr. (born April 21, 1947), known professionally as Iggy Pop, is an American singer, musician, songwriter and actor. Called the "Godfather of Punk", he was the vocalist and lyricist of the proto-punk band The Stooges, formed in Ann Arbor, Michigan in 1967, and is known for his outrageous, unpredictable stage antics and his deep baritone voice.

The Stooges

The Stooges' albums The Stooges (1969), Fun House (1970) and Raw Power (1973) sold poorly on release but became hugely influential on punk and alternative rock; songs like "I Wanna Be Your Dog", "Search and Destroy" and "No Fun" became standards covered by punk bands. Iggy's stage behaviour — writhing shirtless, smearing himself with peanut butter and raw meat, cutting himself with glass, and popularising the stage dive — foreshadowed punk performance.

Solo career

After the Stooges collapsed amid drug addiction, his friend David Bowie helped him restart: the two relocated to Berlin, where Iggy recorded The Idiot and Lust for Life (both 1977), the latter containing "The Passenger" and the title track. His solo career continued through the 1980s hit "Real Wild Child (Wild One)", the acclaimed Brick by Brick (1990), and later experiments including the French-language jazz album Préliminaires (2009) and the Josh Homme collaboration Post Pop Depression (2016).

Legacy

The Stooges were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2010. Iggy has also acted in films including Cry-Baby, Dead Man and Coffee and Cigarettes, and lent his voice to animation and narration. His song "Lust for Life" reached a new generation via the film Trainspotting (1996).
