Scientist (born Hopeton Overton Brown, April 18, 1960), also known as Hopeton Brown, is a Jamaican dub music producer and mixing engineer. Protégé of dub inventor King Tubby, he defined the heavy, spacious sound of early-1980s dub and bridged the roots era and the digital dancehall age.

Career

Brown built his first amplifier as a teenager and repaired radios and televisions before joining King Tubby's studio in Waterhouse, Kingston, where his technical aptitude earned him the name "Scientist". By sixteen he was mixing sessions; by 1979–80 he was Tubby's principal engineer and then the main mixer at Channel One, working with the Roots Radics band — then the most feared rhythm section in reggae.

His albums for Greensleeves Records, with tongue-in-cheek science-fiction and horror titles designed by Tony McDermott — Scientist Rids the World of the Evil Curse of the Vampires (1981), Scientist Meets the Space Invaders (1981), and Scientist Encounters Pac-Man (1982) — became canonical dub LPs. The track "Your Teeth in My Neck" later reached a new audience in the video game Grand Theft Auto III (2001).

Later work

In the mid-1980s Scientist moved to the United States, working in Washington, D.C. and later California, engineering for live sound and continuing to mix dub. Disputes over credits and unpaid royalties on his classic albums led to litigation in the 2000s. His style — thunderous bass, drums drenched in spring reverb, and instruments dropping in and out of the mix — remains the template for dub worldwide.
