Pál Turán (18 August 1910 – 26 September 1976) was a Hungarian mathematician who worked primarily in extremal combinatorics and analytic number theory. He was a lifelong friend and collaborator of Paul Erdős, with whom he wrote 28 joint papers.

Life

Turán was born in Budapest into a Jewish family. He obtained his PhD from Eötvös Loránd University in 1935 under Lipót Fejér. Because of anti-Jewish legislation he could not obtain a university position for some time and worked as a tutor and teacher. During the Second World War he was sent to labour camps. After the war he became a professor at Eötvös Loránd University, where he worked until his death. He was a member of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences and won the Kossuth Prize.

Work

Turán is best known for founding extremal graph theory with his 1941 paper on Turán's theorem, which determines the maximum number of edges in a graph not containing a complete subgraph of a given size; the extremal examples are the Turán graphs. In number theory he founded the field of probabilistic number theory through his work on the distribution of prime factors of integers, and proved the Turán–Kubilius inequality. He also developed methods in approximation theory (Turán's method of power sums) and in the theory of the Riemann zeta function, including work on comparative prime number theory with Stanisław Knapowski.
