Denmark Street

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Denmark Street is a street on the edge of London's West End, running from Charing Cross Road to St Giles High Street in the London Borough of Camden. It is famous for its association with British popular music and is often called the British Tin Pan Alley. The street is home to many music publishers, recording studios, rehearsal rooms, and shops selling musical instruments — particularly guitars.

History

The street was developed in the 1680s and named in honour of Prince George of Denmark. In the early 20th century it became a centre of the British music publishing industry. By the 1950s and 1960s it was the heart of the British music business: songwriters, publishers, managers and studios clustered in its modest Georgian terraces.

Regent Sounds recording studio at No. 4 hosted early recordings by the Rolling Stones, who cut their first album there in 1964. The Giaconda café on the street was a famous meeting place for musicians. Songwriters and performers associated with the street include Lionel Bart, and later the Sex Pistols, who lived and rehearsed at No. 6 in 1975–76. David Bowie frequented the Giaconda and recruited musicians through adverts placed in the street's shops.

Music shops

From the 1990s the publishing offices declined, but the street became renowned for its dense concentration of guitar shops, including long-established instrument retailers and vintage guitar dealers. Redevelopment around Tottenham Court Road station in the 2010s threatened the street's character, prompting campaigns to protect its musical heritage; some buildings received listed status recognition of their cultural significance.

Blue plaques and heritage

The street's musical history is commemorated in heritage schemes and guided walks of London's music history. It remains a pilgrimage site for guitarists and popular-music fans.
