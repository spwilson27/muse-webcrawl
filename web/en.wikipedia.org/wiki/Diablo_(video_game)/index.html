Diablo (video game)

Diablo
Developer: Blizzard North
Publisher: Blizzard Entertainment
Release: 1997
Genre: Action role-playing, hack and slash
Platforms: Windows, Mac OS, PlayStation

Diablo is an action role-playing video game developed by Blizzard North and released by Blizzard Entertainment in January 1997. It is the first instalment in the Diablo series.

Gameplay

The player controls a lone hero belonging to one of three character classes — Warrior, Rogue, or Sorcerer — who descends through the sixteen levels of a dungeon beneath the cathedral of the town of Tristram to confront Diablo, the Lord of Terror. The dungeons are procedurally generated, and gameplay centres on real-time combat, looting equipment of randomised quality, and character progression. Diablo was notable for its dark Gothic atmosphere, mouse-driven interface, and free multiplayer service Battle.net, which supported cooperative play.

Development and reception

The game originated with Condor Games, which was acquired by Blizzard and renamed Blizzard North. Its design drew inspiration from roguelike games such as Moria and Angband, adapted into an accessible real-time format. Diablo was a major commercial and critical success, credited with revitalising the computer role-playing genre. An expansion pack, Diablo: Hellfire, was released later in 1997, and the sequel Diablo II followed in 2000.

Legacy

Diablo popularised the action RPG subgenre and influenced numerous later "Diablo clone" games. The franchise has continued with Diablo III (2012), Diablo Immortal (2022) and Diablo IV (2023).
