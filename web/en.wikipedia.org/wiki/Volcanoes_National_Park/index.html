Volcanoes National Park
Location:Northwestern Rwanda
Area:160 km²
Established:1925
Governing body:Rwanda Development Board

Volcanoes National Park (French: Parc National des Volcans) is a national park in northwestern Rwanda covering 160 km2 (62 sq mi) of rainforest and encompassing five of the eight volcanoes of the Virunga Mountains: Karisimbi, Bisoke, Muhabura, Gahinga and Sabyinyo. It borders Virunga National Park in the Democratic Republic of Congo and Mgahinga Gorilla National Park in Uganda.

History

The park was first gazetted in 1925, as part of the Belgian colonial Albert National Park — among the first national parks created in Africa — and became Rwandan territory at independence in 1962. Its boundaries shrank considerably in the 1950s–1970s as land was cleared for pyrethrum cultivation and settlement. The park was a battleground during the Rwandan Civil War in the early 1990s, and tourism halted until 1999.

Wildlife and tourism

Volcanoes National Park is famous as a refuge of the endangered mountain gorilla and the endangered golden monkey. It was the base for the primatologist Dian Fossey, whose research station Karisoke (founded 1967) and whose life and murder in 1985 are recounted in Gorillas in the Mist; she is buried in the park beside the gorilla cemetery. Regulated gorilla trekking, with permits strictly limited and priced at US$1,500 per person, is Rwanda's flagship tourism product and funds conservation and local communities. Vegetation ranges from bamboo forest to Hagenia woodland and afro-alpine moorland on the volcano slopes, with peaks up to Mount Karisimbi at 4,507 m, Rwanda's highest point.
