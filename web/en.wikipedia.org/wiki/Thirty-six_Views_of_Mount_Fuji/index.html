Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji

Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji (Fugaku sanjūrokkei) is a series of landscape prints by the Japanese ukiyo-e artist Katsushika Hokusai (1760–1849), created around 1830–1832 during the Edo period. Each print depicts Mount Fuji from a different location and in varying seasons and weather conditions, framed by scenes of contemporary Japanese life.

The series

Published by Nishimuraya Yohachi, the series proved so popular that ten additional prints were added, bringing the total to forty-six despite the title. The prints make striking use of the imported synthetic pigment Prussian blue, a feature of the so-called aizuri-e (blue pictures) fashion of the time.

Notable prints

The best-known images in the series are The Great Wave off Kanagawa, in which a huge wave threatens boats beneath a distant, small Fuji; Fine Wind, Clear Morning (the "Red Fuji"), showing the mountain's southern slope tinted red in early autumn light; and Thunderstorm Beneath the Summit (the "Black Fuji").

Influence

Hokusai's series influenced European artists of the later nineteenth century, including the Impressionists and Vincent van Gogh, and helped establish the landscape print as a major genre of ukiyo-e. Hiroshige later produced his own series of the same title in 1858.
