The sinoatrial node (SA node), also called the sinus node, is a group of specialised cells in the wall of the right atrium, near the entrance of the superior vena cava. It is the heart's natural pacemaker: its cells spontaneously generate electrical impulses that initiate each heartbeat and set the sinus rhythm.

Physiology

SA node cells have no stable resting potential; ion flows — notably the "funny" pacemaker current (If) and calcium currents — produce a slow diastolic depolarisation that reaches threshold and fires an action potential. The impulse spreads across the atria, then reaches the atrioventricular node, whose delay allows ventricular filling before the signal passes via the bundle of His and Purkinje fibers to the ventricles. Autonomic nerves modulate the rate: sympathetic stimulation accelerates it and vagal (parasympathetic) activity slows it.

Clinical significance

Dysfunction of the node causes sick sinus syndrome, with inappropriate bradycardia, sinus pauses and sometimes alternating slow and fast rhythms (tachy-brady syndrome), producing fatigue and syncope; treatment may require a pacemaker. In atrial fibrillation, the node's orderly control of rhythm is lost. The resulting P wave and rhythm are assessed by electrocardiography.
