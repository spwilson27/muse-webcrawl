Heart block (HB) is a disorder in which the heart beats too slowly or with an abnormal rhythm because electrical signals are partially or completely blocked between the upper chambers (atria) and lower chambers (ventricles). When the block occurs at the atrioventricular node it is called AV block; disease may also lie in the bundle of His or bundle branches.

Degrees of AV block

  • First-degree — every impulse is conducted, but the PR interval is prolonged beyond 200 ms; usually benign.
  • Second-degree — some impulses fail to conduct. Mobitz I (Wenckebach) shows progressive PR lengthening before a dropped beat; Mobitz II shows sudden dropped beats and is more serious.
  • Third-degree (complete) — no atrial impulses reach the ventricles, which are maintained by a slow escape rhythm; atria and ventricles beat independently.

Causes and symptoms

Causes include age-related fibrosis of the conduction system (Lenègre–Lev disease), ischaemic heart disease, medications such as beta-blockers and digoxin, congenital block, and infiltrative or inflammatory disease. Symptoms range from none to fatigue, breathlessness, presyncope and syncope (Stokes–Adams attacks). Diagnosis is by electrocardiography.

Treatment

Reversible causes are corrected first. Symptomatic second-degree (Mobitz II) and complete heart block are treated with a permanent pacemaker; temporary pacing or atropine may be used in emergencies.
