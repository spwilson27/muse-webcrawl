The bundle of His (BH), also called the atrioventricular bundle, is a collection of specialised heart muscle cells involved in the electrical conduction system of the heart. It transmits impulses from the atrioventricular (AV) node, located at the junction of the atria and ventricles, to the point of the apex of the fascicular branches — the bundle branches — which in turn distribute the signal to the Purkinje fibers.

Anatomy and function

The bundle penetrates the central fibrous body of the heart, the only electrical connection between atria and ventricles in the normal heart. It then divides into right and left bundle branches running down the interventricular septum. Its blood supply comes from branches of the coronary arteries. Together with the delay at the AV node, conduction through the bundle ensures the ventricles contract after the atria have finished filling them.

Clinical significance

Disease within the bundle or its branches produces bundle branch block and, if conduction fails completely, heart block. "His bundle pacing" — placing a pacemaker lead directly on the bundle — is an increasingly used technique that produces more physiological ventricular activation than conventional right-ventricular pacing. The bundle was described by Swiss cardiologist Wilhelm His Jr. in 1893.
