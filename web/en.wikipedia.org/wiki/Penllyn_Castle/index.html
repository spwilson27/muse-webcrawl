Penllyn Castle

Penllyn Castle, Vale of Glamorgan.

Penllyn Castle is a Norman castle and country house near the village of Penllyn in the Vale of Glamorgan, South Wales. The surviving ruins include what is regarded as one of the earliest rectangular Norman keeps in Wales, standing on a commanding site above the River Thaw valley.

History

The castle is traditionally associated with Robert Fitzhamon and the Norman conquest of Glamorgan in the late eleventh century, though the surviving stone keep is usually dated to the mid-twelfth century, possibly built by Robert de Norris or by the earls of Gloucester. The rectangular keep, with its distinctive round corner turrets, is notable in Welsh castle architecture. A later house was built within and beside the ruins, and the estate passed through several families, including the Turbervills. In the nineteenth century the site came into the hands of the Homfray family, ironmasters of Merthyr Tydfil, who made additions to the house.

Description

The keep is a two-storey rectangular block with rounded corner towers, built of local limestone. Remains of the curtain wall and the later domestic ranges adjoin it. Penllyn Castle is a scheduled monument and a listed building, reflecting the architectural and historical importance of its Norman keep.
