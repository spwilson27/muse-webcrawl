Nightmare

SpecialtySleep medicine, psychology
Sleep stageREM sleep
SymptomsFrightening vivid dream causing awakening

A nightmare is a disturbing dream associated with negative feelings, such as anxiety or fear, that awakens the dreamer. Nightmares occur during rapid eye movement (REM) sleep, most often in the second half of the night, and are usually remembered in detail — distinguishing them from night terrors, which arise from deep NREM sleep.

Causes

Occasional nightmares are normal, especially in children. Frequent nightmares may be linked to stress, trauma (including post-traumatic stress disorder), fever, sleep deprivation, and some medications or withdrawal from alcohol and sedatives.

Treatment

When nightmares are recurrent and impairing, imagery rehearsal therapy — rehearsing a changed, less frightening ending to the dream while awake — is an effective treatment; the drug prazosin is sometimes used for trauma-related nightmares. The word itself derives from the Old English mare, a folkloric demon thought to sit on sleepers' chests.
