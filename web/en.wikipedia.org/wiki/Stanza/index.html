Stanza

In poetry, a stanza (from Italian stanza, "room") is a named group of lines forming the basic recurring metrical unit in a poem. Usually it consists of material grouped together and separated in a poem by a blank line or indentation. Stanzas can have regular rhyme and metrical structures.

A stanza is analogous to a paragraph in prose: it structures a poem into units of thought or image, providing both a visual and rhythmic organization. Stanzas are named by the number of lines they contain: a couplet has two lines, a tercet three, a quatrain four, a quintain five, a sestet six, a septet seven, and an octave eight.

Forms

Many fixed forms are defined by their stanzas, including the Italian ottava rima, the eight-line rhyme royal, the Spenserian stanza of nine lines used by Edmund Spenser in The Faerie Queene, and the complex stanza patterns of the ode. In songs, a stanza commonly corresponds to a verse, repeated against a refrain or chorus.
