Vimana (architecture)

In Hindu temple architecture, a vimana is the structure over the garbhagriha (inner sanctum) of a temple. In the Dravidian architecture of South India, the vimana is the pyramidal, stepped tower rising above the sanctum, crowned by a cupola called a shikhara or stupika. The comparable tower in the Nagara architecture of North India is itself called the shikhara.

Features

A typical vimana is built in diminishing storeys (talas), each decorated with miniature shrine motifs (kudu arches, aedicules) and images of deities. The structure symbolises Mount Meru, the axis of the world. Famous examples include the vimana of the Brihadeeswarar Temple at Thanjavur (11th century, about 66 metres tall), the Kailasanathar Temple at Kanchipuram, and the Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram.

In South Indian temples the vimana is often smaller than the entrance gateway towers (gopurams), which came to dominate the skyline in later centuries. The term vimana in Sanskrit also refers to mythological flying palaces or chariots of the gods described in epics such as the Ramayana.
