Commodore (rank)

A commodore's sleeve insignia typically consists of one broad stripe.

Commodore is a title and a military rank used in many navies that is superior to a navy captain, but below a rear admiral. In many navies it corresponds to the NATO rank code OF-6 and is considered a one-star flag rank, although in some services it is a senior captain's appointment rather than a substantive flag rank.

History

The title originates from the French commandeur and was used by the Dutch navy in the seventeenth century for a captain placed temporarily in command of a squadron. The Royal Navy used commodore as a temporary appointment for a senior captain commanding a detached squadron; it became a substantive rank in 1996. The United States Navy used the rank intermittently before replacing it with rear admiral (lower half) in 1985.

Usage by country

Commodore is used as a rank in the navies of the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, India, Pakistan, and many other Commonwealth and non-Commonwealth states. Air forces derived from the British model use the corresponding rank of air commodore. In some merchant navies and yacht clubs, "commodore" is an honorary or senior title given to a senior captain or the head of a yacht club.

Insignia

A commodore's insignia usually consists of a single broad gold band on the sleeve or shoulder board, sometimes with an executive curl or a single star in national systems modelled on the United States pattern.
