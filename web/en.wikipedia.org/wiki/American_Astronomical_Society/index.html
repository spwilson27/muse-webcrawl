The American Astronomical Society (AAS) is an American society of professional astronomers and other interested individuals, headquartered in Washington, D.C. Founded in 1899, its primary objective is to promote the advancement of astronomy and closely related branches of science, and it is the major organisation of professional astronomers in North America, with about 8,000 members including physicists, mathematicians, geologists, and engineers whose research interests lie within the broad spectrum of subjects now comprising contemporary astronomy.

History

The society was founded in 1899 as the Astronomical and Astrophysical Society of America, largely through the efforts of astronomer George Ellery Hale, and adopted its present name in 1915. It assumed responsibility for the Astrophysical Journal, founded by Hale, and later added other publications.

Publications and meetings

The AAS publishes several of the field's leading peer-reviewed journals, including The Astrophysical Journal, The Astronomical Journal, The Astrophysical Journal Letters, The Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series, and Research Notes of the AAS, in partnership with IOP Publishing. The society holds two major meetings each year — a winter meeting, the largest astronomy meeting in the world, and a summer meeting — as well as smaller topical conferences through its divisions, which cover planetary science, solar physics, high-energy astrophysics, laboratory astrophysics, and the history of astronomy.

Prizes

The AAS and its divisions award numerous prizes recognising scientific achievement and service, including the Henry Norris Russell Lectureship, the Newton Lacy Pierce Prize, the Helen B. Warner Prize, the Annie Jump Cannon Award, the Bruno Rossi Prize of the High Energy Astrophysics Division, and the Gerard P. Kuiper Prize of the Division for Planetary Sciences.
