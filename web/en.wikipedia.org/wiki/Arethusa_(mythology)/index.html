Arethusa (mythology)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In Greek mythology, Arethusa was a nymph and daughter of Nereus (hence called a Nereid), who fled from her home in Arcadia beneath the sea to the island of Ortygia at Syracuse, Sicily, where she was transformed into a spring.

Myth

According to Ovid's Metamorphoses, Arethusa, a huntress in the train of Artemis, bathed in the river Alpheus in Arcadia. The river god Alpheus fell in love with her and pursued her. Arethusa called on Artemis, who transformed her into a spring of water that flowed beneath the Peloponnese and under the sea to emerge on Ortygia, the island heart of Syracuse. Alpheus, however, mingled his waters with hers by flowing beneath the sea to the same spring, so the two remained joined forever.

In Syracuse Arethusa became the city's protectress; her image — a beautiful head surrounded by dolphins — appears on the celebrated tetradrachm coins of Syracuse, considered masterpieces of ancient coinage. The Fountain of Arethusa still exists on Ortygia. In the myth of Demeter, Arethusa also told the goddess that she had seen Persephone in the underworld as queen of Hades.

Legacy

The story inspired pastoral poetry, Benjamin Britten's oboe piece Six Metamorphoses after Ovid (whose final movement depicts Arethusa), and the name of a genus of orchids, Arethusa.
