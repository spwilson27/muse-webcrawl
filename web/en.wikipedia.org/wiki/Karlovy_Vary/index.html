Karlovy Vary (German: Karlsbad) is a spa city in western Bohemia, Czech Republic, situated at the confluence of the Ohře, Rolava and Teplá rivers. Renowned for its hot springs and ornate colonnades, it is the country's most famous spa town and, since 2021, part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of the Great Spa Towns of Europe.

History

According to legend, Emperor Charles IV discovered the springs while hunting in the 1350s — a statue of a chamois commemorates the tale — and he granted the settlement town privileges in 1370. Over the following centuries Karlsbad became one of Europe's most fashionable spas, visited by royalty, aristocrats and cultural figures including Peter the Great, Goethe, Beethoven, Mozart and Marx. The town's historic population was largely German-speaking; after the Second World War its German inhabitants were expelled and the town was repopulated by Czechs.

Springs and landmarks

Some 300 springs are documented in the area, with thirteen main springs issuing water up to about 73 °C, rich in minerals and used for drinking cures. The Vřídlo geyser spring shoots water to a height of about 12 metres inside its own colonnade. Landmarks include the Mill Colonnade, the Grandhotel Pupp (a backdrop in films such as Casino Royale), the Church of St. Mary Magdalene and the Diana lookout tower.

The city hosts the Karlovy Vary International Film Festival, one of Europe's oldest film festivals, and produces the herbal liqueur Becherovka, the porcelain and Moser glass, and the traditional spa wafers (lázeňské oplatky).
