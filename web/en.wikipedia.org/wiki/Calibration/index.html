Calibration

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Field: Measurement science (metrology)
Key concept: Traceability to reference standards

In measurement technology and metrology, calibration is the comparison of measurement values delivered by a device under test with those of a calibration standard of known accuracy. Such a standard could be another measurement device of known accuracy, an artefact such as a voltage cell or mass, or a physical constant.

Strictly speaking, calibration documents the relationship between the instrument's indications and the standard's values, including measurement uncertainty; adjustment of the instrument to improve its accuracy is a separate (though often associated) step. The outcome of a calibration is typically recorded in a calibration certificate.

Traceability

A chain of calibrations links an instrument's measurements through accredited laboratories to national measurement institutes and ultimately to the definitions of the SI units. This unbroken chain—metrological traceability—is fundamental to quality systems such as ISO 9001 and to legal metrology, which governs instruments used in trade, such as fuel pumps and shop scales.

Practice

Instruments are recalibrated at intervals determined by their stability, criticality and regulatory requirements, or after repair or shock. Calibration is applied across measurement domains: dimensional, electrical, temperature, pressure, mass, flow and analytical chemistry, and increasingly to software and machine-learning models, where calibration instead means aligning predicted probabilities with observed frequencies.
