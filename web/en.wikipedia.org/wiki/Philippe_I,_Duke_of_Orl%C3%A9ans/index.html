Philippe I, Duke of Orléans

Philippe I, Duke of Orléans (21 September 1640 – 9 June 1701) was the younger son of King Louis XIII of France and his wife Anne of Austria, and the younger brother of King Louis XIV. Known at court as Monsieur, he was the founder of the House of Orléans, a cadet branch of the ruling House of Bourbon.

Life

Philippe was created Duke of Orléans in 1661. Despite being discouraged from political influence by his brother, he proved an able soldier, winning the Battle of Cassel in 1677. His court at Saint-Cloud and the Palais-Royal was a centre of culture and intrigue.

Marriages and issue

His first marriage was to his cousin Henrietta of England, youngest daughter of Charles I; after her sudden death in 1670 he married Elizabeth Charlotte of the Palatinate ("Liselotte"), whose letters are a famous source for court life. His son Philippe II became Regent of France after Louis XIV's death.

Legacy

Through his descendants, Philippe is an ancestor of many European royal houses, and the Orléans line later supplied France's King Louis Philippe I.
