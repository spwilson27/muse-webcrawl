Aga Khan II

Aga Khan II (Persian: آغا خان دوّم; 1830 – 17 August 1885), whose birth name was Ali Shah, was the 47th Imam of the Nizari Isma'ili Muslims. A member of the Iranian royal family, he became Imam in 1881 and held the position for four years, until his death. He succeeded his father, Aga Khan I, and was in turn succeeded by his son, Aga Khan III.

Early life

Ali Shah was born in 1830 at Mahallat, Persia, the eldest son of Aga Khan I (Hasan Ali Shah) and his wife Sarv-i Jahan Khanum, a daughter of Fath-Ali Shah Qajar. He spent his youth in Persia before emigrating with his father to British India, where the family settled in Bombay. Educated in both Islamic learning and the administration of the community, he assisted his father in leading the Nizari Isma'ili community, including its Khoja followers in India and congregations in Central Asia, East Africa and elsewhere.

Imamate

On his father's death in April 1881, Ali Shah succeeded as Aga Khan II. During his brief imamate he worked to improve the organisation and welfare of the community: he strengthened links with Isma'ili communities outside India, encouraged education, and helped establish schools, including in Bombay. He is also remembered for his interest in field sports and horse racing in India.

Family and death

Aga Khan II married three times. His third wife, Lady Ali Shah (Shams al-Muluk), was the mother of his successor, Sultan Muhammad Shah, Aga Khan III, born in Karachi in 1877. Aga Khan II died of pneumonia on 17 August 1885 at Pune, after a reign of only four years, and was buried in the family mausoleum at Hasanabad, Bombay.
