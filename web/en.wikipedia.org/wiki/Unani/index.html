Unani medicine

Unani (from Arabic Yūnānī, "Greek") is a system of traditional medicine practised widely in South Asia and the Middle East, rooted in Greco-Arab medical theory.

Unani medicine is a traditional system of healing that originated in ancient Greece and was developed by Arab and Persian physicians during the Islamic Golden Age. The word derives from Yūnān, the Arabic name for Greece, reflecting the system's foundation in the teachings of Hippocrates and Galen, elaborated by scholars such as al-Razi (Rhazes) and Ibn Sina (Avicenna), whose Canon of Medicine remained a standard text for centuries.

Theory

Unani theory holds that health depends on the balance of four humours (blood, phlegm, yellow bile, and black bile), each associated with a temperament and with qualities of heat, cold, moisture, and dryness. Disease arises from humoral imbalance, and treatment aims to restore balance through diet, regimen, and medicines derived mainly from herbs, minerals, and animal products. Key concepts include the six essential factors (Asbab-e-Sitta Zarooriyah) — air, food and drink, movement and rest, sleep and wakefulness, evacuation and retention, and mental states.

Practice today

Unani is formally recognized in India, where it is regulated under the Ministry of AYUSH and taught in dedicated colleges awarding the BUMS degree. Practitioners, called hakims, diagnose partly by pulse reading (nabz) and examination of urine. Scientific evaluations of Unani remedies have produced mixed results, and some traditional preparations have raised concern because of heavy-metal content, leading to regulatory controls in several countries.
