Nilotic peoples

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nilotic pastoralists of the Upper Nile basin

The Nilotic peoples are a group of closely related ethnic groups indigenous to the Nile Valley and parts of East and Central Africa. They speak Nilotic languages, a branch of the Nilo-Saharan language family, and include such peoples as the Dinka, Nuer, Luo, Maasai, Kalenjin, Turkana, Shilluk, Acholi, Lango, Alur, and Ateker. Their combined population is estimated at well over 40 million.

Distribution

Nilotic peoples are found in South Sudan, Sudan, Ethiopia, Uganda, Kenya, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Rwanda, Burundi, and Tanzania. Their heartland is the Upper Nile basin — the Sudd wetlands of South Sudan, the Ethiopian highland fringes, and the Great Lakes region of East Africa — from which groups migrated southward over many centuries, in some accounts reaching as far as the lands of the Maasai in present-day Kenya and Tanzania.

History

Linguistic and archaeological evidence associates early Nilotic-speaking communities with pastoralist cultures of the eastern Sahara and the Khartoum region during the Neolithic, as the Sahara desiccated and herders moved toward the Nile and its tributaries. Cattle pastoralism, combined in many areas with millet and sorghum cultivation, has remained central to Nilotic economies and social organization. During the 19th century, Nilotic societies were heavily affected by the ivory and slave trades and by colonial conquest; in the 20th and 21st centuries, Sudan's civil wars and South Sudan's independence in 2011 reshaped the political landscape of the largest Nilotic groups.

Culture and society

Many Nilotic societies are historically organized by age sets and age grades rather than centralized states, though kingdoms existed among the Shilluk and elsewhere. Cattle hold deep economic, social, and ritual significance: they are used in bridewealth, sacrifice, and compensation payments, and personal names and poetry often celebrate individual animals. Nilotic peoples are also noted for their tall, slender average stature, an adaptation sometimes linked to life in hot climates, and for distinctive practices such as scarification and, among the Dinka and Nuer, cattle camps that structure the seasonal year.

Languages

The Nilotic languages are divided into three branches: Eastern Nilotic (including Maasai, Turkana, and Bari), Southern Nilotic (including Kalenjin and Datooga), and Western Nilotic (including Dinka, Nuer, Shilluk, and the Luo languages). They are tonal languages, many with advanced tongue root vowel harmony.
