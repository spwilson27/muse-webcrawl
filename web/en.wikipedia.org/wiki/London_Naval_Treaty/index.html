London Naval Treaty

London Naval Treaty
Signed: 22 April 1930
Parties: UK, US, Japan, France, Italy

The London Naval Treaty, officially the Treaty for the Limitation and Reduction of Naval Armament, was an agreement concluded in 1930 between the United Kingdom, the United States, Japan, France and Italy to limit naval construction.

Terms

It extended the capital-ship building holiday of the Washington Naval Treaty, set ratios and tonnage limits for cruisers, destroyers and submarines, and regulated submarine warfare.

Aftermath

Dissatisfaction in Japan, where the treaty was seen as perpetuating inferior ratios, fuelled navalist opposition; Japan gave notice of withdrawal in 1934, and the treaty system collapsed before World War II.
