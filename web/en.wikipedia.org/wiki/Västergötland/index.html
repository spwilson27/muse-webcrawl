Västergötland

Västergötland (Swedish pronunciation: [ˈvɛ̂sːtɛrˌjøːtland]; "West Gothland") is one of the traditional provinces (landskap) of Sweden, in the southwestern part of the country between Lakes Vänern and Vättern. Its largest city is Gothenburg, Sweden's second-largest city, and other important towns include Borås, Trollhättan, Skövde, and Lidköping.

History

The province was the heartland of the Geats and one of the earliest centres of the emerging Swedish kingdom; medieval kings were acclaimed at the Stone of Mora after being elected, and Västergötland's provincial law, the Äldre Västgötalagen (c. 1220s), is Sweden's oldest. The region was Christianized early — tradition names Skara, founded around 1000, as its first diocese — and the Cistercian abbey at Varnhem became a royal burial place.

Geography and culture

Västergötland consists largely of fertile plains, with distinctive flat-topped "table mountains" such as Kinnekulle and Billingen. The Göta älv river and the Trollhätte and Göta canals link its lakes to the sea at Gothenburg. The province's floral emblem is the heather and its animal emblem the common crane.
