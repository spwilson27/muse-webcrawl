Terence Alan "Spike" Milligan KBE (16 April 1918 – 27 February 2002) was an Irish-English comedian, writer, musician, poet, playwright, and actor. Born in British India, he spent the majority of his life in the United Kingdom. He was the co-creator, main writer, and a principal cast member of The Goon Show, the BBC radio comedy that transformed British humour and influenced later acts including Monty Python.

Early life and war service

Milligan was born in Ahmednagar, India, the son of an Irish soldier in the British Indian Army. The family moved to England in the 1930s. A talented trumpeter and double bassist, he performed in dance bands before being called up in the Second World War. He served with the Royal Artillery in North Africa and Italy and was wounded at Monte Cassino in 1944. His seven-volume comic war memoirs, beginning with Adolf Hitler: My Part in His Downfall (1971), became bestsellers.

The Goon Show

From 1951 to 1960 Milligan wrote and starred in The Goon Show alongside Peter Sellers, Harry Secombe, and (initially) Michael Bentine. His characters, including Eccles and Count Moriarty, fronted surreal plots full of sound effects, anachronisms, and wordplay that broke with the conventions of variety comedy.

Later work

Milligan's television series Q5 (1969) and its successors pioneered sketch comedy without conventional punchlines. His books include the comic novel Puckoon (1963), the bestselling children's verse collection Silly Verse for Kids (1959), and the nonsense poem "On the Ning Nang Nong," voted Britain's favourite comic poem in a 1998 poll. He acted in films, wrote plays, and campaigned for environmental and animal-welfare causes.

Personal life and health

Milligan lived with bipolar disorder and spoke candidly about his depression, helping to raise public awareness of mental illness. He was made an honorary Knight Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 2001. He died of kidney failure in 2002; his gravestone in Winchelsea bears the Irish epitaph Dúirt mé leat go raibh mé breoite — "I told you I was ill."
