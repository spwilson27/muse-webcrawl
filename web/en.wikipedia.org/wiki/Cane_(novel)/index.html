Cane (novel)

Cane is a 1923 novel by noted Harlem Renaissance author Jean Toomer. Part novel, part poetry cycle and drama, it is structured in three sections moving between the rural American South, the urban North, and a synthesis of the two.

AuthorJean Toomer
Published1923
PublisherBoni & Liveright
GenreModernist novel / poetry cycle

Structure

The first section, set in rural Georgia, presents vignettes and poems about Black women and men — 'Karintha', 'Becky', 'Blood-Burning Moon' — amid sugar cane fields and racial violence. The second, in Washington, D.C. and Chicago, turns urban and fragmented. The third, 'Kabnis', is a dramatic prose piece about a northern teacher in the South.

Significance

Initially modest in sales, Cane was rediscovered in the 1960s and is now considered a landmark of African-American literature and American modernism, praised by writers from Langston Hughes to Alice Walker for its lyrical experimentation. See Jean Toomer and Harlem Renaissance.
