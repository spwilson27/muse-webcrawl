International Six Days Enduro

The International Six Days Enduro (ISDE), formerly known as the International Six Days Trial (ISDT), is the oldest off-road motorcycle event on the FIM calendar. Held annually since 1913 (with interruptions for the world wars), it is an endurance event in which national teams compete over six days of timed off-road riding.

Format

Riders cover up to roughly 1,500 km over the six days, following a route with liaison sections and timed special tests. Historically, riders had to maintain their own machines with limited assistance — the "enduro" element — though modern rules permit more support. Medals are awarded based on finishing within a percentage of the class winner's time.

Trophies

The premier prize is the World Trophy, competed for by four-rider national teams, alongside the Junior World Trophy (for riders under 23) and the Women's World Trophy. The event is often called the "Olympics of motorcycling".

History

The first ISDT was held in Carlisle, England, in 1913. East Germany, Czechoslovakia, and later the United States, Italy, France, and Australia have all enjoyed dominant periods. The event has been held on five continents.
