Red-giant branch

Red-giant branch
Stage: Post-main-sequence stellar evolution
Applies to: Low- and intermediate-mass stars
Endpoint: Helium flash (low-mass stars)

The red-giant branch (RGB) is the region of the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram populated by evolving low- to intermediate-mass stars after they leave the main sequence. It is also the stage of stellar evolution that follows the subgiant branch and precedes core helium burning.

Evolution

After a star exhausts the hydrogen in its core, hydrogen fusion continues in a shell around an inert helium core. The core contracts and heats while the envelope expands and cools, making the star larger, more luminous, and redder as it climbs the red-giant branch. On the way up, the deepening convective envelope produces the first dredge-up, altering surface abundances, and many stars pass through a temporary slowdown in luminosity known as the RGB bump.

The tip of the red-giant branch

For stars below about 2 solar masses, the helium core becomes degenerate, and ascent ends abruptly at the tip of the red-giant branch (TRGB) with the helium flash, which ignites core helium burning and drops the star onto the horizontal branch. Because the core mass at the flash is nearly constant, the TRGB has a nearly standard luminosity — especially in the I band — making it a widely used cosmic distance indicator, complementary to Cepheids.
