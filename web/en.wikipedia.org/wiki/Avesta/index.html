The Avesta is the primary collection of sacred texts of Zoroastrianism, composed in the Avestan language and transmitted orally for many centuries before being written down, in a specially devised alphabet, in the Sasanian period. Its oldest portion is the Yasna, whose seventeen hymns — the Gathas — are attributed to the prophet Zoroaster himself and are among the oldest surviving Indo-Iranian religious poetry.

Losses and contents

Sasanian tradition held that 21 books (nasks) existed; after the Arab conquest and centuries of attrition roughly a quarter survives: liturgy (Yasna, Visperad), hymns to divinities (Yashts), purity law and cosmology (Vendidad), and the prayer book (Khordeh Avesta). The Avesta's language, closely related to the Rigveda's Sanskrit, is a cornerstone of Indo-European studies.
