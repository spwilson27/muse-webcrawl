English football league system

The English football league system, also known as the football pyramid, is a series of interconnected leagues for men's association football clubs in England, with five teams from Wales also competing. The system has a hierarchical format with promotion and relegation between leagues at different levels, allowing even the smallest club the theoretical possibility of ultimately rising to the very top of the system.

Structure

At the top is the Premier League (level 1, 20 clubs), a single division that broke away from the Football League in 1992. Below it, the English Football League comprises the Championship (level 2), League One (level 3) and League Two (level 4), each of 24 clubs. Beneath the League lies "non-League football": the National League system, headed by the National League (level 5) and its regional sixth tier, followed by the three feeder leagues — the Northern Premier League, Southern League and Isthmian League — and a deep pyramid of regional and county leagues extending beyond level 10. In total, the pyramid encompasses more than 5,000 clubs in over 200 leagues.

Promotion and relegation

Movement between levels is governed by promotion and relegation at the end of each season, with the details varying by level. Three clubs are relegated from the Premier League each season and three promoted from the Championship (two automatically and one via play-offs). Between League Two and the National League, the bottom two clubs are relegated and replaced by the champions and play-off winners, a gateway that has existed in automatic form since 1987.

History

The pyramid evolved gradually from the founding of the Football League in 1888, through the absorption of rival leagues and the creation of nationwide non-League divisions in the late 20th century. Its defenders cite the openness of promotion and relegation as a defining virtue of English football, keeping competition meaningful throughout the system.
