Crystal Palace, London

Crystal Palace is an area in South London, named after the Crystal Palace exhibition building which stood in the area from 1854 until it was destroyed by fire in 1936. The district is situated about 8 miles (13 km) southeast of Charing Cross and straddles the boundary of several London boroughs. The suburb's hilltop park was the site of the relocated palace and remains home to famous full-size model dinosaurs.

The palace

The Crystal Palace was designed by Joseph Paxton and built in Hyde Park for the Great Exhibition of 1851. After the exhibition it was purchased, dismantled and re-erected in an enlarged form on Sydenham Hill in 1854, surrounded by landscaped parkland with fountains and Benjamin Waterhouse Hawkins' pioneering life-size dinosaur sculptures. For over 80 years it hosted concerts, exhibitions and sporting events before being completely destroyed by a fire on 30 November 1936, watched by thousands and seen across London.

The district today

The area around Crystal Palace Park — which contains a national sports centre built in 1964, a concert bowl, a maze and the Dinosaur Court — is a residential suburb with a busy triangle of shops, cafés and Victorian housing. Crystal Palace railway station provides connections to central London and is served by the London Overground.

Sport

Crystal Palace F.C., founded in 1861 by palace grounds staff (and re-formed professionally in 1905), played cup finals at the palace grounds before moving to Selhurst Park in 1924. The park's sports centre hosted athletics meetings and the London Grand Prix for many years.
