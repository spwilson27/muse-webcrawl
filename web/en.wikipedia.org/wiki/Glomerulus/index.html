Glomerulus

A glomerulus (plural glomeruli) is a network of small blood capillaries at the beginning of a nephron in the kidney, where blood filtration takes place. It is surrounded by Bowman's capsule, and together they form a renal corpuscle. The word derives from the Latin glomerulus, "small ball".

Function

Blood enters the glomerulus through an afferent arteriole and leaves through a narrower efferent arteriole, maintaining high pressure that forces water, ions, glucose, amino acids and small molecules through the filtration barrier — fenestrated endothelium, the glomerular basement membrane and podocyte slit diaphragms — into Bowman's capsule. The resulting filtrate passes into the proximal tubule. About 180 litres of filtrate are produced per day in an adult human, nearly all of it reabsorbed.

Clinical significance

The glomerular filtration rate (GFR) is a key measure of kidney function. Diseases of the glomerulus, the glomerulonephritides, include IgA nephropathy, diabetic nephropathy and minimal change disease, and can cause proteinuria, haematuria and kidney failure.
