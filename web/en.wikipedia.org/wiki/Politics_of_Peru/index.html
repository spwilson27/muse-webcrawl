The politics of Peru take place in the framework of a unitary semi-presidential representative democratic republic, whereby the President of Peru is both head of state and, in practice, the dominant executive figure, and of a pluriform multi-party system. Executive power is exercised by the government; legislative power is vested in the Congress of the Republic. The judiciary is independent of the executive and the legislature.

Peru's contemporary political order rests on the Constitution of 1993, promulgated during the presidency of Alberto Fujimori after his 1992 autogolpe (self-coup). The country has been marked in recent decades by weak political parties, frequent turnover of presidents, and recurring conflict between the executive and Congress.

Executive branch

The President of the Republic is elected by popular vote for a five-year term and may not be re-elected for an immediately succeeding term. The president appoints the President of the Council of Ministers (prime minister) and, on the prime minister's advice, the other ministers. Peru's system gives the presidency decree and veto powers, while Congress may censure ministers and, under a contested constitutional clause concerning "permanent moral incapacity", remove the president — a mechanism used repeatedly in the political crises of 2016–2022.

Legislative branch

The Congress of the Republic (Congreso de la República) is a unicameral body of 130 members elected for five-year terms by proportional representation in multi-member constituencies. A 2024 constitutional reform approved the restoration of a bicameral legislature — a Senate and a Chamber of Deputies — to take effect from the 2026 general elections.

Judiciary

The highest court is the Supreme Court of Justice, seated in Lima. A separate Constitutional Tribunal interprets the constitution and hears constitutional complaints. The Public Ministry (prosecution service) and the National Board of Justice play roles in appointments and discipline of judges and prosecutors.

Political parties and elections

Peruvian parties are notoriously fluid; electoral vehicles frequently form around individual candidates and dissolve after elections. Historically significant parties include the American Popular Revolutionary Alliance (APRA, founded by Víctor Raúl Haya de la Torre), Popular Action, and the Christian People's Party. The movement associated with Alberto Fujimori and later his daughter Keiko Fujimori (Popular Force) has been a central pole of politics since the 1990s. General elections combine presidential (with a runoff if no candidate exceeds 50 percent) and congressional contests; voting is compulsory for citizens between 18 and 70.

Recent history

The return to democracy in 2001 after Fujimori's fall was followed by presidencies of Alejandro Toledo, Alan García, Ollanta Humala, and Pedro Pablo Kuczynski. From 2016 Peru entered a period of acute instability: Kuczynski resigned in 2018; his successor Martín Vizcarra was removed by Congress in 2020, triggering protests; Pedro Castillo, elected in 2021, was removed and arrested in December 2022 after attempting to dissolve Congress, and was succeeded by Vice President Dina Boluarte amid widespread protests.
