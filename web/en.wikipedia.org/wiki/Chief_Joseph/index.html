Chief Joseph

Born: March 3, 1840, Wallowa Valley, Oregon
Died: September 21, 1904, Colville Indian Reservation, Washington
Nez Perce name: Hin-mah-too-yah-lat-kekt

Chief Joseph (March 3, 1840 – September 21, 1904), known by his Nez Perce name Hin-mah-too-yah-lat-kekt ("Thunder Rolling Down the Mountain"), was a leader of the Wal-lam-wat-kain (Wallowa) band of Nez Perce, a Native American tribe of the interior Pacific Northwest region of the United States.

Nez Perce War

In 1877, facing forcible removal from the Wallowa Valley to a reservation in Idaho, Joseph and other chiefs led roughly 800 Nez Perce men, women, and children on a fighting retreat of about 1,170 miles (1,883 km) toward Canada, pursued by the U.S. Army under General Oliver O. Howard and others. The campaign, known as the Nez Perce War, ended with Joseph's surrender on October 5, 1877, at the Battle of Bear Paw in Montana, about 40 miles from the Canadian border. He is remembered for the surrender speech attributed to him, concluding with the words "From where the sun now stands, I will fight no more forever."

Later life

Joseph and his band were exiled to Kansas and Indian Territory (Oklahoma) before being allowed to return to the Pacific Northwest in 1885, though never to the Wallowa Valley; he settled on the Colville Indian Reservation in Washington, where he died in 1904. He became a celebrated advocate for his people's rights, meeting with Presidents Rutherford B. Hayes and Theodore Roosevelt.
