Pedro of Castile


Reign: 26 March 1350 – 23 March 1369
Born: 30 August 1334, Burgos
Died: 23 March 1369, Montiel
House: Ivrea (Castilian branch)
Father: Alfonso XI of Castile

Pedro of Castile (30 August 1334 – 23 March 1369), known as Pedro the Cruel (el Cruel) to his enemies and Pedro the Just (el Justiciero) to his supporters, was King of Castile and León from 1350 until his death. His reign was dominated by civil war against his illegitimate half-brother Henry of Trastámara, which became entangled in the Hundred Years' War between England and France.

Reign

Pedro succeeded his father Alfonso XI at sixteen. His early reign was influenced by his favourite Juan Alfonso de Alburquerque and by his mother Maria of Portugal. He is associated with harsh policies toward rebellious nobles and with the encouragement of trade, the arts, and the Jewish and Muslim communities of his realm, whose members served in his administration — policies that later chroniclers, writing under his victorious rivals, used to darken his reputation, earning him the epithet "the Cruel."

Civil war

From 1366 Henry of Trastámara, backed by France and the free companies of Bertrand du Guesclin, invaded and had himself crowned. Pedro responded with the help of Edward, the Black Prince of England, whose victory at the Battle of Nájera (1367) temporarily restored him. When Pedro failed to pay the agreed subsidies, English support evaporated; Henry returned, defeated Pedro, and personally killed him after the Battle of Montiel in March 1369, founding the Trastámara dynasty.

Legacy and reputation

Pedro's image remains contested: the hostile chronicle of Pero López de Ayala underpins the "Cruel" tradition, while later historians have reassessed him as a defender of royal authority against an over-mighty nobility and, in some accounts, as "the Just." Building works associated with him include parts of the Alcázar of Seville, where the Mudéjar palace bears his name and inscriptions.
