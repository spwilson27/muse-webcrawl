Barry Lamar Bonds (born 24 July 1964) is an American former professional baseball left fielder who played 22 seasons in Major League Baseball for the Pittsburgh Pirates and San Francisco Giants. He holds the MLB records for career home runs (762), home runs in a single season (73, set in 2001), career walks (2,558) and career intentional walks, and won a record seven National League Most Valuable Player Awards. He is the son of former major leaguer Bobby Bonds and the godson of Willie Mays.

Career

Bonds debuted with Pittsburgh in 1986, winning MVP awards in 1990 and 1992 and helping the Pirates to three straight division titles. Signing with San Francisco in 1993 in what was then the richest contract in baseball, he won a third MVP in his first Giants season. From 2001 to 2004 he produced the most dominant four-year stretch in the sport's history: he broke the single-season home run record with 73 in 2001, won four consecutive MVP awards, and in 2004 posted a .609 on-base percentage while being walked 232 times, 120 of them intentionally. The Giants retired his number 25 in 2018.

Steroid controversy and legacy

Bonds became a central figure in baseball's performance-enhancing drugs scandal through his association with the Bay Area Laboratory Co-operative (BALCO). He testified that he had unknowingly used substances provided by his trainer; he was convicted of obstruction of justice in 2011, a conviction overturned on appeal in 2015. The controversy has kept him out of the National Baseball Hall of Fame: in his tenth and final year on the writers' ballot in 2022 he received 66 percent of the vote, short of the 75 percent required, and a veterans' committee also passed him over later that year. Statisticians nevertheless routinely rank him among the greatest players in the history of the game.
