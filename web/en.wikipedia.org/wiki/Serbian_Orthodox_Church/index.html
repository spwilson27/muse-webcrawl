Overview

The Serbian Orthodox Church is an autocephalous Eastern Orthodox church, the national church of Serbia and the second-oldest Slavic Orthodox church (after Bulgaria's). It traces its organisation to Saint Sava, who obtained autocephaly in 1219 at Nicaea. Its history includes the Patriarchate of Peć (1346–1463 and 1557–1766), suppression under Ottoman and Habsburg rule, reunification in 1920 in the Kingdom of Yugoslavia, persecution under the WWII Ustaše and the communists, and a central (and contested) role in the Yugoslav wars of the 1990s.

Recent history

The Church's dioceses in Kosovo — including the UNESCO-listed monasteries of Peć, Dečani and Gračanica — are a focus of Serbia–Kosovo tension. In 2022 the Church granted autocephaly to the Macedonian Orthodox Church, ending a schism dating to 1967. The massive Church of Saint Sava in Belgrade, begun in 1935 and consecrated in stages into the 2020s, crowned by a golden dome visible across the city, is its modern emblem. The Church also canonised figures debated beyond Serbia, such as Nikolaj Velimirović.
