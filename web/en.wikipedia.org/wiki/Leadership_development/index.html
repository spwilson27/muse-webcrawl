Leadership development

Leadership development is the process of expanding the capacity of individuals to perform in leadership roles within organizations, and of building an organization's collective leadership capability. Programs range from executive coaching and 360-degree feedback to action learning, mentoring, job rotation, and formal management education.

Approaches

Individual development focuses on self-awareness, interpersonal skills, strategic thinking, and decision-making, often using assessments and feedback instruments. A widely used framework, the 70:20:10 model, holds that most development comes from challenging on-the-job experiences, with smaller contributions from relationships and formal coursework. Organizational approaches embed development into succession planning, talent management, and culture change initiatives.

Evaluation

Because leadership programs represent a large corporate investment, their effectiveness is debated. Evaluation models such as Kirkpatrick's four levels—reaction, learning, behavior, and results—are commonly used, though measuring long-term organizational impact remains difficult, and research suggests context and follow-up matter more than classroom content alone.
