United States Army

The United States Army (USA) is the land service branch of the United States Armed Forces. It is one of the eight U.S. uniformed services, and is designated as the Army of the United States in the U.S. Constitution. The oldest and most senior branch in order of precedence, it traces its roots to the Continental Army, formed on 14 June 1775 during the American Revolutionary War. The Army falls under the Department of the Army within the Department of Defense and is headed by the Chief of Staff of the Army.

History

The Continental Army under George Washington won independence from Britain. The Army fought in the War of 1812, the Mexican–American War, the American Civil War (as the Union Army), the Spanish–American War, both World Wars, Korea, Vietnam, the Gulf War, and the wars in Afghanistan and Iraq. Notable institutions include the U.S. Military Academy at West Point (founded 1802).

Structure

The Army consists of the Regular Army, the Army Reserve, and the Army National Guard. Major functional commands include Forces Command, Training and Doctrine Command, Materiel Command, and Futures Command. Combat organisation centres on brigade combat teams—infantry, Stryker, and armored—grouped into divisions and corps. Ranks run from private to general, with warrants for technical specialists and a professional noncommissioned officer corps.

Mission

Under Title 10 of the U.S. Code the Army is organised, trained and equipped primarily for prompt and sustained combat incident to operations on land, with an official mission to fight and win the nation's wars by providing prompt, sustained land dominance across the full range of military operations.
