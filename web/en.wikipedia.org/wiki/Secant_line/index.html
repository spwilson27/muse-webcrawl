Secant line

A secant line (red) cutting a curve at two points, with a tangent line shown for comparison.

In geometry, a secant line, also simply called a secant, is a line that intersects a curve at a minimum of two distinct points. The word secant comes from the Latin secare, "to cut". For a circle, a secant crosses the circle at two points while the segment between them is a chord.

Type: Line in geometry

Defined by: Two points on a curve

Related concepts: Chord, tangent line, secant method, derivative

Secants of circles

For a circle, the secant-secant theorem relates the lengths of the segments formed when two secant lines intersect inside or outside the circle: the products of the segments of each secant are equal (the power of a point). The secant-tangent theorem similarly links a secant with a tangent drawn from the same external point.

Secants and derivatives

In calculus, the slope of the secant line through two points (x, f(x)) and (x + h, f(x + h)) on the graph of a function is the average rate of change over that interval, given by the difference quotient. As the second point approaches the first (h → 0), the secant line approaches the tangent line and its slope approaches the derivative. The secant method applies the same idea numerically to find roots of equations, using successive secant-line intercepts instead of tangents.
