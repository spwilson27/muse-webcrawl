Royal Ocean Racing Club

The Royal Ocean Racing Club (RORC) is a British yacht club and the principal organiser of offshore yacht races in the United Kingdom and internationally. It was founded in 1925 as the Ocean Racing Club, following the first Fastnet Race, and received its royal prefix in 1931. The club is renowned for organising the Fastnet Race, held biennially, and for developing the rating rules used in international offshore racing.

History

Ocean racing in Britain was galvanised in 1925 by the first race from Cowes around the Fastnet Rock off the south coast of Ireland and back to Plymouth, won by the cutter Jolie Brise, owned by E. G. Martin. The success of the race led to the founding of the Ocean Racing Club that year, with the aim of promoting long-distance ocean racing under a fair handicap rule. The club received permission to use the "Royal" prefix in 1931.

In 1931 the club also founded the famous transatlantic and Admiral's Cup series of team racing (from 1957), which for decades was regarded as the unofficial world championship of offshore racing. The club's London clubhouse at 20 St James's Place was opened in 1942, and it maintains a second clubhouse in Cowes.

Races and rules

The RORC organises a full calendar of offshore races, including the Fastnet Race (now the Rolex Fastnet Race), the Middle Sea Race (co-founded with the Royal Malta Yacht Club), the Caribbean 600, the Transatlantic Race and a domestic season of cross-Channel races. The club's Rating Office developed the IRC (International Rule Club) handicap rule, administered jointly with the French club UNCL, which is used for the majority of the world's offshore racing fleets.

Fastnet Race

The biennial Fastnet Race is among the world's most famous offshore races. Since 2021 the course has run from Cowes, around the Fastnet Rock, and finishes in Cherbourg, France, having previously finished in Plymouth. The 1979 race was struck by a severe storm in which 15 sailors died, leading to major safety reforms in offshore racing. The race now regularly attracts fleets of more than 400 yachts.
