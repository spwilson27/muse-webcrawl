Sasolburg is an industrial town in the far north of the Free State province of South Africa, situated on the Vaal River close to the border with Gauteng. It was founded in 1954 to house the workers of Sasol's first coal-to-liquids plant, and remains closely tied to the petrochemical industry.

The Sasol One plant, among the world's pioneering coal-to-liquid fuel facilities.

History

Sasolburg was planned from scratch as a company town for the South African Coal, Oil and Gas Corporation (Sasol), established to produce liquid fuels from South Africa's abundant coal using Fischer–Tropsch synthesis. The site was chosen for its proximity to coalfields, water from the Vaal River and rail links. The first plant, Sasol One, began production in 1955, and the town grew around it with planned residential suburbs, schools and an industrial zone that later attracted chemical and manufacturing firms such as those in the associated township of Zamdela.

Economy

Petrochemicals dominate the local economy: Sasol's operations at Sasolburg produce fuels, waxes, chemicals and fertiliser feedstocks, and the town forms part of the wider Vaal industrial region alongside Vanderbijlpark and Vereeniging. The town lies about 80 kilometres south of Johannesburg, within commuting distance of the Witwatersrand.
