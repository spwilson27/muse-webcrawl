Polish opera

The Grand Theatre in Warsaw, home of the Polish National Opera

Polish opera is the tradition of opera in Poland and in the Polish language, dating from the early seventeenth century, when Italian opera was introduced at the royal court in Warsaw — one of the earliest operatic centres north of the Alps.

History

Opera was performed at the court of Sigismund III Vasa and his son Władysław IV from the 1620s onwards, with works by Italian composers and Francesca Caccini's music among the earliest productions. A public opera theatre opened in Warsaw in 1778. In the nineteenth century, Stanisław Moniuszko (1819–1872) became the central figure of Polish national opera, with works such as Halka (1848/1858) and Straszny dwór (The Haunted Manor, 1865), which drew on Polish folk music and patriotic themes during the period of Poland's partitions.

Later developments

Karol Szymanowski's Król Roger (King Roger, 1926) is widely regarded as a masterpiece of twentieth-century opera. After the Second World War, composers such as Witold Lutosławski's contemporaries, Krzysztof Penderecki (The Devils of Loudun, 1969), and later Paweł Mykietyn and others continued the tradition. The Polish National Opera performs at the Grand Theatre in Warsaw, one of the largest opera stages in the world.
