GoldSrc

GoldSrc (also known as the Half-Life engine) is a game engine developed by Valve and first used in the 1998 first-person shooter Half-Life. Heavily derived from id Software's Quake engine under licence, with additional code from QuakeWorld and substantial original work, GoldSrc powered a generation of influential games and modifications before being succeeded by the Source engine in 2004.

Development

The name originated as an internal codename: the current build of the engine in development was kept in a directory called "Src", and when Valve branched the code near Half-Life's release, the in-development tree became "GoldSrc" (the "gold" master) while the experimental branch became "Source" — terms that stuck for both engines. Valve rewrote a large majority of the Quake code, adding skeletal animation, a more detailed scripting system for AI encounters, Direct3D support, and improved networking.

Games and mods

GoldSrc titles include Half-Life and its expansions Opposing Force and Blue Shift (developed with Gearbox Software), Team Fortress Classic, Counter-Strike (originally a fan-made modification), Day of Defeat, Deathmatch Classic, Ricochet, and third-party licensed games such as James Bond 007: Nightfire and Gunman Chronicles. The engine's accessible modding tools made it a cradle of the modern mod scene: Counter-Strike and Day of Defeat began as mods, and community projects continue to be released through the reverse-engineered-compatible Xash3D engine family.

Technology

GoldSrc uses BSP-tree level geometry in the Quake tradition, lightmaps for static lighting, MDL skeletal models, and a client–server networking model. Its level editor, Worldcraft (later the Valve Hammer Editor), became the standard tool for its successors.
