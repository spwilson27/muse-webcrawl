Gossypium

[Image: Gossypium]

Gossypium is a genus of flowering plants in the tribe Gossypieae, in the mallow family Malvaceae, from which commercial cotton is harvested. The genus comprises around 50 species, native to tropical and subtropical regions of the Old and New Worlds.

Description

Species range from herbaceous annuals to shrubs and small trees. After pollination the flower produces a fruit capsule called a boll, which splits open to reveal seeds surrounded by soft fibres — the cotton lint used in textiles. Seeds also yield cottonseed oil.

Cultivation

Four species are widely cultivated: Gossypium hirsutum (upland cotton, about 90% of world production), G. barbadense (extra-long staple), G. arboreum and G. herbaceum. Cotton plants are highly sensitive to frost and require long, warm growing seasons. Pests such as the boll weevil and pink bollworm have historically devastated crops, driving the development of Bt cotton varieties.
