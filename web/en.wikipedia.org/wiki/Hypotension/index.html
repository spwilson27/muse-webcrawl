Hypotension

Hypotension
Specialty: Cardiology, internal medicine
Definition: Systolic BP below 90 mmHg or diastolic below 60 mmHg
Symptoms: Dizziness, fainting, fatigue, nausea
Opposite: Hypertension

Hypotension, also known as low blood pressure, is a cardiovascular condition in which blood pressure is abnormally low. Blood pressure is the force of blood pushing against the walls of the arteries as the heart pumps out blood. Hypotension is generally defined as a systolic blood pressure below 90 mmHg or a diastolic below 60 mmHg. For many people, however, low readings cause no symptoms and are normal, particularly in physically fit individuals.

Signs and symptoms

When low pressure reduces blood flow to the brain and other organs, symptoms include dizziness or lightheadedness, syncope (fainting), blurred vision, nausea, fatigue, cold clammy skin, rapid shallow breathing, and difficulty concentrating. Severe hypotension depriving the brain and organs of oxygen constitutes shock, a life-threatening emergency.

Types and causes

Orthostatic (postural) hypotension is a fall in pressure on standing — defined as a drop of at least 20 mmHg systolic or 10 mmHg diastolic within three minutes — common in the elderly and in autonomic dysfunction, dehydration, and with certain drugs. Postprandial hypotension occurs after meals. Neurally mediated (vasovagal) hypotension follows prolonged standing or emotional triggers. Other causes include blood loss, severe infection (septic shock), anaphylaxis, heart failure, arrhythmias, endocrine disorders such as adrenal insufficiency, and medications including antihypertensives and antidepressants.

Physiology

Blood pressure is determined by cardiac output and systemic vascular resistance, regulated by baroreceptors, the autonomic nervous system, and the renin–angiotensin system. Hypotension results when compensatory vasoconstriction and tachycardia fail to maintain pressure.

Treatment

Asymptomatic hypotension usually needs no treatment. Symptomatic cases are managed by treating the cause, increasing fluid and salt intake (where appropriate), compression stockings, physical counterpressure maneuvers, slow position changes, and in selected cases medications such as fludrocortisone or midodrine.
