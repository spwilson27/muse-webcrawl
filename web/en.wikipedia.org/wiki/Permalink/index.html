Permalink

A permalink or permanent link is a URL intended to remain unchanged indefinitely and to point to a specific piece of content, such as a particular blog post, forum entry, or a specific revision of a wiki page. The word is a portmanteau of "permanent" and "link". Permalinks make it possible to cite and bookmark content reliably, in contrast to links to front pages or category listings whose contents change over time.

Use

Permalinks became a standard feature of blogging software in the early 2000s. A blog's front page shows recent entries, but each entry also has its own permalink so that other sites can link to it directly and the link will keep working after the entry scrolls off the front page. Content management systems commonly offer configurable permalink structures, for example including the date and title of a post in the URL.

On wiki software such as MediaWiki, a "permanent link" points to an exact revision of a page, so that readers see precisely the version the linker saw even after later edits. Persistent identifier schemes such as the Digital Object Identifier (DOI), Handle System, and Perma.cc pursue the same goal for scholarly and legal citations.

Link rot

A permalink is a promise rather than a technical guarantee: links can still break when a site closes, is restructured, or fails to maintain redirects — a phenomenon known as link rot. Web archiving services such as the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine mitigate this by preserving snapshots of pages.
