International Code of Signals

The International Code of Signals (ICS) is an international system of signals and codes for use by vessels to communicate important messages regarding safety of navigation and related matters. It is administered by the International Maritime Organization and was adopted in 1965, coming into force in 1969.

Signals

The code assigns meanings to 40 signal flags: 26 letter flags, 10 numeral pennants, 3 substitutes (repeaters), and the code/answering pennant. A single-flag hoist conveys urgent standardized messages — for example, "A" (Alfa) means "I have a diver down; keep well clear", and "O" (Oscar) means "man overboard". Letter flags are also used with phonetic alphabet names shared with the NATO phonetic alphabet.

Other methods

Messages can also be sent by Morse code (by light or radio), by voice using standard message markers, and by flag combinations: two-letter groups cover general subjects, and three-letter groups beginning with "M" form the medical signal code used to request medical advice.

History

The ICS descends from the Commercial Code of Signals of 1857, compiled under Charles Pasley and later the British Board of Trade, which was adopted internationally in the early 20th century and revised after the Second World War to its present compact form.
