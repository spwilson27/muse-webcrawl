Official residence

Official residence (placeholder image)

An official residence is the residence at which a nation's head of state, head of government, governor, or another senior figure officially resides. It may or may not be the same location where the individual conducts work-related functions or lives in their own, personal home.

Well-known official residences include the White House in Washington, D.C. (official residence of the President of the United States), 10 Downing Street in London (of the British Prime Minister), the Élysée Palace in Paris (of the President of France), and the Zhongnanhai in Beijing. Some heads of state also maintain secondary or country residences, such as Camp David in the United States or Chequers in the United Kingdom.

Distinctions

An official residence is provided by the state and is occupied only for the duration of the officeholder's term, as distinct from a private residence owned by the individual. Some monarchies' residences, such as Buckingham Palace, serve simultaneously as official residence, workplace, and ceremonial setting. Official residences can also be provided to ambassadors, senior military officers, judges, and university heads.
