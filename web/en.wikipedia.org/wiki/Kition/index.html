Kition (Ancient Greek: Κίτιον, Kítion; Latin: Citium; Phoenician: 𐤊𐤕, KT, or 𐤊𐤕𐤉, KTY) was an ancient city-kingdom on the southern coast of Cyprus, at the site of modern Larnaca. It was one of the major centres of Phoenician culture in the Mediterranean and an important port for the export of copper from the Troodos Mountains.

History

The city was founded in the Late Bronze Age, around the 13th century BC, and was rebuilt by Mycenaean Greek settlers after about 1200 BC. In the 9th century BC, Phoenician merchants from Tyre established themselves at Kition, refounding its great temple of Astarte around 850 BC. The city later came under Assyrian, Egyptian, and Persian rule, though its local Phoenician kings retained considerable autonomy. In 312 BC the last king, Pumayyaton, was put to death by Ptolemy I, and the kingdom was abolished; the city continued as a Hellenistic and Roman town until its harbour silted up.

Archaeology

Excavations, directed for many years by Vassos Karageorghis, uncovered a major temple complex (Area II, Kathari) with Phoenician inscriptions, workshops for smelting copper, and a famous ship shed and harbour installation (Area I, Bamboula) associated with Phoenician war galleys. Finds from Kition are displayed in the Larnaca District Archaeological Museum and the British Museum.

Notable people

Kition was the birthplace of the Stoic philosopher Zeno of Citium (c. 334 – c. 262 BC), founder of the Stoic school of philosophy in Athens.
