Glory (1989 film)

Glory is a 1989 American historical war drama film directed by Edward Zwick, about the 54th Massachusetts Infantry Regiment, one of the first African-American units in the United States Army during the Civil War. The film stars Matthew Broderick as Colonel Robert Gould Shaw, Denzel Washington as Private Silas Trip, Morgan Freeman as Sergeant Major John Rawlins, and Cary Elwes and Andre Braugher among the supporting cast.

Plot

Wounded at Antietam, the young Boston abolitionist Shaw is given command of the newly raised 54th. The film follows the regiment's training at Camp Meigs, the men's struggle against discrimination — including unequal pay, which they refuse until it is made equal — and Shaw's growth from an inexperienced officer into a leader who earns his soldiers' respect. It culminates in the regiment's assault on Fort Wagner, South Carolina, on 18 July 1863, in which Shaw and roughly half the attacking force are killed; their valour, widely reported, helped spur the enlistment of some 180,000 Black soldiers in the Union cause.

Production and reception

The screenplay by Kevin Jarre drew on Shaw's letters and the books Lay This Laurel and One Gallant Rush. James Horner composed the score, performed with the Boys Choir of Harlem. Glory was praised for its battle sequences and performances, and it won three Academy Awards: Best Supporting Actor for Washington, Best Cinematography for Freddie Francis, and Best Sound. It is frequently ranked among the finest films about the American Civil War, and helped restore the 54th Massachusetts — commemorated by the Saint-Gaudens memorial on Boston Common — to public memory.
