Vizing's theorem

In graph theory, Vizing's theorem states that the edges of any simple undirected graph can be colored with at most Δ + 1 colors, where Δ is the maximum degree of the graph. Since at least Δ colors are always necessary, graphs divide into two classes: class 1 graphs, which need exactly Δ colors, and class 2 graphs, which need Δ + 1.

Background

The theorem was proved by Vadim Vizing in 1964 (and independently approached by Ram Prakash Gupta). Deciding which class a general graph belongs to is NP-complete, though bipartite graphs are always class 1 (Kőnig's line coloring theorem), as are most regular graphs in practice. The proof proceeds by induction using the "Vizing fan" argument.
