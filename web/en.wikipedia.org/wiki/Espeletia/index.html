Espeletia, commonly known as "frailejones" ("big monks"), is a genus of perennial subshrubs, in the family Asteraceae. The genus, which is native mainly to Colombia, Venezuela and Ecuador, was first formally described in 1808. Its best-known member is the frailejón, a plant endemic to the high-altitude páramo ecosystem of the Andes.

The plants live at high altitude, some over 4,500 metres (14,800 ft), where they are subject to intense ultraviolet radiation, low temperatures and nightly frost. Their thick trunks are covered in a dense layer of dead leaves that insulates the stem and protects it from freezing, while the leaves form a rosette covered in fine hairs that trap moisture and reduce water loss.

Distribution and habitat

Espeletia species are characteristic plants of the páramo, the alpine tundra ecosystem found in the northern Andes between the tree line and the snow line. They are keystone species of this ecosystem: their rosettes capture water from mist and rain, slowly releasing it to the soil, and so they play a central role in regulating the water supply of Andean rivers and of cities such as Bogotá that depend on páramo water.

Species

The genus contains well over one hundred species, including Espeletia grandiflora, Espeletia hartwegiana, Espeletia pycnophylla, Espeletia schultzii and Espeletia uribei. The taxonomy of the group has been revised repeatedly, and several related genera in the subtribe Espeletiinae—together informally called the frailejones—were historically included within Espeletia.

Conservation

Many frailejones grow extremely slowly, sometimes only about a centimetre per year, which makes populations vulnerable to burning, grazing, agriculture and climate change. Several species are listed as threatened, and the páramos they inhabit are protected in national parks in Colombia and Venezuela.
