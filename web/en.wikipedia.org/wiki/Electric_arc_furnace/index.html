Electric arc furnace

Electric arc furnace (EAF)
UseSteelmaking from scrap / direct-reduced iron
TemperatureArc ~3,500 °C (6,300 °F)
InventorPaul Héroult (patent 1887)

An electric arc furnace (EAF) is a furnace that heats material by means of an electric arc. Industrial arc furnaces are used primarily for steelmaking, melting scrap steel and direct-reduced iron using graphite electrodes that strike arcs reaching about 3,500 °C.

Operation

Scrap is loaded into the refractory-lined vessel, the roof with electrodes is lowered, and power is applied. After melting, oxygen lancing and alloy additions refine the steel, which is tapped by tilting the furnace. Modern "mini-mills" pair EAFs with continuous casting.

Advantages

EAF steelmaking recycles scrap, is more flexible and less capital-intensive than blast furnace–basic oxygen routes, and can have lower CO2 emissions per tonne depending on electricity source, though electrode consumption and power demand are significant.

History

Paul Héroult patented an arc furnace design in 1887; commercial steel EAFs spread in the early 20th century and expanded greatly after World War II and with mini-mill growth in the United States.
