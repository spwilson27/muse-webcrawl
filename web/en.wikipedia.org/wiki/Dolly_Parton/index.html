Dolly Parton

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Dolly Parton performing in 2014, guitar in hand, rhinestones catching the light, smiling at the crowd]

Dolly Rebecca Parton (born 19 January 1946) is an American singer, songwriter, actress and businesswoman, the most honoured female country performer of all time and a beloved American institution. She wrote — on the same day in 1972, by her famous account — 'Jolene' and 'I Will Always Love You', the latter a standard made planetary by Whitney Houston; her own hits include '9 to 5' (1980), from the film of the same name in which she starred, and the duet 'Islands in the Stream' (1983) with Kenny Rogers.

Beyond music: the Dollywood theme park in her native Smoky Mountains, the Imagination Library (which has given away over 200 million books to children, by its recent counts), film stardom (Steel Magnolias, 1989), and philanthropy including a million-dollar donation toward the Moderna COVID-19 vaccine's research.

From the Smokies to Nashville

Born the fourth of twelve children in a one-room cabin in Locust Ridge, Tennessee, Parton performed on Knoxville radio at ten and arrived in Nashville the day after high school graduation in 1964. Porter Wagoner made her his duet partner and television co-star (1967–74); her leaving him, against his resistance, inspired 'I Will Always Love You' — and she famously refused Elvis Presley's demand of half the publishing to record it, a business decision worth a fortune.

National treasure

Parton's honours include 11 Grammy Awards, the Kennedy Center Honors (2006), the National Medal of Arts, induction into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame (2022 — at first declined, then accepted with a rock album, Rockstar, 2023), and a reputation for kindness so universal that scholars speak of 'Dollyology'. Her literacy programme operates in five countries.
