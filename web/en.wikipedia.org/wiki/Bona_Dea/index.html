Bona Dea
"The Good Goddess"
Cult centre: Aventine Hill, Rome
Festival: 4 December (women only)

Bona Dea ("The Good Goddess") was a goddess in ancient Roman religion associated with chastity, fertility, healing and the protection of the Roman state and plebeians. Her real name and identity were kept secret from men.

Cult

Her temple on the Aventine Hill housed a dispensary of medicinal herbs tended by priestesses. In her December rites, held at the house of a senior magistrate and led by his wife with the Vestal Virgins, men were strictly excluded, wine and myrtle were banned under their own names, and images of males were removed or covered.

Scandal of 62 BC

In 62 BC Publius Clodius Pulcher infiltrated the rites disguised as a woman, allegedly to seduce Pompeia, wife of Julius Caesar — prompting Caesar's divorce and the saying that "Caesar's wife must be above suspicion." See Roman religion, Vestal Virgin, Clodius.
