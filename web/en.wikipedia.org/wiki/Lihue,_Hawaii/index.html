Lihue (Hawaiian: Līhuʻe) is an unincorporated community, census-designated place (CDP) and the county seat of Kauai County, Hawaii, United States. It is the second-largest town on the island of Kauai and the administrative and commercial centre of the county.

History

Lihue grew in the nineteenth century around the sugar industry. The Lihue Plantation, established in 1849, became one of the largest sugar plantations in Hawaii, and the town developed as its hub, with German immigrants — many of them recruited as plantation labourers and managers — leaving a lasting mark on local culture and architecture.

Geography and transport

The community lies on the southeastern coast of Kauai, beside Nawiliwili Bay, which hosts the island's principal seaport, Nawiliwili Harbor. Lihue Airport, just east of the town, is the main air gateway to Kauai. Kalapaki Beach, in front of the harbour area, is a popular swimming and surfing beach.

Points of interest

The Kauai Museum, housed partly in the 1924 Albert Spencer Wilcox Building, presents the island's natural and cultural history. Nearby are the historic Kilohana Plantation estate and the Alekoko ("Menehune") Fishpond, an ancient aquaculture pond attributed in legend to the Menehune people.
