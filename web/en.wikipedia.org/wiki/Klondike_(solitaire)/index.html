Klondike, known in Britain more often as Canfield (not to be confused with the American game of that name), is a patience (solitaire) card game and the best-known solitaire variant in the world. In much of the English-speaking world it is simply called Solitaire. The game is named after the Klondike region of the Yukon, where it is said to have been popularised by gold prospectors in the late 1890s.

Rules

Using a standard 52-card deck, twenty-eight cards are dealt into seven tableau piles: the first pile has one card, the second two, and so on, with only the top card of each pile face up. The remaining cards form the stock. The aim is to build four foundation piles, one per suit, in ascending order from ace to king. In the tableau, cards are built downward in alternating colours; sequences may be moved together, and only kings (or sequences headed by a king) may fill an empty column. Cards are turned from the stock one or three at a time depending on the variant, with or without a limit on redeals.

Winnability and computer versions

Klondike is notorious among mathematicians because its exact winnability under perfect information ("Thoughtful Klondike") resisted analysis for decades; large-scale computation has suggested that around 82% of draw-one deals are winnable. The game achieved immense popularity through Microsoft Solitaire, included with Windows from 1990 onward — originally, its designer Wes Cherry later said, partly to teach users how to drag and drop with a mouse. It became one of the most-played computer games of all time and was inducted into the World Video Game Hall of Fame in 2019.
