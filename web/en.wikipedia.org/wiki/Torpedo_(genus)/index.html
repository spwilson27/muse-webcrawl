Torpedo is a genus of electric rays in the family Torpedinidae. They are bottom-dwelling cartilaginous fishes with greatly enlarged pectoral fins forming a nearly circular disc, and a pair of large electric organs at the base of the disc capable of producing strong electric shocks for defence and prey capture.

The genus name comes from the Latin torpere, "to be stiff or numb", referring to the effect of the ray's discharge on anyone who touches it. Species of Torpedo inspired the name of the naval weapon, the torpedo.

Species

Thirteen or so species are currently recognized in the genus (other species formerly placed here are now assigned to Tetronarce), including:

  • Torpedo torpedo — common torpedo
  • Torpedo marmorata — marbled electric ray
  • Torpedo fuscomaculata — black-spotted torpedo
  • Torpedo panthera — leopard torpedo
  • Torpedo sinuspersici — variable torpedo
  • Torpedo suessii — Red Sea torpedo
  • Torpedo andersoni — Florida torpedo
  • Torpedo bauchotae — rosy torpedo
  • Torpedo mackayana — ringed torpedo
  • Torpedo microdiscus — smalldisk torpedo
  • Torpedo semipelagica — eastern Pacific torpedo
  • Torpedo adenensis — Aden Gulf torpedo

Distribution and habitat

Torpedo species occur in the Atlantic, Indian and Pacific Oceans, mostly in shallow coastal waters of continental shelves, where they rest buried in sand or mud, though some range to depths of several hundred metres.

Electric organs

The electric organs consist of stacks of electrocytes — flattened, modified muscle cells — arranged in honeycomb-like columns. By discharging many cells in series and parallel, the rays generate pulses of tens to over a hundred volts, used to stun fish prey and discourage predators.
