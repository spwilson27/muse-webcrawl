Cycle rickshaw

A cycle rickshaw is a small-scale local means of transport. It is a type of tricycle designed to carry passengers on a for-hire basis, propelled by a rider pedalling at the front or rear. It is also known by a variety of other names such as bike taxi, velotaxi, pedicab, bikecab, cyclo, beca, becak, trisikad, sikad, and rickshaw.

Cycle rickshaws are used extensively across Asia — especially in Bangladesh, India, Indonesia, Vietnam, and Cambodia — and are found, often serving tourists, in many cities in Europe and North America.

History

The cycle rickshaw developed from the hand-pulled rickshaw, invented in Japan around 1869. Adding pedals and a bicycle drivetrain in the early 20th century produced a vehicle that one person could propel faster and with less degrading labour than pulling by hand. The design spread through colonial and post-colonial Asia, where it became an affordable form of public transport and a major source of employment.

Design

Most cycle rickshaws seat two passengers on a bench behind or in front of the driver, protected by a canopy. In South Asia the driver sits at the front pedalling, with passengers behind; in Vietnam the cyclo reverses this, placing passengers in front. Bangladeshi rickshaws are famous for their elaborate painted artwork. Many modern pedicabs add electric pedal assistance, gears, and hydraulic brakes.

Role and regulation

In Dhaka, Bangladesh — often called the rickshaw capital of the world — cycle rickshaws number in the hundreds of thousands and account for a large share of trips. They provide door-to-door, pollution-free transport well suited to narrow, congested streets, but drivers' earnings are low and working conditions hard. Some cities restrict or ban them from major roads, policies that remain controversial because of the livelihoods involved. In Western cities, pedicabs operate mainly in tourist and entertainment districts under municipal licensing.
