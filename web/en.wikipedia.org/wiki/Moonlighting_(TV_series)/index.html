Moonlighting is an American comedy-drama television series that aired on ABC from March 3, 1985, to May 14, 1989. The network aired a total of 67 episodes. Starring Cybill Shepherd and Bruce Willis as private detectives, Allison "Maddie" Hayes and David Addison, the show became notorious for its will-they-or-won't-they relationship between the two leads and for its rapid-fire, overlapping dialogue in the style of classic screwball comedies such as those of Howard Hawks.

Premise

Former model Maddie Hayes discovers that her accountant has embezzled her fortune, leaving her only a string of failed businesses — among them the City Angels detective agency, run by the wisecracking David Addison. Unable to sell it, Maddie keeps the agency, renames it Blue Moon Investigations, and partners with David. Their cases, and constant banter, are supported by dippy receptionist Agnes DiPesto (Allyce Beasley), who answers the phone in rhyme, and, later, clerk Bert Viola (Curtis Armstrong).

Production and style

Created by Glenn Gordon Caron, the show blended mystery, romance, and comedy, frequently breaking the fourth wall, staging dream sequences, a Shakespeare parody episode ("Atomic Shakespeare", a retelling of The Taming of the Shrew), and a film-noir episode filmed in black and white and introduced by Orson Welles. Willis, then little known, won the role over thousands of auditioners and became a major star during the show's run. Production was famously troubled: scripts ran long and episodes were frequently late and over budget, so ABC often aired reruns.

Reception and the "Moonlighting curse"

The series was a critical and ratings success in its early seasons, earning 16 Emmy nominations for its third season, and Willis and Shepherd both won Golden Globes. After the leads' characters finally consummated their relationship at the end of season three, ratings declined — a pattern widely dubbed the "Moonlighting curse" for romantic-tension series. The show ended in 1989 with a self-referential finale in which the set is dismantled around the characters.
