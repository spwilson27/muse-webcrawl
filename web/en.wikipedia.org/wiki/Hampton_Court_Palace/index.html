Hampton Court Palace

Hampton Court Palace is a Grade I listed royal palace in the London Borough of Richmond upon Thames, on the River Thames in southwest London, England. Originally built for Cardinal Thomas Wolsey in 1515, it was seized by Henry VIII and became one of his favourite residences. In the late 17th century Sir Christopher Wren rebuilt much of it in the Baroque style for William III and Mary II, leaving the palace with two contrasting architectural characters: Tudor and Baroque. It is managed today by Historic Royal Palaces and is open to the public.

History

Wolsey transformed a manor of the Knights Hospitaller into a great Renaissance palace, but as he fell from favour he surrendered it to Henry VIII in 1529. Henry expanded the palace with the Great Hall, the Chapel Royal, and vast kitchens, and it was the scene of central Tudor events: the birth of Edward VI and the death there of Jane Seymour, the imprisonment of Catherine Howard's alleged misconduct being investigated at the palace, and the Hampton Court Conference of 1604 under James I, which commissioned the King James Bible. William and Mary commissioned Wren to replace the Tudor lodgings with a Baroque palace rivalling Versailles; the work, halted by Mary's death in 1694, produced the Fountain Court ranges and the formal gardens, including the Privy Garden and the famous Maze, planted around 1700.

Later history and today

George II was the last monarch to reside at Hampton Court. From the 1760s the palace was divided into grace-and-favour apartments. Queen Victoria opened it to the public in 1838. A serious fire in 1986 damaged the King's Apartments, which were restored by 1995. The palace's attractions include the Great Vine (planted 1768, among the largest grapevines in the world), the Tudor kitchens, the Haunted Gallery, and the annual Hampton Court Garden Festival.
