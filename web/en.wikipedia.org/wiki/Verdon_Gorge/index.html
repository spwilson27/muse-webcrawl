The Verdon Gorge (French: Gorges du Verdon) is a river canyon located in the Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur region of southeastern France. It is about 25 kilometres (16 mi) long and up to 700 metres (2,300 ft) deep, carved by the Verdon river into the limestone plateaus of Provence. Often described as one of Europe's most beautiful canyons, it is the largest canyon in Europe by depth and is famous for the striking turquoise-green colour of its river.

Geography

The gorge runs from the town of Castellane eastward to near Moustiers-Sainte-Marie, where the Verdon emerges into the artificial Lake of Sainte-Croix, created by a dam completed in 1973. The canyon was cut during the Quaternary as the Verdon incised itself into thick Jurassic limestone. Scenic roads run along both rims — the Route des Gorges on the north bank and the Corniche Sublime on the south — with viewpoints such as the Point Sublime and the Balcons de la Mescla.

History

The gorge was long considered impassable; its floor was first explored in full in 1905 by the speleologist Édouard-Alfred Martel, commissioned to survey the river for hydroelectric potential. A trail along the canyon floor, the Sentier Martel, now bears his name. Dams built in the mid-20th century, including Sainte-Croix and Esparron, regulate the river today.

Tourism and recreation

The gorge is a major centre for outdoor sports: rock climbing on its limestone walls (with thousands of routes), hiking, white-water rafting, kayaking and canoeing, and pedal-boat trips from the lake into the lower canyon. The surrounding area forms the Verdon Regional Natural Park.
