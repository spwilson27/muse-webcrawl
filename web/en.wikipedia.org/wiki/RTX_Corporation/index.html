RTX Corporation, formerly Raytheon Technologies Corporation, is an American multinational aerospace and defence conglomerate and one of the world's largest defence contractors. It was formed on 3 April 2020 by the merger of the Raytheon Company with the aerospace businesses of United Technologies Corporation (UTC).

History

The merger, announced in 2019 as a "merger of equals", required UTC to spin off its Otis Elevator and Carrier air-conditioning businesses, which became independent companies. The combined firm initially took the name Raytheon Technologies and adopted the shorter name RTX in 2023. Raytheon's lineage reaches back to 1922 in Cambridge, Massachusetts, while UTC's roots included Pratt & Whitney (founded 1925) and Hamilton Standard.

Business segments

RTX operates through three principal segments. Collins Aerospace supplies avionics, interiors and systems for commercial and military aircraft. Pratt & Whitney builds jet engines, including the F135 for the F-35 fighter and the geared turbofan family for airliners. Raytheon makes missiles and air-defence systems — including the Patriot, AMRAAM, Stinger and Tomahawk — plus radars, sensors and space systems. The company employs roughly 180,000 people and derives the majority of its revenue from US government and commercial aerospace customers.
