Truism

Truism
DefinitionA statement so obviously true that it conveys little or no information
Related termsPlatitude, tautology, banality, "motherhood statement"
Example"It is what it is"

A truism is a claim that is so obvious or self-evident as to be hardly worth mentioning—an assertion whose truth is trivially apparent to any reasonable listener. Truisms are frequently used as filler in conversation or rhetoric, or to signal agreement without committing the speaker to anything substantive.

Usage

Unlike a tautology in formal logic—a statement true by its logical form—a truism is true by common knowledge or definition and is "uninformative" in context. The term is usually mildly derogatory: to call a remark a truism is to say it adds nothing. Truisms shade into platitudes and clichés, and in political discourse into "motherhood statements"—positions nobody could oppose.

In art

The word gained a distinctive second life in conceptual art through Jenny Holzer's Truisms series (begun 1977), a collection of one-line aphorisms—some banal, some provocative, such as "Abuse of power comes as no surprise"—displayed on posters, benches, LED signs and buildings worldwide.
