Toledo is a city in and the county seat of Lucas County, Ohio, United States. It lies in northwestern Ohio at the western end of Lake Erie, bordering Michigan, at the mouth of the Maumee River. With a population of about 266,000 in the 2020 census, it is the fourth-most populous city in Ohio.

History

The area was settled by American forces after Fort Industry was established in 1805; the villages of Port Lawrence and Vistula merged in 1833 to form Toledo, named, according to tradition, after Toledo, Spain. Its ownership was contested in the nearly bloodless Toledo War (1835–1836) between Ohio and Michigan Territory, resolved by giving the Toledo Strip to Ohio and the Upper Peninsula to Michigan. The arrival of canals and railroads made Toledo a manufacturing and shipping centre.

Economy and culture

Toledo became a major glassmaking centre — hence "The Glass City" — home to companies such as Libbey and Owens-Illinois, and an important centre of auto-parts manufacturing (Jeep vehicles have been built in Toledo since 1941). Major institutions include the University of Toledo, the Toledo Museum of Art (renowned for its glass collection), the Toledo Zoo, and the Toledo Mud Hens minor-league baseball team, popularised by the television series M*A*S*H.
