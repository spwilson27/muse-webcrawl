Zhiyi

Zhiyi (Chinese: 智顗; pinyin: Zhìyǐ; Wade–Giles: Chih-i; 538–597 CE), also known as Master Zhizhe (智者大師), was a Chinese Buddhist monk and philosopher credited with founding the Tiantai school of Buddhism. He is regarded as the fourth patriarch of Tiantai and is traditionally credited with composing several foundational texts of the school, recorded by his disciple Guanding.

Life

Zhiyi was born in Jingzhou (in present-day Hubei) into a family connected to the Liang dynasty court. After the turmoil that followed the Hou Jing rebellion and the fall of the capital, he lost his parents and decided to become a monk at the age of eighteen. He studied under the meditation master Huisi on Mount Dasu, where he is said to have had a profound awakening while reading the Lotus Sutra. In 575 he withdrew to Mount Tiantai in present-day Zhejiang, where he gathered disciples and developed his system of doctrine and practice. Late in life he accepted the patronage of Yang Guang, Prince of Jin (later Emperor Yang of Sui), who bestowed on him the title "Zhizhe" ("the Wise One"). Zhiyi died in 597 while visiting Shicheng.

Teachings

Zhiyi's system is a comprehensive synthesis of Indian Buddhist doctrine organised around the Lotus Sutra, which he regarded as the Buddha's supreme teaching. His thought is characterised by doctrines such as the "threefold truth" (emptiness, provisional existence, and the Middle), "three thousand realms in a single moment of thought" (ichinen sanzen), and a classification of the Buddha's teachings into five periods and eight categories. His meditation manual, the Mohe Zhiguan ("Great Cessation and Insight"), remains one of the most influential works on Buddhist meditation in East Asia.

Legacy

Zhiyi's works and the Tiantai school he founded had a lasting impact on Buddhism in China, Korea (as Cheontae), and Japan (as Tendai). The Japanese monk Saichō modelled the Tendai school on Zhiyi's system, and Tiantai ideas strongly influenced Chan, Pure Land, and Nichiren Buddhism.
