Voyager Golden Record
Launched1977 aboard Voyager 1 and Voyager 2
Committee chairCarl Sagan
Contents115 images, greetings in 55 languages, 90 minutes of music, natural and human-made sounds

The Voyager Golden Record is a pair of identical phonograph records, one carried aboard each Voyager spacecraft launched in 1977. Intended as a message from humanity to any extraterrestrial intelligence that might find them, the 12-inch gold-plated copper discs are expected to remain playable for over a billion years.

Contents

A committee chaired by Carl Sagan, with Frank Drake, Ann Druyan (creative director) and Jon Lomberg among others, selected "The Sounds of Earth": greetings in 55 languages, whale calls, thunder, and music from Bach, Beethoven, Mozart, Blind Willie Johnson, Chuck Berry's "Johnny B. Goode", gamelan, Navajo chant and more, plus 115 encoded images of science, landscapes, animals and human life. The cover bears etched instructions, a pulsar map locating the Sun, and a uranium-238 clock.

Journey

Voyager 1 is now the most distant human-made object, in interstellar space; the records will pass near other stars on timescales of tens of thousands of years. See Van Allen radiation belt, which the Voyagers crossed on their way out.
