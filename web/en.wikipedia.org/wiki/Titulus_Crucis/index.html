Titulus Crucis


The Titulus Crucis relic at Santa Croce in Gerusalemme

The Titulus Crucis (Latin for "Title of the Cross") is a relic preserved in the church of Santa Croce in Gerusalemme in Rome. Tradition holds it to be a piece of the panel inscribed with the charge against Jesus and placed above his cross at the Crucifixion, as described in the Gospels. The surviving fragment, of walnut wood, bears part of an inscription in Hebrew, Greek, and Latin, written in reverse order from right to left.

History

According to the Gospel of John, Pontius Pilate ordered a sign reading "Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews" (often abbreviated INRI in Latin) to be fastened to the cross, written in Hebrew, Latin, and Greek. The Roman relic is said to have been brought from the Holy Land by Saint Helena in the fourth century. It was rediscovered during restoration work on the church in 1492, sealed in a lead box behind a brick inscribed "Titulus Crucis".

Authenticity debate

Radiocarbon dating performed in 2002 dated the wood of the fragment to between 980 and 1146 CE, indicating that the object is a medieval artefact rather than a first-century original, though supporters of the relic have questioned sampling and contamination issues. Many scholars accordingly regard it as a medieval copy or devotional recreation of the lost original.
