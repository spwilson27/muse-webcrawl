Biomimetic architecture

Biomimetic architecture is a philosophy of architecture that seeks solutions for sustainability in nature, not by replicating the forms of nature per se, but by understanding the rules governing those forms. It is a multi-disciplinary approach to sustainable design that follows a set of principles derived from nature, rather than simply mimicking natural shapes. Related terms include biomimicry in architecture and, for the older practice of imitating natural forms ornamentally, biomorphism.

History

Architects have looked to nature for inspiration since antiquity; Antoni Gaudí's structures, such as the Sagrada Família in Barcelona, derive columns and vaults from tree-like branching forms. In the 20th century, designers such as Buckminster Fuller studied nature's structural efficiency. The modern biomimetic movement in architecture draws heavily on Janine Benyus's 1997 book Biomimicry: Innovation Inspired by Nature, which popularised the idea of emulating nature's time-tested strategies.

Approaches

Biomimicry is commonly analysed at three levels: the organism level (mimicking a specific organism's form or process), the behaviour level (mimicking how an organism behaves or relates to its environment), and the ecosystem level (mimicking the processes and cycles of a whole ecosystem).

Examples

  • Eastgate Centre, Harare, Zimbabwe (Mick Pearce, 1996) — a shopping and office complex with a passive cooling system inspired by the self-cooling mounds of African termites, using dramatically less energy than comparable air-conditioned buildings.
  • Beijing National Stadium (Herzog & de Meuron, 2008) — the "Bird's Nest," whose structure echoes the twig construction of a bird's nest.
  • The Gherkin, London (Foster + Partners, 2003) — its ventilation strategy and form have been compared to those of the Venus' flower basket sponge.
  • Waterloo International railway station — roof design drawing on the flexible scaling of the pangolin.
  • BIQ House, Hamburg — a building with a bioreactor façade of algae panels that generate biomass and heat while providing shade.
