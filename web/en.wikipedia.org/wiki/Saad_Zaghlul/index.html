Saad Zaghlul (Arabic: سعد زغلول; c. 1857 – 23 August 1927) was an Egyptian revolutionary and statesman. He led the Wafd Party and served as Prime Minister of Egypt in 1924, becoming one of the most influential figures of modern Egyptian history and a symbol of the country's nationalist movement against British occupation.

Early life and education

Zaghlul was born in Ibyana, a village in the Kafr El Sheikh Governorate of the Nile Delta, into a family of modest landowners; his father was the village headman (omda). He studied at Al-Azhar University in Cairo and later at the Egyptian School of Law, earning his law degree in 1880. During his studies he was influenced by the pan-Islamic reformer Jamal al-Din al-Afghani and by Muhammad Abduh, whose circle shaped a generation of Egyptian reformers.

Career

Zaghlul worked as a judge and rose through the judiciary, noted for his integrity. In 1906 he became Minister of Education, founding the Egyptian University (later Cairo University) in 1908, and in 1910 he became Minister of Justice. In 1913 he was elected vice-president of the Legislative Assembly, where his outspoken criticism of British interference made him a leading nationalist voice.

The Wafd and the 1919 revolution

After the First World War, Zaghlul formed a delegation — the Wafd (Arabic for "delegation") — which demanded the right to present Egypt's case for independence at the Paris Peace Conference. The British refused and, in March 1919, arrested and exiled Zaghlul to Malta. His exile sparked the Egyptian Revolution of 1919, a nationwide uprising of strikes, demonstrations and unrest that forced Britain to release him and contributed to the Unilateral Declaration of Egyptian Independence in 1922.

Prime Minister and later years

The Wafd won the 1924 elections overwhelmingly, and Zaghlul became Prime Minister. His tenure was cut short by the assassination of Sir Lee Stack, the British Sirdar of the Egyptian Army, in November 1924; Britain issued an ultimatum and Zaghlul resigned rather than accept its terms, though he remained the dominant figure in Egyptian politics. He died in Cairo on 23 August 1927. His house in Cairo, known as Beit El-Umma ("House of the Nation"), is now a museum.

Legacy

Zaghlul is remembered as the father of modern Egyptian nationalism. His mausoleum in Cairo, built in Pharaonic style, and numerous statues, streets and schools across Egypt bear his name. His wife, Safia Zaghlul, was herself a prominent activist, often called the "Mother of the Egyptians".
