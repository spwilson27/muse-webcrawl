Mil Moscow Helicopter Plant
Founded: 1947
Founder: Mikhail Mil
Parent: Russian Helicopters

Mil Moscow Helicopter Plant is a Russian (formerly Soviet) helicopter design bureau headquartered in Moscow, best known for the long line of helicopters bearing the "Mi" prefix. It was founded in 1947 by Mikhail Leontyevich Mil, who led the bureau until his death in 1970.

History

Mil's first production helicopter, the Mi-1, flew in 1948 and became the first Soviet helicopter in series production. The bureau followed with the Mi-4, the turbine-powered Mi-6 heavy lift helicopter, the ubiquitous Mi-8 — one of the most-produced helicopters in history — and its export and gunship derivatives including the Mi-17 and the Mi-24 attack helicopter. Later designs include the Mi-26, the world's largest production helicopter, the Mi-28 attack helicopter and the Mi-38 transport.

Organisation

The design bureau is now part of the National Helicopter Center named after Mil and Kamov within the Russian Helicopters holding company (itself part of Rostec), following a merger of the Mil and Kamov design organisations. Production of Mil designs takes place at plants in Kazan, Ulan-Ude and elsewhere.
