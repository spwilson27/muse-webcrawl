The Durango and Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad (D&SNG) is a heritage railway in southwestern Colorado, United States, operating steam-hauled passenger trains on a 3 ft narrow gauge line between Durango and Silverton. The line was built by the Denver and Rio Grande Railroad and opened in 1882 to serve the silver and gold mines of the San Juan Mountains.

History

Construction began in 1881 and the first train reached Silverton in July 1882. Ore traffic sustained the branch for decades; after mining declined, the line survived largely because of tourist traffic, promoted by the Rio Grande from the late 1940s. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1961. In 1981 the line was sold to Charles Bradshaw Jr., who invested in the operation and promoted it as a tourist attraction; it later passed to American Heritage Railways.

Route

From Durango the line follows the Animas River north through the Animas Canyon, climbing to a high point of about 2,837 metres near Silverton. Famous locations include the "High Line", where the track clings to a ledge above the river gorge. The railroad also operates the Cascade Canyon winter service to a picnic site in the canyon.

Equipment

Trains are hauled by coal- and, increasingly, oil-fired steam locomotives, many of them former Denver and Rio Grande Western K-class Mikados built in the 1920s, along with older 19th-century engines preserved in the D&SNG museum in the Durango roundhouse.
