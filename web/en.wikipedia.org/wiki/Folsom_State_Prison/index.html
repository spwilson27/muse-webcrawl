Folsom State Prison

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Folsom State Prison (FSP) is a California state prison in Folsom, in Sacramento County. Opened in 1880, it is the state's second-oldest prison after San Quentin and one of the first maximum-security prisons in the United States. It is a medium-to-high security institution for men, operated by the California Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation.

History

Construction began in 1878 using inmate labour to quarry the local granite — Folsom is known for its distinctive granite walls. The prison long held California's most dangerous offenders; 93 prisoners were executed by hanging there between 1895 and 1937, when executions moved to the gas chamber at San Quentin. Folsom was the site of numerous violent escapes and riots in the early 20th century, and inmates built Folsom Dam's predecessor works and the prison's own hydroelectric plant, among the first in California.

In popular culture

Folsom is famous worldwide through country singer Johnny Cash, whose 1955 song "Folsom Prison Blues" fixed its image in popular culture. Cash performed two concerts at the prison on 13 January 1968; the recording, At Folsom Prison, became one of his best-known albums and revived his career.

Today

The prison houses several thousand inmates and operates industries including a metal fabrication plant, a print shop and the licence-plate factory for California. The adjacent, separately administered California State Prison, Sacramento opened in 1986. A small museum near the entrance displays artefacts of prison history.
