Overview

Dalmatia is a historical region on the eastern coast of the Adriatic Sea in Croatia, a narrow belt between the Dinaric Alps and the sea, plus a dense archipelago of over a thousand islands and islets. It takes its name from the Illyrian Delmatae tribe.

History

Dalmatia was a Roman province; Diocletian, from Salona, retired to his palace at Split, around which the city grew. The maritime republic of Ragusa (Dubrovnik) and Venetian rule ("Stato da Mar") shaped its coastal cities for centuries, followed by Habsburg, French, Austrian and Yugoslav rule. Dalmatian culture blends Croatian, Italian and Mediterranean influences; the Dalmatian dog breed is associated with the region, though its exact origins are debated.
