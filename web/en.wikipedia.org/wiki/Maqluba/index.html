Maqluba

Maqluba
CourseMain course
Place of originLevant
Main ingredientsRice, meat, cauliflower, eggplant, potato

Maqluba (Arabic: مقلوبة, literally "upside-down") is a traditional Levantine dish of rice, meat and fried vegetables placed in a pot which is flipped upside down when served, hence its name.

Preparation

Vegetables such as cauliflower, eggplant, potatoes and carrots are fried and layered with meat (chicken, lamb or beef) and rice in a pot, seasoned with spices including turmeric, allspice and cinnamon. The pot is cooked, rested, and then inverted onto a serving platter so the dish arrives as a layered cake of rice and vegetables.

Regional variations

Maqluba is popular in Palestine, Jordan, Syria, Lebanon and Iraq. Variations differ in the vegetables used and whether the dish is made with chicken or red meat; a famous variation comes from Baghdad, where it is associated with festive gatherings.

History

A dish resembling maqluba appears in the 13th-century Kitab al-Tabikh cookbook from Baghdad. It is often served on Fridays and at family celebrations, accompanied by salad and yoghurt.
