Antonio Mario Taguba (born 31 October 1950) is a retired major general in the United States Army. He is best known as the author of the Taguba Report, the internal Army investigation into prisoner abuse by U.S. soldiers at Abu Ghraib prison in Iraq in 2003–2004.

Early life and career

Taguba was born in Manila; his father had survived the Bataan Death March. The family emigrated to Hawaii when he was 11. Commissioned through ROTC at Idaho State University in 1972, he served in armor and logistics posts in the United States, Germany and Korea, commanded at brigade level in Kuwait, and rose to become one of the highest-ranking Filipino American officers in U.S. history.

The Taguba Report

In January 2004 Taguba was appointed to investigate the 800th Military Police Brigade after photographs of detainee abuse at Abu Ghraib surfaced. His report, completed in March 2004 and leaked that May, documented "sadistic, blatant and wanton criminal abuses" and found systemic failures of leadership. It became a central document of the scandal.

Later life

Taguba believed his career was ended by the report; he was passed over and directed to retire, leaving the Army in January 2007. He later spoke publicly about accountability, worked with veterans' and human-rights organisations, and served as a leader in the Filipino American community.
