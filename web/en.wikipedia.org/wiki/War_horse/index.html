War horse

A war horse is a horse used for fighting, including cavalry charges, mounted combat, scouting, reconnaissance, and the transport of supplies and messages. Horses have been used in warfare for thousands of years, from the chariot warfare of the ancient Near East to the cavalry of the modern era. The type and role of the war horse changed greatly over time, shaped by the development of weapons, armour and tactics.

Ancient and medieval warfare

Early war horses pulled chariots in Egypt, Mesopotamia and China. Mounted archers became central to the armies of steppe peoples such as the Scythians, Parthians and Mongols. In medieval Europe the heavily armoured knight rode a large, powerful horse often called a destrier, trained to charge with a couched lance. Such horses were expensive and were typically used only in battle, the knight riding a smaller horse, a palfrey, for travel.

Early modern and modern eras

With the rise of firearms, cavalry shifted toward lighter horses used for shock action, dragoon service and reconnaissance. War horses remained essential through the Napoleonic Wars and the American Civil War, and millions were used in the First World War for cavalry, artillery haulage and supply transport, suffering enormous losses. Horses were still widely used in the Second World War, particularly by Germany and the Soviet Union for transport in difficult terrain, though mechanisation steadily displaced them.

Care and losses

Armies maintained extensive veterinary and remount services to supply and care for war horses. Disease, overwork, poor forage and enemy fire killed horses in vast numbers; in the First World War alone some eight million horses, mules and donkeys are estimated to have died. Memorials in many countries commemorate their service.
