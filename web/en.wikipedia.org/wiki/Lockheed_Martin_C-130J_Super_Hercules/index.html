The Lockheed Martin C-130J Super Hercules is a four-engine turboprop military transport aircraft. The C-130J is a comprehensive update of the Lockheed C-130 Hercules, with new Rolls-Royce AE 2100 turboprop engines, Dowty R391 composite six-blade propellers, digital avionics (including head-up displays for each pilot), and other systems. The C-130J is the newest version of the C-130 Hercules and the only model still in production.

Development and design

Externally similar to the classic Hercules, the J-model features significantly updated technology: a two-crew flight deck with full digital avionics replaced the earlier five-person crew, and the new engines with FADEC (full authority digital engine control) give improved range, climb and short-field performance with lower operating costs. Two fuselage lengths are offered, the standard C-130J and the stretched C-130J-30. Specialised variants include the KC-130J tanker, the HC/MC-130J combat search-and-rescue and special-operations aircraft, and the AC-130J Ghostrider gunship.

Operational history

The Super Hercules entered service with the Royal Air Force in 1999 and has been ordered by more than twenty air forces, including those of the United States, India, Australia, Canada, Italy, Norway, Denmark, Israel and Germany (in a joint Franco-German squadron). C-130Js have flown airlift, airdrop, humanitarian-relief and evacuation missions in Iraq, Afghanistan and numerous disaster zones.
