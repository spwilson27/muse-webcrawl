Acronauplia

Acronauplia (Greek: Ακροναυπλία, Akronafplia) is a rocky peninsula and historic fortified citadel overlooking the old town of Nafplio in the Argolic Gulf, Greece. It is the oldest of Nafplio's three fortresses and has been continuously fortified from antiquity into the modern era.

History

The site was inhabited and fortified in prehistoric times and served as the acropolis of ancient Nauplia. Sections of the surviving walls incorporate ancient polygonal masonry from the Classical and Hellenistic periods. During the Byzantine era the fortress guarded the town below, and after the Fourth Crusade it passed to Frankish lords of the Principality of Achaea.

The Venetians, who held Nafplio from 1388, substantially rebuilt the defenses, dividing the citadel into upper and lower castles. The Ottomans captured the town in 1540, and Acronauplia remained an Ottoman stronghold, with interruptions during the Venetian reconquest of the Morea in the late 17th century, until the Greek War of Independence, when Nafplio became the first capital of the modern Greek state.

Modern era

In the 20th century Acronauplia was used as a prison, notorious during the Greek Civil War and the Colonels' regime for holding political prisoners. The prison was later demolished and the site converted to tourism uses, including a hotel complex built during the junta period. Today the citadel is open to walkers, offering views across Nafplio, the Bourtzi island fortress, and the Argolic Gulf, while the later hilltop fortress of Palamidi dominates the skyline above it.
