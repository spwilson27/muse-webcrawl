Consent

Consent occurs when one person voluntarily agrees to the proposal or desires of another. It is a term of common speech in law, medicine, research and sexual relationships. Consent as understood in specific contexts may differ from its everyday meaning. For example, a person with a mental disorder, a low mental age, or under the legal age of sexual consent may willingly engage in a sexual act that nevertheless fails to meet the legal threshold for consent as defined by applicable law.

Types of consent

Express consent is given explicitly, in writing or verbally. Implied consent is inferred from a person's actions, the facts, or the circumstances of a situation. Informed consent, a key concept in medicine and research, requires that a person be given adequate information about the risks, benefits and alternatives of a procedure before agreeing to it. Unanimous consent is used in parliamentary procedure to expedite decisions where no objection is expected.

Legal aspects

In law, consent can be a defence to certain charges, but it must generally be freely given by a person with the capacity to consent, and it may be withdrawn at any time. Consent obtained by deception, duress or coercion is generally invalid. Many legal systems set minimum ages below which a person is deemed incapable of consenting to particular acts, and regulate consent in contract law, medical treatment, data processing and criminal law.

Consent in medicine and research

Medical treatment ordinarily requires the consent of the patient. The modern doctrine of informed consent developed in the 20th century, reinforced by research ethics codes such as the Nuremberg Code (1947) and the Declaration of Helsinki, which require voluntary participation and full disclosure for human research subjects.

Sexual consent

Sexual activity without consent is considered sexual assault or rape in most jurisdictions. Public discussion since the late 20th century has emphasised affirmative, ongoing and revocable consent, and many institutions have adopted "yes means yes" standards in their policies.
