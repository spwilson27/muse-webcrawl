Blenny

SuborderBlennioidei
FamiliesBlenniidae, Chaenopsidae, Clinidae, Dactyloscopidae, Labrisomidae, Tripterygiidae
HabitatShallow marine waters, reefs, tide pools

Blennies are a diverse group of small, mostly marine fishes in the suborder Blennioidei within the order Perciformes. They are found chiefly in shallow tropical and subtropical waters worldwide, living on reefs, in rock pools and among seaweed. Most blennies are bottom-dwellers with elongated, scaleless or small-scaled bodies, blunt heads and often fleshy tentacles called cirri above the eyes.

Families

The term "blenny" is commonly applied to members of several families. The combtooth blennies (family Blenniidae) are the largest group, named for the comb-like teeth in their jaws; they include the algae-eating lawnmower blenny popular in aquaria. Other families include the pikeblennies (Chaenopsidae), kelp blennies (Clinidae), sand stargazers (Dactyloscopidae), scaled blennies (Labrisomidae) and threefin blennies (Tripterygiidae), the latter having the dorsal fin divided into three distinct parts.

Behaviour and diet

Blennies are typically territorial and spend much of their time perched on their pectoral fins, watching for predators and prey. Many feed on algae and detritus, while others eat small crustaceans, molluscs and plankton. Some species, such as the fang blennies of the genus Meiacanthus, possess venomous fangs used in defence. The sabre-toothed blenny mimics the cleaner wrasse and bites unsuspecting fish. Several intertidal blennies can survive out of water for extended periods, breathing through their skin and gill chambers.
