The Battle of Tannenberg was fought between Russia and Germany from 26 to 30 August 1914, in the opening month of the First World War. The German Eighth Army, under Paul von Hindenburg and Erich Ludendorff, encircled and virtually annihilated the Russian Second Army of General Alexander Samsonov in the forests and lake country of southern East Prussia. It was one of the most complete victories of the war and made Hindenburg a national hero.

Background

Russia invaded East Prussia with two armies: Rennenkampf's First Army from the east and Samsonov's Second Army from the south. After the German check at Gumbinnen, the German command was changed, Hindenburg and Ludendorff taking over. Exploiting staff work by Max Hoffmann, the enmity and separation between the two Russian commanders, intercepted uncoded Russian radio messages, and interior rail lines, the Germans concentrated almost their entire army against Samsonov.

The battle

From 26 August German corps struck the flanks of the advancing Russian Second Army, then closed behind it. Samsonov's centre corps were trapped in a pocket around Usdau and the Komusin forest; attempts to break out failed. Samsonov shot himself in the forest on the night of 29/30 August. Tens of thousands of Russians were killed and around 90,000 or more taken prisoner.

Aftermath

The Germans then turned on Rennenkampf and drove the First Army out of East Prussia at the First Battle of the Masurian Lakes in September. The victory's name deliberately recalled the medieval Battle of Tannenberg/Grunwald of 1410, where Teutonic Knights had been defeated by a Polish-Lithuanian army.
