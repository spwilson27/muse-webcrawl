Tortuga (Haiti)

TortugaNative name: Île de la Tortue
Country: Haiti
Area: about 180 km²

Tortuga (French: Île de la Tortue) is an island off the northern coast of Haiti, separated from the mainland by the Tortuga Channel. Named for its turtle-like shape, it is famous as a seventeenth-century stronghold of Caribbean buccaneers.

History

French, English and Dutch freebooters settled Tortuga in the 1620s and 1630s, hunting wild cattle and pigs on Hispaniola and raiding Spanish shipping. Under leaders such as Bertrand d'Ogeron the island became a French colony and a base for the Brethren of the Coast, including figures associated with Henry Morgan's era.

Piracy and decline

As French authority consolidated on western Hispaniola (Saint-Domingue), Tortuga's buccaneering role faded in the late seventeenth century, though its pirate legend endures in literature and film.

Today

The island is administratively a commune of Haiti's Nord-Ouest department, with the main settlement at Palmiste. Its economy is based on fishing and subsistence agriculture.
