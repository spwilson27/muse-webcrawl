Popeye

Popeye the Sailor Man
Created by: E. C. Segar
First appearance: Thimble Theatre, 17 January 1929
Signature food: Spinach
[Image: Popeye the sailor squeezing open a can of spinach, forearms bulging and corncob pipe in his mouth]

Popeye the Sailor Man is a fictional cartoon character created by Elzie Crisler Segar, first appearing in the daily King Features comic strip Thimble Theatre on 17 January 1929. The one-eyed, gravel-voiced sailor — with his huge forearms, corncob pipe, squint and catchphrase "I yam what I yam" — soon eclipsed the strip's original cast, including his lanky girlfriend Olive Oyl, the hamburger-loving Wimpy, and his nemesis Bluto (Brutus).

Spinach

Popeye's defining trait is gaining instant superhuman strength by eating canned spinach. The association made the character a marketing phenomenon: spinach consumption in the United States rose markedly in the 1930s, and the spinach-growing town of Crystal City, Texas, erected a statue of Popeye in 1937 in gratitude. The popular belief that spinach is exceptionally rich in iron — sometimes blamed on a misplaced decimal point in 19th-century data — is itself largely a myth, but Popeye cemented the link between spinach and strength in popular culture worldwide.

Adaptations and legacy

The Fleischer Studios theatrical cartoons (1933–1942) and later Famous Studios shorts made Popeye a rival to Mickey Mouse in popularity; the character has since appeared in television cartoons, comic books, video games and a 1980 live-action film starring Robin Williams. Popeye's spinach-fuelled persona endures as a cultural shorthand for the virtues of eating leafy greens — an unlikely meeting of food culture and comics, alongside food-related characters such as the mayonnaise devotees of Kewpie fame.
