History of baseball in the United States

The history of baseball in the United States traces the sport from English bat-and-ball games such as rounders to its emergence as America's "national pastime." The Knickerbocker Base Ball Club of New York codified rules in 1845 under Alexander Cartwright.

Professional era

The Cincinnati Red Stockings became the first openly professional team in 1869. The National League was founded in 1876 and the American League in 1901; the first World Series was played in 1903. The 1919 Black Sox scandal led to the appointment of Commissioner Kenesaw Mountain Landis.

Integration and expansion

Black players were excluded from the major leagues and formed the Negro leagues until Jackie Robinson broke the colour barrier with the Brooklyn Dodgers in 1947. Franchises expanded westward and southward, free agency arrived in the 1970s, and the sport weathered a players' strike in 1994 and the steroids era.

Modern game

Major League Baseball remains a major commercial and cultural institution, with analytics transforming strategy in the 21st century. See also Baseball.
