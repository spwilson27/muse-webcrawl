The Grande Arche de la Défense, originally the Grande Arche de la Fraternité, is a monument and office building in the La Défense business district west of Paris, France. Designed as a vast hollow cube — 110 m tall, wide, and deep — it forms a modern counterpart to the Arc de Triomphe, standing at the western end of the city's historical axis (Axe historique).

Design and construction

Danish architect Johann Otto von Spreckelsen won the 1982 international competition launched under President François Mitterrand's Grands Projets program. The structure is clad in white Carrara marble and glass; a Teflon-coated fibreglass "cloud" canopy stretches within the cube's void. Spreckelsen resigned in 1986 and died the following year; the building was completed by Paul Andreu and inaugurated in July 1989, during the bicentennial of the French Revolution and the Paris G7 summit.

Use

The two vertical "towers" of the cube house government offices, long associated with the French Ministry of Transport/Ecology. A rooftop terrace and exhibition space reopened to the public in 2017 after renovation. The arch is rotated 6.5° off the historical axis, a deviation imposed by metro and motorway tunnels beneath the site.
