The Kingdom of León (Leonese: Reinu de Llión; Spanish: Reino de León) was an independent kingdom situated in the northwest region of the Iberian Peninsula. It was founded in 910 when the Christian princes of Asturias, another kingdom, moved their capital from Oviedo to the city of León.

History

Emerging from the Kingdom of Asturias, León became the leading Christian power in Iberia during the 10th century, at times dominating Galicia, Asturias, and the emerging county of Castile. Its kings pushed the Reconquista frontier to the Duero and beyond. The county of Portugal was established within its territory in the 9th century, eventually becoming an independent kingdom in 1139.

The Cortes of León of 1188, convened by Alfonso IX, are often cited as among the earliest parliamentary bodies in Europe, including representatives of the towns alongside nobles and clergy. In 1230 Ferdinand III of Castile inherited León, permanently uniting the two crowns as the Crown of Castile, although León retained its own institutions for centuries.

Culture

The kingdom's legacy includes the Romanesque architecture of León Cathedral and San Isidoro, the Leonese language, and its role in the pilgrimage routes to Santiago de Compostela.
