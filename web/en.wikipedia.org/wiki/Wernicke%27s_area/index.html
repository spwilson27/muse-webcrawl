Wernicke's area

Wernicke's area is a region of the brain, in the posterior part of the superior temporal gyrus of the (usually left) cerebral hemisphere, that is involved in the comprehension of written and spoken language. It is named after German neurologist Carl Wernicke, who in 1874 described the aphasia resulting from damage to this area.

LocationPosterior superior temporal gyrus (Brodmann area 22)
FunctionLanguage comprehension
Named afterCarl Wernicke

Wernicke's aphasia

Damage to Wernicke's area produces receptive (fluent) aphasia: speech remains fluent and grammatical in rhythm but is often meaningless, filled with neologisms, while comprehension of language is severely impaired. The area is connected to Broca's area, involved in speech production, by the arcuate fasciculus; modern models place language processing in a broader perisylvian network.
