The Torres Strait Islander flag is the official flag of the Torres Strait Islander peoples, the Indigenous peoples of the Torres Strait Islands between the northern tip of Queensland, Australia, and Papua New Guinea. It was designed by Bernard Namok of Thursday Island, who won a design competition held by the Island Coordinating Council in 1992, when he was fifteen years old.

Design and symbolism

The flag has three horizontal panels: green at the top and bottom, representing the land; a narrow black band through the middle, representing the Torres Strait Islander people; and a blue central panel, representing the sea. Together the blue panel and black band form the shape of the central device.

In the centre stands a white dhari (also spelled dari), the distinctive ceremonial headdress of the Torres Strait, symbolising the Torres Strait Islander people. Beneath the dhari is a white five-pointed star, representing both the star used by Islander seafarers for navigation and the five island groups of the Torres Strait: the Northern, Western, Central, Eastern, and Mainland (Southern) groups.

Recognition and use

The flag was adopted by the Torres Strait Islander Advisory Board and was officially recognised by the Australian Government in 1995, when it was proclaimed a "Flag of Australia" under the Flags Act 1953, alongside the Aboriginal flag. It is flown at government buildings, schools, and ceremonies throughout the Torres Strait and mainland Australia, particularly during NAIDOC Week and Reconciliation Week.
