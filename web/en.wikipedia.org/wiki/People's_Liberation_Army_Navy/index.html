The People's Liberation Army Navy (PLAN), also known as the Chinese People's Liberation Army Navy, the People's Navy or the Chinese Navy, is the naval warfare branch of the People's Liberation Army, the armed forces of China. Established in 1949 during the Chinese Civil War, it has grown from a small coastal defence force into the largest navy in the world by number of vessels, with a battle force of more than 370 ships and submarines as counted by the United States Department of Defense, and a total tonnage second only to the United States Navy.

History

The PLAN traces its origin to the East China Military Area Navy, founded on 23 April 1949 at Baimamiao, Jiangsu, largely from defected Republic of China Navy personnel and vessels. For its first decades it remained a brown-water coastal force, relying on Soviet equipment and doctrine, fast attack craft, mines and coastal artillery, oriented towards a possible conflict over Taiwan. From the 1980s, and especially after the 1995–96 Taiwan Strait Crisis exposed its limitations, China invested heavily in modernisation: importing Russian Sovremenny-class destroyers and Kilo-class submarines while developing indigenous designs.

In the 21st century the PLAN transformed into a blue-water navy. It has conducted counter-piracy patrols in the Gulf of Aden since 2008, opened its first overseas support base in Djibouti in 2017, and commissioned three aircraft carriers: Liaoning (2012, a refitted Soviet hull), Shandong (2019, the first built in China), and Fujian (launched 2022), the last fitted with electromagnetic catapults.

The navy also includes the PLA Naval Air Force, the PLA Navy Marine Corps, and the PLA Navy Submarine Force, operating ballistic missile submarines (Type 094) as the sea-based leg of China's nuclear deterrent, alongside nuclear attack submarines.

Equipment

Major surface combatants include Type 055 large destroyers (classified by NATO as cruisers), Type 052D destroyers, Type 054A frigates, and large amphibious ships including Type 075 amphibious assault ships. Chinese shipbuilding produces warships at a pace unmatched elsewhere, and assessments generally note continuing gaps relative to the US Navy in areas such as carrier aviation, underway replenishment experience, anti-submarine warfare and nuclear submarine quieting, alongside rapid progress in missiles and sensors.
