Shaba II

Shaba II was a brief conflict fought in the Shaba (Katanga) province of Zaire in May and June 1978. Fighters of the Congolese National Liberation Front (FNLC) — Katangese gendarmes exiled in Angola since the 1960s — invaded Shaba from Angola, advancing on the mining town of Kolwezi. The invasion followed the similar but smaller Shaba I incursion of 1977.

Course of the conflict

On 13 May 1978 several thousand FNLC fighters seized Kolwezi, a vital centre of Zaire's copper and cobalt mining, taking thousands of Congolese and several thousand European expatriates as effective hostages. Mobutu Sese Seko's Zairian armed forces proved ineffective, and the regime appealed for foreign help.

France and Belgium intervened: on 19 May, paratroopers of the French Foreign Legion's 2e REP jumped into Kolwezi in Operation Léopard, rescuing hostages and retaking the town with Belgian paracommandos, supported logistically by the United States. Hundreds of hostages and civilians died, including Europeans killed by the FNLC, and hundreds of FNLC fighters were killed. The invaders withdrew toward Angola, and an Inter-African Force subsequently garrisoned Shaba.

Aftermath

As after Shaba I, the crisis exposed the weakness and unpopularity of the Mobutu regime, which survived only through Western backing motivated by Cold War politics and access to strategic minerals. Diplomatic pressure produced a settlement between Zaire and Angola: Mobutu pledged to end support for Angolan rebels, while Angola agreed to restrain the Katangese exiles.
