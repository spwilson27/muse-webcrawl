Submandibular gland

The submandibular gland (historically the submaxillary gland) is one of the three major pairs of salivary glands, located beneath the lower jaw (mandible) on either side of the neck. Roughly the size of a walnut, it produces about 65–70% of the saliva in the unstimulated state, secreting a mixed serous and mucous fluid.

Structure

The gland wraps around the posterior border of the mylohyoid muscle, with superficial and deep parts. Its duct, Wharton's duct (the submandibular duct), runs forward to open at the sublingual caruncle beside the frenulum of the tongue. The gland receives secretomotor parasympathetic fibres from the facial nerve (cranial nerve VII) via the chorda tympani and the submandibular ganglion.

Clinical significance

The submandibular gland is the most common site for salivary stones (sialolithiasis), owing to its viscous, alkaline secretion and the upward course of its duct; stones can cause painful swelling at mealtimes. The gland may also be affected by infection, autoimmune disease such as Sjögren's syndrome, and tumours.
