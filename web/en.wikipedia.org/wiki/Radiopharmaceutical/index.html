Radiopharmaceutical

Radiopharmaceuticals, or medicinal radiocompounds, are a group of pharmaceutical drugs containing radioactive isotopes. Radiopharmaceuticals can be used as both diagnostic and therapeutic agents: a radionuclide is attached to a molecule that carries it to a target organ, tissue, or cell type, where its emissions can be imaged or can destroy diseased tissue.

Diagnostic use

In nuclear medicine imaging, gamma-emitting tracers such as technetium-99m compounds are used with gamma cameras and SPECT, while positron emitters such as fluorine-18 (in fluorodeoxyglucose, FDG) are used in PET scanning to map metabolism, blood flow, and receptor distribution.

Therapeutic use

Therapeutic radiopharmaceuticals deliver cytotoxic radiation to tumours, for example iodine-131 for thyroid cancer and hyperthyroidism, lutetium-177 DOTATATE for neuroendocrine tumours, lutetium-177 PSMA for prostate cancer, and radium-223 for bone metastases. The pairing of diagnostic and therapeutic agents against the same target is known as theranostics.

Production and regulation

Because most medical radionuclides have short half-lives, radiopharmaceuticals are produced in reactors or cyclotrons close to the point of use and are prepared in specialist radiopharmacies under pharmaceutical quality rules adapted to radioactive materials.
