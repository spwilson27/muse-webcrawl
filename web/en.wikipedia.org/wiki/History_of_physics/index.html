History of physics

The history of physics traces the effort to understand matter, motion, energy and the cosmos, from natural philosophy in the ancient world — Greek atomism, Aristotle's physics, Archimedes' statics, and advances in India, China and the Islamic world (Ibn al-Haytham's optics) — through the Scientific Revolution to modern physics.

Milestones: Copernican astronomy and Galileo's mechanics; Newton's laws and universal gravitation (1687) unifying heaven and Earth; 19th-century thermodynamics, Maxwell's electromagnetism and statistical mechanics; then the twin revolutions of relativity (Einstein, 1905/1915) and quantum mechanics (Planck to Heisenberg, Schrödinger, Dirac) that recast space, time and causality; the nuclear and particle era (Standard Model, Higgs boson 2012) and cosmology (expanding universe, cosmic microwave background 1965, dark matter and dark energy problems) — with open questions at every scale today, from quantum gravity to the arrow of time.
