Vevey is a town in the canton of Vaud in Switzerland, on the northern shore of Lake Geneva, near the lake's eastern end below the Lavaux vineyards. It is the seat of the food giant Nestlé, founded there by Henri Nestlé in 1867, and one of the principal towns of the Vaud Riviera.

History

The site was settled in Roman times (as Viviscus) and grew as a market town under Savoy and then Bernese rule. In the 19th century Vevey became a fashionable resort for European aristocracy and writers, drawn by the mild climate and lake scenery; it was a noted place of exile and residence for Russian and English notables. Nestlé's infant-cereal and condensed-milk enterprises, and the Cailler chocolate factory at nearby Broc, made the region a centre of the Swiss food industry.

Culture

Vevey hosts the Fête des Vignerons (Winegrowers' Festival), a vast open-air pageant organized by the Confrérie des Vignerons only about four or five times a century — most recently in 2019 — and inscribed on UNESCO's list of Intangible Cultural Heritage. A giant stainless-steel fork, the Fourchette, stands planted in the lake off the promenade, marking the Alimentarium food museum. Charlie Chaplin spent his last 25 years at the Manoir de Ban in nearby Corsier-sur-Vevey and is buried in the town; his home is now the Chaplin's World museum. The weekly market on the Grande Place is among the largest in the region.
