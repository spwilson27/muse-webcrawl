Phonation

Phonation
FieldPhonetics, speech science
SiteLarynx (vocal folds)
TypesVoiceless, breathy, modal, creaky (and others)
CreatesVoicing — the sound source for speech and singing

Phonation is the process by which the vocal folds in the larynx produce the quasi-periodic sound that serves as the source for voiced speech and singing. As air from the lungs passes between the adducted vocal folds, aerodynamic and elastic forces set them into sustained oscillation — the myoelastic-aerodynamic theory of phonation — generating a buzzing source sound rich in harmonics, which is then filtered by the resonances of the vocal tract (source–filter theory). The rate of vibration determines the perceived pitch of the voice.

The vocal folds during one cycle of vibration.

Phonation types

Phoneticians distinguish several states of the glottis along a continuum of vocal fold tension, aperture, and airflow, notably described by Peter Ladefoged:

  • Voiceless — folds held apart; no vibration (as in [s] or [f]).
  • Breathy voice — folds vibrate but remain partly open, letting turbulent air escape; used contrastively in languages such as Gujarati and Jalapa Mazatec.
  • Modal voice — the ordinary, efficient vibration used for vowels in most languages.
  • Creaky voice (vocal fry) — folds compressed and vibrating slowly and irregularly; contrastive in some languages and a stylistic feature in others.
  • Glottal closure — folds fully shut, as in a glottal stop.

Beyond these, phonation types include harsh and whispery voice, and falsetto — a register in which only the thin edges of the folds vibrate.

Physiology and control

Pitch is controlled mainly by the cricothyroid muscles, which lengthen and tense the folds, and loudness by subglottal air pressure and the pattern of glottal closure. Disorders of phonation include laryngitis, vocal fold nodules and polyps, and spasmodic dysphonia; assessment uses techniques such as laryngoscopy and stroboscopy. Phonation is studied in phonetics, speech-language pathology, singing pedagogy, and speech technology.
