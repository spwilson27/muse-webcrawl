The Cuba libre (Spanish pronunciation: [ˈkuβa ˈliβɾe], "Free Cuba") is a highball cocktail consisting of cola, rum and in many recipes lime juice, served on ice. Traditionally the cola is Coca-Cola and the rum a light rum such as Bacardi; many variations exist. It is an IBA official cocktail.

History

The drink is said to have been invented in Havana around 1900, shortly after the Spanish–American War, when American soldiers mixed Coca-Cola — newly imported to Cuba — with Cuban rum and toasted "¡Por Cuba libre!" ("To a free Cuba!"), the rallying cry of Cuban independence. One popular account, promoted by Bacardi, credits a US Signal Corps officer, Captain Russell, with ordering rum and Coke with lime at a Havana bar in 1900. Early recipes also included gin and bitters in some published versions.

Preparation

The IBA recipe calls for 50 ml white rum and 120 ml cola built in a highball glass over ice, with 10 ml fresh lime juice, garnished with a lime wedge. In Spain the drink is commonly served with a lemon slice and called a cubata; variants using aged or spiced rum, or diet cola, are widespread. In Cuba itself, where imported colas are scarce, locally produced tuKola is often substituted.
