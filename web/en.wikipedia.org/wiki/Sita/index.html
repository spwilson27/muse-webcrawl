Sita (Sanskrit: सीता, Sītā), also called Janaki and Maithili, is a Hindu goddess and the female protagonist of the Ramayana. She is the consort of Rama, the avatar of the god Vishnu, and is herself regarded as an avatar of Lakshmi, goddess of wealth and fortune. Sita is celebrated for her dedication, self-sacrifice, courage, and purity, and is worshipped as a central figure in the Rama tradition.

Life in the Ramayana

Sita was discovered by King Janaka of Mithila as an infant in a furrow while ploughing a field — hence her name, from the Sanskrit word for "furrow" — and raised as his daughter. She married Rama after he broke the great bow of Shiva at her svayamvara. When Rama was exiled for fourteen years, Sita insisted on accompanying him to the forest with Lakshmana. There the demon-king Ravana, tricking her through the golden deer and the disguised ascetic, abducted her to Lanka, where she was held in the Ashoka grove and refused all of Ravana's advances. After Ravana's defeat, Sita proved her purity in a trial by fire (agnipariksha). In the later Uttara Kanda, public gossip led Rama to banish the pregnant Sita to the hermitage of Valmiki, where she raised her twin sons Lava and Kusha; when called to prove herself again, she invoked her mother the Earth, which opened and received her.

Worship and legacy

Sita is worshipped with Rama in temples across India and Nepal; Janakpur in Nepal and Sitamarhi in Bihar claim her birthplace. Her birthday is celebrated as Sita Navami. She is a central figure in the many regional retellings of the Ramayana — including the Jain, Thai (Ramkien), and Cambodian (Reamker) versions — and her story has been read both as an ideal of devotion and, in modern feminist retellings, as a narrative of unjust suffering and quiet resistance.
