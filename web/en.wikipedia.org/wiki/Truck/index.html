Truck

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A modern articulated semi-truck on a highway at sunset]
A long-haul tractor-trailer.

A truck (lorry in British English) is a motor vehicle designed to transport cargo or carry specialised payloads. Trucks range from pickup trucks and vans through rigid \'box trucks\' to articulated tractor-trailers — the semi-trucks that haul most long-distance road freight. In the United States, trucks move roughly 70% of freight by weight; in Europe lorries carry a similar dominant share of inland cargo.

Types

Light trucks include pickups (the best-selling vehicles in the US, e.g. the Ford F-Series) and vans. Medium and heavy trucks serve construction (dump trucks, mixers), distribution (box trucks), and long-haul (tractor units with semi-trailers up to 16.5 m in the EU, longer in the US and Australia\'s road trains). Specialised types include fire engines, tankers, car transporters and refuse trucks.

Technology

Heavy trucks are overwhelmingly diesel, prized for torque and durability; driver hours are regulated by tachographs and electronic logging. Battery-electric trucks (Tesla Semi, eActros) serve regional routes from the early 2020s, and hydrogen and autonomous trucking are in trials, while platooning experiments link convoys electronically.

History

Steam wagons preceded Gottlieb Daimler\'s first motor truck of 1896. Diesel engines, pneumatic tyres and the US Interstate Highway System built the modern industry; manufacturers include Daimler Truck, Volvo, Paccar, Scania, MAN and China\'s FAW and Dongfeng by volume.
