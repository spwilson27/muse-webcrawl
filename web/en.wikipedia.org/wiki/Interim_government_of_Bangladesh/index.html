The interim government of Bangladesh was formed on 8 August 2024, following the resignation and departure of Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina amid the July Revolution, a mass student-led uprising. Nobel laureate Muhammad Yunus was appointed Chief Adviser, heading a council of advisers that functions as the cabinet.

Members of the interim government were sworn in at Bangabhaban on 8 August 2024.

Background

Protests began in June–July 2024 over the reinstatement of a quota system for government jobs, escalating into a nationwide movement after a violent crackdown in which hundreds were killed. On 5 August 2024, Sheikh Hasina resigned and fled to India. Army chief Waker-Uz-Zaman announced an interim government would be formed after consultations with political parties and student leaders.

Formation

Student leaders proposed Muhammad Yunus, founder of Grameen Bank and Nobel Peace Prize laureate, as head of the interim administration. Yunus returned from Paris and was sworn in on 8 August 2024 alongside advisers drawn from civil society, academia, the military, and the student movement, including Nahid Islam and Asif Mahmud, coordinators of the Anti-discrimination Students Movement.

Policies and reforms

The government established a series of reform commissions covering the constitution, electoral system, police, judiciary, public administration, and anti-corruption, and pledged to hold free and fair elections after implementing reforms. It also ordered investigations and trials for the July killings, including through the International Crimes Tribunal, and pursued economic stabilization measures with the IMF and development partners.
