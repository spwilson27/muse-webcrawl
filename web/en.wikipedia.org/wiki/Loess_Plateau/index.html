Loess Plateau

Area: ~640,000 km2

Location: North-central China

Elevation: roughly 800–3,000 m

The Loess Plateau, also known as the Huangtu Plateau, is a plateau in north-central China covering about 640,000 square kilometres across the provinces of Shanxi, Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, and Inner Mongolia. It is named for the thick deposits of loess — fine, wind-blown silt — that mantle the region, reaching depths of 50 to 200 metres and more in places.

Formation and geography

The loess was carried by winds from the deserts of Central Asia and the Gobi over hundreds of thousands of years and deposited across the plateau. The Yellow River flows through the plateau and picks up an enormous load of the easily eroded silt — historically around 1.6 billion tonnes of sediment per year — which gave the river, and the Yellow Sea, their names and colours, and built up the North China Plain downstream. The plateau's soft sediment has been carved by erosion into a dramatic landscape of ridges, gullies, and flat-topped mesas.

History and erosion control

The Loess Plateau was a cradle of early Chinese civilisation; its fertile soil supported farming for millennia, and residents famously dug cave dwellings (yaodong) into the loess cliffs. Overgrazing and deforestation over centuries severely accelerated erosion, making the region one of the most eroded on Earth and contributing to destructive flooding of the Yellow River. Since the 1990s, large-scale restoration projects — including terracing, check dams, tree planting, and the Grain for Green programme — have significantly reduced sediment flow and re-greened large areas, though at notable social and hydrological cost.
