Guitar amplifier

A guitar amplifier (or amp) is an electronic device or system that strengthens the electrical signal from a pickup on an electric guitar, bass guitar, or acoustic guitar so that it can produce sound through one or more loudspeakers. Guitar amplifiers can also be used with acoustic guitars fitted with piezoelectric pickups.

Most guitar amplifiers can modify the instrument's tone by emphasising or de-emphasising certain frequencies using equalizer controls, and by adding electronic effects; distortion and overdrive are often considered integral features, especially in blues and rock music. Amplifiers may use vacuum tubes (valves), solid-state transistors, digital modelling technology, or a hybrid of these.

Amplifiers are produced in two main forms: the combo amplifier, containing amplifier and speakers in a single cabinet, and the head and speaker cabinet configuration, in which the amplifier sits atop one or more separate speaker cabinets. Sizes range from small practice amplifiers of a few watts to 100-watt (or more) stacks used for large venues.

History

The first electrical string instrument amplifiers were introduced by Rickenbacker in the early 1930s. Mainstream adoption followed after World War II, with makers such as Fender, Gibson, Vox and Marshall shaping the sound of popular music.
