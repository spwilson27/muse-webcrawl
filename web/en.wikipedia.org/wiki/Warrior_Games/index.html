Warrior Games

The Warrior Games is an annual multi-sport event for wounded, injured and ill service members and veterans, organised by the United States Department of Defense. Established in 2010, the Games are intended to promote recovery and rehabilitation through adaptive sports, and to foster camaraderie among participants from the U.S. Army, Marine Corps, Navy, Air Force, Space Force, Coast Guard and Special Operations Command, as well as allied nations.

History

The inaugural Warrior Games were held in May 2010 at the U.S. Olympic Training Center in Colorado Springs, Colorado, organised by the U.S. Olympic Committee in partnership with the Department of Defense, with about 200 athletes. The event was held in Colorado Springs for its first several editions before rotating among military installations and host cities, including the U.S. Naval Academy, Quantico, West Point, Chicago (2017), the U.S. Air Force Academy, Tampa (2019), San Antonio and Orlando. The United Kingdom's participation in the Games helped inspire Prince Harry to found the Invictus Games, first held in London in 2014.

Competition

Athletes compete in teams representing their service branch in adaptive sports including archery, cycling, precision air sports (shooting), sitting volleyball, swimming, track and field, wheelchair basketball, wheelchair rugby, powerlifting and indoor rowing, with classification categories reflecting competitors' injuries and impairments. Medals and the Chairman's Cup, awarded to the top-performing service team, are presented at the closing ceremony. The Games form part of the military's broader warrior care and adaptive reconditioning programmes.
