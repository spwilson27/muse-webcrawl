Congregation for the Causes of Saints

The Congregation for the Causes of Saints, since 2022 styled the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints, is the dicastery of the Roman Curia responsible for overseeing the complex process that leads to the canonization of saints by the Catholic Church. It investigates candidates' lives and alleged miracles, and prepares the cases on which the pope decides beatification and canonisation.

The dicastery supervises canonisation causes examined in Vatican City.

History

The congregation traces its origins to the Sacred Congregation of Rites, erected by Pope Sixtus V in 1588, which handled both liturgy and saints' causes. In 1969 Pope Paul VI divided that congregation, creating a separate Congregation for the Causes of Saints. Pope Francis's apostolic constitution Praedicate evangelium (2022) renamed the body a dicastery, as it did for the other congregations of the Curia.

Process

A cause normally begins in the diocese where the candidate lived or died, which gathers testimony and writings. The dicastery then supervises the preparation of the positio, a dossier on the candidate's heroic virtue or martyrdom, examined by historians and theologians and voted on by the dicastery's cardinal and bishop members. Verified miracles — typically medically inexplicable healings scrutinised by medical experts — are required for beatification (except for martyrs) and again for canonisation. Historically, the Promoter of the Faith, popularly the "devil's advocate", argued against the cause; the role was reformed under John Paul II in 1983.
