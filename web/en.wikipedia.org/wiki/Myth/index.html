Myth

A myth is a traditional story, especially one concerning the early history of a people or explaining some natural or social phenomenon, typically involving supernatural beings or events. In scholarly usage within folklore studies, a myth is a sacred narrative usually explaining how the world or humanity came to be in its present form. In popular usage, "myth" often simply means a widely held but false belief — a sense academics generally avoid.

Definitions and terminology

Folklorists classify traditional prose narratives into myths (sacred, set in the remote past, with gods or culture heroes as protagonists, and regarded as true by the societies telling them), legends (regarded as true but set in the human historical world), and folktales (acknowledged fiction told for entertainment). A body of myths belonging to one culture forms its mythology. The word derives from the Greek mythos (μῦθος), "speech, story".

Types

  • Creation myths — how the world and its inhabitants began.
  • Etiological myths — explaining the origin of a custom, name, or natural feature.
  • Eschatological myths — prophecies of the end of the world, such as Ragnarök.
  • Hero myths — the birth, deeds, and death of culture heroes and founders.
  • Nature myths — explanations of seasons, celestial bodies, and natural phenomena.

Theories of myth

Interpretations of myth have varied widely. Euhemerism held that myths are distorted accounts of real historical events. Nineteenth-century comparative mythology (Max Müller) treated myths as remnants of nature worship in language. Bronisław Malinowski argued that myths function as social "charters" validating customs and institutions; Claude Lévi-Strauss analysed myths structurally, as systems of oppositions mediating cultural contradictions; Joseph Campbell popularized the idea of the monomyth or hero's journey; Carl Jung and others read myths as expressions of archetypes of the collective unconscious.

Myth and the modern world

Roland Barthes, in Mythologies (1957), extended the term to modern mass culture, treating "myth" as a system of communication that naturalizes ideological meanings. Myths continue to be created and retold in literature, film, and political discourse, and classical and world mythologies remain foundational source material for the arts.
