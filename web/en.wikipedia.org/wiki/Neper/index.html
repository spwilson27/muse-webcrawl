The neper (symbol: Np) is a logarithmic unit for ratios of measurements of physical field and power quantities, such as gain and loss of electronic signals. It is named after John Napier, the inventor of logarithms. The neper is based on the natural (Napierian) logarithm of the ratio, whereas the more common decibel uses the base-10 logarithm.

Definition

Like the decibel, the neper is a unit of level. For a field quantity ratio F/F0, the level in nepers is L = ln(F/F0) Np. For power quantities, whose level is half that of the corresponding field ratio, L = ½ ln(P/P0) Np. One neper equals 20/ln 10 decibels, approximately 8.686 dB. The neper is a dimensionless unit accepted for use with the SI.

Applications

The neper is used in telecommunications and signal processing, particularly in Europe and in theoretical work, to express attenuation and gain. The complex propagation constant of a transmission line is often written with attenuation in nepers per unit length and phase in radians per unit length, a natural pairing because the neper relates directly to the real part of a complex logarithm.
