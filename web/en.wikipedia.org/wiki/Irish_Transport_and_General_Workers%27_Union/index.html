The Irish Transport and General Workers' Union (ITGWU) was a trade union representing workers in Ireland, founded in 1909 by the syndicalist leader James Larkin. It grew out of Larkin's break with the British-based National Union of Dock Labourers and became the largest and most militant union in Irish history.

History

Larkin founded the ITGWU in Dublin in January 1909 after disputes with the leadership of the NUDL. The union organised unskilled and semi-skilled workers — dockers, carters, factory hands — whom the craft unions had largely ignored. Its headquarters at Liberty Hall in Dublin became a centre of Irish labour activity.

The union's defining moment came in the Dublin Lock-out of 1913, when employers led by William Martin Murphy attempted to break the union by locking out some 20,000 workers. Although the lock-out ended in defeat for the workers in early 1914, the ITGWU survived and rebuilt under leaders including James Connolly, who served as acting general secretary during Larkin's absence in America and was executed after the Easter Rising of 1916.

Later years

Through the twentieth century the ITGWU remained Ireland's largest union, organising workers across transport, manufacturing and the public sector. In 1990 it merged with the Federated Workers' Union of Ireland to form SIPTU (Services, Industrial, Professional and Technical Union).
