Playa del Carmen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Playa del Carmen is a resort city located along the Caribbean Sea in the state of Quintana Roo, Mexico. It is part of the municipality of Solidaridad and lies on the Riviera Maya, roughly 70 kilometres (43 mi) south of Cancún. Long a small fishing town and ferry point for the island of Cozumel, it developed from the 1990s into one of Mexico's fastest-growing tourist destinations, with a population of over 300,000 at the 2020 census.

History

In pre-Hispanic times the area, known to the Maya as Xaman-Ha ("northern waters"), served as a departure point for pilgrims travelling by canoe to the shrine of the goddess Ixchel on Cozumel. The modern town grew slowly through the twentieth century around fishing and the chicle trade. Tourism took off after the development of Cancún and the opening of an airstrip and ferry dock; the pedestrianised Quinta Avenida (Fifth Avenue), running parallel to the beach, became the centre of hotels, restaurants and nightlife.

Tourism and attractions

Playa del Carmen is a base for exploring the Riviera Maya, including the eco-parks Xcaret and Xplor, the cenotes (natural sinkholes) of the Yucatán limestone, and the Maya ruins of Tulum and Cobá. Passenger ferries serve Cozumel, a major cruise-ship and scuba-diving destination. The surrounding reefs form part of the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef System, the second-largest barrier reef in the world.

Climate

The city has a tropical savanna climate with warm temperatures year-round, a wetter season from June to October, and exposure to Atlantic hurricanes — Hurricane Wilma caused severe damage in October 2005.
