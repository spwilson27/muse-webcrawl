Pelagonia

Pelagonia is a geographical region of Macedonia, named after the ancient kingdom of Pelagonia. Today the name refers chiefly to the broad, fertile Pelagonian plain in the south-west of North Macedonia, shared with northern Greece, which contains the cities of Bitola and Prilep on the Macedonian side and Florina on the Greek side.

History

In antiquity Pelagonia was a region of Upper Macedonia inhabited by the Pelagones, bordering Illyria. It was absorbed into the Macedonian kingdom under Philip II. The plain was the site of the Battle of Pelagonia in 1259, a decisive victory of the Empire of Nicaea over the Principality of Achaea and its allies, which shaped the balance of power in the medieval Balkans and paved the way for the Byzantine recovery of Constantinople.

Geography

The plain is drained by the Crna River and is one of North Macedonia's most important agricultural areas, known for tobacco, wheat, and other crops. The Pelagonia statistical region of North Macedonia takes its name from it.
