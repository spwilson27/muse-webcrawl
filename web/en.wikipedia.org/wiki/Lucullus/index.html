Lucullus

Lucius Licinius Lucullus (118 – 57/56 BC) was a Roman general and statesman, consul in 74 BC, who commanded Roman forces in the Third Mithridatic War against Mithridates VI of Pontus. In retirement he became famous for his wealth, gardens and extravagant banquets — the origin of the word "Lucullan".

Career

Lucullus served under Sulla in the Social War and the First Mithridatic War, and as quaestor and proquaestor in the East he raised fleets and revenues for Sulla's campaigns. As consul in 74 BC he obtained command against Mithridates, whom he drove from his kingdom by victories such as the Battle of Cyzicus and the Battle of Tigranocerta (69 BC) against Tigranes II of Armenia.

His reforms of the crushing Roman tax-farming and debt regime in Asia made him unpopular at Rome, and discontent among his troops — stirred by his strict discipline and the long campaigns — led to mutinies. He was progressively stripped of his command, which passed to Pompey in 66 BC.

Retirement

Lucullus retired to lavish estates at Tusculum, Naples and Rome, building magnificent gardens and a celebrated library open to scholars. He is credited with introducing the sweet cherry and the apricot to Italy. Plutarch wrote his biography, pairing him with the Athenian Cimon. The adjective "Lucullan" still denotes sumptuous, luxurious feasting.
