Kokopelli

RegionSouthwestern United States
PeoplesHopi, Zuni, Ancestral Puebloans
DomainsFertility, agriculture, music, trickery

Kokopelli is a fertility deity, usually depicted as a humpbacked flute player (often with feathers or antennae-like protrusions on his head), venerated by some Native American cultures of the Southwestern United States. Like most fertility deities, Kokopelli presides over both childbirth and agriculture, and he is also a trickster god and represents the spirit of music.

Depictions and history

Images of humpbacked flute players appear in rock art across the Southwest in petroglyphs and pictographs dating from roughly 750 to 850 CE among the Hohokam and later Ancestral Puebloan cultures. Spanish missionaries, disapproving of the figure's phallic aspects, sometimes called him by other names or suppressed his imagery. In Hopi tradition, Kokopelli (Kookopölö) is one of the kachinas, and in Zuni tradition he is associated with rain and fertility.

Modern popularity

Since the late 20th century, Kokopelli has become a widespread commercial symbol of the American Southwest, appearing on jewellery, textiles, signage and tourist goods — often in a sanitised form that omits the figure's traditional phallic attributes.
