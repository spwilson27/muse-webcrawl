Mississippi Bubble

The Mississippi Bubble was an economic bubble and financial crisis in France in 1719–1720, centred on the Mississippi Company of the Scottish economist and financier John Law. It is one of the earliest documented speculative bubbles, roughly contemporary with Britain's South Sea Bubble.

Background

Appointed controller-general of finances, Law founded the Banque Générale in 1716 and acquired the Mississippi Company, which held a monopoly on trade with French Louisiana. In his "System", the bank's notes and the company's shares were to absorb France's enormous national debt: investors exchanged government debt for shares, whose price Law supported with banknote issuance.

Collapse

Inflated by speculation and stories of colonial riches, share prices soared through 1719, making fortunes and drawing crowds to the rue Quincampoix in Paris. When confidence faltered in 1720 and shareholders tried to convert shares and notes into coin, the system collapsed; the bank failed, Law fled France, and the episode left a lasting French distrust of banks and paper money.
