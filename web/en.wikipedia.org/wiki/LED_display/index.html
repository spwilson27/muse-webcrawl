LED display

LED display
TechnologyLight-emitting diodes as pixels
ApplicationsSignage, billboards, stadium screens, television backlights
VariantsDirect-view LED, LED-backlit LCD, OLED, microLED

An LED display is a flat-panel display that uses an array of light-emitting diodes as pixels for a video or information display. Their brightness makes them suitable for outdoor signs, billboards and stadium screens, while finer-pitch panels are used indoors.

Types

Direct-view LED displays form each pixel from red, green and blue (sometimes additional) discrete LEDs; pixel pitch ranges from tens of millimetres for billboards down to under a millimetre for indoor "fine pitch" walls. LED-backlit LCDs (marketed as "LED TVs") use white LEDs to illuminate an LCD panel rather than forming the image directly. Emissive variants include OLED displays, which use organic LEDs per pixel, and emerging microLED technology.

History and use

Simple LED matrix signs appeared in the 1970s–80s following practical red LEDs (Nick Holonyak Jr., 1962); the efficient blue LED developed by Shuji Nakamura in the 1990s made full-colour and white-light displays possible. Large LED screens became fixtures of sports venues, concerts, advertising (e.g., Times Square) and control rooms.
