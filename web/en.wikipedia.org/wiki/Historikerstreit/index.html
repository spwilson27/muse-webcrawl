Historikerstreit

The Historikerstreit ("historians' dispute") was a public controversy in West Germany in 1986–87 among historians and philosophers about how Nazi Germany and the Holocaust should be understood and compared with other atrocities, and about the place of the Nazi past in German national identity. Conducted largely in newspaper feuilletons, especially the Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung and Die Zeit, it became a defining episode in Germany's culture of memory (Vergangenheitsbewältigung).

The dispute

The controversy was ignited by an article by the historian Ernst Nolte in June 1986, "The Past That Will Not Pass," which argued that Nazi crimes should be seen partly as a fearful reaction to Bolshevik "Asiatic" violence — the Gulag and the murders of the Russian Revolution being, in his framing, the logical and factual precedent of Auschwitz. A related work by Andreas Hillgruber, Zweierlei Untergang (1986), invited empathy with the German army fighting on the Eastern Front.

The philosopher Jürgen Habermas responded in July 1986 with "A Kind of Settlement of Damages," accusing Nolte, Hillgruber, and others of apologetic revisionism aimed at normalising German history and relativising the singularity of the Holocaust. Critics of Habermas in turn accused him of misquotation and of policing historical scholarship for political ends.

Outcome and legacy

The consensus of mainstream German historiography reaffirmed the uniqueness of the Holocaust and rejected Nolte's causal framing. The debate is widely seen as a milestone that consolidated a memory culture centred on German responsibility, and it has been revisited in the "second Historikerstreit" of the 2020s concerning colonialism, memory, and comparison.
