Mountaineering, mountain climbing or alpinism is a set of outdoor activities that involves ascending mountains — combining hiking, scrambling, rock and ice climbing, and (at altitude) glacier travel and expedition logistics, with summits as the goal.

The sport crystallised in the Alps in the 19th century (the 'golden age' ending with the Matterhorn, 1865), then spread to the greater ranges: the Himalaya and Karakoram, where the 14 eight-thousanders became the ultimate objectives — all climbed by 1964 — and new styles (alpine-style fast ascents, free soloing, ski descents) keep redefining limits. Mountaineering's hazards are objective (avalanches, rockfall, weather, altitude sickness) as well as technical, and its ethics — fixed ropes, oxygen, commercial expeditions — are perennially debated.
