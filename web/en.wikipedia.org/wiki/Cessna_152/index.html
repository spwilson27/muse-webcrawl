The Cessna 152 is an American two-seat, fixed-tricycle-gear, general aviation airplane, used primarily for flight training and personal use. Introduced in 1977 as a development of the long-running Cessna 150, it is a high-wing, single-engine aircraft powered by a 110 hp Lycoming O-235 engine, which replaced the 150's Continental engine and allowed the use of 100LL avgas.

Design and use

The 152 differed from the 150 in its engine, a slightly wider cabin, revised landing gear fairings, and a 28-volt electrical system on later models. An aerobatic version, the Aerobat (A152), was strengthened for limited aerobatic manoeuvres and featured removable doors, skylights and seat harnesses. Production ended in 1985 when Cessna suspended its piston single-engine line; by then 7,584 model 152s had been built in the United States, with additional aircraft assembled by Reims Aviation in France as the F152. For decades the 152 has been one of the world's most widely used primary trainers, valued for its docile handling, low operating costs and robust construction.
