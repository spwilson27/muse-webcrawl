Metacentric height

The metacentric height (GM) is a measurement of the initial static stability of a floating body. It is calculated as the distance between the centre of gravity (G) of a ship and its metacentre (M). A larger metacentric height implies greater initial stability against overturning.

Concept

When a vessel heels, its centre of buoyancy shifts. The metacentre is the point about which a floating body starts to oscillate when inclined at small angles; it is found by extending the line of buoyant force of the heeled vessel until it intersects the ship's centreline. If the metacentre lies above the centre of gravity, the resulting righting moment restores the vessel to upright. If GM is negative, the vessel is unstable and will capsize.

Effects on ships

A ship with an excessively large GM is "stiff" and rolls quickly and uncomfortably, stressing cargo and crew; a ship with a small GM is "tender" and rolls slowly but risks capsizing. Naval architects and loading officers therefore target an appropriate GM range, and GM is measured in practice by an inclining test.
