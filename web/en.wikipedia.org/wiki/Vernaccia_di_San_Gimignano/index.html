Vernaccia di San Gimignano

Vernaccia di San Gimignano is a white Italian wine made from the Vernaccia grape around the hill town of San Gimignano in Tuscany. In 1966 it was the first Italian wine to receive DOC status, and it was elevated to DOCG in 1993 — the first Tuscan white to achieve the higher classification.

Characteristics

The wine is dry, pale straw-yellow, with crisp acidity, citrus and floral notes and a characteristic slightly bitter almond finish. DOCG rules require at least 85% Vernaccia grapes grown within the San Gimignano commune. A Riserva version is aged longer before release.

History

Vernaccia has been grown in the area since at least the 13th century; it was praised in Renaissance times and mentioned by Michelangelo, who wrote that it "kisses, licks, bites, pinches and stings". The grape is unrelated to other Italian varieties also called Vernaccia.
