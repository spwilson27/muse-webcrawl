Hypergeometric differential equation

In mathematics, the hypergeometric differential equation is a second-order linear ordinary differential equation whose solutions are given by the hypergeometric series. It is a Fuchsian equation with three regular singular points at 0, 1 and ∞, and it occupies a central place in the theory of special functions because many classical equations — including those of Legendre, Bessel (in limiting form), Chebyshev and Jacobi — can be reduced to it.

Solutions of the hypergeometric equation include many classical special functions.

The equation

The standard form, associated with Gauss, is z(1 − z) w″ + [c − (a + b + 1)z] w′ − ab w = 0, with complex parameters a, b and c. A solution regular at z = 0 is given by Gauss's hypergeometric series 2F1(a, b; c; z), which converges for |z| < 1 and can be analytically continued to the cut plane.

Solutions and transformations

Kummer found 24 solutions of the equation (the Kummer solutions), connected by linear relations, and the transformation formulas of Euler and Pfaff relate different representations of 2F1. The monodromy of the equation and the Schwarz list classify the parameter values for which the solutions are algebraic functions. Riemann's approach characterises the equation entirely by its singular points and exponents, encoded in the Riemann P-symbol.

Generalizations

Generalized hypergeometric series pFq, the confluent hypergeometric equation (with an irregular singularity formed by merging two regular ones), and the Heun equation (with four regular singular points) extend the theory. The hypergeometric equation also has a q-analogue and appears in modern contexts such as mirror symmetry and the periods of Calabi–Yau manifolds.
