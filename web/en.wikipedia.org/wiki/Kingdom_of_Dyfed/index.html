Kingdom of Dyfed

The Kingdom of Dyfed, one of the Welsh kingdoms that emerged in sub-Roman Britain in the 5th century, occupied the area of modern south-west Wales roughly corresponding to present-day Pembrokeshire, Carmarthenshire west of the Tywi, and part of Ceredigion. It had its roots in the lands of the Demetae tribe of Roman Britain, whose name gave rise to Dyfed.

Dyfed's rulers claimed descent from the Irish Déisi, and its early kings appear in Irish and Welsh genealogies. The kingdom survived repeated Norse and Norman pressure longer than many neighbours, partly because of its geography. In the late 10th century under Hywel Dda it was united with other Welsh kingdoms, and after his death in 950 it again became separate. By the early 12th century Dyfed had been absorbed into the Norman conquest of south Wales and became a lordship and later the county of Pembrokeshire.

History

An inscribed stone tradition, bilingual Latin and ogham inscriptions, attests Irish influence. The kingdom's kings included Aeddan ap... in legend and more historically rulers such as Llywarch Hen's contemporaries. Dyfed was annexed by Rhodri the Great's descendants and formed the core, with Seisyllwg, of Deheubarth.
