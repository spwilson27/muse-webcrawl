List of United States district and territorial courts

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The United States district courts are the general trial courts of the U.S. federal judiciary. There are 94 federal judicial districts: 89 within the 50 states, plus districts for the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and the territories of the U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands. Each district includes a U.S. bankruptcy court. Appeals from the district courts go to the regional courts of appeals, organized into circuits.

Single-district states

Twenty states and territories have a single district court for the whole state, including Alaska, Colorado, Delaware, Hawaii, Idaho, Maine, Montana, Nebraska, Nevada, New Hampshire, New Jersey, North Dakota, Rhode Island, South Dakota, Utah and Wyoming, as well as the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands and the U.S. Virgin Islands. The most populous districts include the Central District of California (the largest, covering Los Angeles), the Southern District of New York, and the Southern District of Florida.

American Samoa has no federal district or territorial court; federal matters are generally handled by the District Court for the District of Columbia or the District of Hawaii. The District Court for Puerto Rico, by contrast, is an Article III court whose judges hold life tenure, like those of the state districts.
