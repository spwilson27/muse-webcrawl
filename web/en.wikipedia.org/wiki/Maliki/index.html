The Maliki school (Arabic: مَالِكِيّ, romanized: Mālikī) is one of the four major schools of Islamic jurisprudence (madhhabs) within Sunni Islam. It was founded by Malik ibn Anas (711–795), a scholar of Medina, in the 8th century. The Maliki school is the predominant school in North Africa and West Africa, including Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Mali, Niger and northern Nigeria, and is also followed in parts of the Arabian Peninsula.

Principles

Maliki jurisprudence relies on the Qur'an and the Sunnah (hadith), as in other schools, but is distinctive in also treating the consensus practice of the people of Medina ('amal ahl al-Madina) as an important source of law, on the grounds that Medinan practice preserves the living tradition of the Prophet's community. It also gives weight to the opinions of the Prophet's companions, juristic preference (istihsan), public interest (maslaha) and local custom ('urf).

Key texts

Malik's own Al-Muwatta ("The Well-Trodden Path") is one of the earliest surviving works of Islamic law, combining hadith and legal doctrine. Later foundational Maliki texts include the Mudawwana of Sahnun and the widely studied Mukhtasar of Khalil ibn Ishaq, which became the standard reference of the school in much of Africa.
