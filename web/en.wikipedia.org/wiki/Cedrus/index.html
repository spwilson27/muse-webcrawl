Cedrus

Cedrus
Family: Pinaceae
Common name: true cedars
Native range: Mediterranean to western Himalaya

Cedrus is a genus of coniferous trees in the pine family Pinaceae, known as the true cedars. They are native to mountains of the western Himalaya and the Mediterranean region, growing at high altitudes. Depending on the taxonomic treatment, the genus contains between one and four species, including the cedar of Lebanon, the Atlas cedar, the Cyprus cedar and the deodar.

Description

Cedars are large evergreen trees, up to 40–60 m tall, with spicy-resinous, durable wood, broad spreading canopies, and needles borne in clusters on short shoots. Their barrel-shaped cones disintegrate on the tree when mature.

Use

Cedar wood is prized for its fragrance, resistance to decay, and insect-repellent properties; it was used in ancient Egyptian and Phoenician construction and shipbuilding. The cedar of Lebanon is the emblem of Lebanon and appears on its flag. Cedars are widely planted as ornamental trees in parks and large gardens.
