28S ribosomal RNA
TypeRibosomal RNA
LocationLarge subunit (60S) of eukaryotic ribosomes
Length~4,700–5,000 nucleotides (varies by species)
Homolog23S rRNA in prokaryotes

28S ribosomal RNA is the largest ribosomal RNA component of the large (60S) subunit of the cytoplasmic ribosomes of eukaryotic cells. It is the structural and catalytic core of the subunit: its peptidyl transferase centre forms peptide bonds during protein synthesis, making the molecule a ribozyme.

Synthesis and processing

In most eukaryotes, 28S rRNA is transcribed as part of a large precursor RNA (the 45S pre-rRNA in mammals) by RNA polymerase I in the nucleolus, together with the 18S and 5.8S rRNAs. The precursor is cleaved and chemically modified before assembly into ribosomal subunits.

Use in phylogenetics

Because the 28S gene contains both highly conserved regions and variable expansion segments, its sequence is widely used in molecular systematics to reconstruct evolutionary relationships, particularly among invertebrates.
