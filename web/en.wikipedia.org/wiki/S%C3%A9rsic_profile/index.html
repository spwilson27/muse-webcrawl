Sérsic profile

Proposed byJosé Luis Sérsic (1963)
Used forGalaxy surface brightness profiles

The Sérsic profile (or Sérsic model) is a mathematical function describing how the surface brightness of a galaxy varies with distance from its centre. Proposed by Argentine astronomer José Luis Sérsic in 1963, it generalises earlier laws such as de Vaucouleurs' r1/4 law for elliptical galaxies and the exponential profile of spiral galaxy disks.

Definition

The profile takes the form I(R) = Ie exp{ −bn [ (R/Re)1/n − 1 ] }, where Re is the half-light radius, Ie the intensity at that radius, and n the Sérsic index. Values near n = 1 describe exponential disks; n = 4 recovers the de Vaucouleurs law typical of large ellipticals.

Applications

Sérsic fits are a standard tool in extragalactic astronomy for measuring galaxy sizes, luminosities and structural parameters in large surveys, and for separating bulge and disk components.
