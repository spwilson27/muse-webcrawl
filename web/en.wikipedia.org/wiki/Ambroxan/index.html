Ambroxan, commonly sold under the trade name Ambrox, is a synthetic terpenoid widely used in perfumery as a substitute for ambergris. It is the principal odour-bearing degradation product of ambrein, the main constituent of natural ambergris, and is chemically a tricyclic ether, (−)-ambroxide.

Production

Ambroxan was first synthesised in the 1950s at Firmenich from sclareolide, derived from clary sage. Production later shifted toward routes starting from labdanoid feedstocks and, increasingly, from engineered yeast fermentation of sugars, which avoids dependence on botanical supply.

Uses

Because natural ambergris is rare, expensive, and restricted as a product of the endangered sperm whale, ambroxan serves as the principal ambergris note in modern perfumes. It provides a long-lasting, warm, woody-ambery base note and a fixative effect, and appears in countless fine fragrances, soaps, and detergents. It rose to particular prominence as a near-solo ingredient in the minimalist perfume Molecule 02 by Escentric Molecules.
