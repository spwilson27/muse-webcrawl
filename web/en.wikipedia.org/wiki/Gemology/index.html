Gemology

[Image placeholder: Gemological examination]

Gemology (also spelled gemmology) is the science dealing with natural and artificial gemstone materials. It is a geoscience and a branch of mineralogy. Some jewellers (and many non-jewellers) are academically trained gemologists and are qualified to identify and evaluate gems.

Gemologists identify gemstones by their physical and optical properties, including specific gravity, refractive index, birefringence, pleochroism, absorption spectrum, fluorescence, and inclusions. A central task of modern gemology is distinguishing natural stones from synthetic (laboratory-grown) stones and from treated stones whose colour or clarity has been enhanced.

Instruments

The traditional toolkit includes the 10× loupe, the refractometer, the polariscope, the dichroscope, the spectroscope, the specific-gravity balance, and the microscope. Advanced laboratories also use spectroscopy (UV-Vis-NIR, FTIR, Raman), X-ray fluorescence, and laser ablation mass spectrometry to determine origin and treatment.

Education and institutions

The first gemological laboratory was established in London in the early 20th century; the Gemological Institute of America (GIA), founded in 1931, is the best-known educational and laboratory organisation, issuing widely used grading reports for diamonds and coloured stones. Other major bodies include the Swiss Gemmological Institute (SSEF), Gübelin Gem Lab, and national gemological associations. Diamond grading follows the "four Cs": carat weight, colour, clarity, and cut.
