Wild Weasel

Wild Weasel is a code name given by the United States Air Force to an aircraft, of any type, equipped with anti-radiation missiles and tasked with the suppression of enemy air defences (SEAD): destroying the radar and surface-to-air missile installations of enemy air defence systems. The task is dangerous, since the aircraft must provoke enemy radars into emitting in order to locate and attack them, a role summarised by the unofficial motto "You Gotta Be Shittin' Me".

An F-16CJ carrying HARM anti-radiation missiles on a Wild Weasel mission.

History

The Wild Weasel concept was developed during the Vietnam War in response to the threat posed by Soviet-supplied SA-2 surface-to-air missiles over North Vietnam, which were inflicting heavy losses on American strike aircraft. The first Wild Weasel aircraft, modified F-100 Super Sabres, flew in 1965, followed by F-105 Thunderchief variants and later the F-4 Phantom II. These aircraft carried radar homing and warning receivers to detect missile sites and anti-radiation missiles such as the AGM-45 Shrike and AGM-78 Standard ARM to destroy them.

Modern use

The specialised F-4G Wild Weasel V served until 1996. The role is now performed principally by the F-16CJ/DJ Fighting Falcon equipped with the HARM Targeting System and the AGM-88 HARM missile, and by the US Navy's EA-18G Growler for electronic attack. Wild Weasel units remain identifiable by their "WW" tail code, carried by the 20th Fighter Wing at Shaw Air Force Base and units in the Pacific.
