Florenz Ziegfeld Jr.

Florenz Edward Ziegfeld Jr. (March 21, 1867 – July 22, 1932) was an American Broadway impresario, notable for his series of theatrical revues, the Ziegfeld Follies, staged from 1907 to 1931 and inspired by the Folies Bergère of Paris.

Early life

Born in Chicago, the son of a German immigrant who founded a conservatory of music, Ziegfeld began as a promoter, famously exhibiting the strongman Eugen Sandow at the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition.

The Follies

The Ziegfeld Follies celebrated the 'Ziegfeld Girl' — elaborately costumed chorus girls — and launched or boosted the careers of performers including Fanny Brice, Will Rogers, W. C. Fields, and Eddie Cantor.

Ziegfeld also produced the landmark musical Show Boat (1927) and built the Ziegfeld Theatre in New York. He was married to actress Billie Burke.
