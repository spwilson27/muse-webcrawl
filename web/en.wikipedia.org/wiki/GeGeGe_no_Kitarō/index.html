GeGeGe no Kitarō (ゲゲゲの鬼太郎) is a Japanese manga series created in 1959 by Shigeru Mizuki, adapted repeatedly into anime, films and video games. It is credited with popularising the yōkai — spirits and monsters of Japanese folklore — in modern popular culture.

Premise

Kitarō is a one-eyed boy, the last survivor of the Ghost Tribe, who fights to keep peace between humans and yōkai. He is accompanied by his father Medama-oyaji, an anthropomorphic eyeball, and friends including the cat-girl Neko Musume, the umbrella monster Kasa-obake, and the scheming Nezumi Otoko (Rat Man). Kitarō's weapons include remote-controlled geta sandals, needle-spitting hair and a chanchanko vest made from the fur of his ancestors.

History

Mizuki drew on kamishibai street-theatre stories from the 1930s about a graveyard boy, publishing his version first in rental manga as Hakaba Kitarō ("Kitarō of the Graveyard") before Weekly Shōnen Magazine serialised it as GeGeGe no Kitarō in 1965–69. The "GeGeGe" of the title echoes Mizuki's childhood nickname "Gege" for his difficulty pronouncing his own name.

Six anime series (1968, 1971, 1985, 1996, 2007 and 2018) made Kitarō a fixture of Japanese childhood across generations; the 2018 series reached 97 episodes, and the prequel film The Birth of Kitaro: The Mystery of GeGeGe (2023) was a major box-office hit. Mizuki's home town of Sakaiminato is lined with bronze yōkai statues from the series.

Legacy

The series revived scholarly and popular interest in yōkai folklore and influenced generations of supernatural manga. Mizuki, who died in 2015, is regarded as a founding figure of the modern yōkai genre.
