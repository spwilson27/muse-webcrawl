Sunzi Suanjing

The Sunzi Suanjing (Chinese: 孫子算經; "The Mathematical Classic of Sunzi") is a Chinese mathematical treatise written during the 3rd to 5th centuries CE and included among the Ten Computational Canons of the Tang dynasty. Its author, Sunzi ("Master Sun"), is otherwise unknown and should not be confused with Sun Tzu of The Art of War. The text survives in three chapters (juan).

Contents

The first chapter describes counting-rod numeration and the rules for multiplication and division with rods, including place-value notation and units of length, volume, and weight. The second and third chapters present 64 problems solved by arithmetic methods: fraction operations, area and volume computations, square roots, distribution problems, and surveying applications.

Chinese remainder theorem

The treatise is famous for problem 26 of the third chapter, the earliest known instance of the problem later solved by the Chinese remainder theorem: "There are certain things whose number is unknown. If we count them by threes, we have two left over; by fives, we have three left over; and by sevens, two are left over. Find the number of things." The answer given is 23, and Sunzi's method (the "Great Derivation" procedures later systematized by Qin Jiushao in 1247) became a landmark of number theory; the puzzle was later popularized in verse form as the "Han Xin counting his soldiers" problem.
