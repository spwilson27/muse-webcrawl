Sino-Soviet conflict (1929)

The Sino-Soviet conflict of 1929 was an armed conflict between the Soviet Union and the Republic of China over the Chinese Eastern Railway (CER) in Manchuria. It was the largest armed clash between China and the USSR before the border conflict of 1969.

Background

The Chinese Eastern Railway, built by Imperial Russia across northern Manchuria as a shortcut for the Trans-Siberian Railway, was jointly managed by China and the Soviet Union under agreements of 1924. Zhang Xueliang, the warlord governing Manchuria, sought to assert full Chinese control, encouraged in part by the Nationalist government in Nanjing. In July 1929 Chinese authorities raided the Soviet consulate in Harbin and seized the railway, arresting and expelling Soviet personnel.

Conflict

The Soviet Union responded by forming the Special Red Banner Far Eastern Army under Vasily Blyukher. After an ultimatum was rejected, Soviet forces launched offensives along the Manchurian borders in the autumn of 1929. Soviet troops and aircraft inflicted heavy defeats on Zhang's Northeastern Army at Manzhouli, Zhalainuo'er and near Suifenhe, and Soviet gunboats destroyed the Chinese Sungari flotilla. Chinese casualties ran into the thousands, while Soviet losses were far lower.

Outcome

By the Khabarovsk Protocol of 22 December 1929, China agreed to restore the status quo ante: joint management of the railway resumed and Soviet employees were reinstated. The defeat exposed the weakness of the Northeastern Army and is considered one of the factors that encouraged Japan's invasion of Manchuria in 1931.
