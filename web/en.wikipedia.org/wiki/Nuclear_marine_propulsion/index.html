Nuclear marine propulsion

Nuclear marine propulsion is propulsion of a ship or submarine with heat provided by a nuclear reactor. The power plant heats water to produce steam for a turbine used to turn the vessel's propeller through a gearbox or through an electric generator and motor. Naval nuclear propulsion is used specifically in certain, mainly larger, classes of warships such as super carriers and submarines.

Principle

Most naval reactors are pressurised water reactors: enriched uranium fuel generates heat, which is carried by pressurised coolant water to a steam generator; the steam drives propulsion turbines and turbo-generators supplying the ship's electrical load. Because the reactor needs no oxygen and produces enormous energy from a small mass of fuel, a nuclear vessel can operate for years without refuelling and, in the case of submarines, remain submerged for months, limited mainly by food and crew endurance.

History

The United States Navy pioneered the technology under Admiral Hyman G. Rickover. The USS Nautilus (SSN-571), launched in 1954, was the world's first operational nuclear-powered submarine, signalling "Underway on nuclear power" in January 1955. The first nuclear surface warship, the cruiser USS Long Beach, and the carrier USS Enterprise followed in 1961. The Soviet Union, United Kingdom, France, China and India subsequently developed naval reactors; Russia also operates nuclear-powered icebreakers for Arctic service.

Civilian use

Civilian nuclear ships have been few: the American NS Savannah, the German Otto Hahn, the Japanese Mutsu and the Russian cargo ship Sevmorput, along with the Soviet/Russian fleet of nuclear icebreakers based at Murmansk. High costs, port-access restrictions and public concern have limited merchant applications.

Safety and decommissioning

Naval reactors are designed with multiple containment barriers and negative feedback characteristics. Decommissioning requires defuelling and long-term management of reactor compartments; several hundred nuclear submarines have been built, and their dismantlement is a major undertaking in Russia and the United States.
