E Ink

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An e-reader using an E Ink display.

E Ink Corporation is a technology company that develops and manufactures electrophoretic displays — the "electronic paper" used in most e-readers. Founded in 1997 as a spin-out from the MIT Media Lab, it is headquartered in Billerica, Massachusetts, and since 2009 has been owned by the Taiwanese company Prime View International, which renamed itself E Ink Holdings. E Ink Holdings is the world's dominant supplier of e-paper displays, used in Amazon Kindle, Kobo and other e-readers, as well as shelf-edge labels, signage and other low-power displays.

Technology

An E Ink display consists of millions of microcapsules, each containing positively charged white particles and negatively charged black particles suspended in a clear fluid. Applying an electric field draws one or other set of particles to the viewing surface, forming text and images. The display is bistable: once set, it consumes no power to hold the image, drawing current only when the page changes. Because the display reflects light like paper rather than emitting it, it is readable in sunlight and easy on the eye, but refresh is slow compared with LCD or OLED screens, making it unsuitable for video.

Generations of the film include Vizplex, Pearl, Carta (used in most modern e-readers, with higher contrast and 300 ppi resolution in many models), Mobius (flexible, plastic-based) and colour products — Kaleido, based on a colour filter array, and Gallery/Spectra, which use multiple coloured pigments for fuller colour in signage and, more recently, e-readers.

Applications

Beyond e-readers, E Ink panels are used for electronic shelf labels in supermarkets (a high-volume market), bus-stop and transport signage, e-notes and writing tablets, smartwatches, phone secondary displays (such as the YotaPhone), luggage tags and architectural surfaces. The company's extremely low power consumption makes it attractive wherever information changes infrequently but must be always visible.
