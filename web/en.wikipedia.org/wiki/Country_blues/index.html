Country blues

Country blues, also known as folk blues or rural blues, is one of the earliest forms of blues music, developed in the rural southern United States in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. It is typically performed by a solo singer accompanying themselves on acoustic guitar or piano, in contrast to the band format of later urban blues.

Styles and artists

Regional styles include the Delta blues of Mississippi, the Piedmont blues of the southeastern states with its ragtime-influenced fingerpicking, and Texas blues. Notable performers include Charley Patton, Robert Johnson, Blind Lemon Jefferson, Son House, Mississippi John Hurt and Elizabeth Cotten.
