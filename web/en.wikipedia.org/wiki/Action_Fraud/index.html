Action Fraud is the United Kingdom's national reporting centre for fraud and financially motivated cyber crime. Members of the public and businesses report incidents to Action Fraud by telephone or through its website rather than to their local police force. Reports are recorded and analysed by the National Fraud Intelligence Bureau (NFIB), which is operated by the City of London Police, the national lead force for fraud.

Operation

Action Fraud does not itself investigate individual crimes. Instead, the NFIB reviews reports, links related cases, and disseminates packages of intelligence to police forces and other agencies for investigation. Victims receive a crime reference number and information about support services. The service covers England, Wales and Northern Ireland; in Scotland, fraud is reported to Police Scotland.

Criticism and reform

The service has been criticised — including in a 2019 undercover investigation by The Times — for poor customer service and the small proportion of reports leading to investigation. In response, the government announced a replacement service with improved victim care and reporting technology, with the new national reporting service intended to replace Action Fraud.
