Les Kurbas

Les Kurbas (Ukrainian: Лесь Курбас; 25 February 1887 – 3 November 1937) was a Ukrainian theatre director, actor, and theorist, regarded as the founder of modern Ukrainian theatre. His experimental Berezil theatre in Kharkiv became one of the most innovative companies in Europe in the 1920s before he was destroyed in Stalin's Great Purge.

Career

Oleksandr-Zenon Kurbas was born in Sambir, Galicia, then part of Austria-Hungary, and studied philosophy in Vienna and Lviv. He acted with Ukrainian companies before founding his own studios: in Kyiv he created the Young Theatre (1916), and in 1922 the Berezil ("March") artistic association, which moved to Kharkiv, then capital of Soviet Ukraine, in 1926. Kurbas's productions — including plays by Mykola Kulish such as Myna Mazailo and Sonata Pathétique — combined expressionism, constructivist staging, and Ukrainian themes, and his "system of transformations" trained actors in psychological and physical re-embodiment.

Persecution and death

In 1933, amid tightening ideological control, Kurbas was dismissed from the Berezil, arrested in Moscow in December 1933, and sentenced to five years in labour camps. He worked in camp theatres at the White Sea Canal and on the Solovetsky Islands. During the Great Terror he was retried by an NKVD troika and shot at Sandarmokh in Karelia on 3 November 1937, in the same massacre that killed much of Ukraine's cultural intelligentsia, the "Executed Renaissance". He was posthumously rehabilitated in the 1950s, and the Berezil's successor company in Kharkiv now bears his name.
