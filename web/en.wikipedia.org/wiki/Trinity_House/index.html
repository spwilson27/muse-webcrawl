Trinity House

The Corporation of Trinity House of Deptford Strond, commonly Trinity House, is the official general lighthouse authority for England, Wales, the Channel Islands, and Gibraltar, responsible for lighthouses, lightvessels, buoys, and other aids to navigation. It is also an official deep-sea pilotage authority and a maritime charity supporting seafarers and cadet training.

History

Trinity House traces its origin to a guild of mariners at Deptford, receiving a royal charter from Henry VIII in 1514 to regulate pilotage on the Thames. Subsequent charters under Elizabeth I gave it authority over seamarks, and from the 17th century it progressively acquired or built lighthouses around the coast, financed by light dues levied on shipping. Its Elder Brethren include senior mariners and, by custom, prominent public figures; the corporation's headquarters, Trinity House on Tower Hill in London, dates from 1796.

Modern role

Trinity House maintains about 66 lighthouses and thousands of buoys and beacons, all automated and monitored from its operations centre at Harwich. It works alongside the Northern Lighthouse Board (Scotland and the Isle of Man) and the Commissioners of Irish Lights (Ireland). Its charitable work funds almshouses, welfare for retired mariners, and bursaries for officer cadets.
