The Madeira-Mamoré Railway (Portuguese: Estrada de Ferro Madeira-Mamoré) was a railway in the Brazilian state of Rondônia, built to bypass the rapids of the Madeira and Mamoré rivers so Bolivian rubber and goods could reach the Amazon and the Atlantic.

History

Under the 1903 Treaty of Petrópolis, Brazil agreed to build the line after acquiring Acre. Construction (1907–1912) by Percival Farquhar's company in dense rainforest cost thousands of workers' lives to disease — earning it the name 'Devil's Railway', with a legend of a death for every sleeper laid. It opened just as the Amazon rubber boom collapsed with Asian plantation competition, and was never profitable. Operations ceased in 1972 after road construction, though a short tourist section near Porto Velho later ran.
