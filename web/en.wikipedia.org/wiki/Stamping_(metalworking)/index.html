Stamping (metalworking)

Stamping (also known as pressing) is the process of placing flat sheet metal, in either blank or coil form, into a stamping press where a tool and die surface forms the metal into a net shape. Stamping includes a variety of sheet-metal forming operations such as punching, blanking, embossing, bending, flanging and coining, carried out individually or in sequence, sometimes in a progressive die that performs several operations at successive stations.

A progressive stamping press in an automotive plant

Process

In a typical operation, a strip or blank of cold-rolled steel, aluminium or another metal is fed into the press; a punch forces the material into a matching die cavity, shearing or forming it. Lubricants reduce friction and protect the tooling. Modern presses range from small bench machines to automotive transfer presses exerting thousands of tons, often integrated with automated coil feed lines, and simulation software is used to predict thinning and springback before dies are built.

Applications

Stamping is used for mass production of parts such as automobile body panels, aircraft skins, appliance housings, electronic enclosures and coins. It is economical when large volumes amortise the high cost of designing and machining the dies, and it produces parts with good surface finish and dimensional repeatability.

History

Stamping grew out of earlier forging and minting practice; stamped bicycle parts appeared in the 1880s, and the automotive industry drove the development of large-scale stamping in the early 20th century.
