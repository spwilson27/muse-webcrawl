Hron

The Hron (German: Gran; Hungarian: Garam) is a 271-kilometre-long left tributary of the Danube and the second-longest river in Slovakia. It rises in the Low Tatra mountains near the village of Telgárt and flows west and then south through central Slovakia, joining the Danube near Štúrovo and Esztergom.

Course

From its source below Kráľova hoľa the Hron flows through the towns of Brezno, Banská Bystrica, Zvolen, Žiar nad Hronom, Žarnovica, Nová Baňa, Tlmače, Levice and Želiezovce before reaching the Danube plain. Its drainage basin covers roughly 5,450 km², about 11 percent of Slovakia's territory.

History

The river formed part of the Roman frontier zone in antiquity: Marcus Aurelius is traditionally said to have written part of his Meditations "on the banks of the Gran" during the Marcomannic Wars. In the Middle Ages the valley was a centre of mining, with copper and silver from Banská Bystrica and the surrounding towns floated down the river. Hard fighting took place along the Hron in the final months of the Second World War during the Bratislava–Brno offensive of 1945.

Today the river is used for hydroelectric power, water supply and recreation, and the upper valley is a corridor for road and rail routes linking central Slovakia with the east of the country.
