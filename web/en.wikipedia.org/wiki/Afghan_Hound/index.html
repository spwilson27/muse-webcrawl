Afghan Hound

The Afghan Hound is a hound distinguished by its thick, fine, silky coat and its tail with a ring curl at the end. The breed is selectively bred for its unique features in the cold mountains of Afghanistan, where it was used to hunt large prey in both desert and mountain terrain.

Characteristics

Afghan Hounds stand 61–74 cm at the shoulder and have an aristocratic, aloof bearing, prominent hipbones, and a long narrow head with a topknot of silky hair. Their temperament is typically independent and dignified, with a clownish side with puppies. The coat requires extensive grooming to prevent matting.

History

The breed is among the most ancient of dog breeds, related to the Saluki type of sighthound. It was brought to Britain in the early twentieth century by military officers returning from Afghanistan, and a dog named Zardin won best in show at Crystal Palace in 1907, popularising the breed in the West.
