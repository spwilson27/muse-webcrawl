Datec. 170 AD (disputed)

SubjectNew Testament canon

The Muratorian fragment, also called the Muratorian Canon, is an early list of books considered authoritative by some Christians. It is generally dated to around 170 AD, though some scholars argue for a later, fourth-century origin. It is the oldest known surviving list of New Testament books.

Content

The fragment begins mid-sentence and enumerates most of the books of the New Testament, including the four Gospels, Acts, the Pauline epistles, Jude, and Revelation. It excludes some books now canonical, such as Hebrews and the Petrine epistles, and accepts the Apocalypse of Peter with reservations.

Discovery

The manuscript, a seventh- or eighth-century codex, was published by the Italian historian Ludovico Antonio Muratori in 1740. The text is written in barbarous Latin and is incomplete at its beginning.
