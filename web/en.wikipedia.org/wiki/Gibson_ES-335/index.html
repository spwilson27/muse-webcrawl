Gibson ES-335

ManufacturerGibson
Period1958 – present
Body typeSemi-hollow, double cutaway
PickupsTwo humbuckers

The Gibson ES-335 is an archtop electric guitar introduced by the Gibson Guitar Corporation in 1958. The first commercial semi-hollowbody guitar, it combines a hollow archtop body with a solid maple centre block, giving the warmth of a jazz guitar with much of the sustain and feedback resistance of a solid-body instrument.

Design and history

Under Gibson president Ted McCarty, the ES-335 ("ES" for Electric Spanish; priced at 35 at launch) featured a laminated maple body, mahogany neck, twin PAF humbucking pickups and a stopbar tailpiece on early "dot-neck" models. Later variants include the ES-345 and ES-355 (with stereo wiring and Varitone), the smaller ES-339, and the budget Epiphone Casino and Sheraton relatives.

Players

The 335 has been used across blues, rock and jazz — by B.B. King (whose signature "Lucille" is a variant), Chuck Berry, Eric Clapton (with Cream), Larry Carlton, Alvin Lee, Dave Grohl and Noel Gallagher. Its versatility made it one of the most recorded electric guitar models of all time.

Legacy

The ES-335 remains in production in Gibson's Nashville and Memphis lines and is widely imitated; vintage 1958–1960 "dot" examples rank among the most collectible electric guitars.
