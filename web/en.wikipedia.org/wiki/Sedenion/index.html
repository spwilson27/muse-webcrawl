Sedenion

The sedenions arise at the fourth doubling of the Cayley–Dickson construction

The sedenions form a 16-dimensional noncommutative and nonassociative algebra over the real numbers, obtained by applying the Cayley–Dickson construction to the octonions. They are traditionally denoted by the symbol 𝕆 or by a blackboard-bold S.

Properties

Like the octonions, sedenion multiplication is neither commutative nor associative. Unlike the octonions, however, the sedenions are not alternative, and they do not form a division algebra: they possess zero divisors — pairs of non-zero sedenions whose product is zero. They do retain power-associativity and a multiplicative identity, and the conjugation and norm of the octonions extend to them, though the norm is no longer multiplicative in the way that would exclude zero divisors.

Arithmetic

A sedenion is a linear combination of sixteen basis units e0, …, e15, with e0 = 1 the real unit and the remaining fifteen imaginary units squaring to −1. Multiplication of the basis units can be tabulated in a 16×16 table derived from the Cayley–Dickson doubling of the octonion product. The 480 ordered pairs of distinct imaginary units that multiply to zero are of particular interest and form patterns studied through combinatorial designs and the geometry of the exceptional Lie groups.

Applications

Sedenions and the algebras beyond them appear in abstract algebra research and have been explored speculatively in theoretical physics and in neural-network and signal-processing generalisations of complex arithmetic, where the zero divisors are an important obstacle.
