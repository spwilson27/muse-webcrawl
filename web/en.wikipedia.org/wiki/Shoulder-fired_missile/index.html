Shoulder-fired missile

A shoulder-fired missile is a projectile weapon small and light enough to be fired from a launcher rested on a single soldier's shoulder. The category includes guided anti-aircraft missiles (man-portable air-defense systems, or MANPADS), guided anti-tank missiles, and unguided rockets and recoilless weapons such as rocket-propelled grenades.

Types

MANPADS such as the American FIM-92 Stinger, the Soviet/Russian 9K38 Igla and the British Starstreak use infrared or laser guidance to engage low-flying aircraft and helicopters. Shoulder-launched anti-tank weapons range from disposable unguided systems like the M72 LAW and AT4 to guided missiles such as the FGM-148 Javelin, which is larger and usually fired by a two-person team but remains man-portable. Unguided rocket-propelled grenades, exemplified by the Soviet RPG-7, are among the most widely distributed weapons in the world.

Proliferation concerns

Because shoulder-fired missiles are portable, concealable and effective against civil aircraft, their proliferation to non-state actors is a major international security concern. Export controls and buy-back programmes have sought to limit the spread of MANPADS since the Cold War, and airlines and regulators have studied onboard countermeasures against them.
