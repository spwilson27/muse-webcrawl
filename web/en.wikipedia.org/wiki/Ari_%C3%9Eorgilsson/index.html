Ari Þorgilsson

Icelandic vellum manuscript of the kind that preserved Ari's works

Ari Þorgilsson (Old Norse: [ˈɑre ˈθorˌɡilsˌson]; 1067/1068 – 9 November 1148), known as Ari the Wise (Ari fróði), was Iceland's earliest known historian. He authored the Íslendingabók (Book of Icelanders), the principal source for early Icelandic history.

Life

Ari was born into a prominent family descended from settlers of Breiðafjörður. Orphaned young, he was fostered at Haukadalur by Hallur Þórarinsson and educated by Teitur Ísleifsson, son of Iceland's first bishop. He became a priest and spent his later life at Staðarstaður on Snæfellsnes.

Works

The Íslendingabók, written c. 1122–1133, covers the settlement of Iceland, the founding of the Althing, the conversion to Christianity, and the early bishops, based on carefully cited oral informants. Ari also contributed to the earliest version of Landnámabók (Book of Settlements) and composed genealogies and a chronicle of Norwegian kings.
