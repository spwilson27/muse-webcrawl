Evoked potential

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An evoked potential (or evoked response) is an electrical potential recorded from the nervous system of a human or animal following presentation of a stimulus, as distinct from spontaneous potentials detected by electroencephalography (EEG) or electromyography. Because the response to a single stimulus is tiny compared with background brain activity, the stimulus is repeated many times and the recordings are averaged, which cancels random noise and leaves the time-locked response waveform.

Types

Visual evoked potentials (VEP) are elicited by a flashing light or, more commonly, a reversing checkerboard pattern, and test the integrity of the optic nerve and visual pathways. Brainstem auditory evoked potentials (BAEP) are generated by click stimuli and consist of a series of waves arising from the auditory nerve and brainstem nuclei. Somatosensory evoked potentials (SSEP) follow electrical stimulation of a peripheral nerve, such as the median or tibial nerve, and travel via the dorsal columns and medial lemniscus to the somatosensory cortex. Motor evoked potentials, produced by transcranial magnetic or electrical stimulation, assess the corticospinal tract.

Clinical uses

Evoked potentials are used to detect lesions of sensory pathways that may be clinically silent. A delayed VEP, for example, is a classic finding in optic neuritis and multiple sclerosis and historically formed part of the diagnostic work-up for MS. Evoked potentials are also widely used in intraoperative neurophysiological monitoring during spinal and brain surgery, in assessing hearing in newborns and unresponsive patients, in evaluating brainstem function in coma, and in confirming brain death in some protocols.

Event-related potentials

When the stimulus involves cognitive processing — surprise, attention, expectation — the recorded responses are called event-related potentials (ERPs). Famous components include the P300, a positive wave roughly 300 ms after an unexpected stimulus, and the N400, associated with semantic incongruity. ERPs are a major tool of cognitive neuroscience and psychophysiology.
