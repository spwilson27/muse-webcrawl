Bugs Bunny

Bugs Bunny is an animated cartoon character created in the late 1930s at Warner Bros. Cartoons (then Leon Schlesinger Productions) and voiced originally by Mel Blanc. Bugs is an anthropomorphic gray-and-white rabbit best known for his laid-back, flippant personality, Brooklyn-like accent, carrot-chewing pose and catchphrase "Eh... What's up, doc?" He is the star of the Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies series and the mascot of Warner Bros.

Development

Prototype rabbits appeared in late-1930s Warner cartoons such as Porky's Hare Hunt (1938). The fully formed character debuted in Tex Avery's A Wild Hare (1940), opposite Elmer Fudd. According to animator lore, the name "Bugs" came from Ben "Bugs" Hardaway, whose sketch an artist labelled "Bugs's bunny". During the Second World War Bugs became hugely popular, appearing in propaganda films and as a mascot for military units.

Personality and films

Bugs is a trickster who rarely starts trouble but always outwits aggressors — hunters Elmer Fudd and Yosemite Sam, the scheming Daffy Duck, Wile E. Coyote, the Tasmanian Devil and Marvin the Martian. Landmark shorts include What's Opera, Doc? (1957), frequently voted the greatest cartoon of all time, Knighty Knight Bugs (1958), which won the Academy Award, and Rabbit Seasoning (1952). He later starred in Who Framed Roger Rabbit (1988), Space Jam (1996) with Michael Jordan, and Space Jam: A New Legacy (2021) with LeBron James.

Legacy

Bugs was the first animated character to receive a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame (1985) and the first cartoon character on a US postage stamp (1997). TV Guide named him the greatest cartoon character of all time. His catchphrase, carrot pose (modelled on Clark Gable in It Happened One Night) and cross-dressing gags remain fixtures of American popular culture.
