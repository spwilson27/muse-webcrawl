Outlawry

Outlawry is the state of being an outlaw: a person declared to be outside the protection of the law. In historical legal systems, an outlaw was a person who had defied the law, for example by ignoring a summons, and who could be killed with impunity because the law no longer protected them.

In English common law, outlawry followed failure to appear in court; a person under sentence of outlawry forfeited goods and civil rights. The concept existed in many legal traditions — in Old Norse law the full outlaw (skógarmaðr) was exiled and could be killed by anyone, while lesser outlawry meant temporary exile.

Outlawry was abolished in civil proceedings in England in 1879 and in criminal proceedings in 1938, though the term survives figuratively and in romanticised accounts of figures such as Robin Hood.
