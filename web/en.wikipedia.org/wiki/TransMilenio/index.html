TransMilenio

TransMilenio is a bus rapid transit (BRT) system that serves Bogotá, the capital of Colombia, and Soacha, a neighbouring city. The system opened to the public on 18 December 2000 and was inspired by Curitiba's Rede Integrada de Transporte in Brazil.

System

TransMilenio consists of trunk corridors with dedicated bus lanes, elevated stations with platform-level boarding, and prepaid fare collection using the TuLlave smart card. Red articulated and bi-articulated buses run on the trunk corridors, while green feeder buses connect outlying neighborhoods to transfer terminals without additional fare. By the 2020s the network comprised about 114 km of trunk corridors across a dozen lines and more than 140 stations, carrying around two million passengers on a typical weekday before the COVID-19 pandemic.

History

The system was developed under Mayor Enrique Peñalosa, whose administration prioritized public transport and cycling infrastructure over highway expansion. Phase I opened in 2000 along Avenida Caracas and Calle 80; Phase II added corridors on Avenida de las Américas, Avenida Suba, and the NQS. Phase III extended service along Calle 26 and Carrera 10. The system has been widely studied as a model BRT but has also faced criticism for overcrowding, fare evasion, and delays to long-planned expansions and the future Bogotá Metro, which is intended to complement it.
