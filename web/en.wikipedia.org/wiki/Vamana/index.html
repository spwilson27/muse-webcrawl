Vamana as Trivikrama, measuring the universe in three steps

Vamana (Sanskrit: वामन, "the dwarf") is the fifth avatar of the Hindu god Vishnu in the standard Dashavatara list. He appears as a dwarf Brahmin who, by taking three gigantic strides (Trivikrama), reclaims the three worlds from the benevolent but over-mighty demon-king Bali. The myth's roots reach back to the Rigveda, where Vishnu's three strides across the universe are repeatedly celebrated, though the full narrative of the dwarf and Bali is developed in the epics and Puranas, especially the Vamana Purana and the eighth book of the Bhagavata Purana.

The legend

Bali, great-grandson of Prahlada, conquered the three worlds and drove out the gods. Born as the dwarf son of the sage Kashyapa and Aditi, Vamana came to Bali's sacrificial rite and asked for a modest gift: as much land as he could cover in three paces. Despite the warnings of his guru Shukra, the generous Bali agreed. Vamana then grew to cosmic size: with his first step he covered the earth, with the second the heavens, and for the third Bali offered his own head. Pleased by Bali's devotion and humility, Vishnu granted him rule over the netherworld (Sutala) and promised to serve as his doorkeeper, while Bali was assured a future position as Indra in the next manvantara.

Significance

The festival of Onam in Kerala is associated with the annual return of King Bali to visit his former subjects, the mirror-image of the Vamana story told from Bali's perspective. Vamana Jayanti is celebrated on the Dwadashi of the bright half of Bhadrapada. Temples dedicated to Vamana include the Vamana temple at Kanchipuram (Ulagalantha Perumal), and the myth is a popular subject in Indian art and dance-drama.
