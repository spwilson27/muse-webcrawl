Licence Raj

The Licence Raj or Permit Raj (from Hindi rāj, meaning "rule") was the system of licences, regulations, and accompanying red tape that governed the establishment and operation of businesses in India between 1947 and the early 1990s.

System

Under legislation such as the Industries (Development and Regulation) Act of 1951, an entrepreneur might need approvals from dozens of government agencies before starting or expanding a business, and the state controlled what could be produced, in what quantity, and at what price. The term was coined by C. Rajagopalachari, founder of the Swatantra Party, playing on "British Raj". Defenders argued the system directed scarce resources toward national priorities; critics blamed it for bureaucracy, corruption, and India's slow "Hindu rate of growth".

Abolition

The balance-of-payments crisis of 1991 prompted the government of P. V. Narasimha Rao, with Manmohan Singh as finance minister, to abolish most industrial licensing as part of the New Economic Policy, opening most sectors to private and foreign investment.
