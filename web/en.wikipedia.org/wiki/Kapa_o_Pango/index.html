Kapa o Pango

"Kapa o Pango" is a haka composed by Derek Lardelli of Ngāti Porou for the New Zealand national rugby union team, the All Blacks. It was first performed on 27 August 2005, before a Tri Nations match against South Africa at Carisbrook, Dunedin, where New Zealand won 31–27. The haka was created to reflect the multi-cultural make-up of the modern team and sits alongside the traditional "Ka Mate" haka.

Background

New Zealand Rugby commissioned the haka in consultation with Māori elders, wanting a composition that spoke of the land, the fern and the team's identity rather than the Ngāti Toa chief Te Rauparaha, composer of "Ka Mate". Lardelli, an expert in tikanga Māori, worked on the piece for more than a year.

Controversy

The closing gesture of the original performance — a thumb drawn across the throat — drew criticism from some commentators and opposing fans who read it as a "throat-slitting" threat. The gesture, in Māori culture, signifies drawing breath and vital energy into the body; after a review, a modified closing action without the thumb-to-throat movement was adopted from 2006.

Usage

"Kapa o Pango" is reserved for special occasions — roughly one match in four — including Rugby World Cup finals in 2011 and 2015, both won by New Zealand.
