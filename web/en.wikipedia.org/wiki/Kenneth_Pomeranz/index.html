Kenneth Pomeranz

Kenneth Pomeranz (born November 4, 1958) is an American historian of China and global economic history. He is best known for his book The Great Divergence: China, Europe, and the Making of the Modern World Economy (2000), which won the John K. Fairbank Prize and reshaped debates about why industrialization occurred first in Europe.

Pomeranz argued that as late as 1750, the most developed regions of China, particularly the Yangzi Delta, were comparable to the most developed parts of Europe in living standards, markets, and ecological pressures, and that the divergence between them resulted from contingent factors — Europe's access to coal deposits near industrial centers and to the resources of New World colonies — rather than deep institutional or cultural differences. He is a leading figure of the so-called California School of comparative history.

Career

He taught at the University of California, Irvine, before joining the University of Chicago in 2012 as University Professor of History. He served as president of the American Historical Association in 2019.
