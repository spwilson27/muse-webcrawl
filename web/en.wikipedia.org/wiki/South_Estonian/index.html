South Estonian

South Estonian is a Finnic language or group of dialects spoken in southern Estonia and nearby areas of Latvia and Russia. It is distinct from standard (North) Estonian, from which it diverged centuries ago, and is considered by many linguists a separate language.

Varieties

The principal South Estonian varieties are Võro, Seto, Tartu and Mulgi. Võro, spoken around Võru, has the strongest modern literary movement; Seto is spoken by the Seto people near the Russian border and is known for its polyphonic singing tradition, leelo, inscribed by UNESCO. Mulgi, spoken around Viljandi, is the most divergent and now the most endangered.

Features

South Estonian preserves vowel harmony, lost in standard Estonian, and has a three-way distinction involving a glottal stop marked with an apostrophe in Võro orthography. It retains archaic features such as final vowels in some forms and distinct past-tense formations.

Status

Soviet-era centralisation favoured standard Estonian, and South Estonian declined sharply in schools and media. Since the 1990s a revival, centred on the Võro Institute (founded 1995), has produced literature, a Wikipedia edition, and some school teaching, though the total number of speakers is generally estimated in the tens of thousands.
