Lumbar plexus

Lumbar plexus
FromL1–L4 spinal nerves (with a contribution from T12)
Major branchesFemoral nerve, obturator nerve, lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh
SuppliesLower abdominal wall, external genitalia, anterior and medial thigh

The lumbar plexus is a network of nerve fibres formed by the ventral rami of the first four lumbar spinal nerves (L1–L4), typically with a contribution from the subcostal nerve (T12). It lies within the substance of the psoas major muscle, anterior to the transverse processes of the lumbar vertebrae, and gives rise to nerves supplying the lower part of the anterior abdominal wall, the external genitalia, and much of the lower limb.

The lumbar plexus formed within the psoas major muscle

Branches

The principal branches, in order of the roots contributing to them, include the iliohypogastric nerve (L1), ilioinguinal nerve (L1), genitofemoral nerve (L1, L2), lateral cutaneous nerve of the thigh (L2, L3), obturator nerve (L2–L4) and the femoral nerve (L2–L4), the largest branch of the plexus. Smaller muscular branches supply the psoas major, quadratus lumborum and intertransverse muscles.

Clinical significance

The lumbar plexus can be anaesthetised by a psoas compartment (lumbar plexus) block for surgery of the hip, thigh and knee. The plexus may be damaged by psoas abscess, retroperitoneal haematoma, tumours or fractures of the pelvis, producing weakness of hip flexion and knee extension and sensory loss over the anterior thigh.
