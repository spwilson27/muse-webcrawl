Director of music

A director of music is a person who holds overall responsibility for the music of an institution, ensemble, or organization. The title is used in several distinct contexts, most commonly in churches and cathedrals, in schools and universities, and in the armed forces.

Church and cathedral music

In a cathedral, collegiate church, or large parish church, the director of music plans and directs the musical life of the foundation: choosing repertoire, rehearsing and conducting the choir, overseeing organists and other musicians, and coordinating music with the liturgy. In many English cathedrals the post is combined with that of principal organist, though in others the roles are held by different people, with an assistant director of music or sub-organist supporting the work. Historically the equivalent figure was the maestro di cappella or Kapellmeister.

Education

In schools, the director of music manages the music department: teaching, scheduling ensembles such as orchestras, bands, and choirs, arranging concerts and examinations, and directing the co-curricular musical programme. In some universities the title attaches to a senior academic or performance post.

Military

In the British armed forces, a Director of Music is a commissioned officer who commands and conducts a military band, for example within the Royal Corps of Army Music. Directors of Music receive professional training, including at the Royal Military School of Music, Kneller Hall, and are responsible for the band's musical standard, ceremonial duties, and engagements.
