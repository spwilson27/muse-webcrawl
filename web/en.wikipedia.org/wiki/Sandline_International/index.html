Sandline International was a British private military company (PMC) founded in 1994 by former Scots Guards officer Tim Spicer and financier Michael Grunberg. Based in London, the company provided military training, operational support, intelligence and security services to governments, and became one of the most prominent — and controversial — private military firms of the 1990s. Sandline ceased operations in April 2004.

Sandline operated in close association with Executive Outcomes, the South African PMC, sharing personnel and undertaking joint planning. Its activities drew international attention in two major controversies: the Sandline affair in Papua New Guinea (1997) and the "Arms to Africa" affair in Sierra Leone (1998).

Sandline affair (Papua New Guinea)

In January 1997 the government of Papua New Guinea, led by Prime Minister Julius Chan, signed a contract worth about US$36 million with Sandline to provide mercenaries and equipment to defeat secessionist rebels on Bougainville, where a civil war had raged since 1988 over the Panguna copper mine. When news of the contract broke, the commander of the Papua New Guinea Defence Force, Brigadier General Jerry Singirok, denounced the plan, detained Spicer and his men, and demanded the government's resignation. The resulting political crisis forced Chan to step aside, Sandline personnel were expelled, and the contract was cancelled. The affair contributed to Chan's defeat in the 1997 general election and helped push the Bougainville conflict toward a negotiated peace.

Arms to Africa affair (Sierra Leone)

In 1998 Sandline was investigated by HM Customs and Excise and scrutinised by a British parliamentary inquiry over allegations that it had shipped arms to Sierra Leone in support of the exiled president Ahmad Tejan Kabbah, in apparent breach of a United Nations arms embargo. Sandline and Spicer maintained that the British government had been aware of, and had tacitly approved, the shipment. The affair caused acute embarrassment to the Foreign and Commonwealth Office; the subsequent Legg Report criticised officials' handling of the matter, though ministers were cleared of deliberately misleading Parliament.

Closure and legacy

Sandline announced its closure on 16 April 2004, stating that government opposition to private military companies had made its position untenable. Spicer went on to found Aegis Defence Services. Sandline's high-profile controversies were an important catalyst for international debate about the regulation of private military companies, contributing to discussions that eventually produced the Montreux Document (2008) and the International Code of Conduct for Private Security Service Providers.
