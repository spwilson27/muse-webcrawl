André Courrèges
Born: 9 March 1923, Pau, France
Died: 7 January 2016, Neuilly-sur-Seine, France
Occupation: Fashion designer
Label: Courrèges
Known for: Miniskirt, Space Age fashion
Courrèges' futuristic white designs defined mid-1960s fashion.

André Courrèges (9 March 1923 – 7 January 2016) was a French fashion designer, celebrated for his ultra-modern, futuristic designs. Trained as a civil engineer, he worked for a decade at Balenciaga before founding his own house in Paris in 1961 with his future wife and creative partner Coqueline Barrière.

Space Age style

Courrèges' spring 1964 collection revolutionized fashion with its architectural, geometric silhouettes: short A-line dresses, trouser suits, white kidskin go-go boots, and large white-framed sunglasses resembling space goggles. He is one of the designers credited with popularizing the miniskirt — a claim he disputed with Mary Quant, holding that he had simply expressed a modern way of life. His "Space Age" look used white and silver, vinyl, and synthetic fabrics to evoke the era of space exploration, and his moon-girl aesthetic became emblematic of the decade.

Later career

The Courrèges house expanded into ready-to-wear, perfumes such as Empreinte, and menswear. André Courrèges retired from active design in the 1990s; the brand was sold and revived in the 2010s. He died in 2016 at the age of 92, having suffered from Parkinson's disease.
