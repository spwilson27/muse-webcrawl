Pong

Developer: Atari, Inc.

Designer: Allan Alcorn

Released: 29 November 1972

Genre: Sports / table tennis simulation

Pong is a 1972 sports video game developed and published by Atari, Inc. It is one of the earliest arcade video games and the first to achieve widespread commercial success, effectively launching the video game industry.

Gameplay

Pong simulates table tennis in two dimensions. Each player controls a paddle — a vertical bar that moves up and down at the edge of the screen — and the players volley a small square "ball" back and forth. A player scores when the opponent fails to return the ball; the first to reach the winning score (typically 11 points) wins. The ball speeds up during a rally, and the point where it strikes the paddle determines its rebound angle.

Development and release

Pong was designed by Atari engineer Allan Alcorn as a training exercise assigned by co-founder Nolan Bushnell, drawing on the table-tennis game included with the Magnavox Odyssey home console. A prototype installed in a Sunnyvale bar, Andy Capp's Tavern, proved so popular that its coin mechanism jammed with quarters. Atari released the arcade cabinet in November 1972 and sold thousands of units; a dedicated home version followed in 1975 through Sears, becoming a holiday bestseller.

Legacy

The game's success spawned numerous clones and a patent dispute with Magnavox, which Atari settled by licensing. Pong is regarded as a landmark of popular culture and is preserved in museum collections, including the Smithsonian Institution. It has been re-released and referenced countless times, and "the dating game" of video game history is often said to begin with it.
