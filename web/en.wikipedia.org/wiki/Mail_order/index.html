Mail order

Mail order is the buying of goods or services by mail delivery. The buyer places an order for the desired products with the merchant through some remote method such as a telephone call or website, and the goods are then delivered to the customer, typically to a home address, though sometimes they may be collected from a nearby post office or carrier's depot. Mail order is a form of distance selling and direct marketing.

History

Catalogue selling arose in the nineteenth century as postal services and rail networks improved. In the United States, Montgomery Ward issued its first catalogue in 1872, followed by Sears, Roebuck and Co., whose large catalogues — the famous "wish books" — brought a department store's range of goods to rural households. In Britain, companies such as Littlewoods built major mail-order businesses in the twentieth century, often selling through local agents on credit instalments.

Decline and legacy

From the 1990s onwards, printed catalogues and postal ordering were largely displaced by e-commerce: online retailers adopted the same fulfilment model — remote ordering, warehouse picking and home delivery — with websites replacing the catalogue. Some long-established mail-order firms converted to online retail, while others closed; the basic logistics of mail order remain the foundation of modern distance retail.
