Empire (1977 video game)

Empire is a turn-based strategy video game written by Walter Bright beginning in 1977 for the PDP-10 computer at the California Institute of Technology. One of the earliest computer wargames, it tasked the player with conquering a continent of cities using armies, fighters, and naval units. Bright later ported the game to the Heathkit H-11 and, most influentially, produced the commercial IBM PC version Empire: Wargame of the Century in 1987.

Gameplay

The game is played on a randomly generated map of land and sea dotted with neutral and enemy cities. Cities produce units; armies capture new cities, ships transport armies across water, and aircraft patrol and attack. The world is hidden by a fog of war and revealed only as the player's units explore. The goal is to conquer all of the computer opponent's cities before it conquers yours.

Legacy

Empire established design elements—city-based unit production, fog of war, and combined land-sea-air operations—that later turn-based strategy series would expand upon, and it influenced games such as Civilization. It was inducted into lists of the greatest video games of all time, and its author, Walter Bright, went on to create the D programming language. A separate mainframe game of the same name was developed for the PLATO system in 1973 as a space combat game; the two are unrelated.
