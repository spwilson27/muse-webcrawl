Deborah

According to the Book of Judges, Deborah (Hebrew: דְּבוֹרָה, "bee") was a prophetess of the God of the Israelites, the fourth judge of pre-monarchic Israel, and the only female judge mentioned in the Hebrew Bible.

Narrative

Deborah rendered her judgments beneath the Palm Tree of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel. She summoned the warrior Barak and commanded him, in God's name, to assemble 10,000 men against the forces of Jabin, king of Canaan, commanded by Sisera. When Barak insisted she accompany him, Deborah prophesied that the honour of killing Sisera would go to a woman — fulfilled when Jael drove a tent peg through Sisera's head as he slept.

Song of Deborah

Judges chapter 5 preserves the Song of Deborah, a victory hymn often considered one of the earliest passages of the Bible, possibly dating to the 12th century BC. The poem praises the tribes who fought and rebukes those who stayed away, ending with the taunting image of Sisera's mother waiting in vain for his return.

Significance

Deborah is a rare example of a female military and religious leader in the ancient Near East and has remained a prominent figure in Jewish and Christian tradition and later feminist readings of scripture.
