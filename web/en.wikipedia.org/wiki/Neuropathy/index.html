SymptomsNumbness, tingling, burning pain, weakness, loss of reflexes

Peripheral neuropathy most often begins in the feet in a "stocking-glove" pattern.

Neuropathy, in the usage common in medicine, means disease or dysfunction of one or more peripheral nerves, typically causing numbness or weakness. More generally the word means any disorder of the nervous system, but in practice it usually refers to peripheral neuropathy — damage to the nerves outside the brain and spinal cord. Depending on the nerves involved, neuropathy may be classified as mononeuropathy (one nerve), multiple mononeuropathy (several individual nerves) or polyneuropathy (many nerves, usually symmetrically).

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms depend on whether sensory, motor or autonomic fibres are affected. Sensory involvement produces tingling ("pins and needles"), burning or shooting pain, numbness, and loss of vibration and position sense; because the longest axons are usually affected first, symptoms characteristically begin in the feet and ascend in a stocking-and-glove distribution. Motor involvement produces weakness, wasting and loss of tendon reflexes. Autonomic involvement can disturb sweating, blood-pressure control, digestion and bladder function. Neuropathic pain is often worse at night and responds poorly to ordinary analgesics.

Causes

The single most common cause worldwide is diabetes mellitus, in which chronic high blood sugar injures nerves and their small blood vessels. Other causes include alcohol misuse, vitamin B12 deficiency, kidney failure, thyroid disease, infections (HIV, Lyme disease, leprosy), autoimmune conditions such as Guillain–Barré syndrome and chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy, inherited disorders such as Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease, exposure to toxins and chemotherapy drugs, and physical compression or trauma. In a substantial minority of cases no cause is found (idiopathic neuropathy).

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on the history and neurological examination, supported by nerve conduction studies and electromyography, blood tests for underlying causes, and occasionally nerve biopsy or genetic testing. Treatment aims at the underlying cause — for example tight glucose control in diabetes — together with symptom relief: drugs such as duloxetine, pregabalin, gabapentin and tricyclic antidepressants are commonly used for neuropathic pain, alongside foot care and physiotherapy to prevent injury to insensate limbs.
