Service work


Service workers in retail and hospitality (placeholder image)

Service work is employment in the service sector of the economy, in which workers provide services directly to customers, clients or patients rather than producing goods. It includes jobs in retail, hospitality and food service, healthcare, education, personal services, call centres and much of the gig economy, and accounts for the majority of employment in most advanced economies.

Characteristics

Service work is typically characterised by direct interaction with the public. Sociologists have highlighted its distinctive demands: Arlie Hochschild's concept of emotional labour describes the requirement to manage one's feelings and expressions as part of the job, while other studies emphasise aesthetic labour, shift work, low pay and precarious or part-time contracts in many service occupations. Tipping is an important component of income in some service roles and countries.

Growth

The expansion of service work accompanied deindustrialisation in the later twentieth century, as manufacturing employment shrank in Western economies and female labour-force participation rose. Service occupations now form the largest employment category in most developed countries, spanning highly paid professions (medicine, law, finance) as well as low-wage front-line work, and the sector was a major focus of labour disputes and policy debates over minimum wages and scheduling practices in the twenty-first century.
