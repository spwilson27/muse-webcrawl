Eastern Qing tombs

The Eastern Qing tombs (Chinese: 清東陵; pinyin: Qīng Dōnglíng) are an Imperial mausoleum complex of the Qing dynasty located in Zunhua, Hebei Province, about 125 kilometres (78 mi) north-east of Beijing. It is the largest, most complete, and best preserved extant mausoleum complex in China, covering some 80 square kilometres. Altogether, five emperors, fifteen empresses, 136 imperial concubines, three princes and two princesses of the Qing dynasty are buried here.

History

The site was chosen in 1661 by the Shunzhi Emperor, the first Qing emperor to rule from Beijing, who is said to have selected the auspicious location beneath Changrui Mountain during a hunting expedition. Construction followed the Chinese principles of feng shui, with the tombs laid out along a sacred way modelled on the Ming tombs. Major mausoleums include Xiaoling (Shunzhi), Jingling (Kangxi), Yuling (Qianlong), Dingling (Xianfeng) and Huiling (Tongzhi), together with the tombs of the Empress Dowager Cixi and Empress Dowager Ci'an.

Looting

In 1928 the necropolis was plundered by troops under the warlord Sun Dianying, who dynamited the underground palaces of the Qianlong Emperor and the Empress Dowager Cixi and stripped them of vast treasures, jewels and pearls — one of the most notorious tomb robberies in Chinese history. Further thefts occurred in the 1940s.

World Heritage status

The Eastern Qing tombs were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000, together with the Western Qing tombs and other Imperial Tombs of the Ming and Qing Dynasties, in recognition of their outstanding architecture, landscape design and testimony to Chinese funerary culture.
