National Film Awards

National Film Awards
Country: India
First awarded: 1954 (for 1953 films)
Presented by: Government of India
Administrator: Directorate of Film Festivals

The National Film Awards are among the most prominent film awards in India. Established in 1954, they are administered by the Government of India's Directorate of Film Festivals and are presented annually to honour films and artists across the country's many language industries.

History

The awards were first given in 1954 for films released in 1953, initially under the name "State Awards for Films". The ceremony traditionally features the President of India presenting the awards. Winners receive medallions — the Golden Lotus (Swarna Kamal) and Silver Lotus (Rajat Kamal) — along with cash prizes and certificates.

Categories

Awards are given in feature film, non-feature film and writing-on-cinema sections, with categories such as Best Feature Film, Best Direction, Best Actor and Best Actress, as well as awards for films in each of the languages specified in the Constitution of India. The Dadasaheb Phalke Award, India's highest award in cinema, is presented at the same ceremony for lifetime contribution to Indian cinema.
