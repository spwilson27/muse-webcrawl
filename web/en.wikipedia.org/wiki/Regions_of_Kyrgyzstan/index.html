Regions of Kyrgyzstan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The seven regions (oblasts) of Kyrgyzstan

Kyrgyzstan is divided into seven regions (Kyrgyz: облус, oblus; Russian: область, oblast), administered by appointed governors, together with two independent cities (Kyrgyz: шаар, shaar) — the capital Bishkek and Osh — which have region-level status. The regions are subdivided into districts (raions), which are further divided into rural communities.

Administration

Each region is headed by a governor (plenipotentiary representative of the government) appointed by the president/central government. Regions coordinate state services — education, health, agriculture and infrastructure — across their districts. The cities of Bishkek and Osh are governed by elected councils and mayors and are not part of any region, although Bishkek serves as the administrative centre of Chüy Region and Osh of Osh Region.

History

The oblast system was inherited from the Soviet Kirghiz SSR, whose divisions were repeatedly redrawn (oblasts were abolished entirely between 1959 and 1970). The modern seven-region structure dates largely from 1990–1999, when Batken Region was created (1999) from parts of Osh Region, and district boundaries in the Fergana Valley were adjusted amid the complex enclaves of the region.
