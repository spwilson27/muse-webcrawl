Castanea

Castanea is a genus of eight or nine species of deciduous trees and shrubs in the beech family Fagaceae, native to temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. They are known as chestnuts; their edible nuts have been a staple food for millennia.

Species

Major species include the sweet chestnut C. sativa of Europe, the American chestnut C. dentata — once dominant in eastern North American forests before chestnut blight devastated it in the 20th century — the Chinese chestnut C. mollissima, and the Japanese chestnut C. crenata.

Uses

Nuts are eaten roasted, ground into flour, or used in confectionery such as marrons glacés. The durable, tannin-rich timber is valued for fencing and furniture. See Chestnut and Chestnut blight.
