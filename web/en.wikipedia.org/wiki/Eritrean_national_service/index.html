Eritrean national service

Eritrean national service
EstablishedNational Service Proclamation, 1995
Legal duration18 months (6 months military training + 12 months service)
Applies toMen and women aged 18–50
Main training centreSawa, Gash-Barka Region

National service in Eritrea is a compulsory programme under which Eritrean citizens are liable for military and civilian service. Although the 1995 proclamation sets a term of 18 months, in practice service has often been extended indefinitely, a policy that has drawn sustained international criticism and driven large numbers of Eritreans to flee the country.

Structure

Conscripts typically undertake their final school year and military training at the Sawa Defence Training Center, followed by deployment either to army units or to ministries, state enterprises and development projects, at minimal pay. Women as well as men are conscripted, with exemptions limited largely to the medically unfit.

Extension and criticism

After the 1998–2000 border war with Ethiopia, demobilisation stalled and service became effectively open-ended under the Warsay-Yikealo development campaign. A UN Commission of Inquiry described the system as amounting to enslavement and a crime against humanity; the Eritrean government defends it as necessary for national defence and development in a hostile region.
