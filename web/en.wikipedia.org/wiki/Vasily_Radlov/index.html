Vasily Vasilyevich Radlov (Russian: Василий Васильевич Радлов; born Friedrich Wilhelm Radloff; 17 January 1837 – 12 May 1918) was a German-born Russian orientalist regarded as the founder of Turkology, the scientific study of the Turkic peoples, their languages, folklore and history.

Career in Siberia

Radlov studied oriental languages and philosophy at Berlin, then moved to Russia in 1858, taking Russian citizenship and the name Vasily Vasilyevich. From 1860 he taught at the mining school in Barnaul in the Altai region of Siberia. Over the following two decades he made repeated expeditions among the Turkic peoples of Siberia and Central Asia — Altaians, Khakas, Shors, Teleuts, Kazakhs, Kyrgyz, Uzbeks and others — recording languages, epics, shamanic ritual and material culture.

Scholarship

His ten-volume Proben der Volkslitteratur der türkischen Stämme ("Specimens of the Folk Literature of the Turkic Tribes", 1866–1907) collected vast quantities of oral texts, including early recordings of the Kyrgyz Manas epic cycle. His four-volume comparative dictionary, Versuch eines Wörterbuches der Türk-Dialecte (1893–1911), remained a standard reference for decades. In 1891 he led the Orkhon expedition to Mongolia, whose findings led to the decipherment context of the Old Turkic runic inscriptions; he published the inscriptions and the monuments of the Second Turkic Khaganate.

Later life

From 1884 Radlov worked in St. Petersburg as an academician, directing the Asiatic Museum and, from 1894, the Museum of Anthropology and Ethnography (Kunstkamera), which he reorganised and opened to wider research use. He trained a generation of Russian Turkologists and ethnographers before his death in Petrograd in 1918.
