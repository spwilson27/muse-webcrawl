Continue (video games)

In video games, a continue is an option that allows a player to keep playing after losing all of their lives, typically restarting from the beginning of the level or the point where the game ended, rather than starting the entire game over. The feature originated in arcade games, where inserting another coin bought additional play, and its countdown screen — often "Continue? 9, 8, 7..." — became a familiar element of arcade culture.

OriginArcade games, late 1970s–1980s
MechanicResume play after game over
Related conceptsLives, game over, checkpoints, save points

History

Continues appeared as arcade operators sought to increase revenue and as games grew longer and harder to finish on a single credit. Games such as Fantasy (1981) and later beat-'em-ups and shoot-'em-ups offered continues, sometimes with limits or at the cost of resetting the player's score. The continue countdown created a moment of pressure in which the player had to decide whether to pay to keep going.

Home versions and modern usage

When arcade games were ported to home consoles, continues were often limited or unlimited for free, changing the difficulty balance. In modern games the classic continue has largely been replaced by checkpoint and save systems, respawning, and difficulty settings, though some genres retain limited continues as a deliberate design constraint. Revival mechanics in contemporary games, sometimes purchased or earned, are a descendant of the arcade continue.
