Tapas
Course: Appetiser / snack
Place of origin: Spain
Serving: Hot or cold, small plates

Tapas (Spanish: [ˈtapas]) are appetisers or snacks in Spanish cuisine, served hot or cold in small portions. They may be combined to make a full meal and are central to Spain's bar culture, enjoyed standing with drinks while moving between bars (tapeo).

History

The word tapa means lid or cover. Popular explanations hold that slices of bread or ham were placed over glasses of sherry or wine to keep out flies and dust, or that King Alfonso X ordered small portions with wine for health. In Andalusia a free tapa traditionally accompanied each drink, a custom still strong in cities such as Granada.

Common tapas

  • Patatas bravas — fried potatoes with spicy sauce and aioli
  • Gambas al ajillo — prawns in garlic oil
  • Tortilla española — potato and egg omelette
  • Jamón ibérico, croquetas, pulpo a la gallega, pimientos de padrón

Culture

Tapas are a social institution: sharing small dishes encourages conversation and grazing over an evening rather than a single seated course. The format has been adopted internationally, influencing small-plates dining worldwide.
