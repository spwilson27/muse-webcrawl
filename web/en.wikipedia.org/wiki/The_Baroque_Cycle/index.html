The Baroque Cycle

The Baroque Cycle is a series of historical novels by Neal Stephenson, published as three volumes — Quicksilver (2003), The Confusion (2004) and The System of the World (2004) — comprising eight books and some 2,600 pages. Set mainly between 1655 and 1715, it follows the birth of modern science, finance and computing amid the wars and court intrigues of Baroque Europe.

Story

The narrative weaves three strands: Daniel Waterhouse, a Puritan natural philosopher and friend of Isaac Newton; Eliza, a resourceful former harem captive who becomes a spy and financier; and "Half-Cocked Jack" Shaftoe, a vagabond soldier and adventurer. Historical figures including Newton, Leibniz, William of Orange, Louis XIV and Samuel Pepys appear throughout. The cycle is a loose prequel to Stephenson's Cryptonomicon, sharing the Waterhouse and Shaftoe families.

Themes

Major themes include the Newton–Leibniz calculus dispute, the creation of central banking and paper money, alchemy's transformation into chemistry, and the Glorious Revolution. The System of the World won the Locus Award for Best Science Fiction Novel.
