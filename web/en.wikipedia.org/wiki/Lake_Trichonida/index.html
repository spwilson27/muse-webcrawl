Lake Trichonida (Greek: Λίμνη Τριχωνίδα) is the largest natural lake in Greece, located in the eastern part of Aetolia-Acarnania in western Greece, southeast of the city of Agrinio and at the foot of Mount Panetoliko. It covers about 98.6 square kilometres, is 19 kilometres long, and reaches a maximum depth of 58 metres.

Geography and ecology

The lake occupies a tectonic depression and is separated by a narrow sill from the smaller Lake Lysimachia to its west, to which it is connected; both lakes drain ultimately to the Acheloos River. Millions of years ago Trichonida formed part of a much larger lake system covering the region. Its shores combine reed beds, olive groves and plane-tree thickets, and its waters and surrounding wetlands are protected under the Natura 2000 network for their birdlife, fish — including endemic species such as the Trichonida goby-like dromitsa — and other fauna. Towns and villages around the lake include Thermo, an inland resort town near the eastern shore.
