Heavy tank

A heavy tank is a class of tank that generally provided equal or greater firepower and armour protection than lighter tanks, at the cost of mobility, weight and size. Heavy tanks played a significant role from the end of the First World War through the early Cold War.

History

Early examples include the French Char 2C and the British Mark series. In the Second World War, Germany fielded the Tiger I, Tiger II and Panzer VIII Maus, while the Soviet Union deployed the KV and IS series; the United States produced the M26 Pershing and the heavy T29/T30 prototypes. Post-war heavies included the American M103, British Conqueror and Soviet T-10.

Decline

By the 1960s improvements in guns, armour and engines allowed the main battle tank to combine heavy-tank firepower and protection with medium-tank mobility, and the heavy tank class became obsolete.
