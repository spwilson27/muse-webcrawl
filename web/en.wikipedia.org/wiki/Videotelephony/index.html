Videotelephony

Videotelephony (also videophony) is the two-way or multipoint reception and transmission of audio and video signals allowing people in different locations to communicate in real time. It encompasses dedicated videophones, room conferencing systems and software video calling.

History

AT&T demonstrated the Picturephone at the 1964 New York World's Fair, but high cost doomed early services. ISDN-based room systems spread in businesses in the 1990s. Broadband internet, webcams and compression standards (H.264, VP8/VP9, AV1) enabled free mass-market services: Skype (video from 2005), FaceTime (2010) and Zoom (2011).

Use

Videotelephony became ubiquitous during the COVID-19 pandemic for remote work, education and telemedicine. Concerns include fatigue ("Zoom fatigue"), privacy and bandwidth inequality.

Technology

Modern systems use packet-switched IP networks, echo cancellation, and adaptive bitrate codecs; protocols include SIP, H.323 and WebRTC.
