Euungulata

Euungulata ("true ungulates") is a clade of hoofed mammals comprising the two living orders of ungulates: the odd-toed ungulates (Perissodactyla), such as horses, rhinoceroses, and tapirs, and the even-toed ungulates (Artiodactyla), such as cattle, deer, pigs, camels, and hippopotamuses. The grouping was proposed on the basis of molecular phylogenetic studies showing that these two orders are sister groups.

Classification and debate

Traditional classification, based on morphology, sometimes united perissodactyls with hyraxes and elephants in groups such as Altungulata, but molecular evidence consistently places Perissodactyla closest to Artiodactyla within the superorder Laurasiatheria. Within Euungulata, cetaceans (whales and dolphins) are nested inside Artiodactyla, making the even-toed ungulates, in the cladistic sense, include the whales; some classifications therefore use the name Cetartiodactyla for that combined group.

Euungulata belongs to the broader clade Euungulata within Ferungulata, which also includes carnivorans, pangolins, and bats' relatives depending on the analysis. The earliest euungulates appeared in the early Eocene, roughly 55 million years ago.
