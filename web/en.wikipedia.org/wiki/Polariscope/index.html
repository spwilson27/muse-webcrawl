Polariscope

A polariscope used to examine a gemstone

A polariscope is an optical instrument for detecting and measuring the optical properties of materials using polarised light. In its simplest form it consists of two polarising filters — a polariser below the specimen and an analyser above it — between which a transparent sample is placed and observed.

Uses

In gemmology, the polariscope is a standard tool for determining whether a gemstone is singly refractive, doubly refractive, or an aggregate, which helps to identify the stone. Because stressed transparent materials become birefringent, polariscopes are also used in engineering photoelasticity to visualise stress distributions in glass and plastic models: under crossed polarisers, stress patterns appear as coloured fringes (isochromatics) and dark bands (isoclinics). Related instruments are used in mineralogy and petrography, where a polarising microscope examines thin sections of rock.
