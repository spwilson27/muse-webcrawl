Karl Georg Büchner (17 October 1813 – 19 February 1837) was a German dramatist and writer of poetry and prose, considered one of the most important figures in German literature despite dying at the age of 23.

Life

Büchner studied medicine at Strasbourg and Giessen, where he became involved in revolutionary politics. His pamphlet The Hessian Courier (1834), with its call "Peace to the huts! War on the palaces!", forced him to flee to Strasbourg. He later moved to Zurich, where he lectured on comparative anatomy before dying of typhus.

Works

His play Danton's Death (1835) examines the French Revolution; the comedy Leonce and Lena satirises court life; the novella Lenz portrays a writer's descent into madness. His unfinished drama Woyzeck, published posthumously, became a landmark of modern theatre and the basis of Alban Berg's opera Wozzeck. Germany's most prestigious literary award, the Georg Büchner Prize, is named after him.
