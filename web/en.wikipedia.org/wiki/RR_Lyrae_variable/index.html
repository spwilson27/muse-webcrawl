RR Lyrae variable

RR Lyrae variables are periodic variable stars, commonly found in globular clusters, that are used as standard candles to measure distances in astronomy. They are pulsating horizontal-branch stars of spectral class A or F, with a mass of around half the Sun's, named after the prototype and brightest example, the star RR Lyrae in the constellation Lyra.

Characteristics

RR Lyrae stars pulsate with periods of typically 0.2 to 1 day, most commonly about 0.5 days, with brightness amplitudes from 0.3 to 2 magnitudes. They are subdivided into RRab stars, with asymmetric steep-rising light curves and longer periods; RRc stars, with more sinusoidal light curves and shorter periods; and RRd stars, which pulsate in two modes simultaneously. Many show the Blazhko effect, a long-term modulation of amplitude and period. Because they are core-helium-burning stars of similar luminosity, their absolute magnitude depends mainly on metallicity, enabling distance estimates.

Distance scale

The period–luminosity–metallicity relations of RR Lyrae variables make them reliable distance indicators out to about a megaparsec with modern calibrations. They are particularly important for tracing old stellar populations, the structure of the Milky Way halo, and distances to globular clusters and nearby dwarf galaxies. Surveys such as OGLE and the Gaia mission have catalogued tens of thousands of them.
