Santillana is a Spanish publishing group founded in 1960 by Jesús de Polanco and Francisco Pérez González. It became the leading educational publisher in Spain and Latin America and the founding core of the PRISA media group, alongside the newspaper El País and the Cadena SER radio network.

History and business

Santillana grew by producing textbooks adapted to the curricula of each Spanish-speaking country, expanding across Latin America during the 1960s and 1970s. Its general-publishing imprints have included Alfaguara, Taurus, Aguilar and Suma de Letras, publishing major Spanish-language authors. In 2020 PRISA, then under the chairmanship legacy of Juan Luis Cebrián's era, sold a majority stake in Santillana's Spanish and Latin American education business to the Finnish group Sanoma, retaining a minority share.
