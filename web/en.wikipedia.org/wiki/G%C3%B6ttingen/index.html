Göttingen is a university city in Lower Saxony, Germany, on the river Leine. First mentioned in 953 as Gutingi, it received town rights around 1200 and was a member of the Hanseatic League. The River Leine flows through the city, and its historic centre is ringed by a medieval rampart.

University

The University of Göttingen (Georg August University), founded in 1734 by King George II of Great Britain in his capacity as Elector of Hanover and opened in 1737, made the city one of Europe's great centres of learning. More than 40 Nobel laureates have been associated with the university and city, including Max Planck, Werner Heisenberg, and Otto Hahn; Carl Friedrich Gauss spent his career at its observatory. A memorial tradition holds that students kiss the Gänseliesel ("Goose Girl") statue on the old town hall fountain upon completing their doctorates.

History

Göttingen suffered little damage in the Second World War and surrendered to American forces in April 1945. The Göttingen Manifesto of 1957, signed by 18 leading nuclear scientists including Hahn and Heisenberg, protested plans to arm the West German military with nuclear weapons. Today the city hosts several Max Planck Institutes and the German Aerospace Center.
