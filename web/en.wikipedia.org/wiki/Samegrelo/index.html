Samegrelo

Samegrelo (Georgian: სამეგრელო), rendered Mingrelia or Mengrelia in English sources, is a historic province of Georgia in the western part of the country, lying between the Black Sea, the Caucasus, and the Rioni and Tskhenistsqali rivers, with Abkhazia to the northwest and Imereti to the east. It is the homeland of the Mingrelians, a sub-ethnic group of Georgians who speak the Mingrelian language, one of the Kartvelian languages. The principal city is Zugdidi; the region's Black Sea port is Poti.

Samegrelo occupies the lowland and foothills between the Black Sea coast and the Greater Caucasus.

History

The territory formed part of the ancient kingdom of Colchis, famed in Greek myth as the land of the Golden Fleece, and later of the kingdom of Lazica (Egrisi). Following the fragmentation of the medieval Georgian kingdom in the fifteenth century, Samegrelo emerged as a semi-independent principality ruled by the Dadiani family, nominally under Imeretian kingship but frequently autonomous, caught between Ottoman and Persian expansion and growing Russian influence. In 1803 Prince Grigol Dadiani placed the principality under Russian protection, and in 1867 it was fully annexed to the Russian Empire.

In the Soviet period the region was part of the Georgian SSR. After Georgian independence, Samegrelo — and particularly Zugdidi — was a stronghold of support for the ousted president Zviad Gamsakhurdia, himself a Mingrelian, during the civil war of the early 1990s. Its position adjoining Abkhazia meant it received large numbers of ethnic Georgian displaced persons after the war in Abkhazia.

Geography and economy

The region comprises the Kolkheti lowland, a humid subtropical plain once extensively marshy, backed by foothills rising toward the Caucasus. Agriculture — tea, citrus, hazelnuts, maize, and viticulture — is the mainstay of the economy, and Mingrelian cuisine is noted for spicy dishes such as elarji (maize meal with sulguni cheese) and for the hot adjika paste.

Administratively, most of historic Samegrelo, together with Upper Svaneti, forms the modern region (mkhare) of Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti.
