Lomond Hills
Highest point: West Lomond, 522 m
Location: Fife, Scotland

The Lomond Hills (sometimes also referred to as the Paps of Fife) are a range of hills in central Fife, Scotland. They lie on the northern shore side of the Firth of Forth area and dominate the skyline of central Fife. The range consists principally of two prominent peaks: West Lomond (522 m / 1,713 ft), the highest point in Fife, and East Lomond (434 m / 1,424 ft), also known as Falkland Hill.

Geology

The hills are formed from Devonian volcanic rocks — igneous intrusions and lava flows resistant to erosion — which stand above the surrounding sandstone lowlands of the Howe of Fife. Outcrops of limestone and sandstone around their flanks were historically quarried.

History and use

East Lomond was the site of an Iron Age hill fort, and a Roman signal station may also have stood there. The hills form the core of the Lomond Hills Regional Park, popular with walkers, paragliders and mountain bikers. Distinctive rock formations on the slopes include the Bunnet Stane and the Maiden Bore. The Falkland Estate and the village of Falkland lie at the foot of East Lomond.
