Thomas Gabriel Read (21 August 1824 – 31 October 1894) was an Australian gold prospector who discovered gold in the Otago region of New Zealand in 1861, triggering the Otago gold rush.

Read was born in Tasmania and worked as a merchant sailor before joining the gold rushes in California and Victoria. Arriving in Otago in early 1861, he prospected in the Tuapeka district and on 25 May 1861 found gold in a gully near the Tuapeka River, a tributary of the Clutha River in the Lawrence area. The site became known as Gabriel's Gully in his honour.

The Otago gold rush

News of Read's discovery spread quickly. Within months thousands of prospectors arrived in Otago, and by the end of 1861 the population of the goldfields had swelled enormously, transforming the young settlement of Dunedin into the largest and wealthiest city in New Zealand for a time. The provincial government rewarded Read with a bonus of £1,000 for the discovery, conditional on its leading to a payable goldfield, which it did.

Later life

Read later prospected elsewhere in New Zealand and returned to Tasmania, where he spent periods in and out of hospital with mental illness. He died in Tasmania in 1894. Gabriel's Gully remains a historic site near Lawrence, Otago, and a memorial marks the location of his find.
