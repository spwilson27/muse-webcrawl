Theatre of Japan

Major forms: Noh, kyōgen, kabuki, bunraku, shingeki
UNESCO listings: Nohgaku (2008), bunraku (2008), kabuki (2008)

Traditional theatre of Japan combines music, dance and mime into highly codified art forms. Its four classical genres are noh, kyōgen, kabuki and bunraku (ningyō jōruri puppet theatre), all of which survive as living traditions and have been inscribed by UNESCO on its list of Intangible Cultural Heritage.

Noh and kyōgen

Noh, developed in the 14th century by Kan'ami and his son Zeami under the patronage of the shōgun Ashikaga Yoshimitsu, is a stately masked drama of ghosts, warriors and gods, performed with chanting (utai), an ensemble of flute and drums, and slow, stylised movement on a roofed, bare stage. Zeami's treatises codified its aesthetic of yūgen (subtle profundity). Kyōgen, performed between noh plays or independently, is its comic counterpart: short farces in colloquial language mocking masters, servants and monks.

Kabuki and bunraku

Kabuki emerged in the early 17th century, traditionally credited to the dancer Izumo no Okuni (1603). After women and then young men were banned from the stage, it developed into an all-male form in which female roles are played by specialists (onnagata). Kabuki is spectacular and popular, featuring revolving stages, trap lifts, elaborate costume and make-up (kumadori), and the dramatic frozen poses called mie. Bunraku, its puppet counterpart, flourished in Osaka in the same period: large puppets worked by three visible handlers are accompanied by a chanter (tayū) and shamisen player; the playwright Chikamatsu Monzaemon wrote masterpieces for both forms.

Modern theatre

From the Meiji period, shimpa and then shingeki ("new theatre") introduced Western realism and playwrights such as Ibsen and Chekhov. Post-war figures like Yukio Mishima and the avant-garde director Shūji Terayama, and later companies such as Yukio Ninagawa's, brought Japanese theatre to international audiences, while the Takarazuka Revue (founded 1913), an all-female musical theatre company, remains a major popular institution.
