Marcomannic Wars

Date: 166 – 180 AD
Location: Danube frontier, Pannonia, northern Italy
Result: Roman victory; frontier restored
Combatants: Roman Empire vs Marcomanni, Quadi, Iazyges and other Germanic and Sarmatian peoples

The Marcomannic Wars were a series of conflicts fought by the Roman Empire against Germanic and Sarmatian peoples along the Danube frontier during the reign of Marcus Aurelius, from about 166 to 180 AD. They mark the opening of the long struggle between Rome and the peoples beyond its northern borders.

Course of the wars

Pressure from migrating peoples pushed the Marcomanni, Quadi, Lombards, and Iazyges against the frontier; in 166–167 invaders crossed the Danube and reached northern Italy, besieging Aquileia — the first time hostile forces had entered Italy in centuries. The Antonine Plague, brought back from the Parthian campaign, devastated the army and empire even as Rome counter-attacked. Marcus Aurelius, ruling with Lucius Verus until 169 and then alone, campaigned almost continuously, establishing the legions II Italica and III Italica and planning new provinces of Marcomannia and Sarmatia.

Conclusion

Marcus Aurelius died at Vindobona (Vienna) in 180 during a final offensive. His son Commodus quickly negotiated peace, abandoning the annexation plans and restoring the Danube frontier. The wars are vividly depicted on the Column of Marcus Aurelius in Rome, including the famous "rain miracle" episode.
