Inverter

A power inverter converts direct current (DC) into alternating current (AC).

An inverter may refer to:

  • Power inverter, an electrical device that converts direct current (DC) to alternating current (AC)
  • Inverter (logic gate), a digital logic gate that implements logical negation (a NOT gate)
  • Solar inverter, a device that converts the DC output of solar panels into AC for the grid or home use

Power inverter

A power inverter changes DC electricity from sources such as batteries or solar panels into AC electricity suitable for powering mains equipment. Inverters range from small units that power a laptop from a car battery to utility-scale devices that form a key part of grid-tied solar and wind installations and of HVDC transmission links. Output waveforms may be square wave, modified sine wave, or pure sine wave, the latter being compatible with all AC loads.

Logic gate

In digital electronics, an inverter or NOT gate is a logic gate that outputs the opposite logic level to its input. In CMOS technology it is implemented with a complementary pair of MOSFETs and forms the basic building block from which more complex gates and memory cells are constructed.
