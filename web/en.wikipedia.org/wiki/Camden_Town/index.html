Camden Town

Camden High Street, the centre of the district's market area.

Camden Town, often shortened to Camden, is a district of northwest London, England, in the London Borough of Camden, about 2.5 miles (4 km) north of Charing Cross. It is identified in the London Plan as one of 35 major centres in Greater London and is famous for its markets, music venues and alternative culture.

History

The area takes its name from Charles Pratt, 1st Earl Camden, and began to be developed in 1791 on what had been the Kentish Town manor. Growth accelerated with the Regent's Canal (opened 1820) and the arrival of the railways, which made Camden an important freight interchange; the Roundhouse locomotive shed of 1847 is now a celebrated arts venue. In the twentieth century the district became associated with artists, writers and musicians.

Camden Market

The markets clustered around Camden Lock and the Stables began in the 1970s and grew into one of London's most-visited attractions, drawing millions of visitors a year to stalls selling fashion, crafts, antiques and street food. Camden's shops are famous for flamboyant facades along Camden High Street.

Culture

Camden has been a centre of live music from the punk era onward, with venues such as the Electric Ballroom, Dingwalls and KOKO. Singer Amy Winehouse lived in the district, where a statue of her stands in the Stables Market. The area is served by Camden Town station on the Northern line of the London Underground.
