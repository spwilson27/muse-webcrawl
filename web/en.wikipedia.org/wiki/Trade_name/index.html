Trade name

A trade name is the name under which a business or individual conducts commerce, which may differ from the legal name of the company or owner. In the United States such names are commonly registered as a DBA ("doing business as"), also called a fictitious business name or assumed name.

Legal aspects

Registering a trade name is typically a local or state-level formality intended to disclose who is behind a business; it does not by itself create trademark rights. A trade name used as a brand for goods or services may, however, function as a trademark and gain protection through use in commerce. The distinction matters: a company's legal name identifies the entity for contracts and liability, while its trade names identify its public-facing businesses.

Examples and practice

Sole proprietors frequently operate under trade names rather than their personal names, and large corporations may trade under many names — for example, a holding company whose consumer brands bear no resemblance to the parent name. Franchises illustrate the split clearly: the storefront bears the franchisor's trade name while the legal operator is the local franchisee's company.
