Brown-water navy

TypeRiverine naval force
RoleInland waterway warfare
ExamplesUnion Navy river squadrons (US Civil War); US Navy River Patrol Force (Vietnam War)

A brown-water navy or riverine navy is a naval force designed and equipped primarily for operations in rivers, estuaries and other shallow inland waters — the "brown water" of sediment-laden rivers, as opposed to the "blue water" of the open ocean and the "green water" of coastal zones.

History

Riverine warfare is ancient, but the term is particularly associated with the United States Navy. During the American Civil War, the Union Navy's river squadrons, including ironclad gunboats, fought for control of the Mississippi River and its tributaries. During the Vietnam War, the US Navy created Task Force 117, the River Patrol Force, operating PBR patrol boats in the Mekong Delta as part of Operation Game Warden.

Modern role

Modern brown-water units employ shallow-draft patrol boats, landing craft and riverine command boats for interdiction, troop insertion and waterway security. In 2006 the US Navy established the Navy Expeditionary Combat Command, reviving dedicated riverine squadrons, later reorganised as Coastal Riverine Force units.
