List of the most intense tropical cyclones

Intensity of a tropical cyclone is conventionally measured by minimum central pressure and by maximum sustained winds. The most intense tropical cyclone ever reliably measured was Typhoon Tip in the north-west Pacific in October 1979, with a minimum pressure of 870 hPa (25.69 inHg) and one-minute sustained winds estimated near 305 km/h (190 mph). In the Atlantic basin, Hurricane Wilma (2005) holds the record with a minimum pressure of 882 hPa.

By basin

  • Atlantic: Wilma (2005), 882 hPa; Gilbert (1988), 888 hPa; the "Labor Day" hurricane of 1935, 892 hPa.
  • North-east Pacific: Patricia (2015), strongest sustained winds reliably measured anywhere, 345 km/h (215 mph).
  • North-west Pacific: Tip (1979), 870 hPa — the world record. The basin produces the most intense cyclones overall.
  • North Indian Ocean: the 1999 Odisha cyclone, estimated 912 hPa.
  • South-West Indian Ocean: Cyclone Gafilo (2004), estimated 895 hPa.
  • Australian region: Cyclone Monica (2006) and Cyclone Marcus (2018) are among the strongest.
  • South Pacific: Cyclone Winston (2016), 884 hPa, the strongest recorded in the Southern Hemisphere.
