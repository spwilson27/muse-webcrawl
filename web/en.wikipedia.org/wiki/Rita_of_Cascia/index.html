Rita of Cascia

Saint Rita of Cascia (born Margherita Lotti; May 1381 – 22 May 1457) was an Italian widow and Augustinian nun venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic Church. She is known as the patroness of lost and impossible causes, sickness, wounds, and marital problems.

BornMay 1381, Roccaporena, Papal States
Died22 May 1457, Cascia
Canonized24 May 1900, by Pope Leo XIII
Feast day22 May

Life

Married young to an abusive husband who was later murdered, Rita forgave his killers and sought to prevent a vendetta. After the deaths of her husband and sons, she entered the Augustinian convent in Cascia, where she lived for forty years, known for her prayer and austerity. Tradition holds that she bore a wound on her forehead associated with the Crown of Thorns.

Veneration

Her body, reportedly incorrupt, rests in the Basilica of Saint Rita in Cascia, a major pilgrimage site. She was beatified in 1626 and canonized by Pope Leo XIII in 1900.
