Majoritarianism

Majoritarianism
ConceptPolitical philosophy / decision rule
Core claimThe majority should hold primary decision-making power
Contrasted withConsensus democracy, consociationalism, minority rights

Majoritarianism is a political philosophy or ideology holding that a majority — based on criteria such as a religion, language, social class or other category — should hold the primary power to make decisions in society, and that majority decisions should prevail. As a model of democracy, it is associated with the Westminster system, in which a single-party majority government typically rules with few institutional checks.

Majoritarian democracy

The political scientist Arend Lijphart contrasted the majoritarian (Westminster) model of democracy with the consensus model. Majoritarian systems typically feature first-past-the-post elections, two-party competition, a unitary and centralised state, an unwritten or flexible constitution, and the concentration of executive power — exemplified by the United Kingdom and New Zealand (before its adoption of proportional representation).

Criticism

Critics, from Alexis de Tocqueville and John Stuart Mill onward, have warned of the "tyranny of the majority": unconstrained majority rule can oppress minorities and ignore their interests. Constitutional limits, bills of rights, proportional representation, federalism and power-sharing arrangements are commonly proposed as correctives. In deeply divided societies, majoritarianism is criticised for permanently excluding ethnic or religious minorities from power.
