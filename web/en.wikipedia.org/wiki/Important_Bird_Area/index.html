Important Bird Area

An Important Bird and Biodiversity Area (IBA), formerly called an Important Bird Area, is a site identified through an internationally agreed set of criteria as being globally important for the conservation of bird populations. The programme was developed and is administered by BirdLife International and its national partners. More than 13,000 IBAs have been identified in over 200 countries and territories, covering wetlands, forests, grasslands, islands, and marine areas.

Wetlands that support large congregations of waterbirds are a classic type of IBA.

Criteria

Sites qualify under standardised categories. Category A1 covers sites regularly holding significant numbers of globally threatened species. Category A2 covers sites holding restricted-range species of an Endemic Bird Area. Category A3 covers sites holding assemblages of species restricted to a biome. Category A4 covers congregations: sites holding at least 1% of the biogeographic population of a waterbird species, or large numbers of migratory birds at bottleneck sites such as straits and mountain passes.

Identification and protection

IBA identification is carried out by national BirdLife partners using local monitoring data, and sites are documented in regional directories and a global database. Designation as an IBA does not by itself confer legal protection, but IBAs have been widely used to inform the selection of protected areas, including Natura 2000 Special Protection Areas in the European Union and sites listed under the Ramsar Convention. Monitoring of IBAs — tracking their state, pressures, and conservation responses — is a core part of the programme, and many IBAs are cared for by local conservation groups.
