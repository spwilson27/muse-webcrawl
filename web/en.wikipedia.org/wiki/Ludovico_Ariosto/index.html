Ludovico Ariosto

Born: 8 September 1474, Reggio Emilia, Duchy of Modena
Died: 6 July 1533 (aged 58), Ferrara
Occupation: Poet
Notable work: Orlando Furioso

Ludovico Ariosto (8 September 1474 – 6 July 1533) was an Italian poet of the Renaissance, best known as the author of the romance epic Orlando Furioso (1516), a continuation of Matteo Maria Boiardo's unfinished Orlando Innamorato. The poem is widely regarded as one of the masterpieces of Italian literature.

Life

Ariosto was born in Reggio Emilia, the eldest son of Count Niccolò Ariosto. He studied law at Ferrara at his father's insistence, but his real passion was poetry. From 1503 he served at the court of Cardinal Ippolito d'Este, undertaking diplomatic missions and military expeditions that he undertook reluctantly. Dismissed by the Cardinal in 1517 after refusing to follow him to Hungary, he entered the service of Duke Alfonso I d'Este, who in 1522 appointed him governor of the wild Garfagnana region. He spent his later years in Ferrara revising his epic.

Orlando Furioso

Published in its first edition in 1516 and in its definitive 46-canto form in 1532, Orlando Furioso interweaves the war of Charlemagne against the Saracens with the love-madness of the paladin Orlando, the romance of Bradamante and Ruggiero (mythical ancestors of the Este), and dozens of other tales of love, magic and chivalry. Written in ottava rima with irony, narrative virtuosity and seamless interlacing of episodes, it became one of the most influential poems in European literature, inspiring writers from Spenser and Shakespeare to Byron.

Other works

Ariosto also wrote comedies in verse modelled on Plautus and Terence, including La Cassaria and I suppositi, seven Satires in terza rima, and lyric poems in Latin and Italian.
