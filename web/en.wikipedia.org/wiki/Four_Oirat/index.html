Four Oirat

MongolianDörben Oirad ("Four Oirats")
Period15th–18th centuries
RegionDzungaria and the Altai, Central Asia
SuccessorsDzungar Khanate, Kalmyk Khanate

The Four Oirat (Mongolian: Дөрвөн Ойрад, Dörben Oirad) was a confederation of the four major tribes of the Oirat Mongols — the Choros, Torghut, Dörbet, and Khoid — that dominated the steppe west of Mongolia from the fifteenth century onward, following the decline of the Northern Yuan.

History

Under leaders such as Esen Taishi, who captured the Ming emperor in 1449, the Oirats briefly unified the Mongols. Internal rivalries later split the confederation: the Torghuts migrated west to the Volga, founding the Kalmyk Khanate, while the Choros-led Dzungar Khanate became the dominant power in Central Asia in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries.

Decline

The Dzungar–Qing wars ended with the Qing conquest of Dzungaria in the 1750s and the destruction of the Dzungar state. Oirat descendants today include the Kalmyks of the Caspian region and Oirat communities in Mongolia and Xinjiang.
