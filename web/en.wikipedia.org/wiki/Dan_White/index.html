Dan White

Daniel James White (2 September 1946 – 21 October 1985) was an American politician who served as a member of the San Francisco Board of Supervisors. He assassinated Mayor George Moscone and Supervisor Harvey Milk inside City Hall on 27 November 1978.

Background

White was born in San Francisco and raised in the Visitacion Valley and Portola districts. He served in the Vietnam War as a paratrooper, then worked as a San Francisco police officer and later as a firefighter. In 1977 he was elected to the Board of Supervisors from District 8, campaigning as a conservative Democrat on a law-and-order platform, and was regarded as the board's voice of traditional blue-collar neighbourhoods.

Resignation and assassinations

Frustrated by politics and by financial pressures, White resigned his seat on 10 November 1978, but within days — after lobbying by constituents and colleagues — he asked Mayor Moscone to reappoint him. Moscone, influenced by Milk and other liberal supervisors who favoured a more progressive appointee, declined. On 27 November 1978, White entered City Hall through a basement window to avoid metal detectors, carrying his police revolver. He shot Moscone in his office, then walked to Milk's office and shot him as well. Both men died.

Trial and aftermath

White was convicted in May 1979 of voluntary manslaughter rather than murder, his defence arguing diminished capacity brought on by depression — a strategy popularly, if imprecisely, labelled the "Twinkie defense". The lenient verdict (seven years) provoked the White Night riots, in which the city's gay community and supporters clashed with police. California subsequently abolished the diminished-capacity defence.

White was paroled in January 1984 after serving five years. On 21 October 1985, he died by suicide in San Francisco. The murders and trial were later dramatised in the film Milk (2008), in which White was played by Josh Brolin.
