DefinitionA mountain summit with topographic prominence of 1,500 metres (4,921 ft) or more

An ultra-prominent peak, or ultra for short, is a mountain summit with a topographic prominence of 1,500 metres (4,921 ft) or greater. Prominence measures the height of a peak relative to the lowest contour line encircling it but no higher summit—the vertical distance one must descend before ascending a higher peak. There are roughly 1,500 ultra-prominent peaks in the world.

Denali, with a prominence of about 6,144 m, is the third most prominent peak on Earth.

Distribution and notable ultras

The most prominent peaks on Earth are Mount Everest, Aconcagua, and Denali; island high points such as Puncak Jaya and Mauna Kea also rank highly because their prominence equals their elevation above sea level. Ultras are distributed across all continents, with large concentrations in the Andes, the ranges of Central Asia, and the archipelagos of Indonesia and Papua. The term was popularized by mountaineer and list-compiler Stephen Fry in the 1980s, adapting a category originally used for peaks in Washington state.

Peak bagging

Climbing all the ultras of a region is a recognised peak bagging challenge; lists exist for areas such as Britain and Ireland (the "P1500s" within the Marilyn classification relate to a lower threshold) and for entire continents.
