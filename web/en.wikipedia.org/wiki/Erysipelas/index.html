Erysipelas (from Greek ἐρυσίπελας, "red skin") is a relatively common acute bacterial infection of the upper dermis and superficial lymphatics, usually caused by Group A Streptococcus (Streptococcus pyogenes). It is characterised by an intensely red, hot, swollen and painful patch of skin with a sharply demarcated, raised border, often accompanied by fever, chills and malaise. The face and legs are the most common sites.

Signs and diagnosis

The infection typically begins abruptly, often entering through a break in the skin such as an ulcer, insect bite, or athlete's foot. The affected area may blister, and regional lymph nodes may be enlarged and tender. Diagnosis is primarily clinical, based on the well-demarcated appearance, which distinguishes erysipelas from the deeper, less sharply bordered infection of cellulitis. Recurrence is common, especially in people with lymphoedema or venous insufficiency.

Treatment

Treatment is with antibiotics, most often oral or intravenous penicillin (or alternatives such as cephalexin, clindamycin or erythromycin in allergic patients), together with elevation of the affected limb and treatment of the portal of entry. Historically, before antibiotics, erysipelas carried significant mortality; the disease was historically known as "St Anthony's fire" (a name also applied to ergotism).
