Matrix norm

In mathematics, a matrix norm is a norm defined on the vector space of matrices. Matrix norms are used to measure the size of a matrix, to bound errors in numerical linear algebra, and to analyse the convergence of iterative algorithms. A matrix norm assigns to each matrix a non-negative number that is zero only for the zero matrix, scales linearly with scalar multiplication, and obeys the triangle inequality.

Induced norms

Given a vector norm, the induced (or operator) norm of a matrix is the largest factor by which the matrix can stretch a vector. The induced 1-norm equals the maximum absolute column sum; the induced infinity-norm equals the maximum absolute row sum; and the induced 2-norm equals the largest singular value of the matrix. Induced norms are submultiplicative, meaning the norm of a product is at most the product of the norms.

Entrywise norms

The best-known entrywise norm is the Frobenius norm, the square root of the sum of the squares of all entries, equivalently the square root of the sum of the squares of the singular values. Other entrywise norms apply the vector p-norms to the entries directly.

Schatten norms

The Schatten p-norms generalise both: they apply the p-norm to the vector of singular values. The case p = 1, the trace or nuclear norm, is widely used in compressed sensing and low-rank matrix recovery.
