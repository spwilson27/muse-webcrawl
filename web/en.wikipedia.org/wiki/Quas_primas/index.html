Quas primas

Quas primas ("In the first") is an encyclical of Pope Pius XI promulgated on December 11, 1925. It introduced the Feast of Christ the King into the liturgical calendar of the Roman Catholic Church.

Pope Pius XI, author of Quas primas

Content

The encyclical responds to the growing secularism of the age, which Pius described as a plague afflicting society — the exclusion of Christ and his law from public and political life. Against this, the Pope proclaimed the universal kingship of Christ over individuals, families, and nations, arguing that peace and order could only be restored when rulers and subjects alike recognized Christ's authority.

Pius XI instituted the feast to be celebrated on the last Sunday of October, before the Feast of All Saints. In 1969, Pope Paul VI moved the celebration to the last Sunday of the liturgical year and raised it to the rank of a solemnity, retitling it the Solemnity of Our Lord Jesus Christ, King of the Universe.

Legacy

The encyclical remains a key document of Catholic social teaching on the relationship between the Church, the state, and secular authority. The Feast of Christ the King it established is observed across much of Western Christianity.
