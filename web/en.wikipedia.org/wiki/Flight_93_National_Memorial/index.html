Flight 93 National Memorial

The Flight 93 National Memorial is a United States national memorial in Stonycreek Township, Somerset County, Pennsylvania, commemorating the passengers and crew of United Airlines Flight 93, who died resisting the hijackers of their aircraft during the September 11 attacks of 2001. The plane crashed in the field where the memorial now stands, killing all 40 passengers and crew and the four hijackers.

The Wall of Names at the memorial

Background

Flight 93 was one of four airliners hijacked on September 11, 2001. Passengers, having learned by phone of the attacks on the World Trade Center, attempted to retake the aircraft. The plane crashed into a field at speeds of over 560 miles per hour. It is believed the hijackers' target was in Washington, D.C., possibly the Capitol or the White House.

Development

A temporary memorial was established near the crash site soon after the attacks. The Flight 93 National Memorial Act of 2002 authorised a permanent memorial administered by the National Park Service. A design competition selected the design by Paul Murdoch Architects. The first phase, including the Wall of Names — forty marble slabs inscribed with the names of the passengers and crew — was dedicated on September 10, 2011, the eve of the tenth anniversary.

Features

The memorial covers about 2,200 acres. The visitor center complex opened in 2015. The Tower of Voices, a 93-foot-tall (28 m) tower containing forty wind chimes — one for each passenger and crew member — was dedicated in 2018 and completed in 2020. The crash site itself, known as the Sacred Ground, is the final resting place of the passengers and crew and is open only to their families.
