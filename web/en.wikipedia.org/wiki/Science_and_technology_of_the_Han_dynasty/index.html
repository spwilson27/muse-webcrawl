Science and technology of the Han dynasty

The Han dynasty (206 BCE – 220 CE) of ancient China, divided between the eras of Western Han (206 BCE – 9 CE, and 25–220 CE for Eastern Han) and the Xin dynasty (9–23 CE), saw great advancements in science and technology. These can be seen in improved techniques for agriculture, metallurgy, and the invention of paper, the seismometer, and other devices.

Writing materials and paper

Before paper, the Chinese wrote on bamboo strips, wooden tablets, silk and oracle bones. In 105 CE, the court eunuch Cai Lun presented the emperor with a process for making paper from tree bark, hemp waste, old rags and fishing nets. Paper was cheaper and more convenient than bamboo or silk, and it gradually spread throughout China and eventually the world.

Astronomy and mathematics

Han astronomers kept meticulous records of eclipses, sunspots and comets; the astronomical chapters of the Book of Han record a sunspot observation in 28 BCE. The armillary sphere was developed for measuring the positions of celestial bodies. Mathematical texts such as The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art compiled solutions for surveying, engineering, taxation and right triangles, and included early use of negative numbers and Gaussian-like elimination for systems of linear equations.

The seismometer

In 132 CE, the polymath Zhang Heng presented to the Han court what many historians consider the first seismometer. His ornate bronze vessel contained a pendulum mechanism; when an earthquake occurred, a ball would drop from one of eight dragon heads into the mouth of a bronze toad below, indicating the direction of the tremor. In one famous instance, the device reportedly detected a distant earthquake in Gansu that had not been felt at the capital, Luoyang.

Metallurgy and agriculture

Han ironmasters improved cast iron production with blast furnaces and developed early steel-making techniques by combining cast and wrought iron. State monopolies on iron and salt funded the empire but also standardised tool quality. Agricultural advances included the iron mouldboard plough, the seed drill (louche), and the winnowing fan.

Medicine

The Huangdi Neijing (Yellow Emperor's Inner Canon) systematised medical theory around yin-yang and the five phases. The physician Hua Tuo is credited with developing an anaesthetic powder (mafeisan) and physical exercises modelled on animals, while Zhang Zhongjing's Shanghan Lun (Treatise on Cold Damage Disorders) remains influential in traditional Chinese medicine.

Other inventions

The Han period is credited with the earliest known use of the wheelbarrow, the stern-post ship rudder, negative numbers in mathematics, and detailed maps drawn to scale. Zhang Heng also improved the water-powered armillary sphere and is credited with a primitive odometer-like device, the south-pointing chariot having been developed earlier.
