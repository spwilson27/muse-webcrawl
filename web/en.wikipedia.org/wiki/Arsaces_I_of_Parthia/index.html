Arsaces I (from Greek Arsákēs; in Parthian, Aršak) was the first king of Parthia and founder of the Arsacid dynasty, reigning from about 247 BC to 217 BC. A leader of the Parni, a nomadic tribe of the Dahae confederation, he conquered the Seleucid satrapy of Parthia in northeastern Iran and founded an empire that would endure for nearly five centuries.


A drachm of Arsaces I, shown wearing the bashlik

Rise

Taking advantage of Seleucid weakness and the revolt of the satrap Andragoras, Arsaces and his brother Tiridates led the Parni into Parthia around 245–238 BC. Arsaces was crowned at Asaak, where an eternal fire was maintained in commemoration. Later Parthian tradition dated the Arsacid era from 247 BC.

Reign

Arsaces consolidated his realm, founded cities such as Nisa — a royal residence and necropolis — and successfully resisted a major expedition by Seleucus II around 228 BC, forcing Seleucid recognition of his position. On his coinage he appears in the soft cap (bashlik) of a nomad chief, seated with a bow, an image that became the template for Parthian royal portraiture.

Legacy

All subsequent Parthian kings took the throne name Arsaces, so that "Arsaces" became effectively a title. The empire he founded grew under Mithridates I into a great power rivalling Rome.
