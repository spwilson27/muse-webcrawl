The Belgium national football team represents Belgium in men's international association football. Nicknamed the Red Devils, the team is governed by the Royal Belgian Football Association and has competed since 1904, making it one of the oldest national sides in the world. Belgium was one of the four European teams to take part in the first FIFA World Cup in 1930.

The Red Devils at the 2018 FIFA World Cup.

History

Belgium won the gold medal at the 1920 Antwerp Olympics and established a long-running rivalry with the Netherlands, contested in the Low Countries derby. After decades of modest results, a first golden era arrived in the 1980s: Belgium reached the final of Euro 1980, losing to West Germany, and finished fourth at the 1986 World Cup in Mexico, led by players such as Jean-Marie Pfaff, Eric Gerets, and Enzo Scifo.

The golden generation

A systematic overhaul of youth development produced a celebrated generation — Eden Hazard, Kevin De Bruyne, Romelu Lukaku, Thibaut Courtois, and captain Vincent Kompany — that carried Belgium to the quarter-finals of the 2014 World Cup and to third place at the 2018 World Cup in Russia, the country's best ever finish. Belgium topped the FIFA World Rankings for long stretches between 2015 and 2022 despite never winning a major trophy.

Colours and identity

The team's red shirts gave rise to the Red Devils nickname, first used in 1906. Supporters famously wear red and devil horns, and the tricolour mascot and the "Tous ensemble" chant are fixtures of tournament campaigns.
