James B. Sumner

James Batcheller Sumner (19 November 1887 – 12 August 1955) was an American chemist. He discovered that enzymes can be crystallized, a finding that demonstrated enzymes are proteins. For this work he shared the 1946 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with John Howard Northrop and Wendell Meredith Stanley.

Biography

Sumner was born in Canton, Massachusetts. At seventeen he lost his left arm in a hunting accident, but went on to study chemistry at Harvard University, graduating in 1910. He received his PhD from Harvard Medical School in 1914 under Otto Folin, and then taught at Mount Allison University in Canada before joining Cornell University in 1914, where he remained for most of his career.

Crystallization of urease

In 1926 Sumner isolated and crystallized the enzyme urease from jack beans (Canavalia ensiformis) and reported that the crystals were pure protein. The claim was initially controversial, but was confirmed in the following decade when Northrop crystallized pepsin and other digestive enzymes. Sumner's result established that enzymes are proteins and could be studied as well-defined chemical substances.

Later life

Sumner was elected to the National Academy of Sciences in 1948. He retired from Cornell in 1955 and died in Buffalo, New York, the same year.
