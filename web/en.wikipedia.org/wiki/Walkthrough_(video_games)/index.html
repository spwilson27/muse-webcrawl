Walkthrough (video games)

A walkthrough is a guide intended to improve the experience of a player in a video game, often by helping them complete the game or overcome specific obstacles. Walkthroughs may be written as text, illustrated guides, or recorded video showing a complete playthrough, sometimes with commentary. They differ from the broader category of strategy guides in that a walkthrough typically provides a step-by-step path through the game's content.

Video and text walkthroughs are common aids for difficult games

History

Print walkthroughs appeared in video game magazines and commercially published strategy guides in the 1980s and 1990s, notably from publishers such as Prima Games and BradyGames. With the growth of the internet, fan-written text walkthroughs flourished on sites such as GameFAQs, which hosted tens of thousands of user-submitted guides in plain ASCII format. Wikis devoted to individual games or franchises later took over much of this role.

Video walkthroughs

From the mid-2000s, video-sharing platforms such as YouTube made recorded walkthroughs and "Let's Play" videos widely available. A walkthrough video generally aims to show the whole game being completed, whereas a Let's Play emphasises the player's reactions and entertainment. Speedrun recordings and "no commentary" playthroughs are related formats. Some players criticise walkthrough use as spoiling discovery, while others regard them as essential accessibility tools for difficult or opaque games.
