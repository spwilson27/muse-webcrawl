Vera Drake

Vera Drake is a 2004 British period drama film written and directed by Mike Leigh. It stars Imelda Staunton as the title character, a working-class London woman in 1950 who secretly performs illegal abortions for young women, without charging them, while working as a domestic cleaner.

Plot

Vera is a cheerful, selfless mother devoted to her family. Unknown to them, for twenty years she has "helped young girls out" by inducing miscarriages with a syringe of carbolic soap solution, believing she takes no money — though her intermediary keeps the fees paid. When one patient nearly dies, police trace Vera, whose arrest devastates her family. She pleads guilty and is sentenced to two and a half years in prison.

Reception

The film won the Golden Lion at the 2004 Venice Film Festival, where Staunton also won Best Actress; she received Academy Award and BAFTA nominations, and the film was widely praised for its period detail and its even-handed treatment of abortion before the Abortion Act 1967.
