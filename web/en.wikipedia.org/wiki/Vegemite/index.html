Vegemite

TypeYeast extract spread
Place of originAustralia
Created1922
InventorCyril Callister
OwnerBega Cheese (since 2017)

Vegemite is a thick, dark-brown Australian food spread made from leftover brewer's yeast extract with various vegetable and spice additives. It is salty, slightly bitter, and rich in B vitamins, and is most commonly spread thinly on buttered toast, sandwiches, and crumpets. Vegemite is regarded as an iconic Australian food.

History

Vegemite was developed in Melbourne in 1922 by food technologist Cyril Callister for the Fred Walker & Co. company, at a time when imports of the British spread Marmite were disrupted after World War I. Made from yeast extract obtained from the Carlton & United brewery, it was enriched with B vitamins and marketed as a nutritious food, including for children. Adoption was initially slow, but sales grew through advertising campaigns, the famous "Happy Little Vegemites" jingle of 1954, and distribution to Australian troops during World War II.

The brand was long produced by Kraft. In 2017 the Australian dairy company Bega Cheese acquired Vegemite, returning the brand to Australian ownership.

Use and culture

Vegemite is typically spread very thinly over buttered bread, often with cheese. It is also used as a flavouring in cooking. Variants have included a reduced-salt version and limited-edition blends. The spread's strong umami taste is famously divisive among non-Australians, and Vegemite is a frequent cultural reference point, mentioned, for example, in the Men at Work song "Down Under".
