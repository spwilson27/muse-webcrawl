Lazio (Italian: [ˈlattsjo]; Latin: Latium) is one of the twenty administrative regions of Italy, in the central peninsular section of the country. Its capital is Rome, which is also the national capital and by far the region's largest city. Lazio corresponds broadly to ancient Latium, the homeland of the Latins and the cradle of Roman civilisation and the Latin language.

Geography

Lazio borders Tuscany, Umbria, and Marche to the north, Abruzzo and Molise to the east, Campania to the south, and the Tyrrhenian Sea to the west. The terrain descends from the Apennines through volcanic hill country — including the Alban Hills with their crater lakes Albano and Nemi — to the coastal plain of the Agro Romano, drained by the Tiber. Offshore lie the Pontine Islands.

History

Inhabited by Italic peoples including the Latins, Volsci, and Hernici, and influenced by Etruscans to the north and Greeks to the south, the region was unified under Rome as it rose from city-state to empire. After Rome's fall, much of Lazio formed the core of the Papal States for a thousand years until Italian unification in 1870. Land reclamation of the Pontine Marshes in the 1930s created the province of Latina and new towns such as Latina and Aprilia.

Economy and culture

Rome dominates the regional economy with government, services, tourism, and media (including the Cinecittà film studios). Agriculture in the countryside produces wine (Frascati, Cesanese), olive oil, artichokes, and kiwifruit, of which Lazio is a major producer. The region's football clubs, SS Lazio and AS Roma, contest the Derby della Capitale.
