James Buchanan Jr. (23 April 1791 – 1 June 1868) was an American lawyer, diplomat, and politician who served as the 15th president of the United States from 1857 to 1861. A member of the Democratic Party, he had one of the longest public careers of any president, but his failure to address the secession crisis that followed Abraham Lincoln's election has led historians to rank him among the worst presidents.

Early career

Born in a log cabin in Franklin County, Pennsylvania, Buchanan became a successful lawyer in Lancaster. He served in the U.S. House of Representatives, as minister to Russia under Andrew Jackson, in the Senate, and as Secretary of State under James K. Polk, helping settle the Oregon boundary question. As minister to Britain under Franklin Pierce he co-authored the controversial Ostend Manifesto.

Presidency

Buchanan won the election of 1856 amid rising sectional conflict over slavery. His presidency was consumed by crises: the Dred Scott decision of 1857 (which he welcomed), the struggle over the pro-slavery Lecompton constitution in "Bleeding Kansas," the Panic of 1857, and a corruption-tainted administration. He held that secession was illegal but that the federal government lacked constitutional power to stop it, and took no effective action as seven Southern states seceded after Lincoln's victory in 1860.

Retirement and reputation

Buchanan retired to his Wheatland estate in Lancaster, defending his record in a memoir blaming abolitionists and Republicans for the Civil War. He died in 1868. Presidential rankings consistently place him at or near the bottom, chiefly for his passivity on the road to disunion.
