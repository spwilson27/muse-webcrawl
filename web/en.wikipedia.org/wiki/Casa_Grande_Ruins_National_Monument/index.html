Casa Grande Ruins National Monument

Casa Grande Ruins National Monument, in Coolidge, Arizona, preserves a group of Hohokam structures surrounded by a compound wall, centred on the "Great House" — a four-storey caliche building constructed in the 14th century, around 1300–1450 CE, by ancestral Sonoran Desert peoples who farmed the Gila River valley with extensive irrigation canals.

History

Jesuit missionary Eusebio Francisco Kino recorded the abandoned ruin in 1694 and named it Casa Grande (Spanish for "Great House"). It became the United States' first archaeological reserve in 1892 and a national monument in 1918, administered by the National Park Service. A steel shelter roof, completed in 1932, protects the fragile earthen walls.

Features

Openings in the Great House align with solstices and equinoxes, suggesting calendrical observation. The site includes ball courts and platform mounds typical of classic-period Hohokam settlements.
