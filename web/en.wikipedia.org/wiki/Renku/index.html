Renku

Renku (連句, "linked verses") is a Japanese form of popular collaborative linked verse poetry. It is a development of the older Japanese poetic tradition of renga, and is specifically a development of haikai no renga ("comic linked verse"). Matsuo Bashō (1644–1694), the great master of haiku, perfected the renku form, and his school produced celebrated sequences composed with his disciples.

Form

A renku is written by two or more poets, who alternate verses of 17 syllables (5-7-5, the kami-no-ku) and 14 syllables (7-7, the shimo-no-ku). Each verse must link to the preceding one yet shift away from earlier verses, following elaborate rules of season, topic, and imagery. The standard length is the kasen of 36 verses; longer forms include the 100-verse hyakuin. The opening verse, the hokku, set the season and tone, and later developed into the independent haiku.

Practice

Renku sessions are social occasions, often presided over by a master who judges each contribution. In modern times the form has been taken up internationally, with English-language renku written in sets of 12, 20, 36 or other verse counts.
