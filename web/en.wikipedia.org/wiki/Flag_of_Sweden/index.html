The flag of Sweden (Swedish: Sveriges flagga) consists of a yellow or gold Nordic cross (an upright cross shifted toward the hoist) on a field of light blue. The design, in use since the 16th century, is traditionally held to have been inspired by the coat of arms of Sweden of 1442 — a golden cross on a blue field — and modelled on the flag of Denmark, the oldest national flag in continuous use. The current specifications were fixed by the Flag Act of 1982; the proportions are 10:16, with the blue shades officially specified in the NCS colour system.

History

The exact origin of the Swedish flag is unknown. Blue and yellow have been used as Swedish colours at least since the royal coat of arms of Magnus III (1275) and the three-crowns arms of Albert of Mecklenburg. A banner with a yellow cross on blue is first reliably documented in the 1560s, during the reign of Eric XIV, though legend attributes the design to Eric IX (“Saint Eric”) seeing a golden cross in the blue sky during a crusade in Finland in the 12th century. During the Swedish–Norwegian union (1814–1905), union flags combined the Swedish and Norwegian colours; after the peaceful dissolution of the union in 1905, the plain cross flag was re-established by law of 22 June 1906.

Usage and flag days

Sweden has official flag days prescribed by ordinance, including New Year's Day, the King's birthday (30 April), National Day (6 June), Midsummer Day, the Queen's birthday (23 December), and election days. The National Day of Sweden, 6 June, commemorates the election of Gustav Vasa as king in 1523 and the adoption of the constitutions of 1809 and 1974. War flags and naval ensigns use a triple-tailed swallowtail variant of the same design.
