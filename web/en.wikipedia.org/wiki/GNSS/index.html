Satellite navigation

A global navigation satellite system (GNSS) is a satellite constellation that provides positioning, navigation, and timing (PNT) services on a global or regional basis. A receiver determines its location by measuring the travel time of signals from at least four satellites, whose precise orbits and atomic-clock time stamps allow trilateration to within a few metres (and far better with augmentation).

Systems

The four global systems are the United States' GPS (operational since the 1990s and the first such system), Russia's GLONASS, the European Union's Galileo, and China's BeiDou. Regional systems include India's NavIC and Japan's QZSS. Modern receivers commonly combine signals from several constellations and frequencies, improving accuracy and reliability in cities and terrain.

Applications

Beyond turn-by-turn navigation, GNSS underpins aviation and maritime navigation, surveying, precision agriculture, disaster response, scientific geodesy, and the synchronisation of telecommunications networks, power grids, and financial time-stamping.
