Barker (occupation)

Occupation typeEntertainment, promotion
SettingsCarnivals, circuses, fairs, sideshows
Also calledTalker, spieler (outside talker in circus usage)

A barker is a person who attempts to attract patrons to entertainment events, such as a circus or fair, by exhorting passing members of the public, announcing attractions, and emphasizing variety, novelty, originality or excitement. A barker may conduct a brief free show, called a "ballyhoo", to attract a crowd, then encourage them to buy tickets to enter an attraction.

Role

The barker's pitch — historically called the "spiel" — is a practised piece of showmanship. Standing before a sideshow or midway attraction, the barker describes wonders within, often in exaggerated terms, manages the flow of the crowd, and times the opening of the attraction. Famous ballyhoo stunts included short musical performances, parades of performers, and free glimpses of an act presented on an outdoor platform (the "bally platform").

Terminology

In traditional American circus slang, the person doing this work is more properly an "outside talker"; "barker" is the term more familiar to the general public and was considered somewhat pejorative by some showmen. P. T. Barnum's exhibitions and the great American traveling shows of the 19th and 20th centuries made the figure a stock character of popular culture.

In popular culture

Barkers appear in numerous films and musicals about carnival and circus life, and the archetypal cry of the barker — "Step right up!" — has entered the English language as a general invitation associated with fairs, games of skill, and salesmanship.
