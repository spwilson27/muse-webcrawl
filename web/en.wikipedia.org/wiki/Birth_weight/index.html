Birth weight

A newborn is weighed shortly after delivery.

Birth weight is the first weight of a newborn baby, usually measured within the first hours after birth. For babies born at term, the international standard average is about 3.3–3.5 kilograms (roughly 7.3–7.7 pounds), and the normal range for a full-term infant is generally considered to be 2.5 to 4.0 kilograms.

Categories

Infants are classified as low birth weight if they weigh less than 2,500 grams at birth, very low birth weight below 1,500 grams, and extremely low birth weight below 1,000 grams. At the upper end, a birth weight above 4,000 or 4,500 grams is termed macrosomia. Low birth weight may result from preterm birth, from intrauterine growth restriction, or from both, and UNICEF and the World Health Organization estimate that roughly 14 percent of babies worldwide are born with low birth weight.

Determinants and health effects

Birth weight is influenced by gestational age, maternal nutrition and health, maternal smoking and alcohol use, infections, multiple pregnancy, genetics and altitude. Low birth weight is strongly associated with neonatal mortality and with later risks of developmental difficulties; epidemiological research following the Barker hypothesis has also linked low birth weight with increased adult risk of cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes. High birth weight is associated with maternal diabetes and with delivery complications.
