Regulation of artificial intelligence

The regulation of artificial intelligence is the development of public sector policies and laws for promoting and regulating artificial intelligence (AI). It is part of the broader field of AI governance and responds to concerns about safety, bias, transparency, accountability and the societal impact of AI systems.

European Union

The European Union's AI Act, adopted in 2024, is the world's first comprehensive AI law. It follows a risk-based approach, banning certain "unacceptable risk" practices (such as manipulative techniques and most real-time biometric identification in public spaces), imposing strict requirements on high-risk systems, and setting transparency duties for general-purpose models.

United States

The United States has historically taken a sector-specific, market-oriented approach, relying on existing regulators such as the FTC and FDA, supplemented by executive orders and voluntary frameworks such as the NIST AI Risk Management Framework.

China and other jurisdictions

China has introduced rules on recommendation algorithms, deep synthesis (deepfakes) and generative AI services. Canada, Brazil, South Korea and others have also proposed or enacted dedicated AI legislation.
