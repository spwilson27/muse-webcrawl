Walter Bruch

Walter Bruch (2 March 1908 – 5 May 1990) was a German electrical engineer and inventor. He is best known as the inventor of the PAL (Phase Alternating Line) colour television system, adopted across much of Europe, Asia, and other regions, and for early work on television cameras.

Career

Bruch studied electrical engineering and joined the Telefunken company in Berlin in 1935. He worked on early iconoscope television cameras used at the 1936 Berlin Olympic Games. During the Second World War he worked on television-guided systems and monitoring equipment.

PAL

At Telefunken's Hanover laboratories in the early 1960s, Bruch led the team that developed PAL, which corrected the hue errors of the American NTSC system by reversing the phase of one colour component on alternate lines — the principle that gave the system its name. PAL was patented in 1962 and publicly demonstrated in 1963; it was adopted by West Germany and Britain for colour broadcasts beginning in 1967, and eventually used in more than 60 countries.

Honours

Bruch received numerous honours, including the Werner von Siemens Ring (1975), one of Germany's highest awards for technical achievement. He retired from Telefunken in 1974 and died in Hanover in 1990.
