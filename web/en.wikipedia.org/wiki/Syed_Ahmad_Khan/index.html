Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (1817–1898) was an Indian Muslim reformer, philosopher, and educationist who pioneered modern education for Muslims in British India. He founded the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College in 1875, which later became Aligarh Muslim University, and his Aligarh Movement sought to reconcile Islamic tradition with modern science and Western learning.

Early life and career

Born in Delhi into a family with ties to the Mughal court, Syed Ahmad joined the East India Company's judicial service, rising to the position of judge. During the Indian Rebellion of 1857 he remained loyal to the British and saved European lives at Bijnor; afterwards he wrote The Causes of the Indian Revolt, arguing the uprising stemmed from British disregard for Indian opinion.

Reform and education

Convinced that Muslims had fallen behind through neglect of modern education, Syed Ahmad founded schools, the Scientific Society (which translated Western works into Urdu), and the journal Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq. His 1869–70 visit to England shaped the model for the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh, modelled partly on Oxford and Cambridge.

Political thought and legacy

Syed Ahmad urged Muslim cooperation with the British and initially advocated Hindu-Muslim unity, though later speeches are associated with the two-nation idea. He was knighted in 1888. He is revered in Pakistan as a founding figure and remembered in India as a transformative educationist.
