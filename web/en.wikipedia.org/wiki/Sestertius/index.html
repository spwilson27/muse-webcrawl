Sestertius

Sestertius
Issued by: Roman Republic / Roman Empire
Value: 2½ asses; ¼ denarius
Material: Silver (early), brass (imperial)

The sestertius (plural sestertii) was a coin of ancient Rome. Its name means "two and a half," referring to its original value of two and a half asses. Introduced as a small silver coin around 211 BC, the sestertius became the standard unit of account in Roman finance, with large sums conventionally expressed in sestertii.

Imperial coinage

Under Augustus the sestertius was reintroduced as a large brass coin worth a quarter of a denarius. These big bronze pieces carried elaborate imperial portraits and propaganda imagery and are prized by collectors today.

Decline

Inflation eroded the coin's value during the 3rd century AD; the sestertius was last struck in significant quantities under Aurelian and disappeared during the monetary reforms of Diocletian.
