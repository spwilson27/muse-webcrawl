Bernard Leach

Bernard Howell Leach CH CBE (5 January 1887 – 6 May 1979) was a British studio potter and art teacher, regarded as the "father of British studio pottery". Together with the Japanese potter Shōji Hamada he founded the Leach Pottery at St Ives, Cornwall, in 1920, and his book A Potter's Book (1940) became a foundational text of the studio pottery movement, blending Eastern and Western traditions.

Life

Leach was born in Hong Kong and spent parts of his childhood in Japan and Singapore. He studied at the Slade School of Fine Art and the London School of Art, initially as an etcher. Returning to Japan in 1909, he took up pottery after attending a raku firing party, studying under Kenzan VI and becoming deeply involved with the Mingei (folk craft) circle around Yanagi Sōetsu.

In 1920 Leach returned to England with Shōji Hamada and established the Leach Pottery at St Ives, building a traditional three-chambered Japanese climbing kiln. The pottery produced both individual "standard ware" tableware and one-off pieces, and trained generations of potters, including his sons David and Michael Leach, Katharine Pleydell-Bouverie and many visiting apprentices. David Leach took over management of the pottery from the 1950s.

Philosophy and influence

Leach advocated a synthesis of East and West: Song-dynasty Chinese, Korean and Japanese folk pottery provided his ideals of simplicity and honesty to materials, applied to English slipware traditions. Through A Potter's Book and his lectures he shaped studio pottery across the English-speaking world. His legacy at St Ives continues through the restored Leach Pottery museum and working studio.
