Great Firewall

The Great Firewall is the combination of legislative actions and technologies enforced by the People's Republic of China to regulate the Internet domestically. The Great Firewall, whose role in Internet censorship in China is to block access to selected foreign websites and to slow down cross-border internet traffic, has blocked access to many foreign information sources, including Google, Facebook, Twitter (X), YouTube, and Wikipedia at various times.

History

Internet censorship in China began in the 1990s as the internet spread. The Golden Shield Project, initiated by the Ministry of Public Security in 1998 and built in stages through the 2000s, provided much of the technical infrastructure of blocking and surveillance. The term "Great Firewall" — a play on the Great Wall of China and a computer firewall — had appeared in print by 1997.

Methods

Blocking techniques include DNS spoofing and poisoning, IP address blocking, URL and keyword filtering, deep packet inspection, and active probing of suspected circumvention servers, with connections reset or throttled. Major foreign platforms are blocked outright, while domestic platforms are required to self-censor content.

Users circumvent the firewall with virtual private networks and other proxy tools, although unauthorised VPN services are themselves restricted. The system has contributed to the growth of domestic Chinese technology companies that operate within its rules. See also Internet censorship in China and Golden Shield Project.
