Clean and jerk

The clean and jerk is a composite of two weightlifting movements, most often performed within the sport of Olympic weightlifting, in which the lifter first lifts a loaded barbell from the floor to the shoulders (the clean), then jerks it overhead to arm's length. Together with the snatch, it is one of the two lifts contested in Olympic weightlifting, and it generally allows the greatest weights to be lifted.

The jerk: the lifter splits under the bar to drive it to arm's length overhead.

Technique

In the clean, the lifter pulls the bar explosively from the platform, drops into a deep front squat to receive it across the fronts of the shoulders and collarbone ("racking"), then stands upright. After adjusting grip and stance, the jerk begins with a shallow dip and violent leg drive that propels the bar upward; the lifter splits the legs front-and-back (split jerk) or drops into a full squat (squat jerk) to receive the bar with locked arms, then recovers to a standing position with feet in line before the judges' down signal.

Records

Clean and jerk world records are the largest weights lifted in the sport; the all-time heaviest clean and jerk in competition is 266 kg, lifted by Georgian superheavyweight Lasha Talakhadze (2021), who also holds the record total. Rules require the lift to be completed in one continuous sequence; a "press-out" of the arms in the jerk invalidates the attempt.
