Monopoly Capital

AuthorsPaul A. Baran and Paul M. Sweezy
Published1966 (Monthly Review Press)
SubjectMarxian economics, monopoly capitalism

Monopoly Capital: An Essay on the American Economic and Social Order is a 1966 book by the Marxian economists Paul A. Baran and Paul M. Sweezy, published by Monthly Review Press. Baran died in 1964 before its completion, and Sweezy finished the work. It is regarded as one of the major works of twentieth-century Marxian political economy.

Argument

The book argues that the competitive capitalism analysed by earlier economists had been superseded by an economy dominated by giant oligopolistic corporations. The central category of the analysis is the economic surplus — the difference between what a society produces and the costs of producing it. Baran and Sweezy argued that under monopoly capitalism the surplus tends to rise, because monopoly prices and cost reductions increase margins, while the system faces a corresponding problem of surplus absorption.

Surplus that cannot be absorbed through consumption or productive investment is taken up by what the authors considered wasteful or unproductive channels: the sales effort (advertising and marketing), military spending and imperialism, government expenditure, and the expansion of finance and speculation. The tendency of the rising surplus, they argued, leads to stagnation unless offset by counteracting forces such as epoch-making innovations, wars, and state spending.

Influence

Monopoly Capital was a major influence on the New Left and on the monthly journal Monthly Review school of Marxist economics. Its framework was extended by later scholars, including work on the financialisation of the surplus-absorption problem by John Bellamy Foster and others associated with the same journal.
