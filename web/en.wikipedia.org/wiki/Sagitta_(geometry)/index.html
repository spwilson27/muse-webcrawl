Sagitta (geometry)

In geometry, the sagitta (sometimes also called the versine) is the depth of an arc or the height of a circular segment — the distance from the centre of the arc to the centre of its base (the chord). More precisely, for a circular segment bounded by a chord of length c and an arc, the sagitta s is the perpendicular distance from the midpoint of the chord to the arc.

If R is the radius of the circle, then by the Pythagorean theorem the sagitta is given by s = R − √(R² − (c/2)²), and the radius can in turn be recovered from chord and sagitta as R = (s² + (c/2)²) / (2s). This inverse relation is widely used in optics and lensmaking, where a spherometer measures the sagitta of a lens surface to determine its radius of curvature, and in machining and architecture for laying out shallow arches and curves.

The word sagitta is Latin for "arrow", a name it shares with the related versed sine function. The concept appears in the chord tables of Ptolemy and in medieval Indian trigonometry.
