Semaglutide

Trade namesOzempic, Rybelsus, Wegovy
ClassGLP-1 receptor agonist
DeveloperNovo Nordisk
AdministrationSubcutaneous injection (weekly); oral (daily)

Semaglutide is an antidiabetic and anti-obesity medication developed by the Danish pharmaceutical company Novo Nordisk. It is a glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist, a modified analogue of the human GLP-1 hormone with a half-life of about one week, allowing once-weekly injection. It is sold under the brand names Ozempic (injectable, for type 2 diabetes), Rybelsus (oral, for type 2 diabetes), and Wegovy (injectable, for chronic weight management).

Medical uses

Semaglutide lowers blood sugar by stimulating insulin release, suppressing glucagon, and slowing gastric emptying; it also reduces appetite. It was approved in the United States in 2017 for type 2 diabetes and in 2021, at a higher dose, for weight management in adults with obesity or overweight with weight-related conditions. Clinical trials showed average weight losses substantially greater than earlier anti-obesity drugs, and cardiovascular outcome trials have shown reduced risk of major adverse cardiovascular events in people with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease.

Adverse effects

The most common side effects are gastrointestinal, including nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and constipation, especially during dose escalation. Rare but serious risks include pancreatitis, gastroparesis, and a boxed warning concerning thyroid C-cell tumours observed in rodent studies. Demand for semaglutide for weight loss has caused periodic shortages and widespread off-label and compounded use.
