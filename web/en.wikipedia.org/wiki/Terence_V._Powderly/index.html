Terence V. Powderly

Terence Vincent Powderly (January 22, 1849 – June 24, 1924) was an American labor union leader, politician, and attorney, best known as head of the Knights of Labor from 1879 to 1893.

Career

Born in Carbondale, Pennsylvania, to Irish immigrant parents, Powderly worked as a machinist and joined the Knights of Labor in 1876. As Grand Master Workman he oversaw the union's growth to roughly 700,000 members by 1886, pressured by successful strikes against railroads. Personally cautious and opposed to strikes in principle, he favoured arbitration, education, and political action, and pursued goals such as the eight-hour day, abolition of child labour, and equal pay. He also served as mayor of Scranton, Pennsylvania (1878–1884).

Later life

After leaving the Knights, he practised law and held federal posts, including Commissioner General of Immigration (1897–1902). He died in Washington, D.C., in 1924.
