The King's Speech

Directed by: Tom Hooper
Written by: David Seidler
Starring: Colin Firth, Geoffrey Rush, Helena Bonham Carter
Released: 2010
Running time: 119 minutes

The King's Speech is a 2010 British historical drama film directed by Tom Hooper and written by David Seidler. It tells the story of King George VI of the United Kingdom, who, plagued by a severe stammer, works with the unorthodox Australian speech therapist Lionel Logue to find his voice on the eve of the Second World War.

Plot

Prince Albert, Duke of York (Colin Firth), second son of George V, dreads public speaking because of his stammer. After the abdication of his brother Edward VIII in 1936, he unexpectedly becomes king. With war looming, the new king must address the nation by radio. Through a difficult, ultimately warm friendship with Logue (Geoffrey Rush), he gains the confidence to deliver the 1939 speech declaring war on Germany.

Reception and awards

The film was a major critical and commercial success, grossing over $400 million worldwide. At the 83rd Academy Awards it won four Oscars, including Best Picture, Best Director for Hooper, Best Actor for Firth, and Best Original Screenplay for Seidler, who had himself overcome a childhood stutter.
