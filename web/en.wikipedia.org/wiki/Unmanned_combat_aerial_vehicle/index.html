Unmanned combat aerial vehicle

The MQ-9 Reaper is among the best-known UCAVs in service.

An unmanned combat aerial vehicle (UCAV), also known as a combat drone, is an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) that carries aircraft ordnance — such as missiles, bombs, or anti-tank guided weapons — and is used for drone strikes. Unlike surveillance UAVs, UCAVs are designed to deliver weapons, and unlike loitering munitions they are typically recoverable and reusable. They are usually controlled in real time by remote operators via satellite links, though they may fly autonomously between waypoints.

History

Armed drones emerged from target and reconnaissance drones of the Cold War. The pivotal step was the arming of the General Atomics MQ-1 Predator with Hellfire missiles, used operationally from 2001 in Afghanistan. Its successor, the MQ-9 Reaper, combined long endurance (over 27 hours) with a substantial weapons load, becoming the archetype of the medium-altitude long-endurance strike drone.

Types and operators

Several nations have developed UCAVs: the United States (Predator, Reaper, and stealth demonstrators such as the X-47B), Turkey (Bayraktar TB2 and Akıncı, widely exported and used in the Nagorno-Karabakh war and the Russo-Ukrainian war), China (Wing Loong and CH series), Israel, and Iran. Stealthy flying-wing designs such as the Russian S-70 Okhotnik and Chinese GJ-11 aim at penetrating defended airspace, while "loyal wingman" programs pair UCAVs with manned fighters.

Legal and ethical debate

UCAV strikes have been controversial, raising questions about civilian casualties, targeted killing, accountability, and the lowering of political thresholds for the use of force. Campaigns have called for bans on fully autonomous weapons, and international discussions continue over the degree of human control required over lethal decisions.
