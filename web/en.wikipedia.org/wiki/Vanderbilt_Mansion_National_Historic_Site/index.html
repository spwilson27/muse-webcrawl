LocationHyde Park, Dutchess County, New York
Area211 acres (85 ha)
Built1896–1899

The Vanderbilt Mansion National Historic Site is a historic house museum in Hyde Park, New York, operated by the National Park Service. It preserves the estate of Frederick William Vanderbilt (1856–1938), a grandson of Cornelius Vanderbilt, and is a notable example of the grand country residences built by wealthy industrialists during the Gilded Age along the Hudson River.

History

Frederick Vanderbilt purchased the Hyde Park property in 1895. The previous mansion on the site was demolished and replaced with a new 54-room Beaux-Arts mansion designed by the prominent architectural firm McKim, Mead & White, with Charles Follen McKim principally in charge. Construction took place from 1896 to 1899, and interiors were executed by leading decorators and furnishing houses of the day. The estate's formal gardens were designed successively by several landscape architects, culminating in an Italian-style terraced garden.

The Vanderbilts used Hyde Park as a seasonal residence, principally in spring and autumn. After Frederick's death in 1938, the estate passed to his niece, Margaret Van Alen, who donated it to the federal government in 1940 at the encouragement of President Franklin D. Roosevelt, whose own home is nearby. It was designated a National Historic Site in 1940.

Description

The mansion is built of Indiana limestone in the Beaux-Arts style, with a central block flanked by pavilions. Its lavish interiors — including the entrance hall, dining room, and drawing room — survive largely as furnished during the Vanderbilts' occupancy. The grounds, overlooking the Hudson River, feature formal gardens, a coach house, and pavilions, and the site is open to the public for tours.
