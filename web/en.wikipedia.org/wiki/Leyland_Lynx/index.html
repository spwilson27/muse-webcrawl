The Leyland Lynx is a step-entrance single-decker bus chassis manufactured by Leyland between 1986 and 1992. Designed for the deregulated British bus market created by the Transport Act 1985, it was intended as a lighter, simpler and cheaper alternative to the heavyweight Leyland Tiger, aimed at stage-carriage work.

Design

The Lynx used a lightweight steel frame chassis with the engine mounted horizontally at the rear, offered initially with Leyland's TL11 engine and later with Cummins L10 and Gardner units, driving through automatic or semi-automatic gearboxes. Most were bodied by Leyland's own Workington plant with a standard integral-look body, though other bodybuilders also supplied bodywork. An updated Lynx II appeared in 1990.

Operators and service

Over 1,000 Lynxes were built. Major customers included the National Bus Company subsidiaries in their final years, the newly privatised operators of the late 1980s, and fleets in Scotland and Wales; exported examples went to operators overseas including in Jamaica. The type became a common sight on British urban and interurban services through the 1990s, before being displaced by low-floor designs required under disability-discrimination legislation.

Legacy

After Leyland Bus was sold to Volvo in 1988, production continued until 1992. A number of Lynxes survive in preservation, valued as representative of the last generation of wholly British-designed step-entrance buses.
