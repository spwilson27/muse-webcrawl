Stone Town

Stone Town (Swahili: Mji Mkongwe, "old town") is the old part of Zanzibar City, the main city of Zanzibar, in Tanzania. It is located on the western coast of Unguja, the main island of the Zanzibar Archipelago. Formerly the capital of the Zanzibar Sultanate, and a flourishing centre of the spice trade as well as the Indian Ocean slave trade in the 19th century, it was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2000.

Architecture

Stone Town's architecture is a fusion of Swahili, Arab, Persian, Indian, and European elements. Buildings are largely constructed of coral rag and limestone with mangrove-pole ceilings, arranged along narrow winding lanes. Its celebrated carved wooden doors — several hundred survive — feature brass studs and elaborate motifs indicating the wealth and trade of their owners. Landmarks include the House of Wonders (Beit al-Ajaib), the Old Fort, the former Sultan's Palace (now the Palace Museum), the Old Dispensary, St Joseph's Cathedral, and the Christ Church Anglican cathedral, built on the site of the former slave market.

History

The settlement grew from a Swahili trading port; Portuguese forces held Zanzibar in the 16th and 17th centuries before Omani Arabs seized control in 1698. In the 19th century, Sultan Seyyid Said moved his capital from Muscat to Zanzibar, and clove plantations made the island the world's largest clove producer. The British protectorate period, the 1964 Zanzibar Revolution, and union with Tanganyika to form Tanzania followed.

Notable associations

Stone Town was the birthplace of singer Freddie Mercury (born Farrokh Bulsara in 1946), and the explorer David Livingstone prepared his final expedition from a house in the town.
