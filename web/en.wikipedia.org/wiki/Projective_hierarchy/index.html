Projective hierarchy

In the mathematical field of descriptive set theory, a subset of a Polish space is projective if it can be obtained from a Borel set by repeatedly applying the operations of complementation and projection. The projective sets form a hierarchy analogous to the arithmetical hierarchy from computability theory, with levels conventionally denoted Σ1n, Π1n, and Δ1n for each natural number n.

Definition

The lowest level of the hierarchy consists of the analytic sets (Σ11) and coanalytic sets (Π11). Higher levels are obtained by taking projections of sets at the previous level along a product space and complementing. A set is projective if it belongs to some level of the hierarchy.

Properties

Questions about the regularity properties of projective sets—such as whether they are Lebesgue measurable, have the property of Baire, or have the perfect set property—cannot be settled by ZFC alone. Projective determinacy, an axiom implied by large cardinal assumptions, guarantees these regularity properties for all projective sets and settles the structure of the hierarchy. The projective hierarchy in the Baire space parallels the arithmetical hierarchy, and the analytical hierarchy extends the same pattern to sets of sets of natural numbers.
