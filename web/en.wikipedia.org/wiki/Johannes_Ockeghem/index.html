Johannes Ockeghem

Johannes Ockeghem (c. 1410/1425 – 6 February 1497, Tours) was a Franco-Flemish composer of the early Renaissance and the most famous member of the Franco-Flemish School in the last half of the 15th century. He served the French royal court for most of his career.

Life

Probably born in Saint-Ghislain in the County of Hainaut, Ockeghem sang at Antwerp Cathedral in the 1440s, where Du Fay may have heard him. By 1452 he was in the service of Charles VII of France, and he remained a chaplain and composer to the French kings Charles VII, Louis XI, and Charles VIII, also holding the lucrative post of treasurer of the abbey of St-Martin at Tours.

Music

Ockeghem's surviving works include some 14 Masses, including the Missa Prolationum, written entirely in mensuration canons, and a Requiem that is the earliest surviving polyphonic requiem setting. He also composed motets and chansons. His style features long, flowing melodic lines, rich sonorities favouring low voices, and intricate contrapuntal artifice.

Influence

After his death, Josquin des Prez composed the lament Nymphes des bois in his honour, and Johannes Lupi and others wrote musical tributes, reflecting his stature among contemporaries.
