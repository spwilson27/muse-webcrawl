The United States Numbered Highway System (often called U.S. Routes or U.S. Highways) is an integrated network of roads and highways numbered within a nationwide grid in the contiguous United States. Established in 1926, the system is coordinated by the American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials (AASHTO), while the roads themselves are maintained by state and local governments.

Route numbers generally follow a grid: even numbers increase from north to south for east-west routes, and odd numbers increase from east to west for north-south routes. Three-digit routes are auxiliary routes of their two-digit parents.

History

The system replaced the informal patchwork of named auto trails with uniform numbering and the distinctive black-and-white shield. Famous routes such as U.S. Route 66 became cultural icons before being decommissioned as the Interstate Highway System, begun in 1956, took over long-distance traffic. U.S. Highways remain important regional corridors today.
