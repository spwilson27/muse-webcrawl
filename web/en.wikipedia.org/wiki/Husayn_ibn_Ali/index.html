Bornc. 10 January 626 CE, Medina

Husayn ibn Ali (Arabic: الحسين بن علي; 626–680) was a grandson of the Islamic prophet Muhammad, the younger son of Ali ibn Abi Talib and Muhammad's daughter Fatimah. He is revered by Shia Muslims as the third Imam and a martyr, and is respected across Islam for his stand against the Umayyad caliph Yazid I.

Road to Karbala

After his brother Hasan's death, Husayn was recognised by his followers as head of the Prophet's family. When Yazid succeeded his father Mu'awiya in 680 — converting the caliphate into a hereditary monarchy in Husayn's view — Husayn refused to pledge allegiance. Invited by the people of Kufa in Iraq who promised support, he set out from Mecca with a small party of family and companions, despite warnings. The Kufans' support collapsed under Umayyad pressure, and Husayn's caravan was intercepted at Karbala.

Battle of Karbala

Cut off from water from the Euphrates, Husayn's party of some 72 fighting men faced an Umayyad army under Umar ibn Sa'd. On 10 Muharram 61 AH (10 October 680), after refusing a final demand for submission, Husayn and nearly all his companions — including his half-brother Abbas and his infant son — were killed; the women and his surviving son Ali Zayn al-Abidin were taken prisoner to Damascus.

Legacy

The martyrdom at Karbala is the defining event of Shia identity, commemorated each year on Ashura and throughout Muharram with processions, passion plays (ta'ziya) and pilgrimage to Husayn's shrine in Karbala, one of the holiest sites in Shia Islam. Husayn's revolt inspired later uprisings and remains a universal symbol of resistance to tyranny; his epithet Sayyid al-Shuhada means "Master of the Martyrs".
