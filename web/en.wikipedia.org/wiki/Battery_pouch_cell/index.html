Battery pouch cell

Pouch cell
TypeRechargeable battery format
ChemistriesLi-ion, Li-polymer
Common usesSmartphones, laptops, EVs, drones

A pouch cell is a type of rechargeable battery in which the electrodes and separator are sealed inside a flexible, flat envelope made of aluminium-laminate film rather than a rigid metal can. The format is widely used for lithium-ion and lithium polymer batteries because it is light, thin and can be manufactured in a wide variety of shapes and capacities.

Construction

The pouch is formed from a multilayer film of nylon, aluminium foil and polypropylene. Inside, stacked or wound electrode sheets are saturated with electrolyte. Because the enclosure is soft, the cell relies on external mechanical support, and a small amount of gas generated over the cell's life can cause the pouch to swell.

Advantages

  • High gravimetric energy density, since the packaging is very light
  • Flexible sizing for device designers
  • Simpler, cheaper manufacture than prismatic cans

Disadvantages

Pouch cells are more vulnerable to puncture and mechanical damage, can swell with age, and need careful compression in large battery packs such as those in electric vehicles.
