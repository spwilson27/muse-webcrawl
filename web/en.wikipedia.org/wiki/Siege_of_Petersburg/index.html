Siege of Petersburg

Date: 9 June 1864 – 25 March 1865
(main operations from 15 June 1864)
Location: Petersburg, Virginia
Result: Union victory; fall of Richmond

The siege of Petersburg was a series of battles and trench warfare operations around Petersburg, Virginia, during the American Civil War, lasting from June 1864 to March 1865. Union forces under Ulysses S. Grant sought to cut the rail lines supplying Robert E. Lee's Army of Northern Virginia and the Confederate capital, Richmond.

Course

After the Overland Campaign, Grant crossed the James River and attacked Petersburg. Failing to take the city by assault, the armies settled into a trench system that eventually stretched over 30 miles. Battles included the Crater (30 July 1864), where a Union mine explosion was followed by a costly failed assault; Ream's Station; Hatcher's Run; and repeated Union offensives extending westward to sever the South Side Railroad.

Conclusion

On 25 March 1865 Lee's attempt to break out at Fort Stedman failed. On 1 April, Sheridan's victory at Five Forks cut the last rail line; Grant's general assault on 2 April forced Lee to evacuate Petersburg and Richmond. Lee surrendered at Appomattox Court House a week later, on 9 April 1865.

Significance

The campaign's extensive trenches, railroads used for supply, and attritional strategy foreshadowed the warfare of the First World War.
