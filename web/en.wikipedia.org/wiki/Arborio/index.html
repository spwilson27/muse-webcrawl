Arborio

Country: Italy

Province: Vercelli, Piedmont

Named for: (also) Arborio rice

Arborio is a small town and comune in the province of Vercelli in the Piedmont region of north-west Italy, with a population of roughly two thousand. It lies in the rice-growing plain of the Po Valley between Vercelli and Novara, an area long associated with Italian rice cultivation.

Arborio rice

The town is best known internationally as the namesake of Arborio rice, an Italian short-grain rice variety developed in the area in the 20th century. Arborio grains are large, stubby, and pearl-white, with a high content of amylopectin starch. When cooked, the grains develop a firm, chewy centre while releasing starch that gives a creamy texture, which is why Arborio became the classic choice for risotto outside Italy, although in Italy itself varieties such as Carnaroli and Vialone Nano are often preferred by cooks.

Geography and agriculture

The surrounding paddies are fed by the network of irrigation canals of the Vercellese plain, including waters from the Sesia river system. Rice farming shapes the local landscape and economy, alongside the processing and trading of grain centred on nearby Vercelli, one of Europe's principal rice markets.
