Ali Hassan al-Majid

Ali Hassan Abd al-Majid al-Tikriti
Born: 30 November 1941, Tikrit, Iraq
Died: 25 January 2010 (aged 68), Baghdad (executed)
Nickname: "Chemical Ali"
Roles: Defence Minister, Interior Minister, Ba'athist commander

Ali Hassan Abd al-Majid al-Tikriti (Arabic: علي حسن عبد المجيد التكريتي; 30 November 1941 – 25 January 2010), commonly known as "Chemical Ali", was an Iraqi military officer and politician under Saddam Hussein, his first cousin. He was Defence Minister and a senior Ba'ath Party figure, notorious for ordering chemical weapons attacks against Kurds.

Role under Saddam Hussein

Rising through the army and party, al-Majid was given sweeping powers as secretary-general of the Ba'ath Party's Northern Bureau in 1987. In the Anfal campaign of 1986–1989 against Iraqi Kurds, forces under his command destroyed thousands of villages and used poison gas, most infamously at Halabja on 16 March 1988, killing thousands of civilians — earning him the nickname "Chemical Ali."

He served as military governor of occupied Kuwait in 1990–91, Minister of the Interior, and Minister of Defence (1991–1995), and directed the suppression of the 1991 Shia uprising in southern Iraq.

Capture, trial and execution

Captured by U.S. forces in August 2003 after the invasion of Iraq, al-Majid was tried by the Iraqi High Tribunal. He was sentenced to death in June 2007 for genocide in the Anfal campaign, and received further death sentences for Halabja and the 1991 uprising and the 1999 crackdown in Sadr City. He was hanged on 25 January 2010.
