Technopole

A technopole (from Greek techne, "craft/technology", and polis, "city") is a planned centre of high-technology industry, research and education, designed to foster innovation by bringing together universities, research institutes and technology-based firms in one location. The term is used especially in France and in international development literature, and is closely related to the concepts of the science park, technology park and innovation cluster.

Concept

Technopoles are usually deliberate creations of public policy, often led by national or regional governments seeking to stimulate economic development, create skilled employment and encourage the transfer of knowledge from laboratories to industry. Unlike organically grown clusters, a technopole is typically planned from scratch on a dedicated site with infrastructure for research, incubation and production.

Examples

Sophia Antipolis in southern France, founded in 1969, is often cited as the archetypal European technopole, hosting thousands of companies around a university and research core. Japan's Technopolis Programme of the 1980s designated more than two dozen cities, including Tsukuba Science City, for development as regional technology centres. Other examples include technology cities in South Korea, India and China modelled partly on Silicon Valley.

Criticism

Scholars have questioned whether planned technopoles can reproduce the informal networks and entrepreneurial culture of naturally occurring clusters, noting that many technopole projects have underperformed expectations while successful high-tech regions often emerged without central planning.
