George Grant Elmslie (20 February 1869 – 23 April 1952) was a Scottish-born American architect and a leading figure of the Prairie School movement.

Career

Elmslie emigrated to the United States as a child and trained in Chicago, joining the firm of Adler & Sullivan in 1888, where he became Louis Sullivan's chief draftsman. He was responsible for much of the firm's celebrated ornament, including work on the Carson, Pirie, Scott Building. In 1909 he entered partnership with William Gray Purcell in Minneapolis, forming Purcell & Elmslie, one of the most productive Prairie School firms.

Works

Notable works include the Merchants National Bank in Winona, Minnesota, the Purcell-Cutts House in Minneapolis, the Woodbury County Courthouse in Sioux City, Iowa (with Purcell and William L. Steele), and numerous Midwestern banks, courthouses, and residences distinguished by intricate terra-cotta and art-glass ornament.
