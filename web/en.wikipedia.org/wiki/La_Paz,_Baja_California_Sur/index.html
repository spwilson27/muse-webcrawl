La Paz, Baja California Sur

La Paz (Spanish for "The Peace") is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Baja California Sur, and the seat of the municipality of La Paz. It lies on La Paz Bay on the eastern coast of the Baja California Peninsula, facing the Gulf of California. The city population was about 250,000 at the 2020 census, with roughly 292,000 in the municipality.

History

The bay was reached in 1535 by Hernán Cortés, who attempted a colony, and was named by the pilot Fortún Jiménez's earlier expedition of 1533–34. A permanent settlement was not established until the Jesuit mission of Nuestra Señora del Pilar de La Paz Airapí was founded in 1720; the mission was abandoned in 1748 after disease and revolt among the Pericú people. The modern town grew in the 19th century around pearl fishing in the bay, and it became the capital of the federal territory — later state — of Baja California Sur. La Paz was also briefly occupied by U.S. forces during the Mexican–American War, and John Steinbeck's novella The Pearl drew on a La Paz legend.

Geography and climate

La Paz has a hot desert climate, with summer temperatures frequently exceeding 40 °C and mild winters. Its waterfront promenade, the malecón, is the city's social centre. Nearby attractions include Balandra and Tecolote beaches, the whale shark tours in the bay, and Espíritu Santo Island, a UNESCO-listed biosphere reserve.

Economy

Tourism, government, fishing, and commerce drive the local economy. The city is served by Manuel Márquez de León International Airport and by ferry routes across the gulf to Topolobampo and Mazatlán in Sinaloa.
