BirdLife International is a global partnership of non-governmental conservation organisations that strives to conserve birds, their habitats, and global biodiversity. Founded in 1922 as the International Council for Bird Preservation (ICBP) — among the world's oldest international conservation organisations — it adopted its present name in 1993. Its secretariat is based in Cambridge, England, and its network comprises more than 120 partner organisations, including the RSPB (United Kingdom), the National Audubon Society (United States), and BirdLife Australia.

Activities

BirdLife is the official scientific authority for birds for the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species, assessing the extinction risk of all of the world's roughly 11,000 bird species. It maintains the network of Important Bird and Biodiversity Areas (IBAs) — more than 13,000 sites identified worldwide as critical for bird conservation — and coordinates programmes on flyways, marine conservation and seabirds, invasive alien species on islands, climate change, and the prevention of extinctions. Its journal is Bird Conservation International, and its flagship periodic assessment is the State of the World's Birds report.

Findings

BirdLife's assessments have documented a broad decline in global bird populations: its 2022 State of the World's Birds reported that nearly half of all bird species are in decline and one in eight is threatened with extinction, driven chiefly by agricultural expansion, logging, invasive species, hunting, and climate change. The partnership has also led successful species recoveries, including island eradications of introduced predators that saved endemic birds from extinction.
