Waikato River

Waikato River
Country: New Zealand
Length: 425 km
Source: Lake Taupō
Mouth: Tasman Sea at Port Waikato

The Waikato River is the longest river in New Zealand, running 425 kilometres through the North Island from the eastern slopes of Mount Ruapehu and Lake Taupō to the Tasman Sea at Port Waikato, south of Auckland.

Course

Leaving Lake Taupō, the river flows northwest through the Waikato Plains past Taupō, Cambridge, Hamilton, Huntly and Mercer. Notable features include the Huka Falls near Taupō and the river's delta and swamp wetlands near its mouth, important for birdlife.

Use

The river supports a chain of hydroelectric dams built in the 20th century (including Arapuni, Karapiro and Maraetai), supplies drinking water to Hamilton and part of Auckland, and is used for irrigation, recreation and the famous Waikato rowing venues at Lake Karapiro.

Cultural significance

The river is a taonga (treasure) of great spiritual importance to the Waikato-Tainui iwi; the Māori King movement (Kīngitanga) is centred in its basin. Co-governance arrangements for the river's health were established by settlement legislation in 2010.
