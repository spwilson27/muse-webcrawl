Sustainable aviation fuel

Sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) is a biofuel or synthetic fuel used to power aircraft that has similar properties to conventional jet fuel but with a substantially smaller carbon footprint. SAF is a "drop-in" fuel: it can be blended with fossil kerosene (currently certified up to 50% blends) and used in existing aircraft and infrastructure.

Production

SAF is produced from feedstocks such as used cooking oil, animal fats, agricultural and forestry residues, municipal waste, and energy crops, most commonly by the HEFA (hydroprocessed esters and fatty acids) pathway. Other routes include Fischer–Tropsch synthesis from biomass or waste, alcohol-to-jet, and power-to-liquid e-fuels made from green hydrogen and captured carbon dioxide. Depending on feedstock and process, lifecycle emissions reductions of around 70–80% versus fossil jet fuel are claimed.

Policy and uptake

Governments have introduced blending mandates and incentives, including the European Union's ReFuelEU Aviation regulation and tax credits in the United States. Production remains a tiny fraction of global jet fuel supply and SAF costs several times more than conventional fuel, so scaling supply is a central challenge in aviation decarbonisation plans.
