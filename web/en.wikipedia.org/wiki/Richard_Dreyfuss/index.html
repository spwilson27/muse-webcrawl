Richard Dreyfuss

Richard Stephen Dreyfuss (born October 29, 1947) is an American actor. He rose to prominence in the 1970s with roles in American Graffiti (1973), Jaws (1975), and Close Encounters of the Third Kind (1977), and won the Academy Award for Best Actor for The Goodbye Girl (1977), becoming at the time the youngest actor to win that award at age 30.

Career

Born in Brooklyn and raised in Los Angeles, Dreyfuss worked steadily in television before his film breakthrough. His later work includes Stand by Me (1986, as the narrator), Stakeout (1987), What About Bob? (1991), and Mr. Holland's Opus (1995), which earned him another Oscar nomination. He also played Dick Cheney in Oliver Stone's W. (2008).

Personal life and advocacy

Dreyfuss has been open about his struggles with drug addiction in the 1980s and about living with bipolar disorder. He founded The Dreyfuss Civics Initiative, a nonprofit advocating for civics education in American schools.
