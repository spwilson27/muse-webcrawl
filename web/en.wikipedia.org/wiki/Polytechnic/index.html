Polytechnic

A polytechnic is an institution of higher or further education emphasising applied science, engineering, and technology, often with a vocational and industry-oriented mission. The word derives from the Greek polytekhnos, "skilled in many arts." Famous early examples include the École Polytechnique in Paris (founded 1794) and the polytechnics of the German-speaking world.

Polytechnics focus on applied technical and engineering education.

By country

In the United Kingdom, polytechnics were public institutions offering degrees validated by the Council for National Academic Awards; under the Further and Higher Education Act 1992 they all gained university status and the name fell out of use. In Finland, polytechnics (ammattikorkeakoulu) form a distinct sector of universities of applied sciences. In India, polytechnics are diploma-level technical institutes distinct from the Indian Institutes of Technology. Polytechnics also form a major tier of tertiary education in countries such as Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, Malaysia, and New Zealand, and in the United States the term survives in names like Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute and California Polytechnic State University.

Character

Historically, polytechnics emphasised teaching and applied research over basic research, part-time and mature students, and close links with local industry — a mission sometimes contrasted with that of traditional research universities, though the boundary has blurred as many polytechnics became universities.
