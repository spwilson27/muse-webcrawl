Uniform star polyhedron

In geometry, a uniform star polyhedron is a self-intersecting uniform polyhedron. They are also sometimes called nonconvex uniform polyhedra. There are 57 nonprismatic uniform star polyhedra, plus infinite families of star prisms and antiprisms.

Enumeration

Harold Scott MacDonald Coxeter and colleagues listed uniform polyhedra in 1954, and Magnus Wenninger built models of all 75 uniform polyhedra (including the convex ones). John Skilling later proved the enumeration complete, allowing one degenerate case with doubled edges, the great disnub dirhombidodecahedron ("Skilling's figure").

Examples

Well-known examples include the Kepler–Poinsot solids (small stellated dodecahedron, great dodecahedron, great stellated dodecahedron and great icosahedron), the tetrahemihexahedron, the octahemioctahedron, and the small cubicuboctahedron. Their faces are regular polygons and their vertex figures are regular (possibly self-intersecting) polygons, with all vertices equivalent under the symmetry group.
