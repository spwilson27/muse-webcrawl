The Académie Royale de Musique (Royal Academy of Music), today known as the Paris Opera, was a company of dancers and musicians that performed at court and in public theatres in Paris. It was founded in 1669 by King Louis XIV, making it one of the oldest opera institutions in the world and the birthplace of classical ballet.

History

Louis XIV granted the poet Pierre Perrin a monopoly privilege to establish an academy for sung drama in French; with composer Robert Cambert, Perrin produced Pomone (1671), often considered the first French opera. In 1672 the privilege passed to Jean-Baptiste Lully, who held a near-monopoly on French operatic production and created the tragédie en musique. The company performed at the Palais-Royal theatre and, from 1681, maintained the first permanent corps of professional female dancers — the origins of the Paris Opera Ballet.

The institution changed names with successive political regimes — becoming the Opéra, the Théâtre des Arts, the Académie Impériale de Musique under Napoleon, and again the Académie Royale under the Restoration — before settling as the Paris Opera.

Legacy

The Academy's traditions shaped French opera and the technique and vocabulary of classical ballet worldwide. It now performs at the Palais Garnier and the Opéra Bastille.
