Speedway Grand Prix

The Speedway Grand Prix (SGP) is a series of stand-alone motorcycle speedway events over the course of a season that determine the Speedway World Champion. The series inaugurated in 1995 replaced the previous single-round World Final that had been held since 1936. Riders race on 500cc single-cylinder motorcycles with no brakes, sliding around oval dirt tracks.

Format

Each Grand Prix features 16 riders contesting 20 qualifying heats, in which every rider races against each other rider once; each heat has four riders racing four laps. Points scored in the heats advance the top riders to semi-finals and a final. Championship points are awarded by overall finishing position in the event, and the rider with the most points at the end of the series is crowned world champion. The field combines the top riders from the previous season, qualifiers from the Grand Prix Challenge, permanent wild cards, and a local wild card at each round.

History

The first Grand Prix season in 1995 consisted of six rounds and was won by Denmark's Hans Nielsen. Venues have included stadiums across Europe — such as Cardiff's Principality Stadium, Warsaw's PGE Narodowy, and Vojens in Denmark — as well as rounds in Australia and New Zealand. Record champions of the Grand Prix era include Tony Rickardsson and Greg Hancock, while Poland's Bartosz Zmarzlik won multiple titles in the modern era, including a run of championships beginning in 2019.

Related competitions

The SGP family includes Speedway Grand Prix 2 (under-21) and SGP3 (under-16) series, and the Speedway World Cup / Speedway of Nations team events. In 2022, Discovery Sports Events took over promotion of the series from BSI Speedway.
