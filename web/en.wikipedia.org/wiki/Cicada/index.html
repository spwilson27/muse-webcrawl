Cicada

An adult cicada shortly after emerging.

Cicadas are insects in the superfamily Cicadoidea, in the order Hemiptera (true bugs). They are characterised by prominent, widely set compound eyes, short antennae, and membranous, conspicuously veined wings. More than 3,000 species are known worldwide, from temperate to tropical climates, and new species continue to be described. Cicadas are best known for the loud songs produced by males using vibrating membranes called tymbals, and for the extraordinary life cycles of the North American periodical cicadas (Magicicada), whose nymphs spend 13 or 17 years underground before emerging in vast synchronised broods.

Life cycle

Female cicadas lay eggs in slits cut in twigs. The tiny nymphs drop to the ground, burrow into the soil, and feed on xylem fluid from plant roots, passing through five instars over periods ranging from one year to (in periodical species) seventeen. Mature nymphs emerge, typically on warm evenings, climb a vertical surface, and moult into winged adults, leaving behind the familiar empty exoskeletons. Adults live only a few weeks, during which males sing to attract females; predators are overwhelmed by sheer numbers, a strategy known as predator satiation.

Song

Only males produce the characteristic cicada song, by buckling ribbed membranes — the tymbals — in and out with a dedicated muscle; resonating chambers in the abdomen amplify the sound, which in some species exceeds 100 decibels at close range. Each species has a distinctive call used in species recognition and courtship, and some cicadas can also modulate their song with movements of the abdomen and wings.

Cultural significance

Cicadas have been symbols of rebirth, immortality, and carefree summer in Chinese, Japanese, Greek, and other traditions since antiquity; Aristotle and other classical authors described them, and cicada-shaped jade carvings were placed in the mouths of the dead in Han-dynasty China. They are eaten in many cultures and featured in folklore, literature, and music around the world.
