The Mutapa Empire (Shona: Mwene Matapa), also known as the Kingdom of Mutapa, was a Shona-speaking empire in what is now Zimbabwe and Mozambique, flourishing from about 1430 to 1760. Its kings, who bore the title Mwene Matapa ("Lord of the Conquered Lands" or "Master Pillager"), controlled the gold trade between the Zimbabwe plateau and the Indian Ocean coast.

Origins

According to oral tradition, the empire was founded by Nyatsimba Mutota, a prince of the state based at Great Zimbabwe, who led his followers north in search of new salt and hunting grounds and built a new state between the Zambezi and Mazowe valleys. His son and successor, Matope, extended Mutapa's authority over a wide region, and the empire reached its greatest extent in the later fifteenth century.

Gold trade and the Portuguese

Mutapa's wealth rested on gold mined on the plateau and exported through the Swahili and later Portuguese trading posts on the Mozambique coast, above all Sofala. From the sixteenth century the Portuguese of Portuguese Mozambique sought direct control of the gold fairs in the interior. Relations deteriorated: in 1629 the Portuguese defeated the Mwene Matapa and imposed a treaty making the empire a Portuguese vassal, entitled to place a garrison and miners in the kingdom.

Decline

Portuguese interference and succession disputes weakened the state, and in the 1690s the empire split: the Changamire dynasty, founders of the rival Rozvi Empire, drove the Portuguese from the plateau and reduced the Mwene Matapa's realm to a remnant in the lower Zambezi valley, where the title survived into the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries as a minor chieftaincy.
