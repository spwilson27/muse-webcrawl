Trailer (vehicle)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A trailer is an unpowered vehicle towed by a powered vehicle. It is commonly used for the transport of goods and materials. Trailers range from small utility trailers towed by cars to the semi-trailers of articulated lorries, and include specialised designs such as travel trailers (caravans), boat trailers and horse trailers (floats). A trailer differs from a semitrailer in that a full trailer bears its own weight on its own axles, whereas a semi-trailer transfers part of its weight to the towing vehicle through its fifth-wheel coupling.

Types

  • Utility/cargo trailers — open or enclosed (box) trailers for general freight, landscape work and moving.
  • Travel trailers (caravans) — living accommodation towed behind a car or truck, including fifth-wheel travel trailers and teardrop trailers.
  • Boat trailers — fitted with rollers or bunks to support a hull, and submersible for launching.
  • Livestock and horse trailers — ventilated enclosures with ramps and dividers.
  • Flatbed, dump and lowboy trailers — for heavy equipment and construction.
  • Semi-trailers — the freight-carrying unit of a tractor-trailer combination, including dry vans, refrigerated trailers ("reefers"), tankers and container chassis.
  • Dollies and converter dollies — short wheeled units that let a semitrailer be towed as a full trailer or combined into road trains.

Components

A typical trailer consists of a frame (chassis), one or more axles with suspension (leaf springs, torsion axles or air suspension) and wheels, a coupler or A-frame drawbar that latches onto the tow vehicle's tow hitch, a jockey wheel or tongue jack for support when unhitched, safety chains, and lighting. Trailers above certain weights require brakes: either electric brakes controlled from the tow vehicle, surge (overrun) brakes operated by the coupling compressing under braking, or air brakes on heavy commercial trailers. Stability depends on correct loading, with roughly 10–15% of the trailer's weight on the coupling ("tongue weight") to prevent sway.

Regulation

Driving licence rules in many countries limit the trailer weight that standard car licences may tow; the European Union's category B licence allows combinations up to 3,500 kg, with B+E for heavier combinations (rules simplified in the United Kingdom in 2021). Trailers must display lights and reflectors, carry the towing vehicle's number plate, and in many jurisdictions heavier trailers require registration and periodic inspection. Speed limits for towing are commonly lower than for solo vehicles.

History

Trailers descend from horse-drawn wagons and were adapted for motor vehicles in the 1910s–1920s; the semi-trailer was patented by August Fruehauf in 1914, and his Fruehauf Trailer Company became a leading manufacturer. The invention of the automatic fifth-wheel coupling by Herman Farrman and others enabled the modern articulated truck. Containerisation from the 1950s made the skeletal container trailer a standard unit of world trade.
