Jhelum River

Jhelum River
Length: about 725 km (450 mi)
Source: Verinag Spring, Kashmir Valley
Mouth: Chenab River, near Trimmu, Punjab, Pakistan
Ancient Greek name: Hydaspes

The Jhelum River is a river in the northern Indian subcontinent, the westernmost of the five rivers of Punjab. It rises at Verinag Spring at the foot of the Pir Panjal range in the Kashmir Valley, flows through Srinagar and Wular Lake, cuts a deep gorge through the mountains past Baramulla and Muzaffarabad, and enters the plains of Punjab in Pakistan before joining the Chenab River near Trimmu. Its total length is about 725 kilometres.

History

Known to the ancient Greeks as the Hydaspes, the river was the site of Alexander the Great's victory over King Porus at the Battle of the Hydaspes in 326 BCE, one of the hardest-fought battles of his campaigns; Alexander founded the cities of Nicaea and Bucephala (named for his horse Bucephalus, who died there) on its banks. In Sanskrit tradition the river is the Vitasta.

Use and treaties

The river is dammed at Mangla in Azad Kashmir, one of Pakistan's largest reservoirs, used for irrigation and hydroelectric power. Under the Indus Waters Treaty of 1960 between India and Pakistan, the Jhelum is one of the "western rivers" allocated primarily to Pakistan, with India permitted limited uses including run-of-the-river hydroelectric projects upstream in Kashmir.
