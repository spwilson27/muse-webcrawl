Stoneman Douglas High School shooting

On February 14, 2018, a mass shooting occurred at Marjory Stoneman Douglas High School in Parkland, Florida, United States. Seventeen people were killed and seventeen others were injured, making it one of the deadliest school shootings in American history. The perpetrator, Nikolas Cruz, a 19-year-old former student of the school, was arrested shortly after the attack and in 2022 was sentenced to life in prison without parole.

Background

Marjory Stoneman Douglas High School, named for the Everglades conservationist, served about 3,000 students in the affluent suburb of Parkland. Cruz had been expelled from the school the previous year for disciplinary reasons. In the years before the attack he had been the subject of numerous reports to local and federal authorities, including a January 2018 tip to the FBI that was not properly followed up.

Shooting

In the afternoon of February 14, Cruz arrived at the school by rideshare carrying a semi-automatic rifle. He entered Building 12, a three-storey classroom block, pulled a fire alarm and shot students and staff in corridors and classrooms over about six minutes. He then abandoned the weapon and fled among evacuating students. Fourteen students and three staff members died; the dead included a geography teacher who held a door for students, the school's athletic director, and an assistant football coach who shielded students.

Aftermath and activism

Surviving students founded the advocacy group Never Again MSD and organised the March for Our Lives demonstration in Washington, D.C. on March 24, 2018, one of the largest youth-led protests in American history, pressing for gun-control legislation. Florida raised the minimum age for purchasing rifles and adopted a "red flag" law in the Marjory Stoneman Douglas High School Public Safety Act. The attack also prompted scrutiny of the Broward County Sheriff's Office response; the school resource officer who remained outside the building was charged (and later acquitted) over his inaction.

Legal proceedings

Cruz pleaded guilty in October 2021 to 17 counts of murder and 17 counts of attempted murder. In November 2022, after a penalty trial in which the jury declined to impose the death penalty, he was formally sentenced to consecutive life sentences without the possibility of parole.
