Exome

The exome is the part of the genome consisting of exons — the protein-coding regions of genes — together in humans amounting to roughly 1–2% of the genome, about 30 megabases, while harbouring a large share of variants known to cause Mendelian disease.

Exome
Part of: Genome
Method: Exome sequencing

Exome sequencing

Whole exome sequencing enriches for coding regions by capture probes and sequences them at high depth, at lower cost than whole-genome sequencing. It is widely used clinically to diagnose rare genetic disorders, identify cancer mutations, and in population genetics, though it misses non-coding regulatory variants and some structural variants.
