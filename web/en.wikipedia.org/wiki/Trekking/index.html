Trekking

Trekking is a form of long-distance walking undertaken on foot in areas where common means of transport are generally not available, typically along trails through mountains and other rural or wilderness areas. Unlike day hiking, trekking usually involves journeys of several days or weeks, with trekkers carrying equipment or relying on porters, pack animals, lodges, or teahouses along the route.

Famous treks

Some of the world's best-known treks include the routes to Everest Base Camp and around the Annapurnas in Nepal, the Inca Trail to Machu Picchu in Peru, the Tour du Mont Blanc in the Alps, and the Milford Track in New Zealand. Long-distance national trails such as the Appalachian Trail in the United States also attract trekkers, though through-hiking them is a distinct pursuit.

Preparation and risks

Trekking requires physical fitness, appropriate clothing and footwear, and preparation for weather, navigation, and altitude. At high elevations, altitude sickness is a serious risk requiring gradual acclimatization. Commercial trekking is a major source of income in regions such as the Himalayas, where local guides and porters support foreign visitors, and concerns have grown about the environmental impact of large numbers of trekkers on fragile mountain ecosystems.
