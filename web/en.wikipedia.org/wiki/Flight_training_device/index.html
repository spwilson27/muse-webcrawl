Flight training device

A flight training device (FTD) is a flight simulator used for pilot training that replicates a specific aircraft's cockpit and systems but, unlike a full flight simulator (FFS), is not required to have a motion platform or a full visual system. Training devices are qualified by regulators such as the FAA and EASA at various levels, and time spent in approved devices can be credited toward pilot licensing and currency requirements.

TypeFlight simulator (non-motion)
RegulatorsFAA, EASA
RelatedFull flight simulator, FNPT

Qualification levels

Under the FAA system, FTDs are qualified at levels 4 through 7, with higher levels requiring progressively more faithful modeling of aerodynamics, systems, and controls; level 7 differs from a full flight simulator mainly in lacking motion and certain visual requirements. In Europe, equivalent device categories include the Flight and Navigation Procedures Trainer (FNPT) and the Flight Training Device categories of EASA's CS-FSTD(A) specification.

Use

FTDs are used for procedural training, instrument flight practice, systems familiarization, and crew resource management exercises at a much lower cost per hour than full flight simulators or actual aircraft. Airlines, flight schools, and universities use them extensively in integrated training programs.
