Eston Hemings

Eston Hemings Jefferson (May 21, 1808 – January 3, 1856) was an American carpenter and musician. Born into slavery at Monticello, he was the youngest son of the enslaved woman Sally Hemings. Historical and DNA evidence indicates that his father was President Thomas Jefferson. After being freed in Jefferson's will in 1826, Hemings moved to Ohio, where he became a prominent musician and bandleader, and later to Wisconsin, where he lived as a white man under the name Eston H. Jefferson.

Early life

Eston Hemings was born at Monticello, the Virginia plantation of Thomas Jefferson. His mother, Sally Hemings, was an enslaved woman who was also the half-sister of Jefferson's late wife. Eston trained as a carpenter in the plantation's joinery under his uncle John Hemings.

Freedom and Ohio years

Jefferson's will freed Eston and his older brother Madison in 1826. The brothers settled in Charlottesville before moving to Chillicothe, Ohio, in the 1830s. There Eston became a well-known professional violinist and bandleader, leading a successful dance band that performed across Ohio.

Wisconsin and later life

In 1852, amid tightening racial laws and the atmosphere after the Fugitive Slave Act of 1850, Hemings moved his family to Madison, Wisconsin, where they lived under the surname Jefferson and were accepted into the white community. He worked as a carpenter and died there in 1856. In 1998, DNA testing of his male-line descendants matched the Jefferson male line, supporting the conclusion that Thomas Jefferson fathered Sally Hemings's children.
