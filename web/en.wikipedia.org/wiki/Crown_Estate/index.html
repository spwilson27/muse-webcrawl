Crown Estate

The Crown Estate is a collection of lands and holdings in the United Kingdom belonging to the British monarch as a corporation sole, making it one of the largest property estates in the United Kingdom. It is neither government property nor part of the monarch's private estate; the revenues it generates are surrendered to the Treasury in return for a sovereign grant.

History

The origins of the Crown Estate lie in the lands held by the English monarch in the medieval period. By 1760, the landed revenues were insufficient to cover the costs of government, and George III surrendered the income from the Crown lands to Parliament in return for a fixed annual payment, the Civil List. This arrangement was renewed by successive monarchs. In 2012 the Civil List was replaced by the Sovereign Grant, calculated as a percentage of the Crown Estate's profits two years in arrears.

Holdings

The estate includes extensive rural landholdings, forestry, agricultural land, and roughly half of the foreshore and almost all of the seabed around the United Kingdom out to the 12 nautical mile limit, giving it a central role in offshore wind leasing. In London, the estate owns much of Regent Street and St James's, as well as retail parks and shopping centres across the country. Its holdings also include Windsor Great Park and Ascot Racecourse.

Management

The Crown Estate is managed by the Crown Estate Commissioners, a statutory body established by the Crown Estate Act 1961, which operates on commercial lines while having regard to the principles of good management. See also Sovereign Grant and Monarchy of the United Kingdom.
