Glyphosate (IUPAC name N-(phosphonomethyl)glycine) is a broad-spectrum systemic herbicide and crop desiccant. It is the most widely used herbicide in the world, sold most famously by Monsanto (now part of Bayer) under the brand name Roundup from 1974. Glyphosate kills plants by inhibiting the enzyme 5-enolpyruvylshikimate-3-phosphate synthase (EPSPS) in the shikimate pathway, which plants and many microorganisms — but not animals — use to synthesise aromatic amino acids.

History and use

Glyphosate was first synthesised in 1950 by Swiss chemist Henri Martin but its herbicidal activity was discovered independently by Monsanto chemist John E. Franz in 1970; Franz received the National Medal of Technology for the work. Use exploded after the mid-1990s introduction of genetically modified "Roundup Ready" crops — soybean, maize, cotton and canola engineered to tolerate the herbicide — which allowed spraying over growing crops. Global agricultural use rose roughly fifteen-fold between 1996 and the mid-2010s, exceeding 800 million kilograms a year, with the majority applied in the Americas. Extensive use has driven the evolution of glyphosate-resistant weeds ("superweeds") in dozens of species, a significant agronomic problem.

Health and environmental debate

Glyphosate has been one of the most contested chemicals in the world. In 2015 the World Health Organization's International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) classified it as "probably carcinogenic to humans" (Group 2A), based largely on evidence for non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Other regulators — including the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, the European Food Safety Authority and the European Chemicals Agency — have concluded that glyphosate is not likely to be carcinogenic at expected exposure levels and have repeatedly re-approved it, most recently the European Union's ten-year renewal in 2023. The divergence stems partly from hazard- versus risk-based assessment and from studies of the pure compound versus commercial formulations containing surfactants, which are more toxic, especially to aquatic organisms.

Mass tort litigation in the United States, triggered by verdicts such as the 2018 Johnson case, led Bayer — which acquired Monsanto in 2018 — to agree in 2020 to pay more than US$10 billion to settle the bulk of Roundup cancer claims, while maintaining that the product is safe. Glyphosate use is banned or restricted in some countries and municipalities, and it remains a central issue in debates over industrial agriculture, soil microbiomes and pollinator health.
