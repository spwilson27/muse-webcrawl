The Battle of Dornach was fought on 22 July 1499 near the village of Dornach, south of Basel, between the forces of the Old Swiss Confederacy and the army of Emperor Maximilian I during the Swabian War. It was the last battle of the war and a decisive Swiss victory.

Background

An Imperial army under Count Heinrich von Fürstenberg was besieging the castle of Dorneck, held by Solothurn. The Confederates assembled a relief force drawn mainly from Bern, Solothurn, Zurich, Lucerne and Zug, and marched to raise the siege.

Battle

The Swiss advanced in the afternoon of 22 July and attacked the Imperial siege works. The fighting was hard and the outcome uncertain until reinforcements from Lucerne and Zug arrived late in the day and struck the Imperial army afresh. The Imperial force broke; Fürstenberg was among the several thousand dead, and the siege artillery and camp fell into Swiss hands.

Aftermath

Dornach ended major fighting in the Swabian War. The Treaty of Basel, signed on 22 September 1499, effectively exempted the Confederacy from Imperial taxes and jurisdiction, confirming Swiss independence in practice, if not yet in formal law.
