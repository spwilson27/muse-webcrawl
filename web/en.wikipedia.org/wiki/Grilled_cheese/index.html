Grilled cheese

A grilled cheese (also toasted cheese sandwich) is a hot sandwich made with cheese filling between slices of bread, heated until the bread browns and the cheese melts, usually cooked with butter or mayonnaise in a frying pan or griddle. It is a comfort food classic in the United States, often served with tomato soup.

TypeSandwich
Place of originUnited States (modern form)
Main ingredientsBread, cheese, butter
ServingHot

History

Cooked cheese-and-bread dishes are ancient, but the modern American grilled cheese arose in the 1920s with inexpensive sliced bread and processed cheese (Kraft Singles from 1949), becoming ubiquitous during the Depression and in school cafeterias. A second slice of bread on top distinguished it from open-faced cheese toasties.

Preparation and variations

Common cheeses include American, cheddar and Gruyère; variations add tomato, bacon, ham (croque-monsieur style) or caramelised onions; the patty melt uses a hamburger patty. See Sandwich and Cheese.
