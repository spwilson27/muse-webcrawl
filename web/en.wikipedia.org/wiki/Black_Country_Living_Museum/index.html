The Black Country Living Museum is an open-air museum of rebuilt historic buildings in Dudley, West Midlands, England. Occupying roughly 26 acres of former industrial land beside the Dudley Canal, it tells the story of the Black Country, one of Britain's first industrial landscapes, through more than fifty shops, houses and industrial buildings dismantled from around the region and rebuilt brick by brick on the site.

History and collections

The museum opened in 1978 and has grown steadily since. Its streets recreate life in the late 19th and early 20th centuries: costumed demonstrators staff a sweet shop, a fish and chip shop, a pub (the Bottle and Glass Inn), a hardware store, a chemist and workshops for chain-making, nail-making and glass-cutting. A coalmine experience takes visitors underground into a drift mine, and the site is linked by a canal arm where historic narrowboats are moored. Working vehicles — including trolleybuses and early motor cars — run through the village, and a 1920s fairground adds period amusements.

In popular culture

The museum became widely known as a filming location for the BBC drama Peaky Blinders, whose creator drew on Black Country and Birmingham gang history; many of the show's street scenes were shot on the museum's set-like streets. Visitor numbers grew sharply in response, and the museum has expanded with a 1940s–1960s high street development, recreating post-war Britain.
