The Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran, codenamed Operation Countenance, was the joint invasion of neutral Iran by the United Kingdom and the Soviet Union in August–September 1941, during the Second World War. The Allies sought to secure the Iranian oil fields and the Trans-Iranian Railway as a supply route to the USSR, and to remove German influence — a demand that Reza Shah expel German nationals, which he refused, precipitated the attack.

Invasion

On 25 August 1941 Soviet forces advanced into northern Iran from the Caucasus and Central Asia, while British and Indian forces invaded from Iraq and the Persian Gulf, landing at Abadan and Bandar Shahpur and bombing Iranian naval vessels. The small Iranian army and navy resisted briefly but were overwhelmed by superior numbers and air power; a ceasefire was accepted on 29–30 August, and Allied troops entered Tehran on 17 September.

Occupation and the Persian Corridor

Under Allied pressure Reza Shah abdicated on 16 September 1941 in favour of his son, Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, and was exiled to Mauritius and then South Africa, where he died in 1944. Iran signed a treaty of alliance in January 1942 and formally declared war on Germany in 1943. The country became the Persian Corridor, through which some 4–5 million tons of American Lend-Lease supplies reached the Soviet Union; American troops took over operation of the southern railway in 1942. Tehran hosted the 1943 conference of Roosevelt, Churchill and Stalin, whose declaration guaranteed Iran's post-war independence and territorial integrity.

Withdrawal crisis

British and American forces withdrew by early 1946, but the Soviet Union delayed its departure and sponsored separatist governments in Iranian Azerbaijan and Kurdistan. The resulting Iran crisis of 1946 — one of the first disputes before the UN Security Council — ended with Soviet withdrawal in May 1946 and the collapse of the puppet regimes. Wartime occupation also brought severe inflation and a famine in 1942–43 that killed large numbers of Iranians.
