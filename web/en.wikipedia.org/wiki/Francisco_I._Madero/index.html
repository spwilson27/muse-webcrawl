Francisco Ignacio Madero González (30 October 1873 – 22 February 1913) was a Mexican statesman and revolutionary who served as the 33rd president of Mexico from 1911 until his assassination in 1913. Coming from one of Mexico's wealthiest landowning families, Madero became the leader of the movement that overthrew the long dictatorship of Porfirio Díaz, launching the Mexican Revolution.

Early life

Madero was born in Parras, Coahuila, into the prominent Madero family, whose fortune rested on mining, banking, and agriculture. He was educated in France and at the University of California, Berkeley. A devout spiritist, Madero became convinced that Mexico needed democratic reform. In 1908 he published La sucesión presidencial en 1910 ("The Presidential Succession in 1910"), calling for free elections, and founded the Anti-Reelectionist Party to oppose Díaz's continued rule.

Revolution and presidency

After Díaz had him imprisoned during the 1910 election, Madero escaped to the United States and issued the Plan of San Luis Potosí, calling for armed revolt effective 20 November 1910. The uprising spread, and in May 1911 Díaz resigned and went into exile. Madero won the ensuing election and took office in November 1911.

As president, Madero introduced democratic reforms, freedom of the press, and political pluralism, but he moved cautiously on social and land reform, disappointing former allies such as Emiliano Zapata, who rebelled under the Plan of Ayala. Conservative forces also conspired against him.

Death

In February 1913, during the Ten Tragic Days (Decena Trágica), General Victoriano Huerta betrayed Madero and seized power. Madero and his vice president, José María Pino Suárez, were arrested and murdered on 22 February 1913 while being transferred to prison. His death galvanised the revolutionary movement, which eventually overthrew Huerta and produced the Constitution of 1917.
