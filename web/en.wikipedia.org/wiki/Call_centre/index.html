Call centre

A call centre (Commonwealth spelling) or call center (American spelling) is a managed capability that can be centralised or remote, used for receiving or transmitting a large volume of enquiries by telephone. An inbound call centre is operated by a company to administer incoming product or service support or information inquiries from consumers. Outbound call centres are operated for telemarketing, debt collection, market research, emergency notifications, and charitable or political solicitation.

Technology

Call centres rely on technologies such as automatic call distributors (ACD), interactive voice response (IVR) menus, computer telephony integration, and customer relationship management (CRM) software. Modern operations increasingly use Voice over IP, cloud-based contact centre platforms, chatbots, and omnichannel support combining phone, email, live chat, and social media.

Industry and outsourcing

From the 1990s onward, many companies outsourced call centre work to countries with lower labour costs, notably India and the Philippines, which became global hubs for English-language customer support. Performance is commonly measured with metrics such as average handling time, first-call resolution, and customer satisfaction scores.

Criticism

Call centres are frequently criticised for long waiting times, scripted interactions, and offshoring of jobs, while employees report high stress, close monitoring, and repetitive work. Automation and artificial intelligence are increasingly reshaping the sector.
