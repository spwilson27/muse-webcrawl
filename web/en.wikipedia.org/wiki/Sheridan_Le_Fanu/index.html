Joseph Thomas Sheridan Le Fanu (28 August 1814 – 7 February 1873) was an Irish writer of Gothic tales, mystery novels, and horror fiction. He was a leading ghost story writer of his time, central to the development of the genre in the Victorian era. M. R. James described Le Fanu as "absolutely in the first rank as a writer of ghost stories." His most famous works include the locked-room mystery novel Uncle Silas, the vampire novella Carmilla, and the collection In a Glass Darkly.

Life

Le Fanu was born in Dublin into a literary family of Huguenot extraction; his father was Dean of Emly. He studied law at Trinity College Dublin, was called to the bar in 1839, but chose journalism, contributing to and later owning several newspapers and becoming editor and proprietor of the Dublin University Magazine. His early work consisted of historical romances such as The Cock and Anchor (1845). After the death of his wife Susanna in 1858 he became increasingly reclusive — known to Dublin acquaintances as "the Invisible Prince" — and produced the mature supernatural fiction for which he is remembered.

Major works

  • The House by the Churchyard (1863), a mystery novel set in Chapelizod.
  • Uncle Silas (1864), a sensation novel of a young heiress menaced by her guardian.
  • In a Glass Darkly (1872), five supernatural tales framed as the casebook of Dr Martin Hesselius, including "Green Tea," "The Familiar," "Mr Justice Harbottle," and "Carmilla."
  • Carmilla (serialised 1871–72), a vampire novella predating Bram Stoker's Dracula by 25 years and a major influence on it.

Legacy

Le Fanu's work influenced M. R. James, Bram Stoker, and much subsequent supernatural fiction. His ghost stories are noted for their subtlety, unreliable narrators, and psychological ambiguity; the Hesselius frame anticipated the occult-detective genre.
