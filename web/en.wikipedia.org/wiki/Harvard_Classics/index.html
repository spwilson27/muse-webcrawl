The Harvard Classics is a 50-volume set of classic works from world literature, compiled and edited by Harvard University president Charles W. Eliot and first published in 1909–1910 by P. F. Collier & Son. The collection is sometimes known as Eliot's "Five Foot Shelf" of books, after Eliot's claim that the essential elements of a liberal education could be obtained by reading diligently from a five-foot shelf of books for fifteen minutes a day.

Eliot, who served as president of Harvard from 1869 to 1909, believed that a small number of carefully chosen books, read with care, could give a reader the equivalent of a college education in the humanities. The set was an enormous commercial success and was widely sold by subscription across the United States in the early twentieth century.

Contents

The volumes cover a broad range of genres, including history, biography, philosophy, drama, fiction, poetry, science, essays, and speeches. Notable inclusions are Benjamin Franklin's autobiography, the works of Homer, Dante, Shakespeare, Milton, and Darwin's On the Origin of Species and The Voyage of the Beagle. Volume 50 contained a general index, and a separate lectures volume introduced each volume's contents.

Later editions

A companion 20-volume set, the Harvard Classics Shelf of Fiction, was published in 1917 under the editorship of William Allan Neilson. The original set has been reprinted many times and the full text is available in digital form through projects such as Project Gutenberg and Bartleby.com.
