Snowbelt
Region: Great Lakes, North America
Cause: lake-effect snow

The Snowbelt is the region of North America surrounding the Great Lakes that receives heavy snowfall, much of it lake-effect snow produced when cold air passes over the relatively warm lake waters and picks up moisture that falls as snow on the downwind shores.

Geography

Snowbelt areas include parts of Michigan's Upper Peninsula, western and northern New York (including Buffalo, Rochester, and Syracuse), northwestern Pennsylvania around Erie, northeastern Ohio, and parts of Ontario, Canada. Some locations receive more than 250 cm (100 in) of snow per season on average.

Effects

The heavy snowfall shapes local culture, infrastructure, and economies, supporting winter tourism and skiing while demanding substantial snow-removal resources. Narrow lake-effect bands can produce intense, highly localised storms with whiteout conditions.
