Fauj-i-Khas

The Fauj-i-Khas ("special army") was the elite brigade of the Sikh Khalsa Army of Maharaja Ranjit Singh of the Sikh Empire in the early nineteenth century. Modelled on European — especially Napoleonic French — lines, it was the best-trained and best-equipped formation in the army.

Organisation

Ranjit Singh employed European officers — veterans of the Napoleonic Wars such as Jean-François Allard, Jean-Baptiste Ventura and Claude Auguste Court — to drill his regular forces. The Fauj-i-Khas, raised from around 1805–1810, comprised picked infantry battalions, cavalry and a powerful artillery train, distinguished by its uniforms and by strict European-style discipline and drill. It formed the core of the Fauj-i-Ain, the regular army, as distinct from the feudal levies (Fauj-i-Be Qawaid) and the Akali religious warriors.

History

The brigade served in Ranjit Singh's campaigns across the Punjab and towards Afghanistan. After his death in 1839 the Sikh Empire fell into factional turmoil; the Fauj-i-Khas and the rest of the Khalsa Army fought the British East India Company in the First and Second Anglo-Sikh Wars (1845–46 and 1848–49), after which the empire was annexed and the army disbanded.
