Censoring (statistics)

In statistics and engineering, censoring occurs when the value of a measurement or observation is only partially known. It is a central concept in survival analysis, where the event of interest — such as death, failure, or recovery — may not have occurred by the time observation ends.

Types

An observation is right-censored if the true value exceeds the recorded one (e.g. a patient is still alive at the end of a study), left-censored if it falls below a threshold (e.g. below a detection limit), and interval-censored if it is known only to lie between two values.

Analysis

Ignoring censoring biases estimates, so specialised methods are used, including the Kaplan–Meier estimator for survival curves, the Cox proportional hazards model, and likelihood methods that incorporate censoring probabilities. Censoring is usually assumed to be non-informative — unrelated to the underlying risk — for standard methods to be valid.
