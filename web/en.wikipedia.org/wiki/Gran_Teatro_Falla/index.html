The Gran Teatro Falla is the principal theatre of Cádiz, Spain. Standing on the Plaza Fragela, it is named in honour of the composer Manuel de Falla (1876–1946), a native of the city, and is best known throughout Spain as the home of the official contest of the Carnival of Cádiz.

History

The first Gran Teatro de Cádiz, a wooden building inaugurated in 1871, burned down in 1881. Construction of a replacement began in 1884 to designs by Adolfo Morales de los Ríos, continued under Juan Cabrera de la Torre, and proceeded slowly for lack of funds; the theatre finally opened in 1910 as the Gran Teatro de Cádiz. In 1926 it was renamed for Manuel de Falla. A substantial restoration in the 1980s returned the building to use after a period of decay.

Architecture

The theatre is a notable example of the Neo-Mudéjar style: its three great horseshoe-arch entrances and polychrome brick façade employ the brick-and-tile vocabulary associated with medieval Islamic and Mudéjar building in Spain. Inside, the horseshoe-shaped auditorium seats about 1,200, with stalls, boxes and gallery decorated in red and gold.

The Carnival

Each February the Gran Teatro Falla hosts the Concurso Oficial de Agrupaciones Carnavalescas (COAC), the official competition of Cádiz Carnival groups — chirigotas, comparsas, coros and cuartetos — whose satirical songs are composed for performance on its stage over several weeks of preliminary rounds, semi-finals and a grand final on the Friday before Shrove Tuesday. The contest is broadcast across Andalusia and is considered one of Spain's most distinctive popular-music traditions. Outside carnival season the theatre presents opera, concerts, drama and dance, including events of the city's Ibero-American Theatre Festival.
