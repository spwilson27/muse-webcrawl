Pargana

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A pargana (also spelled parganah, pergunnah) is an administrative and revenue unit of India and Bangladesh, consisting of a group of villages. Parganas were introduced under the Delhi Sultanate and were extensively used under the Mughal Empire, where they formed the subdivision of a sarkar (district) within a subah (province). The term derives from Persian.

Mughal administration

Under the revenue reforms of Akbar, the pargana was a unit of land measurement and assessment. Its officials included the shiqdar (executive officer responsible for order), the amil or amalguzar (revenue collector), the bitikchi (accountant), the khazandar (treasurer), and hereditary local record-keepers, the qanungo. Village-level records were maintained by the patwari. Parganas varied considerably in size, from a dozen to several hundred villages.

British period and after

The British retained the pargana as a revenue unit, and pargana names survive in land records in Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal and Bangladesh. The best-known modern usage is in the district names of West Bengal and Bangladesh: North 24 Parganas and South 24 Parganas, named after the 24 parganas ceded to the East India Company in 1757 following the Battle of Plassey.

Related units

  • Taluka / tehsil — comparable subdivisions used elsewhere in India
  • Mauza — a village-level revenue unit within a pargana
  • Sarkar — the district-level unit above the pargana in Mughal administration
