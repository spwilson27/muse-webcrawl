Saul (HWV 53) is a dramatic oratorio in three acts written by George Frideric Handel with a libretto by Charles Jennens. It was first performed at the King's Theatre in London on 16 January 1739.

Plot

Taken from the First Book of Samuel, the libretto dramatises the rise and tragic fall of Saul, the first king of Israel: his initial welcome of the young David after the defeat of Goliath, his growing jealousy and madness, his consultation of the Witch of Endor, and his death with his sons at the Battle of Mount Gilboa, after which David laments and is acclaimed king.

Music

Saul is noted for its vivid orchestration, among the richest Handel wrote, including trombones, a carillon, and a large organ part intended for the composer himself. The Dead March in Act III became one of Handel's most famous orchestral pieces and has long been associated with state funerals.

Reception

The oratorio marked the beginning of Handel's collaboration with Jennens, who would later compile the libretto of Messiah, and is regarded as one of the masterpieces of the English oratorio tradition.
