Internal combustion engine

Internal combustion engine
DefinitionEngine burning fuel inside its cylinders
CyclesOtto (petrol), Diesel
DominanceCars, trucks, ships, aircraft pistons for over a century
[Image: Cutaway of a four-cylinder petrol engine showing pistons and valves]

An internal combustion engine is a heat engine in which fuel burns inside the engine itself, driving pistons or a turbine with expanding gases — the generalisation of the heat engine in this crawl. Petrol (Otto-cycle) and diesel engines powered the 20th century's transport revolution.

Decline and legacy

Combustion engines made the car, truck and ship fleets and modern logistics, at the cost of urban air pollution and a large share of CO2 emissions. Electric motors now displace them in cars, though aviation (jets) and shipping will burn fuel far longer.
