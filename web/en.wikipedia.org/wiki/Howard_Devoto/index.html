Howard Devoto (born Howard Andrew Trafford, 15 March 1952) is an English singer and songwriter. He co-founded the punk rock band Buzzcocks with Pete Shelley in 1976, and after leaving that group in 1977, formed the post-punk band Magazine, whose angular, literary music was influential on later generations of musicians.

Early life

Devoto was born in Scunthorpe and raised in Nuneaton and Leeds. He studied at Leeds Grammar School and later at Bolton Institute of Technology, where he met Pete Shelley. Inspired by the Sex Pistols, the two organised the band's famous Lesser Free Trade Hall concert in Manchester in June 1976 and formed Buzzcocks.

Buzzcocks

Devoto sang on Buzzcocks' debut EP, Spiral Scratch (1977), one of the first independently released punk records, issued on their own New Hormones label. Dissatisfied with the direction of punk, he left the band shortly after its release, remarking that what once appealed to him no longer did.

Magazine

In 1977 Devoto formed Magazine with guitarist John McGeoch. The band's debut single "Shot by Both Sides" (1978) reached the UK Top 50, and their albums Real Life (1978), Secondhand Daylight (1979), and The Correct Use of Soap (1980) combined punk energy with art rock and synthesizer textures; the single "A Song from Under the Floorboards" and a cover of Sly Stone's "Thank You (Falettinme Be Mice Elf Agin)" are among their best-known recordings. Magazine split in 1981.

Later career

Devoto released the solo album Jerky Versions of the Dream in 1983, and later formed Luxuria with Noko, releasing two albums in the late 1980s. He subsequently worked for a photographic agency and largely withdrew from music, before reuniting with Shelley for the ShelleyDevoto album Buzzkunst (2002). Magazine reformed in 2009 for concerts and released the album No Thyself in 2011.

Discography

With Buzzcocks: Spiral Scratch EP (1977). With Magazine: Real Life (1978), Secondhand Daylight (1979), The Correct Use of Soap (1980), Magic, Murder and the Weather (1981), No Thyself (2011). Solo: Jerky Versions of the Dream (1983).
