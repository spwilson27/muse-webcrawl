Firecracker

A firecracker (also cracker, noise maker or banger) is a small explosive device primarily designed to produce a large amount of noise, especially in the form of a loud bang, usually for amusement. Firecrackers, along with fireworks, originated in China and are traditionally used in celebrations — notably Chinese New Year — to scare away evil spirits, a custom traced to the legendary beast Nian.

History

Before gunpowder, the Chinese burned bamboo stems, whose air pockets exploded with a crack in the fire — the original "bursting bamboo". Paper-wrapped powder crackers developed by the Song dynasty and became widespread with the fireworks industry centred on Liuyang in Hunan province, still a major production centre today.

Construction

A firecracker is typically a cardboard or paper tube packed with pyrotechnic composition — historically black powder, in many modern consumer crackers flash powder — sealed at both ends and fitted with a short visco fuse. They are often braided into strings that are lit to produce a rapid series of bangs. Large types include the M-80 and cherry bomb, which are illegal as consumer fireworks in many jurisdictions.

Regulation and safety

Because of injuries and noise, firecrackers are restricted or banned for consumers in many countries and cities; several Chinese cities have alternated between bans and partial lifting of bans during Spring Festival. Professional and festival use remains common, and quieter "electronic firecrackers" are marketed as alternatives.
