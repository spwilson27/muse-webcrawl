Home exchange

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Home exchange, also known as house swapping, is a form of accommodation in which two parties agree to exchange homes for a period of time, usually for a holiday. Because no money changes hands for the accommodation itself, a home exchange can substantially reduce the cost of travel, and it gives participants the experience of living in a residential neighbourhood rather than a tourist district.

Types of exchange

In a simultaneous exchange, both parties stay in each other's homes at the same time. In a non-simultaneous exchange, one party visits while the other's home is empty (for example a second home or while the owners are themselves away), with the return visit made later. Hospitality exchange involves hosting guests in one's home while present, in return for being hosted later. Some networks also operate points systems, under which members earn credits by hosting that can be spent on stays elsewhere.

History

Organised home exchange began in the 1950s, notably among teachers seeking affordable long holidays, and grew through printed directories published by exchange clubs. The 1990s and 2000s moved the practice online: websites such as HomeLink (founded 1953 as a print network) and HomeExchange allowed members to publish listings, search by destination and contact one another directly. The 2006 romantic comedy film The Holiday, about two women who swap homes, popularised the idea further.

Practical considerations

Exchangers typically agree terms in writing covering dates, care of pets and plants, use of cars, cleaning and utility costs. Trust is central: most platforms verify identities, publish member reviews, and offer guarantees against damage or cancellation. Participants are advised to check their home insurance and, where relevant, lease terms before exchanging.
