Très Riches Heures du Duc de Berry

The Très Riches Heures du Duc de Berry ("The Very Rich Hours of the Duke of Berry") is an illuminated book of hours commissioned by Jean, Duke of Berry, around 1412–1416. It is widely regarded as the masterpiece of International Gothic painting and one of the most famous manuscripts in the world. It is now held by the Musée Condé at the Château de Chantilly, France.

Contents

The manuscript contains 206 leaves of fine vellum with 131 miniatures. Its most celebrated section is the calendar: twelve full-page scenes showing the labours of the months, each pairing the seasonal work of peasants with the courtly pleasures of the duke against the backdrop of his castles, among them the January banquet scene, the haymaking of June and the grape harvest of September beneath the Château de Saumur.

Production

The main campaign of painting was carried out by the Limbourg brothers, Paul, Johan and Herman, the duke's court artists. Work stopped in 1416 when the brothers and their patron all died, probably of plague. The unfinished manuscript passed through several hands; an intermediate painter, sometimes identified as Barthélemy d'Eyck, worked on it in the 1440s, and Jean Colombe completed it for Charles I, Duke of Savoy, in the 1480s.

Significance

The Très Riches Heures is prized for its naturalistic landscape observation, luminous colour and encyclopaedic depiction of late medieval life, from ploughing and sowing to falconry and feasting. Because of its fragility it is rarely displayed, though facsimiles are widely reproduced.
