Harmonic oscillator

In classical mechanics, a harmonic oscillator is a system that, when displaced from its equilibrium position, experiences a restoring force F proportional to the displacement x: F = −kx, where k is a positive constant.

EquationF = −kx
MotionSimple harmonic motion
FrequencyDepends on mass and spring constant

Simple harmonic oscillator

If F is the only force acting on the system, it is a simple (or undamped) harmonic oscillator, and its motion is sinusoidal oscillation around equilibrium with constant amplitude and a single constant frequency independent of amplitude.

Examples

Examples include a mass on a spring, a pendulum swinging at small angles, and alternating current in an LC circuit. Many physical systems behave approximately as harmonic oscillators near equilibrium.

Damped and driven oscillators

Real oscillators experience friction (damping) and may be driven by an external force, leading to phenomena such as resonance. The quantum harmonic oscillator is a cornerstone model in quantum mechanics.
