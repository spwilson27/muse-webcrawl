Rider–Waite tarot deck

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Rider–Waite tarot deck (also known as the Rider–Waite–Smith, Waite–Smith, or simply Rider deck) is a widely popular deck for tarot card reading. It is one of the most published, enduring and ubiquitous tarot decks in the world, and is the best-selling tarot deck ever. The images were drawn by artist Pamela Colman Smith from the instructions of academic and mystic A. E. Waite and were originally published by the Rider Company in 1909.

The deck has been published in numerous editions and inspired a wide array of variants and spin-offs. It is estimated that more than 100 million copies of the deck exist in more than 20 countries. While the images are originally drawn in a simple style, the details of the background environments and the accompanying symbols are carefully observed, and the scenes are reminiscent of fin de siècle (Victorian and Edwardian) theatre.

Overview

The Rider–Waite deck revolutionized the visual design of tarot. It was the first deck in which every card of the minor arcana bore a fully illustrated scene, rather than simple arrangements of suit symbols (pip cards). This innovation made the deck far more accessible to intuitive readers and helped popularize tarot reading in the English-speaking world.

The deck contains 78 cards: 22 major arcana (trumps), including The Fool, The Magician, The High Priestess, The Empress, The Emperor, The Hierophant, The Lovers, The Chariot, Strength, The Hermit, Wheel of Fortune, Justice, The Hanged Man, Death, Temperance, The Devil, The Tower, The Star, The Moon, The Sun, Judgement and The World; and 56 minor arcana in four suits — Wands, Cups, Swords and Pentacles — each with court cards (King, Queen, Knight and Page).

History

Arthur Edward Waite, a member of the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, commissioned Pamela Colman Smith — also a Golden Dawn member — to illustrate the deck. Smith completed the artwork in about six months in 1909, an unusually fast pace for such a project. The deck was first published in December 1909 by William Rider & Son of London.

Waite replaced the traditional Christian imagery of some older decks with symbolism drawn from a broader range of mystical traditions, and famously swapped the positions of Strength and Justice in the major arcana order, giving Strength the number VIII and Justice the number XI.

Symbolism

Smith's illustrations are rich in occult and literary symbolism. Her designs were influenced by the earlier Sola Busca tarot, the first deck known to illustrate all minor arcana cards, examples of which she saw at the British Museum. Many cards incorporate astrological, Kabbalistic and alchemical references consistent with Golden Dawn teachings.

Copyright status

In the United Kingdom the deck entered the public domain in 2012, seventy years after the death of Pamela Colman Smith (d. 1951). In the United States, the 1909 edition is in the public domain, although modern recolored editions published by U.S. Games Systems may carry their own protections.
