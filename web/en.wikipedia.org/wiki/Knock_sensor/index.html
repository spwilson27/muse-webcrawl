Knock sensor

A knock sensor is a piezoelectric sensor fitted to the engine block of a spark ignition internal combustion engine. It detects the high-frequency vibrations characteristic of engine knocking (detonation) and signals the engine control unit (ECU).

Operation

The sensor, usually a piezoelectric crystal under a bolted mass, generates a voltage when vibrated. The ECU filters the signal for the frequency band typical of knock for that engine, and when knock is detected it retards ignition timing — and in turbocharged engines may reduce boost — until knocking stops, then gradually advances timing again for efficiency.

Purpose

Knock sensors allow engines to run close to the knock limit with higher compression and more advanced timing, improving power and fuel economy while protecting against damage from low-octane fuel or adverse conditions.
