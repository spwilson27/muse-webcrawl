The Academy Award for Best International Feature Film (known as the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film until 2020) is one of the Academy Awards presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences. It is given to a feature-length motion picture produced outside the United States with a predominantly non-English dialogue track. Each country is invited to submit one film per year.

History

From 1947 to 1955 the Academy presented honorary awards to notable foreign-language releases, honouring such films as Shoeshine (Italy) and Rashomon (Japan). A competitive category was created for the 1956 awards, first won by Fellini's La Strada. In 2019 the Academy renamed the category Best International Feature Film, and in 2020 the South Korean Parasite became the first winner of the award also to win Best Picture.

Notable winners

Italian cinema is the most successful in the category's history, with 14 wins, including Cinema Paradiso (1989), Life Is Beautiful (1998) and The Great Beauty (2013). France follows closely. Directors with multiple wins include Federico Fellini and Vittorio De Sica (four each) and Ingmar Bergman (three).

Rules and controversies

The submitting country must have substantial creative control of the film, and the "one country, one film" rule has often been criticised for excluding acclaimed works. The Academy has gradually relaxed requirements on language and theatrical release.
