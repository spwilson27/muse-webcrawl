Roosevelt dime

The Roosevelt dime is the current ten-cent coin of the United States, first issued in 1946. It bears a portrait of President Franklin D. Roosevelt on the obverse and a torch flanked by olive and oak branches on the reverse.

Background

After Roosevelt's death in April 1945, Congress moved to honour him on the dime — a fitting denomination because of his association with the March of Dimes, the charity founded to fight polio, the disease that had paralysed him. The coin replaced the Mercury dime. Chief Engraver John R. Sinnock designed both sides; his initials "JS" on the obverse briefly fuelled a rumour that they stood for Joseph Stalin.

Specifications

The dime was struck in 90% silver until 1964; since 1965 it has been a copper-nickel clad composition. It has a diameter of 17.91 mm, a mass of 2.268 g, and a reeded edge. It remains the smallest and thinnest US coin in circulation.
