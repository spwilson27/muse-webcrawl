Tacos al pastor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A trompo of marinated pork with pineapple, tacos being assembled beside it]

Tacos al pastor are Mexico City's signature taco: pork marinated in achiote, dried chillies and pineapple, stacked on a vertical spit — the trompo — roasted, shaved, and served on small corn tortillas with onion, coriander, salsa and a sliver of the pineapple that crowns the spit. They are shawarma's Mexican grandchildren: Lebanese immigrants to Mexico in the late 19th and 20th centuries brought the vertical spit, and Mexican cooks rebuilt it with pork, achiote and chilli where lamb, tahini and pita had been. The name, "shepherd style", remembers the lamb it left behind.

1. The taquero's art

The taquero's art

A great trompo is architecture — marinated slices pressed into a spinning top of meat — and its carving is theatre: the taquero shaves crisped edges with a long knife and, with a flick, catches a flying piece of pineapple on the tortilla. Claims to the invention are contested among Mexico City taquerías of the 1960s (El Tizoncito, founded 1966, is the loudest claimant). Tijuana's adobada variant, the quesabirria era, and global food media — which voted tacos al pastor the world's best-rated dish in a 2019 TasteAtlas poll — carried the form worldwide, but its home remains the late-night Mexico City stand, eaten standing, two at a time, then two more.
