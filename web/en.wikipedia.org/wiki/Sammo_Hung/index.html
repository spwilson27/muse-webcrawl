Sammo Hung Kam-bo (Chinese: 洪金寶; born 7 January 1952) is a Hong Kong actor, martial artist, film producer, and director, known for his work in martial arts films and Hong Kong action cinema. Despite his large build he is famed for his speed, agility, and acrobatic skill. As the eldest of the "Seven Little Fortunes" Peking opera troupe, he was the senior training brother (da shi xiong, "big brother") of Jackie Chan and Yuen Biao, with whom he starred in a celebrated series of 1980s action comedies.

Career

Hung was enrolled at age nine in the China Drama Academy, the Peking opera school run by Yu Jim-yuen, enduring its famously harsh training for seven years; a serious injury that kept him bedridden led to the weight gain that became part of his screen persona. He began as a child actor and stuntman in the 1960s, choreographed fights for Shaw Brothers and Golden Harvest — including for Bruce Lee, with whom he briefly appears in the opening of Enter the Dragon (1973) — and emerged as a star-director with The Iron-Fisted Monk (1977).

With Chan and Biao, Hung headlined Winners and Sinners (1983), Wheels on Meals (1984), My Lucky Stars (1985), and Dragons Forever (1988), blending kung fu, comedy, and stunt work in a style that defined the genre. His own productions such as Encounters of the Spooky Kind (1980) pioneered the jiangshi horror-comedy, and The Prodigal Son (1981) and Eastern Condors (1987) are considered landmarks. In the United States he starred in the CBS series Martial Law (1998–2000). In later decades he became one of the most sought-after action choreographers in Chinese cinema, designing the fights for the Ip Man films and appearing opposite Donnie Yen in SPL (2005) and Ip Man 2 (2010), and he continues to direct and act, including in The Bodyguard (2016) and the ensemble hit Twilight of the Warriors: Walled In (2024).
