Eukaryotic small ribosomal subunit (40S)

The eukaryotic small ribosomal subunit (40S) is the smaller of the two subunits of the eukaryotic 80S ribosome. It contains the 18S ribosomal RNA and about 33 ribosomal proteins, and its principal role in translation is decoding: it binds messenger RNA and ensures that transfer RNAs are matched correctly to codons.

Structure

The 40S subunit has a characteristic shape with a head, body, and platform, and a beak formed by the 18S rRNA. During initiation, it is recruited to the mRNA as part of the 43S pre-initiation complex, carrying the initiator methionyl-tRNA and numerous initiation factors, and scans the mRNA for the start codon.

Function

Upon recognition of the AUG start codon, initiation factors are released and the large 60S subunit joins to form the complete 80S ribosome, which then proceeds to elongation. The small subunit's decoding centre monitors base pairing between the codon and the anticodon of incoming aminoacyl-tRNAs. Some viruses exploit the 40S subunit through internal ribosome entry sites (IRES) that recruit it directly to viral RNA.
