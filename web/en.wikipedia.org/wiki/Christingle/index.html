Christingle

A Christingle is a symbolic object used in the Advent, Christmas, and Epiphany services of many Christian denominations. It consists of an orange, representing the world, with a candle placed in its centre, representing Jesus Christ as the Light of the World. Christingle services are especially popular with children and are widely held in the United Kingdom, often in support of The Children's Society.

Christingle
ComponentsOrange, candle, red ribbon, four cocktail sticks with sweets or dried fruit
OriginMoravian Church, Marienborn, Germany, 1747
Used duringAdvent and Christmas services

Symbolism

Each element has a meaning: the orange represents the world; the red ribbon encircling it symbolises the blood of Christ and God's love; the four cocktail sticks, pointing in the four directions, represent the four seasons (or the four corners of the earth) and carry sweets and dried fruit signifying the fruits of the earth; and the lit candle represents Jesus as Light of the World.

History

The tradition began in the Moravian Church. On 20 December 1747, Bishop Johannes de Watteville held a service in Marienborn, Germany, at which children were given a candle wrapped with a red ribbon to symbolise Christ's love. Christingle services were introduced to the Church of England in 1968 by John Pensom of The Children's Society, and they have since become a fixture of British Christmas celebrations, raising funds for children's charities.
