Sima Zhao (211 – 6 September 265), courtesy name Zishang, was a Chinese military general, politician, and regent of the state of Cao Wei during the Three Kingdoms period of China. He was the second son of Sima Yi and maintained control of Wei after his elder brother Sima Shi's death in 255.

Rise to power

As regent, Sima Zhao consolidated the Sima family's dominance over the Wei court, deposing the emperor Cao Mao's attempt to resist him — Cao Mao was killed in a failed coup in 260, after which Sima Zhao installed Cao Huan as a puppet emperor. The widely known saying "Sima Zhao's intentions are obvious to every passerby" (司馬昭之心，路人皆知) dates from this period and refers to his transparent ambition for the throne.

Conquest of Shu

In 263, Sima Zhao dispatched the generals Zhong Hui and Deng Ai to conquer Wei's rival state Shu Han, whose emperor Liu Shan surrendered, ending decades of division in the west. He was rewarded with the title King of Jin in 264. Before he could take the throne himself he died in 265; his son Sima Yan forced Cao Huan to abdicate and founded the Jin dynasty, posthumously honouring Sima Zhao as Emperor Wen.
