Medicare (Canada)

Medicare is an unofficial name for Canada's publicly funded health care system. Under the Canada Health Act of 1984, the federal government sets national standards and contributes funding, while the thirteen provinces and territories administer their own insurance plans covering medically necessary hospital and physician services without point-of-care charges.

Principles

The Act's five criteria are public administration, comprehensiveness, universality, portability between provinces, and accessibility. Care is delivered mostly by private or independent providers paid from public funds — a single-payer model for core services — while prescription drugs outside hospital, dentistry and vision care are only partly covered, often through private insurance.

History

Saskatchewan pioneered public hospital insurance in 1947 and physician coverage in 1962 under Premier Tommy Douglas, widely called the "father of Medicare". Federal cost-sharing legislation followed in 1957 and 1966, extending coverage nationwide. The system is a defining feature of Canadian identity, though debates continue over waiting times, funding and the permitted role of private care.
