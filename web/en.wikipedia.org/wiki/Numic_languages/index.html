Numic languages

The Numic languages are a branch of the Uto-Aztecan language family spoken by Indigenous peoples of the Great Basin and adjacent regions of the western United States. The name derives from the word for "person" or "people" (as in nümü, nimi, niimi) found across the languages. Numic peoples traditionally include the Paiute, Shoshone, Ute and Comanche.

Branches

Numic divides into three branches. The Western Numic languages are Northern Paiute (including the Bannock dialect) and Mono. The Central Numic languages are Shoshoni, Panamint (Timbisha) and Comanche, which split from Shoshone around the late 17th or early 18th century as the Comanche moved onto the southern Plains. The Southern Numic languages form a dialect chain including Ute, Southern Paiute (with Chemehuevi) and Kawaiisu.

History

Linguists and archaeologists associate the spread of Numic languages with an expansion out of a homeland near the southwestern edge of the Great Basin — often placed in southeastern California — roughly within the last thousand years, replacing or absorbing earlier languages across the Basin and eastern California. Most Numic languages are now endangered, with small numbers of fluent speakers, though revitalisation programmes are under way in many communities.
