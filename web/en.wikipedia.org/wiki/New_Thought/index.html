New Thought

New Thought is a spiritual movement that developed in the United States in the nineteenth century. It is rooted in the teachings of Phineas Parkhurst Quimby (1802–1866), a clockmaker and mental healer from Maine whose "Science of Health and Happiness" held that disease originates in the mind and can be corrected by right thinking. Adherents generally believe in the power of positive thinking, the law of attraction, creative visualization, and the presence of a divine intelligence within each person.

History

Quimby's ideas were spread by his students and patients, including Mary Baker Eddy, who went on to found Christian Science, though she later distanced herself from Quimby's influence. Figures such as Warren Felt Evans, Emma Curtis Hopkins — often called the "teacher of teachers" — and the Dressers helped systematize New Thought in the 1880s and 1890s. Organizations that emerged from the movement include Unity (founded by Charles and Myrtle Fillmore), Religious Science (founded by Ernest Holmes), and the Church of Divine Science.

Beliefs

Core New Thought teachings hold that God or Infinite Intelligence is supreme, universal, and everlasting; that divinity dwells within each person; that human thought is creative and shapes experience; and that illness and misfortune can be overcome through affirmative prayer and right belief. The movement influenced Norman Vincent Peale's The Power of Positive Thinking (1952) and much of the later self-help and prosperity literature. It is distinct from the later cultural label of New Sincerity, despite the similar name.
