Hemiola

Hemiola
TypeRhythmic device
Ratio3:2
Common inRenaissance, Baroque, and many folk traditions

In music, a hemiola (also hemiolia) is the ratio 3:2, used in rhythm as the interplay of three beats against two, or two against three. The term derives from the Greek hemiolios, meaning "in the ratio of one and a half to one."

Use

A common hemiola occurs in triple time, where two groups of three quavers are regrouped as three groups of two, temporarily suggesting duple metre. Composers of the Renaissance and Baroque — including Brahms, who used it extensively — employed hemiolas to create rhythmic ambiguity and forward drive, particularly at cadences in dances such as the courante. Hemiola patterns are also central to much West African and Latin American music.

In pitch

In tuning and harmony, the 3:2 ratio describes the perfect fifth: a string divided in this ratio produces a fifth above the fundamental. In Pythagorean tuning, consonances were described by such ratios, and the term originally referred to this pitch relationship in ancient Greek theory.
