Íslendingabók (Old Norse: "The Book of the Icelanders") is a historical work by the Icelandic priest and chronicler Ari Þorgilsson (1067–1148). Written in the vernacular in the early 12th century, it is the oldest narrative source for the early history of Iceland and a key document for the chronology of the Viking Age North Atlantic.

Content

The short work traces Icelandic history from the Norse settlement (landnám, beginning about 870) through the founding of the Althing in 930, the conversion to Christianity around the year 1000, the lives of Iceland's early bishops, and the establishment of the two Icelandic dioceses, ending with events of Ari's own lifetime. It includes a genealogy of the yngling kings and lists of lawspeakers.

Ari names his informants — including Hallr Þórarinsson and Teitr Ísleifsson, who had themselves known witnesses of earlier events — and is unusually explicit for a medieval author about checking his material, which has earned him a reputation as a careful, critical historian.

Manuscripts

Ari produced two versions; only the younger survives, preserved in two 17th-century paper copies (AM 113 a and AM 113 b fol.) made by Jón Erlendsson from a medieval manuscript now lost. Íslendingabók is closely related to Landnámabók, the more detailed Book of Settlements.
