Palermo Technical Impact Hazard Scale

The Palermo Technical Impact Hazard Scale is a logarithmic scale used by astronomers to rate the potential hazard of a near-Earth object impact. It compares the likelihood of a predicted impact with the average background risk posed by objects of the same or greater energy over the years until the predicted event.

Definition

The Palermo value is the base-10 logarithm of the ratio between the probability of the impact in question and the background annual impact probability multiplied by the time in years until the event. A value of 0 means the risk equals the background risk; −2 means the event is 1% as likely as a random background event; most newly discovered objects briefly register values between −8 and −2 before further observations rule the impact out.

Notable values

The asteroid 99942 Apophis briefly reached a Palermo value above 0 in December 2004 — the highest recorded — before additional observations eliminated the 2029 impact possibility. The scale is named for Palermo, Sicily, where Giuseppe Piazzi discovered the first asteroid, Ceres, in 1801.
