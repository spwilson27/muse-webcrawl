Shasta (deity)

Shasta (Sanskrit: Śāstā, "teacher, ruler, guide") is a Hindu deity whose worship is concentrated in South India, particularly Kerala and Tamil Nadu. In the Tamil and Malayali traditions, Shasta is most commonly identified with Ayyappa, the deity enshrined at Sabarimala, conceived as the son of Shiva and Vishnu in his female form Mohini — hence Ayyappa's epithet Hariharaputra, "son of Hari (Vishnu) and Hara (Shiva)."

The name and figure of Shasta have older layers: in some texts Śāstā is an epithet applied to other gods, and in Tamil tradition Ayyanar, a village guardian deity worshipped with shrines on settlement boundaries, is closely associated with and often merged into Shasta. Icons of Shasta/Ayyanar may show the god seated, sometimes with consorts, or riding a horse or tiger; Ayyappa at Sabarimala is characteristically shown in a yogic seated posture ("yogapattasana") cinched with a band.

Worship

The Sabarimala pilgrimage, drawing tens of millions of devotees annually, is the most famous expression of Shasta-Ayyappa devotion. Pilgrims traditionally observe a period of austerity (vratham) of 41 days, wear black or saffron clothing, and climb the eighteen sacred steps to the shrine. Shasta temples are also found throughout Kerala and Tamil Nadu, and among Tamil diaspora communities in Sri Lanka, Malaysia, and Singapore.
