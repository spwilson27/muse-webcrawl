"Try to Remember" is a song from the musical comedy The Fantasticks (1960), with music by Harvey Schmidt and lyrics by Tom Jones. It is the opening number of the show, sung by the Narrator (El Gallo), who invites the audience to recall September — "Try to remember the kind of September / When life was slow and oh, so mellow" — setting up the nostalgic, bittersweet fable that follows. Because The Fantasticks became the world's longest-running musical, playing Off-Broadway for 42 years (1960–2002) and thousands of productions worldwide, the song became a widely known standard.

Recordings

The song was first performed by Jerry Orbach, who originated the role of El Gallo, and appears on the original cast album. It was one of three songs from the score (with "Soon It's Gonna Rain" and "They Were You") to achieve independent popularity. Notable recordings include versions by Harry Belafonte, who sang it in his live shows; the Brothers Four; Ed Ames; Roger Whittaker; and Josh Groban, who recorded it for his 2015 album Stages. In 1965, a recording by The Brothers Four and other renditions helped keep the tune in the popular repertoire, and it has been used in films, television, and memorial contexts, particularly around September anniversaries in the United States.

Legacy

The song's gentle waltz melody and lyric built on seasonal metaphor — September ripening into December, and the refrain "follow, follow, follow" — made it a standard of the cabaret and community-theatre repertoire. It remains the signature number of The Fantasticks and is often sung at nostalgia-themed concerts and remembrance events.
