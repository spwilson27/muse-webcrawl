Crop

Wheat is one of the world's most important staple crops.

A crop is a plant that can be grown and harvested extensively for profit or subsistence. In other words, a crop is a plant or plant product that is grown for a specific purpose, such as food, livestock fodder, fuel, or fibre. When plants of the same kind are grown in rows or other systematic arrangements, they are collectively called a crop. Most crops are cultivated in agriculture or hydroponics.

Types

Crops are commonly classified by their use. Food crops include the cereals — wheat, rice and maize (corn) together provide the majority of the world's food energy — as well as legumes, vegetables, fruits and oilseeds. Feed crops are grown for livestock fodder, either grazed, cut green, or stored as hay or silage. Fibre crops such as cotton, flax and hemp are grown for textiles and industrial materials. Ornamental crops are grown for display, and industrial or energy crops are grown for biofuel, rubber and other products.

Importance

A relatively small number of crop species dominate global agriculture: rice, wheat and maize alone account for roughly half of the calories consumed by humans. Crop yields have risen dramatically since the mid-20th century through the Green Revolution's combination of high-yielding varieties, fertiliser, irrigation and mechanisation, though agriculture remains vulnerable to pests, disease, soil degradation and climate change.
