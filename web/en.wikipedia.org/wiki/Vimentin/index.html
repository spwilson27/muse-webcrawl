Vimentin

Vimentin is a type III intermediate filament protein that is expressed in mesenchymal cells and is a major component of their cytoskeleton. In humans it is encoded by the VIM gene on chromosome 10. Vimentin filaments anchor organelles in the cytoplasm, maintain cell shape and integrity, and participate in cell migration, signaling, and wound healing.

Structure

Like other intermediate filament proteins, a vimentin monomer has a central α-helical rod domain flanked by globular head and tail domains. Monomers pair into coiled-coil dimers, which assemble into tetramers and then into unit-length filaments that elongate into mature 10-nanometre filaments.

Clinical significance

Vimentin immunohistochemistry is used in diagnostic pathology: its presence helps identify tumors of mesenchymal origin, such as sarcomas, and it is characteristically expressed in melanoma, renal cell carcinoma, and endometrial carcinoma, among others. Upregulation of vimentin is a hallmark of epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), a program associated with cancer invasion and metastasis. Vimentin is also found on the surface of some pathogens and activated cells and is a target of autoantibodies in certain autoimmune conditions.
