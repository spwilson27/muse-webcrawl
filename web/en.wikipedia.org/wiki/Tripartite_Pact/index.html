Tripartite Pact

The Tripartite Pact, also known as the Berlin Pact, was an agreement signed in Berlin on 27 September 1940 between Germany, Italy and Japan forming the military alliance known as the Axis powers during World War II.

Tripartite Pact
Signed27 September 1940
LocationBerlin, Germany
Original partiesGermany, Italy, Japan

Terms

The signatories recognised each other's spheres — German and Italian leadership in Europe, Japanese in Greater East Asia — and pledged mutual assistance if one was attacked by a power not then involved in the war, aimed chiefly at deterring the United States. Hungary, Romania, Slovakia, Bulgaria, Yugoslavia (briefly) and Croatia later adhered.

Operation

The pact was largely ineffective as a coordinated alliance; Japan did not join the German war on the USSR. See Axis powers and World War II.
