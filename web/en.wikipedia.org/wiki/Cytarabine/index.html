Cytarabine, also known as cytosine arabinoside (ara-C), is a chemotherapy medication used to treat acute myeloid leukemia (AML), acute lymphocytic leukemia (ALL), chronic myelogenous leukemia (CML), and non-Hodgkin's lymphoma. It is given by injection into a vein, under the skin, or into the cerebrospinal fluid. There is also a liposomal formulation for intrathecal use in lymphomatous meningitis.

Chemical structure of cytarabine.

Medical uses

Cytarabine is a mainstay of induction therapy for acute myeloid leukemia, typically combined with an anthracycline such as daunorubicin in the "7+3" regimen — seven days of continuous cytarabine infusion plus three days of anthracycline. High-dose cytarabine (HiDAC) is used in consolidation therapy. Intrathecal cytarabine is used to prevent or treat leukemic involvement of the central nervous system.

Mechanism of action

Cytarabine is a pyrimidine analogue of cytidine. Inside the cell it is phosphorylated to its active triphosphate form, ara-CTP, which competes with deoxycytidine triphosphate for incorporation into DNA. Once incorporated, it inhibits DNA polymerase and interferes with DNA synthesis and repair, causing chain termination. Its cytotoxicity is greatest in the S phase of the cell cycle, making it most active against rapidly dividing cells.

History

The compound was first isolated from the Caribbean sponge Tethya crypta in the 1950s as part of research into naturally occurring nucleosides (spongothymidine and spongouridine). Cytarabine was synthesized in 1959 and approved for medical use in the United States in 1969. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Side effects

Common side effects include bone marrow suppression (leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia), nausea, vomiting, fever, rash, and inflammation of the mouth and gut. High doses can cause cerebellar neurotoxicity ("cytarabine syndrome" with fever, myalgia and rash), conjunctivitis, and pulmonary toxicity. The liposomal intrathecal preparation can cause chemical arachnoiditis, mitigated by co-administered dexamethasone.
