Boer Wars

The Boer Wars were two conflicts fought between the British Empire and the Boer republics — the South African Republic (Transvaal) and the Orange Free State — over British influence in Southern Africa. The First Boer War (1880–1881) ended in a Boer victory; the Second Boer War (1899–1902) ended with British annexation of both republics.

First Boer War

Britain's 1877 annexation of the Transvaal provoked rebellion. Boer forces defeated the British at Majuba Hill in February 1881, and the Pretoria Convention restored self-government under British suzerainty.

Second Boer War

The discovery of gold on the Witwatersrand heightened tensions; the war began in October 1899. After early Boer successes, British reinforcements captured the republican capitals in 1900, but Boer commandos waged a prolonged guerrilla campaign. Britain responded with scorched-earth tactics and concentration camps in which tens of thousands of Boer civilians and Black Africans died. The Treaty of Vereeniging (1902) ended the war; the Union of South Africa followed in 1910.
