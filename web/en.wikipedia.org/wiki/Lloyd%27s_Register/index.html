Lloyd's Register (LR) is a technical and professional services organisation and a maritime classification society, wholly owned by the Lloyd's Register Foundation, a UK charity dedicated to research and education in science and engineering. It is named after Edward Lloyd's 17th-century coffee house in London, where shipowners, merchants, and underwriters gathered for shipping news.

History

In 1760 the customers of the coffee house formed a society that began publishing a register of ships, grading hulls and equipment so underwriters could judge risk — the origin of the term "classification" and of the famous rating "A1". Lloyd's Register issued the first rules for surveying and classifying ships and remained the archetype of the classification society. The society separated from Lloyd's of London insurance market early in its history and is an independent organisation.

Activities

Today Lloyd's Register provides classification, compliance, and consultancy services across marine and offshore, energy, and inspection sectors, employing surveyors and engineers in dozens of countries. Its charitable owner, Lloyd's Register Foundation, funds research into safety and ocean-related challenges.
