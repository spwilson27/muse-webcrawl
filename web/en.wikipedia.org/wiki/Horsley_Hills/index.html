Horsley Hills

Horsley Hills
Location: Annamayya district, Andhra Pradesh, India
Elevation: about 1,290 m (4,230 ft)
Nearest town: Madanapalle (about 27 km)
Named for: W. D. Horsley

Horsley Hills is a series of hills and a popular hill station in Annamayya district (formerly part of Chittoor district) of Andhra Pradesh, India, near the town of Madanapalle. Rising to about 1,290 metres (4,230 ft) above sea level, the hills form part of the southern Eastern Ghats and are sometimes called the "Ooty of Andhra Pradesh" for their cool climate.

History

Local legend associates the hills with a hermit named Mallamma. The hills were developed as a summer retreat during the British period by W. D. Horsley, the Collector of Cuddapah district from 1863 to 1867, who built a residence there in 1870; the hills were named after him.

Flora, fauna and attractions

The hills are noted for dense forests of eucalyptus, jacaranda, allamanda, and gulmohar, and for the nearby Kaigal Falls (Dumukuralla Falls) and the Kaundinya Wildlife Sanctuary, known for elephants. Points of interest include the sunset viewpoint, Mallamma temple, a small zoo and environmental park, and the hilltop governor's bungalow. Adventure tourism offerings include zorbing, rappelling, and trekking. The hills are about 160 km from Bengaluru and 27 km from Madanapalle.
