Safety data sheet

A safety data sheet (SDS), historically called a material safety data sheet (MSDS), is a document that lists information relating to occupational safety and health for the use of various substances and products. SDSs are a widely used system for cataloguing information on chemicals, chemical compounds and chemical mixtures, including instructions for safe use and the potential hazards associated with a material.

A safety data sheet summarises a chemical product's hazards, handling precautions and emergency measures.

Contents

Under the Globally Harmonized System (GHS), an SDS follows a standard sixteen-section format:

  1. Identification
  2. Hazard(s) identification
  3. Composition / information on ingredients
  4. First-aid measures
  5. Fire-fighting measures
  6. Accidental release measures
  7. Handling and storage
  8. Exposure controls / personal protection
  9. Physical and chemical properties
  10. Stability and reactivity
  11. Toxicological information
  12. Ecological information
  13. Disposal considerations
  14. Transport information
  15. Regulatory information
  16. Other information

Regulation

In the United States, the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) requires safety data sheets under its Hazard Communication Standard (29 CFR 1910.1200) for potentially harmful substances in the workplace, and employers must keep them readily accessible to employees. In the European Union, SDSs are governed by the REACH Regulation (EC No 1907/2006), whose Annex II prescribes their content and format. Suppliers are responsible for providing an SDS for hazardous substances and mixtures placed on the market.

History

Safety data sheets grew out of earlier "material safety data sheets" used in industry from the mid-twentieth century. The adoption of the GHS by many jurisdictions in the 2000s and 2010s harmonised the format and terminology, replacing "MSDS" with "SDS" and standardising hazard pictograms, signal words and precautionary statements.
