Georg Neumann (1898–1976) was a German engineer and entrepreneur who founded the Georg Neumann GmbH company in Berlin in 1928. His firm produced the condenser microphones — most famously the U 47 — that became standard equipment in recording studios around the world.

Early career

Neumann trained in telecommunications engineering and worked for the AEG concern before joining Mix & Genest. In the 1920s he became involved in the new field of electrical sound recording and broadcast technology, then dominated by carbon microphones of limited fidelity.

The company

In 1928 Neumann founded his own laboratory in Berlin. His early successes included the CMV 3 "bottle" microphone, one of the first mass-produced condenser microphones, used for the broadcast of the 1936 Berlin Olympic Games. After the Second World War he rebuilt the company in West Berlin.

The U 47 and later designs

Introduced in 1949, the U 47 was the first studio microphone offering switchable polar patterns (cardioid and omnidirectional) and became the definitive vocal microphone of the post-war era, used by Frank Sinatra, the Beatles and countless others. Later classics included the U 67 and the solid-state U 87 (1967), still a studio standard. Neumann's firm also built disc-cutting lathes and mixing consoles. After German reunification the company came under Sennheiser ownership, while the East Berlin branch continued as Microtech Gefell.
