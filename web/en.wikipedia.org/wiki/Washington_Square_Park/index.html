Washington Square Park is a 9.75-acre (3.95 ha) public park in the Greenwich Village neighborhood of Lower Manhattan, New York City. One of the best-known of New York City's public parks, it is an icon as well as a meeting place and center for cultural activity. It is operated by the New York City Department of Parks and Recreation.

The park is an open space, dominated by the Washington Square Arch at its northern gateway and a large central fountain. It is bounded by Washington Square North (Waverly Place), Washington Square East (University Place), Washington Square South (West 4th Street), and Washington Square West (MacDougal Street). New York University buildings surround much of the park.

History

The land was once a marsh fed by Minetta Brook. In the late 18th and early 19th centuries the area served as a potter's field, where an estimated 20,000 indigent and unidentified dead were buried; it was also a site of public executions, and the so-called "Hangman's Elm" survives in the park's northwest corner. In 1826 the city converted the land into the Washington Military Parade Ground, and it officially became a public park the following year.

The Washington Square Arch, designed by architect Stanford White, was erected in 1892 to replace a temporary wooden arch built three years earlier to celebrate the centennial of George Washington's inauguration as president. The white marble triumphal arch, modeled on Roman examples such as the Arc de Triomphe in Paris, stands 77 feet (23 m) tall and has become the park's defining symbol.

Culture

Washington Square Park has long been a gathering place for artists, musicians and political protest. It was a center of the folk music revival of the 1950s and 1960s, closely associated with figures such as Bob Dylan, and the site of the 1961 "folk riot" over a permit dispute. Street performers, chess and Scrabble players in the park's southwest corner, and the Washington Square Outdoor Art Exhibit remain fixtures. The park sits adjacent to the main campus of New York University.
