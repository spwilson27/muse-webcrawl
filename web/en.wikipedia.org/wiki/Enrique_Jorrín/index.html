Enrique Jorrín

Enrique Jorrín Oleaga (25 December 1926 – 12 December 1987) was a Cuban violinist, composer and orchestra leader, celebrated as the creator of the cha-cha-chá, one of the most influential dance rhythms of the twentieth century. His innovations reshaped Cuban popular music and helped launch the international Latin dance craze of the 1950s.

Life and career

Born in Candelaria, Pinar del Río, Jorrín studied violin at the Havana conservatory and began his career in danzón orchestras, the French-derived charangas that played Cuba's national dance music. He played with the Hermanos Contreras orchestra and the charanga of Antonio Arcaño before joining Orquesta Aragón in the 1940s, where his compositions and violin playing gained wide recognition. In 1954 he formed his own orchestra, Orquesta de Enrique Jorrín, which he led for the rest of his life, touring extensively in Latin America, Europe and Africa. He remained in Cuba after the revolution and died in Havana in 1987.

Creation of the cha-cha-chá

While experimenting with the danzón around 1951, Jorrín noticed that dancers struggled with the syncopated rhythms of the mambo-influenced nuevo ritmo. He composed pieces in which the melody and rhythm were squarer and easier to follow, with the singers in unison; dancers improvising responded with a shuffling three-step whose sound on the floor — "cha-cha-chá" — gave the new genre its name. His composition La Engañadora (recorded 1953) is generally regarded as the first cha-cha-chá. Other classics include El Alardoso, El Tunel, Nada para ti and Las muletas traicioneras.

Legacy

The cha-cha-chá spread rapidly through Mexico and the United States and became a fixture of ballroom dancing worldwide, where it survives as one of the five international Latin dances. Jorrín is remembered in Cuba as a master of the danzón as well as the inventor of its most famous offshoot.
