Washington Principles on Nazi-Confiscated Art

The Washington Principles on Nazi-Confiscated Art are a set of eleven non-binding principles adopted at the Washington Conference on Holocaust-Era Assets on 3 December 1998, attended by representatives of 44 governments. They govern the treatment of artworks looted or confiscated during the Nazi era.

Content

The principles call on museums and governments to identify art confiscated by the Nazis and not subsequently restituted, to publish records of such works, to encourage pre-war owners and their heirs to come forward, and to achieve "just and fair solutions" when claims are established — including through alternative dispute resolution rather than litigation.

Implementation

Implementation has varied widely. Some countries, such as Austria, the Netherlands and France, created advisory commissions on restitution; in the United States the principles encouraged provenance research programmes at major museums and led to cases such as the restitution of Gustav Klimt's portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer. Critics note that the principles' voluntary nature and time limits on claims in some jurisdictions have left many disputes unresolved.
