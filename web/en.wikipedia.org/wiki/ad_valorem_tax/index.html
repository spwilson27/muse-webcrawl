Ad valorem tax

An ad valorem tax (Latin for "according to value") is a tax whose amount is based on the value of a transaction or of a property. It is typically imposed at the time of a transaction, as in the case of a sales tax or value-added tax (VAT). An ad valorem tax may also be imposed annually, as in the case of a real or personal property tax, based on the assessed value of the property.

Application

Ad valorem duties are an important source of revenue for governments at all levels. In international trade, an ad valorem tariff is charged as a percentage of the value of imported goods, as opposed to a specific duty, which is a fixed charge per unit. Sales taxes and VAT are ad valorem taxes on consumption: the amount owed rises proportionally with the price of the good or service purchased.

Property tax

The most common annual ad valorem tax is the property tax, levied on real estate and sometimes on tangible personal property such as vehicles. Tax assessors determine the value of the property, and the tax is computed by applying the local millage rate to the assessed value. Because the tax tracks market value, ad valorem property taxes tend to rise when property values appreciate.

Comparison with specific taxes

Unlike specific (per-unit) taxes, ad valorem taxes automatically keep pace with inflation because they are proportional to value. However, they require an accurate valuation of the taxed item, which can be administratively demanding and may invite disputes or undervaluation, particularly in customs administration.
