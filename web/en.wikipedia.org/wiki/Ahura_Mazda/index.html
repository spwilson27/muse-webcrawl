Ahura Mazda ('the Wise Lord') is the supreme deity of Zoroastrianism, proclaimed by the prophet Zoroaster as the uncreated creator of all that is good. In Zoroaster's Gathas, Ahura Mazda is wholly wise and benevolent, author of asha (truth and order), and is attended by the Amesha Spentas ('Bounteous Immortals'), aspects or archangels of his nature. Against him stands Angra Mainyu, the destructive spirit, whose lie (druj) humans must resist with good thoughts, words and deeds.

Theology

Whether Zoroastrianism is purely monotheistic or dualistic is debated by scholars: the Gathas oppose Mazda to the hostile spirit, but Mazda remains supreme and will triumph at the final renovation (Frashokereti), when the dead rise and evil is destroyed. The name survives in the car company Mazda, chosen partly in his honour.
