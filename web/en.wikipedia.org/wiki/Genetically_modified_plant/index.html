Genetically modified plant

Genetically modified plants are plants whose genomes have been altered by genetic engineering to introduce traits not created through traditional breeding. Such plants may be transgenic (containing genes from another species) or cisgenic (containing genes from the same or a crossable species). Most commercial genetically modified plants are crop plants modified for insect resistance or herbicide tolerance.

Techniques

Two methods dominate plant transformation: Agrobacterium tumefaciens-mediated transfer, which exploits the bacterium's natural ability to insert T-DNA into plant genomes, and particle bombardment, in which DNA-coated particles are shot into plant cells. Transformed cells are selected using marker genes and regenerated into whole plants via tissue culture. CRISPR-based editing now permits targeted modification without necessarily leaving foreign DNA.

Model plants

Arabidopsis thaliana was the first plant to have its genome sequenced and is the principal model for GM plant research; tobacco is also widely used as a laboratory host, including for producing pharmaceuticals in "molecular farming".

Applications

Beyond agriculture, genetically modified plants are used to produce vaccines and industrial enzymes, for phytoremediation of contaminated soils, and in research on photosynthesis and development.
