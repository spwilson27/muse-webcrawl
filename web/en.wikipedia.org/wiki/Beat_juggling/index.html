Beat juggling

Beat juggling
TypeTurntablism technique
Origin1970s Bronx hip hop
PioneersGrandmaster Flash, Afrika Bambaataa, DJ Kool Herc (break extension)
EquipmentTwo turntables, DJ mixer, duplicate records

Beat juggling is a technique in turntablism in which a DJ manipulates two copies of the same record (or two records) on two turntables, using the mixer crossfader, to rearrange a drum break or musical passage in real time — looping, pausing, offsetting and recombining beats to create a new rhythm. It developed from DJ Kool Herc's practice of extending breakbeats with two copies of a record, and was refined by Grandmaster Flash and later virtuosos of the 1990s battle scene.

Technique

The DJ cues a beat on one deck while the same beat plays on the other, cutting between them with the crossfader to repeat selected kicks, snares or fills, pull a record back by hand, or let both copies play slightly out of phase ("doubling"). Basic patterns include the chase, the strobe and breakdown patterns that isolate individual drum hits. Mastery demands precise hand control, memorised record positions (often marked with label stickers), and an internal sense of the break's structure.

History and competition

Beat juggling became a centrepiece of DJ battles such as the DMC World DJ Championships, where routines by artists including DJ Babu (who coined much of the technique's vocabulary), the Invisibl Skratch Piklz, DJ Craze and the X-Ecutioners turned it into a virtuoso competitive form. With digital vinyl systems and controllers, the practice moved beyond physical records, but the classic two-copy juggling of a break remains a foundational skill of hip hop DJing.
