Pierre Georges Albert François Henry (9 December 1927 – 5 July 2017) was a French composer, considered a pioneer of musique concrète. Working first with Pierre Schaeffer at the French radio's experimental studio, he produced some of the earliest and most famous works built from manipulated recordings of real-world sounds.

Early life and training

Henry studied at the Paris Conservatoire, where his teachers included Nadia Boulanger, Olivier Messiaen (harmony) and Félix Passerona (percussion). Fascinated by noise and unusual sonorities, he set about integrating sounds of the everyday world into music.

Musique concrète

In 1949 Henry joined Schaeffer's studio at Radiodiffusion-Télévision Française. Their collaboration produced Symphonie pour un homme seul (1950), a landmark of musique concrète made from sounds of the human body and everyday actions. Henry left in 1958 to found his own studio, APSOME, and later the Studio Son/Ré in Paris, which became famous for its walls of loudspeakers and tape machines.

Works and influence

Henry's music reached a wide audience through ballet scores for Maurice Béjart, among them Messe pour le temps présent (1967), whose "Psyché Rock" became a hit and later inspired the Futurama theme. His output includes the electronic oratorio L'Apocalypse de Jean, Variations pour une porte et un soupir ("Variations for a Door and a Sigh"), and vast concert cycles performed on his "concert house" of speakers. He is regarded as the "grandfather of techno" by some electronic musicians, and his sampling techniques anticipated modern production practice.
