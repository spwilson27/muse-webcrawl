Grant (money)

A grant is a fund given by an organisation — commonly a government department, corporation, foundation, or trust — to a recipient, often a nonprofit entity, educational institution, business, or individual, for a particular purpose linked to public benefit. Unlike a loan, a grant does not have to be repaid, though the recipient is normally required to meet conditions, report on results, and account for how the money was spent.

Types of grants

Grants may be competitive, awarded through an application and peer-review process, or formula-based, distributed according to statutory criteria. Governments issue categorical grants for defined purposes and block grants giving recipients broader discretion. In research, grants from agencies such as the US National Institutes of Health and National Science Foundation, UK Research and Innovation, and the European Research Council fund much of the world's scientific work. Charitable foundations, from the Ford and Gates foundations to small community trusts, are major private grant-makers.

Process

Grant-making typically proceeds through a call for proposals, submission of a detailed application and budget, evaluation against published criteria, an award with terms and conditions, and monitoring with interim and final reports. Misuse of funds can lead to repayment and debarment. The discipline of preparing applications is known as grant writing.

Distinction from related funding

Grants differ from subsidies and gifts in being tied to specified purposes and reporting; from contracts in that the funder does not directly receive goods or services of commensurate value; and from prizes in being awarded in advance of the work rather than in recognition of results already achieved.
