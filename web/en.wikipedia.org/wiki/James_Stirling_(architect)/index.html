Sir James Frazer Stirling (22 April 1926 – 25 June 1992) was a British architect, regarded as one of the most important and influential figures in late-20th-century architecture. His designs evolved from the modernism of his early partnership with James Gowan to the playful, historically referential postmodernism of his later work with Michael Wilford.

Early work

Stirling studied at the University of Liverpool. With Gowan he designed the University of Leicester Engineering Building (1959–63), a celebrated essay in red brick and glass, and the Cambridge History Faculty Library (1964–67) — bold, technically daring works that made his reputation while courting controversy for their performance problems.

Later work

His masterpiece is the Neue Staatsgalerie in Stuttgart (completed 1984), which mixes classical stone cladding, ramps, and brightly coloured steel canopies into a popular and critically acclaimed museum. Other major works include the Arthur M. Sackler Museum at Harvard, the Tate's Clore Gallery in London, and No 1 Poultry in the City of London, completed posthumously.

Honours

Stirling won the Pritzker Prize in 1981 and was knighted in 1992, shortly before his death. Britain's premier architecture award, the Stirling Prize, has borne his name since its inception in 1996.
