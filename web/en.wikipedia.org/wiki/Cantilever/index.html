Cantilever

A cantilever is a rigid structural element that extends horizontally and is unsupported at one end, anchored only at the other — like a diving board or a beam fixed to a wall.

TypeStructural element

Mechanics

When loaded at the free end, a cantilever experiences bending: the top fibres are in tension and the bottom in compression (for a downward load), with maximum stress at the fixed support. Deflection follows beam theory and depends on the load, length, and the material's elastic modulus and moment of inertia.

Applications

Cantilevers are used in bridges (the Forth Bridge is a famous cantilever truss), balconies, aircraft wings, cranes, Frank Lloyd Wright's Fallingwater, and microelectromechanical systems: the probe of an atomic force microscope is a tiny cantilever whose deflection measures atomic-scale forces. Cantilever bridges can be built outward without falsework over obstacles.
