Education reform

Education reform is the process of reforming, and the goal of constantly improving, an education system — including its curricula, governance, funding, teacher training and methods of assessment. Reform movements have recurred throughout the history of public schooling, driven by aims such as widening access, raising standards, increasing equity and adapting education to economic change.

Historical movements

Nineteenth-century reformers such as Horace Mann in the United States championed free, universal common schools. In the early twentieth century, John Dewey's progressive education movement emphasized learning by doing and democratic schooling, influencing practice in many countries. Later waves of reform included the standards-based movement of the 1980s and 1990s, school choice policies such as charter schools and academies, and accountability regimes built on standardised testing.

Debates

Education reform is frequently contested: disputes concern the role of testing, teacher evaluation, school funding formulas, privatization, curriculum content, and the balance between central direction and local autonomy. International comparisons, such as PISA rankings, are often invoked by reformers to justify policy change.
