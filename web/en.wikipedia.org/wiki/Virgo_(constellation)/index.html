Virgo is one of the constellations of the zodiac. Its name is Latin for virgin, and its symbol is ♍. Lying between Leo to the west and Libra to the east, it is the second-largest constellation in the sky (after Hydra) and the largest constellation in the zodiac. It can be easily found through its brightest star, Spica.

Location

Virgo is prominent in the spring sky in the Northern Hemisphere, visible all night in March and April. Because the constellation contains the autumnal equinox — the point where the ecliptic crosses the celestial equator into the Southern Hemisphere — it straddles both hemispheres. The Sun passes through Virgo from mid-September to the end of October.

Notable features

Spica (Alpha Virginis) is a blue giant and the fifteenth-brightest star in the night sky. Other notable stars include Porrima (Gamma Virginis), a binary star, and Zavijava (Beta Virginis).

Virgo contains the Virgo Cluster, a rich cluster of roughly 1,300 to 2,000 galaxies centred about 53.8 million light-years away, which includes the giant elliptical galaxy Messier 87 — famous for hosting the first black hole ever directly imaged. Other Messier objects in Virgo include the Sombrero Galaxy (Messier 104) and the interacting Eyes Galaxies. The first exoplanet discovered around a pulsar, in the globular cluster Messier 4's neighbour system PSR B1257+12 in Virgo, was announced in 1992.

Mythology

In Greek mythology Virgo was associated with Dike, goddess of justice, and with Demeter, goddess of the harvest; the star Spica represents an ear of wheat held by the maiden. In Babylonian astronomy the constellation was known as "The Furrow" and linked to the goddess Shala.
