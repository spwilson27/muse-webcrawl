Goodfellas

Directed by: Martin Scorsese
Screenplay by: Nicholas Pileggi, Martin Scorsese
Based on: Wiseguy by Nicholas Pileggi
Starring: Robert De Niro, Ray Liotta, Joe Pesci, Lorraine Bracco, Paul Sorvino
Release date: September 19, 1990
Running time: 146 minutes
Country: United States
Language: English
Budget: $25 million
Box office: $47.1 million

Goodfellas (stylized GoodFellas) is a 1990 American biographical crime drama film directed by Martin Scorsese, written by Scorsese and Nicholas Pileggi, and produced by Irwin Winkler. It is a film adaptation of the 1985 nonfiction book Wiseguy by Pileggi. Starring Robert De Niro, Ray Liotta, Joe Pesci, Lorraine Bracco and Paul Sorvino, the film narrates the rise and fall of mob associate Henry Hill and his friends and family from 1955 to 1980.

Scorsese initially titled the film Wise Guy and postponed making it; he and Pileggi later changed the title to Goodfellas. To prepare for their roles in the film, De Niro, Pesci and Liotta often spoke with Pileggi, who shared research material left over from writing the book. According to Pesci, improvisation and ad-libbing came out of rehearsals wherein Scorsese gave the actors freedom to do whatever they wanted. The director made transcripts of these sessions, took the lines he liked most and put them into a revised script, which the cast worked from during principal photography.

Made on a budget of $25 million, Goodfellas grossed $47 million. The film was nominated for six Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Director, with Pesci winning for Best Supporting Actor. The film won five awards from the British Academy of Film and Television Arts, including Best Film and Best Director. Additionally, Goodfellas was named the year's best film by various critics' groups.

Goodfellas is widely regarded as one of the greatest films ever made, particularly in the gangster genre. In 2000, it was deemed "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant" and selected for preservation in the National Film Registry by the United States Library of Congress. Its content and style have been emulated in numerous other films and television series.

Plot

In 1955, Henry Hill, a teenager living in an Italian-American working-class neighborhood in East New York, Brooklyn, becomes enamored of the criminal life and Mafia presence in his working-class neighborhood. He begins working for local caporegime Paulie Cicero and his associates: Jimmy "the Gent" Conway, an Irish truck hijacker and gangster, and Tommy DeVito, a fellow juvenile delinquent. Henry begins as a fence for Jimmy, and as he slowly rises in the ranks he becomes drawn into the violent double life of a mobster.

The film follows Henry through his marriage to Karen, the lucrative Lufthansa heist at John F. Kennedy International Airport in 1978, his descent into cocaine addiction, and his eventual arrest by narcotics agents in 1980. Facing a lengthy prison sentence and fearing that Jimmy and Paulie will kill him and Karen to prevent him from testifying, Henry agrees to become an FBI informant and enters the Witness Protection Program.

Cast

Ray Liotta as Henry Hill, Robert De Niro as James "Jimmy" Conway, Joe Pesci as Tommy DeVito, Lorraine Bracco as Karen Hill, and Paul Sorvino as Paul "Paulie" Cicero. Supporting roles include Frank Sivero, Tony Darrow, Mike Starr, Frank Vincent, Chuck Low, Catherine Scorsese, Michael Imperioli, and Samuel L. Jackson.

Production

Scorsese read Pileggi's book Wiseguy while working on The Color of Money in 1986. The film was shot in the spring and summer of 1989 in New York City and its surrounding area, including locations in Queens, Long Island, and New Jersey. The famous three-minute Copacabana tracking shot, filmed in a single take with a Steadicam, became one of the most celebrated sequences in cinema history.

Reception

Goodfellas holds a 96% approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes and is frequently cited by critics and filmmakers as one of the finest crime films ever made. Roger Ebert gave the film four out of four stars, calling it "an organized crime movie that refuses to glorify the mobsters it depicts."
