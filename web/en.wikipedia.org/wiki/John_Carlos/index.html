John Carlos

John Wesley Carlos (born June 5, 1945) is an American former track and field athlete and professional football player. He won the bronze medal in the 200 metres at the 1968 Summer Olympics in Mexico City, where he and gold medallist Tommie Smith performed their famous raised-fist protest on the medal podium.

1968 protest

On 16 October 1968, Carlos and Smith mounted the podium shoeless, wearing black socks to symbolise Black poverty, and each raised a black-gloved fist during the US national anthem to protest racial injustice. Australian silver medallist Peter Norman wore a badge of the Olympic Project for Human Rights in support. Carlos and Smith were expelled from the Games by the IOC and ostracised for years afterward, though the gesture is now regarded as one of the most iconic images in sports history.

Athletics career

Carlos equalled the world record in the 100-yard dash and set a world best in the 200 metres in 1968, though it was never ratified because of his brush spikes. He later played briefly in the NFL and CFL, worked for the Los Angeles Olympic Organizing Committee, and became a school counsellor in Palm Springs, California.

Recognition

Carlos and Smith were inducted into the US Olympic Hall of Fame in 2019, and a statue of their protest stands at San José State University.
