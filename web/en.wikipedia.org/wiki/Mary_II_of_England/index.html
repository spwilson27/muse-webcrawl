Mary II (30 April 1662 – 28 December 1694) was Queen of England, Scotland, and Ireland, co-reigning with her husband, King William III, from 1689 until her death in 1694. Mary was the eldest daughter of James, Duke of York, and his first wife Anne Hyde.

Mary and her younger sister Anne were raised as Anglicans at the behest of her uncle, King Charles II, although their parents both converted to Roman Catholicism. At the age of fifteen, she married her first cousin William of Orange, a Protestant. A devout Protestant herself, she supported William's invasion of England in 1688, which resulted in the deposition of her father in the Glorious Revolution; she and William became joint sovereigns the next year following the passage of the Bill of Rights 1689. She reigned jointly with William, who handled much of the administration personally in England, while she assumed most of the royal power when he was abroad fighting the Nine Years' War in Europe.

Early life

Mary, born at St James's Palace in London on 30 April 1662, was the eldest daughter of the Duke of York (the future King James II & VII), and his first wife, Anne Hyde. Mary's uncle was King Charles II, who ruled the three kingdoms of England, Scotland and Ireland, and her maternal grandfather, Edward Hyde, 1st Earl of Clarendon, was Charles's chief adviser. She was baptised into the Anglican faith in the Chapel Royal at St James's.

Marriage

During the autumn of 1677, Mary's father and uncle arranged her marriage to William, Prince of Orange, her first cousin and the Stadtholder of the Dutch Republic. She was reportedly distressed at the match but obeyed; the couple were married on 4 November 1677 at St James's Palace. They settled in the Netherlands, where Mary became popular with the Dutch people.

Reign

When James II fled England in December 1688, Parliament declared that he had abdicated and offered the crown jointly to Mary and William, who were crowned on 11 April 1689 at Westminster Abbey. The Bill of Rights settled the succession and limited royal prerogative. Mary governed the realm during William's absences on campaign, proving a capable and decisive ruler; she ordered the arrest of her uncle the Earl of Clarendon for plotting to restore her father and took charge during the French invasion scare of 1692.

Death and succession

Mary died of smallpox at Kensington Palace on 28 December 1694, aged 32, and was buried in Westminster Abbey. William reigned alone until his death in 1702, when Mary's sister Anne succeeded.
