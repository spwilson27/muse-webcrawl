Ksitigarbha (Sanskrit: क्षितिगर्भ, "Earth Womb" or "Earth Store") is a bodhisattva revered in East Asian Buddhism. He is known for his great vow to take responsibility for the instruction of all beings in the six realms between the death of Gautama Buddha and the rise of Maitreya, as well as his vow not to achieve Buddhahood until all hells are emptied. He is therefore often called the "Bodhisattva of the Great Vow" and is regarded as the saviour of beings suffering in the hell realms and the guardian of children and travellers.

Origins

Ksitigarbha's story is told in the Sutra of the Fundamental Vows of the Bodhisattva Ksitigarbha, translated into Chinese in the Tang dynasty. In past lives narrated in the sutra, Ksitigarbha appears as a Brahmin maiden whose devotion saves her mother from hell, and as a king who vows to save all suffering beings. The historical monk Kim Gyo-gak (Jin Qiaojue), a Silla prince who practised on Mount Jiuhua in Anhui, came to be regarded as Ksitigarbha's earthly manifestation, and Mount Jiuhua became the bodhisattva's sacred mountain in China.

Iconography

Unlike most bodhisattvas, who are shown as princes, Ksitigarbha is depicted as a Buddhist monk with a shaved head, carrying a pilgrim's staff (shakujō) with six rings to open the gates of hell, and a wish-fulfilling jewel (cintāmaṇi) to light the darkness. In Japan, as Jizō, he is one of the most beloved deities, protector of children — especially mizuko, the souls of miscarried, aborted and stillborn infants — as well as of travellers and the underworld; small Jizō statues wearing red bibs and caps line roadsides and temples throughout the country.

Practices

Devotees recite Ksitigarbha's name and mantra ("Om Ha Ha Ha Vismaye Svaha"), copy and chant his sutra, and sponsor rituals for the dead, particularly during the Ghost Festival season, when Ksitigarbha's birthday is traditionally celebrated on the last day of the seventh lunar month.
