Victoria Coach Station

LocationBuckingham Palace Road, Victoria, London, SW1
Opened10 March 1932
OperatorTransport for London (Victoria Coach Station Limited)
StyleArt Deco

Victoria Coach Station in the City of Westminster is the largest coach station in London and a terminal for long-distance express coach services in the United Kingdom. It is operated by Victoria Coach Station Limited, a wholly owned subsidiary of Transport for London. The station serves destinations throughout Britain and, via operators such as FlixBus, BlaBlaBus, and Eurolines, continental Europe.

History

The station was opened on 10 March 1932 by London Coastal Coaches, a consortium of coach operators. Its distinctive Art Deco building on Buckingham Palace Road was designed by architects Wallis, Gilbert and Partners. During the Second World War the station handled large volumes of troop movements. It was extensively refurbished in the 1980s and 1990s, gaining separate arrivals and departures terminals.

Facilities and future

The departures terminal has 22 gates and handles several million passengers a year. Transport for London's lease on the site, which sits near Victoria railway station, has been extended several times while long-term redevelopment plans for the area have been studied; proposals have periodically considered relocating some services to other terminals such as Stratford or Hammersmith, but Victoria remains London's principal coach terminus.
