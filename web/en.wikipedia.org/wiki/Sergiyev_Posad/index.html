Sergiyev Posad (Russian: Се́ргиев Поса́д) is a city and the administrative centre of Sergiyevo-Posadsky District in Moscow Oblast, Russia, located approximately 70 kilometres northeast of Moscow. The city grew up around the Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius, one of the most important Russian Orthodox monasteries, founded in 1337 by St. Sergius of Radonezh. The monastery complex is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the city forms part of the Golden Ring of ancient Russian towns popular with tourists.

History

The settlement began as a posad—a craftsmen's and traders' settlement—serving the monastery founded by Sergius of Radonezh in the fourteenth century. In 1742 the settlement received formal recognition, and in 1782 Catherine the Great granted it town status under the name Sergiyevsky Posad, later shortened to Sergiyev Posad.

During the Soviet period, in 1930, the city was renamed Zagorsk in honour of the Bolshevik official Vladimir Zagorsky. The monastery was closed for much of the Soviet era but reopened after the Second World War, when it became the residence of the Patriarch of Moscow and continued as the Church's spiritual and administrative centre. The city's historical name, Sergiyev Posad, was restored in 1991.

Landmarks

The Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius contains more than fifty buildings from various centuries, including the Trinity Cathedral (1422–23), which houses the relics of St. Sergius and was decorated by the icon painters Andrei Rublev and Daniil Cherny; the Assumption Cathedral with its blue star-spangled domes; and a bell tower of 88 metres. The city is also known as a centre of Russian folk crafts, particularly wooden toy making and matryoshka doll production.

Transport

Sergiyev Posad is connected to Moscow by suburban railway from Yaroslavsky station and by the Yaroslavl highway, making it one of the most accessible Golden Ring towns for day visitors.
