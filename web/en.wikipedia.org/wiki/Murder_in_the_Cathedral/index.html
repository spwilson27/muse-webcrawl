Murder in the Cathedral

Murder in the Cathedral is a verse drama by T. S. Eliot, first performed in 1935. It dramatises the assassination of Archbishop Thomas Becket in Canterbury Cathedral in 1170.

The play was written for the Canterbury Festival and reflects on martyrdom, temptation and the conflict between church and state. Becket is visited by four Tempters, the fourth — the expectation of martyrdom for glory — being the subtlest. A chorus of women of Canterbury comments on events.

Reception

The work marked a revival of poetic drama in English theatre and remains one of Eliot's best-known plays, later adapted into a film in 1951.
