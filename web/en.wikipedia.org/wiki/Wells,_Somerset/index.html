Wells, Somerset

Wells
County: Somerset, England
Population: approx. 10,500 (2011 census)
Status: Cathedral city

Wells is a cathedral city and civil parish in Somerset, England, on the southern edge of the Mendip Hills, 21 miles (34 km) south-east of Weston-super-Mare, 22 mi (35 km) south-west of Bath and 23 mi (37 km) south of Bristol. Although the population at the 2011 census was only 10,536, Wells has had city status since medieval times because of the presence of Wells Cathedral. It is often described as England's smallest city.

History

The name Wells derives from the three wells dedicated to Saint Andrew, one in the market place and two within the grounds of the Bishop's Palace and cathedral. A small Roman settlement grew up around the wells, and the town became an important religious centre in the Anglo-Saxon period. The cathedral was built between about 1175 and 1490, replacing an earlier Saxon church beside the spring. The Bishop's Palace has been the home of the bishops of Bath and Wells for over 800 years and is famous for the swans that ring a bell beside the moat for food.

Landmarks

Wells Cathedral is renowned for its Early English Gothic architecture, its scissor arches, and one of the largest collections of medieval stained glass in England. The cathedral's west front displays around 300 sculpted figures. Vicars' Close, built in the 14th century, is among the oldest continuously inhabited purely residential streets in Europe. The city has also been used as a filming location, notably for the film Hot Fuzz.
