Red Square

Red Square (Russian: Красная площадь, romanized: Krasnaya ploshchad') is one of the oldest and largest squares in Moscow, Russia. It is located in Moscow's historic centre, in the eastern walls of the Kremlin. It is the city landmark of Moscow, with famous buildings such as Saint Basil's Cathedral, Lenin's Mausoleum and the GUM department store. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1990, together with the Kremlin.

The name "Red Square" derives from the Russian word krasnaya, which in older Russian meant "beautiful" and only later came to mean "red"; originally it was applied to Saint Basil's Cathedral and was transferred to the square in the seventeenth century. The square separates the Kremlin, the former royal citadel and now the official residence of the President of Russia, from the historic merchant quarter known as Kitai-gorod.

History

The square originated in the late fifteenth century, when Ivan III ordered the clearing of buildings outside the Kremlin's eastern wall, creating a marketplace (the Torg) and a defensive moat. In 1555–1561 Ivan the Terrible commissioned the Cathedral of the Intercession — Saint Basil's — at the square's southern end to commemorate the conquest of Kazan. The Kazan Cathedral and the Iverskaya Chapel with its Resurrection Gate frame the northern entrance (both demolished under Stalin and rebuilt in the 1990s). The vast GUM department store, built 1890–1893 in Russian Revival style, occupies the eastern side, and the State Historical Museum (1875–1881) the northern end.

Soviet and modern era

After the October Revolution, Red Square became the ceremonial heart of the Soviet state. Lenin's Mausoleum (first built 1924, in stone by 1930, by Alexey Shchusev) stands against the Kremlin wall, and Soviet leaders reviewed military parades from its rostrum — most famously the 1941 October Revolution parade, whose troops marched directly to the front, and the Victory Parade of 24 June 1945. In 1987 the West German teenager Mathias Rust landed a light aircraft near the square, humiliating the Soviet military. Since 1991 the square has hosted concerts, festivals and the annual 9 May Victory Day parade, and at New Year it holds a fair and skating rink.

Principal sights

  • Saint Basil's Cathedral — with its nine onion domes, an emblem of Russia.
  • Lenin's Mausoleum — the stepped granite tomb displaying Lenin's embalmed body.
  • Lobnoye Mesto — the circular stone platform used for proclamations (and, popularly though largely incorrectly, executions).
  • Minin and Pozharsky Monument (1818) — honouring the leaders of the 1612 liberation of Moscow.
  • Kremlin Wall Necropolis — graves of Soviet leaders and heroes along the wall.
