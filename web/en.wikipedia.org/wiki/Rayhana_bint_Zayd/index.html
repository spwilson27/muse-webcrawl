Rayhana bint Zayd (Arabic: ريحانة بنت زيد) was a Jewish woman of the Banu Nadir tribe of Medina — by some accounts married into the Banu Qurayza — who became a companion of the Islamic prophet Muhammad. Islamic sources differ on her precise status: she is variously described as one of Muhammad's wives (umm al-mu'minin) or as a concubine (surriyya) whom he freed and married, or wished to marry.

Biography

According to the early biographies of Muhammad (the sira literature, including Ibn Ishaq as preserved by Ibn Hisham, and al-Waqidi), Rayhana's first husband was a man of the Banu Qurayza named al-Hakam. After the siege of the Banu Qurayza in 627 CE, in which the tribe's men were executed, Rayhana was taken captive and chosen by Muhammad. The sources report that he offered to free and marry her on condition of her embracing Islam; accounts differ as to whether she accepted immediately or after initial refusal, and hence whether she attained the status of wife.

Status and death

The ambiguity in the sources made Rayhana a recurring topic in later Islamic scholarship and in modern discussions of the sira. Some traditions hold that Muhammad freed and married her, after which she was counted among the Mothers of the Believers; others, including reports from Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat, state that she remained a freed concubine. She is said to have died shortly after Muhammad's farewell pilgrimage, around 631 CE, and to have been buried in the Al-Baqi cemetery in Medina.
