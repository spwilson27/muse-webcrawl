Thaya


The Thaya near Hardegg, on the Austrian–Czech border

The Thaya (Czech: Dyje) is a river in Central Europe, the longest tributary of the Morava. It flows through Lower Austria and the South Moravian Region of the Czech Republic. Including its headstreams, it is about 322 km long, and its basin covers roughly 12,772 km².

Course

The Thaya is formed by the confluence of the German Thaya (Deutsche Thaya) and the Moravian Thaya (Moravská Dyje) near Raabs an der Thaya in the Waldviertel of Lower Austria. From there it flows eastward, for long stretches forming the state border between Austria and the Czech Republic, passing the Thayatal National Park (Podyjí National Park on the Czech side) with its deeply incised meanders, and the towns of Hardegg, Drosendorf and Laa an der Thaya.

Continuing into Moravia, the river passes Vranov nad Dyjí, Znojmo, and the Lednice–Valtice Cultural Landscape, before joining the Morava near Hohenau an der March, at the tripoint of Austria, the Czech Republic and Slovakia.

History and nature

During the Cold War the river valley lay along the Iron Curtain, which protected its ecosystems from intensive development. The cross-border national parks, established in the 1990s, preserve one of the last near-natural river valleys in Central Europe, noted for its diversity of habitats and species.
