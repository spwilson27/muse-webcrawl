The Battle of the Standard, sometimes called the Battle of Northallerton, was fought on 22 August 1138 on Cowton Moor near Northallerton in Yorkshire, during the Anarchy, the civil war between Stephen and the Empress Matilda. An English army raised in the north defeated the invading army of King David I of Scotland, who had invaded northern England in support of his niece Matilda and to advance Scottish claims to Northumbria.

Background

David's invasions of 1138 were accompanied by notorious raiding and pillage, vividly described by chroniclers such as Richard of Hexham. With King Stephen occupied in the south, resistance in Yorkshire was organised by Archbishop Thurstan of York, who summoned the northern barons and shire levies to muster at York. The English army took position around a great standard — a mast mounted on a wagon, bearing the banners of St Peter of York, St John of Beverley and St Wilfrid of Ripon, with a consecrated host at its top — from which the battle takes its name.

The battle

The English fought largely on foot, with dismounted knights stiffening formations of archers and spearmen. The Scots attacked in tribal divisions; the men of Galloway charged first and were shot down or repulsed, and the Scottish centre, though Prince Henry of Scotland broke through the English line and reached the horse lines in the rear, could not be supported. When a rumour spread that David had been killed — and a severed head was displayed as proof — the Scottish army disintegrated and fled, suffering heavy losses in the pursuit, traditionally estimated in the thousands.

Aftermath

Despite the defeat, David retained control of much of the north, and by the Treaty of Durham (1139) Stephen conceded the earldom of Northumbria to David's son Henry. The battle nevertheless halted Scottish expansion southward for a generation and became a celebrated example of the English shire levy defeating a royal invader.
