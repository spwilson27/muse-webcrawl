Superstar

A superstar is a person who has achieved an extremely high degree of fame, celebrity, and public recognition, typically in entertainment, sport, or popular culture. The term, popularized in the late 20th century, denotes celebrities whose appeal extends far beyond their immediate field and whose names are recognized globally.

Usage and origins

The word gained wide currency through the 1970 rock opera Jesus Christ Superstar by Andrew Lloyd Webber and Tim Rice, and the hit song "Superstar" recorded by the Carpenters (1971). Economists have studied the phenomenon since Sherwin Rosen's 1981 paper "The Economics of Superstars", which explained how small differences in talent, amplified by mass media and scale economies, can produce enormous differences in earnings and fame.

Characteristics

Superstars typically command premium compensation, extensive media coverage, and substantial influence over public taste and commercial endorsements. The concentration of attention on a small number of performers is a recurring subject of debate regarding cultural diversity and market structure.
