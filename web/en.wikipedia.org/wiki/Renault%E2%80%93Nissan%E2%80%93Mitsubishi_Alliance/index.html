The Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance is a strategic partnership among the automobile manufacturers Renault (France), Nissan (Japan), and Mitsubishi Motors (Japan), linked by cross-shareholding and joint development of platforms, powertrains, and electric vehicles. The Renault–Nissan alliance was formed in 1999, when Renault took a stake in the debt-laden Nissan; Mitsubishi joined in 2016 after Nissan acquired a controlling 34% interest in it.

Structure

The partners remain separate companies with their own brands and management, sharing platforms (such as the Common Module Family), purchasing, and technology. Long-serving alliance chairman Carlos Ghosn was arrested in Japan in 2018 over financial misconduct allegations, an event that strained relations between Renault and Nissan and led to governance reforms.

Restructuring

In 2023 the partners announced a rebalancing of cross-shareholdings, with Renault reducing its stake in Nissan to 15% to match Nissan's stake in Renault, and a renewed focus on regional projects and electric vehicles, including Nissan's investment in Renault's EV venture Ampere.
