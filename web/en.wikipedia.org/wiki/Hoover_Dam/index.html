Hoover Dam is a concrete arch-gravity dam in the Black Canyon of the Colorado River, on the border between the U.S. states of Nevada and Arizona. Constructed between 1931 and 1936 during the Great Depression, it was a monumental public-works project that employed thousands, tamed the flood-prone Colorado, and supplies water and hydroelectric power to Nevada, Arizona and California — including the cities of Las Vegas and Los Angeles. It was dedicated by President Franklin D. Roosevelt in 1935 and named for President Herbert Hoover (having been called Boulder Dam in the interim).

Construction and impact

Building the dam required diverting the river through four tunnels, pouring 3.3 million cubic metres of concrete (cooled by embedded pipes carrying refrigerated water — otherwise the mass would have taken over a century to set), and constructing the then-tallest dam in the world. Ninety-six industrial fatalities were officially recorded. Behind the dam, Lake Mead filled by 1941; in recent decades a prolonged Colorado Basin drought has drawn the lake down drastically — by 2022 to its lowest level since filling, exposing old intake towers and even the barrels of mob-era murder victims — a vivid sign of water stress in the Southwest, watched alongside parks like the nearby Grand Canyon downstream.
