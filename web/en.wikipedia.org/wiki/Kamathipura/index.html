Kamathipura

Kamathipura is a neighbourhood in South Mumbai, India, historically known as one of Asia's oldest and largest red-light districts. It was established in the late 18th century and named after the Kamathis, construction workers and artisans from the Telugu-speaking regions of Andhra Pradesh who settled there.

History

The area developed after the completion of the Hornby Vellard causeway in 1784 joined the islands of Bombay. During the British colonial period Kamathipura became a designated district for sex work, serving sailors and soldiers, and by the late 19th century it was one of the largest such districts in Asia. In the 1990s the AIDS epidemic and changes in policy and policing sharply reduced its population of sex workers, and numbers have continued to decline.

Redevelopment

In the 21st century much of Kamathipura has been targeted for redevelopment: many of its ageing low-rise buildings have been demolished and replaced by high-rise construction, transforming the neighbourhood's character. Social organisations continue to work with residents and the children of sex workers in the area.
