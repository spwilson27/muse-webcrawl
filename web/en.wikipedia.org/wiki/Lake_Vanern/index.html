Lake Vanern

Redirect page: this title is an unaccented spelling of Lake Vänern.

Lake Vänern (Swedish: Vänern) is the largest lake in Sweden and the largest lake entirely within the European Union, with a surface area of about 5,655 square kilometres. It lies in the provinces of Västergötland, Dalsland, and Värmland in southwestern Sweden, at an elevation of about 44 metres above sea level.

Geography

The lake is divided into two main basins separated by the Värmlandsnäs peninsula and islands such as Kållandsö. Major towns on its shores include Karlstad, Kristinehamn, Mariestad, Lidköping, Vänersborg, and Åmål. Its principal tributary is the Klarälven, and its outlet is the Göta älv, which flows through Gothenburg to the Kattegat.

Geology and history

Vänern was formed after the last ice age, about 11,500 years ago, and was initially part of a sea inlet before post-glacial rebound isolated it. The lake's waters contain around 35 species of fish, and it supports commercial fishing and busy shipping via the Trollhätte Canal.
