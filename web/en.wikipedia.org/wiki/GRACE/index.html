GRACE

Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment (GRACE)
Operator: NASA / DLR
Launch: 17 March 2002
End of mission: October 2017
Successor: GRACE-FO (launched 2018)

The Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment (GRACE) was a joint mission of NASA and the German Aerospace Center (DLR) that made detailed measurements of Earth's gravity field anomalies from its launch in March 2002 to the end of its science mission in October 2017. By tracking changes in gravity over time, GRACE provided a new tool to study Earth's oceans, geology, and climate.

Mission design

GRACE consisted of two identical spacecraft, nicknamed "Tom" and "Jerry", flying about 220 km apart in a polar orbit at roughly 500 km altitude. A K-band microwave ranging system measured the distance between the satellites to micrometre precision; as the pair passed over mass anomalies, their separation changed, allowing scientists to construct monthly maps (gravimetric "mascon" solutions) of Earth's gravity field far more accurately than previous methods.

Scientific results

GRACE data were used to quantify the melting of the Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets, groundwater depletion in aquifers such as those beneath California's Central Valley and northern India, ocean mass changes and sea-level rise, and post-glacial rebound. GRACE measurements showed Greenland losing on the order of a few hundred billion tonnes of ice per year during the mission.

Follow-on

The GRACE Follow-On (GRACE-FO) mission, launched on 22 May 2018, continues the data record using the same measurement concept plus an experimental laser ranging interferometer.
