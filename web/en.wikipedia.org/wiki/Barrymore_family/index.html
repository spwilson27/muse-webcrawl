Barrymore family

The Barrymore family is an American theatrical and film dynasty whose members have performed on stage and screen for nearly 150 years. The lineage descends from the English actor Maurice Barrymore (born Herbert Blythe) and Georgiana Drew, of the Drew acting family.

The "Royal Family of Broadway"

Maurice and Georgiana's three children dominated American theatre and early cinema: Lionel Barrymore (1878–1954), a versatile character actor who won an Academy Award for A Free Soul (1931); Ethel Barrymore (1879–1959), the grand tragedienne of Broadway, who won an Oscar for None but the Lonely Heart (1944); and John Barrymore (1882–1942), "the Great Profile", famed for his Hamlet and his film roles in Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde and Grand Hotel. Their story inspired the 1927 play and 1930 film The Royal Family.

Later generations

John's children included actor John Drew Barrymore and Diana Barrymore; his granddaughter Drew Barrymore (born 1975), daughter of John Drew Barrymore, became a major star from childhood in E.T. onward, continuing the family tradition into the twenty-first century.
