Marcus Loew (1870–1927) was an American businessman and a pioneer of the motion picture industry. He founded the Loew's theatre chain and, through it, the Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer (MGM) studio.

Early career

Born to poor Austrian Jewish immigrant parents in New York, Loew left school at nine. Saving from odd jobs, he invested in penny arcades and nickelodeons, building them into Loew's Consolidated Enterprises by 1904.

Loew's and MGM

To guarantee films for his theatres, Loew bought Metro Pictures in 1920 and Goldwyn Pictures in 1924, merging them with Louis B. Mayer Productions to form Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer. The vertically integrated company became one of Hollywood's major studios.

Death and legacy

Loew died of a heart attack in 1927, aged 57, just as MGM was rising to dominance. Loew's Theatres outlived the studio system and his name persisted in the Loews cinema chain for decades.
