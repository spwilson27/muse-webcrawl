Howard Davies (banker)

Sir Howard John Davies (born 12 February 1951) is a British banker and regulator. He was the first chairman of the Financial Services Authority (1997–2003), a deputy governor of the Bank of England, director of the London School of Economics (2003–2011), and chairman of NatWest Group (formerly Royal Bank of Scotland Group) from 2015 to 2024.

Career

Educated at Manchester Grammar School and Merton College, Oxford, Davies worked at the Foreign and Commonwealth Office and HM Treasury before becoming a partner at McKinsey & Company. He served as director general of the Confederation of British Industry (1992–1995) and then as a deputy governor of the Bank of England. In 1997 he was appointed to establish and lead the Financial Services Authority, the new single regulator for UK financial services, a post he held until 2003.

LSE and later roles

As director of the London School of Economics from 2003, Davies oversaw substantial expansion but resigned in 2011 following controversy over the school's acceptance of funding from the Gaddafi regime in Libya. He subsequently chaired the Airports Commission (2012–2015), whose final report recommended a third runway at Heathrow Airport, and became chairman of RBS/NatWest in 2015, overseeing the bank's return to majority private ownership and profitability after its financial-crisis bailout. He has written widely on financial regulation, including the book The Financial Crisis: Who Is to Blame?
