Moore–Penrose inverse

FieldLinear algebra
Named afterE. H. Moore, Roger Penrose
Also calledPseudoinverse
NotationA⁺

In mathematics, and in particular linear algebra, the Moore–Penrose inverse A⁺ of a matrix A is the most widely known generalisation of the inverse matrix. It exists and is unique for every matrix over the real or complex numbers, including rectangular and singular matrices that have no ordinary inverse. The concept was described independently by E. H. Moore in 1920 and by Roger Penrose in 1955, and it is sometimes simply called the pseudoinverse.

Definition

The Moore–Penrose inverse of a matrix A is defined as the unique matrix A⁺ satisfying four conditions, known as the Penrose conditions: AA⁺A = A; A⁺AA⁺ = A⁺; (AA⁺)* = AA⁺; and (A⁺A)* = A⁺A, where * denotes the conjugate transpose. When A is invertible, these conditions are met by the ordinary inverse.

One practical route to the pseudoinverse is through the singular value decomposition: if A = UΣV*, then A⁺ = VΣ⁺U*, where Σ⁺ is formed by taking the reciprocal of each non-zero singular value and transposing.

Applications

The pseudoinverse provides least-squares solutions to systems of linear equations that have no exact solution, and minimum-norm solutions to underdetermined systems. It is fundamental to linear regression, signal processing, control theory and numerical analysis.

In statistics, the fitted coefficient vector of a least-squares regression computed through the pseudoinverse of the design matrix gives the best linear unbiased approximation to the data in the least-squares sense.
