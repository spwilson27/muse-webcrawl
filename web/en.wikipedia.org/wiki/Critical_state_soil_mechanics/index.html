Critical state soil mechanics

Critical state soil mechanics is the framework within soil mechanics describing the mechanical behaviour of saturated remoulded soils on the basis of the concept of the critical state: the condition in which soil continues to deform at constant stress and constant volume. It was developed at Cambridge University in the 1950s and 1960s, principally by Kenneth Roscoe, Andrew Schofield, and Peter Wroth.

Concepts

Soil behaviour is described in terms of mean effective stress, deviator stress, and specific volume. Shearing drives soil samples toward the critical state line, whether they begin loose (contracting) or dense (dilating). Key relationships include the normal compression line and the swelling/recompression lines.

Models

The framework underlies constitutive models such as the original Cam-Clay and Modified Cam-Clay models, widely used to predict the behaviour of foundations, embankments, and excavations in geotechnical engineering.
