Industrial melanism

Classic example: Peppered moth (Biston betularia)

Mechanism: Natural selection driven by pollution

Industrial melanism is an evolutionary phenomenon in which darker (melanic) forms of a species become more common in populations affected by industrial pollution, because dark coloration provides better camouflage on soot-darkened surfaces.

Peppered moth

The classic case is the peppered moth: the dark carbonaria form, first recorded near Manchester in 1848, rose to dominate polluted areas as soot blackened trees and killed pale lichens, while light-coloured moths remained common in unpolluted countryside. Bernard Kettlewell's experiments in the 1950s demonstrated bird predation as the selective agent.

Decline

With clean-air legislation reducing soot, the melanic form has declined sharply — a widely cited example of evolution observed in both directions within a human lifetime.
