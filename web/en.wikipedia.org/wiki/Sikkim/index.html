Sikkim is a state in northeastern India, bordered by Bhutan, Nepal, Tibet (China) and the Indian state of West Bengal. It is India's least populous state and one of the smallest by area, yet it embraces enormous ecological and altitudinal range, from subtropical valleys to Kangchenjunga, at 8,586 m the third-highest mountain in the world. The capital and largest town is Gangtok. Sikkim became India's 22nd state in 1975 after a referendum ended its centuries-old monarchy.

History

The kingdom of Sikkim was founded in 1642 by Phuntsog Namgyal, a descendant of Tibetan migrants, who was consecrated as the first Chogyal (king) at Yuksom by three Buddhist lamas. The indigenous Lepcha and Limbu peoples were incorporated into a Buddhist monarchy. During the 19th century Sikkim became a British protectorate; after 1947 it remained an Indian protectorate under the Chogyal. Popular movements for democracy grew in the 1970s, and in 1975, following a referendum, the monarchy was abolished and Sikkim merged with India as a state.

Geography and nature

Sikkim is famed for its biodiversity: it shelters thousands of flowering plant species, hundreds of orchids and rhododendrons, and wildlife including the red panda, its state animal. The Khangchendzonga National Park, covering over a third of the state, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site recognised for both natural and cultural value. In 2016 Sikkim was declared India's first fully organic state, banning chemical fertilisers and pesticides in farming.

People and economy

The population mixes ethnic Nepalis (the majority), Lepchas, Bhutias and others; Hinduism and Buddhism are the main religions. Tourism, hydropower, agriculture (notably large cardamom, of which Sikkim is a leading producer) and pharmaceuticals drive the economy. Entry of foreigners to many areas requires permits because of the sensitive border.
