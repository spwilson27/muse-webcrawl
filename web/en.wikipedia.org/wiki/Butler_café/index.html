A butler café (Japanese: 執事喫茶, shitsuji kissa) is a type of cosplay restaurant in Japan in which the serving staff dress and act as English-style butlers, treating customers as the lords and ladies of a private mansion. Butler cafés emerged in the mid-2000s as a counterpart to the earlier maid cafés, and were developed particularly to serve female customers (fujoshi and otaku women among them), reversing the gender dynamics of maid cafés.

Concept and experience

Guests — addressed as "my lady" (ojō-sama) or "young master" — are greeted at the door by butlers in tailcoats and white gloves, escorted to their tables, and served afternoon tea and Western-style meals with elaborate formality. Many cafés provide a small bell with which customers summon their butler. The fantasy presented is that of a private residence rather than a shop: cafés often have fictional backstories, named staff characters, and interiors modelled on English country houses.

History

The best-known butler café, Swallowtail, opened in 2006 in Tokyo's Ikebukuro district — the centre of female-oriented otaku culture around Otome Road — and is often credited with popularising the format. Butler cafés have since appeared in other Japanese cities and, occasionally, as temporary or permanent venues elsewhere in Asia and at anime conventions abroad.

Related formats

Variants include dansō cafés, where female staff cross-dress as male butlers or hosts, and butler-themed bars and stage shows. Together with maid cafés, butler cafés are considered a distinctive expression of Japan's service-hospitality (omotenashi) and fandom cultures.
