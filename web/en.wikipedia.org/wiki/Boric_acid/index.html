Boric acid

FormulaH3BO3
Molar mass61.83 g/mol
AppearanceWhite crystalline solid
Acidity (pKa)~9.24
Mineral formSassolite

Boric acid, also called hydrogen orthoborate or boracic acid, is a weak, monobasic Lewis acid of boron with the chemical formula H3BO3. It occurs naturally as the mineral sassolite and is used as an antiseptic, insecticide, flame retardant, neutron absorber, and precursor to other boron compounds.

Properties

Boric acid is a white crystalline solid that is moderately soluble in water. Rather than donating a proton directly, it acts as a Lewis acid by accepting a hydroxide ion from water, releasing a hydrogen ion in the process. On heating, boric acid loses water in stages, passing through metaboric acid and tetraboric acid before forming boric oxide.

Production

Boric acid is prepared industrially by reacting borate minerals such as borax or colemanite with sulfuric acid, or on a large scale from the mineral kernite. Natural deposits occur in volcanic regions such as Tuscany, where it was historically extracted from steam vents.

Uses

Boric acid is a component of many detergents, cosmetics, and buffer solutions. In nuclear reactors, dissolved boric acid is used to control reactivity because boron-10 absorbs neutrons effectively. It is used in glassmaking to improve thermal and chemical resistance, in pyrotechnics to give a green flame, and as a mild antiseptic. As an insecticide, boric acid powder is used against cockroaches and ants.

Toxicity

Boric acid has low acute toxicity for mammals but can cause harm in large doses and is classified as a reproductive toxicant in the European Union, restricting some consumer uses.
