Pump

A pump is a mechanical device that moves fluids — liquids, gases or slurries — usually by converting electrical or mechanical energy into hydraulic energy, raising the fluid's pressure, velocity or elevation.

Types

Positive displacement pumps trap fixed amounts of fluid and force them through the outlet; they include reciprocating piston and diaphragm pumps and rotary gear, vane, screw and peristaltic pumps. Dynamic (rotodynamic) pumps, chiefly centrifugal pumps, impart velocity to the fluid with a rotating impeller and convert it to pressure. Other designs include jet pumps, airlift pumps and electromagnetic pumps.

Applications

Pumps are used in water supply and sewage, irrigation, oil and gas, chemical processing, heating and cooling systems, medicine and countless industrial processes. Performance is described by flow rate, head, efficiency and net positive suction head.
