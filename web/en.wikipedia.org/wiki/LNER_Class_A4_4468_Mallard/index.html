LNER Class A4 4468 Mallard is a London and North Eastern Railway Class A4 4-6-2 "Pacific" steam locomotive built at Doncaster Works in 1938 to a design by Sir Nigel Gresley. It holds the world speed record for steam locomotives, set in July 1938 and never surpassed.

The record run

On 3 July 1938, during brake trials on the East Coast Main Line, Mallard hauled a seven-coach train down Stoke Bank between Little Bytham and Essendine, reaching 126 mph (203 km/h) over a measured stretch — exceeding the previous record of 124.5 mph held by the German DRG Class 05 locomotive. Driver Joseph Duddington and fireman Thomas Bray were on the footplate. A plaque on the locomotive commemorates the achievement. The run slightly damaged the engine's big-end bearing, and the locomotive was taken to Doncaster for repairs afterward.

Service history

The A4 class was designed for the LNER's streamlined high-speed services between London King's Cross and Newcastle and Edinburgh. Mallard carried several liveries during its career: garter blue as built, wartime black, British Railways blue, and finally BR Brunswick green. It was renumbered 60022 under British Railways. Over its 25 years in service it ran approximately 1.4 million miles (2.3 million km) before withdrawal in April 1963.

Preservation

Mallard was preserved as part of the national collection and is displayed at the National Railway Museum in York. It was restored to working order for a period in the 1980s, including a return to steam in 1986 for the record's fiftieth anniversary season, but has since been a static exhibit. In 2013 it was joined by all five of its surviving sister locomotives at York for the "Great Gathering" celebrating the 75th anniversary of the record.
