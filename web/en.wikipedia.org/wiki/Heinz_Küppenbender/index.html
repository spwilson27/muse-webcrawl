Heinz Küppenbender

Born: 1901
Died: 1989
Occupation: Engineer, camera designer
Employer: Zeiss Ikon
Known for: Contax rangefinder cameras

Heinz Küppenbender (1901–1989) was a German engineer who was the chief designer at Zeiss Ikon in Dresden and one of the principal figures in the development of the 35 mm camera in the 1930s. He led the design of the Contax rangefinder cameras with which Zeiss Ikon competed against the Leica of Ernst Leitz.

Career

Küppenbender joined Zeiss Ikon, the camera combine formed in 1926 from several German manufacturers under the Carl Zeiss foundation. Under his direction the company introduced the Contax I in 1932, a sophisticated 35 mm rangefinder camera with a vertically travelling metal focal-plane shutter, a bayonet lens mount and a combined rangefinder-viewfinder in later models. The Contax II (1936) and Contax III, with its built-in light meter, were widely admired by professional photographers.

After the Second World War, Küppenbender was involved in re-establishing camera production in the western part of Germany, as the Zeiss Ikon organisation was split between Dresden in the Soviet zone and Stuttgart in the west. He supervised the design of the post-war Contax IIa and IIIa, produced in Stuttgart from 1950, which refined the pre-war design in a smaller body.

Legacy

The cameras developed under Küppenbender's leadership are regarded as among the most technically ambitious of their era; the rivalry between Contax and Leica drove rapid innovation in 35 mm photography and helped establish the format's dominance in photojournalism.
