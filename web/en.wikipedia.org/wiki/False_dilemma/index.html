A false dilemma, also called false dichotomy or false binary, is an informal fallacy based on a premise that erroneously limits what options are available. The dilemma form of the fallacy typically presents a choice between two mutually exclusive options and implies that only those two options exist, when in fact there are other possibilities that have not been considered.

Form

The classic pattern is: either claim A or claim B is true; claim A is false; therefore claim B is true — an inference that is valid only if A and B genuinely exhaust the possibilities. When they do not, the argument fails. A related variant presents two extremes as the only choices, ignoring intermediate positions ("if you are not with us, you are against us"), a usage often called the either/or fallacy in rhetoric.

Examples

Famous examples include the slogan "You're either with us, or against us," used by political leaders at various times; Morton's fork, a dilemma in which both horns lead to the same unpleasant conclusion; and everyday dilemmas such as "either we cut spending or the country goes bankrupt," which omits options such as raising revenue or restructuring debt. Not every two-option presentation is fallacious: some choices genuinely are binary, such as a light switch being on or off, and arguments that acknowledge a real forced choice commit no fallacy. The fallacy lies in the unjustified exclusion of alternatives.

Related concepts

Black-and-white thinking (splitting) is a related cognitive distortion in psychology, in which a person frames experience in extremes. In logic, the law of excluded middle states that every proposition is either true or its negation is true; confusion between this law and the false-dilemma fallacy arises when a complex proposition is mis-parsed as a simple binary. In debate, presenting an opponent's position as one horn of a false dilemma overlaps with the straw man fallacy.
