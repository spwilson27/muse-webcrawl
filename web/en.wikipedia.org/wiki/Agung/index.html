Agung

The agung is a set of two wide-rimmed, vertically suspended gongs used by the Maguindanao, Maranao, Sama, and Tausug peoples of the southern Philippines, and by peoples of eastern Malaysia and Indonesia, as the deepest-pitched instrument in the kulintang ensemble. The agung's larger gong, the pangandungan, has a lower pitch than the smaller gong of the set, the panentekan.

Description

Agung gongs are cast of bronze or brass, with a wide, turned-down rim and a prominent central knob (boss) that is struck with padded mallets. The pair is suspended vertically from a decorated wooden frame or rack. The larger pangandungan is typically around 90 centimetres in diameter, though sizes vary by region and maker. Historically, agung were valuable prestige goods, traded from foundries in Brunei and elsewhere, and served as heirlooms and markers of wealth and status as well as instruments.

Function in the ensemble

In kulintang music the agung provides the foundational rhythmic patterns and punctuation beneath the melodic kulintang (bossed gong-row) and the other rhythmic instruments such as the gandingan, babandil, and dabakan drum. Although the agung part is structurally simple, it is musically essential, marking the largest cycles of the composition. The agung was also traditionally used to transmit signals and announcements across distances and on ceremonial occasions.
