Waka (poetry)

Waka (和歌, “Japanese poem”) is a classical form of Japanese poetry, literally denoting Japanese-language verse as opposed to kanshi, poetry in Chinese. By extension it came to mean the tanka form: a short poem of five phrases in 5–7–5–7–7 morae (often described as syllables), totalling 31.

Forms

Early waka included the long chōka (alternating 5- and 7-mora lines, ending 7-7), the sedōka, and the short tanka. By the Heian period the tanka dominated so completely that “waka” and “tanka” became near-synonyms. Linked verse (renga) grew from exchanging the tanka's upper and lower halves, and later gave rise to haiku.

Anthologies and court culture

The Man'yōshū (c. 759) collected some 4,500 poems from all social ranks; the imperial Kokin Wakashū (c. 905), prefaced by Ki no Tsurayuki, made waka the prestige art of the Heian court, central to courtship, diplomacy and ritual. Twenty-one imperial anthologies followed. The Hyakunin Isshu, compiled by Fujiwara no Teika, remains popular through the card game karuta.

Themes

Waka treat the seasons, love, travel, transience and grief, using codified diction, pillow words (makurakotoba) and pivot words (kakekotoba). Practised by emperors and courtiers alike, waka composition continues today, including at the Imperial New Year poetry reading.
