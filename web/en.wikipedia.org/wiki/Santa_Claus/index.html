Santa Claus

Santa Claus, also known as Father Christmas, Saint Nicholas, Saint Nick, Kris Kringle, or simply Santa, is a legendary figure originating in Western Christian culture who is said to bring gifts during the late evening and overnight hours on Christmas Eve.

Santa Claus
Also known as: Father Christmas, Saint Nicholas, Kris Kringle
Home: North Pole (tradition)
Associated with: Christmas Eve gift-giving, reindeer sleigh

Origins

The figure derives principally from Saint Nicholas, a 4th-century Greek bishop of Myra famed for secret gift-giving, combined with Dutch Sinterklaas, English Father Christmas, and other traditions. Washington Irving and Clement Clarke Moore's 1823 poem "A Visit from St. Nicholas" ("'Twas the Night Before Christmas") shaped the American Santa with his sleigh and eight reindeer.

Modern image

Cartoonist Thomas Nast drew Santa in the 19th century; 20th-century advertising, notably Coca-Cola campaigns by Haddon Sundblom from 1931, popularised the jolly, rotund, red-suited figure. Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer was added in 1939.

Traditions

Children write letters to Santa, leave out milk and cookies, and are told he keeps naughty-and-nice lists. NORAD has "tracked" Santa since 1955.
