Peoria, Illinois

Peoria is a city in and the county seat of Peoria County, Illinois, United States, on the Illinois River. With a population of roughly 113,000 at the 2020 census, it is the largest city on the Illinois River and a principal city of the Peoria metropolitan area, which has around 400,000 residents.

History

One of the oldest settlements in Illinois, Peoria was first settled by French explorers; Fort Crevecoeur was built nearby in 1680, and the village that grew up took its name from the Peoria tribe of the Illinois Confederation. The city was incorporated in 1845. In the 19th century it became a major whiskey-distilling and river-trading centre, and Abraham Lincoln delivered an important anti-slavery speech here in 1854.

Economy

Peoria is best known as the longtime world headquarters of Caterpillar Inc., the construction-equipment manufacturer, which was based in the city from 1910 until it moved its global headquarters to the Chicago area in 2017 (and later to Texas). Healthcare, led by OSF HealthCare and UnityPoint Health, is now the largest employer sector, alongside Bradley University, founded in 1897.

Culture

The phrase "Will it play in Peoria?" — originating in vaudeville and famously used by the Nixon White House — made the city a byword for mainstream American taste. Peoria hosts the Peoria Civic Center, the Peoria Riverfront Museum, and minor-league teams including the Peoria Chiefs (baseball) and Peoria Rivermen (hockey). Comedian Richard Pryor was raised in Peoria.
