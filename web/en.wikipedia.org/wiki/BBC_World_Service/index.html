The BBC World Service is an international broadcaster owned and operated by the BBC, the world's largest international news service. Founded in 1932 as the BBC Empire Service, it broadcasts news, discussion and features in English and more than 40 other languages on radio, television and digital platforms, and is renowned for editorial independence and reliability, especially during wars and crises when local media are restricted.

History

The Empire Service began shortwave English broadcasts to the dominions in December 1932; foreign-language services followed, starting with Arabic in 1938. During the Second World War the European services became a vital source of uncensored news for occupied Europe, marked by the "V for Victory" campaign and the four opening notes of Beethoven's Fifth (Morse for "V") — also the origin of the famous interval signal era. The Cold War expanded services in Russian and Eastern European languages, countering jamming. The World Service was long funded by Foreign Office grant-in-aid; funding moved to the licence fee in 2014. Long-running English programmes include Newshour, The World Tonight, Outlook and HardTalk.

Reach and reputation

Radio remains crucial in parts of Africa and Asia; major language services include Arabic, Persian, Hausa, Hindi, Urdu, Bengali, Swahili, Russian, Ukrainian, Chinese and Spanish. Surveys consistently rank the BBC among the most trusted international news brands, though its funding and perceived British perspective attract periodic debate.
