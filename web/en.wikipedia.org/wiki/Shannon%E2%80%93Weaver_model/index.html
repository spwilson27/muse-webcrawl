Shannon-Weaver model

Proposed1948
AuthorsClaude Shannon, Warren Weaver
FieldCommunication theory

The Shannon-Weaver model is a foundational model of communication introduced by Claude Shannon in his 1948 paper "A Mathematical Theory of Communication" and popularised by Warren Weaver in 1949. It describes communication as a linear process from an information source to a destination.

Components

The model comprises an information source, a transmitter that encodes the message into a signal, a channel that carries the signal, a receiver that decodes it, and a destination. A noise source represents anything that distorts the signal in transit. Later versions added a feedback loop from receiver to sender.

Influence and criticism

The model became the starting point for communication studies and influenced fields from linguistics to media theory. Critics note that it treats communication as mere transmission, largely ignores meaning and social context, and originally described a one-way process even though human communication is interactive.
