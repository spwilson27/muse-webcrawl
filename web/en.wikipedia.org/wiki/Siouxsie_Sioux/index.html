Siouxsie Sioux (born Susan Janet Ballion, 27 May 1957) is an English singer, songwriter and musician. She is best known as the lead singer of the rock band Siouxsie and the Banshees (1976–1996), with whom she released eleven studio albums and had several UK Top 40 singles including "Hong Kong Garden", "Happy House" and "Cities in Dust". With the Banshees and her second group The Creatures, she became one of the most influential figures in British post-punk and a formative influence on gothic rock, alternative rock and later artists from The Cure and Joy Division to PJ Harvey and LCD Soundsystem.

Early life and punk years

Ballion was born in Southwark, London, and grew up in Chislehurst, Kent. Part of the "Bromley Contingent" of Sex Pistols followers, she appeared in the Pistols' infamous 1976 Bill Grundy television interview. Siouxsie and the Banshees debuted at the 100 Club Punk Festival in September 1976 with a twenty-minute improvisation on "The Lord's Prayer". Their debut single "Hong Kong Garden" reached No. 7 in the UK in 1978.

Career with the Banshees and the Creatures

The Banshees' albums The Scream (1978), Kaleidoscope (1980), Juju (1981), A Kiss in the Dreamhouse (1982) and Peepshow (1988) traced an arc from abrasive post-punk to dark, lush pop; their cover of "Dear Prudence" (1983) was their biggest UK hit. In 1981 Siouxsie formed The Creatures with drummer Budgie, exploring percussion-driven, exotic sounds on albums such as Feast (1983), recorded in Hawaii, and Boomerang (1989), recorded in Spain. The Banshees split in 1996.

Solo work

After The Creatures' final album (2004) and her collaboration with Basement Jaxx, Siouxsie released her debut solo album Mantaray in 2007 to critical acclaim, followed by tours in 2008 and a celebrated return at the Meltdown Festival in 2013. In 2022 Siouxsie and the Banshees' back catalogue returned to prominence when "Spellbound" featured in the series Stranger Things; Siouxsie resumed live performances in 2023.

Influence

Siouxsie's contralto voice, commanding stage presence and dramatic visual style — severe eye makeup, spiked hair, fetish-inflected fashion — made her an icon of alternative music. She received the Ivor Novello Award for Inspiration in 2012 and is routinely cited among the greatest singers and frontwomen in rock.
