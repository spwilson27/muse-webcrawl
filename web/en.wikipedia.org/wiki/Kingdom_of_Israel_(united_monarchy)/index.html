Kingdom of Israel (united monarchy)

United Kingdom of Israel
Traditional datesc. 1047 – c. 930 BCE
CapitalHebron, then Jerusalem

SourceHebrew Bible (Books of Samuel and Kings)

The Kingdom of Israel, often called the united monarchy, is the kingdom described in the Hebrew Bible as uniting the Israelite tribes under the kings Saul, David, and Solomon, before splitting after Solomon's death (c. 930 BCE) into the northern Kingdom of Israel and the southern Kingdom of Judah.

Biblical account

According to the Books of Samuel, the prophet Samuel anointed Saul as the first king; after Saul's fall at Mount Gilboa, David — who had conquered Jerusalem and made it his capital — united the tribes, expanded the kingdom, and was succeeded by his son Solomon, famed for wisdom, trade, and building the First Temple in Jerusalem. On Solomon's death, the northern tribes rejected his son Rehoboam and seceded under Jeroboam.

Historicity debate

The scale and even existence of the united monarchy are debated. "Maximalist" scholars accept a substantial 10th-century BCE kingdom; "minimalists" argue Judah was a small chiefdom and the grand kingdom a later literary construction. Evidence cited includes the Tel Dan stele's reference to the "House of David", monumental building at sites such as Hazor, Megiddo and Gezer, and the fortified Judahite site of Khirbet Qeiyafa. Most scholars today accept a historical Davidic polity of debated size centred on Jerusalem.
