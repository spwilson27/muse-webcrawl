Decentralized social network

A decentralized social network (spelled decentralised social network in British English) is a social networking service that operates across many independent servers rather than under the control of a single organization. Using open protocols, the servers interoperate so that users registered on different servers can follow one another, exchange messages, and share media, in much the same way that email users on different providers can write to each other.

Characteristics

Decentralized social networks typically share several features:

  • No central authority — no single entity can shut down the network or dictate global policy.
  • Local governance — each server or community sets its own rules, membership criteria, and moderation practices, and can block ("defederate" from) servers it does not wish to interact with.
  • Account portability — users can move between servers, in some protocols taking their followers with them.
  • Open protocols — interoperability is based on published standards, allowing independent software implementations.

Major networks and protocols

The largest decentralized social ecosystem is the Fediverse, a federation of services using the ActivityPub protocol, which became a W3C Recommendation in 2018. Its best-known application is Mastodon, a microblogging service launched in 2016, alongside photo-sharing (Pixelfed), video (PeerTube), and other formats. The AT Protocol, developed for Bluesky, emphasizes portable identity and algorithmic choice. Other approaches include the relay-based Nostr protocol, the Diaspora network, and peer-to-peer systems such as Secure Scuttlebutt that synchronize through gossip protocols without any servers at all.

Comparison with centralized services

Centralized platforms offer a uniform experience, global search, and company-run moderation, but concentrate control over data and speech in one company. Decentralized networks trade some of that convenience for user autonomy and pluralism: discovery can be harder, moderation varies by community, and small server operators bear hosting costs and legal exposure. Research on decentralized social media examines how these trade-offs affect misinformation, harassment, and community health.

Growth

Interest in decentralized social networking has surged at moments of controversy at centralized platforms. Mastodon's user base grew rapidly after Twitter's acquisition in 2022, and Bluesky, which began as an invite-only service in 2023, grew to tens of millions of users after opening to the public in 2024. Large platforms have also begun interoperating: Meta's Threads started federating with ActivityPub services in 2024.
