California during World War II

California during World War II was transformed by the war effort into a major center of American military training, shipbuilding, and aircraft production. The state's population and economy grew dramatically between 1941 and 1945.

Home front

California shipyards, including Henry J. Kaiser's Richmond yards, produced Liberty and Victory ships at record pace, while aircraft plants in Southern California built a large share of U.S. warplanes. Hundreds of thousands of Americans migrated to California for defense jobs, straining housing and infrastructure.

Military presence

The state hosted major bases such as Camp Pendleton, Fort Ord, and the Mare Island Naval Shipyard. Fears of Japanese attack led to blackouts, the Battle of Los Angeles incident of February 1942, and coastal defenses.

Japanese American incarceration

Under Executive Order 9066, over 100,000 Japanese Americans — most of them from California — were forcibly removed to incarceration camps such as Manzanar and Tule Lake. The policy was later officially repudiated, and reparations were authorized by the Civil Liberties Act of 1988.
