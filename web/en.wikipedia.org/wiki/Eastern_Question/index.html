Eastern Question

In diplomatic history, the Eastern Question was a geopolitical dilemma posed by the perceived decline of the Ottoman Empire from the late 18th century: how the European great powers should manage Ottoman weakness without upsetting the balance of power, especially regarding Russia's ambitions toward Constantinople and the Straits.

Eastern Question
Period: c. 1774-1923
Actors: Russia, Britain, France, Austria, Ottoman Empire
Key events: Crimean War, Congress of Berlin

History

The question arose after the Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca (1774) and intensified with Greek independence, the Russo-Turkish wars, and the Crimean War (1853-56), in which Britain and France defended the Ottomans against Russia.

Resolution

The Congress of Berlin (1878) redrew the Balkans. The question effectively ended with the Ottoman Empire's defeat in World War I, its partition, and the founding of the Republic of Turkey in 1923.
