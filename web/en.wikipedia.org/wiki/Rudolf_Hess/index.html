Rudolf Hess

Hess in 1933

Rudolf Walter Richard Hess (26 April 1894 – 17 August 1987) was a German politician and a leading member of the Nazi Party. Appointed Deputy Führer to Adolf Hitler in 1933, he held that position until 1941, when he flew solo to Scotland in an attempt to negotiate peace with the United Kingdom during the Second World War. He was taken prisoner and eventually convicted at the Nuremberg trials, spending the rest of his life in Spandau Prison.

Early life and rise

Hess was born in Alexandria, Egypt, the son of a German merchant. He served as an infantryman and pilot in the First World War. He joined the Nazi Party in 1920, took part in the Beer Hall Putsch of 1923, and while imprisoned with Hitler at Landsberg helped transcribe Mein Kampf. He became Hitler's private secretary and, in 1933, Deputy Führer and Reich Minister without Portfolio.

Flight to Scotland

On 10 May 1941 Hess flew a Messerschmitt Bf 110 to Scotland, parachuting near Eaglesham, apparently hoping to contact the Duke of Hamilton and broker peace. He was arrested and held in Britain for the remainder of the war. Hitler denounced him as mad and stripped him of his offices; Martin Bormann succeeded him.

Trial and imprisonment

At Nuremberg, Hess was found guilty of conspiracy and crimes against peace and sentenced to life imprisonment. He served his sentence at Spandau Prison in Berlin, from 1966 as its sole prisoner, until his death by suicide in 1987 at the age of 93. His grave in Wunsiedel became a neo-Nazi pilgrimage site and was removed in 2011.
