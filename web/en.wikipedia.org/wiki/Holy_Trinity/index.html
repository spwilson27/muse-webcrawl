Holy Trinity

In Christianity, the Holy Trinity is the central doctrine concerning the nature of God: one God existing eternally as three distinct persons — God the Father, God the Son (Jesus Christ), and God the Holy Spirit — who are nevertheless of one substance, essence or nature (consubstantial). The doctrine is summarised in the Latin formula tres personae, una substantia: three persons, one being.

Doctrine

Trinitarian doctrine holds that the Father, Son and Holy Spirit are each fully God, are distinct from one another, and yet are a single God. It rejects both tritheism (three separate gods) and modalism (the view that the three are merely modes or masks of one person). The persons are distinguished by their relations of origin: the Father is unbegotten, the Son is eternally begotten of the Father, and the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father (in Western theology, from the Father and the Son — the filioque, a principal cause of the East–West Schism of 1054).

Development

The word "Trinity" does not appear in the Bible, though Trinitarians cite triadic formulae such as the baptismal command of Matthew 28:19 and the benediction of 2 Corinthians 13:14. The doctrine was articulated by theologians such as Tertullian (who coined trinitas), and was defined by the ecumenical councils: the Council of Nicaea (325) affirmed the Son's consubstantiality with the Father against Arianism, and the Council of Constantinople (381) extended the affirmation to the Holy Spirit; the resulting Nicene Creed remains the common statement of Trinitarian faith. The Athanasian Creed and the work of the Cappadocian Fathers and Augustine of Hippo further shaped the doctrine in East and West respectively.

Nontrinitarian positions

Some Christian traditions reject the doctrine, including Unitarians, Jehovah's Witnesses, Oneness Pentecostals and Latter Day Saints, whose conceptions of the Godhead differ substantially. Islam and Judaism likewise reject Trinitarian belief, regarding it as incompatible with strict monotheism.

In art

The Trinity has been a major subject of Christian art, from representations of the three angelic visitors to Abraham (the subject of Andrei Rublev's famous icon) to the Western "Throne of Grace", showing the Father holding the crucified Son with the dove of the Holy Spirit between them. The feast of Trinity Sunday is observed in Western Christianity on the first Sunday after Pentecost.
