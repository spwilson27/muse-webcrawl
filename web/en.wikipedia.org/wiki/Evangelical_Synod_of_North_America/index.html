The Evangelical Synod of North America (in German, Deutsche Evangelische Synode von Nord-Amerika) was a United Protestant denomination in the United States, founded by German immigrants in Missouri in 1840. It carried into American church life the tradition of the Prussian Union of Lutheran and Reformed churches.

History

Six German pastors organised the body in 1840 as the Church Association of the West (Kirchenverein des Westens), serving scattered German settlements along the frontier. It founded Eden Seminary (now Eden Theological Seminary in St Louis) in 1850 to train its ministers. The synod took its later name in 1877 and remained German-speaking well into the twentieth century, maintaining extensive mission, hospital and orphanage work.

Merger

In 1934 the Synod merged with the Reformed Church in the United States to form the Evangelical and Reformed Church. That body in turn united with the Congregational Christian Churches in 1957 to create the United Church of Christ, of which the Evangelical Synod is one of the four historic predecessor traditions.
