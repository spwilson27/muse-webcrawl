The 2016–present purges in Turkey are a series of mass dismissals, arrests and prosecutions carried out by the Turkish government following the failed coup attempt of 15 July 2016. Under a state of emergency that lasted two years, President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's government dismissed well over 150,000 public officials and arrested tens of thousands of people, officially on the grounds of links to the Gülen movement, which Turkey designates a terrorist organisation and blames for the coup. The purges extended across the military, judiciary, police, education, media and civil service.

Background

On the night of 15–16 July 2016, a faction within the Turkish Armed Forces attempted to seize power, bombing parliament and clashing with police and civilians; about 250 people were killed. The attempt failed within hours. The government accused the network of U.S.-based cleric Fethullah Gülen of orchestrating it — a charge Gülen denied — and declared a state of emergency on 20 July 2016, allowing rule by decree.

Scope of the purges

Successive emergency decrees (KHKs) dismissed soldiers (including nearly half of the generals and admirals of the armed forces), some 4,000 judges and prosecutors, tens of thousands of police officers and teachers, and thousands of academics; universities and schools linked to the Gülen movement were closed, as were more than a hundred media outlets. Passports were cancelled, assets seized, and more than a thousand companies confiscated. Critics — including the UN, the Council of Europe and human-rights organisations — argued that many dismissals rested on tenuous evidence such as use of the ByLock messaging app or accounts at a Gülen-linked bank, and that the campaign expanded to encompass Kurdish politicians, leftists and journalists unconnected to the coup.

Aftermath

The state of emergency ended in July 2018, but terrorism prosecutions continued on a large scale, and most dismissed officials' appeals to a special review commission were rejected. Turkey's relations with the European Union and the United States were strained by the purges and by Ankara's demands for Gülen's extradition. The events also preceded the 2017 constitutional referendum that converted Turkey to a presidential system with greatly expanded executive powers.
