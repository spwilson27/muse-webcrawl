Saichō

Saichō (最澄, 767–822), posthumously known as Dengyō Daishi, was a Japanese Buddhist monk who founded the Tendai school of Buddhism in Japan, based on the Chinese Tiantai teachings centred on the Lotus Sutra. He founded Enryaku-ji temple on Mount Hiei near Kyoto.

Life

Born Mitsuno no Obito Hirono, Saichō was ordained young and withdrew to Mount Hiei in 785, building a hermitage that became Enryaku-ji. In 804 he sailed to Tang China with an official embassy, studying Tiantai under masters at Mount Tiantai, as well as esoteric (Mikkyō), Zen and Mahayana precepts, returning in 805.

Tendai in Japan

Saichō sought imperial recognition of an independent Tendai ordination platform on Mount Hiei, breaking the monopoly of the Nara schools; permission was granted a week after his death. His disputes with Nara monks such as Tokuitsu over the universality of Buddha-nature are landmarks in Japanese Buddhist thought. Many later founders — Hōnen, Shinran, Eisai, Dōgen and Nichiren — trained at Enryaku-ji, making Saichō's monastery a cradle of Kamakura Buddhism.
