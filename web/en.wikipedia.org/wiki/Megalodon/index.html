Megalodon

Megalodon (Otodus megalodon, formerly Carcharocles megalodon or Carcharodon megalodon), meaning "big tooth", is an extinct species of giant mackerel shark that lived approximately 23 to 3.6 million years ago, from the early Miocene to the early Pliocene. It was one of the largest and most powerful predators ever to have lived.

Description

Megalodon is known almost entirely from its enormous teeth — triangular, serrated, and up to about 18 cm (7 in) long — and from some vertebral centra, since shark skeletons are cartilaginous and rarely fossilize. Length estimates based on scaling from the great white shark and other lamniforms generally fall between 10 and 15 metres, with some recent studies suggesting maximum lengths approaching 20 metres for the largest females. Its bite force is estimated to have been the strongest of any known animal.

Ecology

Megalodon was cosmopolitan in warm temperate and subtropical seas. It was an apex predator feeding on large prey, including whales (whose bones bear its bite marks), seals, and sea turtles. Juveniles probably inhabited coastal nursery areas. Like some modern lamnid sharks, it may have been regionally endothermic, aiding its activity in cooler waters and wide-ranging hunting.

Extinction

Megalodon disappeared around 3.6 million years ago. Proposed causes include cooling oceans and falling sea levels during the onset of the Pliocene ice ages, which shrank its warm-water habitat and coastal nurseries, declines in the diversity of the baleen whales it preyed upon, and competition with emerging predators such as the great white shark and early killer whales.

Despite persistent popular speculation, there is no evidence that megalodon survives today; the idea recurs in pseudoscientific television programs and films such as The Meg (2018).
