Ethical socialism

Ethical socialism is a political philosophy that appeals to socialism on ethical and moral grounds rather than on the basis of historical materialism, scientific socialism, or economic determinism. It holds that socialism is desirable because it embodies values such as equality, cooperation, fellowship, and social justice, and that capitalism should be rejected because it is unjust and inhumane — not merely inefficient or historically doomed.

History

Ethical socialism has deep roots in the British socialist tradition. The early movement drew on Christian socialism, the writings of John Ruskin and William Morris, and the gradualist advocacy of the Fabian Society. R. H. Tawney, in works such as The Acquisitive Society (1920) and Equality (1931), gave the tradition its most influential statement, attacking the profit motive and defending equality as a moral imperative. Tawney's thought profoundly influenced the British Labour Party, whose constitution and post-war programme were framed in explicitly ethical terms.

In Germany, the neo-Kantian "Marburg school" and figures associated with revisionism in the Social Democratic Party sought to ground socialism in Kantian ethics. Leonard Nelson and others argued that Marxist predictions were unnecessary: socialism followed from the categorical imperative applied to economic life.

Influence

Ethical socialism underpinned the ideology of Clement Attlee's Labour governments, which built the National Health Service and the modern British welfare state after 1945. Anthony Crosland's The Future of Socialism (1956) restated the doctrine for a revisionist generation. In the 1990s, Tony Blair explicitly invoked ethical socialism — and the related communitarian and "Third Way" ideas — as the values base of New Labour, emphasising community, responsibility, and opportunity. Critics, from Marxists on the left to market liberals, have argued that ethical socialism offers moral aspiration without an adequate theory of political economy or of the transition to a socialist society.
