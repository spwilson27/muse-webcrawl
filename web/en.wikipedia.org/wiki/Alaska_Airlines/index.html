Alaska Airlines is a major American airline headquartered in SeaTac, Washington, within the Seattle metropolitan area. Founded in 1932 as McGee Airways in Anchorage, it grew from a single-aircraft bush operation into the fifth-largest airline in the United States by passengers carried, with a network concentrated on the US West Coast, Alaska, Hawaii, Mexico and Central America. It is the flagship carrier of the Alaska Air Group, which also owns regional carrier Horizon Air.

History

Linious "Mac" McGee started flying mail and passengers from Anchorage in 1932; after mergers with other Alaskan carriers the company took the name Alaska Airlines in 1944. It survived colourful post-war decades — including flying the Berlin Airlift and Operation Magic Carpet, which airlifted Yemenite Jews to Israel — and near-bankruptcy in the 1970s, before re-establishing itself under chairman Bruce Kennedy as a disciplined West Coast carrier. After deregulation it expanded down the Pacific coast and, in 2001, survived the crash of Flight 261 (an MD-83 lost off California in 2000 following a jackscrew failure), which led to industry-wide maintenance reforms.

In 2016 Alaska Air Group acquired Virgin America for about $2.6 billion, completing the merger into a single operating certificate in 2018 and retiring the Virgin brand. Alaska joined the Oneworld alliance in March 2021. In December 2023 it announced an agreement to acquire Hawaiian Airlines for approximately $1.9 billion including debt, a deal completed in September 2024 with the two brands retained.

Operations and services

The airline's mainline fleet is all-Boeing, built around the 737-800, 737-900ER and 737 MAX family, while regional flying is performed by Horizon Air with Embraer 175s and by SkyWest under capacity-purchase agreements. Alaska is known for its "Chester" tail logo — the portrait of an Alaska Native man painted on its aircraft since the 1970s — a strong record in customer-satisfaction surveys, and a large cargo and passenger role within the state of Alaska, where it serves remote communities no road reaches. Its frequent-flyer programme, Mileage Plan, is shared across the group and Oneworld partners.
