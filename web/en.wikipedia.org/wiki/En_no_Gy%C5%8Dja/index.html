En no Gyōja (役行者, "En the Ascetic"), also known as En no Ozunu, was a Japanese ascetic and mystic traditionally regarded as the founder of Shugendō, the syncretic mountain religion that combines elements of Buddhism, Shinto and folk beliefs. He is venerated throughout Japan, and statues of him — gaunt, bearded, seated in meditation with an axe and scroll — guard many mountain temples.

Legend and life

According to later chronicles and hagiography, En was born in Katsuragi in Yamato Province (present-day Nara Prefecture) and practised austerities on Mount Katsuragi and Mount Ōmine. He was said to command spirits and demons, including the oni Hitokotonushi, whom he bound to build a bridge. Accused of sorcery by a jealous rival, he was exiled to Izu Ōshima in 699 but was later pardoned. Stories tell of him flying through the air on a five-coloured cloud and ultimately ascending to heaven.

Legacy

The ascetics who follow his path, the yamabushi, still trek the Ōmine Okugakemichi pilgrimage route he is credited with opening. In 1799 the imperial court granted him the posthumous title Jinben Daibōsatsu. His legendary life is recorded in works such as the Nihon Ryōiki and later Shugendō scriptures.
