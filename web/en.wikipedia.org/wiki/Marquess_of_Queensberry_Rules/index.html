Marquess of Queensberry Rules

The Marquess of Queensberry Rules, published in 1867, are a code of generally accepted rules in the sport of boxing. Drafted by John Graham Chambers and endorsed by John Douglas, 9th Marquess of Queensberry, they transformed bare-knuckle prizefighting into the modern gloved sport.

Key provisions

The twelve rules mandated padded gloves of fair size, three-minute rounds with one minute of rest, a ten-second count for a downed fighter who could not continue, and the prohibition of wrestling and hugging. Bouts took place in a 24-foot ring.

Impact

The rules gradually displaced the London Prize Rules in Britain and America; the last bare-knuckle heavyweight championship was fought by John L. Sullivan in 1889. By the end of the 19th century boxing under Queensberry rules had become the standard framework from which modern professional and amateur codes descend.
