Keyser Söze

Keyser Söze is a fictional character and the main antagonist in the 1995 film The Usual Suspects, written by Christopher McQuarrie and directed by Bryan Singer. A legendary, almost mythical crime lord, Söze is renowned for his ruthlessness and elusiveness. In the film's celebrated twist ending, he is revealed to have been present throughout the story in disguise.

Character background

According to the account narrated by small-time con man Roger "Verbal" Kint, Söze began as a low-level Turkish drug dealer. When a rival Hungarian gang held his family hostage and murdered one of his children, Söze killed the hostage-takers, his own remaining family, and everyone connected with the gang, then vanished into the criminal underworld. Thereafter he operated exclusively through intermediaries, so that almost no one who worked for him knew his identity — "the greatest trick the Devil ever pulled was convincing the world he didn't exist".

Role in the film

Five criminals, brought together in a police line-up, are recruited — through Söze's lawyer, Kobayashi — to attack a ship in San Pedro Bay believed to carry a large cocaine shipment. The assault ends in massacre and fire, with a sole survivor telling police that Söze himself was present. As Kint finishes recounting the story to customs agent Dave Kujan and leaves the station, Kujan realises that details of the tale were improvised from objects around the office, and that the limping, seemingly harmless Kint is Söze. By the time Kujan gives chase, Söze has disappeared.

Reception and legacy

The character — played by Kevin Spacey, whose performance won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor — became an enduring cultural reference for a hidden mastermind. The American Film Institute ranked Keyser Söze among its notable screen villains, and the name is widely used in popular culture and journalism to evoke an unseen, manipulative power.
