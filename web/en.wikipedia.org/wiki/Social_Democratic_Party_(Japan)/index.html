The Social Democratic Party is a small social-democratic political party in Japan.

The Social Democratic Party (Japanese: 社会民主党, Shakai Minshutō; SDP) is a political party in Japan. It is the successor of the Japan Socialist Party (JSP), which for most of the postwar period was the largest opposition party to the conservative Liberal Democratic Party. The party is led by Mizuho Fukushima, who has headed it for much of the period since 2003.

History

The JSP was founded in 1945 and briefly led a coalition government under Tetsu Katayama in 1947–48. Anchored by the support of public-sector trade unions and committed to unarmed neutrality and defence of the pacifist Article 9 of the constitution, it held roughly a third of Diet seats for decades. In 1994 JSP leader Tomiichi Murayama became prime minister in coalition with the LDP, a move that required abandoning core positions on the Self-Defense Forces and the U.S.–Japan alliance and precipitated the party's collapse: most of its legislators departed to form the Democratic Party of Japan in 1996, when the remainder renamed itself the Social Democratic Party.

Platform and present status

The SDP advocates social democracy, welfare-state policies, opposition to constitutional revision, abolition of nuclear power, and protection of workers' rights. Since its contraction in the 1990s it has been a minor party, holding only a handful of seats in the National Diet and periodically facing the threshold of losing its legal party status; it narrowly retained it in the 2020s through proportional-representation votes. It remains active in local politics, notably in Okinawa, where it opposes the U.S. military base presence.
