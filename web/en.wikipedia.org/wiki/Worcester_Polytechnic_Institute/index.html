Worcester Polytechnic Institute (WPI) is a private research university in Worcester, Massachusetts. Founded in 1865, it was one of the United States' first engineering and technology universities and now focuses on applied science, engineering, and project-based learning.

History

WPI was chartered in 1865 through the philanthropy of John Boynton, a tinware manufacturer, and Ichabod Washburn, owner of the world's largest wire mill. Instruction began in 1868 in two landmark buildings: Boynton Hall (classrooms) and Washburn Shops (laboratories) — embodying the founding philosophy of combining theory and practice, reflected in the university's two-tower logo and its German motto, Lehr und Kunst.

Academics

WPI operates on four 7-week terms and is known for its project-based curriculum. All undergraduates complete three major projects: the Sufficiency (humanities), the Interactive Qualifying Project (IQP, on societal issues), and the Major Qualifying Project (MQP, in the major field). The Global Projects Program sends students to project centers on six continents. WPI's signature majors include robotics engineering — it offered the first US bachelor's degree in the field — fire protection engineering, and game design (IMGD).

Campus and traditions

Campus traditions include the Goat's Head Award, rope pulls, and the heckling rivalry with the (former) goat mascot. Robert H. Goddard, the father of modern rocketry, was an alumnus (class of 1908). The school's athletic teams are the Engineers.
