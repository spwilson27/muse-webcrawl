The F.B.I. is an American police procedural television series created by Quinn Martin and produced by QM Productions in association with Warner Bros. Television. It aired on ABC for nine seasons from 1965 to 1974, comprising 241 episodes, and was partly sponsored by the Ford Motor Company — whose vehicles appeared throughout the series — and by Alcoa.

The series starred Efrem Zimbalist Jr. as Inspector Lewis Erskine, a widowed FBI agent working out of the Washington headquarters, assisted at various times by Special Agent Tom Colby (William Reynolds) and Special Agent Chris Daniels (Shelly Novack), under the supervision of Assistant Director Arthur Ward (Philip Abbott). Episodes were based on actual FBI cases, fictionalized, and were made with the full cooperation of the Bureau; director J. Edgar Hoover took a personal interest in the program, which presented the FBI in an idealized light.

Format and production

Each episode opened with the announcement that the story was based on FBI files and featured narration by Marvin Miller. Cases ranged from bank robbery, kidnapping, and extortion to espionage and organized crime. Hoover insisted that agents be portrayed as dedicated and incorruptible, that no agent be shown accepting a bribe, and that the Bureau approve scripts; FBI employees with criminal records could not appear even in minor roles.

Legacy

The series was a consistent ratings performer, ranking in the Nielsen top ten during several seasons. Zimbalist, who also narrated the closing credits with road-safety messages, remained closely identified with the role. A short-lived revival, Today's FBI, aired in 1981–82, and the original series inspired later FBI-themed dramas, including the 2018 CBS series FBI.
