Drexel Burnham Lambert Inc. was an American investment banking firm that rose in the 1980s to become the dominant force in the high-yield ("junk bond") market, before collapsing in scandal and filing for bankruptcy in February 1990 — at the time the largest Wall Street firm ever to fail. The firm was formed in 1973 by the merger of Drexel Firestone, whose banking lineage traced to the Philadelphia firm Drexel & Co. founded in 1838, with Burnham & Co. and William D. Witter.

The junk-bond empire

From its Beverly Hills office, Michael Milken built a network of buyers and issuers that transformed low-rated corporate debt into a mass market. Drexel's bonds financed corporate raiders and leveraged buyouts — most famously the $25 billion RJR Nabisco buyout by KKR in 1988 — and its annual "Predators' Ball" conference became a symbol of the decade's deal-making. By 1986 Drexel was the most profitable firm on Wall Street.

Scandal and collapse

The unraveling began with the 1986 insider-trading case of Ivan Boesky, whose cooperation implicated Milken and Drexel. In December 1988 the firm pleaded guilty to six felony counts of fraud and agreed to pay $650 million in penalties. Milken was indicted on 98 counts in 1989, pleaded guilty to six felonies in 1990 and was sentenced to ten years (serving about two). Cut off from credit amid a seizing junk-bond market, Drexel Burnham Lambert filed for Chapter 11 protection on 13 February 1990 and was liquidated, throwing some 5,000 people out of work.
