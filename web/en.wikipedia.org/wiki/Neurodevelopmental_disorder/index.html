Neurodevelopmental disorder

Neurodevelopmental disorders
Brain development and neurodevelopmental conditions
SpecialtyPsychiatry, neurology, pediatrics
OnsetDevelopmental period (childhood)
CausesGenetic, prenatal, perinatal and environmental factors
ClassificationDSM-5; ICD-11

Neurodevelopmental disorders are a group of conditions that arise during the developmental period, typically manifesting early in childhood, and are characterized by developmental deficits that produce impairments of personal, social, academic or occupational functioning. In the DSM-5 the category includes intellectual disability, communication disorders, autism spectrum disorder, attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), specific learning disorders, motor disorders such as developmental coordination disorder and tic disorders, and related conditions.

Causes

These disorders are highly heritable but multifactorial. Contributing factors include chromosomal abnormalities (e.g., Down syndrome, fragile X syndrome), single-gene mutations, prenatal exposure to alcohol or infections, prematurity and low birth weight, and early psychosocial deprivation. Abnormalities of brain development, including altered neurogenesis, synapse formation and pruning, are implicated.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis rests on developmental history, standardized testing and observation, often by multidisciplinary teams. Management is typically supportive and individualized: early intervention, special education, speech and occupational therapy, behavioural interventions, and medication for specific symptoms (for example stimulants in ADHD). Outcomes vary widely; many individuals benefit substantially from early and sustained support.

Related concepts

The concept of neurodiversity frames some neurodevelopmental conditions, particularly autism and ADHD, as natural variations of human cognition rather than purely as deficits, and has influenced debate about diagnostic frameworks and accommodations.
