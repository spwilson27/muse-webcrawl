Young Turk Revolution

Date3–24 July 1908
LocationOttoman Empire (Macedonia)
OrganisersCommittee of Union and Progress
ResultRestoration of the 1876 constitution; start of the Second Constitutional Era

The Young Turk Revolution (July 1908) was a revolt by the Young Turk movement, led by the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP), against the absolute rule of Sultan Abdul Hamid II. It restored the Ottoman constitution of 1876, which the Sultan had suspended for thirty years, and inaugurated the Second Constitutional Era of the Ottoman Empire.

Background

Abdul Hamid II had ruled autocratically since suspending parliament in 1878, relying on censorship, a vast spy network, and pan-Islamic ideology. Opposition coalesced among exiles in Paris and, crucially, among officers of the Third Army in Ottoman Macedonia, where the empire's European provinces were threatened by Bulgarian, Greek, and Serbian insurgencies and by Great Power intervention. In 1907 the Paris congress of Ottoman opposition groups resolved on revolt. Fears that a British–Russian meeting at Reval (June 1908) would partition Macedonia spurred the army conspirators to act.

The revolt

On 3 July 1908 Major Ahmed Niyazi deserted with his men at Resne and took to the hills, issuing a proclamation demanding the constitution. Enver Bey and other CUP officers followed. Uprisings spread through Macedonia; troops sent from Anatolia to suppress them fraternised with the rebels or proved unreliable. Facing the collapse of his authority in the Balkans, Abdul Hamid II capitulated, announcing on 23–24 July 1908 the restoration of the constitution and the recall of parliament. Elections followed, and the new parliament opened on 17 December 1908 amid widespread public celebration across the empire's religious and ethnic communities.

Aftermath

The revolution did not end instability. In April 1909 the "31 March Incident", a counter-revolutionary mutiny in Constantinople, was suppressed by the Action Army from Salonica; Abdul Hamid II was deposed and replaced by his brother Mehmed V. The CUP, initially a background influence, moved steadily toward direct power, seizing government in a coup in January 1913. Under the triumvirate of Enver, Talaat, and Cemal Pashas, the empire entered the First World War on the side of the Central Powers — a course that led to its dissolution and the eventual proclamation of the Republic of Turkey in 1923.
