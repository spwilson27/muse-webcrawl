Overview

Muay Thai (Thai: มวยไทย), sometimes called Thai boxing, is a Thai martial art and full-contact combat sport that uses stand-up striking, sweeps and clinch fighting. It is known as the "Art of Eight Limbs", since fighters strike with the fists, elbows, knees and shins — eight points of contact, in contrast to the two points (fists) of Western boxing or four points (fists and feet) of kickboxing. Muay Thai is the national sport and cultural martial art of Thailand.

History

The art descends from the battlefield techniques of Siamese soldiers (Muay Boran) and was developed over centuries of warfare with neighbouring kingdoms. Legends celebrate King Naresuan and the fighter Nai Khanom Tom, said to have defeated nine or ten Burmese opponents in succession in 1774. In the early 20th century the sport was modernised: boxing rings, gloves, weight classes and timed rounds replaced open grounds and rope hand-wraps. Bangkok's Rajadamnern (1945) and Lumpinee (1956) stadiums became its spiritual homes, and a gambling-fuelled professional circuit turned fighters — often poor boys who began as children — into national celebrities.

Technique and ritual

Signature techniques include the powerful roundhouse kick thrown with the shin, the straight knee and elbow strikes at close range, the teep (push kick), and the double-collar clinch used to control an opponent for knees. Before a bout, fighters perform the wai kru ram muay, a ritual dance honouring teachers, wearing the sacred mongkol headband and armbands (prajioud), while traditional sarama music plays on oboe and drums — and continues during the fight itself.

Global spread

From the 1970s Muay Thai spread worldwide, influencing kickboxing and later mixed martial arts, where its clinch and leg kicks became standard tools. The International Federation of Muaythai Associations received full IOC recognition in 2021, and the sport has been included as a demonstration or medal event at several multi-sport games.
