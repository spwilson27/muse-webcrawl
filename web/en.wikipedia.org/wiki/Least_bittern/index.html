Least bittern

Scientific nameIxobrychus exilis
FamilyArdeidae (herons)
Length28–36 cm
Conservation statusLeast Concern (IUCN)

The least bittern (Ixobrychus exilis) is one of the smallest herons in the world, found in freshwater and brackish marshes with dense vegetation across the Americas, from Canada to northern Argentina. Because of its secretive habits and camouflaged plumage, it is more often heard — giving soft cooing and clucking calls — than seen.

Description and behaviour

Adults measure about 28–36 cm in length with a wingspan of 41–46 cm. Males have a glossy black crown and back contrasting with pale buff underparts and a rich chestnut neck, while females and immatures are browner and more streaked. Rather than wading in open water like larger herons, the least bittern climbs about in cattails and reeds, gripping stems with its feet and striking at small fish, frogs, insects and crustaceans. When alarmed it freezes with bill pointed skyward, mimicking the reeds around it.

Breeding and status

The nest is a small platform built in dense marsh vegetation above the water, holding typically four or five pale eggs. Northern populations migrate to the southern United States, Mexico and the Caribbean in winter. Although listed as of Least Concern globally, the species has declined in parts of its range because of wetland loss and is considered threatened or endangered in several U.S. states and in Canada.
