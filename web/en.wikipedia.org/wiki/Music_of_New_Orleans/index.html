Music of New Orleans

New Orleans, Louisiana, is one of the most musically influential cities in the world, famed as the birthplace of jazz and a cradle of rhythm and blues, funk, and bounce music. Its music grew out of the meeting of African, Caribbean, French, Spanish, and American traditions.

Origins and jazz

In the 19th century, enslaved Africans gathered in Congo Square to drum and dance, preserving African rhythms. Brass bands, ragtime, and blues fused around 1900 into early jazz, pioneered by Buddy Bolden, Jelly Roll Morton, King Oliver, and Louis Armstrong, who was born in the city in 1901. The Storyville district and riverboats spread the new sound up the Mississippi.

Rhythm and blues and rock and roll

In the 1940s–50s, New Orleans R&B shaped early rock and roll: Fats Domino, Little Richard's sessions at Cosimo Matassa's studio, Professor Longhair, Lloyd Price, and producer Dave Bartholomew defined a rolling piano-and-horn sound. The Meters pioneered funk in the late 1960s, and Allen Toussaint became a central songwriter and producer.

Traditions

The city's culture includes second line parades, jazz funerals, Mardi Gras Indian tribes with elaborate suits and chants, and brass bands such as the Dirty Dozen and Rebirth Brass Band. Institutions like Preservation Hall keep traditional jazz alive.

Modern scene

New Orleans gave rise to bounce music, hip-hop labels Cash Money and No Limit (Lil Wayne, Juvenile, Master P), and contemporary stars such as Trombone Shorty, Jon Batiste, and Harry Connick Jr. The annual New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Festival is among the world's major music festivals.
