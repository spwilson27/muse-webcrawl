Society for Psychical Research


Abbreviation: SPR
Formation: 1882
Type: Learned society
Headquarters: London, United Kingdom

The Society for Psychical Research (SPR) is a nonprofit organisation in the United Kingdom. Its stated purpose is to understand events and abilities commonly described as psychic or paranormal. It is considered the first organisation of its kind, founded in London in 1882 by a group of scholars and scientists including Henry Sidgwick, Frederic W. H. Myers, Edmund Gurney, and William F. Barrett.

History

The Society was founded amid Victorian interest in spiritualism and the scientific investigation of mediumship. Its early members investigated thought-transference (a term coined by Myers, later called telepathy), hypnotism, apparitions, and haunted houses. The Census of Hallucinations (1889–1894) was one of its major early surveys. In 1886, Gurney, Myers, and Frank Podmore published Phantasms of the Living, a landmark collection of cases.

Activities

The SPR publishes the Journal of the Society for Psychical Research and the Paranormal Review magazine, maintains a library and archive, and funds research through grants. Its sister organisation, the American Society for Psychical Research, was founded in 1885. The Society takes no corporate position on the reality of paranormal phenomena, describing itself as pursuing scientific inquiry.

Notable members

Past presidents and members have included physicist Lord Rayleigh, philosopher Henry Sidgwick, psychologist William James, physicist Oliver Lodge, and author Arthur Conan Doyle (who later resigned over disagreements).
