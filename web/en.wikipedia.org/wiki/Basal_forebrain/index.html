Basal forebrain

The basal forebrain is a group of structures located in the forebrain, ventral to the anterior commissure and below the striatum. It includes the nucleus basalis of Meynert, the diagonal band of Broca, the medial septal nucleus, and the substantia innominata. These nuclei are the brain's principal source of cholinergic projections to the cerebral cortex and hippocampus.

Function

Basal forebrain cholinergic neurons play a central role in arousal, attention, learning, and memory, modulating cortical activity during wakefulness and REM sleep. The medial septal nucleus and diagonal band provide cholinergic and GABAergic input to the hippocampus that paces the theta rhythm associated with memory encoding. The region also contains populations of GABAergic and glutamatergic projection neurons that regulate cortical oscillations and sleep–wake states.

Clinical significance

Degeneration of the nucleus basalis of Meynert is a hallmark of Alzheimer's disease and contributes to the cholinergic deficit underlying its memory symptoms; most currently approved symptomatic drugs for Alzheimer's are cholinesterase inhibitors that boost the remaining cholinergic transmission. Basal forebrain damage, for example from rupture of an anterior communicating artery aneurysm, can cause severe amnesia and confabulation.
