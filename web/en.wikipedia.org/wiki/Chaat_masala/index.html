Chaat masala

TypeSpice mix (masala)
RegionIndian subcontinent
Key ingredientsAmchoor (dried mango powder), cumin, black salt, coriander, ginger, chilli
FlavourTangy, sour, salty, pungent

Chaat masala is a powder spice mix, or masala, originating from the Indian subcontinent, used to season chaat and other snacks. It typically consists of amchoor (dried mango powder), cumin, coriander, dried ginger, salt (often black salt, kala namak), black pepper, asafoetida, and chili powder. The blend has a distinctive tangy, sour and slightly sulfurous flavour, the black salt contributing an eggy aroma prized in street food.

Use

Chaat masala is sprinkled over dishes such as aloo chaat, fruit chaat, papri chaat, samosas, pakoras and dahi puri, and over cut fruit and salads; it is also stirred into drinks such as lemonade (shikanji) and lassi, and used to season raitas, grilled corn and sandwiches. Unlike garam masala, it is usually added at the end of cooking or at the table, since its souring agents do not require heat.

Variations

Recipes vary by household and manufacturer: mint powder, ajwain (carom seed), pomegranate seed (anardana) and citric acid may be added, and commercial brands sell ready-ground chaat masala throughout South Asia and the diaspora. Fruit chaat versions tend to be milder and sweeter. The name derives from chaat, savoury snacks — itself from a word meaning "to lick" — evoking food tasty enough to lick from one's fingers.
