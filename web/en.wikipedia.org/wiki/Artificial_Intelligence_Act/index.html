Artificial Intelligence Act

The AI Act is the first comprehensive legal framework on AI by a major regulator

The Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), commonly called the EU AI Act, is a European Union regulation on artificial intelligence. Proposed by the European Commission in April 2021 and adopted in 2024, it is the first comprehensive horizontal legal framework for AI anywhere in the world. It entered into force on 1 August 2024, with most obligations applying in stages through 2026 and 2027.

Risk-based approach

The Act classifies AI applications into four risk levels:

  • Unacceptable risk — prohibited practices, including social scoring by public authorities, manipulative AI, and most real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces.
  • High risk — systems used in areas such as critical infrastructure, education, employment, law enforcement and medical devices, subject to conformity assessments, human oversight and documentation duties.
  • Limited risk — systems subject mainly to transparency obligations, such as chatbots and deepfake generators.
  • Minimal risk — most other systems, largely unregulated.

General-purpose AI models, especially those posing systemic risk because of very large training compute (above 1025 FLOPs), face additional evaluation and reporting duties. Fines can reach 7% of global turnover for the gravest breaches. The Act is administered with the support of a new European AI Office.
