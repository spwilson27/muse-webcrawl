Karl Marx-Hof

The Karl Marx-Hof is a municipal housing complex (Gemeindebau) in Heiligenstadt, in Vienna's 19th district of Döbling. Built between 1927 and 1930 to designs by the architect Karl Ehn, a pupil of Otto Wagner, it is one of the most famous examples of the social housing programme of "Red Vienna" and, at over a kilometre in length, one of the longest residential buildings in the world.

Description

The complex contains 1,382 apartments housing some 5,000 residents. Alongside the dwellings, it provided extensive communal facilities unusual for working-class housing of the era: laundries, baths, kindergartens, a library, doctors' and dentists' surgeries, a post office, and shops, arranged around large green courtyards. Only about 20 per cent of the site was built upon, the rest being gardens and playgrounds.

History

During the Austrian Civil War of February 1934, the Karl Marx-Hof became a stronghold of Social Democratic resistance to the Dollfuss government and was shelled by artillery before its defenders surrendered. After the war it was renovated; in the 1950s it was briefly renamed Heiligenstädter Hof under the restored conservative administration before reverting to its original name. Today it remains municipal housing and an architectural monument of interwar Vienna.
