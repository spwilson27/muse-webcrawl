Pica (disorder)

SpecialtyPsychiatry, paediatrics
SymptomsPersistent eating of non-nutritive substances
Named afterPica pica, the Eurasian magpie

Pica is a psychological disorder characterised by an appetite for substances that are largely non-nutritive, such as ice (pagophagia), hair (trichophagia), paper (xylophagia), drywall, soil (geophagia), chalk, faeces (coprophagia), soap, or metal. The name derives from the Latin word for the Eurasian magpie (Pica pica), a bird famed for its unusual eating behaviours.

Diagnosis

According to the DSM-5, pica is diagnosed when the eating of non-nutritive, non-food substances persists for at least one month, is inappropriate to the developmental level of the individual, and is not part of a culturally supported or socially normative practice. Mouthing of objects is normal in children under two years, so the diagnosis generally requires the individual to be at least two years old.

Causes and associations

Pica is most commonly seen in children, pregnant women, and people with developmental disabilities such as autism spectrum disorder and intellectual disability. It is associated with iron-deficiency anaemia and other mineral deficiencies, and in some cases treating the deficiency resolves the craving. Certain forms, such as geophagia (earth-eating), are culturally accepted practices in parts of Africa and elsewhere.

Complications and treatment

Complications can include lead poisoning (from paint or soil), parasitic infections, intestinal obstruction, dental damage and poisoning. Treatment typically involves correcting nutritional deficiencies, behavioural therapy, and addressing underlying psychiatric or developmental conditions.
