Northern Sámi

Northern Sámi (Northern Sámi: davvisámegiella) is the most widely spoken of the Sámi languages, with roughly 20,000–25,000 speakers in the northern parts of Norway, Sweden and Finland. It belongs to the Western Sámi group of the Uralic language family.

Geographic distribution

Northern Sámi is spoken across the Sápmi region, with concentrations in the Norwegian municipalities of Kautokeino (Guovdageaidnu) and Karasjok (Kárášjohka), the Finnish municipality of Utsjoki (Ohcejohka) and northern Sweden including Kiruna (Giron). It serves as a common written language for Sámi in all three countries and is used in education, media — including NRK Sápmi broadcasts — and official administration in designated language districts.

Grammar

Like other Sámi languages, Northern Sámi is agglutinative, with three numbers (singular, dual and plural), seven cases, and a complex system of consonant gradation and vowel alternations affecting word stems. Verbs conjugate for person, number, tense and mood, and negation is expressed with a negative auxiliary verb. Word order is typically subject–verb–object.

Writing system

Northern Sámi is written in a Latin-based alphabet, whose modern pan-national orthography was adopted in 1979 in Norway and Sweden and 1978 in Finland. The alphabet includes special letters such as á, č, đ, ŋ, š, ŧ and ž. Earlier missionary orthographies, dating to the 18th and 19th centuries, differed by country.

Status

Decades of assimilation policies ("Norwegianisation" and similar programmes) drastically reduced the number of speakers during the 20th century. Since the 1970s, language revitalisation efforts, the establishment of Sámi parliaments and legal protections have strengthened Northern Sámi, though it remains classified as endangered.
