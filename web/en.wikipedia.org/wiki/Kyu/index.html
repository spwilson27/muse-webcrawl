Kyū

Kyū (Japanese: 級, pronounced [kʲɯː]) is a Japanese term used in modern martial arts (budō), in chadō (tea ceremony), flower arranging, go, shogi, academic tests and other similar activities to designate various grades or levels of proficiency below the master or dan grades. Kyū grades are usually numbered in descending order: a beginner may start at a higher-numbered grade such as 10th or 6th kyū and progress upward toward 1st kyū, after which the student becomes eligible for the dan ranks beginning with shodan (1st dan).

Martial arts

The kyū/dan ranking system was adapted from the game of go and introduced into judo by its founder, Kanō Jigorō, in the 1880s; from judo it spread to karate, aikido, kendo, jujutsu and many other arts, and thence worldwide. In many styles, kyū students wear coloured belts, with the specific colour sequence varying by art and federation, while in others all kyū students wear white belts and dan holders black. The term for a practitioner below dan grade is mudansha, as opposed to yūdansha for dan holders. Some organisations use junior grades below the regular kyū numbering for children.

Other uses

In go and shogi, amateurs are ranked by kyū below the dan scale, with professional ranks separate. Japanese language and other proficiency examinations, abacus (soroban) certification, and traditional arts such as tea ceremony and ikebana likewise use kyū-style gradings, reflecting a broadly shared Japanese convention of numbered pre-mastery levels.
