Brilliant Pebbles

Brilliant Pebbles was a ballistic missile defence concept developed as part of the United States Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI) in the late 1980s. It proposed placing thousands of small, autonomous space-based kinetic interceptors in low Earth orbit, which would detect Soviet intercontinental ballistic missiles and destroy them by direct collision during their boost and midcourse phases.

Concept

The idea originated at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, where physicist Lowell Wood championed a design for lightweight interceptors — each only a few kilograms, equipped with sensors, a computer, and small rocket thrusters — housed in protective orbiting "life jackets". Because each pebble could operate autonomously, the system avoided dependence on large, vulnerable battle-management satellites. Advocates estimated a constellation of several thousand interceptors could be deployed at a cost far below earlier SDI architectures.

History

Brilliant Pebbles became the centrepiece of the Global Protection Against Limited Strikes (GPALS) plan adopted under President George H. W. Bush, which reoriented SDI towards defending against limited or accidental launches. A flight experiment, using related sensor technology, flew in the early 1990s. With the end of the Cold War the programme was cancelled in 1993, although its interceptor and sensor concepts influenced later ground-based midcourse defence systems.
