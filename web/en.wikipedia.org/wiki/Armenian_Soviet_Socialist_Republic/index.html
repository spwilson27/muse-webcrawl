The Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic, also known as Soviet Armenia, was one of the constituent republics of the Soviet Union. It existed from 1920 until 1991, when Armenia declared its independence during the dissolution of the USSR.

History

Armenia was sovietised in late 1920 after the Red Army entered the country, ending the short-lived First Republic of Armenia. From 1922 to 1936 the Armenian SSR formed part of the Transcaucasian SFSR together with Georgia and Azerbaijan, before becoming a separate union republic of the USSR in 1936.

The republic industrialised rapidly during the Soviet era, developing chemical, electronics and machine-building industries, while Yerevan grew into a major city. Armenia was also affected by the devastating Spitak earthquake of December 1988, which killed tens of thousands and severely damaged the north of the country.

Geography

The Armenian SSR was a landlocked republic in the Lesser Caucasus, bordering Turkey, Iran, and the Azerbaijani and Georgian SSRs. Its territory included Mount Aragats, the highest peak of modern Armenia, and Lake Sevan, one of the largest high-altitude lakes in Eurasia.

Demographics

By 1989 the population was about 3.29 million, predominantly ethnic Armenians, with minorities including Azerbaijanis, Russians, Kurds and Greeks. The late 1980s saw growing tensions over Nagorno-Karabakh, an Armenian-majority autonomous oblast within Azerbaijan, which became a central issue of the Karabakh movement.
