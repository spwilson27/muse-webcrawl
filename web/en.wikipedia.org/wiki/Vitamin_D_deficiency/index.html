Vitamin D deficiency

Vitamin D deficiency is a low level of vitamin D in the body, most commonly defined by a blood 25-hydroxyvitamin D concentration below thresholds around 20–30 ng/mL, though exact cut-offs remain debated. It results from inadequate sunlight exposure, low dietary intake, malabsorption, or disorders of metabolism.

Effects

Vitamin D is essential for calcium absorption and bone mineralisation. Severe deficiency causes rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults, and contributes to osteoporosis. Mild deficiency is extremely common worldwide, especially at high latitudes, in winter, and in people with darker skin or limited sun exposure.

Treatment and prevention

Management involves sensible sun exposure, dietary sources such as oily fish and fortified foods, and oral vitamin D2 or D3 supplements. Many health authorities recommend routine supplementation for at-risk groups, particularly during autumn and winter.
