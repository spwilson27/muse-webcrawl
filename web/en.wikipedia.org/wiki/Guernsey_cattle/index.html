Guernsey cattle

The Guernsey is a breed of dairy cattle from the island of Guernsey in the Channel Islands. It is renowned for its rich, golden-yellow milk, which is high in butterfat and beta-carotene, and for its docile temperament and efficient conversion of pasture to milk.

The breed was developed over centuries on the island, with influences traditionally attributed to Norman cattle from Brittany and Normandy. A ban on imported cattle from 1819 helped fix the island breed's characteristics. Guernseys were exported widely, particularly to the United States, Britain and Commonwealth countries, in the late 19th and 20th centuries.

Guernsey cows are fawn or red and white in colour and of medium size. Their milk's distinctive colour and high proportions of A2 beta-casein have been used in marketing "Golden Guernsey" dairy products. Numbers declined in the late 20th century as the higher-yielding Holstein became dominant, and the breed is now monitored on rare-breed watchlists in some countries.
