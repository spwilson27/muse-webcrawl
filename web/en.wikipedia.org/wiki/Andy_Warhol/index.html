Andy Warhol

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Andy Warhol in the 1960s: silver wig, dark glasses, black clothes, holding a camera]

Andy Warhol (born Andrew Warhola; 6 August 1928 – 22 February 1987) was an American visual artist, film director and producer. A leading figure in the Pop art movement, he explored the relationship between artistic expression, advertising and celebrity culture that flourished by the 1960s. His New York studio, The Factory, became a famous gathering place for artists, musicians and socialites.

After a successful career as a commercial illustrator, Warhol rose to fame with his silkscreen paintings of consumer products and celebrities: the Campbell's Soup Cans (1962), Marilyn Diptych (1962) and portraits of Elvis Presley, Elizabeth Taylor and Mao Zedong. He produced experimental films such as Sleep and Chelsea Girls, managed the Velvet Underground, founded Interview magazine and coined the expression '15 minutes of fame'.

Death and legacy

Warhol survived an assassination attempt by Valerie Solanas in 1968 and died in 1987 following gallbladder surgery. The Andy Warhol Museum in his native Pittsburgh is the largest museum in the United States dedicated to a single artist, and his work commands some of the highest prices in the art market.
