Permutahedron

In mathematics, the permutahedron of order n is an (n − 1)-dimensional polytope embedded in n-dimensional space, whose vertices are formed by permuting the coordinates of the vector (1, 2, 3, …, n). It therefore has n! vertices, one for each permutation of the numbers 1 through n.

Examples

The permutahedron of order 3 is a regular hexagon in the plane x + y + z = 6. The permutahedron of order 4 is the truncated octahedron, an Archimedean solid with 24 vertices, 6 square faces, and 8 regular hexagonal faces; it tiles three-dimensional space by translation.

Properties

Two vertices are joined by an edge when the corresponding permutations differ by swapping two adjacent values. The facets correspond to the nonempty proper subsets of the coordinate set. Permutahedra are zonotopes and appear in combinatorics, optimization, and the study of sorting networks; their generalizations, called generalized permutahedra, include the associahedron and many other polytopes central to algebraic combinatorics.
