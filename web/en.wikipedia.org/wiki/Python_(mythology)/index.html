Python (mythology)

In Greek mythology, Python (Greek: Πύθων) was a monstrous serpent or dragon that lived at Delphi, in the centre of the world as the Greeks conceived it, guarding the oracle there. Python was the offspring of Gaia (Earth), born from the mud left after the great flood, and was variously said to have been sent by Hera to persecute Leto, the mother of Apollo and Artemis.

Apollo's victory

The infant god Apollo, only days old, tracked Python to its lair at Delphi and slew it with his arrows. Because he had shed the blood of a child of Gaia, Apollo was obliged to perform penance and founded the Pythian Games to atone. Thereafter Apollo took possession of the oracle; his priestess at Delphi was called the Pythia, a name derived from Python — its root, pythein, "to rot", recalled the serpent's rotting corpse. The Homeric Hymn to Apollo is the earliest surviving account of the myth.

Legacy

The myth gave English the word "python" for the great constricting snakes, the genus Python being named for the legendary serpent. The story exemplifies the Olympian gods' triumph over older chthonic powers of the earth.
