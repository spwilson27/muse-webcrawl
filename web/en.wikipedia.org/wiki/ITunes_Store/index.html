iTunes Store

The iTunes Store is a digital media store operated by Apple Inc. It opened on April 28, 2003, as the iTunes Music Store, and as of April 2020 operated in 167 countries. The store was the centerpiece of Steve Jobs's push to make legal digital music sales simple: songs were originally priced at 99 cents each, protected by the FairPlay digital rights management system, and integrated with the iPod.

History

The store launched with 200,000 songs from the five major record labels and sold one million songs in its first week. It quickly became the largest music retailer in the United States, overtaking Walmart in 2008. The catalogue expanded beyond music to podcasts (2005), movies and television shows (2005–2006), audiobooks, and apps were sold through the related App Store from 2008. DRM was removed from music downloads between 2007 and 2009 under the "iTunes Plus" programme, with variable pricing introduced at the same time.

Decline of downloads

With the rise of streaming, sales of downloads fell sharply. Apple launched its streaming service, Apple Music, in 2015, and in 2019 split the iTunes application on macOS into separate Music, TV, and Podcasts apps, though the iTunes Store continues to sell music, films, and television programmes.
