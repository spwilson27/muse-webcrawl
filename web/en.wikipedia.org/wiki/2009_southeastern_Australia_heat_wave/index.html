The 2009 southeastern Australia heat wave was an extreme heat event that affected south-eastern Australia in late January and early February 2009. A slow-moving high-pressure system over the Tasman Sea drew hot continental air across Victoria and South Australia for nearly two weeks, producing some of the highest temperatures ever recorded in the region.

Records

Melbourne recorded three consecutive days above 43 °C in late January, unprecedented in its records, and then 46.4 °C on Saturday 7 February 2009 — its hottest day ever. Adelaide endured a prolonged spell above 35 °C. Hopetoun, in Victoria's Mallee, reached 48.8 °C, the highest temperature recorded in Victoria.

Impacts

Heat-related illness caused an estimated several hundred excess deaths, mostly among the elderly. Electricity networks failed under air-conditioning demand, leaving hundreds of thousands of Melbourne homes without power, and rail lines buckled, cancelling trains. The Australian Open tennis tournament in Melbourne was disrupted, with play suspended under its extreme heat policy.

Black Saturday

The heat wave culminated on 7 February 2009 — "Black Saturday" — when extreme heat, drought and strong winds produced catastrophic bushfires in Victoria that killed 173 people, the deadliest bushfire disaster in Australian history. The heat wave broke soon afterwards with a cool change.
