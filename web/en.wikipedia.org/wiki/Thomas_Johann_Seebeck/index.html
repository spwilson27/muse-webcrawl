Thomas Johann Seebeck (9 April 1770 – 10 December 1831) was a German physicist who in 1821 discovered the thermoelectric effect, now called the Seebeck effect.

Life

Born into a wealthy merchant family in Reval, Seebeck studied medicine at Göttingen but turned to physics, working in Jena in the circle of Goethe—whose theory of colours he supported—and later in Berlin, where he was elected to the Prussian Academy of Sciences.

Thermoelectricity

In 1821 Seebeck found that a circuit of two dissimilar metals deflects a compass needle when the junctions are at different temperatures. He interpreted it as thermomagnetism; it was soon understood as an electric current generated by a temperature difference—the principle of the thermocouple and of thermoelectric generators. Combined with the later Peltier and Thomson effects, it forms the basis of thermoelectricity.

Other work

Seebeck also worked on optics, photoelasticity (polarisation patterns in stressed glass), and the action of light on silver salts.
