Discipline and Punish

Discipline and Punish: The Birth of the Prison (French: Surveiller et punir: Naissance de la prison) is a 1975 book by French philosopher Michel Foucault. It is an analysis of the social and theoretical mechanisms behind the changes that occurred in Western penal systems during the modern age, based on historical documents from France.

Discipline and Punish
Author: Michel Foucault
Published: 1975 (French), 1977 (English)
Subject: Prisons, power, discipline

Summary

Foucault argues that prison did not simply replace earlier punishment but that the whole economy of punishment changed: from public torture of the body — the book opens with the gruesome 1757 execution of regicide Robert-François Damiens — to the disciplined, surveilled soul, illustrated by a prison timetable from 1838.

Main ideas

  • Torture — sovereign power displayed on the condemned body in public spectacle.
  • Punishment — reformers sought humane, representative penalties.
  • Discipline — techniques of training bodies through timetables, drills and examinations in prisons, schools, barracks, hospitals and factories.
  • The Panopticon — Jeremy Bentham's prison design in which inmates, never knowing whether they are watched, internalise surveillance; Foucault generalises it into "panopticism" as a model of modern disciplinary power.
  • The prison — the carceral system produces delinquency even as it claims to reform.

Influence

The book is one of Foucault's most influential works, shaping criminology, sociology, surveillance studies and critical theory. Critics have questioned its historical accuracy and its functionalist account of power.
