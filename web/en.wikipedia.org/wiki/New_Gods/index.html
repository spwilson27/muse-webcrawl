The New Gods are a fictional race of deities appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. Created by writer-artist Jack Kirby, they debuted in 1971 as part of Kirby's 'Fourth World' saga, centred on the twin planets New Genesis and Apokolips.

Publication history

After leaving Marvel Comics, Jack Kirby came to DC in 1970 with an interlocking epic told across four titles: The New Gods, Mister Miracle, The Forever People and Superman's Pal Jimmy Olsen. Kirby conceived the saga as a finite mythological story about a war between good and evil gods following the death of the old gods of Norse-like legend.

The original titles were cancelled in the early 1970s, but the characters — above all the tyrant Darkseid — became central to the DC Universe and have been revived many times.

Fictional history

The New Gods inhabit two contrasting worlds: idyllic New Genesis, ruled by the benevolent Highfather, and the hellish industrial planet Apokolips, ruled by Darkseid, who seeks the Anti-Life Equation that would allow him to control all living beings.

To stave off war, Highfather and Darkseid exchanged infant sons: Orion, son of Darkseid, was raised on New Genesis, while Scott Free, son of Highfather, was raised in Apokolips's orphanages and became the escape artist Mister Miracle. The New Gods draw power from the cosmic energy known as the Source.

In other media

New Gods characters have appeared in animated series such as Superman: The Animated Series and Justice League, and Darkseid appeared in the film Zack Snyder's Justice League (2021). A New Gods feature film was in development at Warner Bros. for several years.
