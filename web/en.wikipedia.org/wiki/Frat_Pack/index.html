Frat Pack

The Frat Pack is a nickname given to a group of American comedy actors who have appeared together in many of the highest-grossing comedy films since the mid-1990s and 2000s. The term, coined by USA Today in 2004 as a play on Frank Sinatra's "Rat Pack" and the "Brat Pack" of the 1980s, refers to the actors' frequent collaborations and the fraternity-house humor of their films.

Members

The core members are generally considered to be Ben Stiller, Owen Wilson, Luke Wilson, Will Ferrell, Steve Carell, Jack Black, and Vince Vaughn. Frequent associates include Paul Rudd, David Koechner, and directors such as Judd Apatow, Todd Phillips, and Adam McKay.

Films

Signature Frat Pack films include Old School (2003), Anchorman: The Legend of Ron Burgundy (2004), Dodgeball: A True Underdog Story (2004), Wedding Crashers (2005), Talladega Nights: The Ballad of Ricky Bobby (2006), Zoolander (2001), Starsky & Hutch (2004), and Step Brothers (2008). The group helped define mainstream American screen comedy in the 2000s, with members often making cameo appearances in one another's films.
