Tribulation

In Christian eschatology, the Tribulation is a period of great suffering and upheaval believed to precede the Second Coming of Jesus Christ. The concept draws chiefly on the Book of Daniel, the Olivet Discourse in the Gospels (Matthew 24, Mark 13, Luke 21), and the Book of Revelation. Jesus is quoted as saying there would be "great tribulation, such as was not since the beginning of the world" (Matthew 24:21, KJV).

Interpretations

Interpretations vary widely. Dispensationalists commonly teach a future seven-year period based on Daniel's "seventieth week" (Daniel 9:24–27), during which the Antichrist rules and divine judgments fall on the earth; within this school, views differ on whether the Rapture of the church occurs before (pretribulationism), during (midtribulationism), or after (posttribulationism) the Tribulation. Preterists hold that most of the prophecies were fulfilled in the first century, especially with the destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70. Historicist and idealist readings treat the Tribulation as symbolising the church's long-running struggles or timeless spiritual conflict.

The "Great Tribulation"

Revelation 7:14 speaks of a great multitude who "came out of great tribulation", and some systems distinguish an especially severe second half of the seven years, associated with the "time of Jacob's trouble" (Jeremiah 30:7) and the abomination of desolation.
