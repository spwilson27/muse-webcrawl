Boat lift

A boat lift, also called a ship lift or lift lock, is a machine for transporting boats between water at two different elevations. It is an alternative to the canal lock. Boat lifts may be vertical, like the Falkirk Wheel in Scotland, or an inclined plane, like the Ronquières inclined plane in Belgium.

History

The earliest boat lifts appeared in the late 18th century, when engineers sought faster ways to move barges over large height differences than flights of locks allowed. The Strépy-Thieu boat lift in Belgium, one of the largest in the world, overcomes a 73.15 m difference. China's Three Gorges Dam ship lift is the largest by capacity, able to raise vessels of 3,000 tonnes through 113 m.

Types

Lifts are classified as wet (the boat floats in a water-filled caisson) or dry (the boat rests on a cradle). Vertical lifts, rotating lifts, floats and inclined planes are all in use on canals and rivers worldwide.
