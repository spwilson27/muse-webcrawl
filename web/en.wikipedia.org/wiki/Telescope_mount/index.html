Telescope mount

A telescope mount is a mechanical structure which supports a telescope. Telescope mounts are designed to support the mass of the instrument and allow for accurate pointing and tracking of the telescope. Over the years many types of mount have been developed, with the majority of effort being put into systems that can track the motion of the fixed stars as the Earth rotates.

Fixed mounts

The simplest mounts do not move at all: the telescope is fixed in place, as in transit instruments and zenith telescopes that observe objects only as they cross the meridian.

Altazimuth mounts

An altazimuth mount moves the telescope in altitude (up and down) and azimuth (left and right). It is mechanically simple and sturdy, and is used by most very large professional telescopes, where computer control rotates both axes at varying rates to track the sky; the field of view rotates during tracking, which is corrected with an instrument rotator. The Dobsonian mount is a simple, inexpensive altazimuth design popular with amateur astronomers.

Equatorial mounts

An equatorial mount has one axis (the polar axis) aligned parallel to the Earth's axis of rotation. Rotating the telescope about this single axis at the sidereal rate compensates for the Earth's rotation, so tracking requires motion in one axis only and the field of view does not rotate. Variants include the German equatorial mount, in which the telescope sits on a declination axis offset from the polar axis with a counterweight, and the fork mount.
