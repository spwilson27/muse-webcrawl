The Fontana dei Quattro Fiumi (Fountain of the Four Rivers) is a monumental fountain in the Piazza Navona in Rome, Italy. It was designed in 1651 by the Baroque sculptor Gian Lorenzo Bernini for Pope Innocent X, whose family palace, the Palazzo Pamphili, faced the square. It is considered one of the masterpieces of Roman Baroque sculpture.

Description

The fountain supports an ancient Egyptian obelisk, surmounted by the Pamphili family emblem of a dove bearing an olive twig. At its base, four colossal marble river gods sit on a rocky travertine outcrop rising from the basin, representing the four great rivers of the four continents then known to Europeans:

  • The Danube, representing Europe, touching the Pope's coat of arms;
  • The Ganges, representing Asia, holding a long oar;
  • The Nile, representing Africa, with its head veiled, symbolising that the river's source was then unknown;
  • The Río de la Plata, representing the Americas, seated on a pile of coins symbolising the wealth of the New World, and recoiling from a serpent.

Around the figures are carved plants, animals and a sea monster, giving the base the character of a fragment of wild nature set into the city.

History

Pope Innocent X commissioned the fountain as the centrepiece of his embellishment of Piazza Navona. Bernini, initially out of favour with the Pope, famously won the commission when a model of his design was placed where Innocent would see it. The fountain was unveiled in June 1651 to public celebration. A popular legend claims the Nile figure's veil and the Plata figure's raised arm express Bernini's contempt for the church of Sant'Agnese in Agone, designed by his rival Francesco Borromini, facing the fountain — although the church's facade was built after the fountain, making the story chronologically impossible.
