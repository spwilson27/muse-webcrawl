The 1992 Los Angeles riots were a series of riots and civil disturbances that occurred in Los Angeles County, California, in April and May 1992. The unrest began in South Central Los Angeles on 29 April, after a jury in Simi Valley acquitted four Los Angeles Police Department officers charged with using excessive force in the videotaped arrest and beating of Rodney King. The riots were the largest in the United States since the 1960s and the deadliest since the 1863 New York draft riots.

Background

King had been beaten by officers after a high-speed chase on 3 March 1991; the beating, filmed by plumber George Holliday, became a national scandal. Racial tensions in Los Angeles were further inflamed by the killing of 15-year-old Latasha Harlins by shopkeeper Soon Ja Du — who received probation — and by long-standing grievances over policing, unemployment and disinvestment in South Central. The 1991 Christopher Commission had already documented patterns of excessive force within the LAPD under Chief Daryl Gates.

Course of the unrest

Violence erupted within hours of the verdict, beginning at the intersection of Florence and Normandie Avenues, where truck driver Reginald Denny was pulled from his cab and beaten — an attack broadcast live from news helicopters. Arson and looting spread across the city, and Korean-owned businesses were heavily targeted. The LAPD's slow, uncoordinated response drew lasting criticism. Order was restored after a citywide curfew, the deployment of thousands of California Army National Guard troops, federal soldiers and Marines, and mass arrests. The riots ended by 4 May; 63 people had been killed and damage was estimated at around $1 billion.

Aftermath

Two officers, Stacey Koon and Laurence Powell, were later convicted in a federal civil-rights trial and sentenced to 30 months in prison. Chief Gates resigned in 1992. The riots reshaped debate about policing, race and urban policy in the United States, and the rebuilding programme Rebuild LA sought — with mixed success — to revive the devastated neighbourhoods.
