Three-language formula

The three-language formula is a language education policy of the Government of India, first formulated in the National Policy on Education of 1968, intended to promote multilingualism and national integration in a country of immense linguistic diversity.

The formula

Under the formula, students in Hindi-speaking states study (1) Hindi, (2) English, and (3) a modern Indian language, preferably a South Indian language. Students in non-Hindi-speaking states study (1) their regional language, (2) Hindi, and (3) English. The policy sought reciprocity: Hindi-speaking students would learn another Indian language just as non-Hindi students learned Hindi.

Implementation and criticism

Implementation has been uneven. Many Hindi-speaking states teach Sanskrit as the third language rather than a South Indian language, while Tamil Nadu has consistently rejected the formula, following a two-language policy of Tamil and English after the anti-Hindi agitations of the 1960s. Critics cite shortages of qualified teachers and the burden placed on students; supporters regard the scheme as essential to inter-state communication.

Later policy

The National Education Policy 2020 retained the three-language approach but added flexibility, stating that no language would be imposed on any state and that the languages chosen would be decided by states, regions and students, provided at least two of the three are native to India.
