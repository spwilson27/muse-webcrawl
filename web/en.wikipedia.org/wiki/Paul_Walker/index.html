Paul William Walker IV (September 12, 1973 – November 30, 2013) was an American actor. He was best known for his role as Brian O'Conner in the Fast & Furious franchise. Walker began his career as a child actor in the 1980s, gaining recognition in the 1990s after appearing in the television soap opera The Young and the Restless. He received praise for his performances in the teen comedy She's All That and the comedy-drama Varsity Blues (both 1999), and rose to international fame by starring in The Fast and the Furious (2001).

Early life

Walker was born in Glendale, California, the son of Cheryl, a fashion model, and Paul William Walker III, a sewer contractor and boxer who was a two-time Golden Gloves champion. He was raised in the Sunland community of Los Angeles and attended Village Christian School. He began acting as a toddler in television commercials and appeared in shows such as Highway to Heaven and Who's the Boss? before moving into film roles as a teenager.

Career

After supporting roles in Pleasantville (1998) and Varsity Blues (1999), Walker headlined the street-racing film The Fast and the Furious (2001) alongside Vin Diesel; its success launched a long-running franchise in which Walker's undercover-cop-turned-outlaw Brian O'Conner became a central figure. He reprised the role in most of the sequels. Outside the franchise, he starred in films including Joy Ride (2001), Into the Blue (2005), Eight Below (2006), Running Scared (2006), Clint Eastwood's Flags of Our Fathers (2006), and Takers (2010).

Personal life and death

Walker had a keen interest in marine biology and motor racing, competed in racing series, and founded the disaster-relief charity Reach Out Worldwide. On November 30, 2013, he died as a passenger in a single-car crash in Santa Clarita, California, when the Porsche Carrera GT driven by his friend Roger Rodas struck a concrete lamp post and trees and burst into flames. He was 40. Production of Furious 7 (2015) was completed using his brother Cody as a stand-in and computer-generated imagery, and the film was dedicated to his memory.
