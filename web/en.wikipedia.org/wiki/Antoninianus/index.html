Antoninianus

The antoninianus was a coin used during the Roman Empire, thought to have been valued at 2 denarii. It was introduced by the emperor Caracalla (whose full name was Marcus Aurelius Antoninus) in early 215 AD. The coin featured the emperor wearing a radiate crown, indicating its double-denarius value, while empresses were shown with the bust resting on a crescent.

History and debasement

Although tariffed at two denarii, the antoninianus initially contained only about 1.5 denarii's worth of silver. Over the 3rd century it was progressively debased: by the reign of Gallienus in the 260s it had become essentially a bronze coin with a thin silver wash, containing as little as 2% silver, while inflation rendered the denarius obsolete. The antoninianus is thus a physical record of the Crisis of the Third Century.

Reform

Aurelian reformed the coinage around 274 AD, issuing antoniniani of more consistent fineness — many marked "XXI" or "KA", interpreted as indicating a ratio of 20 parts copper to 1 of silver. The denomination finally disappeared in Diocletian's currency reform of the 290s, which introduced the argenteus and the follis.
