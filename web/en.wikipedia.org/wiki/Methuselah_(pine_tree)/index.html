Methuselah is a Great Basin bristlecone pine (Pinus longaeva) growing high in the White Mountains of Inyo County, eastern California. Long regarded as the world's oldest known living non-clonal organism, it germinated around 2833 BCE, making it more than 4,800 years old. The tree was dated by dendrochronologist Edmund Schulman in 1957 using core samples counted by Tom Harlan; it is named after the biblical patriarch Methuselah, said to have lived 969 years.

Location and protection

Methuselah grows at roughly 2,900–3,000 metres (9,500–9,800 ft) elevation in the Ancient Bristlecone Pine Forest of Inyo National Forest. Its exact location is kept secret by the U.S. Forest Service to protect it from vandalism; the Methuselah Trail in the Schulman Grove passes among other ancient pines, and visitors are not told which tree is Methuselah. The harsh, dry, high-altitude environment and dolomitic soil slow the trees' growth and decay, contributing to their extreme longevity.

Age records

An older bristlecone, Prometheus, was cut down in 1964 and found to be about 4,900 years old. In 2012 another bristlecone in the same region was reported to be about 5,060 years old, which would surpass Methuselah, though Methuselah remains the most famous and the best documented of the ancient trees for much of the record's history.
