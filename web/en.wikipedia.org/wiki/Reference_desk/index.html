Reference desk

Type: Library service point
Staffed by: Reference librarians
Services: Research help, information queries

A reference desk or information desk is a public service counter in a library or archive where professional librarians — reference librarians — provide assistance to users seeking information. It is one of the traditional focal points of public and academic library services.

Services

Staff at a reference desk help users locate books and other materials, answer factual and research questions, demonstrate the use of catalogues and databases, and provide guidance on citation and research strategy. In academic libraries, reference services often include scheduled one-to-one research consultations and instruction sessions.

History

Formal reference services developed in American public libraries in the late nineteenth century; Samuel Swett Green of the Worcester Free Public Library is often credited with articulating the philosophy of personal assistance to readers in 1876. The physical desk became a standard feature of library design through the twentieth century.

Modern developments

Reference services have expanded beyond the physical desk to include telephone, e-mail, chat and video consultations, collectively called virtual reference. Many libraries have merged reference and circulation points into a single service desk, and roving reference staff assist users throughout the building.
