Japanese Black

The Japanese Black (Japanese: 黒毛和種, Kuroge Washu) is a Japanese breed of beef cattle. It is one of six native Japanese cattle breeds, and one of the four breeds known as wagyū; it is the most numerous of them, accounting for over 90% of all wagyū cattle.

History

Cattle were brought to Japan from Korea from about the second century AD for use as draught animals. In the Meiji era, after 1868, regional native stocks were crossbred with imported European breeds — including Brown Swiss, Devon, Shorthorn, Simmental, Ayrshire and Holstein — but the crossbreeding was judged unsuccessful and was halted in 1912; the resulting populations were selectively bred as pure native stock. Breeding was closed in 1944 when the Japanese Black was recognised as a distinct breed. Regional strains include the Tajima of Hyōgo Prefecture, source of the famed Kobe beef.

Characteristics

Japanese Black cattle are solid black, of medium size, and are valued for the intense intramuscular fat (marbling) of their meat, which gives wagyū beef its tenderness and flavour. The breed is also exported, and much "wagyu" production abroad descends from Japanese Black animals exported in the 1990s or from crosses with them.
