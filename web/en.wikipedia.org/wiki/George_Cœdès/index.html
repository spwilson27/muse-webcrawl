George Cœdès

George Cœdès (10 August 1886 – 2 October 1969) was a French scholar of Southeast Asian archaeology and history. He directed the École française d'Extrême-Orient (EFEO) and deciphered many Old Khmer and Sanskrit inscriptions of the region.

George Cœdès
Born: 10 August 1886, Paris, France
Died: 2 October 1969, Neuilly-sur-Seine
Fields: Southeast Asian history, epigraphy

Career

Born in Paris, the son of bankers of Hungarian Jewish descent, Cœdès went to Indochina in 1911, joined the EFEO, and worked in Siam (Thailand) helping organise its National Library and archaeological service before directing the EFEO in Hanoi from 1929 to 1947, and also writing on Siamese literature and Thai chronicles.

Scholarship

Cœdès defined the concept of the "Indianized states" of Southeast Asia in Les États hindouisés d'Indochine et d'Indonésie (1944; English ed. The Indianized States of Southeast Asia, 1968), showing how Indian religion, kingship and Sanskrit shaped Funan, Chenla, Angkor, Srivijaya and other polities while local cultures adapted them. His posthumous synthesis The Making of South East Asia (1966) remains influential. He also identified Srivijaya's likely centre near Palembang from inscriptions.

Legacy

Cœdès's epigraphic corpus and framework dominated the field for decades; later scholars have revised his Indianisation emphasis toward local agency, but his editions of inscriptions remain foundational.
