A customs broker is a private individual, partnership, association, or corporation licensed and regulated by a national customs authority to act on behalf of importers and exporters in their dealings with customs. Brokers prepare and transmit the information and payments required to clear goods through customs, including tariff classification, valuation, origin determination, and the filing of entry documents.

Role

Moving goods across borders involves detailed compliance work: classifying products under the Harmonized System tariff schedule, calculating duties and taxes, claiming preferential treatment under trade agreements, and satisfying the requirements of other government agencies that regulate food, pharmaceuticals, vehicles, and hazardous materials. Importers hire brokers for their expertise in this regulated paperwork and for their electronic links to customs systems.

Licensing

In the United States, customs brokers are licensed by U.S. Customs and Border Protection after passing a rigorous examination on customs law, classification, and valuation; applicants must be U.S. citizens of good moral character. Many other countries similarly license or register brokers, though in some jurisdictions unlicensed agents may act under different titles such as customs agent or clearing agent.
