Nicolaes Pieterszoon Berchem (1 October 1620 – 18 February 1683) was a highly esteemed and prolific Dutch Golden Age painter of pastoral landscapes, populated with mythological or biblical figures, but also a few allegories and genre pieces. He was one of the most popular and successful of the so-called "Dutch Italianates", painters who created Italian-style landscapes bathed in golden light without necessarily having visited Italy — though Berchem is often thought to have travelled there in the 1650s.

Life

Berchem was born in Haarlem, the son of the still-life painter Pieter Claesz, and took his surname from his father's birthplace, Berchem in Flanders. He studied with his father and with Jan van Goyen, Pieter de Grebber and others. He joined the Haarlem Guild of St. Luke in 1642 and later moved to Amsterdam, where he died in 1683. According to Houbraken, his name derived from the cry "Berg hem!" ("Save him!") during a childhood incident.

Work

Berchem produced some 850 paintings, as well as etchings and drawings. His luminous pastoral scenes — herdsmen and cattle crossing sunlit rivers, southern harbours, mountain passes — were eagerly collected across Europe and influenced landscape painting into the 18th century, including the French Rococo. His pupils and followers included Karel Dujardin and Abraham Begeyn.
