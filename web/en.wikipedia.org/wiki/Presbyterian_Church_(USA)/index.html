Presbyterian Church (USA)

The Presbyterian Church (USA), abbreviated PC (USA), is a mainline Protestant denomination in the United States. It is the largest Presbyterian denomination in the country, with about 1.1 million members in the early 2020s. The church is part of the Reformed tradition, tracing its theological roots to John Calvin and John Knox.

History

The PC (USA) was formed on 10 June 1983 by the reunion of the Presbyterian Church in the United States (the "southern" church) and the United Presbyterian Church in the United States of America (the "northern" church), healing a split that dated to the American Civil War. Its headquarters are in Louisville, Kentucky.

Beliefs and polity

The denomination is governed by a representative system of councils — sessions of local congregations, presbyteries, synods, and the biennial General Assembly — under its constitution, the Book of Confessions and the Book of Order. The PC (USA) ordains women and, following a 2014 constitutional amendment, permits same-sex marriages to be performed by its ministers. It is known for relatively liberal positions on social issues, along with emphases on social justice, education and mission.
