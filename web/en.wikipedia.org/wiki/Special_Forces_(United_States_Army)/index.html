Special Forces (United States Army)

United States Army Special Forces
Nickname: Green Berets
Founded: 1952
Country: United States
Branch: United States Army
Motto: De oppresso liber ("To free the oppressed")

The United States Army Special Forces, colloquially known as the Green Berets after their distinctive headgear, are a special operations force of the U.S. Army. Their primary mission set includes unconventional warfare, foreign internal defense, special reconnaissance, direct action, and counterterrorism.

History

The first Special Forces unit, the 10th Special Forces Group, was activated in 1952 at Fort Bragg, North Carolina, under Colonel Aaron Bank, drawing on the experience of the wartime OSS and units such as the First Special Service Force. Green Berets served extensively in Vietnam, and President John F. Kennedy's support — he authorized the green beret as their official headgear in 1961 — made them an emblem of Cold War special warfare.

Organization and selection

Special Forces soldiers are organized into five active-duty groups and two National Guard groups, each oriented toward a region of the world. The basic operational unit is the 12-man Operational Detachment Alpha (ODA or "A-team"). Candidates must complete the Special Forces Qualification Course (the "Q Course"), which includes language and cultural training reflecting the force's emphasis on working with indigenous partners.
