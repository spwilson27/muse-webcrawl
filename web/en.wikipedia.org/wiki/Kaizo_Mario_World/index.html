Kaizo Mario World, also known by its Japanese title Jikū Mario World ("Selfish Mario World"), is a series of three ROM hacks of Nintendo's Super Mario World (1990) created by T. Takemoto. The hacks are notorious for their extreme difficulty, featuring precise platforming, hidden traps and tricks that kill the player without warning. They popularised the term "Kaizo" (改造, "reorganised" or "modded") for a whole genre of ultra-difficult fan-made Mario levels and games.

Gameplay

Taking advantage of the player's knowledge of Super Mario World mechanics, the levels are filled with instant-death traps: invisible coin blocks placed over jumps, post-goal traps that kill Mario after completing a stage, and sequences requiring frame-precise manoeuvres. Takemoto designed the hacks for a friend, R. Kiba, expecting them to be played with emulator save states, recording his attempts in early viral gameplay videos.

Influence and legacy

Videos of frustrated playthroughs of Kaizo Mario World became a phenomenon on Japanese video site Nico Nico Douga and later YouTube and Twitch, shaping "rage game" and let's-play culture. "Kaizo" became the name of a difficulty genre encompassing hacks and later official-style levels in Super Mario Maker, with community conventions such as shell jumps and mid-air tricks. Kaizo competitions are now featured at speedrunning events such as Games Done Quick.
