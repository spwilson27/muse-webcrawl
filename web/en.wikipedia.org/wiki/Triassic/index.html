The Triassic ( /traɪˈæsɪk/ ) is a geologic period and system which spans 50.5 million years from the end of the Permian Period 251.9 million years ago to the beginning of the Jurassic Period 201.4 million years ago. It is the first and shortest period of the Mesozoic Era.

Overview

The Triassic began in the aftermath of the Permian–Triassic extinction event, the most severe mass extinction in Earth's history, and life on land and in the seas gradually recovered and diversified. Nearly all land was united in the supercontinent Pangaea, whose vast interior was hot and arid.

Life

During the Triassic the first dinosaurs evolved, along with the first mammals, pterosaurs (the first flying vertebrates) and the first ichthyosaurs and other marine reptiles. Modern-style coral reefs and modern groups of marine invertebrates such as bivalves became established. On land, archosaurs became the dominant large vertebrates, and conifers were the dominant trees.

End of the period

The period ended with the Triassic–Jurassic extinction event, about 201.4 million years ago, associated with massive volcanism of the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province and the break-up of Pangaea; the extinction cleared ecological niches that allowed dinosaurs to dominate the Jurassic.

Subdivisions

The Triassic is divided into three epochs: Early, Middle and Late Triassic, corresponding to the Lower, Middle and Upper Triassic rock series, originally named for a three-fold succession of strata in Germany.
