Kris Kristofferson

Kris Kristofferson Born: Kristoffer Kristofferson, June 22, 1936, Brownsville, Texas, U.S.
Died: September 28, 2024 (aged 88), Hana, Hawaii, U.S.
Occupations: Singer-songwriter, actor
Genres: Country, outlaw country, folk
Instruments: Vocals, guitar, harmonica
Years active: 1959–2021

Kristoffer Kristofferson (June 22, 1936 – September 28, 2024) was an American singer, songwriter, and actor. A Rhodes Scholar and former U.S. Army helicopter pilot who famously left a teaching post at West Point to sweep floors at a Nashville recording studio, he became one of the defining songwriters of the outlaw country movement, writing standards such as "Me and Bobby McGee", "Help Me Make It Through the Night", "Sunday Mornin' Comin' Down", and "For the Good Times". He was also a successful film actor, starring in Alice Doesn't Live Here Anymore (1974), A Star Is Born (1976), and the Blade film trilogy.

Early life and education

Kristofferson was born in Brownsville, Texas, the son of Mary Ann (née Ashbrook) and Lars Henry Kristofferson, a U.S. Army Air Corps officer who later became a major general in the U.S. Air Force. Of Swedish descent on his father's side, he moved frequently as a military child. He attended Pomona College in California, where he played rugby and football, graduated summa cum laude with a degree in literature in 1958, and was awarded a Rhodes Scholarship to the University of Oxford. At Oxford's Merton College he studied English literature, boxed, played rugby, and began recording songs as "Kris Carson". He was commissioned into the U.S. Army, became a helicopter pilot, and served in West Germany, attaining the rank of captain. Offered a position teaching English literature at West Point, he instead resigned his commission in 1965 to pursue songwriting in Nashville.

Songwriting breakthrough

In Nashville, Kristofferson worked as a janitor at Columbia Recording Studios while pitching songs. Legend holds that he landed a helicopter in Johnny Cash's yard to deliver a tape — a story he partly embellished but never entirely denied. Cash recorded "Sunday Mornin' Comin' Down", which won the Country Music Association's Song of the Year in 1970. Roger Miller and Ray Price scored hits with his songs, and Janis Joplin's posthumous recording of "Me and Bobby McGee" reached number one in 1971. Kristofferson's own debut album, Kristofferson (1970), and its follow-up The Silver Tongued Devil and I (1971) established him as a performer in his own right.

Music career

Often grouped with the outlaw country movement alongside Willie Nelson, Waylon Jennings, and Johnny Cash, Kristofferson recorded a long series of albums for Monument and Columbia Records. His duet albums with Rita Coolidge, to whom he was married from 1973 to 1980, won two Grammy Awards. In 1985 he formed the supergroup The Highwaymen with Cash, Nelson, and Jennings; their debut single "Highwayman" topped the country chart. He was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 2004. Later albums such as This Old Road (2006) and Feeling Mortal (2013) were recorded in a spare, reflective style. He retired from performing in 2021.

Acting career

Kristofferson made his film debut in The Last Movie (1971) and won acclaim in Martin Scorsese's Alice Doesn't Live Here Anymore and as the male lead opposite Barbra Streisand in A Star Is Born, for which he won a Golden Globe Award for Best Actor. His starring role in Michael Cimino's Heaven's Gate (1980) coincided with that film's famous commercial failure, but he worked steadily thereafter, appearing in Lone Star (1996), as Abraham Whistler in the Blade trilogy (1998–2004), and in Planet of the Apes (2001).

Personal life and death

Kristofferson was married three times and had eight children. In later years he lived on the island of Maui, Hawaii. He suffered from memory loss that was initially attributed to Alzheimer's disease but was later linked to Lyme disease. He died at his home in Hana, Hawaii, on September 28, 2024, at the age of 88.
