Spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) is a form of synaptic plasticity in which the direction and magnitude of the change in synaptic strength depend on the precise relative timing of action potentials ("spikes") in the presynaptic and postsynaptic neurons. It is regarded as a temporally precise implementation of Hebb's rule — often summarized as "cells that fire together wire together."

The learning rule

In the canonical form of STDP, if a presynaptic spike arrives shortly before a postsynaptic spike (typically within about 10–20 ms), the synapse is strengthened — a form of long-term potentiation (LTP). If the order is reversed and the presynaptic spike arrives shortly after the postsynaptic spike, the synapse is weakened — a form of long-term depression (LTD). As the time difference grows, the change decays roughly exponentially, and spike pairs separated by much more than about 50–100 ms produce little change.

Δw = A+ e−Δt/τ+ for Δt > 0 (pre before post); Δw = −A− eΔt/τ− for Δt < 0 (post before pre)

The logic is causal: a presynaptic spike that helps to fire the postsynaptic cell is taken as evidence that the synapse contributed to the firing, so it is reinforced; a spike arriving just after firing cannot have contributed, so it is weakened.

Biological evidence

Timing-dependent plasticity was demonstrated in several preparations in the 1990s, notably by Markram and colleagues in cortical slices and, most influentially, by Guo-qiang Bi and Mu-ming Poo (1998) in cultured hippocampal neurons, who mapped the temporal window for potentiation and depression. Related timing rules have since been reported in the cortex, hippocampus, cerebellum-like structures, and other systems, though the exact shape of the window varies with synapse type, firing frequency, and the neuron's recent history.

Mechanisms

The leading mechanistic account involves NMDA receptors, which open only when presynaptic glutamate release coincides with postsynaptic depolarization, admitting calcium. Large, rapid calcium elevations tend to trigger LTP, while smaller, prolonged elevations trigger LTD. Backpropagating action potentials in the dendrite provide the timing signal that interacts with arriving synaptic input.

Function and applications

STDP is thought to underlie sequence learning, the formation of receptive fields in sensory systems, and competitive reorganization of neural circuits during development. In computational work, STDP is widely used as a local, unsupervised learning rule in spiking neural networks and in neuromorphic hardware, where it allows synapses to adapt using only information available at the synapse itself.
