Twenty-second Dynasty of Egypt

The Twenty-second Dynasty of ancient Egypt, often called the Libyan or Bubastite dynasty, ruled from about 943 BC to 716 BC, during the Third Intermediate Period. Its kings were of Libyan (Meshwesh) origin, descended from Libyan military settlers and chiefs who had risen to power in the Nile Delta. The dynasty was founded by Shoshenq I, who united Egypt after the fragmentation of the late New Kingdom.

History

Shoshenq I (r. c. 943–922 BC) reasserted central authority, appointed his son as High Priest of Amun at Thebes to control Upper Egypt, and campaigned in the Levant — his invasion of Judah and Israel, recorded on the walls of Karnak, is commonly identified with the biblical campaign of "Shishak." Under successors such as Osorkon I and Osorkon II the kingdom remained wealthy, but control gradually loosened: collateral lines of Libyan chiefs and kings (the Twenty-third Dynasty) ruled parts of the country, and by the late eighth century Egypt had fragmented into competing principalities, easing the way for the Kushite conquest of the Twenty-fifth Dynasty.

Kings

Principal rulers include Shoshenq I, Osorkon I, Takelot I, Osorkon II, Shoshenq III, Shoshenq V and Osorkon IV. The royal burials at Tanis, discovered in 1939–40, yielded treasures rivalling Tutankhamun's tomb, including the silver coffin of Shoshenq II.
