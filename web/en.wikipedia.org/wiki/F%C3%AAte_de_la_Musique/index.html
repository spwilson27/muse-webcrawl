Fête de la Musique

Observed21 June annually
First held1982, Paris, France
TypeMusic festival

The Fête de la Musique, also known as World Music Day or Make Music Day, is an annual celebration of music held on 21 June, the summer solstice in the northern hemisphere. On this day, musicians — amateur and professional alike — are encouraged to perform free concerts in streets, parks and public spaces.

Origins

The festival was launched in Paris in 1982 by Jack Lang, France's Minister of Culture, together with the composer and music journalist Maurice Fleuret, under the slogan "Faites de la musique" ("Make music"), a homophone of the festival's name. The idea drew on a study showing that a large share of French people played an instrument, yet public musical life was dominated by a narrow range of events.

Around the world

The Fête de la Musique spread rapidly and is now celebrated in more than a hundred countries, with thousands of free performances marking the longest day of the year in cities from Berlin to New York and Tokyo.
