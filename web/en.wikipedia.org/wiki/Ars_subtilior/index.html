Ars subtilior

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The heart-shaped score of Baude Cordier's "Belle, bonne, sage" in the Chantilly Codex

Ars subtilior ("subtler art") is a musical style characterized by rhythmic and notational complexity, centered in Paris and Avignon in southern France at the end of the fourteenth century. The style also is found in the music of Franco-Flemish composers, as well as in works by composers of the era from Italy, Aragon, Cyprus, and England. The term was coined by musicologist Ursula Günther in 1960 to describe the sudden appearance of extremely complex compositions from the ars nova period.

Musical characteristics

In terms of musical expression and goals, the ars subtilior is often compared to, but distinct from, the mannerism of later ages. Listeners of the era would have been unlikely to grasp the complexities of some of the music, and the visual appeal of the elaborate and unusual scores may have been as important as the sound.

One of the principal sources of ars subtilior music is the Chantilly Codex, which contains 112 pieces. Other notable manuscripts include the Modena Codex (Mod A) and the Turin Manuscript.

Composers

Notable composers of the style include Baude Cordier, Solage, Johannes Ciconia (in his earlier works), Matteo da Perugia, and Philippus de Caserta. Jacob Senleches' "La harpe de mélodie", written in the shape of a harp, is among the most famous examples.
