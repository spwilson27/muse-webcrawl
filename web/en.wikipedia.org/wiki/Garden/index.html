Garden

A garden is a planned space, usually outdoors, set aside for the cultivation, display, and enjoyment of plants and other forms of nature. The single feature identifying even the wildest wild garden is control. The garden can be a purely aesthetic creation, or it can assist in the growing of food, in which case it may be called a kitchen garden.

Gardening is the activity of growing and maintaining the garden. This work is often done by a gardener. A gardener might also work in a non-garden setting, such as being a park ranger, a groundskeeper, or in landscape design or horticulture.

Types of gardens

  • Flower gardens — devoted to ornamental flowering plants, often in beds or borders.
  • Vegetable gardens — for growing vegetables and herbs for consumption.
  • Herb gardens — culinary and medicinal herbs, a feature of monastic and cottage gardens.
  • Rock gardens — alpine plants grown among rocks and gravel.
  • Water gardens — ponds and aquatic plants, sometimes with fish such as koi.
  • Zen gardens — Japanese dry landscape gardens of raked gravel and stone.
  • Botanical gardens — curated collections of plants maintained for science, conservation, and education.
  • Community gardens — shared urban plots cultivated collectively by local residents.
  • Roof and vertical gardens — planting on rooftops and walls in dense cities.

History

The earliest gardens were essentially food plots enclosed for protection, and enclosed sacred groves appear in many ancient cultures. The Hanging Gardens of Babylon were counted among the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. Persian paradise gardens influenced Islamic garden design, with the four-part charbagh symbolizing the four elements and the rivers of paradise. In China and Japan, gardens developed as idealized miniature landscapes for contemplation.

In Europe, medieval monastery gardens supplied medicine and food; Renaissance gardens in Italy and France became formal displays of geometry and power, culminating in André Le Nôtre's designs at Versailles. The 18th-century English landscape garden, associated with Capability Brown, reacted with naturalistic parkland. The 19th and 20th centuries saw the rise of public parks, suburban gardens, and the cottage-garden style championed by Gertrude Jekyll.

Garden design

Garden design is the art and process of designing and creating plans for the layout and planting of gardens and landscapes. Elements include paths, walls, water features, sitting areas, and decorative structures, arranged with regard to soil, climate, aspect, and the growth habits of plants. Many gardens also serve ecological functions, providing habitat for pollinators and other wildlife.
