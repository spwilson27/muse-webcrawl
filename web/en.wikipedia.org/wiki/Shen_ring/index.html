Shen ring

A shen ring is an ancient Egyptian symbol of eternity and protection, represented in hieroglyphs (Gardiner sign V9) as a circle of rope with a short tangential line — a loop of rope tied at one point, forming an unbroken, endless ring.

Meaning and use

The word šn (shen) is related to notions of "encircling" — the circuit of the sun, and hence all that the sun encompasses — and to the verb "to protect". The ring signified eternal protection and the cyclical, boundless order of the cosmos. It appears in temple and tomb art from the Old Kingdom onward, often held in the talons of Horus falcons or by deities such as Isis and Nekhbet hovering protectively over the king, and a falcon bearing a shen ring appears on the Narmer Palette. The ring carried over the king's name expressed that the monarch was protected for eternity.

Relation to the cartouche

The elongated form of the shen ring is the cartouche, the oval rope loop that encloses royal names in Egyptian inscriptions — essentially a shen ring stretched to fit the name it eternally protects.
