Netherlands at the Winter Olympics

The Netherlands has competed at most editions of the Winter Olympic Games, first participating at the 1928 Games in St. Moritz. Dutch winter success is overwhelmingly concentrated in speed skating, a sport deeply embedded in the country's culture of skating on frozen canals.

History

The Netherlands won its first Winter Olympic medals in 1952 and its first gold medals at the 1964 Innsbruck Games through Sjoukje Dijkstra in figure skating and speed skaters including Kees Verkerk. Ard Schenk won three golds at Sapporo 1972. At the 2014 Sochi Games the Dutch speed skating team enjoyed its greatest triumph, winning 23 of the 36 available speed skating medals, including four clean podium sweeps, and the Netherlands finished fifth in the overall medal table — remarkable for a country with almost no alpine terrain.

Medals

In total, Dutch athletes have won well over 130 Winter Olympic medals, the great majority in speed skating, with additional success in short track speed skating (boosted by stars such as Suzanne Schulting), snowboarding, and figure skating. Ireen Wüst is the most decorated Dutch Winter Olympian, with six gold medals among thirteen total across five Games.

Organisation

The Dutch team is organised by the NOC*NSF. The nation has never hosted the Winter Olympics.
