Mordecai Gist (February 22, 1743 – September 2, 1792) was a brigadier general in the Continental Army during the American Revolutionary War. A native of Maryland, he was distinguished for his service with the Maryland Line, one of the most celebrated units of the Continental Army, and fought in many of the war's major engagements, from the opening campaigns around New York to the final southern campaigns in South Carolina.

Early life

Gist was born in Baltimore, Maryland, in 1743, into a prominent merchant family. He was educated for a commercial career and worked as a merchant before the outbreak of the Revolution. When tensions with Britain escalated, Gist became active in the patriot cause in Baltimore, helping to organize resistance to British measures.

Revolutionary War service

In 1776 Gist was appointed major of Smallwood's Maryland Regiment, later the 1st Maryland Regiment. He fought at the Battle of Long Island in August 1776, where the Maryland troops won enduring fame for their repeated charges against British forces at the Old Stone House, which allowed much of Washington's army to escape across Gowanus Creek. Gist continued to serve with the Maryland Line through the battles of White Plains, Brandywine, Germantown, and Monmouth.

Promoted to colonel and then to command of the 3rd Maryland Regiment, Gist was sent south with the Maryland troops in 1780. At the disastrous Battle of Camden in August 1780, the Maryland Continentals stood firm after much of the militia fled, and Gist helped rally the survivors. He subsequently served under Nathanael Greene in the southern campaign, fighting at Cowpens and Eutaw Springs. In January 1782 he was promoted to brigadier general. In August 1782, he commanded American forces at the Battle of the Combahee River, one of the last engagements of the war, in which the young officer John Laurens was killed.

Later life

After the war, Gist settled in Charleston, South Carolina, where he became a planter and merchant. He was a founding member of the Society of the Cincinnati in Maryland. Gist died in Charleston in 1792. Gist's family produced other notable soldiers: his nephew, States Rights Gist, became a Confederate brigadier general killed at the Battle of Franklin in 1864, and his great-nephew Sequoyah — no relation in fact — is often confused in name only through the inventor of the Cherokee syllabary, George Gist, who was Mordecai Gist's nephew and took the Cherokee name Sequoyah.
