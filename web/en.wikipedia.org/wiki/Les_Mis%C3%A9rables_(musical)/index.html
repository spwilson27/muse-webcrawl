Les Misérables, colloquially known as Les Mis, is a sung-through musical composed by Claude-Michel Schönberg with lyrics by Alain Boublil and Jean-Marc Natel, adapted from Victor Hugo's 1862 novel. The English-language version, produced by Cameron Mackintosh with English lyrics by Herbert Kretzmer, opened in London in 1985 and became one of the longest-running musicals in history.

Synopsis

Set in early nineteenth-century France, the story follows Jean Valjean, a former convict hounded by the police inspector Javert, as he seeks redemption while caring for the orphaned Cosette. Around them unfold the stories of Fantine, Marius, Éponine and the students of the June Rebellion of 1832, which ends at the barricades of Paris.

Songs and productions

Famous numbers include "I Dreamed a Dream", "On My Own", "Bring Him Home", "One Day More" and "Do You Hear the People Sing?". The original London production ran continuously until 2019 (later reopening in a staged concert and a revised production), making it the longest-running musical in the West End. The Broadway production (1987–2003) won eight Tony Awards. A film adaptation was released in 2012.
