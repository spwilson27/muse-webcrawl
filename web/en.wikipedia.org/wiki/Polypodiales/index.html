Polypodiales

The order Polypodiales encompasses the major lineages of polypod ferns, which comprise more than 80% of today's fern species. They are found in many parts of the world, including in the tropics, in semitropical areas and in temperate areas in Europe, North America, and East Asia. Species of this order are particular in having sporangia with a vertical annulus interrupted by the stalk and stomium — the structures that fling spores out of the sporangium.

Classification

In the Pteridophyte Phylogeny Group classification of 2016 (PPG I), the Polypodiales are divided into six suborders (Aspleniineae, Athyriineae, Blechnineae, Dennstaedtiineae, Polypodiineae, and Saccolomatineae) and include some 26 families and around 9,500 species. Large families include Dryopteridaceae, Polypodiaceae, Pteridaceae, Aspleniaceae, and Athyriaceae.

Characteristics

Members of the order are leptosporangiate ferns whose sporangia develop from a single epidermal cell and typically bear an annulus — a row of thickened cells — that dries and snaps back to disperse the spores. Their sori (clusters of sporangia) are often protected by flap-like indusia, and many species are epiphytes or rock-dwelling plants as well as ground ferns.
