Sawm (Arabic: صَوْم, ṣawm) is the practice of fasting in Islam. Most prominently, it refers to the obligatory fast observed by Muslims during the month of Ramadan, in which believers abstain from food, drink, and marital relations from dawn until sunset. Sawm is the fourth of the Five Pillars of Islam.

The Ramadan fast

The fast is prescribed in the Quran (Surah Al-Baqarah, verse 183), which presents fasting as a means of attaining taqwa (God-consciousness). Each day of fasting begins with a pre-dawn meal, suhoor, and ends at sunset with the meal of iftar, often shared communally. The month concludes with the festival of Eid al-Fitr.

Exemptions

Children who have not reached puberty, the elderly, the chronically ill, pregnant or menstruating women, and travellers are exempted from fasting, though missed days may be made up later or compensated by feeding the poor (fidya). Acts that break the fast include eating, drinking, and vomiting deliberately.

Spiritual and voluntary fasts

Beyond Ramadan, voluntary fasts are observed on occasions such as the Day of Arafah, Ashura, Mondays and Thursdays, and the six days of Shawwal. Jurists classify fasting as obligatory, recommended, disliked, or forbidden — the fast is prohibited on the two Eid festivals. The spiritual tradition treats fasting not merely as abstention from food but as discipline of speech, appetite, and conduct.
