Port Howard

Port Howard (Spanish: Puerto Mitre) is the largest settlement on West Falkland in the Falkland Islands, with a population of roughly twenty people. It stands on an inlet on the east coast of the island at the southern end of Falkland Sound.

Economy

The settlement is the centre of a large sheep farm of some 81,000 hectares, divided in 1986 into smaller units when ownership passed from the Falkland Islands Company to local buyers. Port Howard Lodge offers accommodation noted for trout fishing on the Warrah and Chartres rivers.

Falklands War

During the 1982 Falklands War the settlement was occupied by Argentine forces. On 25 May 1982, Captain John Hamilton of the SAS, who had been observing Argentine positions from a nearby hill, was killed in an engagement near Port Howard; he was posthumously awarded the Military Cross, and a small museum and memorial in the settlement commemorate the war.

Transport

Port Howard has an airstrip served by the Falkland Islands Government Air Service and is the West Falkland terminal of the ferry across Falkland Sound to New Haven on East Falkland.
