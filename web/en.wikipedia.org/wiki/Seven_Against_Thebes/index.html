Seven Against Thebes

The Seven Against Thebes (Greek: Ἑπτὰ ἐπὶ Θήβας, Hepta epi Thēbas) is a famous episode of Greek mythology: the war fought by the exiled Theban prince Polynices and six allies against his brother Eteocles for the throne of Thebes. The story belongs to the Theban cycle of epics and is the subject of Aeschylus's tragedy Seven Against Thebes (467 BC) and the final part of Statius's Latin epic Thebaid.

The story

After the fall of Oedipus, his sons Eteocles and Polynices agreed to rule Thebes in alternate years, but Eteocles refused to yield the throne. Polynices fled to Argos, married the daughter of King Adrastus, and persuaded his father-in-law to lead an expedition to restore him. Seven champions were appointed, each to attack one of Thebes's seven gates. In the canonical list they were Adrastus, Polynices, Tydeus, Capaneus, Hippomedon, Parthenopaeus and Amphiaraus — the last a prophet who foresaw the disaster and went unwillingly. Eteocles posted a Theban defender at each gate; Capaneus, boasting that not even Zeus could stop him, was struck by lightning as he scaled the wall. All the attackers except Adrastus were killed, and the brothers slew each other in single combat.

Aftermath

Creon, succeeding to power, forbade the burial of Polynices — the decree whose defiance by Antigone forms the plot of Sophocles' Antigone. A generation later the sons of the Seven, the Epigoni, mounted a second expedition that finally captured Thebes.
