Echoes arise when sound reflects from a distant surface.

Echo

An echo is a reflection of sound that arrives at the listener with a delay after the direct sound, so that it is heard as a distinct repetition. The delay is proportional to the distance of the reflecting surface: since sound travels at about 343 metres per second in air, a surface some 17 metres away produces a noticeable echo of about a tenth of a second.

Acoustic principles

Echoes occur when a sound wave strikes a hard, smooth surface — a cliff, wall or building — and is reflected back. Multiple reflections produce multiple echoes or, when delays are very short, reverberation, in which reflected sounds merge into a decaying wash. True distinct echoes require the reflector to be at a sufficient distance relative to the duration of the sound.

Uses

Animals such as bats and dolphins exploit echoes in echolocation to navigate and hunt. Human technologies using the same principle include sonar for underwater detection, medical ultrasonography, and echo sounding for measuring water depth. In audio engineering, artificial echo and delay effects are staples of recorded music.

Mythology

In Greek mythology, Echo was a nymph cursed to repeat only the last words spoken to her — the origin of her unrequited love for Narcissus.
