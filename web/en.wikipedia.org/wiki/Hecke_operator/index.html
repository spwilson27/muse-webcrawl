Hecke operator

Hecke operator
FieldNumber theory, modular forms
Introduced byErich Hecke, 1937
NotationTn, Tp (p prime)
Key objectsEigenforms, Hecke algebra, L-functions

In mathematics, in particular in the theory of modular forms, a Hecke operator is a certain kind of averaging operator that plays a significant role in the structure of vector spaces of modular and cusp forms. The operators Tn, for each positive integer n, were defined by Erich Hecke in 1937, building on earlier work of Mordell on Ramanujan's conjectures.

Definition

For a modular form f of weight k, the operator Tn acts by averaging f over a finite set of related lattices: schematically, (Tₙf)(z) = nᵏ⁻¹ Σ f((az+b)/d) over matrices of determinant n. On the Fourier expansion f(z) = Σ a(m) qᵐ, the action for a prime p is explicit: the m-th coefficient of Tpf is a(mp) + pᵏ⁻¹ a(m/p), where a(m/p) is read as zero unless p divides m.

Properties

The Hecke operators commute with one another and are self-adjoint with respect to the Petersson inner product on cusp forms; consequently spaces of cusp forms admit a basis of simultaneous eigenforms. For an eigenform (Hecke eigenform), the Fourier coefficients are themselves the eigenvalues, giving multiplicative relations among them and yielding an Euler product for the associated L-function — the Hecke L-function. The Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture, proved by Deligne as a consequence of the Weil conjectures, bounds these eigenvalues.

Significance

Hecke theory is the backbone of the modern theory of automorphic forms: it underlies the Langlands program, the modularity theorem used in the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem, and the arithmetic geometry of modular curves, where the operators arise geometrically from correspondences.
