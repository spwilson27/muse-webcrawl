Ole Einar Bjørndalen (born 27 January 1974) is a retired Norwegian biathlete, widely regarded as the greatest biathlete of all time. Nicknamed the "King of Biathlon," he won 13 Winter Olympic medals — eight of them gold — more than any other male athlete in Winter Olympic history at the time of his retirement, and 20 World Championship titles.

Career

Bjørndalen grew up in Simostranda and followed his older brother Dag into biathlon. He made his World Cup debut in 1993 and won his first Olympic gold in the sprint at Nagano 1998. His crowning achievement came at the 2002 Salt Lake City Games, where he won all four biathlon events — the individual, sprint, pursuit, and relay — a feat unmatched in the sport.

Across six Olympic Games he collected medals at every edition from 1998 to 2014, winning his final golds in the sprint and the inaugural mixed relay at Sochi 2014 at age 40. He also competed at the highest level in cross-country skiing, winning a World Cup freestyle race at Gällivare in 2006 — the first biathlete to win a cross-country World Cup event. His 95 World Cup victories (94 in biathlon and one in cross-country) made him the most successful male athlete in the history of the winter World Cups combined.

Retirement and later work

Bjørndalen announced his retirement in April 2018 after failing to qualify for the Pyeongchang Olympics. He was elected to the International Olympic Committee's Athletes' Commission in 2014. In 2019 he became head coach of the Chinese national biathlon team alongside his wife, Belarusian biathlete Darya Domracheva, herself a four-time Olympic champion.

Personal life

Bjørndalen was previously married to Italian-Belgian biathlete Nathalie Santer. He married Darya Domracheva in 2016; they have a daughter. He has lived for much of his career in Obertilliach, Austria, and in Minsk, Belarus.
