Ralph Kimball (born 1944) is an American computer scientist and author widely regarded as one of the original architects of the data warehousing and business intelligence industry. He is the founder of the Kimball Group, known for its training and consulting in data warehousing.

Contributions

Kimball is best known for popularising dimensional modeling, a design technique for presenting data in frameworks that are intuitive to business users and deliver fast query performance. His approach organises warehouse data into fact tables and conformed dimension tables arranged in star schemas.

His book The Data Warehouse Toolkit (1996, with later editions) became a standard reference for practitioners. Together with Bill Inmon, he is one of the two figures most associated with the founding philosophies of data warehousing, though their approaches differ — Inmon advocating a top-down normalised enterprise warehouse, Kimball a bottom-up architecture of integrated data marts built on a bus architecture.

Career

Kimball earned a doctorate from Stanford University and worked at Xerox PARC before moving into the database industry, holding roles at Metaphor Computer Systems, Red Brick Systems and DecisionWorks Consulting before founding the Kimball Group.
