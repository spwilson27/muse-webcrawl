Dilma Rousseff

Dilma Vana Rousseff (born 14 December 1947) is a Brazilian economist and politician who served as the 36th president of Brazil from 2011 until her impeachment and removal from office on 31 August 2016. She was the first woman to hold the Brazilian presidency.

Early life and career

Born in Belo Horizonte to a Bulgarian immigrant father, Rousseff joined the resistance to Brazil's military dictatorship as a young woman and was imprisoned and tortured between 1970 and 1972. After studying economics, she held state-level posts in Rio Grande do Sul. Under President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva she served as Minister of Mines and Energy (2003–2005) and then as Chief of Staff (2005–2010), becoming Lula's chosen successor in the Workers' Party (PT).

Presidency and impeachment

Rousseff won the 2010 election and was re-elected narrowly in 2014. Her second term was marked by a severe recession, the Petrobras corruption scandal (Operation Car Wash) and mass protests. Impeached over alleged violations of budgetary laws (the pedaladas fiscais), she was removed by the Senate in 2016 and succeeded by Michel Temer, though she maintained that the process was a coup.

Later career

In 2023 Rousseff became president of the New Development Bank (the BRICS bank), headquartered in Shanghai.
