Nine Years' War

The Nine Years' War (1688–1697), also known as the War of the League of Augsburg or War of the Grand Alliance, was a conflict between France under Louis XIV and a coalition of European states led by the Dutch Republic and England. It was fought mainly in Europe — the Rhineland, the Spanish Netherlands, Catalonia, Ireland and Scotland — with related fighting in North America known as King William's War.

Causes

The war arose from Louis XIV's expansionist policies, including the Réunions and the invasion of the Palatinate in 1688, and from the Glorious Revolution, which brought William of Orange to the English throne and aligned England firmly against France.

Course

France won notable victories at Fleurus (1690), Steenkerque (1692) and Neerwinden (1693), and at sea at Beachy Head (1690), but the naval defeats at Barfleur and La Hogue (1692) ended invasion hopes. In Ireland, William's victory at the Boyne (1690) and Aughrim (1691) secured the revolution settlement. Exhaustion and famine pushed both sides toward peace.

Peace of Ryswick

The Treaties of Ryswick (1697) restored most French conquests made since Nijmegen, recognised William III as King of England, and left the Spanish succession — the great question of the next war — unresolved.
