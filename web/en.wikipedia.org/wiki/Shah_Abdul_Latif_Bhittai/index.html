Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai

Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai (1689 – 1752), often called Latif, was a Sindhi Sufi scholar, mystic, saint and poet, widely considered the greatest poet of the Sindhi language. His collected verse, the Shah Jo Risalo, is one of the masterpieces of Sindhi literature and a cornerstone of the cultural identity of Sindh in present-day Pakistan.

Life

He was born in 1689 in Hala Haveli, near modern Hala in Sindh, then part of the Mughal Empire. A descendant of a family of Sayyids, he received limited formal education but travelled widely through Sindh and neighbouring regions, spending time with Hindu yogis and Sufi wanderers. He eventually settled on a sandhill — bhit in Sindhi, hence "Bhittai" — near Hala, building a small settlement that grew into the town of Bhit Shah, where he died in 1752.

Poetry

The Shah Jo Risalo organises his verse into some thirty surs (musical modes or chapters), many retelling Sindhi folk romances — Sassui Punhun, Sohni Mehar, Marui, Momal Rano, Lilan Chanesar, Sorath Rai Diyach and Noori Jam Tamachi — through which he explores divine love, human suffering, and the search for truth. His poetry blends Islamic Sufi themes with the landscape, music and everyday life of Sindh.

Legacy

His shrine at Bhit Shah draws hundreds of thousands of devotees each year for his Urs, and his verses are sung nightly by faqirs at the shrine to the accompaniment of the danburo, a five-stringed instrument he is said to have refined. The Risalo has been translated into Urdu, English and many other languages.
