Alakaʻi Wilderness Preserve

Alakaʻi Wilderness Preserve
Location: Kauaʻi, Hawaiʻi, United States
Area: about 9,000 acres (36 km²)
Elevation: up to ~1,700 m (Mount Waiʻaleʻale vicinity)
Governing body: Hawaiʻi Division of Forestry and Wildlife

The Alakaʻi Wilderness Preserve, popularly known as the Alakaʻi Swamp, is a montane wet forest and bog preserve on the Hawaiian island of Kauaʻi, situated on a plateau near Mount Waiʻaleʻale, one of the wettest places on Earth. The preserve protects roughly 9,000 acres of native cloud forest, bogs and shrubland in Kauaʻi's rugged interior.

Ecology

The Alakaʻi is home to many rare and endangered Hawaiian plants and forest birds, including the ʻakikiki, ʻakekeʻe and puaiohi, which survive here at higher elevations relatively free from the avian malaria that has devastated lowland species. Stunted ʻōhiʻa trees grow in waterlogged bogs alongside sedges, ferns and the endemic violets. The preserve adjoins Kōkeʻe State Park and is crossed by the Alakaʻi Swamp Trail, noted for its boardwalks over the bogs and views into the Kalalau Valley.

Conservation

Threats include invasive plants, feral pigs, and mosquito-borne disease advancing upslope with climate warming. Fencing and predator- and ungulate-control programmes aim to safeguard the last stronghold of Kauaʻi's endemic honeycreepers.
