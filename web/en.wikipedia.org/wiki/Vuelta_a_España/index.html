Vuelta a España

The Vuelta a España (Spanish for "Tour of Spain") is an annual multi-stage bicycle race primarily held in Spain, while also occasionally making passes through nearby countries. Inspired by the success of the Tour de France and the Giro d'Italia, the race was first organised in 1935. Together with those two races it forms cycling's prestigious Grand Tours — the three-week stage races that are the sport's biggest events. The race is held in late August and September, finishing in Madrid.

History

The Vuelta was first run in 1935 with 50 entrants over 3,411 km; Belgian Gustaaf Deloor won the first two editions. The race suffered interruptions from the Spanish Civil War and the Second World War, and in its early decades was sometimes reduced in prestige and length. Through the 1960s–1990s winners included Jacques Anquetil, Eddy Merckx, Bernard Hinault, Pedro Delgado, and Tony Rominger (three consecutive wins, 1992–1994). In 1995 the race moved from spring to its current late-summer slot. Alberto Contador won three editions (2008, 2012, 2014), and Primož Roglič won four (2019–2022 in sequence plus 2024). Roberto Heras holds the record with four overall titles.

Classifications and jerseys

The overall leader wears the red jersey (la roja), adopted in 2010 after earlier eras of yellow and gold. Other competitions include the points classification (green jersey), the mountains classification (white jersey with blue polka dots), and the young rider classification (white jersey); a combativity prize and team classification are also awarded. The Vuelta is famous for steep summit finishes such as the Alto de l'Angliru and Lagos de Covadonga, which favour explosive climbers.

Route

The race usually covers about 3,300 km over 21 stages in three weeks, with two rest days. It often starts outside Spain (Lisbon, Utrecht, and others have hosted the Gran Salida) and traditionally finishes on a circuit in Madrid.
