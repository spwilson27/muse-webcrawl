The petroleum industry in Azerbaijan produces about 800,000 barrels of oil per day and roughly 30 billion cubic metres of gas per year, and is the dominant sector of the country's economy. Azerbaijan is one of the birthplaces of the modern oil industry: wells were drilled around Baku from the 1840s, and by 1900 the Absheron Peninsula supplied about half of the world's oil.

History

Oil had been extracted by hand from surface seeps near Baku for centuries. Industrial drilling began in the 1840s–1870s, attracting investors including the Nobel brothers and the Rothschilds, whose companies made Baku a centre of the early global oil business. Under the Soviet Union, Azerbaijan's fields supplied much of the USSR's oil early in the Second World War, and offshore Caspian production began in 1949 at Neft Daşları ("Oil Rocks"). After independence in 1991, Azerbaijan signed the 1994 "Contract of the Century" with a BP-led consortium to develop the Azeri–Chirag–Gunashli (ACG) offshore fields.

Modern sector

The state oil company SOCAR oversees the sector. ACG is the principal oil-producing project, while the Shah Deniz gas field supplies domestic demand and exports to Europe via the Southern Gas Corridor, including the Trans-Anatolian and Trans-Adriatic pipelines. Oil is exported mainly through the Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan pipeline to the Mediterranean. Oil and gas revenues fund the State Oil Fund (SOFAZ) and a large share of the state budget, prompting ongoing debate about economic diversification.
