Khichdi (pronounced [kʰɪtʃɽiː]) is a dish of the Indian subcontinent made by cooking rice and lentils together, typically with mild spices. Simple, soft and easily digested, it is one of the subcontinent's oldest comfort foods — commonly given to babies as a first solid food, to the elderly and the convalescent — yet it also appears in elaborate festive versions enriched with vegetables, ghee and whole spices.

History

Dishes of rice and pulses boiled together are described in ancient Indian texts, and khichdi was noted by foreign travellers: the Moroccan explorer Ibn Battuta mentioned it in the fourteenth century, and it was served at the Mughal court, where the Ain-i-Akbari lists several recipes. The British adapted it in India into kedgeree — rice, lentils (later replaced by fish and eggs) — which became a Victorian breakfast dish.

Preparation and variations

The most common form uses split moong dal with rice; other versions use masoor, toor or mixed dals, or replace rice with millets, quinoa or cracked wheat. The mixture is cooked with turmeric, cumin and salt to textures ranging from dry and separate-grained (as in the Gujarati style) to a soft porridge. Regional variations include Bengal's festive bhoger khichuri served during Durga Puja, Hyderabad's khichdi-khatta, and masala khichdi with potatoes, peas and cauliflower. It is traditionally served with ghee, yoghurt, pickle and papad — the "four friends" of khichdi in an Indian proverb.
