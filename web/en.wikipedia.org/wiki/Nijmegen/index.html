Nijmegen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The Waal riverfront at Nijmegen, with the Stevenskerk tower.

Nijmegen (Dutch: [ˈnɛimeːɣə(n)]; historically also spelled Nijmwegen, German: Nimwegen) is a city in the Dutch province of Gelderland, on the Waal river close to the German border. It is considered the oldest city in the Netherlands: in 2005 it celebrated its 2,000th year of existence. The municipality had a population of roughly 180,000 in the early 2020s, making it one of the ten largest cities in the country.

History

The oldest traces of settlement in the Nijmegen area date to the Roman era. Around the beginning of the Common Era the Romans built the military camp Oppidum Batavorum on the Hunnerberg hill, followed by Ulpia Noviomagus Batavorum, which received market rights around AD 98 under the emperor Trajan. The name derives from the Latin Novio Magus, "new market". After the departure of the Romans, the settlement persisted; Charlemagne built a palace (palts) on the Valkhof in the eighth century, and the city later joined the Hanseatic League.

In the Second World War Nijmegen suffered heavily. On 22 February 1944 American bombers mistakenly bombed the city centre, killing nearly 800 people. In September 1944 the city became a front-line battleground during Operation Market Garden; American paratroopers of the 82nd Airborne Division crossed the Waal in canvas boats to capture the road bridge, an action depicted in the film A Bridge Too Far. The Waal Bridge, opened in 1936, remains a city landmark, joined in 2013 by a second bridge, De Oversteek ("The Crossing"), named in honour of the 1944 crossing.

Geography

Nijmegen lies in the east of the Netherlands, a few kilometres from the German border, on the south bank of the Waal, the main distributary of the Rhine. The city centre sits on a moraine ridge, giving it an unusually hilly character for a Dutch city. To the south lies the wooded Reichswald, and to the north the river landscape of the Ooijpolder.

Education and culture

The city is home to Radboud University (founded 1923) and the HAN University of Applied Sciences, giving it a large student population. Nijmegen is famous for the Vierdaagse (Four Days Marches), the world's largest walking event, held every July and attracting more than 40,000 participants who walk 30, 40 or 50 kilometres a day for four days; the accompanying festivities, the Vierdaagsefeesten, draw over a million visitors. The oldest building in the city is the octagonal St. Nicholas Chapel on the Valkhof (c. 1030), one of the oldest stone buildings in the Netherlands.
