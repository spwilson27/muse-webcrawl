Hwatu

OriginKorea (from Japanese hanafuda)
Cards48, in 12 monthly suits
Popular gamesGo-Stop, Seotda

Hwatu (화투, "flower cards") are Korean playing cards derived from the Japanese hanafuda, introduced during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The deck consists of 48 cards divided into twelve suits, one for each month of the year, each decorated with flowers or plants and featuring bright cards, animal cards, ribbon cards and plain "junk" cards.

Games

The most popular hwatu game is Go-Stop, in which players match cards by month and accumulate points by completing scoring combinations, choosing at thresholds whether to "go" for more points or "stop" and collect. Other games include Seotda, a poker-like gambling game using two-card combinations. Hwatu play is strongly associated with family gatherings at holidays such as Seollal and Chuseok, though the cards also carry a long association with gambling in Korea.
