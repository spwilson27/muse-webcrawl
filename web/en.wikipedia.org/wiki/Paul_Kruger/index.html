Stephanus Johannes Paulus Kruger (10 October 1825 – 14 July 1904), better known as Paul Kruger and affectionately as Oom Paul, was a South African politician. He was one of the dominant political and military figures in 19th-century South Africa, and State President of the South African Republic (or Transvaal) from 1883 to 1900.

Early life

Kruger was born near the eastern frontier of the Cape Colony to a family of Dutch, German, and Huguenot descent. As a boy he took part in the Great Trek, the migration of Dutch-speaking settlers (Voortrekkers) into the interior, and was present in the aftermath of conflicts with the Matabele and Zulu. He grew up with little formal education but developed a formidable reputation as a frontiersman, hunter, and commando leader, fighting in numerous engagements during the settlement of the Transvaal.

Political career

Kruger rose through the politics of the South African Republic, becoming commandant-general and then vice president. When Britain annexed the Transvaal in 1877, he led deputations to London seeking the restoration of independence, and when diplomacy failed he became one of the leaders of the resistance that culminated in the First Boer War (1880–1881). The Boer victory at Majuba Hill led to the Pretoria Convention and the restoration of self-government.

Elected State President in 1883, Kruger was re-elected three times. His presidencies coincided with the Witwatersrand gold rush, which transformed the republic's economy and drew tens of thousands of mostly British foreign workers (uitlanders), whose demands for political rights Kruger resisted. His face became internationally famous, and his name is commemorated in the Krugerrand coin and the Kruger National Park.

Second Boer War and exile

Relations with Britain deteriorated through the 1890s, worsened by the Jameson Raid of 1895–96, and ended in the Second Boer War of 1899–1902. As British forces advanced on Pretoria in 1900, Kruger left the country aboard the Dutch warship Gelderland, sent by Queen Wilhelmina of the Netherlands. He spent the rest of his life in exile in Europe, dying at Clarens, Switzerland, on 14 July 1904. His remains were returned to South Africa and buried in Pretoria.

Legacy

Kruger remains a revered figure in Afrikaner nationalism and a symbol of resistance to British imperialism, though modern assessments also reckon with the racially exclusive political order he defended.
