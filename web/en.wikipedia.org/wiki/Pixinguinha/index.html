Pixinguinha

Pixinguinha (born Alfredo da Rocha Viana Jr., 23 April 1897 – 17 February 1973) was a Brazilian composer, arranger, flautist, and saxophonist born in Rio de Janeiro. He is regarded as one of the greatest composers of Brazilian popular music and the father of choro, composing standards including "Carinhoso" and "Lamentos".

Career

A child prodigy on the flute, Pixinguinha joined the group Oito Batutas in 1919, which toured Paris in 1922 and helped introduce Brazilian music to Europe. In the 1930s he worked as an arranger for RCA Victor in Brazil, writing sophisticated orchestrations for singers such as Carmen Miranda and Francisco Alves. His compositions blended Afro-Brazilian rhythms with European harmony and jazz-influenced counterpoint in the choro tradition.

Legacy

Pixinguinha's birthday, 23 April, is celebrated in Brazil as National Choro Day. His works, such as "Um a Zero", "Segura Ele", and "Ingênuo", remain standards of the choro repertoire, and he is celebrated as a foundational figure of modern Brazilian music.
