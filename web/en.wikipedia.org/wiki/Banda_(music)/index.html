Banda (music)

Banda is a term to designate a style of Mexican music and the musical ensemble in which wind instruments, mostly of brass, and percussion are performed. The name is also used for other musical styles.

History

Banda music originated in the northwestern Mexican state of Sinaloa in the 19th century, when local musicians adopted the brass instruments and repertoire of European immigrant and military bands, blending them with Mexican folk song forms such as the ranchera, corrido, cumbia and bolero. Clarinetists, often drawn from the indigenous communities of the region, became a defining feature of the Sinaloan sound.

During the 20th century banda spread across Mexico and, through migration, into the southwestern United States. In the 1990s the genre achieved mass commercial popularity, with large ensembles recording cumbias, romantic ballads and dance music for radio; the "technobanda" movement around Guadalajara mixed banda with electronic instruments.

Instrumentation

A traditional banda sinaloense typically includes clarinets, trumpets, valve trombones or slide trombones, alto horns, a tuba or sousaphone (the tuba providing the bass line), a tambora (large bass drum played with a padded stick), tarolas (snare drums) and cymbals. Vocalists were added prominently from the 1990s onward.
