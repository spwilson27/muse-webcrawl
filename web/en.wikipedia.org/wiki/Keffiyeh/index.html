Keffiyeh

The black-and-white houndstooth keffiyeh is a symbol of Palestinian identity.

The keffiyeh (Arabic: كوفية, kūfiyya), also known as a ghutra, shemagh or hatta, is a traditional Arab headdress made of a square scarf, usually of cotton, folded and worn around the head and held in place by a cord called an agal. It protects the wearer from sun, wind, dust and cold.

Varieties and symbolism

Patterns and colours vary by region: the black-and-white fishnet pattern is associated with Palestine, the red-and-white with Jordan and the wider Levant, and plain white ghutras are common in the Gulf states. The Palestinian keffiyeh became an emblem of national identity during the 1936–39 Arab revolt and was famously worn by Yasser Arafat, who arranged it to evoke the map of Palestine. Solidarity movements abroad adopted it as a political symbol.

Manufacture and fashion

Traditionally woven on looms, keffiyehs were once produced in many Palestinian workshops; production later shifted largely to imports, with the Hirbawi factory in Hebron among the last local makers. The scarf has also appeared in global fashion, sometimes controversially.
