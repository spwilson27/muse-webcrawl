Lawrence Oates

Captain Lawrence Edward Grace Oates
Born: 17 March 1880, Putney, London
Died: 17 March 1912, Ross Ice Shelf, Antarctica
Service: British Army; Terra Nova Expedition

Lawrence Edward Grace "Titus" Oates (17 March 1880 – 17 March 1912) was a British army officer and Antarctic explorer who died during the Terra Nova Expedition of Robert Falcon Scott. He is remembered for his final act of self-sacrifice, recorded in Scott's diary.

Oates, in charge of the expedition's ponies.

Terra Nova Expedition

Oates joined Scott's expedition in 1910 as the expert in charge of the ponies. He was selected for the five-man party that reached the South Pole on 18 January 1912, finding that Roald Amundsen's team had preceded them. On the return journey the party was beset by extreme cold, hunger, and injury; Oates suffered severely frostbitten feet and, believing he was slowing his companions' chance of survival, walked out of the tent into a blizzard on his 32nd birthday. Scott recorded his words: "I am just going outside and may be some time."

Legacy

Oates's death, and his famous last words, became one of the most celebrated episodes of the Heroic Age of Antarctic Exploration. His body was never found; a memorial cross stands near the presumed site.
