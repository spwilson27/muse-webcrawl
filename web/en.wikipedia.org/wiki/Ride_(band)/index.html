Ride are an English rock band formed in Oxford in 1988. Consisting of vocalists and guitarists Andy Bell and Mark Gardener, bassist Steve Queralt, and drummer Loz Colbert, the band were leading figures of the British shoegaze scene with a sound that combined swirling, effects-laden guitars with strong pop melodies and lush harmonies. Their debut album Nowhere (1990) is widely regarded as one of the definitive shoegaze records.

History

Formation and early EPs (1988–1990)

Gardener and Bell had played together at art college in Banbury before moving to Oxford, where they recruited Queralt and Colbert. After catching the attention of Alan McGee's Creation Records, the band released three acclaimed EPs in 1990 — Ride, Play, and Fall — building a devoted following through the indie charts and relentless touring.

Nowhere and Going Blank Again (1990–1992)

Ride's first album, Nowhere, released in October 1990, was a critical and commercial success, reaching the UK top 20 and producing the signature track "Vapour Trail." The follow-up, Going Blank Again (1992), expanded their palette with the singles "Leave Them All Behind" and "Twisterella" and reached number 5 in the UK. That same period, the band's 1992 reading of "Grasshopper" and contributions to compilations solidified their standing.

Britpop turn and breakup (1993–1996)

As the British music press turned toward Britpop, Ride's third album Carnival of Light (1994) moved toward classic rock influences and exposed tensions between Bell and Gardener over creative control. The fractious Tarantula (1996) was recorded amid open acrimony; the band split before its release, and Creation withdrew the album within a week. Bell went on to join Oasis as bassist, while Gardener pursued solo and collaborative work.

Reunion (2014–present)

The band reformed in 2014 for a world tour, including headline shows and a set at Coachella. They returned to recording with Weather Diaries (2017), produced by Erol Alkan, followed by This Is Not a Safe Place (2019) and Interplay (2024), restoring their reputation as a major live act.
