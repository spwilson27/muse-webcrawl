Celery

Celery (Apium graveolens) is a cultivated plant in the family Apiaceae that has been grown as a vegetable since antiquity. Depending on location and cultivar, either its stalks, leaves, or hypocotyl are eaten and used in cooking. It is a marshland plant native to the Mediterranean region and widely naturalised elsewhere.

Description

Celery is a biennial plant producing pinnate to bipinnate leaves and, in its second year, umbels of small white flowers. The familiar stalk celery is grown for its long, fibrous leaf petioles; celeriac is grown for its large bulbous hypocotyl, and leaf celery (smallage) for its flavourful leaves and seeds.

Uses

Celery stalks are eaten raw in salads and crudités, and cooked as part of the aromatic base mirepoix in French cooking and the soffritto and Creole "holy trinity" of other cuisines. Celery seed is used as a spice, and celery salt features in the Bloody Mary cocktail. In traditional medicine celery preparations were used as a diuretic.

Health

Celery is mostly water and is very low in calories. It is, however, among the foods that more commonly cause allergic reactions, and its furanocoumarins can contribute to phytophotodermatitis in sensitive individuals.
