Super Mario (also known as Super Mario Bros. and Mario) is a platform game series created by Nintendo starring their mascot, Mario. It is the central series of the greater Mario franchise. At least one Super Mario game has been released for every major Nintendo video game console, beginning with Super Mario Bros. for the Nintendo Entertainment System in 1985. The games follow Mario's adventures, typically in the fictional Mushroom Kingdom with his younger brother Luigi and others, to rescue the kidnapped Princess Peach from the villain Bowser.

Gameplay

The early games are side-scrolling platformers in which Mario runs and jumps across platforms and atop enemies through themed worlds (called "courses"), collecting coins and power-ups such as the Super Mushroom, which makes him grow; the Fire Flower, which lets him throw fireballs; and the Super Star, which grants temporary invincibility. Super Mario 64 (1996) moved the series into 3D, introducing free-roaming hub worlds and missions to collect Power Stars; later 3D entries include Super Mario Sunshine, the Super Mario Galaxy games, and Super Mario Odyssey. The New Super Mario Bros. sub-series revived the 2D style, and Super Mario Maker let players build and share their own levels.

Sales and legacy

With over 430 million copies sold for the main series alone, Super Mario is one of the best-selling video game franchises of all time, and Mario is among the most recognisable characters in popular culture. The series is widely credited with helping revive the North American video game industry after the crash of 1983 and with defining the platform genre in both 2D and 3D. Its characters and worlds have expanded into spin-off series such as Mario Kart, Mario Party and Super Smash Bros. (in which Mario appears), as well as theme parks and the 2023 animated film The Super Mario Bros. Movie.
