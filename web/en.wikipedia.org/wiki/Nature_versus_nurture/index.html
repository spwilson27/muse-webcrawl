Nature versus nurture

Nature versus nurture is a long-standing debate in psychology and biology concerning the relative contributions of innate qualities (nature — genetic endowment and biological predisposition) and personal experience (nurture — environment, upbringing, culture, and learning) to human development and behaviour.

History

The phrase was popularised by Francis Galton, a cousin of Charles Darwin, who was influenced by Shakespeare's Prospero describing Caliban in The Tempest as "a devil, a born devil, on whose nature nurture can never stick". Earlier thinkers had framed similar contrasts: John Locke's notion of the mind as a tabula rasa emphasised experience, while others stressed innate ideas.

Evidence

Twin studies and adoption studies are central methods. Comparisons of identical (monozygotic) twins with fraternal (dizygotic) twins suggest substantial heritability for traits such as intelligence, personality dimensions, and risk of schizophrenia, while also showing clear environmental effects. Molecular genetics and genome-wide association studies have identified many small-effect variants associated with behavioural traits. Research on gene–environment interaction and epigenetics — for example, how early-life stress can alter stress-response systems — has blurred any simple dichotomy.

Modern consensus

Most contemporary scientists reject the "versus" framing: virtually all complex traits arise from continuous interaction between genetic predispositions and environments. As Donald Hebb put it, asking whether heredity or environment contributes more to behaviour is like asking whether length or width contributes more to the area of a rectangle.
