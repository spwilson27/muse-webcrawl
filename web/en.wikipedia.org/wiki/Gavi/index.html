Gavi is a comune (municipality) in the Province of Alessandria in the Italian region of Piedmont, located about 100 kilometres (62 mi) southeast of Turin and about 35 kilometres (22 mi) southeast of Alessandria. It lies in the valley of the Lemme river, on the historic route between the Piedmontese plain and the Ligurian coast at Genoa.

History

The area was inhabited in antiquity, possibly by the Ligurian Statielli tribe. In the Middle Ages Gavi was contested between the Republic of Genoa, the Marquisate of Montferrat, and the Duchy of Milan, owing to its strategic position guarding the pass toward Genoa. The town is dominated by its fortress, the Forte di Gavi, a massive Genoese stronghold enlarged in the 17th century, which in later centuries served as a prisoner-of-war camp.

Wine

Gavi gives its name to a celebrated dry white wine, Gavi DOCG (also labelled Cortese di Gavi), made from the Cortese grape grown in the surrounding hills. The wine is known for its crisp, floral, and mineral character and is one of Piedmont's best-known whites.
