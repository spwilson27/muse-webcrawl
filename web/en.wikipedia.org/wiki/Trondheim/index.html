Trondheim

Trondheim is a city and municipality in Trøndelag county, Norway, at the mouth of the river Nidelva where it meets the Trondheim Fjord. Founded in 997 by King Olav Tryggvason, it served as Norway's first capital and remains the country's historic coronation city. With a population of around 210,000, it is Norway's third-most populous municipality and a major centre of education and technology research.

Landmarks

Nidaros Cathedral, built over the burial site of Saint Olaf, is the world's northernmost medieval cathedral and the traditional coronation church of Norwegian monarchs. Other landmarks include the Archbishop's Palace, the colourful warehouses along the Nidelva, the Old Town Bridge (Gamle Bybro), and the Kristiansten Fortress.

Education and research

Trondheim hosts the main campus of the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU), the largest university in Norway, and the SINTEF research foundation. Its large student population gives the city a youthful character.
