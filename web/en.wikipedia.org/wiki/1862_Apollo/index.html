1862 Apollo

1862 Apollo is a stony asteroid, approximately 1.5 kilometres in diameter, that was the first recognised member of the Apollo group of near-Earth asteroids — objects whose orbits cross that of Earth. It was discovered by German astronomer Karl Reinmuth at Heidelberg Observatory on 24 April 1932, but was then lost until 1973.

Orbit and characteristics

Apollo orbits the Sun with a period of about 1.81 years, on an eccentric orbit that carries it inside the orbit of Venus and out beyond Mars. It is a Q-type asteroid and a source, together with other near-Earth objects, of the Quadrantid meteor shower's parent population debates. Radar observations have shown it to be an elongated body.

Satellite

In 2005, radar observations at Arecibo Observatory revealed that Apollo has a small moon, about 80 metres across, orbiting roughly 3 kilometres from the primary — making it one of the known binary near-Earth asteroids.
