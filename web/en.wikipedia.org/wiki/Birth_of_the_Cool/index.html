Birth of the Cool is a compilation album by American jazz musician Miles Davis, released in February 1957 on Capitol Records. It compiles eleven tracks recorded by Davis's nonet between 1949 and 1950 in three sessions, originally issued as 78-rpm singles. The music is notable for its unusual instrumentation (including French horn and tuba), arranged, chamber-like textures, and relaxed tempos — a departure from the fast, fiery bebop of the era — and is credited with launching the cool jazz movement.

Background

The nonet grew out of discussions at arranger Gil Evans's New York apartment, a gathering place for young musicians including Davis, baritone saxophonist Gerry Mulligan, pianist John Lewis, and alto saxophonist Lee Konitz. Inspired by the Claude Thornhill Orchestra, the group aimed for a blended, vocal-like ensemble sound. The band played a brief engagement opening for Count Basie at the Royal Roost in 1948 before recording for Capitol.

Music

Tracks include Mulligan's "Jeru" and "Venus de Milo", Lewis's "Rouge", Johnny Carisi's "Israel", and the Davis–Evans collaboration "Boplicity". The instrumentation — trumpet, trombone, French horn, tuba, alto and baritone saxophones, piano, bass, and drums — produced a soft, singing sonority that contrasted with bebop combos.

Legacy

Though the singles sold modestly at first, the recordings became hugely influential, shaping West Coast jazz and the cool school. Capitol issued the tracks (with "Darn That Dream" added) as the 12-inch LP Birth of the Cool in 1957. The album is routinely listed among the greatest jazz albums of all time and appears in the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry.
