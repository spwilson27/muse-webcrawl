Brooks' theorem

Brooks' theorem
Field: graph theory
Proved by: R. L. Brooks (1941)
Statement: χ(G) ≤ Δ(G), with two exceptions

In graph theory, Brooks' theorem states that in any connected undirected graph G with maximum degree Δ, the chromatic number of G is at most Δ, unless G is a complete graph or an odd cycle, in which case the chromatic number is Δ + 1.

Statement

Formally: for any connected graph G that is neither a complete graph nor an odd cycle, χ(G) ≤ Δ(G). The theorem, published by Rowland Leonard Brooks in 1941, improves the trivial greedy bound of Δ + 1 colours and is tight for cliques and odd cycles.

Proofs and extensions

László Lovász gave a simpler proof in 1975. Brooks' theorem has been extended in several directions, including list colouring versions and bounds for graphs avoiding large cliques (the Reed's conjecture area of research).
