Janus kinase inhibitor

A Janus kinase inhibitor (also called a JAK inhibitor or jakinib) is a class of medication that blocks the activity of one or more enzymes of the Janus kinase family (JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and TYK2), thereby interfering with the JAK–STAT signalling pathway used by many cytokines and growth factors. JAK inhibitors are used chiefly to treat autoimmune and inflammatory diseases and certain blood cancers.

Mechanism

Cytokines such as interleukins and interferons bind cell-surface receptors that rely on intracellular Janus kinases to phosphorylate STAT transcription factors. By occupying the ATP-binding site of the kinase, JAK inhibitors interrupt this signal cascade, dampening immune-cell activation and inflammation. Unlike biologic drugs that target a single extracellular cytokine, JAK inhibitors are small molecules, taken orally (and in some formulations topically), that can suppress several cytokine pathways at once.

Approved drugs and uses

  • Tofacitinib (JAK1/JAK3) — rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ulcerative colitis, and other indications; the first JAK inhibitor approved for rheumatoid arthritis (2012).
  • Ruxolitinib (JAK1/JAK2) — myelofibrosis and polycythaemia vera; also a topical cream for atopic dermatitis and vitiligo.
  • Baricitinib (JAK1/JAK2) — rheumatoid arthritis, alopecia areata, and hospitalised COVID-19 patients.
  • Upadacitinib (JAK1) — rheumatoid arthritis, atopic dermatitis, ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease, and others.
  • Filgotinib, abrocitinib, fedratinib, pacritinib, momelotinib, and ritlecitinib — approved for various rheumatic, dermatologic, gastrointestinal, and haematologic conditions, and deucravacitinib, a TYK2 inhibitor, for plaque psoriasis.

Adverse effects and warnings

Common side effects include upper respiratory infections, headache, and raised lipid and liver enzyme levels; herpes zoster reactivation is a recognised risk. Following a safety review of tofacitinib, regulators in the United States added boxed warnings for the class concerning serious infections, malignancy, major adverse cardiovascular events, thrombosis, and mortality, particularly in older patients with cardiovascular risk factors.
