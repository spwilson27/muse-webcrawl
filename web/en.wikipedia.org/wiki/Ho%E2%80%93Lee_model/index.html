Ho–Lee model

The Ho–Lee model is a short-rate model widely used in mathematical finance for modelling interest rate movements. Developed in 1986 by Thomas Ho and Sang Bin Lee, it was the first arbitrage-free model of the term structure of interest rates.

Model

The model describes the evolution of the short rate as a normal (Gaussian) process with constant drift and constant volatility. In continuous time it is written as drt = θt dt + σ dWt, where θt is a time-dependent drift chosen so that the model exactly reproduces the initial yield curve, σ is the constant volatility, and Wt is a Wiener process under the risk-neutral measure.

Features

Because the short rate is normally distributed, the model admits closed-form prices for zero-coupon bonds and simple options, making it tractable for calibration and teaching. Its main drawbacks are that it permits negative interest rates and has no mean reversion. It is closely related to the later Hull–White model, which adds mean reversion to the same framework.
