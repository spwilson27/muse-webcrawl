Renaissance in Poland

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Renaissance in Poland (Polish: Renesans w Polsce) lasted from the late 15th century to the late 16th century and is widely considered the Golden Age of Polish culture. Ruled by the Jagiellonian dynasty, the Kingdom of Poland actively participated in the European Renaissance, drawing on Italian influence transmitted through royal marriages, trade, and the movement of artists and scholars. Renaissance culture flourished in the multinational and tolerant Polish state, which saw the rise of learning, literature in the Polish language, and distinctive architecture.

Origins

Renaissance ideas reached Poland largely through Hungary, where the court of Matthias Corvinus at Buda gathered Italian humanists. The Italian humanist Filippo Buonaccorsi (Callimachus) arrived in Poland in the 1470s and became a tutor to the sons of Casimir IV Jagiellon. The marriage of Sigismund I the Old to Bona Sforza of Milan in 1518 greatly strengthened Italian cultural influence at the Kraków court.

The Jagiellonian University in Kraków was a major center of learning: Nicolaus Copernicus studied there in the 1490s before publishing De revolutionibus orbium coelestium (1543), the work that founded modern heliocentric astronomy.

Literature and thought

The period saw the flourishing of Polish-language literature. Mikołaj Rej was among the first major writers to write in Polish; Jan Kochanowski became the greatest Polish poet of the era, authoring the Laments and numerous songs and epigrams. Andrzej Frycz Modrzewski wrote the influential political treatise On the Repair of the Commonwealth. The Statute of Lithuania and Polish translations of statutes and the Bible (including the first complete Polish Bible, the Leopolita Bible of 1561, and the Protestant Brest Bible of 1563) advanced the vernacular.

Poland's relative religious tolerance, formalized by the Warsaw Confederation of 1573, made it a refuge for heterodox thinkers and communities at a time of religious wars elsewhere in Europe.

Architecture

Renaissance architecture in Poland blended Italian forms with local traditions, often characterized by ornate attic gables. Landmarks include the rebuilt Wawel Castle in Kraków with its arcaded courtyard, the Sigismund Chapel at Wawel Cathedral (1519–1533) — often described as the finest work of the Italian Renaissance north of the Alps — the town hall of Poznań, and the ideal Renaissance town of Zamość, founded by Chancellor Jan Zamoyski and designed by Bernardo Morando. The cities of Kazimierz Dolny and Tarnów preserve notable Renaissance burgher houses.

Art and music

Italian artists such as Bartolomeo Berecci, Giovanni Battista di Quadro, and Santi Gucci worked at Polish courts. Tapestries commissioned by Sigismund II Augustus from Brussels workshops formed one of Europe's great collections. In music, composers including Wacław of Szamotuły produced works in both Polish and Latin.

Decline

The transfer of the capital from Kraków to Warsaw by Sigismund III Vasa, the Counter-Reformation, and the wars of the mid-seventeenth century brought the period to a close, as Baroque culture and the Sarmatian ideology of the nobility reshaped Polish taste.
