Lamborghini Countach

The Lamborghini Countach is a rear mid-engine, rear-wheel-drive sports car produced by the Italian manufacturer Lamborghini from 1974 to 1990. Its dramatic wedge-shaped body, designed by Marcello Gandini at Bertone, pioneered the sharply angled "Italian Wedge" design language and popularised scissor doors; it became one of the most recognisable poster cars of the 1970s and 1980s. The name is an exclamation of astonishment in the Piedmontese language.

The design was first shown as the LP500 prototype at the 1971 Geneva Motor Show. Production began with the LP400 (1974–78), whose clean lines lacked the flares and spoilers of later cars. Subsequent versions — the LP400 S, LP500 S, LP5000 Quattrovalvole (with a 5.2-litre four-valve V12 producing about 455 PS) and the 25th Anniversary edition of 1988 — grew wider wheel-arch flares, huge rear tyres and an optional rear wing, progressively defining the Countach's flamboyant image. Top speeds of the fastest variants approached 290–295 km/h (180–183 mph).

Legacy

The Countach established the template for Lamborghini's flagship V12 line continued by the Diablo, Murciélago, Aventador and Revuelto, and its silhouette remains an icon of automotive design. In 2021 Lamborghini unveiled the Countach LPI 800-4, a limited-production hybrid homage based on the Sián, reviving the nameplate.
