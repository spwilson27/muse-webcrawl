Klaus Kinski

Klaus Kinski Born: Klaus Günter Karl Nakszynski, 18 October 1926, Zoppot (Sopot), Free City of Danzig
Died: 23 November 1991, Lagunitas, California, United States
Occupation: Actor
Known for: Collaborations with Werner Herzog
Children: Nastassja Kinski, Nikolai Kinski

Klaus Kinski (born Klaus Günter Karl Nakszynski; 18 October 1926 – 23 November 1991) was a German actor known for his intense, volatile performances and equally volatile off-screen temperament. He appeared in more than 130 films, from Spaghetti Westerns to art cinema, and achieved his greatest acclaim in five films directed by Werner Herzog.

Career

Born in Danzig, Kinski grew up in poverty in Berlin and served in the German army during the Second World War. After the war he turned to theatre and developed a reputation for mesmerising one-man performances of Villon, Rimbaud and Shakespeare. Film roles followed in the 1960s, including appearances in For a Few Dollars More (1965) and Doctor Zhivago (1965), and many Edgar Wallace crime films and Spaghetti Westerns.

Herzog collaborations

His partnership with Herzog produced Aguirre, the Wrath of God (1972), Nosferatu the Vampyre (1979), Woyzeck (1979), Fitzcarraldo (1982) and Cobra Verde (1987). The shoots were legendary for their difficulty: on the Peruvian set of Fitzcarraldo Kinski's outbursts were so extreme that, according to Herzog, local extras offered to kill him. Herzog later chronicled their love-hate relationship in the documentary My Best Fiend (1999). Kinski's late work included his only directorial effort, the notorious Paganini (1989).

Personal life

Kinski's posthumous reputation was further complicated by allegations of abuse made by his daughters. He died of a heart attack in California in 1991, aged 65.
