Altice is a group of telecommunications and media companies founded and led by the French-Israeli billionaire Patrick Drahi. Built through a long series of debt-fuelled acquisitions of cable and telecom operators beginning in the early 2000s, the group came to control major operators in France, Portugal, Israel, the Dominican Republic and the United States.

History

Drahi began assembling cable assets in France in the 1990s and 2000s, eventually forming Numericable. In 2014 Altice acquired SFR, France's second-largest mobile operator, merging it with Numericable to create Numericable-SFR (later SFR Group and Altice France). It also acquired Portugal Telecom in 2015.

The US arm, Altice USA, was created through the acquisitions of Suddenlink Communications (2015) and Cablevision (2016, for about $17.7 billion), whose Optimum brand serves the New York metropolitan area; Altice USA was spun off and listed separately in 2018. Altice Europe was taken private by Drahi in 2021. The group's very high debt load — tens of billions of euros — has been a persistent theme, leading to asset sales and, in the mid-2020s, creditor negotiations and restructuring efforts involving SFR.
