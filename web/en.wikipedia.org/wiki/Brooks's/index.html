Brooks's is a gentlemen's club in London, located at 60 St James's Street. Founded in 1764 by William Almack, it is one of the oldest and most famous of the London clubs, historically associated with the Whig political tradition and with high-stakes gambling.

History

The club began as Almack's, a gaming and dining club. In 1778 it moved to its present Palladian clubhouse designed by Henry Holland and took the name of its manager, a wine merchant and money-lender named Brooks. Charles James Fox was its most famous early member; members famously bet on almost anything, recording wagers on events from elections to births in the club's betting book. Brooks's was closely linked with the Whig aristocracy, in contrast to the more Tory White's club nearby.

Today

Brooks's remains a private members' club known for its dining room, its Great Subscription Room, and its tradition of political and social exclusivity. It has remained a men-only club.
