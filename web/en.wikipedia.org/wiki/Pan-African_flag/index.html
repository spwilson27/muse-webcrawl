Pan-African flag

The Pan-African flag — also known as the UNIA flag, Afro-American flag, Black Liberation flag, and RBG flag — is a horizontal tricolour of red, black, and green. It was adopted by the Universal Negro Improvement Association and African Communities League (UNIA) in 1920 and has since become a symbol of Pan-Africanism, Black nationalism, and African liberation movements worldwide.

History

The flag was formally adopted on August 13, 1920, during the UNIA's month-long convention at Madison Square Garden in New York City, where delegates from around the world adopted the Declaration of the Rights of the Negro Peoples of the World. Marcus Garvey, founder of the UNIA, promoted the flag as a unifying symbol for people of African descent, partly in response to a popular 1900 racist novelty song asserting that Black people had no flag of their own.

Symbolism

The three colours are commonly interpreted as: red, for the blood shed by Africans in the fight for liberation; black, for the African people; and green, for the natural wealth and fertility of the African continent. The colours have influenced the national flags of many post-independence African states, beginning with Ghana in 1957, and variations appear on flags of countries such as Kenya, Malawi, South Sudan, and Libya (1977–2011).

Usage

The flag is widely displayed during Black History Month, Juneteenth, Kwanzaa, and African Liberation Day events. Several American cities have officially recognized it. In 2020 and afterward it saw renewed prominence during racial justice protests, and local governments in the United States have raised it over public buildings.
