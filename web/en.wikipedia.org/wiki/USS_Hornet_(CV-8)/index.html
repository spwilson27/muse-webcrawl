USS Hornet (CV-8), the seventh U.S. Navy vessel of that name, was a Yorktown-class aircraft carrier of the United States Navy. During World War II in the Pacific Theater, she launched the Doolittle Raid on Tokyo and participated in the Battle of Midway and the Buin-Faisi Raid. In the Solomon Islands campaign, she was involved in the capture and defense of Guadalcanal and the Battle of the Santa Cruz Islands, where she was irreparably damaged by enemy torpedo and dive bombers. Faced with an approaching Japanese surface force, Hornet was abandoned and later torpedoed and sunk by approaching Japanese destroyers on 27 October 1942.

Doolittle Raid

In April 1942, Hornet carried sixteen B-25B Mitchell medium bombers of the United States Army Air Forces, commanded by Lieutenant Colonel James H. Doolittle, deep into the western Pacific. On 18 April the bombers were launched to strike Tokyo and other Japanese cities — the first air raid on the Japanese home islands, which lifted American morale and caused Japan to retain forces for home defense.

Midway and the Solomons

At the Battle of Midway (4–7 June 1942), aircraft from Hornet, Enterprise, and Yorktown sank four Japanese fleet carriers. Hornet's torpedo squadron, VT-8, was annihilated in its attack, with only Ensign George Gay surviving. Hornet later supported the Guadalcanal campaign. At Santa Cruz on 26 October 1942, Japanese carrier aircraft disabled her with bombs and torpedoes; after salvage efforts failed, she was scuttled.

In February 2019, the research vessel Petrel located Hornet's wreck in more than 17,000 feet (5,200 m) of water near the Santa Cruz Islands.
