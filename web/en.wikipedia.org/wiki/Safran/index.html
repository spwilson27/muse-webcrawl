Safran S.A. is a French multinational aerospace and defence corporation, the world's third-largest aerospace company by revenue. It designs, develops and manufactures aircraft engines, rocket engines, aerospace components and defence systems. The company was formed in 2005 by the merger of Snecma, the state-owned engine manufacturer, and Sagem, an electronics and defence firm.

Activities

Safran is best known for aircraft propulsion: through CFM International, its 50/50 joint venture with GE Aerospace, it builds the CFM56 and LEAP engines that power Airbus A320neo and Boeing 737 MAX airliners — among the best-selling jet engines in history. Other businesses include landing gear, wheels and brakes, nacelles, aircraft interiors and seats, avionics, optronics and navigation systems, and rocket propulsion through ArianeGroup, its joint venture with Airbus.

History

The group's roots lie in Gnôme et Rhône, founded in 1905 and nationalised after the Second World War as Snecma. The 2005 merger created a combined group initially named Safran (the French word for saffron, and also a rudder component). In 2018 Safran acquired Zodiac Aerospace, greatly expanding its cabin interiors business.
