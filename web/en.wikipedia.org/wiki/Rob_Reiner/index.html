Rob Reiner

Rob Reiner
Born: March 6, 1947, Bronx, New York, U.S.
Occupations: Actor, film director, producer, activist
Years active: 1966–present

Robert Reiner (born March 6, 1947) is an American actor, filmmaker and political activist. He first became famous as Michael "Meathead" Stivic on the 1970s sitcom All in the Family, winning two Emmy Awards, and then directed a celebrated run of films including This Is Spinal Tap (1984), Stand by Me (1986), The Princess Bride (1987), When Harry Met Sally... (1989), Misery (1990) and A Few Good Men (1992).

Early life

Reiner was born in the Bronx, New York City, to Estelle (née Lebost) and Carl Reiner, the comedian, actor and director. He grew up in New Rochelle, New York, and Los Angeles, and studied at UCLA Film School. As a young man he worked as an apprentice at the Bucks County Playhouse and performed in television roles before his breakthrough on Norman Lear's All in the Family (1971–1979).

Directing career

Reiner's directorial debut was the heavy-metal mockumentary This Is Spinal Tap (1984), in which he also starred as filmmaker Marty Di Bergi. His subsequent films spanned comedy, drama and thriller genres with unusual consistency of critical and commercial success. A Few Good Men was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture. Later films include The American President (1995), Ghosts of Mississippi (1996), The Bucket List (2007), LBJ (2016) and Shock and Awe (2017). He co-founded the production company Castle Rock Entertainment in 1987.

Activism

Reiner is a prominent liberal activist. He co-founded the American Foundation for Equal Rights, which challenged California's Proposition 8, and chaired the campaign for California's Proposition 10 (1998), which taxed tobacco products to fund early-childhood programs; he served as the first chairman of the resulting First 5 California commission. He has been a frequent critic of Republican administrations and a supporter of Democratic candidates.

Personal life

Reiner was married to actress and director Penny Marshall from 1971 to 1981. In 1989 he married photographer Michele Singer, whom he met during the making of When Harry Met Sally...; the meeting famously prompted him to change the film's ending so that Harry and Sally end up together.
