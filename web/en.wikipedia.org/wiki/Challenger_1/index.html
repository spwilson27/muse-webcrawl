The FV4030/4 Challenger 1 is a British main battle tank (MBT) used by the British Army from 1983 to 2001 and by the Royal Jordanian Army, where it is known as Al-Hussein. It was developed from the Chieftain line after the cancellation of the Anglo-German MBT-80 project, and the design originated in an order from the Shah of Iran for an improved Chieftain variant, the FV4030/3 (Shir 2).

Development

After the Iranian Revolution of 1979 cancelled the order, the British Ministry of Defence adopted the design to meet its own requirement. Challenger 1 combined the firepower of the 120 mm L11 rifled gun with Chobham composite armour — among the most effective tank protection of its era — and a 1,200 hp Rolls-Royce Condor diesel engine with David Brown transmission and hydrogas suspension.

Service history

Challenger 1 first deployed operationally in the 1991 Gulf War (Operation Granby), where 221 tanks destroyed roughly 300 Iraqi armoured vehicles without a single loss to enemy fire. The type also served with British forces in Bosnia and Kosovo as part of IFOR and SFOR. Its gunnery was initially criticised, but upgrades including the Improved Fire Control System and TOGS thermal sight greatly improved performance; a Challenger 1 recorded one of the longest-range confirmed tank kills of the war, at a range of about 5,100 metres.

Replacement

The Challenger 1 was progressively replaced by the Challenger 2 from 1998, and the last British regiments converted by 2001. Surplus vehicles were transferred to Jordan, which received over 400 tanks and operated them after local upgrades.
