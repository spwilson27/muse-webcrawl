Guillaume Apollinaire

Guillaume Apollinaire (26 August 1880 – 9 November 1918) was a French poet, playwright, short-story writer, novelist and art critic of Polish-Italian descent, born Wilhelm Albert Włodzimierz Apolinary Kostrowicki in Rome. He is considered one of the foremost poets of the early 20th century and a central figure of the Parisian avant-garde.

Apollinaire coined the terms "cubism" (in his writing on the painters) and "surrealism" (in 1917, in the programme notes for the ballet Parade and his play Les Mamelles de Tirésias). His poetry collections Alcools (1913) and Calligrammes (1918) blend lyricism with modern urban imagery; his "calligrammes" are poems whose typography forms pictures. His best-known poem, "Le Pont Mirabeau", mourns the passing of love and time.

Apollinaire volunteered for the French army in the First World War, was wounded in the head by shrapnel in 1916, and died in Paris two years later during the Spanish flu pandemic, days before the Armistice.
