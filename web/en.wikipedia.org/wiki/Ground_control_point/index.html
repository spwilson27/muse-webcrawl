Ground control point

A ground control point (GCP) is a point on the surface of the Earth with known coordinates that can be identified in remotely sensed imagery or other spatial data. Ground control points are used to georeference data — to tie it accurately to a real-world coordinate system.

Use in photogrammetry and mapping

In aerial and satellite photogrammetry, GCPs are typically markers clearly visible in the imagery (such as painted crosses, checkerboard targets or distinctive surveyed features) whose positions are measured precisely on the ground, usually with RTK or PPK GNSS equipment. By identifying these markers across a set of overlapping photographs, software can correct for lens distortion, terrain relief and camera position errors through bundle adjustment and orthorectification, producing maps and 3D models with centimetre-level accuracy. Drone mapping workflows generally require a minimum of about five well-distributed GCPs per survey area, supplemented by independent checkpoints used to verify accuracy rather than to adjust the model.

Characteristics

Good ground control points are unambiguous, durable, evenly distributed across the area of interest including its edges, and located on stable surfaces. Their measured accuracy should substantially exceed the accuracy required of the final product.
