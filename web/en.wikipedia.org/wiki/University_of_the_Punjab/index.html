University of the Punjab

The University of the Punjab (Punjabi/Urdu: جامعہ پنجاب) is a public research university located in Lahore, Punjab, Pakistan. Founded in 1882, it is the oldest and one of the largest public universities in Pakistan, with more than 45,000 students enrolled on campus and many more through its affiliated colleges.

History

The university was established on 14 October 1882 by the British colonial government, following the model of the Universities of Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras but with an emphasis on Oriental learning alongside Western subjects. It was the fourth university founded in British India. After the Partition of India in 1947, the university remained in Lahore, while a separate Panjab University was established in India.

Campuses

The university's two main Lahore campuses are the Allama Iqbal Campus (the historic "Old Campus") and the Quaid-i-Azam Campus (the "New Campus") on Canal Road, with additional campuses in Gujranwala, Jhelum, and Khanspur. It comprises numerous faculties and institutes covering arts, sciences, law, medicine, engineering, and commerce.

Notable alumni

Alumni include two Nobel laureates, physicist Abdus Salam and chemist Har Gobind Khorana (both educated at the university before Partition), and the philosopher-poet Muhammad Iqbal, for whom the old campus is named.
