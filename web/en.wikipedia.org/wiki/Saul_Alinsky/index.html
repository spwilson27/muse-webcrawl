Saul David Alinsky (January 30, 1909 – June 12, 1972) was an American community activist and political theorist, widely regarded as the founder of modern community organising. Working from the late 1930s in poor urban neighbourhoods, beginning with Chicago's Back of the Yards district, he developed a pragmatic method by which disempowered communities could build their own organisations and win concrete improvements from business and government.

Alinsky founded the Industrial Areas Foundation in 1940 to train organisers.

Career

Trained in sociology at the University of Chicago, Alinsky began organising in 1938–39 with the Back of the Yards Neighborhood Council, uniting packinghouse workers, churches and ethnic associations. In 1940 he founded the Industrial Areas Foundation (IAF), which trained generations of organisers and seeded campaigns across the United States, including the Woodlawn Organization in Chicago and FIGHT in Rochester, New York. His approach emphasised self-interest, conflict, negotiation from a position of organised power, and building broad-based local institutions rather than imposing outside leadership.

Books and legacy

Alinsky's two books, Reveille for Radicals (1946) and the bestselling Rules for Radicals (1971), set out thirteen tactical rules for challenging entrenched power and remain standard texts for activists across the political spectrum. His methods influenced the civil rights movement, farmworker organising under Cesar Chavez, and later political figures including Hillary Clinton (who wrote her college thesis on his work) and Barack Obama, who worked as a community organiser in Chicago in the Alinsky tradition.
