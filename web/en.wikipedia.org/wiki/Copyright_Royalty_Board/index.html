Copyright Royalty Board

The Copyright Royalty Board (CRB) is a U.S. system of three Copyright Royalty Judges who determine fair royalty rates for statutory licenses under the Copyright Act of the United States. The Board was created by the Copyright Royalty and Distribution Reform Act of 2004, which became effective on May 31, 2005, replacing the earlier ad-hoc Copyright Arbitration Royalty Panels.

The Copyright Royalty Judges are appointed by the Librarian of Congress, in consultation with the Register of Copyrights, and serve six-year terms with eligibility for reappointment. The Board sits within the Library of Congress.

Functions

The CRB conducts proceedings (known by designations such as Web I–VI for webcasting and Phonorecords I–IV for mechanical royalties) to set rates and terms for compulsory licenses covering webcasting and streaming of sound recordings, mechanical reproductions of musical works, satellite radio, and cable and satellite retransmission of broadcast programming. It also oversees distribution of royalty funds collected under statutory licenses when claimants cannot agree on shares.

Notable proceedings

The Board's webcasting rate decisions have been repeatedly litigated and adjusted on appeal, including its controversial 2007 decision that sharply raised per-performance rates for internet radio. Phonorecords proceedings set the headline mechanical royalty rates paid by streaming services to music publishers and songwriters.
