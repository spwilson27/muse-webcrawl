Polarity item

A polarity item is a linguistic expression whose occurrence is limited to certain grammatical environments. A negative polarity item (NPI) occurs only in negative or downward-entailing contexts, while a positive polarity item (PPI) is excluded from such contexts.

Examples

In English, any and ever are NPIs: "I didn't see anyone" and "Have you ever been there?" are grammatical, while "*I saw anyone" (in the relevant reading) is not. Expressions like some behave as PPIs in certain uses, resisting narrow scope under negation. Dutch hoeven ("need to") is a well-known NPI.

Licensing

Theories of polarity licensing, notably those based on downward entailment and nonveridicality, seek to explain which environments permit NPIs — negation, questions, conditionals, comparatives and certain quantifiers among them.
