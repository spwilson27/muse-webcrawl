Beat Happening

Beat Happening is an American indie rock band formed in Olympia, Washington, in 1982. The group — Calvin Johnson, Heather Lewis and Bret Lunsford — became one of the most influential acts in American underground music through their deliberately minimal, lo-fi sound: simple guitar lines, sparse drumming, and Johnson's deep baritone and Lewis's contrasting vocals singing direct, childlike lyrics about love and youth. The band anchors the Olympia scene and Johnson's K Records label, and is regarded as a founding influence on indie pop, twee pop and riot grrrl.

History

Johnson, Lewis and Lunsford met at The Evergreen State College in Olympia and began playing in 1982, taking their name from a student art film. Members initially lacked and traded instruments, institutionalising amateurism as an aesthetic and democratic principle. Their self-titled debut album appeared in 1985, followed by Jamboree (1988), produced with members of Screaming Trees, and Black Candy (1989). Dreamy (1991) and You Turn Me On (1992) — recorded with greater polish and widely considered their masterpiece — closed their main run of albums; the EP Music to Climb the Apple Tree By and later compilations followed, along with occasional reunions.

Influence

Kurt Cobain, who had a K Records tattoo, championed the band; their ethos — that anyone could make music without virtuosity or corporate mediation — fed directly into the punk, indie and riot grrrl movements of the Pacific Northwest. Beat Happening's records are staples of "essential indie" lists, and the band's DIY model helped define the American independent underground of the 1980s and 1990s.
