The Rapture is an eschatological concept held by some Christians, particularly within American evangelicalism, concerning the end times. It holds that shortly before or during the Second Coming of Jesus Christ, living and dead believers will be raised into the heavens to meet Christ in the air. The term derives from the Latin rapturus ("caught up"), used in the Vulgate translation of 1 Thessalonians 4:17: "Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air."

Views on timing

Believers disagree about the Rapture's timing relative to the Tribulation, a period of suffering described in biblical prophecy:

  • Pre-tribulationism — the Rapture occurs before the Tribulation, sparing the church from it.
  • Mid-tribulationism — the Rapture occurs at the Tribulation's midpoint.
  • Pre-wrath — the Rapture occurs after most of the Tribulation but before God's wrath is poured out.
  • Post-tribulationism — the Rapture and the Second Coming are a single event after the Tribulation.
  • Partial rapture — only faithful believers are raptured, at different times according to their readiness.

History

Although elements of the idea appear in earlier writers, the modern doctrine was systematized in the 1830s by the Anglo-Irish preacher John Nelson Darby, founder of dispensationalism. It spread widely through the Scofield Reference Bible (1909), prophecy conferences, and later popular works such as Hal Lindsey's The Late Great Planet Earth (1970) and the Left Behind novel series (1995–2007) by Tim LaHaye and Jerry B. Jenkins. Most historic Christian traditions — Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and most mainline Protestant churches — do not teach a separate Rapture distinct from the general resurrection and Second Coming.
