The Süleymaniye Mosque (Turkish: Süleymaniye Camii) is an imperial mosque in Istanbul, Turkey, commissioned by Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent and designed by the imperial architect Mimar Sinan. Built between 1550 and 1557 on the Third Hill overlooking the Golden Horn, it is the largest mosque of the classical Ottoman period in the city and is regarded as Sinan's masterpiece in Istanbul.

Architecture

The mosque follows the classical Ottoman scheme developed from the Hagia Sophia: a vast central dome, 26.5 metres in diameter, carried on four great piers and buttressed by two half-domes to the east and west and smaller domes and semi-domes at the corners. Four slender minarets — said to signify that Suleiman was the fourth Ottoman sultan of Istanbul, with ten balconies for the tenth sultan of the dynasty — frame the courtyard. Sinan hid hundreds of hollow jars in the dome's construction to improve acoustics, and designed an ingenious ventilation system that collected lamp soot in a chamber above the door, the soot being used to make ink.

The complex

The mosque stands within a large külliye (charitable complex) including madrasas, a hospital, a hospice and soup kitchen, a caravanserai, a bathhouse, shops, and the mausoleums (türbe) of Suleiman, his wife Hürrem Sultan (Roxelana), and Sinan himself, whose tomb lies in a small garden at the corner of the complex. The terrace behind the mosque offers celebrated views over the Golden Horn.

History

The mosque survived fires and earthquakes, including damage in 1660 and after the 1766 earthquake; it was restored in the 19th century and again in 2007–2010. It forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of the Historic Areas of Istanbul.
