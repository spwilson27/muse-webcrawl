Hierarchical Bayes model

A hierarchical Bayes model is a statistical model written in multiple levels: observed data depend on parameters, which themselves are drawn from a distribution governed by hyperparameters, which may in turn be given prior distributions.

Rationale

Hierarchy lets related units — schools, hospitals, players — "borrow strength": each unit's estimate is shrunk toward the group mean in proportion to its sample size, as in James–Stein estimation and partial pooling. Posterior inference is typically by Markov chain Monte Carlo, e.g. Gibbs sampling in BUGS or Hamiltonian Monte Carlo in Stan.

Applications

Hierarchical models are standard in small-area estimation, meta-analysis, ecology, and the famous eight-schools problem used by Rubin and Gelman to illustrate Bayesian computation.

References: Gelman et al., Bayesian Data Analysis, 3rd ed.
