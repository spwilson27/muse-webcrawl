United Nations Buffer Zone in Cyprus

The United Nations Buffer Zone in Cyprus is a demilitarised zone patrolled by the United Nations Peacekeeping Force in Cyprus (UNFICYP). Established in 1964 and extended in length and importance after the events of 1974, it separates the internationally recognised Republic of Cyprus in the south from the Turkish Cypriot-administered north. The zone stretches about 180 km across the island, from Kokkina in the west to Famagusta in the east, and varies in width from 3.3 metres in central Nicosia to 7.4 km near Morphou Bay. It covers roughly 3.3 per cent of the island's area.

History

A "Green Line" was first drawn across Nicosia at Christmas 1963 by British Major-General Peter Young, commander of a truce force, after intercommunal violence between Greek and Turkish Cypriots. UNFICYP was deployed in March 1964 under Security Council Resolution 186. Following the July 1974 coup and Turkish military intervention, the buffer zone was extended to its present form between the ceasefire lines of the two sides. The deserted beach resort of Varosha, on the edge of Famagusta, lies within the zone's eastern sector, as does the long-abandoned Nicosia International Airport.

Administration

UNFICYP divides the zone into three sectors, with contingents responsible for monitoring ceasefire lines, preventing provocations and facilitating civilian activity. Around 10,000 people live in villages inside the buffer zone, and farmers work land within it under UN arrangements. In Nicosia the Ledra Palace Hotel, inside the zone, hosts bicommunal meetings; the Ledra Street crossing opened in 2008, reconnecting the divided capital's old shopping street.

The zone remains a visible symbol of one of the world's longest-running peacekeeping deployments, its abandoned buildings and rusting barriers standing largely unchanged since 1974.
