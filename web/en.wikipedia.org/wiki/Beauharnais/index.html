Beauharnais (also de Beauharnais) is a French noble family originating in Brittany and later established at Orléans. Through the marriage of Joséphine de Beauharnais to Napoleon Bonaparte, the family became connected to the imperial houses of France, Italy, Bavaria, Sweden, Brazil and Baden.

Notable members

Alexandre, vicomte de Beauharnais (1760–1794), was a general during the French Revolutionary Wars and first husband of Joséphine; he was guillotined during the Reign of Terror. His widow, Joséphine (1763–1814), married Napoleon in 1796 and became Empress of the French. Her children from her first marriage were Eugène de Beauharnais (1781–1824), Viceroy of Italy and Duke of Leuchtenberg, and Hortense de Beauharnais (1783–1837), Queen of Holland and mother of Napoleon III.

Legacy

Through Eugène's marriage to Princess Augusta of Bavaria, the Leuchtenberg line married into the royal families of Sweden (Josephine of Leuchtenberg, Queen consort of Oscar I), Portugal and Russia. Through Hortense, the Beauharnais bloodline continued in the Second French Empire of Napoleon III. The family's descendants remain part of several European royal genealogies.
