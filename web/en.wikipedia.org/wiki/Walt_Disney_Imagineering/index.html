Walt Disney Imagineering

Walt Disney Imagineering is the research and development arm of the Walt Disney Company, responsible for the creation, design, and construction of Disney theme parks and attractions worldwide. It was founded by Walt Disney in 1952 as WED Enterprises (from the initials of Walter Elias Disney) to design Disneyland in Anaheim, California.

History

WED Enterprises began as a small group of Disney studio artists and engineers tasked with designing Disneyland, which opened in 1955. The team pioneered Audio-Animatronics with the Enchanted Tiki Room (1963) and created landmark attractions for the 1964 New York World's Fair, including "it's a small world" and the Carousel of Progress. In 1986, WED Enterprises was renamed Walt Disney Imagineering, combining "imagination" and "engineering".

Work

Imagineering's staff — "Imagineers" — include illustrators, architects, engineers, landscape designers, writers, and show producers. The organisation designs entire parks (Disneyland Paris, Tokyo DisneySea, Hong Kong Disneyland, Shanghai Disneyland), individual attractions, cruise ship interiors, and retail and resort environments. Its research groups have produced work in robotics, projection mapping, ride systems, and materials science, holding hundreds of patents.

Disney Legends and notable Imagineers

Many Imagineers have been honoured as Disney Legends, including Marty Sklar (long-time creative head), John Hench, Mary Blair (who influenced style at the studio level), Tony Baxter, and Joe Rohde, known for Disney's Animal Kingdom. Imagineering's Glendale campus also maintains the archive of its designs and the "blue sky" concept process for developing new attractions.
