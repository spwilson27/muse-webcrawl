Hyakunin Isshu

The Hyakunin Isshu (百人一首, "One Hundred Poets, One Poem Each") is a classical Japanese anthology of one hundred waka poems, each by a different poet. It was compiled by the courtier and critic Fujiwara no Teika in the 13th century (around 1235–1241), according to tradition at the request of his son's father-in-law for sliding-door panels at a villa near Mount Ogura in Kyoto — hence its other name, the Ogura Hyakunin Isshu.

The anthology forms the basis of the karuta card game.

Contents

The poems span from the 7th century to Teika's own time and are drawn from the imperial anthologies. Poets represented include Emperor Tenji, whose autumn-field poem opens the collection, the monk-poets Kisen and Saigyō, court ladies such as Ono no Komachi, Izumi Shikibu, and Murasaki Shikibu, and statesmen like Sugawara no Michizane. The poems are mostly tanka of love, the seasons, travel, and parting — the canonical themes of court waka — and the collection became the standard primer through which later generations learned classical poetry.

Karuta

The anthology is famous today as the text of uta-garuta, a card-matching game in which a reader recites the first half of a poem and players race to seize the card bearing its second half. Competitive karuta (kyōgi karuta) is played at a high level in Japan with national championships, requiring memorisation of all one hundred poems and split-second reactions. The game and the anthology feature prominently in the manga and anime Chihayafuru, which spurred renewed popular interest.

Legacy

Teika's selection shaped the Japanese poetic canon for centuries; innumerable commentaries, illustrated editions, and parodies were produced in the Edo period, and the poems remain widely memorised in Japan today.
