Balangay

A replica balangay, the plank boat of pre-colonial Philippines

A balangay, or barangay, is a type of lashed-lug plank boat from the pre-colonial Philippines. It is the oldest known watercraft found in the Philippines, with the earliest examples — the Butuan boats — carbon-dated to around 320 CE. The term also gave its name to the barangay, the smallest political unit in the Philippines, reflecting how early Filipino communities were organised around these vessels and their crews.

Construction

Balangay were built using plank-built techniques: carved planks were edge-joined with wooden dowels and lashed to protruding lugs with fibre cordage, without metal nails. They were commonly equipped with outriggers and raised sails, and some war vessels, the karakoa, were larger and more elaborate versions of the same tradition. The construction belongs to the wider Austronesian lashed-lug boatbuilding tradition spread across Maritime Southeast Asia.

Archaeology and legacy

Since 1976, at least eleven balangay hulls have been excavated near Butuan in Agusan del Norte, Mindanao. The boats are preserved and displayed by the National Museum of the Philippines. In 1987, President Corazon Aquino declared the balangay a national cultural treasure, and it was later recognised as the National Boat of the Philippines. Replica voyages, such as the 2009–2010 Voyage of the Balangay, retraced ancient Austronesian migration routes across Southeast Asia.
