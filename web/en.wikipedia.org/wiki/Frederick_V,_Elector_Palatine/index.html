Frederick V (German: Friedrich V.; 26 August 1596 – 29 November 1632) was the Elector Palatine of the Rhine in the Holy Roman Empire from 1610 to 1623 and reigned as King of Bohemia from 1619 to 1620. Because his Bohemian reign lasted a single winter, he is known as the Winter King (German: Winterkönig).

Early life and marriage

Frederick succeeded his father, Frederick IV, in 1610 under the regency of John II of Zweibrücken. As leader of the Protestant Union, he stood at the head of the Calvinist cause in the Empire. In 1613 he married Elizabeth Stuart, daughter of James I of England, a dynastic match celebrated across Protestant Europe; the couple made Heidelberg a brilliant court.

King of Bohemia and the Thirty Years' War

After the Defenestration of Prague (1618), the Protestant estates of Bohemia deposed the Habsburg Ferdinand II and offered the crown to Frederick, who accepted against the advice of many allies. His army was crushed by Imperial and Catholic League forces at the Battle of White Mountain on 8 November 1620, and he fled into exile in the Netherlands. His acceptance of the crown is widely seen as a precipitating miscalculation of the Thirty Years' War.

Exile and death

Frederick was placed under the Imperial ban, stripped of the Palatinate — which was occupied by Spanish and Bavarian troops — and his electoral title was transferred to Maximilian of Bavaria in 1623. He spent the rest of his life in exile, chiefly at The Hague, sustained by his wife Elizabeth, the "Winter Queen", and died at Mainz in 1632. His son Charles Louis recovered part of the Palatinate in the Peace of Westphalia (1648). Frederick's daughter Sophia became Electress of Hanover and ancestress of the British royal house from George I.
