Saddle seat

Saddle seat is a style of English horseback riding designed to show off the high-stepping, animated gaits of certain horse breeds, above all the American Saddlebred, and also Morgans, Arabians, Tennessee Walking Horses and National Show Horses. It is the discipline of the "saddle" breeds in the show ring, exemplified by the World's Championship Horse Show held each August in Louisville, Kentucky.

Position and tack

The rider sits farther back than in hunt seat, with a longer stirrup, hands held high, and weight centred over the horse's loins to free the forehand for high knee action. The saddle is a flat "cutback" saddle, set back to accommodate the horse's shoulder, used with a double bridle in most divisions. Horses may be shown with long, untrimmed tails and, in three-gaited classes, a roached (shaved) mane.

Divisions

Classes include three-gaited (walk, trot, canter), five-gaited (adding the slow gait and the rack), fine harness, pleasure, park and equitation. Saddle seat equitation, including the prestigious USEF Saddle Seat Medal, is judged on the rider's form and control. The style developed in the American South and Kentucky from the plantation tradition of breeding smooth, showy riding horses.
