Cirrhosis

Cirrhosis is late-stage scarring (fibrosis) of the Liver from long-term damage: healthy tissue is replaced by scar and regenerative nodules, distorting the liver's architecture and blood flow until it fails.

Causes

Leading causes are chronic Alcohol misuse, viral Hepatitis (B and C) and fatty liver disease from Obesity / Metabolic syndrome (MASLD). Others include autoimmune hepatitis, haemochromatosis (iron overload) and bile-duct diseases.

Complications and treatment

Portal hypertension causes fluid build-up (ascites), engorged veins that can bleed catastrophically (varices) and an enlarged spleen; failing detoxification causes hepatic encephalopathy. Cirrhosis also raises liver-cancer risk. Early fibrosis can regress if the cause is removed (abstinence, antiviral cure); advanced cirrhosis's only definitive treatment is liver transplantation.
