The Gaza Empire (1820s–1895) was an African empire established by the Nguni general Soshangane during the Mfecane, the period of upheaval following the rise of Shaka Zulu. It extended across much of what is today southern Mozambique and parts of south-eastern Zimbabwe, and was the last of the great Nguni kingdoms to fall to European colonialism.

History

Soshangane, a general of the Ndwandwe who had broken with Zulu power, migrated north with his followers, conquered the peoples between the Komati and Zambezi rivers, and assimilated large numbers of local Tsonga and Shona subjects into regiments organised on the Nguni model. His successors Mawewe and Mzila fought a civil war (Mzila won with Portuguese assistance), and Mzila's son Gungunhana (Ngungunyane) became the last emperor.

The Gaza state based its power on military regiments, control of the ivory trade and tribute, and raids for cattle and slaves. Gungunhana resisted increasing Portuguese encroachment but was defeated and captured at Chaimite in 1895 and exiled to the Azores, where he died in 1906.
