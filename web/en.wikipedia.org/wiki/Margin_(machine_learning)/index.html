Margin (machine learning)

In machine learning, the margin of a classifier refers to the distance between the decision boundary and the nearest training examples. Large margins are associated with better generalisation, a principle formalised in statistical learning theory and embodied in support vector machines.

Support vector machines

A support vector machine (SVM) finds the hyperplane that maximises the margin — the gap between the closest points of different classes, called support vectors. Maximising the margin minimises an upper bound on the expected classification error, related to VC dimension bounds.

Soft margins

For non-separable data, the soft-margin formulation (Cortes and Vapnik, 1995) allows some points to violate the margin at a cost controlled by a regularisation parameter, implemented via slack variables and hinge loss.

Margin in boosting and perceptrons

Margin theory also explains the effectiveness of boosting algorithms such as AdaBoost, which continue to increase margins even after training error reaches zero. The perceptron algorithm's convergence depends on the margin of a separating hyperplane.
