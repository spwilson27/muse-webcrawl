Hill system

The Hill system is a system of chemical nomenclature devised by the American chemist Edwin A. Hill and published in 1900, used for writing the empirical and molecular formulas of chemical compounds. It is the standard system used in chemical databases and printed indexes.

Rules

In the Hill system, carbon atoms are cited first, followed by hydrogen atoms, and then all other elements in alphabetical order. For example, acetic acid is written as C2H4O2. If the compound contains no carbon, all elements, including hydrogen, are listed alphabetically; for example, ammonia is written as H3N.

Use

The system is used by Chemical Abstracts Service and in most chemical formula indexes, allowing compounds to be located by their composition.
