Drass

Country India
Union territory Ladakh
District Kargil
Elevation about 3,300 m (10,800 ft)

Drass (also spelled Dras) is a town and hill station in the Kargil district of Ladakh, India. Situated on National Highway 1 between Srinagar and Leh, near the Zoji La pass, it is often called the "Gateway to Ladakh".

Climate

Drass is frequently cited as one of the coldest inhabited places on Earth. Winter temperatures regularly fall below −40 °C, and a temperature of −60 °C was reported during the winter of 1995. The town's extreme cold results from its high altitude and location in a valley exposed to frigid winds descending from the surrounding Himalaya.

History

Historically, Drass lay on the trade route between Kashmir and Ladakh and Central Asia. During the Kargil War of 1999, the heights above Drass — including Tololing and Tiger Hill — were occupied by infiltrators and recaptured by the Indian Army; the Kargil War Memorial at Drass commemorates the battle. The local population is largely Shina-speaking and Balti, predominantly Muslim.
