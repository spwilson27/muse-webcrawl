Dhabiha

Meat slaughtered by the dhabiha method is certified as halal

Dhabīḥah (Arabic: ذبيحة) is the prescribed method of ritual slaughter of animals in Islamic law. Meat from animals slaughtered in accordance with these requirements is considered halal (permitted) for consumption by Muslims, while animals that die by other means — of themselves, by strangling, a blow, a fall or goring, or those dedicated to other than God — are prohibited in the Quran.

Requirements

Traditional requirements include that the slaughterer be a sane adult Muslim (or, by many interpretations, one of the People of the Book); that the name of God be invoked at slaughter with the tasmiyah ("Bismillah"); that a sharp knife be used to sever the throat, windpipe and the blood vessels of the neck in a single swift motion while sparing the spinal cord; and that the blood be drained from the carcass. The animal should be healthy and, in contemporary discussion, treated humanely; stunning before slaughter is accepted by some certifying authorities and rejected by others, a major point of debate in modern halal certification.

Practice and regulation

Dhabiha slaughter underlies the global halal food industry, with certification bodies in many countries. Animal welfare organisations and some governments have disputed non-stun ritual slaughter, and several European countries restrict or regulate it, balancing religious freedom against animal welfare law.
