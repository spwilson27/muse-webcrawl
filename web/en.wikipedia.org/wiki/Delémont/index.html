Delémont

Canton: Jura

Country: Switzerland

Population: approx. 12,500

Language: French

Delémont (French pronunciation: [dəlemɔ̃]; German: Delsberg) is a municipality in northwestern Switzerland and the capital of the canton of Jura. It lies on the river Sorne in a valley of the Jura Mountains, roughly 30 km southwest of Basel, and is the canton's largest town.

History

The area was settled from early times and the town is first mentioned in the early ninth century. Delémont belonged for centuries to the Prince-Bishopric of Basel, was annexed by France in the 1790s, and passed to the canton of Bern at the Congress of Vienna in 1815. The town was the centre of the Jurassic separatist movement that campaigned for a French-speaking canton separate from Bern; after a series of plebiscites, the Republic and Canton of Jura was created on 1 January 1979 with Delémont as its capital.

Geography and transport

Delémont is a railway junction on the line between Basel and Biel/Bienne, with trains also toward Porrentruy and Belfort in France, and stands on the A16 motorway (Transjurane). Its old town preserves gates, fountains, and churches including Saint-Marcel.

Economy and culture

Local industries include watchmaking, machine tools, and the knife maker Wenger, long based in Delémont, one of the two makers of the Swiss Army knife. The town hosts the cantonal government and a branch of the Jura cantonal museum.
