Riot police

Riot police
RoleCrowd control, public order, riot suppression
EquipmentShields, helmets, batons, tear gas, water cannon

Riot police are police officers or units specially trained and equipped to control crowds, disperse unlawful assemblies, and suppress riots and violent public disorder. They are distinguished from ordinary police by their protective equipment — typically helmets with visors, body armour, shields and batons — and by the use of less-lethal weapons and formations designed for crowd control.

Role and tactics

Riot units are deployed at demonstrations, strikes, sporting events and civil unrest where there is a risk of violence. Their tactics include forming shield walls and cordons to contain or channel crowds, snatch squads to arrest identified ringleaders, mounted police and police dogs, and the graduated use of chemical irritants such as tear gas and pepper spray, water cannon, rubber and plastic bullets, and baton charges. In some jurisdictions containment tactics such as "kettling" have been used to hold crowds in a confined area for extended periods.

Modern public-order policing in many countries emphasises dialogue, facilitation of lawful protest and "no surprises" graduated responses, reserving riot formations for serious disorder.

Units around the world

Many countries maintain dedicated formations. France's Compagnies républicaines de sécurité (CRS) and the mobile gendarmerie are among the best known, and their style of equipment — padded armour, round shields and long batons — has been widely copied. Germany's Bereitschaftspolizei, Japan's Kidō-tai, South Korea's combat police, and specialist public-order units of British police forces are other examples. In the United States, riot response is usually provided by ordinary departments augmented with tactical teams rather than a permanent national riot force.

Criticism

Riot policing is frequently criticised for escalating tensions, for the injuries caused by less-lethal munitions and chemical agents, and for suppressing lawful protest. International human rights bodies have issued guidance on the use of force in assemblies, stressing necessity and proportionality.
