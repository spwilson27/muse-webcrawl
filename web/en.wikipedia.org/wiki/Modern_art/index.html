Modern art

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A gallery wall of modern paintings: bold abstract canvases in primary colours beside a Cubist portrait]

Modern art includes artistic work produced roughly from the 1860s to the 1970s, denoting the styles and philosophies of the art of that era. Modern artists experimented with new ways of seeing and with fresh ideas about the nature of materials and the functions of art, moving away from narrative toward abstraction.

The pioneers are often taken to include the Impressionists, after which followed movements such as Post-Impressionism, Fauvism, Cubism, Expressionism, Dada, Surrealism, and later Abstract Expressionism and Pop art. Modern art is distinguished from 'contemporary art', which follows it.

Characteristics

Modernism in art involved a self-conscious rejection of academic tradition: Édouard Manet and the Impressionists broke Salon conventions; Picasso and Braque fractured form in Cubism; Kandinsky and Mondrian pursued pure abstraction. New materials and technologies — tube paints, photography, industrial fabrication — reshaped practice.

Institutions

Major collections include the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) in New York, Tate Modern in London and the Centre Pompidou in Paris.
