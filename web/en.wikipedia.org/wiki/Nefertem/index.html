Nefertem, god of the lotus, perfume and healing, as a child of the primordial lotus.

Nefertem (also spelled Nefertum) was an ancient Egyptian god associated with the lotus flower, perfume, healing and the first sunlight of creation. In the theology of Memphis he formed a triad with his father Ptah and his mother, the lioness goddess Sekhmet; in other traditions his mother was the cat goddess Bastet.

Role and symbolism

Nefertem was said to have emerged from the primordial waters of Nun as a child sitting upon — or as the blossom of — the sacred blue lotus (Nymphaea caerulea), whose flower opens with the rising sun. In the Pyramid Texts he is called "the lotus blossom which is before the nose of Ra", linking him to fragrance, breath and the daily rebirth of the sun. Because the lotus was used in perfumes and medicines, Nefertem became a god of perfume, aromatics and healing, and he was invoked for relief from pain and illness.

Iconography

Nefertem is depicted as a handsome young man wearing a headdress of a lotus blossom flanked by two plumes and menat necklaces, sometimes as a child seated on a lotus, and occasionally with leonine features reflecting his descent from Sekhmet. He could also be shown as a lion or a lion-headed man. Small faience amulets and bronze figures of the god were common in the Third Intermediate and Late Period, when his cult gained popularity.
