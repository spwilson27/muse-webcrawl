Paititi

Paititi is a legendary Inca lost city or utopian rich land said to lie east of the Andes, hidden within the dense rainforests of southeast Peru, north Bolivia, or southwest Brazil. According to legend, after the Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire, Incas withdrew to the jungles and established a secret refuge where their treasures were hidden and their culture preserved, free of European influence.

Legend

The name is sometimes associated with the Inkari myth, in which the last Inca retreats to await the restoration of his kingdom. Colonial-era documents, including reports by Jesuit missionaries, mention Paititi as a wealthy kingdom beyond the frontier of Spanish settlement. Some scholars link the legend to real Inca activity in the lowlands east of Cuzco, where the Incas traded with forest peoples and maintained outposts, or to the legendary Vilcabamba, the last Neo-Inca capital, which was identified in the twentieth century at Espíritu Pampa.

Searches

Numerous expeditions have searched for Paititi, particularly in the Madre de Dios and Pantiacolla regions of Peru. In 2001, the Italian archaeologist Mario Polia discovered the report of the Jesuit missionary Andrea Lopez in the archives of the Vatican, a document from around 1600 describing a large, wealthy city called Paititi inhabited by people who traded with the Inca. Modern satellite imagery and lidar surveys of the Peruvian and Bolivian jungle have revealed genuine pre-Columbian settlements and earthworks, lending plausibility to lowland occupation, though no site has been proven to be the legendary Paititi itself.

In popular culture

Paititi has featured in novels, films, and video games as a prototypical lost city of gold, alongside related legends such as El Dorado.
