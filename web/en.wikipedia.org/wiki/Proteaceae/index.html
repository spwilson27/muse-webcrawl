The Proteaceae form a family of flowering plants predominantly distributed in the Southern Hemisphere. The family comprises about 80 genera with about 1,700 species, and together with the Platanaceae it makes up the order Proteales. Well-known genera include Protea, Banksia, Grevillea, Hakea, Leucadendron, Macadamia and Telopea (waratah).

Distribution

The family is a classic example of a Gondwanan distribution: its greatest diversity is in Australia (about 45 genera) and South Africa (about 14 genera), with smaller numbers in South and Central America, Asia, New Guinea and the Pacific. This pattern reflects the family's origin before the breakup of the supercontinent Gondwana.

Description

Members are mostly shrubs and trees, rarely herbs. Leaves are often tough and leathery with stomata protected in pits. Flowers are usually grouped in showy heads, spikes or racemes; each flower typically has four tepals, and in many genera the style protrudes conspicuously before the flower opens. Fruits range from nuts and drupes to woody follicles that open after fire.

Ecology

Proteaceae dominate many nutrient-poor landscapes, notably the fynbos of South Africa and the kwongan and banksia woodlands of Australia. Most species have cluster (proteoid) roots that mine phosphorus from impoverished soils — a trait that also makes many species extremely sensitive to phosphorus-rich fertilisers. Many are adapted to fire, either resprouting from lignotubers or releasing canopy-stored seed.

Uses

The family includes important ornamentals (proteas, banksias, grevilleas), timber species, and the macadamia nut, the only internationally traded food crop from the family.
