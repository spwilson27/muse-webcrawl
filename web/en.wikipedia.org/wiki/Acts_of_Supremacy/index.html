Acts of Supremacy

Acts of Supremacy
1534: Henry VIII
1558: Elizabeth I
Effect: Monarch as head / supreme governor of Church of England

The Acts of Supremacy are two acts passed by the Parliament of England in the 16th century that established the English monarchs as the head of the Church of England. The 1534 Act declared Henry VIII supreme head on earth of the Church of England, and the 1558 Act under Elizabeth I restored a similar settlement after the reign of Mary I, using the title Supreme Governor.

Act of 1534

Henry VIII, seeking annulment of his marriage to Catherine of Aragon and break with papal authority, had Parliament pass the Act of Supremacy in November 1534. It confirmed that the king was, and had been, the only supreme head on earth of the Church of England, called Ecclesia Anglicana, with power to visit, reform, and correct heresies and abuses.

Repeal and restoration

Mary I repealed the act as she restored papal authority and Roman Catholicism. After her death, Elizabeth I's Act of Supremacy 1558 revived the Henrician legislation, repealed Mary's, and required an Oath of Supremacy from clergy and officeholders, while softening the royal title to Supreme Governor.

Legacy

The acts are foundational to the English Reformation and to the monarch's continuing role as Supreme Governor of the Church of England, although a later act under James I and subsequent history modified their penal provisions.
