Soviet Air Forces

The Soviet Air Forces (Russian: Военно-воздушные силы, Voyenno-vozdushnye sily, VVS) were the air arm of the Soviet Armed Forces, formally established in 1918 from the remnants of the Imperial Russian air service. By the late Cold War they were among the largest air forces in the world.

Organisation

The VVS was divided between Frontal Aviation, supporting ground forces with fighters, fighter-bombers and helicopters, and Long-Range Aviation, operating strategic bombers such as the Tu-95 and Tu-160. Air defence of the homeland was largely the separate mission of the PVO (Voyska PVO), with its own interceptors and missile forces, until the services were merged in the Russian Federation after 1991. Soviet Naval Aviation was also separate.

History

Devastated in the German invasion of 1941, the VVS rebuilt with aircraft such as the Il-2 Shturmovik — the most-produced combat aircraft in history — and by 1945 fielded tens of thousands of machines. In the Cold War it developed successive generations of MiG and Sukhoi fighters, pioneered operational jet aviation, and supplied aircraft and advisers to allied states. After the dissolution of the USSR in 1991, its units and equipment were divided among the successor states, principally Russia.
