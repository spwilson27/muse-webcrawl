George Martin

Sir George Henry Martin CBE (3 January 1926 – 8 March 2016) was an English record producer, arranger, composer and musician.

Born: Holloway, London
Died: Wiltshire, England
Known for: Producing the Beatles; founder of AIR Studios

Sir George Martin was an English record producer often called the "fifth Beatle" for his extensive involvement in the work of the Beatles. As head of EMI's Parlophone label he signed the band in 1962 and produced nearly all of their recordings, contributing arrangements and performing on several tracks.

Early life and Parlophone

Martin was born in Holloway, London, learned piano by ear, and served in the Fleet Air Arm during the Second World War. He studied at the Guildhall School of Music and worked at the BBC before joining EMI in 1950, becoming head of Parlophone in 1955. There he produced classical and comedy records, including hits for Peter Sellers and the Goons, as well as chart-toppers for Cilla Black, Gerry and the Pacemakers, and Billy J. Kramer.

The Beatles

Martin auditioned the Beatles in June 1962 after they had been rejected by other labels, signed them, and suggested replacing drummer Pete Best with Ringo Starr. From "Please Please Me" onward he shaped their sound: writing string arrangements ("Yesterday", "Eleanor Rigby"), playing the piano solo on "In My Life", arranging the orchestral crescendo of "A Day in the Life", and overseeing the experimental techniques of Revolver and Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band. His last Beatles album as producer was Abbey Road (1969).

Later career

In 1965 Martin co-founded Associated Independent Recording (AIR) and built AIR Studios in London and later on Montserrat. He produced artists including Jeff Beck, Elton John, and Celine Dion, composed film scores such as Live and Let Die, and in 1997 produced Elton John's re-recorded "Candle in the Wind 1997", the best-selling single in UK and US chart history. He was knighted in 1996 and died in 2016, aged 90.
