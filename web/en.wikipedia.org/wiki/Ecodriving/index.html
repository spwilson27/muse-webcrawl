Ecodriving

Ecodriving (also written eco-driving) is a term used to describe the economical and environmentally conscious use of a vehicle, through driving techniques, vehicle maintenance, and journey planning that reduce fuel consumption and emissions. Studies commonly find that ecodriving can cut fuel use by roughly 5–15% for the average driver, with larger gains for aggressive drivers.

Techniques

Common ecodriving practices include:

  • Accelerating gently and anticipating traffic flow to avoid unnecessary braking
  • Maintaining a steady speed and using the highest suitable gear
  • Reducing idling by switching off the engine during longer stops
  • Removing excess weight and roof racks to cut aerodynamic drag
  • Keeping tyres correctly inflated and the engine properly serviced
  • Avoiding short trips, combining journeys, and considering alternatives to driving

Programmes and technology

Many governments and fleet operators run ecodriving training programmes; the European Union has promoted ecodriving as part of its climate policy, and some countries include it in driver licensing tests. Modern vehicles assist with gear-shift indicators, start-stop systems, real-time consumption displays, and navigation that can route for fuel economy. Ecodriving also generally improves road safety, since smoother anticipation reduces collision risk.
