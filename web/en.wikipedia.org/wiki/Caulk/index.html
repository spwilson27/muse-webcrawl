Caulk

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Caulk (also caulking) is a material used to seal joints or seams against leakage in various structures and piping. The term also denotes the process of applying the material. Caulking is used to make joints airtight and watertight, to close gaps around windows, doors, baths and plumbing, and in traditional wooden shipbuilding to seal the seams between hull planks.

History

Traditional ship caulking drove oakum — tarred hemp or jute fibre — into the seams between planks with a caulking iron and mallet, then sealed them with hot pitch. The trade of the caulker was a skilled shipyard craft. In building construction, early sealants included mortar, putty and oil-based compounds, replaced in the 20th century by synthetic elastomeric products.

Types

Acrylic latex caulk is water-based, paintable and easy to clean up, suited to interior trim. Silicone caulk is highly water- and temperature-resistant, ideal for baths and kitchens, but generally not paintable. Polyurethane sealants are durable and paintable for exterior joints; butyl rubber is used for gutters and concealed joints; hybrid "siliconised acrylic" products combine paintability with improved water resistance. Fire-rated and specialist acoustic caulks are used in construction detailing.

Application

Caulk is usually dispensed from a cartridge with a caulking gun, tooled smooth with a wet finger or tool, and cured by evaporation, moisture or chemical reaction depending on chemistry. Unlike structural sealants and adhesives, caulk is intended primarily as a filler and weather seal in low-movement joints.
