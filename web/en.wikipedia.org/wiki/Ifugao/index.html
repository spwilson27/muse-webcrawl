Ifugao

Ifugao is a landlocked province in the Cordillera Administrative Region of the Philippines, on the island of Luzon. It is named after the Ifugao people, an Igorot ethnolinguistic group renowned for building and farming the great rice terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras, five clusters of which are inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The provincial capital is Lagawe.

Geography

Ifugao lies in the rugged Gran Cordillera Central mountain range of northern Luzon, bordered by Mountain Province, Isabela, Nueva Vizcaya, and Benguet. The terrain is mountainous and forested, cut by the Ibulao and other rivers. The province has eleven municipalities, including Banaue, Kiangan, Hungduan, and Mayoyao, whose terraced valleys are among the most photographed landscapes in the Philippines.

The Ifugao people and their terraces

The Ifugao (from i-pugo, "people of the earth") traditionally organized society around kin groups and wet-rice cultivation on irrigated terraces (payoh) fed by communal forests (muyung) that protect the watershed. The terraces of Batad, Bangaan, Hapao, Kiangan, and Mayoyao — some long attributed an age of two millennia, though recent scholarship proposes more recent dates — were inscribed by UNESCO in 1995 as the first property under the "cultural landscape" category. Ifugao culture is also known for the hudhud harvest chants, proclaimed by UNESCO a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, and for elaborate prestige feasts, woodcarving, and the bulul rice-god statues.

History

The Ifugao resisted Spanish colonization longer than lowland peoples, partly owing to their mountain strongholds. During the Second World War, Kiangan was the site of the surrender of General Tomoyuki Yamashita in September 1945; the Kiangan Shrine commemorates the event. Ifugao became a separate province in 1966 when the old Mountain Province was divided. In recent decades the province has faced challenges from out-migration, terrace abandonment, and the giant earthworm damage to terrace walls, prompting conservation programs.
