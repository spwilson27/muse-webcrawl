The Miracles were an American vocal group formed in Detroit, Michigan, in 1955 — the first group signed to Berry Gordy's Tamla label and the act that launched Motown Records. Fronted by lead singer and songwriter Smokey Robinson, the group was billed from 1967 as Smokey Robinson and the Miracles.

Career

The group, originally called the Five Chimes and then the Matadors, came to Gordy's attention in 1957; Robinson and Gordy formed a songwriting and production partnership that helped define the Motown sound. Their single "Shop Around" (1960) became Motown's first million-selling record and its first major national hit. Through the 1960s the Miracles scored a string of classics written largely by Robinson, including "You've Really Got a Hold on Me", "Mickey's Monkey", "I Second That Emotion", "Going to a Go-Go", and "The Tracks of My Tears" — widely regarded as one of the greatest soul records ever made. In 1970, "The Tears of a Clown", recorded in 1967, topped the charts in both the US and UK.

After Robinson

Robinson left the group in 1972 to pursue a solo career and his role as a Motown executive. With new lead singer Billy Griffin, the Miracles scored one more major hit, the disco number one "Love Machine" (1975), before disbanding in 1978.

Legacy

The Miracles were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2012 — twenty-five years after Robinson's solo induction in 1987, a delay long criticised by fans and historians. Several of their recordings are in the Grammy Hall of Fame, and the group received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 2009.
