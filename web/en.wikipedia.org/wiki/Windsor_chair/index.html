Windsor chair

A Windsor chair is a chair built with a solid wooden seat into which the chair-back and legs are round-tenoned, in contrast to standard chairs whose back legs and uprights are continuous. The seats are often sculpted for comfort. Windsor chairs take many forms, including bow-back, comb-back, hoop-back and continuous-arm designs.

History

Windsors originated in England around the turn of the 18th century, taking their name from the town of Windsor, and spread rapidly to North America, where chair-makers in Philadelphia and New England developed distinctive regional styles. They were commonly painted, traditionally in green. Notable makers include Ebenezer Tracy of Connecticut.
