Bight (geography)

In geography, a bight is a bend or curve in a coastline, river, or mountain range. A coastal bight is typically a large, open, gently curving bay formed by a shallow recess in the shoreline — generally wider and less enclosed than a bay, though the terms are often used loosely.

Examples

Well-known bights include the Great Australian Bight, a huge open bay along Australia's southern coast; the Bight of Benin and the Bight of Biafra on West Africa's Gulf of Guinea; the New York Bight at the mouth of the Hudson River; the German Bight in the North Sea; and the Canterbury Bight in New Zealand.

Terminology

The word derives from Old English byht, meaning a bend or bay, related to the verb "to bow". In nautical usage, "bight" also refers to a slack curve or loop in a rope, a distinct meaning from the geographical one.
