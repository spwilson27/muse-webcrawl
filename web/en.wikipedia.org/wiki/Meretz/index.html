Meretz (Hebrew: מֶרֶצ, lit. "vigour") is a left-wing political party in Israel. It was formed in 1992 as an electoral alliance of three parties — Ratz, Mapam and Shinui — and became a unified party in 1997. Meretz is a social-democratic, secular and green party which supported a two-state solution to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict and was a prominent voice for civil rights, religious pluralism and LGBT rights in Israel.

History

In the 1992 elections the alliance won 12 seats in the Knesset and joined Yitzhak Rabin's government, where Shulamit Aloni served as Minister of Education and Yossi Sarid as Minister of the Environment. The party's influence peaked in the 1990s. In the 1999 elections, led by Sarid, it won 10 seats. Fortunes then declined: six seats in 2003, five in 2006, three in 2009, recovering to six in 2013 and five in 2015.

Under Tamar Zandberg and then Nitzan Horowitz the party won six seats in September 2019 (as part of the Democratic Union) and six in 2021, joining Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid's governing coalition, with Horowitz as Minister of Health. In the 2022 elections, led by Zehava Galon, Meretz fell below the electoral threshold for the first time and won no seats. In 2024 Meretz and the Israeli Labor Party agreed to merge into a new party, The Democrats, led by Yair Golan, ending Meretz's independent existence.

Ideology

Meretz advocated a negotiated two-state settlement based roughly on the 1967 lines, a secular state with separation of religion and state, civil marriage, public transport on Shabbat, equality for Israel's Arab citizens, social-democratic economic policy, environmental protection and strong protections for minorities and the LGBT community.
