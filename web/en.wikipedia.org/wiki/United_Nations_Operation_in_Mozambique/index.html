United Nations Operation in Mozambique

The United Nations Operation in Mozambique (ONUMOZ, from its Portuguese name) was a UN peacekeeping operation established by Security Council Resolution 797 in December 1992 to help implement the Rome General Peace Accords ending the Mozambican Civil War between the FRELIMO government and RENAMO rebels.

AbbreviationONUMOZ
Formation13 October / 16 December 1992
DissolvedJanuary 1995
HeadAldo Ajello (SRSG)

Mandate and course

ONUMOZ monitored the ceasefire, demobilised and reintegrated some 90,000 combatants, monitored police and elections, and coordinated humanitarian aid and refugee repatriation for over a million Mozambicans. Under Special Representative Aldo Ajello, the mission overcame delays and RENAMO's mistrust, using a trust fund to help transform RENAMO into a political party.

Outcome

Multiparty elections in October 1994 were judged free and fair; Joaquim Chissano and FRELIMO won, RENAMO accepted the results and took seats in parliament. ONUMOZ withdrew by January 1995 and is widely cited as one of the UN's most successful peacekeeping operations of the post–Cold War era.
