Breda

Breda (Dutch pronunciation: [breːˈdaː]) is a city and municipality in the southern Netherlands, in the province of North Brabant, near the Belgian border. With a population of around 185,000, it is one of the largest cities in the south of the country. Breda grew up around a castle at the confluence of the rivers Mark and Aa of Weerijs, was the ancestral seat of the counts of Nassau who became the Dutch royal house of Orange-Nassau, and is known for its historic centre, its Royal Military Academy, and its carnival traditions.

History

Breda was first recorded in 1125 and received city rights in 1252. In 1403 the lordship of Breda passed by marriage to Engelbert I of Nassau, founding the Dutch branch of the House of Nassau; the Renaissance tomb of Engelbert II and Cimburga of Baden in the Grote Kerk (Church of Our Lady) is a masterpiece of Netherlandish sculpture. William of Orange (William the Silent) inherited Breda as part of the Nassau patrimony and resided there; his first wife, Anna van Egmont, is buried in the church. The city changed hands repeatedly in the Eighty Years' War: Spanish forces took it in 1581, Maurice of Nassau recaptured it in 1590 with the famous stratagem of soldiers hidden in a peat barge (the "Trojan horse of Breda"), Ambrogio Spinola retook it after an eleven-month siege in 1625 — the event immortalised in Velázquez's painting The Surrender of Breda — and Frederick Henry finally secured it for the Dutch Republic in 1637. The Declaration of Breda (1660) by Charles II of England and the Treaty of Breda (1667), ending the Second Anglo-Dutch War, both took their names from the city.

Landmarks and culture

The Grote Kerk, a Brabantine Gothic church with a 97-metre tower, contains the mausoleum of the early Nassaus and the funerary monuments of the lords of Breda; the nearby Begijnhof and the Spanjaardsgat water gate survive from the old defences. Breda Castle houses the Royal Military Academy (KMA), founded in 1828, which trains officers for the Dutch armed forces. The city hosts Breda University of Applied Sciences and Avans University, the NAC Breda football club, and one of the Netherlands' most exuberant carnival celebrations, when the city takes the name Kielegat. Annual events include the Breda Jazz Festival, among the oldest in Europe.

Economy and transport

Breda is a regional service and logistics centre on the A16 and A27 motorways between Rotterdam, Antwerp, and the Ruhr, with an Intercity direct rail connection to Amsterdam and Brussels. Historic industries included brewing, matches, and the Kwatta chocolate factory; today employment centres on services, education, healthcare, and distribution.
