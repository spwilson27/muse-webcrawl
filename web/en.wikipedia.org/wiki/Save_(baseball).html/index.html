Save (baseball)


Abbreviation: SV
Type: Baseball statistic
All-time leader: Mariano Rivera (652)

In baseball, a save (abbreviated SV) is credited to a relief pitcher, usually the closer, who finishes a game for the winning team under certain prescribed circumstances described below. The save statistic was invented in 1960 by sportswriter Jerome Holtzman, who felt that existing statistics did not measure the effectiveness of relief pitchers; it became an official Major League Baseball statistic in 1969.

Criteria

A relief pitcher records a save when all of the following conditions are met:

  • He is the finishing pitcher in a game won by his team;
  • He is not the winning pitcher;
  • He is credited with at least one-third of an inning pitched; and
  • He satisfies one of the following: (a) he enters the game with a lead of no more than three runs and pitches for at least one inning; (b) he enters the game, regardless of the count, with the potential tying run either on base, at bat, or on deck; or (c) he pitches for at least three innings.

Notable records

The all-time career saves leader is Mariano Rivera of the New York Yankees with 652, followed by Trevor Hoffman with 601. Francisco Rodríguez holds the single-season record with 62 saves for the Los Angeles Angels in 2008. The rising emphasis on saves helped elevate the closer role in modern baseball, though analysts debate how much the statistic reflects true pitcher value.
