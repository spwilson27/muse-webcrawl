Glencoe

Glencoe (Scottish Gaelic: Gleann Comhann) is a glen in the Lochaber area of the Scottish Highlands, within the council area of Highland. It lies in the north of Argyll, close to the village of Glencoe and the head of Loch Leven. The glen is noted for its dramatic mountain scenery, formed by an ancient volcanic caldera and sculpted by glaciers.

Geography

The glen is U-shaped and about 16 km long. It is flanked by steep mountains including Buachaille Etive Mòr at its eastern entrance and the ridge of Aonach Eagach and the Three Sisters (Bidean nam Bian) to the south. The River Coe runs through it to Loch Leven.

Massacre of Glencoe

Glencoe is historically notorious as the site of the Massacre of Glencoe on 13 February 1692, in which 38 members of Clan MacDonald of Glencoe were killed by government troops billeted with them, after the clan's chief was judged late in swearing allegiance to William III and Mary II. The event remains a powerful symbol of betrayal in Scottish history.

Today

Much of the glen is owned by the National Trust for Scotland. It is a popular destination for hillwalking, climbing and skiing (at Glencoe Mountain Resort) and lies on the A82 road and the West Highland Way.
