Isamu Akasaki

Isamu Akasaki
Born30 January 1929, Chiran, Kagoshima, Japan
Died1 April 2021 (aged 92), Nagoya, Japan
Known forGallium nitride p-n junction; blue LED
AwardsNobel Prize in Physics (2014)
[Image: Photograph of Isamu Akasaki in his Nagoya laboratory]
Akasaki in 2014.

Isamu Akasaki (30 January 1929 – 1 April 2021) was a Japanese physicist who shared the 2014 Nobel Prize in Physics with Hiroshi Amano and Shuji Nakamura 'for the invention of efficient blue light-emitting diodes which has enabled bright and energy-saving white light sources'.

While most researchers abandoned gallium nitride (GaN) in the 1970s as an impossible crystal, Akasaki persisted at Nagoya University: in 1986 his group grew high-quality GaN on a sapphire substrate using a low-temperature buffer layer, and in 1989 achieved the first GaN p–n junction (activating p-type material by electron-beam irradiation, understood with Amano) — the two breakthroughs that made blue LEDs, and with his student Amano's and Nakamura's subsequent work, the solid-state lighting revolution, possible.

Impact

Blue was the missing primary colour: with efficient blue LEDs came white LED lighting (blue plus phosphor), full-colour displays, and Blu-ray lasers. LED lighting now displaces incandescent and fluorescent sources worldwide, saving a significant fraction of global electricity. Akasaki's decades of unfashionable persistence — he told students to follow their conviction even when the field moves on — are a staple of Japanese science lore.
