The 1884 United States presidential election was the 25th quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, November 4, 1884. In a close election marked by unusually bitter personal attacks, Democratic governor Grover Cleveland of New York defeated Republican former secretary of state James G. Blaine of Maine. Cleveland was the first Democrat elected president after the Civil War, ending a 24-year run of Republican presidencies.

Campaign

The campaign is remembered as one of the most acrimonious in American history — the archetypal "mudslinging" election. Republicans publicised the fact that Cleveland, a bachelor, was supporting an illegitimate child, prompting the chant "Ma, Ma, where's my Pa?" Democrats attacked Blaine's railroad stock dealings and the "Mulligan letters," in which Blaine appeared to solicit bribes, and answered with "Blaine, Blaine, James G. Blaine, the continental liar from the state of Maine."

Cleveland ran as a reformer opposed to corruption and high tariffs. His cause was helped by reform-minded Republicans known as "Mugwumps," who bolted their party over Blaine's reputation for corruption. In the final week, a Blaine supporter, the Reverend Samuel Burchard, denounced the Democrats as the party of "Rum, Romanism, and Rebellion," offending Irish-American Catholic voters in New York — a gaffe many historians credit with costing Blaine the election.

Cleveland won the decisive state of New York by only about 1,100 votes — the state both nominees called home — giving him the electoral majority. After Cleveland's victory, Democrats taunted Republicans with the completed chant: "Ma, Ma, where's my Pa? Gone to the White House, ha ha ha!"
