Fujiwara no Teika

Fujiwara no Teika (藤原 定家, 1162 – September 26, 1241), also known as Fujiwara no Sadaie, was a Japanese anthologist, calligrapher, literary critic, novelist, poet, and scribe of the late Heian and early Kamakura periods. He is regarded as one of the greatest masters of waka poetry and the most influential arbiter of Japanese poetic taste for centuries.

Fujiwara no Teika
Born: 1162, Kyoto, Japan
Died: 26 September 1241
Occupation: Poet, anthologist, critic

Work

Teika, son of the poet Fujiwara no Shunzei, helped compile the eighth imperial anthology, the Shin Kokin Wakashū (c. 1205), and was commissioned by the retired Emperor Go-Toba to compile the ninth, the Shinchokusen Wakashū. He is traditionally credited with selecting the Ogura Hyakunin Isshu, the famous anthology of one hundred poems by one hundred poets that underlies the karuta card game (his own self-portrait image is widely reproduced from this tradition). His critical writings, such as the Maigetsushō, and his diary Meigetsuki ("Record of the Clear Moon"), kept for over fifty years, are major sources for court life and poetics. His calligraphy and his careful copying of classical literary manuscripts preserved texts—including parts of The Tale of Genji tradition—for posterity.

His aesthetic ideals, including yōen (ethereal beauty) and the fusion of novelty with classical diction, shaped waka, renga and Noh for generations.
