Caesars Palace

Caesars Palace is a luxury hotel and casino in Paradise, Nevada, United States, on the Las Vegas Strip. The hotel is situated on the west side of the Strip, between Bellagio and The Mirage. It is one of Las Vegas's largest and best-known landmarks. Opened on August 5, 1966, the resort was developed by Jay Sarno and is decorated with an opulent Greco-Roman theme (the name has no apostrophe: Sarno reasoned that every guest should feel like a Caesar, so all guests are "Caesars").

History

Financed in part by Teamsters pension fund loans arranged through Jimmy Hoffa, Caesars Palace opened in 1966 with a lavish ceremony. It was sold several times — Sarno sold in 1969 — and repeatedly expanded with new hotel towers (including the Augustus and Octavius towers). The Forum Shops, opened in 1992, pioneered luxury retail in Las Vegas casinos. The 4,300-seat Colosseum theatre, built for Celine Dion's residency in 2003, hosted long-running residencies by Dion, Elton John, Cher and others.

In popular culture

The resort hosted major heavyweight boxing title fights in its outdoor arena (including Larry Holmes vs. Muhammad Ali, 1980, and several Sugar Ray Leonard and Marvelous Marvin Hagler bouts) and the Caesars Palace Grand Prix Formula One races of 1981–82. Evel Knievel famously crashed attempting to jump its fountains in 1967. It appears prominently in films such as Rain Man and The Hangover.
