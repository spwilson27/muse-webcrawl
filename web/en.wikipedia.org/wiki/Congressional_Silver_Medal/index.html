Congressional Silver Medal

A Congressional Silver Medal is an award bestowed by the United States Congress. Each medal is authorised by a specific act of Congress recognising a person or group for notable achievements or service, in a manner analogous to the Congressional Gold Medal but struck in silver.

Background

Congress has authorised commemorative medals since the Revolutionary War, when gold medals were voted to victorious commanders. Gold medals became the highest civilian expression of national appreciation granted by Congress, and silver (and bronze) versions were frequently authorised for wider groups of recipients, or as the principal honour when a gold medal was not judged appropriate. Designs are executed by the United States Mint, and bronze duplicates are often sold to the public.

Notable awards

Silver medals have recognised, among others, military units, first responders, and civil-rights participants. For example, the "Four Chaplains" of the Second World War were honoured with a special medal, Native American code talkers received Congressional Gold Medals for tribal nations' leaders with silver medals for individual veterans, and participants in the 1965 Selma-to-Montgomery voting-rights marches were recognised under the same 2015 act that awarded a gold medal collectively.
