C minor is a minor scale based on C, consisting of the pitches C, D, E♭, F, G, A♭, and B♭. Its key signature has three flats. Its relative major is E-flat major and its parallel major is C major.

The natural minor scale is: C – D – E♭ – F – G – A♭ – B♭ – C. In the harmonic minor, the B♭ is raised to B natural, and in the melodic minor the A♭ and B♭ are raised to A and B natural when ascending.

Character and notable compositions

C minor has long been regarded as a key of tragedy, heroic struggle and lament. Ludwig van Beethoven used it for some of his most turbulent works, including the Pathétique Piano Sonata, Op. 13, the Piano Sonata No. 32, Op. 111, the Piano Concerto No. 3, and the Fifth Symphony, which opens with the famous four-note motif in C minor before resolving into a triumphant C major finale. Mozart's Piano Concerto No. 24 and his Great Mass in C minor are other landmarks, and Johannes Brahms opened his First Symphony in C minor. Gustav Mahler's Second Symphony ("Resurrection") likewise begins in C minor and ends in E-flat major.

Scales and chords

The C minor tonic chord consists of C, E♭ and G. The dominant chord is G major (G–B–D) in harmonic practice, reflecting the raised leading tone. Because of its three-flat signature, C minor is comfortable for wind instruments and strings alike and sits at the centre of the circle of fifths' flat side for minor keys.
