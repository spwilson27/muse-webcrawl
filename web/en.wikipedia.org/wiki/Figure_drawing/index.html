Figure drawing

A figure drawing is a drawing of the human form in any of its various shapes and postures using any of the drawing media. The term can also refer to the act of producing such a drawing. The degree of representation may range from highly detailed, anatomically correct renderings to loose and expressive sketches. A life drawing is a drawing of the human figure from observation of a live model. A figure drawing may be a composed work of art or a figure study done in preparation for a more finished work such as a painting.

History

The depiction of the human figure is among the oldest subjects in art, appearing in prehistoric cave paintings. In classical antiquity, Greek and Roman artists studied the nude figure as an ideal of beauty and proportion. During the Renaissance, artists such as Leonardo da Vinci and Michelangelo made systematic anatomical studies, dissecting cadavers to understand musculature and movement, and figure drawing became central to artistic training.

From the founding of the European academies of art in the 16th and 17th centuries, drawing from the live model — the académie — became the cornerstone of formal art education. In the 19th century, ateliers such as that of the Académie Julian admitted students, including women, who were often excluded from state academies. In the 20th and 21st centuries, life drawing classes remain a staple of art schools and community workshops worldwide.

Models and sessions

Figure drawing sessions typically employ a nude model, because clothing obscures the body's structure, though draped and costumed models are also used. A session usually begins with short gesture poses lasting from thirty seconds to five minutes, progressing to longer sustained poses of twenty minutes to several hours, allowing the artist to develop proportion, tonal modelling, and detail.

Techniques and media

Common media include graphite, charcoal, conté crayon, ink, and chalk. Artists attend to:

  • Gesture — capturing the overall movement and rhythm of the pose.
  • Proportion — relating parts of the body to one another, often measured in head-lengths.
  • Anatomy — understanding the skeleton and muscle groups beneath the surface.
  • Value — the pattern of light and shadow that models form.
  • Contour and construction — describing edges, or building the figure from simple volumes such as boxes and cylinders.

Figure study

A figure study is a drawing or painting of the nude figure made as an exercise or in preparation for a larger composition. Historically, studies allowed artists to resolve anatomy, lighting, and pose away from the commission itself; many studies by Old Masters are now prized as independent works.
