Cigar Lake mine

The Cigar Lake mine is a large underground uranium mine in northern Saskatchewan, Canada, located on the south-western shore of Cigar Lake in the Athabasca Basin. It is the world's largest producing uranium mine, and its high-grade unconformity-associated deposit is among the richest uranium orebodies ever discovered.

Geology

The deposit was discovered in 1981. It lies at the unconformity between the Athabasca Group sandstones and the underlying metamorphic basement rocks, roughly 450 metres below surface. Average grades exceed 15% U3O8 in parts of the orebody — more than a hundred times the grade of most uranium deposits worldwide.

Mining method and the 2006 flood

Because the ore is hosted in weak, water-bearing ground, mining uses ground freezing and a remote "jet boring" method in which high-pressure water jets cut ore from tunnels below the deposit, avoiding exposing miners to the extremely radioactive ore. Construction was set back in October 2006 by a major water inflow that flooded the mine; remediation took years and production finally began in 2014. Ore slurry is trucked to the McClean Lake mill for processing.

Production

At full capacity the mine produces about 18 million pounds of U3O8 per year, supplying a substantial share of world uranium output.
