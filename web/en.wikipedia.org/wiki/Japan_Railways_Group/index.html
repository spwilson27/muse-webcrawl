The Japan Railways Group, more commonly known as the JR Group (JRグループ, Jeiāru Gurūpu), is a group of successors of the government-owned Japanese National Railways (JNR). The JR Group lies at the heart of Japan's railway network, operating almost all intercity rail services and a large proportion of commuter rail services.

History

On 1 April 1987, the debt-laden Japanese National Railways was divided and privatized. Its operations were taken over by six regional passenger railway companies, a nationwide freight company, and several smaller organizations. The division was intended to improve efficiency and service while addressing JNR's enormous accumulated debt, which was transferred to a settlement corporation.

Member companies

  • Hokkaido Railway Company (JR Hokkaido)
  • East Japan Railway Company (JR East)
  • Central Japan Railway Company (JR Central)
  • West Japan Railway Company (JR West)
  • Shikoku Railway Company (JR Shikoku)
  • Kyushu Railway Company (JR Kyushu)
  • Japan Freight Railway Company (JR Freight)

The three largest passenger companies — JR East, JR Central, and JR West — were fully privatized and are listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange, while JR Hokkaido, JR Shikoku, and JR Freight remain under government ownership through the Japan Railway Construction, Transport and Technology Agency.

Operations

The JR companies operate the Shinkansen high-speed lines as well as conventional railways covering the four main islands of Japan. The Japan Rail Pass, available to foreign visitors, is valid across the JR network.
