Proscenium

A proscenium is the vertical plane of space in a theatre that separates the stage from the auditorium and through which the audience watches the performance. In the classic picture-frame stage, the proscenium is framed by a decorative proscenium arch, with the curtain hanging just behind it.

History

The term comes from the Greek proskēnion, the front wall of the stage building in ancient Greek theatre. The modern arch developed in Italian Renaissance theatres — the Teatro Farnese in Parma (1618) is an early surviving example — and became the standard arrangement for opera houses and playhouses through the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, enabling elaborate scenery, hidden lighting and the illusionistic "fourth wall".

Alternatives

Twentieth-century theatre practice also developed thrust stages, theatre in the round, black-box and site-specific formats that abandon the proscenium. The area of stage in front of the arch is the apron; safety (iron) curtains and house tabs are fitted within the proscenium opening in many venues.
