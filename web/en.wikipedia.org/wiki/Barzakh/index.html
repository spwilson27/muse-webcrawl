Barzakh
Arabicبرزخ (barrier, partition)
ReligionIslam
MeaningIntermediate state between death and resurrection

Barzakh (Arabic: برزخ, "barrier" or "partition") is an Arabic word used in the Qur'an for a barrier between two things — for instance between fresh and salt water — and in Islamic eschatology for the intermediate state in which the soul resides after death and before the Day of Resurrection (Qiyamah).

In the Qur'an and hadith

The key verse, Q23:100, speaks of a barzakh behind the dead "until the day they are resurrected". Later tradition elaborated the concept into an account of life in the grave, including the questioning of the dead by the angels Munkar and Nakir, and foretastes of paradise or punishment. Sunni and Shia theologians have discussed the nature of this existence extensively, and in Sufi thought — notably in Ibn Arabi — barzakh also became a broader metaphysical notion of an intermediate, imaginal realm between the spiritual and physical worlds.
