Utqiagvik, Alaska

Utqiaġvik (Inupiaq: Utqiaġvik, IPA: [ut.qe.ɑʁ.vik]), formerly known as Barrow, is the borough seat and largest city of the North Slope Borough in the U.S. state of Alaska. Located north of the Arctic Circle, it is one of the northernmost cities in the world and the northernmost in the United States.

History

The area has been inhabited for over 1,500 years by the Iñupiat, an Indigenous Inuit ethnic group; the nearby Birnirk archaeological site is a National Historic Landmark. The city was named Point Barrow/Barrow after Sir John Barrow of the British Admiralty. In 2016 voters approved renaming the city Utqiaġvik, its traditional Iñupiaq name, meaning "a place for gathering wild roots".

Geography and climate

Utqiaġvik lies about 320 nautical miles north of the Arctic Circle. It has a polar climate with long, extremely cold winters and short cool summers; the sun does not set for about 82 days in summer ("midnight sun") and does not rise for about 65 days in winter ("polar night"). The average population is around 4,900.

Economy

The North Slope Borough, funded largely by taxes on Prudhoe Bay oil infrastructure, is the main employer; subsistence hunting of bowhead whales and other marine mammals remains central to Iñupiaq culture.
