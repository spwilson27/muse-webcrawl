James Stewart (Appin Murder)

Bornc. 1698, Scotland
Died8 November 1752, Ballachulish, Scotland (executed)
Known forConviction and hanging for the Appin Murder

James Stewart (c. 1698 – 8 November 1752), known as James of the Glen (Seumas a' Ghlinne), was a Scottish leader of the Stewarts of Appin. He was hanged in 1752 for the murder of Colin Roy Campbell of Glenure, the government factor of the forfeited Ardsheal estate — the crime known as the Appin Murder — in what is widely regarded as a miscarriage of justice.

The murder and trial

On 14 May 1752, Campbell was shot dead by a concealed marksman in the woods of Lettermore near Ballachulish, Argyll. James Stewart, who had acted as agent for his evicted half-brother Charles Stewart of Ardsheal and had clashed with Campbell over evictions, was arrested and tried at Inveraray before a jury dominated by Campbells, presided over by the Duke of Argyll, the Campbell clan chief. Although the likely gunman was Alan Breck Stewart, who had fled to France, James was convicted as an accomplice largely on circumstantial evidence.

Execution and legacy

Stewart was hanged at Ballachulish on 8 November 1752, and his body was left on the gibbet for years as a warning. The case became famous through Robert Louis Stevenson's novels Kidnapped and Catriona, in which both James and Alan Breck appear. Campaigns for a posthumous pardon have continued into modern times.
