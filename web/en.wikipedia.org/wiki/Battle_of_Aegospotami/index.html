The Battle of Aegospotami was a naval confrontation fought in 405 BC at the mouth of the Aegospotami river in the Hellespont, during the final phase of the Peloponnesian War. The Spartan fleet under Lysander destroyed the Athenian navy, effectively ending the war.

Background

Athens depended on grain shipped through the Hellespont from the Black Sea. After the Athenian victory at Arginusae in 406 BC, Lysander, restored to effective command of the Peloponnesian fleet with Persian funding, sailed to Lampsacus and seized it, drawing the Athenian fleet of about 180 triremes to the open shore at Aegospotami opposite.

The battle

For several days Lysander refused battle, keeping his crews ready while Athenian crews scattered ashore to forage. According to Xenophon, the exiled Alcibiades rode down to warn the Athenian generals of their exposed position and was rebuffed. On the fifth day, Lysander's scouts signalled that the Athenians had dispersed; the Spartan fleet swept in and captured nearly the entire Athenian fleet on the beach. Only Conon escaped with a handful of ships, nine or so, fleeing to Cyprus.

Aftermath

Athens, starved of grain and without a navy, surrendered in 404 BC. Its Long Walls were demolished, its empire dissolved, and the oligarchic Thirty Tyrants installed. Aegospotami thus closed more than a quarter-century of war and ended the Athenian maritime empire.
