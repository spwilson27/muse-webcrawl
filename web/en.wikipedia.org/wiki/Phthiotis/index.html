Phthiotis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Spercheios valley near Lamia.

Phthiotis (Greek: Φθιώτιδα, Phthiótida) is one of the regional units of Greece. It is part of the region of Central Greece, and its capital is the city of Lamia. The regional unit covers the northern part of the historical region of Phthiotis and the Spercheios river valley, bordering the Malian Gulf.

Geography

Phthiotis lies northeast of Phocis and north of Boeotia, south of Magnesia and Larissa, and east of Evrytania and Aetolia-Acarnania. The terrain ranges from the coastal plain on the Malian Gulf to the mountains of Othrys in the north and Oeta in the south; the Spercheios river flows eastward through the region to the gulf. The famous pass of Thermopylae, site of the battle of 480 BC, lies within Phthiotis.

History

Ancient Phthiotis was the southernmost district of Thessaly and, in Greek myth, the homeland of the Myrmidons and of Achilles, whose father Peleus ruled there. The modern prefecture was created in the nineteenth century after Greek independence; under the 2011 Kallikratis reform the Phthiotis regional unit retained the boundaries of the former prefecture, with municipalities including Lamia, Domokos, Makrakomi, Stylida, and Amfikleia-Elateia.
