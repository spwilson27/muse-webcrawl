Enlisted rank

An enlisted rank (also called an enlisted grade) is a military rank held by personnel who are not commissioned officers. Enlisted members join the armed forces by enlistment rather than by receiving a commission, and they form the great majority of any military's strength. Above the most junior enlisted grades stand the non-commissioned officers (NCOs) — corporals and sergeants — who exercise direct leadership over small units and are often called the "backbone of the army".

Structure

Enlisted ranks typically progress from entry-level grades (private, seaman recruit, airman basic) through journeyman grades to junior NCOs and then senior NCOs (sergeant major, master chief petty officer, chief master sergeant), who advise commanders and represent the enlisted force. NATO standardises these grades with the codes OR-1 to OR-9 ("other ranks") to allow comparison between countries. In the United States, enlisted pay grades run from E-1 to E-9 across all services, though the rank titles differ: an E-7 is a sergeant first class in the Army, a gunnery sergeant in the Marine Corps, a chief petty officer in the Navy and a master sergeant in the Air Force.

Distinctions

Enlisted personnel are distinguished from commissioned officers, who hold authority granted by the head of state, and from warrant officers, who occupy a specialist position between the two in some countries. Historically, enlisted soldiers signed on for a fixed term of service and were subject to a separate disciplinary code and separate living quarters from officers. Promotion within the enlisted ranks is generally competitive and based on time in service, examinations, performance evaluations and available vacancies.

By country

In the British Armed Forces, enlisted personnel are known as "other ranks"; senior grades include lance corporal, corporal, sergeant, staff/colour sergeant, and warrant officers class 2 and 1 at the top of the enlisted structure. Many Commonwealth and European forces follow similar patterns, with conscript armies often employing a larger proportion of junior enlisted grades on short engagements.
