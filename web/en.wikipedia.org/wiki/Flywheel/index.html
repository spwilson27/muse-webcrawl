Flywheel

A flywheel stores rotational energy and smooths the delivery of power.

A flywheel is a mechanical device which uses the conservation of angular momentum to store rotational energy; a form of kinetic energy proportional to the product of its moment of inertia and the square of its rotational speed. In particular, assuming the flywheel's moment of inertia is constant, when a torque is applied, the flywheel's rotational speed changes; conversely, if the flywheel is spinning, applying a load causes it to slow and release energy.

Common uses of a flywheel include smoothing the power output of an energy source. For example, flywheels are used in reciprocating engines because the torque produced by an individual piston is intermittent; this smoothing action helps reduce vibration and uneven wear. Flywheels are also used for energy storage in flywheel energy storage systems, as an alternative to batteries for applications such as uninterruptible power supplies, grid frequency regulation and regenerative braking in vehicles (KERS).

Physics

The kinetic energy stored in a rotating flywheel is E = ½Iω², where I is the moment of inertia and ω the angular velocity. For a solid cylinder, I = ½mr², so most of the mass concentrated at the rim (as in a hoop or a rimmed wheel) maximises stored energy for a given mass. The maximum practical rotational speed is limited by the tensile strength of the material, which is why high-performance flywheels are built from carbon-fibre composites and spin in vacuum enclosures on magnetic bearings to minimise friction losses.

History

The principle is ancient: the potter's wheel and the spindle whorl exploit stored rotational energy. Flywheels were integral to the steam engines of the Industrial Revolution — James Watt's engines used large flywheels to maintain steady rotation — and to early internal combustion engines. In the 20th century they were used in gyrocompasses, punch presses and experimental flywheel-powered buses (the Gyrobus). Modern research focuses on high-speed composite flywheels for grid storage and spacecraft attitude control (momentum wheels).

Applications

  • Reciprocating engines — smoothing torque pulses
  • Flywheel energy storage systems for power grids and data centres
  • Kinetic energy recovery systems in motorsport and hybrid vehicles
  • Riveting machines, presses and shears, where stored energy delivers brief high power
  • Attitude control in satellites
