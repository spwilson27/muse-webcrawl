Frailty syndrome

Frailty is a common geriatric syndrome that embodies an elevated risk of catastrophic declines in health and function among older adults. Frailty is a condition associated with ageing, characterised by reduced physiological reserve and increased vulnerability to stressors such as illness, surgery or minor injury.

Definition and assessment

Two dominant approaches define the syndrome. The Fried frailty phenotype (2001) counts five criteria — unintentional weight loss, exhaustion, weak grip strength, slow walking speed and low physical activity; a person meeting three or more is classified as frail. The Rockwood frailty index instead measures the accumulation of health deficits (symptoms, diseases, disabilities) as a proportion of those considered.

Causes and consequences

Frailty is thought to arise from cumulative dysregulation across multiple systems — inflammatory, endocrine, musculoskeletal (sarcopenia) and cardiovascular. It predicts falls, hospitalisation, disability, institutionalisation and death. Interventions with evidence of benefit include resistance exercise, nutritional support and comprehensive geriatric assessment; identifying pre-frailty is a target for prevention.
