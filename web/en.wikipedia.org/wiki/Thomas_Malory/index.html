Thomas Malory

Sir Thomas Malory (c. 1415–1418 – 14 March 1471) was an English writer, the author of Le Morte d'Arthur, the classic English-language chronicle of the Arthurian legend, compiled from French and other sources and published posthumously by William Caxton in 1485. The identification of the author with Sir Thomas Malory of Newbold Revel, Warwickshire, is widely accepted, though not beyond dispute.

Life

Malory of Newbold Revel served in France under Richard Beauchamp, Earl of Warwick, and sat in Parliament for Warwickshire in 1445. From 1450 he was accused of a string of violent crimes — ambush, robbery, cattle theft and rape — and spent much of the 1450s in and out of London prisons, though he repeatedly escaped or was bailed. A Yorkist during the Wars of the Roses, he was imprisoned again under Edward IV and excluded from pardons in 1468 and 1470, dying in 1471, probably in prison near Newgate.

Le Morte d'Arthur

Malory describes himself in the work as a "knight prisoner" who finished his book in the ninth year of Edward IV (1469–1470). Drawing mainly on French prose romances of the Vulgate Cycle and the Prose Tristan, together with English poems such as the Alliterative Morte Arthure, he reshaped the sprawling Arthurian material into a coherent English prose narrative of the rise and fall of Arthur's kingdom.

Caxton's printed edition of 1485 long remained the only known text; in 1934 a manuscript version (the Winchester Manuscript) was discovered at Winchester College, differing in wording and in how the tales are divided.

Legacy

Le Morte d'Arthur became the principal source for nearly all later English retellings of the Arthurian legend, from Tennyson's Idylls of the King to T. H. White's The Once and Future King.
