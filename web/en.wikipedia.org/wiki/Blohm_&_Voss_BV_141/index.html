The Blohm & Voss BV 141 was a German World War II tactical reconnaissance aircraft, notable for its striking structural asymmetry: the crew gondola was mounted on the starboard side of the wing while the engine and tail boom sat to port. It was designed by Richard Vogt to give the crew an unobstructed field of view.

Development

The 1937 Air Ministry specification called for a single-engined observation aircraft. Vogt's radical layout balanced engine torque with the offset fuselage. The prototypes flew well from 1938, but the Luftwaffe preferred the conventional twin-engined Focke-Wulf Fw 189, and a shortage of BMW 801 engines, reserved for the Fw 190 fighter, ended the programme. No examples survive.
