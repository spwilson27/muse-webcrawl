Battle of Denain

The Battle of Denain was fought on 24 July 1712, during the War of the Spanish Succession. A French army under Marshal Villars defeated a Dutch–Imperial force under the Earl of Albemarle at Denain in northern France. It was one of the last major battles of the war and rescued France from a deteriorating military position.

Background

By 1712 the British government of the Duke of Ormonde had secretly restricted its forces from fighting while pursuing separate peace talks, leaving the Allied army under Prince Eugene of Savoy weakened. Eugene besieged Landrecies while his supply line ran back through Denain and Marchiennes. Villars saw the opportunity to strike the exposed Allied communications.

The battle

After a feint towards Landrecies, Villars force-marched his army by night, crossed the Scheldt and fell on Albemarle's camp at Denain before Eugene could reinforce it. The French attacked in dense columns and overwhelmed the entrenched Dutch–Imperial troops; thousands were killed, drowned in the river, or captured, including Albemarle himself. Eugene arrived too late and withdrew.

Aftermath

Denain unhinged Eugene's offensive. The French went on to recapture the Allied supply depot at Marchiennes and the fortresses of Douai, Le Quesnoy and Bouchain. The victories restored French bargaining power and helped shape the settlement reached at the Treaty of Utrecht in 1713.
