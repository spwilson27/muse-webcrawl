Astaxanthin

Astaxanthin gives salmon, trout and flamingos their pink-red colour.
Astaxanthin
ClassCarotenoid (xanthophyll); ketocarotenoid
Chemical formulaC40H52O4
AppearanceRed-orange pigment
SourcesMicroalgae (Haematococcus pluvialis), krill, shrimp, salmonids
UsesAquaculture feed colourant, dietary supplement, food colouring (E161j)

Astaxanthin is a keto-carotenoid pigment with a characteristic red-orange colour. It is produced naturally by certain microalgae, yeasts and bacteria, and accumulates in the animals that eat them — notably krill, shrimp, salmon, trout and flamingos — colouring their flesh and feathers.

Sources and production

The richest natural source is the freshwater microalga Haematococcus pluvialis, which forms astaxanthin-rich resting cysts under stress. Commercial astaxanthin is produced by algal cultivation, by fermentation of the yeast Xanthophyllomyces dendrorhous, or by chemical synthesis; most feed-grade material is synthetic, while supplement-grade "natural" astaxanthin commands a premium.

Uses

In aquaculture, astaxanthin is added to the feed of farmed salmon and trout to reproduce the pink flesh consumers expect; without it, farmed fish flesh would be pale grey. It is approved as a food colour (E161j) and widely sold as an antioxidant dietary supplement, though health claims for it remain weakly supported by clinical evidence. Unlike beta-carotene, astaxanthin is not converted to vitamin A in humans.
