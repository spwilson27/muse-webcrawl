Insurrection of 31 May – 2 June 1793

Date31 May – 2 June 1793
LocationParis, France
ResultFall of the Girondins; 29 Girondin deputies arrested; the Mountain takes control of the Convention

The insurrection of 31 May – 2 June 1793 (French: Journées du 31 mai et du 2 juin 1793) was an uprising by the Parisian sans-culottes and the radical Paris Commune that forced the National Convention to expel and arrest its leading Girondin deputies. Organized by the Montagnards in alliance with the sections of Paris, it brought the Mountain to power and opened the way to the Reign of Terror.

Background

By the spring of 1793 France faced military disaster on its frontiers, civil war in the Vendée, soaring bread prices, and bitter conflict in the Convention between the Girondins and the Montagnards led by Maximilien Robespierre and Jean-Paul Marat. When the Girondin-dominated Convention created a Commission of Twelve that arrested radical leaders in Paris, the sections rose in protest. A first rising on 31 May failed to win the deputies' expulsion, but pressure continued.

The purge

On 2 June a huge armed force — tens of thousands of National Guardsmen under François Hanriot, with cannon trained on the Tuileries — surrounded the Convention and refused to let the deputies leave until they voted the arrest of the Girondins. The Convention capitulated: 29 Girondin deputies and two ministers were placed under house arrest. Many escaped to the provinces, where their attempts to resist helped ignite the Federalist revolts.

Consequences

The purge destroyed the Girondins as a political force and consolidated power in the hands of the Mountain and the Committee of Public Safety. Combined with the emergencies of war and counter-revolution, it set the stage for the revolutionary government of 1793–94, the Law of Suspects, and the Terror.
