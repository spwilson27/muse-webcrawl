Police officer

A police officer (also called a policeman, policewoman, or constable) is a warranted employee of a police force. Police officers are charged with the apprehension of suspects, the prevention and detection of crime, and the maintenance of public order; they generally have powers of arrest and the legal authority to use reasonable force in carrying out their duties. In most countries officers wear a distinctive uniform, carry identification, and may be armed, though in a few countries — notably the United Kingdom, Ireland, Norway, and New Zealand — routine policing is unarmed, following the principle of policing by consent.

Duties

Day-to-day duties commonly include patrolling assigned areas on foot or by vehicle, responding to emergency and non-emergency calls, investigating reported crimes and securing evidence, making arrests, interviewing witnesses and suspects, directing traffic and responding to accidents, and testifying in court. Specialist roles include detectives, traffic officers, dog handlers, mounted and marine units, firearms officers, and community liaison officers. Officers are usually organised in ranks, from constable or patrol officer up through sergeant, inspector or lieutenant, and senior command ranks.

Training and entry

Recruits typically must meet minimum age, education, fitness, and background requirements and complete academy training covering criminal law and procedure, defensive tactics, firearms (where applicable), driving, first aid, and community relations, followed by a probationary period under a field training officer. Training lengths vary from a few months to several years; some countries, such as Germany and Finland, require extended professional or degree-level education.

History

The modern police officer emerged with the creation of professional, uniformed forces in the nineteenth century, notably London's Metropolitan Police, founded in 1829 by Sir Robert Peel, whose "Peelian principles" emphasised crime prevention and public approval. Earlier law enforcement had been carried out by watchmen, constables, thief-takers, and soldiers. The professional model spread worldwide during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, and women began to be appointed as officers in meaningful numbers in the early twentieth century.
