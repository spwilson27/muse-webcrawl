Johann Sebastian Bach

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Johann Sebastian Bach
Born: 21 March 1685 (O.S.), Eisenach
Died: 28 July 1750, Leipzig
Era: Baroque
Known for: Brandenburg Concertos; Mass in B minor; The Well-Tempered Clavier

Johann Sebastian Bach (1685–1750) was a German composer and musician of the late Baroque period, now regarded as one of the greatest composers of all time. He is celebrated for the intellectual depth, technical command and expressive range of works in nearly every genre except opera.

Born into a dynasty of musicians, Bach held posts as organist and court musician at Arnstadt, Weimar and Köthen — where the Brandenburg Concertos and much instrumental music were written — before becoming Thomaskantor in Leipzig in 1723, supplying weekly cantatas for the city's churches for 27 years. His output includes over 200 surviving church cantatas, the St Matthew and St John Passions, the Mass in B minor, the Goldberg Variations, The Well-Tempered Clavier and The Art of Fugue.

Largely considered old-fashioned at his death, Bach was revived in the nineteenth century — Mendelssohn's 1829 performance of the St Matthew Passion is the famous turning point — and his music has been central ever since, influencing composers from Mozart (see Mozart) to jazz musicians. Of his twenty children, four, including C.P.E. and J.C. Bach, became significant composers.
