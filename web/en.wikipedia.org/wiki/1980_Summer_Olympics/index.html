The 1980 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXII Olympiad, were held from 19 July to 3 August 1980 in Moscow, Soviet Union — the first Olympic Games held in a socialist country. They are chiefly remembered for the largest boycott in Olympic history, led by the United States in protest at the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan.

The boycott

Following the Soviet intervention in Afghanistan in December 1979, U.S. President Jimmy Carter demanded Soviet withdrawal and, when the deadline passed, championed a boycott. Some 66 eligible nations stayed away entirely or in part, including the United States, West Germany, Japan, Canada, and China; several Western nations that did attend, such as Britain and France, competed under the Olympic flag rather than their national flags. The Soviet Union and its allies retaliated with a boycott of the 1984 Los Angeles Games.

Sporting highlights

With many Western champions absent, Soviet and East German athletes dominated: the USSR won 80 gold medals and East Germany 47. Memorable contests included the middle-distance duels between British runners Sebastian Coe and Steve Ovett, who each won the other's favoured event; swimmer Vladimir Salnikov's record-breaking 1,500 m freestyle, the first under 15 minutes; and Cuban boxer Teófilo Stevenson's third consecutive heavyweight gold. The closing ceremony, in which the mascot Misha the bear shed a tear, became an iconic Soviet television image.

Legacy

The Moscow Games demonstrated both the spectacle of Soviet organisation and the vulnerability of the Olympic movement to Cold War politics, prompting later IOC efforts to insulate the Games from governmental boycotts.
