A dioptre (also spelled diopter) is a unit of measurement with dimension of reciprocal length, equal to one reciprocal metre (1 dpt = 1 m−1). It is used internationally to measure the optical power of a lens or curved mirror, which is defined as the reciprocal of the focal length measured in metres. A lens with a focal length of 0.5 m has a power of +2.00 dioptres; a focal length of −0.25 m corresponds to −4.00 dioptres.

Use in optometry

Dioptres are the standard unit in prescriptions for spectacles and contact lenses. Positive powers correct hyperopia (long-sightedness) with converging lenses; negative powers correct myopia (short-sightedness) with diverging lenses. Prescriptions also specify cylindrical power in dioptres for astigmatism. The powers of thin lenses in contact add approximately, so a combination of a +2 D and a +3 D lens behaves roughly like a single +5 D lens. The human eye has a total optical power of roughly +60 dioptres, of which the cornea contributes about +43 D.

History

The term was proposed by the French ophthalmologist Ferdinand Monoyer in 1872, building on Johannes Kepler's earlier use of the related concept in his Dioptrice (1611). Although the dioptre is not an SI unit, it is widely used in optics and vision science worldwide.
