Color balance

Color balance
Field: Photography, digital imaging
Synonym: White balance
Adjusts: Relative intensities of red, green, blue

Color balance is the global adjustment of the intensities of colours—typically the red, green, and blue primaries—in an image. Its main purpose in photography is to render colours, especially neutral tones, accurately under a given light source; the closely related process performed in cameras is called white balance. Color balance can also be used creatively to warm or cool an image's mood.

Purpose

Light sources differ in colour temperature and spectral composition: tungsten lamps render scenes orange, while shade gives a blue cast. Color balancing compensates by scaling each channel so that objects which are neutral in reality appear neutral in the image, which generally makes the whole scene look natural.

Methods

Cameras offer automatic white balance and presets for daylight, shade, tungsten, and fluorescent light, as well as manual settings in kelvins or calibration from a grey card. In film photography, colour conversion filters and film stocks balanced for daylight or tungsten served the same purpose. In post-processing, raw converters and image editors provide temperature and tint sliders, eyedropper tools, and per-channel curves.

Algorithms

Automatic algorithms include grey world, which assumes the average scene colour is neutral, and white patch methods, which take the brightest pixel as the illuminant reference. Modern computational photography combines multiple cues and learning-based estimation for mixed lighting.
