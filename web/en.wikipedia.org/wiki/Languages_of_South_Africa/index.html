Languages of South Africa

South Africa has twelve official languages: isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, Sepedi, Setswana, English, Sesotho, Xitsonga, siSwati, Tshivenda and isiNdebele, plus South African Sign Language, recognised by constitutional amendment in 2023. The number of official languages is among the highest of any country.

Speaker numbers

By first language (2022 census, approximate): isiZulu is the most common home language at about 24–25% of the population, followed by isiXhosa (~16%), Afrikaans (~10–11%) and Sepedi (~10%). English is the first language of under 9% of South Africans but is the dominant language of government, business and higher education and the most common second language. The Nguni languages (Zulu, Xhosa, Swati, Ndebele) and the Sotho–Tswana languages form the two great Bantu groupings; Afrikaans descends from Dutch and is also widely spoken by Coloured and white communities.

History

Before colonisation the region was home to Khoisan languages noted for click consonants, several of which survive in small communities, and to Bantu languages that spread over the last two millennia. Under apartheid, language policy was bound up with the "Bantustan" system and the 1976 Soweto uprising, which began as a protest against compulsory Afrikaans-medium instruction. The 1996 constitution deliberately gave equal official status to eleven languages to redress that history; in practice English dominates national public life.
