SST's catalogue defined the sound of American underground rock in the 1980s.

SST Records is an American independent record label formed in 1978 in Long Beach, California, by musician Greg Ginn, guitarist of Black Flag. The label began as Solid State Tuners, a mail-order electronics business Ginn started as a teenager, and was repurposed to release Black Flag's first EP, Nervous Breakdown (1978).

History

During the 1980s SST became the most influential label in American hardcore punk and independent rock. Its roster included Black Flag, Minutemen, Hüsker Dü, Meat Puppets, Bad Brains, Sonic Youth, Dinosaur Jr., Soundgarden, Saint Vitus, the Descendents and fIREHOSE. The label's relentless touring circuit and mail-order distribution helped build the nationwide underground network that preceded alternative rock's mainstream breakthrough.

SST also released jazz, instrumental and spoken-word records, and launched subsidiary labels including New Alliance Records (acquired from the Minutemen's D. Boon and Mike Watt). By the early 1990s, disputes over royalties and unpaid earnings led several key artists, including Sonic Youth, Dinosaur Jr. and Meat Puppets, to litigate to recover their master recordings. The label's output declined sharply, and it relocated from California to Taylor, Texas.

Legacy

SST's catalogue — including Hüsker Dü's Zen Arcade, Sonic Youth's Sister and Daydream Nation (originally co-released), the Minutemen's Double Nickels on the Dime and Dinosaur Jr.'s You're Living All Over Me — is regarded as foundational to American indie and alternative rock.
