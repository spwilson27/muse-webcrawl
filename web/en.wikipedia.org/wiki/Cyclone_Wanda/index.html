Cyclone Wanda

ImpactDevastating floods; 16 deaths in the associated flooding; extensive damage

Cyclone Wanda was a tropical cyclone that, in late January 1974, contributed to the disastrous 1974 Brisbane flood, one of the worst floods in the history of Queensland, Australia. Although Wanda itself was a relatively weak cyclone when it crossed the coast, it stalled and combined with a monsoon trough to dump immense amounts of rain over south-east Queensland over the Australia Day long weekend.

Meteorological history

Wanda developed over the Coral Sea and made landfall near Double Island Point on 24 January 1974. After crossing, the low moved slowly southward and lingered, drawing in moist tropical air. Brisbane recorded around 600 mm (some 24 inches) of rain over five days.

The 1974 Brisbane flood

The Brisbane River broke its banks, inundating thousands of homes — about 13,000 buildings were affected and roughly 6,700 houses were flooded in the Brisbane area alone, with further damage in Ipswich and the Gold Coast hinterland. Sixteen people died in the flooding, and total damage was estimated at around A$980 million (in contemporary values, with later conversions higher). The flood led to reforms in flood forecasting and land-use planning; the Wivenhoe Dam, completed in the 1980s, was built in part to mitigate such events.
