Fast casual

IndustryRestaurants
Typical checkHigher than fast food, lower than casual dining
ExamplesChipotle, Panera Bread, Shake Shack

Fast casual is a restaurant category that combines the speed and counter service of fast food with the higher-quality ingredients, made-to-order preparation, and more comfortable atmosphere of casual dining, without full table service.

Characteristics

Fast casual restaurants typically prepare food to order in view of the customer, use fresher and less processed ingredients than quick-service chains, charge noticeably more per meal, and offer alcohol in some locations. Décor is a step above fast food, and many chains emphasize customization along an assembly-line format — bowls, burritos, salads, and sandwiches built to the customer's specification.

Growth

The segment emerged in the United States in the 1990s and grew rapidly in the 2000s and 2010s, led by chains such as Chipotle Mexican Grill, Panera Bread, Five Guys, and Shake Shack. Industry analysts long described fast casual as the fastest-growing segment of the restaurant business, drawing customers trading up from fast food and trading down from casual dining.
