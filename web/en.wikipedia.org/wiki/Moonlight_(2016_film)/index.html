Moonlight is a 2016 American coming-of-age drama film written and directed by Barry Jenkins, based on Tarell Alvin McCraney's unpublished semi-autobiographical play In Moonlight Black Boys Look Blue. It stars Trevante Rhodes, André Holland, Janelle Monáe, Ashton Sanders, Jharrel Jerome, Naomie Harris, and Mahershala Ali.

Plot

The film presents three chapters in the life of Chiron, a young Black man growing up in the Liberty City neighbourhood of Miami. In "Little", the shy child Chiron is taken in by drug dealer Juan and his girlfriend Teresa, while his mother Paula succumbs to addiction. In "Chiron", the teenage protagonist is bullied at school and shares a brief, tender encounter on the beach with his friend Kevin — followed by a violent betrayal. In "Black", the adult Chiron has become a drug dealer in Atlanta, hardened and estranged, until a late-night phone call from Kevin leads to a quiet, cathartic reunion in a Miami diner.

Production

Jenkins and McCraney both grew up in Liberty City, and McCraney's mother, like Paula, struggled with addiction and died of AIDS. The film was shot in Miami in 25 days on a small budget, with cinematographer James Laxton using distinct colour palettes for each chapter. Nicholas Britell's score applies the "chopped and screwed" hip-hop technique to classical-style strings.

Reception and awards

Moonlight premiered at the Telluride Film Festival in September 2016 to universal acclaim. At the 89th Academy Awards it won Best Picture, Best Supporting Actor (Mahershala Ali), and Best Adapted Screenplay from eight nominations. Its Best Picture victory was famously announced in error — presenters initially read out La La Land after an envelope mix-up, before the mistake was corrected on stage. Moonlight became the first film with an all-Black cast and the first LGBTQ-themed film to win Best Picture, and is frequently cited among the greatest films of the 21st century.
