Colonization of Mars

The colonization of Mars is the hypothetical permanent settlement and exploitation of Mars by humans. Advocates argue it would ensure the long-term survival of the human species and enable scientific and economic development; major challenges include radiation, low gravity, a thin unbreathable atmosphere and supply logistics.

Rationale

Robert Zubrin's The Case for Mars (1996) popularised plans for semi-autonomous settlements using in-situ resource utilization — producing propellant, water and oxygen from local materials. SpaceX founder Elon Musk has made Martian settlement a central goal of the Starship program.

Challenges

Mars has an atmosphere about 0.6% the pressure of Earth's, composed mostly of carbon dioxide; surface temperatures average around −63 °C, cosmic and solar radiation is unshielded by a magnetosphere, and the health effects of 0.38 g gravity are unknown. Communication delays of up to 22 minutes one-way rule out real-time support from Earth.

Ethics and law

The 1967 Outer Space Treaty forbids national appropriation of celestial bodies but is ambiguous about private settlement. Planetary-protection concerns about contaminating possible Martian life remain debated.
