Dog agility

Dog agility is a dog sport in which a handler directs a dog through an obstacle course in a race for both time and accuracy. Dogs run off leash, and the handler can use only voice and body signals — no food, toys, or touching the dog or obstacles as incentives during a competition run.

The course

Standard obstacles include jumps, weave poles, tunnels, an A-frame, a dog walk, a seesaw (teeter), a pause table, and tire jumps. Courses are set by a judge, number 14–20 obstacles, and are unknown to competitors until they walk the course without their dog beforehand. Faults are given for knocked bars, missed contact zones on the ramps, wrong courses, and exceeding the standard course time; a fault-free run is called a "clean run".

History

Agility was introduced as an entertainment demonstration at Crufts in 1978 by trainer Peter Meanwell and teams organized by John Varley. Its popularity exploded, and it is now governed by bodies such as the Fédération Cynologique Internationale (FCI), The Kennel Club in the UK, the American Kennel Club, and the United States Dog Agility Association. Dogs compete in height classes measured at the withers, so small and large dogs race against comparable peers.
