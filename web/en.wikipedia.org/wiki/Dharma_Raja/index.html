Dharma Raja

Dharma Raja Karthika Thirunal Rama Varma (1724 – 17 February 1798), often known simply as Dharma Raja, was the Maharajah of Travancore in southern India from 1758 until his death in 1798. He succeeded his uncle Marthanda Varma, the founder of the modern kingdom, and is remembered for enlightened rule, administrative reform and resistance to the expansion of Tipu Sultan of Mysore.

Reign

Dharma Raja preserved Travancore's independence at a time of upheaval in South India. He strengthened the administration with the help of his Dewan, Raja Kesavadas, developed Trivandrum as a commercial and cultural centre, patronised Kathakali and the arts, and granted asylum to thousands fleeing Tipu Sultan's campaigns in Malabar — Hindus and Christians alike — which earned him enduring gratitude.

Wars with Mysore

When Tipu Sultan attacked the Travancore lines (the Nedumkotta) in 1789–90, Dharma Raja's forces, aided by his treaty with the English East India Company, repulsed the assault — an episode that helped trigger the Third Anglo-Mysore War, in which Travancore fought as a British ally.

Legacy

His title "Dharma Raja" ("King of Righteousness") reflects his reputation for just and benevolent government. He was succeeded by Balarama Varma.
