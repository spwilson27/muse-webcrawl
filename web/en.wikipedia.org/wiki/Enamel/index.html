Enamel

MeaningGlassy coating; tooth enamel; paint type

Enamel may refer to several related substances and techniques: a vitreous (glassy) coating fused to metal, ceramic, or glass; the hard outer layer of a tooth; and a type of glossy paint. This article gives an overview of the principal senses of the term.

Vitreous enamel

Vitreous enamel, also called porcelain enamel, is a material made by fusing powdered glass to a substrate by firing, usually between 750 and 850 °C. The powder melts and hardens into a smooth, durable, chemically resistant coating. It has been used for jewellery and decorative art since ancient times — the technique of cloisonné is a famous example — and is widely used industrially on cookware, bathtubs, signs, and appliances.

Tooth enamel

Tooth enamel is the hardest and most highly mineralised substance in the human body. It covers the crown of each tooth and consists primarily of hydroxyapatite crystals. Unlike most tissues it contains no living cells and cannot regenerate once lost, which is why protecting it from acid erosion and decay is central to dentistry.

Enamel paint

Enamel paint is a coating that dries to a hard, glossy, opaque finish. Modern enamel paints are usually oil- or latex-based and are used on surfaces requiring a durable, washable finish, such as doors, trim, and metalwork.
