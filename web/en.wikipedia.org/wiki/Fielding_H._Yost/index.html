Fielding Harris Yost (April 30, 1871 – August 20, 1946) was an American college football player and coach, athletics administrator, and lawyer. He served as head football coach at the University of Michigan for 25 seasons, where his "Point-a-Minute" teams of 1901–1905 won four consecutive national championships and outscored opponents by extraordinary margins.

Michigan career

Yost arrived at Michigan in 1901. His first team went 11–0, outscored opponents 550–0, and defeated Stanford 49–0 in the inaugural Rose Bowl. The 1901–1904 teams won 43 consecutive games before a 2–0 loss to Chicago in 1905 ended the streak. Known for his rapid pace of play, he earned the nickname "Hurry Up" Yost. Yost won six national championships and ten Big Ten titles at Michigan and later served as the university's athletic director from 1921 to 1941, overseeing construction of Michigan Stadium, which opened in 1927.

Legacy

Yost influenced the development of college football's rules and coaching profession, mentored many coaches, and was inducted into the College Football Hall of Fame as a coach in 1951. Michigan's Yost Ice Arena is named in his honour. His overall career record of 198–35–12 gave him one of the highest winning percentages in college football history.
