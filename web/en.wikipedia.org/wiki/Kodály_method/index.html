Kodály method

The Kodály method, also referred to as the Kodály concept, is an approach to music education developed in Hungary in the mid-20th century by composer and educator Zoltán Kodály and his colleagues.

Principles

Kodály held that musical literacy is for everyone, that music education should begin early, and that singing — especially of folk songs in the child's mother tongue — is the foundation of musicianship. The method uses movable-do solfège, rhythm syllables, and Curwen hand signs, proceeding in carefully sequenced steps from the known to the unknown.

Influence

The concept reshaped Hungarian school music teaching and spread internationally, influencing choral and classroom practice worldwide; Kodály-based organisations exist in many countries, and the method was inscribed by UNESCO in recognition of its safeguarding of musical heritage.
