Laura Mulvey

Born: 1941
Occupation: Film theorist, filmmaker
Known for: "Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema" (1975); the concept of the male gaze

Laura Mulvey (born 1941) is a British feminist film theorist and filmmaker, long a professor of film and media studies at Birkbeck, University of London. She is best known for her essay "Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema" (1975), which introduced the concept of the male gaze into film theory.

Work

Drawing on psychoanalysis (Freud and Lacan), Mulvey argued that classical Hollywood cinema structures looking relations around an active male viewer and a passive female image, producing scopophilic pleasure. The essay became foundational for feminist film theory and screen theory, and "the male gaze" entered wider critical vocabulary. Mulvey later qualified and extended her argument in "Afterthoughts" (1981), writings on melodrama and on stillness and the moving image in Death 24x a Second (2006).

Filmmaking

With Peter Wollen she co-directed avant-garde films including Penthesilea (1974) and Riddles of the Sphinx (1977), which put feminist theory into cinematic practice through fragmented narrative and direct address.
