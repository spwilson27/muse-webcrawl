Frank Jackson (philosopher)

Frank Cameron Jackson AC FASSA (born 31 August 1943) is an Australian philosopher, formerly head of the School of Philosophy at the Australian National University (ANU) in Canberra and a regular visiting professor at Princeton University. He is best known for the knowledge argument, a thought experiment intended to refute physicalism, and for his work on qualia, intentionality and conceptual analysis.

Born: 31 August 1943, Melbourne, Australia
Nationality: Australian
Institutions: Australian National University, Princeton University, Monash University
Main interests: Philosophy of mind, metaphysics, epistemology
Notable ideas: Knowledge argument, Mary's Room, Canberra Plan

Life and career

Jackson was educated at the University of Melbourne, where he studied mathematics and philosophy. He taught at the University of Adelaide, La Trobe University and Monash University, where he was professor of philosophy, before moving to the ANU in 1986 as head of the Philosophy Program in the Research School of Social Sciences. He directed the Research School from 1998. He is the son of the philosopher A. C. "Camo" Jackson.

The knowledge argument

In his 1982 paper "Epiphenomenal Qualia" and the 1986 article "What Mary Didn't Know", Jackson presented the story of Mary, a scientist who knows everything physical there is to know about colour vision but has lived her whole life in a black-and-white room. When Mary leaves the room and sees a red tomato for the first time, Jackson argued, she learns something new — what it is like to see red. If so, there are facts about conscious experience that physical information cannot capture, and physicalism, the doctrine that everything is physical, must be false. The argument became one of the most discussed in modern philosophy of mind, associated with the notion of qualia and the explanatory gap.

Jackson later changed his mind. In writings from the late 1990s and in Mind and Illusion (2003) he defended a representationalist form of physicalism and repudiated the knowledge argument he had created.

Other work

Jackson is a leading figure of the "Canberra Plan", the ANU-associated programme of conceptual analysis: analyse a topic by mapping folk platitudes about it and locating the properties that satisfy them. His books include Perception: A Representative Theory (1977), Conditionals (1987), From Metaphysics to Ethics: A Defence of Conceptual Analysis (1998) and, with Philip Pettit and Michael Smith, Mind, Morality, and Explanation (2004). He gave the John Locke Lectures at Oxford in 1995 on location and content.

Honours

Jackson was appointed a Companion of the Order of Australia in 2006. He is a fellow of the Australian Academy of the Humanities and of the Academy of the Social Sciences in Australia.
