Word play

Word play or wordplay is a literary technique in which the words used themselves become the main subject of the work, primarily for amusement, wit or rhetorical effect.

Forms

Word play includes puns (exploiting multiple meanings or similar sounds), spoonerisms, anagrams, palindromes, portmanteaus, double entendres, malapropisms, and phonetic play such as alliteration and onomatopoeia.

Examples and use

Shakespeare was famed for punning dialogue; Lewis Carroll coined portmanteaus in 'Jabberwocky'; comedians, crossword setters and advertisers rely on word play to surprise and delight. It appears across languages wherever ambiguity or sound similarity can be exploited.
