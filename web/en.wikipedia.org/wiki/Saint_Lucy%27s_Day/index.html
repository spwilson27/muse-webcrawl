Saint Lucy's Day

Saint Lucy's Day, also called the Feast of Saint Lucy, is a Christian feast day observed on 13 December. The observance commemorates Saint Lucy, a 3rd-century martyr of Syracuse who, according to legend, brought food to Christians hiding in the catacombs, wearing a candle-lit wreath to light her way.

Scandinavian celebrations

The feast is most widely celebrated in Scandinavia, especially Sweden, where a girl dressed in white with a crown of candles leads a procession of attendants (luciatåg), singing the song "Sankta Lucia". Saffron buns called lussekatter are traditionally eaten.

Other observances

The day is also observed in Finland, Norway, Denmark, Estonia, Italy, Croatia and Malta, where the town of Santa Luċija bears the saint's name. Falling near the winter solstice, the feast is associated with light in the darkest time of the year.
