Bodily integrity

Bodily integrity is the principle that each person has autonomy over their own body.

Bodily integrity is the inviolability of the physical body. It emphasises the importance of personal autonomy, self-ownership and self-determination of human beings over their own bodies. In the field of human rights, violation of the bodily integrity of another person is regarded as an unethical infringement, intrusive and possibly criminal.

Concept

The principle holds that every human being has the right to control what happens to their own body, and that non-consensual interference — from physical assault to forced medical treatment — requires strong justification. It underlies legal doctrines such as informed consent in medicine, the torts of battery and assault, and prohibitions on torture and corporal punishment. Two related formulations are bodily autonomy (the right to make decisions about one's body) and bodily integrity (the right not to have one's body interfered with at all).

Government and law

Bodily integrity is protected indirectly by international law: Article 3 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights guarantees security of person; the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights protects against torture and cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment and against medical experimentation without consent; and the European Court of Human Rights has located bodily integrity within the Article 8 right to private life. National constitutions and courts have applied the principle in cases concerning forced medication, blood transfusions, sterilisation and police searches of the body.

Applications and debates

The concept is invoked in debates about abortion and reproductive rights, vaccination mandates, organ donation and presumed consent, circumcision of minors, intersex medical interventions, euthanasia, drug prohibition, and the rights of children and people with disabilities. Feminist and disability-rights theorists have been central to developing the idea, while critics note tensions between individual bodily rights and public health or the claimed rights of others.
