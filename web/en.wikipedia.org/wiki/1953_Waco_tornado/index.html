1953 Waco tornado

DateMay 11, 1953
LocationWaco, Texas, United States
RatingF5 on the Fujita scale
Deaths114
Injuries597

The 1953 Waco tornado struck Waco, Texas, on the afternoon of May 11, 1953. Rated F5 in later assessments, it cut through the downtown business district during a severe weather outbreak and became one of the deadliest tornadoes in Texas and United States history. The storm killed 114 people and injured 597.

Storm and impact

The tornado developed southwest of Waco and moved into the city as rain and hail obscured the funnel. Buildings in the central business district collapsed, including multi-storey brick structures where workers, shoppers and motorists were trapped. The Dennis Company furniture store building became one of the best-known sites of death and rescue. Cars were crushed, utilities failed and streets were blocked by rubble just as rescue efforts began.

Aftermath

Rescuers, including Baylor University students, soldiers from nearby bases and volunteers from surrounding towns, dug through debris for survivors. The disaster exposed weaknesses in tornado warning dissemination and public awareness; many residents had believed Waco lay in a protected bowl where tornadoes could not strike. Reconstruction reshaped downtown, and the event remains a reference point in Texas severe-weather memory.

The tornado occurred during the destructive spring of 1953, which also included major tornadoes at Flint, Michigan and Worcester, Massachusetts. See also Waco, Texas, Tornado, Fujita scale and 1953 Flint tornado.
