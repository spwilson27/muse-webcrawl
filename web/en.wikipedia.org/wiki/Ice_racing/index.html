Ice racing

Ice racing
TypeMotorsport
SurfaceFrozen lakes, rivers, and prepared ice circuits
VehiclesCars, motorcycles, snowmobiles, ATVs

Ice racing is a form of motorsport held on frozen lakes and rivers or on groomed ice circuits, in which cars, motorcycles, and other vehicles race using studded tyres for traction. It is popular in northern latitudes, including Scandinavia, Canada, Russia, and the Alpine regions of France.

Forms

Motorcycle ice racing, governed internationally by the FIM, uses highly modified speedway-style bikes with hundreds of long spikes per tyre; riders lean at extreme angles, and the sport is especially strong in Russia. Car ice racing ranges from amateur club events on frozen lakes to France's professional Andros Trophy series, contested by silhouette cars with four-wheel steering on mountain circuits.

The Trophée Andros, founded in 1990, became the world's most prominent car ice racing championship and in its final seasons used fully electric cars before concluding in 2024.

Equipment and safety

Studded tyres may carry hundreds of metal spikes per tyre, giving grip on hard ice comparable to gravel. Venues require thick, monitored ice; warmer winters and unreliable freezing have increasingly threatened traditional lake circuits.
