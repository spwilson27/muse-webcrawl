G. N. Ramachandran

Gopalasamudram Narayana Ramachandran (8 October 1922 – 7 April 2001) was an Indian physicist and biophysicist who made foundational contributions to structural biology. He is best known for the Ramachandran plot, the standard diagram used to describe the permitted conformations of protein backbones.

Career

Ramachandran studied physics at the University of Madras and earned his doctorate at Cambridge under Sir Lawrence Bragg, working on X-ray crystallography. Returning to India, he built a leading crystallography school at Madras. He later founded the Molecular Biophysics Unit at the Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore, and spent his final years at the National Institute of Advanced Studies.

Contributions

In 1963, with C. Ramakrishnan and V. Sasisekharan, Ramachandran published the plot of backbone torsion angles (φ, ψ) showing which conformations are sterically allowed in polypeptides; the diagram became a universal tool for validating protein structures. Earlier, in 1954, he and Gopinath Kartha proposed the triple-helical structure of collagen — one of the first protein structures determined anywhere, and the only major one from India at the time. He also developed methods of convolution and image reconstruction relevant to tomography.

Honours

Ramachandran was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1977 and received many Indian honours, including the Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Prize and the Srinivasa Ramanujan Medal.
