The German occupation of Czechoslovakia (1938–1945) began with the annexation of the Sudetenland under the Munich Agreement of September 1938 and was completed on 15 March 1939, when German troops occupied Prague and the remaining Czech lands in defiance of that agreement.

From Munich to occupation

Britain and France had pressured Czechoslovakia to cede the largely German-speaking borderlands; Poland and Hungary also seized territory. Under threat of invasion, President Emil Hácha, summoned to Berlin on the night of 14–15 March 1939, signed a declaration placing his country under German "protection". Bohemia and Moravia became a Protectorate within the Reich, while Slovakia became a nominally independent client state under Jozef Tiso, and Carpatho-Ukraine was annexed by Hungary.

The Protectorate

The Protectorate's industry — including the great Škoda works — was integrated into the German war economy. Repression escalated under Acting Reich Protector Reinhard Heydrich, who combined terror with improved rations for armaments workers. After Heydrich's assassination by Czechoslovak agents in Operation Anthropoid (1942), the Nazis retaliated by destroying the villages of Lidice and Ležáky and murdering their inhabitants. Of the Protectorate's Jews, most were deported via the Theresienstadt ghetto and murdered in the Holocaust.

Resistance and liberation

A government-in-exile under Edvard Beneš operated from London, and Czechoslovak airmen and soldiers fought with the Allies. The Slovak National Uprising of 1944 and the Prague uprising of May 1945 marked the end of the occupation; Soviet and American forces liberated the country in the war's final days.
