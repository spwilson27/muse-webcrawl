Georgetown is the capital and largest city of Guyana, and the country's main port and commercial and administrative centre. Located on Guyana's Atlantic coast at the mouth of the Demerara River, it lies below high-tide level and is protected from the sea by a seawall, with a network of canals and kokers (sluices) used for drainage. It is nicknamed the "Garden City of the Caribbean" for its broad tree-lined avenues and many wooden colonial buildings.

History

The city began as a small Dutch settlement; in 1781 the town was laid out and named Stabroek after Nicolaas Geelvinck, Lord of Stabroek, a director of the Dutch West India Company. It was captured by the British in 1781, retaken by the French (who developed it and called it La Nouvelle Ville) in 1782, returned to the Dutch in 1784, and finally ceded to Britain in the early 19th century along with the colonies of Demerara, Essequibo, and Berbice. In 1812, under British rule, the city was renamed Georgetown in honour of King George III. Georgetown became the capital of British Guiana and, in 1966, of independent Guyana. Fires in 1945 and 1948 destroyed much of the commercial district, which was rebuilt in concrete.

Geography and landmarks

Georgetown's streets are laid out in a grid, with wide avenues planted with flowering and shade trees dating from the Dutch era. Notable landmarks include St. George's Cathedral (completed in the 1890s), one of the tallest wooden churches in the world; the Stabroek Market (1880s) with its prominent clock tower; the Gothic Revival City Hall (1889); Parliament Building; the Guyana Botanical Gardens and zoo; and the Walter Roth Museum of Anthropology. Demerara Harbour Bridge, one of the longest floating bridges in the world when built (1978), connects Georgetown's East Bank with the West Bank of the Demerara.

Economy and transport

Georgetown handles most of Guyana's imports and exports — sugar, rice, timber, bauxite, gold, and diamonds pass through its port. It is the seat of the University of Guyana (founded 1963, located at Turkeyen) and of the Secretariat of the Caribbean Community (CARICOM). The main international airport is Cheddi Jagan International Airport at Timehri, about 41 km south of the city; Ogle Airport serves regional Caribbean flights.
