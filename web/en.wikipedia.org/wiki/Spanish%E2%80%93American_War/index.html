The Spanish–American War (April–August 1898) was an armed conflict between Spain and the United States. It began after the U.S. demanded a resolution to the Cuban War of Independence and the battleship USS Maine exploded and sank in Havana harbour in February 1898, an incident inflamed by the sensationalist "yellow journalism" of the era.

Course of the war

Commodore George Dewey destroyed the Spanish Pacific squadron at the Battle of Manila Bay on 1 May 1898. In the Caribbean, U.S. forces landed in Cuba, won the battles of San Juan Hill — made famous by Theodore Roosevelt's Rough Riders — and destroyed the Spanish fleet at Santiago de Cuba. Puerto Rico was occupied in July, and an armistice followed in August.

Consequences

Under the Treaty of Paris (1898), Spain ceded the Philippines, Puerto Rico, and Guam to the United States for $20 million, and Cuba became formally independent, though under heavy American influence. The war marked the end of the Spanish Empire in the Americas and the emergence of the United States as a global power. It also led directly to the Philippine–American War (1899–1902) as Filipino revolutionaries resisted annexation.
