Army Group Centre

Army Group Centre (German: Heeresgruppe Mitte) was the name of two distinct German army groups that fought on the Eastern Front in the Second World War. The first was created in 1941 for Operation Barbarossa, the invasion of the Soviet Union.

Operation Barbarossa

Under Field Marshal Fedor von Bock, Army Group Centre made the main thrust toward Moscow, winning huge encirclement battles at Białystok–Minsk and Smolensk, before being halted in the Battle of Moscow in December 1941. It remained the largest German formation on the Eastern Front.

Destruction and re-formation

The army group was effectively destroyed by the Soviet Operation Bagration in the summer of 1944, losing some 28 divisions. A new Army Group Centre was formed in January 1945 from the remnants of Army Group A and fought in East Prussia, Silesia and Bohemia until the end of the war.
