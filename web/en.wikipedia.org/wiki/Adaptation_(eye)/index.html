Adaptation (eye)

In visual physiology, adaptation is the ability of the retina of the eye to adjust to various degrees of darkness and light.

Light adaptation

When moving from darkness into bright light, the visual system adjusts within seconds to minutes. Photoreceptor sensitivity decreases, the pupil constricts, and colour vision mediated by cone cells becomes dominant.

Dark adaptation

Dark adaptation is slower, typically taking 20–30 minutes to reach full sensitivity. Rod cells regenerate rhodopsin, the pupil dilates, and peripheral vision improves for detecting dim objects. The process follows a biphasic curve: an early cone phase and a later rod phase, separated by the Kohlrausch kink.

Mechanisms

Adaptation involves photochemical changes in photopigments, neural gain control in the retina, and pupillary reflexes. Failure of dark adaptation can signal vitamin A deficiency or retinal diseases such as retinitis pigmentosa.
