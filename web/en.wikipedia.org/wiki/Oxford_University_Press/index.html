Oxford University Press (OUP) is the publishing house of the University of Oxford. It is the largest university press in the world. Its first book was printed in Oxford in 1478, with the Press officially granted the legal right to print books by decree in 1586. It is the second oldest university press after Cambridge University Press, which was founded in 1534.

It is a department of the University of Oxford and is governed by a group of 15 academics, the Delegates of the Press, appointed by the vice-chancellor of the University of Oxford. The Delegates are led by the Secretary to the Delegates, who serves as OUP's chief executive and as its major representative on other university bodies.

Oxford University Press has had a similar governance structure since the 17th century. The press is located on Walton Street, Oxford, opposite Somerville College, in the inner suburb of Jericho.

History

The University of Oxford began printing books around 1478, just after Caxton set up the first printing press in England. Initially the university's printing was marginal, consisting largely of Latin scholarly editions. In 1586 the university obtained a decree from the Star Chamber confirming its privilege to print all manner of books. The press grew substantially in the 17th century under figures such as Archbishop William Laud and John Fell, who acquired types and presses and established the Fell types collection.

Publishing

OUP publishes thousands of titles a year across academic, educational, and reference categories, including the Oxford English Dictionary, the Oxford English Dictionary being its flagship reference work. It also publishes academic journals, school textbooks, children's books, and the Very Short Introductions series. OUP has offices worldwide, including in New York, Melbourne, Cape Town, and Delhi.
