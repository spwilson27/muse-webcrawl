Wavelength

Wavelength
Common symbol:λ (lambda)
SI unit:metre (m)
Relation:λ = v / f (wave speed ÷ frequency)
Dimension:length

In physics, the wavelength is the spatial period of a periodic wave — the distance over which the wave's shape repeats. It is the distance between consecutive corresponding points of the same phase on the wave, such as two adjacent crests, troughs or zero crossings.

Definition

Wavelength is commonly designated by the Greek letter lambda (λ). The term applies to periodic waves of any kind — sound, light, water waves and radio waves. The inverse of wavelength is the spatial frequency or wavenumber.

Relationship to frequency

Assuming a wave travels at speed v, its wavelength is λ = v/f, where f is the frequency. For electromagnetic radiation in vacuum, v is the speed of light (c ≈ 3×108 m/s), so visible light spans wavelengths of roughly 380–750 nanometres.

Examples

Sound audible to humans (20 Hz–20 kHz) has wavelengths from about 17 metres down to 17 millimetres in air. Radio waves range from millimetres to thousands of kilometres. The wavelength of a wave determines many of its interactions with matter, including diffraction around obstacles of comparable size.

De Broglie wavelength

In quantum mechanics, particles exhibit wave-like behaviour with a wavelength λ = h/p, where h is Planck's constant and p is momentum — the de Broglie wavelength, fundamental to wave–particle duality.
