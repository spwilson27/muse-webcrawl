Galois connection

Diagram of two partially ordered sets joined by a Galois connection.

In mathematics, especially in order theory, a Galois connection is a particular correspondence, typically between two partially ordered sets (posets). It generalizes the correspondence between the subgroups of a group and the subfields of a Galois extension, the correspondence after which it is named. Galois connections find applications in various mathematical theories, including lattice theory, domain theory, and program semantics in computer science.

Definition

Suppose that (A, ≤) and (B, ≤) are two partially ordered sets. A monotone Galois connection between these posets consists of two monotone functions: F : A → B and G : B → A, such that for all a in A and b in B, we have F(a) ≤ b if and only if a ≤ G(b). In this situation, F is called the lower adjoint (or left adjoint) and G is called the upper adjoint (or right adjoint) of each other. An antitone Galois connection between A and B consists of two antitone functions F and G such that F(a) ≥ b if and only if a ≤ G(b); equivalently, it is a monotone connection between A and the dual (opposite order) of B.

Examples

The archetypal example comes from Galois theory: given a finite Galois extension E/F of fields, mapping each intermediate field to its group of automorphisms fixing it, and each subgroup to its fixed field, yields an inclusion-reversing (antitone) Galois connection between the lattice of intermediate fields and the lattice of subgroups of the Galois group.

Another example arises in formal concept analysis, where a relation between objects and attributes induces a Galois connection between the powersets, whose closed pairs are the formal concepts. In abstract interpretation of programs, Galois connections between concrete and abstract domains provide the mathematical foundation for sound static analysis.

Properties

From the defining equivalence several properties follow immediately. The composition G ∘ F is a closure operator on A: it is extensive (a ≤ G(F(a))), monotone, and idempotent. Symmetrically, F ∘ G is a kernel (interior) operator on B. The lower adjoint preserves all existing suprema (joins) and the upper adjoint preserves all existing infima (meets); indeed, a function between complete lattices is a lower adjoint if and only if it preserves all suprema. Galois connections between complete lattices are uniquely determined by either of their two adjoints.

Applications

Galois connections appear in algebraic logic, where they connect syntax and semantics; in topology, via the correspondence between topologies and closure operators; and in data analysis through formal concept analysis. The notion has been generalized in category theory to that of adjoint functors, of which Galois connections are the special case for thin categories (preorders).
