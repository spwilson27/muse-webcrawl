The Emir of the State of Qatar (Arabic: أمير قطر) is the monarch and head of state of Qatar. The emir is also the commander-in-chief of the Armed Forces and guarantor of the constitution. The emir holds the most powerful position in the country, and has a prominent role in foreign relations. The emirs are members of the House of Thani, whose origins are in the Banu Tamim tribe, one of the largest tribes in the Arabian Peninsula.

The current emir is Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, who ascended the throne on 25 June 2013 following the abdication of his father, Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani.

Role

Under the 2003 constitution of Qatar, the emir is head of state and represents the country internally and externally. The emir appoints the prime minister and the Council of Ministers, ratifies laws passed by the Advisory Council (Shura Council), signs treaties, and serves as supreme commander of the armed forces. In practice, executive authority is exercised by the emir together with the government.

List of rulers

The Al Thani family has ruled Qatar since the mid-19th century. Notable rulers include Sheikh Mohammed bin Thani (1851–1878), regarded as the first ruler of unified Qatar; Sheikh Jassim bin Mohammed Al Thani (1878–1913), founder of the State of Qatar; Sheikh Ahmad bin Ali Al Thani (1960–1972), emir at independence from Britain in 1971, deposed by his cousin Khalifa bin Hamad Al Thani (1972–1995); and Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani (1995–2013), who deposed his father and oversaw Qatar's rapid development, the founding of Al Jazeera, and the winning bid for the 2022 FIFA World Cup.
