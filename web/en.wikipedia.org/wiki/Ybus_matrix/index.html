Ybus matrix

In power engineering, the bus admittance matrix, usually written Ybus, is the nodal admittance matrix of an electrical power system. It relates injected bus currents to bus voltages through the relation I = Ybus V and is the central data structure in power-flow (load-flow) studies, short-circuit analysis, and many other network computations.

Construction

Each diagonal entry Yii is the sum of the admittances of all elements connected to bus i, including transmission lines, transformers, shunt capacitors and reactors. Each off-diagonal entry Yij is the negative of the admittance of the branch directly connecting buses i and j, and is zero when no such branch exists. Because each bus connects to only a few others, real-world Ybus matrices are extremely sparse, and practical software stores and factorizes them using sparse-matrix techniques and near-optimal node ordering to limit fill-in.

Use in power flow

Substituting the current relation into the complex-power expression S = V I* gives the nonlinear power-flow equations solved by the Newton–Raphson, Gauss–Seidel, and fast-decoupled methods: each bus's real and reactive power injection is a sum over all buses of terms involving |Vi||Vj| and the magnitude and angle of Yij. The Jacobian used in Newton–Raphson iterations is built from partial derivatives of these expressions and inherits much of the sparsity of Ybus.

Related matrices

The inverse of Ybus (or of its reduced form) is the bus impedance matrix Zbus, used in fault analysis; methods exist to build Zbus branch by branch without full inversion. In short-circuit studies, fault currents are obtained directly from Zbus diagonal (Thévenin) impedances.
