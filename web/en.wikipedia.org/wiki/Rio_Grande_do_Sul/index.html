Rio Grande do Sul is the southernmost state of Brazil, bordered by Santa Catarina to the north, Argentina to the west, Uruguay to the south, and the Atlantic Ocean to the east. Its capital and largest city is Porto Alegre. The state is known for its gaúcho culture — the South American cowboy tradition it shares with Uruguay and Argentina — and for a strong European immigrant heritage, chiefly Portuguese, German and Italian.

Geography

The state spans some 281,730 square kilometres. Its landscapes include the rolling pampas grasslands of the south and west, the high basalt plateau of the Serra Gaúcha in the northeast with canyons such as Itaimbezinho in Aparados da Serra National Park, and a sandy coastal plain enclosing two great lagoons, Lagoa dos Patos and Lagoa Mirim. The Jacuí, Uruguay and other rivers drain toward the Atlantic. The climate is humid subtropical, with cold winters by Brazilian standards and occasional snow in the highlands.

History

The region was contested between Spain and Portugal for centuries; Jesuit missions among the Guaraní stood in the northwest. Portuguese settlement expanded in the 18th century, and Azorean couples were brought to populate the coast. In 1835 the province rebelled against the Brazilian Empire in the Ragamuffin War (Guerra dos Farrapos), proclaiming the Riograndense Republic, which held out until 1845. Waves of German (from 1824) and Italian (from 1875) immigration shaped the interior colonies. In May 2024 the state suffered catastrophic flooding, the worst in its recorded history, inundating Porto Alegre and displacing hundreds of thousands.

Economy and culture

Rio Grande do Sul is one of Brazil's most prosperous states, with major production of soybeans, rice, maize, tobacco, wine — the Vale dos Vinhedos is the country's premier wine region — and livestock. Industry includes footwear, machinery, petrochemicals and food processing. Gaúcho traditions centre on cattle ranching, the churrasco barbecue, mate tea (chimarrão) drunk from a gourd, and folk music and dance preserved by Centros de Tradições Gaúchas. Porto Alegre's two great football clubs, Grêmio and Internacional, sustain one of Brazil's fiercest rivalries, the Grenal derby.
