Palm Springs, California

Palm Springs is a desert resort city in Riverside County, California, within the Coachella Valley. It is famous for its hot springs, stylish hotels, golf courses, and large concentration of mid-century modern architecture. At the 2020 census its population was about 44,500, though the wider Palm Springs area swells seasonally with visitors and part-time residents.

History

The area was long inhabited by the Cahuilla people, who knew the site for its natural hot springs, Sec-he ("boiling water"). Spanish explorers recorded the springs in the 1770s. In the early twentieth century the settlement grew as a health resort; by the 1930s it had become a fashionable winter retreat for Hollywood stars, drawn by its sunshine and its location within the two-hour drive of Los Angeles then required by studio contracts. The city was incorporated in 1938.

Architecture and culture

Palm Springs holds one of the largest concentrations of mid-century modern residential architecture in the United States, with work by architects such as Richard Neutra, Albert Frey, and Donald Wexler. The annual Modernism Week festival celebrates this heritage. The city also hosts the Palm Springs International Film Festival and is known for one of the largest LGBTQ communities in the United States.

Geography and climate

The city sits at the base of the San Jacinto Mountains; the Palm Springs Aerial Tramway climbs to Mount San Jacinto State Park. Palm Springs has a hot desert climate, with summer highs routinely above 40 °C and some of the sunniest weather in the country.
