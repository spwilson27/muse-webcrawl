Nicole Oresme

Bornc. 1320–1325, Normandy, France
Died11 July 1382, Lisieux, France
OccupationPhilosopher, mathematician, economist, bishop

Nicole Oresme (c. 1320–1325 – 11 July 1382), also known as Nicolas Oresme, was a French philosopher of the later Middle Ages. He wrote influential works on economics, mathematics, physics, astrology and astronomy, theology, and philosophy of language. He served as bishop of Lisieux and as a translator and adviser to King Charles V of France. He is regarded as one of the most original thinkers of the 14th century.

Life

Oresme was probably born in Normandy and studied at the University of Paris, where he became grand master of the Collège de Navarre in 1356. He entered the service of Charles V, translating works of Aristotle into French at the king's request and coining many French scientific terms in the process. In 1377 he was made bishop of Lisieux, where he died in 1382.

Mathematics and natural philosophy

In his Tractatus de configurationibus qualitatum et motuum, Oresme introduced a method of graphically representing qualities and motions that anticipated aspects of coordinate geometry, centuries before Descartes. He proved that the harmonic series diverges, gave early arguments concerning fractional exponents, and developed a geometric proof of the mean speed theorem, showing that a uniformly accelerated body covers the same distance as a body moving at the mean speed. He also argued that the Earth might rotate daily on its axis, presenting the hypothesis as physically plausible while ultimately deferring to scripture.

Economics

His De origine, natura, jure et mutationibus monetarum (c. 1360) is one of the earliest treatises on money. Oresme argued that coinage belongs to the community rather than the prince, and condemned the debasement of currency as a form of tyranny and fraud.
