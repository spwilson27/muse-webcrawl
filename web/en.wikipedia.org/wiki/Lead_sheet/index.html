Lead sheet

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A lead sheet or fake sheet is a form of musical notation that specifies the essential elements of a popular song: the melody (on a single staff, in the treble clef), the lyrics, and the harmony written as chord symbols above the staff. The lead sheet does not describe chord voicings, voice leading, bass lines, or other aspects of the accompaniment; these are specified later by the arranger or improvised by the performers.

Contents and use

A lead sheet typically contains a single verse or chorus, or all of the song's verses when they share the same melody. It is the basis for a "gig"—an informal group of musicians improvising an accompaniment based on the outline given by the lead sheet—hence the terms "fake sheet" and "fakebook". The lead sheet is thus frequently the medium used by a "fake book", a collection of lead sheets for many songs compiled into a single volume.

Lead sheets are the primary form of sheet music for jazz musicians, who often memorize the melody and chords and improvise on them. Lead sheets may use either letter notation for chords (such as C, Am, G7) or, less commonly, Roman numeral analysis.

History

The predecessor of the lead sheet was the "tune sheet" used by song pluggers in the early 20th century. Illegal collections of lead sheets circulated among jazz and dance-band musicians from the 1940s onward; the best known, The Real Book, was compiled by students at Berklee College of Music in the 1970s and later published legally by Hal Leonard.
