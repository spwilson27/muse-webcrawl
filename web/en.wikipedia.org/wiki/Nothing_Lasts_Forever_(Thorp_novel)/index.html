Nothing Lasts Forever (Thorp novel)

Nothing Lasts Forever is a 1979 action-thriller novel by American author Roderick Thorp. It is a sequel to Thorp's 1966 novel The Detective, following former police detective Joe Leland. The book is best known today as the basis for the 1988 film Die Hard.

Plot

Retired detective Joe Leland flies to Los Angeles on Christmas Eve to visit his daughter Stephanie, an executive at the Klaxon Oil Corporation, whose headquarters occupy a 40-storey skyscraper. During the company Christmas party, a group of German terrorists led by Anton "Little Tony" Gruber seizes the building and takes the partygoers hostage, intending to expose Klaxon's corrupt dealings and steal from its vault. Leland, barefoot and separated from the others, wages a one-man guerrilla campaign through the tower, communicating with a sympathetic LAPD officer, Sergeant Al Powell, by radio. Unlike the film, the novel is darker in tone and ending: Leland is older and more battered, and his daughter dies in the climax when Gruber falls from the building clutching her.

Adaptation

Twentieth Century Fox had acquired the rights as part of its deal for The Detective (filmed in 1968 with Frank Sinatra). When the sequel novel appeared, the studio eventually developed it as Die Hard (1988), renaming Leland John McClane and Gruber Hans Gruber, changing the daughter to the hero's estranged wife, and giving the story a more upbeat ending. The film's success eclipsed the novel, which has since been reissued noting the connection. See also Die Hard and Roderick Thorp.
