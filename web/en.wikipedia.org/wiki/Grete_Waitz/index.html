Grete Waitz

Grete Waitz (born Grete Andersen; 1 October 1953 – 19 April 2011) was a Norwegian marathon runner and former world record holder. In 1979, she became the first woman in history to run the marathon in under two and a half hours. Waitz won nine New York City Marathons, women's division, between 1978 and 1988, the most of any runner in history. She also won a silver medal at the 1984 Olympic Games in Los Angeles and a gold medal at the 1983 World Championships in Athletics in Helsinki.

Early career

Born in Oslo, Waitz began as a track runner, competing in the 1500 metres at the 1972 Munich Olympics as a teenager. She set world records at 3000 metres and won the European Championships bronze medal at that distance in 1974, but initially resisted road racing before being persuaded to enter the 1978 New York City Marathon.

Marathon career

In her first marathon in 1978, Waitz won New York in 2:32:30, a world record. She lowered the record twice more in the following years, to 2:27:33 in 1979 and 2:25:41 in 1980. She won the London Marathon in 1983 and 1986, the Stockholm Marathon in 1988, and five World Cross Country Championships titles between 1978 and 1983. Her career stretched across 33 marathons.

Later life and legacy

After retiring, Waitz coached runners and worked to promote running and public health, founding the Aktiv mot kreft ("Active Against Cancer") foundation in 2007. She ran the New York City Marathon again in 1992 alongside race founder Fred Lebow, then receiving treatment for brain cancer. Waitz died of cancer in Oslo in 2011, aged 57. A statue of her stands outside the Bislett Stadium in Oslo, and another in the Norway pavilion at Epcot in Florida.
