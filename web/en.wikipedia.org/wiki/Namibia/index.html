Namibia, officially the Republic of Namibia, is a country in southern Africa whose western border is the Atlantic Ocean. It shares land borders with Angola and Zambia to the north, Botswana to the east and South Africa to the south and east; the Caprivi (Zambezi) strip touches Zimbabwe. Namibia is the driest country in sub-Saharan Africa, dominated by the Namib Desert along its coast — often cited as the world's oldest desert — and the Kalahari to the east.

History

Namibia was colonised by Germany from 1884 (German South West Africa); the German campaign of 1904–08 against the Herero and Nama peoples is recognised as the first genocide of the 20th century. South Africa occupied the territory in the First World War and administered it under a League of Nations mandate, later imposing apartheid despite UN opposition. The liberation movement SWAPO's long armed struggle, international pressure, and the 1988 settlement ended in independence in 1990 with Sam Nujoma as first president — one of the last African colonies to be freed.

Geography and economy

Landmarks include the Fish River Canyon (one of the largest canyons in the world), the dunes of Sossusvlei, the foggy Skeleton Coast, and Etosha National Park, whose salt pan draws elephants, lions and black rhinos watched by conservation bodies such as those using the IUCN Red List. The economy rests on mining (diamonds, uranium — Namibia is among the top uranium producers), beef, fishing and tourism, including community conservancies that have made the country a conservation success story. Like neighbouring Tanzania further north, it balances wildlife protection with rural livelihoods.
