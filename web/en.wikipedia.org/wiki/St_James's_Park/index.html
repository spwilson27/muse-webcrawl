St James's Park

St James's Park is a 23-hectare (57-acre) park in the City of Westminster, central London. It is the oldest of the Royal Parks of London and lies at the southernmost tip of the St James's area, which was named after a leper hospital dedicated to St James the Less. The park is bounded by Buckingham Palace to the west, The Mall to the north, Horse Guards Parade to the east, and Birdcage Walk to the south.

History

In 1532 Henry VIII acquired the marshy land from Eton College and enclosed it as a deer park for the new St James's Palace. Charles II, who had seen the formal gardens of France during his exile, had the park redesigned with a long canal, avenues of trees and semi-wild animals and birds; he opened it to the public and famously fed the park's pelicans. A Russian ambassador presented pelicans to the king in 1664, and their descendants still live on the lake.

In 1826–27 the park was remodelled in a more romantic, naturalistic style by John Nash under commission from the future George IV: the canal became the present winding lake, and The Mall was created as a ceremonial route. In the 1850s the lake was remodelled again. Duck Island, at the eastern end of the lake, historically housed a bird keeper's cottage; the ornamental Blue Bridge across the lake offers celebrated views of Buckingham Palace to the west and the Foreign Office and London Eye to the east.

Wildlife

The park is famous for its waterfowl: pelicans are fed daily on the east bank of the lake, and the island and shores support ducks, geese, swans, coots and herons. Water voles and wood mice inhabit the reed beds, and pipistrelle bats hunt over the lake at dusk.

Surroundings

The park is surrounded by some of Britain's most important ceremonial and governmental buildings: Buckingham Palace, Clarence House, St James's Palace, Horse Guards and the Cabinet War Rooms. The Victoria Memorial stands at its western end, and the St James's Park underground station serves its south side.
