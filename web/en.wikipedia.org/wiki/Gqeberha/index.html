Gqeberha, formerly known as Port Elizabeth and colloquially "PE", is a major seaport and the most populous city in the Eastern Cape province of South Africa. It is the seat of the Nelson Mandela Bay Metropolitan Municipality, South Africa's second-largest metropolitan district by area. The city was renamed Gqeberha in February 2021, taking the Xhosa name for the Baakens River that flows through it.

History

The area was home to San and Khoikhoi peoples and later to Xhosa communities. British settlers founded Port Elizabeth in 1820, naming it after the late wife of Sir Rufane Donkin, acting governor of the Cape Colony. The city grew as a harbour for the Eastern Cape's wool trade and later became a centre of the automotive industry, earning the nickname "the Friendly City" and, for its frequent breezes, "the Windy City".

Economy and attractions

Gqeberha's port handles vehicle exports and bulk cargo, and the nearby Coega industrial development zone hosts an additional deep-water port. The city is a gateway to the Addo Elephant National Park and is known for its beaches along Algoa Bay and its mild climate.
