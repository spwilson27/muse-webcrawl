Harvey Samuel Firestone (December 20, 1868 – February 7, 1938) was an American businessman and the founder of the Firestone Tire and Rubber Company, one of the first global manufacturers of automobile tires. He was a close friend and associate of Henry Ford and Thomas Edison, with whom he made celebrated annual camping trips as part of the self-styled "Vagabonds".

Early life

Firestone grew up on a farm in Columbiana County, Ohio. After working for the Columbus Buggy Company in Columbus, Ohio, he started his own company in Chicago in 1890 making rubber tires for carriages, before merging it into another venture.

Firestone Tire and Rubber Company

In 1900, seeing the coming market for automobile tires, Firestone founded the Firestone Tire and Rubber Company in Akron, Ohio. The young company scored a decisive breakthrough in 1906 when Henry Ford chose Firestone to supply tires for the Ford Model T, cementing a lifelong personal and business alliance between the two men. Firestone pioneered mass production of tires, promoted pneumatic truck tires and the "ship by truck" movement, and campaigned for better highways. In the 1920s his company established a vast rubber plantation in Liberia — eventually about a million acres — to secure its own supply of rubber independent of British and Dutch colonial monopolies.

The Vagabonds

Between 1918 and 1924 Firestone, Ford, Edison and the naturalist John Burroughs made a series of widely publicised summer camping trips through the American countryside, joined on one occasion by President Warren G. Harding. The "Four Vagabonds" tours became a fixture of 1920s popular culture.

Death

Firestone died in his sleep of a coronary thrombosis at his Miami Beach estate in 1938. His sons, including Harvey S. Firestone Jr., succeeded him in the company.
