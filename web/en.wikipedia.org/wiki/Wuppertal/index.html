Wuppertal (German pronunciation: [ˈvʊpɐtaːl], lit. "Wupper Valley") is a city in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, in and around the valley of the River Wupper, east of Düsseldorf and south of the Ruhr. With a population of roughly 355,000, it is the largest city in the Bergisches Land region. The city was formed in 1929 by the merger of the industrial towns of Elberfeld and Barmen with Vohwinkel, Ronsdorf, Cronenberg and other communities; the name Wuppertal ("in the Wupper valley") was adopted in 1930.

History

The Wupper valley was an early centre of German industrialisation, specialising in textiles, ribbon weaving and later chemicals — the Bayer company was founded in Barmen in 1863. Friedrich Engels, co-author of The Communist Manifesto, was born in Barmen in 1820. The city was heavily bombed in 1943, when firestorm raids destroyed much of Barmen and Elberfeld.

The Schwebebahn

Wuppertal's landmark is the Schwebebahn, a suspension railway opened in 1901, in which trains hang from an overhead rail and travel largely above the river through the city. It remains a working part of the public transport network and a symbol of the city; in 1950 the young elephant Tuffi famously leapt from a Schwebebahn carriage into the Wupper and survived.

Culture

The city is home to Pina Bausch's Tanztheater Wuppertal, one of the world's most influential modern dance companies, and to the Von der Heydt Museum of art. The philosopher and poet Else Lasker-Schüler was born here, as was the choreographer Bausch herself in nearby Solingen.
