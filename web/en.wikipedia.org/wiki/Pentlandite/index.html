Pentlandite

Pentlandite is an iron–nickel sulfide mineral with the chemical formula (Fe,Ni)9S8. It is the most important ore of nickel, typically containing roughly equal proportions of iron and nickel. The mineral is named after the Irish naturalist Joseph Barclay Pentland, who first noted it in the Sudbury district of Ontario, Canada.

Properties

Pentlandite has a metallic lustre and a bronze-yellow to brownish colour, with a light bronze-brown streak. It is relatively soft (Mohs hardness 3.5–4) and non-magnetic. It crystallises in the isometric system but is usually found in massive granular aggregates rather than distinct crystals.

Occurrence

Pentlandite almost always occurs intergrown with pyrrhotite in mafic and ultramafic igneous rocks, where it forms by exsolution from monosulfide solid solution as magmatic sulfide deposits cool. Major deposits occur in the Sudbury Basin of Canada, at Norilsk in Russia, in Western Australia, and in South Africa. Sudbury pentlandite was historically the backbone of the world's nickel supply. The mineral is commonly associated with chalcopyrite, and these sulfide assemblages also yield by-product copper, cobalt, and platinum-group elements.
