Rupert Holmes

Rupert Holmes (born David Goldstein, 24 February 1947) is a British-American composer, singer-songwriter, dramatist and author. He is best known for the number-one hit song "Escape (The Piña Colada Song)" (1979) and for writing the Tony Award-winning musical The Mystery of Edwin Drood (1985).

Early life and music career

Holmes was born in Northwich, Cheshire, England, to an American army officer father and an English mother, and grew up in the New York suburbs. He studied at the Manhattan School of Music and began as a session musician, arranger, and songwriter, writing for artists including Barbra Streisand, whose 1975 film A Star Is Born sessions used several of his songs. His own albums of the 1970s, such as Widescreen (1974), were critically admired story-songs. In 1979 "Escape" became the last number-one single of the 1970s on the Billboard Hot 100, followed by the top-ten hit "Him".

Theatre and writing

Holmes adapted Dickens's unfinished novel for the stage as The Mystery of Edwin Drood, the first Broadway musical with multiple endings decided by audience vote; he won Tony Awards for book, music, and lyrics. His play Solitary Confinement starred Stacy Keach, and he created the AMC television series Remember WENN (1996–1998), set in a 1930s radio station, writing its songs as well. His novels include Where the Truth Lies (2003), filmed in 2005, Swing (2005), and The McMasters Guide to Homicide (2023).
