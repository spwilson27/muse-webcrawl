Dolores Hidalgo, officially Dolores Hidalgo Cuna de la Independencia Nacional ("Cradle of National Independence"), is a city and municipality in the state of Guanajuato, Mexico. It is famous as the place where Father Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla issued the Grito de Dolores, the call that began the Mexican War of Independence.

History

Founded in the colonial era as the Dolores parish, the town was where Hidalgo, its parish priest, rang the church bell in the early hours of 16 September 1810 and called his parishioners to arms against Spanish rule. The town was renamed in his honour, and the original bell — the Campana de Dolores — is preserved in the National Palace in Mexico City, where the president rings a replica each 15 September. The Casa de Hidalgo, the priest's home, is now a museum.

Economy

The city is a noted centre for Talavera-style ceramics and pottery, with hundreds of workshops producing tiles and tableware, and is also known for unusual ice-cream flavours sold in the main plaza and for the tomb and museum of the ranchera singer José Alfredo Jiménez, who was born here.
