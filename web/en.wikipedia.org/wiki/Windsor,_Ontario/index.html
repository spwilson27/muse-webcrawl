Windsor is a city in south-western Ontario, Canada, on the south bank of the Detroit River directly across from Detroit, Michigan, in the United States. It is Canada's southernmost city and one of the oldest continuously inhabited European settlements in the country, founded by French colonists in 1749. Windsor is known as the "Automotive Capital of Canada" for its central role in the Canadian car industry, and the metropolitan area had a population of about 422,630 at the 2021 census.

History

French farmers settled the south shore of the Detroit River beginning in 1749, making the Windsor area the oldest European settlement west of Montreal. After Britain gained control of the region in 1763, the settlement grew with trade and, following the American Revolution, Loyalist migration. The town was incorporated in 1892. In the 20th century Windsor became the heart of Canada's automobile manufacturing, home to major plants of Ford, General Motors, and Chrysler (now Stellantis), as well as Hiram Walker's distillery in Walkerville.

Border crossing

Windsor is connected to Detroit by the Ambassador Bridge — the busiest international border crossing in North America by trade value — and the Detroit–Windsor Tunnel. The Gordie Howe International Bridge, under construction immediately downstream, is expected to open as an additional crossing in the mid-2020s. Roughly a quarter of all Canada–U.S. trade passes through the Windsor–Detroit corridor.

Economy and culture

Alongside automotive manufacturing and tool-and-die making, Windsor's economy includes agriculture in surrounding Essex County, tourism anchored by Caesars Windsor casino, and the University of Windsor. The city is nicknamed the "City of Roses" for its many rose gardens, and its riverfront parks offer views of the Detroit skyline.
