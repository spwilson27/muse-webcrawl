Elevation (architecture)

In architecture, an elevation is an orthographic projection drawing of one side of a building — a flat, two-dimensional representation of a facade as seen from directly in front of it, without perspective distortion. Elevations are among the standard drawings in an architectural drawing set, together with plans and sections.

Types

Exterior elevations are conventionally named for the direction they face (north, south, east, west elevation) or for the side of the building they show (front, rear, side). Interior elevations depict the vertical faces of rooms, showing wall finishes, built-in furniture, and fixtures. Elevations show heights, materials, openings such as doors and windows, rooflines, and decorative elements, and are drawn to scale so dimensions can be read directly.

Conventions

Because an elevation is an orthographic projection, elements nearer the viewer do not appear larger. Line weights are used to suggest depth, with the outlines of features closest to the picture plane often drawn heavier. Elevations may be annotated with material notes, level markers (datums), and grid references, and in presentation drawings they are often rendered with shadows, colour, and surrounding landscape.
