Volvulus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A volvulus is a twisting of the intestine that can cut off its blood supply.

A volvulus is when a loop of intestine twists around itself and the mesentery that supports it, resulting in a bowel obstruction. Symptoms include abdominal pain, abdominal bloating, vomiting, constipation, and bloody stool. Onset of symptoms may be rapid or gradual. The twisting can cut off the blood supply to the affected part of the intestine, leading to tissue death (gangrene) if untreated.

Causes and risk factors

The most commonly affected parts of the intestines are the sigmoid colon and the cecum. Risk factors include a birth defect known as intestinal malrotation, an enlarged colon, Hirschsprung disease, pregnancy, and abdominal adhesions. Long-term constipation and a high-fibre diet may also increase the risk of sigmoid volvulus; in parts of Africa and the Middle East, sigmoid volvulus is a particularly common cause of bowel obstruction.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is typically made with medical imaging. Plain abdominal X-rays may show a massively dilated loop of colon — the classic "coffee bean sign" in sigmoid volvulus — while CT scanning is the most accurate method and can show the "whirl sign" of the twisted mesentery. Contrast enema may show a "bird's beak" narrowing at the site of the twist.

Treatment

Sigmoid volvulus may be treated initially by detorsion during sigmoidoscopy or with a barium enema, but surgery is often ultimately required to prevent recurrence. Cecal volvulus and any volvulus with signs of bowel compromise generally require surgery, which may involve untwisting the bowel, fixing it in place, or resecting non-viable segments. Volvulus is a surgical emergency when blood supply is threatened.
