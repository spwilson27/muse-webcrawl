Integrator

An integrator is a device or system whose output is the integral of its input with respect to time — that is, its output accumulates the input signal. Integrators appear in many fields:

In electronics and control

The classic electronic integrator is an operational-amplifier circuit with a resistor at the input and a capacitor in the feedback path; its output voltage is proportional to the time integral of the input voltage. Integrators are fundamental building blocks of analog computers, where chains of integrators solve differential equations, and they form the "I" term of PID controllers, eliminating steady-state error. Real integrators drift because of offset voltages and capacitor leakage, limitations handled with reset switches and compensation.

In mechanics and computation

Mechanical integrators — most famously the ball-and-disk integrators of Vannevar Bush's differential analyzer — performed integration physically. In numerical analysis, integrators are algorithms that step a simulation forward in time, from simple Euler methods to symplectic integrators that conserve energy in orbital and molecular dynamics. In industry, the word also denotes a systems integrator: a firm that assembles subsystems into a working whole.
