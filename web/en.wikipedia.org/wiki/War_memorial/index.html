War memorial

A war memorial is a building, monument, statue, or other edifice to celebrate a war or victory, or (predominating in modern times) to commemorate those who died or were injured in a war.

History

Ancient precedents include triumphal arches. Modern communal memorials proliferated after World War I, when villages and towns across Europe and the Commonwealth listed local dead — e.g. the Cenotaph in London and Tomb of the Unknown Soldier sites.

Symbolism

Common symbols include obelisks, crosses, eternal flames, and lists of names, as on the Vietnam Veterans Memorial in Washington, D.C. Remembrance services are held on days such as Remembrance Sunday and Memorial Day. See Memorial and Triumphal arch.
