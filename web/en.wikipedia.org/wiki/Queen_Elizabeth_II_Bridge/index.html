Queen Elizabeth II Bridge

Carries: A282 road traffic (southbound)
Crosses: River Thames
Locale: Dartford, Kent / Thurrock, Essex
Design: Cable-stayed bridge
Total length: 2,872 m (9,422 ft)
Longest span: 450 m (1,480 ft)
Height: 137 m (449 ft) towers
Opened: 30 October 1991

The Queen Elizabeth II Bridge is a cable-stayed bridge spanning the River Thames between Dartford in Kent and Thurrock in Essex, England. It forms the southbound half of the Dartford Crossing, one of the most important river crossings in the United Kingdom, carrying the A282—effectively the eastern section of London's M25 orbital motorway—over the Thames east of London.

History

The two Dartford tunnels (opened 1963 and 1980) were unable to cope with growing traffic, so a bridge was proposed to add capacity. Construction began in 1988, and the bridge was opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 30 October 1991. At the time of its opening it was the largest cable-supported bridge in Europe and had one of the longest cable-stayed main spans then built. The total construction cost was about £120 million for the bridge and £30 million for the approach roads.

Design

The bridge was designed by the German civil engineer Hellmut Homberg. It has a main span of 450 metres supported by two steel-and-concrete pylons 137 metres high, with approach viaducts bringing the total length to 2,872 metres. The deck carries four lanes of southbound traffic; northbound traffic uses the twin tunnels. The crossing operates an electronic free-flow charging scheme known as the Dart Charge, introduced in 2014 to replace toll booths.

The bridge is sometimes closed in high winds, when traffic is diverted through the tunnels. It carries on average well over 100,000 vehicles a day together with the tunnels.
