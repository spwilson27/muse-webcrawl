Chinese surname

Order: Surname precedes given name

Most common: Wang, Li, Zhang (mainland China)

Classic text: Hundred Family Surnames (Baijiaxing)

Chinese surnames are used by Han Chinese and by many ethnic minorities and diaspora communities influenced by Chinese naming customs. In Chinese, the surname comes first, before the given name. Thousands of surnames are recorded historically, though only a few hundred are in common use today, and a small number account for a large share of the population.

History

Chinese surnames are among the oldest family names in continuous use, with origins in the early dynastic period. Anciently, two kinds of name were distinguished: xing, associated with clan ancestry and often written with the "woman" radical, and shi, denoting a branch lineage, fief or occupation. By the Han dynasty the two had merged, and surnames were inherited patrilineally by commoners as well as nobles. The Song-dynasty primer Hundred Family Surnames lists 438 surnames in a rhyming text long used to teach children characters.

Most common surnames

In mainland China the three most common surnames — Wang (王), Li (李) and Zhang (張/张) — are each borne by roughly 70–100 million people. In Taiwan, Chen (陳) is the most common. Romanisation varies by region and dialect: the Cantonese Chan corresponds to Mandarin Chen; Cheung to Zhang; and Wong to Wang or Huang.

Compound surnames

Most surnames are a single character, but a minority are compound (two characters), such as Ouyang (歐陽), Sima (司馬), Zhuge (諸葛) and Situ (司徒). Compound surnames are relatively rare and often carry strong historical or literary associations.
