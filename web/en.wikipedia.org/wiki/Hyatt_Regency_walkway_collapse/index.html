Hyatt Regency walkway collapse

The Hyatt Regency walkway collapse was a catastrophic structural failure that occurred on July 17, 1981, at the Hyatt Regency hotel in Kansas City, Missouri, United States. Two suspended walkways spanning the hotel's atrium lobby collapsed onto a crowded tea dance, killing 114 people and injuring 216. It was the deadliest structural collapse in United States history at the time, and remains a landmark case study in engineering ethics and professional responsibility.

Background

The hotel, which opened in 1980, featured a multi-story atrium crossed by three walkways at the second, third, and fourth floors. The second- and fourth-floor walkways were stacked one above the other on the same side and were suspended from the atrium roof by steel tie rods.

Design change and failure

The original design called for a single set of long tie rods passing continuously through both walkways. The fabricator found this impractical and proposed using two shorter sets of rods instead — one set from the roof to the fourth-floor walkway, and a second set from the fourth-floor walkway down to the second. This change, approved without adequate analysis, doubled the load on the fourth-floor box-beam connections. On the evening of the collapse, the box beams split along their welds, and both walkways fell.

Investigation and consequences

Investigators from the National Bureau of Standards determined the design change was the fundamental cause. The structural engineers of record were found guilty of gross negligence; their licenses were revoked and their firm was convicted of criminal negligence, though the verdict was later overturned on appeal. The disaster led to major reforms in how design changes are reviewed and remains a standard teaching case in engineering programs.
