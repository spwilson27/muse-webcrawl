Vincenzo Galilei (c. 1520 – 2 July 1591) was an Italian lutenist, composer and music theorist. His children included the astronomer and physicist Galileo Galilei and the lutenist Michelagnolo Galilei. Vincenzo was a figure in the musical life of late Renaissance, and contributed significantly to the musical revolution that marks the beginning of the Baroque era.

Vincenzo Galilei, father of Galileo

Life and work

Born near Pisa, Galilei studied the lute and, in Venice, music theory with the theorist Gioseffo Zarlino. He was associated with the Florentine Camerata of Giovanni de' Bardi, the circle whose experiments with monody and dramatic music contributed to the invention of opera. His Dialogo della musica antica et della moderna (1581) attacked contemporary polyphony and advocated a return to the music of ancient Greece, in which text was delivered by a solo voice.

Acoustics

Galilei conducted systematic experiments on strings under tension, discovering that the pitch of a string varies with the square root of its tension — a non-linear relationship that contradicted Pythagorean assumptions. These experiments, performed with his son Galileo's assistance at times, are regarded as an early landmark of experimental acoustics and scientific method. He also published collections of lute music and madrigals, including the Fronimo (1568, revised 1584), a treatise on lute playing and intabulation.
