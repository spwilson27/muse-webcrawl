The Women's Institutes (WI) movement began in Canada in 1897 and spread to Britain during the First World War, where the National Federation of Women's Institutes (NFWI) was formed in 1915. Intended to encourage countrywomen to grow and preserve food during wartime, the WI developed into the largest women's voluntary organisation in the United Kingdom, combining education, crafts, fellowship and formidable campaigning on issues from rural housing to climate change and violence against women.

The first WI in Britain met at Llanfairpwllgwyngyll in Anglesey, Wales, in 1915. The movement's unofficial anthem is "Jerusalem", sung at meetings since the 1920s, and its public image — jam-making, baking and the produce show, famously sent up in the film Calendar Girls (2003) — coexists with a long record of policy resolutions debated at national level and put to government.

Origins

The first Women's Institute was founded at Stoney Creek, Ontario, in 1897 by Adelaide Hoodless. In Britain the movement was promoted by the Agricultural Organisation Society, with Canadian organiser Madge Watt — founder of the first British WI — and chairman John Nugent Harris. The earliest institutes formed in Wales and southern England in 1915.

Growth and wartime

Membership grew rapidly through the 1920s; Denman College, the WI's residential college near Oxford, opened in 1948. During the Second World War the WI organised evacuation, fruit preservation on a national scale (saving shipping tonnage), and markets for garden produce — activities vital to the home front.

Activities

Local institutes meet monthly for talks, demonstrations and outings. County federations run shows of cookery, craft and gardening; the WI's association with jam — members' preserved fruit sweetened scarce wartime diets — and the hymn "Jerusalem" are central to its traditions. Newer urban and evening institutes have broadened its appeal to younger members.

Campaigns

Resolutions passed at the annual meeting have driven campaigns on equal pay, cervical screening, plastic pollution ("End Plastic Soup"), rural post offices and mental health. In 2000 WI members famously slow-handclapped Prime Minister Tony Blair's speech at their conference at Wembley.

Cultural image

The 1999 charity calendar by the Rylstone & District WI — members posing discreetly nude behind crafts and baking — raised millions for leukaemia research and inspired the film and stage musical Calendar Girls.
