Alan Alexander Milne (18 January 1882 – 31 January 1956) was an English writer best known for his books about the teddy bear Winnie-the-Pooh, as well as for children's poetry. Before the enormous success of Pooh, Milne was primarily a playwright and humorist associated with Punch magazine.

Life

Milne was born in Kilburn, London, and educated at Westminster School and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he read mathematics and edited the undergraduate magazine Granta. One of his teachers at school had been H. G. Wells. He joined the staff of Punch in 1906, served as an officer in the First World War — including on the Somme — and afterward built a successful career writing plays such as Mr Pim Passes By (1919) and the detective novel The Red House Mystery (1922).

Winnie-the-Pooh

For his son Christopher Robin, born in 1920, Milne created stories based on the boy's stuffed animals. Winnie-the-Pooh (1926) and The House at Pooh Corner (1928), illustrated by E. H. Shepard, along with the verse collections When We Were Very Young (1924) and Now We Are Six (1927), became classics of children's literature. Milne later felt overshadowed by the Pooh books, and Christopher Robin had a famously ambivalent relationship with the fame they brought him.

Later years

Milne continued writing for adults, including an autobiography, It's Too Late Now (1939), and pacifist essays. He retired to Cotchford Farm in Sussex — the setting that inspired the Hundred Acre Wood — and died there in 1956.
