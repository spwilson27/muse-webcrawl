Indoor positioning system

An indoor positioning system (IPS) is a system to locate objects or people inside a building using radio waves, magnetic fields, acoustic signals, or other sensory information. IPS technologies are needed because global navigation satellite systems such as GPS are generally inaccurate or unavailable indoors due to signal attenuation and multipath reflection.

Technologies

Approaches include Wi-Fi positioning based on received signal strength fingerprinting, Bluetooth Low Energy beacons, ultra-wideband (UWB) ranging with centimetre-level accuracy, visible light communication, magnetic field matching, and inertial dead reckoning from smartphone sensors, often combined through sensor fusion.

Applications

IPS is used for navigation in airports, hospitals, malls, and museums; asset tracking in warehouses and hospitals; location-based services and advertising; and augmented reality applications.

Challenges

Multipath propagation, non-line-of-sight propagation, interference, and the need for site surveys or infrastructure installation remain obstacles to accuracy and deployment.
