The Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) is Australia's national statistical agency. It provides official statistics on the economy, population, environment and society, most notably the five-yearly Census of Population and Housing, the monthly Labour Force Survey, the Consumer Price Index and national accounts. The ABS is headed by the Australian Statistician and operates under the Australian Bureau of Statistics Act 1975 and the Census and Statistics Act 1905.

ABS House, Canberra.

History

Federation in 1901 required uniform national statistics; the Commonwealth Bureau of Census and Statistics was established in 1905 under the first Commonwealth Statistician, George Knibbs, and conducted the first national census in 1911. State statistical offices were progressively integrated, a process completed with Tasmania in 1924. The bureau adopted its present name in 1975. The 2016 Census attracted controversy when the online collection system was taken offline on census night following distributed denial-of-service attacks, prompting a Senate inquiry and reforms culminating in the largely online 2021 Census.

Functions

Beyond the census, the ABS publishes statistics on employment, inflation, retail trade, building, agriculture, health, crime and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, including the National Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health Survey. In 2017 it conducted the Australian Marriage Law Postal Survey. Statistical confidentiality is protected by law; census data are used to determine electoral representation and government funding allocations.
