Changchun

Changchun, capital of Jilin province

Changchun is the capital and largest city of Jilin Province in northeastern China. It is a sub-provincial city with a population of around nine million in its metropolitan area. The name means "long spring" in Chinese.

History

Changchun was founded in 1800 as a small trading post. In the 1930s it became Hsinking, the capital of Manchukuo, the Japanese-controlled puppet state, and was extensively rebuilt as a planned capital city with wide boulevards and parks. The former imperial palace of Puyi, the last emperor of China, is preserved as a museum.

Economy

Changchun is a major centre of China's automobile industry and is home to FAW Group, one of the country's largest carmakers; it is sometimes called China's "Motor City". The city is also known for the Changchun Film Studio, one of the oldest film studios in China, and for research institutes such as the Changchun Institute of Optics.

Transport

The city is served by Changchun Longjia International Airport and is a hub of the Harbin–Dalian high-speed railway. Its metro system opened in 2002 with one of the first light rail lines in modern China.
