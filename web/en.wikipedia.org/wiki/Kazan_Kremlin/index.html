Kazan Kremlin

The Kazan Kremlin (Russian: Казанский Кремль; Tatar: Казан кирмәне) is the main historic citadel of Kazan, capital of Tatarstan, Russia, standing on a hill above the left bank of the Kazanka River near its confluence with the Volga. Built on the orders of Ivan the Terrible on the ruins of the khan's castle after the conquest of Kazan in 1552, it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the official residence of the President (Rais) of Tatarstan.

Landmarks

The white limestone walls enclose a distinctive mix of Russian Orthodox and Tatar Islamic architecture. The Qolşärif Mosque, completed in 2005 on the site of the legendary mosque destroyed in 1552, is one of the largest mosques in Russia, with four 57-metre minarets. The Annunciation Cathedral (1561–62) is among the oldest buildings, and the leaning Söyembikä Tower, named for the last queen of Kazan, tilts noticeably from the vertical. The complex also contains the Governor's Palace, the Saviour-Transfiguration Monastery remains, and an Hermitage-Kazan museum centre.

History

A fortified Bulgar and later Khanate citadel occupied the site from at least the 10th–12th centuries. Ivan IV destroyed the khanate fortress in 1552 and had Pskov masons rebuild it in white stone. The Kremlin later served as the administrative seat of the Kazan Governorate and, after 1992, of the Republic of Tatarstan.
