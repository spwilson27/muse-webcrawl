Governorate

Governorates are the principal first-level subdivisions of many Arab states.

A governorate is a territorial administrative division headed by a governor. The term is most commonly used as the English translation of the Arabic muḥāfaẓa (محافظة), the first-level subdivision of many countries in the Middle East and North Africa, and it has also been applied historically to provinces of other states, such as the guberniyas of the Russian Empire.

Usage in Arab countries

In most Arab states the governorate is the largest administrative unit below the national government, roughly equivalent to a province or state in other federations and unitary states, although their powers vary widely. Governors are usually appointed by the head of state or central government rather than elected, though the details differ by country:

  • Egypt is divided into 27 governorates (muḥāfaẓāt), each headed by a governor appointed by the president; governorates are subdivided into regions, centres and cities.
  • Iraq has 19 governorates, which since 2005 possess constitutionally recognised autonomy, with elected provincial councils.
  • Syria is divided into 14 governorates, and Jordan into 12.
  • Lebanon has nine governorates (muḥāfaẓāt), subdivided into districts (qaḍāʾ).
  • Tunisia is divided into 24 governorates (wilāyāt), the largest units of its administration.
  • Yemen, Oman, Bahrain, Kuwait and Palestine also organise their territory into governorates, typically subdivided into districts, sub-districts or directorates.
  • Saudi Arabia uses governorates as second-level divisions: its 13 regions (emirates) are subdivided into about 136 governorates. See Governorates of Saudi Arabia.

Historical usage

"Governorate" is a standard rendering of the Russian guberniya, the principal subdivision of the Russian Empire from Peter the Great's reforms of 1708 until the administrative reorganisations of the Soviet era — eight governorates were created initially, and by 1914 there were more than eighty. The term has also been used for subdivisions of the Spanish colonial empire, for the Governatorato units of Fascist Italy (such as the Governorate of Dalmatia), and for the German-administered Generalgouvernement (General Governorate) in occupied Poland during the Second World War.

Terminology

The Arabic plural is muḥāfaẓāt. In some countries the same Arabic word is translated "province" or "governorate" interchangeably. Subdivisions below the governorate level include the qaḍāʾ (district), minṭaqa (region) and markaz (centre), depending on the state.
