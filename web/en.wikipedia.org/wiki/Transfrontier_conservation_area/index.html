Transfrontier conservation area

A transfrontier conservation area (TFCA) is a protected area that straddles the boundaries of two or more countries, where the management authorities cooperate to protect ecosystems and wildlife that do not respect political borders. The concept — sometimes expressed through the term "Peace Parks" — is most developed in southern Africa.

Major examples

The Kavango–Zambezi (KAZA) TFCA, agreed by Angola, Botswana, Namibia, Zambia and Zimbabwe in 2011, is the largest terrestrial example at roughly 520,000 km² and contains the world's largest elephant population. The Great Limpopo Transfrontier Park links Kruger National Park (South Africa) with parks in Mozambique and Zimbabwe; the Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park between South Africa and Botswana, created in 2000, was the first formally declared example.

Objectives and criticism

TFCAs aim to restore wildlife migration routes, pool anti-poaching resources, and raise tourism revenue shared among neighbours. Critics raise concerns about the displacement or exclusion of local communities, uneven benefits, and the challenge of harmonising laws across states.
