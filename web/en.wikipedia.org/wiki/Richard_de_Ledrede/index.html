Richard de Ledrede

Richard de Ledrede (died 1360/61) was a Franciscan friar and Bishop of Ossory in Ireland, best known for prosecuting the Kilkenny witchcraft trials of 1324, among the earliest witch trials in Europe, and for composing Latin religious lyrics preserved in the Red Book of Ossory.

Bishop of Ossory

Appointed bishop in 1317, Ledrede was a zealous reformer who clashed repeatedly with local secular authorities, including Arnold le Poer, Seneschal of Kilkenny, and with Archbishop Alexander Bicknor of Dublin. He sought to enforce clerical discipline, collect dues, and root out heresy.

Kilkenny witch trials

In 1324 Ledrede accused Alice Kyteler, a wealthy Kilkenny woman, and her associates of heresy, sorcery, and consorting with a demon. Alice fled to England, but her servant Petronilla de Meath was flogged and burned at the stake — one of the first recorded executions for witchcraft in Ireland. The affair drew on Ledrede's use of the papal bull Super illius specula.

Red Book of Ossory

The Red Book of Ossory, kept at St Canice's Cathedral, contains Ledrede's Latin hymns and songs written, he explained, so that his clergy would not sing secular songs. He died around 1360 after a long and turbulent episcopate.
