Cheomseongdae

Cheomseongdae (Korean: 첨성대; Hanja: 瞻星臺) is an astronomical observatory in Gyeongju, South Korea. Cheomseongdae is the oldest surviving astronomical observatory in Asia, and possibly even in the world. It was constructed in the 7th century in the kingdom of Silla, whose capital was Seorabeol (now Gyeongju). Cheomseongdae was designated as South Korea's National Treasure No. 31 on December 20, 1962.

History

According to the Samguk yusa, Cheomseongdae was constructed during the reign of Queen Seondeok of Silla (632–647). The name Cheomseongdae means "star-gazing tower" in Korean; cheom means "to observe" and seong means "star". The structure was built near the royal palace and tomb complexes of the Silla kingdom.

Structure

The tower stands 9.17 metres high and is built from 362 pieces of granite, sometimes interpreted as representing the 362 days of a lunar year. Its body is bottle-shaped: a square base supports a cylindrical tower of 27 circular layers of stone, topped by a square frame-like structure that may once have supported an observation platform or astrolabe. A small opening halfway up the south face may have given access to the interior via a ladder. The interior is filled with earth and rubble up to the level of the opening.

Significance

Cheomseongdae is one of the oldest surviving scientific installations on Earth and a symbol of the advanced astronomy of the Silla kingdom. It forms part of the Gyeongju Historic Areas, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The observatory's design is thought to reflect the cosmology of the time, blending astronomical function with symbolic elements drawn from East Asian philosophy.
