Dog health

The health of dogs is a well-studied area in veterinary medicine. Common concerns include infectious diseases prevented by vaccination, parasitic infestations, dental disease, obesity, and hereditary conditions associated with particular breeds. The domestic dog has an average lifespan of roughly 10–13 years, varying greatly by size and breed.

Infectious diseases and prevention

Core vaccines for dogs protect against rabies, canine distemper, canine parvovirus, and infectious canine hepatitis (adenovirus). Other commonly used vaccines target leptospirosis, Bordetella (kennel cough), and Lyme disease. Regular deworming and flea and tick control prevent parasitic diseases.

Common conditions

Frequent health problems include periodontal disease (affecting most dogs by age three), osteoarthritis, ear infections, allergies and skin disorders, hip dysplasia in large breeds, bloat (gastric dilatation-volvulus), epilepsy, hypothyroidism, and various cancers, which are a leading cause of death in older dogs.

Nutrition and care

Preventive care includes a balanced diet appropriate to life stage, weight management, exercise, dental care, neutering or spaying where appropriate, and annual veterinary examinations. Certain human foods — including chocolate, xylitol, grapes and raisins, onions, and macadamia nuts — are toxic to dogs.
