Montana

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Montana
Statehood: 8 November 1889 (41st state)
Capital: Helena
Largest city: Billings
Nickname: Big Sky Country; the Treasure State

Montana is a state in the Mountain West of the United States, the fourth-largest by area and among the least densely populated, with just over a million residents. Its name, from the Spanish montaña (mountain), fits its western third of Rocky Mountain ranges and high valleys; its eastern two-thirds are northern Great Plains of wheat, cattle and coal.

The Lewis and Clark Expedition crossed Montana in 1805–06; the fur trade, gold rushes (1860s), the Indian Wars — including the Lakota and Cheyenne victory at the Little Bighorn in 1876 — copper kings of Butte, and homesteading followed. Glacier National Park (1910), the "Crown of the Continent", and the northern gateway to Yellowstone anchor its modern identity as a landscape state; the Missouri River begins here at Three Forks and drains toward St. Louis.

Today Montana balances ranching, mining and timber against tourism, amenity migration to Bozeman and Missoula, and the politics of public land — over a third of the state is federally owned. Seven Native American reservations preserve the sovereignty of peoples whose presence long predates the state.
