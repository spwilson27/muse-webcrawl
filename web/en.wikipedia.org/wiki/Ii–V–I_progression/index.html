Ii–V–I progression

The ii–V–I progression is a common chord progression used in Western harmony, especially in jazz. It consists of the supertonic chord (ii) moving to the dominant chord (V) and resolving to the tonic chord (I). In a major key the ii chord is a minor seventh chord, the V chord a dominant seventh, and the I chord a major (often major seventh) chord: for example, Dm7–G7–Cmaj7 in C major.

Function

The progression follows the circle of fifths: each root descends by a perfect fifth, producing strong harmonic motion. The ii chord functions as a predominant, preparing the dominant, whose tritone resolves into the tonic. In minor keys the variant iiø–V–i uses a half-diminished ii chord and a minor tonic.

Use in jazz

The ii–V–I is the fundamental building block of jazz harmony, appearing in countless standards and serving as the target of modulations. Players extend and alter the chords with tensions, apply tritone substitution to the V chord, and chain ii–V units together, as in the "rhythm changes" of I Got Rhythm. Extended forms such as iii–VI–ii–V–I add a preceding cycle, and the backdoor progression and turnarounds are closely related devices.
