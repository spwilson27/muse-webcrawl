Winter X Games

A superpipe competition at the Winter X Games in Aspen

The Winter X Games are an annual winter sports event, owned and produced by ESPN, focusing on extreme action winter sports. The inaugural Winter X Games were held in January 1997 at Big Bear Lake, California. The event has been held in Aspen, Colorado, since 2002, at the Buttermilk ski area, under a long-running arrangement with Aspen Skiing Company.

The Winter X Games feature events in skiing, snowboarding, and snowmobiling, including SuperPipe, Slopestyle, Big Air, and (historically) Snowmobile SnoCross. They show, both on television and on site, some of the world's best winter action sports athletes competing for medals and prize money.

History

Following the success of the summer X Games, first held in 1995, ESPN launched a winter counterpart in 1997. Early editions were held at Big Bear Lake, California (1997) and Crested Butte, Colorado (1998–1999), and Mount Snow, Vermont (2000–2001), before settling in Aspen in 2002. The games grew rapidly in prominence as sports such as snowboarding and freestyle skiing gained popularity, and many Winter X Games events later became part of the Winter Olympic Games program, including the halfpipe, slopestyle, and big air disciplines.

Notable moments in the event's history include Shaun White's dominance of the snowboard SuperPipe and Slopestyle events, including a perfect score of 100 in the SuperPipe in 2012; Tanner Hall's and Sarah Burke's successes in ski events; and snowmobiler Caleb Moore's fatal crash in 2013, the first death in X Games history. Snowmobile events were subsequently dropped from the program for several years.

Events

Current and former disciplines include:

  • Ski and snowboard SuperPipe
  • Ski and snowboard Slopestyle
  • Ski and snowboard Big Air
  • Knuckle Huck
  • Snowmobile SnoCross, freestyle, and speed and style (discontinued or intermittent)
  • Snow bike and adaptive events

Notable athletes

Shaun White is the most decorated athlete in Winter X Games history, with 15 gold medals across snowboarding events (and additional medals at the summer games in skateboarding). Other prominent athletes include Jamie Anderson, Mark McMorris, Chloe Kim, Kelly Clark, Henrik Harlaut, and Marcus Kleveland.
