Qiang people

The Qiang people (Chinese: 羌族; Qiang: Rrmea) are an ethnic group recognised by the People's Republic of China, numbering roughly 300,000. They live mainly in the mountainous northwest of Sichuan Province, in Mao County, Wenchuan, Li County, Heishui and Songpan, along the upper reaches of the Min River and its tributaries.

Language

The Qiang languages belong to the Qiangic branch of the Tibeto-Burman family. They divide broadly into Northern Qiang and Southern Qiang, which are not mutually intelligible. Most Qiang languages are tonal or pitch-accented and were traditionally unwritten; a Latin-based script was developed for Northern Qiang in the 1990s. Many Qiang now speak Sichuanese Mandarin as their everyday language, and the native languages are considered endangered.

Culture

Qiang villages are famed for their tall stone watchtowers, called diaolou, and for fortified stone houses built on steep mountainsides. Traditional religion centres on nature worship, white stones placed on roofs and towers as sacred objects, and ritual specialists known as shibi (or duangong), who preside over ceremonies using drums, chants and epic recitation. Distinctive embroidered clothing, cloud-pattern shoes, the Qiang flute (羌笛) and polyphonic folk singing are hallmarks of Qiang culture. The Qiang New Year, held on the first day of the tenth lunar month, is inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, having been placed on the urgent safeguarding list after the 2008 earthquake.

History

The term "Qiang" appears in oracle bone inscriptions of the Shang dynasty and in early Chinese texts referred to western pastoral peoples. Over centuries many Qiang groups were absorbed into the Han Chinese and Tibetan populations. The modern Qiang heartland was devastated by the 2008 Sichuan earthquake, whose epicentre at Wenchuan lay within Qiang territory; many villages, towers and cultural relics were destroyed and the community suffered heavy losses.
