Peridotite

Peridotite
Type: Igneous, ultramafic
Composition: chiefly olivine and pyroxene
Colour: dark green to black

Peridotite is a dense, coarse-grained igneous rock consisting mostly of the silicate minerals olivine and pyroxene. Peridotite is ultramafic, as the rock contains less than 45% silica. It is high in magnesium, reflecting the high proportions of magnesium-rich olivine, and is believed to be the dominant rock of Earth's upper mantle.

Types

  • Dunite — more than 90% olivine.
  • Harzburgite — olivine plus orthopyroxene.
  • Lherzolite — olivine with both orthopyroxene and clinopyroxene; the most commonly cited mantle composition.
  • Wehrlite — olivine plus clinopyroxene.

Occurrence and significance

Peridotite reaches the surface in ophiolite complexes, as xenoliths carried up by kimberlite and basaltic eruptions, and in orogenic massifs. Partial melting of peridotite generates basaltic magmas, making the rock central to understanding volcanism and the composition of the mantle. Serpentinised peridotite can host deposits of chromite, nickel and platinum-group elements, and peridotite terrains have been studied for natural carbon mineralisation.
