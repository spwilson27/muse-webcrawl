Kwashiorkor

Kwashiorkor
SpecialtyPaediatrics, nutrition
SymptomsOedema, enlarged liver, hair depigmentation, skin lesions, irritability
CausesSevere protein deficiency / severe acute malnutrition
TreatmentCareful nutritional rehabilitation, treatment of infections

Kwashiorkor is a form of severe acute malnutrition characterised by oedema (swelling), an enlarged liver with fatty infiltrates, skin and hair changes, and preserved subcutaneous fat giving a deceptively "well-fed" appearance. It primarily affects young children in regions of food insecurity.

Signs and symptoms

Hallmark features include pitting oedema beginning in the feet, a distended abdomen, reddish-orange depigmentation and thinning of the hair, flaky-paint dermatosis, apathy and irritability, growth failure, and hepatomegaly. Children are highly susceptible to infections, which often precipitate deterioration.

Causes

Kwashiorkor was classically attributed to diets adequate in calories but severely deficient in protein (e.g., weaning onto starchy gruels), though modern understanding of severe acute malnutrition emphasises a multifactorial picture involving infection, oxidative stress and gut dysfunction. It contrasts with marasmus, dominated by wasting without oedema. The name derives from the Ga language of Ghana, popularly associated with the illness of a child displaced from the breast by a new sibling; the condition was described in the medical literature by Cicely Williams in the 1930s.

Treatment

Treatment follows phased protocols (such as WHO F-75 then F-100 therapeutic feeds or ready-to-use therapeutic food), correcting electrolytes, hypoglycaemia and hypothermia, and treating infections before and during nutritional rehabilitation. Refeeding must be cautious to avoid refeeding syndrome.
