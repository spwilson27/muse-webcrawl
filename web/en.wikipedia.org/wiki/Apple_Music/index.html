Apple Music is a music streaming service developed by Apple Inc. Users select music to stream on demand or listen to curated playlists, and the service includes the live radio stations Apple Music 1, Apple Music Hits, and Apple Music Country, broadcasting in over 160 countries. The service was announced on June 8, 2015, and launched on June 30, 2015. It grew out of Apple's acquisition of Beats Electronics in 2014, the largest acquisition in the company's history at $3 billion, which brought the Beats Music streaming service and the involvement of Jimmy Iovine and Dr. Dre.

Apple Music's Listen Now tab offers personalised mixes, playlists, and recommendations.

Features

Apple Music combines a catalogue of over 100 million songs with the user's own iCloud Music Library. Recommendations are generated both algorithmically and by human curators. Since 2021 the service has offered lossless audio (ALAC, up to 24-bit/192 kHz) and Spatial Audio with Dolby Atmos at no extra cost. Other features include time-synced lyrics, Apple Music Sing (karaoke-style sing-along, 2022), collaborative playlists, artist pages with the "Connect" social feature (discontinued in 2018), and exclusives and live sessions. A separate app, Apple Music Classical, launched in March 2023 following Apple's acquisition of the Primephonic service, tailored to classical repertoire and metadata.

Subscriber growth

The service reached 10 million subscribers within six months, 20 million by December 2016, and Apple last officially reported 60 million in June 2019; third-party estimates since have placed it second globally behind Spotify among paid music streaming services. Pricing tiers include an individual plan, family and student plans, and the lower-cost Apple Music Voice Plan (2021–2023, discontinued). Apple Music is bundled in Apple One subscriptions.

Business and criticism

During its 2015 launch, Apple faced criticism from artists over a plan not to pay royalties during free trials; after an open letter from Taylor Swift, Apple reversed the policy. Royalty rates and discovery algorithms remain subjects of debate among musicians and labels. The service's integration with the iPhone and other Apple devices has also featured in antitrust complaints, including a European Commission case that in 2024 fined Apple €1.8 billion over anti-steering rules affecting music streaming apps — a penalty Apple appealed.
