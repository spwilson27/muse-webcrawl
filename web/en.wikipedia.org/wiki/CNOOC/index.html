CNOOC

China National Offshore Oil Corporation, or CNOOC Group (Chinese: 中国海洋石油集团有限公司), is the third-largest national oil company in China, after CNPC (parent of PetroChina) and China Petrochemical Corporation (Sinopec Group). The CNOOC Group focuses on the exploitation, exploration and development of crude oil and natural gas in offshore China, and is owned entirely by the Government of the People's Republic of China.

History

CNOOC was founded in 1982 when China opened its offshore waters to cooperation with foreign oil companies. Its listed arm, CNOOC Limited, is incorporated in Hong Kong and listed there and in Shanghai (formerly also New York, until delisting under a U.S. executive order in 2021). In 2005 CNOOC Limited's US$18.5 billion bid for the American company Unocal was withdrawn amid political opposition in the United States; it later acquired Canada's Nexen in 2013 for US$15.1 billion.

Operations

Operations include the Bohai Bay, South China Sea, East China Sea and Yellow Sea offshore areas, plus overseas assets in Canada, the United States, Brazil, Guyana (as a partner in the Stabroek block), Africa and elsewhere. CNOOC's activities in disputed waters of the South China Sea have drawn diplomatic controversy.
