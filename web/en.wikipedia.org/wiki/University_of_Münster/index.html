The University of Münster (German: Universität Münster, until 2023 officially the Westfälische Wilhelms-Universität Münster) is a public research university in Münster, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. With around 43,000 students it is one of the largest universities in Germany.

History

The university traces its origins to 1780, when a university was founded in Münster under the Prince-Bishopric. It was dissolved in the early 19th century and refounded in 1902 by order of Kaiser Wilhelm II, from whom it took its former name. The university's main building is the baroque Schloss, the former residence of the prince-bishops of Münster.

In 2023 the university renamed itself simply "University of Münster", dropping the reference to Wilhelm II following debate about the Kaiser's colonial and antisemitic legacy.

Academics and research

The university has 15 faculties covering Catholic and Protestant theology, law, economics, medicine, education and social sciences, psychology, history and philosophy, philology, mathematics and computer science, physics, chemistry and pharmacy, biology, and geosciences. It hosts Clusters of Excellence including "Religion and Politics" and "Mathematics Münster: Dynamics – Geometry – Structure", and is known for its battery research center (MEET) and the Center for Soft Nanoscience.

Campus and student life

University buildings are spread across the city, around the Schlossplatz and the Aasee lake. Münster is a classic German university city, famous for its bicycle culture and for the Peace of Westphalia, negotiated in the city in 1648.
