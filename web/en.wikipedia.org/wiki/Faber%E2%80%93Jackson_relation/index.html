Faber–Jackson relation

Named afterSandra Faber and Robert Jackson
Published1976
Applies toElliptical galaxies

The Faber–Jackson relation is an empirical power-law relation between the luminosity of an elliptical galaxy and the velocity dispersion of its stars. It was first proposed by astronomers Sandra Faber and Robert Earl Jackson in 1976.

Relation

The relation states that a galaxy's luminosity L scales approximately as the fourth power of its central stellar velocity dispersion σ: L ∝ σ⁴. More luminous ellipticals thus have stars moving at higher random speeds, reflecting their deeper gravitational potential wells.

Significance

Together with the size of a galaxy, the Faber–Jackson relation defines the fundamental plane of elliptical galaxies, and it can be used as a distance indicator: measuring a galaxy's velocity dispersion predicts its intrinsic luminosity, which compared with its apparent brightness gives its distance. It is the elliptical-galaxy counterpart of the Tully–Fisher relation for spiral galaxies.
