Posterior abdominal wall

Muscles and structures of the posterior abdominal wall.

The posterior abdominal wall is the back wall of the abdominal cavity in human anatomy. It is formed centrally by the lumbar vertebrae and intervertebral discs, and by a group of muscles: the psoas major, quadratus lumborum and iliacus, with the diaphragm above and the transversus abdominis and aponeuroses laterally. Its surface is largely covered by parietal peritoneum, behind which lie the retroperitoneal organs.

Structures

Against the posterior abdominal wall lie the kidneys and adrenal glands, the ureters, the abdominal aorta and inferior vena cava, the ascending and descending colon, and parts of the duodenum and pancreas. The sympathetic trunks run along the medial borders of the psoas muscles, and the lumbar plexus forms within the substance of psoas major, giving off nerves including the iliohypogastric, ilioinguinal, genitofemoral, lateral femoral cutaneous, obturator and femoral nerves, which emerge around the muscle's borders.

Muscles

  • Psoas major — arises from the lumbar vertebrae and passes beneath the inguinal ligament to insert on the lesser trochanter of the femur; with iliacus it is the main flexor of the hip.
  • Quadratus lumborum — a quadrilateral muscle between the iliac crest, the twelfth rib and the lumbar transverse processes; it stabilises the trunk and laterally flexes it.
  • Iliacus — lines the iliac fossa and joins psoas major to form iliopsoas.
  • Diaphragm — its crura and arcuate ligaments form the upper part of the wall and provide passages for the aorta, oesophagus and inferior vena cava.

Clinical significance

The posterior abdominal wall is a common site of retroperitoneal haemorrhage after trauma and of psoas abscess, classically a complication of spinal tuberculosis. The surface anatomy of the wall, including the lumbar (Petit) triangle, is relevant to rare lumbar hernias. Weakness or irritation of structures here can cause back and flank pain that mimics disease of the retroperitoneal organs.
