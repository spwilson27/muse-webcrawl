Fiorello Henry La Guardia (December 11, 1882 – September 20, 1947) was an American attorney and politician who represented New York in the U.S. House of Representatives and served as the 99th mayor of New York City from 1934 to 1945. Known as "the Little Flower", the translation of his Italian first name Fiorello, he was a short (5 ft 2 in, 1.57 m), dynamic reformer who fought the Tammany Hall machine, restored honest government after the scandals of the Walker era, and, as a three-term mayor, presided over New York's recovery from the Great Depression and its mobilisation in the Second World War.

Early life and Congress

La Guardia was born in New York to an Italian immigrant father, a U.S. Army bandmaster, and a Jewish mother from Trieste; he grew up on Army posts in Arizona and worked for the U.S. consular service in Budapest and Fiume before returning to New York as an interpreter at Ellis Island. He earned a law degree from NYU and was elected to Congress in 1916 from East Harlem. In the First World War he served as a U.S. Army aviator on the Italian–Austrian front. Back in Congress (1923–1933), he co-sponsored the Norris–La Guardia Act of 1932, which restricted federal injunctions in labour disputes, and became a leading progressive Republican voice against Prohibition and for public power.

Mayor of New York

Elected mayor in 1933 on a Fusion ticket amid the Depression, La Guardia worked closely — despite political differences — with President Franklin D. Roosevelt, funnelling New Deal funds into the city. With Parks Commissioner Robert Moses he built parks, playgrounds, bridges (including the Triborough Bridge), parkways, public housing (the NYCHA was founded in 1934), and the IND subway expansions; he unified the transit system in 1940 and opened New York Municipal Airport in 1939, renamed LaGuardia Airport in his honour in 1953. During the war he directed the federal Office of Civilian Defense (1941–42) and read the comics aloud on the radio during a 1945 newspaper delivery strike — one of the most famous episodes of his flamboyant rapport with the public.

La Guardia declined to run for a fourth term in 1945 and became director-general of the UN Relief and Rehabilitation Administration (UNRRA) in 1946. He died of pancreatic cancer in 1947. Historians routinely rank him among the greatest American mayors; New York's LaGuardia Airport, LaGuardia Community College, and the Fiorello H. LaGuardia High School bear his name.
