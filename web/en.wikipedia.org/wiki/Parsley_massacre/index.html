Parsley massacre

The Parsley massacre (Spanish: el corte, "the cutting") was a mass killing of Haitians living in the Dominican Republic's northwestern frontier and the Cibao region in October 1937, ordered by Dominican dictator Rafael Trujillo. Estimates of the death toll range from several thousand to more than 20,000.

Events

Over several days, Dominican soldiers and civilians killed Haitian men, women and children, largely with machetes and bayonets to disguise the killings as peasant violence. The massacre takes its popular name from the shibboleth allegedly used to identify Haitians: victims were asked to pronounce perejil (Spanish for parsley), whose trilled "r" and "j" sounds were difficult for French and Haitian Creole speakers.

Aftermath

Under international pressure, Trujillo's government agreed to pay an indemnity to Haiti, though only part was delivered and little reached survivors' families. The massacre, part of Trujillo's policy of "Dominicanisation" and anti-Haitianism, remains a defining trauma in Haitian–Dominican relations.
