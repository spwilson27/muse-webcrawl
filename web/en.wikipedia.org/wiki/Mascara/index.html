Mascara

Mascara is a cosmetic commonly used to darken, lengthen, curl, and thicken the eyelashes. It is applied with a brush or wand to the upper and lower eyelashes, and is typically available in black, brown, and clear formulas, as well as fashion colours. Modern mascara consists of pigments, waxes, oils, and preservatives in an emulsion, packaged in a tube with a brush applicator.

History

Eye darkening preparations date to ancient Egypt, where kohl made from malachite, charcoal, and soot was applied to the lashes and eyelids. In the 19th century, perfumer Eugène Rimmel created a popular commercial preparation from petroleum jelly and coal dust; "rimmel" remains a word for mascara in several languages. In 1915, T. L. Williams founded Maybelline, named for his sister Mabel, selling a cake mascara of petroleum jelly and carbon black. The modern tube-and-wand format was introduced in the 1950s, notably by Helena Rubinstein in 1957, which made application far more convenient.

Formulation and types

Mascaras are marketed for volumising, lengthening, curling, or waterproof wear. Waterproof formulas use volatile silicones and waxes and resist water but require oil-based removers; tubing mascaras coat each lash in polymer tubes that slide off with warm water. Ingredients are regulated as cosmetics in most jurisdictions, and preservatives are included to inhibit bacterial growth; manufacturers recommend replacing mascara roughly every three months to reduce the risk of eye infection.
