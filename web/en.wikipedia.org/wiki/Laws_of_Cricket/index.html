Laws of Cricket

The Laws of Cricket are the official rules of the sport of cricket, maintained by the Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC) at Lord's Cricket Ground in London. First codified in 1744 and substantially revised in 1788, the Laws now comprise 42 laws covering the players, equipment, conduct of the match, methods of dismissal, and the roles of the umpires.

Lord's, home of the MCC, custodian of the Laws.

History

The earliest known code dates from 1744; the MCC, founded in 1787, issued its first code in 1788 and has revised the Laws periodically since, with major editions in 1947, 1980, 2000, and 2017 — the latter the first to be written in gender-neutral language and to introduce a red-card sanction for serious player misconduct.

Structure

The Laws are preceded by a Preamble, the "Spirit of Cricket", exhorting players to play hard but fairly and to respect opponents and officials. Match formats not covered fully by the Laws — such as limited-overs and Twenty20 cricket — are additionally governed by playing conditions issued by the International Cricket Council (ICC).
