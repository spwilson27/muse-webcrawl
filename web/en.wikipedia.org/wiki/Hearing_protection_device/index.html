Hearing protection device

A hearing protection device (HPD), also known as a hearing protector, is an ear protection device worn in or over the ears while exposed to hazardous noise to help prevent noise-induced hearing loss. HPDs reduce (but do not entirely eliminate) the level of noise entering the ear, and are also used to protect against annoyance and to improve concentration in noisy environments.

Types

  • Earplugs — inserted in the ear canal; include pre-moulded, formable (foam), custom-moulded and canal-cap designs.
  • Earmuffs — cups with sound-attenuating material held over the ears by a headband.
  • Electronic devices — active protectors that amplify quiet sounds (speech, game calls) while limiting loud ones, and level-dependent or communication headsets used by military and aviation personnel.
  • Double protection — plugs plus muffs, recommended at very high noise levels.

Ratings and effectiveness

Manufacturers give laboratory ratings such as the Noise Reduction Rating (NRR, in the United States) and Single Number Rating (SNR, in Europe). Real-world attenuation is typically much lower than laboratory values because of poor fitting, so agencies apply derating schemes; fit-testing systems now allow individual measurement of attenuation. Effectiveness depends heavily on consistent, correct use throughout the exposure period — removing a protector for even a few minutes at high noise levels greatly reduces total protection.

Regulation and use

Occupational regulations (e.g. OSHA in the U.S., the Control of Noise at Work Regulations in the UK) require employers to provide hearing protection above specified action levels and to offer it as part of hearing conservation programmes, alongside engineering controls, which are preferred in the hierarchy of controls.
