A coach is a type of bus built for longer-distance service, in contrast to transit buses used within a city. Coaches are characterized by a raised passenger deck above a large underfloor luggage compartment, high-backed reclining seats, air conditioning, and often an on-board toilet and entertainment systems.

Design

Most coaches have a single deck, though double-decker coaches are used for sightseeing and some high-capacity routes. They are usually built either as complete vehicles by manufacturers such as Scania, Volvo, MAN and Mercedes-Benz, or as chassis fitted with bodywork by specialist coachbuilders. Engines are typically rear-mounted diesels, increasingly supplemented or replaced by battery-electric drivetrains on shorter routes.

Uses

Coaches operate scheduled intercity networks (such as Greyhound in North America and FlixBus in Europe), private charters, sports team and tour transport, school excursions, and airport shuttle services. The word "coach" derives from the horse-drawn carriages — themselves named after the Hungarian town of Kocs — that performed the same role before motor transport.
