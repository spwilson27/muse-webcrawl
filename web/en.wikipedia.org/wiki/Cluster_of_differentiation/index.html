The cluster of differentiation (also known as cluster of designation or classification determinant and often abbreviated as CD) is a protocol used for the identification and investigation of cell surface molecules providing targets for immunophenotyping of cells. It was established at the first International Workshop and Conference on Human Leukocyte Differentiation Antigens (HLDA), held in Paris in 1982.

Nomenclature

The system gives each unique cell surface molecule, as identified by a cluster of monoclonal antibodies reacting with the same epitope, a unique CD number. Well-known examples include CD4, expressed by helper T cells and the receptor exploited by HIV, and CD8, expressed by cytotoxic T cells. CD numbers have been assigned well beyond CD350, covering leukocytes and many other cell types including stem cells and endothelium.

Applications

CD markers are fundamental to flow cytometry, the diagnosis and classification of leukaemias and lymphomas, monitoring of HIV infection through CD4 counts, and the design of therapeutic monoclonal antibodies such as rituximab (anti-CD20).
