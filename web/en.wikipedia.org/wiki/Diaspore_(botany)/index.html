Diaspore (botany)

In botany, a diaspore is a plant dispersal unit consisting of a seed or spore plus any additional tissues that assist dispersal. In some seed plants, the diaspore is a seed and fruit together, or a seed and an elaiosome. In a few plants, the diaspore is most or all of the plant, as in a tumbleweed.

Role in dispersal

Diaspores are central to seed dispersal, by which offspring are carried away from the parent plant. Dispersal structures are adapted to particular agents: wings and plumes for wind dispersal (anemochory), hooks and barbs for attachment to animals (epizoochory), fleshy attractive tissues and elaiosomes for animal ingestion or transport (endozoochory and myrmecochory), and buoyant tissues for water dispersal (hydrochory).

Types

Examples of diaspores include the winged samara of maples, the pappus-bearing achene of dandelions, the burrs of burdock, and the entire infructescence shed by some grasses. The concept contrasts with the seed alone, emphasising the functional unit actually dispersed.
