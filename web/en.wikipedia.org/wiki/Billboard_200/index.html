The Billboard 200 is a record chart ranking the 200 most popular music albums and EPs in the United States. Published weekly by Billboard magazine, it is widely regarded as the standard measure of an album's commercial success in the American market, and reaching number one on the chart is a major industry milestone.

The Billboard 200 ranks the top albums in the United States.

History

Billboard published its first album chart in 1945, initially listing only a handful of best-selling records. A regular weekly chart of 200 positions emerged by the mid-1960s, eventually taking the name "Top LPs" and, in 1992, "The Billboard 200." In 1991 the chart began using Nielsen SoundScan point-of-sale data, replacing store reports with actual scanned sales and dramatically increasing its accuracy. In December 2014 the methodology was broadened from pure album sales to a multi-metric consumption model of "equivalent album units," incorporating track sales and on-demand streams.

Methodology

Charts are compiled from data collected by Luminate. One equivalent album unit equals one album sale, ten individual track downloads from an album, or a set number of official audio and video streams of album songs (1,250 premium or 3,750 ad-supported streams). Chart weeks run Friday through Thursday, with charts dated the second Tuesday after the tracking week ends.

Records

The Beatles hold the record for the most number-one albums (19), and their Abbey Road era contemporaries are joined by modern acts such as Taylor Swift, who has accumulated the most number ones among female artists. The longest-running number-one album is the West Side Story soundtrack, with 54 weeks at the top, while Adele's 21 is among the longest-charting albums overall. Pink Floyd's The Dark Side of the Moon holds the record for total weeks on the chart, with more than 900.
