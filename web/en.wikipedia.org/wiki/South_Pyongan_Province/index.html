South Pyongan Province

South Pyongan Province (Korean: 평안남도, Phyŏngan-namdo) is a province of North Korea, created in 1896 when the old Pyongan Province was divided into northern and southern halves. Its capital is Pyongsong. The province surrounds, but does not include, the capital city Pyongyang, which is administered separately.

Geography

The province borders Pyongyang and North Hwanghae to the south, Jagang and Ryanggang to the north and east, and the Yellow Sea (Korea Bay) to the west. The Taedong River flows through it to the port of Nampo, a special city within its coastal area. Much of the interior is mountainous, while the western plains are an important rice-growing region.

Economy

South Pyongan contains major coal fields around Anju, heavy industry at Nampo and Pyongsong, and the West Sea Barrage at the Taedong estuary. Pyongsong is known as a wholesale distribution and light-industry centre and hosts a number of scientific institutions.

Administrative divisions

The province is divided into cities including Pyongsong, Anju, Kaechon, Sunchon, Tokchon and Nampo (a special city), and numerous counties. Its population was estimated at over four million in North Korea's 2008 census.
