Markov decision process

A Markov decision process (MDP) is a discrete-time stochastic control process used in mathematics, economics and artificial intelligence to model decision-making in situations where outcomes are partly random and partly under the control of a decision maker. MDPs are named after the Russian mathematician Andrey Markov and generalise Markov chains by adding actions and rewards.

Definition

An MDP is formally a tuple of states, actions, transition probabilities and rewards. At each time step the process is in some state; the decision maker chooses an action, the process moves randomly to a new state according to transition probabilities that depend only on the current state and action (the Markov property), and the decision maker receives a numerical reward. The goal is to find a policy — a rule for choosing actions — that maximises the expected cumulative discounted reward.

Solution methods

Optimal policies can be found by dynamic programming algorithms such as value iteration and policy iteration, which solve the Bellman optimality equation. When the transition model is unknown, reinforcement learning algorithms such as Q-learning estimate optimal behaviour from experience. Partially observable MDPs (POMDPs) extend the framework to situations where the state cannot be observed directly.

Applications

MDPs are used in robotics, automated control, inventory and queueing systems, finance, and the training of game-playing and language-model agents through reinforcement learning from feedback.
