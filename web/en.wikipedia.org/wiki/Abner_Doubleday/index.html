Abner Doubleday (26 June 1819 – 26 January 1893) was a career United States Army officer and Union general in the American Civil War. He fired the first shot in defence of Fort Sumter in 1861 and fought at Antietam, Fredericksburg, and Gettysburg. He is popularly — but erroneously — credited with inventing baseball.

Military career

A graduate of West Point (1842), Doubleday served in the Mexican–American War and on the frontier. At Fort Sumter he aimed the first Union gun fired in reply to the Confederate bombardment. As a corps commander he took over I Corps at Gettysburg when John Reynolds was killed on 1 July 1863, holding the field until relieved by George Meade, whose loss of confidence ended Doubleday's senior field service.

The baseball myth

In 1907 the Mills Commission, promoted by sporting-goods magnate Albert Spalding, declared on the sole testimony of Abner Graves that Doubleday had invented baseball at Cooperstown, New York, in 1839. Doubleday, then a cadet at West Point, never claimed the honour in his extensive writings, and historians regard the story as a myth; nonetheless Cooperstown became home to the National Baseball Hall of Fame.

Later life

After retiring from the army in 1873, Doubleday became a prominent member of the Theosophical Society in New York and wrote extensively on military history and spiritualism. He died in Mendham, New Jersey, and is buried in Arlington National Cemetery.
