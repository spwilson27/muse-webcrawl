The European–African–Middle Eastern Campaign Medal is a military award of the United States Armed Forces, established by Executive Order 9265 of President Franklin D. Roosevelt on 6 November 1942. It recognised service performed in the European Theater (including North Africa and the Middle East) during the Second World War, between 7 December 1941 and 2 March 1946.

Design

The bronze medal depicts an LST landing craft and troops disembarking in the foreground with aircraft above. The ribbon is brown, green, white, red and blue, representing the theatre's terrain and the colours of the Allied and Axis powers. Campaign participation is denoted by bronze and silver service stars, and the Arrowhead device denotes amphibious assault landings.

Criteria

The medal was awarded to personnel serving within the theatre boundaries or in combat operations therein during the eligibility period.
