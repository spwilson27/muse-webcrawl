The Hawaiian monk seal (Neomonachus schauinslandi) is an endangered species of earless seal in the family Phocidae, endemic to the Hawaiian Islands. It is one of only two surviving species of monk seal, the other being the Mediterranean monk seal; a third species, the Caribbean monk seal, is extinct.

Description

Adults are grey-brown, measuring about 2.1–2.4 m (7–7.5 ft) in length and weighing 230–280 kg (500–610 lb); pups are born black. Monk seals are largely solitary. They feed on reef fish, cephalopods, and crustaceans, diving to depths of several hundred metres, and haul out on sandy beaches to rest and give birth.

Conservation

Hunted nearly to extinction in the nineteenth century, the species is protected under the U.S. Endangered Species Act and Marine Mammal Protection Act, and is Hawaii's state mammal. Most of the population lives in the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands within Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument, though numbers in the main Hawaiian Islands have been increasing. Threats include food limitation, shark predation, entanglement in marine debris, disease such as toxoplasmosis, and habitat loss from sea-level rise. The population, long in decline, has grown slowly in recent years to around 1,600 animals.
