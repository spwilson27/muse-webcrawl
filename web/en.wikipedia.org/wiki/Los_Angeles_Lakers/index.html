Los Angeles Lakers

The Los Angeles Lakers are an American professional basketball team based in Los Angeles. The Lakers compete in the National Basketball Association (NBA) as a member of the Western Conference's Pacific Division. They play their home games at Crypto.com Arena, shared with the Los Angeles Sparks of the WNBA. The Lakers are one of the most successful teams in NBA history, having won 17 NBA championships, tied for the most in league history with the Boston Celtics.

History

The franchise was founded in 1947 as the Minneapolis Lakers, taking its name from Minnesota's nickname, "Land of 10,000 Lakes". Led by centre George Mikan, the Lakers won five championships in Minneapolis before relocating to Los Angeles in 1960. The 1970s teams featured Jerry West, Elgin Baylor and Wilt Chamberlain, winning the 1972 title. The "Showtime" Lakers of the 1980s, led by Magic Johnson and Kareem Abdul-Jabbar and coached by Pat Riley, won five championships with a fast-paced style. Under Phil Jackson, Shaquille O'Neal and Kobe Bryant led the team to three consecutive titles from 2000 to 2002, with Bryant winning two more in 2009 and 2010. LeBron James and Anthony Davis led the Lakers to their 17th championship in 2020.

Rivalries and legacy

The Lakers' rivalry with the Boston Celtics is among the most famous in sports; the teams have met a record twelve times in the NBA Finals. Numerous Lakers players, including Johnson, Abdul-Jabbar, West, Chamberlain, O'Neal and Bryant, are in the Basketball Hall of Fame.
