RASopathy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

RASopathy
Pathway:RAS/MAPK (RAS–ERK) signalling
Cause:Germline mutations in pathway genes
Includes:Noonan syndrome, Costello syndrome, cardiofaciocutaneous syndrome, neurofibromatosis type 1

The RASopathies are a group of developmental syndromes caused by germline mutations in genes that encode components or regulators of the RAS/MAPK signalling pathway. Because the pathway is essential for cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival, its dysregulation during development produces a recognisable constellation of congenital abnormalities.

Included syndromes

  • Noonan syndrome — the most common RASopathy, caused by mutations in PTPN11, SOS1, RAF1, KRAS and other genes.
  • Costello syndrome — caused by activating HRAS mutations.
  • Cardiofaciocutaneous (CFC) syndrome — usually caused by BRAF, MAP2K1, or MAP2K2 mutations.
  • Neurofibromatosis type 1 — caused by loss-of-function mutations in NF1, which encodes the RAS-GAP neurofibromin.
  • Legius syndrome, LEOPARD syndrome (Noonan syndrome with multiple lentigines), and capillary malformation–arteriovenous malformation syndrome are also commonly classified among the RASopathies.

Clinical features

Shared features include characteristic facial appearance, short stature, congenital heart disease (especially pulmonary valve stenosis and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy), developmental delay and learning difficulties, skin and hair abnormalities, skeletal anomalies such as pectus deformity, and — in several of the syndromes — an increased risk of certain cancers in childhood.

Genetics and treatment

Most RASopathies are autosomal dominant. Somatic mutations in the same pathway genes are among the most common drivers of human cancer, which is why the field is closely connected to oncology. Treatment is largely supportive and directed at specific complications; MEK inhibitors, developed for cancer, have shown benefit in some severe cases, such as the hypertrophic cardiomyopathy of infants with RAF1- or RIT1-related Noonan syndrome.
