Overview

A degree, usually denoted by °, is a unit of angular measurement equal to one 360th of a full rotation. Degrees are the everyday unit for angles in navigation, surveying and geography: latitude and longitude are traditionally quoted in degrees, arcminutes (′) and arcseconds (″), one degree containing 60 minutes and each minute 60 seconds — a legacy of the Babylonian sexagesimal (base-60) number system.

In mathematics the radian is preferred for calculations, one degree being π/180 radians; the gradian, 1/400 of a turn, survives in some surveying traditions.

Why 360?

The division of the circle into 360 parts is ancient, inherited from Babylonian astronomy and Greek geometry: 360 is divisible by many small numbers, and it roughly matches the number of days in a year, so the Sun's daily motion along the ecliptic is about one degree. Hipparchus and Ptolemy both worked in degrees, and the convention has never been displaced in practical navigation.
