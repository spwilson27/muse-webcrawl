Sir William Schwenck Gilbert (18 November 1836 – 29 May 1911) was an English dramatist, librettist, poet and illustrator best known for his collaboration with composer Arthur Sullivan, which produced fourteen comic operas, the most famous being H.M.S. Pinafore, The Pirates of Penzance and one of the most frequently performed works in the history of musical theatre, The Mikado.

Gilbert in later life; his libretti defined the English comic opera.

Career

Gilbert began as a writer of comic verse for the magazine Fun, published as the Bab Ballads, and of burlesques and plays for the London stage. Producer Richard D'Oyly Carte brought him together with Sullivan in 1875 for Trial by Jury; their partnership, staged at Carte's Savoy Theatre, ran until The Gondoliers (1889), after which a quarrel over expenses broke the collaboration, briefly resumed for two final operas.

Style and legacy

Gilbert's libretti are noted for their "topsy-turvy" logic, in which absurd premises are pursued with rigorous reason, and for patter songs that influenced later lyricists from Cole Porter to Tom Lehrer. He was knighted in 1907, the first English writer to receive a knighthood for his plays alone.
