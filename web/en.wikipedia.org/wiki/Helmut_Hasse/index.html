Helmut Hasse (25 August 1898 – 26 May 1979) was a German mathematician working in algebraic number theory, known for fundamental contributions to class field theory, the application of p-adic numbers to local class field theory and diophantine geometry (the Hasse principle), and to local zeta functions.

Life and career

Hasse was born in Kassel and served in the Imperial German Navy during the First World War. He studied at the University of Göttingen under David Hilbert and others, and completed his doctorate under Kurt Hensel at Marburg in 1921, with a dissertation containing the Hasse–Minkowski theorem on quadratic forms over number fields. He held chairs at Kiel, Halle, and Marburg, and in 1934 succeeded Hermann Weyl at Göttingen. His career under the Nazi regime was controversial, and after the war he returned to Berlin before moving to Hamburg in 1950.

Work

Hasse proved the Hasse norm theorem for cyclic extensions, developed the theory of local class field theory, and proved the Riemann hypothesis for zeta functions of elliptic curves over finite fields. His Zahlentheorie (Number Theory) became a classic text. The Hasse diagram of a partially ordered set and the Hasse invariant are named after him.
