Syllabus of Errors

The Syllabus of Errors (Latin: Syllabus Errorum) is a document issued by the Holy See under Pope Pius IX on 8 December 1864, as an appendix to his encyclical Quanta cura. It consists of a list of eighty propositions that the Church condemned as errors, drawn from earlier papal allocutions, encyclicals and letters.

Contents

The condemned propositions are grouped under ten headings, covering pantheism, naturalism and absolute rationalism; moderate rationalism; indifferentism and latitudinarianism; socialism and communism; errors about the Church and her rights; errors about civil society; errors concerning natural and Christian ethics; errors concerning Christian marriage; errors regarding the civil power of the Pope; and errors related to modern liberalism. The final, most famous proposition condemns the claim that "the Roman Pontiff can, and ought to, reconcile himself, and come to terms with progress, liberalism and modern civilization."

Reception

The Syllabus provoked sharp controversy: liberal governments and commentators read it as a declaration of war on modernity, while Catholic commentators such as Bishop Félix Dupanloup argued for a moderate interpretation, distinguishing the condemned sense of each thesis from its opposite (the "hypothesis" versus "thesis" distinction). The document remains a touchstone in debates over the Church's relationship to religious liberty, later addressed by the Second Vatican Council's Dignitatis humanae (1965).
