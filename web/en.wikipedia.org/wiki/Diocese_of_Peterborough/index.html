The Diocese of Peterborough forms part of the Province of Canterbury in the Church of England. Its cathedral is Peterborough Cathedral, and its bishop is the Bishop of Peterborough. The diocese covers the counties of Northamptonshire and Rutland and the Soke of Peterborough in Cambridgeshire, and serves a population of around three quarters of a million people through roughly 350 parishes.

History

The diocese was founded on 4 September 1541, during the English Reformation, as one of the new dioceses created by Henry VIII out of the dissolved monasteries: the great abbey church of Peterborough became the cathedral, and the last abbot, John Chambers, became the first bishop. The territory was taken from the vast medieval Diocese of Lincoln.

Organisation

The diocese is divided into two archdeaconries — Northampton and Oakham — and a series of deaneries. The Bishop of Peterborough is assisted by a suffragan, the Bishop of Brixworth. The diocesan offices are based in Peterborough, and the diocese maintains a strong network of church schools and chaplaincies across Northamptonshire and Rutland.

Cathedral

Peterborough Cathedral, dedicated to St Peter, St Paul and St Andrew, is one of the finest Norman cathedrals in England, famous for its Early English Gothic west front. It is the burial place of Catherine of Aragon, first queen of Henry VIII, and was briefly the burial place of Mary, Queen of Scots, before her remains were moved to Westminster Abbey.
