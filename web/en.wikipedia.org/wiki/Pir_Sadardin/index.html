Pir Sadardin

Pir Sadardin (also Sadruddin) was a fourteenth-century Ismaili missionary (da'i) and saint of the Nizari Ismaili tradition in the Indian subcontinent. He is credited with founding the Khoja Ismaili community of Gujarat and Sindh and with composing a large body of devotional hymns known as ginans.

Life and mission

According to tradition, Pir Sadardin was sent from Persia by the Nizari Imam to propagate the faith in western India. At Uch in Sindh he established himself and began preaching, then travelled into Gujarat, where he converted members of the Lohana trading caste, who became known as Khojas. He is said to have lived to a great age and died in the 14th or early 15th century; his tomb is associated with Uch Sharif.

Ginans and the Satpanth tradition

Pir Sadardin is the most prolific attributed author of ginans, the devotional literature of the Satpanth tradition. Works credited to him include the Das Avatar, a long composition that presents the Ismaili Imam as the awaited tenth avatar of Vishnu for a Hindu audience, and the Saloko Moto. He is also credited with devising or promoting the Khojki script used to record the ginans.

Legacy

The Khoja communities of Gujarat and Sindh — today divided among Ismaili, Sunni and Twelver Shia branches — trace their conversions to Pir Sadardin. His ginans remain central to the religious life of Nizari Ismailis in South Asia and its diaspora.
