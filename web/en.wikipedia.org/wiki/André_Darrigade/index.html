André Darrigade (24 April 1929 – 2024) was a French professional road racing cyclist, regarded as one of the finest sprinters of his era. Between 1953 and 1966 he rode fourteen Tours de France, winning 22 stages — among the highest totals ever for a non-overall winner — and the green jersey points classification in 1959 and 1961. In 1959 he also won the UCI Road World Championship in Zandvoort, and he took the Giro di Lombardia in 1956 and Paris–Brussels in 1956. His record of winning the opening stage of the Tour five times stood for decades.

Career

Born near Dax in south-western France, Darrigade turned professional in 1951 and quickly emerged as the dominant French sprinter of the 1950s, riding for teams including La Perle, Mercier, and Saint-Raphaël. He won stages in the Giro d'Italia and the Vuelta a España as well as the Tour, and wore the Tour's yellow jersey on multiple occasions in the opening days of the race. His career is also remembered for tragedy: in the 1958 Tour's final stage at the Parc des Princes, he collided with an official, who died of his injuries days later.

Legacy

Darrigade was made an officer of the Legion of Honour, and a statue of him stands in his home region of the Landes. He lived to 95, remaining one of the oldest surviving Tour stage winners and world champions until his death in 2024.
