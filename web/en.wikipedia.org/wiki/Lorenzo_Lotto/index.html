Lorenzo Lotto

Bornc. 1480, Venice
Died1556/57, Loreto
Known forPainting
MovementHigh Renaissance

Lorenzo Lotto (c. 1480 – 1556/57) was an Italian Renaissance painter, draughtsman and illustrator, traditionally placed in the Venetian school but working much of his career in other north Italian cities, especially Bergamo. He is particularly known for his psychologically penetrating portraits, rich in symbolic and allegorical detail.

Career

Lotto trained in Venice, probably in the orbit of Giovanni Bellini, and worked in Treviso, Rome — where he contributed to the Vatican Stanze under Raphael's influence — and for long periods in Bergamo (1513–1525), where he painted major altarpieces and the celebrated intarsia panels for Santa Maria Maggiore. His later years were restless, marked by periods in Venice, Ancona and finally Loreto, where he became an oblate of the Holy Sanctuary in 1554.

Works

Among his best-known works are the Martinengo Altarpiece, the Recanati Polyptych, the Family Group and portraits such as the Man with a Felt Hat. Long overshadowed by his Venetian contemporaries, Lotto was reappraised in the late 19th century, largely through the writings of Bernard Berenson, as one of the most individual and emotionally expressive painters of the Italian Renaissance.
