Sergei Diaghilev


Sergei Pavlovich Diaghilev (1872–1929) (placeholder image)

Sergei Pavlovich Diaghilev (31 March 1872 – 19 August 1929) was a Russian art critic, patron and ballet impresario. He founded the Ballets Russes (1909–1929), the most influential ballet company of the twentieth century, which brought together leading choreographers, composers, dancers and visual artists and reshaped modern dance, music and stage design.

Early career

Born in Selishchi, Novgorod Governorate, Diaghilev moved to St Petersburg, where he co-founded the journal Mir iskusstva (World of Art) in 1898 and organised exhibitions of Russian art. After presenting seasons of Russian opera and art in Paris, he turned to ballet, drawing dancers from the Imperial theatres including Anna Pavlova, Tamara Karsavina and Vaslav Nijinsky.

Ballets Russes

From its first Paris season in 1909, the company dazzled audiences with productions choreographed by Michel Fokine (The Firebird, Petrushka, Scheherazade) and Nijinsky, whose The Rite of Spring (1913), with music by Igor Stravinsky, provoked a famous near-riot at its premiere. Diaghilev commissioned scores from Stravinsky, Prokofiev, Ravel, Debussy, Satie and others, and designs from Léon Bakst, Pablo Picasso, Henri Matisse and Coco Chanel. Later choreographers included Léonide Massine, Bronislava Nijinska and George Balanchine. Diaghilev himself could neither dance, compose nor paint; his genius lay in recognising and combining the talents of others. His frequently quoted instruction to Jean Cocteau — "Étonne-moi!" ("Astonish me!") — summed up his artistic creed.

Death and legacy

Diaghilev died of diabetes in Venice in 1929 and is buried on the island of San Michele. The Ballets Russes disbanded with his death, but its alumni seeded ballet companies across Europe and America, and its ideal of the collaborative, modernist production permanently transformed the art form.
