The Eastern Cape (Xhosa: iMpuma-Koloni; Afrikaans: Oos-Kaap) is one of the nine provinces of South Africa. Its capital is Bhisho, and its largest city is Gqeberha, formerly known as Port Elizabeth. The province was formed on 27 April 1994, the day of South Africa's first democratic elections, out of the eastern part of the former Cape Province together with the former "homelands" of Transkei and Ciskei.

It is the country's second-largest province by area and is traditionally regarded as the home of the Xhosa people. The Eastern Cape was the birthplace of many prominent anti-apartheid figures, including Nelson Mandela, who was born in the village of Mvezo near Mthatha.

History

The region was long inhabited by Khoisan peoples and later by Xhosa-speaking chiefdoms. From the late eighteenth century the expanding Cape Colony came into conflict with the Xhosa in a series of nine Frontier Wars (1779–1879), among the longest-running conflicts of colonial Africa. British settlers arrived in large numbers in 1820, landing at Algoa Bay. During the apartheid era, much of the province was assigned to the Transkei and Ciskei bantustans, which the South African government declared nominally independent in 1976 and 1981 respectively; neither was recognised internationally.

Geography

The Eastern Cape stretches from the Indian Ocean coast — including the rugged Wild Coast in the northeast — to the high interior plateau of the Great Karoo and the Drakensberg foothills on the Lesotho border. Its climate ranges from Mediterranean conditions in the west to humid subtropical conditions in the east. Major rivers include the Great Fish, the Kei and the Mthatha.

Demographics

The majority of the population speaks isiXhosa as a first language (about 81 per cent), followed by Afrikaans and English. The population is predominantly Black African, with significant Coloured, White and Indian minorities.

Economy

The economy is centred on the automotive industry around Gqeberha and East London, agriculture (including citrus, pineapples and livestock), and tourism along the coast and in game reserves such as Addo Elephant National Park. The province remains one of South Africa's poorest, with high unemployment.
