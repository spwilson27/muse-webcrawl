Smith College is a private liberal arts women's college in Northampton, Massachusetts. It was chartered in 1871 by a bequest from Sophia Smith and opened its doors in 1875 with 14 students. Smith is the largest member of the historic Seven Sisters colleges and a member of the Five College Consortium with Amherst College, Mount Holyoke College, Hampshire College and the University of Massachusetts Amherst, allowing students to cross-register for courses.

Academics

Smith has an open curriculum with no required courses outside the major, apart from a first-year writing-intensive seminar. It was the first women's college in the United States to grant its own undergraduate degrees in engineering, through the Picker Engineering Program established in 1999. The college also offers graduate degrees and the Ada Comstock Scholars Program for non-traditional-age students.

Campus

The 147-acre campus was designed in part as a botanical garden by Frederick Law Olmsted. Distinctive traditions include the house system — students live in houses of varied architectural styles rather than dormitories — Mountain Day, and Ivy Day during alumnae weekend. The Smith College Museum of Art and the Lyman Conservatory and Botanic Garden are notable facilities.

Notable alumnae

Alumnae include former First Lady Barbara Bush (who left before graduating), chef and author Julia Child, poet Sylvia Plath, feminist authors Betty Friedan and Gloria Steinem, and U.S. Senator Tammy Baldwin.
