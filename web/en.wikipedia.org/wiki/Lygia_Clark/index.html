Lygia Clark

Lygia Clark (23 October 1920 – 25 April 1988) was a Brazilian artist best known for her painting and installation work. She was often associated with the Brazilian Constructivist movements of the mid-20th century and the Tropicalia movement.

Life and work

Born in Belo Horizonte, Clark studied in Rio de Janeiro and Paris. In 1959 she co-founded the Neo-Concrete movement with artists including Hélio Oiticica, signing the Neo-Concrete Manifesto. Her Bichos ("animals/creatures") — hinged metal sculptures that viewers could manipulate into different configurations — broke down the boundary between artwork and spectator. In her later career she moved toward participatory and therapeutic "propositions", such as the Objetos relacionais (Relational Objects), used in sensory therapeutic sessions with participants.

Legacy

Clark is regarded as one of the most influential Latin American artists of the twentieth century; major retrospectives have been held at the Museum of Modern Art in New York (2014) and elsewhere.
