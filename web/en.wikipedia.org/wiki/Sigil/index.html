Sigil

Sigils: symbolic signatures of spirits and intentions.

A sigil (from Latin sigillum, "seal") is a type of symbol used in magic. In medieval and early modern grimoires, a sigil was the pictorial signature of a spirit — an angel, demon or other entity — which the magician used to invoke and control it. In modern occultism, especially chaos magic, the term usually denotes a constructed symbol that represents a desired outcome, created by the practitioner and "charged" through ritual.

Historical use

The best-known sigils appear in medieval grimoires such as the Key of Solomon and the Lesser Key of Solomon, where each demon of the Ars Goetia is assigned a distinctive seal. Such seals were often derived from magic squares (kameas): the letters of a spirit's name were traced across a square of numbers associated with a planet, producing a characteristic line figure. Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa described this method in his Three Books of Occult Philosophy (1531–33).

Modern practice

The artist and occultist Austin Osman Spare developed the influential modern method: write a statement of intent, remove repeated letters, and combine the remaining letters into a single abstract glyph, which is then charged in a state of gnosis and forgotten. Chaos magicians adopted Spare's technique as a core practice. Corporate logos and personal monograms are sometimes compared to sigils, though the resemblance is superficial unless magical intent is involved.
