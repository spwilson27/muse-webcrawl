Very-long-baseline interferometry

Very-long-baseline interferometry (VLBI) is a type of astronomical interferometry used in radio astronomy in which signals from a source are recorded at multiple radio telescopes on Earth or in space and later combined to produce images with resolution equivalent to a single telescope thousands of kilometres across.

Method

Each station records the radio waves together with precise timestamps from atomic clocks; the data are correlated centrally to reconstruct interference fringes. Angular resolution can reach fractions of a milliarcsecond, the finest in astronomy.

Applications

VLBI networks such as the Very Long Baseline Array (VLBA), the European VLBI Network and the Event Horizon Telescope study active galactic nuclei, masers, pulsars and the cosmic distance scale. The Event Horizon Telescope, a global VLBI array at millimetre wavelengths, produced the first images of black hole shadows — in M87* (2019) and Sagittarius A* (2022). Geodetic VLBI measures continental drift, Earth rotation and the celestial reference frame.
