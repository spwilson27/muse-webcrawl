Four Big Pollution Diseases of Japan

The Four Big Pollution Diseases of Japan (四大公害病, yondai kōgai-byō) were a group of man-made diseases all caused by environmental pollution due to improper handling of industrial waste by Japanese corporations. The first occurred in 1912, and the other three occurred in the 1950s and 1960s, during Japan's rapid post-war industrialisation.

The four diseases

  • Itai-itai disease (Toyama Prefecture) — chronic cadmium poisoning from mining waste discharged into the Jinzū River, causing severe bone pain, softening of the bones and kidney failure.
  • Minamata disease (Kumamoto Prefecture) — methylmercury poisoning from wastewater of the Chisso chemical plant, causing severe neurological damage in people who ate contaminated fish and shellfish.
  • Niigata Minamata disease (Niigata Prefecture) — a second outbreak of methylmercury poisoning caused by the Showa Denko plant discharging into the Agano River.
  • Yokkaichi asthma (Mie Prefecture) — respiratory disease caused by sulfur dioxide air pollution from petrochemical complexes in Yokkaichi.

Consequences

Lawsuits by the victims in the late 1960s and early 1970s ended in landmark victories and compensation settlements. The cases drove the creation of Japan's Environment Agency in 1971 and a comprehensive body of pollution control law, making the four diseases a turning point in Japanese environmental policy and in the global history of industrial pollution litigation.
