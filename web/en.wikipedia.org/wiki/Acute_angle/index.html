An acute angle is an angle that measures less than a right angle, that is, less than 90 degrees (π/2 radians) and greater than zero. Angles smaller than a right angle are "sharp", a sense preserved in the word acute itself, from the Latin acutus, "sharpened".

Properties

Two angles whose measures sum to 90° are called complementary; the complement of an acute angle is also acute. A triangle in which all three angles are acute is an acute triangle; in such a triangle the circumcentre and orthocentre lie inside the triangle. Every triangle can have at most one obtuse or right angle, so it always has at least two acute angles.

Trigonometric values

For an acute angle, all six trigonometric functions are positive, and the angle forms the domain of the familiar right-triangle definitions: sine as opposite over hypotenuse, cosine as adjacent over hypotenuse, and tangent as opposite over adjacent. Standard acute angles include 30°, 45°, and 60°, whose trigonometric values can be derived exactly from equilateral and isosceles right triangles.
