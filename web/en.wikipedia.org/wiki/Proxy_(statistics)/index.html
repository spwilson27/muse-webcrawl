Proxy (statistics)

In statistics, a proxy or proxy variable is a variable that is not itself directly of interest, but which stands in place of an unobservable or immeasurable variable of interest. For a proxy to be useful it must be correlated with the variable it replaces, and it must not be affected by the treatment or outcome in a way that biases the analysis.

Uses

Proxies are ubiquitous in empirical research. In econometrics, years of education are often used as a proxy for unmeasured ability when estimating wage equations; in epidemiology, a biomarker may serve as a proxy for disease exposure. In palaeoclimatology, proxy data — such as tree rings, ice cores, pollen and ocean sediment cores — are used to reconstruct past climates for periods before instrumental records exist.

Validity

The quality of inference depends on how faithfully the proxy tracks the latent variable. Measurement error in a proxy tends to attenuate estimated relationships, and if the proxy is itself influenced by the independent variable of interest, using it as a control variable can introduce bias ("bad control"). A related concept is the instrumental variable, which must satisfy stronger exclusion conditions.
