Pljevlja (Montenegrin Cyrillic: Пљевља) is a town and the centre of Pljevlja Municipality in northern Montenegro, close to the borders with Serbia and Bosnia and Herzegovina. It is the third-largest municipality in Montenegro by area and an industrial centre of the country.

History

Human settlement in the area dates to prehistory. In Roman times a settlement known as Municipium S stood near the present town (at the village of Komini). During the Ottoman period the town was known as Taşlıca and was an important trading post; the Husein-paša Mosque (1569), with a slender minaret, dates from this era, as does the Sahat-kula clock tower. Pljevlja later came under Austro-Hungarian occupation and then became part of Yugoslavia before joining Montenegro.

Economy

The town's economy rests on coal mining and the Pljevlja thermal power plant, Montenegro's only coal-fired power station, together with related cement, timber, and agricultural industries. Air pollution from coal has been a persistent local concern.

Culture

Landmarks include the Husein-paša Mosque, the Monastery of the Holy Trinity (Sveta Trojica) in the surrounding municipality, and the local heritage museum.
