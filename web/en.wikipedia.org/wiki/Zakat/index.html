Zakat

Zakat (Arabic: زكاة, "that which purifies") is a form of almsgiving, often collected by the Muslim community, and one of the Five Pillars of Islam. It is a religious obligation for all Muslims whose wealth exceeds a threshold amount known as the nisab, and is traditionally paid as a fixed proportion — most commonly 2.5% — of a Muslim's savings and assets held for a full lunar year.

Qur'anic basis

The Qur'an repeatedly commands zakat alongside prayer (salah): "And establish prayer and give zakat" (2:43). Verse 9:60 specifies eight categories of recipients: the poor (fuqara), the needy (masakin), zakat administrators, those whose hearts are to be reconciled, those in bondage, debtors, those striving in the cause of God, and the stranded traveller. Zakat is distinct from voluntary charity (sadaqah) and from zakat al-fitr, the smaller alms given at the end of Ramadan before the Eid al-Fitr prayer.

Calculation

Zakat is levied on qualifying assets — cash, gold and silver, trade goods, livestock and agricultural produce — above the nisab, traditionally the value of 85 grams of gold (or 595 grams of silver). Monetary wealth and trade goods are generally assessed at 2.5%; agricultural produce at 5% (irrigated) or 10% (rain-fed); livestock by detailed tables; and buried treasure (rikaz) at 20%. Debts and essential living costs are generally deducted. The obligation recurs each lunar year (hawl) the threshold remains met, on the Islamic calendar.

Distribution and administration

Historically, rulers collected and distributed zakat through the treasury (bayt al-mal); the first caliph, Abu Bakr, fought the Ridda wars partly against tribes refusing to remit it. Today practice varies: some states (such as Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, Pakistan and Sudan) operate official zakat collection, while elsewhere payment is a private matter handled by individuals, mosques and charities. Global zakat flows are estimated in the hundreds of billions of dollars annually, and zakat is a significant instrument of poverty relief in the Muslim world.
