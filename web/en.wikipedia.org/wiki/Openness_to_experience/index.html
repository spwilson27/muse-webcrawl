Openness to experience

Openness covers imagination, aesthetic sensitivity and intellectual curiosity.

Openness to experience is a personality trait included in the Big Five model of personality (and the HEXACO model). It describes individual differences in imagination, aesthetic appreciation, intellectual curiosity, creativity and willingness to entertain novel ideas and unconventional values. People high in openness tend to be imaginative, curious and artistically sensitive; people low in openness tend to be conventional, concrete and prefer familiar routines.

Facets

In the NEO framework (Costa and McCrae), openness comprises six facets:

FacetHigh scorers tend to…
Fantasyhave vivid imaginations and rich inner lives
Aestheticsbe deeply moved by art, music and beauty
Feelingsattend to and value their emotions
Actionsseek novelty and variety
Ideasenjoy intellectual pursuits and abstract thinking
Valuesquestion tradition and authority

Correlates

Openness is the Big Five trait most associated with creativity, intelligence (especially crystallised and verbal ability), and engagement with the arts and sciences. It modestly predicts liberal political attitudes and tolerance of ambiguity. It is substantially heritable, like the other Big Five traits, and tends to increase through young adulthood before declining in old age. In the HEXACO model the corresponding factor is called Openness to Experience as well, defined by aesthetic appreciation, inquisitiveness, creativity and unconventionality.

Clinical aspects

Very high openness is associated with schizotypal traits such as magical thinking and unusual perceptual experiences, and openness predicts responsiveness to psychotherapy approaches involving insight; low openness is associated with rigid, concrete thinking styles. Openness is also the trait linked to the reported personality changes under psychedelic substances in clinical studies.
