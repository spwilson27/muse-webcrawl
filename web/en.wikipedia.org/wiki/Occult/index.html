Occult

The occult, from the Latin word occultus ("clandestine, hidden, secret"), is a category of esoteric or supernatural beliefs and practices which generally fall outside the scope of organized religion and mainstream science. The term can refer to subjects such as astrology, alchemy, magic, extra-sensory perception, numerology, divination, spiritualism, and the belief in hidden correspondences or agencies such as angels, spirits and demons. "Occultism" in the modern sense emerged in 19th-century France and England and is closely associated with Western esotericism.

History

Renaissance thinkers such as Marsilio Ficino, Pico della Mirandola and Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa systematised a learned tradition of "occult philosophy" drawing on Hermeticism, Kabbalah, Neoplatonism and natural magic. In the 19th century, figures such as Éliphas Lévi revived occultism, and organisations including the Theosophical Society, the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn and later Aleister Crowley's Thelema brought occult practice to a broad public. Scholars of Western esotericism, notably Antoine Faivre and Wouter Hanegraaff, have studied the occult as a historically significant current of thought encompassing "rejected knowledge" excluded from both orthodox religion and Enlightenment science.

Occult sciences and practices

Historical classifications grouped astrology, alchemy and magic (or natural magic) as the "occult sciences". Contemporary occult practice ranges from ceremonial magic, tarot and astrology to chaos magic and modern pagan witchcraft (Wicca). The occult has had an extensive influence on literature, art, film and music, from Gothic fiction and Symbolism to heavy metal and popular fantasy.
