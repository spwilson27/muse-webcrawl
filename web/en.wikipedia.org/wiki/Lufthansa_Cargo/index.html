Lufthansa Cargo AG is a German cargo airline and a wholly owned subsidiary of the Lufthansa Group. It operates worldwide air freight and logistics services, both with its own dedicated freighter aircraft and by marketing the belly-hold cargo capacity of the Lufthansa Group's passenger airlines. Its main hub is Frankfurt Airport, where it runs one of the largest air cargo terminals in Europe, the Lufthansa Cargo Center. Lufthansa Cargo is a member of the WOW cargo alliance's successor arrangements and historically co-founded the WOW Alliance with SAS Cargo and Singapore Airlines Cargo.

History

Lufthansa's cargo activities were reorganised into an independent company in 1994, when Lufthansa Cargo AG was founded as part of a restructuring of the group. Earlier, German Cargo Services had operated dedicated freighters on Lufthansa's behalf from 1977. The airline built a large fleet of McDonnell Douglas MD-11 freighters, which for many years formed the backbone of its long-haul operations, before transitioning to the more fuel-efficient Boeing 777F. The last MD-11 was retired in October 2021, ending more than two decades of trijet freighter service.

Fleet

The airline's dedicated freighter fleet has consisted of Boeing 777F aircraft (including aircraft operated for the AeroLogic joint venture with DHL, based in Leipzig), supplemented since 2022 by Airbus A321P2F freighters operated by Lufthansa CityLine for short- and medium-haul European services. Lufthansa Cargo has also ordered the Boeing 777-8F as a future freighter type.

Operations

Beyond aircraft capacity, Lufthansa Cargo sells the belly space of Lufthansa, Austrian Airlines, Brussels Airlines, Eurowings Discover and SunExpress flights, giving it a network of several hundred destinations. It markets specialised products for pharmaceuticals, automotive parts, live animals (through Frankfurt's Animal Lounge), valuables and perishables.
