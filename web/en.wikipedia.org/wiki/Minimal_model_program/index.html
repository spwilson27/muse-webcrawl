Minimal model program

In algebraic geometry, the minimal model program (MMP) is an approach to the birational classification of algebraic varieties. Its goal is to simplify a given variety by a sequence of birational transformations until reaching either a minimal model — a variety whose canonical divisor is nef — or a Mori fibre space, from which the variety's geometric structure can be understood.

Background

For curves, the minimal model is simply the smooth projective model. For surfaces, the classical theory of Castelnuovo and Enriques contracts (−1)-curves until no more can be contracted. In higher dimensions the program was developed by Shigefumi Mori in the 1980s; Mori proved the existence of the necessary operations — divisorial contractions and the new phenomenon of flips — for threefolds, work that earned him the Fields Medal in 1990.

Key results

The cone theorem describes the structure of the cone of curves and underlies the contraction process. A landmark advance came in 2006–2007, when Birkar, Cascini, Hacon, and McKernan (BCHM) proved the existence of minimal models for varieties of general type in all dimensions, subject to the existence of flips, a body of work for which Birkar received the Fields Medal in 2018. The program is closely connected to the classification of Fano and Calabi–Yau varieties, and open problems remain about termination of flips in general. The MMP is one of the central frameworks of modern algebraic geometry.
