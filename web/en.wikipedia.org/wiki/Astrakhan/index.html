Astrakhan

Astrakhan (Russian: Астрахань)
Country: Russia
Federal subject: Astrakhan Oblast
Founded: 1558
Population: approx. 475,000 (2021)
River: Volga

Astrakhan (Russian: Астрахань, IPA: [ˈastrəxənʲ]) is a city in southern Russia and the administrative centre of Astrakhan Oblast. The city lies on two banks of the Volga, in the upper part of the Volga Delta, on eleven islands of the Caspian Depression, 60 miles (100 km) from the Caspian Sea.

Astrakhan was once the capital of a Tatar state, the Khanate of Astrakhan. Located on the caravan and water routes, it developed into one of the largest trading centres of the era. The city was captured by Ivan the Terrible in 1556 and refounded in 1558 on its present site, becoming Russia's gateway to the Caspian and the Caucasus.

History

The region passed through the hands of the Khazars, the Golden Horde, and the Astrakhan Khanate before its annexation by the Tsardom of Russia. In the 17th century Astrakhan was the scene of the revolt of Stenka Razin, who held the city in 1670–71. Peter the Great established a shipyard and port there in 1722 as a base for his Persian campaign, cementing Astrakhan's role as a naval and commercial hub on the Caspian.

Economy

Astrakhan is a major port and railway junction. Its economy centres on shipbuilding and repair, fishing and fish processing — historically famous for sturgeon and caviar from the Volga delta — and on the gas and chemical industries of the surrounding oblast. The Volga–Caspian shipping canal connects the city with the sea.

Landmarks

The Astrakhan Kremlin, built in the late 16th and early 17th centuries, dominates the old town with its white walls and the Assumption Cathedral. The city is also known for its wooden architecture, its mosques reflecting centuries of Tatar presence, and the nearby Volga delta nature reserves famous for lotus flowers and birdlife.

Climate

Astrakhan has a cold semi-arid climate, bordering on a cold desert climate, with hot, dry summers and cold winters moderated slightly by the Volga and the Caspian Sea.
