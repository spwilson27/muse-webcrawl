The Tribune Tower is a 36-story neo-Gothic skyscraper at 435 North Michigan Avenue in Chicago, Illinois, United States. Completed in 1925, it was built as the headquarters of the Chicago Tribune newspaper and is famous as the product of one of the most influential architectural competitions of the twentieth century.

The 1922 competition

In 1922 the Tribune announced an international competition for "the most beautiful office building in the world", offering a $50,000 prize. More than 260 entries arrived from 23 countries. The winning design, by New York architects John Mead Howells and Raymond Hood, was a Gothic Revival tower crowned with flying buttresses modelled on Rouen Cathedral. The second-place design by Eliel Saarinen—a sleek, setback massing—was arguably more prophetic of modern skyscraper design and influenced buildings across America. Other notable entries came from Walter Gropius and Adolf Loos, whose giant Doric column proposal became a celebrated avant-garde statement.

The building

The tower's steel frame is clad in Indiana limestone. Its crown is decorated with gargoyles (including one depicting Hood howling at the moon) and flying buttresses. In a celebrated tradition begun by publisher Robert R. McCormick, fragments of famous buildings and sites from around the world—from the Parthenon, the Great Wall of China, and the Taj Mahal to the Berlin Wall and the World Trade Center—are embedded in the tower's exterior walls at street level, each labelled with its origin.

Later history

The Tribune Media company occupied the tower until 2018, when the newspaper moved out. The building was sold and converted into luxury condominiums (Tribune Tower Residences), with the conversion completed in the early 2020s, preserving its landmark exterior and the inscribed lobby quotations about freedom of the press.
