Robert Bellarmine

Born: October 4, 1542, Montepulciano, Tuscany
Died: September 17, 1621, Rome
Venerated in: Roman Catholic Church
Canonized: 1930 by Pope Pius XI
Feast: September 17

Robert Bellarmine (Italian: Roberto Francesco Romolo Bellarmino; October 4, 1542 – September 17, 1621) was an Italian Jesuit theologian and cardinal of the Catholic Church. A leading intellectual of the Counter-Reformation, he was canonized in 1930 and named a Doctor of the Church in 1931.

Life and works

Bellarmine was born in Montepulciano, the nephew of Pope Marcellus II, and joined the Society of Jesus in 1560. He taught theology at Louvain in the Spanish Netherlands, where he began the research that produced his masterwork, the Disputationes de Controversiis Christianae Fidei (1586–1593), a systematic defense of Catholic doctrine against Protestant reformers that became the standard Catholic controversial theology for centuries. Returning to Rome, he taught at the Roman College, served as spiritual director to the young Aloysius Gonzaga, and wrote catechisms whose larger and smaller versions were translated into dozens of languages.

Created cardinal in 1599 and later Archbishop of Capua, Bellarmine advised popes on theological and political questions. His treatise De Laicis argued that even papal power over temporal rulers was indirect, and his doctrine of the people's role in transferring authority to rulers influenced later political thought, while also bringing his works into conflict with absolute monarchs such as James I of England.

The Galileo affair and canonization

Bellarmine played a central role in the Church's first proceedings against Galileo Galilei. As a consultor of the Inquisition he delivered the 1616 admonition that Galileo not hold or defend the Copernican doctrine, while in an earlier letter to Paolo Foscarini he had allowed that heliocentrism could be accepted if a true demonstration were produced. He also presided over aspects of the trial of Giordano Bruno. Bellarmine died in Rome in 1621; after a long cause he was beatified in 1923 and canonized by Pius XI in 1930, and he is the patron saint of catechists.
