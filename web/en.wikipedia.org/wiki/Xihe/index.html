Xihe

Xihe (Chinese: 羲和; pinyin: Xīhé) is a figure in Chinese mythology, most often described as a sun goddess and the mother of the ten suns. In the Shanhaijing (Classic of Mountains and Seas) she lives beyond the East Sea at Sweet Abyss (Gan Yuan), where she bathes the suns. She is the wife of Di Jun, a high god of the east.

The ten suns

According to the myth, Xihe's ten sun-children normally took turns crossing the sky, one per day, riding in a carriage driven by their mother. Once, however, all ten rose together, scorching the earth, until the archer Yi shot down nine of them — a myth sometimes read as an explanation of drought and of the ten-day cycle (xun) of the early Chinese calendar.

Astronomers Xi and He

In other early texts, "Xi and He" (Xi-He) appear as a pair of astronomer-officials. The Book of Documents describes the legendary emperor Yao commissioning the brothers Xi and He to observe the heavens, regulate the calendar, and mark the seasons — a founding legend for the Chinese astronomical bureaucracy. Commentators have long debated how the goddess and the officials relate.

Modern usage

The name Xihe has been given to a Chinese solar observation mission, the Chinese H-alpha Solar Explorer (CHASE), launched in 2021, reflecting her solar associations.
