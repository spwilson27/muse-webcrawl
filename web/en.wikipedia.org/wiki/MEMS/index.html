MEMS (microelectromechanical systems) is the technology of microscopic devices incorporating both electronic and moving parts. MEMS are made up of components between 1 and 100 micrometres in size, and MEMS devices generally range in size from 20 micrometres to a millimetre. They usually consist of a central unit that processes data (an integrated circuit chip) and several microcomponents — such as microsensors — that interact with the surroundings.

Fabrication

MEMS are fabricated using modified semiconductor device fabrication technologies also used for integrated circuits: photolithography, ion implantation, and etching. Techniques include bulk micromachining, which etches structures into a silicon substrate, surface micromachining, which builds structures up in deposited layers, and LIGA, which uses X-ray lithography to create tall microstructures in plastic, metal, or ceramic.

Applications

Commercial MEMS include accelerometers used to trigger car airbags and to detect orientation in smartphones, gyroscopes, inkjet printer nozzles, pressure sensors, silicon microphones, and the micromirror arrays used in digital light processing (DLP) projectors. MEMS oscillators and microphones have largely displaced larger mechanical equivalents in mobile devices.
