Triple Crown of Motorsport

The Triple Crown of Motorsport is an unofficial achievement in motor racing, generally defined as winning three of the most prestigious automobile races in the world during a driver's career: the Indianapolis 500, the 24 Hours of Le Mans, and the Monaco Grand Prix. An alternative definition substitutes the Formula One World Championship for the Monaco Grand Prix.

Only winner

British driver Graham Hill is the only competitor to have completed the Triple Crown under the Monaco definition: he won the Monaco Grand Prix five times (1963–1965, 1968, 1969), the Indianapolis 500 in 1966, and the 24 Hours of Le Mans in 1972. Under the championship-based definition he also qualifies, having won the Formula One World Championship in 1962 and 1968.

Near misses

Drivers who have won two of the three legs include Juan Manuel Fangio, Jack Brabham, Jim Clark, Emerson Fittipaldi, Mario Andretti, Fernando Alonso, and Juan Pablo Montoya. Alonso, with two Monaco wins and two Le Mans victories, contested Indianapolis in pursuit of the crown, while Montoya has two Indianapolis 500 wins and a Monaco victory but no Le Mans win.
