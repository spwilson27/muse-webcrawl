Fasilides

Emperor of Ethiopia
Reign: 1632–1667
Predecessor: Susenyos I
Successor: Yohannes I
Died: 18 October 1667
Throne name: Alam Sagad

Fasilides (Ge'ez: ፋሲለደስ; c. 1603 – 18 October 1667), throne name Alam Sagad, was Emperor of Ethiopia from 1632 until his death in 1667. A member of the Solomonic dynasty, he is best remembered for founding the city of Gondar and for restoring the Ethiopian Orthodox Church to primacy after his father's experiment with Roman Catholicism.

Reign

Fasilides was the son of Emperor Susenyos I, whose conversion to Catholicism and alliance with Portuguese Jesuits had provoked years of civil war. On ascending the throne in 1632, Fasilides reversed his father's religious policy: he re-established the traditional faith, confiscated Jesuit lands, and in 1633 expelled the Jesuit missionaries, the remnants of the mission retreating to Fremona and then leaving Ethiopia altogether. In subsequent years he repeatedly barred European missionaries and effectively closed the empire to European contact for more than a century, while maintaining relations with Muslim neighbours and exchanging embassies with Yemen and Mughal India.

Gondar and building works

Around 1636 Fasilides founded a permanent capital at Gondar, ending the era of the roving imperial court. There he built the great stone castle that bears his name within the royal enclosure later known as Fasil Ghebbi, along with churches, bridges, and the pavilion known as Fasilides' Bath. He is also credited with several of the stone bridges over the Blue Nile and its tributaries. His building programme, employing local and foreign craftsmen, inaugurated the distinctive Gondarine style of Ethiopian architecture.

Later reign and death

Fasilides spent much of his later reign campaigning against rebellious provinces, including the Agaw and the Oromo, and dealing with succession intrigues among his many sons, some of whom he confined or exiled. He died in 1667 and was succeeded by his son Yohannes I. He was buried on Daga Island in Lake Tana, at the monastery of St. Stephen.
