The Madurese are an ethnic group native to the island of Madura, off the north-eastern coast of Java, Indonesia. They are among the largest ethnic groups in the country, with substantial populations also settled across eastern Java, especially in the so-called "horseshoe" region of Pasuruan, Probolinggo, Jember and Banyuwangi, and in other parts of the archipelago through migration.

Culture

Madurese society is strongly shaped by Islam, with the kyai (religious scholar) holding high social standing alongside family networks; the traditional hierarchy of respect is often summarised as devotion to father and mother, teacher, and ruler. The Madurese language is an Austronesian language with speech levels reflecting social deference.

Madura is famous for kerapan sapi, bull races in which pairs of bulls pull a rider on a wooden sled, and for its salt production, batik, and spicy cuisine including sate Madura, a widely popular style of satay across Indonesia.

Economy and migration

Madura's dry, limestone soils support limited agriculture, and Madurese have migrated for centuries as traders, sailors and labourers. The Suramadu Bridge, opened in 2009, links the island directly to Surabaya. Traditional occupations include salt farming, fishing, cattle raising and small-scale trade; remittances from migrant workers are economically important.
