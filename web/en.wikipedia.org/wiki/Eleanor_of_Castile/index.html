Eleanor of Castile (1241 – 28 November 1290) was Queen of England as the first wife of King Edward I, whom she married in 1254 as part of a diplomatic agreement. She was also Countess of Ponthieu in her own right.

Life

Daughter of Ferdinand III of Castile and Joan, Countess of Ponthieu, Eleanor accompanied Edward on crusade in 1270–72, famously (if legendarily) said to have saved his life after an assassination attempt. Their marriage was notably close; she bore at least fourteen children, of whom six daughters and one son, the future Edward II, survived to adulthood.

Land acquisition

Eleanor was an active acquirer of estates, sometimes controversially buying up debts owed by landowners. Her revenues funded charitable and building works.

Death and Eleanor crosses

She died at Harby in 1290. Edward I commemorated her funeral procession to Westminster Abbey with twelve ornate Eleanor crosses, including Charing Cross in London, marking each night's resting place of her bier.
