Cirrus cloud

Cirrus (cloud classification symbol: Ci) is a genus of high cloud made of ice crystals. Cirrus clouds typically appear delicate and wispy with white strands. In the Earth's atmosphere, cirrus are usually the highest clouds, forming at altitudes above about 5,000 metres (16,500 ft) in temperate regions and higher in the tropics, where temperatures are below freezing. The word cirrus comes from the Latin word for a lock of hair or a tuft.

Cirrus clouds are thin, wispy strands of ice crystals.

Description

Cirrus clouds form from the ascent of dry air, which makes the small quantities of water vapour in it deposit into ice crystals (a process known as deposition). They are composed almost entirely of ice crystals large enough that their fall speed gives the clouds a drawn-out, fibrous appearance, often called "mare's tails". Because cirrus clouds occur high in the troposphere where the air is very dry, they generally produce no precipitation reaching the ground, although streaks of falling ice crystals (virga) are common.

Cirrus can cover up to 30 percent of the Earth at any given time. They have a complex effect on climate: they reflect some incoming sunlight back to space (a cooling effect) but also trap outgoing infrared radiation from the Earth (a warming effect). Studies suggest the net effect of naturally occurring cirrus is a modest warming.

Species and varieties

The genus cirrus includes several species, including cirrus fibratus, cirrus uncinus, cirrus spissatus, cirrus castellanus and cirrus floccus. Cirrus spissatus, the densest form, often develops from the anvil tops of cumulonimbus clouds. Cirrus uncinus has characteristic hooked shapes at the ends of its strands.

Weather prediction

An increase and thickening of cirrus, especially when it develops into cirrostratus, can indicate the approach of a warm front and rain within 24 to 36 hours, giving rise to folk observations such as "mare's tails and mackerel scales make lofty ships carry low sails".

Contrails

Aircraft condensation trails (contrails) are a form of artificial cirrus. Persistent contrails can spread out and evolve into cirrocumulus or cirrus-like cloud decks, and their contribution to climate forcing is an active area of research.
