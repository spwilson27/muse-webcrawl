The Pfizer–BioNTech COVID-19 vaccine, sold under the brand name Comirnaty and known during development as BNT162b2, is an mRNA-based vaccine against COVID-19 developed by the German company BioNTech in partnership with the American company Pfizer. It was the first COVID-19 vaccine to receive emergency authorisation from a stringent regulatory authority, and the first mRNA vaccine ever authorised for general human use.

Development and authorisation

BioNTech designed the vaccine within days of the SARS-CoV-2 genome being published in January 2020; Pfizer joined in March 2020 to run large-scale trials and manufacturing. A phase III trial of about 44,000 participants reported approximately 95% efficacy against symptomatic COVID-19. The United Kingdom authorised it on 2 December 2020, followed by the United States, the European Union and many other countries; the US FDA granted full approval in August 2021. Updated formulations targeting Omicron variants were later authorised as boosters.

Technology and use

The vaccine delivers nucleoside-modified messenger RNA encoding the viral spike protein, encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles. It was originally given as two doses about three weeks apart and required ultra-cold storage at around −70 °C, a logistical challenge later eased by data supporting longer refrigerated storage. Billions of doses were supplied worldwide, making it one of the most widely used COVID-19 vaccines.

Safety

Common side effects include injection-site pain, fatigue, headache and fever. Rare serious adverse events monitored by regulators include anaphylaxis and myocarditis/pericarditis, particularly in young males after the second dose; regulators have consistently concluded that the benefits of vaccination outweigh these risks.
