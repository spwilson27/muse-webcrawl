Char Dham

The Char Dham (literally "four abodes") is a set of four Hindu pilgrimage sites in India, held to be the dwelling places of the divine. One circuit, the Chota Char Dham in Uttarakhand, comprises Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath and Badrinath in the Garhwal Himalayas; the longer, India-spanning Char Dham defined by Adi Shankara consists of Badrinath (north), Dwarka (west), Puri (east) and Rameswaram (south).

Chota Char Dham yatra

The Himalayan circuit is traditionally undertaken west to east, beginning at Yamunotri and ending at Badrinath, during the season from late spring to autumn, when snow permits access. The journey covers mountain roads to temples at elevations between about 3,100 and 3,600 metres. The pilgrimage draws hundreds of thousands of devotees annually and is among the most important in Hinduism; completing it is believed to wash away sins and aid liberation (moksha).

The greater Char Dham

The 8th-century philosopher Adi Shankara is credited with organising the four cardinal monasteries (mathas) associated with the greater circuit: Badrinath in the north (Uttarakhand), Dwarka in the west (Gujarat), Puri in the east (Odisha) and Sringeri/Rameswaram in the south (Tamil Nadu region), binding the subcontinent into a single sacred geography.

Modern development

The Indian government's Char Dham Highway project has widened roads to the four Himalayan shrines for strategic and pilgrim access, a programme that has also raised environmental concerns about landslides and slope stability in the fragile Himalayan terrain; the region suffered catastrophic floods in 2013, centred on Kedarnath.
