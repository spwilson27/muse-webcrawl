Mendeley is a reference manager software developed by Mendeley Ltd. and owned by Elsevier. Launched in 2008, it helps researchers organise scholarly papers, generate bibliographies, and collaborate online. It is named after the biologist Gregor Mendel and the chemist Dmitri Mendeleev.

Features

Mendeley combines a desktop application, Mendeley Reference Manager, with a web service. It extracts metadata and full text from PDF files, syncs libraries across devices, offers a citation plug-in for word processors, and formerly supported an academic social network in which researchers shared reading lists and usage statistics.

Acquisition and controversy

Elsevier's acquisition of Mendeley in 2013 was controversial among open-science advocates who distrusted the publisher's business model. Later decisions, such as encrypting the local database and discontinuing the legacy desktop application and mobile apps, drew further criticism from long-time users.
