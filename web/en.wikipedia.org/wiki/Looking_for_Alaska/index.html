Looking for Alaska

Looking for Alaska is the debut novel by American author John Green, published in March 2005 by Dutton Juvenile. Based on his own experiences at Indian Springs School in Alabama, the novel follows teenager Miles "Pudge" Halter as he enrolls at a boarding school to seek his "Great Perhaps", befriends Chip "The Colonel" Martin, Takumi, Lara, and the self-destructive, mysterious Alaska Young. The book won the 2006 Michael L. Printz Award from the American Library Association.

Plot summary

Obsessed with famous last words, Miles leaves Florida for Culver Creek Preparatory School in Alabama. His roommate Chip "The Colonel" Martin introduces him to Alaska Young — clever, funny, and emotionally troubled — with whom Miles falls in love. The narrative is structured in two parts, "Before" (counting days down to an unnamed event) and "After": Alaska dies in a car crash after leaving campus distraught on the anniversary of her mother's death, and Miles and the Colonel, consumed by guilt, investigate whether it was an accident or suicide, ultimately learning to live with unanswered questions.

Themes

The novel explores grief, guilt, the search for meaning ("the labyrinth of suffering", drawn from the supposed last words of Simón Bolívar), friendship, and coming of age. Despite — and partly because of — its themes, the book has frequently been challenged in school libraries for profanity, sexual content, and depictions of drinking and smoking, appearing on the ALA's most-challenged lists.

Adaptation

After earlier film plans stalled, Looking for Alaska was adapted as an eight-episode limited series for Hulu, created by Josh Schwartz and Stephanie Savage, released on 18 October 2019 and starring Charlie Plummer as Miles and Kristine Froseth as Alaska.
