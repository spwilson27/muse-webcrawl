Winter Line

The Winter Line was a series of German and Italian military fortifications in Italy, constructed during the Second World War by Organisation Todt. The main line, the Gustav Line, ran across Italy from the Tyrrhenian Sea in the west to the Adriatic Sea in the east, following the Garigliano and Rapido rivers in the west and the Sangro river in the east, and centred on the rugged terrain around Monte Cassino.

Construction

Following the Allied invasion of mainland Italy in September 1943 and the Italian armistice, Field Marshal Albert Kesselring prepared successive defensive lines to delay the Allied advance up the peninsula. The Winter Line complex exploited Italy's mountainous spine: gun pits were blasted into rock, rivers were mined and their banks wired, and roads and bridges were prepared for demolition. In front of the Gustav Line stood the Bernhardt (Reinhard) Line, and behind it the Hitler Line, later renamed the Senger Line.

Fighting on the line

The US Fifth Army reached the Bernhardt Line in December 1943, fighting the costly Battle of San Pietro Infine. The Gustav Line itself resisted four major Allied assaults between January and May 1944 in the Battle of Monte Cassino; the abbey above the town was destroyed by Allied bombing in February. The Allied landing at Anzio in January 1944, intended to outflank the line, was contained in its beachhead for months. The line was finally broken in May 1944 during Operation Diadem, after which the Allies liberated Rome on 4 June 1944 and the Germans fell back to the Gothic Line in the northern Apennines.
