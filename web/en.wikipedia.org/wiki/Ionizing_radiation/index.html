Ionizing radiation

The trefoil is the standard warning symbol for ionizing radiation.

Ionizing radiation (also spelled ionising radiation) is radiation that carries enough energy to detach electrons from atoms or molecules, thereby ionizing them. It is made up of energetic subatomic particles, ions, or atoms moving at high speeds, and/or electromagnetic waves in the higher energy end of the electromagnetic spectrum.

Types

The principal types are alpha particles, beta particles, neutrons, gamma rays, and X-rays. Ultraviolet light at the highest energies also has ionizing capability. Alpha particles are stopped by skin or paper; beta particles by a few millimetres of aluminium; gamma rays and neutrons are highly penetrating and require dense shielding such as lead or concrete.

Sources

Natural sources include cosmic rays and the decay of radioactive elements in the Earth, notably radon gas; artificial sources include medical imaging and treatment, nuclear power generation, and industrial radiography. Units of measurement include the becquerel for activity, the gray for absorbed dose, and the sievert for equivalent dose.

Health effects

Ionizing radiation can damage living tissue and DNA, causing burns and acute radiation syndrome at high doses and increasing cancer risk at lower doses. It is also put to beneficial use in radiation therapy, medical imaging, and sterilisation.
