Drusen

Drusen
Type: Retinal deposits
Location: Between retinal pigment epithelium and Bruch's membrane
Associated with: Age-related macular degeneration
First described: 1850s

Drusen, from the German word for 'node' or 'geode', are small yellow or white extracellular deposits that accumulate between the retinal pigment epithelium and Bruch's membrane in the eye, visible on dilated examination and retinal imaging.

Types and significance

Hard drusen are small and well-defined and may occur with normal ageing; large, soft, confluent drusen are a hallmark risk factor for age-related macular degeneration (AMD), predisposing to geographic atrophy and choroidal neovascularisation. Drusen are composed of lipids, proteins including complement factors, and cellular debris.

Other drusen

Optic disc drusen are separate calcified deposits in the optic nerve head that can mimic papilloedema. Retinal drusen are graded by size (<63 µm small, 63–124 µm medium, ≥125 µm large) in AMD classification systems such as AREDS. Management centres on monitoring, lifestyle measures, and AREDS2 supplements for intermediate AMD.
