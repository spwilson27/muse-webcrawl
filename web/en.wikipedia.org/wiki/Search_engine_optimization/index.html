Search engine optimization

Search engine optimization (SEO) is the process of improving the quality and quantity of website traffic to a website or a web page from search engines. SEO targets unpaid search traffic (often referred to as "organic" or "natural" results) rather than direct traffic, referral traffic, social media traffic, or paid traffic.

As an internet marketing strategy, SEO considers how search engines work, the computer-programmed algorithms that dictate search engine behavior, what people search for, the actual search queries or keywords typed into search engines, and which search engines are preferred by the targeted audience. SEO is performed because a website will receive more visitors from a search engine the higher the website ranks in the search engine results page (SERP). These visitors can then potentially be converted into customers.

History

Webmasters and content providers began optimizing websites for search engines in the mid-1990s, shortly after the first search engines were cataloging the early Web. Early search engines, such as Infoseek and AltaVista, ranked pages largely by keyword density and meta tags, which site operators could manipulate easily. In 1998, Google co-founders Larry Page and Sergey Brin published "The Anatomy of a Large-Scale Hypertextual Web Search Engine," introducing the PageRank algorithm, which weighed the quality and quantity of inbound links. The growing importance of links gave rise to link building, reciprocal linking schemes, and eventually to search engines' countermeasures against manipulation, including major algorithm updates such as Google's Panda (2011), Penguin (2012), and Hummingbird (2013).

Methods

Getting indexed

Search engines use crawlers to discover pages, which are then added to an index. Site owners can aid discovery through sitemaps, robots.txt directives, and internal linking, and can submit URLs directly through tools such as Google Search Console.

On-page factors

On-page optimization includes the use of descriptive title tags, headings, structured content, keyword-relevant text, image alt attributes, and internal links. Increasingly, engines reward content that demonstrably satisfies user intent, demonstrates expertise and trustworthiness, loads quickly, and is usable on mobile devices.

Off-page factors

Off-page optimization centers on earning links and mentions from other reputable sites. Because links can be bought or manufactured, engines discount manipulative schemes such as link farms, paid link networks, and comment spam.

White hat and black hat

"White hat" SEO follows search engine guidelines and focuses on a human audience. "Black hat" techniques — keyword stuffing, cloaking, hidden text, doorway pages — violate guidelines and risk penalties or removal from the index. "Gray hat" describes practices in between.

As a marketing strategy

SEO is one component of search engine marketing (SEM), alongside paid search advertising. Because organic rankings cannot be bought directly, SEO outcomes are uncertain and slow relative to pay-per-click campaigns, but successful optimization can deliver durable, compounding traffic. The industry includes consultants, agencies, and in-house specialists, and professional bodies and conferences devoted to the practice.

International markets

Although Google dominates in most markets, optimizers in some countries target regional engines such as Baidu in China, Yandex in Russia, and Naver in South Korea, each with distinct ranking factors and content requirements.
