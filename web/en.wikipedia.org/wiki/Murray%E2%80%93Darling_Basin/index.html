The Murray–Darling Basin is a vast drainage basin in southeastern Australia, covering about one million square kilometres — roughly 14 percent of the continent. It is drained by the Murray River, Australia's longest river, and its principal tributary, the Darling.

Geography

The basin spans parts of Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria, South Australia, and the Australian Capital Territory. It supports around 40 percent of Australia's agricultural production by value, including cotton, rice, fruit, and livestock.

Water management

Heavy irrigation diversions have caused major ecological stress, including salinity and reduced flows to the Coorong wetlands at the river mouth. The Basin Plan of 2012 set sustainable diversion limits, administered by the Murray–Darling Basin Authority.
