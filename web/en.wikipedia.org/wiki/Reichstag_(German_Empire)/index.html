Reichstag (German Empire)

The Reichstag was the parliament of the German Empire from 1871 to 1918, and previously of the North German Confederation from 1867. It was elected by universal male suffrage and secret ballot, an unusually democratic franchise for the time, though its powers were constrained by the constitutional position of the Emperor, the Chancellor and the federal council, the Bundesrat.

Powers

The Reichstag debated and voted on imperial legislation and the budget, but it could not appoint or dismiss the Imperial Chancellor, who was responsible to the Kaiser rather than to parliament. This division of authority meant that major parties could be excluded from government despite electoral success; the Social Democrats became the largest party in 1912 without entering government.

Building

From 1894 the Reichstag met in the Reichstag building in Berlin, designed by Paul Wallot and famous for its inscription "Dem deutschen Volke" ("To the German people").

End

In October 1918, constitutional reforms made the government responsible to the Reichstag shortly before the Empire collapsed in the German Revolution; the institution continued in altered form in the Weimar Republic.
