2 Maccabees is a deuterocanonical book of the Bible which focuses on the Maccabean Revolt against Antiochus IV Epiphanes and concludes with the defeat of the Seleucid general Nicanor. It is believed to be an abridgement (epitome) of a lost five-volume work by Jason of Cyrene, prefaced by two letters from the Jews of Jerusalem.

Contents

The book covers roughly 176–161 BCE, overlapping the events of the first half of 1 Maccabees but from a more theological and Jerusalem-centred perspective. It recounts the corruption of the high priesthood, the persecution under Antiochus IV, the martyrdoms of Eleazar and of a mother and her seven sons, the campaigns of Judas Maccabeus, the purification and rededication of the Temple (the origin of Hanukkah), and the death of Nicanor. Striking episodes include visions, angelic horsemen, and the story of Heliodorus being repelled from the Temple treasury.

Theological significance

2 Maccabees is a key early source for the doctrines of the resurrection of the dead, prayers for the dead, and the intercession of the saints (notably in the episode of the high priest Onias and the prophet Jeremiah praying for the nation). Its account of Judas collecting an offering for soldiers who had died with idolatrous amulets (2 Maccabees 12:43–45) has historically been cited in support of purgatory.

Authorship and date

The anonymous epitomist states that he has condensed the work of Jason of Cyrene. The letters prefixed to the book, one dated to 124 BCE, give a rough earliest bound for the compilation; most scholars date the finished book to the late second or early first century BCE.
