Caretaker prime minister

A caretaker prime minister is the head of a caretaker government, serving temporarily when the previous government has fallen, resigned, or lost an election and a successor has not yet been appointed or confirmed. The caretaker prime minister leads an administration that is generally expected to conduct only the routine business of government and to refrain from major policy decisions, new spending commitments, or significant appointments until a new government with a fresh mandate takes office.

Conventions

In Westminster systems, caretaker conventions apply from the dissolution of parliament before a general election until the election result is clear or a new government is formed. The incumbent prime minister normally remains in a caretaker capacity during this period. In other cases a neutral technocrat or senior figure is appointed to supervise elections — a practice used notably in countries such as Bangladesh and Pakistan, where caretaker governments were introduced to administer polls, and in Greece and Italy, where technocratic cabinets have bridged political crises.

Examples

Examples include interim prime ministers appointed during election periods in Nepal, caretaker cabinets in the Netherlands that govern after a coalition collapses (demissionair kabinet), and acting premiers elsewhere. The powers of caretakers vary by constitution and convention; some may serve only days, others for many months.
