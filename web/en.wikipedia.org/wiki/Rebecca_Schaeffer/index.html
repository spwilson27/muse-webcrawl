Rebecca Lucile Schaeffer (November 6, 1967 – July 18, 1989) was an American actress and model. She began her career as a teen model before moving to acting, gaining notice for the soap opera One Life to Live and starring as Patricia "Patti" Russell in the CBS sitcom My Sister Sam (1986–1988). Her murder by an obsessed fan at age 21 drew national attention to stalking and led to significant legal changes in California and beyond.

Early life and career

Schaeffer was born in Eugene, Oregon, and raised in Portland, the only child of a writer and a child psychologist. While still in school she began modelling, appearing in department store catalogs and television commercials, and moved to New York City as a teenager to pursue the work full-time. She appeared on the cover of Seventeen magazine and had a small role in Woody Allen's Radio Days (1987). Her sitcom My Sister Sam, co-starring Pam Dawber, debuted strongly in 1986 before declining ratings and a schedule change led to cancellation in 1988. She subsequently appeared in the black comedy Scenes from the Class Struggle in Beverly Hills (1989).

Murder

On July 18, 1989, Schaeffer was shot and killed at the door of her West Hollywood apartment by Robert John Bardo, a 19-year-old fan from Tucson who had stalked her for three years. Bardo had obtained her home address through a private investigator who traced it via California Department of Motor Vehicles records. He was convicted of first-degree murder in 1991 and sentenced to life in prison without parole.

Aftermath and legacy

Her death prompted California to enact the nation's first anti-stalking law in 1990, followed by similar statutes in all other U.S. states, and the federal Driver's Privacy Protection Act of 1994, which restricted access to DMV records. Schaeffer's parents founded a foundation in her name, and her story has been covered in numerous documentaries about celebrity stalking.
