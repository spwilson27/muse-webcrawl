Afroasiatic languages

Afroasiatic
Branches: Semitic, Berber, Chadic, Cushitic, Egyptian, Omotic
Speakers: ~400–500 million
Region: North Africa, Horn of Africa, Middle East

The Afroasiatic languages (or Afro-Asiatic, sometimes also Hamito-Semitic) are a language family of about 300 languages spoken predominantly in the geographic subregions of West Asia, North Africa, the Horn of Africa, and parts of the Sahara and Sahel. With around 495 million native speakers, it is the fourth largest language family by number of speakers after Indo-European, Sino-Tibetan, and Niger-Congo.

Branches

Generally accepted branches are Semitic (including Arabic, Hebrew, Amharic, and Tigrinya), Berber (Amazigh languages), Chadic (including Hausa), Cushitic (including Somali and Afar — spoken by the Afar people), Egyptian (extinct, including Coptic), and Omotic (debated as a separate branch).

Features

Common traits include consonantal roots (especially in Semitic and Egyptian), emphatic consonants in some branches, grammatical gender (masculine/feminine), and verb derivation systems. Proto-Afroasiatic is among the oldest reconstructed protolanguages, with proposed homelands in Northeast Africa or the Levant dating to 10,000+ years ago.
