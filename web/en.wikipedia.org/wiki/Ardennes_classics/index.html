Ardennes classics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Ardennes classics are a series of one-day professional road cycling races held in mid-to-late April in the Ardennes region of Belgium and the neighbouring Netherlands. They mark the end of the spring classics season and, unlike the earlier cobbled classics of Flanders, favour climbers and puncheurs on hilly terrain.

The races

The three principal races are the Amstel Gold Race (held in the Dutch province of Limburg), La Flèche Wallonne (finishing atop the Mur de Huy), and Liège–Bastogne–Liège, nicknamed La Doyenne ("The Old Lady") as the oldest of the five Monuments of cycling, first run in 1892. The races are usually held within a single week, with Flèche Wallonne on the Wednesday between the Sunday Amstel Gold Race and Liège–Bastogne–Liège the following Sunday. Women's editions of all three races are also held.

Doubles and trebles

Winning two of the three in one week is known as an Ardennes double. Only a handful of riders — including Ferdi Kübler, Eddy Merckx, Moreno Argentin, Davide Rebellin, Philippe Gilbert, Alejandro Valverde, and Tadej Pogačar — have achieved the treble of all three in a single year or combinations approaching it; Rebellin (2004), Gilbert (2011), and Pogačar (2025) are credited with winning all three in one season. Gilbert's 2011 feat also included Brabantse Pijl, making an "Ardennes week" grand slam of four.
