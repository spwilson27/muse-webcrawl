Echuca is a town in northern Victoria, Australia, situated on the banks of the Murray River and Campaspe River, close to the junction of the Murray and the Goulburn River. It is the administrative centre of the Shire of Campaspe and lies directly across the Murray from its twin town of Moama in New South Wales. The name is generally taken from an Aboriginal word meaning "meeting of the waters."

History

Echuca grew rapidly after the 1850s to become Australia's largest inland port. Its wharf, opened in stages from 1865, and the railway connection to Melbourne (1864) made the town the hub of the Murray–Darling paddle steamer trade: wool, grain, and timber were shipped downriver to Echuca and railed to the coast, while supplies travelled inland to the Riverina. At its peak in the 1870s and 1880s, scores of paddle steamers and barges worked the rivers, and Echuca's red gum wharf stretched more than 300 metres. The river trade declined with the spread of railways and drought in the early twentieth century.

Today

The restored Port of Echuca precinct, with its historic wharf, steam-driven sawmill, and a fleet of preserved paddle steamers — including the PS Pevensey and PS Adelaide, among the world's oldest wooden-hulled paddle steamers — is a major tourist attraction, and Echuca is often described as Australia's paddle steamer capital. The town also serves a rich irrigation farming district producing dairy, fruit, and vegetables, and hosts the Southern 80 water-ski race on the Murray.
