Walt Kelly

Walter Crawford Kelly Jr. (August 25, 1913 – October 18, 1973) was an American animator and cartoonist, best known for the comic strip Pogo. He began his animation career in 1936 at Walt Disney Studios, contributing to films including Pinocchio, Fantasia, and Dumbo.

Career

Kelly left Disney during the animators' strike of 1941 and worked in comic books for Dell Comics, where he created Pogo the possum for Animal Comics in 1942. In 1948 he became political cartoonist for the short-lived New York Star, where Pogo began as a newspaper strip; it was syndicated nationally from 1949 and ran until 1975, outliving Kelly by two years.

Set in the Okefenokee Swamp, Pogo combined lush brushwork, whimsical verse, and sharp political satire. Kelly famously used the strip to caricature Senator Joseph McCarthy as the bobcat "Simple J. Malarkey." His most quoted line, spoken by Pogo, is "We have met the enemy and he is us," first used on an Earth Day poster in 1970 and in the strip in 1971.

Legacy

Kelly was a founding influence on many later cartoonists and was inducted into the Will Eisner Comic Book Hall of Fame. Pogo remains regarded as one of the great American comic strips for its combination of humour, language play, and social commentary.
