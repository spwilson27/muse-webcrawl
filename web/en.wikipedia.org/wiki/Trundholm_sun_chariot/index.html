The Trundholm sun chariot (Danish: Solvognen) is a Nordic Bronze Age artifact discovered in Denmark. It is a representation of the sun chariot, a bronze statue of a horse and a large bronze disk, which are placed on a device with spoked wheels.

The chariot was discovered in 1902 in the Trundholm Mose bog on the northwest coast of the island of Zealand (Sjælland) in Denmark, in a part of the bog known as Trundholm Mose. It was ploughed up by a farmer, and is now in the collection of the National Museum of Denmark in Copenhagen. It is dated to around 1400 BC.

Description

The horse stands on a bronze rod supported by four wheels. The rod below the horse is connected to the disk, which is supported by two wheels. All of the wheels have four spokes. The artifact was cast in the lost-wax method. The sculpture is 54 cm (21 in) wide and 35 cm (14 in) high.

The disk has a diameter of approximately 25 cm (9.8 in). It has a gold sheet applied to one side only; scholars interpret the gilded side as the sun of the day and the dark bronze side as the sun of the night, or as the sun's return journey through the underworld.

Interpretation

The chariot is thought to have been produced as a votive offering and deposited in a wetland. It illustrates the Bronze Age belief, known across Northern Europe, that the sun was drawn across the sky by a divine horse — a motif also found in later Norse mythology, where the sun is drawn by the horses Árvakr and Alsviðr, and the god Dagr rides across the sky.
