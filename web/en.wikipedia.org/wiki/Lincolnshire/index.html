Lincolnshire (abbreviated Lincs) is a ceremonial county in eastern England, bordered by the North Sea to the east, the Humber Estuary to the north, and counties including Norfolk, Cambridgeshire, Northamptonshire, Leicestershire, Nottinghamshire, South Yorkshire and the East Riding of Yorkshire. Its county town is the city of Lincoln; other major settlements include Grimsby, Scunthorpe, Boston, Grantham, Stamford and Gainsborough.

It is the second-largest ceremonial county in England by area but one of the more sparsely populated, with an economy historically dominated by agriculture — it is nicknamed the "breadbasket of England", and its flat Fens are famous for vegetables, bulbs and flowers.

Geography

The county spans several landscapes: the chalk hills of the Lincolnshire Wolds (an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty, rising to 168 m at Wolds Top), the low marshy Fens in the south-east reclaimed from the sea since Roman and especially 17th-century Dutch-engineered drainage, the limestone ridge of the Lincoln Cliff running north–south through Lincoln, and the flat coastal marshland. The rivers Witham, Welland, Nene and Trent water it.

History

The area was the territory of the Corieltauvi tribe; the Romans founded Lindum Colonia (Lincoln) and built Ermine Street and the Fosse Way through it. In the Danelaw the county's towns — Lincoln, Stamford and others — were among the Five Boroughs. Medieval Lincoln was one of England's richest wool cities; its cathedral was for a time the world's tallest building. The Pilgrim Fathers' leaders attempted to sail from near Boston in 1607 before succeeding in 1620; Boston, Massachusetts took its name from the Lincolnshire town. In the Second World War the county's flat terrain hosted many RAF bomber stations — "Bomber County".

Economy

Agriculture and food processing dominate: Lincolnshire produces a large share of the UK's vegetables, potatoes, poultry and cereals, with major food companies around Spalding, Boston and Grimsby (also a historic fishing and food-processing port). Tourism concentrates on the coast (Skegness, Cleethorpes) and historic towns.

Culture and dialect

The Lincolnshire dialect, now receding, and the dialect poetry of Alfred, Lord Tennyson — born at Somersby in the Wolds — mark local identity. Traditional foods include Lincolnshire sausages (sage-seasoned), stuffed chine, haslet and the Lincolnshire Poacher cheese. The county flag, registered in 2005, combines a red cross, gold castle and green and blue quarters symbolising coast and agriculture.
