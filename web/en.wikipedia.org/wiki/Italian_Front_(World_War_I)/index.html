Italian Front (World War I)

Date: 23 May 1915 – 3 November 1918
Location: North-eastern Italy / Austro-Hungarian border
Result: Italian victory; Treaty of Saint-Germain

The Italian Front was a theatre of the First World War fought between Italy and Austria-Hungary (later supported by Germany) along Italy's north-eastern frontier, largely in mountainous Alpine terrain and along the Isonzo river.

Course of the war

Italy entered the war in May 1915 under the Treaty of London, seeking Trentino, Trieste and other territories. General Luigi Cadorna launched eleven offensives along the Isonzo between 1915 and 1917 with limited gains. In October–November 1917 the Central Powers broke through at Caporetto, driving Italy back to the Piave river. Under Armando Diaz, Italy held the Piave in June 1918 and won the decisive Battle of Vittorio Veneto in October–November 1918, leading to the Armistice of Villa Giusti.

Cost

The fighting, much of it at high altitude in extreme weather, cost Italy roughly 650,000 dead; Austro-Hungarian losses were comparable.
