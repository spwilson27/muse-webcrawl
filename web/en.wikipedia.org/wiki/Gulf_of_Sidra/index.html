Gulf of Sidra

The Gulf of Sidra (Arabic: خليج سرت, Khalij Sirt), also called the Gulf of Sirte, is a large gulf of the Mediterranean Sea on the northern coast of Libya, extending from Misrata in the west to Benghazi in the east, about 440 km across. It is named for the port city of Sirte.

LocationLibya, Mediterranean Sea
Width~440 km
CitiesSirte, Misrata, Ra's Lanuf, Brega
Ancient nameSyrtis Major

Geography and history

Known to the Romans as Syrtis Major, its shoals were feared by sailors. Oil-export terminals at Sidra, Ra's Lanuf and Brega line its shore, making it vital to Libya's petroleum economy.

'Line of Death'

In 1973 Muammar Gaddafi claimed the gulf as Libyan territorial waters, drawing a 'Line of Death' the US refused to recognise. Freedom-of-navigation operations led to air incidents in 1981 and 1989 in which US Navy F-14s shot down Libyan jets, and to the 1986 US operation El Dorado Canyon context. See Libya and Sirte.
