Ember days

Ember days are quarterly periods of prayer and fasting in the liturgical calendar of Western Christian churches. The Ember Days occur four times a year, near the start of each season, and traditionally consist of the Wednesday, Friday, and Saturday of a designated week.

Timing

The traditional Ember weeks fall after the First Sunday of Lent (spring), after Pentecost (summer), after the Exaltation of the Holy Cross on September 14 (autumn), and after the feast of Saint Lucy on December 13 (winter). A traditional mnemonic was provided by the verse: "Sant Crux, Lucia, Cineres, Charismata Dia / Ut sit in angaria quarta sequens feria".

Origins and observance

The observance is of Roman origin, possibly adapted from pagan agricultural festivals connected with sowing, harvest, and vintage. The Ember days were traditionally days of fasting and abstinence, intended to thank God for the gifts of nature, teach moderation, and assist the needy. They were also customary times for ordinations to the priesthood.

After the Second Vatican Council, the observance of Ember days in the Roman Catholic Church was left to the discretion of national bishops' conferences, and their character was redirected toward particular intentions such as vocations and peace. Ember days continue to be observed in the Anglican Communion and in some Lutheran and traditional Catholic communities.
