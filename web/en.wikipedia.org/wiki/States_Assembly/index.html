States Assembly

The States Assembly (French: Assemblée des États; Jèrriais: Êtats d'Jèrri) is the parliament of Jersey, a Crown Dependency in the Channel Islands. It is formally known as the States of Jersey. The Assembly makes primary legislation for the island and elects the government, led by the Chief Minister of Jersey.

History

The States originated in the early modern period as a body combining the island's jurats, rectors and constables under the presidency of the Bailiff. Over the centuries it evolved from a body subordinate to the Royal Court into the island's legislature. Major reforms in 2005, following the Clive Committee review, introduced ministerial government, and further reforms implemented for the 2022 general election abolished the office of Senator and restructured the composition of the Assembly.

Composition

Since the 2022 reforms, the elected membership consists of 37 Deputies elected from nine multi-member constituencies and 9 Connétables (constables) of the parishes — 46 elected members in total. Non-voting members include the Lieutenant Governor, the Bailiff (who presides), the Dean of Jersey, the Attorney General and the Solicitor General. Jurats no longer sit in the Assembly, having been removed in 2005.

Functions

The Assembly enacts laws (subject to royal assent by the King in Council), approves taxation and public expenditure, and scrutinises the Council of Ministers. It elects the Chief Minister, who in turn nominates ministers. Sittings take place in the States Chamber in the Royal Square, St Helier, and proceedings are conducted in English, though French retains a formal role.

Related bodies

The States Assembly should not be confused with the States of Deliberation, the parliament of Guernsey. Jersey's legislature is unicameral.
