Gross tonnage

Gross tonnage (GT)
A dimensionless measure of a ship's total internal volume, defined by the International Tonnage Convention of 1969.

Gross tonnage (GT, G.T. or gt) is a nonlinear measure of a ship's overall internal volume. It is calculated from the total volume of all enclosed spaces of the ship and is used to determine things such as a vessel's manning regulations, safety rules, registration fees, and port dues. It is not a measure of weight or displacement, and must not be confused with net tonnage or deadweight tonnage.

History

The International Convention on Tonnage Measurement of Ships, 1969, adopted by the International Maritime Organization (IMO), introduced gross tonnage as a universal system of tonnage measurement, replacing the older gross register tonnage. The convention entered into force in 1982 and applied to all ships by 18 July 1994.

Calculation

Gross tonnage is calculated using the formula GT = K1V, where V is the total volume of all enclosed spaces in cubic metres and K1 = 0.2 + 0.02 log10 V. The result is a dimensionless number. Because the coefficient depends on the volume itself, gross tonnage slightly compresses the scale for very large vessels.

Uses

Gross tonnage is widely used as the basis for port dues, canal tolls (such as those of the Panama and Suez canals, which use their own variants), pilotage charges, and the application of international safety conventions such as SOLAS.
