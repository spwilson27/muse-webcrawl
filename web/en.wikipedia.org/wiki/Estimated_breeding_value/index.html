Estimated breeding value

An estimated breeding value (EBV) is a prediction of the genetic merit of an individual animal for a particular trait, expressed as the deviation of its progeny's expected performance from a population baseline. EBVs are a central tool of modern animal breeding, allowing breeders to select parents whose offspring will show improved production, health, fertility or conformation.

Concept

An animal's true breeding value is the sum of the average effects of the genes it carries; because only half of an individual's genes are passed to each offspring, the breeding value is defined as twice the expected deviation of its progeny from the population mean. Since the true value cannot be observed directly, it is estimated from the performance of the animal itself, its relatives and its progeny, weighted by the trait's heritability and the amount of information available.

Methods

Classical EBVs are computed with best linear unbiased prediction (BLUP), using mixed models that simultaneously account for fixed effects such as herd, year and season, and random genetic effects structured by the pedigree relationship matrix. Since the 2000s, genomic estimated breeding values (GEBVs) have supplemented pedigree information with dense marker (SNP) genotypes, substantially increasing accuracy in young animals without progeny records, and genomic selection based on EBVs has transformed dairy cattle breeding in particular.

Accuracy and index selection

Each EBV is published with an accuracy or reliability value reflecting the quantity of information behind it. Breeders commonly combine several EBVs into an economic selection index that weights traits by their contribution to profit or breeding goals.
