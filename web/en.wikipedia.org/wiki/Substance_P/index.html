Substance P (SP) is an undecapeptide (a peptide composed of a chain of 11 amino acid residues) member of the tachykinin neuropeptide family. It is a neuropeptide, acting as a neurotransmitter and neuromodulator. Substance P and its closely related neuropeptide neurokinin A (NKA) are produced from the precursor gene preprotachykinin A (TAC1). The amino acid sequence of substance P is Arg-Pro-Lys-Pro-Gln-Gln-Phe-Phe-Gly-Leu-Met-NH2.

Discovery

Substance P was originally discovered in 1931 by Ulf von Euler and John H. Gaddum in extracts of equine brain and gut, which caused intestinal contraction and vasodilation. The "P" in the name stands for "powder" (or "preparation"), referring to the dried powdered extract. The peptide was purified and its sequence determined in the early 1970s by Susan Leeman and colleagues.

Function

Substance P is released from the terminals of specific sensory nerves and is found in the brain and spinal cord. It is associated with the perception of pain: it is released by primary afferent fibres in the dorsal horn and transmits nociceptive signals to the central nervous system. Acting mainly on the neurokinin 1 (NK1) receptor, it also contributes to neurogenic inflammation, vasodilation, plasma extravasation, smooth muscle contraction in the gut, salivation, and the vomiting reflex — the NK1 antagonist aprepitant is used clinically as an antiemetic. It has also been implicated in mood regulation, anxiety, stress responses, and inflammatory diseases such as inflammatory bowel disease.
