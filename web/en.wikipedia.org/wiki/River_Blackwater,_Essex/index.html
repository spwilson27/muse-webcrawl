The River Blackwater is a river in Essex, England. It rises near Debden in the north-west of the county, flows east through Braintree, Coggeshall and Witham, and joins the River Chelmer at Maldon before entering the wide tidal Blackwater Estuary, which opens into the North Sea south of Mersea Island. The river was historically known as the Pant in its upper reaches above Braintree; the lower river and estuary were recorded by the Romans as the Idumania.

Course and estuary

The non-tidal river runs about 58 kilometres from its source to Maldon. Below the town the channel broadens into one of the East Coast's great estuaries, flanked by saltmarsh and mudflats at Tollesbury, Bradwell-on-Sea and Osea Island. The estuary is an internationally important site for overwintering wildfowl and waders and is protected under national and European designations; its saltmarshes are among the sites of early "managed realignment" coastal experiments at Tollesbury.

History and economy

The Blackwater was a working river: Maldon grew as a port and shipbuilding town, famous for Thames sailing barges and for Maldon sea salt, harvested from the estuary's salterns since at least the medieval period and still produced today. The estuary is renowned for oysters, farmed around Mersea Island and Tollesbury since Roman times, and for its fleets of traditional fishing smacks. In 991 the Battle of Maldon, fought near the causeway to Northey Island, saw the English ealdorman Byrhtnoth killed by a Viking raiding force; the defeat is commemorated in the Old English poem The Battle of Maldon. The Chelmer and Blackwater Navigation, opened in 1797, linked Chelmsford to the estuary at Heybridge and carried freight into the 20th century; it survives as a leisure waterway.

Leisure

The estuary is a centre for sailing, with clubs at Maldon, West Mersea, Bradwell and Tollesbury, and the muddy foreshore hosts an annual charity mud race at Maldon, held since the 1970s.
