Glomeromycota

Glomeromycota (often referred to as glomeromycetes) is one of eight currently recognized divisions within the kingdom Fungi, with approximately 230 described species. Members of the Glomeromycota form arbuscular mycorrhizas (AMs) with the thalli of bryophytes and the roots of vascular land plants, a symbiosis of enormous ecological importance found in the great majority of plant families.

These fungi are obligate symbionts: they cannot complete their life cycles without a plant host, from which they obtain carbohydrates, while supplying the plant with mineral nutrients — particularly phosphate — absorbed by their extraradical hyphae. Their spores are large, multinucleate and formed on subtending hyphae in soil or within roots.

The lineage is ancient: fossil evidence of arbuscular mycorrhiza-like associations dates to the Ordovician, around 460 million years ago, and molecular clock estimates suggest the group may be older still. Because they lack an observed sexual stage, glomeromycetes are unusual in that a single spore can contain hundreds of genetically distinct nuclei.
