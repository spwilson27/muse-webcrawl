Kaddare alphabet

The Kaddare alphabet is a writing system invented for the Somali language. It was devised in 1952 by Sheikh Hussein Sheikh Ahmed Kaddare, a Somali religious scholar and poet from the Abgal Hawiye clan, after whom the script is named. It is one of three major indigenous scripts developed in the twentieth century for writing Somali, alongside the Osmanya alphabet and the Borama alphabet.

History

For centuries Somali was written in an adapted Arabic script known as the Wadaad writing, but in the colonial and early independence era there was a sustained national debate over which orthography the new Somali state should adopt. Kaddare presented his script in 1952. It was designed to represent the full phonology of Somali, including its vowel length contrasts and ejective and implosive consonants.

When the Supreme Revolutionary Council of Somalia finally settled the script question in 1972, it chose a modified Latin alphabet devised by the linguist Shire Jama Ahmed. The Latin script became the official orthography, and the Kaddare alphabet fell into limited use, though it is still known to scholars of Somali language and literature.

Structure

The Kaddare script has distinct characters for Somali consonants and vowels. Its letter forms are original creations not derived from either Arabic or Latin models, which supporters argued gave it a distinctively Somali identity. The script is written from left to right.
