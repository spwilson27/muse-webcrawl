Screaming (music)

Screaming is an extended vocal technique that is mostly used in subgenres of heavy metal, punk rock and hardcore. Screamed vocals range from the high-pitched shrieks of black metal to the low growls of death metal, and are typically produced with the false vocal cords, diaphragmatic pressure and heavy amplification rather than by straining the true vocal folds.

History

Occasional screams appear in blues, rock and roll and soul — artists such as Howlin' Wolf, Little Richard and James Brown used them expressively. In the 1970s and 1980s, singers in heavy metal and hardcore punk developed sustained screaming as a primary vocal style. Death metal added the low "death growl", black metal the raspy shriek, and metalcore and screamo popularised alternation between screamed verses and sung choruses.

Technique and health

Coaches distinguish techniques such as the fry scream and false-cord scream. While improper screaming can injure the voice, studies of trained extreme-metal vocalists have found that correct technique produces the harsh timbre through structures above the vocal folds, allowing some performers to sustain careers without lasting damage.
