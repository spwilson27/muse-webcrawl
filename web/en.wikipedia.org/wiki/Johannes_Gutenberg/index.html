Johannes Gutenberg

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A printer's workshop in Mainz: a wooden press and a compositor's case of metal type sorts]

Johannes Gensfleisch zur Laden zum Gutenberg (c. 1398-1468) was a German inventor and craftsman who invented the movable-type printing press in Europe around 1440. His system — a hand mould for casting uniform metal type, oil-based ink, and a screw press — made mass production of books practical for the first time in the West.

His masterwork, the Gutenberg Bible (c. 1455), was the first major book printed with movable type in Europe, printed in about 180 copies. Gutenberg's business ended in debt — his financier Johann Fust foreclosed and took over the workshop — but within decades presses operated all over Europe.

Legacy

Gutenberg is routinely ranked among the most influential people of the second millennium, because cheap print transformed religion, science, education and politics.
