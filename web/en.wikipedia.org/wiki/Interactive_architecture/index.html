Interactive architecture

Interactive architecture refers to buildings and spaces that respond to the actions of their occupants and to environmental conditions, using sensors, actuators, embedded computing and kinetic structures to change their form, appearance or behaviour in real time.

History

The idea has roots in the cybernetic architecture of the 1960s, such as Cedric Price and Joan Littlewood's Fun Palace and Gordon Pask's conversation theory. Work at the MIT Media Lab and by practices such as Diller Scofidio + Renfro and Kas Oosterhuis advanced the field from the 1990s.

Examples

Notable examples include media façades that react to passers-by, kinetic shading systems that track the sun, and installations such as the Blur Building at Expo 2002 in Switzerland, whose mist envelope responded to weather data.
