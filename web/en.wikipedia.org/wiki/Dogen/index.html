Dōgen Zenji (1200–1253) was a Japanese Zen Buddhist monk, writer and philosopher who founded the Soto school of Zen in Japan. After study in China under Rujing, he taught that practice and enlightenment are one: seated meditation (zazen) in the posture of 'just sitting' (shikantaza) is not a means to awakening but awakening itself enacted. His masterwork, the Shōbōgenzō, is among the most profound philosophical texts in Japanese, exploring being-time (uji), Buddha-nature and language.

Legacy

Dogen founded Eihei-ji, still a head temple of Soto Zen. Long a sectarian treasure, his writings were rediscovered in the 20th century and now anchor Japanese philosophy's modern canon and global Zen practice alike.
