London, Brighton and South Coast Railway
Reporting mark: LB&SCR
Dates of operation: 1846–1922
Successor: Southern Railway
Headquarters: London Bridge, London

The London, Brighton and South Coast Railway (LB&SCR, known also as "the Brighton line") was a railway company in the United Kingdom from 1846 to 1922. Its lines formed a network in much of present-day Greater London, Surrey and Sussex, serving the south coast resorts of Brighton, Eastbourne, Worthing, Littlehampton and Bognor Regis, the ports of Newhaven and Shoreham-by-Sea, and inland towns including Horsham, Tunbridge Wells and Lewes.

An LB&SCR locomotive in the company's famous "improved engine green" (ochre) livery.

History

The company was formed by amalgamation of the London and Croydon Railway, the London and Brighton Railway and other lines in 1846. For its first two decades its management and finances were turbulent — its accounts scandal of 1867 nearly broke it — but under locomotive superintendents William Stroudley (from 1870), R. J. Billinton and L. B. Billinton it acquired a reputation for handsome, well-engineered locomotives, and under chairman Samuel Laing and successors it ran one of Britain's densest suburban networks.

Services and electrification

The LB&SCR was a pioneer of overhead electric traction in Britain, electrifying its South London line in 1909 using the "Elevated Electric" system; by 1922 several suburban routes used 6.6 kV AC overhead. After grouping, the Southern Railway replaced this with third-rail DC electrification.

Grouping

On 1 January 1923 the company became part of the Southern Railway under the Railways Act 1921 grouping. Its characteristic umber-and-cream Pullman services and expresses such as the Southern Belle (later the Brighton Belle) long outlived it.
