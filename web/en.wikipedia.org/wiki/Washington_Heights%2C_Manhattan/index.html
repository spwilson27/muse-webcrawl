Washington Heights, Manhattan

Washington Heights is a neighbourhood in the northern part of the New York City borough of Manhattan. It is bordered by Inwood to the north, Harlem to the south, the Hudson River to the west and the Harlem River to the east.

History

The neighbourhood is named for Fort Washington, a fortified position held by American forces during the Revolutionary War and captured by the British in November 1776. High, rocky terrain gives the area some of Manhattan's highest natural points, including Bennett Park, the island's highest ground. In the twentieth century the Heights became home to large Jewish, Greek and later Dominican communities; it remains the centre of Dominican life in the United States.

Landmarks

The Cloisters museum, a branch of the Metropolitan Museum of Art devoted to medieval art, sits in Fort Tryon Park. Other landmarks include the George Washington Bridge, which connects to New Jersey at 178th Street, the United Palace theatre, and the Columbia University Irving Medical Center campus.
