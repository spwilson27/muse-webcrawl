White-label product

A white-label product is a product or service produced by one company (the producer) that other companies (the marketers) rebrand to make it appear as if they made it. The name derives from the image of a white label on packaging that can be filled in with the marketer's own trade dress.

Uses

White-label production is common in mass-produced generic consumer electronics, store-brand groceries and household goods, banking and financial services (such as white-label ATMs and credit cards), and software, where white-label platforms let many businesses offer the same underlying service under their own branding.

Advantages and drawbacks

For marketers, white labelling allows a company to offer a product without investing in manufacturing or technology, concentrating instead on branding, sales, and customer service. The producer gains volume sales. Drawbacks include limited control over quality and features, and difficulty differentiating from competitors selling the same underlying product.
