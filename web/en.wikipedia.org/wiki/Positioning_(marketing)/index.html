Positioning (marketing)

In marketing, positioning is the process of establishing the image or identity of a brand or product in the minds of target consumers relative to competing products. The concept was popularized by Al Ries and Jack Trout, first in articles in the early 1970s and then in their influential 1981 book Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind. A successful position gives consumers a clear, distinctive, and desirable reason to choose the offering.

Concept

Ries and Trout argued that in an over-communicated society the mind accepts only a few brands per category, arranged like rungs on a ladder; a new entrant must either create a new ladder or displace an existing rung. Classic examples include Avis's "We try harder" (embracing the number-two position against Hertz), 7-Up's "The Uncola," and Volvo's long-running association with safety.

Positioning statements

A typical positioning statement follows the form: "For [target audience], [brand] is the [frame of reference] that [point of difference], because [reason to believe]." Closely tied concepts include the unique selling proposition (USP) and the value proposition. Marketers use perceptual (positioning) maps to plot consumer perceptions of brands on attribute axes such as price and quality.

Strategies and repositioning

Bases for positioning include product attributes, price/quality, use or application, product user, product class, cultural symbols, and competitors. Repositioning adjusts an established position in response to market change; depositioning attempts to shift perceptions of a rival. Errors include under-positioning (no clear image), over-positioning (too narrow), confused positioning, and doubtful positioning (claims consumers do not believe).
