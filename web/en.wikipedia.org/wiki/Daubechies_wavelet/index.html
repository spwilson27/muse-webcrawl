Daubechies wavelet

The Daubechies wavelets, based on the work of Ingrid Daubechies, are a family of orthogonal wavelets defining a discrete wavelet transform and characterised by a maximal number of vanishing moments for a given support width. They were a breakthrough in wavelet theory when introduced in 1988, giving the first smooth, compactly supported orthogonal wavelet bases.

Properties

For each vanishing-moment count N, the wavelet and scaling filters have length 2N, so the wavelets have support width 2N − 1. The family is usually denoted dbN: db1 is the Haar wavelet, db2 (D4) has four filter coefficients, and so on. Except for db1 the Daubechies wavelets are neither symmetric nor antisymmetric, and their scaling functions have fractal-like irregularity that increases with N, though regularity grows as more vanishing moments are added.

Applications

Daubechies wavelets are used extensively in signal and image processing, numerical analysis and compression; a related biorthogonal pair derived from this work (the CDF 9/7 wavelet) underlies the JPEG 2000 image compression standard. The FBI's fingerprint compression standard (WSQ) also uses wavelet-based coding.
