Job guarantee

A job guarantee (JG) is an economic policy proposal in which the government offers a job to anyone willing and able to work, at a socially acceptable wage, typically as an employer of last resort. It is a central proposal of Modern Monetary Theory (MMT) economists and is intended to provide full employment and price stability through a buffer stock of employed workers.

Concept

Advocates such as Hyman Minsky, Warren Mosler, L. Randall Wray, and Pavlina Tcherneva argue that a federally funded, locally administered programme paying a living wage with benefits would eliminate involuntary unemployment, set a floor for private-sector wages and conditions, and act as an automatic stabiliser: spending rises in recessions and falls in expansions.

Proposed work includes care services, environmental restoration, infrastructure maintenance, arts, and community projects. The JG wage is proposed as the effective minimum wage.

History and examples

Precursors include New Deal programmes such as the Works Progress Administration and Civilian Conservation Corps in the United States, India's Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA, 2005), which guarantees 100 days of rural wage employment, and Argentina's Jefes de Hogar programme after 2001.

Criticism

Critics raise concerns about cost, inflation, administration, displacement of private employment, and whether guaranteed jobs would match workers' skills. Supporters reply that the programme stabilises prices by replacing an unemployment buffer stock with an employment buffer stock.
