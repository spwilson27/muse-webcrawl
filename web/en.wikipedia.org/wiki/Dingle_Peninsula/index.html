Dingle Peninsula

The Dingle Peninsula (Irish: Corca Dhuibhne) is a peninsula in County Kerry, on the south-west coast of Ireland. It stretches about 48 kilometres into the Atlantic Ocean between Tralee Bay to the north and Dingle Bay to the south, and takes its name from its main town, Dingle. The western part of the peninsula is a Gaeltacht, an area where Irish remains a community language; official signage there is in Irish only.

Geography

The spine of the peninsula is formed by the Brandon mountain range: Mount Brandon (952 m) is one of Ireland's highest peaks and dominates the eastern end, while the Conor Pass road (one of Ireland's highest mountain passes) crosses towards Dingle town. The peninsula's western tip, at Dunmore Head, is among the most westerly points of mainland Europe. Off the coast lie the Blasket Islands, evacuated by the Irish government in 1953 after a declining population could no longer be sustained; the islands produced a celebrated body of Irish-language literature, including Peig Sayers' autobiography and Tomás Ó Criomhthain's The Islandman.

Archaeology and history

The peninsula is exceptionally rich in early Christian and prehistoric remains: the Gallarus Oratory, a perfectly preserved dry-stone church; Kilmalkedar Church with its Ogham stone; clochán beehive huts; the Riasc monastic site; and promontory forts such as Dunbeg, battered by Atlantic storms. Ogham inscriptions in the area form one of the greatest concentrations in Ireland.

Tourism

The Slea Head Drive, a loop from Dingle town along the R559, is one of Ireland's best-known scenic routes. The town became famous for Fungie, a wild bottlenose dolphin resident in Dingle harbour from 1983 until his disappearance in 2020, and as a location for the films Ryan's Daughter (1970) and Far and Away (1992).
