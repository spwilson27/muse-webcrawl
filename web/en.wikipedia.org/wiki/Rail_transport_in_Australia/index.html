Rail transport in Australia

Rail transport in Australia is a crucial aspect of the Australian transport network. Rail in Australia is to a large extent state-based. As of 2018, the Australian rail network consisted of some 33,000 km of track. Freight rail is dominated by bulk haulage of iron ore and coal, including some of the heaviest and longest trains in the world in the Pilbara region of Western Australia.

Australia's railways historically suffered from the "gauge muddle": the colonies adopted three different track gauges — broad (1,600 mm) in Victoria and South Australia, standard (1,435 mm) in New South Wales, and narrow (1,067 mm) in Queensland, Western Australia and Tasmania — which hindered interstate transport for more than a century. Interstate standard-gauge links between all mainland capitals were completed in 1995.

Passenger services

Urban rail networks serve Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth, and Adelaide. Long-distance passenger trains include the Indian Pacific (Sydney–Perth) and The Ghan (Adelaide–Darwin), among the world's longest passenger rail journeys. The interstate network is managed largely by the Australian Rail Track Corporation (ARTC), while heavy-haul mining railways in the Pilbara are privately owned by mining companies such as Rio Tinto, BHP, and Fortescue.
