The Bishop of Salisbury is the ordinary of the Church of England's Diocese of Salisbury in the Province of Canterbury. The diocese covers much of the counties of Dorset and Wiltshire. The bishop's seat (cathedra) is located in the Cathedral Church of the Blessed Virgin Mary, Salisbury. The bishop's residence is the South Canonry, in the cathedral close; the historic Bishop's Palace now forms part of Salisbury Cathedral School.

History

The see traces its origin to the diocese of Sherborne, founded around 705, and to the diocese of Ramsbury. In 1075 the sees were united and moved to Old Sarum under Bishop Herman; Osmund, bishop from 1078 and later canonised, built the cathedral there and compiled the Sarum Use, the liturgical rite that spread across much of England. In the early 13th century Bishop Richard Poore moved the see to the new city of Salisbury (New Sarum), where the present cathedral was begun in 1220 and consecrated in 1258.

During the Middle Ages the bishops of Salisbury included notable scholars and statesmen. At the Reformation the diocese became part of the Church of England. The diocese's territory has changed over time: Dorset, previously in the diocese of Bristol, was added in the 19th century. The area bishops of Sherborne and Ramsbury serve as suffragans within the diocese.

Notable bishops

  • Osmund (1078–1099) — builder of Old Sarum Cathedral, compiler of the Sarum Use, canonised 1457.
  • Richard Poore (1217–1228) — founder of New Sarum and its cathedral.
  • Seth Ward (1667–1689) — astronomer and Savilian Professor at Oxford.
  • John Wordsworth (1885–1911) — scholar and founder of Salisbury Theological College.

In the 21st century the see was held by David Stancliffe (1993–2010), Nick Holtam (2011–2021), and Stephen Lake, who became bishop in 2022.
