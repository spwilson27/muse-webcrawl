Culture of Korea

The culture of Korea is the shared heritage of the Korean people, developed over thousands of years on the Korean Peninsula. Although the cultures of North Korea and South Korea have diverged since the division of Korea in 1945, both rest on common foundations: the Korean language and its Hangul script, Confucian social values, Buddhism and indigenous shamanic traditions, and distinctive arts, cuisine and dress.

Traditional arts

Korean traditional music includes court music (aak, hyangak) and folk forms such as pansori — an epic sung storytelling tradition inscribed by UNESCO — and nongak (farmers' dance). Dance ranges from court dances to the fan dance (buchaechum) and mask dances. Korean ceramics are renowned, from Goryeo-dynasty celadon to Joseon white porcelain (baekja). Calligraphy, ink painting and landscape painting flourished under Confucian and Buddhist patronage, and Korean architecture is exemplified by palaces such as Gyeongbokgung in Seoul and the temple of Bulguksa.

Language and literature

Korean is written in Hangul, the phonetic alphabet promulgated by King Sejong the Great in 1443 and explained in the Hunminjeongeum. Classical literature includes the Samguk Yusa (13th century), sijo poetry and Joseon-era novels; modern Korean literature and cinema have won international recognition, and Hangul Day is a public holiday in South Korea.

Social values and customs

Confucianism deeply shaped Korean society: respect for elders, filial piety, hierarchical speech levels in the language, and elaborate etiquette around bowing, dining and gift-giving. Major holidays are Seollal (Lunar New Year) and Chuseok (the autumn harvest festival), when families gather, honour ancestors in the charye rite and eat traditional foods such as tteokguk and songpyeon. The traditional dress, the hanbok, is worn on festive occasions.

Cuisine

Korean cuisine is centred on rice, soups and shared side dishes (banchan). Kimchi — fermented vegetables, usually napa cabbage with chilli — is the emblematic food; kimjang, the communal making of kimchi, is on UNESCO's heritage list. Other staples include bulgogi, bibimbap, doenjang (fermented soybean paste) and Korean barbecue.

Contemporary culture

Since the late 1990s the "Korean Wave" (Hallyu) has carried South Korean popular culture worldwide: K-pop groups such as BTS and Blackpink, television dramas, and films including Bong Joon-ho's Parasite (2019), the first non-English-language film to win the Academy Award for Best Picture. Taekwondo, developed in Korea, is an Olympic sport, and Korean popular culture now coexists with strong promotion of traditional heritage.
