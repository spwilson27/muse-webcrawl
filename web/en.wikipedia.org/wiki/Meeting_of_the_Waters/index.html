Meeting of the Waters

The Meeting of the Waters (Portuguese: Encontro das Águas) is the confluence between the dark Rio Negro and the pale sandy-coloured Amazon River (Solimões) near Manaus, Brazil. For more than 6 km (3.7 mi), the two rivers' waters run side by side without mixing.

Cause

The phenomenon results from differences in temperature, density and flow speed: the Rio Negro flows slowly at about 28 °C, its water darkened by humic acids from flooded forest, while the Solimões runs faster and cooler, laden with sediment from the Andes. Eventually turbulence blends the two into the unified Amazon.

Tourism and protection

The meeting is one of the principal tourist attractions of Manaus, visited by boat tours year-round, with the contrast sharpest in the rainy season. The site is protected as natural heritage of Amazonas state, a status invoked in disputes over port construction at the confluence.
