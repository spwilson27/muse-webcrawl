Staff sergeant

Staff sergeant is a rank of non-commissioned officer (NCO) used in the armed forces of many countries. It is generally senior to sergeant and junior to the senior NCO grades such as master sergeant, first sergeant or colour sergeant, though exact placement varies by service and nation.

United States

In the United States Army, a staff sergeant (E-6) is typically a squad leader responsible for a squad of nine to ten soldiers, or serves in staff positions at company and battalion level. Promotion requires completion of the Advanced Leader Course and selection by a centralised board or semi-centralised promotion process. In the United States Marine Corps, a staff sergeant (also E-6) is the first of the staff non-commissioned officer grades and commonly serves as a platoon sergeant. In the United States Air Force, staff sergeant is the E-5 grade — the first NCO rank — while in the Space Force the same title was used at E-5 before rank titles were revised.

United Kingdom and Commonwealth

In the British Army and Royal Marines, a staff sergeant ranks above sergeant and below warrant officer class 2. In infantry regiments the equivalent appointment is colour sergeant. Staff sergeants often fill senior technical, instructional or quartermaster roles, such as company quartermaster sergeant. Similar grades exist in the armies and police forces of Commonwealth countries including Australia, Canada, and Singapore, where staff sergeant is also a senior police rank.
