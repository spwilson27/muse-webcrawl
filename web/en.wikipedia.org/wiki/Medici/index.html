House of Medici

The House of Medici was an Italian banking family and political dynasty that first consolidated power in the Republic of Florence under Cosimo de' Medici during the first half of the 15th century. The family originated in the Mugello region of Tuscany and rose to wealth through the Medici Bank, which became the largest in Europe in its time.

Rise and rule

Giovanni di Bicci de' Medici founded the Medici Bank in 1397. His son Cosimo returned from exile in 1434 to dominate Florence informally for thirty years. Cosimo's grandson Lorenzo the Magnificent (r. 1469–1492) made Florence a centre of Renaissance art and learning, surviving the Pazzi conspiracy of 1478. The family was twice expelled (1494 and 1527) and twice restored, ruling as hereditary Dukes of Florence from 1532 and Grand Dukes of Tuscany from 1569.

Patronage

The Medici patronised Botticelli, Leonardo da Vinci and Michelangelo, financed Brunelleschi's dome for Florence Cathedral, and built the Uffizi and the Laurentian Library. Four Medici became popes, and two — Catherine and Marie — became queens of France. The main line ended with Gian Gastone in 1737, and the Grand Duchy passed to the House of Lorraine.
