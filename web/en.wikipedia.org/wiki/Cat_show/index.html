Cat show

A cat show is a competitive exhibition event in which owners present pedigree (and often household) cats to be judged against breed standards. Judges examine each cat for conformation, condition and temperament, awarding rosettes, titles and points toward championships under the rules of registries such as the CFA, TICA, FIFe and GCCF.

History

The first major cat show was organised by the artist and cat enthusiast Harrison Weir at London's Crystal Palace in July 1871, attracting some 160 exhibits and helping to launch the "cat fancy". Shows soon followed in the United States, with a notable exhibition at Madison Square Garden in 1895.

Judging

Cats are benched in decorated cages and brought in turn to judging rings, where a judge handles each animal and compares it with the written standard for its breed — specifying head shape, coat, colour, body and proportions. Competitions are divided by breed, sex, colour and status (kitten, adult, neuter/premier), and top cats compete for Best in Show. Household pet classes judge non-pedigree cats on condition, beauty and personality.
