Quercus

Quercus (oaks)

Family: Fagaceae
Species: c. 500
Fruit: acorn

Quercus is a genus of trees and shrubs in the beech family, Fagaceae, commonly known as oaks. The genus comprises roughly 500 species distributed across the Northern Hemisphere, from cool temperate regions to tropical Asia and the Americas.

Description

Oaks have spirally arranged leaves, often with lobed margins, and produce nuts called acorns borne in cup-shaped cupules. Species may be deciduous or evergreen. The genus is divided into subgenera including the red oaks, white oaks, and intermediate oaks of North America, and the holm and cork oaks of the Mediterranean.

Ecology and uses

Oaks support more wildlife than any other British trees and are keystone species in many forests. Oak timber is strong, durable, and valued for furniture, flooring, shipbuilding, and barrel-making — wine and spirits are aged in oak casks. Cork is harvested from the bark of the cork oak (Quercus suber). Acorns are an important food for deer, pigs, and birds.

Cultural significance

The oak symbolises strength and endurance in many cultures and is the national tree of numerous countries, including England, Germany, and the United States.
