Guinness

Guinness is an Irish dry stout that originated in the brewery of Arthur Guinness at St. James's Gate, Dublin, in 1759. Dark — nearly black — from roasted barley, with a creamy head enhanced by nitrogen, it is one of the world's best-known beer brands, owned by Diageo and sold in over 120 countries.

History

Arthur Guinness famously signed a 9,000-year lease at £45 per year. By 1886 Guinness was the world's largest brewer. The harp logo (1862) echoes Ireland's Brian Boru harp. Statistician William Gosset ('Student') developed the t-test at the brewery. The Guinness Book of Records began in 1955 to settle pub arguments.

The beer

Guinness Draught's two-part pour and widget (1988) reproduce the nitrogenated cascade. The Guinness Storehouse in Dublin is Ireland's most-visited tourist attraction. Advertising slogans include 'Guinness is good for you' and the toucan. See Stout and Arthur Guinness.
