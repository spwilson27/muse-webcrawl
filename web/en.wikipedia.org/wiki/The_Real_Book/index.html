The Real Book

The Real Book is a compilation of lead sheets for jazz standards, popular with musicians in the US and Europe. It was originally compiled and published for profit in the 1970s by two students at the Berklee College of Music in Boston, and sold illegally for many years; it is the best-known of the "fake books" — collections of melodies and chord changes that let a small group fake its way through hundreds of tunes. Legal editions have been published by Hal Leonard since 2004.

History

Earlier fake books, like the Tune-Dex cards and the volumes associated with George Goodwin, contained popular songs but often with simplified or incorrect harmonies. In the early 1970s, two Berklee students (their identities long kept anonymous; Barry Kernfeld's research attributes much of the work to students working with input from teachers) transcribed tunes as actually played by jazz musicians — including compositions by Chick Corea, Herbie Hancock, Wayne Shorter, and Steve Swallow, whose handwritten styles influenced the book's layout. The clear, hand-copied look of the original was designed by bassist Steve Swallow and became so iconic that music fonts imitate it. The bootleg Real Book, followed by volumes 2 and 3, circulated worldwide despite copyright infringement; some editions notoriously contain errors that generations of players have nonetheless learned as canonical.

Legal editions

In 2004 Hal Leonard published a licensed sixth edition, The Real Book: Sixth Edition, with most songs cleared, some removed for licensing reasons, and many errors corrected — marking the first legal version of the classic. Hal Leonard has since issued numerous genre volumes, and the original's influence persists in software such as iReal Pro, created to display chord charts in the Real Book style.
