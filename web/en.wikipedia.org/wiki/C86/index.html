C86 is a cassette compilation released by the British music weekly NME in 1986, featuring new bands from the United Kingdom's independent music scene. The tape took its name from the labelling conventions of blank media and followed earlier NME compilations such as C81. "C86" subsequently became shorthand for a whole movement of mid-1980s British indie music — jangly guitar pop, shambling amateurishness, and a self-conscious anti-rock-star ethos — and for the genre now generally called indie pop.

The tape

Readers obtained the cassette by mail-order coupon from the paper. Its 22 tracks included Primal Scream ("Velocity Girl"), The Pastels, The Soup Dragons, The Wedding Present, The Shop Assistants, The Mighty Lemon Drops, The Wolfhounds, McCarthy, Half Man Half Biscuit, and the track "Beatnik Girl" by The Railway Children among others; the Jesus and Mary Chain, notably, declined to appear. The selection championed a network of small labels — Creation, Sarah Records' predecessors, el, Ron Johnson, and the Subway Organisation — and of fanzines and provincial scenes.

The "C86 sound" and its critics

Journalists attached the "C86" label, often pejoratively, to bands sharing a jangly, Byrds-influenced guitar sound, naive or whimsical lyrics, and deliberate amateurism — sometimes derided as "shambling" or "anorak pop," after the fans' characteristic dress. Some featured bands rejected the tag, and the tape itself is more varied than the stereotype, including noise, scratchy funk, and industrial-tinged acts. Nevertheless, C86 became a foundational document for British indie: its descendants include the twee pop of Sarah Records, the shoegazing and Britpop scenes, and international indie pop revivals.

Legacy

The compilation was reissued on CD in 2006 for its 20th anniversary, and in 2014 Cherry Red Records released an expanded three-CD edition with 50 additional tracks. Retrospective concerts and documentaries have cemented C86's status as a landmark of independent music culture.
