Solar Hijri calendar

Solar Hijri calendar
Also called: Jalali calendar; Shamsi calendar
Epoch: Hijrah, 622 CE
In use: Iran and Afghanistan
Year begins: March equinox (Nowruz)

The Solar Hijri calendar (Persian: گاه‌شماری هجری خورشیدی), also called the Shamsi calendar or Persian calendar, is the official calendar of Iran and is also used in Afghanistan. It is a solar calendar whose epoch is the Hijrah of Muhammad in 622 CE, like the lunar Islamic calendar, but whose years are counted in true solar years beginning at the March equinox. It descends from the Jalali calendar devised in the 11th century by a commission of astronomers including Omar Khayyam under Sultan Jalal al-Din Malik Shah.

Structure

The year has twelve months. The first six months have 31 days, the next five have 30 days, and the last month, Esfand, has 29 days in ordinary years and 30 in leap years. The months are named for Zodiac signs in Iran (Farvardin, Ordibehesht, Khordad, Tir, Mordad, Shahrivar, Mehr, Aban, Azar, Dey, Bahman, Esfand). The new year, Nowruz, falls on the day of the vernal equinox as determined by astronomical observation at Tehran.

Leap years

Because the new year is fixed by the observed equinox, the calendar stays aligned with the seasons with great precision. Intercalation follows complicated patterns (often described by 33-year cycles containing eight leap years), making the Solar Hijri calendar one of the most accurate solar calendars, with an error of roughly one day in several hundred thousand years by common estimates.

History

Iran officially adopted the Solar Hijri calendar in 1925 under Reza Shah Pahlavi, replacing the lunar Hijri calendar for civil purposes. Afghanistan adopted it in 1922. The year count is about 621–622 less than the Gregorian year: Solar Hijri year 1403 began on 20 March 2024 CE.
