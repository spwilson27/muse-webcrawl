Puerto Ricans in New York City


The annual Puerto Rican Day Parade on Fifth Avenue, Manhattan.

Puerto Ricans in New York City include recent migrants and people born in the city of Puerto Rican descent. New York City has the largest Puerto Rican population of any city in the world, and New York is the largest metropolitan area for Puerto Ricans outside of the island itself.

History

Puerto Ricans have lived in New York since the 19th century, when the island was still a Spanish colony and exiles and merchants settled in the city. Migration expanded after the Jones–Shafroth Act of 1917 granted Puerto Ricans U.S. citizenship, and after World War II it became a mass movement: cheap air travel and the demand for labor brought hundreds of thousands of Puerto Ricans to New York between the 1940s and 1960s, a period known as the "Great Migration" (La Gran Migración). Many settled in East Harlem ("Spanish Harlem" or El Barrio), the South Bronx, the Lower East Side (Loisaida), Williamsburg and Bushwick in Brooklyn.

Culture

The New York Puerto Rican community—whose younger, city-born members came to be called Nuyoricans—made lasting contributions to American culture. Salsa music developed in New York in the 1960s and 1970s from Cuban and Puerto Rican musical forms, with artists promoted by Fania Records. The Nuyorican literary movement, centered on the Nuyorican Poets Cafe founded in 1973, produced writers such as Pedro Pietri and Miguel Piñero. The community also played a role in the birth of hip hop in the Bronx and in forms of Latin jazz and boogaloo.

Puerto Rican Day Parade

The Puerto Rican Day Parade has been held on Fifth Avenue in Manhattan since 1958 and is one of the largest parades in the United States, drawing millions of spectators and celebrating Puerto Rican heritage and pride.

Demographics

Puerto Ricans numbered roughly 700,000–800,000 in New York City in recent decades, though the population declined from its mid-20th-century peak as many families moved to other states, particularly Florida, or returned to the island. Significant communities remain in the Bronx, Brooklyn, Manhattan, and Queens.
