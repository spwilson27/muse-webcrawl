Herzog & de Meuron

Herzog & de Meuron Basel Ltd. is a Swiss architecture firm headquartered in Basel, founded in 1978 by Jacques Herzog and Pierre de Meuron. Both architects studied at ETH Zurich. The firm is known for its inventive use of materials and for major cultural and sports buildings worldwide, and won the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2001.

Notable works

  • Tate Modern, London (2000; Switch House extension 2016) — conversion of Bankside Power Station
  • Allianz Arena, Munich (2005), with its colour-changing inflated panels
  • Beijing National Stadium (“Bird's Nest”) for the 2008 Olympics, with Ai Weiwei as artistic consultant
  • Elbphilharmonie, Hamburg (2016)
  • 1111 Lincoln Road, Miami Beach; VitraHaus, Weil am Rhein; M+ museum, Hong Kong (2021)

Approach

Herzog described their work as exploring materials and surfaces without a predetermined style. Projects range from the minimalist Ricola storage building to ornate facades printed, perforated or woven.
