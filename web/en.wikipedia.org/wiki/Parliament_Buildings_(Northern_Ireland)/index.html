Parliament Buildings

Parliament Buildings (Stormont)
Location: Stormont Estate, Belfast, Northern Ireland
Architect: Arnold Thornely
Style: Greek Classical / Neoclassical
Opened: 16 November 1932
Occupant: Northern Ireland Assembly

The Parliament Buildings, known as Stormont because of their location in the Stormont area of Belfast, are the seat of the Northern Ireland Assembly and were formerly the seat of the Parliament and Government of Northern Ireland. Designed by Sir Arnold Thornely in a Greek Classical style and built of Portland stone, the building was officially opened by Edward, Prince of Wales, on 16 November 1932.

Architecture and grounds

The main building, some 365 feet (111 m) long — symbolising one foot for each day of the year — is approached along the mile-long Prince of Wales Avenue, lined with lime trees and dominated by a statue of Lord Carson. The Great Hall contains a great chandelier presented by the Kaiser and later re-gifted; the Senate and Commons chambers are richly panelled. Two Churchill-designed wartime extensions and various office blocks, including Stormont Castle (the seat of the Northern Ireland Executive), stand in the grounds.

History

After the Government of Ireland Act 1920 created Northern Ireland, its new parliament first met at Belfast City Hall and the Presbyterian College before moving to the purpose-built Stormont in 1932. During the Second World War the building was camouflaged with paint and manure to protect it from bombing. When the Northern Ireland Parliament was prorogued and then abolished in 1972–73, the building housed government departments. Following the Good Friday Agreement of 1998, it became the home of the new devolved Northern Ireland Assembly, first sitting there in 1998 (in shadow form) and fully from 1999, apart from periods of suspension.
