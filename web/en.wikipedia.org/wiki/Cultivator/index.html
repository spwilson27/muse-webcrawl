Cultivator


A tractor-drawn field cultivator with spring-loaded tines

A cultivator (also known as a tiller or rotavator in some forms) is a piece of agricultural equipment used for secondary tillage. One sense of the name refers to frames with teeth (also called shanks) that pierce the soil as they are dragged through it linearly. Another sense refers to machines that use rotating discs or tines to accomplish a similar result, such as a rotary tiller.

Cultivators stir and pulverise the soil, either before planting (to prepare a smooth, loose seedbed) or after the crop has begun growing (to kill weeds near the crop plants). Unlike a harrow, which disturbs the entire surface of the soil, cultivators are designed to disturb the soil in careful patterns, sparing the crop plants but disrupting the weeds.

Types

  • Field cultivator — a tractor-drawn frame with rows of spring or rigid tines used for seedbed preparation and stubble management.
  • Row crop cultivator — fitted with shovels or sweeps spaced to pass between rows of growing maize, soybeans or cotton, uprooting weeds and aerating the soil.
  • Rotary tiller (rotavator) — powered rotating tines driven by a tractor PTO or its own engine; common in gardening and horticulture.
  • Hand cultivator — a small hand tool with prongs for loosening soil and weeding in gardens.

History

Horse-drawn cultivators with iron shovels spread through the nineteenth century, greatly reducing the hand-hoeing of row crops. With tractor mechanisation in the twentieth century came wide multi-row cultivators and powered rotary tillers. In modern industrial agriculture, herbicides displaced much mechanical weed control, but cultivators remain central to organic farming and to seedbed preparation.
