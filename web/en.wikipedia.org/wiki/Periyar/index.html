Periyar

E. V. Ramasamy "Periyar"
Born: 17 September 1879, Erode, Madras Presidency
Died: 24 December 1973 (aged 94)
Known for: Self-Respect Movement, Dravidian movement

Erode Venkatappa Ramasamy (17 September 1879 – 24 December 1973), commonly known as Periyar (meaning "respected one" or "elder" in Tamil), was an Indian social activist and politician who started the Self-Respect Movement and the Dravidar Kazhagam. He is known as the "Father of the Dravidian movement" and rebelled against Brahminical dominance and gender and caste inequality in Tamil Nadu.

Early life and Congress years

Born into a wealthy family in Erode, Ramasamy left school early and worked in his father's business before becoming a municipal chairman. He joined the Indian National Congress in 1919, participated in the non-cooperation movement and the Vaikom Satyagraha against untouchability, and was imprisoned several times. He resigned from Congress in 1925, coming to see it as dominated by Brahmin interests.

Self-Respect Movement

Periyar founded the Self-Respect Movement to promote rationalism, self-respect marriages conducted without priests, women's rights and the abolition of caste. His journal Kudi Arasu spread his views. He later led the Justice Party, renamed the Dravidar Kazhagam in 1944, and campaigned against the imposition of Hindi and for a separate Dravida Nadu for a period. His atheism and sharp criticism of religion and superstition made him a controversial but enduring influence on Tamil politics; the Dravidian parties that dominate the state trace their lineage to his movement.
