Chebyshev polynomials

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Plot of the first few Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind on the interval [-1, 1]]

The Chebyshev polynomials are two sequences of orthogonal polynomials, named after Pafnuty Chebyshev, that arise in approximation theory and are closely related to the cosine function. They are usually distinguished as polynomials of the first kind, denoted Tn, and of the second kind, denoted Un.

Definition

The Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind are defined by the relation

Tn(cos θ) = cos(nθ)

so that T0(x) = 1, T1(x) = x, T2(x) = 2x² − 1, T3(x) = 4x³ − 3x, and so on. They satisfy the recurrence

Tn+1(x) = 2x Tn(x) − Tn−1(x)

The polynomials of the second kind are defined by Un(cos θ) = sin((n+1)θ) / sin θ, and obey the same recurrence with U0(x) = 1 and U1(x) = 2x.

Properties and applications

Chebyshev polynomials are orthogonal on the interval [−1, 1] with respect to the weight 1/√(1−x²) (first kind). They are famous for the minimax property: among all monic polynomials of degree n, 21−nTn deviates least from zero on [−1, 1], which makes them fundamental in polynomial approximation and interpolation — sampling a function at Chebyshev nodes suppresses the Runge phenomenon. Chebyshev filters in signal processing are named after the equiripple behaviour of these polynomials, and they also appear in numerical methods such as Chebyshev iteration and spectral methods for differential equations.
