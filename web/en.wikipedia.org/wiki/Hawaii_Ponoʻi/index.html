Hawaiʻi Ponoʻī

"Hawaiʻi Ponoʻī" is the state song of Hawaii and was previously the national anthem of the Hawaiian Kingdom. Its lyrics were written in 1874 by King David Kalākaua and its music composed by Captain Henri Berger, then royal bandmaster. The title means "Hawaii's own" or "Hawaii's righteous [people]."

History

The song was first performed publicly on Kalākaua's birthday in November 1874 by the Royal Hawaiian Band. Its melody was influenced by hymns and by the Prussian hymn "Heil dir im Siegerkranz," which Berger knew well. After the overthrow of the monarchy and annexation by the United States, the song retained ceremonial prominence, and in 1967 the Hawaii State Legislature made it the official state song.

Lyrics and use

Sung in Hawaiian, the anthem praises the land and its chiefs and calls on Hawaiians to be loyal and united. It is performed at official events, sporting occasions, and school assemblies, and opens and closes many formal gatherings in the islands.
