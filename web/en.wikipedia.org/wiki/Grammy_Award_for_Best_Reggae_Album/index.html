Grammy Award for Best Reggae Album

Grammy Award for Best Reggae Album
Presented byNational Academy of Recording Arts and Sciences (Recording Academy)
First awarded1985
OriginallyBest Reggae Recording

The Grammy Award for Best Reggae Album is an honour presented at the Grammy Awards, established to recognise quality albums in the reggae music genre. It was first awarded in 1985 as the Grammy Award for Best Reggae Recording, and the name was changed to Best Reggae Album in 1992. The award usually goes to the artist, and sometimes also to the producer and engineer/mixer.

History

The inaugural winner was Black Uhuru for Anthem. Members of the Marley family have dominated the category: Ziggy Marley has won multiple times both solo and with the Melody Makers, and Damian Marley and Stephen Marley have each won several times; their father Bob Marley's genre-defining career predated the award. Other multiple winners include Shabba Ranks, Buju Banton and Bunny Wailer. In 2022 the award went to the American band SOJA for Beauty in the Silence, a result that drew debate in Jamaica about the state of the domestic reggae industry. Koffee became the youngest and first female solo winner in 2020 with the EP Rapture. Kabaka Pyramid won in 2023 for The Kalling.
