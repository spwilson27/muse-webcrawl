Charles Borromeo (Italian: Carlo Borromeo; 2 October 1538 – 3 November 1584) was an Italian Catholic prelate who served as Archbishop of Milan from 1564 until his death. A cardinal and nephew of Pope Pius IV, he was a leading figure of the Counter-Reformation, working to implement the decrees of the Council of Trent, reform clerical discipline, and care for the poor. He was canonised a saint in 1610.

Early life

Borromeo was born at the castle of Arona on Lake Maggiore into the powerful Borromeo family, counts of Arona. He studied canon and civil law at the University of Pavia, earning a doctorate in 1559. When his uncle Giovanni Angelo Medici became Pope Pius IV, Charles was summoned to Rome, made a cardinal-deacon at only 21, and appointed administrator of the Archdiocese of Milan and effectively papal secretary of state.

Reform in Milan

After the Council of Trent closed, Borromeo took up residence in Milan in 1565 — the first resident archbishop in decades — and set about a comprehensive reform. He held provincial councils and diocesan synods, founded seminaries for the training of priests (a Tridentine requirement), established the Confraternity of Christian Doctrine for lay instruction, and enforced discipline on clergy and religious orders, sometimes against fierce resistance; in 1569 a disaffected friar attempted to assassinate him.

Plague of 1576–1578

Borromeo's reputation for sanctity rests heavily on his conduct during the famine and plague that struck Milan in 1576–1578 (the "Plague of Saint Charles"). While civil authorities fled, he remained in the city, organised relief, fed thousands daily at his own expense, sold church valuables to buy food, and ministered personally to the sick, organising processions of penance.

Death and legacy

Worn out by austerities and overwork, he died in Milan on 3 November 1584, aged 46. His writings, especially the Acta Ecclesiae Mediolanensis, became a model for episcopal governance across Catholic Europe. The Borromean rings and the motto Humilitas remain associated with his family, and he is the namesake of seminaries and churches worldwide.
