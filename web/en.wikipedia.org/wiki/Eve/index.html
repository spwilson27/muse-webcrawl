Eve (Hebrew: חַוָּה, Ḥawwā; Greek: Εὖα) is a figure in the Book of Genesis in the Hebrew Bible. According to the creation myth of the Abrahamic religions, she was the first woman, created by God from a rib taken from Adam, to be Adam's companion. Adam and Eve are the Bible's first man and first woman.

In the Genesis creation narrative in chapters 2 and 3, Eve is created in the Garden of Eden. The serpent tempts her to eat the forbidden fruit from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, and she shares it with Adam. As a consequence, both are expelled from Eden — an event known as the Fall of Man.

Etymology

The name Eve is traditionally connected with the Hebrew root meaning "living" or "source of life": Genesis 3:20 states that Adam named her Eve "because she was the mother of all living".

In Islam

In Islam, Eve (Arabic: حواء, Ḥawwāʾ) is honoured as the wife of Adam and the mother of humanity, though she is not blamed for the Fall in the same way; in the Qur'an both Adam and Eve share responsibility for eating from the forbidden tree, and both are forgiven.

Interpretations

Eve has been the subject of extensive theological, literary and artistic interpretation. Traditional readings have often held her primarily responsible for the Fall, a view that feminist theologians and later scholars have critically re-examined. She appears throughout Western art, frequently depicted in Eden with Adam and the serpent.
