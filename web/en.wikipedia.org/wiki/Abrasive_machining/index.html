Abrasive machining

Abrasive machining is a machining process where material is removed from a workpiece using a multitude of small abrasive particles. Common examples include grinding, honing, and lapping. Abrasive machining is usually used in finishing operations because it can produce parts with high precision, tight tolerances and very smooth surface finishes.

Processes

In grinding, abrasive grains are bonded into a wheel that rotates at high speed against the workpiece. In honing, a rotating tool carrying abrasive stones is moved back and forth inside a bore to improve its geometry and surface texture, for example in engine cylinders. Lapping uses loose abrasive grains suspended in a fluid between the workpiece and a lap plate to achieve extremely flat, fine finishes. Other processes include abrasive flow machining, waterjet cutting and ultrasonic machining.

Abrasives

Common abrasive materials include aluminium oxide, silicon carbide, cubic boron nitride and synthetic diamond. The choice depends on the hardness and toughness of the workpiece material: diamond is used for carbides and ceramics, while aluminium oxide suits steels.

Mechanics

Each abrasive grain acts as a tiny cutting edge. Material removal occurs through a combination of cutting, ploughing and rubbing, depending on the grain geometry and depth of engagement. Because many grains engage simultaneously, specific energies are high but the resulting finish is fine and dimensional accuracy good.
