Degrassi (franchise)

Degrassi is a Canadian teen drama television franchise created by Linda Schuyler and Kit Hood. Beginning with The Kids of Degrassi Street in 1979, the franchise comprises five main series set around the fictional Degrassi Community School in Toronto, and is noted for tackling serious social issues with teenage characters played by age-appropriate actors. It is one of the longest-running and most recognisable franchises in Canadian television history.

Overview

The original series grew out of short films made by Schuyler, a former teacher, and Hood through their company Playing With Time. Degrassi Junior High and Degrassi High, produced by Epitome Pictures, gained an international following for storylines dealing with pregnancy, HIV, suicide, abuse and sexuality, and ended with the television film School's Out! in 1992. The franchise was revived in 2001 with Degrassi: The Next Generation, which ran for 14 seasons and launched the careers of performers including Drake and Nina Dobrev. Next Class continued the franchise on Netflix and Family Channel.

A further revival for HBO Max was announced in 2022 but was cancelled later that year before production. The franchise has won numerous awards, including International Emmy Awards and Peabody recognition for its issue-based storytelling.
