Entertainment

Entertainment is a form of activity that holds the attention and interest of an audience or gives pleasure and delight. It can be an idea or a task, but is more likely to be one of the activities or events that have developed over thousands of years specifically for the purpose of keeping an audience's attention. Although people's attention is held by different things — because individuals have different preferences — most forms of entertainment are recognisable and familiar.

Forms

Storytelling, music, drama, dance and different kinds of performance exist in all cultures, were supported in royal courts, and developed into sophisticated forms over time. Other forms include film, television, radio, video games, sport, comedy, circuses, magic shows, games and reading. Entertainment is commonly distinguished from education and from the fine arts, though the boundaries are contested and much entertainment also informs or achieves artistic merit.

Psychology and social function

Entertainment serves psychological needs for relaxation, arousal regulation and mood management, and social functions including shared identity, ritual and the reinforcement or subversion of cultural norms. Scholars have theorised entertainment through concepts such as play, catharsis, escapism and flow, and through audience-reception ideas such as the "uses and gratifications" approach, which treats audiences as actively selecting media to satisfy needs.

Industry

The production and distribution of entertainment is a major global industry spanning film studios, broadcasters, streaming platforms, music companies, publishers, sports leagues and games developers. Technological shifts — from printing and recorded sound to broadcasting, cable, home video and the internet — have repeatedly transformed both the forms of entertainment and its business models, and made entertainment a significant driver of global culture and trade.

History

The history of entertainment runs from prehistoric ritual, music and storytelling through theatre in ancient Greece, Roman spectacles and medieval minstrelsy and mystery plays, to the commercial theatre, music hall and cinema of the industrial era and today's digital media. Each era combined inherited forms with new technology, while older forms continued alongside the new.
