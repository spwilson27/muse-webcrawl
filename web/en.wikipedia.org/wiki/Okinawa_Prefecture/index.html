Okinawa Prefecture (Japanese: 沖縄県, Okinawan: Uchinaa) is the southernmost and westernmost prefecture of Japan. It consists of over 160 islands in the Ryukyu chain stretching some 1,000 kilometres between Kyushu and Taiwan, of which 49 are inhabited. The capital and largest city is Naha, on Okinawa Island. The prefecture has a distinct history, culture and languages derived from the former Ryukyu Kingdom, noted for longevity, karate, and a subtropical climate with rich coral-reef ecosystems.

History

The Ryukyu Kingdom, unified in the 15th century, prospered as a maritime trading state between China, Japan and Southeast Asia before being annexed by Meiji Japan in 1879. In 1945 Okinawa was the scene of one of the bloodiest battles of the Pacific War, in which a large share of the civilian population died. The islands remained under United States administration until 1972, when they reverted to Japanese sovereignty. US military bases remain concentrated in Okinawa — a persistent political issue — occupying a significant share of the main island's land area while the prefecture hosts the majority of US forces in Japan.

Geography and culture

Okinawa has a humid subtropical climate, typhoon exposure, and renowned beaches and dive sites; the Yanbaru forest of northern Okinawa Island and Iriomote Island are UNESCO World Heritage sites. Ryukyuan languages, though endangered, survive alongside Japanese. Okinawan culture gave the world karate and kobudō, distinctive music with the three-string sanshin, and a cuisine based on pork, sweet potato, goya (bitter melon) and awamori spirits — factors often linked to the islanders' exceptional longevity.

Economy

Tourism is the leading industry, drawing millions of visitors annually, alongside agriculture (sugarcane, pineapple), fisheries, construction and public spending connected with the bases and central-government subsidies.
