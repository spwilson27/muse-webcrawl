Axe murder incident

Panmunjom axe murders

The axe murder incident (Korean: 판문점 도끼 살인 사건) was the killing of two United States Army officers by North Korean soldiers on 18 August 1976 in the Joint Security Area (JSA) at Panmunjom, in the Korean Demilitarized Zone. The officers, Captain Arthur Bonifas and First Lieutenant Mark Barrett, had been part of a work party trimming a poplar tree that obstructed the view between UN observation posts, when North Korean troops attacked them with axes and clubs.

Background and attack

The tree stood near the Bridge of No Return. An earlier attempt to prune it had been halted by North Korean guards claiming it had been planted by Kim Il Sung. On 18 August a UN work party returned; after work began, a North Korean officer ordered it stopped and, when the order was ignored, North Korean soldiers attacked the party, killing Bonifas and Barrett and injuring several others. The incident caused an international crisis, with US forces in Korea raised to DEFCON 3.

Operation Paul Bunyan

Three days later the United States and South Korea mounted Operation Paul Bunyan: a large show of force—including hundreds of troops, helicopters, armoured vehicles and B-52 bombers circling nearby—under cover of which engineers cut down the tree with chainsaws in 42 minutes. North Korea subsequently expressed regret for the killings, the first such statement since the armistice, and the crisis subsided. The tree stump was left standing and later replaced by a monument to the fallen officers.
