Mac OS 9

Mac OS 9 is the ninth and final major release of the classic Mac OS, the operating system for Apple Computer's Macintosh computers. Released on October 23, 1999, it was promoted by Apple as the "Best Internet OS Ever", with features such as the Sherlock 2 search software and integration with Apple's suite of online services known as iTools.

Features

Mac OS 9 introduced improved multi-user support, allowing several user accounts on one machine, software update automation, and a redesigned Sound control panel. It retained the Platinum appearance introduced in Mac OS 8 and ran on PowerPC-based Macs.

Transition to Mac OS X

Although marketed as a major advance, Mac OS 9 was the last release of the classic Mac OS line. It was succeeded by Mac OS X in 2001, and Apple continued to support it through the Classic Environment, which allowed older applications to run under the new system until the transition to Intel processors ended that support.

System requirements

Mac OS 9 required a Macintosh with a PowerPC G3 or G4 processor in later updates, though the original release supported a broader range of PowerPC hardware. Version 9.2.2, released in December 2001, was the final update.
