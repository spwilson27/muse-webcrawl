Disney Princess, also called the Princess Line, is a media franchise and toy line owned by the Walt Disney Company. Created by Disney Consumer Products chairman Andy Mooney in 2000, it brings together the heroines of Disney's animated fairy-tale films in a single brand for merchandise, even though the characters never appear together in their original films.

History

Mooney conceived the franchise after noticing young girls at a Disney on Ice show wearing homemade princess costumes; within a few years the line grew into a multi-billion-dollar business spanning dolls, dress-up clothing, home goods and publishing — achieved, famously, with almost no advertising. Members are inducted in coronation ceremonies at the Disney parks: Rapunzel in 2011, Merida in 2013, Moana in 2019 and Raya in 2023, bringing the official roster to thirteen. Tinker Bell was briefly included before being moved to head the separate Disney Fairies franchise, much of it produced by DisneyToon Studios.

Characters and criteria

Members are generally the female leads of Walt Disney Animation Studios films, of royal birth, married into royalty, or performing a heroic act of comparable standing — Mulan qualifies by the last route. Anna and Elsa of Frozen are excluded in practice because their own franchise is managed and merchandised separately. The villains who oppose them are marketed in the parallel Disney Villains line.

Criticism

The franchise has been both commercially celebrated and criticised: scholars and parents have debated its emphasis on beauty, romance and passive heroines, and the racial and cultural representation of its non-European members. Studies, such as a widely reported 2016 Brigham Young University study, have associated heavy engagement with princess culture with more gender-stereotypical behaviour in young children, while Disney has responded by emphasising the heroines' courage and independence in campaigns such as "Dream Big, Princess".
