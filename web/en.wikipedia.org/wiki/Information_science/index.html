Information science

Information science is an interdisciplinary field concerned with the analysis, collection, classification, storage, retrieval, movement, and dissemination of information. It studies how people interact with information and technology, and it draws on library science, computer science, cognitive science, communication, and the social sciences.

History

The roots of information science lie in documentation: in the late 19th and early 20th centuries Paul Otlet and Henri La Fontaine developed the Universal Decimal Classification and envisioned universal bibliographic control. After the Second World War, Vannevar Bush's 1945 essay "As We May Think" imagined the Memex, a personal information-retrieval device. The term "information science" came into use in the 1950s and 1960s as computer-based retrieval systems and the American Documentation Institute (later ASIS&T) grew; academic units known as "iSchools" now teach the field in many countries.

Topics

Major areas include information retrieval (search engines and ranking), human–computer and human–information interaction, bibliometrics and scientometrics, metadata and knowledge organisation, digital libraries and archives, data curation, and the social and ethical dimensions of information systems.

Relation to other fields

Information science overlaps with library science — historically its closest relative — but is broader, covering information in all media and contexts, including scientific communication and organisational knowledge management.
