Quantity surveyor

A quantity surveyor (QS) is a professional working within the construction industry concerned with construction costs and contracts. Quantity surveyors manage the financial and contractual aspects of building and civil engineering projects from inception to completion.

Duties

Traditional duties include preparing feasibility studies and cost estimates, producing bills of quantities and tender documentation, evaluating tenders, valuing work in progress for interim payments, managing variations and change orders, and agreeing the final account with contractors. Modern QS practice also covers cost planning, value engineering, risk management, procurement advice, and dispute resolution.

Professional bodies

The principal professional body is the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), founded in 1868, whose members may use the designation MRICS or FRICS. Other bodies include the Chartered Institute of Building (CIOB) and, in Australia, the Australian Institute of Quantity Surveyors (AIQS).

Education

Quantity surveyors typically hold an accredited degree in quantity surveying or a related subject, followed by a period of structured training and assessment of professional competence. The role is sometimes summarised as that of the construction industry's accountant, combining knowledge of construction technology, contract law, and economics.
