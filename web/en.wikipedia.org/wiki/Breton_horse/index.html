Breton horse

Country of originFrance (Brittany)
UseDraught work, meat, leisure
Height1.52–1.63 m
ColourChestnut, bay, roan

The Breton horse is a breed of draught horse developed in Brittany, in north-western France. It is a compact, powerful and hardy horse, traditionally used for agricultural work, pulling artillery, and in more recent times for meat production and leisure driving.

History

Draught horses have been bred in Brittany for centuries. Local mares were crossed with heavier breeds such as the Percheron and the Ardennes to produce a strong but active draught animal suited to the small farms and poor soils of the region. Three types were historically recognised: the Trait Breton (heavy draught), the Postier Breton (lighter, influenced by the Norfolk Trotter), and the Corlay type.

Characteristics

The Breton is a medium-sized draught horse with a well-proportioned head, short strong neck, deep chest, powerful hindquarters and feathered legs. The most common colour is chestnut, often with flaxen mane and tail, although bay and roan also occur. The breed is known for its docility, endurance and an energetic, free trot unusual in heavy horses.

Uses

With mechanisation, numbers declined and the breed was increasingly bred for meat. Today the Breton is also used for leisure driving, logging in forests where vehicles cannot go, and for crossing with other breeds. It is exported to several countries, notably Italy and Japan, and has contributed to breeds such as the Hispano-Bretón in Spain.
