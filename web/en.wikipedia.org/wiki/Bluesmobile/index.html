The Bluesmobile is a fictional automobile featured in the 1980 musical comedy film The Blues Brothers and its sequel Blues Brothers 2000. In the original film it is a 1974 Dodge Monaco sedan, formerly a Mount Prospect, Illinois police car, purchased by Elwood Blues at auction to replace the Cadillac his brother Jake had traded for a microphone.

In the film

Elwood introduces the car with the famous description: "It's got a cop motor, a 440-cubic-inch plant. It's got cop tires, cop suspension, cop shocks. It's a model made before catalytic converters so it'll run good on regular gas." The Bluesmobile survives (and performs) implausible stunts throughout the film's record-setting chase sequences, including jumping an opening drawbridge and driving through a shopping mall, before literally falling apart when the brothers reach the Cook County Assessor's office — the joke being that the car had been held together by the brothers' "mission from God".

Production

The production used thirteen different 1974 Dodge Monacos and numerous other vehicles; the film set a then-record for cars destroyed in a single picture, with over 100 wrecked across production. Cars were prepared by mechanics working around the clock during the Chicago shoot, and several Monacos were modified for specific stunts, including one engineered to disintegrate on cue in the finale. For Blues Brothers 2000, the Bluesmobile was a 1990 Ford LTD Crown Victoria, also a former police car.

Legacy

The Bluesmobile is one of the most recognisable film cars; replicas are common at car shows and Blues Brothers tribute events, usually complete with the giant loudspeaker the brothers used to promote their concert (itself mounted on the roof in the film). The original surviving cars are held by collectors and museums.
