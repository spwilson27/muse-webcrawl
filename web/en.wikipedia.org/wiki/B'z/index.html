B'z (pronounced "B's") is a Japanese rock duo consisting of guitarist and producer Tak Matsumoto and vocalist and lyricist Koshi Inaba, formed in 1988. They are the best-selling music act in Japanese history, with sales exceeding 82 million records in Japan alone, and hold the record for the most consecutive number-one singles on the Oricon chart — every single since 1990's "Taiyō no Komachi Angel" has debuted at number one, a streak of more than 50 singles.

Career

The duo debuted in September 1988 with the album B'z and single "Dakara Sono Te wo Hanashite". Their early dance-rock sound gave way to hard rock on albums such as Risky (1990), In the Life (1991), Run (1992) and Loose (1995), which includes the multimillion-selling single "Love Me, I Love You". Signature songs include "Ultra Soul" (2001), used as a World Swimming Championships theme, and "Ichibu to Zenbu" (2009).

In 2007 B'z became the first Asian act inducted into Hollywood's RockWalk, placing their handprints on Sunset Boulevard. Matsumoto won a Grammy Award in 2011 for his collaboration with Larry Carlton, Take Your Pick. The pair also maintain extensive solo careers, and their stadium tours remain among Japan's biggest live draws.
