Musta'li Ismailism

Musta'li Ismailism (Arabic: المستعلية, romanized: al-Mustaʿliyya) is a branch of Isma'ilism that broke off from the Fatimid Caliphate following a dispute over succession in 1094. The Musta'lis recognized al-Musta'li Billah, the younger son of the Fatimid caliph al-Mustansir Billah, as the rightful imam-caliph, while the rival Nizari branch upheld the claim of his elder brother, Nizar.

History

When the Fatimid caliph-imam al-Mustansir died in 1094, the powerful vizier al-Afdal Shahanshah installed the younger son, al-Musta'li, on the throne in Cairo. Supporters of the elder son Nizar rejected the succession, giving rise to the Nizari–Musta'li schism, the last major split within Ismailism.

Under al-Musta'li's successor al-Amir (r. 1101–1130), Musta'li Ismailism remained the state doctrine of the Fatimid Caliphate. When al-Amir was assassinated in 1130, his infant son al-Tayyib Abu'l-Qasim disappeared. His followers — the Tayyibi Musta'lis — held that al-Tayyib had entered occultation and became the hidden imam, in whose absence the community would be led by a succession of deputies known as the Da'i al-Mutlaq ("absolute missionary"). Another faction, the Hafizi Musta'lis, recognized al-Amir's cousin al-Hafiz as imam, but Hafizism died out after the fall of the Fatimid dynasty in 1171.

Tayyibi tradition and the Bohras

The Tayyibi da'wa was established in Yemen under Queen Arwa al-Sulayhi, who appointed al-Dhu'ayb ibn Musa as the first Da'i al-Mutlaq. From Yemen the mission spread to Gujarat in western India, where it won many converts among Hindu trading castes. In the 16th century the headquarters of the da'wa moved to India. Subsequent disputes over the succession of the Da'i produced the principal Musta'li communities of today:

  • Dawoodi Bohras — the largest group, led by the Da'i al-Mutlaq based in Mumbai.
  • Sulaymani Bohras — centred in Yemen and Najran, following a line of Yemeni da'is.
  • Alavi Bohras — based in Vadodara, Gujarat.
  • Hebtiahs Bohras — a small community in Maharashtra and Gujarat.

Beliefs and practices

Musta'lis share the core Ismaili doctrines of the imamate and the seven pillars of Islam as enumerated in Ismaili jurisprudence, including walaya (devotion to the imam), tahara (purity), prayer, almsgiving, fasting, pilgrimage, and jihad. Tayyibi theology, codified by scholars such as Idris Imad al-Din, preserves a rich esoteric literature in Arabic, much of it kept within the community.
