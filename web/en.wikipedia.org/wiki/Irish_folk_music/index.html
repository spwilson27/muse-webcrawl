Irish folk music

Irish folk music (also known as Irish traditional music, or Irish trad) is a genre of folk music that developed in Ireland. It encompasses instrumental dance tunes — jigs, reels, hornpipes and polkas — as well as ballads and songs in both Irish and English.

Instruments

Common instruments include the fiddle, tin whistle, Irish flute, uilleann pipes, concertina, button accordion, bodhrán (frame drum), harp, banjo, mandolin and guitar. Much of the repertoire is played in unison at informal gatherings known as sessions, usually held in pubs.

History

The tradition descends in part from the music of itinerant harpers such as Turlough O'Carolan (1670–1738). Dancing masters spread tunes around the country in the 18th and 19th centuries, and mass emigration carried the music to North America, where recordings by musicians such as Michael Coleman in the 1920s fed back into Ireland. A revival from the 1960s, driven by groups such as The Chieftains and Planxty, brought Irish traditional music to worldwide audiences.

Styles

Regional fiddle styles are associated with Donegal, Sligo, Clare and East Galway. The sean-nós ("old style") singing tradition, usually unaccompanied and highly ornamented, is strongest in the Irish-speaking Gaeltacht areas.
