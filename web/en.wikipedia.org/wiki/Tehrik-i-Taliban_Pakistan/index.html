Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan

Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP)
Also known as: Pakistani Taliban
Founded: December 2007
Founder: Baitullah Mehsud
Ideology: Deobandi jihadism, Pashtun nationalism
Area of operations: Pakistan (esp. Khyber Pakhtunkhwa) and Afghanistan
Designated a terrorist organisation by Pakistan, the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, and the United Nations

Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP), commonly known as the Pakistani Taliban, is an umbrella organisation of militant groups based primarily in the northwestern tribal areas of Pakistan along the Afghan border. Formed in December 2007 under Baitullah Mehsud, the TTP aims to overthrow the Pakistani state and impose its interpretation of Islamic law. It is distinct from, though allied in ideology with, the Afghan Taliban.

History

The TTP emerged from militant groups operating in the Federally Administered Tribal Areas after Pakistani military operations against al-Qaeda-linked fighters in the mid-2000s. Baitullah Mehsud was killed in a U.S. drone strike in 2009 and succeeded by Hakimullah Mehsud, who was likewise killed in 2013. Under Mullah Fazlullah (2013–2018), the group carried out the December 2014 Peshawar school massacre, in which over 140 people, mostly children, were killed. Pakistan responded with the military operation Zarb-e-Azb and the National Action Plan, which significantly degraded the TTP's presence inside Pakistan and pushed many fighters into Afghanistan.

Activities

The TTP has claimed responsibility for numerous attacks on Pakistani security forces, government targets, and civilians, including suicide bombings, assassinations of politicians and polio vaccination workers, and the 2012 shooting of schoolgirl Malala Yousafzai, who survived. After the Afghan Taliban returned to power in Kabul in 2021, the TTP regrouped and increased attacks in Pakistan's Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province; a ceasefire negotiated in 2022 collapsed later that year. Noor Wali Mehsud has led the group since 2018.

Designation

The United States designated the TTP a Foreign Terrorist Organization in 2010, and the group is listed by the United Nations Security Council's sanctions regime for its association with al-Qaeda and the Taliban.
