Malice at the Palace

The Malice at the Palace was a brawl between players and fans during an NBA game between the Indiana Pacers and the Detroit Pistons at The Palace of Auburn Hills in Michigan on November 19, 2004. It is widely regarded as the most infamous brawl in NBA history.

The incident

With 45.9 seconds remaining in the game and Indiana leading 97–82, Pacers forward Ron Artest committed a foul on Pistons center Ben Wallace. Wallace shoved Artest in retaliation, and a scuffle between players broke out. Artest retreated to the scorer's table and lay down on it in an attempt to calm the situation.

Moments later, a fan named John Green threw a cup of Diet Coke that struck Artest. Artest charged into the stands and grabbed a fan he wrongly believed had thrown the cup, sparking a chaotic melee involving players, fans and security. Pacers players Stephen Jackson and Jermaine O'Neal also fought with fans who came onto the court. The game was halted and the remaining time was never played, with Indiana awarded the win.

Consequences

NBA commissioner David Stern issued some of the harshest penalties in league history. Artest was suspended for the remainder of the season, a total of 86 games. Jackson was suspended 30 games, O'Neal 25 games (later reduced to 15), and Wallace 6 games, among other sanctions. Several players and fans faced criminal charges for assault and battery.

Aftermath

The brawl prompted the NBA to tighten arena security, restrict alcohol sales late in games, and revise its disciplinary policies. The incident significantly damaged the reputations of the players involved and remains a landmark moment in discussions of player–fan relations in professional sports.
