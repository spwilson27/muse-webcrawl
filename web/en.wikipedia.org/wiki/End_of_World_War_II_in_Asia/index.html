End of World War II in Asia

World War II officially ended in Asia on 2 September 1945, when Japan formally signed the Instrument of Surrender aboard the United States Navy battleship USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay. The ceremony followed Emperor Hirohito's announcement of Japan's acceptance of the Potsdam Declaration on 15 August 1945.

Background

Following the end of the war in Europe in May 1945, the Allies demanded Japan's unconditional surrender in the Potsdam Declaration of 26 July 1945. After Japan's initial silence, the United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki on 6 and 9 August, and the Soviet Union declared war on Japan and invaded Manchuria on 9 August.

Surrender

On 14–15 August 1945, after heated debate within the Japanese government and an attempted military coup, Emperor Hirohito announced Japan's acceptance of the Potsdam terms in a recorded radio broadcast, the first time most Japanese subjects had heard their emperor's voice. Foreign Minister Mamoru Shigemitsu signed the surrender document on behalf of the Japanese government on 2 September 1945, with General Douglas MacArthur presiding as Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

Aftermath

The surrender began the Allied occupation of Japan, which lasted until 1952. Across Asia, Japanese forces surrendered in local ceremonies over the following weeks, and the end of the war reshaped the region: Korea was divided between Soviet and American occupation zones, the Chinese Civil War resumed, and independence movements accelerated in European colonies throughout Southeast Asia.
