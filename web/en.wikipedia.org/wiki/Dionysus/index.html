Dionysus is a god of ancient Greek religion and mythology, god of wine, the theatre, religious ecstasy and madness — the youngest of the twelve Olympians and the only one born of a mortal mother. In Rome he was worshipped as Bacchus, and his ecstatic rites as the Bacchanalia.

Birth and myths

Dionysus was born twice: his mother Semele, tricked by Hera, asked Zeus to appear in his full glory and was consumed by lightning; Zeus rescued the unborn child and sewed him into his thigh until term. Raised in concealment, Dionysus wandered as far as India, spreading viticulture and encountering resistance he punished terribly: King Pentheus of Thebes, who banned his worship, was torn apart by maenads including his own mother — the plot of Euripides' Bacchae. He rescued Ariadne, abandoned by Theseus on Naxos, and made her his wife.

Cult and theatre

Dionysian worship — ecstasy, dance, wine, masks and the dissolution of ordinary identity — stood at a deliberate counterpoint to Apollonian order; Nietzsche's The Birth of Tragedy (1872) built its famous Apollonian–Dionysian contrast on him. Greek tragedy and comedy themselves originated in festivals of Dionysus at Athens, above all the City Dionysia, where the plays of Aeschylus, Sophocles and Euripides premiered. His retinue of satyrs and maenads and his emblem, the pinecone-tipped thyrsus, fill Greek vase painting.
