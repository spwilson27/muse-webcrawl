Europe Day

Europe Day is the name of two annual observances held on 5 May by the Council of Europe and on 9 May by the European Union, each celebrating peace and unity in Europe. The 9 May date commemorates the Schuman Declaration of 1950, in which French foreign minister Robert Schuman proposed the creation of a European Coal and Steel Community, regarded as the foundational moment of what is now the European Union.

History

The Council of Europe instituted its Europe Day in 1964, choosing 5 May, the date of its own founding in 1949. The European Community later adopted 9 May at the Milan European Council of 1985, to mark the anniversary of Schuman's declaration. Both dates continue to be observed by their respective institutions.

Observance

Europe Day is a symbol of the European Union, along with the flag, anthem, motto, and the single currency. EU institutions hold open days in Brussels and Strasbourg, and representations across member states organise events for the public. In some member states, including Luxembourg and Kosovo, 9 May is a public holiday. In Ukraine, Europe Day has been observed on 9 May since 2023.

The Schuman Declaration

Delivered on 9 May 1950, the Schuman Declaration proposed placing French and German coal and steel production under a common High Authority, open to other European countries. Its aim was to make war between historic rivals "not merely unthinkable, but materially impossible".
