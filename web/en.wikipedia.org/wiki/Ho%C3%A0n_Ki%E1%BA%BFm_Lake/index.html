Hoàn Kiếm Lake

Hoàn Kiếm Lake
Location: Hoàn Kiếm district, Hanoi, Vietnam
Coordinates: 21.0286° N, 105.8522° E
Surface area: about 12 hectares
Average depth: 1–1.4 m

Hoàn Kiếm Lake (Vietnamese: Hồ Hoàn Kiếm, "Lake of the Restored Sword"; historically Hồ Gươm) is a freshwater lake in the historical centre of Hanoi, the capital of Vietnam. It is one of the city's most famous scenic spots and landmarks, surrounded by the Old Quarter and the French Quarter.

Legend

According to legend, the fifteenth-century emperor Lê Lợi, who led the resistance against Ming Chinese occupation, was given a magical sword by the Golden Turtle God (Kim Qui). After victory, while boating on the lake, a great golden turtle surfaced and reclaimed the sword, which Lê Lợi returned, giving the lake its name. Turtle Tower (Tháp Rùa) stands on a small island near the lake's southern end in commemoration.

Landmarks

On an island at the northern end stands the Temple of the Jade Mountain (Ngọc Sơn Temple), reached by the red-painted wooden Huc Bridge ("Bridge of the Rising Sun"). The lake was long home to giant softshell turtles of the species Rafetus leloii (formerly grouped with the Yangtze giant softshell turtle); the last known individual, affectionately called Cụ Rùa ("great-grandfather turtle"), died in January 2016, and its preserved body is displayed in the temple.
