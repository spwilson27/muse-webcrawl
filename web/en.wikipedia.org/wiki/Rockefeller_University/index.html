Rockefeller University is a private graduate university in New York City dedicated to research and graduate education in the biomedical and natural sciences. Founded in 1901 by John D. Rockefeller as The Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research — modelled on European institutes such as the Pasteur Institute — it was the first biomedical research centre in the United States. It adopted its current name in 1965 when it began awarding degrees.

Research and organisation

The university is organised not into departments but into laboratories headed by faculty members, roughly 75 in number, encouraging interdisciplinary work. It offers PhD and MD-PhD programmes but no undergraduate degrees, and its small size contrasts with an outsized scientific record: more than two dozen Nobel laureates have been associated with the institution. Landmark work at Rockefeller includes Oswald Avery's demonstration (1944) that DNA is the hereditary material, Peyton Rous's discovery of tumour viruses, the development of the pneumococcal vaccine, and advances in cell biology, neuroscience and immunology.

Campus

The 14-acre campus occupies a stretch of York Avenue between 63rd and 68th Streets on Manhattan's Upper East Side, beside the East River and near other major medical institutions. Founder's Hall, completed in 1906, is a designated New York City landmark.

Rockefeller University Press

The university's press publishes the Journal of Experimental Medicine, the Journal of Cell Biology and the Journal of General Physiology, three of the oldest and most respected journals in their fields.
