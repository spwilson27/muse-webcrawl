Hôtel particulier

Grand urban mansion in French architecture

A Parisian hôtel particulier, between court and garden

An hôtel particulier is a grand townhouse or mansion in French-speaking regions, built to be the residence of a single wealthy family, an embassy, or an institution. Unlike an ordinary hôtel, the word "particulier" (private) distinguishes it from a hotel for paying guests. The classic Parisian examples were built from the sixteenth to the nineteenth centuries, most characteristically in the Marais and the Faubourg Saint-Germain.

Layout

The canonical plan places the mansion entre cour et jardin — between an entrance courtyard and a garden. A carriage entrance (porte cochère) leads from the street into the courtyard, from which the main corps de logis is reached; service wings enclose the courtyard on the sides, while the garden side presents the family's principal rooms. Decorative programmes were designed to display the owner's rank and taste.

Examples

Famous examples include the Hôtel de Soubise and Hôtel Carnavalet in the Marais, the Hôtel Matignon (residence of the French prime minister), and the Hôtel de Salm in Paris. Many former hôtels particuliers now house museums, ministries, and embassies.
