Dhundiraj Govind Phalke (30 April 1870 – 16 February 1944), popularly known as Dadasaheb Phalke, was an Indian producer, director and screenwriter, regarded as the Father of Indian cinema. He made India's first full-length feature film, Raja Harishchandra (1913).

Early life

Phalke was born in Trimbak, in present-day Maharashtra, and studied at the Sir J. J. School of Art in Bombay. He worked as a photographer and in a printing press, and became interested in magic and film after seeing a screening of The Life of Christ in 1910.

Career

After travelling to London to study filmmaking and buy equipment, Phalke returned and shot Raja Harishchandra, released in 1913. He founded the Phalke Films Company and later Hindustan Cinema Films Company, making films such as Mohini Bhasmasur, Satyavan Savitri, Lanka Dahan and Kaliya Mardan. He made his last film, Gangavataran (1937), his only talkie.

Legacy

The Government of India instituted the Dadasaheb Phalke Award in 1969, the country's highest award in cinema, presented annually at the National Film Awards.
