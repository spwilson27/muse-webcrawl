The Association of American Medical Colleges (AAMC) is a nonprofit organisation based in Washington, D.C., representing accredited medical schools, major teaching hospitals and health systems, and academic societies in the United States. Founded in 1876, it works to improve medical education, clinical care and biomedical research.

Services

The AAMC administers the Medical College Admission Test (MCAT), required by most US medical schools, and operates centralised application services: AMCAS for medical school applications and ERAS for residency and fellowship applications. Together with the National Resident Matching Program's Match, these systems structure the path from undergraduate study through medical school to residency training. The association also collects extensive data on enrolment, tuition, faculty and the physician workforce, publishing widely cited statistics and projections of doctor shortages.

History and role

The AAMC played a key part in raising standards of American medical education in the era of the 1910 Flexner Report and in the post-war expansion of academic medicine and federal research funding. It advocates for its members on issues including graduate medical education funding, research appropriations for the National Institutes of Health, and healthcare policy.
