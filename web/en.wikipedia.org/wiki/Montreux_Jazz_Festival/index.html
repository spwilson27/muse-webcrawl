Montreux Jazz Festival

LocationMontreux, Switzerland
Founded1967 by Claude Nobs
WhenTwo weeks each July
Attendanceapprox. 250,000

The Montreux Jazz Festival is a music festival in Switzerland, held annually in early July in Montreux on the Lake Geneva shoreline. It is the second-largest annual jazz festival in the world after Canada's Montreal International Jazz Festival. Since its founding in 1967 by Claude Nobs, it has expanded far beyond jazz to encompass blues, rock, pop, soul, and electronic music.

History

The festival was launched in 1967 by Claude Nobs, a local tourism officer, with a budget of 10,000 Swiss francs, featuring artists including Charles Lloyd and Keith Jarrett. Originally held at the Montreux Casino, the festival became linked to rock history in 1971 when the casino burned down during a Frank Zappa concert — an event witnessed by Deep Purple, who immortalised it in their song "Smoke on the Water". The festival later moved principally to the Montreux Music & Convention Centre, with main stages including the Stravinski Auditorium and the Lake Stage.

Over the decades, it has hosted landmark performances by Miles Davis, Nina Simone, Ella Fitzgerald, Aretha Franklin, Prince, David Bowie, Radiohead, and countless others. Nearly all concerts have been recorded and filmed; the festival's vast audio-visual archive was inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World Register in 2013.

The festival today

The event runs for about two weeks and draws roughly 250,000 visitors, including a large free programme of concerts along the lakefront. After Claude Nobs's death in 2013, the festival has been directed by Mathieu Jaton. The Montreux Jazz Artists Foundation supports young musicians and education around the festival.
