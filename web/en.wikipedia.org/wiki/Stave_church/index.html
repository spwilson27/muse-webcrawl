Stave church

A stave church is a medieval wooden Christian church building once common in north-western Europe. The name derives from the building's structure of post and lintel construction, a type of timber framing where the load-bearing ore-pine posts are called stafr in Old Norse (stav in modern Norwegian).

History

Stave churches were built across Scandinavia and elsewhere in northern Europe during the 12th and 13th centuries, replacing earlier post churches. More than a thousand may once have stood in Norway alone; today only about 28 survive there, including the famous Borgund and Urnes stave churches. Urnes, dating to around 1130, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the church at Heddal is the largest surviving example.

Architecture

The churches are noted for their steep multi-tiered roofs, carved portals decorated with dragon motifs and vine scrolls, and interiors that blend Christian iconography with older Norse artistic traditions. Their construction on stone foundations (sill beams raised off the ground) protected the timbers from rot and accounts for their remarkable longevity.
