The Midlands

The Midlands is the central part of England, bordered by Wales, Northern England, Southern England and the North Sea. It corresponds broadly to the historic kingdom of Mercia and is usually divided into the West Midlands and the East Midlands.

Major citiesBirmingham, Coventry, Leicester, Nottingham, Derby, Stoke-on-Trent
CountiesDerbyshire, Leicestershire, Lincolnshire, Northamptonshire, Nottinghamshire, Rutland, Shropshire, Staffordshire, Warwickshire, Worcestershire, West Midlands, Herefordshire

Geography and economy

The region is largely lowland, drained by the Trent and Severn river systems. It was a heartland of the Industrial Revolution — Birmingham and the Black Country for metalworking, the Potteries for ceramics, and the East Midlands for textiles, hosiery and footwear.

Culture

The Midlands is associated with William Shakespeare (born in Stratford-upon-Avon), the writer D. H. Lawrence, heavy metal music (Black Sabbath and Judas Priest came from Birmingham) and the balti curry. Its dialects range from Brummie to the East Midlands varieties.
