McCune–Albright syndrome

McCune–Albright syndrome is a rare genetic disorder characterised by a triad of polyostotic fibrous dysplasia of bone, café-au-lait skin pigmentation, and endocrine abnormalities, most commonly precocious puberty.

Cause

The syndrome results from a postzygotic activating mutation in the GNAS gene, which encodes the alpha subunit of the stimulatory G protein. Because the mutation arises after fertilisation, it produces a mosaic pattern of affected tissues and is not inherited from the parents.

Features

Fibrous dysplasia can weaken bones, causing pain, deformity and fractures. Endocrine features include precocious puberty (especially in girls), hyperthyroidism, growth hormone excess and Cushing syndrome. The café-au-lait macules often have irregular "coast of Maine" borders and tend to respect the midline.

History

The condition was described independently by Donovan James McCune in 1936 and Fuller Albright in 1937.
