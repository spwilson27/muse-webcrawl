Idiolect

An idiolect is an individual's unique variety of language, encompassing their distinctive vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation. In linguistics, an idiolect is the specific form of a language used by a given individual; no two speakers share exactly the same idiolect, even within a single dialect or speech community.

The concept matters in forensic linguistics, where an author's habitual word choices, spellings, and constructions can be used to attribute disputed texts, and in literary studies, where an author or character's idiolect is part of their style. The term combines the Greek idio- ("own, personal, private") with -lect, abstracted from dialect.

Language as an idiolect collection

Some linguists argue that a language, considered as a social object, is really an abstraction over millions of overlapping idiolects, none of which is identical to any other; individuals adjust their idiolects toward their interlocutors in a process called accommodation. Related concepts include the sociolect of a social group and the register used in a particular situation.
