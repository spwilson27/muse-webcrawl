Sunshine Harvester Works

The Sunshine Harvester Works was a major agricultural machinery factory in Sunshine, Victoria, Australia, operated by H. V. McKay Pty Ltd and later by Massey Ferguson. At its peak it was the largest manufacturing plant in Australia, employing thousands of workers, and it gave the surrounding suburb of Sunshine its name. The works are historically significant as the site whose wage dispute produced the landmark 1907 Harvester Judgement, which established the concept of a basic (living) wage in Australian law.

History

Inventor and industrialist Hugh Victor McKay developed the Sunshine Harvester, the first commercially successful combine harvester, which stripped, threshed and winnowed grain in one operation. His company moved production to a large site at Braybrook Junction in 1906–07, and the suburb around the factory was renamed Sunshine after his works. The plant produced harvesters, tractors and other farm machinery, much of it exported worldwide.

Harvester Judgement

In 1907 McKay sought an exemption from excise duties on his harvesters on the condition he paid "fair and reasonable" wages. Justice H. B. Higgins of the Commonwealth Court of Conciliation and Arbitration heard evidence and determined that a fair wage for an unskilled labourer was 7 shillings per day — enough to support a family of about five in "frugal comfort". The decision became a foundation of the Australian wage-fixing system for most of the twentieth century.

Later years

McKay's company merged into Sunshine Massey Harris in 1930 and the works passed to Massey Ferguson in 1958. Production declined in the later twentieth century and the factory closed progressively; much of the site has been redeveloped, though heritage elements and the H. V. McKay Memorial Gardens remain.
