House of Gonzaga

The House of Gonzaga (Italian: Casa Gonzaga) was an Italian princely family that ruled Mantua in Lombardy, northern Italy, from 1328 to 1708. Over nearly four centuries the family rose from captains of the people (capitani del popolo) to marquesses and then dukes of Mantua, and cadet branches ruled the Duchy of Nevers in France and several smaller Italian principalities.

Rise to power

In 1328 Luigi (Ludovico) Gonzaga overthrew the Bonacolsi lordship and was acclaimed captain of Mantua. His descendants consolidated power, and in 1433 Gianfrancesco I received the title of Marquess of Mantua from the Emperor Sigismund. Under Ludovico II and his wife Barbara of Brandenburg, Mantua became a brilliant Renaissance court: Leon Battista Alberti designed churches there, Andrea Mantegna served as court painter, and Isabella d'Este, wife of Francesco II, became one of the most celebrated patrons of the age.

Ducal Mantua

In 1530 Federico II was created Duke of Mantua by Charles V, and the family also acquired the Marquisate of Montferrat. The Gonzaga amassed one of Europe's great art collections, including works commissioned from Titian, Rubens and Monteverdi, whose opera L'Orfeo premiered at the Mantuan court in 1607. The direct line failed in 1627, provoking the War of the Mantuan Succession; the French-backed Gonzaga-Nevers branch inherited the duchy.

Decline and extinction

The last duke, Ferdinando Carlo of Gonzaga-Nevers, sided with France in the War of the Spanish Succession and was deposed by the Emperor in 1708, Mantua passing to Austrian Habsburg rule. The main line died with him in 1708, though cadet lines such as the Gonzaga of Vescovato survive into the present day.
