Sagaing Region

Country: Myanmar
Capital: Monywa
Area: 93,705 km²
Population (2014): 5,325,347
Largest city: Monywa

Sagaing Region is an administrative region of Myanmar, located in the north-western part of the country between latitude 21°30' north and longitude 94°47' to 97°36' east. It is bordered by India's Nagaland, Manipur and Arunachal Pradesh states to the west and north, Kachin State, Shan State and Mandalay Region to the east, Mandalay Region and Magway Region to the south, and Chin State and India to the west. The region covers 93,705 square kilometres (36,181 sq mi), making it one of Myanmar's largest regions. Until 2010 its capital was Sagaing; the capital was then moved to Monywa.

Geography

The region is traversed by the Irrawaddy River and its great tributary the Chindwin. The historic city of Sagaing, at the south-eastern corner of the region facing Mandalay across the Irrawaddy, is a major religious centre famous for its hundreds of pagodas and monasteries on the Sagaing Hills.

Demographics

According to the 2014 census, the region's population was about 5.3 million. Ethnic Bamars form the majority, with significant Shan, Chin and Naga populations in the west and north, and small Kachin and other communities. The Naga Self-Administered Zone lies within the region.

Economy

Agriculture is the main occupation, producing rice, wheat, sesame, groundnuts, pulses, cotton and tobacco. The region is also known for timber, and for gold and other mineral extraction along the Chindwin valley. The city of Monywa is an important trading centre for agricultural goods from the surrounding plains.
