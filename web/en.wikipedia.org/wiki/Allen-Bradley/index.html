Allen-Bradley is a brand of factory automation equipment owned by Rockwell Automation. The company, founded in 1903 in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, became famous for its motor controls, resistors and, later, programmable logic controllers (PLCs), which revolutionised industrial automation.

History

The company was founded as the Compression Rheostat Company by Dr Stanton Allen and Lynde Bradley, with an initial investment of $1,000. It was renamed the Allen-Bradley Company in 1910. During the First World War it expanded rapidly supplying electrical controls; during the Second World War much of its output went to military production. The company was a major Milwaukee employer, and its four-faced clock tower in Milwaukee — completed in 1962 — was for many years the largest four-faced clock in the world.

In the 1970s and 1980s Allen-Bradley became a leader in programmable controllers; engineer Odo J. Struger is credited with a leading role in developing the PLC-5 family and modern ladder-logic programming. In 1985 Rockwell International acquired Allen-Bradley for $1.65 billion, then the largest acquisition in Wisconsin history. The automation businesses were later spun off as Rockwell Automation (2001), which retains Allen-Bradley as its flagship product brand, including the ControlLogix and CompactLogix controller families, FactoryTalk software and PowerFlex drives.
