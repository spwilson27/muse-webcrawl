USCGC Eagle (WIX-327), formerly the Horst Wessel and also known as the Barque Eagle, is a 295-foot (90 m) barque used as a training cutter for future officers of the United States Coast Guard. She is one of only two active commissioned sailing vessels in American military service, alongside USS Constitution. Each summer, Eagle deploys with cadets from the United States Coast Guard Academy and candidates from the Officer Candidate School for periods ranging from a week to two months.

History

The ship was built at the Blohm+Voss shipyard in Hamburg, Germany, in 1936 and commissioned as the German Navy (Kriegsmarine) sail-training ship Horst Wessel, named after the Nazi stormtrooper whose song became a party anthem. Albert Leo Schlageter, a member of her first crew, was later commemorated in the name of a sister ship. After World War II the vessel was taken by the United States as war reparations and commissioned into the Coast Guard on 15 May 1946.

Eagle serves as "America's Tall Ship" and as a goodwill ambassador, visiting ports around the world and participating in tall-ship gatherings and races. She has undergone extensive refits over her long service to extend her working life, including a multi-year service life extension programme completed in the 2020s.
