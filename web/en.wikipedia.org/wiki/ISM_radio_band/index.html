ISM radio band

The ISM radio bands are portions of the radio spectrum reserved internationally for industrial, scientific and medical (ISM) purposes, other than telecommunications. Examples of applications for the use of radio frequency (RF) energy in these bands include radio-frequency process heating, microwave ovens, and medical diathermy machines.

Bands

The ISM bands are defined by the ITU Radio Regulations. They include bands centered at 6.78 MHz, 13.56 MHz, 27.12 MHz, 40.68 MHz, 433.92 MHz (in Region 1), 915 MHz (in Region 2), 2.45 GHz, 5.8 GHz, 24.125 GHz, and higher frequencies up to 245 GHz. Details vary by region and country.

Communications use

Although the bands were reserved for non-communications purposes, they have become enormously popular for unlicensed short-range communications, because users need no license provided their equipment meets power and interference constraints. Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, cordless phones, NFC/RFID (at 13.56 MHz), baby monitors, and wireless sensor networks all operate in ISM bands — most prominently 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz. The trade-off is that such devices must tolerate interference from ISM equipment such as microwave ovens.
