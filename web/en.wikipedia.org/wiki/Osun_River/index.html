Osun River

The Osun River (also spelled Oshun) is a river in south-western Nigeria that flows southward through Osun State and neighbouring states into the Lekki Lagoon and ultimately the Atlantic Ocean at the Gulf of Guinea.

The river is named after Osun, one of the most venerated orishas in Yoruba religion — the goddess of love, fertility, beauty and fresh water. According to Yoruba tradition, the river was formed from the transformation of Osun into flowing water. Along its banks at Osogbo lies the Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the river is the focus of the annual Osun-Osogbo Festival.

The river has faced pollution concerns in recent years, including reports of contamination from mining activities in its upper catchment, which have affected its use for drinking water and religious purposes.
