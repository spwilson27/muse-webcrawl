Harbhajan Singh Khalsa (born Harbhajan Singh Puri; 26 August 1929 – 6 October 2004), known as Yogi Bhajan and Siri Singh Sahib, was an Indian-born American yoga teacher and entrepreneur. He introduced his version of Kundalini Yoga to the United States and founded the 3HO Foundation (Healthy, Happy, Holy Organization) in 1969, building a worldwide movement of principally Western converts to a Sikh-inspired spiritual path.

Life

Harbhajan Singh was born in Punjab and studied yoga from an early age, later claiming Sant Hazara Singh as his principal teacher. He worked as an officer in the Indian customs service before emigrating to Canada in 1968 and then moving to Los Angeles, where he began teaching yoga classes. In 1969 he established 3HO, and through the 1970s the movement grew, with ashrams and yoga centres opening across the United States and abroad. Students adopted Sikh religious practices, including wearing turbans and white clothing, and many took the name Khalsa.

Organisations and business

Beyond teaching, Yogi Bhajan founded the Kundalini Research Institute (1972), the International Kundalini Yoga Teachers Association, and businesses associated with the community, including the Yogi Tea company and the security firm Akal Security. In 1973, the Akal Takht recognised him with the title Siri Singh Sahib, and he became a minister of the Sikh Dharma organisation in the West. He organised annual events such as the summer and winter solstice sadhana gatherings in New Mexico and Florida.

Death and legacy

Yogi Bhajan died in 2004 in Española, New Mexico, where the movement's headquarters community had been established. Kundalini Yoga as taught by Yogi Bhajan continues to be practised through teacher trainings worldwide. After his death, and particularly from 2020 onward, published accounts by former students and an independent investigation alleged sexual misconduct and abuse of power during his lifetime, prompting institutional responses and public debate within the organisation he founded.
