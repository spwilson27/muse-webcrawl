School district

A school district is a special-purpose district that operates local public primary and secondary schools. It is a distinct legal entity with its own powers, most commonly found in the United States, where school districts are usually independent governmental units with the power to levy taxes and elect governing boards.

United States

The United States has roughly 13,000 public school districts. Districts vary enormously in size — from single-school rural districts to the New York City Department of Education, which serves over a million students. Most districts are governed by an elected school board that hires a superintendent, sets budgets, and establishes local policy within state law. Funding comes mainly from local property taxes supplemented by state and federal aid.

Elsewhere

In Canada, school boards (or districts) administer public schools at the provincial level. In the United Kingdom and most other countries, schools are more often administered directly by municipalities or national ministries rather than by independent districts, though similar local education authority structures exist.
