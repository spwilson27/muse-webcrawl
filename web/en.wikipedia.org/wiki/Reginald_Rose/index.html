Reginald Rose (10 December 1920 – 19 April 2002) was an American screenwriter and playwright. He is best known for the courtroom drama Twelve Angry Men, originally written as a television play and first broadcast in 1954, and for his work in the golden age of American television drama.

Career

Rose served in the U.S. Army during the Second World War and began selling scripts to television in 1951, contributing to anthology series such as Studio One, Four Star Playhouse and The Jane Wyman Show. Twelve Angry Men, inspired by his own service on a jury, was broadcast on Studio One in September 1954 and won Rose an Emmy Award. He expanded it into the celebrated 1957 film directed by Sidney Lumet and starring Henry Fonda, for which Rose received an Academy Award nomination for Best Adapted Screenplay; a stage version followed, and the play has been revived continuously worldwide.

Other work

Rose created the television series The Defenders (1961–1965), whose issue-driven legal dramas won numerous Emmy Awards. His film screenplays include Crime in the Streets (1956), Dino (1957), The Wild Geese (1978), The Sea Wolves (1980) and Whose Life Is It Anyway? (1981). He also wrote the teleplay An Early Frost (1985), the first major American television drama about AIDS.

Style and legacy

Rose's writing characteristically addressed social and ethical questions — prejudice, justice, civil liberties — through tightly constructed, dialogue-driven courtroom and committee-room settings. Twelve Angry Men is widely used in schools and law courses and is preserved in the United States National Film Registry.
