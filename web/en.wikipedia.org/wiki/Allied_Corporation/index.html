Allied Corporation was an American conglomerate with interests in chemicals, aerospace, automotive parts and energy. It began in 1920 as the Allied Chemical & Dye Corporation, a combination of five chemical companies formed to give the United States an industry independent of German suppliers after the First World War.

History

Allied Chemical was assembled by chemist William H. Nichols and financier Eugene Meyer from Barrett Company, General Chemical, National Aniline & Chemical, Semet-Solvay and Solvay Process Company. The combined company became one of America's largest chemical producers, making soda ash, ammonia, dyes, and later nylon fibres and plastics.

From the 1960s the company diversified beyond chemicals, acquiring the Bendix Corporation in 1982 — a bitterly contested takeover — and shortening its name to Allied Corporation in 1981. In 1985 Allied merged with the Signal Companies to form Allied-Signal, which in 1999 acquired Honeywell and adopted the Honeywell name; the present-day Honeywell International is the corporate descendant of Allied.

Environmental legacy

Allied's operations left notable pollution legacies. Its chromium works in New Jersey and the former Solvay Process waste beds on Onondaga Lake in Syracuse, New York, became major contaminated sites subject to long-running federal cleanup efforts.
