Moment (physics)

A moment is a mathematical expression involving the product of a distance and a physical quantity such as a force or electric charge. Moments are usually defined with respect to a fixed reference point and refer to physical quantities located some distance from the reference point. In this way, the moment accounts for the quantity's location or arrangement.

A force F applied at distance r from a pivot produces a moment (torque) about that point.

Moment of a force

The most elementary moment is the moment of a force, often called torque (and in mechanical engineering, the bending moment). It measures the tendency of a force to rotate an object about an axis or pivot. For a force F applied at a position r relative to the pivot, the moment is the cross product:

M = r × F

Its magnitude is rF sin θ, where θ is the angle between the position and force vectors, and it is measured in newton metres (N·m) in SI units. For a body in static equilibrium, the sum of all moments about any point must be zero, along with the sum of all forces.

General definition and examples

In its general form, an n-th order moment of a distribution of a quantity q at locations x is ∫ xn q(x) dx. Special cases include:

  • Moment of inertia — the second moment of mass, governing resistance to angular acceleration.
  • Angular momentum — the moment of linear momentum, L = r × p.
  • Electric dipole moment — the first moment of an electric charge distribution.
  • Magnetic moment — a measure of the strength and orientation of a magnetic source.
  • Moments in statistics — mean, variance, skewness and kurtosis are moments of a probability distribution.

History

The concept descends from Archimedes' work on levers and centres of gravity in the 3rd century BCE, summarised in the principle that equal weights at equal distances balance, and that a smaller force can balance a larger weight when applied at a proportionally greater distance. The word "moment" comes from the Latin momentum (movement), via the idea of importance or turning effect. In the Middle Ages, writers on the science of weights, such as Jordanus de Nemore, elaborated the notion of "positional gravity", and the concept was formalised during the development of classical mechanics.
