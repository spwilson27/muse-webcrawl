In the Hebrew Bible, Amram (Hebrew: עַמְרָם, ʿAmrām) was the husband of Jochebed and the father of Aaron, Moses and Miriam. He was a member of the tribe of Levi, the son of Kohath, grandson of Levi, and he heads the Kohathite branch of the Levites from which the Israelite priesthood descended.

Biblical account

The Book of Exodus names Amram as a son of Kohath who married his father's sister Jochebed and lived 137 years. His children — Aaron (three years older than Moses) and Miriam — play central roles in the Exodus narrative, although Amram himself never appears in the main storyline; he died in Egypt before the Exodus. Later genealogies in Numbers and Chronicles trace the priestly line of Aaron through Amram, and the "sons of Amram" are listed among the divisions of the Levites who served in the Temple.

In later tradition

Amram is honoured in Jewish tradition as a righteous leader of his generation; rabbinic literature (including the apocryphal Visions of Amram found among the Dead Sea Scrolls at Qumran) expands his story, portraying him as a scholar who kept the traditions of the patriarchs alive in Egypt. He is also recognised in Islam as a figure of the family of ʿImrān — the name given to the third chapter (sūrah) of the Qur'an, "Āl ʿImrān" ("Family of Imran") — though in Islamic tradition Imran is often identified as the father of Maryam (Mary) as well.
