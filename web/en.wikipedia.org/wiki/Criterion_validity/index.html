Criterion validity

Criterion validity is usually expressed as the correlation between test scores and a criterion measure.

In psychometrics, criterion validity (or criterion-related validity) is the extent to which a measure is related to an outcome or "criterion" — an external standard that the test is intended to predict or estimate. It provides evidence for how well scores on a test correspond to a particular behaviour, performance or status that is measured independently of the test itself.

Types

Predictive validity concerns how well a test forecasts future performance on the criterion — for example, whether admission test scores predict later academic success. Concurrent validity concerns how well test scores agree with criterion measures obtained at the same time, such as whether a new short questionnaire agrees with an established longer instrument. The relationship is typically quantified with a correlation coefficient, sometimes called a validity coefficient.

Issues

Criterion validity can be undermined by an unreliable or poorly chosen criterion ("criterion contamination" or "criterion deficiency"), by restriction of range in the sample studied, and by shrinkage when a prediction equation is applied to new samples. In employment testing, demonstrating criterion validity is one of the principal strategies for showing that a selection procedure is job-related, alongside content validity and construct validity approaches.
