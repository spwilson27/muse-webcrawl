Blushing

[Image: A young woman with visibly flushed cheeks looking away]

Blushing is the reddening of a person's face due to psychological reasons. It is normally involuntary and triggered by emotional stress associated with passion, embarrassment, shyness, fear, anger, or romantic stimulation. It is thought to be a uniquely human response; Charles Darwin called it "the most peculiar and most human of all expressions".

Physiology

Blushing is caused by dilation of the blood vessels of the face, controlled by the sympathetic nervous system and the release of adrenaline. Unlike general flushing, blushing is confined largely to the face, ears and upper chest, and the veins of the face respond to adrenaline in a way that veins elsewhere generally do not.

Psychology

Blushing is associated with perceived social evaluation and self-consciousness, and typically occurs when a person feels exposed to the attention or judgement of others. Severe blushing is common in people who have social anxiety disorder. Psychologists have proposed that blushing acts as a nonverbal apology or appeasement signal, defusing aggression and helping to preserve social bonds.

Treatment

For disabling cases, treatments range from cognitive behavioural therapy to, in extreme instances, endoscopic thoracic sympathectomy, a surgery that carries significant side effects and is controversial.
