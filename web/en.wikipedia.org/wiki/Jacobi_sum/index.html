Jacobi sum

In mathematics, a Jacobi sum is a type of character sum formed with Dirichlet characters and named after Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi, who introduced them in the early nineteenth century in connection with cyclotomy and the theory of cyclotomic fields.

Definition

Let q be a prime power and let χ and ψ be nontrivial multiplicative characters of the finite field Fq. The Jacobi sum J(χ, ψ) is defined as the sum of χ(x)ψ(1 − x) taken over all elements x of Fq for which x ≠ 0 and x ≠ 1.

Properties

Jacobi sums are closely related to Gauss sums: the product of two Gauss sums can be expressed in terms of a Jacobi sum, and when χψ is nontrivial, J(χ, ψ) = g(χ)g(ψ) / g(χψ). When both characters are nontrivial and χψ is also nontrivial, the absolute value of the Jacobi sum is √q. Jacobi sums satisfy simple symmetry and transformation formulas, such as J(χ, ψ) = J(ψ, χ).

Applications

Jacobi sums appear in the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem for certain exponents, in counting points on curves over finite fields, in the evaluation of the number of solutions of diagonal equations, and in primality testing algorithms such as the Adleman–Pomerance–Rumely test, which uses Jacobi sums of higher order in cyclotomic extensions.
