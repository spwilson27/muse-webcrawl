Pearl

CompositionCalcium carbonate (aragonite/calcite) and conchiolin (nacre)
Produced byPearl oysters and other bivalve molluscs
Mohs hardness2.5–4.5
BirthstoneJune

A pearl forms inside the shell of a mollusc as a defence against an irritant.

A pearl is a hard, glistening object produced within the soft tissue (specifically the mantle) of a living shelled mollusc. Just like the shell of a mollusc, a pearl is composed of calcium carbonate in minute crystalline form, deposited in concentric layers. The ideal pearl is perfectly round and smooth, but many other shapes occur. The finest quality natural pearls have been highly valued as gemstones and objects of beauty for many centuries.

Formation

Pearls form when an irritant—typically a grain of sand, a parasite, or in cultured pearls a deliberately inserted nucleus—becomes lodged in the mollusc's mantle. The animal coats the irritant with layer upon layer of nacre, the same iridescent material lining its shell, gradually building a pearl sac and a pearl.

Types

Natural pearls are extremely rare; nearly all pearls sold today are cultured, produced by inserting a bead nucleus into freshwater mussels or saltwater pearl oysters such as Pinctada fucata (Akoya) and Pinctada maxima (South Sea). Major varieties include Akoya, South Sea, Tahitian (black), and freshwater pearls, mostly produced in China.

Value and uses

Pearls are judged by lustre, surface quality, shape, colour and size. They are used in jewellery—necklaces, earrings and rings—and historically symbolised purity and wealth. Unlike other gemstones, pearls are organic and require no cutting or polishing.
