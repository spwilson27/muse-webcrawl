Clio

Clio (Greek: Κλειώ, Kleiṓ; from κλέω/κλείω, "to make famous" or "to celebrate") is the Muse who presides over history in Greek mythology. Like her eight sisters, she is a daughter of Zeus and the Titaness Mnemosyne (Memory). In later tradition each Muse was assigned a distinct sphere, and Clio became the muse of history and, in some accounts, of lyre playing.

Iconography

Clio is commonly depicted holding an open parchment scroll or a set of tablets, sometimes with a chest of books beside her, a trumpet (to proclaim fame), and occasionally a water clock. She appears in this form in Johannes Vermeer's The Art of Painting (c. 1666–1668), where the model posing as Clio wears a laurel wreath and holds a trumpet and a great folio, symbolising fame and history. According to some myths, Clio was the mother of Hyacinthus by the Macedonian king Pierus, and she was said to have been punished with love for Adonis by Aphrodite for criticising the goddess's own affair.

Legacy

Clio's name is given to the history prizes and journals of several universities and historical societies, to the asteroid 84 Klio, and to many literary and historical publications. The discipline of quantitative history is called cliometrics after her, and practitioners founded the Cliometric Society in 1983. The word also appears in the names of historical awards such as the Clio Awards for advertising (named in her honour as muse of history) and in the Renault Clio car.
