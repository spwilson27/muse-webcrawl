Owner's manual

An owner's manual, also called a user manual, user guide, or instruction manual, is an instructional book or booklet supplied with almost all technologically advanced consumer products such as vehicles, home appliances, and computer peripherals. Information contained in the owner's manual typically includes:

  • Safety instructions, intended to keep the user safe and reduce the risk of injury
  • Assembly instructions, for products that arrive in pieces for easier shipping
  • Installation instructions, for products that need to be installed in a home or workplace
  • Setup instructions, such as for connecting a computer to the Internet
  • Normal usage instructions
  • Programming instructions, for products featuring programmable components
  • Maintenance instructions
  • Troubleshooting instructions, when a device misbehaves
  • Specifications, such as dimensions, weight, and input requirements
  • Warranty information
  • Regulatory code compliance information

Until the last decade or two, most owner's manuals were provided in paper form. Because of the ease of use and storage provided by electronic media, many companies now provide manuals in digital format, such as a CD-ROM or a link to a website, instead of or in addition to a paper copy.

History

The term owner's manual first appears in print in the 19th century. With increasingly complex mechanical products in the Industrial Revolution, the need for such instructions grew. One of the first known manuals was that of the Singer Sewing Machine, whose instructions were published in the 19th century. Car manufacturers began including them shortly after the automobile became common.
