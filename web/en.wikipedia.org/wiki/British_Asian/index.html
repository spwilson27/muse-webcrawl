British Asians are citizens or residents of the United Kingdom whose ancestral roots lie in Asia. In British usage, "Asian" most commonly refers to people of South Asian descent — Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, and Sri Lankan — while people of East and Southeast Asian origin are also included, notably the British Chinese community. The Asian population of the United Kingdom grew substantially through migration after the Second World War and forms one of the country's largest ethnic groupings.

History

Small numbers of South Asians — lascar sailors, servants, and students — lived in Britain from the 17th century, and the first mosques and Sikh temples appeared in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Large-scale migration followed the Second World War, driven by labour demand and by upheaval including the Partition of India in 1947. Migrants from Punjab, Gujarat, Mirpur (Azad Kashmir), and Sylhet settled in industrial towns of the Midlands, the North, and London. The Commonwealth Immigrants Acts of 1962 and 1968 and the Immigration Act 1971 progressively restricted primary migration, though family reunion continued. In 1972, Idi Amin's expulsion of Asians from Uganda brought some 27,000 largely Gujarati refugees, many of whom settled in Leicester.

Demographics

In the 2021 census for England and Wales, the Asian ethnic group numbered about 5.5 million (9.3% of the population), comprising roughly 1.9 million Indians, 1.6 million Pakistanis, 650,000 Bangladeshis, 450,000 Chinese, and around 970,000 people in other Asian groups. Concentrations are highest in London, Birmingham, Leicester, Bradford, Luton, and parts of Greater Manchester.

Culture and influence

British Asian communities have shaped British food (the curry house tradition and dishes such as chicken tikka masala), music (bhangra and the Asian Underground of the 1990s), sport (notably cricket and football), business, and politics. British Asians have served at the highest levels of government, including as Prime Minister (Rishi Sunak, of Indian heritage, from 2022 to 2024), Home Secretary, Chancellor of the Exchequer, and Mayor of London. Religious life is diverse: the largest groups are Muslims of Pakistani and Bangladeshi heritage, Hindus and Sikhs of Indian heritage, and smaller Buddhist, Christian, and Jain communities.

Terminology

Official statistics in the UK use categories such as "Asian/Asian British: Indian", "Pakistani", "Bangladeshi", "Chinese", and "Other Asian". The umbrella term is contested, as it aggregates communities with distinct languages, religions, and migration histories.
