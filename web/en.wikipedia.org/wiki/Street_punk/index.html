Street punk

Street punk is a subgenre of punk rock that emerged in the early 1980s as a working-class reaction against the perceived artiness of some punk and the commercial direction of new wave. Closely related to Oi!, street punk is characterised by fast, aggressive songs with singalong choruses, simple chord structures, and lyrics about working-class life, football, drinking, and social frustration.

A street punk band performing.

History

The first wave of street punk grew out of the UK82 (UK hardcore) scene, with bands such as The Exploited, Discharge, GBH, and Chaos UK playing a faster, harsher style than first-generation punk. The associated Oi! bands — Cockney Rejects, Angelic Upstarts, The 4-Skins, and Sham 69 — emphasised terrace-style choruses.

1990s revival

A second wave in the 1990s and 2000s brought international prominence to bands such as The Casualties, A Global Threat, Monster Squad, and Cheap Sex in the United States, and The Virus, while British bands like The Business continued the Oi! tradition. The distinctive street punk fashion includes mohawk hairstyles, spiked and studded leather jackets, band T-shirts, tartan or camouflage trousers, and Dr. Martens boots.
