Fred Saberhagen

Fred Thomas Saberhagen (May 18, 1930 – June 29, 2007) was an American science fiction and fantasy author most famous for his Berserker series of science fiction short stories and novels. He also wrote a series of vampire novels in which Dracula is the central character, and a popular series of post-apocalyptic mythical-merging novels beginning with Empire of the East and continuing through the Swords and Lost Swords books.

Life and career

Saberhagen was born and raised in Chicago, Illinois. He served in the U.S. Air Force during the Korean War, worked as an electronics technician, and from 1967 to 1973 worked for the Encyclopædia Britannica, writing articles on science and technology. His first published story appeared in Galaxy magazine in 1961, and his first novel, The Golden People, appeared in 1964. He later moved to Albuquerque, New Mexico, where he lived until his death.

The Berserker series

The Berserker stories, begun with the 1963 story "Fortress Ship", concern vast self-replicating robotic war machines programmed to destroy all life, survivors of an ancient interstellar war. Humanity's struggle against the Berserkers — often turning on the machines' inability to understand life and deception — is told across numerous short stories and novels collected in volumes such as Berserker (1967) and The Ultimate Enemy.

Other works

Saberhagen's Dracula novels, beginning with The Dracula Tape (1975), retell and extend Bram Stoker's story from Dracula's own sympathetic viewpoint. His fantasy Swords trilogy and the eight-volume Book of Lost Swords series follow magical blades forged by the gods, each with a unique power and drawback. He also wrote the science fiction novel The Black Throne with Roger Zelazny and several novels based on his own original settings.
