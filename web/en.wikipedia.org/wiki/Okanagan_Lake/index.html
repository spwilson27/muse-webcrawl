Okanagan Lake

Okanagan Lake is a large, deep lake in the Okanagan Valley of British Columbia, Canada. The lake is about 135 km (84 mi) long, between 4 and 5 km (2.5 and 3.1 mi) wide, and has a surface area of 351 km², making it one of the largest lakes in the province. Major communities on its shores include Kelowna, West Kelowna, Vernon, Peachland, Summerland, and Penticton at its southern end.

Geography

Okanagan Lake is a fjord lake, carved by repeated glaciation during the Pleistocene. It reaches a maximum depth of about 232 m (761 ft). The lake drains southward through the Okanagan River at Penticton, eventually reaching the Columbia River system.

Ogopogo

The lake is famous in folklore as the home of Ogopogo, a lake monster said to inhabit its waters. The legend derives in part from Indigenous traditions of a water spirit known as N'ha-a-itk, and Ogopogo has become a popular symbol of the region.

Recreation and economy

The warm, dry climate of the valley makes the lake a centre for tourism, boating, swimming, and lakeside resorts, and its shores support orchards and vineyards of the British Columbia wine industry.
