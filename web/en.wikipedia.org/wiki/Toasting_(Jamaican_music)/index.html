Toasting (Jamaican music)

Toasting
Stylistic origins: Ska, rocksteady, sound system culture
Cultural origins: Late 1960s, Jamaica
Key figures: U-Roy, Big Youth, I-Roy, Yellowman

Toasting, also called deejaying in Jamaican terminology, is the act of talking or chanting, usually in a monotone melody, over a rhythm or beat, performed by a deejay over records at Jamaican sound system dances.

History

Sound system operators such as Count Machuki in the 1950s began talking over records with jive patter inspired by American radio DJs. In the late 1960s deejays like U-Roy began recording toasts over dub versions of hits — U-Roy's 'Wake the Town' (1970) was a major hit — making the deejay a recording artist in his own right.

Influence

Toasting is a direct ancestor of American hip hop: Jamaican-born DJ Kool Herc carried sound-system practices to the Bronx in the 1970s. It also shaped dancehall, jungle and UK garage MCing. Unlike rapping's usual origin in song, toasting developed from live commentary, boasts, news and crowd exhortation. See Dancehall, Dub music, and Sound system.
