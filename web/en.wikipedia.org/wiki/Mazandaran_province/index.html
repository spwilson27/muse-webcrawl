Mazandaran province (Persian: استان مازندران) is one of the 31 provinces of Iran, located along the southern coast of the Caspian Sea between the provinces of Gorgan (Golestan) to the east and Gilan to the west. Its capital is Sari. The province occupies a narrow, fertile plain backed by the Alborz mountain range, whose forests and high rainfall give Mazandaran a humid, green landscape strikingly different from Iran's arid plateau.

Geography

Mazandaran combines Caspian coastline, rice paddies and citrus orchards on the plain with dense Hyrcanian forests on the Alborz foothills. Mount Damavand, Iran's highest peak at 5,610 metres, stands on the province's southwestern border with Tehran province. Major cities include Sari, Babol, Amol, Qaem Shahr, Chalus and Ramsar.

History

The region was historically known as Tabaristan and long resisted Arab and later central-government control thanks to its mountainous terrain, maintaining local dynasties such as the Bavandids for centuries after the Islamic conquest. It was incorporated into Persia under the Safavids. The modern province took shape in the 20th century, losing its eastern districts to the new Golestan province in 1997.

Economy and culture

Agriculture — especially rice, citrus fruit, tea and caviar from Caspian sturgeon — anchors the economy, while the Caspian resorts make Mazandaran Iran's premier domestic tourist destination. The Mazandarani language, a Northwestern Iranian language distinct from Persian, is widely spoken, and the province has its own musical and wrestling traditions.
