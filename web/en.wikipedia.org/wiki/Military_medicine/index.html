Military medicine

Military medicine is the branch of medicine concerned with the health of armed forces personnel, the treatment of battlefield casualties, and the medical aspects of military operations, including preventive medicine in austere environments.

Scope

Military medical services provide care in echelons: point-of-injury first aid and combat lifesaver skills, forward resuscitation and damage-control surgery, field hospitals, and definitive care in home-country facilities, linked by medical evacuation by ground, helicopter, and fixed-wing aircraft. Triage — sorting casualties by urgency and survivability — is central under mass-casualty conditions.

Contributions

War has repeatedly driven medical advances adopted by civilian practice, including ambulance systems, blood transfusion and banking, antibiotics for wound infection, reconstructive and plastic surgery, prosthetics, and modern trauma systems. Military medicine also covers occupational health, infectious-disease control in deployments, mental health, and protection against chemical, biological, radiological, and nuclear threats.
