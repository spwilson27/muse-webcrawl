Big Bounce

The Big Bounce is a hypothetical cosmological scenario in which the expansion of the universe follows a phase of contraction, so that the Big Bang is reinterpreted as the rebound of a previous collapsing universe rather than an absolute beginning. In bounce models the universe reaches a minimum, non-zero size at extremely high density, avoiding the singularity predicted by classical general relativity.

Contraction, bounce, expansion: a non-singular origin.

Background

Cyclic or oscillating models date to the work of Alexander Friedmann and Richard Tolman in the 1920s and 1930s, though early cyclic models struggled with entropy growth across cycles. Modern bounce scenarios draw on theories of quantum gravity and modified gravity.

Models

In loop quantum cosmology, quantum geometry effects generate a repulsive force at densities near the Planck scale, producing a bounce that connects a contracting branch to our expanding universe. Ekpyrotic and matter-bounce scenarios invoke scalar fields and sometimes a violation of the null energy condition to generate a nearly scale-invariant spectrum of fluctuations. Bounce models attempt to address the horizon and flatness problems addressed by cosmic inflation, and researchers study whether they leave observable signatures, such as specific non-Gaussianities, in the cosmic microwave background.
