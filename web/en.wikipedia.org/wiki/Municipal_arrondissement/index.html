Municipal arrondissement

Not to be confused with the arrondissements of Paris's prefecture or departmental arrondissements.

A municipal arrondissement (French: arrondissement municipal) is a subdivision of certain large French communes (municipalities), serving as an administrative district below the level of the commune itself. Municipal arrondissements exist in only three French cities: Paris, Lyon and Marseille. They were created by the law of 31 December 1982, commonly known as the PML law (for Paris, Marseille, Lyon), which reorganised the administration of France's three largest cities.

Each municipal arrondissement has its own mayor (maire d'arrondissement) and council (conseil d'arrondissement), elected at the same time as the municipal council of the city as a whole. The arrondissement council manages local facilities such as schools, nurseries, gymnasiums and local roads, and must be consulted on matters affecting the arrondissement, but the city's mayor and central council retain most executive power, including the budget and police matters.

Paris

Paris is divided into 20 municipal arrondissements, numbered 1 to 20 in a clockwise spiral starting from the centre of the city on the Right Bank of the Seine. They were originally created in 1860, when Paris annexed surrounding communes and expanded from 12 to 20 arrondissements. Since the 2020 municipal elections, the first four arrondissements have been grouped for electoral and administrative purposes into a single sector, Paris Centre, which elects one mayor while the four arrondissements retain their numbers.

Lyon

Lyon is divided into 9 municipal arrondissements, largely dating from 1852, when the city absorbed neighbouring communes such as La Guillotière, Vaise and Croix-Rousse.

Marseille

Marseille is divided into 16 municipal arrondissements, grouped into 8 sectors (secteurs), each sector electing a mayor. The arrondissements were created in 1946.
