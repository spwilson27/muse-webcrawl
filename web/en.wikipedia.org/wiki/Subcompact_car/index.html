Subcompact car

Subcompact car is a North American classification for automobiles smaller than a compact car, roughly equivalent to the B-segment in Europe and the supermini category in Britain. According to the United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), subcompact cars have an interior volume between 85 and 99.9 cubic feet (about 2,400–2,830 litres).

History

The term came into use in the 1970s, when American manufacturers responded to the fuel crisis with smaller models such as the Ford Pinto, Chevrolet Vega, and AMC Gremlin. Japanese imports including the Honda Civic and Toyota Corolla — initially classed as subcompacts — helped define the segment with front-wheel drive and hatchback designs. Modern examples include the Honda Fit/Jazz, Ford Fiesta, Hyundai Accent, and Toyota Yaris.

Characteristics

Subcompacts typically have a length under about 4.2 metres, small-displacement engines (or electric powertrains), and seating for four or five. They are marketed on low price, fuel economy, and ease of urban parking, though in recent years rising consumer preference for crossover SUVs has shrunk the segment in North America.
