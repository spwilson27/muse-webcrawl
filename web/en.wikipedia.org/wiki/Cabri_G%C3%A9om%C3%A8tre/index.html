Cabri Geometry

DeveloperCabrilog / Jean-Marie Laborde
TypeInteractive geometry software
First released1980s–1990s
PlatformsWindows, macOS, calculators, web

Cabri Geometry (French: Cabri Géomètre) is an interactive geometry software program for teaching and learning geometry and trigonometry. It allows users to construct geometric figures — points, lines, circles, conics, and transformations — and to drag elements dynamically while preserving geometric relationships.

The name is a play on the French 'cahier de brouillon interactif' (interactive draft notebook).

History

Cabri was developed beginning in the 1980s by Jean-Marie Laborde and colleagues at Joseph Fourier University in Grenoble, France, and commercialised by Cabrilog. It was among the first dynamic geometry environments and was adopted in schools in many countries.

Features

Users can construct figures from primitive objects and construction tools, measure lengths, angles, and areas, animate points along paths, and explore loci. Versions have been produced for personal computers, graphing calculators such as the TI-92, and the web as Cabri Express.

Use in education

Cabri is used in primary through university mathematics to support conjecture and proof: students drag a figure to test whether a property holds generally, building intuition before formal demonstration.
