ReplayTV

ReplayTV was a brand of digital video recorder (DVR) and the company that pioneered commercial DVR technology alongside TiVo. The first ReplayTV units were demonstrated at the Consumer Electronics Show in January 1999 and shipped later that year, allowing television viewers to record programmes to a hard disk, pause live television, and schedule recordings from an on-screen guide.

Features

ReplayTV offered features considered advanced for its time, including automatic recording based on themes and keywords, the ability to share recordings between units over a home network or the internet ("Send Show"), and — most controversially — Commercial Advance, which automatically skipped commercials during playback, and a 30-second skip button.

Litigation

In 2001, a coalition of television networks and movie studios sued ReplayTV's then-owner, SONICblue, alleging that Commercial Advance and show-sharing infringed their copyrights. The litigation (part of the wider Paramount Pictures Corp. v. ReplayTV dispute) was closely watched as a test of fair-use time-shifting principles established in the Betamax case. SONICblue filed for bankruptcy in 2003 before the case was resolved.

Later ownership and end of service

The ReplayTV assets were acquired by Digital Networks North America (a subsidiary of D&M Holdings), which continued selling units for a time, later shifting to PC software. The electronic programme guide service that the hardware depended on was discontinued on 31 July 2011, rendering most standalone ReplayTV units largely unusable for scheduled recording.
