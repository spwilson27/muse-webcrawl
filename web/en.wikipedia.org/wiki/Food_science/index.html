Food science

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Food science is the interdisciplinary study of food — its composition, processing, preservation, safety, quality and interaction with the human body. It applies chemistry, microbiology, physics, engineering and nutrition to everything from raw agricultural produce to the packaged meal.

Scope and subdisciplines

Major areas include food chemistry (the composition and reactions of proteins, fats, carbohydrates and additives), food microbiology (spoilage, fermentation and foodborne pathogens), food engineering (unit operations such as pasteurisation, drying, freezing and extrusion), sensory science (how flavour, texture and appearance are perceived), and food safety and quality assurance, including systems such as HACCP. Food scientists also work on packaging, shelf-life, new product development and fortification.

History

Empirical food preservation — salting, smoking, fermenting, pickling — is ancient, but the discipline dates from the early 19th century: Nicolas Appert's canning process (1810), Pasteur's work on fermentation and pasteurisation, and Justus von Liebig's food chemistry. The field professionalised in the 20th century with university departments, the founding of the Institute of Food Technologists in 1939, and the demands of wartime rationing and mass food manufacturing.

Practice

Food scientists are employed by manufacturers, retailers, regulators and research institutes. Their work underlies modern conveniences from frozen vegetables to plant-based meat analogues, as well as responses to challenges such as reducing salt, sugar and food waste.
