Malkasten ("paintbox") is an artists' association in Düsseldorf, Germany, founded on 6 August 1848 during the revolutionary year. It is one of the oldest and most storied artists' societies in Germany and remains active today.

Foundation and early years

The association was founded by painters of the Düsseldorf school of painting, among them members of the circle around the Kunstakademie Düsseldorf such as Carl Friedrich Lessing, Johann Wilhelm Schirmer and Andreas and Oswald Achenbach. It took its playful name from the painter's paintbox and was intended as a convivial, democratic association across artistic camps — famous for its costume balls, theatrical performances (the "Malkastenbühne") and its journal and club sociability.

The Malkastenpark

From 1860 the association acquired the garden of the philosopher Friedrich Heinrich Jacobi in Pempelfort, creating the Malkastenpark, with the artists' house built in 1864–1867. The site was heavily damaged in the Second World War and rebuilt afterwards. The park, with its pond, venerable trees and statues of Düsseldorf artists, is now a protected monument and venue for concerts and festivals.

Notable members

Members and regulars over the years have included the painters of the Düsseldorf school, the Norwegian painter Hans Gude, the American artist Emanuel Leutze (associated with the circle), and in the 20th century figures linked with the modern art scene of the Rhineland.
