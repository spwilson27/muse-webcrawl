Jubilee Hall (Fisk University)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Jubilee Hall, Fisk University, Nashville]

Jubilee Hall is a historic building on the campus of Fisk University in Nashville, Tennessee, United States. Completed in 1876, it is regarded as one of the oldest academic buildings in continuous use at a historically Black university in the United States and was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1974.

History

The hall was funded largely by proceeds from the world tours of the Fisk Jubilee Singers, a student choral ensemble formed in 1871 to raise money for the struggling university. Their performances of African American spirituals across the United States and Europe earned enough to purchase land and construct the building.

Architecture

Designed in the Victorian Gothic style by architect Stephen D. Morgan, the six-story building features distinctive twin turrets and a central tower. Inside hangs a famous portrait of the original Jubilee Singers, a gift from Queen Victoria, painted by her court painter Edmund Havel.
