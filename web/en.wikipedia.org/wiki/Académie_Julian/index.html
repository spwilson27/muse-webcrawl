The Académie Julian was a private art school for painting and sculpture founded in Paris in 1867 by the French artist Rodolphe Julian. It was one of the most famous art schools of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, notable for accepting women students (in separate studios) at a time when the official École des Beaux-Arts excluded them, and for attracting large numbers of foreign students, especially Americans.

History

Julian modelled his school on the life classes of the École des Beaux-Arts, with established academic painters such as William-Adolphe Bouguereau, Gustave Boulanger, Jules Lefebvre and Tony Robert-Fleury visiting to correct students' work. Monthly competitions prepared students for the Prix de Rome and the Salon.

Students

Many notable artists studied there, including Henri Matisse, André Derain, Marcel Duchamp and Fernand Léger; members of Les Nabis such as Édouard Vuillard, Pierre Bonnard and Maurice Denis met in its studios. Women students included Marie Bashkirtseff, whose journal records the school, and Louise Catherine Breslau. American students included Thomas Eakins (briefly), Robert Henri and Cecilia Beaux.

The school survived into the 20th century and in 1968 merged with the Penninghen school of art (ESAG Penninghen).
