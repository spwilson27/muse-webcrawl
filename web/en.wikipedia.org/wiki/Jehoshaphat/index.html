Jehoshaphat

Jehoshaphat (Hebrew: יְהוֹשָׁפָט, Yehoshafat, "Yahweh has judged"), son of Asa, was the fourth king of the Kingdom of Judah. According to the Hebrew Bible he reigned for twenty-five years in the ninth century BCE. The Books of Kings and Chronicles portray him as a pious reformer and ally of the northern Kingdom of Israel, remembered for judicial and religious reforms and for the deliverance of Judah from an invading coalition.

Biblical account

Jehoshaphat succeeded his father Asa at the age of thirty-five (1 Kings 22). The Chronicler credits him with removing high places and Asherah poles, sending officials and Levites throughout Judah to teach the Book of the Law, and reorganising the judiciary by appointing judges in the fortified cities and a central court in Jerusalem (2 Chronicles 17–19). Tribute came from Philistines and Arabs, and Judah's army was numbered at over a million men in the Chronicler's account.

Alliance with Israel

Jehoshaphat made peace with Israel and sealed it by the marriage of his son Jehoram to Athaliah, daughter of King Ahab and Jezebel. He joined Ahab in the campaign to recover Ramoth-Gilead, insisting first on consulting a prophet; the prophet Micaiah foretold disaster, and Ahab was killed while Jehoshaphat survived (1 Kings 22). Later he allied with Ahab's son Jehoram in a campaign against Moab (2 Kings 3), and with Ahaziah in a joint shipping venture at Ezion-Geber, which failed.

The invasion and the valley

2 Chronicles 20 recounts an invasion by Moabites, Ammonites, and Meunites. Jehoshaphat proclaimed a national fast and prayed at the Temple; the Levite Jahaziel prophesied victory without fighting, and the invaders destroyed one another. The king's name attaches to the Valley of Jehoshaphat, mentioned in the Book of Joel as the place where God judges the nations — often identified with part of the Kidron Valley in Jerusalem.

Assessment

The biblical writers judge Jehoshaphat favourably overall, though he is rebuked by the seer Jehu for aiding the wicked Ahab, and criticised for the Israelite marriages that brought Baal worship into Judah in the next generation. He was buried in the City of David and succeeded by his son Jehoram.
