Michoacán

Capital: Morelia
Largest city: Morelia
Area: 58,599 km²

Michoacán, formally Michoacán de Ocampo, is one of the 31 states of Mexico, located in west-central Mexico on the Pacific coast. Its capital and largest city is Morelia, known for its well-preserved colonial centre built of pink stone, which is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The state borders Jalisco and Guanajuato to the north, Querétaro and the State of Mexico to the east, Guerrero to the south, Colima to the west, and the Pacific Ocean to the southwest.

History

Before the Spanish conquest, Michoacán was the heartland of the Purépecha (Tarascan) Empire, the second-largest state in Mesoamerica after the Aztec Empire, and one of the few that the Aztecs never conquered. The Purépecha were renowned metalworkers, producing copper and bronze tools and ornaments. After the Spanish conquest in the 1520s and 1530s, the region became part of New Spain. During the Mexican War of Independence, the priest José María Morelos, after whom Morelia is named, was a leading insurgent figure. The state is named in honour of independence leader Melchor Ocampo.

Geography and culture

The state spans the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt and the Sierra Madre del Sur. It contains Lake Chapala's eastern shore, Lake Pátzcuaro, and the Monarch Butterfly Biosphere Reserve, a UNESCO site where millions of monarch butterflies overwinter in oyamel fir forests. The Parícutin volcano famously erupted from a farmer's cornfield in 1943. Michoacán is Mexico's leading avocado-producing state and a centre of avocado exports. Cultural highlights include the Day of the Dead celebrations around Lake Pátzcuaro, especially on the island of Janitzio.
