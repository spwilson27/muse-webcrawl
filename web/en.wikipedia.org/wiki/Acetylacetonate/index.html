Acetylacetonate

NamesPentane-2,4-dionate
Abbreviationacac−

Acetylacetonate is an anion derived from acetylacetone (pentane-2,4-dione) by the loss of a proton from the central carbon. It is commonly abbreviated acac. The anion is a versatile bidentate ligand that binds metal ions through both of its oxygen atoms, forming a stable six-membered chelate ring. Metal acetylacetonate complexes are used throughout inorganic chemistry, catalysis, and materials science.

Bonding and structure

The negative charge in acetylacetonate is delocalised over the O–C–C–C–O framework, and the carbon–oxygen and carbon–carbon bonds in the chelate ring have intermediate lengths consistent with this delocalisation. Acetylacetonate typically occupies two cis coordination sites on a metal centre. Depending on the metal's oxidation state and preferred coordination number, complexes of the form M(acac)2 and M(acac)3 are common.

Notable complexes

  • Tris(acetylacetonato)iron(III), Fe(acac)3, a deep red complex.
  • Copper(II) acetylacetonate, Cu(acac)2, a blue-grey solid.
  • Nickel(II) acetylacetonate, which exists as a trimer in the solid state.
  • Vanadyl acetylacetonate, VO(acac)2, a catalyst precursor.
  • Aluminium acetylacetonate, Al(acac)3.

Applications

Metal acetylacetonates are widely used as catalysts and catalyst precursors, as volatile precursors for chemical vapour deposition of metal and metal-oxide thin films, as NMR shift reagents (in the case of certain lanthanide complexes with modified ligands), and as soluble sources of metals in organic solvents. Substituted analogues such as hexafluoroacetylacetonate (hfac) and tetramethylheptanedionate (tmhd) tune volatility and solubility.
