Tsugaru Strait
Location: Between Honshu and Hokkaido, Japan
Coordinates: 41.48°N 140.87°E
Min. width: approx. 19.5 km

The Tsugaru Strait (Japanese: 津軽海峡, Tsugaru-kaikyō) is a strait between Honshu and Hokkaido in northern Japan, connecting the Sea of Japan with the Pacific Ocean. It is named after the western part of Aomori Prefecture. The Seikan Tunnel passes under the strait, linking the two islands by rail.

Geography

The strait is about 19.5 km wide at its narrowest point, between Tappi Misaki on Honshu and Shirakami Misaki on Hokkaido. Its maximum depth is roughly 200 m. The Tsugaru Warm Current, a branch of the Tsushima Current, flows eastward through the strait from the Sea of Japan to the Pacific.

Crossings

Before the Seikan Tunnel opened in 1988, the Seikan Ferry connected Aomori and Hakodate across the strait. The tunnel, at 53.85 km one of the world's longest undersea tunnels, carries both conventional trains and the Hokkaido Shinkansen. Car and passenger ferries still operate on the route today.
