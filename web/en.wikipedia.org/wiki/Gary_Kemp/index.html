Gary James Kemp (born 16 October 1959) is an English musician, songwriter, actor, and author. He is best known as the guitarist, principal songwriter, and backing vocalist of the new wave band Spandau Ballet, for whom he wrote international hits including "True", "Gold", "Through the Barricades", and "To Cut a Long Story Short". His younger brother, Martin Kemp, is the band's bassist.

Early life

Kemp was born in Islington, London, to Eileen and Frank Kemp, and grew up in Islington, attending Dame Alice Owen's School. He and his brother both acted as children; Gary appeared in the 1972 film Hide and Seek. The brothers formed the band that became Spandau Ballet in the late 1970s, emerging from London's Blitz club scene at the heart of the New Romantic movement.

Spandau Ballet

Spandau Ballet became one of the most successful British bands of the 1980s, selling more than 25 million albums worldwide. The 1983 ballad "True", written by Kemp, topped the UK chart and reached number four in the United States, and has become one of the most performed songs in popular music. The band split acrimoniously in 1990, followed by a court case in 1999 in which three members unsuccessfully sued Kemp over songwriting royalties. The original line-up reunited in 2009 for a successful world tour, and performed together until 2019.

Acting and later work

As an actor, Kemp played Ronnie Kray opposite his brother Martin as Reggie Kray in the 1990 film The Krays, and appeared in The Bodyguard (1992) and other productions, as well as on stage. Since 2018 he has toured with Nick Mason's Saucerful of Secrets, performing early Pink Floyd material. He released his second solo album, This Destination, in 2025, following Little Bruises (1995) and Insolo (2021). His memoir I Know This Much: From Soho to Spandau was published in 2009.
