Codigoro is a town and comune in the province of Ferrara, Emilia-Romagna, Italy, located in the Po Delta near the Adriatic coast. It lies about 40 kilometres east of Ferrara, in a low-lying area of reclaimed wetlands criss-crossed by canals and drainage channels.

Geography

The municipality occupies part of the Po Delta plain, between the Po di Volano and Po di Goro branches of the river. The surrounding territory is largely agricultural, with rice fields, orchards, and fish-farming valleys, and forms part of the Po Delta Regional Park. The hamlet of Pomposa is known for its medieval abbey.

History

The settlement developed in the early Middle Ages near the Benedictine Abbey of Pomposa, founded in the ninth century, which became one of the most important cultural centres of northern Italy. Codigoro grew as a river port and market town on the Po di Volano. During the Second World War the area saw fighting in the final Allied advance of spring 1945, and the town was liberated in April of that year.

Main sights

  • Abbey of Pomposa — a Romanesque masterpiece with fourteenth-century frescoes and a celebrated mosaic pavement depicting scenes from the Old and New Testaments.
  • Palazzo del Vescovo — the bishop's palace in the town centre.
  • The nineteenth-century water-scooping plant and drainage works, monuments of the great land-reclamation era.

Economy

The local economy is based on agriculture (rice, cereals, fruit), aquaculture in the delta lagoons, food processing, and tourism linked to the abbey and the delta's birdwatching sites.
