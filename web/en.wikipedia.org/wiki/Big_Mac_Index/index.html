Big Mac Index

The Big Mac index is a price index published since 1986 by The Economist as an informal way of measuring the purchasing power parity (PPP) between two currencies and providing a test of the extent to which market exchange rates result in goods costing the same in different countries.

Big Mac index
Publisher: The Economist
First published: September 1986
Invented by: Pam Woodall
Purpose: Informal PPP comparison

Overview

The index compares the price of a McDonald's Big Mac across countries. Under PPP theory, in the long run exchange rates should move so that an identical basket of goods costs the same everywhere. A Big Mac serves as a simplified, widely available basket containing food, labour, rent and other inputs.

Methodology

The implied PPP is the local Big Mac price divided by the US price. Comparing this with the actual exchange rate suggests whether a currency is under- or over-valued. The Economist also publishes a GDP-adjusted version to account for the expectation that prices are lower in poorer countries.

Criticism

Economists note that local taxes, profit margins, import costs, demand and non-traded inputs differ, and that a burger is not traded internationally. McDonald's pricing strategies also vary.

Related indices

Variants include the Tall Latte index and other single-product comparisons. See also law of one price and Burgernomics.
