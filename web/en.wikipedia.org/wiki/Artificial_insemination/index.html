Artificial insemination

Artificial insemination is the deliberate introduction of sperm into a female's reproductive tract for the purpose of achieving a pregnancy by means other than sexual intercourse. It is a fertility treatment for humans, and is a common practice in animal breeding, including dairy cattle and pigs. Artificial insemination may employ assisted reproductive technology, sperm donation and animal husbandry techniques.

Humans

In humans, artificial insemination may be used when a woman or her partner has difficulty conceiving by natural means. Techniques include intracervical insemination (ICI) and intrauterine insemination (IUI). In IUI, washed and concentrated sperm is placed directly into the uterus around the time of ovulation, often combined with ovarian stimulation using drugs such as clomifene citrate or gonadotropins. The sperm may come from a partner or from a donor obtained through a sperm bank. Historically the practice dates to experiments in the late 18th century, including those of John Hunter and, in the 19th century, J. Marion Sims and William Pancoast, who performed an early documented donor insemination in 1884.

Animals

Artificial insemination is used extensively in modern animal husbandry. It allows a single male of superior genetics to sire many offspring, reduces the spread of venereal diseases, and permits the use of frozen semen transported over long distances. The technique was developed on a practical scale in Russia in the early 20th century by Ilya Ivanov and his successors, and was later widely adopted for dairy cattle; the development of frozen semen storage in liquid nitrogen in the 1940s and 1950s greatly expanded its use. It is applied to cattle, pigs, sheep, horses, poultry, dogs and many other species, as well as in conservation programmes for endangered animals.

Legal and social issues

The use of donor sperm raises questions about anonymity, the rights of donor-conceived people to learn about their origins, and the legal parentage of resulting children, which are regulated differently in different jurisdictions. Many countries limit the number of families that may be created from a single donor.
