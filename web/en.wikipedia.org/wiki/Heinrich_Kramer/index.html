Heinrich Kramer

Born: c. 1430, Sélestat, Alsace
Died: 1505, Kroměříž, Moravia
Occupation: Dominican friar, inquisitor, author
Known for: Malleus Maleficarum

Heinrich Kramer (c. 1430 – 1505), also known under his Latinised name Henricus Institoris, was a German churchman and inquisitor. He is best known as the author of the Malleus Maleficarum ("Hammer of Witches"), published in 1487, which became the most influential handbook for the prosecution of witchcraft in early modern Europe.

Life

Kramer was born in Sélestat (Schlettstadt) in Alsace and joined the Dominican Order as a youth. He rose to become prior and was appointed an inquisitor for parts of southern Germany. His zealous witch trials in Tyrol brought him into conflict with the Bishop of Brixen, Georg Golser, who expelled him from Innsbruck in 1485 after objecting to his methods and his obsession with the sexual behaviour of accused women.

Malleus Maleficarum

Frustrated by his expulsion, Kramer obtained the papal bull Summis desiderantes affectibus (1484) from Pope Innocent VIII and wrote the Malleus Maleficarum to refute arguments that witchcraft did not exist and to guide magistrates in identifying, trying and sentencing witches. The work was long attributed jointly to Kramer and his fellow Dominican Jacob Sprenger, though modern scholarship holds Kramer to be its principal, probably sole, author. The book was repeatedly reprinted and shaped witch trials for two centuries, though both the Church and secular authorities at times treated it with reserve; the theological faculty of Cologne condemned parts of it, and Kramer falsely implied its endorsement.

Kramer spent his later years preaching and writing in Bohemia and Moravia, where he died in 1505.
