The World Zionist Organization (WZO; Hebrew: ההסתדרות הציונית העולמית) is a non-governmental organisation founded at the initiative of Theodor Herzl at the First Zionist Congress in Basel in August 1897, originally under the name Zionist Organization. It served as the umbrella body of the Zionist movement, whose goal — set out in the Basel Program — was the establishment of a publicly and legally assured home for the Jewish people in Palestine.

History

Herzl was the organisation's first president (1897–1904). The Zionist Congress, meeting periodically, functioned as its legislature, electing an Actions Committee and executive. The WZO founded key institutions including the Jewish Colonial Trust (1899), the Jewish National Fund (1901) and the Keren Hayesod (1920). Its diplomatic efforts contributed to the Balfour Declaration of 1917 and the League of Nations Mandate for Palestine.

Chaim Weizmann served as president from 1921 to 1931 and again from 1935 to 1946. After the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948, the Jewish Agency — created as the WZO's operative arm in 1929 — assumed many practical functions, and the WZO's role shifted toward strengthening ties between Israel and Jewish communities worldwide, promoting Hebrew education and encouraging aliyah (immigration to Israel). The organisation adopted its present name in 1960.

Structure

The World Zionist Congress, elected by Zionist federations and world Zionist unions around the globe, remains its supreme body; major Israeli political parties and diaspora movements compete in its elections.
