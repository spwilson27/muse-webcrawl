Prasutagus

Prasutagus (died c. AD 60) was king of the Iceni, a British Celtic tribe inhabiting what is now Norfolk and parts of Suffolk, during the early years of the Roman occupation of Britain. He is best known as the husband of Boudica, who led a major revolt against Roman rule after his death.

Life

After the Roman invasion of AD 43, Prasutagus ruled as a nominally independent ally and client king of Rome. He had presumably been installed or confirmed in power following the disarmament crisis of AD 47, when the Iceni were among the tribes required to surrender their weapons.

Death and aftermath

Seeking to preserve his kingdom and protect his family, Prasutagus made the Roman emperor co-heir to his kingdom together with his two daughters. When he died, however, Roman officials ignored the will, annexed the kingdom outright, seized Iceni lands and property, and — according to Tacitus — flogged Boudica and violated her daughters. These abuses provoked the Boudican revolt of AD 60–61, in which Camulodunum, Londinium and Verulamium were burned before the rebels were defeated.
