Richard Riot

The Richard Riot was a riot on March 17, 1955 (Saint Patrick's Day), in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. The riot erupted during a National Hockey League game between the Montreal Canadiens and the Detroit Red Wings after NHL president Clarence Campbell suspended Canadiens star Maurice "Rocket" Richard for the remainder of the season and the Stanley Cup playoffs. The suspension followed an incident in which Richard struck a linesman during a stick fight with Boston's Hal Laycoe.

DateMarch 17, 1955
LocationMontreal Forum and downtown Montreal, Quebec
CauseSuspension of Maurice Richard
OutcomeGame forfeited to Detroit; dozens arrested

The game

Campbell attended the St. Patrick's Day game at the Montreal Forum despite warnings. The crowd booed him throughout the first period. During the intermission a tear gas canister was thrown near his box; the game was forfeited to Detroit, and the Forum was evacuated.

The riot

Outside, the angry crowd spilled onto Sainte-Catherine Street, smashing windows, looting shops and overturning streetcars into the early morning. Dozens were arrested and many injured. Richard went on the radio the next day, in both English and French, pleading for calm and promising to return the following season.

Significance

Because Richard was a hero of French-speaking Quebec, many saw the suspension as an insult by the English-speaking league establishment. The riot is often cited as an early expression of Quebec nationalism and a precursor to the Quiet Revolution.
