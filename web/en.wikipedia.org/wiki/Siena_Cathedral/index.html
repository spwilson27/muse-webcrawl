Siena Cathedral

Siena Cathedral (Italian: Duomo di Siena) is a medieval Roman Catholic cathedral in Siena, Italy, dedicated to Our Lady of the Assumption. Built in the Italian Romanesque-Gothic style between the 12th and 14th centuries, it is one of the most celebrated churches in Italy, famous for its striped marble façade and bell tower, its inlaid marble mosaic floor, the Piccolomini Library frescoed by Pinturicchio, and sculpture by Nicola Pisano, Donatello, Michelangelo and Bernini, among others.

History

A church stood on the site from at least the 9th century. The present building was constructed from the early 1200s; the choir and transepts took shape by mid-century and the dome was completed by 1264. The lower façade, designed by Giovanni Pisano, was built between about 1284 and 1299; the upper façade was finished later, in the 1360s–1380s.

In 1339 Siena, then wealthy and populous, resolved to enlarge the cathedral into one of the largest churches in Christendom: the existing building would have become merely the transept of a vast new nave, the "Duomo Nuovo". Work began, but the Black Death of 1348 devastated the city, and structural problems in the new work led to its abandonment; the unfinished nave walls and the soaring incomplete façade (the Facciatone) still stand beside the cathedral.

Exterior

The cathedral is faced inside and out with alternating bands of white and greenish-black marble, the colours of Siena's civic arms. Giovanni Pisano's lower façade is richly carved with prophets and philosophers. The bell tower (campanile), in the same striped masonry, has six orders of windows and dates to 1313.

Interior and artworks

The marble mosaic floor, created between the 14th and 19th centuries, comprises 56 panels depicting sibyls, biblical scenes and allegories; the most famous include Giovanni di Lorenzo's Massacre of the Innocents and the Wheel of Fortune. It is fully uncovered for only part of each year. Nicola Pisano carved the marble pulpit (1265–1268) with assistance from his son Giovanni and Arnolfo di Cambio. The Piccolomini Altar contains four statues carved by the young Michelangelo (1501–1504). In the Chigi Chapel are statues by Gian Lorenzo Bernini. The Piccolomini Library, built for Pope Pius III, is covered with frescoes by Pinturicchio (c. 1502–1508) illustrating the life of Pope Pius II, and holds the Roman marble group of the Three Graces. Beneath the choir lie the crypt frescoes rediscovered in 1999, and opposite the cathedral stands the Museo dell'Opera del Duomo, which preserves Duccio di Buoninsegna's great double-sided altarpiece, the Maestà (1308–1311).
