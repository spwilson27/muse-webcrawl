International Monetary Fund

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) is a major financial agency of the United Nations, and an international financial institution funded by 190 member countries, with headquarters in Washington, D.C. Conceived at the Bretton Woods Conference of July 1944 and established in 1945, it works to foster global monetary cooperation, secure financial stability, facilitate international trade, promote high employment and sustainable economic growth, and reduce poverty.

Functions

The Fund's main activities are surveillance of members' economies and policies (Article IV consultations), financial assistance to countries in balance-of-payments difficulties (stand-by arrangements, extended facilities, and concessional lending to low-income countries), and capacity development. Lending typically carries conditionality — economic reforms the borrower commits to — a feature both defended as necessary discipline and criticised for austerity and one-size-fits-all prescriptions, notably after the Asian financial crisis (1997–98) and in the euro-area programmes of 2010–2015. Its unit of account is the special drawing right (SDR), a reserve asset whose basket contains the US dollar, euro, renminbi, yen and sterling.

Governance

Voting power is quota-based, giving the United States an effective veto over major decisions and large economies the dominant voice — a long-standing grievance of emerging and developing countries, partly addressed by quota reforms and by the convention (increasingly questioned) that the managing director is European while the World Bank president is American.
