Triple-alpha process

The triple-alpha process is a set of nuclear fusion reactions by which three helium-4 nuclei (alpha particles) are transformed into carbon-12 in the cores of stars. It is the principal route by which carbon is produced in the universe, and hence is essential for the existence of carbon-based life.

Mechanism

Two alpha particles first fuse to form beryllium-8, which is highly unstable, decaying back with a half-life of about 8×10−17 seconds. At temperatures above roughly 108 kelvin and high densities, an equilibrium concentration of beryllium-8 builds up, allowing a third alpha particle to strike before decay, forming an excited state of carbon-12 that decays to the stable ground state with the release of energy.

The Hoyle state

The reaction rate is enormously enhanced by a resonance in carbon-12 at 7.65 MeV — the "Hoyle state" — whose existence was predicted by astronomer Fred Hoyle in 1954 on the grounds that carbon must be produced efficiently in stars, and confirmed experimentally soon after. The episode is often cited in discussions of fine-tuning and the anthropic principle.

Role in stellar evolution

The process ignites in the helium flash in low-mass red giants and burns steadily in the cores of asymptotic giant branch stars. A further alpha capture can convert carbon-12 to oxygen-16.
