Heraklion (Greek: Ηράκλειο, Irákleio) is the largest city and administrative capital of the island of Crete, and the fourth most populous city in Greece. It is a major Mediterranean port and ferry hub, situated close to the ruins of Knossos, the centre of Minoan civilisation.

History

The site was the harbour of ancient Knossos. The modern city was founded by the Saracens in 824–25, who dug a defensive ditch around it — giving it the Arabic name al-Khandaq, "the moat", which became Candia under the Venetians. Venice ruled from 1204, building the massive defensive walls and the harbour fortress (the Rocca a Mare, or Koules) that still define the old town. The Ottoman siege of Candia (1648–1669), among the longest in history, ended in Ottoman conquest; the city officially took the name Heraklion in the 19th century, derived from the Roman port of Heracleum, and became part of Greece in 1913.

Sights

The Heraklion Archaeological Museum holds the world's finest collection of Minoan art, including the Phaistos Disc and the snake goddess figurines. Nearby, the excavated Palace of Knossos is Crete's most visited archaeological site. Other landmarks include the Venetian Loggia, the Morosini Fountain in Lions Square, the cathedral of Agios Minas, and the tomb of the writer Nikos Kazantzakis in the city walls. Heraklion International Airport, named for Kazantzakis, is Greece's second busiest.
