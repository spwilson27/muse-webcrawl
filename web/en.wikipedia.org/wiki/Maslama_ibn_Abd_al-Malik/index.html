Maslama ibn Abd al-Malik

Maslama ibn Abd al-Malik (Arabic: مسلمة بن عبد الملك, died 24 December 738) was an Umayyad prince and one of the most distinguished Arab generals of the early 8th century. A son of Caliph Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan, he is best known for leading the great Siege of Constantinople in 717–718.

Campaigns against Byzantium

Maslama commanded the annual summer expeditions (sawa'if) into Byzantine Anatolia for decades, capturing fortresses such as Tyana, and campaigned in the Caucasus against the Khazars. In 717 he crossed into Europe and laid siege to Constantinople with a huge army supported by a fleet, while Leo III defended the city. The siege failed through winter hardship, Bulgar attacks, and Byzantine use of Greek fire; Maslama withdrew in August 718 after devastating losses, a defeat celebrated in Byzantine tradition as the salvation of the empire.

Governorships

Maslama served as governor of Iraq, Armenia, and Azerbaijan at various times under his brothers the caliphs al-Walid I, Sulayman, Umar II, Yazid II, and Hisham. Under Yazid II he suppressed the revolt of Yazid ibn al-Muhallab in Iraq (720–721). Unlike his brothers, he never became caliph — reportedly because his mother was a slave concubine — despite his military prestige.

Death and legacy

Maslama died in 738 at his estate in Syria. He was remembered in Arabic sources as a paragon of the warrior-prince and in Byzantine chronicles as the formidable adversary of 717–718. He also endowed building works, including reconstruction associated with the Great Mosque of Damascus, and canals and estates in northern Syria.
