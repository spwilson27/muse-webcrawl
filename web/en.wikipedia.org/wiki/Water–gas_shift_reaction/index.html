Water–gas shift reaction

Water–gas shift reaction
ReactionCO + H2O ⇌ CO2 + H2
ΔH (298 K)−41.1 kJ/mol (mildly exothermic)
Discovered1780, Felice Fontana
UsesHydrogen production, ammonia synthesis, Fischer–Tropsch adjustment

The water–gas shift reaction (WGSR) describes the reaction of carbon monoxide and water vapour to form carbon dioxide and hydrogen:

CO + H2O ⇌ CO2 + H2    ΔH298 = −41.1 kJ/mol

The reaction was discovered by Italian physicist Felice Fontana in 1780. It is a central step in the industrial production of hydrogen and in adjusting the composition of synthesis gas.

Applications

In steam reforming of natural gas and in the manufacture of ammonia and hydrogen, the shift reaction increases the yield of H2 while converting toxic carbon monoxide — which poisons many catalysts and fuel cells — into more easily removed CO2. It is also used to tune the H2:CO ratio of syngas for Fischer–Tropsch and methanol synthesis, and in integrated gasification combined cycle (IGCC) power plants to enable pre-combustion carbon capture.

Process conditions and catalysts

Because the reaction is mildly exothermic but slow at low temperature, industry uses two stages. The high-temperature shift (HTS) runs at roughly 350–450 °C over an iron oxide–chromium oxide catalyst and leaves a few percent CO; the low-temperature shift (LTS) follows at about 180–250 °C over a copper–zinc oxide–alumina catalyst, reducing CO to fractions of a percent. The equilibrium favours products at low temperature, but kinetics favour high temperature, hence the two-stage compromise. Sulfur poisons copper LTS catalysts, so feed gases must be desulfurised first. A variant, the sour shift, uses cobalt–molybdenum catalysts tolerant of sulfur in gasification service.

Mechanism

Two broad mechanisms are discussed: a redox (regenerative) mechanism, in which CO reduces the catalyst surface and water re-oxidises it, releasing H2; and an associative mechanism involving adsorbed intermediates such as formate or carboxyl species. Which dominates depends on the catalyst and operating conditions.
