Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376 – 27 June 444) was the Patriarch of Alexandria from 412 to 444 and a central figure in the Christological controversies of the 5th century. A prolific theologian and a Doctor of the Church, he is best known for his conflict with Nestorius, Patriarch of Constantinople, over the title Theotokos ("God-bearer") for the Virgin Mary.

Early life and patriarchate

Cyril succeeded his uncle Theophilus as patriarch in 412 after a contested election. His tenure was marked by assertive use of episcopal power: conflicts with the imperial prefect Orestes, the expulsion of Jews from Alexandria after communal violence, and the murder of the philosopher Hypatia by a Christian mob in 415, an event for which Cyril's responsibility has been debated ever since.

Nestorian controversy

When Nestorius taught that Mary should be called Christotokos rather than Theotokos, Cyril responded with letters insisting on the unity of Christ's person. His Third Letter to Nestorius, with its Twelve Anathemas, became a touchstone. At the Council of Ephesus (431), convened under Emperor Theodosius II, Cyril's party deposed Nestorius; the council affirmed Mary as Theotokos, and Nestorianism was condemned. In 433 Cyril accepted the Formula of Reunion with John of Antioch, balancing his emphasis on Christ's unity with Antiochene concern for Christ's full humanity.

Writings and legacy

Cyril wrote extensive biblical commentaries (on John, Isaiah, and the Minor Prophets) and treatises on the Trinity and Christology; his formula "one nature of God the Word incarnate" (mia physis) became foundational for later Miaphysite theology. He is venerated as a saint, with feast days of 27 June (West) and 9 June/18 January (East).
