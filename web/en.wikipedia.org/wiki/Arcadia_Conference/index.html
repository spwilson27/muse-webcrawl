Arcadia Conference

Arcadia Conference
DatesDecember 22, 1941 – January 14, 1942
LocationWashington, D.C., United States
ParticipantsFranklin D. Roosevelt, Winston Churchill
OutcomeDeclaration by United Nations; Germany-first strategy; Combined Chiefs of Staff

The Arcadia Conference (codenamed Arcadia) was a series of military and political meetings between the British and American governments held in Washington, D.C., from 22 December 1941 to 14 January 1942, shortly after the United States entered the Second World War. President Franklin D. Roosevelt hosted Prime Minister Winston Churchill and their senior military staffs.

Agreements

The conference confirmed the "Germany first" (or "Europe first") grand strategy: despite the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor, the defeat of Nazi Germany was agreed to be the Allies' first priority. The two governments established the Combined Chiefs of Staff, based in Washington, to coordinate Anglo-American strategy, along with combined boards for allocating munitions and shipping.

On 1 January 1942, during the conference, representatives of 26 nations signed the Declaration by United Nations, pledging to employ their full resources against the Axis powers and not to make a separate peace. The declaration adopted the principles of the Atlantic Charter and gave the Allied coalition its enduring name — later carried into the postwar United Nations organisation.

Significance

Arcadia set the institutional and strategic framework for Anglo-American cooperation for the rest of the war. It also established a unified American command structure (the Joint Chiefs of Staff) and agreed on initial measures, including American bomber forces deploying to Britain and the reinforcement of positions in the Pacific.
