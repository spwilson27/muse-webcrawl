Health at Every Size

Health at Every Size (HAES) is a weight-inclusive approach to public health and health care that promotes health-enhancing behaviours — such as balanced nutrition, enjoyable physical activity and self-care — without focusing on weight loss or a specific body weight. It emerged from the fat acceptance movement and critiques of weight-centred health policy.

Principles

The HAES framework, maintained by the Association for Size Diversity and Health, rests on principles including weight inclusivity, health enhancement, respectful care, eating for well-being and life-enhancing movement. Advocates argue that weight stigma itself harms health and that long-term weight loss is rarely sustained.

Evidence and criticism

Some studies of HAES interventions report improvements in blood pressure, blood lipids, eating behaviour and psychological measures without weight loss. Critics, including many obesity researchers, argue that the approach understates the health risks of severe obesity and that the evidence base is small and short-term. HAES remains a debated position within medicine and public health.
