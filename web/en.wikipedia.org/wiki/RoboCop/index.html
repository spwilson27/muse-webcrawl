RoboCop is a 1987 American science fiction action film directed by Paul Verhoeven and written by Edward Neumeier and Michael Miner. In a near-future, crime-ridden Detroit, terminally wounded police officer Alex Murphy (Peter Weller) is resurrected by the megacorporation Omni Consumer Products (OCP) as RoboCop, a cyborg law enforcer who gradually recovers fragments of his human memory. The film blends extreme violence with sharp satire of corporate power, privatization, gentrification, and American media culture.

Plot

OCP has privatized Detroit's police force and plans to demolish old Detroit to build "Delta City." After the malfunctioning ED-209 enforcement droid kills an executive in a boardroom demonstration, executive Bob Morton proposes the RoboCop program. Officer Alex Murphy is brutally murdered by Clarence Boddicker's gang; OCP salvages his brain and body parts into a heavily armed cyborg programmed with four prime directives — the fourth, classified, prevents him from acting against OCP executives. Partnered with his former colleague Anne Lewis, RoboCop wages a brutally effective campaign against crime, but recovered memories lead him to investigate his own murder, ultimately confronting Boddicker and the corrupt OCP vice president Dick Jones.

Production

Neumeier conceived the story after working on Blade Runner. Dutch director Paul Verhoeven initially rejected the script before embracing its satirical and Christ-like allegorical dimensions. The film was shot in Dallas in 1986. Rob Bottin designed the RoboCop suit and the stop-motion ED-209. The film's graphic violence initially earned an X rating; cuts were made for an R.

Reception and legacy

The film was a critical and commercial success, praised for its action, satire, and thematic depth, and it won an Academy Award for Best Sound Effects Editing. It spawned a media franchise: sequels RoboCop 2 (1990) and RoboCop 3 (1993), animated and live-action television series, comics, video games, and a 2014 remake. A statue of RoboCop was erected in Detroit in 2025 after a long-running crowdfunding campaign.
