Dolma (Turkish: dolma) is a family of stuffed dishes found in Ottoman cuisine, encompassing vegetables and leaves wrapped around a filling of rice, minced meat, or both. The word derives from the Turkish verb dolmak, "to be stuffed". Common varieties include grape leaves stuffed with rice and herbs (also called sarma in Turkish, dolmade in Greek, and warak enab in Arabic), as well as stuffed peppers, zucchini, eggplant, cabbage leaves, tomatoes, and onions.

Dolma dishes spread across the former Ottoman Empire into Balkan, Caucasian, Central Asian, and Middle Eastern cuisines, with regional variants in Azerbaijan, Armenia, Greece, Iran (where they are called dolmeh), Iraq, and the Levant. In 2017, UNESCO inscribed dolma making and sharing in Azerbaijan on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Preparation

For grape-leaf dolma, blanched leaves are wrapped around a mixture of rice, herbs such as dill, mint, and parsley, aromatics, and sometimes minced lamb or beef; olive-oil versions without meat ("yalancı dolma", "liar's dolma") are served cold. The rolls are packed tightly in a pot, weighted, and simmered in water, stock, or a lemon or tomato sauce. Meat-filled vegetable dolmas are usually served hot, often with yogurt or a tomato-based sauce; in Greek cuisine an egg-lemon sauce (avgolemono) is common.

Varieties

  • Yaprak sarma / dolmades — stuffed grape leaves.
  • Lahana sarma — stuffed cabbage leaves.
  • Biber dolması — stuffed peppers.
  • Midye dolma — stuffed mussels, a street food of Istanbul.
  • Soğan dolması — stuffed onions.
  • Kalamar dolması — stuffed squid.

History

Stuffed vegetables appear in medieval Arab and Persian cookery, and the dish family was elaborated in the imperial kitchens of Ottoman Constantinople, from where it diffused throughout the empire's provinces. Local variants took on the produce, fats, and seasonings of each region.
