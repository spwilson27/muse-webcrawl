Tōmei Expressway

The Tōmei Expressway (東名高速道路, Tōmei Kōsoku-dōro) is a national expressway on the island of Honshū in Japan. Operated by Central Nippon Expressway Company, it runs from Tokyo westward through Kanagawa, Shizuoka and Aichi prefectures to Komaki, north of Nagoya, a distance of about 347 kilometres. It was Japan's first major intercity expressway corridor linking the capital with the Chūbu region.

History

Construction began in the 1960s during Japan's postwar economic boom, and the expressway opened in stages between 1968 and 1969. Together with the Meishin Expressway, which continues from Nagoya toward Kobe, it formed the first continuous high-speed road link between Tokyo and the Kansai region, mirroring the historic Tōkaidō route.

Route

The expressway begins at the Tōmei Tokyo Interchange in Setagaya, Tokyo, and passes Yokohama, Atsugi, Shizuoka, Hamamatsu and Toyohashi before ending at Komaki. It is a major freight and passenger corridor, and chronic congestion led to the construction of a parallel route, the Shin-Tōmei Expressway, which opened in stages from 2012.
