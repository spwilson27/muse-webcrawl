Royal Liverpool Golf Club

Royal Liverpool Golf Club, Hoylake
Location: Hoylake, Wirral, England
Established: 1869
Type: Private links
Total holes: 18
Tournaments hosted: The Open Championship, Walker Cup

The Royal Liverpool Golf Club, commonly known as Hoylake, is a golf club in Wirral, Merseyside, England. Founded in 1869, it is one of the oldest and most prestigious links courses in England and a regular venue for The Open Championship.

History

The club was founded in 1869 on the site of the racecourse of the Liverpool Hunt Club. The course was laid out by Robert Morris of St Andrews and George Morris, and later redesigned by Harry Colt in the 1920s, with further changes by Donald Steel and Martin Hawtree for modern championship play. The club received its "Royal" designation from the Duke of Connaught in 1871.

Hoylake hosted the first Open Championship held in England in 1897, won by Harold Hilton, himself a Hoylake member. The club was the home of the legendary amateur John Ball, the first Englishman to win The Open (1890) and winner of eight Amateur Championships.

The Open Championship

Royal Liverpool has hosted The Open Championship on numerous occasions, including victories by Bobby Jones (1930, part of his Grand Slam), Peter Thomson (1956), Roberto De Vicenzo (1967), Tiger Woods (2006), Rory McIlroy (2014) and Brian Harman (2023). Woods's 2006 victory was notable for his strategy of using his driver only once all week, navigating the burnt, fast-running links with irons from the tee.

The course is famous for its internal out of bounds near the first and practice ground, and for finishing holes played into the prevailing wind off the Irish Sea.
