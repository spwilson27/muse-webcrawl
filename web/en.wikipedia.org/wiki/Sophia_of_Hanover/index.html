Sophia (14 October 1630 – 8 June 1714) was Electress of Hanover from 1692 to 1698 as the wife of Ernest Augustus. The youngest daughter of Frederick V of the Palatinate and Elizabeth Stuart, daughter of James I of England, she was named heir presumptive to the British thrones by the Act of Settlement 1701. She died a few weeks before Queen Anne, so the succession passed to her son, who became George I—the first monarch of the House of Hanover. All subsequent British monarchs descend from her, and she remains the ancestress through whom the Protestant succession to the British crown is legally traced.

Early life

Sophia was born in exile in The Hague, where her parents, the "Winter King and Queen" of Bohemia, had fled after losing the Palatinate in the Thirty Years' War. Raised in the Dutch Republic, she was noted for her intelligence and wit. In 1658 she married Ernest Augustus of Brunswick-Lüneburg, who became the first Elector of Hanover in 1692.

Life in Hanover

At the court of Hanover, Sophia created the great baroque gardens of Herrenhausen and corresponded with leading intellectuals, most famously the philosopher Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, a close friend and frequent guest. Her memoirs and letters are valued sources for the court culture of the period.

The succession

The English Act of Settlement 1701 settled the crown, after Anne, on "the most excellent Princess Sophia... and the heirs of her body being Protestants," bypassing dozens of Catholic claimants with stronger hereditary rights. Sophia died at Herrenhausen in June 1714, aged 83, reportedly after hurrying to shelter from a sudden shower in the gardens. Her son George Louis succeeded Anne on 1 August 1714 as George I.
