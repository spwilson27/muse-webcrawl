The Kadisha Valley (Arabic: وادي قاديشا), also romanized as the Qadisha Valley, is a gorge carved by the Kadisha River in northern Lebanon, in the Bsharri and Zgharta Districts of the North Governorate. The valley, whose name derives from an Aramaic word meaning "holy", is one of the most important early Christian monastic settlements in the world.

History

From the early centuries of Christianity, the valley's caves, cliffs, and terraces sheltered hermits and monastic communities, including Maronite monks who fled persecution. Its monasteries — among them Qannubin, the Monastery of Saint Anthony of Kozhaya, and the Monastery of Our Lady of Hawqa — became centres of religious life. The Maronite Patriarchate was seated at Qannubin for centuries.

World Heritage status

In 1998 UNESCO inscribed the valley, together with the nearby Forest of the Cedars of God (Horsh Arz el-Rab), as a World Heritage Site, recognising its outstanding universal value as a sacred landscape and a refuge of early Christianity.
