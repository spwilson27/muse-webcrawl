Large extra dimensions

Large extra dimensions are a concept in theoretical particle physics, associated with the ADD model proposed by Nima Arkani-Hamed, Savas Dimopoulos and Gia Dvali in 1998, in which the universe contains extra spatial dimensions that are large compared to the Planck length, while the Standard Model fields are confined to a four-dimensional brane.

Motivation

The model addresses the hierarchy problem: gravity appears far weaker than the other fundamental forces. In ADD, gravity spreads into the extra dimensions, diluting its apparent strength, while the true fundamental scale of gravity could be as low as the TeV scale. The observed Planck scale then arises from the volume of the extra-dimensional space.

Experimental tests

Large extra dimensions predict deviations from Newton's inverse-square law of gravitation at sub-millimetre distances, missing-energy signals from graviton emission at colliders, and possible production of microscopic black holes at particle accelerators. Searches at the Large Hadron Collider and precision short-range gravity experiments have constrained the size and number of extra dimensions but found no confirmed evidence for them.
