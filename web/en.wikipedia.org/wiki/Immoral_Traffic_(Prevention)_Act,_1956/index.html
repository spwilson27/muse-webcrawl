Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956

Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956
Parliament of India
Enacted: 1956 (as the Suppression of Immoral Traffic in Women and Girls Act)
Amended: 1978, 1986

The Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956 (ITPA) is the principal Indian legislation dealing with trafficking for commercial sexual exploitation. It was originally enacted as the Suppression of Immoral Traffic in Women and Girls Act, 1956 (SITA), following India's ratification of the 1949 UN convention on the suppression of trafficking.

Provisions

The Act penalises keeping a brothel, living on the earnings of prostitution, procuring or detaining persons for prostitution, and soliciting in public places. The 1986 amendment renamed the Act, made it gender-neutral in coverage of victims, and increased penalties. The Act does not criminalise prostitution itself, but many activities around it.

Criticism

Commentators have criticised provisions on soliciting for falling heavily on sex workers themselves, and reform proposals have periodically sought to shift penalties toward traffickers and clients.
