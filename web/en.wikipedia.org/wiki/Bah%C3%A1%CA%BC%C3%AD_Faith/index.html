The Baháʼí Faith is a monotheistic religion founded in the 19th century by Baháʼu'lláh. It teaches the essential worth of all religions, the unity of all people, and the progressive revelation of religious truth through Messengers of God including Abraham, Moses, Buddha, Jesus, Muhammad, the Báb, and Baháʼu'lláh.

Beliefs and teachings

Core principles include the oneness of God, the oneness of religion, and the oneness of humanity. Baháʼís advocate the elimination of prejudice, the equality of women and men, universal education, the harmony of science and religion, and the establishment of a world commonwealth of nations. The faith has no clergy; its affairs are administered by elected councils at local, national, and international levels.

History

The religion emerged in Persia from the Bábí movement founded by the Báb in 1844. Baháʼu'lláh, exiled from Persia, declared himself the messenger foretold by the Báb in 1863. He spent the remaining decades of his life as a prisoner and exile in the Ottoman Empire, eventually confined near Acre in present-day Israel. Baháʼís have faced persecution in Iran, where the faith is not officially recognised.

World centre

The Baháʼí World Centre is located in Haifa and Acre, Israel, and includes the Shrine of the Báb on Mount Carmel, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
