Airports of London

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The metropolitan area of London, England, United Kingdom is served by six international airports and several smaller airfields. Together they make up the busiest airport system in the world by passenger numbers. The six airports are Heathrow, Gatwick, Stansted, Luton, London City, and Southend.

History

London's first airport was at Croydon, which served as the capital's main airport in the interwar years before closing in 1959. Heathrow opened in 1946 on the site of a small airfield and grew to become Britain's principal gateway. Successive governments debated a third runway at Heathrow and other expansion schemes for decades; the need for additional capacity in the South East has been a recurring subject of British politics.

Traffic

In a typical pre-pandemic year, the London airport system handled more than 180 million passengers, with Heathrow alone accounting for roughly 80 million. The system recovered strongly after the COVID-19 pandemic, and Heathrow set new passenger records in the mid-2020s.

Other airfields

Smaller airfields serving the London area include Biggin Hill, Farnborough (across the Hampshire border, a major business aviation airport), Northolt (a Royal Air Force station also used by private flights), and London Heliport at Battersea.
