Warnock Report

The Warnock Report is the common name for Special Educational Needs (1978), the report of the Committee of Inquiry into the Education of Handicapped Children and Young People in the United Kingdom. The committee was chaired by the philosopher Mary Warnock and had been appointed in 1974.

Recommendations

The report replaced the old statutory categories of "handicap" with the broader concept of special educational needs, estimating that up to one in five children might have such needs at some point in their schooling. It recommended that children with special needs be educated in mainstream schools wherever possible — an approach that came to be known as integration, and later inclusion — and introduced the idea of a "statement" of a child's needs, giving parents a formal role in assessment and placement decisions. It also called for improved teacher training, better provision for children under five and for young people over sixteen, and closer cooperation between education, health and social services.

Legacy

Most of the report's proposals were enacted in the Education Act 1981, transforming special education in England and Wales. Warnock herself later revisited her conclusions, publishing a critical pamphlet in 2005 in which she questioned aspects of inclusion policy and the statementing system as it had developed.
