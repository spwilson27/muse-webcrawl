Howard Aiken

Howard Hathaway Aiken
Born: 8 March 1900, Hoboken, New Jersey, U.S.
Died: 14 March 1973, St. Louis, Missouri, U.S.
Known for: Harvard Mark I, Harvard Mark II
Field: Computer science, physics

Howard Hathaway Aiken (March 8, 1900 – March 14, 1973) was an American physicist and a pioneer in computing, being the original conceptual designer behind IBM's Harvard Mark I computer.

Early life and education

Aiken was born in Hoboken, New Jersey, and raised in Indianapolis. He studied electrical engineering at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, worked for the Madison Gas and Electric Company, and later earned his PhD in physics from Harvard University in 1939.

The Mark computers

While working on his doctorate, Aiken encountered tedious systems of nonlinear equations and conceived a large-scale automatic calculating machine. With support from IBM — in particular Thomas J. Watson — the electromechanical Harvard Mark I (officially the Automatic Sequence Controlled Calculator) was completed and dedicated at Harvard in 1944. It was followed by the Mark II (1947), the fully electronic Mark III, and the Mark IV (1952).

Later career

Aiken founded Harvard's Computation Laboratory and established one of the first degree programs in computer science. After retiring from Harvard in 1961 he moved to Florida, founded an engineering consultancy, and was a professor at the University of Miami. He received the Harry H. Goode Memorial Award in 1964 and the IEEE Edison Medal in 1970.
