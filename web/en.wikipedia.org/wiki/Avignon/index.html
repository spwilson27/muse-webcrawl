Avignon

Avignon (Provençal: Avinhon) is a commune in south-eastern France, in the department of Vaucluse, on the left bank of the Rhône. It is famous as the seat of the papacy from 1309 to 1376, when seven successive popes resided there rather than in Rome. Its historic centre, including the Palais des Papes and the Pont Saint-Bénézet, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

A settlement of the Gallic Cavares tribe, later Roman Avennio, Avignon passed through Burgundian, Frankish and Provençal hands. In 1309 Pope Clement V moved the papal court to Avignon under French influence, beginning the Avignon Papacy; his successors built the vast fortified Palais des Papes, the largest Gothic palace in Europe. Gregory XI returned the court to Rome in 1377, but the Western Schism (1378–1417) saw rival "antipopes" again seated in Avignon. The city remained papal territory until annexed by revolutionary France in 1791.

Culture

Avignon hosts the Festival d'Avignon, founded in 1947 by Jean Vilar, one of the world's leading performing-arts festivals, each July. The half-ruined Pont Saint-Bénézet (the "Pont d'Avignon" of the famous children's song Sur le pont d'Avignon) extends partway across the Rhône. Well-preserved medieval ramparts encircle the old town.
