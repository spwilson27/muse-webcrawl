U.S. Route 101, or U.S. Highway 101 (US 101), is a north–south United States Numbered Highway that runs along the West Coast of the United States, through California, Oregon, and Washington. It is one of the original U.S. Highways established in 1926 and is famously associated with the California coast, the redwood forests, and the historic El Camino Real corridor.

Route description

The highway's southern terminus is in Los Angeles, where it runs north through the San Fernando Valley, Ventura, and Santa Barbara, then turns inland through San Luis Obispo and the Salinas Valley before reaching the San Francisco Bay Area. North of San Francisco it crosses the Golden Gate Bridge and continues through the redwoods of Mendocino and Humboldt counties. In Oregon it follows the Pacific coastline closely, and in Washington it loops around the Olympic Peninsula before ending near Olympia.

History

Large portions of US 101 in California have been upgraded to freeway or bypassed by Interstate 5, but the route remains a major scenic and regional corridor. In Southern California the segment through downtown Los Angeles is part of the Hollywood Freeway and the Santa Ana Freeway. The highway's three-digit number reflected the original numbering scheme in which the westernmost principal north–south route was 101.
