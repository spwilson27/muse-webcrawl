Artemisia (plant)

Artemisia is a large, diverse genus of plants belonging to the daisy family Asteraceae, with between 200 and 400 species. Common names for various species in the genus include mugwort, wormwood, and sagebrush.

Description

Artemisia species grow in temperate climates of both hemispheres, usually in dry or semi-dry habitats. Many have silvery, aromatic foliage due to trichomes and volatile oils.

Uses

Artemisia absinthium (wormwood) flavours absinthe and vermouth. A. dracunculus is tarragon. A. annua (sweet wormwood) is the source of artemisinin, a key antimalarial drug, work recognised by Tu Youyou's 2015 Nobel Prize. Mugwort is used in traditional medicine and cuisine in Asia.

Ecology

Sagebrush species dominate large areas of western North America and are crucial habitat for sage-grouse.
