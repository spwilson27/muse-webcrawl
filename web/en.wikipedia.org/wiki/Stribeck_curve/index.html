Stribeck curve

The Stribeck curve is a fundamental concept in tribology, named after the German engineer Richard Stribeck. It plots the coefficient of friction in a lubricated sliding or journal bearing against a dimensionless parameter combining lubricant viscosity, sliding speed and applied load (often called the Hersey number), thereby showing how friction depends on the lubrication regime.

Lubrication regimes

At low values of the parameter, the bearing operates in boundary lubrication: the surfaces are separated only by adsorbed molecular films and friction is high. As speed or viscosity rises, the system passes through mixed lubrication, where friction falls steeply to a minimum, and then into hydrodynamic (full-film) lubrication, in which a complete fluid film separates the surfaces and friction slowly rises again with viscous shear. Elastohydrodynamic lubrication governs concentrated contacts such as gears and rolling bearings.
