Aneurin "Nye" Bevan (15 November 1897 – 6 July 1960) was a Welsh Labour politician, best known as the Minister of Health who founded the National Health Service (NHS) in 1948. One of the most gifted orators of his generation, he led the left wing of the Labour Party in the 1950s.

Early life

Bevan was born in Tredegar, Monmouthshire, the son of a coal miner, and went down the pit at thirteen. A scholarship took him to the Central Labour College in London. Returning to Wales, he became a trade-union organiser, prominent in the 1926 General Strike, and in 1929 was elected MP for Ebbw Vale, a seat he held for life.

Minister of Health

In Clement Attlee's post-war government, Bevan, the youngest cabinet member, was given the combined housing and health brief. Against fierce opposition from the medical profession, he established the NHS on 5 July 1948: a comprehensive health service free at the point of use, funded from general taxation. He also oversaw a large council-house building programme. In 1951 he resigned from the cabinet in protest at the introduction of NHS charges for dentures and spectacles to pay for rearmament.

Later years

The "Bevanites" formed the left faction of Labour through the 1950s. Bevan served as shadow foreign secretary and deputy leader, and surprised supporters in 1957 by opposing unilateral nuclear disarmament. His book In Place of Fear (1952) remains a classic statement of democratic socialism. He died in 1960.
