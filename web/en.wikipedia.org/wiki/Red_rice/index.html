Red rice

Red rice
Type: Whole-grain rice varieties with red bran
Notable forms: Bhutanese red rice, Camargue red rice, Thai red cargo rice, Kerala matta
Weedy form: Red rice (Oryza sativa f. spontanea), a major rice-field weed

Red rice is a variety of rice that is coloured red by anthocyanin pigments in its bran layer. It is eaten unhulled or partially hulled, has a nutty flavour, and retains more fibre and micronutrients than polished white rice. Well-known forms include Bhutanese red rice, the medium-grain staple of Bhutan; Camargue red rice from the Rhône delta in France; Thai red cargo rice; and the rosematta (Kerala matta) rice of South India.

Nutrition

Like other whole-grain coloured rices, red rice provides magnesium, zinc, iron, and B vitamins, and its antioxidant anthocyanins are concentrated in the bran. It generally has a lower glycaemic index than white rice and requires a longer cooking time.

Weedy red rice

The term also denotes a weedy form of rice (Oryza sativa f. spontanea) that infests rice fields worldwide. Weedy red rice competes aggressively with the crop, shatters its grain early, and contaminates harvests; its reddish pericarp downgrades milled grain. It is a persistent problem in direct-seeded rice systems, managed partly through herbicide-tolerant Clearfield varieties and certified clean seed.

Other uses

Red yeast rice — rice fermented with the mould Monascus purpureus — is a distinct product used as a food colouring and in traditional Chinese medicine; it is the source of the natural statin monacolin K.
