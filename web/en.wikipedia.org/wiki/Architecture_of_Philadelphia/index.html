Architecture of Philadelphia

Philadelphia City Hall (1901), long the tallest habitable building in the world

The architecture of Philadelphia spans more than three centuries, from early colonial brick rowhouses to some of the most significant works of American modernism. The city preserves one of the largest collections of 18th-century architecture in the United States, alongside landmark examples of Federal, Greek Revival, Victorian, and International Style design.

Colonial and Federal eras

Founded in 1682 on William Penn's grid plan, Philadelphia grew with brick rowhouses in neighbourhoods such as Society Hill and Elfreth's Alley, often called the nation's oldest residential street. Independence Hall (1732–1753), originally the Pennsylvania State House, is the era's best-known building. After independence, Federal and Greek Revival styles flourished in works by William Strickland and Thomas U. Walter.

Victorian and skyscraper eras

Frank Furness brought a vigorous, idiosyncratic Victorian style to buildings such as the Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts. Philadelphia City Hall, completed in 1901 in the French Second Empire style, was for years the tallest habitable building in the world and remains the largest municipal building in the United States.

Modern era

The PSFS Building (1932) was the first International Style skyscraper in the United States. Louis Kahn, who taught at the University of Pennsylvania, designed the Richards Medical Research Laboratories and left a lasting mark on the city's architectural culture. A long-standing gentlemen's agreement kept buildings below the statue of William Penn atop City Hall until One Liberty Place broke the height limit in 1987.
