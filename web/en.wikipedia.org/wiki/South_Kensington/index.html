South Kensington is a district of West London, England, in the Royal Borough of Kensington and Chelsea. It is best known as the home of several of Britain's great national museums and of Imperial College London, concentrated around Exhibition Road.

Albertopolis

Much of the district's character derives from the cultural quarter nicknamed "Albertopolis", developed after the Great Exhibition of 1851 under the influence of Prince Albert. Profits from the exhibition were used to purchase land on which were built the Victoria and Albert Museum, the Natural History Museum, the Science Museum, the Royal Albert Hall, the Royal Colleges of Art and Music, and Imperial College London. Exhibition Road, the quarter's spine, was remodelled in 2011 as a shared-space streetscape.

Character

Around the museum quarter lie elegant Victorian stucco terraces and garden squares such as Queen's Gate and Onslow Square. The area has a notable French community, centred on the Lycée Français Charles de Gaulle, and is sometimes called a "French quarter" of London. South Kensington tube station, on the District, Circle and Piccadilly lines, serves the district; a pedestrian tunnel runs from the station to the museums.
