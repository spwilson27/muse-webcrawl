Postage stamps and postal history of Germany

This is a survey of the postage stamps and postal history of Germany. Before unification in 1871, numerous German states and free cities issued their own stamps; afterwards the German Empire, the Weimar Republic, the Third Reich, the occupation zones, West and East Germany, and the reunified Federal Republic each produced distinctive issues closely tracked by philatelists.

Early posts and the German states

The Thurn-und-Taxis family operated an imperial postal service across the Holy Roman Empire for centuries. After the introduction of adhesive stamps, the first German state issue was Bavaria's "Schwarzer Einser" (Black One Kreuzer) of 1 November 1849. Other states followed, including Saxony, Prussia, Hanover, Württemberg, Baden, and the free cities of Hamburg, Bremen, and Lübeck. The Austro-German Postal Union of 1850 standardized rates among members.

Empire and Republic

The North German Confederation issued stamps from 1868, and the German Empire from 1872, beginning with the "eagle with shield" (Brustschild) issues. The Germania definitives became emblematic of Imperial and Weimar philately. During the 1923 hyperinflation, denominations climbed into the billions of marks. German colonies and foreign post offices (in China, Morocco, and the Ottoman Empire) received their own overprinted issues.

Division and reunification

After 1945, Allied occupation zones issued provisional stamps, followed by the Bizone and the separate issues of the Federal Republic of Germany (Bund) and the German Democratic Republic (DDR) from 1949. West Berlin also had its own issues inscribed "Deutsche Bundespost Berlin." Following reunification in 1990, a single German postal administration resumed; stamps are now issued by Deutsche Post AG.
