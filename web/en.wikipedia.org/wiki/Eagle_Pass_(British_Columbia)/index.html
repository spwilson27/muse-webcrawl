Eagle Pass (British Columbia)

Eagle Pass (elevation 550 m / 1,804 ft) is a mountain pass through the Monashee Mountains in British Columbia, Canada, between Sicamous on Shuswap Lake and Revelstoke on the Columbia River. It carries both the Trans-Canada Highway (Highway 1) and the Canadian Pacific Railway main line.

History

The pass was discovered for the railway in 1865 by Walter Moberly, a surveyor and engineer working on route-finding for a transcontinental railway; according to legend, he followed a flight of eagles through the valley, giving the pass its name, and blazed a tree with the claim that the pass would carry the railway to the Pacific. Eagle Pass was indeed the route chosen for the Canadian Pacific Railway, and it was at Craigellachie, near the western side of the pass, that the Last Spike of the transcontinental railway was driven on November 7, 1885.

Geography

The pass follows the Eagle River westward from Revelstoke through the Monashees. It is lower and less avalanche-prone than the higher Rogers Pass used further east across the Selkirks, but the highway and railway diverge after Revelstoke: both use Eagle Pass, then cross Rogers Pass to reach Golden. The community of Craigellachie and the Last Spike monument stand near the summit area of the route.
