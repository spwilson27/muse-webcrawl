Precision Strike Missile

The Precision Strike Missile (PrSM) is a surface-to-surface ballistic missile developed by Lockheed Martin for the United States Army. It is designed to replace the MGM-140 ATACMS, offering roughly twice the range — beyond 500 kilometres in its first increment — while allowing two missiles to be carried in each launch pod of the M270 MLRS and M142 HIMARS launchers, doubling their magazine depth compared with ATACMS.

Development

The programme grew out of the Army's Long Range Precision Fires effort. Lockheed Martin's design won the competition against a Raytheon entry, and flight testing began at White Sands Missile Range in 2019. The withdrawal of the United States from the Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces Treaty in 2019 removed the 500 km ceiling that had constrained the design, enabling later increments to reach substantially farther.

Service

The first production PrSM missiles were delivered to the US Army in December 2023, achieving an early operational capability as Increment 1. Later increments are planned to add a multi-mode seeker for engaging moving land and maritime targets (Increment 2), extended range (Increment 3 and beyond), and the ability to launch from autonomous vehicles. Australia has joined the programme as a partner and intends to acquire and co-produce the missile.
