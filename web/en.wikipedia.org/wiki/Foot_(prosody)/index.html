Foot (prosody)

The foot in prosody is the basic repeating rhythmic unit that forms part of a line of poetry. A foot usually consists of a combination of stressed and unstressed syllables (in accentual-syllabic verse) or of long and short syllables (in the quantitative verse of classical Greek and Latin). A line of verse is described by the type of foot it uses and the number of feet per line: for example, iambic pentameter consists of five iambs per line.

History

The classification of feet derives from the metrical theory of ancient Greek and Roman poetry, where verse was quantitative and built from patterns of long and short syllables. English prosodists adapted the classical terminology to the stress-based metre of English verse, so that an English iamb is a weak–strong pair. Debates continue among prosodists about whether foot division genuinely reflects the rhythm of English poetry, or whether other analytical frameworks (such as metrical phonology) describe it better.

Line length

Lines are named by the number of feet they contain: monometer (1), dimeter (2), trimeter (3), tetrameter (4), pentameter (5), hexameter (6), heptameter (7), and octameter (8). Thus the classical dactylic hexameter of Homer and Virgil has six dactyls (or substitutions) per line, while the iambic pentameter of Shakespeare and Milton has five iambs.
