Automotive industry in France


Image placeholder: French car factory

The automotive industry in France is one of the oldest and largest in the world. France was the world's leading automobile producer in the early 1900s and has remained a major centre of production, organised chiefly around the Renault Group and the French operations of Stellantis (Peugeot, Citroën and DS Automobiles).

History

French makers pioneered the automobile: Panhard et Levassor and Peugeot built cars from 1891, and De Dion-Bouton was briefly the world's largest manufacturer. Louis Renault founded his company in 1899 and André Citroën in 1919. Iconic postwar models included the Citroën Traction Avant (1934) and 2CV (1948), the Renault 4 (1961) and the Citroën DS (1955). After a series of mergers — Peugeot's takeover of Citroën (1976) creating PSA, Renault's alliance with Nissan (1999), and the 2021 merger of PSA with Fiat Chrysler to form Stellantis — the industry consolidated around two groups.

Production and employment

French plants assemble roughly 1.3–1.5 million vehicles in recent years, with further production by French groups abroad. The wider sector, including suppliers such as Valeo, Forvia and Michelin, employs several hundred thousand people. Government policy supports electrification through purchase bonuses and battery "gigafactory" investment in northern France.
