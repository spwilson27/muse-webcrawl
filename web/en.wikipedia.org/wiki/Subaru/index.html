Subaru (スバル) is the automobile manufacturing division of Japanese transportation conglomerate Subaru Corporation (formerly known as Fuji Heavy Industries, renamed in 2017). The name "Subaru" is the Japanese name for the Pleiades star cluster, which appears stylised in the company's six-star logo — a reference to the stars also invoked in the merger of companies that formed Fuji Heavy Industries, itself descended from the Nakajima Aircraft Company.

History

Fuji Heavy Industries began producing automobiles in the 1950s, beginning with the Subaru 360 minicar in 1958. Subaru introduced the Leone in 1971 and pioneered mass-market all-wheel drive passenger cars with the Leone 4WD station wagon in 1972. Symmetrical All-Wheel Drive and horizontally opposed "boxer" engines became the brand's technical hallmarks.

Products and identity

Well-known models include the Impreza (and its rally-derived WRX and WRX STI variants), the Legacy, the Outback crossover, the Forester SUV, the Crosstrek (XV), and the BRZ sports coupe developed jointly with Toyota. Subaru of Indiana Automotive in Lafayette, Indiana, is the brand's only manufacturing plant outside Japan.

Motorsport

Subaru achieved international fame in rallying: Subaru World Rally Team cars, prepared by Prodrive and famously driven by Colin McRae, Richard Burns and Petter Solberg, won three manufacturers' and three drivers' World Rally Championship titles in the 1990s and 2000s in their signature blue-and-yellow livery. Subaru withdrew from the WRC at the end of 2008 but remains active in rallying and circuit racing through Subaru Tecnica International (STI), its performance division.

Markets

Subaru sells the great majority of its cars in North America, where the brand has cultivated associations with safety, outdoor lifestyles, and — particularly since the 1990s — loyal followings including among LGBTQ customers, a noted case in marketing history.
