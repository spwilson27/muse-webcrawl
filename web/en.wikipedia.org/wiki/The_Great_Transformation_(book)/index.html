The Great Transformation (1944) is Karl Polanyi's most famous work.

The Great Transformation: The Political and Economic Origins of Our Time is a 1944 book by the Austro-Hungarian economic historian Karl Polanyi, written during his exile in England and the United States during World War II. It is a work of economic history and social theory that explains the rise of market society in 19th-century Europe and the catastrophes that followed.

Argument

Polanyi argues that the self-regulating market economy was a radical, state-engineered creation of the 19th century, not a natural development: "laissez-faire was planned." The attempt to treat land, labor, and money as commodities — "fictitious commodities," since none was produced for sale — disembedded the economy from society and devastated communities. Society protected itself in a "double movement": as market expansion proceeded, counter-movements arose demanding protection, from factory laws to, ultimately, fascism and socialism. Polanyi traces the collapse of the 19th-century order — the balance of power, the gold standard, the liberal state, and the market economy — to the tensions of this utopian experiment.

Influence

Long overshadowed by Keynes and Hayek, the book's influence grew from the 1970s onward in economic sociology, anthropology, and political science, and it is now regarded as a classic critique of market liberalism. Its concepts of embeddedness and the double movement remain standard tools of analysis.
