Albert Hermann Dietrich (28 August 1829 – 20 November 1908) was a German composer and conductor, best remembered today for his close friendship with Johannes Brahms and Robert Schumann and for his part in a celebrated musical collaboration.

Life and career

Dietrich studied composition in Dresden and at the Leipzig Conservatory. In Düsseldorf in 1851 he entered the Schumanns' circle; it was in Dietrich's home in October 1853 that the young Brahms was introduced to Robert and Clara Schumann, a meeting that changed Brahms's life. From 1861 Dietrich served as court music director at Oldenburg, where he championed Brahms's works, retiring to Berlin in 1890.

The F-A-E Sonata

In 1853 Dietrich, Schumann and Brahms jointly composed a violin sonata for their friend Joseph Joachim, based on the notes F–A–E, standing for Joachim's motto Frei aber einsam ("free but lonely"). Dietrich wrote the substantial first movement. The complete F-A-E Sonata was published only in 1935.

Works

Dietrich's own compositions include a Symphony in D minor (Op. 20), the opera Robin Hood, chamber music and choral works. His memoirs of Brahms, published in 1898, remain a valuable primary source on the composer's early years.
