Grand Duchess Anastasia Nikolaevna (18 June 1901 – 17 July 1918) was the youngest daughter of Tsar Nicholas II of Russia and Tsarina Alexandra. She was executed with her parents, her three sisters, her brother Alexei and members of the imperial household by Bolshevik revolutionaries in Yekaterinburg in July 1918.

Life

Anastasia grew up at the Alexander Palace at Tsarskoye Selo with her sisters Olga, Tatiana and Maria — the four were known by the acronym OTMA — and her younger brother, the haemophiliac heir Alexei. She was known for a lively, mischievous temperament. During the First World War she visited wounded soldiers in palace hospitals. After the February Revolution the family was held under house arrest and eventually exiled to Tobolsk and then Yekaterinburg.

Survival rumours and identification

For decades rumours claimed Anastasia had survived the execution, and several women — most famously Anna Anderson — claimed to be her; DNA testing in the 1990s disproved Anderson's claim. The Romanov remains were excavated near Yekaterinburg, and in 2007–2009 DNA analysis confirmed the remains of all seven members of the imperial family, closing the question. Anastasia was canonised by the Russian Orthodox Church as a passion bearer.
