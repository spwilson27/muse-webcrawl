Outdoor ethics

"Pack it in, pack it out": a core outdoor ethic

Outdoor ethics is the set of principles and practices concerned with minimising the environmental and social impact of outdoor recreation. It aims to guide the behaviour of hikers, campers, anglers, hunters, climbers and other users of natural areas so that ecosystems, wildlife and other visitors are disturbed as little as possible.

Leave No Trace

The best-known codification is Leave No Trace (LNT), whose seven principles, developed in the United States and now promoted internationally, are:

  1. Plan ahead and prepare
  2. Travel and camp on durable surfaces
  3. Dispose of waste properly
  4. Leave what you find
  5. Minimise campfire impacts
  6. Respect wildlife
  7. Be considerate of other visitors

The roots of outdoor ethics reach back to conservationists such as Aldo Leopold, whose "land ethic" in A Sand County Almanac (1949) argued for an ethical relationship between people and the land. As backcountry visitation surged in the mid-20th century, land management agencies promoted "pack it in, pack it out" and similar campaigns; these were formalised into the Leave No Trace programme through cooperation between US federal agencies and the National Outdoor Leadership School in the 1990s.

Related codes

Related codes include the Countryside Code in England and Wales, the Scottish Outdoor Access Code, angling and hunting ethics concerning fair chase and catch limits, and tread lightly principles for motorised recreation.
