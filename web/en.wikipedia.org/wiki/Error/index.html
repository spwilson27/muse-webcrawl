Error

An error (from the Latin error, meaning "wandering") is an action which is inaccurate or incorrect. In some usages, an error is synonymous with a mistake. The term is used across many disciplines: in statistics, an error is the difference between a computed, estimated or measured value and the true value; in engineering and computing it denotes a deviation from intended behaviour; and in law a reversible error can overturn a judgment.

In science and statistics

In experimental science, errors are classified as random errors, which arise from unpredictable fluctuations in measurement, and systematic errors, which consistently bias results in one direction. The statistical concept of observational error underlies error analysis, error bars and confidence intervals. In numerical analysis, approximation errors and rounding errors are studied to assess the reliability of computations.

In computing

In computing, an error may be a software bug, an error message displayed to a user, or an exceptional condition handled by error-handling code. Error detection and correction techniques — checksums, parity bits and error-correcting codes — allow systems to detect and sometimes repair corrupted data.

Human error

In safety science and human factors, human error refers to actions or decisions that produce unintended outcomes, a central concern in aviation, medicine and process industries. Approaches such as error management distinguish slips, lapses and mistakes, and emphasise system design that tolerates and recovers from error rather than relying solely on individual vigilance.
