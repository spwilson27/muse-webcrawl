Fruit tree pruning

Fruit tree pruning is the cutting and removal of selected parts of a fruit tree to control its growth, shape its structure, remove dead or diseased wood, and improve the quantity and quality of its fruit. It is a fundamental practice in orchard management and home fruit growing.


Winter pruning of an apple tree

Purposes

Pruning encourages a strong framework of scaffold branches able to bear heavy crops, admits light and air into the canopy, which reduces fungal disease and helps fruit ripen, and keeps the tree at a manageable size for harvesting. Removing crossing, crowded or damaged branches prevents bark rubbing and wounds through which pathogens can enter.

Timing

Most pruning of deciduous fruit trees is done during dormancy in late winter, before bud break. Stone fruits such as cherries and plums are often pruned in summer in some climates to reduce the risk of silver leaf disease. Light summer pruning can also be used to control vigour and encourage fruit bud formation.

Techniques

Two basic cut types are used. Heading cuts shorten a branch, stimulating bushy growth below the cut. Thinning cuts remove an entire branch at its origin, opening the canopy without stimulating dense regrowth. Common training systems include the open centre (vase), central leader, and espalier forms. Cuts should be made just outside the branch collar so the tree can seal the wound by forming callus tissue.
