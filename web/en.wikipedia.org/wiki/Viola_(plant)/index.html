Viola is a genus of flowering plants in the violet family, Violaceae, and the largest genus in that family, with roughly 680 species distributed mainly through the temperate Northern Hemisphere and at altitude in the tropics and Andes. Its members are commonly known as violets and pansies.

Description

Violas are usually perennial or annual herbs, occasionally small shrubs. The flowers are bilaterally symmetrical, with five petals: two upper, two lateral, and a lower petal that is often spurred and veined as a nectar guide for pollinating insects. Many species also produce inconspicuous self-pollinating (cleistogamous) flowers later in the season. Seed capsules split explosively, and many seeds bear oil-rich appendages (elaiosomes) that attract ants, which disperse them.

Cultivation and uses

The garden pansy (Viola × wittrockiana) is one of the world's most popular bedding plants, bred from species including V. tricolor (heartsease). Sweet violet (V. odorata) is grown for perfume and for crystallised flowers used in confectionery. Violets have long served as emblems — of Athens in antiquity, of modesty in Victorian floriography, and as the state flower of several U.S. states, including Illinois and New Jersey.
