Paschal candle

The Paschal candle is a large, white candle used in liturgies of Western Christianity — Roman Catholic, Anglican, Lutheran and others — during the Easter season. It symbolises the risen Christ, the Light of the World, and recalls the pillar of fire that guided Israel in the Exodus. It is distinct from, though lit during, the Paschal Triduum.

Blessing and use

At the Easter Vigil the candle is blessed, inscribed with a cross, the Greek letters Alpha and Omega, and the numerals of the current year, and studded with five grains of incense representing the wounds of Christ. Lit from the newly blessed fire, it is carried in procession into the darkened church while the deacon sings Lumen Christi ("The Light of Christ"), and the congregation lights small candles from it; the Exsultet, or Easter Proclamation, is then sung beside it.

Through the year

The candle stands near the altar throughout Eastertide until Pentecost. For the rest of the year it is placed near the baptismal font: new Christians are baptised with its light, and at funerals it stands by the coffin as a sign of the hope of resurrection.
