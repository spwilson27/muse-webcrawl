Chance (magazine)

Chance is a quarterly magazine of the American Statistical Association (ASA), published in partnership with Taylor & Francis. Founded in 1988, it is written for a broad audience of statisticians and statistically interested readers rather than for specialists, presenting statistics, probability, and data analysis through articles about sports, gambling, medicine, public policy, history, and everyday life — in keeping with its subtitle, "a magazine for people interested in the analysis of data".

History and scope

Chance was launched by Springer in 1988 with the statistician William Kruskal among its founding advocates (the magazine's "Editor's Notebook" tradition traces to its first editors). It later passed to the American Statistical Association, which publishes it with Taylor & Francis. The magazine's mission is to communicate the excitement and usefulness of statistical thinking to a wide readership; articles are refereed but written to be accessible, with minimal mathematics and an emphasis on real-world data.

Content

Regular features have included columns on sports statistics ("A Statistician Reads the Sports Pages"), visual data display ("Visual Revelations", long written by Howard Wainer), the history of statistics ("Looking Back"), statistics in the media, and humour ("A Feuilleton"). Special issues have addressed topics such as baseball, election forecasting, forensic science, and data visualisation. The magazine also publishes interviews with prominent statisticians and obituaries and historical retrospectives.
