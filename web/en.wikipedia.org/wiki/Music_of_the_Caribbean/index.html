Music of the Caribbean

The music of the Caribbean comprises a diverse group of musical genres originating in the Caribbean region, shaped by the blending of African, European and Indigenous musical traditions brought together through colonisation and slavery. Caribbean music has had a profound influence on global popular music, giving rise to genres including calypso, reggae, salsa, soca, dancehall, merengue, bachata, zouk and reggaeton.

Characteristics

Common features include syncopated rhythms derived from African drumming, call-and-response singing, and the use of percussion instruments such as congas, bongos, timbales and the steelpan — invented in Trinidad and Tobago and widely regarded as the only acoustic instrument invented in the 20th century. Rhythmic cells such as the clave, tresillo and habanera patterns underpin many Afro-Caribbean styles.

Genres by region

Spanish Caribbean

Cuba produced son cubano, mambo, cha-cha-chá, bolero and rumba, and was central to the development of salsa in New York. Puerto Rico contributed plena and bomba, and later reggaeton. The Dominican Republic is the home of merengue and bachata.

Anglophone Caribbean

Trinidad and Tobago gave the world calypso and soca and the steelband tradition of Carnival. Jamaica developed mento, ska, rocksteady, reggae, dub and dancehall, with reggae inscribed by UNESCO on its list of intangible cultural heritage in 2018. Barbados is known for spouge and tuk, and the Bahamas for rake-and-scrape and Junkanoo music.

Francophone Caribbean

Haiti is known for compas (konpa) and rara; Martinique and Guadeloupe developed zouk with the band Kassav', alongside older biguine and gwo ka traditions.

Influence

Caribbean musicians and diaspora communities in London, New York and Toronto spread these styles worldwide, influencing jazz, hip hop, R&B and electronic dance music.
