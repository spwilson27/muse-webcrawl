Thiepval Memorial
LocationThiepval, Somme, France
ArchitectSir Edwin Lutyens
Unveiled1 August 1932, by Edward, Prince of Wales
CommemoratesMore than 72,000 missing British and South African soldiers of the Somme, 1915–1918

The Thiepval Memorial to the Missing of the Somme is a war memorial near the village of Thiepval, France, commemorating more than 72,000 British and South African soldiers who died in the Battles of the Somme during the First World War and who have no known grave. It is the largest Commonwealth memorial to the missing in the world.

Design

Designed by Sir Edwin Lutyens and built between 1928 and 1932, the memorial is a vast arch of red brick and stone, 45 metres high, formed of interlocking arches of four orders. The names of the missing are carved on panels of Portland stone on the piers of the arches, arranged by regiment. On the top terrace, reached by staircases, a Stone of Remembrance bears the inscription "Their Name Liveth For Evermore." Behind the memorial lies an Anglo-French cemetery of 300 British and 300 French graves, symbolising the joint sacrifice of the two armies. The names of sixteen Victoria Cross recipients are among those commemorated.

History and commemoration

Thiepval village, on high ground, was a German strongpoint captured during the 1916 Battle of the Somme at enormous cost. The memorial was unveiled on 1 August 1932 by the Prince of Wales in the presence of the President of France. A visitor centre opened in 2004. The memorial remains a focal point of Somme commemoration; major ceremonies marked the centenary of the battle on 1 July 2016. As remains are identified, names are removed from the memorial and the soldiers receive named burials.
