Tehri Dam

The Tehri Dam is a multi-purpose rock and earth-fill embankment dam on the Bhagirathi River in Uttarakhand, India. At 260.5 metres (855 ft) high, it is one of the tallest dams in the world and the tallest in India. It is the main dam of the Tehri Hydro Development Corporation (THDC) complex.

Project

Construction began in 1978, and the first generating units were commissioned in 2006. The dam's power station has an installed capacity of 1,000 MW, with the associated Koteshwar Dam downstream adding 400 MW; a 1,000 MW pumped-storage plant was added later. The reservoir supplies irrigation water and drinking water to Delhi and Uttar Pradesh.

Controversy

The project faced decades of protests led by environmentalist Sunderlal Bahuguna and others over the submergence of the old town of Tehri, displacement of more than 100,000 people, and fears about dam safety in the seismically active Himalayan region.
