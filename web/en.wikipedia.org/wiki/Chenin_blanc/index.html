Chenin blanc is a versatile white wine grape originating in the Loire Valley of France, documented there since the 9th century.

Characteristics

Naturally high in acidity and prone to noble rot, Chenin can produce dry, demi-sec, lusciously sweet and sparkling wines, with flavours of apple, quince, pear, honey and chamomile, and an ability to age for decades.

Regions

Classic Loire appellations include Vouvray, Montlouis-sur-Loire, Savennieres and the sweet wines of Coteaux du Layon and Quarts de Chaume. South Africa grows more Chenin (long called Steen) than France, making everything from everyday whites to acclaimed old-vine bottlings; it is also planted in California, Australia and elsewhere.

Viticulture

The variety buds early and ripens unevenly, often requiring several passes through the vineyard at harvest, and yields must be controlled for quality.
