The Threepenny Opera

The Threepenny Opera (German: Die Dreigroschenoper) is a "play with music" by German dramatist Bertolt Brecht and composer Kurt Weill, in collaboration with translator Elisabeth Hauptmann. It premiered in Berlin in 1928 at the Theater am Schiffbauerdamm. An adaptation of John Gay's 1728 ballad opera The Beggar's Opera, it offers a socialist critique of the capitalist world.

Plot and songs

Set in a mock-Victorian London, the play follows the antihero Macheath ("Mack the Knife"), whose marriage to Polly Peachum enrages her father, the king of the beggars, who schemes to have Macheath hanged; a last-minute royal reprieve parodies operatic convention. Its songs — including "The Ballad of Mack the Knife" ("Mack the Knife"), "Pirate Jenny" and "The Cannon Song" — became standards; "Mack the Knife" was later popularised in English by Louis Armstrong and Bobby Darin.

Reception and influence

The work was a sensation in Weimar Berlin and was translated and performed worldwide, though it was banned in Nazi Germany. Marc Blitzstein's 1954 English-language adaptation ran for years off-Broadway and helped establish the work's international reputation. It remains one of the most influential works of twentieth-century musical theatre and a landmark of Brecht's "epic theatre".
