Larissa

Larissa (Greek: Λάρισα)
City and municipality in Thessaly, Greece
Capital of the Larissa regional unit
Population (2021): about 165,000 in the municipality
Site of Greece's First Ancient Theatre

Larissa (Greek: Λάρισα, Lárisa) is the capital and largest city of the Thessaly region of Greece and the seat of the Larissa regional unit. Built on the banks of the Pineios River in the fertile Thessalian plain, it is a major agricultural, commercial and transport centre, and one of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in Greece.

History

Legend held that Larissa was founded by Acrisius, grandfather of Perseus. The city was the chief seat of the Aleuadae, the leading aristocratic family of Thessaly, and its cavalry was renowned in classical Greece. Larissa flourished under Macedonian rule and in the Hellenistic and Roman periods; its two ancient theatres, agora and sanctuaries attest to its wealth. The philosopher-physician tradition associates the city with the school of Hippocrates, who was said to have died there.

In the Byzantine and Ottoman periods Larissa (Turkish: Yenişehir, "new city") remained the principal town of Thessaly and a centre of trade and textile production. It passed to the modern Greek state in 1881, when Thessaly was ceded by the Ottoman Empire. In the twentieth century the city grew rapidly, becoming an important railway and road junction and the base of the Hellenic Air Force headquarters and NATO's Joint Force Command elements in Greece.

Geography and economy

Larissa sits in the eastern Thessalian plain, Greece's principal agricultural region, producing cotton, cereals, fruit and livestock products. The climate is Mediterranean with hot, dry summers—among the hottest in Greece—and cool winters. Mount Olympus lies to the northeast and the Vale of Tempe, through which the Pineios reaches the sea, to the east.

Sights

The city's landmarks include the First Ancient Theatre (3rd century BC, seating some 10,000), a second smaller theatre, the fortress hill with Byzantine and Ottoman remains, the Alkazar park on the Pineios, and the Diachronic Museum of Larissa, whose collections span Thessalian history from the Palaeolithic onward. The city's football club, AEL, won the Greek championship in 1988, the only club from outside Athens and Thessaloniki to do so for decades.
