Power-on self-test

A typical POST screen reporting the memory test and detected devices.

A power-on self-test (POST) is a process performed by firmware or software routines immediately after a computer or other digital electronic device is switched on. The routines are part of the device's pre-boot sequence; if they do not complete successfully, the device usually cannot be used. On personal computers the POST is performed by the BIOS or UEFI firmware stored in ROM.

Functions

The POST resets and initialises the processor registers, verifies the integrity of the firmware itself, tests and sizes the system memory, identifies and initialises devices such as the keyboard, storage controllers and video adapter, and inventories expansion buses. The familiar single "beep" from many PCs indicates that the tests have passed. Once complete, control passes to a bootstrap loader, which locates and starts the operating system.

Error reporting

When the POST detects a fatal fault before the display is available, it reports the error through beep codes — patterns of short and long beeps whose meanings vary by manufacturer — or through a small two-digit display on the motherboard showing POST codes. Non-fatal errors are shown as on-screen messages, such as a keyboard error or a failed drive, sometimes with the option to continue booting. Diagnostic cards that read POST codes from an expansion bus are used by repair technicians for faults that prevent any display.

Other devices

Similar self-tests are performed by printers, routers, industrial controllers, avionics and many embedded systems, in which the test may include checks of sensors and actuators and the lighting of status indicators in sequence (a "lamp test"). Modern PCs with UEFI firmware and fast-boot options may abbreviate or skip parts of the traditional memory test to shorten start-up times.
