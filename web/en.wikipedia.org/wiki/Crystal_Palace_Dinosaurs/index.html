Crystal Palace Dinosaurs

The Crystal Palace Dinosaurs are a series of life-size sculptures of extinct animals, including dinosaurs, in Crystal Palace Park in south London. Commissioned in 1852 and unveiled in 1854, they were sculpted by Benjamin Waterhouse Hawkins under the scientific direction of the anatomist Richard Owen, and were the first dinosaur sculptures in the world. The models stand on artificial islands in a landscaped "geological court" whose rock strata illustrate successive geological periods.

The models

The sculptures include Iguanodon, Megalosaurus and Hylaeosaurus — the three genera on which Owen had founded the Dinosauria — together with ichthyosaurs, plesiosaurs, pterosaurs, a giant ground sloth and other prehistoric animals. Reflecting the limited fossil evidence of the 1850s, several reconstructions are now known to be inaccurate: the Iguanodon is shown as a heavy quadruped with its thumb spike placed on its nose like a horn.

Conservation

The dinosaurs were designated Grade I listed structures in 2007, a status shared by few sculptures. Restoration campaigns have repaired decades of weathering and vandalism, and the models remain among the best-known public artworks of Victorian science.
