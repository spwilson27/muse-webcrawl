Marina City is a mixed-use residential-commercial building complex in the River North district of downtown Chicago, Illinois, United States, occupying nearly an entire city block on State Street on the north bank of the Chicago River. The complex opened in the 1960s and consists of two 179-metre (587 ft), 65-story apartment towers, which have become iconic symbols of Chicago.

Designed by architect Bertrand Goldberg, the towers are famous for their distinctive cylindrical form and semicircular balconies, which give them the appearance of corn cobs. When finished, they were among the tallest reinforced concrete buildings in the world.

History

Marina City was commissioned by the Building Service Employees International Union (later the SEIU) as an investment intended to reverse the flight of city dwellers to the suburbs — a "city within a city" where residents could live, work, park, shop and be entertained without leaving the complex. Construction of the first tower began in 1961 and the towers were completed in 1964 and 1968. The complex originally included a saddle-shaped theatre building (now the House of Blues), an office building (now a hotel), a marina on the river for recreational boats, skating rink, bowling alley, shops and restaurants, with parking occupying the lower 19 floors of each tower in an exposed spiral ramp.

The project fell into financial difficulty and foreclosure in the 1970s; the residential towers were converted from rental apartments to condominiums in 1977.

Architecture

Each tower is built around a cylindrical concrete core containing elevators and services. Apartments radiate outward in wedge-shaped plans, nearly all with semicircular balconies. The repeated semicircular arcs of the balconies produce the celebrated "corncob" silhouette. Goldberg's design reflected his interest in organic, sculptural forms and in the social ideal of comprehensive urban living, expressed through curvilinear geometry rather than the rectilinear glass boxes of International Style modernism.

In popular culture

The towers appear on the cover of Wilco's 2002 album Yankee Hotel Foxtrot and have featured in numerous films and television programmes set in Chicago. The complex was designated a Chicago Landmark in 2016.
