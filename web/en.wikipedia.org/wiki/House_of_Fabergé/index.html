House of Fabergé

The House of Fabergé was a Russian jewellery firm founded in 1842 in Saint Petersburg by Gustav Fabergé and brought to international fame by his son Peter Carl Fabergé. The firm is best known for the jewelled Fabergé eggs created for the Russian imperial family between 1885 and 1917.

History

Gustav Fabergé, of Huguenot descent, opened a jewellery shop in Bolshaya Morskaya Street, Saint Petersburg, in 1842. His son Peter Carl Fabergé took over in 1870 and expanded the business into the largest jewellery enterprise in Russia, with branches in Moscow, Kyiv, Odesa, and London.

In 1885 Tsar Alexander III commissioned an Easter egg from the firm as a gift for the Empress Maria Feodorovna. The tradition continued under Nicholas II, producing 50 imperial eggs in total. Fabergé's workshops employed master goldsmiths such as Michael Perkhin and Henrik Wigström and produced hardstone figures, enamelled objects, and luxury items of every kind.

Decline and legacy

The firm was nationalised after the Russian Revolution of 1917, and the Fabergé family fled Russia. The Fabergé name passed through various owners in the 20th century and was later revived as a luxury brand. Original imperial eggs are now held by museums and collectors worldwide and rank among the most famous decorative art objects ever made.
