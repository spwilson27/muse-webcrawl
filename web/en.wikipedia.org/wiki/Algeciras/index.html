Algeciras (Spanish: [alxeˈθiɾas]) is a port city in the province of Cádiz, Andalusia, in southwestern Spain, on the Bay of Gibraltar opposite the Rock of Gibraltar. Its port is among the busiest in Europe and the largest in the Mediterranean by total cargo, profiting from its position on the Strait of Gibraltar, one of the world's great shipping crossroads.

History

The site was occupied in antiquity and refounded in 711 AD as al-Jazīra al-Khaḍrāʾ ("the Green Island") — the first city founded by the Muslim conquerors in Iberia and the source of the modern name. It was destroyed in 1369 by Muhammad V of Granada to prevent its use by Christian forces, and the area remained largely deserted after Gibraltar was taken in 1704, when refugees from Gibraltar resettled around the chapel of Algeciras. The modern city grew with the creation of the port at the end of the 19th century. In 1906 Algeciras hosted the international conference that attempted to resolve the First Moroccan Crisis between France and Germany.

Port and economy

The Port of Algeciras handles over 100 million tonnes of cargo a year, including major container transshipment, oil and ferry traffic across the strait to Tangier and Ceuta. The surrounding Campo de Gibraltar hosts petrochemical and steel industries. Despite its industrial character, the city is also a gateway to the beaches of the Costa de la Luz and the Los Alcornocales natural park. The flamenco guitarist Paco de Lucía was born in Algeciras in 1947.
