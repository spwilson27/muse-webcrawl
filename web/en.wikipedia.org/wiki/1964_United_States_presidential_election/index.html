The 1964 United States presidential election was the 45th quadrennial presidential election, held on November 3, 1964. Democratic incumbent Lyndon B. Johnson, who had become president after the assassination of John F. Kennedy, defeated Republican senator Barry Goldwater of Arizona in one of the largest landslides in American history.

Campaign

Johnson campaigned on the Great Society, civil rights legislation, economic growth and continuity after national trauma. Goldwater represented the rising conservative movement; his acceptance speech line that “extremism in the defense of liberty is no vice” and views on nuclear weapons and federal programs allowed Democrats to portray him as dangerous. The Johnson campaign's “Daisy” advertisement, aired once as a paid spot, became a landmark of political television.

Results and consequences

Johnson won 61.1 percent of the popular vote and 486 electoral votes. Goldwater carried Arizona and five Deep South states, where backlash against the Civil Rights Act of 1964 transformed regional politics. The Democratic landslide brought large congressional majorities that enacted Medicare, Medicaid, voting rights and other Great Society programs, while Goldwater's campaign laid foundations for later conservative victories.
