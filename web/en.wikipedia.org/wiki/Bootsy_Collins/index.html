William Earl "Bootsy" Collins (born October 26, 1951) is an American bass guitarist, singer and songwriter. Rising to prominence playing with James Brown in the early 1970s and later with George Clinton's Parliament-Funkadelic, he is regarded as one of the most influential bassists in funk, famed for his effects-laden, rubbery bass lines and flamboyant star-shaped "Space Bass".

Career

Collins formed The Pacemakers in Cincinnati with his brother Phelps "Catfish" Collins. In 1970, after most of Brown's band quit in a pay dispute, the Pacemakers were recruited as the new J.B.'s; the teenage Bootsy's playing drives Brown classics such as "Get Up (I Feel Like Being a) Sex Machine" and "Super Bad".

Joining Clinton's P-Funk collective in 1972, Collins adopted a cartoonish stage persona and co-wrote classics including "Give Up the Funk (Tear the Roof off the Sucker)" and "Flash Light". His own Bootsy's Rubber Band released a string of R&B hits, including "I'd Rather Be with You" (1976). He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997 as a member of Parliament-Funkadelic.

Influence

Collins's use of envelope filters and Mu-Tron effects, his emphasis on "the one", and his visual style influenced generations of funk, hip-hop and pop bassists. He remains an active collaborator and mentor, running the educational Funk University ("Funk U").
