The American Kennel Club (AKC) is a registry of purebred dog pedigrees in the United States. In addition to maintaining its pedigree registry, this kennel club also promotes and sanctions events for purebred dogs, including the Westminster Kennel Club Dog Show, an annual event which predates the official formation of the AKC. The AKC recognises about 200 dog breeds, organised into groups such as Sporting, Hound, Working, Terrier, Toy, Non-Sporting, and Herding.

History

The AKC was founded in 1884, modelled on The Kennel Club of England, by a group of American dog-show clubs seeking uniform rules and a national stud book. Its registry grew to become the largest purebred dog registry in the United States. Breeds not yet fully recognised are enrolled in the Foundation Stock Service programme while breed clubs work toward full recognition.

Activities

Beyond conformation dog shows, the AKC sanctions obedience, rally, agility, tracking, herding, lure coursing, and field trials, and administers the Canine Good Citizen program, founded in 1989 to reward well-mannered dogs and responsible owners. The organisation also supports canine health research through the AKC Canine Health Foundation, operates a museum, and advocates on legislation affecting dog ownership. It has been criticised by animal-welfare advocates over registration of dogs from large-scale commercial breeders and breed standards associated with health problems; the AKC responds that registration is not a guarantee of health or quality and promotes its breeder-inspection and education programs.
