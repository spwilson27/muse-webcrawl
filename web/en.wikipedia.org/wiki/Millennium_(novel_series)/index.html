Millennium is a series of crime novels originally created by Swedish author and journalist Stieg Larsson (1954–2004). The series features Lisbeth Salander, an antisocial, brilliant hacker and abuse survivor, and Mikael Blomkvist, an investigative journalist with the magazine Millennium, from which the series takes its name. It became one of the best-selling book series of all time, with sales exceeding 100 million copies worldwide.

Larsson's trilogy

Larsson, an editor of the anti-fascist magazine Expo, delivered three manuscripts shortly before his sudden death from a heart attack in 2004. They were published posthumously: The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo (Swedish title Män som hatar kvinnor, "Men Who Hate Women", 2005), The Girl Who Played with Fire (2006), and The Girl Who Kicked the Hornets' Nest (2007). The novels mix locked-room mystery, financial and political conspiracy, and an unflinching treatment of violence against women. A celebrated Swedish film trilogy (2009) starred Noomi Rapace as Salander; David Fincher's English-language adaptation of the first book appeared in 2011.

Continuation

Larsson's estate commissioned David Lagercrantz to continue the series, producing The Girl in the Spider's Web (2015) — adapted into a 2018 film — The Girl Who Takes an Eye for an Eye (2017), and The Girl Who Lived Twice (2019). Swedish author Karin Smirnoff subsequently took over with The Girl in the Eagle's Talons (2022) and further volumes, moving much of the action to northern Sweden.
