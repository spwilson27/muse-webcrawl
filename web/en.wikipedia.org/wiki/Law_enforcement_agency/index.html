Law enforcement agency

Police officers are the most visible personnel of law enforcement agencies

A law enforcement agency (LEA) is any government agency responsible for the enforcement of the law. Such agencies typically engage in activities such as deterring, investigating, and apprehending suspected criminals, as well as responding to emergencies and maintaining public order. The most common type of law enforcement agency is the police, but the term also covers bodies such as sheriff's offices, customs and border services, federal investigative bureaus, drug enforcement administrations, and regulatory inspectorates with enforcement powers.

Organisation and jurisdiction

Law enforcement agencies operate within defined jurisdictions, which may be national (federal), regional, or local. Their powers are typically conferred and limited by statute. In the United States, there are roughly 18,000 law enforcement agencies at federal, state, county, and municipal levels. In the United Kingdom, policing is principally carried out by territorial police forces, overseen nationally by bodies such as the National Crime Agency.

Duties and powers

Depending on the agency, personnel may be empowered to arrest, detain, search, and use reasonable force. Law enforcement agencies are generally distinguished from military organisations, although in some countries gendarmerie-type forces combine police duties with military organisation. International coordination between agencies is facilitated by organisations such as Interpol and Europol.

Oversight

Because of their coercive powers, law enforcement agencies are normally subject to oversight mechanisms including courts, legislatures, independent complaints bodies, and inspectorates, and their personnel may be held accountable for misconduct through disciplinary and criminal processes.
