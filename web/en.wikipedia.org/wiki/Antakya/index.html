Antakya is the capital of Hatay Province in southern Turkey, near the border with Syria. It stands on the Orontes River at the site of the ancient Hellenistic city of Antioch, one of the great cities of the ancient world and an early centre of Christianity.

History

Antioch was founded in 300 BCE by Seleucus I Nicator and became capital of the Seleucid Empire. Under Rome it grew into the third-largest city of the empire, famed for its colonnaded streets, the grove of Daphne, and its role in early Christianity — believers were first called "Christians" at Antioch (Acts 11:26). The city was repeatedly struck by earthquakes, captured by Persians in 540, and taken by Arab armies in 637. Crusaders seized it after the Siege of Antioch in 1098, and it became the capital of the Principality of Antioch until conquered by the Mamluks in 1268, after which it declined.

Under Ottoman rule Antakya was a modest town. After World War I it lay in the French Mandate of Syria; the Republic of Hatay joined Turkey in 1939 following a referendum.

2023 earthquake

Antakya was devastated by the February 2023 Turkey–Syria earthquakes, which destroyed much of the historic old town, including the Habibi Neccar Mosque and the city's synagogue and churches. Rebuilding efforts are ongoing.

Culture

The Hatay Archaeology Museum holds one of the world's finest collections of Roman mosaics. The city is noted for its religious diversity, its cave Church of St Peter, and its cuisine, including künefe, a syrup-soaked cheese dessert of which Antakya is a famed centre.
