Fukuoka (Japanese: 福岡市, Fukuoka-shi) is the capital city of Fukuoka Prefecture and the largest city on the island of Kyushu, Japan. Situated on the northern coast of Hakata Bay, it has long been a gateway between Japan and mainland Asia, lying closer to Seoul than to Tokyo. The modern city was formed in 1889 by the merger of the castle town of Fukuoka with the neighbouring port of Hakata, whose name survives in Hakata Station and Hakata-ku ward.

History

Hakata Bay was the landing place of the Mongol invasions of Japan in 1274 and 1281, repelled with the help of storms remembered as kamikaze. During the Edo period Fukuoka was the seat of the Kuroda clan, builders of Fukuoka Castle. The city grew rapidly after the Second World War into Kyushu's commercial and administrative hub.

Economy and culture

Fukuoka is known for its relatively open, service-based economy, a high concentration of start-ups, and Fukuoka Airport, unusually close to the city centre. It is famous for Hakata ramen — rich tonkotsu (pork bone) broth noodles served at open-air yatai food stalls along the Nakasu riverfront — and for festivals such as Hakata Gion Yamakasa, a July festival of racing festival floats, and Hakata Dontaku in May. The Fukuoka SoftBank Hawks baseball team plays at the Mizuho PayPay Dome. The city regularly ranks among Japan's most liveable and fastest-growing large cities.
