Nuevo León is a state in northeastern Mexico, bordered by Tamaulipas to the north and east, San Luis Potosí to the south, and Coahuila to the west, with a short stretch of border with Texas in the United States. Its capital and largest city is Monterrey, one of Mexico's most important industrial and commercial centres.

History

The region took its name from the Spanish province of León. Monterrey was founded in 1596 by Diego de Montemayor. During the nineteenth century the state was a frontier region affected by the Mexican–American War and French intervention. Industrialisation accelerated under governor Bernardo Reyes in the Porfirian era, with steel, brewing (Cuauhtémoc Brewery) and cement industries.

Economy and geography

Nuevo León consistently ranks among Mexico's states with the highest human development and income per capita, driven by manufacturing, nearshoring investment and services. The terrain ranges from the Sierra Madre Oriental — including the emblematic Cerro de la Silla — to semi-arid northern plains.
