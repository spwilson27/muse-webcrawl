Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM)
Specialty: Cardiology
Prevalence: ~1 in 500
Genetics: Often autosomal dominant
Genes: MYH7, MYBPC3 and others

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is a disease in which a portion of the heart muscle becomes hypertrophied without an obvious cause, creating functional impairment. It is a leading cause of sudden cardiac death in young athletes. The septum between the ventricles is commonly thickened, sometimes obstructing blood flow from the left ventricle (hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy).

Signs and symptoms

Many people are asymptomatic. Others develop breathlessness, chest pain, palpitations, light-headedness and syncope, particularly on exertion. Complications include heart failure, atrial fibrillation with stroke risk, and ventricular arrhythmias causing sudden death.

Causes and diagnosis

Most cases are inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern, caused by mutations in genes encoding sarcomere proteins such as MYH7 and MYBPC3. Diagnosis is typically by echocardiography showing unexplained left ventricular wall thickness of 15 mm or more; ECG, cardiac MRI and genetic testing assist. It must be distinguished from athlete's heart and hypertensive heart disease. See Cardiomyopathy and Echocardiography.

Management

Treatment includes beta blockers, the myosin inhibitor mavacamten for obstructive disease, septal myectomy or alcohol septal ablation for refractory obstruction, and implantable cardioverter-defibrillators for those at high risk of sudden death. Family screening is recommended. Most people with HCM have a normal life expectancy.
