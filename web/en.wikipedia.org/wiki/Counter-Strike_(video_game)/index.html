Counter-Strike is a tactical first-person shooter video game developed by Valve. It began in 1999 as a modification ("mod") of Half-Life created by Minh Le and Jess Cliffe; Valve hired the pair and acquired the rights, releasing the game commercially in 2000. It pits a team of counter-terrorists against a team of terrorists in round-based objective gameplay, and became one of the most influential and widely played shooters of all time.

Gameplay

Players join either the terrorist or counter-terrorist team. In the most popular scenario, bomb defusal, terrorists attempt to plant a C4 explosive at a bombsite while counter-terrorists try to stop them or defuse the bomb; a second scenario involves hostage rescue. Players earn money for kills, objectives and round wins, which they spend on weapons and equipment at the start of each round. Dead players wait until the next round, making each one-life round tense and tactical. Iconic maps include Dust II, Inferno, Nuke and Mirage.

Development and release

Le and Cliffe released the first beta in June 1999; the mod's popularity grew rapidly. Valve's retail release followed in November 2000, and version 1.6 (2003), bundled with Steam, became the definitive edition of the original game for years. The franchise continued with Counter-Strike: Condition Zero and Counter-Strike: Source (2004), Counter-Strike: Global Offensive (2012) and Counter-Strike 2 (2023).

Legacy

Counter-Strike was a foundational title for competitive gaming, headlining early esports leagues and tournaments, and its economy-based round system shaped the design of countless later tactical shooters. The original game maintained a large community for decades after release.
