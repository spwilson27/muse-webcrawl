Alcoa Corporation (a portmanteau of "Aluminum Company of America") is an American industrial corporation and one of the world's largest producers of aluminium. It operates bauxite mines, alumina refineries and aluminium smelters in numerous countries, and is headquartered in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania.

History

The company was founded in 1888 as the Pittsburgh Reduction Company, based on Charles Martin Hall's electrolytic process for producing aluminium cheaply (the Hall–Héroult process). It adopted the name Aluminum Company of America in 1907, shortened to Alcoa in 1999. For decades it held a virtual monopoly on aluminium production in the United States until antitrust action in 1945 (the United States v. Alcoa case) helped spur competition.

2016 split

In November 2016 the company split in two: Alcoa Corporation retained the upstream bauxite, alumina and aluminium businesses, while the downstream value-added products business became Arconic (later split into Howmet Aerospace and Arconic Corporation).

Operations

Alcoa operates in segments covering bauxite mining, alumina refining and aluminium smelting and casting, with major facilities in Australia, Brazil, Canada, Iceland, Norway, Spain and the United States. It has pursued low-carbon aluminium products, including its Sustana line, and in 2024 agreed to acquire Alumina Limited of Australia.
