Angus (Scottish Gaelic: Aonghas) is one of the 32 local government council areas of Scotland, a registration county and a lieutenancy area. The council area borders Aberdeenshire, Dundee City and Perth and Kinross. Mainland Angus is known for its rural landscape, its coastline on the North Sea, and the Angus Glens in the north-west, which form part of the southern edge of the Grampian Mountains.

History

Angus was historically a Pictish territory and later one of the traditional counties of Scotland, also known historically as Forfarshire after its county town of Forfar. The Declaration of Arbroath, asserting Scottish independence, was issued from Arbroath Abbey in 1320. The county is also associated with the "Red Harlaw" traditions and with the dramatic cliffs and smuggling history of its coast.

Geography and towns

The principal towns of Angus include Arbroath, Brechin, Carnoustie, Forfar, Kirriemuir, Monifieth and Montrose. Carnoustie is internationally known for its championship golf links, which regularly host The Open Championship. The region's economy is based on agriculture — including the famous Angus beef cattle and soft fruit — fishing, and tourism.
