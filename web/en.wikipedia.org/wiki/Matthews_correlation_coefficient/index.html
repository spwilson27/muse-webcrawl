Matthews correlation coefficient
FieldMachine learning, statistics
Introduced byBrian W. Matthews (1975)
Range−1 to +1

The Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) or phi coefficient is used in machine learning as a measure of the quality of binary (two-class) classifications, introduced by biochemist Brian W. Matthews in 1975. The MCC is defined similarly to Pearson's correlation coefficient and is generally regarded as a balanced measure that can be used even if the classes are of very different sizes.

The MCC returns a value between −1 and +1. A coefficient of +1 represents a perfect prediction, 0 no better than random prediction, and −1 indicates total disagreement between prediction and observation.

Definition

Given a confusion matrix of true positives (TP), true negatives (TN), false positives (FP) and false negatives (FN), the MCC is:

MCC = (TP·TN − FP·FN) / √((TP+FP)(TP+FN)(TN+FP)(TN+FN))

Properties

Unlike accuracy or the F1 score, the MCC takes into account all four entries of the confusion matrix, so it is not misleadingly high on imbalanced datasets. It is equivalent to the Pearson correlation coefficient between the observed and predicted binary classifications, and its square is related to the chi-squared statistic. The coefficient was first used by Matthews to compare protein secondary structure predictions and has since become a standard metric in bioinformatics and classification evaluation.

Multiclass generalisation

The MCC can be generalised to multiclass problems by extending the formula over the full confusion matrix, yielding a single score that reflects the correlation between predictions and ground truth across all classes.
