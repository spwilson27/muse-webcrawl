The Battle of the Mareth Line (16–31 March 1943) was an engagement of the Tunisia Campaign of the Second World War. The British Eighth Army under General Bernard Montgomery attacked the Mareth Line, a series of fortifications built by the French before the war along the Wadi Zigzaou in southern Tunisia, then held by the Italo-German army of General Giovanni Messe.

Background

After the Second Battle of El Alamein and the long pursuit across Libya, the Eighth Army reached the Mareth Line in February 1943. The position ran from the coast to the Matmata Hills and was considered formidable: a wide anti-tank ditch (the wadi itself), minefields, wire and concrete strongpoints. Montgomery planned a frontal attack (Operation Pugilist) combined with a wide "left hook" through the desert south of the hills, guided by the Long Range Desert Group, which had found a passable route through the Tebaga Gap.

The battle

Operation Pugilist began on the night of 20–21 March. The 50th (Northumbrian) Division forced a bridgehead across the wadi but could not exploit it: rain turned the ground to mud, anti-tank guns could not be got forward, and a counter-attack by the 15th Panzer Division on 22 March largely wiped out the bridgehead. Montgomery therefore reinforced the left hook, sending the New Zealand Corps under Lieutenant-General Bernard Freyberg — with the 2nd New Zealand Division and the 8th Armoured Brigade — through the Tebaga Gap. In Operation Supercharge II, on 26 March, the New Zealanders, supported by a concentrated artillery barrage and low-level air attacks, broke through the Tebaga defences. Threatened with encirclement, Messe withdrew from the Mareth Line on 27–28 March to a new position at Wadi Akarit.

Aftermath

The battle broke the strongest Axis position in southern Tunisia and forced the Axis forces into a shrinking perimeter. The Eighth Army followed up with the Battle of Wadi Akarit in April, and Tunis fell to the Allies in May 1943, ending the North African campaign.
