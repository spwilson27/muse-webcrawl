Julian's Persian expedition

DateMarch – July 363 AD
LocationMesopotamia, Sasanian Empire
CombatantsRoman Empire vs. Sasanian Empire
OutcomeSasanian victory; death of Emperor Julian

Julian's Persian expedition was a campaign of the Roman–Persian wars launched in 363 AD by the emperor Julian against the Sasanian Empire of Shapur II. The largest Roman invasion of Persia in decades, it ended in failure with Julian's death and the retreat of his army.

Background

Julian, the last non-Christian emperor, sought both to secure the eastern frontier and to win military glory. He assembled a large army at Antioch and advanced in two columns, while a fleet of over a thousand vessels moved down the Euphrates to supply the invasion.

Advance to Ctesiphon

The Romans defeated Persian forces and reached the Sasanian capital, Ctesiphon, winning a battle outside its walls. Unable to besiege the fortified city, and with his supplies strained, Julian burned his fleet and began a difficult retreat northward, harassed constantly by Persian attacks.

Death of Julian and aftermath

On 26 June 363, Julian was mortally wounded in a skirmish near Samarra. His successor, Jovian, concluded a humiliating peace treaty, surrendering Roman territories in Mesopotamia, including Nisibis and Singara, in return for safe passage home. The campaign is described in detail by the historian Ammianus Marcellinus, who accompanied it.
