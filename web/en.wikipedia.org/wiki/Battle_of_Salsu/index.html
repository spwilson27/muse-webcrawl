Battle of Salsu

The Battle of Salsu was fought in 612 AD between the Korean kingdom of Goguryeo and the Sui dynasty of China, during the Goguryeo–Sui War. A Goguryeo army commanded by Eulji Mundeok destroyed a huge Sui invasion force at the Salsu River — usually identified with the Chongchon River in present-day North Korea. It is one of the most celebrated victories in Korean military history.

Background

In 612 Emperor Yang of Sui invaded Goguryeo with an army the Chinese sources number at over a million men. A detached force of some 300,000 marched directly on the Goguryeo capital, Pyongyang. General Eulji Mundeok harassed the Sui army with feigned retreats and scorched-earth tactics, drawing it deep into Goguryeo territory while cutting its supplies; starving and exhausted, the Sui force began to withdraw.

The battle

According to the traditional account, Goguryeo engineers dammed the Salsu River. As the retreating Sui army was halfway across, the dam was released and the flood swept away thousands; Goguryeo cavalry then attacked the divided and drowning enemy. Korean and Chinese sources alike describe catastrophic losses: of the Sui detachment, legend says only about 2,700 men escaped back to Liaodong.

Aftermath

The defeat, together with further failed invasions in 613 and 614, wrecked Sui finances and prestige, hastening the dynasty's collapse in 618. Eulji Mundeok is honoured in Korea as a national hero.
