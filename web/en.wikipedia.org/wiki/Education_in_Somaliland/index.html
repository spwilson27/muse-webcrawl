Education in Somaliland

Education in Somaliland is provided by a mix of public schools, private institutions and Qur'anic schools, under the oversight of the Ministry of Education and Science of Somaliland, the self-declared republic in the Horn of Africa. Formal schooling is organised as eight years of primary education (four lower and four upper) followed by four years of secondary education.

Reconstruction

Most educational infrastructure in the region was destroyed during the Somali civil war of the late 1980s. After Somaliland declared independence from Somalia in 1991, communities, diaspora groups and aid organisations rebuilt schools largely from scratch. Primary education in public schools was declared free, and primary enrolment rose from a very low base to cover a majority of children, though participation remains lower for girls and for rural and nomadic populations.

Curriculum and languages

Somali is the language of instruction in early primary years, with English and Arabic introduced later; secondary education is taught mainly in English, and students sit national examinations at the end of primary and secondary cycles. Qur'anic schools (madrasas) remain an important parallel sector, particularly for young children.

Higher education

Universities founded since the 1990s include the University of Hargeisa, Amoud University in Borama, and Burao University. Because Somaliland's independence is not internationally recognised, its certificates and examinations lack formal recognition abroad, which complicates student progression and donor funding; the sector depends heavily on fees, diaspora remittances and international NGOs.
