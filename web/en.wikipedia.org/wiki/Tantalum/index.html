Tantalum is a chemical element with the symbol Ta and atomic number 73. It is a rare, hard, blue-grey, lustrous transition metal that is highly corrosion-resistant. It is part of the refractory metals group, widely used as components of strong high-melting-point alloys.

History

Tantalum was discovered in Sweden in 1802 by Anders Gustaf Ekeberg in minerals from Finland and Sweden. He named it after Tantalus, a figure from Greek mythology, because of the element's frustrating resistance to dissolving in acid — echoing Tantalus's punishment of standing in water he could not drink. For decades tantalum was confused with the very similar element niobium; Heinrich Rose showed in 1844 that the two were distinct, and Jean Charles Galissard de Marignac first isolated the metal in 1864 by reducing tantalum chloride with hydrogen.

Properties

Tantalum is extremely resistant to corrosion by acids at ordinary temperatures, being attacked appreciably only by hydrofluoric acid and hot concentrated sulfuric acid. It forms a protective oxide layer (tantalum pentoxide, Ta₂O₅), which underlies its main application in capacitors. The metal melts at 3,017 °C, among the highest melting points of all elements, and is ductile enough to be drawn into fine wire. Nearly all natural tantalum is the isotope tantalum-181; the rare isotope tantalum-180m is notable as a metastable nuclear isomer.

Occurrence and production

Tantalum occurs principally in the mineral tantalite and in coltan (columbite–tantalite) ores, mined notably in Australia, Brazil, Canada, and Central Africa, including the Democratic Republic of the Congo, where its extraction as a "conflict mineral" has drawn international scrutiny and supply-chain regulation.

Applications

The largest use of tantalum is in electronic capacitors, prized for high capacitance in small volumes, used in mobile phones, computers and automotive electronics. Tantalum alloys strengthen turbine blades and corrosion-resistant chemical plant equipment; the metal's inertness in body fluids makes it valuable for surgical implants, and tantalum carbide is used in cutting tools.
