Indian religions


Symbols of the Indian religions

Indian religions, also called Dharmic religions, are the religions that originated in the Indian subcontinent: principally Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism. Together they account for a substantial share of the world's population and share a family of concepts including dharma, karma, saṃsāra (the cycle of rebirth), and mokṣa or nirvāṇa (liberation from that cycle), though each tradition interprets these ideas differently.

History

The roots of Indian religions lie in the Vedic religion of the Indo-Aryans of the second millennium BCE, which evolved into the diverse traditions of Hinduism. In the sixth to fifth centuries BCE the śramaṇa movements — among them Buddhism and Jainism — challenged Vedic ritual authority and spread across Asia. Sikhism arose in the Punjab in the fifteenth century under Guru Nanak. Indian religions spread widely: Buddhism became a major religion across East and Southeast Asia, while Hindu and Sikh diasporas now span the globe.

Shared and distinctive themes

Hindu traditions generally accept the authority of the Vedas and worship a variety of deities within frameworks ranging from monism to polytheism. Buddhism rejects a creator god and the authority of the Vedas, centering on the Four Noble Truths and the Noble Eightfold Path. Jainism emphasizes radical nonviolence (ahiṃsā) toward all living beings. Sikhism is monotheistic, teaching devotion to one God (Ik Onkar), equality, and service. Ayyavazhi and various tribal and folk traditions of the subcontinent are also sometimes grouped among Indian religions.

Demographics

India today is about 80 percent Hindu, with significant Muslim, Christian, Sikh, Buddhist, and Jain minorities. Hinduism is the world's third-largest religion; Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism together count tens of millions of adherents worldwide.
