Season (television)

A television season groups episodes produced and broadcast together

In television, a season is a set of episodes of a television programme produced and broadcast within a defined period, typically corresponding to one production cycle and often to a part of the calendar year. In the United States the traditional broadcast season runs from September to May, while in the United Kingdom and some other countries the term series is used for the same concept.

Structure

A season usually comprises a fixed order of episodes — historically 22 to 26 for American network dramas, though cable and streaming productions commonly use 6 to 13. A season typically begins with a season premiere and ends with a season finale, which frequently resolves or escalates major story arcs, sometimes ending on a cliffhanger to encourage viewers to return.

Series may also be divided into half-seasons or blocks, with a mid-season break (hiatus) between groups of episodes, and networks sometimes air a mid-season replacement — a show that debuts midway through the season to fill a slot vacated by a cancelled series.

Development and production

Television seasons follow an annual cycle in the American industry: pilots are developed in winter, new series are announced at the spring upfront presentations, production takes place over the summer and through the broadcast year, and shows are renewed or cancelled largely on the basis of ratings, traditionally assessed during "sweeps" periods. A programme that survives for several seasons accumulates episodes toward the threshold — conventionally around 88 to 100 episodes — that makes it attractive for syndication.

Streaming era

Streaming services have loosened the seasonal model: entire seasons are often released at once for binge-watching, production schedules no longer follow the September–May calendar, and episode counts per season vary widely. Nevertheless, the season remains the standard unit for organising, marketing and awarding eligibility for television programmes.

Terminology in other countries

In British usage, a "series" denotes what Americans call a season, while "series" in American usage refers to the programme as a whole. Australian and other Commonwealth broadcasters generally follow the British convention, though American terminology has spread with global streaming platforms.
