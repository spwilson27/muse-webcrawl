Video game controversy

Video game controversies are societal and scientific disputes about the content and alleged effects of video games, particularly concerning violence, sexual content, and addiction. Such controversies have accompanied the medium since the 1970s arcade era and have periodically prompted legislative proposals, regulatory hearings and industry self-regulation.

Violence debates

Concerns about violent content intensified in the early 1990s with games such as Mortal Kombat and Night Trap, which led to hearings in the United States Congress and the creation of the Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB) in 1994. Later titles in the Grand Theft Auto series drew sustained criticism and litigation. In 2011 the U.S. Supreme Court ruled in Brown v. Entertainment Merchants Association that video games qualify for First Amendment protection and struck down a California law restricting sales of violent games to minors. Scientific research on whether violent games cause real-world aggression remains contested, with meta-analyses reaching differing conclusions.

Other controversies

Other recurring issues include the portrayal of women and minorities, gambling-like mechanics such as loot boxes, concerns about gaming addiction — recognised as "gaming disorder" in the World Health Organization's ICD-11 — and controversies over specific content, such as the "Hot Coffee" modification in Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas and disputed historical or political depictions. Moral panics over games are frequently compared by commentators to earlier panics over comic books, rock music and film.
