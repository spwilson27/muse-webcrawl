Household Cavalry

The Household Cavalry Mounted Regiment on parade in London

The Household Cavalry (HCav) is a corps of the British Army and forms part of the Household Division. It consists of two regiments: the Life Guards and the Blues and Royals (Royal Horse Guards and 1st Dragoons), which together provide both an armoured reconnaissance regiment and a mounted ceremonial regiment based in London. The Household Cavalry ranks first in the order of precedence of the British Army, reflecting its role as the sovereign's bodyguard.

Regiments

The Life Guards were formed in 1658 from Royalist exiles and gentlemen who had served Charles II in exile. The Blues and Royals were created in 1969 by the amalgamation of the Royal Horse Guards ("the Blues") and the 1st (Royal) Dragoons ("the Royals"). Both regiments wear distinctive ceremonial dress: the Life Guards in red tunics with white helmet plumes, the Blues and Royals in blue tunics with red plumes; both wear polished metal cuirasses.

Roles

The Household Cavalry Regiment (HCR), based at Bulford in Wiltshire, is a formation reconnaissance regiment equipped with armoured fighting vehicles such as the Scimitar and later the Ajax family. The Household Cavalry Mounted Regiment (HCMR), based at Hyde Park Barracks in Knightsbridge, London, carries out ceremonial duties: it provides the King's Life Guard at Horse Guards, escorts at state occasions, and the mounted bands and trumpet calls heard at events such as Trooping the Colour and state visits.

History

Household cavalry regiments have served in most major British campaigns, including the Battle of Waterloo in 1815, the Boer War, both world wars, the Falklands War, and operations in Iraq and Afghanistan. A famous episode was the action of Corporal of Horse Michael Flynn and the armoured cars of the Blues and Royals in the Falklands; in 1982 the regiments' ceremonial life was also scarred by the Hyde Park bombing, which killed soldiers and horses of the Blues and Royals.
