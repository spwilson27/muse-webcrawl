Headroom (audio signal processing)

In digital and analogue audio, headroom is the amount by which the maximum signal-handling capability of a system exceeds its nominal or operating level. In digital systems the absolute maximum is 0 dBFS, and recordings are typically made with peak levels below this to leave headroom; in broadcasting, European practice aligns nominal level so that peaks leave about 9 dB of headroom.

Purpose

Headroom accommodates transient peaks that are higher than the average signal level, preventing clipping and distortion. Live sound and recording engineers balance headroom against noise floor: recording too quietly wastes dynamic range and raises the relative noise, while recording too hot risks clipping transients.

Related concepts

Headroom is closely related to dynamic range and to the programme loudness standards used in broadcasting, such as EBU R 128 and ATSC A/85, which define alignment levels and permitted maximum true-peak levels for delivered audio.
