Geneva Accords (1954)

Geneva Accords
Signed20–21 July 1954
LocationGeneva, Switzerland
ContextEnd of the First Indochina War

The Geneva Accords were a set of agreements reached at the Geneva Conference of 1954 that ended the First Indochina War and temporarily partitioned Vietnam. They were signed on 20 and 21 July 1954 by France and the Viet Minh, following the French defeat at the Battle of Dien Bien Phu.

Terms

The accords provided for a ceasefire and the temporary division of Vietnam at the 17th parallel (the Ben Hai River), with Viet Minh forces regrouping to the north and French and State of Vietnam forces to the south. The division was explicitly provisional: nationwide elections to reunify the country were scheduled for July 1956, to be supervised by an International Control Commission of India, Canada and Poland. Laos and Cambodia were recognised as independent and neutral, and foreign troops were to be withdrawn.

Aftermath

The United States and the State of Vietnam did not sign the accords, though the U.S. issued a unilateral declaration taking note of them. The scheduled 1956 elections were never held, as the government of Ngo Dinh Diem in the south, backed by the United States, refused to participate. The partition hardened into two states — North Vietnam and South Vietnam — setting the stage for the Vietnam War.
