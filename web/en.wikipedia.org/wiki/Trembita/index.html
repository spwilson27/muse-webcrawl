Trembita

The trembita (Ukrainian: трембіта) is a long alpine horn made of wood, traditionally played by the Hutsul people of the Carpathian Mountains in Ukraine and neighbouring regions of Romania and Poland. It is among the longest wind instruments in the world, commonly measuring about 3 metres (10 feet) and sometimes more.

The instrument is made from a long piece of spruce or pine, split in half, hollowed out, rejoined and bound with rings of birch or willow bark. It has no finger holes or valves; the player produces the natural harmonic series by varying lip tension, like a natural trumpet.

Traditional use

Historically the trembita was a signalling instrument of mountain shepherds: its powerful sound carries over long distances across valleys, and it was used to announce events in the village — weddings, funerals, the start of the grazing season, or the approach of visitors. Distinct calls existed for different announcements, including a characteristic mournful call for deaths.

Modern use

Today the trembita is a symbol of Hutsul and Ukrainian highland culture, played at festivals and folk performances, and it features in Ukrainian ethnographic ensembles and film. In 2022, trembita calls were famously used in the Eurovision-winning Ukrainian entry's wider cultural moment, cementing its status as a national folk emblem.
