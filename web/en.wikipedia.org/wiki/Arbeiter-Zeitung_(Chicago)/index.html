The Arbeiter-Zeitung ("Workers' Newspaper") was a German-language daily newspaper published in Chicago from 1877 to 1931. Founded as a socialist labour paper, it became the leading voice of Chicago's revolutionary anarchist movement in the 1880s.

Haymarket affair

Under editor August Spies, the paper championed the eight-hour-day movement. After the Haymarket bombing of 4 May 1886, Spies and several associates were arrested; a famous "Revenge!" circular printed by the paper was used as prosecution evidence. Spies was hanged in 1887, and the paper, though suppressed for a time, resumed publication and continued for decades as an anarchist organ.

Legacy

The Arbeiter-Zeitung is a key primary source for the history of German-American radicalism and the early American labour movement.
