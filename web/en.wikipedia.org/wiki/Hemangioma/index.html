Hemangioma

A hemangioma is a usually benign vascular tumor derived from blood vessel cell types. The most common form, seen in infants, is an infantile hemangioma, a benign endothelial tumor that appears shortly after birth as a red or purple mark on the skin, often called a "strawberry mark". Hemangiomas can occur in the skin, subcutaneous tissue, muscle, and internal organs such as the liver and brain.

Types

Hemangiomas are classified by the type of vessel involved and their appearance. Capillary hemangiomas consist of small, closely packed capillaries; cavernous hemangiomas are composed of larger, dilated blood-filled spaces. Infantile hemangiomas typically grow rapidly during the first months of life and then slowly involute over several years, often resolving completely.

Diagnosis and treatment

Most cutaneous hemangiomas are diagnosed clinically. Imaging such as ultrasound or MRI may be used for deeper lesions or those affecting internal organs. Many require no treatment, but therapy — including beta-blockers such as propranolol, corticosteroids, laser therapy, or surgery — is used when lesions are large, ulcerated, or threaten vital structures such as the airway or eyes.
