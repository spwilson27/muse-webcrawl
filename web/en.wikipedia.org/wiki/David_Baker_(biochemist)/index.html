David Baker (born October 6, 1962) is an American biochemist and computational biologist who has pioneered methods to predict the three-dimensional structures of proteins and to design entirely novel proteins. He is a professor at the University of Washington School of Medicine and director of its Institute for Protein Design.

Rosetta and protein prediction

Baker's laboratory developed the Rosetta software suite, which predicts protein structures from amino-acid sequences by assembling fragments of known structures and minimising an energy function. To harness idle computing power worldwide, the group launched the distributed computing project Rosetta@home, and later the Foldit game, in which human players helped solve protein structures. The laboratory repeatedly led the CASP structure-prediction competitions before the advent of deep-learning methods such as AlphaFold, and subsequently incorporated those advances into tools including RoseTTAFold.

De novo protein design

Beyond predicting natural proteins, Baker's group designs proteins from scratch with specified shapes and functions — including novel enzymes, self-assembling nanomaterials, and a nanoparticle COVID-19 vaccine developed with colleagues. In 2024 he shared one half of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry "for computational protein design", the other half going to Demis Hassabis and John Jumper for protein structure prediction.
