Weierstrass M-test

In mathematics, the Weierstrass M-test is a test for determining whether an infinite series of functions converges uniformly and absolutely. It is named after the German mathematician Karl Weierstrass (1815–1897) and is the function-series analogue of the comparison test for series of numbers.

Statement

Suppose that (fn) is a sequence of real- or complex-valued functions defined on a set A, and that there is a sequence of non-negative numbers (Mn) such that |fn(x)| ≤ Mn for all n and all x in A, and such that the series Σn Mn converges. Then the series Σn fn(x) converges absolutely and uniformly on A.

More generally, the same result holds for functions taking values in any normed vector space (indeed any Banach space), with the absolute value replaced by the norm: if ‖fn(x)‖ ≤ Mn for all x and ΣMn converges, then Σfn converges uniformly.

Proof idea

The proof applies the comparison test pointwise to obtain absolute convergence at each x, and then uses the Cauchy criterion for uniform convergence: the tail of Σfn is bounded, uniformly in x, by the tail of the convergent series ΣMn, which can be made arbitrarily small.

Applications

The M-test is a standard tool for justifying the interchange of summation with integration and differentiation. For example, it shows that a power series converges uniformly on any closed disc strictly inside its circle of convergence (by bounding the terms with a geometric series), so its sum is continuous and can be integrated term by term. It is likewise used to prove that the series defining functions such as the Riemann zeta function on half-planes Re(s) ≥ 1 + δ converges uniformly, hence that the zeta function is analytic there. The test gives a sufficient, not necessary, condition: a series may converge uniformly even when no summable dominating sequence exists.
