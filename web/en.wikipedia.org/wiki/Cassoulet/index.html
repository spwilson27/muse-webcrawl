Cassoulet

Cassoulet is a rich, slow-cooked casserole of white beans and meat originating in the Languedoc region of southern France. It is named after the cassole, the deep earthenware pot in which it is traditionally cooked and served.

Place of originFrance (Languedoc)
Main ingredientsWhite beans, duck confit, sausage, pork
VariationsCastelnaudary, Toulouse, Carcassonne

Origins

Legend links cassoulet to the defence of Castelnaudary during the Hundred Years' War, when besieged townspeople pooled beans and meats into a communal stew. Whatever its precise origin, the dish became the defining speciality of the Lauragais plain, rich in haricot beans.

Regional versions

Three towns claim primacy. Castelnaudary's cassoulet uses white beans, duck or goose confit, pork and sausage. Toulouse adds Toulouse sausage and sometimes duck breast; Carcassonne may include mutton and, in season, partridge. A saying calls Castelnaudary the father, Carcassonne the son and Toulouse the Holy Spirit of cassoulet.

Preparation

Beans are soaked and simmered, layered with confit and meats in the cassole, covered with breadcrumbs, and baked slowly for hours. Cooks traditionally break the crust that forms and push it back into the stew several times — seven times, purists in Castelnaudary insist. See also French cuisine.
