Component car

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A component car being assembled from a supplied kit and donor parts

A component car, often called a kit car, is an automobile that is sold as a set of parts that a buyer assembles into a complete vehicle, rather than being purchased fully built from a manufacturer. Component cars are commonly purchased as a hobby, as a way to own a replica of a rare or expensive classic, or as a route into motorsport and custom car building at lower cost than a factory-built sports car.

How they are sold

Manufacturers typically supply a chassis and a body — frequently moulded in fibreglass — together with bespoke fittings and an assembly manual. The builder sources a donor car for the engine, gearbox, suspension, brakes, and other running gear, or buys those components separately. Some kits are sold in stages, from a bare body/chassis package up to a "comprehensive" kit containing nearly everything except the donor mechanicals. A few designs, such as those based on the Volkswagen Beetle floorpan, became famous for letting builders create dune buggies and replicas with minimal fabrication.

Replicas and original designs

Many component cars are replicas of celebrated models that are otherwise rare or valuable, such as the AC Cobra, Ford GT40, Lotus Seven, and classic Porsche Speedster. Others are original designs, including lightweight track-day cars and exoskeleton-style sports cars. The long-running British kit-car industry and builders in the United States, Australia, and elsewhere have produced thousands of designs since the 1950s.

Registration and regulation

Because a component car is not built by a recognized manufacturer, registering one for road use involves special procedures in most jurisdictions. In the United Kingdom, most kit cars must pass the Individual Vehicle Approval (IVA) test; in the United States, registration is handled state by state, often under "specially constructed vehicle" rules, with emissions requirements sometimes based on the donor engine's year. Builders must keep documentation of major components to prove the vehicle's identity and provenance.
