Three Seas Initiative

The Three Seas Initiative (3SI or TSI), also known as the Baltic, Adriatic, Black Sea (BABS) Initiative, is a forum of states in Central and Eastern Europe situated between the Baltic, Adriatic and Black Seas, launched in 2016 to strengthen regional cooperation in energy, transport and digital infrastructure, particularly along the north–south axis. Its participating states are members of the European Union, and the initiative is supported by partners including the United States and Germany.

History

The initiative was launched by Croatian president Kolinda Grabar-Kitarović and Polish president Andrzej Duda, and the first summit was held in Dubrovnik in August 2016. Subsequent summits took place in Warsaw (2017, attended by U.S. president Donald Trump), Bucharest (2018), Ljubljana (2019), Tallinn (2020, held partly virtually), Sofia (2021), Riga (2022), Bucharest (2023, where Greece became the thirteenth member) and Vilnius (2024).

Projects and investment

The initiative maintains a list of priority interconnection projects — gas interconnectors and LNG terminals, rail and road corridors such as Via Carpathia and Rail Baltica, and digital networks. The Three Seas Initiative Investment Fund, established in 2019 with backing from regional development banks, invests commercially in such cross-border infrastructure.

Context

The forum is intended to reduce the region's dependence on east–west infrastructure inherited from the Cold War era and on Russian energy supplies, a goal given added urgency by Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022; Ukraine and Moldova have been granted participating-partner status.
