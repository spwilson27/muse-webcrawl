Nickel (United States coin)

Value5 cents (0.05 USD)
Diameter21.21 mm
Composition75% copper, 25% nickel
Years of minting1866–present

A nickel, in American usage, is a five-cent coin struck by the United States Mint. Composed of cupronickel (75% copper and 25% nickel), the piece has been issued since 1866, having replaced the silver half dime. Five-cent pieces had been minted since 1792 under other names, but the term "nickel" properly applies to the cupronickel coinage.

Designs

Major designs include the Shield nickel (1866–1883), the Liberty Head "V" nickel (1883–1913), the Buffalo nickel (1913–1938), and the Jefferson nickel (1938–present), whose obverse bears a portrait of Thomas Jefferson and whose reverse since 2006 depicts Monticello. The rare 1913 Liberty Head nickel is among the world's most valuable coins.

Wartime issue

From 1942 to 1945, "war nickels" were struck in a copper-silver-manganese alloy to save nickel for the war effort; they carry a large mint mark above Monticello.
