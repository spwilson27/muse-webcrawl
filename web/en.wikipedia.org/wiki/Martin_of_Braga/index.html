Martin of Braga

Martin of Braga (c. 520–580), called Martin of Dumio and the "Apostle of the Suevi", was a Pannonian-born missionary and archbishop of Bracara Augusta (Braga) in Gallaecia (now northern Portugal) who converted the Suevi from Arianism to Chalcedonian Christianity.

Life

Martin was born in Pannonia and travelled to the Holy Land before arriving in Gallaecia about 550. He founded the monastery of Dumio (Dume), of which he became abbot and then bishop, and around 562 he became Archbishop of Braga. He presided over or influenced the First Council of Braga (561) and convened the Second Council of Braga (572), which consolidated Catholic practice in the Suevic kingdom.

Writings

Martin wrote several works of moral and pastoral instruction. His De correctione rusticorum, addressed to Bishop Polemius of Astorga, is a key source on surviving pagan practices among the rural population of northwestern Iberia, condemning the veneration of trees, stones, and springs and the observance of days named for pagan gods — a passage credited with popularising the replacement of planetary weekday names by numbered ferias in Portuguese. His Formula vitae honestae, a treatise on the four cardinal virtues, was widely copied in the Middle Ages and often misattributed to Seneca. He also translated the sayings of the Egyptian desert fathers (Sententiae patrum Aegyptiorum) and compiled a collection of canon law, the Capitula Martini.

Veneration

Martin died in 580 and is venerated as a saint in both the Catholic and Orthodox Churches; his feast day is 20 March.
