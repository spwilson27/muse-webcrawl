Garrya is a genus of flowering plants in the family Garryaceae, native to western North America and Central America, from the western United States and Mexico to Guatemala and the Greater Antilles. The species are commonly known as silk tassel bushes. The genus is named after Nicholas Garry, a deputy governor of the Hudson's Bay Company, who assisted the plant explorer David Douglas.

Description

Garrya species are evergreen shrubs or small trees, growing from about 1 to 8 metres tall, with thick, leathery, opposite leaves that often have wavy margins and silky hairs beneath. The plants are dioecious, with male and female flowers on separate plants. The flowers are produced in long, pendulous, silky catkin-like inflorescences — grey-green to silver in colour — which appear in late winter and early spring and give the plants their ornamental value. The fruit is a dry, berry-like drupe containing two seeds.

Species

Depending on the authority, between about 13 and 18 species are recognised. Well-known species include:

  • Garrya elliptica — coast silk tassel, from coastal California and Oregon; widely grown as an ornamental, the cultivar 'James Roof' having exceptionally long male catkins
  • Garrya flavescens — ashy silk tassel
  • Garrya fremontii — bearbrush or Fremont's silk tassel, used historically as a source of quinine-like fever remedies
  • Garrya veatchii — canyon silk tassel
  • Garrya laurifolia — from Mexico and Central America

Cultivation

Several species and cultivars are valued in gardens for their winter catkins, evergreen foliage and tolerance of coastal exposure, dry soils and shade. They are usually propagated from semi-ripe cuttings, and male plants are preferred for ornamental planting because their catkins are longer and more conspicuous.
