The North American International Auto Show (NAIAS), now held under the name the Detroit Auto Show, is an annual automobile exhibition held in Detroit, Michigan, United States. For most of its modern history it was held in January at Huntington Place (formerly Cobo Center/Cobo Hall), and for decades it was regarded as one of the most important auto shows in the world, alongside the shows in Frankfurt, Geneva, Paris, and Tokyo. It is organized by the Detroit Auto Dealers Association (DADA).

History

The first Detroit auto show was held in 1907 at Beller's Beer Garden, with 17 exhibitors. The show grew steadily and was held annually except during wartime and in 2020–2021, when it was cancelled because of the COVID-19 pandemic. In 1987 the show was rebranded as the North American International Auto Show, officially beginning with the 1989 show, as organizers sought to attract debuts from international manufacturers on par with the major European shows.

The show historically drew hundreds of thousands of public visitors and thousands of accredited journalists from around the world, and its charity preview was a major fundraising event. Numerous production cars and concept cars have debuted at Detroit, and the North American Car and Truck of the Year awards were traditionally announced at the show.

Changes of date and format

In 2018, organizers announced the show would move from January to June, beginning in 2020, in part to escape competition with the Consumer Electronics Show and to allow outdoor displays. The 2020 and 2021 editions were cancelled because of the pandemic. The show returned in September 2022 with a greater emphasis on electric vehicles and indoor and outdoor ride experiences. Beginning in 2025 the show returned to January and to the "Detroit Auto Show" name.
