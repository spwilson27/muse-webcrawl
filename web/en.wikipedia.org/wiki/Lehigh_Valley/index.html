The Lehigh Valley is a geographic and metropolitan region in eastern Pennsylvania centred on the Lehigh River, a tributary of the Delaware River. Its principal cities are Allentown, Bethlehem and Easton, and the Allentown–Bethlehem–Easton metropolitan statistical area covers Lehigh and Northampton counties (with Carbon County sometimes included in the wider region).

History

The valley was settled in the colonial era by German ("Pennsylvania Dutch") immigrants and became an industrial heartland in the 19th and 20th centuries, producing iron, steel (notably Bethlehem Steel), cement and anthracite coal shipped via the Lehigh Canal and railroads. The decline of heavy industry in the late 20th century hit the region hard.

Economy today

The modern economy is based on healthcare, education, logistics and warehousing along the Interstate 78 corridor, light manufacturing, and a growing number of residents commuting or relocating from the New York and Philadelphia metropolitan areas. Lehigh University in Bethlehem and Lafayette College in Easton are among its major educational institutions.
