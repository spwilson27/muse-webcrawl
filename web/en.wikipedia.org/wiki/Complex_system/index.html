Complex system

A complex system is a system composed of many components which may interact with each other. Examples include Earth's global climate, organisms, the human brain, infrastructure such as power grids, social and economic organisations, and ecosystems. Complex systems are studied in complexity science because their collective behaviour cannot be easily inferred from the properties of the parts alone.

Properties

Recurring features include emergence, self-organisation, adaptation, feedback loops, nonlinearity, networks of interactions, and sensitivity that makes long-term prediction difficult even when the underlying rules are known. The whole exhibits behaviour — such as flocking, market dynamics or consciousness — not present in any single component.

Study

Complexity science draws on statistical physics, information theory, nonlinear dynamics, computer simulation (including agent-based modelling) and network theory. Institutions such as the Santa Fe Institute helped establish the field in the 1980s.
