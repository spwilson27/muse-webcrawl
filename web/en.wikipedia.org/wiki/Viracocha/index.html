Viracocha

Viracocha (also Wiraqocha, Huiracocha; Quechua: Wiraqucha) is the creator deity in the pre-Inca and Inca mythology of the Andes region of South America. He is one of the most important deities in the Inca pantheon and seen as the creator of all things — or the substance from which all things are created — and intimately associated with the sea.

The central figure of the Gate of the Sun is often identified with Viracocha

Mythology

According to myth recorded by Spanish chroniclers, Viracocha created the universe, sun, moon, stars, time (by ordering the sun to move over the sky) and civilization itself. Rising from Lake Titicaca (or from the Pacific Ocean in some versions), he made the sun, moon and stars, then created humankind by breathing into stones. His first creations, giants, displeased him, and he destroyed them in a great flood and made humans anew from smaller stones. Viracocha eventually disappeared across the Pacific Ocean — walking on water — and was expected one day to return.

Worship

As a remote creator god, Viracocha received relatively little direct worship compared with Inti, the sun god, but temples and shrines were dedicated to him, including at Cacha and in Cusco. The eighth Inca ruler took the name Viracocha Inca after reportedly receiving a vision of the god before battle. When the Spanish conquistadors arrived, some early accounts claimed the Inca mistook them, or their leader, for returning divine messengers associated with Viracocha — a claim modern historians treat with scepticism.
