The Association of American Universities (AAU) is an organisation of leading research universities in the United States and Canada, devoted to maintaining a strong system of academic research and education. Founded in 1900 by fourteen universities awarding the PhD, it has grown to 71 member institutions, and membership — by invitation only — is widely regarded as a mark of research prestige.

History and membership

The founding members included Harvard, Columbia, Johns Hopkins, the University of Chicago and the University of California, Berkeley. New members are elected by three-quarters of existing members based on the breadth and quality of their research and graduate programmes, measured largely by competitive federal research funding and faculty honours. Membership is occasionally withdrawn: the University of Nebraska–Lincoln was removed in 2011, and some institutions, such as Syracuse University, have withdrawn voluntarily over disagreements about the indicators used.

Activities

The AAU advocates in Washington for federal research funding, science policy and graduate education, and convenes presidents and senior administrators of its member universities. Its members collectively perform a large share of federally funded university research in the United States and award a large proportion of American doctorates.
