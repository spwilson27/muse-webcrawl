Monnickendam is a city in the Dutch province of North Holland, a part of the municipality of Waterland. It lies on the shore of the Markermeer, about 15 km northeast of Amsterdam. The name derives from a dam built by monks (monniken) in the river where the town grew up.

History

Monnickendam received city rights in 1355 from William V, Count of Holland. In the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries it was a prosperous port: its shipyards built vessels for Baltic and Mediterranean trade, and the town profited from herring fishing, butter and cheese exports, and the processing of whale oil. A major fire in 1499 and another in 1513 destroyed much of the medieval town, after which it was rebuilt in brick.

The town played a role in the Dutch Revolt: in 1573 Spanish forces besieging the region were defeated in the naval Battle on the Zuiderzee fought near Monnickendam, after which the town remained in States hands.

Sights

Landmarks include the Grote Kerk (Church of St Nicholas), a Gothic hall church begun around 1400; the Speeltoren, the freestanding bell tower of 1591 housing one of the oldest carillons in the Netherlands; the weigh house (Waegh) of 1668; and the Monnickendam Museum in the former orphanage. The old harbour remains busy with traditional sailing barges, and the town is a popular recreational port on the Markermeer.
