Greek dances

Greek dances (horoi) are a large and varied family of traditional dances from Greece and the Greek diaspora, numbering more than 4,000 documented dances. Most are circle or line dances performed at weddings, festivals (panigyria), baptisms, and other celebrations, accompanied by instruments such as the lyra, clarinet, violin, laouto, and baglama, and frequently by singing.

Regional traditions

Dances are strongly regional. Mainland dances include the Syrtos, the slow and stately Tsamiko—the national dance, danced by men with leaps and turns led by the first dancer—and Kalamatianos, in 7/8 rhythm, one of the oldest known dances. Island dances are livelier, including the Sousta and the Pentozali of Crete, the Ikariotikos of Ikaria, and the Ballos. Thrace and Macedonia favor line dances such as the Zonaradikos. The Hasapiko, originally the butchers' guild dance of Constantinople, and the solo, improvisational Zeibekiko are associated with urban and rebetiko culture; the Hasaposerviko is a fast variant. The Sirtaki, famous from the film Zorba the Greek (1964), is a modern choreography combining slow hasapiko and fast hasaposerviko movements rather than an ancient folk dance.

Form and function

Dancers usually hold hands, shoulders, or handkerchiefs in an open circle or line, with a leader at the head improvising ornamented steps. Dance remains a central medium of regional and national identity, taught in schools and by cultural associations in Greece and in diaspora communities worldwide. Religious and village festivals, name days, and weddings almost invariably feature dancing into the night.
