Marshallese (Marshallese: Kajin M̧ajeļ), also known as Ebon, is a Micronesian language of the Austronesian language family spoken in the Marshall Islands. The language of the Marshallese people, it is spoken by nearly all of the country's population of about 55,000, as well as by Marshallese communities abroad, notably in the United States. There are two major dialects corresponding to the two island chains of the archipelago: the western Rālik chain and the eastern Ratak chain.

Phonology

Marshallese is noted for a vertical vowel system: phonemically, vowels are distinguished mainly by height, while front/back and rounding qualities are allophonic, determined by neighbouring consonants, which carry secondary articulations (palatalized, velarized, and labialized velarized). The consonant inventory therefore has paired plain and "heavy" series. In the standard orthography, diacritics such as the cedilla (̧) mark some of these consonant qualities.

Grammar

Marshallese is a verb–object–subject language in many constructions, with extensive systems of demonstratives organized by distance and visibility, classifier-like possessive constructions, and directionals that encode motion relative to the lagoon and ocean sides of an atoll — a reflection of the seafaring orientation of Marshallese culture. Pronouns distinguish singular, dual, and plural and an inclusive/exclusive contrast in the first person.

Writing system

Marshallese is written in a Latin-based orthography devised by missionaries in the 19th century and revised in the mid-20th century; the modern standard spelling is used in the Marshallese–English Dictionary (1976) compiled by Abo, Bender, Capelle, and DeBrum. A Bible translation was completed in the 19th century and remains influential.
