Nivkh (ни́вхгу диф), formerly called Gilyak, is a language isolate spoken by the Nivkh people in the lower Amur region and on northern Sakhalin Island in the Russian Far East.

Classification and dialects

Nivkh has no demonstrated relatives, though it is included in proposed groupings such as Paleosiberian for areal convenience. Its main varieties — Amur, East Sakhalin and North Sakhalin — are sometimes treated as separate languages because they are not mutually intelligible.

Grammar

The language is agglutinative with a complex system of consonant alternations in which initial consonants of words mutate depending on the preceding word — a sandhi phenomenon unusual worldwide. Word order is typically subject–object–verb.

Status

Nivkh is severely endangered: most ethnic Nivkhs speak Russian, and fluent Nivkh speakers number only in the low hundreds at most, mostly elderly. It is written in a Cyrillic-based alphabet and has a small literature and newspaper tradition.
