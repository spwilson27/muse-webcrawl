Normed vector space

In mathematics, a normed vector space or normed space is a vector space over the real or complex numbers on which a norm is defined. A norm is a generalisation of the intuitive notion of "length" in the physical world.

Definitions

If the norm is defined by an inner product, the space is an inner product space; a complete normed space is a Banach space, and a complete inner product space is a Hilbert space. A weaker structure, where the "norm" may fail positive definiteness, is a seminormed space.

Topology and structure

The norm defines a metric, and hence a topology, on the space. Finite-dimensional normed spaces are all equivalent: every norm induces the same topology, and the space is complete. Infinite-dimensional spaces are richer; important examples include the sequence spaces ℓp and function spaces Lp.

History

Normed spaces emerged from the work of Stefan Banach, Hans Hahn, and Norbert Wiener in the early 1920s, becoming the foundational setting of functional analysis.
