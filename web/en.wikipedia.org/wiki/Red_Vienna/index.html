Red Vienna

Red Vienna (German: Rotes Wien)
Period: 1918–1934
Governing party: Social Democratic Workers' Party (SDAPÖ)
Known for: Municipal housing (Gemeindebauten), social welfare reforms

Red Vienna (German: Rotes Wien) is the name given to the Austrian capital Vienna during the period between 1918 and 1934, when the Social Democratic Workers' Party governed the city and carried out an ambitious programme of social reform — one of the emblematic experiments of Austromarxism.

The housing programme

Faced with catastrophic overcrowding and a profiteering rental market, the city council, financed by steeply progressive taxes on luxury goods and large properties championed by finance councillor Hugo Breitner, built over 60,000 municipal flats in the celebrated Gemeindebauten. The largest, the Karl-Marx-Hof (1927–1930), stretches over a kilometre and housed some 5,000 people. The complexes integrated laundries, baths, kindergartens, clinics, libraries, and green courtyards.

Other reforms

Under Julius Tandler the city expanded public health, infant welfare, and school medical services; Otto Glöckel's school reforms introduced comprehensive education and free learning materials. Vienna also became a centre of the workers' cultural movement, with libraries, adult education, sports associations, and the 1932 Workers' Olympiad.

End

Red Vienna stood in escalating conflict with the conservative federal government. After the July Revolt of 1927 the right-wing Heimwehr grew stronger; in February 1934 the Dollfuss regime crushed the Social Democrats in the Austrian Civil War, shelled the Karl-Marx-Hof, and dissolved the party, ending the experiment.
