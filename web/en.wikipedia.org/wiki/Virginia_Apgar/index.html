Virginia Apgar

Virginia Apgar (June 7, 1909 – August 7, 1974) was an American physician, obstetrical anesthesiologist and medical researcher, best known as the inventor of the Apgar score, a quick and standardised method of assessing the health of a newborn baby immediately after birth.

Early life and career

Born in Westfield, New Jersey, Apgar graduated from Mount Holyoke College and earned her MD from Columbia University's College of Physicians and Surgeons in 1933. Discouraged from surgery by her mentor, she trained in the new field of anesthesiology, becoming director of anesthesia at Columbia-Presbyterian in 1938 and, in 1949, the first woman appointed a full professor at the College of Physicians and Surgeons, as professor of anesthesiology.

The Apgar score

In 1952 Apgar proposed, and in 1953 published, a five-point scoring system evaluating a newborn's heart rate, respiration, muscle tone, reflex response, and colour at one and five minutes after birth. The score (later remembered by the backronym Appearance, Pulse, Grimace, Activity, Respiration) transformed newborn care by giving clinicians a rapid, reproducible measure to identify babies needing resuscitation, and it remains in routine use worldwide.

Later work

From 1959 Apgar worked with the March of Dimes, campaigning for research into the prevention of birth defects and writing the popular book Is My Baby All Right? (1972). She was also an accomplished violinist and instrument maker. In 1994 she was honoured on a US postage stamp, and she was inducted into the National Women's Hall of Fame in 1995.
