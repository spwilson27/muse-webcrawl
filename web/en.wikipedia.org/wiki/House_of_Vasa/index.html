House of Vasa

House of Vasa
Swedish: Vasaätten
Founder: Gustav I of Sweden
Founded: 1523
Royal house of: Sweden (1523–1654); Poland–Lithuania (1587–1668)
Named for: the sheaf (vase) in its arms

The House of Vasa (Swedish: Vasaätten) was an early modern royal house founded by Gustav I in 1523, ruling Sweden from 1523 to 1654 and the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth from 1587 to 1668. Its members included some of the most prominent monarchs of early modern Europe.

Swedish branch

Gustav Vasa led Sweden out of the Kalmar Union and founded the hereditary monarchy. His sons Erik XIV, John III, and Charles IX succeeded him, followed by Gustavus Adolphus the Great, whose reign made Sweden a great power during the Thirty Years' War, and his daughter Christina, whose abdication in 1654 ended the Swedish Vasa line on the throne.

Polish branch

Sigismund III, son of John III and the Polish princess Catherine Jagiellon, was elected King of Poland in 1587 and briefly held the Swedish crown too, before being deposed in Sweden (1599) partly over his Catholicism. His sons Władysław IV and John II Casimir ruled the Commonwealth until the latter's abdication in 1668, after which the Vasa dynasty's royal lines ended; the family name derives from a sheaf of grain (Swedish vase) borne in the family arms.
