The Penn Central Transportation Company, commonly known as Penn Central, was a Class I railroad headquartered in Philadelphia, created on 1 February 1968 by the merger of the Pennsylvania Railroad and the New York Central Railroad—formerly bitter rivals. In 1969 it also absorbed the bankrupt New York, New Haven and Hartford Railroad. The merger produced a network of some 20,000 route-miles across the northeastern and midwestern United States and Canada.

Bankruptcy

The merger was beset by incompatible operating practices, computer systems and management cultures, mounting passenger-service losses, restrictive regulation and a declining industrial base. On 21 June 1970 Penn Central filed for bankruptcy—the largest corporate failure in American history to that date. Its collapse, and that of other northeastern railroads, pushed the federal government first to create Amtrak (1971) for intercity passenger service and then the Consolidated Rail Corporation (Conrail), which took over Penn Central's rail operations on 1 April 1976.

Legacy

The non-rail assets of the company—real estate and other holdings—continued as the Penn Central Corporation and eventually evolved into part of American Premier Underwriters. The bankruptcy remains a landmark case in business history and prompted reforms in railway regulation, including the Staggers Rail Act of 1980.
