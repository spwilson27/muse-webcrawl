Neuengamme concentration camp

Neuengamme was a network of Nazi concentration camps in northern Germany, centred on the main camp at Neuengamme, a district of Hamburg. Established in 1938 as a satellite of Sachsenhausen and becoming an independent camp in 1940, it held more than 100,000 prisoners from across occupied Europe, roughly half of whom died.

History

Prisoners were used primarily as forced labour in brickworks producing bricks for monumental Nazi building projects in Hamburg, as well as in armaments production. Neuengamme administered more than 80 satellite camps. Conditions were brutal, and many prisoners died from exhaustion, disease, starvation and executions.

Aftermath and memorial

Britain occupied the site after the war, using it first as an internment camp and later as prisons. Since 2005 the Neuengamme Concentration Camp Memorial has preserved the grounds and former buildings as a site of remembrance, research and education.
