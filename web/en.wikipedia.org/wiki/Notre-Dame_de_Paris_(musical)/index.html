Notre-Dame de Paris (musical)

Notre-Dame de Paris is a French musical with music by Riccardo Cocciante and lyrics by Luc Plamondon, adapted from Victor Hugo's 1831 novel The Hunchback of Notre-Dame. It premiered at the Palais des congrès de Paris on September 16, 1998, and became one of the most successful French-language musicals of all time, launching the modern wave of French pop musicals.

Synopsis

In fifteenth-century Paris, the poet Gringoire narrates the story of the cathedral bell-ringer Quasimodo; the archdeacon Frollo, consumed by desire; the beautiful Romani dancer Esmeralda; and Captain Phoebus, to whom she is drawn. As the outcasts of Paris — the "sans-papiers" of the Court of Miracles led by Clopin — seek sanctuary in Notre-Dame, Frollo's obsession leads him to stab Phoebus and frame Esmeralda. Despite Quasimodo's devotion and attempt to save her, Esmeralda is hanged; Quasimodo kills Frollo and dies beside her body.

Music

Unusually, the score was recorded and released before the premiere, and its songs became hits in their own right. "Belle," sung by Quasimodo, Frollo, and Phoebus, was a massive success in France, and other well-known numbers include "Le temps des cathédrales," "Déchiré," "Tu vas me détruire," and "Vivre" (adapted in English as "Live for the One I Love").

Productions

The original Paris production starred Daniel Lavoie as Frollo, Bruno Pelletier as Gringoire, Luck Mervil as Clopin, and Hélène Ségara as Esmeralda; the role of Quasimodo was created by Garou, whose breakthrough performance launched his solo career. An English-language version, with lyrics by Will Jennings, opened in London in 2000, and the show has toured internationally for decades, with productions and tours across Europe, Asia, and North America seen by millions of spectators.
