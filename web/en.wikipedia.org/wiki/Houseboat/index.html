Houseboat

A houseboat is a boat that has been designed or modified to be used primarily as a dwelling. Some houseboats are not motorised, because they are usually moored, kept stationary at a fixed point, and often tethered to land to provide utilities. However, many are capable of operation under their own power.

Types and regions

Houseboats are found on waterways around the world. In the Netherlands, canals in Amsterdam host thousands of residential barges. In Kerala, India, converted rice barges called kettuvallam serve as tourist houseboats on the backwaters. The Dal Lake in Kashmir is famous for its ornate cedar houseboats, and communities of floating homes exist in Sausalito, California; Seattle, Washington; and on the canals of London.

Design

Houseboats range from converted narrowboats and barges to purpose-built pontoon vessels with full domestic facilities: sleeping quarters, kitchens, bathrooms, and utility hookups for water, electricity, and sewage. Mooring regulations, licensing, and residential zoning vary widely by jurisdiction.

In popular culture

Houseboats feature in films such as Houseboat (1958), starring Cary Grant and Sophia Loren, and are associated with alternative and artistic lifestyles in many harbour cities.
