Hemiballismus

Hemiballismus (or hemiballism) is a rare hyperkinetic movement disorder characterised by sudden, violent, involuntary flinging or ballistic movements of the limbs on one side of the body. It is considered a severe form of chorea affecting one side, and the two are sometimes grouped as hemiballismus–hemichorea.

Causes

The classic cause is a lesion of the contralateral subthalamic nucleus, most often from a small stroke. Other causes include non-ketotic hyperglycaemia (a common modern cause, associated with characteristic putaminal changes on MRI), tumours, trauma, infections, and other lesions of the basal ganglia such as the striatum and thalamus. Damage reduces excitatory drive to the globus pallidus internus, disinhibiting thalamocortical motor activity and producing the abnormal movements.

Presentation and treatment

Movements are proximal, large-amplitude, and can be exhausting and injurious; they typically lessen during sleep. Many cases, especially post-stroke, improve spontaneously over weeks to months. Symptomatic treatment may include dopamine-receptor blockers (e.g. haloperidol), tetrabenazine, or anticonvulsants; refractory cases have been treated with deep brain stimulation or pallidotomy.
