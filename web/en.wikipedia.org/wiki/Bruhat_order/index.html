In mathematics, the Bruhat order (also called the strong order or strong Bruhat order) is a partial order on the elements of a Coxeter group, named after the French mathematician François Bruhat. It plays a fundamental role in the geometry of flag varieties and in the representation theory of algebraic groups.

Definition

For a Coxeter group with a set of simple reflections, one element u is less than or equal to another element v in the Bruhat order if some (equivalently, every) reduced expression for u appears as a subword of a reduced expression for v. The longest element of a finite Coxeter group is the unique maximal element.

Applications

The Bruhat order describes the inclusion relations among Schubert varieties in a flag variety: one Schubert variety contains another precisely when the corresponding group elements are comparable in the appropriate direction. It is also connected with the Bruhat decomposition of algebraic groups and with Kazhdan–Lusztig theory. A related weaker notion is the weak Bruhat order, defined using only deletions from the right (or left) end of reduced words.
