Flood basalt

A flood basalt (or plateau basalt) is the result of a giant volcanic eruption or series of eruptions covering large stretches of land or ocean floor with basalt lava. Flood basalt provinces, also called large igneous provinces and traps, may cover over a million square kilometres and are linked to mass extinctions and continental breakup.

Flood basalt
Type: Large igneous province
Examples: Deccan Traps, Siberian Traps, Columbia River Basalt
Cause: Mantle plume / rifting (debated)

Formation

Enormous volumes of low-viscosity basaltic magma erupt from fissures over geologically short periods, flowing long distances and stacking in layers hundreds of metres thick. Many scientists attribute them to mantle plumes rising to the lithosphere, often as continents split apart — for example the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province accompanying the opening of the Atlantic.

Examples and effects

The Siberian Traps (~252 million years ago) coincide with the Permian–Triassic mass extinction, Earth’s largest; the Deccan Traps in India overlap the end-Cretaceous extinction that killed non-avian dinosaurs, alongside the Chicxulub impact. Gases released — carbon dioxide and sulfur dioxide — can drive climate warming and acid rain.
