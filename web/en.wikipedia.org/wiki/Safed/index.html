Safed

Safed (Hebrew: צְפַת, Tzfat; Arabic: صفد, Ṣafad) is a city in the Northern District of Israel. Located at an elevation of up to 937 metres (3,074 ft), Safed is the highest city in the Galilee and in Israel. It has been identified with Sepph, a fortified Jewish town in the Upper Galilee mentioned in the writings of Josephus.

History

Safed rose to prominence in the 16th century under Ottoman rule, when it became a major centre of Jewish learning after the expulsion of Jews from Spain in 1492. Refugees and scholars settled in the city, among them Rabbi Joseph Karo, author of the Shulchan Aruch, and Rabbi Isaac Luria (the Ari), whose teachings transformed Kabbalah and gave the city its enduring reputation as a centre of Jewish mysticism. The first Hebrew printing press in the region was established in Safed in 1577. Together with Jerusalem, Hebron, and Tiberias, Safed is one of the Four Holy Cities of Judaism.

The city suffered severely in the Galilee earthquake of 1837, which killed several thousand inhabitants. During the 1947–49 Palestine war, Safed's Arab population departed or was expelled in 1948, and the city came under Israeli control.

Culture

The old Jewish quarter and the artists' quarter, with their narrow lanes, medieval synagogues — including the Abuhav and the Ari Ashkenazi and Sephardi synagogues — and galleries, draw large numbers of visitors. The city hosts klezmer music festivals, and the nearby Mount Meron tomb of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai is the site of the annual Lag BaOmer pilgrimage.

Climate

Safed has a Mediterranean climate with warm, dry summers moderated by its altitude, and cool, rainy winters that occasionally bring snow.
