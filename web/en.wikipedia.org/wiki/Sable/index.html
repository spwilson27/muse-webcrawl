Sable

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Mammalia
Order: Carnivora
Family: Mustelidae
Genus: Martes
Species: M. zibellina
Binomial name: Martes zibellina (Linnaeus, 1758)

The sable (Martes zibellina) is a species of marten, a small omnivorous mammal primarily inhabiting the forest environments of Russia, from the Ural Mountains throughout Siberia, and northern Mongolia. Its habitat also borders eastern Kazakhstan, China, North Korea and Hokkaido, Japan. Historically, its range extended westwards across European Russia into Poland and Scandinavia.

Description

Males measure 38–56 centimetres (15–22 in) in body length, with a tail measuring 9–12 centimetres (3.5–4.7 in), and weigh 880–1,800 grams (1.94–3.97 lb). Females have a body length of 35–51 centimetres (14–20 in) and a tail length of 7.2–11.5 centimetres (2.8–4.5 in). The winter pelage is longer and more luxurious than the summer coat. The fur ranges in colour from light brown to nearly black, and is among the most valued of all furs.

Behaviour

Sables inhabit dense forests dominated by spruce, pine, larch, Siberian cedar, and birch. They build nests in burrows or under tree roots, often near river banks. They are skillful climbers and are active mainly at twilight and night, though they may also be active during the day in the mating season. Their diet consists of squirrels, mice, small birds and their eggs, berries, and pine nuts; they also follow tracks in the snow of larger predators to scavenge kills.

Fur trade

The term "sable" also refers to the animal's pelt, which has been a highly prized item in the fur trade since the early Middle Ages. The fur is unique because it retains its smoothness whichever way it is stroked. The richest, darkest pelts come from the Barguzin region of Siberia. Overhunting in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries caused severe declines; Soviet conservation measures, closed seasons and reintroductions restored populations, and the species is now classified as Least Concern by the IUCN.
