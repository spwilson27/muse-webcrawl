La Noche Triste ("The Sad Night") is the name given to the disastrous retreat of Hernán Cortés and his Spanish conquistadors and Tlaxcalan allies from Tenochtitlan on the night of 30 June–1 July 1520, during the Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire. A large part of the retreating force was killed as it fought its way along the Tacuba causeway.

Background

Cortés had returned to Tenochtitlan in June 1520 after defeating the rival expedition of Pánfilo de Narváez on the coast. In his absence, Pedro de Alvarado's massacre of Mexica nobles at the festival of Toxcatl had provoked an uprising; the emperor Moctezuma II died in confused circumstances — Spanish accounts said he was stoned by his own people — and the Spanish were besieged in the palace of Axayacatl.

The retreat

Deciding to escape by night, the Spanish loaded themselves with looted gold and attempted to slip out over the Tacuba causeway, using a portable bridge to cross gaps in the roadway. They were discovered — according to tradition, by a woman drawing water who raised the alarm — and attacked by warriors in canoes and along the causeway; the portable bridge became stuck and could not be moved to the other gaps, so men, horses and treasure fell into the lake. Many Spaniards drowned, weighed down by gold. Estimates of the losses vary widely, but several hundred Spaniards and thousands of Tlaxcalan allies died.

Aftermath

Legend holds that Cortés wept at the disaster beneath an ahuehuete tree in Popotla — the "Árbol de la Noche Triste". The survivors escaped to Tlaxcala, regrouped, and returned the following year to besiege and destroy Tenochtitlan. In 2020 the Mexico City government renamed the site's commemoration the "Noche Victoriosa" ("Victorious Night") to honour the Mexica defence.
