Isaac Stern (21 July 1920 – 22 September 2001) was an American violinist. Regarded as one of the foremost violinists of the 20th century, he was renowned for his recordings, his championing of young musicians, and his role in saving New York's Carnegie Hall from demolition in 1960.

Life and career

Stern was born in Kremenets, then in Poland (now Ukraine), and emigrated with his family to San Francisco as an infant. He studied at the San Francisco Conservatory and with Naoum Blinder, making his orchestral debut in 1936 and his Carnegie Hall debut in 1943. Unlike many peers he built his career in the United States rather than Europe.

His extensive discography for Columbia Records included the major concerto repertoire and much chamber music, often with the Stern–Rose–Istomin trio. He premiered works by composers including Bernstein, Penderecki and Rochberg, and his 1960 cultural tour film From Mao to Mozart: Isaac Stern in China (1979) won the Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature.

Legacy

Stern led the citizens' campaign that rescued Carnegie Hall in 1960 and served as its president for four decades; its main auditorium is named for him. He helped found the National Endowment for the Arts and mentored musicians such as Itzhak Perlman, Pinchas Zukerman and Yo-Yo Ma. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1992 and a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award.
