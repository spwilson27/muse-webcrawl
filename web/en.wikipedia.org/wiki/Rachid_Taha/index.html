Rachid Taha

Born: 18 September 1958, Oran, Algeria
Died: 12 September 2018, Paris, France
Genres: Raï, rock, world
Years active: 1980–2018

Rachid Taha (Arabic: رشيد طه; 18 September 1958 – 12 September 2018) was an Algerian singer based in France. His music was influenced by many different styles including rock, electronic, punk and raï. He first rose to prominence as frontman of the band Carte de Séjour in the 1980s.

Career

Born in Oran, Taha emigrated with his family to France at age ten. In Lyon he formed Carte de Séjour ("residence permit"), whose 1986 cover of Charles Trenet's "Douce France" — sung ironically as a protest against racism — became a landmark for the beur generation. As a solo artist he fused Algerian chaabi and raï with rock and techno; his 1998 album Diwân and its single "Ya Rayah" brought international success. His version of The Clash's "Rock the Casbah" ("Rock el Casbah", 2004) was praised by Joe Strummer's admirers, and he collaborated with Steve Hillage as producer. In 1998 he performed in the celebrated 1,2,3 Soleils concert with Khaled and Faudel.

Taha died of a heart attack in Paris in September 2018, shortly before his 60th birthday.
