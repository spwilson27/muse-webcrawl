Party-list proportional representation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Party-list proportional representation (list-PR) is a system of proportional representation in multi-member districts where seats are allocated to political parties in proportion to their vote shares, and are then filled from ranked candidate lists presented by each party. It is the most widely used family of electoral systems in the world, employed for most European parliaments and many others.

Variants and mechanics

Key design choices include: the allocation formula (highest-averages methods such as D'Hondt, which mildly favours large parties, or Sainte-Laguë, which is more neutral; or largest-remainder methods using quotas); list type — closed lists (party-determined order), open lists (voters reorder candidates), flexible and free (panachage) variants; district magnitude (from small regional districts to Israel's and the Netherlands' single nationwide constituency); legal thresholds (commonly 3–5 per cent) to exclude splinter parties; and apparentement or alliance rules. List-PR reliably yields multiparty systems and coalition governments, strong party organisations (list control is candidate-selection power), and easy accommodation of quota mechanisms for women's representation — properties its advocates celebrate and its critics, preferring personal mandates and single-member accountability, treat as partyocracy.
