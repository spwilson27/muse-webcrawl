Giovanni Artusi

Giovanni Maria Artusi (c. 1540 – 18 August 1613) was an Italian music theorist, composer and Augustinian canon, best remembered for his fierce published attack on the "imperfections of modern music" — the new practices of Claudio Monteverdi — which helped define the famous controversy between the prima pratica and seconda pratica at the birth of the Baroque.

The controversy

In his dialogue L'Artusi, ovvero delle imperfezioni della moderna musica (1600, with a second part in 1603), Artusi quoted passages from Monteverdi madrigals — including Cruda Amarilli — that had circulated in manuscript before publication, and condemned their unprepared dissonances, irregular resolutions and licence with the rules of counterpoint as laid down by his teacher Gioseffo Zarlino. Monteverdi replied in the preface to his Fifth Book of Madrigals (1605), distinguishing the old prima pratica, in which harmony rules the words, from a seconda pratica in which the words are mistress of the harmony; his brother Giulio Cesare expanded the defence in 1607.

Life and works

Artusi was a canon regular of the Congregation of San Salvatore in Bologna and Ferrara. A conservative in taste, he published two books of canzonettas and other music of his own, now little performed, and corresponded with musicians and patrons across Italy. Although history sided with Monteverdi, Artusi's detailed critique preserves early examples of the new music and articulates the values of the Renaissance polyphonic tradition at the moment of its eclipse.
