The Dassault-Breguet Super Étendard is a French carrier-borne strike fighter aircraft designed by Dassault-Breguet for service with the French Navy. A development of the earlier Étendard IV, it entered service in 1978 and was notably flown from the aircraft carriers Clemenceau and Foch, and later Charles de Gaulle.

Operational history

The Super Étendard gained international prominence during the 1982 Falklands War, when Argentine Navy aircraft armed with Exocet anti-ship missiles sank the British destroyer HMS Sheffield and the container ship Atlantic Conveyor. French aircraft also saw combat from carriers over Lebanon, the former Yugoslavia, Afghanistan, and Libya.

Design

The single-seat aircraft is powered by a Snecma Atar 8K-50 turbojet and is subsonic. It could carry the AM39 Exocet, laser-guided bombs, and the ASMP nuclear missile in French service. France retired the type in 2016, replacing it with the Dassault Rafale M; Argentina continues to operate a small number.
