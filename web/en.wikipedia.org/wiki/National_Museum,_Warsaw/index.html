The National Museum in Warsaw (Polish: Muzeum Narodowe w Warszawie, abbreviated MNW) is a national museum in Warsaw, one of the largest museums in Poland and the national repository of Polish art. Founded in 1862 as the Museum of Fine Arts, it houses a collection of some 830,000 works spanning ancient art, Polish and European painting, sculpture, and the decorative arts.

Collections

Highlights include the Faras Gallery, displaying unique medieval Nubian Christian frescoes rescued during the UNESCO salvage campaign in Sudan; the Gallery of Polish Painting, with works by Jan Matejko (including the monumental Battle of Grunwald), Józef Chełmoński, Olga Boznańska, and Jacek Malczewski; and collections of Dutch, Flemish, and Italian old masters. The museum's main building on Aleje Jerozolimskie, a modernist edifice designed by Tadeusz Tołwiński, was erected in 1927–1938.

History

The museum suffered heavy losses during the Second World War: its building was damaged in 1939 and 1944, and thousands of works were looted or destroyed. After 1945 it absorbed the collections of several Warsaw institutions and grew into Poland's principal art museum. It administers branches including the Poster Museum at Wilanów and the Królikarnia sculpture museum.
