Maid café
Japaneseメイド喫茶 (meido kissa)
OriginAkihabara, Tokyo, Japan (2001)
TypeCosplay restaurant

Maid cafés (Japanese: メイド喫茶, meido kissa) are a subcategory of cosplay restaurants found predominantly in Japan. In these cafés, waitresses dressed in maid costumes act as servants and treat customers as masters (and mistresses) of a private household rather than as café patrons. The first permanent maid café, Cure Maid Café, opened in Akihabara, Tokyo, in March 2001.

Concept and service

Customers are greeted with phrases such as "Welcome home, master" (okaerinasaimase, goshujin-sama). Maids draw pictures in ketchup on omelette rice (omurice), perform songs and dances, cast playful "spells" (moe moe kyun) to make food taste better, and play games with customers for additional fees. Menu items are often decorated with cute designs, and staff may perform on small stages within the café.

History and spread

Maid cafés emerged from Japan's otaku culture and the popularity of maid characters in anime, manga, and video games. The concept expanded rapidly in Akihabara in the 2000s and spread to other Japanese cities as well as overseas, including South Korea, China, Thailand, Taiwan, Canada, and the United States, often in connection with anime conventions. Popular chains include Maidreamin and @home café.

Related establishments

Male counterparts include butler cafés, where staff dress as manservants, and cross-dressing maid cafés (otokonoko cafés) staffed by men in maid costume. Maid cafés are generally considered a mainstream, non-sexual themed dining experience, though media coverage abroad has sometimes conflated them with hostess bars.
