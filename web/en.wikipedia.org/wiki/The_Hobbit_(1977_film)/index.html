The Hobbit is a 1977 American animated musical television film created by Rankin/Bass Productions, based on J. R. R. Tolkien's 1937 novel The Hobbit. It first aired as an NBC "family special" on November 27, 1977, and was scripted by Romeo Muller.

Plot and production

The film follows Bilbo Baggins (voiced by Orson Bean), recruited by the wizard Gandalf (John Huston) to join Thorin Oakenshield (Hans Conried) and his company of dwarves on their quest to reclaim the Lonely Mountain from the dragon Smaug (Richard Boone). Along the way Bilbo encounters trolls, elves, Gollum (Brother Theodore) and his magic ring, giant spiders, and the Battle of Five Armies. Animation was produced in Japan by Topcraft, the studio whose staff later formed the core of Studio Ghibli. The soundtrack features folk-style songs by Glenn Yarbrough, including "The Greatest Adventure".

Reception

The film won a Peabody Award and introduced many American children to Tolkien; it was followed by Rankin/Bass's animated The Return of the King (1980). Elements such as its character designs and songs have remained fondly remembered, though Tolkien purists note the compressed, softened treatment of the book.
