Steelpan

The steelpan (also known as a steel drum) is a musical instrument originating in Trinidad and Tobago. It is played with a pair of straight sticks tipped with rubber, and the modern pan is a chromatic instrument made from a 55-gallon industrial steel drum, whose playing surface is hammered into a concave bowl with distinct note areas tuned to specific pitches. The steelpan is the national instrument of Trinidad and Tobago, and steelbands are central to its Carnival.

History

The pan grew out of the tamboo bamboo bands that replaced African drumming after colonial bans on drums and stick-fighting in the late nineteenth century. Players experimented with metal objects — biscuit tins, dustbins, brake drums and oil barrels — during the 1930s and 1940s in the poor neighbourhoods of Port of Spain. Innovators such as Winston "Spree" Simon, who is credited with the first multi-note melody pan, Ellie Mannette, who sank the drum head into its concave shape and tuned notes with harmonic overtones, and Anthony Williams, designer of the "fourths and fifths" tenor pan layout, turned the instrument into a fully chromatic voice. Steelbands spread through the Caribbean and worldwide after the Second World War; the Trinidad All-Steel Percussion Orchestra (TASPO) performed at the Festival of Britain in 1951.

Construction and tuning

A pan maker ("tuner") sinks the head of the drum with heavy hammers, marks out note areas, grooves them, tempers the metal by fire, and tunes each area by hammering from above and below, often striking the fundamental and its first two overtones into tune. The drum's skirt is cut to different lengths depending on the instrument's range: tenor (lead) pans have short skirts, while bass pans use full-length drums, six of them in a set played by one performer.

The steelband

A steel orchestra includes tenor pans, double tenors, double seconds, double guitars, cellos, quadraphonics, tenor basses and six-basses, supported by an "engine room" of percussion — drum kit, congas, scratcher, iron and cowbell. Large bands of over a hundred players compete at Carnival in the Panorama competition, performing elaborate calypso arrangements of up to ten minutes.
