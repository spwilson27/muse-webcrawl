Human Potential Movement


The Esalen Institute in Big Sur, California, a center of the movement.

The Human Potential Movement (HPM) arose out of the counterculture of the 1960s and formed around the concept of extraordinary potential that its advocates believe lies largely untapped in all people. The movement takes as its premise the belief that through the development of one's "human potential", humans can experience a life of happiness, creativity, and fulfillment, and that such people will direct their actions within society from a perspective of moral values.

Origins and ideas

The movement drew on humanistic psychology, particularly the work of Abraham Maslow and his concept of self-actualization, and Carl Rogers. It was centered on institutions such as the Esalen Institute in California, founded in 1962, and was associated with encounter groups, sensitivity training, and figures such as Fritz Perls (Gestalt therapy) and Rollo May.

Legacy

The movement influenced later developments including life coaching, some large-group awareness trainings, and parts of the self-help industry. It is also regarded as an important precursor of positive psychology.
