Shell plc is a British multinational oil and gas company headquartered in London. One of the "supermajors", it is among the world's largest companies by revenue, operates in more than 70 countries, and runs roughly 44,000 branded service stations — the largest single-branded retail network of its kind.

History

Shell grew from Marcus Samuel's London trading firm, which shipped kerosene in the 1890s and named its tankers after seashells. In 1907 it combined with Royal Dutch Petroleum in a 60:40 Dutch-British group that endured for nearly a century. The dual structure was unified as Royal Dutch Shell plc in 2005, and in 2022 the company moved its headquarters and tax residence to London, dropped "Royal Dutch" and became Shell plc.

Defining episodes include the 1995 Brent Spar and Ogoniland controversies in Nigeria, the 2004 reserves-overbooking scandal that cost top executives their jobs, and the landmark 2016 acquisition of BG Group, which made Shell the world's leading liquefied natural gas trader.

Operations

Shell's businesses span upstream oil and gas, integrated gas and LNG, refining and chemicals, and a growing power and renewables arm. A 2021 Dutch court ruling ordering deeper emissions cuts — later overturned on appeal in 2024 in part — made Shell a test case for corporate climate liability.
