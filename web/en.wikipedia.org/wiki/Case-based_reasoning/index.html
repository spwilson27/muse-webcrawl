Case-based reasoning

FieldArtificial intelligence
Key ideaSolve new problems using solutions to similar past problems

Case-based reasoning (CBR) is a paradigm of artificial intelligence and problem solving in which new problems are solved by recalling and adapting solutions that worked for similar past problems. It is grounded in the observation that human experts — physicians, lawyers, engineers — routinely reason from remembered cases and precedents.

The CBR cycle

The canonical model, described by Aamodt and Plaza (1994), consists of four processes: Retrieve the most similar case or cases from a case base; Reuse the retrieved solution, adapting it to the new problem; Revise the proposed solution through testing or expert evaluation; and Retain the new, validated case in the case base so the system learns from experience.

History

CBR grew out of Roger Schank's work on dynamic memory and scripts at Yale in the early 1980s. Janet Kolodner's CYRUS system was an early implementation. The field developed through systems for diagnosis, design, law (where reasoning from precedent is natural), help desks and planning.

Applications and relations

Case-based systems have been used in medical diagnosis, fault diagnosis in machinery, customer support, cooking (the CHEF program), and legal reasoning. CBR complements rule-based systems, which require explicit general rules, and modern machine learning, to which it is related through similarity-based and instance-based learning methods such as k-nearest neighbours.
