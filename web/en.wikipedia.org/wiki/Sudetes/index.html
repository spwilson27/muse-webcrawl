The Sudetes (Polish: Sudety; Czech: Sudety; German: Sudeten) are a mountain range in Central Europe, extending from eastern Germany across northern Czechia to southwestern Poland. Some 300 km long, they form the northeastern rim of the Bohemian Massif and include the highest ground between the Alps and the Baltic. Their name—recorded by Ptolemy as the Soudeta ore—became politically famous through the "Sudetenland", the German-speaking borderlands of interwar Czechoslovakia whose annexation by Nazi Germany under the 1938 Munich Agreement precipitated the crisis before the Second World War.

Geography

The range is conventionally divided into the Western, Central and Eastern Sudetes. The highest subrange is the Krkonoše (German: Riesengebirge, "Giant Mountains"), whose summit Sněžka (Śnieżka), 1,603 m, is the highest point in Czechia. Other notable subranges include the Jizera Mountains, the Orlické Mountains, the Owl Mountains (Góry Sowie) and the Jeseníky, whose Praděd (1,491 m) is the highest peak of Moravian Silesia. Major rivers rising in the Sudetes include the Elbe, the Oder and the Morava.

Geology

The Sudetes are an old Variscan mountain belt, uplifted during the Carboniferous collision that formed Pangaea and later re-elevated by faulting during the Alpine orogeny. They consist largely of gneisses, schists, granites and volcanic rocks, contrasting with the younger fold mountains of the Carpathians to the east.

Climate and nature

The higher Sudetes have a cool, wet mountain climate and extensive spruce forests; large areas are protected in national parks: Krkonoše National Park on both the Czech and Polish sides, and Stołowe Mountains National Park in Poland, famous for its sandstone rock labyrinths. The mountains suffered severe forest dieback from acid rain in the 1970s–80s but have substantially recovered.

Tourism

The Sudetes are a major Central European recreation area: hiking and skiing resorts such as Špindlerův Mlýn, Karpacz and Szklarska Poręba, spa towns like Kudowa-Zdrój and Cieplice, and the Adršpach-Teplice rock cities draw visitors year-round.
