Numerical control

Numerical control (NC) is the automated control of machining tools and other manufacturing equipment by means of precisely programmed instructions encoded on a storage medium, rather than by manual operation through levers and handwheels. When a computer is used to store and execute the program, the system is called computer numerical control (CNC).

History

Numerical control was developed in the late 1940s and early 1950s in the United States, notably through the work of John T. Parsons and the MIT Servomechanisms Laboratory for the US Air Force, which demonstrated a milling machine controlled by punched-tape data in 1952. Early NC machines used punched paper tape carrying instructions in codes such as G-code. The spread of inexpensive minicomputers and later microprocessors from the 1970s turned NC into CNC, allowing programs to be edited at the machine and integrated with CAD/CAM software.

Applications

CNC control is now used for milling machines, lathes, routers, grinders, laser and plasma cutters, electrical discharge machines, 3D printers and robotic systems. It enables repeatable, high-precision production of complex parts in industries from aerospace and automotive manufacturing to medical devices and woodworking.
