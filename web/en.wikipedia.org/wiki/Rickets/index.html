Rickets

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Rickets is the softening and weakening of bones in children, caused by defective mineralisation of growing bone — most often from severe vitamin D deficiency, and sometimes from calcium or phosphate problems or inherited disorders. Its hallmark is bowing of the legs in toddlers; the equivalent condition in adults is osteomalacia.

Causes and features

Vitamin D, made in skin exposed to sunlight and obtained from oily fish, eggs and fortified foods, is needed to absorb calcium. Rickets was common in smoky industrial cities in the 19th and early 20th centuries (nicknamed 'the English disease') and declined with better diet and cod liver oil; it persists where sunlight, diet or covering clothing limit vitamin D, and is reappearing in some wealthy countries. Signs include delayed growth and teething, bone pain, bowed legs or knock knees, thickened wrists, and a deformed chest or pelvis.

Treatment and prevention

Treatment is vitamin D and calcium replacement, with surgery for severe deformity; inherited (hypophosphataemic) rickets needs phosphate therapy and newer antibody treatment. Prevention is adequate sunlight exposure, diet and vitamin D supplements for at-risk infants and children, as many health authorities recommend.
