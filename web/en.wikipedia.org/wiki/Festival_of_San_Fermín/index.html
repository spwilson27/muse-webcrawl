The Festival of San Fermín (Spanish: Sanfermines) is a week-long, historically rooted celebration held annually in the city of Pamplona, Navarre, Spain. It runs from noon on 6 July to midnight on 14 July, in honour of Saint Fermín, the co-patron of Navarre.

The festival is best known worldwide for the running of the bulls (Spanish: encierro), in which participants run in front of a group of bulls through the old town's streets to the bullring, where the bulls are later fought in bullfights that afternoon. The event was popularised internationally by Ernest Hemingway's 1926 novel The Sun Also Rises.

History

The origins of the festival combine religious celebrations in honour of Saint Fermín, commercial fairs, and bullfighting events that were gradually merged between the 14th and 19th centuries. Originally held in October, the festivities were moved to July in 1591 to take advantage of better weather.

Events

The festival opens with the chupinazo, the firing of a rocket from the balcony of the city hall at noon on 6 July, after which thousands of participants in the square below celebrate. Participants traditionally wear white clothing with a red scarf (pañuelico) and red sash.

Each morning at 8 a.m. the running of the bulls takes place over an 875-metre course through the cobbled streets of the old town. Each afternoon, bullfights are held in the Plaza de Toros. Other events include the religious procession of Saint Fermín on 7 July, traditional sports, concerts, fireworks competitions, and the traditional Riau-Riau march. The festival closes at midnight on 14 July with the singing of "Pobre de mí" ("Poor me") in the city hall square.

In popular culture

The festival gained worldwide fame through Ernest Hemingway, who first visited in 1923 and depicted it in The Sun Also Rises and later in The Dangerous Summer. It remains one of Spain's most internationally recognised festivals, attracting around a million visitors.
