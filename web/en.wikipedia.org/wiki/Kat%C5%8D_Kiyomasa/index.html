Katō Kiyomasa (加藤 清正, 25 July 1562 – 2 August 1611) was a Japanese daimyō of the Azuchi–Momoyama and early Edo periods. A loyal retainer of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, he was one of the "Seven Spears of Shizugatake" and became notorious in Korea as a commander during Hideyoshi's invasions of Korea (1592–1598). He later founded the Katō clan's rule at Kumamoto in Higo Province.

Early career

Kiyomasa was born in Owari Province, the son of a blacksmith, and entered Hideyoshi's service as a youth. He distinguished himself at the Battle of Shizugatake in 1583 and rose rapidly, receiving northern Higo Province after Hideyoshi's conquest of Kyushu and the suppression of the Higo revolt.

Korean campaigns

During the invasions of Korea, Kiyomasa led the Second Division and advanced deep into the northeast of the peninsula, campaigning as far as the Tumen River against the Jurchen. His forces were noted for harsh treatment of Korean civilians and for tiger hunts that entered folklore. Rivalry with Konishi Yukinaga, a Christian daimyō commanding the First Division, was also intense.

Later life

After the Battle of Sekigahara (1600), in which he supported Tokugawa Ieyasu, Kiyomasa's holdings were confirmed and expanded to cover all of Higo. He built Kumamoto Castle, one of Japan's great fortresses, and governed as a devout follower of Nichiren Buddhism and a patron of castle and hydraulic engineering. He died in 1611, and was later enshrined and venerated in Kumamoto.
