Nahalal (Hebrew: נַהֲלָל) is a moshav in northern Israel, located in the western Jezreel Valley. Founded in 1921, it was the first moshav ovdim ("workers' moshav"), a form of cooperative agricultural settlement that combined private family farming with collective purchasing, marketing and mutual aid.

Design

Nahalal is famous for its distinctive circular plan, designed by architect Richard Kauffmann: public buildings at the centre, surrounded by a circular road, then family farmsteads arranged in a ring, with fields radiating outward. This egalitarian "circle of equals" became the iconic layout for many later moshavim. The site had been marshy and malarial; settlers drained the swamps of the valley plain at considerable hardship.

History and notable people

The founders were pioneers of the Second and Third Aliyah from Eastern Europe. Nahalal established one of the first agricultural schools for girls in the country (later the Nahalal Youth Village). Its best-known son is Moshe Dayan, the Israeli general and statesman, who was born in Degania but raised in Nahalal and is buried in its cemetery overlooking the valley, by his request without military honours. The settlement's name derives from a biblical town in the territory of Zebulun.
