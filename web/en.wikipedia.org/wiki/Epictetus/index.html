Epictetus (c. 50 – c. 135 AD) was a Greek Stoic philosopher born into slavery in Phrygia, who served in Rome as secretary to Epaphroditus, a wealthy freedman of Nero. Freed after Nero's death, he taught philosophy in Rome until Domitian banished the philosophers in 93 AD, then founded a school at Nicopolis. His pupil Arrian recorded the Discourses and condensed them into the Enchiridion ('Handbook').

Teaching

Epictetus' core doctrine is the dichotomy of control: some things are up to us — our judgements, impulses and desires — and others (body, property, reputation) are not; freedom and tranquillity consist in wanting what depends on us rightly and accepting the rest. His lame leg (injured, tradition says, by his master) and slave's perspective give his teaching its distinctive insistence that no external power can harm the true self.
