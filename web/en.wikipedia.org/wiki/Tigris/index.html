Tigris

The Tigris is the eastern of the two great rivers that define Mesopotamia, the other being the Euphrates. It flows about 1,850 kilometres from the Taurus Mountains of eastern Turkey through Iraq, passing Mosul, Tikrit, Baghdad and Basra, and joins the Euphrates to form the Shatt al-Arab, which empties into the Persian Gulf.

The Tigris watered the ancient Assyrian capitals of Nineveh and Ashur and later Baghdad, founded on its banks in 762 CE. Its faster, deeper flow than the Euphrates made it the livelier of the two rivers and the more prone to destructive flooding. Modern dams in Turkey (notably Ilısu) and Iraq regulate its flow amid regional water disputes.
