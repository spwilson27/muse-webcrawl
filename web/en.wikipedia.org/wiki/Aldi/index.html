Aldi (stylised as ALDI) is the common brand of two German family-owned discount supermarket chains, Aldi Nord and Aldi Süd, which together operate over 12,000 stores in 18 countries. The name is a syllabic abbreviation of Albrecht Diskont, meaning "Albrecht Discount".

History

The business was founded by brothers Karl and Theo Albrecht, who took over their mother Anna Albrecht's small food store in Essen in 1946 and expanded it into a chain built on a strict no-frills, low-cost model. In 1960, following a disagreement over whether to sell cigarettes, the brothers divided the company into Aldi Nord (north) and Aldi Süd (south), which have operated independently ever since, though they occasionally cooperate, for example on product lines and the merger of their German operations discussions.

Aldi began expanding internationally in the late 1960s and 1970s. Aldi Süd entered the United States in 1976 and the United Kingdom in 1990; Aldi Nord acquired the Trader Joe's chain in the US in 1979. Both groups grew rapidly as discount grocery retailing expanded, and by the 2010s Aldi had become one of the world's largest retailers, putting significant competitive pressure on traditional supermarket chains.

Business model

Aldi stores are deliberately austere: relatively small, with a limited assortment of around 1,400–2,000 mostly private-label products, goods displayed in their shipping cartons, and cost-saving practices such as coin-deposit shopping trolleys, no free plastic bags, and customers packing their own purchases. The model prioritises low prices through high sales volumes, efficient logistics and minimal advertising, though the chain has increasingly added fresh food, premium "Specially Selected" ranges and weekly non-food "Special Buys".
