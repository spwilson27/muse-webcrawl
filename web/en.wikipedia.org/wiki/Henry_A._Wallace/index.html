Henry A. Wallace

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Henry Agard Wallace (October 7, 1888 – November 18, 1965) was an American politician, journalist, farmer, and businessman who served as the 33rd vice president of the United States from 1941 to 1945, under President Franklin D. Roosevelt. He had previously served as the 11th U.S. Secretary of Agriculture from 1933 to 1940 and as the 10th U.S. Secretary of Commerce from 1945 to 1946.

The eldest son of Henry C. Wallace, who served as U.S. Secretary of Agriculture from 1921 to 1924, Wallace was born in rural Iowa in 1888. After graduating from Iowa State University in 1910, he worked as a writer and editor for his family's farm journal, Wallaces' Farmer. In 1920, he founded the Hi-Bred Corn Company, a hybrid corn company that became extremely successful and made him wealthy.

Secretary of Agriculture

Wallace entered Roosevelt's cabinet as Secretary of Agriculture in 1933, overseeing the Agricultural Adjustment Administration and other New Deal farm programs designed to raise crop prices by reducing production during the Great Depression. His department also dealt with the Dust Bowl and pioneered soil conservation measures.

Vice presidency

In 1940, Roosevelt selected Wallace as his running mate, angering party regulars who considered him too liberal and eccentric. As vice president, Wallace chaired the Board of Economic Warfare and undertook goodwill missions to Latin America and Asia. Democratic leaders, concerned about Wallace's views and temperament, replaced him on the 1944 ticket with Senator Harry S. Truman.

Later career

After serving as Secretary of Commerce, Wallace was dismissed by President Truman in 1946 after publicly criticizing Truman's foreign policy toward the Soviet Union. In 1948 he ran for president as the nominee of the Progressive Party, advocating friendly relations with the Soviet Union, but won no electoral votes. He later broke with the Progressives over the Korean War and returned to farming and plant breeding in New York, where he died in 1965.
