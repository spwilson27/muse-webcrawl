Idanha-a-Nova

Idanha-a-Nova is a town and municipality in the Castelo Branco District of central Portugal, close to the border with Spain. The municipality covers an area of about 1,416 km² — one of the largest in Portugal — and has a population of roughly 9,000 (2021), while the town itself has about 2,300 inhabitants. The municipality is subdivided into 13 civil parishes (freguesias).

History

The town was founded by the Templars: in the early 13th century the Order built a castle at Idanha-a-Nova, near the older settlement of Idanha-a-Velha, a Roman and Visigothic city (Egitania) that had been bishopric seat in late antiquity. The area was settled as part of the Christian reconquest of the Beira frontier against Muslim rule and later became part of the defensive line along the Spanish border.

Geography and culture

The municipality includes part of the Naturtejo Geopark and the International Tagus Natural Park along the Tagus river. The village of Monsanto — often called "the most Portuguese village in Portugal", famous for houses built among giant granite boulders — lies within the municipality, as does the historic village of Idanha-a-Velha. Since 2002 the municipality has hosted the Boom Festival, a large biennial psychedelic trance and world culture festival held on the shores of the Marechal Carmona reservoir. Idanha-a-Nova is also designated a UNESCO Creative City of Music.
