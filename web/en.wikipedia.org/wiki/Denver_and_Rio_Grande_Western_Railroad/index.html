LocaleColorado, Utah, and adjacent states

The Denver and Rio Grande Western Railroad (reporting mark DRGW), often shortened to the Rio Grande, was an American Class I railroad that began as a 3 ft narrow-gauge line between Denver and Colorado Springs in 1871. Its mottoes included "Through the Rockies, not around them" and "Main line through the Rockies."

History

Founded by William Jackson Palmer, the railroad expanded through the Royal Gorge — the subject of the "Royal Gorge War" with the Santa Fe — and across Colorado's mining districts to Leadville, Aspen, and Durango. It reached Utah and Salt Lake City, and in 1934 the Dotsero Cutoff and the Moffat Tunnel gave it a direct Denver–Salt Lake route. The railroad operated the famous California Zephyr passenger train jointly from 1949 to 1970, then ran its own Rio Grande Zephyr until 1983.

Later years

In 1988 the Rio Grande's parent company purchased the Southern Pacific Railroad and adopted its name; the combined system was in turn acquired by Union Pacific in 1996. Several former narrow-gauge lines survive as heritage railways, including the Durango and Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad and the Cumbres and Toltec Scenic Railroad.
