Michael Robert Milken (born July 4, 1946) is an American financier and philanthropist. He is best known for his role in developing the market for high-yield bonds — popularly called "junk bonds" — at the investment bank Drexel Burnham Lambert during the 1970s and 1980s, which financed corporate takeovers and the leveraged buyout boom. He is also known for his conviction in 1990 on securities fraud charges.

Career at Drexel

Milken joined Drexel in 1969 after studying at UC Berkeley and earning an MBA from Wharton. Building on academic research showing that low-rated bonds outperformed their risk, he created a trading and underwriting operation for high-yield debt, moving it to Beverly Hills in 1978. By the mid-1980s his department dominated the market, underwriting bonds for companies such as MCI and Turner Broadcasting and financing leveraged buyouts and hostile takeovers; in 1987 he earned an estimated $550 million in a single year.

Indictment and conviction

Following the insider-trading scandal around Ivan Boesky, federal prosecutors led by Rudy Giuliani indicted Milken in 1989 on 98 counts including racketeering. In 1990 he pleaded guilty to six counts of securities and reporting violations, was fined $200 million, and was sentenced to ten years in prison, of which he served about 22 months. He was permanently barred from the securities industry. In February 2020 President Donald Trump granted him a full pardon.

Philanthropy

After his release, and following a prostate cancer diagnosis, Milken devoted himself to philanthropy through the Milken Family Foundation, the Milken Institute think tank, and the Prostate Cancer Foundation, funding medical research and education initiatives. Fortune magazine dubbed him "The Man Who Changed Medicine" in 2004.
