Frederick Ashton

Sir Frederick William Mallandaine Ashton (17 September 1904 – 18 August 1988) was a British ballet dancer and choreographer. He was founder choreographer of The Royal Ballet and served as its artistic director from 1963 to 1970. With Ninette de Valois and Marie Rambert, he was a founding figure of British ballet, and his lyrical, musical style defined the English school.

Early life

Ashton was born in Guayaquil, Ecuador, where his father was a diplomat, and raised in Lima, Peru. Inspired by seeing Anna Pavlova dance in Lima, he returned to England and studied with Léonide Massine and then Marie Rambert, who encouraged his first choreography, A Tragedy of Fashion (1926).

Career

Ashton created works for Rambert's Ballet Club and the Camargo Society before joining de Valois's Vic-Wells Ballet (later The Royal Ballet) as principal choreographer in 1935. His many ballets include Les Rendezvous (1933), Symphonic Variations (1946), Cinderella (1948) — the first full-length English ballet — Sylvia (1952), La fille mal gardée (1960), Marguerite and Armand (1963), created for Margot Fonteyn and Rudolf Nureyev, and A Month in the Country (1976).

Ashton also choreographed for opera, film and revue. He was knighted in 1962, appointed a Companion of Honour in 1970 and admitted to the Order of Merit in 1977.

Style and legacy

Ashton's style is noted for its épaulement, intricate footwork, musicality and wit. The Frederick Ashton Foundation oversees the rights in his ballets, which remain central to the repertories of The Royal Ballet and companies worldwide.
