Infrared homing

Infrared homing is a passive weapon guidance system which uses the infrared (IR) light emission from a target to track and follow it seamlessly. Missiles using infrared seeking are often called "heat-seekers" since infrared is radiated strongly by hot bodies such as aircraft engines.

Principle

An infrared seeker uses a sensor sensitive to infrared radiation, typically based on materials such as lead sulfide or indium antimonide, often cooled to improve sensitivity. The seeker produces steering signals that keep the target centred in its field of view, guiding the missile by proportional navigation.

Generations

Early seekers of the 1950s, such as those in the AIM-9 Sidewinder, could only engage targets from behind, where the hot exhaust was visible. Later generations became sensitive to cooler emissions from airframe heating, enabling all-aspect engagement, and modern imaging infrared seekers form a picture of the target, making them far more resistant to decoys.

Countermeasures

Aircraft defend against infrared missiles with flares that produce hotter decoy signatures, with directional infrared countermeasures that dazzle the seeker, and by reducing their own infrared signature.
