Performance appraisal

A performance appraisal, also known as a performance review, performance evaluation, or employee appraisal, is a regular process within organisations in which an employee's job performance is assessed, usually by their line manager, against previously agreed objectives and competencies. The results typically inform decisions about pay, promotion, training, and development.

Purpose

Appraisals serve several purposes: providing feedback to employees; setting goals for the coming period; identifying training and development needs; documenting performance for administrative decisions such as performance-related pay, promotion, or dismissal; and improving communication between managers and staff.

Methods

  • Rating scales — the appraiser scores the employee on a set of traits or competencies.
  • Management by objectives — performance is judged against specific, measurable goals agreed in advance.
  • 360-degree feedback — input is gathered from the employee's manager, peers, subordinates, and sometimes customers.
  • Behaviourally anchored rating scales (BARS) — scale points are illustrated with concrete examples of behaviour.
  • Self-assessment — the employee evaluates their own performance, often as an input to the appraisal interview.

Criticism

Performance appraisals are frequently criticised for subjectivity, recency bias (judging the whole period by recent events), halo effects, and leniency or severity in ratings. Forced ranking systems, in which a fixed proportion of staff must be rated low, have proved controversial. In response, some large employers have replaced the annual appraisal with continuous feedback and regular check-in conversations, and have separated developmental discussions from pay decisions.
