The Bakhtiari language (Persian: گویش بختیاری) is a dialect of the Luri language spoken by the Bakhtiari people, an Iranian nomadic and semi-nomadic group inhabiting the Zagros Mountains of southwestern Iran, chiefly in the provinces of Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari, Khuzestan, Isfahan and Lorestan.

Classification

Bakhtiari belongs to the Southwestern branch of the Western Iranian languages. It is closely related to other Luri dialects and to Persian, with which it shares much vocabulary and grammar, though it preserves a number of archaic features lost in standard Persian. Linguists generally treat Bakhtiari as part of the Southern Luri dialect continuum.

Dialects

The language is divided broadly into northern and southern varieties, differing in phonology and lexicon. Northern varieties are spoken around Shahr-e Kord and in the highland pastures, while southern varieties predominate in the lowlands of Khuzestan near Masjed Soleyman and Izeh.

Phonology

Bakhtiari retains the Old Iranian consonant clusters and vowel distinctions that distinguish it from colloquial Persian in several respects, and its intonation and stress patterns are noticeably different to Persian speakers.

Usage and status

The language is primarily oral; most speakers are bilingual in Persian, which dominates education, media and administration. Bakhtiari has a rich tradition of epic poetry, proverbs and folk songs connected to the tribe's nomadic pastoral way of life.
