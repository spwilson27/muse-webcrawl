Zaragoza

Zaragoza is the capital of the autonomous community of Aragon in north-eastern Spain, on the Ebro river midway between Madrid and Barcelona. With about 680,000 inhabitants it is Spain's fifth-largest city. Founded by the Romans as Caesaraugusta — the only city named for Augustus in full — it is famed for the vast baroque Basilica of Our Lady of the Pillar on the Ebro, the mudéjar Aljafería palace, and for its two ferocious sieges in the Peninsular War (1808–1809), which became legends of Spanish resistance.

History

Caesaraugusta flourished as a Roman colony of veterans; its forum, walls and theatre survive in part. Muslim Saraqusta was a taifa capital whose Taifa kings built the Aljafería, one of the finest Islamic palaces in Spain; Alfonso I of Aragon took the city in 1118, making it his capital. The painter Francisco Goya was born in nearby Fuendetodos and formed in Zaragoza. In 1808–09 the city, defended by General Palafox and its citizens — with heroines such as Agustina de Aragón, who famously fired a cannon at the Portillo gate — endured two French sieges of house-to-house fighting that killed tens of thousands before capitulating in February 1809. Zaragoza hosted Expo 2008, devoted to water, which left Zaha Hadid's Bridge Pavilion among its landmarks.
