Myopathy

In medicine, myopathy is a disease of the muscle in which the muscle fibres do not function properly. This results in muscular weakness. Myopathy is a broad category encompassing inherited and acquired disorders of skeletal muscle, distinct from diseases of the nerves or neuromuscular junction.

Classification

Myopathies may be congenital, developing at or before birth, or acquired later in life. Inherited forms include the muscular dystrophies (such as Duchenne muscular dystrophy), congenital myopathies, metabolic myopathies such as glycogen storage diseases affecting muscle, and mitochondrial myopathies. Acquired forms include inflammatory myopathies (polymyositis, dermatomyositis and inclusion body myositis), endocrine myopathies, and toxic or drug-induced myopathies, including those associated with statins and alcohol.

Signs, diagnosis and treatment

Symptoms typically include proximal muscle weakness, muscle pain or cramps, fatigue and, in some forms, muscle wasting or enlargement. Diagnosis combines clinical examination, blood tests (notably creatine kinase), electromyography, muscle imaging, biopsy and genetic testing. Treatment depends on the cause: inflammatory myopathies may respond to immunosuppression, while many inherited myopathies are managed supportively with physiotherapy and monitoring for cardiac and respiratory complications.
