Flight dispatcher

OccupationAirline operations
Also known asAircraft dispatcher, flight operations officer
LicensingFAA Aircraft Dispatcher certificate (U.S.); national licences elsewhere

A flight dispatcher (also known as an aircraft dispatcher or flight operations officer) is a licensed airman who, jointly with the pilot in command, shares legal responsibility for the safe conduct of an airline flight under operational control systems such as the U.S. Federal Aviation Regulations Part 121. Dispatchers are sometimes described as "the pilots on the ground".

Responsibilities

Before departure, the dispatcher plans the flight: selecting the route and altitude using weather forecasts, winds aloft, NOTAMs, aircraft performance and airspace restrictions; computing fuel requirements including reserves and alternates; and preparing the dispatch release, which the captain must sign. During flight, the dispatcher monitors progress and weather, and must inform the crew of any condition affecting safety, recommending or ordering diversions, delays or cancellations when necessary. A dispatcher typically oversees many flights simultaneously from the airline's operations control centre.

Qualification

In the United States, dispatchers must hold an FAA Aircraft Dispatcher certificate, requiring a minimum age of 23, an approved course or qualifying experience, and written and practical examinations covering meteorology, navigation, regulations, aircraft systems and human factors. Comparable licensing exists in many other jurisdictions under ICAO guidance for flight operations officers.
