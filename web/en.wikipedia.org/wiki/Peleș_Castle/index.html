Peleș Castle

LocationSinaia, Prahova County, Romania
Construction1873–1914
Architectural styleNeo-Renaissance (German)
Commissioned byKing Carol I of Romania

Peleș Castle (Romanian: Castelul Peleș) is a Neo-Renaissance palace placed in the Carpathian Mountains, near Sinaia, in Prahova County, Romania, on an existing medieval route linking Transylvania and Wallachia. It was constructed for King Carol I of Romania between 1873 and 1914 and served as the summer residence of the Romanian royal family.

History

Carol I first visited Sinaia in 1866 and decided to build a summer palace in the valley of the Peleș stream. The foundation stone was laid in 1875, and the castle was inaugurated in 1883, though work on the interiors continued until 1914, the year of the king's death. King Michael I was born at the neighbouring Foișor lodge, and Peleș remained a royal residence until 1947, when the monarchy was abolished; the communist state later opened it as a museum.

Architecture and collections

The castle, designed principally by Johannes Schultz with later work by Karel Liman, combines German Neo-Renaissance exteriors with richly varied interiors — including a Hall of Honour in carved wood, a Florentine room, a Moorish salon, and one of Europe's early private cinemas. It was among the first European castles with central heating and electric lighting. Its collections include some 2,000 paintings and an important armoury of medieval and early-modern weapons.

Today

After 1989 the castle was restituted to former King Michael, who leased it back to the Romanian state; it continues to operate as a museum and is one of Romania's most visited monuments. The nearby smaller Pelișor Castle, built for Ferdinand and Marie, forms part of the same complex.
