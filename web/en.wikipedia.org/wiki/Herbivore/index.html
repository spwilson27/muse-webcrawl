Herbivore

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A herbivore is an animal that eats primarily plants — leaves, stems, roots, seeds, fruit, nectar or algae. Herbivores are the primary consumers in most food webs, converting plant material into animal tissue eaten in turn by carnivores, and they exert powerful selection on plant defences such as thorns, toxins and tough leaves.

Digestive adaptations

Because plant cell walls (cellulose) cannot be digested by animal enzymes, herbivores rely on microbial fermentation: ruminants (cattle, deer) ferment in a multi-chambered stomach and chew the cud, while hindgut fermenters (horses, rabbits) use an enlarged caecum and colon, sometimes re-ingesting droppings. Other adaptations include flat grinding molars, long guts, detoxifying livers and, in insects, highly specialised host-plant relationships.

Ecological role

Grazers and browsers shape landscapes — elephants open woodland, prairie dogs and bison maintain grasslands, sea urchins can convert kelp forests to barrens. Herbivory moves nutrients, disperses seeds and can trigger plant chemical defences that affect whole communities.
