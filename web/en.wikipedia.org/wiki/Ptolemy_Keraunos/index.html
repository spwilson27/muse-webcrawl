Ptolemy Keraunos (Greek: Πτολεμαῖος Κεραυνός, "Ptolemy the Thunderbolt"; c. 319 BC – January/February 279 BC) was a member of the Ptolemaic dynasty and king of Macedon from 281 BC to 279 BC. He was the eldest son of Ptolemy I Soter, founder of Ptolemaic Egypt, and his first wife Eurydice, daughter of Antipater.

Exile and rise

When Ptolemy I designated his younger son by Berenice, the future Ptolemy II, as heir, Keraunos left Egypt and took refuge at the court of Lysimachus, king of Thrace and Macedon, and later with Seleucus I. After Seleucus defeated and killed Lysimachus at the Battle of Corupedium in 281 BC, Ptolemy murdered Seleucus — reportedly in revenge for wrongs done to his family — and had himself proclaimed king of Macedon by the army.

Reign

Keraunos consolidated his position by marrying (briefly) his half-sister Arsinoe II, the widow of Lysimachus, though he soon had two of her young sons killed, driving her into exile. He repelled an invasion by Antigonus II Gonatas and defeated the Illyrian king Monunius.

Death

In 279 BC, during the great Gallic invasion of Greece, Keraunos refused offers of help from the Dardanians and marched against the Gauls under Bolgios. His army was destroyed near Lysimacheia; wounded and captured, he was beheaded by the Gauls, and his head was carried on a spear. Macedon fell into anarchy for two years after his death.
