Scottish Premiership

Founded: 2013
Country: Scotland
Teams: 12
Relegation to: Scottish Championship

Sponsor name: William Hill Premiership

The Scottish Premiership is the top division of the Scottish Professional Football League (SPFL), the league competition for professional football clubs in Scotland. It was established in July 2013 when the SPFL was formed by the merger of the Scottish Premier League and the Scottish Football League.

Format

Twelve clubs compete, playing each other three times (33 matches) before the league splits into a "top six" and "bottom six" for a final five fixtures, giving a 38-game season — a format inherited from the SPL that regularly produces the anomaly of some clubs finishing with more points than teams above them. The bottom club is relegated to the Scottish Championship; the 11th-placed club enters a play-off with Championship sides. The champions qualify for the UEFA Champions League qualifying rounds, with further places in the Europa League and Conference League depending on Scotland's UEFA coefficient, and the Scottish Cup winners also enter Europe.

History and dominance

The Premiership era has been dominated by Celtic, who won the first nine titles (completing nine-in-a-row in 2020), before Rangers interrupted the run in 2020–21 with an unbeaten season under Steven Gerrard; Celtic then resumed winning under Ange Postecoglou and Brendan Rodgers. The Old Firm derby between the two Glasgow clubs is the league's marquee fixture and one of world football's fiercest rivalries. Recurring debates about the league concern the split format, the imbalance of resources between the Old Firm and the rest, and the quality of the television deal, currently with Sky Sports.

Clubs

Regular participants include Celtic, Rangers, Hearts, Hibernian, Aberdeen, Dundee United, Dundee, Motherwell, Kilmarnock, St Mirren, St Johnstone, and Ross County.
