Jim Carrey

James Eugene Carrey (born January 17, 1962) is a Canadian-American actor and comedian known for his energetic slapstick performances and rubbery facial expressions. After breaking through on the sketch show In Living Color, he became one of the biggest film stars of the 1990s with Ace Ventura: Pet Detective, The Mask and Dumb and Dumber (all 1994), and later won critical acclaim for dramatic roles in The Truman Show (1998) and Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (2004).

Carrey in 2020.

Early life

Carrey was born in Newmarket, Ontario, Canada, and performed stand-up in Toronto clubs as a teenager, developing impressions and physical comedy. His family's periods of poverty — at one point living in a van — shaped his drive to succeed. After moving to Los Angeles, he worked the comedy-club circuit and appeared in small television and film roles through the 1980s.

Career

Carrey's breakthrough came on Fox's In Living Color (1990–1994), where characters such as Fire Marshall Bill became audience favourites. His 1994 film trio made him a superstar, and he became the first actor to command a $20 million salary, for The Cable Guy (1996). He balanced comedy hits such as Liar Liar (1997) and Bruce Almighty (2003) with acclaimed dramatic turns in The Truman Show, Man on the Moon (1999) and Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind, winning two Golden Globe Awards. Later roles include Dr. Robotnik in the Sonic the Hedgehog films.

Personal life

Also a painter and political cartoonist, Carrey has spoken publicly about his experiences with depression. He became a US citizen in 2004 while retaining Canadian citizenship.
