Kefaya

Kefaya demonstrations in Cairo in the mid-2000s.

Kefaya (Arabic: كفاية, "enough") is the informal name of the Egyptian Movement for Change, a grassroots coalition formed in 2004 to oppose the continued rule of President Hosni Mubarak and the prospect that he would pass power to his son Gamal.

Origins and activity

The movement emerged in the summer of 2004, when a manifesto signed by several hundred intellectuals and activists demanded genuine democratic reform and an end to the state of emergency. Its first public protest in December 2004 broke a long-standing taboo by directly criticising the president in street demonstrations. Kefaya brought together Islamists, leftists, liberals and Nasserists in an unusually broad coalition and pioneered tactics — small, mobile, media-savvy protests — later used by Egyptian opposition movements.

Decline and legacy

After the 2005 elections, in which Mubarak was re-elected amid low turnout and controversy, the movement lost momentum amid internal divisions and official pressure. Nevertheless, Kefaya is widely seen as a forerunner of the protest movements that culminated in the Egyptian revolution of 2011, and its name and slogan became bywords for opposition to the regime.
