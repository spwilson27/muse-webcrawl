The Crusades were a series of religious wars initiated by the Latin Church in the medieval period, principally the campaigns between 1095 and 1291 to recover Jerusalem and the Holy Land from Muslim rule. Pope Urban II's call at Clermont launched the First Crusade, which took Jerusalem in 1099.

Course and legacy

Crusader states (Edessa, Antioch, Tripoli, Jerusalem) endured two centuries amid further Crusades — including the Third, against Saladin, and the Fourth, which sacked Christian Constantinople in 1204. Orders such as the Templars and Hospitallers arose from the movement. The Crusades deepened East–West exchange and antagonism, with a contested legacy.
