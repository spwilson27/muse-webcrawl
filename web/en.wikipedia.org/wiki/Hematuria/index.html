Hematuria

SpecialtyNephrology, urology
TypesGross (macroscopic); microscopic
DiagnosisUrinalysis, urine microscopy

Hematuria or haematuria is the presence of blood in the urine. When the blood is visible to the naked eye the condition is called gross or macroscopic hematuria; when it is detectable only under a microscope or by dipstick testing it is microscopic hematuria. It is a symptom rather than a disease in itself and has a wide range of possible causes.

Causes

Common causes include urinary tract infection, kidney stones, benign prostatic hyperplasia, and vigorous exercise. More serious causes include bladder, kidney or prostate cancer, and glomerular diseases such as IgA nephropathy and glomerulonephritis. In children, causes include urinary infection, congenital abnormalities and glomerular disease; transient microscopic hematuria after exercise or fever is common and usually benign.

Evaluation

Evaluation begins with urinalysis and microscopy to confirm true hematuria (distinguishing it from haemoglobinuria, myoglobinuria and pigmenturia from foods or drugs). The presence of dysmorphic red blood cells, red-cell casts or significant proteinuria suggests a glomerular source and points toward nephrology referral; otherwise imaging of the urinary tract and cystoscopy may be used to look for stones, tumours or other structural causes, particularly in older patients and those with risk factors for malignancy.

Treatment

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause — antibiotics for infection, procedures for stones or obstruction, and specific therapy for glomerular or malignant disease. Gross hematuria with clot retention is a urological urgency requiring bladder irrigation or catheterisation.
