Food labelling

FieldFood law / consumer information
CoversIngredients, nutrition, allergens, dates, origin, claims

Food labelling is the body of laws, regulations and practices governing the information printed on food packaging: the product's identity, ingredients, nutritional content, allergens, net quantity, date marks and the name and address of the manufacturer or seller.

Elements

Most jurisdictions require an ingredients list in descending order of weight, declaration of specified allergens (such as nuts, milk, egg, gluten-containing cereals and soya), and nutrition information — in the United States the standardised Nutrition Facts panel, mandated by the Nutrition Labeling and Education Act of 1990, and in the European Union a nutrition declaration required under Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011. Date marks include "use by" dates (safety) and "best before" dates (quality). Voluntary schemes include front-of-pack traffic-light labels in the UK, Nutri-Score in several European countries, organic and fair-trade certification marks, and health and nutrition claims, which are separately regulated.

Purposes and debates

Labels aim to inform consumers, protect people with allergies and intolerances, and prevent misleading marketing. Recurring debates concern serving sizes, added-sugar disclosure, country-of-origin labelling, and whether front-of-pack interpretive labels change purchasing behaviour.
