Hiʻiaka is the larger, outer moon of the dwarf planet Haumea. It is named after Hiʻiaka, the patron goddess of Hawaiʻi and of hula dancers, who in Hawaiian mythology was born from the mouth of Haumea. The moon was discovered in 2005 by a team led by Michael Brown at the W. M. Keck Observatory, working from observations of the Haumea system.

Discovery and naming

Hiʻiaka was discovered on 26 January 2005 and nicknamed "Rudolph" by the discovery team before receiving its official name. It was the first moon of Haumea found; the smaller inner moon, Namaka, was discovered later the same year.

Physical properties

With an estimated diameter of roughly 320 km, Hiʻiaka is by far the larger of Haumea's two moons and is composed mostly of water ice, with a surface spectrum dominated by strong water-ice absorption bands. It orbits Haumea in about 49 days at a distance of roughly 49,000 km, in a nearly circular orbit. Because Haumea's mass was determined mainly from Hiʻiaka's orbit, the moon played a key role in characterising the system. Like the rest of the Haumea collisional family, Hiʻiaka is thought to be a fragment knocked off Haumea by an ancient impact.
