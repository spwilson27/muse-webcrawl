Allah

Allah (Arabic: الله, romanized Allāh) is the standard Arabic word for God and the name used for God in Islam. In the Islamic tradition, Allah is the one, unique, and omnipotent creator of the universe, and the central tenet of the faith — expressed in the shahada — is that "there is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah." The word is also used by Arabic-speaking Christians and Jews for God.

Etymology

The term is generally explained as a contraction of the definite article al- with ilāh ("god, deity"), yielding "the God." Cognates appear in other Semitic languages, such as Hebrew Eloah and Aramaic Alaha. The word was used in pre-Islamic Arabia for the supreme deity before becoming, in the Quran, the proper name of the one God.

In Islam

Islamic theology stresses the absolute oneness of God (tawhid) and rejects any association of partners with Him (shirk). The Quran describes Allah with numerous attributes — merciful, compassionate, omniscient, just — and tradition enumerates 99 names (al-asmāʾ al-ḥusnā) expressing these qualities. Chapter 112, Al-Ikhlas, is a concise statement of His unity: "Say: He is Allah, the One... He begets not, nor is He begotten, and there is none like unto Him."

Usage beyond Islam

Arab Christians and Jews have used "Allah" for God for over a millennium, and the word appears in Arabic Bible translations. In Malaysia and Indonesia the term's use by non-Muslims has been legally contested. In the West, "Allah" is sometimes treated as a distinct proper name, though Muslims regard it simply as the Arabic word for the same God worshipped in Judaism and Christianity.

Typography and calligraphy

The word has a standardised calligraphic form (ﷲ) honoured in Islamic art, where figural depiction of God is prohibited and calligraphy of His name is a central devotional art.
