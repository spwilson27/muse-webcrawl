Nexstar Media Group, Inc. is an American publicly traded media company with interests in television stations, networks and digital media throughout the United States. Headquartered in Irving, Texas, it is the largest owner of television stations in the country, operating more than 200 stations in over 115 markets reaching roughly two-thirds of U.S. television households. The company also owns the cable news channel NewsNation and a majority stake in The CW television network.

History

Nexstar was founded in 1996 by Perry A. Sook, beginning with the purchase of WYOU in Scranton, Pennsylvania. It grew steadily through acquisitions, completing an initial public offering in 2003. A major expansion came with the acquisition of Media General in 2017, which made Nexstar the second-largest station owner in the United States at the time.

In December 2018, Nexstar announced the acquisition of Tribune Media for about $4.1 billion, the largest broadcaster acquisition in U.S. history at that time; the deal closed in September 2019, with some stations divested to satisfy regulators, and made Nexstar the largest U.S. television station operator. In 2022, Nexstar acquired a 75% ownership stake in The CW from Warner Bros. Discovery and Paramount. In 2025, Nexstar announced a proposed acquisition of rival broadcaster Tegna, subject to regulatory approval.

Properties

  • More than 200 owned or operated broadcast television stations
  • NewsNation, a national cable news channel (relaunched from WGN America in 2021)
  • A 75% stake in The CW Network
  • Digital properties including local station websites and The Hill, a Washington, D.C.–based political newspaper and website (acquired 2021)
  • Ownership stakes in TV Food Network and other ventures

Criticism

Nexstar has been involved in notable retransmission consent disputes with cable and satellite providers resulting in temporary station blackouts, and its news operations have drawn commentary over the balance between local and nationally produced content.
