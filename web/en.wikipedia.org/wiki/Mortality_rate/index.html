Mortality rate

Mortality rate
FieldEpidemiology, demography
DefinitionDeaths per unit of population per unit of time
Common scalingPer 1,000 or per 100,000 population per year
Related measuresInfant mortality rate, case fatality rate, life expectancy

Mortality rate, or death rate, is a measure of the number of deaths (in general, or due to a specific cause) in a particular population, scaled to the size of that population, per unit of time. Mortality rate is typically expressed in units of deaths per 1,000 individuals per year; thus, a mortality rate of 9.5 in a population of 1,000 would mean 9.5 deaths per year in that entire population.

Crude death rate = (number of deaths during a period / population at risk) × 1,000

Types of mortality rate

The crude death rate measures all deaths in a population but is strongly affected by its age structure: an older population will have a higher crude rate than a younger one with identical age-specific risks. Demographers therefore also compute age-specific mortality rates and standardized mortality rates that adjust for differences in population composition, allowing fair comparison between countries and over time.

Other widely used measures include the infant mortality rate (deaths of children under one year per 1,000 live births), the maternal mortality ratio (maternal deaths per 100,000 live births), cause-specific mortality rates, and the case fatality rate, which is the proportion of people diagnosed with a disease who die of it — a different quantity from the population mortality rate.

Global trends

Global crude death rates fell through most of the 20th century as infectious disease mortality declined, reaching roughly 7–8 per 1,000 per year in the early 21st century, though rates vary widely between countries and rose temporarily during the COVID-19 pandemic. Mortality statistics underpin life tables and the calculation of life expectancy.
