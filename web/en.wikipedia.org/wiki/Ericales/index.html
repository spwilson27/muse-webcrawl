The Ericales are a large and diverse order of dicotyledons, including, for example, tea, persimmon, blueberry, Brazil nut, and azalea. The order includes trees, bushes, lianas, and herbaceous plants. Together with ordinary autophytic plants, the Ericales include chlorophyll-deficient mycoheterotrophic plants (e.g., Sarcodes sanguinea) and carnivorous plants (e.g., the genus Sarracenia).

Description

Many species have five petals, often growing together. Mycorrhizal associations are common in the order, and many members accumulate aluminium. Flowers are often showy, with a superior ovary in most families. The order contains roughly 11,500 species in about 320 genera, distributed in 22 families under the APG IV classification.

Families

Families include the Ericaceae (heather family), Actinidiaceae (including kiwifruit), Ebenaceae (ebony and persimmon), Primulaceae, Sapotaceae, Theaceae (tea family), Lecythidaceae (including the Brazil nut), Polemoniaceae, Styracaceae, Symplocaceae, Pentaphylacaceae, Sladeniaceae, Roridulaceae, Sarraceniaceae, Clethraceae, Cyrillaceae, Mitrastemonaceae, Marcgraviaceae, Balsaminaceae, Tetrameristaceae, Fouquieriaceae and Diapensiaceae.

Economic importance

Probably the most used member of the order is tea (Camellia sinensis) from the family Theaceae. The order also includes important fruit crops (blueberry, cranberry, kiwifruit, persimmon), timber trees such as ebony, and many popular ornamental plants including azalea, rhododendron, camellia and primula.
