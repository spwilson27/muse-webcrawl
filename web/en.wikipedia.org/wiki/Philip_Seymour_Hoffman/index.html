Philip Seymour Hoffman (23 July 1967 – 2 February 2014) was an American actor. Known for his distinctive supporting and character roles, he is widely regarded as one of the greatest actors of his generation. He won the Academy Award for Best Actor for his portrayal of Truman Capote in Capote (2005).

Life and career

Hoffman was born in Fairport, New York, and studied acting at the Tisch School of the Arts. After an early screen appearance in Scent of a Woman (1992), he built a reputation through acclaimed supporting roles in films by Paul Thomas Anderson — Boogie Nights (1997), Magnolia (1999), Punch-Drunk Love (2002) — and in The Talented Mr. Ripley (1999), Almost Famous (2000) and Cold Mountain (2003).

His performance as Truman Capote brought him the Academy Award, BAFTA, Golden Globe and Screen Actors Guild Award. He received further Oscar nominations for Charlie Wilson's War (2007), Doubt (2008) and The Master (2012). Hoffman was also an accomplished stage actor and director, serving as artistic director of the LAByrinth Theater Company in New York; he earned Tony Award nominations for True West, Long Day's Journey into Night and Death of a Salesman. Later film roles included Plutarch Heavensbee in The Hunger Games series.

Death and legacy

Hoffman, who had spoken openly about struggles with drug addiction, was found dead in his Manhattan apartment on 2 February 2014; the cause was determined to be acute mixed drug intoxication. He was survived by his partner Mimi O'Donnell and their three children. Critics have consistently ranked him among the finest screen actors of his era.
