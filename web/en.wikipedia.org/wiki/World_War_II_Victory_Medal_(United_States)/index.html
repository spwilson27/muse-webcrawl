The World War II Victory Medal is a service medal of the United States military which was established by an Act of Congress on 6 July 1945 and awarded for service between 7 December 1941 and 31 December 1946. There was no minimum time limit for service; any member of the armed forces, including members of the Armed Forces of the Government of the Philippine Islands, who served on active duty or as a reservist during the eligibility period qualified.

Design

The medal, designed by Thomas Hudson Jones, depicts the figure of Liberation holding a broken sword in one hand and the palm of peace in the other, with the rising sun in the background. The reverse bears the Four Freedoms — Freedom of Speech, Freedom of Worship, Freedom from Want and Freedom from Fear — surrounded by a wreath. The ribbon is predominantly red with rainbow edges adapted from the First World War victory medal design.

Eligibility

Because it was awarded to all who served during the period, the World War II Victory Medal is one of the most widely distributed United States decorations, with millions of recipients. Merchant mariners who served in the Second World War later received a separate Merchant Marine World War II Victory Medal.
