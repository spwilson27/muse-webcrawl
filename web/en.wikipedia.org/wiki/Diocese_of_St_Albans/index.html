Diocese of St Albans

The Diocese of St Albans is a Church of England diocese covering the counties of Hertfordshire and Bedfordshire, much of the London Borough of Barnet, and part of the parish of Wexham in Buckinghamshire. It was created on 4 May 1877 from parts of the Diocese of Rochester and the Diocese of Ely.

Its cathedral is St Albans Cathedral, the former abbey church built over the shrine of St Alban, the first British Christian martyr, executed in the Roman city of Verulamium. Unusually for a cathedral, it remains also a parish church. The Bishop of St Albans is assisted by the suffragan bishops of Bedford and Hertford, and by three archdeacons (St Albans, Bedford, and Hertford).

The diocese, in the Province of Canterbury, embraces both fast-growing London commuter towns such as St Albans, Watford, Luton, and Stevenage and deep rural parishes. Its administrative offices, Holywell Lodge, stand in St Albans near the cathedral.
