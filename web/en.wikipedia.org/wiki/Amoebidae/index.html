The Amoebidae are a family of Amoebozoa, including naked and testate amoeba, most of which are free-living in fresh water. The family includes the classic genus Amoeba, whose best-known member, Amoeba proteus, is a standard subject of school and university biology teaching, and the giant amoeba Chaos carolinense.

Characteristics

Members of the Amoebidae move and feed by means of lobose pseudopodia — broad, blunt extensions of the cytoplasm — engulfing bacteria, algae, and small protists by phagocytosis. Cells lack a fixed shape and continually change outline as they move. Most species reproduce asexually by binary fission, and many form dormant cysts under adverse conditions.

Genera

  • Amoeba — including Amoeba proteus
  • Chaos — large, multinucleate amoebae
  • Metachaos
  • Polychaos
  • Parachaos
  • Trichamoeba

Habitat

Amoebids occur in freshwater ponds, streams, damp soils, and the surface films of aquatic plants. They are important grazers of bacteria and contribute to nutrient cycling in aquatic ecosystems.
