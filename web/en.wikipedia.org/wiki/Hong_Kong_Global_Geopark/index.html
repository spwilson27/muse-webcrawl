Hong Kong Global Geopark

The Hong Kong UNESCO Global Geopark, formerly Hong Kong National Geopark (香港國家地質公園), was inaugurated on 3 November 2009. It is a single entity of 150 km², located in the northeast of Hong Kong, featuring eight geo-areas distributed across the Sai Kung Volcanic Rock Region and the Northeast New Territories Sedimentary Rock Region.

History

On 15 September 2011, UNESCO announced the decision to include the Hong Kong Geopark as part of its Global Geoparks Network. Since November 2015, it has been named Hong Kong UNESCO Global Geopark.

Features

The Sai Kung Volcanic Rock Region is noted for its hexagonal columnar joints of volcanic tuff, formed around 140 million years ago following explosive volcanic eruptions. The Northeast New Territories Sedimentary Rock Region preserves sedimentary rocks formed from the Devonian to the Paleogene, including the red conglomerates and sandstones of Port Island (Chek Chau) and the siltstones of Tung Ping Chau.
