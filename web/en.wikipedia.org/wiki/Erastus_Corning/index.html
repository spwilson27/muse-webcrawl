Erastus Corning

Erastus Corning (December 14, 1794 – April 9, 1872) was an American businessman and politician from Albany, New York. A leading iron manufacturer and railroad promoter, he was a founder of the New York Central Railroad and served in the United States House of Representatives, the New York State Senate, and as mayor of Albany.

Business career

Born in Norwich, Connecticut, Corning moved to Albany as a young man and entered the hardware and iron trade, building the Erastus Corning & Co. into one of the largest iron works in the United States; its foundry cast the iron segments of the dome of the United States Capitol. He became president of the Utica and Schenectady Railroad and in 1853 engineered the consolidation of ten New York railroads into the New York Central Railroad, serving as its first president until 1864.

Political career

Corning was mayor of Albany from 1834 to 1837, a state senator, and a Democratic member of Congress from 1857 to 1863 (returning briefly in 1864). A "War Democrat" during the Civil War, he was a member of the 1861 Peace Convention and later a critic of some of President Lincoln's policies, though he supported the Union cause.

Legacy

Corning's descendants carried on his Albany influence; his great-grandson, Erastus Corning 2nd, served as mayor of Albany for over four decades in the twentieth century. The Corning Tower in Albany's Empire State Plaza is named for the younger Corning.
