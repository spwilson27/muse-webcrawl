HarperCollins Publishers LLC is an Anglo-American publishing company that is considered to be one of the "Big Five" English-language publishers, along with Penguin Random House, Hachette, Macmillan, and Simon & Schuster. The company is headquartered in New York City and is a subsidiary of News Corp.

History

HarperCollins was formed in 1989 when News Corporation, which had acquired the American publisher Harper & Row in 1987, purchased the British publisher William Collins, Sons in 1989 and merged the two. Harper & Row itself traced its origins to 1817, when brothers James and John Harper founded their New York printing business. The name HarperCollins combines the two houses' names.

Imprints and authors

The company publishes under numerous imprints, including Harper, William Morrow, Avon, Ecco, Amistad, and the children's publisher Katherine Tegen Books. Its catalogue includes works by Agatha Christie, J. R. R. Tolkien, C. S. Lewis, and Maurice Sendak, and it publishes the Collins English Dictionary. In 2012 HarperCollins acquired part of Thomas Nelson, strengthening its position in Christian publishing.

Operations

HarperCollins has publishing operations in some 17 countries and publishes approximately 10,000 new books per year in 16 languages. It was an early mover among major publishers in digitising its catalogue and selling e-books.
