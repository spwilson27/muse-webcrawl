Super Famicom

The Super Famicom (スーパーファミコン, Sūpā Famikon, officially abbreviated SFC) is a 16-bit home video game console developed by Nintendo and released in Japan on November 21, 1990. It is the Japanese version of the system sold internationally as the Super Nintendo Entertainment System (SNES), succeeding the Famicom (the Japanese counterpart of the NES).

Hardware

The Super Famicom is built around a Ricoh 5A22 processor derived from the 65C816, with 128 KB of work RAM, a custom picture processing unit able to display 32,768 colours (256 on screen at once) and hardware effects such as Mode 7 rotation and scaling, and a Sony SPC700-based audio subsystem designed by Ken Kutaragi. Cartridges could contain enhancement chips — such as the Super FX polygon chip used in Star Fox (1993) — extending the hardware's capabilities over the console's life.

Regional differences

The Japanese and PAL-region Super Famicom/SNES shells were rounded, with controllers bearing red, yellow, green, and blue buttons, while the North American SNES received a chunkier purple-and-lavender redesign. Cartridge shapes and lockout chips provided regional lockout. In South Korea the system was distributed by Hyundai as the Super Comboy.

Library and legacy

The console's library included Super Mario World (its pack-in launch title), The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past, Super Metroid, Final Fantasy VI, Chrono Trigger, and Donkey Kong Country. Despite Sega's Mega Drive competition during the "console wars," the Super Famicom became the best-selling 16-bit console, with 49.1 million units of the combined SNES/Super Famicom sold worldwide. Nintendo revived the design in 2017 with the Super Famicom mini (the Japanese edition of the SNES Classic Edition). Peripherals included the Satellaview satellite broadcast add-on in Japan.
