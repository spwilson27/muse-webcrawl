Newsnight is a British daily current affairs television programme broadcast by the BBC on BBC Two. First aired on 28 January 1980, it provides in-depth analysis of the day's news, extended interviews with politicians and public figures, and coverage of the arts and culture. It is one of the BBC's flagship news programmes.

Presenters

The programme's presenters have included Peter Snow, Charles Wheeler, Donald MacCormick, Jeremy Paxman, Kirsty Wark, Emily Maitlis, Gavin Esler and Victoria Derbyshire. Jeremy Paxman, who presented from 1989 to 2014, became especially associated with its combative interview style; in a famous 1997 encounter he asked the Home Secretary, Michael Howard, the same question about a dozen times without receiving a direct answer.

Notable episodes and controversies

Newsnight has been at the centre of several major journalistic episodes. In 2011–12, its handling of investigations into the sex offender Jimmy Savile — a shelved report followed by a separate, mistaken report that led to the misidentification of a former politician — precipitated a crisis at the BBC and the resignation of Director-General George Entwistle. Emily Maitlis's November 2019 interview with Prince Andrew about his friendship with Jeffrey Epstein drew worldwide attention and won major awards.

Format changes

In 2023, as part of cost-cutting at BBC News, Newsnight was reformatted: from 2024 it became a shorter, 30-minute programme focused on studio discussion and debate, with its dedicated reporting team disbanded — a change that prompted public criticism from former presenters.
