Internet Society

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Internet Society
AbbreviationISOC
Founded1992
FoundersVint Cerf, Robert Kahn, Lyman Chapin
TypeNon-profit advocacy and standards support
[Image: Photograph of an Internet Society community networking workshop in a developing region]

The Internet Society (ISOC) is an American nonprofit advocacy organisation founded in 1992 with the stated mission 'to promote the open development, evolution, and use of the Internet for the benefit of all people throughout the world.'

It was co-founded by Internet pioneers Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn to provide an institutional and financial home for the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF), whose standards work it still supports. The Society also works on Internet access and policy: community networks in underserved areas, encryption advocacy, and the annual Global Internet Report. Its chapters in more than a hundred countries bring local engineers and users into global governance discussions.

ISOC is also the parent of Public Interest Registry, which operates the .org top-level domain — a relationship that drew scrutiny in 2019–2020 when the registry was nearly sold to a private equity firm, a transaction ultimately blocked after ICANN withheld consent.
