The Kodokan Judo Institute (Japanese: 講道館, Kōdōkan), generally known as the Kodokan, is the headquarters of the worldwide judo community and the governing institution founded by Kanō Jigorō, the creator of judo, in 1882. Its name means "the place for teaching the way."

History

Kanō Jigorō founded the Kodokan in 1882 in a small room at the Eishō-ji temple in Tokyo, with a practice mat area of about twelve tatami and nine students in its first year. As judo grew, the institute moved several times before settling in its present eight-storey headquarters in Bunkyō, Tokyo, opened in 1958, which contains multiple dōjō including a main hall holding around 1,300 tatami.

Functions

The Kodokan teaches and promotes Kodokan judo, awards dan and kyū ranks, certifies instructors, preserves kata (forms), and maintains a library and museum on the history of judo. Its ranking system is recognised throughout the world, although national federations affiliated with the International Judo Federation also grant ranks. The institute holds the monthly kōhaku shiai (red-and-white contests) and the annual All Japan Judo Championships.
