Guidance system

A guidance system is a virtual or physical device, or a group of devices, implementing a guidance process used for controlling the movement of a vehicle, ship, aircraft, missile, satellite, or other moving object. Typically, the guidance system is integrated with the navigation system and the flight control system: navigation determines where the vehicle is, guidance computes the course needed to reach the destination, and control moves the vehicle accordingly.

History

Early guidance systems were mechanical and gyroscopic, such as the autopilots of the early 20th century and the gyroscopic guidance of the German V-2 rocket. The development of inertial navigation, radar and digital computers during and after the Second World War produced the sophisticated guidance systems of modern missiles, spacecraft and airliners.

Types

Guidance methods for missiles include inertial guidance, command guidance, beam riding, homing guidance (active, semi-active and passive, using radar, infrared or laser seekers), celestial navigation, and satellite navigation (GPS/ GNSS). Spacecraft guidance, exemplified by the Apollo Guidance Computer, computes trajectories and performs mid-course corrections to reach targets such as the Moon.

Components

A guidance system typically comprises sensors (gyroscopes, accelerometers, seekers), a guidance computer running navigation and steering laws, and interfaces to actuators such as control surfaces, gimballed engines or reaction control thrusters.
