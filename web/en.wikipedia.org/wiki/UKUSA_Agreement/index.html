UKUSA Agreement
Signed5 March 1946
Original partiesUnited Kingdom, United States
Later partiesCanada, Australia, New Zealand
TypeSignals intelligence treaty

The UKUSA Agreement (also rendered UK-USA) is a multilateral agreement for cooperation in signals intelligence between Australia, Canada, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, and the United States, also known as the Five Eyes. Originally a bilateral pact between the United Kingdom and the United States signed on 5 March 1946, it grew out of the wartime BRUSA Agreement of 1943 and the extraordinary codebreaking collaboration of the Second World War.

Provisions

Under the agreement, the parties divide global signals intelligence collection among themselves, share raw intercepted material and finished intelligence, and agree not to spy on one another—an undertaking that has been both celebrated and questioned. The principal agencies are the US National Security Agency (NSA), the UK Government Communications Headquarters (GCHQ), Canada's Communications Security Establishment, Australia's Signals Directorate, and New Zealand's Government Communications Security Bureau.

Disclosure

The existence of the agreement was long an official secret in some member states. Australia acknowledged it in 1973 and Canada in 1974, while the UK and US did not formally release the text until June 2010, when the agreement documents were published by GCHQ and the NSA. The alliance's activities, including the ECHELON interception system, became a major subject of public debate, amplified by the 2013 disclosures of Edward Snowden concerning global surveillance programmes.
