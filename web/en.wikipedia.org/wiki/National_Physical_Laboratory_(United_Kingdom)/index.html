National Physical Laboratory (United Kingdom)

The National Physical Laboratory (NPL) is the national measurement standards laboratory of the United Kingdom. Based at Bushy Park in Teddington, London, it was founded in 1900 and is responsible for maintaining the UK's primary measurement standards and providing the measurement infrastructure underpinning science, engineering, and commerce. NPL is operated by NPL Management Limited, a company wholly owned by the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology.

History

NPL was established at Bushy House in 1900 under the direction of its first director, Richard Glazebrook. It grew into one of the world's leading centres of physics research. Notable achievements include the development of radar-related work, Barnes Wallis's bouncing bomb research, the first accurate atomic clock (built by Louis Essen and Jack Parry in 1955 using caesium, which redefined time measurement), and pioneering work on computing: Alan Turing worked at NPL after the Second World War and designed the ACE computer, and Donald Davies later developed the concept of packet switching there, influencing the Internet.

Work

NPL maintains national standards for units including the metre, kilogram (following the SI redefinition via the Kibble balance), second, ampere, kelvin, mole, and candela. It operates NPLTime, a national time service, and conducts research in areas such as quantum technologies, advanced materials, environmental measurement, and data science, working with industry and academia.
