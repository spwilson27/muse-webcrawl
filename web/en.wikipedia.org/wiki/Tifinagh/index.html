Tifinagh is an abjad script used to write the Berber (Amazigh) languages. It descends from the ancient Libyco-Berber script, survived among the Tuareg of the Sahara, and was revived and standardised as Neo-Tifinagh in the 20th century.

History

Libyco-Berber inscriptions, some over two thousand years old, are found across North Africa, often bilingual with Punic. The Tuareg preserved the consonantal script (tifinagh, plural of tafinaq) for messages, inscriptions and love notes carved on rock and wood.

Neo-Tifinagh

Morocco's Royal Institute of Amazigh Culture adopted a standard Neo-Tifinagh in 2003 for teaching Tamazight in schools, adding vowel letters and harmonising regional variants. Tifinagh now appears on Moroccan public signage, in Algerian and Libyan Amazigh activism, and was encoded in Unicode in 2005.
