38th parallel north

The 38th parallel north is a circle of latitude that is 38 degrees north of the Earth's equatorial plane. It crosses Europe, the Mediterranean Sea, Asia, the Pacific Ocean, North America, and the Atlantic Ocean. The 38th parallel north has been especially important in the recent history of Korea.

Korea

At the end of the Second World War in August 1945, the United States and the Soviet Union agreed to divide the Korean Peninsula, then under Japanese rule, at the 38th parallel as a temporary measure for accepting the Japanese surrender: Soviet forces took the surrender north of the line and U.S. forces to the south. The line, reportedly chosen by U.S. officers Dean Rusk and Charles Bonesteel using a National Geographic map, placed the capital, Seoul, in the American zone.

As Cold War tensions hardened, separate governments were established in 1948: the Republic of Korea in the south and the Democratic People's Republic of Korea in the north, each claiming the whole peninsula. On 25 June 1950 North Korea invaded the South across the parallel, beginning the Korean War. After three years of fighting that repeatedly swept across the parallel, the 1953 Korean Armistice Agreement fixed a Military Demarcation Line close to, but not exactly on, the parallel, surrounded by the Korean Demilitarized Zone (DMZ), which remains the effective border today.

Other regions

In the United States the parallel passes through states including California, Nevada, Utah, Colorado, Kansas, Missouri, Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Virginia and Maryland. In Japan it crosses northern Honshu, near the cities of Sendai's north and the Shimokita Peninsula region.
