Politics of Trinidad and Tobago

The politics of Trinidad and Tobago function within a framework of a unitary state regulated by the constitution of 1976, which replaced the independence constitution of 1962. Trinidad and Tobago is a parliamentary republic with a Westminster-style system: the President is head of state, while the Prime Minister is head of government and exercises effective executive power.

Legislative power is vested in the bicameral Parliament, consisting of the Senate (31 members, appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister and Leader of the Opposition) and the House of Representatives (41 elected members). The island of Tobago has its own devolved assembly, the Tobago House of Assembly, established in 1980.

Political parties

The party system has long been dominated by two parties: the People's National Movement (PNM), founded by Eric Williams in 1956, which governed for long stretches including the first three decades after independence, and the United National Congress (UNC), founded in 1989. Politics has historically divided substantially along ethnic lines, with the PNM drawing support mainly from Afro-Trinidadians and the UNC mainly from Indo-Trinidadians.

Judiciary

The judiciary is independent, headed by the Chief Justice. Trinidad and Tobago is a member of the Caribbean Court of Justice, though the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in London has remained the final court of appeal for most matters.
