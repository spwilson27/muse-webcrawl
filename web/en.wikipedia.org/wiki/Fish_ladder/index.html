Fish ladder

TypeFish passage structure
Used atDams, weirs, locks, culverts

A fish ladder, also known as a fishway or fish pass, is a structure built on or around artificial barriers such as dams and weirs to allow migratory fish to pass upstream to their spawning grounds. Most designs slow the water in a series of steps so that fish can swim or leap gradually past the obstruction without exhausting themselves.

Designs

Common types include the pool-and-weir ladder, a channel divided by weirs into resting pools; the vertical-slot fishway, in which water flows through slots between baffles; the Denil fishway, using closely spaced U-shaped baffles in a steep chute; and natural-style bypass channels that imitate a stream. Fish lifts and locks are used at very high dams, such as those on the Columbia River, where ladders at Bonneville Dam include viewing windows used for counting migrating salmon.

Effectiveness

Effectiveness varies widely by species: ladders designed for strong-swimming salmonids often pass weak swimmers, bottom-dwelling fish and downstream migrants poorly. Poorly functioning fishways can delay migration and deplete energy reserves, and modern fish passage design increasingly accounts for a wider range of species and for safe downstream passage.
