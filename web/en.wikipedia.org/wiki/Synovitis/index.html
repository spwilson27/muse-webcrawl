Synovitis

Synovitis is the medical term for inflammation of the synovial membrane, the tissue that lines the inner surface of joints, tendon sheaths, and bursae. It commonly occurs in association with rheumatoid arthritis and other inflammatory arthritides, but can also result from infection, trauma, overuse, or crystal deposition diseases such as gout.

Signs and symptoms

The affected joint is typically painful, swollen, warm, and stiff, with symptoms often worse in the morning. In rheumatoid arthritis, proliferating inflamed synovium (pannus) can erode cartilage and bone. Transient synovitis of the hip is a common cause of acute hip pain and limping in young children, usually following a viral illness.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis relies on clinical examination, imaging such as ultrasound or MRI, and analysis of synovial fluid; arthroscopy allows direct visualisation and biopsy. Treatment addresses the underlying cause and may include rest, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, corticosteroid injections, disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs, or, in refractory cases, surgical or chemical synovectomy.
