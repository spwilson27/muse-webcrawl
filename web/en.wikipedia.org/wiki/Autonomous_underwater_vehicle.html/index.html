Autonomous underwater vehicle

An autonomous underwater vehicle (AUV) is a robot that travels underwater without requiring continuous input from an operator. AUVs constitute part of a larger group of undersea systems known as unmanned underwater vehicles, a classification that includes non-autonomous remotely operated underwater vehicles (ROVs).

Applications

AUVs are used in oceanography to map the seafloor and measure water properties, in the offshore oil and gas industry for pipeline and infrastructure surveys, in defence for mine countermeasures and reconnaissance, and in research under ice shelves and at hydrothermal vents. Notable examples include the Autosub series (UK), REMUS vehicles (Woods Hole), HUGIN (Kongsberg) and the deep-diving Alvin-replacement class of survey AUVs.

Design

Typical AUVs are torpedo-shaped, propelled by a propeller and steered by fins, navigating by inertial systems aided by Doppler velocity logs and acoustic positioning. Power usually comes from lithium-ion or primary batteries, with newer vehicles using fuel cells or gliding (buoyancy-driven) propulsion for long endurance. Sensors include multibeam and side-scan sonar, cameras, CTD sensors and magnetometers.
