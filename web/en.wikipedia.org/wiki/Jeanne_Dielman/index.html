Jeanne Dielman

A still from Jeanne Dielman, showing the title character in her apartment kitchen

Jeanne Dielman, 23, quai du commerce, 1080 Bruxelles is a 1975 Belgian film directed by Chantal Akerman. Starring Delphine Seyrig as a widowed housewife whose rigid daily routines slowly unravel over three days, it is regarded as a landmark of feminist and slow cinema. In the 2022 Sight and Sound critics' poll, it was voted the greatest film of all time.

Synopsis

Jeanne Dielman, a meticulous widow living with her teenage son Sylvio in Brussels, follows an unvarying schedule of housework — cooking, bathing, polishing shoes, preparing potatoes — and receives male clients each afternoon for sex work to support the household. Across three days filmed in near-real time, small disruptions accumulate: a dropped spoon, a missed button, an overcooked meal, until the routine catastrophically breaks on the third day.

Style

Shot by Babette Mangolte with a mostly static camera at waist height, the 201-minute film refuses close-ups and psychological commentary, instead giving domestic labor the duration and attention conventionally reserved for dramatic action. Akerman, only 25 at its release, drew on her observation of women of her mother's generation in Brussels' Jewish community.

Reception and legacy

Initially divisive, the film was hailed by the New York Times as "the first masterpiece of the feminine in the history of the cinema." It steadily rose in critical esteem, placing first in the 2022 Sight and Sound poll — the first film directed by a woman to top it. Its influence on slow cinema and on filmmakers' treatment of everyday time is extensive.
