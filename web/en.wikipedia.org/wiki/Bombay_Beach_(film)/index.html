Bombay Beach (film)

Bombay Beach
Directed by: Alma Har'el
Release: 2011
Genre: Documentary
Setting: Bombay Beach, Salton Sea, California
Music: Zach Condon (Beirut), Bob Dylan

Bombay Beach is a 2011 documentary film directed by Israeli filmmaker Alma Har'el. It profiles residents of Bombay Beach, a small, impoverished community on the shore of the Salton Sea in California's Imperial Valley, once a thriving resort that declined as the lake became increasingly saline and polluted.

Synopsis

The film intertwines the stories of three residents: Benny Parrish, a young boy diagnosed with bipolar disorder whose parents struggle with his medication and education; CeeJay Thompson, a teenage football player who moved from Los Angeles after the killing of his cousin; and Red, an elderly former smuggler and lifelong resident of the desert community. Dream-like musical sequences, choreographed to songs by Beirut's Zach Condon and incorporating music by Bob Dylan, are woven through the documentary footage.

Reception

The film premiered at the 2011 Tribeca Film Festival, where it won the Best Feature Documentary award. It was praised by critics for its lyrical, stylized approach to documentary storytelling, blending vérité observation with staged, choreographed passages.
