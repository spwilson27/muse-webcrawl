Lin-Manuel Miranda (born 16 January 1980) is an American composer, lyricist, actor, singer, playwright and filmmaker. He created the Broadway musicals In the Heights (2005) and Hamilton (2015), the latter winning the Pulitzer Prize for Drama, eleven Tony Awards, and widespread acclaim for retelling Alexander Hamilton's life through hip-hop and a colour-conscious cast.

Early life

Miranda was born in Washington Heights, Manhattan, to Puerto Rican parents, and educated at Hunter College High School and Wesleyan University, where he began writing In the Heights.

Career

In the Heights reached Broadway in 2008 and won four Tonys. Hamilton premiered off-Broadway in 2015 before transferring to Broadway. Miranda wrote songs for Disney's Moana (2016), including "How Far I'll Go", and for Encanto (2021), including "We Don't Talk About Bruno", which topped charts. He starred as Jack in Mary Poppins Returns (2018), played Lee Scoresby in His Dark Materials, and directed the film Tick, Tick... Boom! (2021). He is a MacArthur "Genius" Fellow and an Emmy, Grammy and Tony winner — a Pulitzer winner close to EGOT status.
