Sea ice

FoundArctic and Southern oceans
FormsFrazil, pancake, first-year, multiyear ice
[Image: Broken sea ice floes in polar waters]
Broken sea ice floes in polar waters

Sea ice is frozen seawater that forms, grows and melts in the polar oceans. In the Arctic it caps the Arctic Ocean and neighbouring seas; around Antarctica it fringes a continent. Sea ice cover expands each winter and shrinks each summer in one of the planet's largest seasonal changes.

Properties and importance

New sea ice begins as frazil and pancake ice and thickens into first-year ice; ice surviving a summer melt season becomes thicker, fresher multiyear ice. Bright ice reflects sunlight (the ice-albedo feedback), insulates the ocean, and drives deep-water formation as brine is rejected during freezing. It is habitat and hunting platform for polar bears, seals and Arctic peoples. Satellite records since 1979 show a marked decline in Arctic summer ice extent; Antarctic sea ice, long roughly stable, reached record lows in the early 2020s.
