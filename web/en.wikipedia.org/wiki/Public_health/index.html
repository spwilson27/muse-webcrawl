Public health

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A vaccination clinic and a handwashing poster — everyday public health in action]

Public health is the science and art of preventing disease, prolonging life and promoting health through the organised efforts of society: sanitation, vaccination, disease surveillance, health education and policy, rather than treatment of individual patients.

Milestones include John Snow's 1854 cholera investigation in London, germ theory, clean water and sewerage, childhood immunisation, smoking control, and the global eradication of smallpox (certified 1980). National and international bodies — from local health departments to the World Health Organization (founded 1948) — carry the work.

Modern challenges

Pandemics (COVID-19), antimicrobial resistance, obesity, air pollution and health inequalities dominate current agendas.
