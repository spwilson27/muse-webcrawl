Johann Heinrich Alfred Escher vom Glas (20 February 1819 – 6 December 1882) was a Swiss politician, business magnate and railway pioneer, and one of the most influential figures in the economic development of 19th-century Switzerland.

Career

Escher led the liberal government of the canton of Zurich and sat in the National Council from 1848, serving repeatedly as its president. Convinced that Switzerland needed modern infrastructure, he promoted railway construction and founded the Swiss Northeastern Railway in 1853. To finance such projects he co-founded the Schweizerische Kreditanstalt (now part of UBS) in 1856 and the insurance company Schweizerische Rentenanstalt (Swiss Life) in 1857. He was also a driving force behind the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology (ETH Zurich), founded in 1855, and the driving figure behind the Gotthard railway, which linked northern and southern Europe through the Alps; its first tunnel opened in 1882, the year of his death.

Legacy

Escher's combination of political office and private business power — sometimes called the "Escher system" — drew criticism for conflicts of interest, but his institutions shaped modern Zurich as a financial centre. A monument to him stands outside Zurich Hauptbahnhof.
