Nitric acid

Nitric acid (HNO₃) is a strong, highly corrosive mineral acid and a powerful oxidising agent. The concentrated acid ('aqua fortis' of the alchemists) is about 68% HNO₃ and fumes; the red fuming grade contains dissolved nitrogen dioxide.

Most nitric acid becomes ammonium nitrate fertiliser; it also makes explosives (TNT, nitroglycerin, RDX involve nitration), dyes and plastics. It is manufactured by the Ostwald process — catalytic oxidation of ammonia over platinum–rhodium gauze.

[Image: A bottle of fuming nitric acid with brown nitrogen dioxide vapour]

Reactivity

Unlike most acids, nitric acid attacks copper and silver because it oxidises the metal, releasing brown NO₂ (with concentrated acid). Mixed with hydrochloric acid as aqua regia (1:3) it dissolves even gold and platinum — hence the name 'royal water'.

History

Nitric acid was described by alchemists from at least the 13th century, made by distilling saltpetre with vitriol; its composition was established by Cavendish, who synthesised it by sparking air (1785).
