Integrated services

In computer networking, integrated services (IntServ) is an architecture that specifies the elements needed to guarantee quality of service (QoS) on networks, reserving resources along the path of a flow so that applications such as voice and video receive assured bandwidth and bounded delay.

Mechanism

IntServ uses the Resource Reservation Protocol (RSVP) to signal reservations through routers. It defines service classes including guaranteed service, which provides firm bounds on delay, and controlled-load service, which approximates the behaviour of a lightly loaded network.

Limitations

Because every router must track the state of individual flows, IntServ scales poorly in large networks. The simpler differentiated services (DiffServ) architecture, which classifies traffic into aggregate classes, saw wider deployment.
