Sesshū Tōyō (雪舟等楊, 1420 – 26 August 1506) was one of the most prominent Japanese master painters of the Muromachi period. A Zen Buddhist monk, he is best known for his ink wash paintings (suiboku-ga) and is regarded by many as the greatest master of Japanese ink painting.

Life and career

Born in Bitchū Province (present-day Okayama Prefecture), Sesshū entered the Zen temple Shōkoku-ji in Kyoto, where he studied painting under Shūbun. In 1467 he travelled to Ming China as part of a trading mission, where he was honoured as a painter and studied Chinese landscape painting at its source. Returning to Japan in 1469, he settled in Yamaguchi under the patronage of the Ōuchi clan and built a studio he called Unkoku-an.

Style and works

Sesshū synthesised Chinese models with a distinctly Japanese boldness, developing a vigorous, architectonic brush manner. His celebrated works include the Long Landscape Scroll (Sansui chōkan), the Landscapes of the Four Seasons, the splashed-ink Haboku sansui (1495), and the view of Amanohashidate. Several of his paintings are designated National Treasures of Japan.
