The Battle of Mount Gilboa is the final battle of King Saul described in the Hebrew Bible (1 Samuel 31 and 1 Chronicles 10). Fought between the Israelites under Saul and the Philistines on the slopes of Mount Gilboa near the Jezreel Valley, it ended with the death of Saul and three of his sons, including Jonathan.

Biblical account

According to the biblical narrative, the Philistines mustered at Aphek and advanced to Shunem, while Saul gathered Israel's forces at the spring of Jezreel. On the eve of battle, a despairing Saul — having received no answer from God — consulted the Witch of Endor, who summoned the spirit of Samuel; the apparition foretold that Saul and his sons would die the next day.

In the battle the Israelites were routed and fled up Mount Gilboa, where the Philistine archers mortally wounded Saul. The king, fearing capture and abuse, fell on his own sword; his armour-bearer did likewise. Jonathan, Abinadab and Malki-Shua were also slain. The Philistines found the bodies the next day, decapitated Saul, and displayed his corpse on the wall of Beth-shan, from which the men of Jabesh-Gilead later rescued it for burial.

Legacy

The catastrophe at Gilboa cleared the way for David's rise to the throne, and David's lament over Saul and Jonathan ("the beauty of Israel is slain upon thy high places") is among the most famous elegies in the Bible. David is said to have cursed the mountain: "Ye mountains of Gilboa, let there be no dew, neither let there be rain, upon you." The account's historicity, like much of the early monarchy narrative, is debated among scholars, though Egyptian and biblical sources attest Philistine power in the region in the early Iron Age.
