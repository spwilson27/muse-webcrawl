Breakthrough album

A breakthrough album typically launches an artist to mainstream recognition.

A breakthrough album is a term in the music industry for a recording that suddenly and dramatically increases an artist's popularity, sales and public recognition, typically after earlier releases that received little attention or only a cult following. The breakthrough may come with an artist's debut or several albums into a career, when a hit single, critical acclaim, touring exposure or a change of sound brings the artist to a mass audience.

Characteristics

Breakthrough albums often produce multiple hit singles, achieve multi-platinum certification, and define the artist's sound in the public imagination. Well-known examples include Nevermind by Nirvana, which brought alternative rock into the mainstream in 1991, and Fleetwood Mac's Rumours, which transformed a moderately successful band into global superstars. A breakthrough can also create pressure to repeat the success, and some artists never match the sales of their breakthrough record.
