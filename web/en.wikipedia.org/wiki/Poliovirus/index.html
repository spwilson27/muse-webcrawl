Poliovirus

Poliovirus, the causative agent of polio (also known as poliomyelitis), is a serotype of the species Enterovirus C, in the family of Picornaviridae. There are three poliovirus serotypes: types 1, 2, and 3.

Poliovirus is composed of an RNA genome and a protein capsid. The genome is a single-stranded positive-sense RNA approximately 7,500 nucleotides long. The virus infects humans exclusively, entering via the fecal–oral route, and in a small proportion of cases spreads to the central nervous system, destroying motor neurons and causing flaccid paralysis.

Eradication

Vaccines developed by Jonas Salk (inactivated) and Albert Sabin (oral) drove a global eradication campaign from 1988. Wild poliovirus type 2 was declared eradicated in 2015 and type 3 in 2019; type 1 remains endemic only in Afghanistan and Pakistan, though vaccine-derived poliovirus outbreaks occur where immunization coverage is low.
