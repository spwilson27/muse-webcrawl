Wulingyuan

Wulingyuan (Chinese: 武陵源) is a scenic and historic interest area in the Wulingyuan District of Zhangjiajie, in Hunan Province, China. It is famous for its more than 3,000 quartzite sandstone pillars and peaks, many of which rise over 200 metres (660 ft) above the valley floor, together with ravines, gorges, streams, pools, waterfalls and two large natural bridges. In 1992 Wulingyuan was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

Geography

The area lies in the Wuling Mountains of northwestern Hunan. Its pillars are the product of long-term physical erosion of quartzite sandstone, largely along vertical joints, rather than the chemical dissolution typical of limestone karst. The region's subtropical monsoon climate keeps much of it forested and frequently shrouded in mist.

Component areas

Wulingyuan comprises four main parts: Zhangjiajie National Forest Park, Suoxiyu Nature Reserve, Tianzi Mountain Nature Reserve and Yangjiajie. Zhangjiajie National Forest Park, established in 1982, was the first national forest park in China. Notable features include the Bailong Elevator, a 326-metre glass lift built into a cliff face, and the tall natural rock bridge known as Xianrenqiao ("Bridge of the Immortals").

In popular culture

The floating "Hallelujah Mountains" in James Cameron's 2009 film Avatar were partly inspired by the Wulingyuan pillars, and one prominent pillar in the park was officially renamed "Avatar Hallelujah Mountain" in 2010.
