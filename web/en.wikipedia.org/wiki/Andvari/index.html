Andvari

In Norse mythology, Andvari (Old Norse: "careful one") is a dwarf who lives beneath a waterfall in the shape of a pike and guards a great treasure of gold. His hoard, and his curse upon it, set in motion the tragedy of Sigurd and the Völsungs. The story is told in the Eddic poem Reginsmál, in Völsunga saga and in Snorri Sturluson's Skáldskaparmál.

The curse of Andvari's gold

The gods Odin, Hœnir and Loki, travelling together, killed an otter by a waterfall; the otter was in fact Ótr, son of the magician Hreidmar, who demanded as compensation that the gods fill and cover the otter's skin with gold. Loki borrowed the net of the sea-goddess Rán and caught Andvari in his pike form. To buy his life, the dwarf surrendered all his gold, including the ring Andvaranaut, which had the power to renew the treasure. As Loki took even this last piece, Andvari cursed the gold: it would bring death to two brothers and grief to many heroes, and no one would enjoy possessing it.

Consequences

The cursed hoard was paid to Hreidmar, whose sons Fafnir and Regin demanded a share; Fafnir killed his father for the gold and became a dragon to guard it. Regin later fostered Sigurd and forged the sword Gram, with which Sigurd slew Fafnir and seized the treasure — passing the curse on to the Völsung line, whose destruction through the Burgundian kings forms the climax of the legend.

Influence

Andvari's cursed ring is a principal source for Alberich's ring in Wagner's Der Ring des Nibelungen and, through Wagner and Norse tradition, for the One Ring of J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium. Tolkien also drew the dwarf-names of The Hobbit partly from the same Eddic lists that name Andvari.
