Mont Ventoux is a mountain in the Provence region of southern France, about 20 km northeast of Carpentras, Vaucluse. At 1,909 m it is the highest mountain in the region and dominates the surrounding Rhône plain. Its bare limestone summit, blasted by the mistral wind — which has been recorded at speeds approaching 320 km/h — gives it the appearance of being snow-capped year-round and earns it the name "the Bald Mountain."

Petrarch

The mountain is famous in cultural history for the ascent made on 26 April 1336 by the poet Petrarch, accompanied by his brother. Petrarch's letter describing the climb, in which he reached the summit and then read a passage from Augustine's Confessions, is often cited as a symbolic beginning of the Renaissance attitude toward nature and the inner life.

Cycling

Mont Ventoux is one of the most feared climbs in the Tour de France, first used in 1951. The ascent from Bédoin covers 21.5 km at an average gradient of about 7.5 percent. British rider Tom Simpson died on its slopes during the 1967 Tour, a death linked to heat, exertion and amphetamines; a memorial stands near the summit. The mountain is also a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, valued for its exceptional range of habitats and endemic plants between Mediterranean lowlands and alpine summit.
