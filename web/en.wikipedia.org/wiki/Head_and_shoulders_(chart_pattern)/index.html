Head and shoulders (chart pattern)

On a chart of a security's price, a head and shoulders pattern forms when a market trend is in the process of reversing from a bullish trend to a bearish trend. It is considered one of the most reliable trend reversal patterns in technical analysis.

Formation

The pattern consists of three successive peaks:

  • Left shoulder — the price rises to a peak and then declines.
  • Head — the price rises again, forming a higher peak, then declines again to roughly the level of the first decline.
  • Right shoulder — the price rises a third time, but only to about the level of the first peak, before declining again.

A line drawn along the troughs between the peaks is called the neckline. The pattern is considered complete when the price falls below the neckline after the right shoulder, often on increasing volume; technicians then project a price target by subtracting the height of the head (measured from the neckline) from the breakout point. The inverse head and shoulders, formed at market bottoms, signals a reversal from bearish to bullish.

Interpretation

Proponents interpret the pattern as a shift in the balance between buyers and sellers: the failure of the right shoulder to exceed the head indicates weakening demand. Academic studies of the pattern's profitability are mixed; the head and shoulders is commonly cited as among the more rigorously studied of chart patterns, with some research (e.g. by Federal Reserve researchers simulating pattern-recognition algorithms) finding modest predictive value in certain markets.
