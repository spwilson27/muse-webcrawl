Politics of Qatar

Polity type: Unitary authoritarian de facto absolute monarchy

Head of state: Emir of Qatar

Head of government: Prime Minister of Qatar

The politics of Qatar take place within the framework of an absolute monarchy ruled by the House of Thani. The Emir is head of state and holds extensive executive and legislative authority. Qatar has been described variously as a constitutional monarchy and as an authoritarian state; political parties are not permitted.

Constitution

A permanent constitution was approved by referendum in 2003 and came into force in 2005. It provides for a 45-member Advisory Council (Shura Council), of whom 30 are to be elected and 15 appointed by the Emir. The first general election for the Shura Council was held in October 2021, with two-thirds of the seats elected; suffrage was limited to descendants of Qataris who held citizenship in 1930, a restriction that drew some protest.

Executive branch

The Emir appoints the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers. Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani became Emir in 2013, when his father, Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani, abdicated in his favour — an unusual peaceful transfer of power in the region.

Legal system

Qatar's legal system combines civil law and Islamic law (Sharia), which is a main source of legislation. The judiciary is formally independent, though the Emir appoints judges.

Human rights and labour

Qatar's treatment of its large migrant workforce under the kafala sponsorship system has drawn sustained international criticism, particularly in the run-up to the 2022 FIFA World Cup. Reforms announced from 2017 onward, in cooperation with the International Labour Organization, partially dismantled kafala elements, allowing most workers to change jobs and leave the country without employer permission, and introduced a non-discriminatory minimum wage.
