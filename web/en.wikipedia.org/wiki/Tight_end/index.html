The tight end (TE) is a position in American football and Canadian football on the offense. The tight end is often a hybrid position with the characteristics and roles of both an offensive lineman and a wide receiver. Like offensive linemen, they are usually lined up on the offensive line and are large enough to be effective blockers; on the other hand, they are also eligible receivers able to catch passes.

Role

On running plays, tight ends block defensive players at the edge of the line of scrimmage, often assisting the offensive tackle. On passing plays, they may stay in to protect the quarterback or release into pass patterns, typically short and intermediate routes over the middle of the field. Because of their size and strength, tight ends are favoured targets in the red zone and on third downs.

History

The position evolved from the end position of early football. Players such as Mike Ditka in the 1960s demonstrated the tight end's receiving potential, and later generations—including Tony Gonzalez, Rob Gronkowski, Antonio Gates, and Travis Kelce—made pass-catching tight ends central figures in modern offenses. Some offenses use two or more tight ends simultaneously ("12 personnel"), while others split a tight end away from the line as a flex or "move" tight end.
