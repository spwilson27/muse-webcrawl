Gary Snyder

Gary Snyder (born May 8, 1930) is an American poet, essayist, lecturer and environmental activist. A member of the Beat Generation and the San Francisco Renaissance, he won the Pulitzer Prize for Poetry for Turtle Island (1974).

Gary Snyder
Born: May 8, 1930, San Francisco, California, U.S.
Occupation: Poet, essayist, activist
Education: Reed College
Movement: Beat Generation, Deep ecology

Life

Snyder grew up in Washington and Oregon, studied anthropology and literature at Reed College, and worked as a seaman, logger, trail crew member and forest lookout. In the 1950s he studied Zen in Kyoto, Japan, living there intermittently for more than a decade. He was fictionalised as Japhy Ryder in Jack Kerouac's The Dharma Bums. He participated in the famous 1955 Six Gallery reading alongside Allen Ginsberg.

Work

Early collections include Riprap (1959), Myths & Texts (1960) and The Back Country (1968). Turtle Island — a Native American name for North America — fused poetry and prose essays on wilderness and reinhabitation. Later works include Axe Handles (1983), the long poem Mountains and Rivers Without End (1996, worked on for 40 years), and Danger on Peaks (2004).

Themes

Snyder's writing blends Zen Buddhism, Native American lore, ecology and bioregionalism. He taught at the University of California, Davis, and helped found a community at Kitkitdizze in the Sierra foothills. He has been called the poet laureate of deep ecology.
