Mit brennender Sorge (German: "With burning concern") is an encyclical of Pope Pius XI, issued during Holy Week in March 1937 and read from the pulpits of German Catholic churches on Palm Sunday. Written in German rather than the customary Latin, it condemned the Nazi regime's violations of the 1933 Reichskonkordat, its persecution of the Church, and the neo-pagan and racial ideology of Nazism.

Background

Despite the concordat signed between the Vatican and Nazi Germany in 1933, the regime harassed Catholic organisations, closed church schools, and arrested clergy. The encyclical was drafted with the help of Cardinal Eugenio Pacelli (the future Pius XII) and Munich's Cardinal Michael von Faulhaber, smuggled into Germany, and printed in secret so that it could be read simultaneously in churches across the country before the Gestapo could confiscate it.

Contents

The encyclical denounced the exaltation of race, nation, and state to the status of idols, affirmed that whoever sets a race or the state above their ordained value and divinises them "perverts and falsifies the order of things created by God", and defended the Old Testament against attempts to purge Christianity of its Jewish roots. It catalogued the regime's breaches of the concordat and called German Catholics to steadfastness.

Reaction

The Nazi regime responded angrily: copies were confiscated, the presses that printed it were seized, and persecution of the Church intensified, including staged morality trials of clergy. The encyclical is regarded as the first official public document to criticise Nazism and one of the strongest condemnations issued by the Vatican during the period, though later commentators have debated whether it went far enough, as it did not explicitly name antisemitism or Hitler.
