Ecstasy of Saint Teresa

The Ecstasy of Saint Teresa (also known as Saint Teresa in Ecstasy) is a marble sculpture by the Italian Baroque artist Gian Lorenzo Bernini, completed between 1647 and 1652. It forms the centrepiece of the Cornaro Chapel in the church of Santa Maria della Vittoria in Rome and is regarded as one of the masterpieces of Baroque art.

Subject

The sculpture depicts a vision described by the Spanish mystic Saint Teresa of Ávila in her autobiography: an angel appeared to her and pierced her heart repeatedly with a golden spear tipped with fire, leaving her in a state of mingled pain and divine rapture. Bernini shows Teresa swooning on a cloud, her drapery in turbulent folds, while the smiling angel holds the arrow above her breast. Gilded bronze rays descend behind the figures, lit by a hidden window.

The Cornaro Chapel

The chapel was commissioned by Cardinal Federico Cornaro. On the side walls Bernini placed sculpted members of the Cornaro family in theatre-like boxes, apparently witnessing and discussing the miracle, an effect critics such as those of the period compared to a stage set. Coloured marbles, stucco and painting complete the ensemble, a characteristic Baroque union of the arts (bel composto).

Reception

The work has been praised for its virtuosity and criticised, notably in the 18th and 19th centuries, for the intensely sensuous character of Teresa's expression — a reading Bernini's defenders answer by pointing to the saint's own account. The sculpture remains one of Rome's most visited Baroque monuments.
