The Vickers machine gun or Vickers gun is a water-cooled .303 British (7.7 mm) machine gun produced by Vickers Limited, originally for the British Army. The gun was operated by a three-man crew but typically required six to eight men to move and maintain it in the field. It served from before the First World War until the 1960s, and air-cooled versions were fitted to many early Allied fighter aircraft.

History

The Vickers was based on the successful Maxim gun of the late 19th century. After purchasing the Maxim company in 1896, Vickers took over the design and improved it, inverting the action and reducing its weight by using lighter materials and a muzzle booster. The British Army formally adopted the Vickers as its standard machine gun in November 1912.

Use in the First World War

The gun was famed for its reliability: it was water-cooled, with a jacket holding around a gallon of water to prevent overheating, allowing very long periods of sustained fire. In one famous action in August 1916, during the Battle of the Somme, ten Vickers guns of the 100th Company of the Machine Gun Corps fired nearly one million rounds in twelve hours without a breakdown. The Vickers was usually fired from a tripod and fed from 250-round canvas belts.

Later service

The Vickers remained in British and Commonwealth service through the Second World War and the Korean War, finally being declared obsolete in 1962 and withdrawn from British service by 1968. Variants were produced in several calibres, and the gun was also used as vehicle armament and, in air-cooled form, as aircraft armament during the First World War.
