The Lincoln Memorial is a U.S. national memorial honoring Abraham Lincoln, the 16th president of the United States. It is located on the western end of the National Mall in Washington, D.C., across from the Washington Monument, and is in the form of a large neoclassical temple. The memorial's architect was Henry Bacon. The designer of the memorial interior's large central statue, Abraham Lincoln (1920), was Daniel Chester French; the Lincoln statue was carved by the Piccirilli brothers.

The building is in the form of a Greek Doric temple and contains a large seated sculpture of Abraham Lincoln and inscriptions of two well-known speeches by Lincoln, "The Gettysburg Address" and his second inaugural address. The memorial has been the site of many famous speeches, including Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream" speech, delivered on August 28, 1963, during the rally at the end of the March on Washington for Jobs and Freedom.

History

Although earlier memorial associations had proposed monuments, Congress chartered the Lincoln Memorial Commission in 1911. The site in the reclaimed Potomac flats was controversial — Speaker Joseph Cannon remarked that he would never let a memorial to Lincoln sit "in that swamp" — but John Russell Pope's and Bacon's classical design prevailed. Construction ran from 1914 to 1922, and President Warren G. Harding dedicated the memorial on 30 May 1922, with Lincoln's son Robert Todd Lincoln present. At the dedication, seating was segregated, an irony noted by the press given the memorial's meaning.

Design and statue

The temple is ringed by 36 fluted Doric columns, representing the 36 states in the Union at Lincoln's death; their names, with those of the later states, are inscribed around the building. Inside, French's statue of the seated, contemplative president is 19 feet (5.8 m) tall — it would be 28 feet if standing — carved from Georgia white marble. Above it is inscribed: "In this temple as in the hearts of the people for whom he saved the Union the memory of Abraham Lincoln is enshrined forever."

Inscriptions

The south wall carries the Gettysburg Address; the north wall, the Second Inaugural Address. Murals by Jules Guerin above the texts depict principles evident in Lincoln's life: Freedom, Liberty, Immortality, Justice and Law on one side; Unity, Fraternity and Charity on the other.

Symbolic setting

The memorial anchors the Mall axis facing the Washington Monument and the Capitol. In 1939, after the Daughters of the American Revolution barred her from Constitution Hall, Marian Anderson sang from its steps before 75,000 people. It remains America's pre-eminent stage for appeals to national conscience, depicted on the penny and the five-dollar bill.
