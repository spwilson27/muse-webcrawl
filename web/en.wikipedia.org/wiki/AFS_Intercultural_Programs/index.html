AFS Intercultural Programs is an international youth exchange organization consisting of a network of independent, not-for-profit AFS partner organizations in more than 50 countries. It was founded on the heritage of the American Field Service, a volunteer ambulance corps in both world wars, whose drivers — led by A. Piatt Andrew and later Stephen Galatti — transformed the organization after 1946 into a scholarship program for intercultural student exchanges intended to promote peace and understanding.

History

The American Ambulance Field Service was founded in 1914 at the American Hospital in Paris, and its volunteer drivers served with French and later American forces in both world wars; more than 2,500 volunteers served in the Second World War. After the war, Galatti established AFS scholarships, and in 1947 the first group of students from ten countries traveled to the United States. In 1970–71 AFS became multinational, sending students between many countries rather than only to and from the United States.

Programs

AFS offers school-based exchanges for secondary students (a year, semester, or short-term stay with a host family), 18+ programs including gap-year, language study, volunteer service, and educator exchanges. Each year roughly 13,000 participants go abroad, supported by tens of thousands of volunteers. AFS holds consultative status with UNESCO and ECOSOC and received a special citation from the United Nations in 1989.

Notable alumni

Alumni include former French president's family members, astronauts, and public figures in many countries; the organization estimates more than 500,000 people have participated since 1947.
