Trucial States

The sheikhdoms of the Trucial Coast on the Persian Gulf

The Trucial States (also known as the Trucial Coast, Trucial Oman or the Trucial Sheikhdoms) were a group of tribal sheikhdoms in the south-eastern Arabian Peninsula that were British protectorates from 1820 until 1971. Their name derives from the maritime truces concluded with Britain in the 19th century.

History

After British expeditions against piracy on what the British called the "Pirate Coast", the local rulers signed the General Maritime Treaty of 1820, followed by a Perpetual Maritime Truce in 1853 and Exclusive Agreements in 1892, under which the sheikhdoms accepted British protection and conducted their foreign relations through Britain while retaining internal autonomy.

The states were Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Sharjah, Ajman, Umm Al Quwain, Fujairah and Ras Al Khaimah. Pearling was the traditional mainstay of their economies until its collapse in the 1930s; oil was discovered in commercial quantities in Abu Dhabi in the 1950s and in Dubai in the 1960s.

Formation of the UAE

When Britain announced in 1968 that it would withdraw from the Gulf, the rulers negotiated a federation. On 2 December 1971 six of the states formed the United Arab Emirates; Ras Al Khaimah joined in February 1972.
