Harvey Weinstein (born March 19, 1952) is an American former film producer. He co-founded the entertainment company Miramax with his brother Bob in 1979, and later The Weinstein Company in 2005. As a producer, Weinstein was behind a string of critically acclaimed independent films, including Sex, Lies, and Videotape (1989), The Crying Game (1992), Pulp Fiction (1994), The English Patient (1996), Shakespeare in Love (1998), and The King's Speech (2010). He won an Academy Award for producing Shakespeare in Love and was nominated for Gangs of New York.

Sexual abuse allegations and trials

In October 2017, investigative reports in The New York Times and The New Yorker published allegations of sexual harassment and assault against Weinstein by dozens of women spanning decades. He was expelled from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences and dismissed from his company. The revelations helped ignite the worldwide #MeToo movement against sexual abuse and harassment.

Weinstein was arrested in New York in May 2018 and in February 2020 was convicted of rape in the third degree and a criminal sexual act, receiving a 23-year prison sentence. That conviction was overturned by New York's highest court in 2024, which ordered a new trial. Separately, in December 2022 a Los Angeles jury convicted him of rape and other counts, for which he was sentenced to 16 years in prison.
