Coconut water is the clear liquid inside young, green coconuts. It is a popular drink in tropical regions and is sold worldwide as a natural sports drink. Unlike coconut cream and coconut milk, which are extracted from the flesh, coconut water is the fruit's liquid endosperm.

Composition

Coconut water is about 95% water and contains carbohydrates, amino acids, and electrolytes including potassium, sodium and magnesium. It has been marketed as naturally replenishing, though its sodium content is lower than that of formulated sports drinks.

Harvesting and production

Coconuts for water are harvested while still green, typically at five to seven months of age, when the liquid volume is greatest. The water is drunk fresh from the fruit in producing countries and is also pasteurised and packaged for export.

Medical use

In remote settings coconut water has occasionally been used as an emergency intravenous rehydration fluid, though this is not a recommended practice because its electrolyte composition differs from plasma.
