The Book of Jubilees, sometimes called the Lesser Genesis, is an ancient Jewish religious work of 50 chapters, dating to about 160–150 BC. It retells the stories of Genesis and the first part of Exodus as a revelation given by an angel to Moses on Mount Sinai, organising history into "jubilees" — periods of 49 years (seven "weeks" of years) — and supplying exact dates for biblical events.

Content and character

Jubilees is a leading example of "rewritten Bible": it retells the patriarchal narratives while smoothing difficulties, expanding legal material, and insisting that the laws of Moses — Sabbath, festivals, circumcision, dietary rules — were observed by the patriarchs and are engraved on heavenly tablets. It advocates a 364-day solar calendar, in opposition to the lunar calendar it associates with error, and presents a strongly dualistic worldview in which the demon prince Mastema leads the forces of evil. The book also explains the origin of evils through the story of the Watchers and their giant offspring.

Transmission

Written in Hebrew, Jubilees was translated into Greek and from Greek into Ge'ez (classical Ethiopic); the complete text survives only in Ethiopic manuscripts. Fragments of some fourteen Hebrew manuscripts found among the Dead Sea Scrolls at Qumran showed the book's importance in Second Temple Judaism. It is canonical in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church and in the Beta Israel tradition; in other Jewish and Christian traditions it is classed as pseudepigrapha. A Latin fragment covering about a third of the work also survives.
