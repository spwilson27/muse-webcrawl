Entamoeba

DomainEukaryota
PhylumAmoebozoa
FamilyEntamoebidae
GenusEntamoeba

Entamoeba is a genus of amoeboid protozoa in the family Entamoebidae, found as internal parasites or commensals of animals. Species of Entamoeba have a simple life cycle with a feeding, moving trophozoite stage and a dormant, resistant cyst stage that is passed in faeces and transmits the infection to new hosts.

Species

The best-known species is Entamoeba histolytica, the causative agent of amoebiasis (amoebic dysentery and amoebic liver abscess) in humans, infecting tens of millions of people worldwide. Other human-associated species are generally commensals of the gut and include E. coli, E. dispar (morphologically identical to E. histolytica but non-pathogenic), E. gingivalis (in the mouth), E. hartmanni and E. moshkovskii. Entamoeba invadens causes serious disease in reptiles and is a laboratory model organism.

Biology

Entamoeba cells characteristically have a single nucleus with a small central karyosome, and they move by means of a single blunt pseudopodium. They lack typical mitochondria, instead possessing reduced forms called mitosomes. Cysts of different species vary in the number of nuclei — four in E. histolytica, eight in E. coli — a feature used in microscopic diagnosis, though molecular methods are now preferred for distinguishing pathogenic from harmless species.
