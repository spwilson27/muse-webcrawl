Syndicate

A syndicate is a self-organizing group of individuals, companies, corporations, or entities formed to transact a specific business, pursue a joint interest, or promote a common cause. Members pool resources to undertake projects that would be too large or risky for any one of them alone, sharing both risk and profit.

Finance

In finance, an underwriting syndicate is a group of investment banks that jointly underwrite and distribute a new issue of securities, spreading the risk of a large offering. Loan syndicates similarly allow multiple banks to fund a large loan to a single borrower. In insurance, Lloyd's of London is organized around syndicates of investors (Names) that underwrite policies.

Other uses

The term also describes news and comic syndicates that distribute content to many outlets; gambling syndicates that pool bets, such as in lotteries; and, pejoratively, organized-crime groups — the American Mafia was once overseen by a body informally called the National Crime Syndicate. In labor contexts, the French word syndicat simply means a trade union, and syndicalism is a political movement built on them.
