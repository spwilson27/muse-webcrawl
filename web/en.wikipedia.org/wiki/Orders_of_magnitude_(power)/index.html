Orders of magnitude (power)

This page lists examples of power — the rate of energy transfer, measured in watts — by order of magnitude, from the faintest detectable signals to the luminosity of galaxies.

Selected examples

PowerExamples
10−15 WApproximate power of a GPS satellite signal received on Earth
10−3 WA laser pointer (a few milliwatts)
100 WA phone charger at idle; power radiated by a human body is about 100 W
103 WAn electric kettle (1.5–3 kW); an average horse's sustained output (746 W = 1 horsepower)
105 WA large car engine (about 100–300 kW)
107 WA diesel locomotive; a large wind turbine (several megawatts)
109 WA large power station (about 1 GW); average global lightning activity
1012 WTotal average electrical generating capacity of the world (several terawatts)
1015 WSunlight intercepted by Earth (about 174 PW)
1026 WTotal luminosity of the Sun (3.828 × 1026 W)
1036+ WBright quasars and gamma-ray bursts (the latter transiently far higher)

For comparison, human civilisation's total primary power use is on the order of 20 terawatts (2 × 1013 W).
