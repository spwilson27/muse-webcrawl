Chromosome 22 is one of the 23 pairs of chromosomes in human cells. Humans normally have two copies of this chromosome. Chromosome 22 spans about 51 million base pairs, representing between 1.5 and 2 percent of the total DNA in cells. It is the second-smallest human chromosome, larger only than chromosome 21, despite its numbering — the chromosomes were originally numbered by apparent size, and chromosome 22 was later found to be slightly larger than chromosome 21.

Sequencing

Chromosome 22 was the first human chromosome to have its sequence essentially completed, an achievement announced in December 1999 by an international consortium led by the Sanger Centre in the United Kingdom. The published sequence covered the long (q) arm, comprising about 33.5 million base pairs, and identified hundreds of genes, providing an early landmark for the Human Genome Project.

Genes

Among the genes located on chromosome 22 are the immunoglobulin lambda locus, involved in antibody production; NF2, encoding the tumour suppressor merlin; CHEK2, involved in DNA damage response; and a cluster of genes in region 22q11.2 implicated in several developmental disorders.

Disorders and abnormalities

Several conditions are associated with chromosome 22. Deletion of a small segment at 22q11.2 causes 22q11.2 deletion syndrome (encompassing DiGeorge syndrome and velocardiofacial syndrome), one of the most common human microdeletion syndromes. Duplications of the same region cause 22q11.2 duplication syndrome, and the reciprocal microdeletion is associated with cat-eye syndrome in its tetrasomy form. Translocations involving chromosome 22 include the Philadelphia chromosome — a reciprocal translocation between chromosomes 9 and 22 that creates the BCR-ABL1 fusion gene found in chronic myeloid leukaemia — although the fused gene itself resides on the derivative chromosome 22. Emanuel syndrome results from a supernumerary derivative chromosome 22. Mutations in NF2 cause neurofibromatosis type II, and aberrations of chromosome 22 are common in Ewing sarcoma and meningioma.
