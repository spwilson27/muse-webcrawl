The Constitutional Council (French: Conseil constitutionnel) is the highest constitutional authority in France. It was established by the Constitution of the Fifth Republic on 4 October 1958 to ensure that constitutional principles and rules are upheld. It is housed in the Palais Royal in Paris. Its main activity is to rule on whether proposed statutes conform with the Constitution, after they have been voted by Parliament and before they are signed into law by the president of the Republic (a priori review), or, since 2008, to rule on the constitutionality of laws already promulgated when referred by a court in the course of litigation (a posteriori review, the question prioritaire de constitutionnalité or QPC).

History

The Fifth Republic created the Council largely to protect the executive against parliamentary encroachment, a reversal of the usual logic of judicial review. The Council's powers grew significantly with its landmark decision of 16 July 1971 (Liberté d'association), in which it held that the preamble of the 1958 Constitution, and by reference the 1789 Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen and the preamble of the 1946 Constitution, had constitutional value, thereby greatly expanding the body of rights against which statutes could be measured. A 1974 constitutional amendment allowed 60 deputies or 60 senators to refer a bill to the Council, which turned referral into a routine weapon of the parliamentary opposition.

Powers and tasks

The Council rules on the conformity of organic laws and ordinary statutes with the Constitution before promulgation when referred by the president of the Republic, the prime minister, the presidents of the National Assembly or Senate, or 60 members of either house. It also supervises the regularity of presidential and parliamentary elections and referendums, proclaiming their results, and verifies that the president is not incapacitated. It is not a supreme court above the ordinary and administrative courts; the Court of Cassation and the Conseil d'État remain the highest courts of their respective orders.

Membership

The Council consists of nine members appointed for a single non-renewable nine-year term, one third being replaced every three years. Three members are appointed by the president of the Republic, three by the president of the National Assembly, and three by the president of the Senate. Former presidents of the Republic are members ex officio for life, although some have chosen not to sit. The president of the Council is appointed by the president of the Republic from among the members.
