Hypothesis

A hypothesis (plural: hypotheses) is a proposed explanation for a phenomenon. For a hypothesis to be scientific, the scientific method requires that it can be tested. Scientists generally base hypotheses on previous observations that cannot satisfactorily be explained by the available scientific theories. A hypothesis is not the same as a theory: a scientific theory is a well-substantiated explanation that has survived repeated testing, whereas a hypothesis is a tentative, working proposition awaiting evidence.

Characteristics

A useful hypothesis should be testable and, in the influential account of Karl Popper, falsifiable — it must make predictions that could in principle prove it wrong. It should also be consistent with existing knowledge, be stated simply and clearly, and identify the variables it relates. In statistical hypothesis testing, a hypothesis is a statement about a population parameter, assessed against a null hypothesis of no effect; the observed data yield a probability (a p-value) used to decide whether to reject the null.

Formation and testing

Hypotheses are formed by reasoning — deduction, induction, and abduction (inference to the best explanation) — often guided by analogy and prior experiment. A "working hypothesis" is provisionally accepted as a basis for further research. When a hypothesis is repeatedly confirmed it may be incorporated into a broader theoretical framework; when it is refuted, it is revised or discarded. Concepts such as the Riemann hypothesis in mathematics use the term for an unproven conjecture of great importance.
