Raised-cosine filter

The raised-cosine filter is a filter frequently used for pulse shaping in digital modulation, able to minimise intersymbol interference (ISI). Its name stems from the fact that the non-zero portion of its frequency spectrum is a cosine function raised above the horizontal axis.

Characteristics

The filter's shape is governed by the roll-off factor β (0 ≤ β ≤ 1), which determines the excess bandwidth beyond the Nyquist minimum. At β = 0 the response degenerates to a brick-wall Nyquist filter; at β = 1 it has a pure raised-cosine shape with twice the Nyquist bandwidth. The impulse response passes through zero at all other symbol instants, satisfying the Nyquist ISI criterion. In practice the filter is split into two root-raised-cosine filters, one at transmitter and one at receiver, which also optimises the signal-to-noise ratio in additive white Gaussian noise.
