Victorian Football Association

The Victorian Football Association (VFA), founded in 1877, is an Australian rules football association and the governing body of the Victorian Football League (VFL), the state-level league in Victoria and a feeder league to the Australian Football League (AFL).

History

The VFA was formed on 7 May 1877 and was the top-level football competition in Victoria until eight of its clubs broke away in 1897 to form the Victorian Football League (which became the AFL). The VFA continued as the second-tier senior competition, famous for innovations such as Sunday football and a strong tribal following for suburban clubs like Port Melbourne, Williamstown, Coburg and Preston.

Modern era

The VFA's senior competition took the VFL name in 1996 after the former VFL became the AFL. The modern VFL includes AFL reserve teams, standalone clubs and interstate teams from New South Wales, Queensland and the ACT. Famous VFA products include the 16-a-side game and the two-division era with promotion and relegation (1961–1988).
