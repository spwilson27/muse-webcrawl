Hong Kong cuisine


Dim sum, a hallmark of Hong Kong cuisine

Hong Kong cuisine is mainly influenced by Cantonese cuisine, European cuisines (especially British), and other Asian cuisines, particularly Japanese, Korean, and Southeast Asian, reflecting Hong Kong's history as a British colony and an international port city. The food culture ranges from street stalls and dai pai dong to cha chaan teng tea restaurants and fine-dining establishments recognised by the Michelin Guide.

Styles and venues

The cha chaan teng (tea restaurant) is the archetypal Hong Kong eatery, serving fusion dishes such as Hong Kong-style milk tea, pineapple buns with butter, macaroni in soup with ham, French toast, and baked pork chop rice. Dai pai dong, open-air food stalls, traditionally served wok hei stir-fries, congee, and noodle dishes. Dim sum—small dishes such as har gow (shrimp dumplings), siu mai, and char siu bao—is served with tea in restaurants, especially at weekend family gatherings known as yum cha.

Signature dishes

Notable dishes include wonton noodles, roasted meats (char siu, siu yuk, and roast goose), claypot rice, curry fish balls, egg tarts, egg waffles, beef brisket curry, and Typhoon Shelter crab. Hong Kong-style milk tea, brewed strong and strained through cloth (giving it the nickname "pantyhose tea"), is a local icon, as are yuenyeung (a mix of coffee and tea) and lemon tea.

Influences

Hong Kong cuisine is often considered a prime example of East-meets-West fusion. Post-war soy sauce Western food (si jap sai chaan) adapted Western dishes with Chinese seasonings. As a major trading port, Hong Kong also absorbed ingredients and techniques from around the world, while its Cantonese base emphasises freshness, seafood, and subtle seasoning.
