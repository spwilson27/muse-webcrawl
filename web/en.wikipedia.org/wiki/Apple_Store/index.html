The Apple Store is a chain of retail stores owned and operated by Apple Inc., selling Apple computers, consumer electronics, software and accessories, including the iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch and Apple TV. The stores also provide technical support and repairs through the Genius Bar, and free creative sessions under the "Today at Apple" programme.

The first Apple Stores opened on May 19, 2001, at Tysons Corner Center in Virginia and at the Glendale Galleria in California. Championed by CEO Steve Jobs and retail chief Ron Johnson, the stores were designed with open floor plans, light wood tables and hands-on product displays — a formula widely imitated across the technology industry.

History

Frustrated by the poor presentation of Apple products in third-party retailers, Jobs authorised the creation of company-owned stores in 2000. Ron Johnson, formerly of Target, was hired to develop the concept. The first two stores opened in May 2001, during a weak retail economy, prompting some analysts to predict failure. Instead the stores became among the most profitable retail spaces per square foot in the world, and Apple expanded internationally, opening a London store on Regent Street in 2004 and hundreds more thereafter.

In 2016 Apple began dropping the word "Store" from some individual store names (for example, "Apple Union Square" in San Francisco), while the chain continues to be widely known as the Apple Store.

Design

Apple Stores are noted for their minimalist architecture: large glass façades, glass staircases in flagship locations, stainless steel panelling and stone floors. Flagship stores such as Apple Fifth Avenue in New York — recognisable by its glass cube entrance — have become landmarks in their own right.

Genius Bar

The Genius Bar is a technical support station located inside most Apple Stores, where customers can receive product advice, repairs and troubleshooting from trained staff ("Geniuses"). Appointments are made online or via the Apple Store app.
