Ethiopian–Adal war
Date: 1529–1543
Location: Horn of Africa
Belligerents: Ethiopian Empire (with Portuguese aid) vs Adal Sultanate (with Ottoman support)
Result: Ethiopian–Portuguese victory

The Ethiopian–Adal war was a military conflict between the Christian Ethiopian Empire and the Muslim Adal Sultanate from 1529 to 1543. Led by Imam Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi, Adal forces overran much of Ethiopia, destroying churches and forcing Emperor Lebna Dengel to flee.

Course

After decisive victories such as Shimbra Kure (1529), Ahmad's armies controlled most of the empire. Emperor Gelawdewos, aided by a Portuguese expedition under Cristóvão da Gama — son of Vasco da Gama — eventually turned the tide. Ahmad was killed at the Battle of Wayna Daga in 1543, after which his forces collapsed. Both sides were exhausted, reshaping the political balance of the Horn of Africa and opening the region to Oromo migrations.
