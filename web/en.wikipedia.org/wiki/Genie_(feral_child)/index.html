Genie (feral child)

Genie
Born18 April 1957, Arcadia, California, U.S.
Discovered4 November 1970, age 13
Known forExtreme childhood isolation; case study in language acquisition and the critical period hypothesis

Genie (born 1957) is the pseudonym of an American feral child who was a victim of severe abuse, neglect, and social isolation. Her circumstances are prominently recorded in the annals of linguistics and abnormal child psychology. From the time she was about 20 months old until her discovery at age 13 years 7 months, Genie spent most of her life locked in her bedroom, strapped to a child's toilet or bound in a crib, and was punished if she made any sound; she had almost no exposure to language.

Discovery and research

Los Angeles child welfare authorities discovered Genie on 4 November 1970 after her mother sought help at a welfare office. Genie weighed only 59 pounds (27 kg), could not chew solid food, walked with a distinctive "bunny walk", and could not speak. A team at Children's Hospital Los Angeles, including linguist Susan Curtiss, studied her intensively with NIMH funding. Genie rapidly learned hundreds of words and combined them, but never mastered normal grammar, providing evidence relevant to the critical period hypothesis for language acquisition.

Later life

Research funding ended in 1974 amid disputes among the scientists and concerns that research had been prioritised over Genie's care. After periods in the home of researcher David Rigler and with her mother, she was moved through a series of foster homes where she suffered further abuse and regressed, becoming mostly silent. She later lived in an adult care home in California; her case is documented in Russ Rymer's book Genie: A Scientific Tragedy and the documentary Secrets of the Wild Child.
