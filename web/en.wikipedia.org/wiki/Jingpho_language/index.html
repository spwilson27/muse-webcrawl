Jingpho language

Jingpho (also spelled Jinghpaw; endonym Jinghpaw Ga), known in China as part of the Jingpo nationality's language, is a Tibeto-Burman language spoken by the Jingpho people of Kachin State in northern Myanmar and by communities in Yunnan Province, China, where it is called Jingpo.

Use and dialects

Jingpho serves as a lingua franca among the diverse peoples collectively known as the Kachin, many of whom speak it as a second language alongside their own languages such as Zaiwa. Major dialect areas include the standard dialect based on the region northeast of Bhamo. In Yunnan it is spoken in the Dehong Dai-Jingpo Autonomous Prefecture.

Writing and phonology

Jingpho is written in a Latin-based alphabet developed by American Baptist missionaries in the late 19th century. The language is tonal, with four tones in the standard dialect, and has a syllable structure notable for a large set of consonant clusters and checked syllables ending in stops.
