Sencha (煎茶) is a type of Japanese ryokucha (green tea) which is prepared by infusing the processed whole tea leaves in hot water. It is the most popular tea in Japan, accounting for the great majority of the country's tea production.

Production

Sencha is made from the leaves of Camellia sinensis grown in full sunlight. After picking, the leaves are steamed — the defining step of Japanese green tea processing — to halt oxidation, then rolled and dried into fine needle-like shapes, and finally sorted and blended. Shading the bushes before harvest produces the higher-grade gyokuro instead, while sencha fields remain unshaded.

Preparation and flavour

Sencha is typically brewed with water at about 80 °C for around one minute; hotter water or longer steeping draws out more bitterness. The liquor is a yellow-green colour with a fresh, grassy, slightly sweet and umami-rich flavour. Grades range from everyday bancha-adjacent blends to premium first-flush shincha. Sencha is drunk throughout the day in Japan, served to guests, and also canned or bottled as a ubiquitous convenience drink.
