Puerto Princesa


Puerto Princesa Bay

Puerto Princesa, officially the City of Puerto Princesa, is a first-class highly urbanized city in the Mimaropa region of the Philippines. It is the capital of the province of Palawan and, in terms of land area, is the second-largest city in the country after Davao City, covering about 2,381 square kilometres. According to the 2020 census, it has a population of about 307,000 people.

History

The city was founded by Spanish colonisers on 4 March 1872. Its name, "Princess Port", was given in honour of Princess Eulalia of Spain, and was later also associated with Princess Asunción, who died young. During the Second World War, the city was the site of the Palawan massacre of December 1944, in which Japanese forces killed about 139 American prisoners of war.

Tourism

Puerto Princesa is best known as the gateway to the Puerto Princesa Subterranean River National Park, whose 8.2 km navigable underground river is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and was voted one of the New7Wonders of Nature in 2011. Other attractions include Honda Bay island-hopping, the Iwahig Penal Colony, firefly watching on the Iwahig River, and the city's reputation as the cleanest and greenest in the Philippines.

Geography

The city lies in the mid-section of Palawan island, bounded by the South China Sea to the west and the Sulu Sea to the east. Despite its large land area and its status as provincial capital, as a highly urbanized city it is administratively independent of Palawan province.
