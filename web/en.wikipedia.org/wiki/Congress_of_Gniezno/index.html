Date: March 1000
Location: Gniezno, Poland

Congress of Gniezno

The Congress of Gniezno (Polish: Zjazd gnieźnieński) was a meeting between the Polish duke Bolesław I the Brave and Emperor Otto III, held at Gniezno in March 1000. Otto made a pilgrimage to the tomb of Saint Adalbert of Prague, who had been martyred in 997 and was buried at Gniezno.

Events

According to the chronicler Thietmar of Merseburg, Otto was received with extraordinary splendor. During the visit the emperor established an archbishopric at Gniezno — the first Polish ecclesiastical province — with new bishoprics at Kraków, Wrocław, and Kołobrzeg, independent of the German archbishopric of Magdeburg. Otto is said to have placed his imperial diadem on Bolesław's head and presented him with a replica of the Holy Lance, gestures interpreted as recognition of Bolesław as a brother and partner of the Empire.

Significance

The congress is regarded as a founding moment of the Polish Church and state, confirming Poland's place within Latin Christendom and paving the way for Bolesław's coronation as the first king of Poland in 1025.
