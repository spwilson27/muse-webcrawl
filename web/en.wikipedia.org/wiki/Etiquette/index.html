Etiquette

Etiquette is the set of conventional rules and norms of polite behaviour in a society, profession, or social setting — governing greetings, conversation, dining, dress, correspondence, and digital communication. The word comes from the French étiquette, originally a label or ticket; at the court of Louis XIV, cards listing court ceremonial were called étiquettes. Unlike laws, etiquette is enforced by social approval rather than legal sanction.

History

Codified manners appear in ancient texts such as the Egyptian Instructions of Ptahhotep (c. 2400 BCE). In Europe, Renaissance courtesy books — Castiglione's The Book of the Courtier (1528) and Erasmus's On Civility in Children (1530) — shaped aristocratic conduct. Norbert Elias's The Civilizing Process (1939) traced how manners progressively regulated bodily functions and emotions as state formation advanced. In the twentieth century, writers such as Emily Post (Etiquette, 1922) democratized and popularized rules for a mass readership.

Domains

Major domains include dining etiquette (table manners, place settings, toasting), business etiquette (meetings, introductions, email), social etiquette (weddings, funerals, gifts), and netiquette for online conduct. Norms vary greatly by culture: punctuality, tipping, footwear indoors, forms of address, and acceptable conversational topics differ widely between, for example, Japan, the Middle East, and the United States.

Etiquette and manners

Writers often distinguish etiquette — the specific rules of a context — from manners, the underlying consideration and respect for others from which rules derive. Critics have sometimes treated etiquette as mere class signaling, while defenders see it as a lubricant that makes social life predictable and comfortable.
