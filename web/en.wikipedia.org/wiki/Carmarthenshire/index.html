Carmarthenshire (Welsh: Sir Gaerfyrddin) is a county in south-west Wales. One of the thirteen historic counties and a principal area since 1996, it is among the most Welsh-speaking parts of Wales and is known for its rolling agricultural landscape, sometimes called the "Garden of Wales".

Geography

The county is bounded by Cardigan Bay to the west, Pembrokeshire to the south-west, Swansea Bay to the south, and the Brecon Beacons and Black Mountain to the east. The River Towy (Tywi), the longest river entirely in Wales, flows through Carmarthen to the sea near Llansteffan. Principal towns include Carmarthen (the county town and one of Wales's oldest towns), Llanelli, Ammanford, Burry Port and Llandeilo.

History

Carmarthen was a Roman town (Moridunum) and later the legendary birthplace of Merlin in Welsh tradition. The area formed part of the kingdom of Deheubarth. In the 19th century the county saw the Rebecca Riots, protests against toll gates. Tinplate and coal industries flourished around Llanelli, while the rural interior remained agricultural.

Welsh language

Carmarthenshire has one of the highest proportions of Welsh speakers of any Welsh county — around 44 per cent of residents in the 2021 census — and Welsh is prominent in local government, education and cultural life.
