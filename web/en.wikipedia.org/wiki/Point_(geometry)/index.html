Point (geometry)

In geometry, a point is an abstract idealisation of an exact position or location in space. A point has no size — no length, area, or volume — and no dimension. Points are among the fundamental building blocks of geometry: a line can be thought of as a set of points extending infinitely in both directions, and planes and spaces likewise.

In Euclid and modern mathematics

Euclid defined a point as "that which has no part". In modern mathematics, points are usually taken as primitive, undefined elements of a set (such as Euclidean space) whose properties are fixed by axioms. In analytic geometry a point in the plane is an ordered pair (x, y) of real numbers, and in n dimensions an n-tuple. Points in finite geometry, topological spaces, and algebraic geometry (where points correspond to prime ideals or maximal ideals of rings) generalise the same idea in different directions.

Notation

Points are conventionally labelled with capital letters (A, B, P) and drawn as small dots, though the dot is only a representation of the dimensionless object.
