George Gerbner

George Gerbner (August 8, 1919 – December 24, 2005) was a Hungarian-American communication scientist.

Born: Budapest, Hungary
Died: Philadelphia, Pennsylvania
Known for: Cultivation theory; Mean World Syndrome

George Gerbner was a professor of communication and the founder of cultivation theory, which examines the long-term effects of television on viewers' perceptions of the world. He served as dean of the Annenberg School for Communication at the University of Pennsylvania from 1964 to 1989.

Life

Gerbner was born in Budapest and emigrated to the United States in 1939, fleeing the rise of fascism in Hungary. He studied journalism at the University of California, Berkeley, served in the U.S. Army and the Office of Strategic Services during the Second World War, and earned a PhD from the University of Southern California in 1955.

Cultivation theory

At Annenberg, Gerbner launched the Cultural Indicators project, a long-running content analysis of American network television. His research found that heavy television viewers tended to perceive the world as more dangerous and mean than light viewers — a phenomenon he called the "Mean World Syndrome" — and that television "cultivates" shared, mainstreamed views of social reality regardless of viewers' backgrounds.

Later work

Gerbner testified before Congress on television violence and founded the Cultural Environment Movement in 1996 to advocate for greater diversity and public control in media storytelling. He died in Philadelphia in 2005.
