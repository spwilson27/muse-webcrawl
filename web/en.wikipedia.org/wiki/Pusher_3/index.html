Pusher 3 (Danish: Pusher III: I'm the Angel of Death) is a 2005 Danish crime film written and directed by Nicolas Winding Refn. It is the third and final instalment of the Pusher trilogy, following Pusher (1996) and Pusher II (2004). Where the earlier films centred on the dealer Frank and his enforcer Tonny, the third film shifts perspective to Milo, the Serbian drug lord who appeared as an antagonist in the first film.

Plot

Milo, now middle-aged, is trying to stay in control of Copenhagen's drug trade while attending Narcotics Anonymous meetings and preparing the christening celebration for his daughter. A shipment he expected to be heroin turns out to be ecstasy — a drug he does not deal in and does not understand — forcing him into desperate deals with younger criminals and Albanian traffickers who show him little respect. As the day spirals, Milo is humiliated, cheated and drawn back into extreme violence, including the disposal of a body, while struggling to appear at his daughter's party as a respectable father. The film portrays an ageing gangster whose authority and world are slipping away.

Production and themes

Refn wrote and shot the sequels back to back after his company faced financial difficulties following the failure of Fear X. Like its predecessors, Pusher 3 was filmed in a raw, handheld style with largely chronological shooting and extensive use of non-professional supporting actors from Copenhagen's milieu. Critics noted the film's blend of grim realism, black comedy (Milo's inept attempts to cook for the christening) and its portrait of generational change in organised crime.

Reception

The trilogy as a whole is regarded as a landmark of Danish cinema and launched Refn's international career. Pusher 3 was praised for Zlatko Burić's performance as Milo, humanising a character who had been a figure of menace in the original film.
