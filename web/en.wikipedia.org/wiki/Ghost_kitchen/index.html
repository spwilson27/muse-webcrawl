Ghost kitchen

A ghost kitchen prepares food exclusively for delivery orders

A ghost kitchen (also known as a dark kitchen, cloud kitchen, virtual kitchen, or delivery-only restaurant) is a food preparation facility that has no dine-in area or customer-facing storefront and serves customers exclusively through online food ordering and delivery. Ghost kitchens emerged with the growth of food delivery apps in the 2010s and expanded rapidly during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Business model

Ghost kitchens may be operated by a single restaurant brand, host several brands under one roof, or rent shared kitchen space to multiple independent operators. Because they do not pay for prime retail frontage, dining areas, or front-of-house staff, ghost kitchens have lower overheads than conventional restaurants, and operators can launch new "virtual brands" quickly to test menus on delivery platforms such as DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Deliveroo.

Companies in the sector have included Kitchen United, CloudKitchens (founded by former Uber CEO Travis Kalanick), and Reef Technology. Some established restaurant chains also opened ghost locations to extend their delivery reach.

Criticism

Critics note that customers may not realise a delivery listing is a virtual brand operating from an industrial kitchen rather than a conventional restaurant, and multiple unrelated-sounding brands may be cooked at a single address. The model has also faced high failure rates, thin margins, and scrutiny over food-safety oversight. After the pandemic-era boom, several prominent ghost kitchen companies contracted, shut down, or pivoted.
