Draw (terrain)

A draw is a terrain feature formed by two parallel ridges or spurs with low ground in between. Draws are similar to valleys on a smaller scale; however, while valleys are by definition formed by rivers, a draw may or may not contain a watercourse. A draw that contains a stream is often called a hollow or coulee depending on region.

Formation

Draws are formed by erosion: the slopes of the surrounding spurs shed water that carves a channel down the middle. In arid regions of the western United States, draws are dry most of the year but can carry flash floods after storms.

Military usage

In terrain analysis, a draw is one of the five major terrain features (hill, valley, ridge, saddle, depression). Draws can offer concealed routes of approach and are important in land navigation and battle planning.
