Governance

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Governance is the process of making and implementing decisions within an organisation, system or society — broader than government, because it encompasses the rules, norms, networks and institutions (public, private and civic) through which collective action is organised. The term rose to prominence from the late 1980s: the World Bank's governance agenda (its 1989 report on Sub-Saharan Africa diagnosed a “crisis of governance”) made “good governance” — accountability, rule of law, transparency, capacity — a condition of development lending, measured in indicators such as the Worldwide Governance Indicators.

In political science, governance theory describes the shift from command-and-control government to steering through networks: multi-level governance in the EU (Hooghe and Marks), public-service delivery by mixed economies of providers, and global governance for problems (climate, finance, pandemics) exceeding any state's reach. Critics caution that governance can diffuse accountability — decisions everywhere and responsibility nowhere — and that the “good governance” agenda smuggled in particular institutional preferences. Corporate governance is the sister field for firms.
