Snow leopard

The snow leopard (Panthera uncia), also called the ounce, is a species of large cat native to the mountain ranges of Central and South Asia. It is listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List, with an estimated wild population of roughly 4,000–6,500.

Snow leopard
Scientific name: Panthera uncia
Range: Central & South Asian mountains
Diet: Wild ungulates, marmots, livestock
Status: Vulnerable (IUCN)

Description

Snow leopards have thick smoky-grey fur patterned with rosettes and spots, a long bushy tail for balance and warmth, broad paws acting as snowshoes, and cannot roar. Adults weigh 22–55 kg and live at elevations of 3,000–4,500 m in alpine and subalpine zones.

Behaviour and ecology

Solitary and crepuscular, they prey mainly on blue sheep (bharal) and Siberian ibex, marking territories with scent and scrapes. Females raise 2–3 cubs for about two years.

Threats and conservation

Threats include poaching for fur and bones, retaliatory killing for livestock predation, prey decline and climate change shrinking alpine habitat. Conservation is coordinated across 12 range states through the Global Snow Leopard & Ecosystem Protection Program (Bishkek Declaration, 2013).
