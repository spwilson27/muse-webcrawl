The Yemeni Civil War (Arabic: الحرب الأهلية اليمنية) is an ongoing civil war that began in September 2014, when the Houthi movement (Ansar Allah) seized the capital, Sanaa, from the government of President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi. In March 2015, a coalition led by Saudi Arabia intervened militarily in support of the government, making the conflict a wider regional proxy war. The war has produced one of the world's worst humanitarian crises.

Background

After the 2011 Yemeni revolution forced President Ali Abdullah Saleh from power, his deputy Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi took office amid economic collapse, a southern separatist movement, an al-Qaeda insurgency, and the Houthi movement's grievances in the north. A National Dialogue Conference attempted to draft a new constitution, but a disputed federalisation plan and fuel-subsidy cuts in 2014 triggered mass protests led by the Houthis, who had allied, tactically, with their former enemy Saleh.

Course of the war

Houthi forces took Sanaa in September 2014 and dissolved parliament in February 2015. Hadi fled first to Aden and then to Riyadh. On 26 March 2015 Saudi Arabia launched Operation Decisive Storm, an air campaign and naval blockade intended to restore the government. The coalition retook Aden and much of the south, but the front lines largely stalemated from 2016. The alliance between the Houthis and Saleh collapsed in December 2017, and Saleh was killed by Houthi fighters. The battle for the Red Sea port of Hudaydah in 2018 led to the UN-brokered Stockholm Agreement. In the south, the UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council (STC) seized Aden from the government in 2019 before the Riyadh Agreement attempted to paper over the split; in April 2022 Hadi transferred power to a Presidential Leadership Council.

A UN-brokered truce from April to October 2022 sharply reduced fighting, and although it was never formally renewed, the de facto de-escalation largely held. From late 2023, Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping, launched in declared solidarity with Gaza, drew US and British airstrikes.

Humanitarian impact

The United Nations has described Yemen's situation as among the world's worst humanitarian crises. UNDP estimated that by the end of 2021 the conflict had caused about 377,000 deaths, roughly 60 percent of them indirect — from hunger, disease and collapsing services — rather than from direct fighting. Millions have been displaced, and the country suffered the largest cholera outbreak in recorded history, beginning in 2016.
