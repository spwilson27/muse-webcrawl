DMX512 is a standard for digital communication networks that are commonly used to control lighting and effects in theatres, concerts and architectural installations. The name is short for Digital Multiplex with 512 channels; a single network segment, called a universe, carries 512 channels of 8-bit dimmer or parameter values.

History

DMX512 was developed by the Engineering Commission of the United States Institute for Theatre Technology (USITT) in 1986 to provide a standard digital alternative to proprietary and analogue 0–10 V dimmer control. It was revised in 1990 (DMX512/1990) and later adopted as an ANSI standard (E1.11, "DMX512-A"), with the remote device management extension RDM (ANSI E1.20) adding bidirectional communication in 2006.

Technical details

The protocol runs over an EIA-485 differential pair at 250 kbit/s, transmitting frames of up to 513 slots (a start code followed by 512 channel values) continuously, with a full frame at 512 channels refreshing about 44 times per second. Devices are daisy-chained and addressed by their starting channel; each fixture consumes as many channels as it has parameters, such as intensity, pan, tilt, colour and gobo. Related networking standards such as Art-Net and sACN transport DMX data over Ethernet for installations needing many universes.
