The World Press Freedom Index is an annual ranking of countries compiled and published by Reporters Without Borders (Reporters sans frontières, RSF), an international non-governmental organisation based in Paris. It reflects the degree of freedom available to journalists in 180 countries and territories, based on questionnaires completed by media professionals, lawyers and sociologists, combined with data on abuses and violence against journalists.

Methodology

Since a major methodological overhaul in 2022, the Index scores each country from 0 to 100 across five indicators: the political context, legal framework, economic context, sociocultural context and safety of journalists. Countries are grouped into five categories from "very serious" (shown in dark red) to "good" (white). Scandinavian countries, particularly Norway, Denmark and Sweden, along with Ireland and Estonia, have regularly occupied the top positions in recent editions, while countries such as North Korea, Eritrea, Afghanistan, Syria and China have ranked at or near the bottom.

Reception

The Index is widely cited by governments, international organisations and the media. Some governments criticise its methodology as subjective, a criticism RSF acknowledges in part, noting that perceptions data complement its record of concrete abuses.
