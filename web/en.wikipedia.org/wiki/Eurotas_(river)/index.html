Eurotas (river)

Location: Laconia, Peloponnese, Greece

Length: approx. 82 km

Mouth: Gulf of Laconia, Mediterranean Sea

The Eurotas (Greek: Ευρώτας) is the main river of Laconia in the southern Peloponnese, Greece. It rises in the Arcadian mountains near Skortsinos, flows south-east through the fertile Laconian plain between the ranges of Taygetus to the west and Parnon to the east, passes the modern city of Sparta, and empties into the Gulf of Laconia after a course of about 82 kilometres. The river is largely fed by the springs at its head and by tributaries from Taygetus; in summer many stretches shrink to pools or dry out entirely, and its water is heavily used for irrigation of olive and citrus groves.

History and mythology

Ancient Sparta stood on the west bank of the Eurotas, and the river was central to Spartan life and identity; young Spartans trained on its banks, and the reeds of the river were a recurring image in poetry. In Greek mythology Eurotas was a king of Laconia, father of Sparta, who was said to have drained the marshy plain by cutting a channel to the sea, giving the river his name. The myth of Leda and the swan was set on its banks, and Helen of Troy was associated with a plane tree by the river. Excavated sanctuaries near the river include the Menelaion, dedicated to Menelaus and Helen.
