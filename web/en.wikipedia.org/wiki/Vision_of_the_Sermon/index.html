Vision of the Sermon

Vision of the Sermon (Jacob Wrestling with the Angel), 1888

Vision of the Sermon, also known as Jacob Wrestling with the Angel (French: La Vision du sermon), is an oil painting by the French artist Paul Gauguin, completed in 1888 at Pont-Aven in Brittany. It is held by the Scottish National Gallery in Edinburgh. The work is regarded as a landmark of Symbolism and a founding statement of the Synthetist style Gauguin developed with Émile Bernard.

Description

The painting depicts a group of Breton women in traditional dress and white coifs who, leaving church after a sermon on the biblical episode of Jacob wrestling with the angel (Genesis 32), imagine the struggle taking place before them in a field. A bold red ground dominates the canvas: on it, Jacob and the angel grapple, observed by a priest at the right edge and by a cow in the upper left. A tree trunk cuts diagonally across the composition, separating the real world of the women from the visionary scene — a device borrowed from Japanese woodblock prints (ukiyo-e), which Gauguin admired.

Style and significance

Painted the same summer as Bernard's Breton Women in the Meadow, the canvas exemplifies cloisonnism: flat areas of unmodulated colour enclosed by dark outlines, with traditional perspective and modelling deliberately abandoned. Gauguin exhibited the painting reluctantly in 1888 and offered it, unsuccessfully, to the church at Nizon; the Pont-Aven parish priest rejected it. The work passed through the Paris art market and was bought for Scotland in 1925. Art historians cite it as the moment Gauguin broke decisively with Impressionism in favour of a painting of ideas, faith and imagination, paving the way for the Nabis and much of modernist painting.
