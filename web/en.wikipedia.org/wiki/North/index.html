North

North is one of the four cardinal directions or points of the compass. It is the opposite of south and is perpendicular to east and west. North is a noun, adjective, or adverb indicating direction or geography.

West is directly opposite east on the compass. By international convention, the top side of a map is often north, although reversed maps preserving this convention are sometimes used as well.

Etymology

The word north is related to the Old High German nord, both descending from the Proto-Indo-European unit *ner-, meaning "left" or "below", because north is to the left when facing the rising Sun. Similarly, the other cardinal directions are also related to the Sun's position.

The Latin word borealis comes from the Greek boreas (βορέας), the north wind, personified in Greek mythology as Boreas. The term Arctic likewise derives from the Greek arktikos (ἀρκτικός), "of the bear", referring to the northern constellations Ursa Major and Ursa Minor.

Navigation and maps

By convention, the top or upward-facing side of a map is north. To go north using a compass for navigation, set a bearing or azimuth of 0° or 360°. North is also the reference direction for defining the other points of the compass and for the system of geographic coordinates: latitudes are measured north or south of the Equator, and the North Pole is the northernmost point on Earth.

Magnetic north and true north

The north magnetic pole is the point on Earth's surface toward which the north end of a compass needle points. It differs from the geographic North Pole (true north), and the angular difference between the two directions at any location is called magnetic declination. The magnetic pole moves over time; in recent decades it has drifted from northern Canada toward Siberia.

Roles of north as prime direction

Because "up" on most maps is north, north has become the prime or default direction in cartography and navigation. In astronomy, the north celestial pole is the point in the sky around which the stars appear to rotate in the Northern Hemisphere, close to the star Polaris. In many languages and cultures, north carries associations of coldness, darkness, and the lands beyond the known world.

Other uses

"The North" is used as a proper name for various northern regions and political groupings, such as the northern states during the American Civil War, Northern England (as opposed to "the South"), and the "Global North" in development economics, denoting the world's wealthier industrialised countries as opposed to the Global South.
