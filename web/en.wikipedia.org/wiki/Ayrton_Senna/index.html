Ayrton Senna da Silva (21 March 1960 – 1 May 1994) was a Brazilian racing driver who won the Formula One World Drivers' Championship in 1988, 1990, and 1991 with McLaren. Widely regarded as one of the greatest drivers in the history of the sport, Senna was renowned for his exceptional qualifying speed — he held the record for pole positions (65) for over a decade — and for his mastery in wet conditions, most famously at Monaco in 1984, the 1985 Portuguese Grand Prix, and Donington Park in 1993. He died from injuries sustained in a crash while leading the 1994 San Marino Grand Prix at Imola.

Career

After success in karting and junior formulae in Britain — winning the 1981 Formula Ford 1600, 1982 Formula Ford 2000, and 1983 British Formula Three championships and the Macau Grand Prix — Senna entered Formula One with Toleman in 1984, announcing himself with a near-win in the rain at Monaco. Three seasons with Lotus brought his first victories (Portugal and Belgium, 1985) but an increasingly uncompetitive car.

Joining McLaren in 1988 alongside Alain Prost, Senna won the first of three world titles in a season dominated by the MP4/4, which won 15 of 16 races. His rivalry with Prost became one of the fiercest in sport, marked by championship-deciding collisions at Suzuka in 1989 and 1990. Senna won further titles in 1990 and 1991, six consecutive pole positions and home victories at Interlagos becoming iconic. For 1994 he moved to Williams, then the dominant team, but the season began with new regulations banning electronic driver aids and a difficult car.

Death and legacy

On 1 May 1994, the day after Roland Ratzenberger's fatal crash in qualifying, Senna's Williams left the road at the Tamburello corner at Imola and struck a concrete wall; he died from head injuries. Brazil declared three days of national mourning, and an estimated three million people lined the streets of São Paulo for his funeral. The accident prompted sweeping safety reforms in Formula One, and no driver was killed at a Grand Prix weekend for twenty years afterwards.

Senna remains a national hero in Brazil; the Instituto Ayrton Senna, founded by his family, supports education for millions of Brazilian children. In a 2009 Autosport poll of 217 drivers he was voted the greatest Formula One driver of all time.
