Esperanto

Type: International auxiliary language (constructed)

Symbol: green star and green flag
[Image: The green Esperanto flag with a white canton bearing a green five-pointed star.]

Esperanto is the world's most widely spoken constructed language, created by the Polish ophthalmologist L. L. Zamenhof, who published it in 1887 under the pseudonym "Doktoro Esperanto" ("one who hopes") — the name that stuck to the language itself. Zamenhof, who grew up in multilingual Białystok amid ethnic tension, designed Esperanto as a neutral, easy-to-learn second language for international communication, belonging to no nation.

Design

Esperanto grammar is famously regular: sixteen rules, no exceptions. All nouns end in -o, adjectives in -a, adverbs in -e, and verb endings are invariant by tense. A system of prefixes and suffixes builds large word families from single roots (sana "healthy" → malsana "ill", malsanulejo "hospital"). Vocabulary draws mainly on Romance and Germanic roots. The Fundamento de Esperanto (1905) fixed the language's core, and the first world congress met at Boulogne-sur-Mer the same year.

The movement

Esperanto survived suppression under Hitler and Stalin and today supports a global community with annual Universala Kongreso congresses, original literature, music, and one of the larger Wikipedias among smaller languages. Estimates of speakers range widely, from about 100,000 fluent users to two million with some knowledge, including perhaps a thousand native speakers raised in Esperanto-speaking families.
