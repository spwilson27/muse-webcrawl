Portal venous system

Portal venous system
Part of: Circulatory system

A portal venous system is a vascular arrangement in which blood from one capillary network flows through a vein into a second capillary network, rather than returning directly to the heart. Humans have two portal systems, of which the hepatic portal system is by far the more significant.

Hepatic portal system

The hepatic portal system carries venous blood from the gastrointestinal tract, spleen, pancreas and gallbladder to the liver via the hepatic portal vein, formed by the union of the superior mesenteric and splenic veins. This arrangement allows the liver to process absorbed nutrients and detoxify substances before blood enters the systemic circulation through the hepatic veins and inferior vena cava.

Hypothalamo-hypophyseal portal system

A second, smaller portal system in the pituitary stalk carries releasing hormones from the hypothalamus to the anterior pituitary gland, allowing direct endocrine control without dilution in the general circulation.

Clinical significance

In cirrhosis, increased resistance to portal blood flow produces portal hypertension, leading to portosystemic shunting, splenomegaly, ascites, and oesophageal varices.
