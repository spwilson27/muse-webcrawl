Norris Geyser Basin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Norris Geyser Basin is the hottest, oldest, and most dynamic of Yellowstone National Park's thermal areas. It lies northwest of the Yellowstone Caldera near Norris Junction, where the Grand Loop Road heads toward Mammoth Hot Springs. The basin covers an area that has hosted thermal activity for at least 115,000 years.

Features

Norris is home to Steamboat Geyser, the tallest active geyser in the world, capable of eruptions over 300 feet (90 m), and to Echinus Geyser, a large acidic geyser. Because it sits at the intersection of three fault systems — the Norris–Mammoth corridor, the Hebgen Lake fault, and a rim fracture of the caldera — the basin changes frequently: new hot springs form and others cool or clog, sometimes year to year.

The basin is divided into the Porcelain Basin, a barren, milky-white flat of sinter dotted with colorful hot springs, and the Back Basin, a forested area of scattered geysers and pools.

Temperatures

Ground and water temperatures at Norris are the highest measured in Yellowstone. A monitoring borehole recorded temperatures of 237 °C (459 °F) at a depth of about 326 m (1,070 ft), and many springs hover near boiling at the basin's elevation of roughly 2,270 m. The basin's waters include rare strongly acidic thermal features.

Name

The basin is named for Philetus W. Norris, the second superintendent of Yellowstone (1877–1882), who explored and described the area and built roads through the region.
