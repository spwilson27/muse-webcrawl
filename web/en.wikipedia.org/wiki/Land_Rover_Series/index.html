Land Rover Series

The Land Rover Series I, II, and III — commonly referred to as "Series" Land Rovers, to distinguish them from later models — are a series of four-wheel-drive off-road vehicles manufactured by the Rover Company and later Land Rover. Produced from 1948 until 1985, they were inspired by the wartime Jeep and became iconic British utility vehicles used by farmers, the military, and explorers worldwide.

History

The original Land Rover was launched at the Amsterdam Motor Show in 1948 with an 80-inch wheelbase. The Series II followed in 1958 with improved styling, the Series IIA in 1961, and the Series III in 1971 with a plastic grille and relocated headlights. Production ended in 1985 when the coil-sprung One Ten and Ninety (later Defender) took over.

Features

Series vehicles used aluminium alloy body panels on a steel ladder chassis, selectable four-wheel drive with a two-speed transfer box, and leaf-spring suspension. Engines ranged from small petrol and diesel four-cylinders to the Rover V8 in the Stage One model.
