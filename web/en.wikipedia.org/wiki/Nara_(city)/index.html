Nara (奈良市, Nara-shi) is the capital city of Nara Prefecture, Japan. As of 2022, Nara has an estimated population of about 355,000. The city was Japan's first permanent capital, established in 710 as Heijō-kyō and serving until 784; the period it gives its name to, the Nara period, saw the flowering of Buddhism and the compilation of Japan's earliest histories and poetry.

Sights

Nara's eight component sites form the UNESCO World Heritage listing "Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara" (1998). Chief among them is Tōdai-ji, whose Great Buddha Hall (Daibutsuden) — among the world's largest wooden buildings — houses a 15-metre bronze Great Buddha (Daibutsu) dedicated in 752. Nearby are the great shrine Kasuga Taisha, famous for its thousands of bronze and stone lanterns; Kōfuku-ji, the clan temple of the Fujiwara; the excavated Heijō Palace site; and the temples Yakushi-ji and Tōshōdai-ji. Nara Park is home to more than a thousand tame sika deer, regarded in Shinto tradition as messengers of the gods of Kasuga, which bow to visitors in expectation of special deer crackers (shika senbei).

Modern city

Modern Nara, incorporated as a city in 1898, is a residential and tourism centre within the Osaka–Kyoto metropolitan sphere, with universities including Nara Women's University. Annual events include the Omizutori water-drawing ceremony at Tōdai-ji's Nigatsu-dō (held since 752) and the Wakakusa Yamayaki, the burning of the dry grass of Mount Wakakusa each January.
