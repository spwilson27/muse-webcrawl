Grágás

Grágás (Old Norse for "grey goose") is the common name given to the law code of the Icelandic Commonwealth, in force from the establishment of the Althing in 930 until Iceland's submission to the Norwegian crown in 1262–64.

According to tradition, the laws were first brought from Norway by Úlfljótr and were recited from memory by the Lawspeaker at the Althing; they began to be written down in the winter of 1117–18. The code survives principally in two manuscripts, the Konungsbók and the Staðarhólsbók, both from the second half of the 13th century.

Grágás covers constitutional arrangements, Christian law (in its church sections), procedures of the courts and the five courts of appeal, outlawry, inheritance, wergild-like compensation, and detailed rules on everyday matters from stray livestock to the duties of householders to feed travellers. It is one of the richest sources for the society of medieval Iceland and for early Germanic law generally.
