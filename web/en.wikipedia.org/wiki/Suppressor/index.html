Suppressor

A suppressor, also known as a silencer or sound moderator, is a muzzle device attached to or integral with the barrel of a firearm that reduces the acoustic intensity of the muzzle report and also reduces the recoil and muzzle rise when a shot is fired, by modulating the speed and pressure of the propellant gas from the muzzle.

Design and function

Suppressors work by trapping and slowly releasing the hot, high-pressure gases that follow a bullet out of the muzzle, typically through a series of baffles or an expansion chamber inside a cylindrical housing. They reduce the sound of a gunshot substantially but do not make firearms silent: the crack of a supersonic bullet and the mechanical noise of the action remain audible.

History and regulation

The first commercial suppressor was patented by Hiram Percy Maxim around 1902 and marketed for sporting rifles. Regulation varies widely by country: suppressors are heavily restricted in the United States under the National Firearms Act, while in much of Europe and in New Zealand they are encouraged or freely available for hearing protection in hunting and sport shooting.
