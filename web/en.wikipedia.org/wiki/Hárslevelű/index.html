Hárslevelű

Hárslevelű (Hungarian for "linden leaf") is a white Hungarian wine grape variety grown chiefly in the Tokaj wine region of Hungary, where together with Furmint it is one of the principal grapes used in the production of Tokaji wines. It is also grown in Slovakia, where it is known as Lipovina, and to a lesser extent in other Central European wine regions.

Characteristics

The grape takes its name from the shape of its leaves, which resemble those of the linden (lime) tree. Hárslevelű produces aromatic wines with notes of linden flowers, honey and spice; it is later-ripening and looser-bunched than Furmint, making it somewhat less susceptible to botrytis, though it is essential to the production of sweet Tokaji Aszú, to which it contributes aroma and roundness. DNA profiling has shown Hárslevelű to be an offspring of Furmint.
