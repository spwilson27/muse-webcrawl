Mod (Scotland)

The Mod (Scottish Gaelic: Am Mòd) is a festival of Scottish Gaelic song, arts and culture. The Royal National Mod (Am Mòd Nàiseanta Rìoghail), held annually each October, is the principal event, organised by An Comunn Gàidhealach, the society founded in Oban in 1891 to support the Gaelic language and culture. Provincial and local mods are held throughout Scotland in the months leading up to the national event.

History

The Mod was modelled in part on the Welsh Eisteddfod. The first Mod was held in Oban in 1892, with competitions in singing, poetry and prose. It quickly grew into the central institution of the Gaelic cultural revival, rotating among host towns in the Highlands, Islands and Lowlands. The event was suspended during the two world wars and in 2020 was held online because of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Competitions

The Mod's most prestigious awards include the Gold Medals for solo singing — the men's and women's medals dating to the 1890s — the Traditional Gold Medals, and the choral competitions, of which the Lovat and Tullibardine Shield for area choirs and the Marjory Kennedy-Fraser Cup are among the best known. Competitions also cover instrumental music (fiddle, clàrsach, accordion, pipes), Gaelic literature, drama, and art. Children's competitions occupy the first part of the week, with adult competitions following. A fringe programme of concerts, cèilidhs, workshops and book launches surrounds the formal events, and the Mod is a major economic and media event for its host community, with coverage from BBC Alba.
