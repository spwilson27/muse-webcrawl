M1918 Browning Automatic Rifle

The Browning Automatic Rifle (BAR) is a family of American automatic rifles and machine guns used by the United States and numerous other countries all over the world. The primary variant of the BAR series was the M1918, chambered for the .30-06 Springfield rifle cartridge and designed by John Browning in 1917 for the American Expeditionary Forces in Europe as a replacement for the French-made Chauchat and M1909 Benét–Mercié machine guns.

The BAR was designed to be carried by infantrymen during an assault advance while supported by the sling over the shoulder, or to be fired from the hip, a concept called "walking fire" thought to be necessary for the individual soldier during trench warfare. The BAR never entirely lived up to the original hopes for it as either a rifle or a machine gun, but as a light machine gun it proved effective, and it remained in United States service into the Vietnam War era in some roles. It was used extensively during World War II, the Korean War, and the Vietnam War.

Design

The M1918 is a gas-operated, air-cooled, selective-fire automatic rifle fed from a detachable 20-round box magazine. It weighs between about 7 and 9 kg (16–19 lb) depending on the variant — heavier than a rifle, lighter than contemporary machine guns — and fires at a cyclic rate of roughly 500–650 rounds per minute for the M1918A2. The M1918A2, the definitive wartime variant, had a bipod, a hinged buttplate, a carrying handle in later production, and two selectable automatic rates of fire but no semi-automatic mode.

History

John Browning demonstrated the rifle to the US Army in February 1917; it was adopted as the M1918 and rushed into production by Winchester and Colt. Some 52,000 had been produced by the end of World War I. Between the wars the design was licensed and adapted abroad, notably by FN Herstal in Belgium and in Poland and Sweden. Updated US variants, the M1918A1 and M1918A2, served as the standard squad automatic weapon of US infantry through the Second World War and Korea.
