Anime

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anime (Japanese: アニメ) is Animation originating from Japan, characterised by distinctive art styles, vibrant characters and fantastical or futuristic themes. It encompasses hand-drawn and computer-generated works across every genre and audience — from children's series to adult drama — produced by a large domestic industry and exported worldwide, where it became a defining element of global popular culture in the twenty-first century.

History

Japanese animation began in 1917; the modern form was shaped after the Second World War by Osamu Tezuka, whose Manga-derived style — large eyes, kinetic editing — powered Astro Boy (1963), the first popular TV anime. Limited-animation economics created the industry's aesthetic. The 1970s–80s added mecha epics (Mobile Suit Gundam), Studio Ghibli's features under Hayao Miyazaki (founded 1985) and the OVA home-video boom; Akira (1988) and Ghost in the Shell (1995) announced anime's artistic ambitions to the world.

Industry and culture

Japan produces hundreds of series yearly through a committee system of sponsors, with studios such as Toei Animation, Sunrise, Kyoto Animation and MAPPA; working conditions and low animator pay are persistent concerns. Around the industry grew Otaku culture — conventions, cosplay, Doujinshi — and pilgrimage tourism to real-life settings (seichi junrei). Franchises like Pokémon became the world's highest-grossing media properties.

Global reach

Streaming platforms made anime a mainstream Western youth culture in the 2010s–20s; Spirited Away (2001) won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature, and Demon Slayer's Mugen Train (2020) became the highest-grossing film worldwide of its year. The word 'anime' in Japanese denotes all animation; abroad it signifies the Japanese form specifically.
