Pentium

Pentium is a brand of microprocessors developed and manufactured by Intel. Beginning with the original Pentium processor released in March 1993, the name was used for a series of x86-architecture chips that formed Intel's mainstream desktop and laptop line for decades. The original Pentium (P5) succeeded the Intel 80486 and was the first x86 processor to use a superscalar architecture, executing two instructions per clock cycle.

History

Intel had planned to name the chip "586", following the 286, 386, and 486 pattern, but because numbers could not be trademarked, it coined Pentium from the Greek pente (five) plus the Latin suffix -ium. The first Pentiums ran at 60 and 66 MHz. The brand subsequently covered the Pentium Pro, Pentium II, Pentium III, and Pentium 4 lines, as well as the Pentium M mobile chips.

The FDIV bug

In 1994 a flaw in the floating-point division unit of early Pentium chips — the Pentium FDIV bug — produced incorrect results in rare cases. After public pressure, Intel offered to replace affected processors, taking a $475 million charge, and the episode became a landmark case study in crisis management.

Later use of the brand

From 2006 the high end of Intel's line moved to the Core brand, and Pentium was repositioned as a mid-range and later entry-level badge (Pentium Dual-Core, and later Pentium Gold and Silver). In 2022 Intel announced that the Pentium and Celeron names would be retired in favour of the plain "Intel Processor" branding for entry-level laptop chips from 2023.
