Thanksgiving dinner

The centrepiece of contemporary Thanksgiving in the United States and Canada is the Thanksgiving dinner (informally called turkey dinner), a large meal generally centred on a roasted turkey. In the United States it is eaten on the fourth Thursday of November; in Canada, on the second Monday of October.

Traditional foods

Typical dishes include roast turkey with bread stuffing (or dressing), turkey gravy, cranberry sauce, mashed potatoes, sweet potatoes or candied yams, green beans (often as a casserole), corn, dinner rolls and pumpkin pie for dessert, frequently with whipped cream. Regional variations add macaroni and cheese in the American South, rutabaga in New England, or wild rice in the Upper Midwest.

History

The meal commemorates the harvest feast shared by Plymouth colonists and the Wampanoag in 1621, though most now-standard dishes date from the 19th century, when Sarah Josepha Hale's campaign made Thanksgiving a national holiday under Lincoln in 1863. Tens of millions of turkeys are consumed each year; the US President traditionally "pardons" a turkey before the holiday.
