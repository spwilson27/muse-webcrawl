ACT UP

ACT UP, the AIDS Coalition to Unleash Power, is an international, grassroots political group working to end the AIDS pandemic. Founded in March 1987 at the Lesbian and Gay Community Services Center in New York City by playwright Larry Kramer and others, it became famous for confrontational direct-action protests intended to draw attention to the AIDS crisis and to press governments, pharmaceutical companies and medical institutions to act.

Actions and influence

ACT UP's best-known campaigns include the 1987 Wall Street action over the price of AZT, which contributed to manufacturer Burroughs Wellcome cutting the drug's price; the 1988 "Seize Control of the FDA" demonstration, after which the U.S. Food and Drug Administration reformed and accelerated drug-approval procedures; and protests with the Silence=Death Project, whose pink-triangle poster became an emblem of AIDS activism. Activists educated themselves in the science of HIV and clinical trials, and members of ACT UP's Treatment and Data Committee — some later forming the Treatment Action Group — won seats at the tables where research priorities and trial designs were decided.

Chapters spread internationally, and ACT UP's methods influenced later health activism, including campaigns for affordable treatment in the global South. The organisation continues to exist, though far smaller than at its early-1990s peak.
