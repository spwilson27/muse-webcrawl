HIV and Measles

Topic: Co-infection / interaction
Key risks: More severe measles, prolonged viral shedding, higher mortality in immunocompromised children
Prevention: Measles vaccination, antiretroviral therapy

This article summarises the interaction between HIV infection and measles. People living with HIV, especially children with low CD4 counts, are at increased risk of severe, atypical and fatal measles, may not develop the typical rash, and can shed virus for longer.

Clinical features

In HIV-infected children measles may present with severe pneumonitis without rash, and secondary bacterial infections are common. Loss of measles antibodies after HIV infection can also occur, reducing prior immunity.

Vaccination

Measles vaccine is a live vaccine. Guidance generally recommends measles vaccination for children with HIV who are not severely immunocompromised, often at earlier ages and with an extra dose, because wild measles risk outweighs vaccine risk; severely immunocompromised patients should not receive live vaccines. See HIV/Vaccination.

Public health

High measles coverage, early antiretroviral therapy and post-exposure prophylaxis with immunoglobulin for susceptible severely immunocompromised contacts are central to prevention.
