Silence = Death

Silence = Death is a slogan and graphic emblem associated with the AIDS activist movement. The image — the words "SILENCE = DEATH" beneath an upward-pointing pink triangle — was created in 1986–87 in New York by the six-person Silence=Death Project collective and became the visual signature of ACT UP, the AIDS Coalition to Unleash Power, founded in March 1987.

Design and symbolism

The poster pairs a stark slogan with the pink triangle, the badge that gay men were forced to wear in Nazi concentration camps, reclaimed after the war as a symbol of gay pride. Inverting the badge to point upward signified defiance and active resistance rather than victimhood. The equation in the slogan asserted that political silence — by government, the media and affected communities themselves — in the face of the epidemic was equivalent to death, demanding protest and public action.

Spread and legacy

Wheatpasted across New York before ACT UP's first demonstrations, the poster was adopted by the organisation as its emblem and reproduced on banners, placards and T-shirts at protests targeting the Food and Drug Administration, pharmaceutical companies and public officials. The Silence = Death graphic, and the slogan paired with it, remain among the most recognisable images of 20th-century protest art, and the collective's approach influenced later activist design groups such as Gran Fury.
