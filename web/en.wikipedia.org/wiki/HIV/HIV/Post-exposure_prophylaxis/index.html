Post-exposure prophylaxis (HIV)

Other namesPEP; post-exposure prevention
IndicationPrevention of HIV infection after a potential exposure
TimingMust start within 72 hours of exposure; sooner is better
Duration28 days
Typical regimenTenofovir/emtricitabine plus an integrase inhibitor such as dolutegravir or raltegravir

Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) is a course of antiretroviral medication taken after a potential exposure to HIV in order to prevent infection. It is an emergency intervention: treatment must begin as soon as possible, and no later than 72 hours after the exposure, and is continued for 28 days.

Indications

PEP is recommended after exposures carrying a substantial risk of HIV transmission, including unprotected sexual contact with a partner of unknown or positive HIV status, condom breakage, sexual assault, sharing of injection equipment, and occupational exposures such as needlestick injuries in healthcare workers. It is distinguished from pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), which is taken before and independently of any specific exposure event.

Regimens

Modern guidelines favour a three-drug regimen built on a backbone of tenofovir disoproxil fumarate with emtricitabine, combined with an integrase strand transfer inhibitor — dolutegravir or raltegravir — because of their potency and tolerability. Older regimens based on boosted protease inhibitors were effective but caused more gastrointestinal side effects and poorer completion rates.

Evidence and follow-up

No randomised trial of PEP after sexual exposure exists for ethical reasons; evidence comes from occupational-exposure case-control studies, animal models and observational cohorts, all indicating a substantial reduction in transmission risk when PEP is started promptly and completed. Follow-up includes baseline and repeat HIV testing (typically at 6 and 12 weeks), screening for other sexually transmitted infections, and counselling.

Access

PEP is available through emergency departments, sexual health clinics and occupational health services in many countries, though cost, awareness and the strict 72-hour window remain barriers to access.
