UNAIDS, the Joint United Nations Programme on HIV/AIDS, is the main advocate for accelerated, comprehensive and coordinated global action on the HIV/AIDS pandemic. It began operations in 1996, succeeding the World Health Organization's Global Programme on AIDS, and is headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland.

Mandate and structure

UNAIDS unites the work of eleven UN co-sponsors — including WHO, UNICEF, UNDP, UNFPA, UNESCO, the World Bank and UNHCR — together with a secretariat. It is governed by a Programme Coordinating Board that notably includes representatives of non-governmental organisations, including associations of people living with HIV.

Work

The programme collects and publishes global epidemiological data on HIV, sets targets for the response — such as the "95-95-95" treatment targets (95% of people living with HIV knowing their status, 95% of those on treatment, and 95% of those virally suppressed) — and advocates for funding, human rights and the removal of punitive laws affecting key populations. UNAIDS coordinates World AIDS Day each 1 December and publishes the annual Global AIDS Update.
