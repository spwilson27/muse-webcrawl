Larry Kramer

Died27 May 2020 (aged 84), New York City, U.S.

Larry Kramer (June 25, 1935 – May 27, 2020) was an American playwright, author, film producer and gay rights and AIDS activist. His confrontational advocacy was instrumental in mobilising the response to the HIV/AIDS epidemic in the United States.

Activism

In 1981, as a mysterious disease began killing gay men in New York, Kramer co-founded the Gay Men's Health Crisis (GMHC), which became the largest private organisation assisting people with AIDS. Frustrated by the slow pace of government action and drug approval, he founded the AIDS Coalition to Unleash Power (ACT UP) in 1987, whose demonstrations — including the "Silence = Death" campaign — transformed AIDS activism and accelerated access to experimental treatments.

Writing

Kramer's play The Normal Heart (1985), a semi-autobiographical account of the early epidemic, premiered at the Public Theater and was later produced on Broadway, winning the Tony Award for Best Revival of a Play in 2011; an HBO film adaptation followed in 2014. His novel Faggots (1978) was a controversial bestseller, and his later works include The Destiny of Me (1992) and the vast historical novel The American People (2015, 2020).

Death

Kramer, who was himself HIV-positive and received a liver transplant in 2001, died of pneumonia in New York City on 27 May 2020.
