Tenofovir disoproxil

Trade nameViread; component of Truvada, Atripla and other combinations
ClassNucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NtRTI)
IndicationsHIV-1 treatment and prevention (PrEP/PEP); chronic hepatitis B
RouteOral (300 mg tablet, usually as the fumarate salt)
Approved2001 (United States)

Tenofovir disoproxil, sold under the brand name Viread among others, is an antiretroviral medication used to treat and prevent HIV/AIDS and to treat chronic hepatitis B. It is a prodrug of tenofovir, an acyclic nucleotide analogue that inhibits reverse transcriptase, and it is almost always used in combination with other antiretrovirals.

Medical uses

In HIV treatment, tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF) with emtricitabine formed the backbone of first-line therapy worldwide for more than a decade. The same fixed-dose combination (marketed as Truvada) was approved in 2012 for pre-exposure prophylaxis in people at high risk of HIV acquisition, and TDF-containing regimens are standard for post-exposure prophylaxis. TDF is also a first-line therapy for chronic hepatitis B, against which it has a high barrier to resistance.

Adverse effects

TDF is generally well tolerated, but long-term use is associated with a gradual decline in kidney function and with reduced bone mineral density, effects attributed to tenofovir accumulation in renal proximal tubules. These concerns motivated the development of tenofovir alafenamide (TAF), a newer prodrug that achieves higher intracellular concentrations with lower plasma levels and less renal and bone toxicity.

History

Tenofovir was discovered in the 1980s through collaboration between researchers in Prague and Rega Institute scientists, and was developed by Gilead Sciences. The disoproxil prodrug, which greatly improves oral bioavailability, was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2001. Generic versions have made TDF one of the most widely used antiretrovirals in low- and middle-income countries.
