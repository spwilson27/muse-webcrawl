Silence=Death Project

Founded1987, New York City
FoundersSix-member Silence=Death Project collective
SymbolPink triangle above the slogan "SILENCE = DEATH"
AffiliationAdopted by ACT UP

SILENCE = DEATH is a political poster and slogan created in 1987 in New York City by the six-person collective known as the Silence=Death Project. It became one of the most recognisable images of AIDS activism and was adopted as an emblem by ACT UP (AIDS Coalition to Unleash Power).

Design

The poster shows an upward-pointing pink triangle — the symbol gay men were forced to wear in Nazi concentration camps, reclaimed and inverted by activists — set on a black background above the words "SILENCE = DEATH". Smaller text beneath urged readers to fight bigotry, government inaction and media silence surrounding the epidemic.

History and impact

The collective, whose members included Avram Finkelstein, began wheat-pasting the poster around New York in early 1987, shortly before ACT UP was founded at the Lesbian and Gay Community Services Center. The image was released for free use within the movement and has since been reproduced on T-shirts, placards and memorials worldwide. The pink triangle and the equation "Silence = Death" remain enduring symbols of the demand that governments and institutions respond openly to HIV/AIDS.
