Vaccination in people with HIV

Topic: Immunisation and HIV
Principles: Inactivated vaccines generally safe; live vaccines restricted in severe immunodeficiency; vaccinate early / on ART

Vaccination is an important part of care for people living with HIV. Immune deficiency reduces vaccine responses and raises risk from vaccine-preventable diseases, so schedules are adapted by CD4 count, age and antiretroviral therapy status.

General principles

Inactivated, subunit and mRNA vaccines are not contraindicated by HIV, though responses improve after viral suppression. Live attenuated vaccines — such as MMR, varicella and yellow fever — are generally avoided when CD4 counts are severely low because of risk of vaccine-strain disease.

Recommended vaccines

Guidelines commonly recommend influenza (annual, inactivated), pneumococcal, hepatitis B (with post-vaccination antibody checks), HPV, COVID-19, Tdap, meningococcal and recombinant zoster vaccines, with timing and extra doses varying by jurisdiction.

HIV vaccine research

No preventive HIV vaccine is licensed. Decades of research — including the partially effective RV144 trial in Thailand — continue into mosaic, mRNA and broadly neutralising antibody approaches.
