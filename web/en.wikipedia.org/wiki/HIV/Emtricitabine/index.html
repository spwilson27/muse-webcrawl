Emtricitabine

Trade namesEmtriva
ClassNucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI)
Legal statusPrescription only
RoutesBy mouth

Emtricitabine (commonly called FTC), sold under the brand name Emtriva, is an antiretroviral medication used to prevent and treat HIV/AIDS. It is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI) that interferes with the reverse transcriptase enzyme HIV needs to replicate. It is generally used in combination with other antiretrovirals.

Medical uses

Emtricitabine is a component of many standard combination regimens for HIV infection and is combined with tenofovir disoproxil (Truvada) or tenofovir alafenamide (Descovy) for pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP). It is also active against hepatitis B virus. Common side effects include headache, nausea, diarrhoea and skin discolouration; it is generally well tolerated.

History

Emtricitabine was discovered by researchers at Emory University, including Dennis C. Liotta, and was licensed to Triangle Pharmaceuticals, later acquired by Gilead Sciences. It was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 2003. Emtricitabine is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
