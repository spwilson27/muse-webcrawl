Post-exposure prophylaxis

Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) is the use of antiretroviral drugs after a potential exposure to HIV in order to prevent infection. For HIV, PEP consists of a 28-day course of combination antiretroviral therapy, started as soon as possible and no later than 72 hours after the exposure.

Use

PEP is recommended after occupational exposures such as needlestick injuries in healthcare settings, and after non-occupational exposures including unprotected sex with a partner of positive or unknown HIV status, sharing of injection equipment, and sexual assault. Assessment considers the type of exposure, the source person's HIV status and viral load, and the exposed person's baseline test results. Follow-up HIV testing is performed over the weeks after completion.

Regimens and effectiveness

Preferred regimens combine two nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors — commonly tenofovir/emtricitabine — with an integrase inhibitor such as dolutegravir or raltegravir. Observational evidence from occupational exposure studies indicates that prompt PEP substantially reduces the risk of seroconversion; completing the full course is important because side effects can lead some people to stop early. PEP differs from pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), which is taken before possible exposures on an ongoing or event-driven basis.
