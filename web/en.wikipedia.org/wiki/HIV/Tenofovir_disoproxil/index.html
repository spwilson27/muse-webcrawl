Tenofovir disoproxil

Trade nameViread, others
ClassNucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitor
UsesHIV treatment and prevention; hepatitis B
AdministrationBy mouth

Tenofovir disoproxil, sold under the brand name Viread among others, is an antiretroviral medication used to treat chronic hepatitis B virus infection and to treat and prevent HIV/AIDS. It is a prodrug of tenofovir, a nucleotide analogue reverse transcriptase inhibitor, and is generally used in combination with other antiretrovirals. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

As tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), the drug is co-formulated with emtricitabine in widely used once-daily tablets for HIV treatment and for pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), and it also appears in several single-tablet regimens. It is active against hepatitis B, and abrupt discontinuation in people co-infected with HBV can provoke severe hepatitis flares.

Side effects

TDF is generally well tolerated, but it is associated with declines in kidney function and bone mineral density in a minority of users, prompting monitoring of renal function. A newer prodrug, tenofovir alafenamide (TAF), delivers tenofovir to cells more efficiently at lower plasma levels and was developed in part to reduce these effects.

History

Tenofovir was developed by Gilead Sciences from compounds first described by researchers including Antonín Holý in Prague. Tenofovir disoproxil was approved for HIV treatment in the United States in 2001 and became a backbone of global antiretroviral programmes.
