The Normal Heart

Written byLarry Kramer
Premiere21 April 1985, Public Theater, New York City
SubjectThe early years of the HIV/AIDS epidemic in New York
AdaptationHBO film, 2014, directed by Ryan Murphy

The Normal Heart is a largely autobiographical play by Larry Kramer, first produced Off-Broadway at the Public Theater in 1985. Set in New York City between 1981 and 1984, it follows writer and activist Ned Weeks — a stand-in for Kramer — as he struggles to raise awareness of a mysterious disease killing gay men while confronting indifference from the city government, the media and the medical establishment.

Plot

Ned Weeks helps found a crisis organisation modelled on the Gay Men's Health Crisis, but his confrontational tactics clash with closeted colleagues who fear visibility. The play charts his relationship with Felix Turner, a New York Times reporter who falls ill, and his alliance with Dr Emma Brookner, a physician pressing for research funding. It ends amid mounting deaths and Ned's expulsion from the organisation he founded.

Reception and revivals

The play was one of the first major theatrical works about the AIDS epidemic and has been produced in hundreds of productions worldwide. The 2011 Broadway production won the Tony Award for Best Revival of a Play. Ryan Murphy's 2014 HBO film adaptation, starring Mark Ruffalo as Ned Weeks and Julia Roberts as Dr Brookner, won the Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Television Movie.
