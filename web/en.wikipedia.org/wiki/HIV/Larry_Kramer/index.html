Larry Kramer (June 25, 1935 – May 27, 2020) was an American playwright, author, film producer and gay rights and AIDS activist. As the HIV/AIDS epidemic emerged in New York in the early 1980s, Kramer became one of its most forceful public voices, demanding a faster governmental and medical response.

Activism

In 1981 Kramer co-founded the Gay Men's Health Crisis (GMHC), the first community organisation providing services to people with AIDS, though he later broke with it over strategy. His 1983 essay "1,112 and Counting", published in the New York Native, was a landmark polemic on the scale of the epidemic. In 1987 he was a founder of ACT UP (AIDS Coalition to Unleash Power), whose confrontational protests — under the slogan Silence = Death — reshaped drug approval processes and public debate about AIDS.

Writing

Kramer's autobiographical play The Normal Heart (1985) dramatised the early years of the epidemic in New York and became one of the most widely produced plays about AIDS; a 2011 Broadway revival won the Tony Award for Best Revival of a Play, and an HBO film adaptation followed in 2014. His earlier novel Faggots (1978) was a controversial portrait of gay life in New York before the epidemic. Kramer, who was himself HIV-positive, died in New York in 2020.
