Hill bagging

Hill bagging is an activity in which hillwalkers and mountaineers attempt to reach the summits of a defined collection of hills or mountains — known as a "list" or "register". The activity is a form of peak bagging, particularly associated with the United Kingdom and Ireland, where numerous overlapping lists of summits have been compiled.

British and Irish lists

  • Munros — Scottish peaks over 3,000 ft (914.4 m); the original list, compiled by Sir Hugh Munro in 1891. Completers are "Munroists".
  • Corbetts — Scottish peaks between 2,500 and 3,000 ft with a prominence of at least 500 ft
  • Grahams — Scottish peaks between 600 and 762 m
  • Wainwrights — the 214 fells of the English Lake District described by Alfred Wainwright
  • Hewitts and Nuttalls — hills in England, Wales and Ireland over 2,000 ft
  • Marilyns — any hill in Britain with a prominence of at least 150 m

Culture

Completions are recorded by bodies such as the Scottish Mountaineering Trust (for Munros and Corbetts), and websites such as the Database of British and Irish Hills (DoBIH) maintain authoritative list data. Some enthusiasts pursue all the Marilyns of Britain (more than 2,000 hills), county tops, or island high points, making hill bagging a structured lifetime pursuit for many walkers.
