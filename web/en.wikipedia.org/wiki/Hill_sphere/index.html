Hill sphere

The Hill sphere of an astronomical body is the region in which it dominates the attraction of satellites. It is named after American astronomer George William Hill. For a planet orbiting the Sun, the Hill sphere is approximately the region between the L1 and L2 Lagrange points.

Formula

The radius of the Hill sphere is approximately r ≈ a (1 − e) (m / 3M)1/3, where a is the semi-major axis of the body's orbit, e its eccentricity, m the mass of the body, and M the mass of the body it orbits. Earth's Hill sphere extends about 1.5 million kilometres, and stable satellite orbits generally lie within one-half to one-third of that radius.

Stability

Within the Hill sphere, a smaller body such as a moon can be retained in orbit; beyond it, the gravitational influence of the larger body tends to strip satellites away. The related concept of the Roche lobe applies in binary star systems.
