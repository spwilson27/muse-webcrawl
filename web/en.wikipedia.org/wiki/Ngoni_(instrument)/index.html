Ngoni (instrument)

Ngoni
ClassificationString instrument (lute)
RegionWest Africa (Mali, Guinea, Burkina Faso, Senegal)
Related instrumentsKora, kamale ngoni, xalam, akonting

The ngoni (also spelled n'goni or nkoni) is a traditional West African lute, a plucked string instrument with a hollowed-out wooden body covered with animal skin, a fretless neck, and typically four to seven strings. It is one of the characteristic instruments of the griot (jeli) tradition of the Mandé peoples and is considered an ancestor of the banjo, carried to the Americas through the transatlantic slave trade.

Types

The traditional donso ngoni ("hunter's harp/lute"), with six strings, is associated with the hunters' societies of the Wassoulou region and with healing and ritual music. The jeli ngoni is the smaller lute played by griots to accompany praise singing and epics. In the late 20th century the larger kamale ngoni ("youth's ngoni"), with ten or more strings and a calabash resonator, was developed in Mali and popularised worldwide by players such as the late Donso tradition innovators; virtuoso Bassekou Kouyaté brought the instrument to international audiences with his band Ngoni Ba.

Construction and playing

The body is carved from a single piece of wood (or, for the kamale ngoni, made from a large calabash) and covered with cow or goat skin. Strings, traditionally of gut or fibre and now usually nylon fishing line or metal, run over a bridge to tuning pegs or rings on the neck. The player plucks the strings with thumb and forefinger, often in fast, repeating ostinato patterns that interlock with other instruments.
