Mad Professor (born Neil Joseph Stephen Fraser, March 27, 1955) is a Guyanese-born British dub music producer and engineer. A central figure in the second generation of dub, he founded the Ariwa label and studio in London and carried the form into the digital era, collaborating with artists from Lee "Scratch" Perry to Massive Attack.

Career

Fraser emigrated from Guyana to London at thirteen and built a reputation repairing and constructing electronic equipment — the source of his "Mad Professor" moniker. He opened Ariwa Studios in Peckham in 1979, recording lovers rock and roots acts, and began his long-running Dub Me Crazy album series in 1982, which applied increasingly sophisticated studio effects while keeping dub's bass weight intact.

Mad Professor has remixed and produced for Sly and Robbie, Pato Banton, Macka B, and the Orb; his best-known crossover work is No Protection (1995), his full-length dub remix of Massive Attack's Protection, which introduced dub to a new generation of electronic listeners. He also worked extensively with Lee "Scratch" Perry in the 1990s and has mentored British dub and reggae artists through Ariwa, which remains active in South London.

Style and legacy

Known for immaculate, futuristic mixes and live dub performances at the mixing desk, Mad Professor helped keep British sound-system culture at the center of dub's global development, influencing dubstep, jungle, and trip hop producers.
