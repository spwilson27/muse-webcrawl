Strathspey, Scotland

Strathspey is the valley (strath) of the River Spey in the Scottish Highlands, in the historic county of Moray and extending into Badenoch. It is one of the great whisky-producing regions of Scotland, home to a large concentration of single malt distilleries along the Speyside whisky trail.

The River Spey, one of Scotland's fastest-flowing rivers, in Strathspey.

Geography

The Spey rises in the Corrieyairack Forest south of Fort Augustus and flows about 172 km (107 miles) northeast to the Moray Firth at Spey Bay. The strath runs through towns and villages including Newtonmore, Kingussie, Aviemore, Grantown-on-Spey, Aberlour, Craigellachie, and Rothes. Much of the upper valley lies within or beside the Cairngorms National Park.

Whisky

Speyside malts are known for their elegance and complexity, ranging from light, grassy styles to rich, sherried whiskies. Famous distilleries in the valley include Glenfiddich, The Glenlivet, The Macallan, Aberlour, and Glenfarclas. The Spirit of Speyside Whisky Festival is held annually in the region.

Transport and heritage

The Strathspey Railway is a heritage steam railway operating between Aviemore, Boat of Garten, and Broomhill. The valley also gives its name to the strathspey, a slow Scottish dance in 4/4 time and the fiddle music associated with it, characterised by the "Scotch snap" rhythm.
