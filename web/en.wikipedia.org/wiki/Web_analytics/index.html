Web analytics

Web analytics is the measurement, collection, analysis and reporting of web data to understand and optimise web usage. It underpins digital marketing, product management and online publishing by quantifying how visitors find a website and behave once there.

Methods

Two classical data sources are server log file analysis and page tagging, in which a snippet of JavaScript on each page reports hits to a collection server, usually supported by cookies. Modern tools combine tagging with event-based models tracking clicks, scrolls, video plays and conversions. Key metrics include sessions, users, pageviews, bounce rate, session duration, traffic sources and conversion rate.

Tools and history

Early log analysers such as Analog and AWStats gave way to hosted services. Urchin, acquired by Google in 2005, became Google Analytics, which grew to dominate the market; alternatives include Matomo, Adobe Analytics and privacy-focused, cookieless tools. The shift to Google Analytics 4 introduced an event-based data model and cross-device reporting.

Privacy

Web analytics raises privacy questions around tracking and profiling. Regulations such as the GDPR and ePrivacy rules in Europe require consent for most analytics cookies, and browser restrictions on third-party cookies have pushed the field toward first-party and server-side measurement.
