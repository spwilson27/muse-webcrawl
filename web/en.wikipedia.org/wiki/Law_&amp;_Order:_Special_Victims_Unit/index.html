Law &amp; Order: Special Victims Unit

SVU, the longest-running primetime live-action series in American television history

Law & Order: Special Victims Unit (often abbreviated as Law & Order: SVU or simply SVU) is an American police procedural drama television series created by Dick Wolf for NBC. The first spin-off of Law & Order, it premiered on September 20, 1999. The series follows the detectives of the Special Victims Unit of the New York City Police Department, based at the fictional 16th Precinct, who investigate sexually based offences, child abuse, and crimes against the elderly.

Overview

The series originally starred Christopher Meloni as Detective Elliot Stabler and Mariska Hargitay as Detective Olivia Benson, a partnership that anchored the show for its first twelve seasons. After Meloni's departure in 2011, Hargitay's Benson became the central character, rising through the ranks to captain. Other long-running cast members include Richard Belzer as John Munch, Dann Florek as Captain Donald Cragen, and Ice-T as Odafin "Fin" Tutuola, one of the longest-serving characters in American television.

Records and reception

In 2019 SVU began its twenty-first season, surpassing its parent series and Gunsmoke to become the longest-running primetime live-action scripted series in American television history, and it has continued for further seasons since. Hargitay won the Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Drama Series in 2006, and the show has been credited with raising public awareness of sexual assault issues; Hargitay founded the Joyful Heart Foundation to support survivors.
