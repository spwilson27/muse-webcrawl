Ale's Stones (Swedish: Ales stenar or Ale stenar) is a megalithic monument at Kåseberga in Scania, southern Sweden. It is the largest preserved stone ship in Scandinavia: 59 massive boulders arranged in the outline of a ship 67 metres (220 ft) long and 19 metres (62 ft) wide, standing on a headland above the Baltic Sea.

Description and dating

The stones, each weighing up to about 5 tonnes, form the hull outline of a vessel, with taller stones marking the stem and stern. Radiocarbon dating of material from the site has suggested dates in the late Nordic Iron Age, broadly between about AD 500 and 1000, though the monument's age and purpose remain debated.

Local legend connects the stones with the mythical King Ale (Ale the Strong), said to be buried beneath them. Scholars have variously interpreted the monument as a burial site, a cult place, and an astronomical calendar: the ship's axis aligns approximately with the sunset at the summer solstice and the sunrise at the winter solstice, leading some researchers to describe it as a sun calendar.

History

The site was partly excavated and restored in 1916 and again in the late 20th century; several fallen stones were re-erected to their presumed original positions. Managed by the Swedish National Heritage Board, Ale's Stones is one of Scania's most visited ancient monuments, attracting tens of thousands of visitors annually to the cliff above the fishing village of Kåseberga.
