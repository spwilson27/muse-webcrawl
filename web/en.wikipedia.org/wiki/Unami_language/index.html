Unami language

Unami (Unami: Wënami èlixsuwakàn) is an Algonquian language, one of the two Delaware languages spoken by the Lenape (Delaware) peoples. It was originally spoken in what is now New Jersey, southeastern Pennsylvania and northern Delaware, with northern and southern dialects.

History

Unami speakers were among the first Indigenous peoples to encounter Dutch, Swedish and English colonists in the Delaware Valley. Through the 18th and 19th centuries, removal policies displaced Lenape communities westward to Indiana, Kansas and finally Oklahoma, where the Delaware Tribe of Indians and Delaware Nation maintained the language. Moravian missionaries such as David Zeisberger produced early dictionaries and grammars.

Status and revitalization

The last fully fluent first-language speakers died in the early 2000s, but extensive documentation — including recordings and the Lenape Talking Dictionary project — supports active language revitalization and teaching programs in Oklahoma and in the homeland communities of the Northeast.
