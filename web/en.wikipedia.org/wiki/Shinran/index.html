Shinran

Shinran
Born: 21 May 1173, Kyoto, Japan
Died: 16 January 1263, Kyoto
Founder of: Jōdo Shinshū (True Pure Land Buddhism)

Shinran (1173–1263) was a Japanese Buddhist monk and the founder of Jōdo Shinshū ("True Pure Land") Buddhism, the largest Buddhist tradition in Japan. A disciple of Hōnen, he radicalised Pure Land teaching.

Life and teaching

Ordained at nine and a Tendai monk on Mount Hiei for twenty years, Shinran despaired of attaining enlightenment by self-power (jiriki). Under Hōnen he embraced exclusive reliance on the nembutsu — reciting Amida Buddha's name — as entrusting to Other Power (tariki). Exiled to Echigo after Hōnen's teaching was suppressed, he married — breaking monastic celibacy — and called himself "neither monk nor layman". His main work is the Kyōgyōshinshō; the Tannishō records his sayings.
