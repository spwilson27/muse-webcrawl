Capitol Records, LLC (known legally as Capitol Records, Inc. until 2007) is an American record label owned by Universal Music Group through its Capitol Music Group imprint. It was founded as the first West Coast–based record label "of note" in the United States in 1942 by Johnny Mercer, Buddy DeSylva, and Glenn E. Wallichs. Capitol was acquired by British music conglomerate EMI as its North American subsidiary in 1955. EMI was acquired by Universal Music Group in 2012, and was merged with the company a year later, making Capitol and the Capitol Music Group both distributed by UMG.

The label's iconic circular headquarters building, the Capitol Records Building, is located in Hollywood, Los Angeles, California, and is a recognized landmark of the city.

History

Founding and early years

Songwriter Johnny Mercer founded Capitol Records after a conversation with producer Buddy DeSylva and music retailer Glenn Wallichs. With DeSylva's financial backing and Wallichs as general manager, the label began operating in early 1942 and released its first records that spring — unusually for a new label, during the Second World War and just before the American Federation of Musicians' recording ban of 1942–44. Early signings included Ella Mae Morse, whose single "Cow-Cow Boogie" was a major hit, along with Freddie Slack, Margaret Whiting, and Mercer's own recordings.

EMI acquisition and the Capitol Tower

In 1955, the British company EMI purchased Capitol for $8.5 million. Between 1954 and 1956 the company built the Capitol Records Tower, a landmark thirteen-storey building in Hollywood designed by Welton Becket and Louis Naidorf, resembling a stack of records on a turntable. The blinking light at the top of the tower spells out "Hollywood" in Morse code.

Notable artists

Capitol's roster has included some of the most successful recording artists of the 20th and 21st centuries, among them Frank Sinatra, Nat King Cole, The Beach Boys, and The Beatles — whose records were released by Capitol in North America from 1963 onward and were a major financial success for the label. Other significant artists have included Dean Martin, Peggy Lee, Judy Garland, Pink Floyd (in North America), Duran Duran, Radiohead (for a period), Coldplay, Katy Perry, Sam Smith, and Halsey.

Capitol Records Building

The Capitol Records Building, also known as the Capitol Records Tower, is located just north of the intersection of Hollywood and Vine in Los Angeles. The building was completed in April 1956 and houses recording studios, including the famed Studio A and Studio B, as well as echo chambers designed by guitarist and audio innovator Les Paul. In 2006 the building was designated a Los Angeles Historic-Cultural Monument.
