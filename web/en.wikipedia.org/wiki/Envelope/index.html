Envelope

TypePackaging
MaterialPaper
Used forEnclosing letters and flat documents

An envelope is a common packaging item, usually made of thin, flat material. It is designed to contain a flat object, such as a letter or card. Traditional envelopes are made from sheets of paper cut to one of three shapes: a rhombus, a short-arm cross, or a kite, and folded so that the flaps overlap and are sealed.

History

Envelopes of cloth, animal skins, and clay were used in the ancient world; the Babylonians used hollow clay envelopes around 3000 BC. Paper envelopes were hand-made until the nineteenth century, when machine-made envelopes, gummed flaps, and window envelopes became standard with the expansion of postal services.

Types

Envelopes come in standardised sizes, from small announcement sizes to large catalogue envelopes. Common features include security tinting, self-seal adhesive, padded mailers for fragile contents, and window envelopes that display the recipient's address from the document inside.
