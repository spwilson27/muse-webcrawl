Milne Bay Province is a province of Papua New Guinea at the south-eastern tip of the island of New Guinea. Its capital is Alotau. The province covers about 14,345 square kilometres of land and a far larger area of sea, encompassing more than 600 islands, of which about 160 are inhabited, including the D'Entrecasteaux Islands, the Trobriand Islands, the Woodlark Island group and the Louisiade Archipelago.

History

The bay was named by Captain John Moresby in 1873 after Sir Alexander Milne. The area saw early missionary and colonial activity, and during the Second World War it was the site of the Battle of Milne Bay (August–September 1942), in which Australian and American forces defeated a Japanese landing — often described as the first decisive defeat of a Japanese land force in the war.

Geography and people

Most of the population lives in small coastal and island communities and speaks a wide variety of Austronesian and Papuan languages. The provincial economy depends on subsistence agriculture, copra, palm oil, fishing, and cultural tourism, particularly around the Trobriand Islands and the annual National Kenu and Kundu Festival at Alotau.

Districts

The province is divided into the districts of Alotau, Esa'ala, Kiriwina-Goodenough and Samarai-Murua, each with its own local-level government areas.
