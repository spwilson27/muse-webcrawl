The geography of the Philippines describes an archipelago in Southeast Asia of about 7,641 islands in the western Pacific Ocean, with a total land area of roughly 300,000 square kilometres. The three main island groups are Luzon in the north, the Visayas in the centre, and Mindanao in the south.

Physical features

The islands are mostly of volcanic origin, forming part of the Pacific Ring of Fire. Mountain ranges dominate the interiors, including the Cordillera Central and Sierra Madre on Luzon. The highest peak is Mount Apo on Mindanao. Active volcanoes include Mayon, Taal, and Mount Pinatubo, whose 1991 eruption was one of the largest of the twentieth century. The longest river is the Cagayan River on Luzon.

Climate

The climate is tropical maritime, with a wet monsoon season and a dry season, and the country is struck by an average of about twenty typhoons a year. Earthquakes are frequent along the Philippine Trench and other fault systems.
