The 2,000 Guineas Stakes is a Group 1 flat horse race in Great Britain open to three-year-old thoroughbred colts and fillies. It is run on the Rowley Mile course at Newmarket over a distance of one mile (1,609 metres), usually in late April or early May. It is the first of Britain's five Classics and the opening leg of the Triple Crown, followed by the Derby and the St Leger.

History

The race was first run on April 18, 1809, five years before the female equivalent, the 1,000 Guineas. Its name derives from the original prize fund of 2,000 guineas. The Jockey Club, which then had its headquarters at Newmarket, established the race as a championship test for the Classic generation over a mile.

Records and notable winners

Notable winners include Nijinsky (1970), the last horse to win the British Triple Crown; Brigadier Gerard (1971); Sea the Stars (2009); and Frankel (2011), whose fifteen-length front-running victory is regarded as one of the greatest performances in the race's history. The most successful trainer is Aidan O'Brien, and leading owners have included the Aga Khan and the Coolmore partnership.

Triple Crown

Fifteen horses have completed the British Triple Crown of the 2,000 Guineas, Derby and St Leger, most recently Nijinsky in 1970. Fillies contest their own Triple Crown beginning with the 1,000 Guineas.
