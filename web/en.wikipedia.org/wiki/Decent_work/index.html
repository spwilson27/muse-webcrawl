Decent work

Decent work is employment that respects the fundamental rights of the human person as well as the rights of workers in terms of safety, remuneration and conditions, and respect for physical and mental integrity. The concept was launched by the International Labour Organization (ILO) in 1999 under Director-General Juan Somavía and became central to the global development agenda.

The four pillars

  1. Employment creation — productive jobs and sustainable enterprises.
  2. Social protection — social security and safety nets.
  3. Rights at work — core labour standards: freedom of association and collective bargaining, elimination of forced and child labour, and non-discrimination.
  4. Social dialogue — negotiation and consultation, including tripartism between governments, employers and workers.

Decent work and development

Decent work is Sustainable Development Goal 8 ("Decent Work and Economic Growth") of the UN 2030 Agenda. The ILO, a UN specialized agency, runs Decent Work Country Programmes and measures deficits such as informality, working poverty, and gender pay gaps. Debates concern how to extend decent work to informal and gig-economy workers.
