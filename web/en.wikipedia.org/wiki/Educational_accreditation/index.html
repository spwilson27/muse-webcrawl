Educational accreditation

Educational accreditation is a type of quality assurance process under which services and operations of educational institutions or programmes are evaluated and verified by an external body to determine whether applicable, recognised standards are met. If standards are met, accredited status is granted by the agency. Accreditation may be institutional (covering a whole school or university) or programmatic (covering a specific course of study, such as medicine, law or engineering). In many jurisdictions accreditation is a precondition for an institution's students to receive government financial aid, for credit transfer, and for graduates to enter licensed professions.

Accreditation in the United States

The United States has no central ministry of education controlling accreditation. Instead, private non-profit accrediting agencies — historically regional accreditors for institutions, plus national institutional accreditors and specialised/programmatic accreditors — review colleges and schools through self-studies and peer-review site visits. The U.S. Department of Education and the Council for Higher Education Accreditation (CHEA) "recognise" accrediting agencies; recognition as an institutional accreditor acts as a gatekeeper for federal student-aid (Title IV) funding. Because accreditation is tied to funding, loss of accreditation is usually fatal to an institution. The system has been criticised for conflicts of interest (institutions fund their accreditors), weak oversight of some for-profit colleges, and barriers to new entrants; unaccredited "diploma mills" and unauthorised "accreditation mills" are an associated abuse.

Accreditation elsewhere

In most other countries, quality assurance is led by government bodies or statutory agencies: for example, the Quality Assurance Agency in the United Kingdom, the UGC and NAAC in India, and national qualifications frameworks in the European Higher Education Area created by the Bologna Process, which rely on standards set by the European Association for Quality Assurance (ENQA). International agreements such as the Washington Accord recognise the substantial equivalence of accredited engineering degrees across signatory countries.
