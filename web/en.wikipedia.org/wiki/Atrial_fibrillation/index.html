Atrial fibrillation

SpecialtyCardiology
SymptomsPalpitations, breathlessness, fatigue, sometimes none
RisksStroke, heart failure

Atrial fibrillation (AF or A-fib) is an abnormal heart rhythm characterised by rapid and irregular beating of the atria, with loss of coordinated atrial contraction. It is the most common serious cardiac arrhythmia.

Signs and causes

Many people have no symptoms; others experience palpitations, shortness of breath, chest discomfort and fatigue. Risk factors include hypertension, heart failure, valvular disease, obesity, alcohol, hyperthyroidism and age. On ECG there are no distinct P waves and the ventricular response is irregularly irregular.

Complications and treatment

Blood stasis in the atria, especially the left atrial appendage, raises stroke risk about fivefold. Treatment includes rate control (beta blockers, calcium-channel blockers), rhythm control (cardioversion, antiarrhythmics, catheter ablation), and anticoagulation guided by scores such as CHA2DS2-VASc, using warfarin or direct oral anticoagulants.
