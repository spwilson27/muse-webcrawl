Archaeogenetics is the study of ancient DNA (aDNA) recovered from archaeological remains — bones, teeth, hair and sediments — to reconstruct the history, movements and relationships of past populations. The term was popularised by Colin Renfrew.

Development

Early work in the 1980s–1990s recovered short mitochondrial sequences; high-throughput sequencing after 2010 made whole ancient genomes routine, culminating in the Neanderthal and Denisovan genomes and Svante Pääbo's 2022 Nobel Prize. Strict clean-lab protocols guard against modern contamination.

Findings

Ancient DNA has rewritten prehistory: it documented massive Steppe migrations into Bronze Age Europe, farming's spread from Anatolia, admixture between modern humans and archaic hominins, and the population turnovers of the Americas and Oceania, complementing archaeology and linguistics.
