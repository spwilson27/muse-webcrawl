Adolphe Charles Adam (1803–1856)

Adolphe Charles Adam (24 July 1803 – 3 May 1856) was a French composer, teacher and music critic. He is best known today for his ballets Giselle (1841) and Le Corsaire (1856), his operas Le postillon de Lonjumeau (1836) and Si j'étais roi (1852), and the Christmas carol "Cantique de Noël" (1847), known in English as "O Holy Night".

Life and career

Adam was born in Paris, where his father, Jean-Louis Adam, was a professor at the Paris Conservatoire. He studied under François-Adrien Boieldieu and won the Prix de Rome second prize in 1825. He began composing for the Parisian popular theatres, producing a stream of opéras comiques distinguished by melodic charm, light orchestration, and theatrical craftsmanship.

Works

Adam composed more than forty operas and fourteen ballets. Le postillon de Lonjumeau, with its famous tenor aria "Mes amis, écoutez l'histoire", became an international success. Giselle, choreographed by Jean Coralli and Jules Perrot to a libretto by Théophile Gautier, is regarded as the masterpiece of Romantic ballet and remains in the repertory of every major ballet company. His carol "Minuit, chrétiens", set to words by Placide Cappeau, is sung worldwide at Christmas.

Later years

In 1847 Adam founded the Théâtre National in Paris, an enterprise ruined by the Revolution of 1848, leaving him heavily in debt. He repaid his creditors through prolific music journalism and teaching, and was appointed professor of piano at the Conservatoire in 1849. He died in Paris in 1856, the day after the premiere run of his last ballet, Le Corsaire.
