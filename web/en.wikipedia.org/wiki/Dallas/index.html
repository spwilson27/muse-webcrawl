Dallas is a city in the U.S. state of Texas and the most populous city in the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex, the fourth-largest metropolitan area in the United States with about 7.6 million residents. The city's population of roughly 1.3 million makes it the ninth-most populous city in the U.S. and the third-most in Texas after Houston and San Antonio.

History

Dallas was founded in 1841 by trader John Neely Bryan, who established a trading post on the Trinity River. The arrival of railroads in the 1870s made it a major commercial and cotton-trading centre. The discovery of oil in East Texas in 1930 turned Dallas into a hub of the petroleum and banking industries.

On 22 November 1963, President John F. Kennedy was assassinated in Dealey Plaza as his motorcade passed through downtown Dallas — an event commemorated today by the Sixth Floor Museum in the former Texas School Book Depository. The city later gained worldwide recognition through the television series Dallas (1978–1991).

Economy

Dallas is a major centre for banking, commerce, telecommunications, technology, energy, healthcare and transportation. The region hosts numerous Fortune 500 headquarters, including AT&T, ExxonMobil (in nearby Irving) and American Airlines (in Fort Worth). Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport, between the two cities, is one of the busiest airports in the world; within the city, Dallas Love Field serves as the headquarters and hub of Southwest Airlines.

Culture and sports

The Dallas Arts District is the largest contiguous urban arts district in the United States, home to the Dallas Museum of Art, the Nasher Sculpture Center and the Winspear Opera House. Professional sports teams include the Dallas Cowboys (NFL, playing in nearby Arlington), Dallas Mavericks (NBA), Dallas Stars (NHL) and FC Dallas (MLS).
