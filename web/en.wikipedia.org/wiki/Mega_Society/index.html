The Mega Society is a high-IQ society open to people who have scored at or above the one-in-a-million level on an IQ test accepted by the society. Founded in 1982 by Ronald K. Hoeflin, it is one of the most selective of the high-IQ societies — far more restrictive than Mensa, which admits the top 2 percent.

History and admission

Hoeflin, a philosopher, founded several nested societies (including the Triple Nine Society's stricter counterparts, the Prometheus Society and the Mega Society) aiming at progressively rarer ability levels. Admission to Mega is based on very high scores on unsupervised tests devised by Hoeflin, such as the Mega Test and the Titan Test, which were publicized in the 1980s through Omni magazine. Membership has always been small, numbering only a few dozen people.

Publications and criticism

The society publishes the journal Noesis, much of it devoted to difficult puzzles and tests. Because Hoeflin's tests are unsupervised power tests that cannot be normed in the usual psychometric way, their claimed one-in-a-million cutoffs are controversial among psychologists, and mainstream IQ testing does not attempt to measure reliably at such extremes. The society and its tests have nonetheless been widely covered in discussions of intelligence testing's limits.
