Hôtel Matignon

The Hôtel Matignon is a hôtel particulier in the 7th arrondissement of Paris, at 57 rue de Varenne. Since 1935 it has been the official residence and workplace of the Prime Minister of France. It is one of the grandest private mansions in Paris and is set in a large park of about three hectares, the largest private garden in the capital.

History

The mansion was commissioned in 1722 by Christian-Louis de Montmorency-Luxembourg, prince de Tingry, and designed by the architect Jean Courtonne. Financial difficulties forced its sale before completion, and it passed through several owners, including the dancer Anne-Marguerite de Montmorency and, in the early 19th century, the statesman Talleyrand, who held lavish receptions there.

In the later 19th century the house belonged to the Galliera family; the Duchess of Galliera offered it to the city of Paris, and after legal disputes it became state property. In 1935 President of the Council Pierre Laval installed the offices of the head of government at Matignon, a role it has retained ever since. The Matignon Agreements of 1936, negotiated there by Léon Blum's Popular Front government, granted major labour rights including paid holidays.

The park

The garden, laid out in the French and English styles, contains a celebrated tree-planting tradition: since the 1970s each prime minister has planted a tree in the garden during their term of office.
