Granule cell

The term granule cell is used for several types of small neuron, most famously the cerebellar granule cells — the most numerous neurons in the brain — and the granule cells of the dentate gyrus, olfactory bulb, and cerebral cortex.

Granule cell
TypeSmall excitatory neuron
LocationsCerebellum, dentate gyrus, olfactory bulb, cortex
NeurotransmitterGlutamate (cerebellar and dentate)
Cell body size~5–8 μm (cerebellar)

Cerebellar granule cells

Cerebellar granule cells account for roughly half of all neurons in the human brain — estimates run to tens of billions. They receive input from mossy fibres and send their axons up into the molecular layer, where they bifurcate into parallel fibres that synapse on the dendrites of Purkinje cells. This massively divergent circuitry is central to theories of cerebellar learning and motor coordination.

Other granule cells

In the dentate gyrus of the hippocampus, granule cells are the principal neurons and one of the few populations that continue to be generated in adulthood. Granule cells of the olfactory bulb, by contrast, are inhibitory interneurons lacking axons, modulating mitral cell output through dendrodendritic synapses.
