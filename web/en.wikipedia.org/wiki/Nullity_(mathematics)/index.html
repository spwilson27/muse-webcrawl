Nullity (mathematics)

In mathematics, the nullity of a linear map or matrix is the dimension of its null space (kernel). It is unrelated to nullity in law. If A is an m × n matrix, its nullity is the number of free variables in the system Ax = 0, i.e. n minus the number of pivot columns.

Rank–nullity theorem

The fundamental relation connecting nullity and rank is the rank–nullity theorem: for a linear map T : V → W with V finite-dimensional,

dim ker(T) + dim im(T) = dim V,

that is, nullity + rank = number of columns (for a matrix). A square matrix is invertible exactly when its nullity is zero; a nonzero nullity signals infinitely many solutions to the associated homogeneous system and that the matrix is singular.

Other uses

In graph theory, the nullity of a graph is the multiplicity of the eigenvalue zero in the spectrum of its adjacency matrix, equal to n − rank for an n-vertex graph; it is studied in chemical graph theory, where for molecular graphs it relates to the stability of conjugated systems. The term also appears for linear operators in functional analysis, where the kernel may be infinite-dimensional.
