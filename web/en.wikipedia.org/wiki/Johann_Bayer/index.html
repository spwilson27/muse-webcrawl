Johann Bayer (1572 – March 7, 1625) was a German lawyer and uranographer (celestial cartographer). He was born in Rain, Lower Bavaria, and practiced law in Augsburg, where he became a city legal consultant. His star atlas Uranometria, published in Augsburg in 1603, was the first atlas to cover the entire celestial sphere.

Uranometria

The Uranometria contained 51 star charts with engravings by Alexander Mair, one for each of the 48 Ptolemaic constellations, one for the southern sky incorporating the observations of Dutch navigators such as Pieter Dirkszoon Keyser and Frederick de Houtman — through which Bayer introduced twelve new southern constellations — and two planispheres. Its positions drew on the accurate catalog of Tycho Brahe. Bayer is remembered above all for the system of star designations introduced in the atlas: stars within each constellation are labeled with Greek letters, roughly in order of brightness, followed by the genitive of the constellation name — the Bayer designations, such as Alpha Centauri, still in universal use today.

Legacy

The lunar crater Bayer is named in his honor. His atlas set a new standard for celestial cartography in artistic quality and comprehensiveness, and remained influential throughout the 17th century.
