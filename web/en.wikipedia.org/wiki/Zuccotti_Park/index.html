Zuccotti Park, formerly called Liberty Plaza Park, is a 33,000-square-foot (3,100 m²) publicly accessible park in the Financial District of Lower Manhattan, New York City. It is a privately owned public space (POPS) owned by Brookfield Properties, created in 1968 under a zoning agreement that granted extra building height in exchange for a public plaza. The park gained worldwide fame in 2011 as the site of the Occupy Wall Street encampment.

History

The plaza was originally built by U.S. Steel as Liberty Plaza Park, across from the World Trade Center. It was heavily damaged by debris from the September 11 attacks in 2001 and used as a staging area during recovery operations. Brookfield renovated the square with granite paving, honey locust trees, benches and in-ground lighting, reopening it in June 2006 and renaming it after John E. Zuccotti, a former deputy mayor and Brookfield chairman. The park contains public art including Mark di Suvero's Joie de Vivre sculpture.

Occupy Wall Street

On 17 September 2011, protesters opposed to economic inequality and corporate influence established an encampment in the park, giving rise to the Occupy Wall Street movement and the slogan "We are the 99%." Because the park, as a bonus plaza, was required to remain open to the public around the clock, the camp persisted for nearly two months, developing a kitchen, library and media centre. In the early hours of 15 November 2011, New York City police cleared the encampment; subsequent rules prohibited tents and sleeping in the park.
