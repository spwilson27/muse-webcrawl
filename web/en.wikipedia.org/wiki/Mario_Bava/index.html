Mario Bava (31 July 1914 – 25 April 1980) was an Italian filmmaker who worked variously as a director, cinematographer, special effects artist and screenwriter. Renowned for his atmospheric use of colour and lighting, he is considered a founding figure of Italian horror cinema and a pioneer of the giallo and slasher genres.

Career

The son of cinematographer Eugenio Bava, Mario trained as a cameraman and effects artist before completing I Vampiri (1957) and being given his solo directorial breakthrough with Black Sunday (1960), starring Barbara Steele. Black Sabbath (1963), The Whip and the Body (1963) and Blood and Black Lace (1964) established the giallo template; Planet of the Vampires (1965) influenced later science-fiction horror; and A Bay of Blood (1971) helped prototype the slasher film. His work strongly influenced Dario Argento, Martin Scorsese, Quentin Tarantino and Tim Burton.
