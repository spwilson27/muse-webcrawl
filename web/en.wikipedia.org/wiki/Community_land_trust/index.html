Community land trust

CLTs keep housing permanently affordable by retaining ownership of the land.

A community land trust (CLT) is a nonprofit corporation that holds land on behalf of a place-based community, while providing secure, affordable access to land and housing for community residents. The CLT acquires land and retains ownership permanently, leasing it under long-term ground leases (typically 99 years) to homeowners, cooperatives, or other users who own the buildings on the land.

How it works

By separating ownership of land from ownership of buildings, a CLT removes the land cost from the purchase price of housing. Resale formulas in the ground lease limit the price at which homes can be sold, preserving affordability for future low- and moderate-income buyers while allowing sellers to build some equity. Governance is typically tripartite: a board divided among leaseholders, other community residents, and public representatives.

History

The model was inspired by the Garden City movement, Georgist land ideas, Gramdan villages in India, and moshav communities. The first CLT in the United States, New Communities Inc., was founded in Georgia in 1969 by civil rights activists seeking secure land tenure for Black farmers. The model has since spread widely in the US, the United Kingdom, Canada, Belgium, Kenya and elsewhere, and is used for housing, urban agriculture, commercial space and land conservation.
