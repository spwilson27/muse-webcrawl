Second Reality (originally titled Unreal II — the sequel to Unreal in pre-release form) is a landmark demo created by the Finnish demogroup Future Crew, released in July 1993 at The Party demoparty in Denmark, where it won the PC demo competition. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest and most influential demos ever made, showcasing real-time effects previously thought impossible on an IBM PC of the era, synchronised to a multi-part soundtrack.

Contents and techniques

The demo strings together a succession of real-time effects: a hidden-line vector world with a flying ship, polygon cubes and morphing objects, a rotating chessboard-like landscape, vector balls, plasma, a pre-rendered-style 3D city fly-through illusion, rotating zooming imagery (rotozoom), and a famous sequence in which a picture is revealed on a waving flag. Much of the 3D was achieved through clever tricks — polygon sorting, texture approximations and precomputation — running on a modest 386/486 PC with VGA graphics. The music, composed mainly by Skaven and Purple Motion, switches styles with each part and was a showcase for tracker music and the Gravis Ultrasound sound card.

Reception and legacy

Second Reality won numerous awards and "best demo" polls, and Future Crew's earlier demo Unreal (1992) and Second Reality together defined the PC demoscene's golden age. In 1999 the online magazine Next Generation listed the demo among notable feats of programming; the source code was released publicly by the group decades later. Elements of the demo's style fed into the games and graphics industries, and members of Future Crew went on to found or join companies such as Remedy Entertainment and Futuremark (3DMark).
