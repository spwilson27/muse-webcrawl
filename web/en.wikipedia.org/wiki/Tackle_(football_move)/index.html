Tackle (football move)

A tackle is a playing technique in many codes of football, in which a defending player attempts to dispossess an opponent of the ball. The word means different things in different codes: in association football (soccer) it describes using the feet to take the ball from an opponent; in American and Canadian football it means physically bringing the ball carrier to the ground; in the rugby codes, it is the act of grasping an opponent and bringing them to ground.

Association football

In association football, a tackle is an interception of the ball as it is in the possession of an opposing player. Tackles are usually made with the foot, from the front or the side; a sliding tackle is performed while sliding along the ground. A tackle that misses the ball and strikes the opponent, or that is careless, reckless or uses excessive force, is a foul punishable by a free kick and possibly a caution or sending-off. Tackling from behind that endangers an opponent has been prohibited since the 1990s. The shoulder charge, in which a player uses the shoulder against an opponent's shoulder while contesting the ball, is legal.

American and Canadian football

In gridiron football, to tackle is to physically interfere with the forward progress of a player in possession of the ball, causing the ball carrier to touch the ground with some part of his body other than the feet or hands, or run out of bounds. Defensive statistics record solo and assisted tackles, and tackles for loss. Dangerous forms of tackling — such as horse-collar tackles, facemask fouls and hits on defenseless players — are penalised, and the quarterback sack is a tackle of the passer behind the line of scrimmage.

Rugby

In rugby union and rugby league, a tackle occurs when the ball carrier is held by one or more opponents and is brought to ground, or the ball touches the ground. Tackles must be made below the shoulders, and a tackled player must release or pass the ball, leading to the ruck in union or the play-the-ball in league.
