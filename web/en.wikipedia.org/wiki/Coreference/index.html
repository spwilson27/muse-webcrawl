Coreference

In linguistics, coreference occurs when two or more expressions in a text refer to the same person or thing; they have the same referent. For example, in "Mary said she would help," the proper noun Mary and the pronoun she corefer. Coreference is a central phenomenon in discourse and a key problem for natural language processing.

Types

The most studied case is anaphora, where a pronoun or other expression (the anaphor) takes its reference from an earlier expression (the antecedent): "John arrived, but nobody saw him." The reverse pattern, where the pronoun precedes its full referent, is called cataphora: "When he arrived, John sat down." Expressions may also corefer with material outside the sentence, in the shared situation or world knowledge.

Not all apparent coreference is identity of reference: in bound readings, as in "Every farmer who owns a donkey beats it," the pronoun's value varies with a quantifier rather than denoting one fixed individual.

Coreference resolution

In computational linguistics, coreference resolution is the task of clustering all mentions of each entity in a text. It supports information extraction, question answering, summarization and machine translation. Early systems were rule-based; modern systems use machine learning, including neural models, evaluated on corpora such as OntoNotes with metrics including MUC, B³ and CEAF.
