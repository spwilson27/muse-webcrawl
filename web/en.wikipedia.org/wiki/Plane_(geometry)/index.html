Plane (geometry)

In mathematics, a plane is a flat, two-dimensional surface that extends indefinitely. A plane is the two-dimensional analogue of a point (zero dimensions), a line (one dimension) and three-dimensional space. Planes can arise as subspaces of some higher-dimensional space, as with the walls of a room extended infinitely far, or they may enjoy an independent existence in their own right, as in the setting of two-dimensional Euclidean geometry.

In Euclidean geometry

In a Euclidean space of any number of dimensions, a plane is uniquely determined by any of the following:

  • three non-collinear points (points not on a single line);
  • a line and a point not on that line;
  • two distinct but intersecting lines;
  • two distinct parallel lines.

Many fundamental tasks in mathematics, geometry, trigonometry, graph theory, and graphics are performed in a two-dimensional space, often in the complex plane.

Representation in three dimensions

In three-dimensional space, a plane can be described by a point on it and a normal vector perpendicular to it, leading to the point–normal form of the equation of a plane: ax + by + cz + d = 0, where the vector (a, b, c) is normal to the plane. Alternatively, a point on the plane together with two linearly independent vectors lying in the plane gives a parametric description. The distance from a point to a plane, and the angle between two planes (the dihedral angle), can be computed from their normal vectors.

Properties

Two distinct planes in three-dimensional space are either parallel or intersect in a single line. A line is either parallel to a plane, intersects it at a single point, or is contained in the plane. The line perpendicular to every line in a plane through a given point is called the normal line at that point.

Generalizations

In higher dimensions, the analogue of a plane is a hyperplane — an affine subspace of dimension one less than the ambient space. In abstract mathematics, a plane can also refer to other flat surfaces satisfying suitable axioms, such as the projective plane.
