The United Arab Emirates (UAE), or simply the Emirates, is a country in West Asia, in the Middle East, at the eastern end of the Arabian Peninsula. It is a federal monarchy composed of seven emirates: Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Sharjah, Ajman, Umm Al Quwain, Ras Al Khaimah and Fujairah. The capital and second-largest city is Abu Dhabi; Dubai, the largest city, is an international hub of trade, finance and tourism.

Dubai, home to the Burj Khalifa, the world's tallest building

History

The area of the modern Emirates was inhabited for well over 100,000 years and hosted Bronze Age cultures such as the Umm Al Nar, followed by trading societies linked to Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley. Islam arrived in the seventh century CE. From the sixteenth century the coast was contested by Portuguese, Ottoman, Dutch and British interests; in the nineteenth century the local rulers signed a series of treaties with Britain, culminating in the Perpetual Maritime Truce of 1853, after which the area was known as the Trucial States, a British protectorate.

Oil was discovered in Abu Dhabi in 1958 (offshore) and exports began in 1962, transforming the region's economy. When Britain announced its withdrawal from east of Suez, the rulers negotiated a federation. On 2 December 1971 six emirates formed the United Arab Emirates, with Ras Al Khaimah joining in February 1972. Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan of Abu Dhabi became the federation's first president and is regarded as its founding father; he served until his death in 2004 and was succeeded by his son Khalifa, and in 2022 by Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan.

Government and politics

The UAE is a federation of absolute monarchies: each emirate is ruled by its own hereditary ruler, and the seven rulers together form the Federal Supreme Council, which elects the president and vice president from among its members — by convention the ruler of Abu Dhabi is president and the ruler of Dubai is vice president and prime minister. The Federal National Council is a partly elected advisory body. Political parties are banned, and the judiciary combines federal courts with, in some emirates, separate local systems.

Economy

The UAE has one of the most diversified economies in the Gulf. Hydrocarbons — principally Abu Dhabi's oil and natural gas — remain a major source of government revenue, but their share of GDP has fallen to roughly a third or less, as Dubai in particular has built major sectors in trade, logistics (Jebel Ali is among the world's busiest ports), aviation (Emirates and Etihad airlines), finance, real estate and tourism. The country is a member of OPEC. Landmark developments include the artificial Palm Islands and the Burj Khalifa (829.8 m), the tallest human-made structure in the world. The UAE hosted Expo 2020 in Dubai and the COP28 climate summit in 2023.

Demographics

The population is unusual in that expatriates greatly outnumber citizens: Emirati nationals make up only about 11–12 percent of residents. The largest expatriate communities are from India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and the Philippines. Islam is the official religion and most citizens are Sunni Muslims; the population also includes significant numbers of Christians, Hindus and others. Arabic is the official language, while English is widely used in business and daily life.

Geography

The UAE borders Oman to the east and Saudi Arabia to the south and west, and has coastlines on the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman. Most of the country is flat desert of the Arabian interior; the Hajar Mountains rise in the east, where Jebel Jais in Ras Al Khaimah reaches about 1,934 m. The climate is hot and arid, with mild winters, extremely hot summers and very low rainfall.
