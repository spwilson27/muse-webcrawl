Urashima Tarō

Urashima Tarō (浦島太郎) is the protagonist of a Japanese fairy tale (otogi banashi) about a fisherman who visits the undersea Dragon Palace and returns to find that centuries have passed. It is one of Japan's most famous folk tales, with versions recorded as early as the 8th century in texts such as the Fudoki and the Man'yōshū.

Story

Urashima Tarō, a kind-hearted fisherman, rescues a sea turtle — in some versions from tormenting children, in others he catches it and releases it. In gratitude, the turtle, who is in fact the transformed princess Otohime, carries him to the Dragon Palace (Ryūgū-jō) beneath the sea. After what seems to him only a few days of feasting, Tarō grows homesick. Before he leaves, Otohime gives him a jewelled box (tamatebako) with the warning never to open it. Returning to his village, he finds everyone he knew gone: hundreds of years have passed. Distraught, he opens the box; white smoke escapes and he ages instantly into an old man — the box contained his lost years.

Themes and legacy

The tale is a classic story of time dilation, paralleled by tales such as Rip Van Winkle and the Irish legend of Oisín. "Urashima Tarō" is also used in Japanese as a metaphor for someone bewildered after returning to a changed world, and the "Urashima effect" is a term used in Japan for time dilation under relativity.
