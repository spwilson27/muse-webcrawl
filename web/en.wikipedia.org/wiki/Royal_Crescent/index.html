Royal Crescent

LocationBath, Somerset, England
Built1767–1774
ArchitectJohn Wood, the Younger
StyleGeorgian
Listed statusGrade I listed

The Royal Crescent is a row of 30 terraced houses laid out in a sweeping crescent in the city of Bath, Somerset, England. Designed by the architect John Wood, the Younger and built between 1767 and 1774, it is among the greatest examples of Georgian architecture in the United Kingdom and is a Grade I listed building.

Design and construction

Wood designed the great curved façade, with Ionic columns and a central pediment, as a unified piece of "palace-front" architecture. Behind the façade, however, the houses were built by individual speculators, who were free to arrange the interiors — and even the rear elevations — as they wished, giving the Crescent its famously irregular backs. The crescent is 150 metres (500 feet) long, with 114 Ionic columns on the first floor. In front of the houses runs a private lawn separated from the public ground below by a ha-ha, believed to be the earliest example in Bath, which preserves the residents' view over what is now Royal Victoria Park.

The crescent was originally called simply "The Crescent"; the "Royal" prefix was added at the end of the 18th century after Prince Frederick, Duke of York and Albany, stayed at Nos. 1 and 16.

Residents and today

Famous residents have included the novelist's family connections of Jane Austen's circle, the eccentric William Beckford at No. 20 in earlier Bath life nearby, and, in the twentieth century, the saga of No. 22, whose owner Miss Amabel Wellesley-Colley provoked a national controversy in the 1970s by painting her front door yellow instead of the traditional white — a battle she eventually won.

Today the houses remain mostly private residences. No. 1 Royal Crescent is a museum run by the Bath Preservation Trust, furnished as a wealthy townhouse of the period, and the eastern end houses the Royal Crescent Hotel. The Crescent is an iconic image of Bath, appearing in countless films and television period dramas.
