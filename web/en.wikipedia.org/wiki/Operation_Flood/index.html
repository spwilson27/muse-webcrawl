Operation Flood, launched in 1970, was the world's largest dairy development programme and a landmark project of India's National Dairy Development Board (NDDB). It transformed India from a milk-deficient nation into the world's largest milk producer — a change often called the White Revolution.

Model

The programme replicated the "Anand pattern" of the Amul cooperative in Gujarat: village-level dairy cooperative societies collected milk twice daily from small farmers, district unions processed and marketed it, and state federations handled branding — giving producers a large share of the consumer price. It was initially financed by selling donated European skimmed milk powder and butter oil.

Phases and impact

Operation Flood ran in three phases (1970–1980, 1981–1985, 1985–1996), building a national milk grid linking rural producers to urban markets. Architect Verghese Kurien is credited with empowering millions of rural households, especially women; by 1998 India had overtaken the United States as the largest milk producer.
