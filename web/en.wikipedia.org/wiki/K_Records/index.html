K Records is an independent record label in Olympia, Washington, founded in 1982 by Calvin Johnson. It has been a major influence on the development of indie rock, particularly the lo-fi and twee pop movements, and played a key role in the early careers of artists such as Beck, Modest Mouse and Built to Spill. The label's "K shield" logo is one of the most recognisable symbols of American independent music; Kurt Cobain famously had it tattooed on his arm.

History

Calvin Johnson, a DJ at KAOS community radio at The Evergreen State College, started K as a cassette label to document the local Olympia scene, releasing music by his own band Beat Happening and friends. The label expanded into vinyl with an aggressively DIY distribution model and a manifesto-like belief in "International Pop Underground" — a network of independent labels, fanzines and venues outside corporate music. From 1991 the label staged the International Pop Underground Convention in Olympia, a landmark event in the emergence of the riot grrrl movement, with bands like Bikini Kill.

Artists

Artists who have released music on K include Beat Happening, Built to Spill, Modest Mouse (early singles), Beck (the album One Foot in the Grave), Mirah, The Microphones/Mount Eerie, Karl Blau, Dub Narcotic Sound System, and many others. Johnson later founded the Dub Narcotic Studio in Olympia, where numerous K releases were recorded.

Legacy

K Records helped define the aesthetics of American indie rock: deliberately unpolished production, small-scale releases, and community-based distribution. Johnson also co-founded the related Dub Narcotic imprint. Financial difficulties and disputes over unpaid royalties in the 2010s and 2020s shadowed the label's later years, but its catalogue remains a cornerstone of indie music history.
