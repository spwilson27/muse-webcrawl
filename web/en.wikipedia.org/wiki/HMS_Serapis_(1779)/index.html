HMS Serapis was a 44-gun Roebuck-class two-decked fifth-rate frigate of the Royal Navy. She is famous for her single-ship action on 23 September 1779 against the American warship Bonhomme Richard, commanded by John Paul Jones — one of the most celebrated naval duels of the American Revolutionary War.

Battle with Bonhomme Richard

While escorting a Baltic convoy off Flamborough Head, Yorkshire, Serapis, under Captain Richard Pearson, engaged Jones's squadron to allow the convoy to escape. In a fierce night action the two ships became lashed together and fought at point-blank range for hours. When called upon to surrender, Jones is said to have replied, "I have not yet begun to fight!" Despite his own ship being shattered and sinking, Jones's crew eventually boarded and captured Serapis. Bonhomme Richard sank two days later, and Jones sailed his prize to the neutral Netherlands. Pearson, though he lost his ship, had saved the convoy and was knighted on his return to Britain.

Later career

Because of diplomatic complications in Dutch waters, Serapis was transferred to France. In French service she was lost in 1781, wrecked off Madagascar after a fire. The anchor and other relics attributed to the battle remain famous, and the engagement is commemorated on both sides of the Atlantic.
