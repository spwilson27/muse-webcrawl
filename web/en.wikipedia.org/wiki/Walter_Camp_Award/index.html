Walter Camp Award

The Walter Camp Player of the Year Award is given annually to the collegiate American football player of the year, as decided by a group of National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) Division I FBS head coaches and sports information directors under the auspices of the Walter Camp Football Foundation.

History

The award was first presented in 1967 to O. J. Simpson of USC, who won it again in 1968. It is named for Walter Camp, the influential Yale coach and rules pioneer known as the "Father of American Football". Notable recipients include Roger Staubach, Tony Dorsett, Barry Sanders, Desmond Howard, and Reggie Bush.

The Walter Camp Football Foundation also names annual All-America teams, continuing the selection tradition Walter Camp himself began in 1889, and presents the award at a banquet in New Haven, Connecticut.
