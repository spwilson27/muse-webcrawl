Interline agreement

Interlining lets a passenger travel on multiple airlines under a single ticket.

An interline agreement is a commercial relationship between individual air carriers to handle passengers travelling on itineraries that require multiple airlines. Interlining agreements differ from codeshare agreements in that they are usually more basic: they cover acceptance of each other's tickets and baggage transfer, without coordinated marketing or flight-number sharing.

How it works

Under an interline agreement, one airline can sell and issue a ticket (an "interline ticket") that includes segments flown by another carrier, and baggage may be checked through to the final destination. Revenue is divided between the carriers using prorate rules administered through IATA clearing-house mechanisms. Most members of the International Air Transport Association (IATA) maintain interline agreements with each other under standard multilateral frameworks.

Interline vs codeshare vs alliance

Interlining is the most basic form of airline cooperation. Codesharing goes further by letting one carrier market another's flight under its own code. Airline alliances such as Star Alliance, SkyTeam and Oneworld bundle interlining and codesharing with shared frequent-flyer benefits and lounge access. Low-cost carriers historically avoided interlining to keep operations simple, though "virtual interlining" services now sell self-connecting itineraries.

Disruption handling

Because a through ticket is a single contract of carriage, the ticketing airline is generally responsible for rebooking passengers onto later flights after delays or cancellations, even when another carrier operates the disrupted segment.
