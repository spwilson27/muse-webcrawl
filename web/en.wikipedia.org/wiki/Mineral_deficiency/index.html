Mineral deficiency

Mineral deficiency is a lack of the dietary elements, the minerals, required for human health. Minerals are inorganic elements that the body cannot synthesize and must obtain from food and water. Deficiencies may result from inadequate intake, impaired absorption, increased losses, or increased physiological requirements.

Essential minerals

The macrominerals required in relatively large amounts include calcium, phosphorus, potassium, sodium, chloride, magnesium and sulfur. Trace elements required in small amounts include iron, zinc, copper, manganese, iodine, selenium, molybdenum, chromium and fluoride. Each plays specific roles: iron is central to hemoglobin, iodine is required for thyroid hormones, and calcium and phosphorus form the mineral matrix of bone.

Common deficiencies

Iron deficiency is the most common nutritional deficiency worldwide and a leading cause of anemia, particularly in women and children. Iodine deficiency is the foremost preventable cause of intellectual disability and is addressed in many countries by iodized salt. Calcium and vitamin D deficiencies contribute to rickets in children and osteoporosis in adults, while zinc deficiency impairs growth and immune function and magnesium deficiency may cause weakness, arrhythmias and seizures.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis typically combines dietary history, clinical signs and laboratory measurement of blood or tissue levels. Treatment involves dietary modification, fortified foods and supplements, together with treatment of underlying causes such as malabsorption syndromes. Because excess intake of some minerals is toxic, supplementation is generally guided by demonstrated need.
