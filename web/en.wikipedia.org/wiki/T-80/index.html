The T-80 is a main battle tank designed and manufactured in the Soviet Union. Entering service in 1976, it was a development of the T-64 and was the second tank in the world to be equipped with a gas turbine engine as its main powerplant.

Design

Like the T-64 and T-72, the T-80 has a three-man crew with an autoloader for its 125 mm smoothbore gun, allowing a lower profile than four-crew Western designs. Its GTD gas turbine engine gives high power output and easy cold-weather starting but high fuel consumption. Later variants include the T-80B with improved fire control, the T-80U with Kontakt-5 explosive reactive armour, and the Ukrainian-built T-80UD with a diesel engine. Russia's T-80BVM modernisation upgraded surviving tanks in the late 2010s.

Service

T-80s have been used by Russia, Ukraine, and several export customers, and saw combat in Chechnya and extensively in the Russo-Ukrainian War, where both sides fielded them.
