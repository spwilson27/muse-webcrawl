Ranks and insignia of the Waffen-SS

The ranks and insignia of the Waffen-SS were the paramilitary titles and badges used by the Waffen-SS, the armed wing of the Nazi Party's SS, between 1933 and 1945. The system derived from the ranks of the Allgemeine SS and paralleled, but remained distinct from, the rank structure of the German Wehrmacht.

Structure

Rank was displayed primarily on collar patches (Kragenspiegel): the right patch bore the SS runes (or, for some foreign volunteer units, alternative emblems), while the left bore rank insignia of oak leaves, pips, and bars. Shoulder boards, adopted in patterns resembling the army's, were added for most ranks from 1938–1942. Titles included, from senior officers downward, SS-Oberst-Gruppenführer, SS-Obergruppenführer, SS-Gruppenführer, SS-Brigadeführer, SS-Oberführer, SS-Standartenführer, SS-Obersturmbannführer, SS-Sturmbannführer, SS-Hauptsturmführer, SS-Obersturmführer and SS-Untersturmführer, with corresponding non-commissioned and enlisted grades such as SS-Scharführer, SS-Rottenführer and SS-Schütze (later SS-Grenadier and other branch-specific titles).

Equivalent Wehrmacht ranks existed for pay and protocol purposes, and Waffen-SS generals held dual designations such as "SS-Obergruppenführer und General der Waffen-SS". Distinctive features included cuff titles naming the wearer's regiment or division.
