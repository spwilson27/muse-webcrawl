Hama

Hama (Arabic: حماة)
Country: Syria
Governorate: Hama
River: Orontes
Known for: Norias of Hama

Hama is a city in west-central Syria, on the banks of the Orontes River, north of Homs. It is famous for its giant wooden water wheels, the Norias of Hama, some dating in design to Byzantine times.

History

Settled since ancient times, Hama was an Aramaean and later Hellenistic, Roman and Byzantine centre, then an important Islamic city under the Ayyubids and Mamluks. In 1982 the Syrian army crushed a Muslim Brotherhood uprising in the Hama massacre, killing thousands to tens of thousands and destroying much of the old city.

Syrian civil war

Hama saw large protests in 2011 and fighting thereafter. In late November–December 2024, opposition forces captured Hama during an offensive that led to the fall of the Assad regime.

Landmarks

Besides the norias, landmarks include the Great Mosque, al-Nuri Mosque, Azem Palace and the citadel mound.
