Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences

The Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS), often called simply the Academy, is a professional honorary organization of more than 10,000 film industry professionals. Founded in Los Angeles on May 11, 1927, by 36 industry leaders including MGM studio chief Louis B. Mayer, it is best known as the presenter of the annual Academy Awards, popularly called the Oscars.

Purposes and activities

The Academy's stated purposes are to advance the arts and sciences of motion pictures, foster cooperation among creative leaders, and promote educational and cultural activities. Beyond the Oscars, it operates the Margaret Herrick Library in Beverly Hills, a major research archive; the Academy Film Archive, dedicated to preservation; the Academy Museum of Motion Pictures, which opened in Los Angeles in 2021; and programs including the Nicholl Fellowships in Screenwriting and the Student Academy Awards.

Organization

Membership is by invitation of the Board of Governors, which is elected from the Academy's branches — there are 19 branches representing disciplines from acting and directing to executives and marketing. A president serves as the public head of the organization, and a chief executive manages day-to-day operations. In the 2010s, following the #OscarsSoWhite controversies, the Academy undertook a major membership expansion and diversity initiative, roughly doubling its size and international representation.

The Academy Awards

The first Academy Awards ceremony was held on May 16, 1929, at the Hollywood Roosevelt Hotel. Awards are voted by the membership, with most categories decided by members of the corresponding branch at the nomination stage and by the full membership for final winners. PricewaterhouseCoopers (now PwC) has tabulated ballots since the 1930s.
