Green Bay Packers

The Green Bay Packers are a professional American football team based in Green Bay, Wisconsin. Founded in 1919 by Earl "Curly" Lambeau and George Calhoun, the Packers joined the National Football League (NFL) in 1921 and are the third-oldest franchise in the league. They are unique in American professional sports as a publicly owned, non-profit team, owned by hundreds of thousands of shareholders.

History

The Packers take their name from the Indian Packing Company, Lambeau's employer, which sponsored the original team. Under Lambeau and later the legendary Vince Lombardi, Green Bay became "Titletown": the Packers won six NFL championships under Lambeau and five in seven years under Lombardi, including the first two Super Bowls (1966 and 1967 seasons). The trophy awarded to the Super Bowl winner is named the Vince Lombardi Trophy.

The team returned to prominence in the 1990s under quarterback Brett Favre, winning Super Bowl XXXI, and won Super Bowl XLV under Aaron Rodgers in the 2010 season. The Packers hold the record for the most overall championships (13) of any NFL franchise.

Stadium and supporters

Since 1957 the Packers have played at Lambeau Field, famous for the "Lambeau Leap" and for the 1967 NFL Championship Game, the "Ice Bowl", won in temperatures of −13 °F (−25 °C). Despite representing by far the smallest media market in the NFL, the team has sold out every home game since 1960 and has a season-ticket waiting list decades long. The Packers' fiercest rivalry is with the Chicago Bears, the oldest in the league.

Ownership

Green Bay Packers, Inc. is a publicly held nonprofit corporation; shares confer voting rights but no equity appreciation or dividends, and no individual may own more than about 4% of shares. Stock sales, most recently in 2021–22, have funded stadium improvements. This structure, grandfathered against current NFL rules requiring a single controlling owner, keeps the team in the league's smallest city.
