Federation architecture

Federation architecture is a pattern in enterprise architecture and information systems in which multiple systems, organisations or architectural domains — each retaining a degree of autonomy — are coordinated so that they work together and appear as an integrated whole. Rather than integrating all elements into a single monolithic design, a federation maintains local architectures that conform to shared standards, interfaces and agreements at the points where they interact.

Characteristics

A federated architecture typically involves several operating units that build and manage their own systems, a small central function that sets common principles, reference models and standards, and agreed interoperability mechanisms. It is commonly adopted by large, decentralised organisations — multinational companies, government departments and military alliances — where full centralisation is impractical or politically impossible. The pattern appears in areas such as identity management, federated databases, federated search and federated learning.

Federated enterprise architecture

In federated enterprise architecture, a central architecture authority maintains the enterprise-wide framework and standards while business units or national components develop their own segment architectures aligned with them. Frameworks such as the NATO Architecture Framework and the US Department of Defense DoDAF employ federation concepts to combine the architectures of many programmes and nations into a coherent capability picture.
