The International Aikido Federation (IAF) is a world federation of aikido organisations. It was founded in 1976 and serves as the principal international body uniting national aikido federations that recognise the authority of the Aikikai Foundation and its Hombu Dojo in Tokyo, Japan. Its member organisations are the officially recognised aikido bodies of their respective countries.

History

Aikido, the Japanese martial art founded by Morihei Ueshiba, spread internationally after the Second World War through instructors sent abroad by the Aikikai. As national organisations formed, the need for an international coordinating body grew, and the IAF was established in 1976 at a founding congress in Tokyo. The federation maintains close ties with the Ueshiba family; the Doshu, the hereditary head of aikido, is closely associated with its leadership.

Activities

The IAF organises an international congress, generally held every four years in Japan in conjunction with a large demonstration and training event, and coordinates international seminars taught by senior Hombu Dojo instructors. It represents aikido within the wider world of sport: the federation is a member of the International World Games Association, and aikido has participated in the World Games as a demonstration sport. The IAF is also involved with SportAccord/GAISF activities and promotes aikido's non-competitive philosophy — unlike judo or karate, aikido traditionally has no tournaments, a principle the federation defends within sporting bodies.

Membership

Membership is limited to one organisation per country, which must be recognised by the Aikikai Foundation. Member federations span Europe, the Americas, Asia, Africa and Oceania, representing the bulk of the world's aikido practitioners training in the Aikikai tradition.
