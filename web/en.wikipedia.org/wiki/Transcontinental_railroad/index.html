Transcontinental railroad

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A transcontinental railroad is a contiguous network of railway trackage that crosses a continental land mass with terminals at different oceans or continental borders. The most famous example is the First Transcontinental Railroad in the United States, completed on 10 May 1869, which connected the existing eastern rail network at Council Bluffs/Omaha with the Pacific coast at the San Francisco Bay, reducing a journey of months to about a week.

First Transcontinental Railroad (United States)

Authorised by the Pacific Railway Acts of 1862 and 1864, construction was undertaken by two federally chartered companies racing toward each other: the Union Pacific, building westward from Omaha, Nebraska, largely with Irish immigrant labour, and the Central Pacific, building eastward from Sacramento, California, largely with Chinese immigrant labour, through the Sierra Nevada. The companies were paid in government bonds and land grants per mile of track laid, which encouraged speed — and, at times, hasty construction.

The lines met at Promontory Summit, Utah, where Leland Stanford drove the ceremonial Golden Spike (the "Last Spike") on 10 May 1869. The road transformed the settlement and economy of the American West, slashed freight and passenger journey times between the coasts, and contributed to the standardisation of time into time zones. It also accelerated the displacement of Native American peoples and the near-extermination of the buffalo herds on the Plains.

Other transcontinental railways

  • Canada: the Canadian Pacific Railway, completed in 1885 with its last spike at Craigellachie, British Columbia, fulfilled the promise that had brought British Columbia into Confederation.
  • Russia: the Trans-Siberian Railway (completed 1916; through services from 1904) links Moscow with Vladivostok over some 9,289 km, the longest railway line in the world.
  • Australia: an east–west transcontinental line was completed in 1917, joining Port Augusta and Kalgoorlie across the Nullarbor, though breaks of gauge long hindered through running.
  • Panama: the Panama Railroad (1855) was the first railway to cross the Americas, linking the Atlantic and Pacific across the isthmus.
