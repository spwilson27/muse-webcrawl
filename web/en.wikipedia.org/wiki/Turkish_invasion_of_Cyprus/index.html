Turkish invasion of Cyprus

The Turkish invasion of Cyprus began on 20 July 1974, when Turkey launched a military operation on the island of Cyprus in response to a coup d'état backed by the Greek military junta that had deposed President Makarios and aimed at union (enosis) with Greece. Turkey cited its rights as a guarantor power under the 1960 Treaty of Guarantee.

Course of the operation

Turkish forces landed near Kyrenia on the north coast and linked up with Turkish Cypriot enclaves around Nicosia. A UN-brokered ceasefire took hold on 22 July, and the Greek junta collapsed the following day. After peace talks in Geneva failed, Turkey launched a second offensive on 14 August 1974, advancing to occupy roughly the northern 37 percent of the island up to the Attila Line.

Consequences

The fighting and partition displaced an estimated 160,000–200,000 Greek Cypriots from the north and some 50,000 Turkish Cypriots from the south; more than a thousand people remained missing for decades. The UN buffer zone, patrolled since 1964 by UNFICYP, came to divide the island along the Green Line through Nicosia. The Turkish Cypriot unilateral declaration of independence in 1983 was declared invalid by the UN Security Council, and the Cyprus dispute remains unresolved despite many rounds of negotiations.
