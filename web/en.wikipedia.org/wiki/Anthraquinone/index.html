Anthraquinone

IUPAC nameAnthracene-9,10-dione
Chemical formulaC14H8O2
Molar mass208.21 g/mol
AppearanceYellow crystalline solid
Melting point286 °C

Anthraquinone, also called anthracenedione or dioxoanthracene, is an aromatic organic compound with the formula C14H8O2. It is the oxidised form of anthracene, with ketone groups on the central ring, and forms yellow needles that are nearly insoluble in water. Several isomers are possible; the common 9,10-anthraquinone is the parent of a large family of dyes and industrial chemicals.

Production

Anthraquinone is produced industrially by the oxidation of anthracene (obtained from coal tar) or by condensation routes such as the Friedel–Crafts reaction of benzene with phthalic anhydride followed by ring closure. Substituted anthraquinones are manufactured as intermediates for dyes.

Uses

The most important application is in the anthraquinone process for manufacturing hydrogen peroxide, in which a 2-alkylanthraquinone is hydrogenated and then oxidised by air, regenerating the quinone and releasing H2O2. Anthraquinone derivatives include alizarin, historically extracted from madder root and one of the first natural dyes to be synthesised, as well as anthraquinone vat dyes, laxatives of the senna and rhubarb type (anthraquinone glycosides), and the antimalarial and anticancer anthracenediones such as mitoxantrone. Anthraquinone is also used as a catalyst in alkaline wood pulping.
