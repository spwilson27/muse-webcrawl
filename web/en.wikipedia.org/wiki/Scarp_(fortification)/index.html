Scarp (fortification)

In military architecture, a scarp is the inner side of a ditch below the parapet of a fortification, or the steep slope of earth or masonry descending from the rampart into the ditch. The corresponding slope on the outer side of the ditch is called the counterscarp. Together they form the profile of the defensive ditch that an attacker must descend and then climb under fire.

Design

In medieval castles the scarp was the steepened base of a curtain wall, sometimes sloped outward (a batter or talus) to resist mining and to deflect projectiles so that objects dropped from above would bounce outward into attackers. In early modern bastion fortifications, masonry scarp walls lined the ditch, with galleries and caponiers built to sweep the ditch with fire. A detached scarp wall standing free in the ditch (a Carnot wall) allowed defenders to fire through loopholes while remaining protected from bombardment.

Scarping as a tactic

"Scarping" also describes artificially steepening a natural slope or hillside to hinder an enemy's advance, a technique used in hillforts and fieldworks.
