Royal Birkdale Golf Club

Royal Birkdale Golf Club is a golf course in Southport, Merseyside, England, and one of the clubs on the rota for The Open Championship. Founded in 1889 as Birkdale Golf Club, it moved to its present links among the sand dunes of the Lancashire coast in 1894 and received its "Royal" status in 1951. The course, noted for its towering dunes, deep bunkers and art deco clubhouse (opened in 1935), is consistently ranked among the finest links courses in the world.

History

The club hosted its first Open Championship in 1954, won by Peter Thomson, who also won there in 1965. In total Royal Birkdale has staged The Open ten times, most recently in 2017. Memorable editions include Arnold Palmer's victory in 1961, marked by a plaque where he played a famous recovery shot from a bush; Lee Trevino's win in 1971; Tom Watson's fifth Open title in 1983; Mark O'Meara's play-off victory in 1998; Pádraig Harrington's defence of the title in 2008; and Jordan Spieth's dramatic win in 2017, featuring his recovery from beside the practice ground on the 13th hole. In 1998, 17-year-old Justin Rose finished fourth as an amateur, holing his final shot from the rough.

Royal Birkdale has also hosted the Ryder Cup (1965 and 1969 — the latter ending in the famous tie and Jack Nicklaus's conceded putt to Tony Jacklin), the Women's British Open several times, the Walker Cup and the Curtis Cup.

The course

The layout threads through high dune ridges, with fairways set in valleys between them; unlike many links courses, few holes run directly along the sea. The course demands accurate driving to avoid deep revetted bunkers and punitive rough, and its difficulty increases sharply in the prevailing winds off the Irish Sea.
