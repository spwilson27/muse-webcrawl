'Allo 'Allo! is a British sitcom broadcast on BBC One from 1982 to 1992, created by David Croft and Jeremy Lloyd. A parody of wartime dramas — particularly the BBC series Secret Army — it is set in German-occupied France during the Second World War and follows café owner René Artois (Gorden Kaye), who is unwillingly entangled with the French Resistance, the Gestapo and a valuable painting, the Fallen Madonna with the Big Boobies, hidden inside a knockwurst sausage.

Style and catchphrases

The show's humour relied on running gags and exaggerated accents: French, German and English characters all spoke English with cod accents, with policeman Crabtree's mangled "Franglais" ("Good moaning") a particular favourite. Famous catchphrases include Michelle of the Resistance's "Listen very carefully, I shall say this only once", René's "You stupid woman!" to his wife Edith, and Herr Flick's clipped menace. René's narration to the camera recapped the increasingly farcical plot at the start of each episode.

Reception and legacy

The series ran for nine series and 85 episodes, including a long stage adaptation, and remains one of Britain's best-loved sitcoms, syndicated worldwide. A one-off special, The Return of 'Allo 'Allo!, aired in 2007.
