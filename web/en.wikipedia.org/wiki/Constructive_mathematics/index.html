Constructive mathematics

Constructive mathematics is mathematics done in accordance with the principle that one must provide an explicit construction of a mathematical object in order to prove that it exists. It rejects non-constructive methods such as proof by contradiction applied to existence claims and the unrestricted law of excluded middle.

Varieties

  • Intuitionism (L. E. J. Brouwer) — mathematics as mental constructions; rejects excluded middle for infinite sets.
  • Bishop-style constructivism (Errett Bishop, Foundations of Constructive Analysis, 1967) — rebuilds analysis constructively while remaining compatible with classical practice.
  • Russian constructivism (Andrey Markov Jr.) — emphasizes algorithms and computability.

Constructive logic

Constructive mathematics is naturally formalized in intuitionistic logic, in which a proof of A ∨ B must exhibit which disjunct holds, and a proof of ∃x P(x) must produce a witness. This gives constructive proofs computational content via the Curry–Howard correspondence.

Relation to classical mathematics

Many classical theorems have constructive versions; others (e.g. the intermediate value theorem in full generality) fail constructively. Constructive results are generally acceptable classically, but not conversely.
