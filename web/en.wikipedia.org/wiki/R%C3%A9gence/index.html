The Régence (French for "Regency") is the period in French history between 1715 and 1723, when King Louis XV was a minor and the kingdom was governed by his uncle, Philippe II, Duke of Orléans, as regent. The era followed the long reign of Louis XIV and preceded the king's personal rule.

Government

On taking power, the Regent moved the court from Versailles back to Paris and experimented with the polysynody, a system replacing individual ministers with governing councils that included members of the high aristocracy. The experiment was abandoned by 1718.

Finance and the System

The period is closely associated with the Scottish economist John Law, whose "System" introduced paper money and the Banque Générale in an attempt to reduce the royal debt. The scheme collapsed in the Mississippi bubble of 1720, causing a major financial crisis.

Culture

The Régence also lends its name to a transitional decorative style between the Baroque of Louis XIV and the Rococo of Louis XV, characterised by lighter, more playful ornament.
