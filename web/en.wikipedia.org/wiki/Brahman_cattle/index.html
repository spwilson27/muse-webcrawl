The Brahman is an American breed of zebuine beef cattle. It was developed in the United States from about 1885 from cattle originating in India, principally of the Gir, Guzerá (Kankrej), and Nelore breeds, imported at various times to the Gulf Coast states. The American Brahman Breeders Association was founded in 1924, and J. D. Hudgins of Texas is widely regarded as the father of the breed.

Characteristics

Brahmans are readily identified by the prominent hump over the shoulders and neck, long drooping ears, abundant loose skin (dewlap and sheath), and short, glossy hair. They are usually light grey to red or black in colour. Their sweat glands and physiology give them exceptional tolerance of heat and humidity, and they show strong resistance to insects and to many tick-borne diseases, thanks in part to a sebaceous secretion and the ability to shake pests from their loose skin.

Use

The Brahman is kept primarily for beef. Its greatest contribution, however, has been through crossbreeding: Brahman-cross cattle combine zebu hardiness with the beef qualities of European breeds. Famous derivatives include the Santa Gertrudis, Brangus (Brahman × Angus), Braford (Brahman × Hereford), Beefmaster, and Droughtmaster. Brahman genetics underpin much of the beef industry of the Gulf Coast of the United States, northern Australia, and tropical Latin America and Asia.
