Nord Stream 2

[Image: Nord Stream pipelines route]

Nord Stream 2 was a pair of offshore natural gas pipelines in Europe running under the Baltic Sea from Ust-Luga, Russia, to Lubmin, Germany, parallel to the earlier Nord Stream 1 pipeline. The 1,234-kilometre (767 mi) system was designed to carry 55 billion cubic metres of gas per year, doubling the capacity of the first Nord Stream line.

Construction and certification

The project was developed by Nord Stream 2 AG, a subsidiary of the Russian state company Gazprom, with financing from European firms including Engie, OMV, Royal Dutch Shell, Uniper, and Wintershall. Construction began in 2018 and the pipelines were completed in September 2021, despite United States sanctions that delayed pipelaying. On 22 February 2022, shortly before Russia's invasion of Ukraine, German Chancellor Olaf Scholz halted the certification process, and the pipeline never entered service.

2022 sabotage

On 26 September 2022, explosions damaged both Nord Stream 1 and Nord Stream 2 near the Danish island of Bornholm, causing large methane leaks. One of the two Nord Stream 2 strings remained intact but unused. Investigations by Denmark, Sweden, and Germany followed; Sweden and Denmark later closed their inquiries, while Germany's investigation continued, with reporting linking a Ukrainian-linked group to the attack — an attribution Ukraine denied.

Reactions

The project had long been opposed by the United States, Poland, Ukraine, and the Baltic states, which argued it increased European dependence on Russian gas and bypassed Ukraine's transit system. Supporters in Germany framed it as a commercial project securing energy supply.
