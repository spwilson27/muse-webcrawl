Quo Vadis (novel)

Quo Vadis: A Narrative of the Time of Nero
AuthorHenryk Sienkiewicz
LanguagePolish
GenreHistorical novel
Published1895–1896 (serial), 1896 (book)

Quo Vadis: A Narrative of the Time of Nero is a historical novel by the Polish author Henryk Sienkiewicz, published in 1895–1896. Set in Rome under the emperor Nero, it tells of the love between the Roman tribune Marcus Vinicius and Lygia, a Christian hostage, against the background of the Great Fire of Rome, Nero's persecution of the Christians, and the ministries of the apostles Peter and Paul.

The title comes from the legend in which Peter, fleeing Rome, meets Christ and asks "Quo vadis, Domine?" ("Where are you going, Lord?").

Plot

Vinicius, aided by his worldly uncle Petronius, Nero's arbiter of taste, pursues the Christian maiden Lygia. After the fire of Rome in AD 64, Nero blames the Christians, and Lygia is seized for the arena; she is saved when her giant bodyguard Ursus kills the aurochs to which she has been bound. Vinicius converts to Christianity. The apostle Peter, fleeing the city, encounters a vision of Christ on the Appian Way and returns to Rome to be crucified upside down.

Reception and adaptations

The novel was an international bestseller, translated into more than fifty languages, and contributed to Sienkiewicz receiving the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1905. It has been adapted many times for film, most famously the 1951 MGM epic Quo Vadis starring Robert Taylor and Deborah Kerr, and a 1913 Italian silent version among the earliest feature films.
