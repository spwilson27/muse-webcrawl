Convection oven

A countertop convection oven

A convection oven (also known as a fan-assisted oven or simply a fan oven) is an oven that has fans to circulate air around food, which gives a more even heat distribution and faster cooking than a conventional oven relying on natural convection. Because moving air accelerates heat transfer to the food surface, convection ovens cook at lower temperatures and in shorter times; a common rule of thumb reduces the set temperature by about 25 °F (14 °C) relative to a conventional recipe.

Operation

In a conventional oven, a layer of cooler air insulates the surface of the food. The fan in a convection oven continually strips this boundary layer away, producing crisper skins, more even browning, and reduced hot spots, allowing several trays to be baked at once with more uniform results. Fans may simply circulate air heated by the oven's elements (mechanical convection) or have their own heating element ("true convection").

History

Fan-assisted ovens appeared in commercial kitchens in the early twentieth century; an early design was the Maxson Whirlwind Oven of 1945, developed for heating frozen meals on aircraft. Domestic convection ovens became common from the 1970s onward, and countertop models, including convection toaster ovens and air fryers — essentially small, high-powered convection ovens — became popular in the 2010s.
