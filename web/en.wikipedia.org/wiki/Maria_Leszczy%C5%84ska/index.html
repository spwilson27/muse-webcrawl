Maria Karolina Zofia Felicja Leszczyńska (1703–1768) was Queen of France as the wife of King Louis XV from their marriage in 1725 until her death. The daughter of the deposed Polish king Stanisław Leszczyński, she is the longest-serving queen consort of France.

Marriage

Chosen from a list of candidates because her father's Catholic faith and lack of political entanglements suited French needs, Maria married Louis XV by proxy in August 1725 and in person at Fontainebleau in September. The couple had ten children, including the Dauphin Louis and Mesdames, the king's daughters.

Life at court

Pious, cultured and charitable, Maria maintained a Polish household at Versailles, patronised artists and musicians, and popularised Polish customs — her name is linked to dishes such as baba au rhum adapted at her father's court in Lorraine. As Louis XV took a series of famous mistresses, she withdrew into devotion and family life but remained personally respected.

Death

She died at Versailles in 1768, one day after her 65th birthday, and was buried at Saint-Denis; her heart was placed at Nancy beside her parents.
