Hue

Hue
DefinitionThe attribute of a colour by which it is identified as red, green, blue, etc.
Measured asAn angle around the vertical axis of a colour solid (0–360°)
Related attributesSaturation (chroma) and lightness (value/brightness)

Hue is one of the main properties of a color, defined technically (in the CIECAM02 model) as "the degree to which a stimulus can be described as similar to or different from stimuli that are described as red, green, blue, and yellow". It is the attribute that distinguishes the colour families of the spectrum—red from orange, blue from violet—apart from how light or dark (lightness) or how vivid or muted (saturation) the colour is.

Quantifying hue

In colour spaces designed for selecting colours, hue is represented as an angle around a central axis: in the HSL and HSV (HSB) models, red is at 0°, green at 120° and blue at 240°, running through yellow, cyan and magenta between them. The Munsell system divides the hue circle into ten named hue families (Red, Yellow-Red, Yellow, and so on), each subdivided into ten steps. In the CIELAB colour space, hue is expressed as a hue angle computed from the a* (green–red) and b* (blue–yellow) coordinates. Computing a hue from RGB values requires converting the amounts of the red, green and blue primaries into an angular position on this circle.

Hue and wavelength

For monochromatic light, hue correlates with dominant wavelength across the visible spectrum, from violet at about 380 nm through blue, green and yellow to red at about 700 nm; but the relationship is not one-to-one. Magenta and other purples are non-spectral—they arise only from mixtures of red and blue light. Moreover, perceived hue shifts somewhat with luminance (the Bezold–Brücke effect) and with saturation (the Abney effect), which precise colour models take into account.

Usage

Hue is the property meant in ordinary language by "what colour is it?" Painters organise pigments by hue on the colour wheel and exploit hue contrasts and harmonies; digital design tools expose hue sliders (the H in HSL/HSV pickers) for exactly this purpose. Some languages lack separate words for certain hue distinctions (such as blue vs. green), which has been a longstanding topic in research on colour naming and perception.
