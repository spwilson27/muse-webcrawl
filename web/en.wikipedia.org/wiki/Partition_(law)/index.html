Partition (law)

Partition is a term used in the law of real property to describe an act, by a court order or otherwise, to divide up a concurrent estate into separate portions representing the proportionate interests of the owners of the property. It is sometimes described as a forced sale. The legal action to achieve this division is called an action of partition.

Concurrent estates

Partition applies where two or more persons hold land concurrently, as joint tenants or tenants in common. Any co-owner generally has the right to seek partition, ending the shared ownership. Historically at common law a joint tenant could compel partition while the rights of tenants in common were more limited; statutes in most jurisdictions now allow any co-owner to bring an action.

Types

Partition in kind (also called actual partition) physically divides the property into parcels corresponding to each owner's share, and is generally preferred where it can be done fairly and without great prejudice. Partition by sale orders the property sold and the proceeds divided, and is used where physical division is impracticable or would cause substantial injury — for example, a single house.

Procedure

A partition action begins with a complaint filed by a co-owner. The court determines the parties' interests, and may appoint commissioners or referees to divide the land or conduct a sale. Proceeds and costs are distributed proportionately, and courts may adjust for unequal contributions, improvements, or occupation by one party. Voluntary partition by agreement of all co-owners achieves the same result without litigation.

Related concepts

The term is distinguished from partition of a state or territory in public international law, and from partitioning of personal property, though analogous rules exist for jointly owned chattels in some jurisdictions.
