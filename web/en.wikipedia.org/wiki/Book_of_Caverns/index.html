Book of Caverns

Book of Caverns
TypeAncient Egyptian funerary text (Underworld book)
PeriodNew Kingdom, 19th–20th Dynasties (c. 13th–12th century BCE)
Earliest copyOsireion at Abydos (reign of Seti I)
SubjectRa's journey through the Duat; punishment of the damned; union with Osiris

The Book of Caverns is an important ancient Egyptian funerary text of the New Kingdom. Like the other underworld books, it was inscribed inside royal tombs for the benefit of the deceased. It describes the journey of the sun god Ra through the six caverns (or divisions) of the underworld, the Duat, focusing especially on the interaction between Ra and the gods and inhabitants of each region.

Content

The text is divided into six caverns, arranged in a tabular layout of text and image unlike the hour-by-hour structure of the Amduat. Themes include the destruction of the enemies of the gods in the later caverns — depicted as bound, decapitated figures in fiery pits guarded by goddesses — and the climactic union of Ra with Osiris, the two represented together in a single oval coffin-like form (sarcophagus imagery), from which the sun god is reborn.

Attestations

The earliest known version is in the Osireion at Abydos, from the reign of Seti I. Nearly complete versions appear in the tomb of Ramesses VI (KV9) in the Valley of the Kings — where it was carved opposite the Book of Gates — and in the tombs of Ramesses VII and Ramesses IX, with excerpts in other Ramesside tombs and on later papyri and sarcophagi.
