Louis Joseph, Duke of Vendôme

Died11 June 1712, Vinaròs, Spain
AllegianceKingdom of France

Louis Joseph de Bourbon, Duke of Vendôme (1654–1712), often called simply Vendôme, was a French general and Marshal of France, great-grandson of Henry IV through the legitimised Vendôme line. He was one of Louis XIV's most capable and aggressive commanders.

Wars of Louis XIV

Vendôme distinguished himself in the Nine Years' War, defeating the Allies at Calcinato and serving in Italy and the Low Countries. In the War of the Spanish Succession he campaigned in Italy against Prince Eugene of Savoy, winning at Cassano (1705) and drawing at Luzzara, before being transferred to Flanders, where French fortunes collapsed at Oudenarde (1708) amid his quarrels with the Duke of Burgundy.

Spain and legacy

Sent to Spain in 1710, Vendôme restored the Bourbon cause of Philip V, retaking Madrid and winning the battles of Brihuega and Villaviciosa, which secured the Spanish throne for Philip. He died suddenly at Vinaròs in 1712, reportedly of food poisoning from a meal of fish, and was buried at El Escorial. Voltaire praised him as a commander equal to any of his age.
