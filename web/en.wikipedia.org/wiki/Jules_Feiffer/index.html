Jules Ralph Feiffer (1929–2025) was an American cartoonist, playwright, screenwriter and children's book author, considered one of the most influential satirists of the 20th century.

Cartooning career

After assisting Will Eisner on The Spirit, Feiffer began his long-running strip Feiffer (originally Sick Sick Sick) in The Village Voice in 1956. His loose, sketchy drawings and anxious, self-analyzing characters satirized Cold War politics, relationships and liberal pieties for more than four decades. He won the Pulitzer Prize for editorial cartooning in 1986.

Film, theater and books

Feiffer wrote the animated short Munro, which won the Academy Award in 1961, and the screenplays for Carnal Knowledge and Popeye. His plays include Little Murders. For children he wrote The Phantom Tollbooth illustrations — providing the beloved drawings for Norton Juster's classic — and authored picture books such as Bark, George. In his nineties he published graphic novels for young readers.
