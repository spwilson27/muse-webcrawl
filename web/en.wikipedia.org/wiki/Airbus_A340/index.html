Airbus A340

The Airbus A340 is a long-range, wide-body passenger airliner developed and produced by Airbus. It was launched in 1987 alongside the twin-engine A330, sharing its fuselage and wing design but using four engines. The A340 first flew on 25 October 1991 and entered service with Lufthansa and Air France in March 1993.

Variants

The initial A340-200 and longer A340-300 were powered by four CFM56 engines. In the late 1990s Airbus launched the stretched A340-600 and the ultra-long-range A340-500, powered by Rolls-Royce Trent 500 engines; the -500 could fly routes such as Singapore–Newark, for years the world's longest scheduled flight. A prominent feature distinguishing the later variants is a four-wheel main bogie added to handle higher weights.

End of production

The four-engine layout, attractive in the era of ETOPS restrictions, became a disadvantage as fuel prices rose and efficient large twins such as the Boeing 777 gained range. Airbus announced the end of A340 production in November 2011 after 380 aircraft; the type was succeeded in the Airbus lineup by the A350 XWB. Many A340s remain in service with airlines and as VIP and government transports.
