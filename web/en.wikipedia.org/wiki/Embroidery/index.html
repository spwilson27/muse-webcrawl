Embroidery

Embroidery is the craft of decorating fabric or other materials using a needle to apply thread or yarn. Embroidery may also incorporate other materials such as pearls, beads, quills, and sequins. In modern days, embroidery is usually seen on clothing, hats, coats, blankets, dress shirts, denim, stockings, and golf shirts.

History

Embroidery has been practiced worldwide for thousands of years. Surviving examples include elaborately decorated garments from ancient Egypt, Persia, India, and China. In medieval Europe, embroidery was a high-status art; the Bayeux Tapestry, despite its name, is a famous 11th-century work of needlework embroidery depicting the Norman conquest of England. English ecclesiastical embroidery, known as opus anglicanum, was prized across Europe.

Techniques

Common stitches include the running stitch, backstitch, satin stitch, French knot, and cross-stitch. Major traditions include crewel work, goldwork, whitework, Chinese embroidery (such as Suzhou embroidery), Indian zardozi, and Japanese sashiko.

Machine embroidery

The development of sewing and embroidery machines in the 19th century, and computer-controlled embroidery machines in the 20th, made mass-produced embroidered decoration routine in the garment industry.
