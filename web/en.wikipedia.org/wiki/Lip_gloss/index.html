Lip gloss

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lip gloss is a cosmetic used primarily to give lips a glossy lustre, and sometimes to add a subtle colour. It is distributed as a liquid or a soft solid and generally has a shorter wear time than lipstick. Lip gloss ranges from fully transparent — used over lipstick or alone for a natural shine — to opaque and brightly coloured, and finishes may be frosted, glittery, glassy or metallic.

History

Lip gloss is credited to Max Factor, who created it in 1930 for the film industry: actresses' lips needed to shine on black-and-white film. The first commercially available gloss, Max Factor's X-Rated, was released to the public in 1932. Roll-on and flavoured glosses made the product a teenage staple in the 1970s (Bonne Bell's Lip Smackers, introduced in 1973, were the first flavoured lip glosses), and the sheer, glossy look defined much of 1990s and 2000s beauty fashion before matte liquid lipsticks surged in the 2010s; gloss returned to popularity in the 2020s.

Composition and types

Glosses are typically oil-based (castor oil, lanolin, mineral oil or synthetic emollients) with waxes and film-formers controlling viscosity, plus pigments, flavour and sometimes "plumping" agents such as menthol, ginger or capsaicin derivatives that mildly irritate the lips to make them appear fuller. They are applied with a doe-foot wand, rollerball, squeeze tube or fingertip. Unlike lipstick, gloss offers little coverage and wears off quickly when eating or drinking.
