Emperor Keikō (景行天皇, Keikō-tennō) was the 12th legendary Emperor of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession. His reign is conventionally dated from AD 71 to 130. Like other early emperors, his existence and biography rest on the semi-legendary accounts of the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki chronicles, and historians regard details of his life as largely legendary.

Legendary narrative

Keikō is best known as the father of Prince Yamato Takeru, one of the great heroes of Japanese legend. According to the chronicles, the emperor twice sent his son on campaigns to subdue rebellious peoples—the Kumaso of Kyushu and the Emishi of the east—fearing the prince's violent temperament. Yamato Takeru died on his return journey, and the grieving emperor is said to have mourned him deeply.

Keikō is also credited with expanding imperial administration, appointing provincial governors and undertaking tours of the eastern provinces. His mausoleum (misasagi) is traditionally identified at Shibutani Mukaiyama Kofun in Tenri, Nara Prefecture.
