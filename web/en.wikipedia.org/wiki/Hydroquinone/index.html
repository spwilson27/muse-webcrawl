Hydroquinone

Hydroquinone, also known as benzene-1,4-diol or quinol, is an aromatic organic compound with the formula C6H4(OH)2. It is a white crystalline solid consisting of a benzene ring bearing two hydroxyl groups in the para (1,4) positions. It is the principal chemical in photographic developing agents and a well-known skin-depigmenting agent.

Properties and production

Hydroquinone is a reducing agent and is readily oxidized to p-benzoquinone, with which it forms the dark quinhydrone complex. Industrially it is produced mainly by the dialkylbenzene (Hock-type) process from p-diisopropylbenzene, or by aniline oxidation. It occurs naturally in some plants and in the defensive glands of bombardier beetles, which spray hot quinones at predators.

Uses

Hydroquinone has long been a standard photographic developer for black-and-white film and paper. In cosmetics, creams containing it inhibit melanin production and were widely used to lighten hyperpigmentation and age spots, though concerns about ochronosis and possible carcinogenicity have led to bans or restrictions on over-the-counter sales in the European Union and the United States. It is also used as a polymerization inhibitor (to stabilize monomers such as acrylics) and as an antioxidant.
