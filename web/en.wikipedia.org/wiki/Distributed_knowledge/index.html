Distributed knowledge

Distributed knowledge is knowledge that is held across multiple knowers — people, devices, or systems — such that no single knower holds all of it, yet the group as a whole can be said to know or be able to act on it. The concept appears in epistemology, cognitive science, organizational theory, and multi-agent systems.

In logic

In epistemic logic, distributed knowledge of a group is formalized with an operator that captures what the group would know if its members pooled all their information. Distributed knowledge is stronger than what any individual knows and can exceed even "everyone knows" and common knowledge formulations in some analyses.

In organizations

Organizational research examines how knowledge is distributed among employees, documents, and technologies, and how transactive memory systems — shared awareness of who knows what — allow teams to retrieve and combine specialised knowledge efficiently.

Related concepts

Distributed cognition studies how cognitive processes are spread across people and artifacts in an environment. In computer science, distributed systems coordinate knowledge and state across networked nodes that communicate by message passing.
