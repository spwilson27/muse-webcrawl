Wyoming County is a county in the U.S. state of New York, located in the western part of the state between the cities of Buffalo and Rochester. As of the 2020 census, the population was roughly 40,500. Its county seat is Warsaw. The county was created in 1841 from part of Genesee County, and it was named after a modified form of a Lenape (Delaware) Native American word meaning "broad bottom lands."

Geography

According to the U.S. Census Bureau, the county has a total area of about 596 square miles, of which roughly 593 square miles are land and about 3 square miles are water. The terrain is largely rolling hills and farmland. The county contains Letchworth State Park, often called the "Grand Canyon of the East," along the Genesee River gorge on its eastern edge, and Silver Lake in the town of Perry.

Communities

Towns in the county include Arcade, Attica, Bennington, Castile, Covington, Eagle, Gainesville, Genesee Falls, Java, Middlebury, Orangeville, Perry, Pike, Sheldon, Warsaw, and Wethersfield. Villages include Arcade, Attica, Castile, Gainesville, Perry, Pike, Silver Springs, Warsaw, and Wyoming. The village of Wyoming gives the county part of its early identity; the county was in fact named for the Wyoming Valley of Pennsylvania, itself named from the same Native word.

Economy and attractions

Agriculture, especially dairy farming, has long been central to the county's economy. Notable employers and attractions include the Attica Correctional Facility, the Wyoming County Fair held in Pike, and the Arcade and Attica Railroad, a heritage railroad operating excursion trains from Arcade.
