Sheep

Sheep (Ovis aries)
Kingdom: Animalia
Family: Bovidae
Genus: Ovis
Domesticated: c. 11,000–9,000 BCE

Sheep (Ovis aries) are domesticated ruminant mammals typically kept as livestock, raised for their wool, meat (lamb and mutton) and milk. One of the earliest animals domesticated for agriculture — probably from the wild mouflon in Mesopotamia — sheep number over one billion worldwide.

Characteristics

Sheep are relatively small ruminants with crimped hair called wool and often horns forming a lateral spiral. They are highly social flock animals with strong following behaviour, a fact reflected in many languages' metaphors. Selective breeding has produced more than 200 distinct breeds adapted to varied climates and purposes.

Sheep in culture

Sheep feature prominently in religion and folklore: Abrahamic traditions use the lamb sacrificially and symbolically, astrology has Aries the ram, and counting sheep is a folkloric cure for insomnia. Dolly the sheep (1996) was the first mammal cloned from an adult somatic cell.
