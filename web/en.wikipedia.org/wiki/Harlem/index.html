Harlem is a neighbourhood in Upper Manhattan, New York City, bounded roughly by the Hudson River to the west, the Harlem River to the north and east, and Central Park North (110th Street) and 155th Street at its edges. Founded by Dutch settlers in 1658 as Nieuw Haarlem, it is world-famous as a historic centre of African American culture, above all for the Harlem Renaissance of the 1920s.

History

Harlem began as a Dutch farming village and remained rural through the American Revolution and most of the nineteenth century. Speculative apartment and brownstone construction in the 1880s–1900s, aimed at white middle-class commuters, overshot the market; from about 1905, Black-led real-estate firms such as Philip Payton's Afro-American Realty Company opened the housing stock to African American tenants. The Great Migration brought hundreds of thousands of Black southerners to Harlem, and by the 1920s it was the largest Black community in the United States.

Harlem Renaissance and culture

In the 1920s Harlem produced an extraordinary flowering of literature (Langston Hughes, Zora Neale Hurston, Claude McKay), music (Duke Ellington at the Cotton Club, the Savoy Ballroom's Lindy Hop), theatre and visual art. The Apollo Theater on 125th Street (opened to Black audiences in 1934) became a proving ground for performers from Ella Fitzgerald to James Brown. Harlem was also a political capital: Marcus Garvey's UNIA, A. Philip Randolph's labour organising, Malcolm X's ministry at Temple No. 7, and Adam Clayton Powell Jr.'s congressional career centred there.

Later twentieth century and today

The Great Depression, restrictive covenants elsewhere, and disinvestment brought decades of economic hardship, worsened by the crack epidemic of the 1980s. From the 1990s reinvestment and gentrification transformed the neighbourhood, raising housing costs and shifting its demographics while prompting debate about the preservation of its Black cultural institutions — historic churches, the Studio Museum in Harlem, the Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture, and the National Black Theatre.
