Sliotar

A sliotar or sliothar (Irish: [ˈʃl̠ʲɪt̪ˠəɾˠ]; English: /ˈʃlɪtər/ SHLIT-ər) is a hard solid sphere slightly larger than a tennis ball, consisting of a cork core covered by two pieces of leather stitched together. Sometimes called a "hurling ball", it resembles a baseball with more pronounced stitching. It is used in the Gaelic sport of hurling.

Specifications

Under the rules of the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA), a standard sliotar used in senior hurling must:

  • have a diameter between 69 mm and 72 mm (not including the rim);
  • weigh between 110 and 120 grams;
  • have a circumference between 220 mm and 230 mm;
  • have a rim height between 2 mm and 2.8 mm and a rim width between 8.5 mm and 10.5 mm.

The ball is struck with a hurley (camán), and can travel at speeds exceeding 150 km/h (93 mph), making it one of the fastest balls in world sport. A point is scored by sending the sliotar between the opposition's goalposts.

History

Early sliotars were made of various materials, including hollow bronze, wood, and animal hair wrapped in leather or twine. Cork-core leather balls became standard in the twentieth century. Since 2020, the GAA has required all championship sliotars to carry an official stamp and a distinctive yellow colour, introduced to make the ball easier to follow for players and spectators and to standardise manufacture after concerns about inconsistent, lighter balls.

Variants

Smaller and softer sliotars are used in underage hurling and in the women's game of camogie. A similar ball is used in the hybrid game of shinty–hurling played between Ireland and Scotland.
