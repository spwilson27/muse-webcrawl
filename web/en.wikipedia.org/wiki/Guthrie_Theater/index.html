The Guthrie Theater, founded in 1963, is a center for theater performance, production, education, and professional training in Minneapolis, Minnesota. It is the result of the desire of Sir Tyrone Guthrie, Oliver Rea, and Peter Zeisler to create a resident acting company that would produce and perform the classics in an atmosphere removed from the commercial pressures of Broadway.

History

The theater opened on 7 May 1963 with a production of Hamlet, directed by Guthrie himself, in a building adjacent to the Walker Art Center designed by Ralph Rapson, featuring the famous thrust stage designed with Tanya Moiseiwitsch. Its opening is regarded as a landmark of the American regional theater movement. In 1982 the Guthrie won the Regional Theatre Tony Award.

Current building

In 2006 the Guthrie moved to a new $125 million facility on the Mississippi River riverfront in downtown Minneapolis, designed by French architect Jean Nouvel — his first completed building in North America. The blue-clad complex houses three stages: the 1,100-seat Wurtele Thrust Stage, the 700-seat McGuire Proscenium Stage, and the flexible 200-seat Dowling Studio. Its signature feature is the Endless Bridge, a 178-foot cantilevered lobby extending over the riverfront, and an amber-lit upper box office level known for its views of St. Anthony Falls.

Artistic leadership

Artistic directors have included Tyrone Guthrie (1963–1969), Liviu Ciulei, Garland Wright, Joe Dowling (1995–2015), and Joseph Haj, who became artistic director in 2015. The Guthrie's annual production of Charles Dickens' A Christmas Carol has been a Minneapolis holiday tradition since 1975.
