Communities, regions and language areas of Belgium

Belgium's federal structure combines three regions and three communities.

Belgium is a federal state comprising communities and regions. The country is divided into three communities — Flemish, French and German-speaking — and three regions — Flanders, Wallonia and the Brussels-Capital Region. It is also divided into four language areas, which determine the official language used by administrations.

Communities

The communities are organised around language and culture, with powers over education, culture, language use, and person-related matters such as health policy and assistance to individuals.

  • Flemish Community — Dutch-speaking, covering Flanders and Dutch-speaking institutions in Brussels.
  • French Community — French-speaking, covering most of Wallonia and French-speaking institutions in Brussels (styled the Wallonia-Brussels Federation).
  • German-speaking Community — covering nine municipalities in the east of Wallonia.

Regions

The regions are territorial entities with powers over the economy, employment, agriculture, water policy, housing, public works, energy, transport, the environment, town and country planning, and supervision of local authorities.

  • Flemish Region (Flanders) — five provinces in the north; its institutions are merged with those of the Flemish Community.
  • Walloon Region (Wallonia) — five provinces in the south.
  • Brussels-Capital Region — the bilingual capital area of nineteen municipalities.

Language areas

The four language areas — Dutch, French, German, and the bilingual area of Brussels-Capital — were fixed in legislation in 1962–63 and written into the Constitution. They determine the language of administration and public education in each municipality, with special "facility" arrangements in some border municipalities.
