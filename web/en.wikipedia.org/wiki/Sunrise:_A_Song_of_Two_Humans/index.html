Sunrise: A Song of Two Humans, also known as Sunrise, is a 1927 American silent romantic drama film directed by the German director F. W. Murnau in his American debut. Adapted by Carl Mayer from Hermann Sudermann's short story "Die Reise nach Tilsit" ("A Trip to Tilsit"), it stars George O'Brien and Janet Gaynor as a farmer and his wife whose marriage is threatened by a seductive woman from the city (Margaret Livingston).

Plot

A farmer, bored with his country life, falls for a vacationing city woman who persuades him to drown his wife and sell his farm so they can run away together. At the last moment he cannot go through with the murder; his terrified wife flees to the city, and he follows, begging forgiveness. Over the course of a day in the city — visiting a barber, a photographer's studio, a church wedding, a funfair and a dance hall — the couple fall in love again. Returning home by boat under moonlight, they are caught in a storm; believing his wife lost, the husband goes to attack the city woman, but his wife is found alive, and the couple are reconciled as the sun rises.

Reception and legacy

At the first Academy Awards in 1929, Sunrise won the one-time award for "Unique and Artistic Picture", while Gaynor won Best Actress and Rosher and Struss won for cinematography. Renowned for its fluid camerawork, elaborate sets, forced-perspective cityscapes and emotional universality — its intertitles are famously few — the film is routinely ranked among the greatest ever made; it appeared in the top ten of the Sight and Sound critics' poll in 2002 and 2012, and was selected for the United States National Film Registry in 1989.
