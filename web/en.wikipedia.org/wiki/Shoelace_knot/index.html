Shoelace knot

The shoelace knot, commonly called a bow, is the knot most people use to fasten shoelaces. It is a doubly slipped reef knot: a reef knot in which the second half knot is tied with loops ("bights") of the laces, so that pulling the free ends unties it instantly.

Tying

The usual method first ties a half knot, then forms a loop in each lace and ties a second half knot with the loops — the "bunny ears" method — or wraps one loop around the other (the "around the tree" method). If the two half knots are tied with the same handedness, the result is an insecure granny-knot variant whose bow sits crooked and tends to come undone; opposite-handed half knots yield a balanced reef form that lies flat across the shoe and holds far better.

Variants

Numerous secure variants exist, including the surgeon's shoelace knot (an extra wrap), the Ian knot — popularised by Ian Fieggen as a very fast two-loop method — and double knots used for children's shoes and sport. The physics of why shoelace knots fail under the repeated impacts of running was studied in a widely reported 2017 paper, which found that the combination of swinging and impact forces loosens the knot progressively.
