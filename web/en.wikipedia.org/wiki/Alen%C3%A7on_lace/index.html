Alençon lace

TypeNeedle lace
OriginAlençon, Normandy, France
Introduced17th century
RecognitionUNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (2010)

Alençon lace (French: point d'Alençon) is a fine needle lace produced in the town of Alençon in Normandy, France. Nicknamed the "Queen of lace", it is distinguished by its fine net ground, precisely worked designs, and raised outlining cordonnet.

History

Lacemaking in Alençon began in the 16th century, and the industry was formalised in 1665 when Jean-Baptiste Colbert established a royal workshop to rival Venetian needle lace. Alençon lace became a luxury fabric at the French court and remained highly prized through the 18th and 19th centuries, used for garments, veils, and church textiles.

Technique and heritage

The lace is made entirely by hand with a needle and linen thread in a long sequence of stages, historically divided among many specialised workers. In 2010 the craftsmanship of Alençon needle lace-making was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
