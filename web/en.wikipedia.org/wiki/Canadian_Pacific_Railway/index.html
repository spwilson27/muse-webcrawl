The Canadian Pacific Railway (French: Chemin de fer Canadien Pacifique), known simply as CP, was a Canadian Class I railway incorporated in 1881. The railway was owned by Canadian Pacific Railway Limited, which began operations as legal owner in a corporate restructuring in 2001. In 2023, CP merged with Kansas City Southern to form Canadian Pacific Kansas City (CPKC), the first single-line railway connecting Canada, the United States, and Mexico.

Construction

The railway was built between eastern Canada and British Columbia between 1881 and 1885, fulfilling a promise extended to British Columbia when it entered Confederation in 1871. Construction across the Canadian Shield and the Rocky Mountains employed thousands of workers, including many Chinese labourers. The last spike was driven by Donald Smith at Craigellachie, British Columbia, on 7 November 1885.

Operations and diversification

CP became a transportation conglomerate, operating hotels (including the Banff Springs Hotel and Château Frontenac), a steamship line, an airline, and telecommunications. Its red trains, beaver logo, and transcontinental passenger services such as The Canadian became national symbols. Passenger services were transferred to Via Rail in 1978.
