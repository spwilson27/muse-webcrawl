José de San Martín

José Francisco de San Martín y Matorras (25 February 1778 – 17 August 1850) was an Argentine general and statesman who led the southern and central parts of South America's struggle for independence from the Spanish Empire. Alongside Simón Bolívar, he is regarded as one of the great liberators of Spanish South America. He led the Crossing of the Andes in 1817, liberated Chile, and proclaimed the independence of Peru in 1821.

Early life

San Martín was born in Yapeyú, in present-day Argentina, to Spanish parents; his father was the town's lieutenant governor. The family returned to Spain in 1784, and he joined the Spanish army as a cadet at age eleven, fighting against France in the Pyrenees and against Britain and Portugal. During the Peninsular War he served under Spanish and British command and reached the rank of lieutenant colonel.

Return to South America

In 1812 San Martín returned to Buenos Aires and offered his sword to the independence movement. He raised the Regiment of Mounted Grenadiers, won the Battle of San Lorenzo (1813), and briefly commanded the Army of the North before becoming governor of Cuyo, where he organized the Army of the Andes in Mendoza.

Crossing of the Andes and liberation of Chile and Peru

In January 1817 San Martín led some 5,000 troops across the Andes in one of the great feats of military logistics, descending into Chile and defeating the royalists at Chacabuco (12 February 1817) and, decisively, at Maipú (5 April 1818), securing Chilean independence under Bernardo O'Higgins. In 1820 he sailed with a liberating expedition to Peru, landed at Paracas, and proclaimed Peruvian independence in Lima on 28 July 1821, serving as Protector of Peru.

Guayaquil, retirement, and death

After the Guayaquil Conference with Bolívar in July 1822, San Martín resigned his protectorate and withdrew from public life, frustrated by civil conflicts. He lived in exile in Europe, chiefly in France, and died at Boulogne-sur-Mer in 1850. His remains were repatriated to Buenos Aires Cathedral in 1880. He is the national hero of Argentina and is revered in Chile and Peru.
