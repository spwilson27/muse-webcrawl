Vehicle carrier

A vehicle carrier is a cargo ship designed to transport wheeled vehicles — principally cars, trucks and construction machinery — which are driven on and off the vessel under their own power (roll-on/roll-off, or ro-ro). The largest and most specialised type is the pure car carrier (PCC) and its larger variant the pure car and truck carrier (PCTC).

Design

Vehicle carriers have a boxy, slab-sided superstructure enclosing multiple vehicle decks — often 10 to 13 — connected by internal ramps. Vehicles are loaded through stern or side quarter ramps. A large PCTC can carry 6,000–8,000 car-equivalent units (CEU). Because the cargo is light relative to volume, these ships have high freeboard and are notably sensitive to wind.

Trade

The vehicle carrier fleet serves the global automotive trade, carrying new cars from manufacturing countries such as Japan, South Korea, China and Germany to markets worldwide. Major operators include Wallenius Wilhelmsen, EUKOR, "K" Line, MOL and NYK. High-profile casualties, such as the fires aboard Felicity Ace (2022) and Fremantle Highway (2023), drew attention to the fire risks of vehicles — including electric vehicles with lithium-ion batteries — carried in enclosed decks.
