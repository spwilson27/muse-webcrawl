Ropax

Ropax is a contraction of roll-on/roll-off passenger and describes a class of ferry in which the emphasis is on passenger accommodation rather than exclusively on freight. A ropax vessel carries both passengers (typically with services such as cabins, restaurants, lounges and entertainment) and wheeled cargo — cars, trucks and trailers — which are loaded and unloaded via ramps under their own power.

Characteristics

Ropax ferries are distinguished from pure ro-ro cargo ferries by their substantial passenger facilities and from cruise ships by their freight decks. They are common on short-sea routes in Europe, such as crossings of the Baltic Sea, the Irish Sea, the English Channel and the Mediterranean, as well as in Japan and other island regions. Typical ships carry from a few hundred to over two thousand passengers and have lane lengths of 1,500–3,000 metres for vehicles.

Operation

Because routes are usually short (a few hours to overnight), ropax vessels turn around quickly in port, with simultaneous loading of cars and freight through bow, stern or side ramps. Operators include companies such as Stena Line, DFDS, Tallink, Viking Line, Brittany Ferries and Grimaldi Lines. The term is also used in naval architecture and classification to denote the design standard applied to such mixed passenger–freight ships.
