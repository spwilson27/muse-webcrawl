Car carrier

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A car carrier, in shipping, is a cargo ship designed to transport large numbers of automobiles. Pure car carriers (PCC) and pure car and truck carriers (PCTC) are specialised roll-on/roll-off vessels whose box-like superstructures enclose many vehicle decks; cars are driven aboard via stern or side ramps and lashed in place for the voyage.

Design

Modern car carriers have capacities of up to roughly 8,000 car-equivalent units (CEU) spread across 10 or more decks, some of which are hoistable to accommodate taller vehicles such as trucks and buses. Their tall, slab-sided hulls give them a distinctive appearance and large windage. Loading and unloading is performed by specialised stevedores who drive each vehicle on and off, allowing a full cargo to be handled in hours.

Trade

Car carriers serve the global automotive trade, moving new vehicles from factories in Japan, South Korea, China, Europe, and North America to markets worldwide. Major operators include Wallenius Wilhelmsen, NYK Line, Mitsui O.S.K. Lines, K Line, and Höegh Autoliners. Demand fluctuates with global vehicle sales and, more recently, the surge in Chinese electric-vehicle exports.
