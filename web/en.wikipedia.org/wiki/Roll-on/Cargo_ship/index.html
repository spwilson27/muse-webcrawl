Cargo ship

A cargo ship (or freighter) is a merchant vessel that carries cargo, goods and materials from one port to another. Thousands of cargo carriers ply the world's seas and oceans each year, handling the bulk of international trade. Specialised types include container ships, bulk carriers, oil and chemical tankers, general cargo ships, refrigerated ships and roll-on/roll-off vessels.

Types

  • Barges — flat-bottomed vessels for rivers and canals.
  • Bulk carriers — carry unpackaged bulk cargo such as grain, coal, ore and cement in hold compartments.
  • Container ships — carry standardised intermodal containers; the largest exceed 24,000 TEU capacity.
  • Tankers — carry liquids such as crude oil, petroleum products, chemicals and liquefied gas.
  • General cargo ships — carry packaged goods and break bulk.
  • Roll-on/roll-off (ro-ro) ships — carry wheeled cargo such as cars and trucks that drive on and off.
  • Refrigerated ships (reefers) — carry perishable goods.

History

Merchant shipping dates to antiquity; the Phoenicians, the Hansa cog and the great clipper ships carried world trade for centuries. Steam power, steel hulls and the opening of the Suez and Panama canals industrialised freight in the 19th century. The revolution of containerisation, beginning with Malcom McLean's Ideal X voyage in 1956, standardised cargo handling and dramatically lowered the cost of shipping, enabling modern global supply chains.

Operation

Cargo ships are classified by size classes such as Handysize, Panamax, Capesize and Chinamax, defined by the ports and canals they can use. They operate as tramp ships (chartered per voyage) or liner services on fixed schedules. Most are powered by slow-speed diesel engines the size of a small house, and are flagged in open registries such as Panama, Liberia and the Marshall Islands. The industry is regulated internationally by the International Maritime Organization (IMO) under conventions including SOLAS and MARPOL.

Environmental issues

Shipping produces roughly 3% of global greenhouse-gas emissions; the IMO has adopted an emissions reduction strategy, and the sector faces rules on sulfur content, ballast water and energy efficiency. Piracy, particularly historically off Somalia, and accidents such as container losses and the 2021 Suez Canal blockage by the Ever Given illustrate the system's vulnerabilities.
