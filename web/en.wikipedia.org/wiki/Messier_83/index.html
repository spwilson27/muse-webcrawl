Messier 83 or M83, also known as the Southern Pinwheel Galaxy and NGC 5236, is a barred spiral galaxy approximately 15 million light-years away in the constellation Hydra. It is one of the closest and brightest barred spiral galaxies in the sky, and is visible with binoculars.

Discovery and observation

M83 was discovered by Nicolas-Louis de Lacaille on 23 February 1752 at the Cape of Good Hope, and was later included in Charles Messier's catalogue. Its southern location made it difficult for northern observers; Messier himself struggled to observe it from Paris.

Characteristics

Spanning roughly 55,000 light-years, M83 is a starburst galaxy with vigorous star formation along its spiral arms and in its central region, which contains a double nucleus. Six supernovae have been observed in M83, more than in almost any other galaxy. M83 is the brightest member of the M83 Group, one of the two subgroups of the Centaurus A/M83 Group.
