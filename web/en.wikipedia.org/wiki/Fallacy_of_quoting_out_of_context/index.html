Fallacy of quoting out of context

The practice of quoting out of context (sometimes called contextomy or quote mining) is an informal fallacy in which a passage is removed from its surrounding material in such a way as to distort its intended meaning. Arguments based on this fallacy typically take two forms: quoting selectively to misrepresent an author's position, or presenting an opponent's words in isolation to make them seem more extreme.

Usage

Quote mining is a well-known tactic in debates over evolution, where passages from Charles Darwin and later scientists — such as Darwin's remarks on the complexity of the eye — are cited without the surrounding text that resolves the apparent difficulty. The term "quote mining" was popularized by critics of creationist literature.

Related practices

Contextomy overlaps with cherry picking and the straw man fallacy, but differs in that the quoted words are genuine; the distortion lies in omission. Journalistic and academic norms require that quotations preserve the sense of the original, with omissions and alterations marked by ellipses and brackets.
