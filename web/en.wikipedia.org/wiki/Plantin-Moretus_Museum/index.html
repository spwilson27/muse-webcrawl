The Plantin-Moretus Museum (Dutch: Museum Plantin-Moretus) is a museum in Antwerp, Belgium, about the history of printing and the printers Christophe Plantin and Jan Moretus. It occupies the former residence and printing establishment of the Plantin Press, founded in the 16th century, on the Vrijdagmarkt (Friday Market) in Antwerp. In 2005 the museum became the first museum to be added to the UNESCO World Heritage List.

History

Christophe Plantin, a French-born bookbinder who settled in Antwerp, founded his printing office in 1555 and moved it to the house on the Vrijdagmarkt he named De Gulden Passer (The Golden Compasses). At its height in the 1570s, the Officina Plantiniana was the largest printing house in Europe, operating more than twenty presses and publishing works of scholarship, liturgy, cartography, and science, including the eight-volume Plantin Polyglot Bible (1568–1573), printed with the support of Philip II of Spain.

Plantin's son-in-law Jan Moretus succeeded him in 1589, and the Moretus family continued the business, increasingly as printers to the Catholic Church and city, until 1866. In 1876 the last owner, Edward Moretus, sold the house, its workshops, archives, and collections to the city of Antwerp and the Belgian state, and the museum opened to the public in 1877.

Collections

The museum preserves the two oldest surviving printing presses in the world (dating from around 1600), complete sets of punches, matrices, and type — including Garamond and Granjon faces — and the firm's extraordinarily intact business archive of ledgers, correspondence, and proof sheets, covering some 300 years of activity. The library holds around 25,000 old printed books, among them the Biblia Polyglotta, the oldest extant Dutch Bible printed in Antwerp, and a Gutenberg Bible leaf collection; the rooms are also decorated with family portraits, several by Peter Paul Rubens, who illustrated books for the firm and was a friend of Balthasar Moretus.

World Heritage

UNESCO inscribed the Plantin-Moretus House-Workshops-Museum Complex in 2005, judging it an outstanding testimony to European printing and publishing from the Renaissance onward, and the museum's archives were added to UNESCO's Memory of the World Register in 2001.
