Rocketdyne J-2

Rocketdyne J-2
Country: United States
Manufacturer: Rocketdyne
Propellants: LOX / liquid hydrogen
Thrust (vacuum): 1,033 kN (232,250 lbf)

The Rocketdyne J-2 was a liquid-fuel cryogenic rocket engine used on NASA's Saturn IB and Saturn V rockets during the Apollo programme. Burning liquid hydrogen with liquid oxygen, it produced about 1,033 kN (232,250 lbf) of thrust in vacuum and was remarkable for its time as a large, restartable hydrogen-fuelled upper-stage engine. It was manufactured by Rocketdyne in Canoga Park, California.

Use on Saturn rockets

A cluster of five J-2 engines powered the S-II second stage of the Saturn V, and a single J-2 powered the S-IVB third stage (also used as the second stage of the Saturn IB). The S-IVB's J-2 had to restart in Earth orbit to perform translunar injection, sending Apollo spacecraft toward the Moon; restart capability, achieved with a spark ignition system, was a key design requirement. On Apollo 13, the centre engine of the S-II shut down early due to pogo oscillations, but the remaining engines compensated. In total the J-2 flew on every Saturn V mission from Apollo 4 through Skylab and Apollo–Soyuz.

Design

The J-2 used a gas-generator cycle: a small fraction of propellants burned in two gas generators drove the fuel and oxidiser turbopumps, with the exhaust helping to cool and pressurise systems and providing roll control. The engine was gimballed for steering, had a specific impulse of about 421 seconds in vacuum, and weighed about 1,579 kg (3,480 lb). It was throttleable over a modest range and capable of multiple starts.

Legacy

The J-2's successor, the J-2X, was developed for the Ares rockets of the cancelled Constellation programme and considered for the Space Launch System's upper stage, which ultimately used the RL10 instead. The J-2 remains one of the largest hydrogen-burning engines flown and a landmark of 1960s rocket engineering.
