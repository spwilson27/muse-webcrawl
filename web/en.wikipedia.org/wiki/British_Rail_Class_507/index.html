The British Rail Class 507 is a class of three-car electric multiple unit built by British Rail Engineering Limited (BREL) at York between 1978 and 1980 for the Merseyrail network in Merseyside. Together with its near-identical sister, the Class 508, it has formed the backbone of Merseyrail's third-rail services for more than four decades.

History

The Class 507 was ordered as part of the 1970s modernisation of the Merseyrail network, which included the construction of the city-centre Loop and Link lines under Liverpool. The trains are based on the PEP prototype design of the early 1970s, sharing the aluminium body, sliding-pocket doors and 750 V DC third-rail equipment of the wider PEP family used in London and Glasgow. Thirty-three units were delivered, numbered 507001–507033.

The fleet was refurbished between 2002 and 2005, receiving new interiors, passenger information systems and the Merseyrail yellow livery's later variants. After more than 40 years in service, the class began to be replaced by the new Stadler-built Class 777 trains from 2023 onwards.

Operations

Class 507s have worked Merseyrail's Northern Line services to Southport, Ormskirk, Kirkby and Hunts Cross, and Wirral Line services via the Mersey Railway Tunnel to Chester, Ellesmere Port, West Kirby and New Brighton. Units are maintained at Birkenhead North and Kirkdale depots.
