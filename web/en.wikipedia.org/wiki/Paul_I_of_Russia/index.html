Paul I of Russia

Paul I (Russian: Павел Петрович; 1 October 1754 – 23 March 1801) was Emperor of Russia from 1796 until his assassination in 1801. The son of Peter III and Catherine the Great, he spent decades as heir apparent in a strained relationship with his mother, who kept him from power.

Reign

On succeeding Catherine, Paul reversed many of her policies, curbed the privileges of the nobility granted in her Charter, imposed strict military discipline and Prussian-style drill, and pursued erratic reforms motivated by chivalric ideals and resentment of his mother's reign. His most lasting act was the 1797 Pauline Laws on succession, establishing male primogeniture and a clear order of inheritance that governed the Russian throne until 1917. In foreign policy he joined the Second Coalition against revolutionary France, then broke with Britain, famously ordering a Cossack expedition toward British India.

Assassination

Paul's arbitrary rule, humiliations of officers and guards regiments, and feared purges provoked a conspiracy of nobles and officers. On the night of 23 March 1801 (11 March Old Style), conspirators entered his bedroom in the newly built St Michael's Castle in St Petersburg and murdered him after he refused to abdicate. He was succeeded by his son Alexander I, who had known of the plot to depose, though not to kill, his father.
