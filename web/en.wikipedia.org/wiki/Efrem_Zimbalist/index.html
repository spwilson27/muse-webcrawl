Efrem Zimbalist

Born: April 30, 1889, Rostov-on-Don, Russian Empire

Died: February 22, 1985 (aged 95), Reno, Nevada, U.S.

Occupation: Violinist, composer, conductor, teacher

Spouses: Alma Gluck (m. 1914; died 1938); Mary Louise Curtis Bok (m. 1943)

Efrem Zimbalist (April 30, 1889 – February 22, 1985) was a Russian-born American concert violinist, composer, conductor and director of the Curtis Institute of Music in Philadelphia.

Early life

Zimbalist was born in Rostov-on-Don, the son of a conductor who was his first teacher. At the age of twelve he entered the Saint Petersburg Conservatory, where he studied with Leopold Auer and won the gold medal and the Rubinstein Prize on his graduation in 1907. He made his Berlin debut the same year and his American debut with the Boston Symphony Orchestra in 1911, after which the United States became his home.

Career

Zimbalist enjoyed a long international career as a soloist, performing with the world's major orchestras and giving notable chamber music recitals, often with his first wife, the soprano Alma Gluck. He joined the faculty of the Curtis Institute of Music at its founding in 1924 and served as its director from 1941 to 1968. Though he formally retired from the concert stage in 1949, he returned to perform on several occasions, including the 1951 premiere of Gian Carlo Menotti's Violin Concerto, written for him.

Compositions

His compositions include a violin concerto, the "American Rhapsody", an opera, Landara (1956), chamber works and numerous pieces for violin, among them the Sarasateana suite based on themes from Bizet's Carmen.

Personal life

Zimbalist was the father of the actor Efrem Zimbalist Jr. He made his home in Reno, Nevada, in his later years, where he died in 1985 at the age of 95.
