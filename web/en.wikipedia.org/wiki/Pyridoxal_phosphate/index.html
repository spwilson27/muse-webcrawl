RoleCoenzyme (active form of vitamin B6)

Pyridoxal phosphate (PLP), also called pyridoxal 5′-phosphate or P5P, is a coenzyme and the active form of vitamin B6. It is involved in a remarkable variety of reactions—more than 140 enzymatic activities are catalogued as PLP-dependent—particularly in amino acid metabolism.

Chemical role

PLP works through its reactive aldehyde group, which forms a Schiff base (an "internal aldimine") with a lysine residue of the enzyme, and then with the amino group of an incoming amino-acid substrate (an "external aldimine"). The electron-withdrawing pyridinium ring acts as an electron sink, stabilising carbanionic intermediates and thereby enabling transamination, decarboxylation, racemisation, and elimination or replacement reactions at the α-carbon of amino acids.

Enzymes and pathways

PLP-dependent enzymes include the aminotransferases (such as ALT and AST, familiar from clinical blood tests), amino-acid decarboxylases that produce neurotransmitters—glutamate decarboxylase makes GABA, and aromatic L-amino-acid decarboxylase makes dopamine and serotonin—and enzymes of haem synthesis (ALA synthase), glycogen breakdown (glycogen phosphorylase), and one-carbon and sulphur metabolism.

Clinical relevance

PLP status reflects vitamin B6 nutrition. The drug isoniazid can inactivate PLP and cause peripheral neuropathy, which is why pyridoxine is co-prescribed. Certain rare epilepsies of infancy, such as pyridoxine-dependent epilepsy, arise from defects in PLP metabolism and respond to vitamin B6 therapy.
