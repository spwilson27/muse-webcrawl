Yaremche
Ukrainian: Яремче
Country: Ukraine
Oblast: Ivano-Frankivsk
Population: about 8,000
Known as: Resort town in the Carpathians

Yaremche (Ukrainian: Яремче) is a city and resort town in Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast, western Ukraine, situated in the valley of the Prut River in the Ukrainian Carpathian Mountains. Often called the "pearl of the Carpathians," it is one of Ukraine's most popular tourist destinations, serving as a gateway to the Hoverla massif and to the Bukovel ski resort area.

Geography and tourism

The town lies at an elevation of about 525–585 metres, surrounded by forested mountains within and near Carpathian National Nature Park. Its best-known natural sight is the Probiy waterfall on the Prut River in the town center, crossed by a bridge beside a large Hutsul souvenir market. The area is a center of Hutsul culture, with wooden architecture, crafts, and cuisine; nearby attractions include the Dovbush rocks and trails associated with the legendary outlaw Oleksa Dovbush.

History

Yaremche was founded in 1787 and developed as a fashionable resort in the late 19th century under Austria-Hungary, when the railway arrived and villas were built for visitors from Vienna and Lviv. It passed through Polish, Soviet, and (since 1991) Ukrainian rule. The town's administrative area also encompasses nearby villages such as Mykulychyn and Vorokhta along the Prut valley.
