Imperial Household Agency

Formed1949 (as the Imperial Household Agency)
HeadquartersChiyoda, Tokyo, Japan
ParentCabinet Office
ChiefGrand Steward

The Imperial Household Agency (Japanese: 宮内庁, Kunai-chō) is a government agency of Japan responsible for state matters concerning the Imperial House, including the Emperor's official duties, ceremonies, imperial residences, treasures and the health and household affairs of the imperial family. It is an external bureau of the Cabinet Office and is headed by a Grand Steward.

Functions

The agency manages the Imperial Palace in Tokyo and other residences, organises court ceremonies and state banquets attended by the Emperor, maintains the Imperial Household Archives, and administers imperial tombs and cultural properties such as the Shōsōin treasure house in Nara. It also regulates access by the press and public to the imperial family, a role that has sometimes drawn criticism for conservatism.

History

The agency traces its origins to the Imperial Household Ministry of the Meiji period. Under the post-war constitution its status was reduced from a ministry to an agency within the Prime Minister's Office, reflecting the Emperor's new role as symbol of the state.
