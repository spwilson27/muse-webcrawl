Robert C. Pike (born 1956) is a Canadian programmer and author. He is best known for his work on the Go programming language while at Google and his work at Bell Labs, where he was a member of the Unix team and was involved in the creation of the Plan 9 and Inferno operating systems, as well as the programming language Limbo. He also co-developed the Blit graphical terminal.

Career

Pike studied at the University of Toronto and Caltech. At Bell Labs, he worked with Ken Thompson and others on Unix, and he and Thompson designed the UTF-8 character encoding in 1992, now the dominant encoding of the World Wide Web. He wrote the Sam and Acme text editors for Plan 9, and co-authored, with Brian Kernighan, the books The Unix Programming Environment (1984) and The Practice of Programming (1999).

In 2002 Pike joined Google, where he worked on search systems and, from 2007, co-created the Go programming language with Robert Griesemer and Ken Thompson; Go was publicly released in 2009 and version 1.0 followed in 2012. He has also written influential essays and talks on software design, including "Notes on Programming in C" and a series of rules for programming often quoted alongside Kernighan's.

Personal life

Pike is married to Renée French, an author and illustrator who designed the Go gopher mascot. He holds numerous patents and is a member of the US National Academy of Engineering.
