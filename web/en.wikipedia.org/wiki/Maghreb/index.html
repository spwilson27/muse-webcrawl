Maghreb

The Maghreb is the western part of North Africa and the Arab world, comprising Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia and usually Libya (and sometimes Mauritania). The name comes from Arabic for 'the west' — the place where the sun sets. The Atlas Mountains run through it, separating the fertile Mediterranean coast from the Sahara.

The Maghreb was Berber (Amazigh) land, successively ruled by Phoenicians (Carthage), Romans, Vandals, Byzantines and Arab dynasties such as the Almoravids and Almohads, then absorbed into the Ottoman sphere (except Morocco) and colonised by France, Spain and Italy in the 19th-20th centuries. Independence came in the 1950s-60s; the region began the Arab Spring in Tunisia in 2011. The Maghreb Union (1989) has been largely frozen by the Western Sahara dispute between Morocco and Algeria.
