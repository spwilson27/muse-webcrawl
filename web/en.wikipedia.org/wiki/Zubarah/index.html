Zubarah

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Al Zubarah (Arabic: الزبارة), also known as Zubarah, is a ruined and abandoned town on the northwestern coast of Qatar, in the municipality of Al Shamal. Founded in the mid-18th century by the Al Khalifa, the Utub tribe of Kuwaiti merchants, it grew into one of the most important pearling and trading ports in the Persian Gulf before being largely destroyed in 1811 and finally abandoned in the early 20th century.

History

At its peak, Zubarah had an estimated population of 6,000–9,000 people. The town was defended by walls and a fort, Al Zubarah Fort, built in 1938 under Sheikh Abdullah bin Jassim Al Thani, which still stands near the archaeological site. The town's prosperity rested on the pearl trade; its merchants maintained links across the Gulf and to India.

World Heritage Site

In 2013, the Al Zubarah Archaeological Site was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, Qatar's first. Excavations have revealed the layout of houses, mosques, markets, and palatial residences, along with evidence of date presses, fish traps, and imported ceramics testifying to its far-reaching trade networks.
