United States Fifth Fleet

The United States Fifth Fleet is a numbered fleet of the United States Navy responsible for naval forces in the Middle East and surrounding waters. Its headquarters are at Naval Support Activity Bahrain in Manama. The Fifth Fleet commander also serves as commander of United States Naval Forces Central Command (NAVCENT) and of the multinational Combined Maritime Forces.

Area of responsibility

The fleet's area includes the Persian Gulf, Gulf of Oman, Arabian Sea, Red Sea, Gulf of Aden and parts of the Indian Ocean—approximately 2.5 million square miles of water containing strategic shipping routes and energy chokepoints such as the Strait of Hormuz, Suez Canal and Bab el-Mandeb. Forces assigned may include carrier strike groups, amphibious ships, patrol craft, mine warfare vessels, submarines, logistics ships and maritime patrol aircraft, varying with operational requirements.

History

A Fifth Fleet was first established in 1944 for the Central Pacific drive of the Second World War, alternating command identity with the Third Fleet as Admirals Raymond Spruance and William Halsey rotated. That organization was abolished in 1947. The modern Fifth Fleet was re-established on 1 July 1995 to provide a permanent forward naval headquarters in the Gulf after the Persian Gulf War. Its forces supported operations in Afghanistan and Iraq, counter-piracy, maritime security and deterrence missions.
