Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse

The Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse are figures described in the sixth chapter of the Book of Revelation, the last book of the New Testament. Summoned as the first four of the seven seals are opened by the Lamb, the horsemen ride out on white, red, black and pale horses and are traditionally identified as Conquest (or Pestilence), War, Famine and Death.

The four riders

The first horseman, on a white horse, carries a bow and wears a crown, riding forth "conquering and to conquer"; he has been interpreted variously as Christ, the Antichrist, or Pestilence. The second, on a red horse, wields a great sword and takes peace from the earth — War. The third, on a black horse, carries a pair of scales and announces inflated prices for wheat and barley — Famine. The fourth, on a pale (greenish) horse, is named Death, and Hades follows after him; together they are given power over a fourth of the earth.

In art and culture

The horsemen have been depicted by artists from Albrecht Dürer, whose 1498 woodcut is among the most famous images of the Apocalypse, to Viktor Vasnetsov. The phrase "Four Horsemen" has passed into general usage for any quartet of calamities or formidable figures — applied, for example, to the exponents of the New Atheism and to the wrestlers of the Four Horsemen stable.
