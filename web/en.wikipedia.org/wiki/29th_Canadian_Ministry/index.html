The 29th Canadian Ministry was the federal cabinet of Canada formed after the 2015 federal election and led by Prime Minister Justin Trudeau of the Liberal Party. The ministry was sworn in on 4 November 2015, following the Liberals' majority victory in the election of 19 October 2015, which ended nearly a decade of Conservative government under Stephen Harper.

History

Trudeau's first cabinet was noted for being the first gender-balanced federal cabinet in Canadian history, with 15 women and 15 men besides the prime minister himself. The ministry governed with a majority through the 42nd Parliament (2015–2019), then as a minority government following the 2019 and 2021 elections, the latter period sustained after 2022 by a confidence-and-supply agreement with the New Democratic Party.

Major initiatives of the ministry included the legalization of recreational cannabis (2018), the Canada Child Benefit, the carbon-pricing framework, the response to the COVID-19 pandemic, and the renegotiation of NAFTA into the Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement (CUSMA).

End of the ministry

Trudeau announced his intention to resign as Liberal leader and prime minister on 6 January 2025. He was succeeded by Mark Carney, whose 30th Canadian Ministry was sworn in on 14 March 2025.
