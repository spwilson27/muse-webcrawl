The Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macao Greater Bay Area (GBA), commonly called the Greater Bay Area, is a megalopolis and economic region in southern China comprising the nine prefecture-level cities of the Pearl River Delta in Guangdong province — Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Foshan, Huizhou, Dongguan, Zhongshan, Jiangmen and Zhaoqing — together with the special administrative regions of Hong Kong and Macao. With a population of more than 86 million and a GDP comparable to that of a G20 economy (around US$2 trillion in the early 2020s), it is one of the largest and wealthiest bay areas in the world.

Development

The concept was championed by the Chinese central government as part of a strategy to integrate Hong Kong and Macao more closely with the mainland, deepen reform and opening-up, and create a world-class city cluster rivalling the San Francisco Bay Area and Greater Tokyo. A Framework Agreement was signed in Hong Kong on 1 July 2017, and the Outline Development Plan was published in February 2019.

The plan designates Hong Kong as an international financial, shipping and trade centre; Macao as a world tourism and leisure centre and a platform for trade with Portuguese-speaking countries; Guangzhou as a national core city and transport hub; and Shenzhen as a special economic zone, innovation centre and technology hub. It envisages a "one-hour living circle" linking the eleven cities.

Infrastructure

Major links include the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge (55 km, opened 2018), the world's longest sea crossing; the Guangzhou–Shenzhen–Hong Kong Express Rail Link (opened 2018); the Shenzhen–Zhongshan Link (opened 2024); and an expanding intercity metro and expressway network. Cooperation zones such as Hengqin (Zhuhai–Macao), Qianhai (Shenzhen–Hong Kong), Nansha (Guangzhou) and the Hong Kong–Shenzhen Innovation and Technology Park at the Lok Ma Chau Loop anchor cross-boundary development.

Challenges

Integration must bridge "one country, two systems": three customs territories, three currencies (renminbi, Hong Kong dollar, Macanese pataca), and differing legal systems, with border controls between the SARs and the mainland. Measures such as the Wealth Management Connect, Bond Connect and talent schemes are intended to ease flows of capital, goods and people within the region.
