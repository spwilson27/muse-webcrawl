Battle of the Hornburg

The Battle of the Hornburg, also called the Battle of Helm's Deep, is a fictional battle in J. R. R. Tolkien's fantasy novel The Lord of the Rings (1954–55). It forms the climax of the second volume, The Two Towers, and depicts the defence of the fortress of Helm's Deep in Rohan against the army of the wizard Saruman. The battle is one of the best-known episodes in the novel and was a centrepiece of Peter Jackson's 2002 film adaptation.

Context

After the defeat at the Fords of Isen, King Théoden of Rohan withdraws with his people to the ancient stronghold of Helm's Deep in the White Mountains, a fortress that had never been taken. Saruman, seeking to destroy Rohan before turning against Gondor, sends his entire army of Uruk-hai, Orcs and Dunlending allies westward.

The battle in the novel

The assault begins on a rainy night. The attackers breach the Deeping Wall by exploding a culvert with a "blasting fire", and fighting rages on the walls and at the gate. At dawn Théoden and Aragorn lead a desperate cavalry sortie from the Hornburg, while Gandalf arrives with reinforcements under Erkenbrand. The fleeing enemy is driven into a forest of Huorns — tree-like beings from Fangorn — from which none escapes.

Adaptations

The battle occupies an extended sequence in The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers (2002), directed by Peter Jackson, where it was filmed on a large-scale set and miniature in New Zealand and is often cited among the greatest battle scenes in cinema. The earlier animated adaptation by Ralph Bakshi (1978) also depicts the siege.
