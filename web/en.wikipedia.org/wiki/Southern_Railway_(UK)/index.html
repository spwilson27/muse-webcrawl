Southern Railway (UK)

The Southern Railway was one of the "Big Four" railway companies created by the Railways Act 1921 in Britain. Formed in 1923 from the London and South Western Railway, the London, Brighton and South Coast Railway, the South Eastern and Chatham Railway and smaller constituents, it operated passenger and freight services across southern England until nationalisation in 1948.

Character

The Southern was the smallest of the Big Four by route mileage but carried the heaviest passenger traffic, thanks to dense London commuter services. Under general manager Sir Herbert Walker it pursued an ambitious electrification programme using third-rail direct current, creating by 1939 the largest electrified suburban network in the world. Its steam fleet, under chief mechanical engineers Richard Maunsell and later Oliver Bulleid, produced designs such as the Schools class and the innovative Merchant Navy pacifics.

Services

Famous trains included the Golden Arrow boat train to Paris, the Brighton Belle Pullman and the Atlantic Coast Express. The company also ran ferry services from Dover, Folkestone, Southampton and other ports, and promoted seaside resorts across the south coast.

Legacy

On nationalisation the Southern became the Southern Region of British Railways. Its malachite green livery and "Sunshine" lettering remain iconic, and the modern Southern train operator revives its name on many of the same routes.
