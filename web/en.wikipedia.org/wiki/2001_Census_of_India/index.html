The 2001 Census of India was the 14th in a series of decennial censuses conducted in India since 1872, and the 6th since independence. It recorded the population of India as 1,028,610,328, making India only the second country in the world (after China) to exceed one billion inhabitants.

Key findings

The census recorded a decadal growth rate of 21.54 percent, a decline from 23.87 percent in 1991. The overall literacy rate rose to 64.8 percent (75.3 percent for males and 53.7 percent for females). The sex ratio stood at 933 females per 1,000 males, a slight improvement on 927 in 1991, although the child sex ratio (ages 0–6) fell to 927, a matter of considerable concern.

Uttar Pradesh was the most populous state with over 166 million people — more than most countries — while Kerala recorded the highest literacy rate (90.9 percent) and the most favourable sex ratio (1,058). Hindus formed about 80.5 percent of the population and Muslims about 13.4 percent, with Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists and Jains making up most of the remainder.

Conduct

Enumeration took place in February 2001, preceded by a house-listing operation in 2000, and employed more than two million enumerators, mostly schoolteachers. The census was headed by the Registrar General and Census Commissioner of India.
