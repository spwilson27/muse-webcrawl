Tesla Supercharger

The Tesla Supercharger network is an electric vehicle fast charging network built and operated by Tesla, Inc. It consists primarily of 480-volt DC fast chargers intended to enable long-distance travel in Tesla vehicles, and since 2023 has progressively opened to other manufacturers' vehicles. The network was introduced in September 2012 with the first stations in California, initially serving the Model S. It has grown to tens of thousands of charging stalls at thousands of stations worldwide, making it one of the largest fast-charging networks in the world.

Technology

Superchargers deliver direct current (DC) power directly to the vehicle's battery, bypassing the onboard charger. Early V1 and V2 stalls delivered up to 120–150 kW, shared between paired stalls. V3 Superchargers, introduced in 2019, deliver up to 250 kW per vehicle with liquid-cooled cables and no power sharing; V4 cabinets support still higher rates and longer cables to accommodate non-Tesla vehicles. In North America, Tesla's connector was standardized as the North American Charging Standard (NACS) and adopted by most other automakers from 2025 onward.

Usage and pricing

Early Model S and Model X cars included free Supercharging for life; later vehicles use pay-per-use billing by the kilowatt-hour or by the minute, with idle fees to discourage occupying stalls after charging completes.
