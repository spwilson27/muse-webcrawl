Søren Aabye Kierkegaard (1813–1855) was a Danish philosopher and theologian widely regarded as the father of existentialism. Writing much of his work under pseudonyms, he explored how an individual becomes a self through choice, anxiety and faith, and attacked both Hegelian system-building and the comfortable state Christianity of his day, which he called 'Christendom'.

Thought

Kierkegaard distinguishes three spheres of existence: the aesthetic (pleasure), the ethical (duty and commitment), and the religious (faith). Fear and Trembling retells Abraham's binding of Isaac to show faith as a paradoxical 'teleological suspension of the ethical'. The Concept of Anxiety and The Sickness unto Death analyse despair as the self's refusal to be itself. His broken engagement to Regine Olsen haunted his life and writing. Rediscovered in the 20th century, he deeply influenced theology (Karl Barth), existentialism and psychology.
