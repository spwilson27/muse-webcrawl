Alexei Nikolaevich, Tsarevich of Russia

Alexei Nikolaevich (Russian: Алексей Николаевич; 12 August 1904 – 17 July 1918) was the youngest child and only son of Emperor Nicholas II and Empress Alexandra of Russia, and heir apparent to the Imperial throne. He suffered from hemophilia, which profoundly affected the imperial family's life and helped bring the mystic Grigori Rasputin to influence at court. He was murdered with his family by the Bolsheviks in 1918, aged thirteen.

Early life and illness

Alexei was born at Peterhof during the Russo-Japanese War. His hemophilia — inherited from his mother, a granddaughter of Queen Victoria — was revealed when bleeding from his navel would not stop in infancy. The disease caused painful internal bleeding episodes that sometimes brought him near death, most famously during the family's stay at Spała in 1912, after which Rasputin's intervention was credited by the Empress with saving him.

Revolution and captivity

After Nicholas II abdicated in March 1917 — for himself and, controversially, on Alexei's behalf — the family was held under house arrest at Tsarskoe Selo, then exiled to Tobolsk and finally to Yekaterinburg. Weakened by a bleeding episode provoked by a coughing fit, Alexei could not walk during his final weeks and was carried by his father.

Death and identification

In the early hours of 17 July 1918 the family was shot in the basement of the Ipatiev House. Alexei's remains, missing from the mass grave found in 1991, were discovered in a separate burial in 2007 and identified by DNA testing. He was canonized by the Russian Orthodox Church as a passion bearer.
