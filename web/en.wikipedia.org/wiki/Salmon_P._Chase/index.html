Salmon Portland Chase (January 13, 1808 – May 7, 1873) was an American politician and jurist who served as the sixth chief justice of the United States from 1864 to his death in 1873. He had earlier served as the 23rd governor of Ohio, represented Ohio in the United States Senate, and served as the 25th United States secretary of the treasury under President Abraham Lincoln during the American Civil War.

Born in Cornish, New Hampshire, Chase studied law under Attorney General William Wirt and moved to Cincinnati, Ohio, where he became known as a defender of fugitive slaves and a leading antislavery lawyer, earning the nickname "attorney general for fugitive slaves." He helped found the Liberty Party and later the Free Soil Party, and was elected to the Senate in 1849 as a Free Soiler. Chase served as governor of Ohio from 1856 to 1860 and helped build the new Republican Party.

Secretary of the Treasury

After narrowly losing the 1860 Republican presidential nomination to Abraham Lincoln, Chase accepted appointment as Secretary of the Treasury. In that role he financed the Union war effort, championing the Legal Tender Act of 1862, which authorized the issue of paper currency ("greenbacks"), and the National Banking Acts of 1863 and 1864, which created a system of federally chartered banks and a uniform national currency. His portrait appeared on the $1 bill of 1862 and, later, on the $10,000 bill.

Chase repeatedly maneuvered for the presidency, and after his resignation was accepted in June 1864, Lincoln nominated him Chief Justice following the death of Roger B. Taney.

Chief Justice

As Chief Justice, Chase presided over the impeachment trial of President Andrew Johnson in 1868, winning praise for his impartiality. His notable opinions include Texas v. White (1869), which held that the Union was indestructible and secession legally void, and Veazie Bank v. Fenno (1869), upholding Congress's taxing power. In the Legal Tender Cases his position shifted with the Court's composition, and he dissented in Knox v. Lee.

Despite serving on the bench, Chase continued to seek the presidency, pursuing the Democratic nomination in 1868 and the Liberal Republican nomination in 1872. He died in New York City in 1873 and was buried in Spring Grove Cemetery, Cincinnati. Chase National Bank, a predecessor of JPMorgan Chase, was named in his honor.
