Cheque

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A cheque (or check in American English) is a document that orders a bank, building society or other financial institution to pay a specific amount of money from a person's account to the person in whose name the cheque has been issued. The person writing the cheque is the drawer; the person named to receive the payment is the payee; and the institution directed to pay is the drawee. Cheques are a type of negotiable instrument known as a bill of exchange.

History

Precursors of the cheque include the sakk used by merchants in the medieval Islamic world and the bills of exchange of early modern Europe. Cheques drawn on London goldsmith-bankers proliferated in the 17th century, and printed cheque forms appeared in the 18th century. The Bills of Exchange Act 1882 codified cheque law in the United Kingdom, and the Cheques Act 1992 restricted the practice of crossing cheques "account payee."

Features and clearing

A modern cheque typically shows the date, payee name, amount in words and figures, the drawer's signature, and a machine-readable MICR line encoding the bank sort code (or ABA routing number in the United States), account number and cheque number. Historically, cheques were cleared by physically exchanging paper at clearing houses; most systems now use image-based clearing — truncation — under which an electronic image is exchanged instead (in the UK, the Image Clearing System introduced from 2017; in the US, under the Check 21 Act of 2003).

Decline

Cheque usage has fallen sharply in most developed economies with the rise of debit cards, direct debits and instant electronic payments. The Netherlands, and several Nordic countries, have effectively eliminated cheques, and UK cheque volumes have dropped from about 4 billion in 1990 to a small fraction of that number. Cheques remain comparatively common in the United States and in parts of Asia and the Middle East.

Types

Varieties include bearer and order cheques, crossed cheques, banker's drafts (cashier's checks), travellers' cheques, payroll cheques and government warrants.
