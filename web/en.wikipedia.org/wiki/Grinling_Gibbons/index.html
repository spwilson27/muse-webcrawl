Grinling Gibbons (4 April 1648 – 3 August 1721) was a Dutch-British sculptor and wood carver known for his work in England, including St Paul's Cathedral, Blenheim Palace, and Hampton Court Palace. He was born in Rotterdam to English parents and moved to London by 1667. Gibbons is widely regarded as the finest wood carver of his era.

Career

Gibbons was discovered by the diarist John Evelyn, who presented him to King Charles II. His workshop produced astonishingly naturalistic carvings in limewood — swags of flowers, fruits, leaves, fish, and birds — as well as work in stone and bronze. Appointed master carver in wood to the Crown, he executed major commissions for Charles II, William III, and George I.

Works

His carvings decorate the choirs and organ screens of St Paul's Cathedral, the state rooms at Hampton Court and Kensington Palace, Petworth House, and many country houses and churches. Gibbons also produced the bronze statue of James II now in Trafalgar Square and the marble font at St James's Church, Piccadilly.

Legacy

Horace Walpole called him a carver "who has given a new character to wood." A revival of interest in his work led to the founding of the Grinling Gibbons Society and a major tercentenary exhibition in 2021.
