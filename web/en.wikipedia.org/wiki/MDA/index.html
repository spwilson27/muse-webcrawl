MDA

MDA most commonly refers to 3,4-methylenedioxyamphetamine, also known as tenamfetamine, an entactogen and psychedelic drug of the amphetamine class. It is closely related to MDMA (ecstasy), of which it is also a metabolite, and produces a combination of stimulant, euphoric and mildly hallucinogenic effects. MDA is a controlled substance in most countries.

History

MDA was first synthesised in 1910 by the German chemists Carl Mannich and Willy Jacobsohn while working on sympathomimetic compounds, and was later investigated by the pharmaceutical company Smith, Kline & French as an appetite suppressant and cough medicine. During the 1960s it circulated as a recreational drug — sometimes known as the "love drug" — and was reported to have been tested by the US military in experiments involving interrogation aids. It was placed under control in the United States in 1970 and listed in Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act.

Effects

Typical doses are in the range of 80–160 mg taken orally, with effects lasting six to eight hours — somewhat longer than MDMA. Users report euphoria, increased empathy and sociability, enhanced sensory perception and visual effects that are more pronounced than those of MDMA. Adverse effects include increased heart rate and blood pressure, jaw clenching, hyperthermia and, at high doses or with repeated use, serotonergic neurotoxicity.

Pharmacology

MDA acts primarily as a releasing agent and reuptake inhibitor of serotonin, norepinephrine and dopamine, and directly activates serotonin receptors including 5-HT2A, which accounts for its psychedelic component. One of its two enantiomers is more serotonergic while the other is more stimulant. MDA is also formed in small amounts in the body when MDMA is metabolised.

Other uses of the abbreviation

The abbreviation MDA is also used for, among other things, Magen David Adom (Israel's national emergency medical service), the Missile Defense Agency of the United States, the IBM Monochrome Display Adapter, malondialdehyde in biochemistry, and model-driven architecture in software engineering.
