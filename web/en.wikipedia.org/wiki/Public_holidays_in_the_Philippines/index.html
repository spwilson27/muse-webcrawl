Public holidays in the Philippines

Public holidays in the Philippines are of two kinds: regular holidays, on which employees who do not work are generally entitled to full pay, and special non-working days, which follow a "no work, no pay" principle unless company policy provides otherwise. The list, consolidated under Executive Order No. 292 (the Administrative Code of 1987) as amended by Republic Act No. 9492 of 2007, combines national, historical and religious observances reflecting the country's predominantly Catholic heritage together with Muslim holidays recognised nationwide or in the Bangsamoro region.

Special non-working days

Special days include Chinese New Year, Black Saturday, Ninoy Aquino Day (21 August), All Saints' Day (1 November), All Souls' Day (2 November, when declared), the Feast of the Immaculate Conception (8 December, made a special non-working holiday by Republic Act No. 10966), and the last day of the year (31 December). Additional special days are frequently proclaimed by the President, and local holidays mark provincial and city foundation days and patronal fiestas.

Holiday economics

Republic Act No. 9492 moved the observance of several holidays to the nearest Monday to create long weekends and promote domestic tourism — a policy known as "holiday economics". The Philippines' Holy Week (Semana Santa) shutdown and the long Christmas season, among the longest in the world, anchor the holiday calendar culturally and commercially.
