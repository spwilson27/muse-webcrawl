Peruvian booby

The Peruvian booby (Sula variegata) is a seabird of the booby family, Sulidae, found along the Pacific coast of South America within the cold, nutrient-rich waters of the Humboldt Current. It is distinguished by its white head and underparts, dark brown wings and back flecked with white, and a long grey pointed bill.

Ecology

Peruvian boobies feed almost entirely on anchoveta, plunge-diving in large flocks over the shoals. They breed colonially on offshore islands and rocky headlands off Peru and northern Chile. Their numbers rise and crash with the anchoveta population: during El Niño events, when the anchoveta disperse or collapse, mass starvation and breeding failure can reduce the population by millions.

Guano

Together with the guanay cormorant and the brown pelican, the Peruvian booby is one of the great guano-producing birds of the Peruvian guano islands. Its accumulated droppings were a major source of fertiliser in the 19th century and are still harvested under state management; protecting the birds' food supply is central to modern conservation of the islands.
