Cape Coral is a city located in Lee County, Florida, United States, on the Gulf of Mexico. Founded in 1957 and developed as a planned community, the city grew with an elaborate system of canals, making it known as a "Waterfront Wonderland". With more than 640 km (400 miles) of navigable waterways, Cape Coral has more miles of canals than any other city in the world.

History

Cape Coral was founded in 1957 by brothers Leonard and Jack Rosen, real estate developers who purchased a 103 km² tract known as Redfish Point on the Caloosahatchee River. The Gulf American Land Corporation developed the community, digging canals and marketing lots heavily in northern cities. The first residents moved in during 1958, and the city was incorporated in 1970.

Geography

The city occupies a large peninsula bordered by the Caloosahatchee River to the south and Matlacha Pass to the west. Its canal system provides many residents with direct boating access to the Gulf of Mexico. The climate is subtropical, with hot, humid summers and mild winters; the city was severely affected by Hurricane Ian in 2022.

Demographics

Cape Coral is the largest city between Tampa and Miami by population and is a principal city of the Cape Coral–Fort Myers metropolitan area. Retirees and seasonal residents form a significant part of the population.
