Robert Ervin Howard (22 January 1906 – 11 June 1936) was an American writer who wrote pulp fiction in a diverse range of genres. He created the character Conan the Barbarian and is regarded as the founder of the sword and sorcery subgenre of fantasy.

Life

Howard was born in Peaster, Texas, and spent most of his life in the small town of Cross Plains. The son of a travelling country doctor, he was a voracious reader and an introspective, bookish youth who took up boxing and bodybuilding. He sold his first story, "Spear and Fang", to Weird Tales in 1925, and became one of the magazine's defining contributors alongside H. P. Lovecraft, with whom he corresponded extensively, and Clark Ashton Smith.

Works

Howard wrote in many pulp markets: horror (the Solomon Kane Puritan adventurer stories, beginning with "Red Shadows" in 1928), historical adventure (the Kull of Atlantis stories from 1929), boxing tales, westerns both humorous and straight, and the Conan saga, which began with "The Phoenix on the Sword" in December 1932. In all he wrote twenty-one Conan stories, including the novel The Hour of the Dragon. His work is noted for its vigorous, driving prose, its sense of "barbarism triumphant," and dark, cyclical views of civilisation.

Death and legacy

Deeply devoted to his mother, who had long been ill, Howard shot himself on 11 June 1936 as she slipped into a coma from which she was not expected to recover; he died later that day, aged thirty. His reputation grew posthumously: paperback reprints of Conan in the 1960s, edited by L. Sprague de Camp and illustrated by Frank Frazetta, sparked a fantasy boom, and his unexpurgated texts were restored in scholarly editions from the 2000s. The Robert E. Howard Museum in Cross Plains hosts annual Howard Days celebrations.
