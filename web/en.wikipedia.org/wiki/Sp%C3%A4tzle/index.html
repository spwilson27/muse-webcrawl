Spätzle

TypeEgg noodle / dumpling
RegionSwabia, southern Germany; also Austria, Switzerland, Alsace, Hungary
Main ingredientsEggs, flour, salt, water or milk

Spätzle (Swabian diminutive of Spatz, "sparrow") is a type of soft egg noodle or dumpling found in the cuisines of southern Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Hungary, Slovenia, and Alsace. Made from a batter of eggs, flour, and salt, the dough is scraped, pressed, or dropped into boiling water, where the noodles cook until they float.

Preparation

Traditional methods include the hand-scraped Handspätzle, formed on a wet board with a knife; the Spätzlepresse, similar to a potato ricer; the perforated Spätzlehobel; and the Knöpfle, shorter button-shaped noodles made through a coarser sieve. The noodles are typically served with butter, fried onions, or gravy.

Dishes

Well-known dishes include Kässpätzle (with melted cheese and fried onions), Linsen mit Spätzle (with lentils, a Swabian staple), Krautspätzle (with sauerkraut), and Hungarian nokedli or galuska, served with stews such as goulash and chicken paprikash. Spätzle from Swabia have enjoyed EU protected geographical indication status since 2012.

History

The dish's origins are debated, with written recipes dating to the 18th century and images of spätzle presses from 1725. It became emblematic of Swabian identity and poverty cooking, since the ingredients were cheap and filling.
