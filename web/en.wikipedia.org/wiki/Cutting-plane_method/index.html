Cutting-plane method

In mathematical optimization, the cutting-plane method is any of a variety of optimization methods that iteratively refine a feasible set or objective function by means of linear inequalities, termed cuts.

Integer programming

For integer linear programming, the method was pioneered by Ralph Gomory in 1958. One solves the linear programming relaxation, then adds a linear constraint (a "cutting plane") that separates the fractional solution from the feasible set without excluding any integer feasible point, and repeats until an integer solution is found.

Convex optimization

Related cutting-plane methods, such as Kelley's method and the ellipsoid method, apply to convex optimization problems by iteratively approximating the feasible region with polyhedra. Modern mixed-integer solvers combine cutting planes with branch and bound in "branch and cut" algorithms.
