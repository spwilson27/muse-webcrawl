A metallophone is any musical instrument in which the sound-producing body is a piece of metal (other than a metal string), such as tuned metal bars, tubes, rods, bowls or plates. The term is commonly applied to instruments consisting of tuned metal bars struck with mallets — the metal-bar counterpart of the wooden-barred xylophone.

Types

In Western music the best-known metallophones are the glockenspiel (orchestral bells) and the vibraphone, with motor-driven rotating discs in its resonators; the celesta is a keyboard metallophone whose hammers strike steel plates. Tubular bells (chimes) use metal tubes. In Indonesian gamelan, metallophones form the melodic core of the ensemble: the saron family has thick bronze keys over trough resonators, while the gender has thin keys suspended over bamboo tube resonators, played with padded mallets. Thai ranat thum lek and Cambodian roneat are related instruments. Carl Orff's school music programme popularised removable-bar soprano and alto metallophones in classrooms worldwide, alongside xylophones and glockenspiels.
