Sarajevo Haggadah
Datec. 1350
Place of originBarcelona, Crown of Aragon (Spain)
LanguageHebrew
MaterialIlluminated manuscript on bleached calfskin (vellum); 142 leaves
LocationNational Museum of Bosnia and Herzegovina, Sarajevo

The Sarajevo Haggadah is an illuminated Haggadah — the text read at the Passover Seder recounting the Exodus from Egypt — created in Barcelona around 1350. It is among the oldest and most valuable Sephardic Haggadahs in existence, celebrated both as a masterpiece of medieval Jewish book art and for its extraordinary history of survival. It is kept at the National Museum of Bosnia and Herzegovina in Sarajevo.

Description

The manuscript, written on bleached calfskin, contains 34 pages of illuminated miniatures depicting scenes from the Hebrew Bible — from the Creation to the death of Moses — a feature unique among medieval Haggadahs, followed by the Haggadah text and additional liturgical poems and commentaries. Unusually for a Jewish manuscript of the period, its images include human figures, contrary to a strict reading of the prohibition on graven images; the style shows the influence of Gothic art in the Crown of Aragon. Wine stains and marginal notes accumulated over centuries of use testify to its long service at family Seders.

History

The Haggadah's early history is uncertain. It is believed to have left Spain with Jewish exiles after the Alhambra Decree of 1492 expelled the Jews, later appearing in Italy, where a censor's note of 1609 confirms its presence. It reached Sarajevo, a centre of Sephardic life, at an unknown date, and was sold to the National Museum in 1894 by a member of the Koen family.

During the Second World War, the museum's director Jozo Petrović and chief librarian Derviš Korkut, a Muslim scholar, hid the Haggadah from Nazi officers who sought it in 1941–42, reportedly smuggling it to a village in the mountains. During the Bosnian War and the siege of Sarajevo in the 1990s it survived in a bank vault; false reports of its sale or destruction circulated, prompting its public display in 1995. Derviš Korkut and his wife Servet were later recognised by Yad Vashem as Righteous Among the Nations for also sheltering a Jewish girl during the Holocaust.

Conservation and recognition

The manuscript was restored with international funding and placed on permanent display in a specially secured room of the National Museum in 2002. In 2017 UNESCO included the Sarajevo Haggadah in its Memory of the World register. Its story inspired Geraldine Brooks's bestselling novel People of the Book (2008).
