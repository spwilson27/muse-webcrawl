Fool's gold

Fool's gold is a nickname for the mineral pyrite (iron pyrite, FeS2), whose pale brass-yellow hue and metallic luster have caused it to be mistaken for gold by inexperienced prospectors. The name is also sometimes applied to chalcopyrite and to weathered biotite mica.

Distinguishing pyrite from gold

Despite the resemblance, pyrite and gold are readily distinguished. Gold is soft (Mohs hardness 2.5), malleable, and leaves a yellow streak; pyrite is much harder (6–6.5), brittle — it shatters rather than dents when struck — and leaves a greenish-black streak. Pyrite crystals commonly form cubes with striated faces, whereas native gold occurs as nuggets, flakes, and wires.

History

During gold rushes of the nineteenth century, many hopeful miners were deceived by pyrite, giving rise to the expression. Ironically, pyrite is often found in association with real gold deposits, and can itself contain minute, economically recoverable traces of gold. See also Pyrite.
