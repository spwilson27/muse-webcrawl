Zaydism

Zaydism is a branch of Shia Islam that emerged in the 8th century CE following Zayd ibn Ali's unsuccessful revolt against the Umayyad Caliphate in 740. Zaydis are the oldest surviving Shia sect and are today concentrated in northern Yemen.

Zaydism
Founder: Zayd ibn Ali
Region: Northern Yemen
Branch: Shia Islam
School: Mu'tazila-influenced theology; Hanafi-like fiqh

Doctrine

Zaydis hold that any learned descendant of Hasan or Husayn who rises against tyranny may be imam; they do not believe in the infallibility or occultation of imams, distinguishing them from Twelver Shia. Zaydi theology is close to Mu'tazilism, and its jurisprudence is relatively close to Sunni schools.

History

Zaydi states were founded in Tabaristan (northern Iran) and in Yemen, where the imamate established by al-Hadi Yahya in 897 endured, in varying form, until the 1962 civil war ended the Mutawakkilite Kingdom. In modern Yemen, Zaydis form a large minority; the Houthi movement (Ansar Allah) emerged from Zaydi revivalism in Saada in the 1990s–2000s.
